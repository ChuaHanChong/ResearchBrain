# EgoActor: Grounding Task Planning into Spatial-aware Egocentric Actions for Humanoid Robots via Visual-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoActor, developed by BAAI, introduces a unified Vision-Language Model framework enabling humanoid robots to translate natural language instructions into a range of spatially-aware, egocentric actions. It achieves high success rates in real-world human-robot interaction and mobile manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming navigation-only baselines.

## Problem

- Integrating perception, locomotion, and manipulation into a cohesive system for humanoid robots in dynamic, real-world settings.
- Coordinating diverse humanoid behaviors (movement, head orientation, manipulation, human interaction) from high-level natural language instructions.
- Developing advanced spatial understanding and reasoning for robots to perform contextually appropriate and precise actions.

## Method

- Instantiates "EgoActing," a task formulated for a unified VLM (based on Qwen3-VL) that directly predicts low-level, egocentric actions in textual format (==Structured Language Actions== and Natural Language Actions).
- Employs a diverse and scalable data acquisition strategy, including real-world demonstrations, spatial reasoning datasets, and virtual environment trajectories, to train the VLM.
- Integrates with existing, calibrated low-level skills for manipulation, locomotion, and human interaction, allowing the VLM to coordinate these capabilities in real-time.

## Results

- Achieved high success rates (11/12 to 12/12) in real-world human-robot interaction and mobile manipulation tasks, successfully handling multi-person disambiguation and unseen objects.
- Demonstrated superior traversability in narrow real-world spaces, with 7/8 to 8/8 successes for entering/leaving rooms in unseen environments, significantly outperforming navigation-only baselines (0/8 to 5/8 successes).
- Exhibited strong generalization in unseen virtual environments, with the 8B model achieving **51.4%** success at <0.5m distance and **89.9%** at <3.0m, dramatically surpassing navigation-only baselines (**6.3%** to **8.8%** success at <0.5m).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single Vision-Language Model can unify the prediction of diverse humanoid actions, including locomotion, active perception, manipulation, and human interaction, by grounding high-level instructions into egocentric, textual commands.
> - Extensive and varied supervision, drawing from real-world demonstrations and synthetic data, is critical for achieving robust generalization and fluent action inference in dynamic, unseen environments.
> - Directly predicting spatially-aware, low-level actions from egocentric observations allows for superior collision avoidance, adaptive movements, and human-like behaviors, enhancing robot robustness and interaction quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

EgoActor operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Vision-Language Models (VLMs), embodied AI, mobile manipulation, and humanoid robotics.

**VLM-based Embodied Agents:** Traditional VLM-based embodied agents, such as SayCan, often rely on predefined skill libraries and are frequently evaluated in simulated or structured environments. While recent surveys highlight progress in multimodal embodied agents, they also identify key challenges in grounding, long-horizon reasoning, and real-world deployment. EgoActor distinguishes itself by focusing specifically on **humanoid robots** and directly predicting **low-level, egocentric, executable actions**, thus bridging the gap between high-level textual instructions and low-level motor control for complex robotic platforms. This contrasts with approaches that typically abstract away the robot's physical embodiment or rely on simplified action spaces.

**Mobile Manipulation:** Previous work in mobile manipulation has explored combining language models with affordance-based planners or developing simulation platforms for joint navigation and manipulation. However, these systems often decompose tasks into modular subgoals or stage-wise controllers for perception, locomotion, and manipulation. EgoActor unifies decision-making at an "egocentric level," integrating reasoning over locomotion, posture, spatial perception, manipulation, and human interaction within a **single VLM-based action predictor**. This integrated approach is crucial for enabling smooth transitions between different action types and robust adaptation in dynamic, unseen environments, a significant advancement over modular, pipeline-driven systems.

**Visual-Language Navigation (VLN):** VLN has been a core embodied AI problem, primarily focusing on mapping language instructions to navigational trajectories. Works like R2R, Touchdown, and more recent VLM-enhanced methods such as VLN-R1, NaVid, Uni-NaVid, and Navila aim to improve generalization and long-horizon reasoning for navigation tasks. Object-goal navigation extends this by requiring navigation to specified object categories. EgoActor significantly expands beyond these navigation-centric paradigms. It coordinates **whole-body humanoid behaviors** by jointly reasoning over locomotion, posture control, active perception, manipulation, and human-interaction. This enables it to ground high-level instructions into actionable, egocentric motor sequences suitable for real-world humanoid control in dynamic environments, tackling a much broader range of tasks than pure navigation.

In summary, EgoActor pushes the boundaries by moving beyond modular approaches and simplified embodiments, demonstrating a unified, real-world deployable solution for complex, multi-modal task execution on full humanoid robots. Its focus on directly predicting low-level, spatially aware actions from egocentric observations for a diverse set of behaviors represents a crucial step towards more generalist and autonomous humanoid agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental challenge addressed by this paper is the **deployment of humanoid robots in real-world settings**, which demands a tight and robust integration of perception, locomotion, and manipulation under partial information and dynamic environments, alongside seamless transitions between diverse sub-tasks. Humanoid robots are inherently unstable and sensitive to timing and precision, making even minor control inaccuracies problematic. While low-level control for locomotion and dexterous manipulation has seen advances, these capabilities often operate in isolation and require sophisticated coordination based on visual and spatial understanding.

The paper identifies that real-world tasks rarely involve isolated actions; instead, they require the **coordinated use of movement, head orientation, hand manipulation, and human-robot interactions** in contextually appropriate sequences. For example, a robot might need to stop walking, tilt its head to perceive a target, grasp an object, and then resume locomotion, all as part of a single coherent instruction. Achieving this coordination necessitates not only reliable motor control but also advanced **spatial understanding and reasoning** to infer how actions interact with the environment and when and how to combine multiple skills effectively.

To tackle these challenges, the authors introduce a novel task formulation called **EgoActing**. The key objectives of EgoActing, and by extension EgoActor, are:
- **Directly grounding high-level natural language instructions into various, precise, spatially aware humanoid actions** based on egocentric observations, action history, and available skills.
- **Developing a unified and scalable Vision-Language Model (VLM), EgoActor**, that can predict a comprehensive range of low-level actions: locomotion primitives (walk, turn, strafe, change height), head movements (active perception), manipulation commands, and human-robot interactions. This aims to coordinate perception and execution in real-time.
- **Leveraging diverse and scalable supervision** from real-world demonstrations, spatial reasoning question-answering, and simulated environments to enable robust, context-aware decision-making and fluent action inference (under 1 second).
- **Demonstrating generalization** across diverse tasks and unseen environments in both simulated and real-world settings, effectively bridging abstract task planning and concrete motor execution for humanoid robots.

The ultimate motivation is to enable humanoid robots to conduct long-horizon, multi-step tasks described in natural language, moving closer to practical and generalist humanoid autonomy in unstructured real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoActor instantiates the newly defined **EgoActing** task, which involves taking a direct, actionable natural language instruction and egocentric observations, then predicting the next concrete actions for a humanoid robot. The framework is designed around a unified VLM that processes visual inputs and language instructions to output a sequence of low-level, executable robot actions.

**A. Task Definition (EgoActing):**
The task is formalized as predicting the action $a_t$ that maximizes the probability $P(a | I, O_{1:t}, a_{1:t-1}, \Pi)$, where $I$ is the natural language instruction, $O_{1:t}$ is the history of egocentric RGB observations, $a_{1:t-1}$ is the past action history, and $\Pi$ is the set of available low-level whole-body control and manipulation policies. The instruction is high-level but explicit, describing movements and goals without low-level motor details.

**B. Language-based Actions:**
EgoActor predicts actions in a textual format, categorized into:
- **Structured Language Actions (SLAs):** Used for precise spatial control, including movement (e.g., "Turn left 30.5 degrees", "Move forward 0.26 meters", "Left sidewalk 0.40 meters", "Rise up 0.12 meters", "Lower down 0.08 meters") and active perception (e.g., "Look up 10.0 degrees"). These actions specify type, direction, and magnitude, enabling the robot to interpret spatial relationships and position itself appropriately.
- **Natural Language Actions (NLAs):** Used for manipulation (e.g., "Pick up the water bottle", "Open the door") and human interaction (e.g., "Ask 'How you doing?'", "Say hi to the boy"). NLAs provide generalization beyond fixed skill sets, allowing the interpretation of novel instructions and context-aware commands for complex, open-ended interactions. "Stop and no action" is also an NLA.
An action parser converts these textual actions into robot-specific commands: SLAs become velocity/angle commands, and NLAs trigger predefined motions or VLA models based on keywords.

**C. Model Structure and Training Setup:**
- **Base Model:** Qwen3-VL, a transformer-based VLM with dynamic resolution support, is used as the base architecture.
- **Fine-tuning:** LoRA is applied to fine-tune all linear layers of the model, enabling efficient adaptation.
- **Model Sizes:** Both 4B and 8B parameter variants are trained to explore the trade-off between inference speed and performance. Inference latency is sub-second, supporting real-time interaction.
- **Data Format:** The input prompt explicitly exposes the model to egocentric visual context, temporal history, and recent observation-action pairs. It includes 10 sampled historical observations (240p) and the most recent 3 observation-action pairs (480p) as key anchors for predicting the next action.

**D. Data Acquisition (Diverse Supervision):**
A cornerstone of EgoActor's methodology is its extensive and diverse data acquisition strategy, minimizing human annotation requirements:
- **Internet Video Data:** EgoTaskQA dataset (160,000 samples) and 130 additional internet videos (7,111 samples).
- **Local Environment Data (EgoActing):** 398 egocentric videos recorded in local environments with frequent layout changes (150,214 samples).
- **Virtual Environment Data (Navigation):** Sampled 3% of VLN-CE (Room-to-Room) training set for controlled spatial navigation (60,000 samples).
- **Virtual Environment Data (EgoActing):** Manually collected and annotated 714 EgoActing-style trajectories from Habitat-Sim (76,821 samples).
- **Spatial Reasoning Data (MindCube):** 50% of MindCube training set to strengthen spatial reasoning (44,160 samples).
- **Visual-Language Understanding Data:** 300,000 instances from GQA dataset and 35,652 GPT-4o annotated descriptions from local environments.
- **Visual-Language Planning Data:** High-level planning data from RoboVQA, EgoPlan, and ALFRED (241,603 samples).
- **Unsupervised Movement Prediction Data:** 10,575 samples predicting movement transitions between image pairs to enhance spatial understanding.
- **DAgger Experience Data:** 70 successful on-policy trajectories from real-world executions using DAgger (3,629 samples), providing direct feedback.

Data is processed by extracting step-by-step movement actions (using MASt3R for camera poses), aggregating navigation actions, and constructing interleaved observation-action pairs. Oversampling turning actions and NLAs is applied to balance the distribution.

**E. Skill Setup:**
- **Manipulation:** A fine-tuned GROOT-N 1.5 model handles manipulation tasks.
- **Locomotion:** The official Unitree walking policy is adopted as the movement controller, with manual calibration for positional (5 cm) and angular (5 degrees) precision. Stand-up and crouch-down skills are implemented in simulation.
- **Human Interaction:** Speech-related NLAs are converted to audio via text-to-speech, predefined interaction keywords invoke preset motions, and other NLAs are forwarded to VLA models. Forward movement distances are amplified by 1.2 for faster, more human-like motion, and actions like moving forward and turning are merged.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a unified VLM architecture with diverse, scalable data and a flexible action representation, enables EgoActor to bridge the complexities of high-level task planning and low-level motor execution for humanoid robots in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EgoActor's performance was rigorously evaluated in both real-world and virtual environments across diverse tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in grounding instructions into executable actions for humanoid robots.

**A. Real-world Benchmarking:**
- **Human-robot Interaction (Tables I & II):**
 - EgoActor (both 4B and 8B variants) consistently achieved high success rates (11/12 to 12/12) in approaching a single person and performing basic interactions (say hi, ask for location, request items), significantly outperforming baseline navigation models (NaVILA, NaVid, UniNaVid) that only approach the person but cannot interact.
 - In multi-person scenarios requiring person disambiguation based on attributes (clothing, accessories, posture, direction, gender), the 8B model showed stronger performance (e.g., 11/12 for clothing, 12/12 for direction) compared to the 4B model, which exhibited more failures, particularly when fine-grained identification was needed. This highlights the importance of model capacity for complex visual reasoning.
 - Qualitative case studies (Appendix XIV-C) confirmed EgoActor's ability to translate task intent into appropriate dialogue (e.g., "Ask 'How do you cook Kung Pao Chicken?'").
- **Mobile Manipulation (Table III):**
 - Evaluated in an unseen environment layout with objects at various positions. The 8B EgoActor demonstrated strong robustness, successfully navigating to and supporting manipulation (pick and place) for both seen (e.g., apple, bottle) and unseen objects (e.g., pen holder, pink cup) in most cases (5/6 to 6/6 successes).
 - The 4B model showed weaker performance, primarily failing when attempting manipulation from too far away.
 - The model demonstrated adaptive motion for mobile manipulation, taking larger steps to approach the workspace and then refining with smaller, fine-grained movements for precise object alignment, even in cluttered conditions (Appendix XIV-A).
- **Traversability (Table IV):**
 - EgoActor significantly outperformed baseline VLM-based navigation models in safely traversing narrow spaces, such as doorways, in both seen and unseen real-world environments. For instance, in unseen environments, EgoActor-4B and 8B achieved 7/8 to 8/8 successes for entering/leaving rooms, while baselines struggled (0/8 to 5/8).
 - Existing VLN models often collided with door frames or exhibited unnecessary rotations, underscoring EgoActor's improved robustness in confined spaces and its ability to avoid collisions. Qualitative results (Section V-E, Appendix XIV-B) further illustrated its ability to generalize to unseen obstacles (e.g., a rope) and find alternative paths.

**B. Virtual Environment Benchmarking (Table V):**
- Evaluated on 205 EgoActing samples from unseen virtual environments, EgoActor generalized well. The 8B model achieved 51.4% success at <0.5m distance and 89.9% at <3.0m, with an NLA F1 score of 0.62. The 4B model showed comparable overall performance.
- EgoActor dramatically outperformed navigation-only baselines (NaVILA, NaVid, UniNaVid), which achieved only 6.3% to 8.8% success at <0.5m and around 50-60% at <3.0m. This highlights EgoActor's superior precision in stopping at appropriate locations for interaction, where baselines often hallucinated continued navigation.
- Failure cases for EgoActor primarily stemmed from ambiguous instructions or visually degraded virtual environments, especially in unfamiliar scene types (e.g., churches).

**C. Case Studies (Section V-E):**
- **Obstacle Avoidance:** The robot rarely collided, even with unseen obstacles like a rope, demonstrating adaptive path adjustments. It often looked downward to verify obstacle positions.
- **Active Perception:** EgoActor actively moved its head to explore surroundings, fixating on targets, and adjusting its gaze. An example showed it actively backing up and looking upward to identify a person's upper-body clothing when only lower bodies were initially visible, fulfilling the task of greeting a specific person (Figure 5).
- **Spatial Understanding:** Unlike simulation-trained VLMs, EgoActor (trained on human videos) showed nuanced spatial reasoning, predicting different movement distances based on path width and adjusting turning angles for various spatial configurations.
- **Human-like Behaviors:** The model exhibited natural behaviors like moving backward when too close to obstacles, reorienting after manipulation, and combining forward, turning, and strafing actions for smooth, human-like corner turns (Figures 5 & 6). Height adjustments were also observed in virtual environments (Figure 7).

In essence, the results validate EgoActor's core hypothesis: a unified VLM, trained with diverse data, can effectively ground high-level instructions into precise, multi-modal actions for humanoid robots, demonstrating robust generalization, superior collision avoidance, and natural interaction capabilities in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoActor represents a significant step forward in the quest for generalist humanoid robot autonomy, addressing several long-standing challenges in the field. Its primary significance lies in its ability to **bridge the gap between abstract task planning and concrete motor execution** for complex humanoid platforms, moving beyond fragmented approaches.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
1. **Unified and Comprehensive Action Space:** EgoActor unifies a broad spectrum of humanoid capabilities – movement (locomotion, posture), active perception (head movements), manipulation (hand/arm actions), and human interaction (gestures, utterances) – within a single VLM. This integrated approach, dubbed "EgoActing," allows for seamless, coordinated execution of multi-step tasks that traditionally require complex, modular pipelines. This unification is critical for fluid and natural robot behavior in real-world scenarios.
2. **Robust Instruction Grounding:** By directly grounding high-level natural language instructions into low-level, spatially aware egocentric actions, EgoActor enables humanoid robots to interpret and execute complex commands without requiring human intervention for low-level motor programming. This enhances the robot's versatility and usability.
3. **Real-world Generalization and Robustness:** The extensive evaluation in both simulated and, crucially, diverse real-world environments demonstrates EgoActor's strong generalization capabilities. It handles unseen layouts, objects, and even novel obstacles with impressive robustness, outperforming existing VLM-based navigation models, particularly in challenging scenarios like narrow-space traversability and precise object interaction.
4. **Scalable Data-driven Approach:** The methodology leverages a diverse and easily scalable data acquisition strategy, including real-world demonstrations, spatial reasoning datasets, and synthetic data. This approach minimizes reliance on costly, bespoke human annotations and sets the stage for future performance improvements through increased data.
5. **Real-time Performance:** With sub-second inference latency, EgoActor supports real-time interaction and control on humanoid platforms, a prerequisite for dynamic and responsive real-world deployment.
6. **Enhanced Spatial Understanding and Human-like Behaviors:** The training on egocentric videos and diverse spatial data imbues EgoActor with strong spatial reasoning, enabling adaptive movements, active perception (e.g., looking to disambiguate targets), and even human-like movement patterns that enhance natural interaction and efficiency.
7. **Open Science Contribution:** The authors commit to releasing open-source code, models, datasets, and benchmarks. This is a crucial contribution that will facilitate reproducibility, accelerate future research in humanoid robotics, and establish a new baseline for the EgoActing task.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Humanoid Robot Deployment:** EgoActor offers a practical and scalable step towards deploying humanoid robots in unstructured environments for a variety of tasks, from household assistance to industrial logistics and human-robot collaboration.
- **Foundation for Generalist Agents:** By integrating diverse capabilities into a single framework, EgoActor contributes to the development of more generalist embodied AI agents capable of handling complex, open-ended tasks without extensive retraining for each new scenario.
- **Advancement of VLM Applications:** This work showcases the power of VLMs in a highly challenging domain, pushing the boundaries of what these models can achieve in terms of real-world physical embodiment and intelligent action generation.
- **New Benchmarking Paradigms:** The introduction of the EgoActing task and its associated benchmarks provides a new framework for evaluating the integrated capabilities required for truly autonomous humanoid robots.

**Limitations and Future Work (from paper's Section VII):**
The paper acknowledges that EgoActor relies on external components (high-level planners and downstream skills like locomotion and manipulation policies), meaning it is not a fully end-to-end system. Future work could explore integrating these capabilities into a single, unified framework. Another limitation is its handling of long-term context, where the model might occasionally fall into locally optimal but incorrect decision patterns during extended or multi-stage tasks. Addressing these limitations will be crucial for further enhancing humanoid autonomy.

In conclusion, EgoActor represents a significant advancement in grounding abstract instructions into comprehensive, spatially-aware actions for humanoid robots. Its unified VLM approach, coupled with extensive real-world validation, offers a promising pathway towards more capable, generalist, and deployable humanoid agents, with substantial implications for future research and applications in embodied AI.
