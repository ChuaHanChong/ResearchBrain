# Direct-a-Video: Customized Video Generation with User-Directed Camera Movement and Object Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> A text-to-video generation framework called Direct-a-Video enables users to independently control camera movement and multiple object motions within synthesized videos. The approach achieves superior video quality and more precise camera and object control compared to existing methods, as evidenced by lower FVD/FID-vid scores and improved flow error, mIoU, and CLIP-sim metrics.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-Video (T2V) models lack precise and disentangled user control over camera movement and individual object motions.
- Current T2V methods often implicitly couple camera and object motions, leading to ambiguity and limited flexibility in video customization.
- Training controllable T2V models typically requires large, labor-intensive datasets annotated with explicit camera poses and object trajectories.

## Method

- Direct-a-Video introduces a text-to-video generation framework that decouples camera movement and object motion control.
- Camera control is achieved through a self-supervised training approach, utilizing camera augmentation on static videos and a novel camera module with a separated panning/zooming embedder.
- Object motion control is implemented via a training-free inference-time method that modulates spatial ==cross-attention== within the ==diffusion model== using user-defined bounding box trajectories.

## Results

- Direct-a-Video achieves superior visual quality (lower FVD and FID-vid scores) compared to AnimateDiff and VideoComposer.
- The framework demonstrates more precise camera control (lower Flow error) and object control (higher CLIP-sim, mIoU, and AP50) than competing methods like VideoComposer and Peekaboo.
- It successfully generates videos with jointly controlled, disentangled camera movements (e.g., pan, zoom) and multiple independently moving objects, proving its enhanced directorial capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling camera movement and object motion control allows for precise and flexible video direction.
> - Self-supervised training using synthetically augmented camera motion can effectively equip text-to-video models with quantitative camera control without manual annotation.
> - A training-free attention modulation strategy during inference can guide object motion based on bounding boxes, bypassing the need for extensive annotated video grounding datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly advancing field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on **Text-to-Video (T2V) generation** using diffusion models. The landscape has seen immense progress, largely spurred by the success of **Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models** (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, Imagen) which can generate high-quality and diverse images from textual prompts. T2V models are a natural extension of T2I models, incorporating temporal layers to maintain consistency and motion across frames.

However, a critical challenge in T2V generation has been the lack of precise and disentangled user control over video dynamics. Existing T2V methods, while impressive in synthesizing coherent video clips, often fall short in allowing users to dictate specific **camera movements** (like panning, zooming) and **object motions** independently. The broader research landscape can be categorized by motion control approaches:

- **Image-to-Video:** Focuses on animating static images, often using sparse controls like key points (e.g., DragVideo). These methods offer intuitive interaction but are limited in controlling large-scale motions.
- **Video-to-Video (Motion Transfer):** Involves transferring motion from a source video to a target video, relying heavily on existing motion priors or dense/sparse feature conditioning (e.g., VideoSwap, Tune-a-Video). While effective, they require a source video with the desired motion, which may not always be available.
- **Text-to-Video with Controllable Motion:** This is the category Direct-a-Video falls into. Previous works in this area, such as AnimateDiff, offer specific camera movements but lack quantitative control. VideoComposer provides global motion guidance via pixel-wise motion vectors, which is cumbersome for users and couples camera and object motions. Concurrent works like Peekaboo focus on object trajectory but typically lack multi-object support or camera control. MotionCtrl allows separate 2D object and 3D camera control but requires labor-intensive data annotation.

"Direct-a-Video" addresses a significant gap by offering **decoupled and user-directed control over both camera movement and multiple object motions within a single framework**. This disentanglement is crucial because overall video motion is a complex interplay of both elements. By providing independent control, the paper offers a more flexible and intuitive solution compared to prior works that either implicitly couple these motions or provide limited, non-quantitative control. The authors' strategy of using **self-supervised learning for camera control** and a **training-free approach for object motion control** also positions it as a computationally efficient solution that bypasses the need for extensive, labor-intensive motion annotations, a common bottleneck in video research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a text-to-video generation framework, named **Direct-a-Video**, that empowers users with **independent and joint control over camera movement and object motion**. The system aims to allow users to "direct a video" as if they were behind the camera and dictating object actions, creating highly customized video content.

The motivation behind Direct-a-Video stems from several limitations observed in existing text-to-video diffusion models:

- **Lack of Disentangled Control:** Current T2V models often treat overall video motion as a monolithic entity. However, video motion is a combination of camera movement (e.g., pan, zoom) and individual object motion. The inability to control these aspects separately leads to ambiguity. For instance, an object moving right in the frame could mean the object itself is moving, or the camera is panning left, or both are moving in tandem. Disentangling these allows for precise creative expression.
- **Limited Flexibility and Customization:** Without fine-grained control, users are limited to the model's inherent motion priors, making it difficult to generate specific and personalized video narratives. This reduces the utility of T2V models for professional and creative applications.
- **Data Annotation Challenges:** Training T2V models with controllable motion typically requires large datasets annotated with explicit camera poses and object trajectories. Such frame-by-frame annotation is exceedingly laborious and costly, making the acquisition of suitable datasets a significant barrier to research and development. Furthermore, real-world video data often inherently couples camera and object movements, making it difficult for models to learn to distinguish between them.
- **Computational Expense:** Training large video models on extensive, highly annotated datasets is computationally very expensive, limiting accessibility and iteration speed.

To overcome these challenges, Direct-a-Video proposes a strategy that avoids intensive data collection for motion annotations and computationally heavy training for object control, while still achieving highly controllable and high-quality video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Direct-a-Video proposes a simple yet effective strategy for decoupling camera and object control, integrating two orthogonal controlling mechanisms: a self-supervised training approach for camera movement and a training-free inference-time method for object motion. The framework builds upon a pre-trained T2V diffusion model (specifically, Zeroscope T2V model).

#### 3.1. Camera Movement Control

- **Parameters:** Users specify camera movement using three quantitative parameters: an x-pan ratio ($c_x$), a y-pan ratio ($c_y$), and a zoom ratio ($c_z$). This offers precise, quantitative control, a feature often lacking in prior works.
- **Data Construction and Augmentation:** To circumvent the need for explicit camera movement annotations, the authors propose a self-supervised training approach using camera augmentation. They start with videos captured by a stationary camera (e.g., from MovieShot dataset). Camera movement ($c_{cam} = [c_x, c_y, c_z]$) is simulated by algorithmically manipulating a cropping window over the original video frames, applying shifting and scaling. This allows for generating synthetic camera motion data without manual annotation.
- **Camera Embedding:** The camera parameters ($c_x, c_y, c_z$) are encoded into a camera embedding. This is done using a Fourier embedder followed by two separate Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): one for panning ($c_x, c_y$) and another for zooming ($c_z$). This design choice of separating panning and zooming encoding was empirically validated to help the model distinguish between these distinct movements.
- **Camera Module:** A novel set of trainable temporal cross-attention layers, termed the "camera module," is introduced. These layers are appended after the existing temporal self-attention layers within each U-Net block of the T2V model. Analogous to how spatial cross-attention interprets textual information, this camera module interprets the "camera language" (i.e., the camera embedding), infusing camera movement information into the visual features through attention mechanisms. A learnable scalar $\alpha$ controls the influence, ensuring gradual learning from the pre-trained state.
- **Training:** To preserve the extensive prior knowledge of the base T2V model, only the newly added camera embedder and camera module are trained, while the original U-Net weights are frozen. Training is performed using the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss, conditioned on the augmented video samples, camera parameters, and textual captions. A coarse-to-fine training strategy is adopted, starting with smaller resolutions and then fine-tuning on larger ones.
- **Inference:** During inference, a classifier-free guidance mechanism is extended to enhance camera control, extrapolating predictions from an unconditional branch (static camera) towards a conditional branch (desired camera parameters). Camera conditioning is primarily applied during the initial diffusion steps, as general temporal transitions are largely determined early on.

#### 3.2. Object Motion Control

- **Control Signal:** Bounding boxes are chosen as the primary control signal for object motion, as they align well with attention modulation techniques and offer a balance between user-friendliness (simpler than pixel-wise) and expressiveness (more than sparse key points). Users define an object's spatial-temporal trajectory by drawing start, end, and intermediate bounding boxes, which are then interpolated.
- **Training-Free Approach:** To bypass the need for extensive, well-annotated video grounding datasets, Direct-a-Video leverages the inherent priors of the pre-trained T2V model through **spatial cross-attention modulation** during inference.
- **Attention Amplification:** For a specified object within a bounding box at a given frame, the attention values for its corresponding textual tokens are amplified within that box region. The amplification strength is inversely related to the box area (smaller boxes get larger amplification). This ensures the object appears in the desired location and size. This modulation is primarily applied during the early diffusion stages (for timesteps $t \geq \tau$), as these stages establish the coarse layout, allowing later stages to refine details naturally.
- **Attention Suppression:** To prevent semantic interference, especially in multi-object scenarios where semantics of one object might bleed into another, attention values for unmatched query-key token pairs are suppressed. This is applied throughout the entire sampling process.
- **Temporal Coherence:** Although the attention modulation is performed independently for each frame, the pre-trained temporal layers of the base diffusion model effectively maintain temporal continuity in the generated videos.

By combining these two decoupled mechanisms, Direct-a-Video allows for individual or simultaneous control of camera movement and object motion, offering significant flexibility in video synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of Direct-a-Video across camera movement, object motion, and joint control scenarios, both qualitatively and quantitatively.

#### 4.1. Camera Movement Control

- **Qualitative Results:** Direct-a-Video successfully generates videos with various camera movements, including single types (pan, zoom) and hybrid combinations. Unlike AnimateDiff, it supports quantitative control of movement parameters. Crucially, Direct-a-Video achieves disentangled control where foreground object motion can be independent of camera movement, a contrast to VideoComposer where object and background motions often appear coupled. The visual quality of generated videos is also noted to be high.
- **Quantitative Results:** The method achieves the best FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) and FID-vid (Fréchet Inception Distance for video) scores compared to AnimateDiff and VideoComposer, indicating superior visual quality. Furthermore, Direct-a-Video exhibits a significantly lower "Flow error" (comparing generated optical flow with ground truth based on camera parameters), demonstrating more precise and accurate camera control.

#### 4.2. Object Motion Control

- **Qualitative Results:** Direct-a-Video excels in generating objects at desired static locations and, more impressively, handling scenarios with multiple moving objects. This addresses a significant limitation of baselines like Peekaboo (which lacks multi-object support in its implementation) and VideoComposer (which struggles with semantic mixing and object absence when dealing with multiple objects due to its global motion vector approach and lack of explicit object binding). The attention modulation strategy effectively guides each object's spatial-temporal placement and prevents semantic bleed.
- **Quantitative Results:** In terms of video quality, Direct-a-Video's FVD and FID-vid scores are comparable to Peekaboo (both leveraging the same high-quality base model) and superior to VideoComposer. For object control metrics, Direct-a-Video achieves higher CLIP-sim (image-text similarity within the box), mIoU (mean Intersection over Union with input boxes), and AP50 (average precision at 0.5 IoU threshold). This suggests superior object-prompt and object-box alignment, largely attributed to the attention amplification strategy.

#### 4.3. Joint Control of Camera Movement and Object Motion

The paper successfully demonstrates the unique capability of Direct-a-Video to jointly control both camera and object motions. Examples show that the same static bounding box for an object can result in the object appearing static, moving right, or moving left, depending on the camera's pan direction. Similarly, a moving bounding box does not necessarily mean the object itself is in motion; it could be stationary while the camera moves. This highlights the flexibility offered by disentangled control, allowing users to define the overall video motion by independently specifying foreground and background movements, which is a key advantage over methods that implicitly couple these motions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Attention Amplification:** Its removal significantly decreases grounding ability (mIoU, AP50), confirming its crucial role in object localization and adherence to specified bounding boxes.
- **Attention Suppression:** Its absence leads to semantic mixing issues in multi-object generation (e.g., tiger texture on a bear), validating its role in maintaining object semantic integrity.
- **Camera Embedding Design:** Separately encoding panning and zooming movements (as opposed to joint encoding) proved more effective for camera control, resulting in a significantly lower flow error.
- **Effect on Quality:** The introduction of camera and object control mechanisms was found to have only a minor impact on overall video quality metrics (FVD, FID-vid), indicating that the control is achieved without significantly degrading the generative quality of the base model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Direct-a-Video represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable video generation, addressing a crucial unmet need for fine-grained, disentangled control over video dynamics.

### 6. Significance

- **Enhanced User Control:** The core significance lies in empowering users with intuitive and quantitative control over two fundamental aspects of video: camera perspective and object actions. This transforms text-to-video from a simple generation tool into a powerful "video directing" platform.
- **Decoupled Motion Control:** By successfully decoupling camera movement and object motion, the framework resolves ambiguities inherent in combined motion and offers unprecedented flexibility for creative expression. This architectural innovation allows for a wider range of cinematic narratives and visual styles.
- **Efficiency in Training and Inference:** The adoption of a self-supervised training scheme for camera control (using readily available static camera footage augmentation) and a training-free approach for object motion control (leveraging attention modulation) is highly significant. It bypasses the laborious and costly process of collecting large-scale, explicitly annotated video datasets, making the approach more practical and scalable.
- **Robustness to Complex Scenarios:** The ability to handle multiple moving objects and their interactions, while maintaining semantic integrity, is a notable achievement that distinguishes it from many existing methods.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Democratization of Video Production:** Direct-a-Video could significantly lower the barrier to entry for video creation, enabling individuals and small teams without extensive technical or production skills to generate high-quality, customized video content for various purposes, from social media to marketing and education.
- **New Creative Tools:** It provides a powerful new tool for artists, designers, and content creators, allowing them to experiment with complex motion sequences and visual narratives that were previously difficult or impossible to achieve without professional software and expertise.
- **Advancements in Controllable AI:** The methodologies introduced, particularly the self-supervised camera module training and the sophisticated attention modulation for objects, offer valuable insights for future research into making generative AI models more controllable and interpretable across various modalities.
- **Integration with Existing Workflows:** The intuitive bounding box and parameter-based control could be seamlessly integrated into existing video editing software or 3D animation pipelines, augmenting current capabilities with AI-powered generation.

**Limitations to Address for Future Impact:**

The paper acknowledges certain limitations that highlight avenues for future research:
- **Conflicting Inputs:** User-specified camera and object motions can sometimes conflict (e.g., static object in a panning shot resulting in an unrealistic moving object). Future work could explore mechanisms to detect and resolve such conflicts, or guide users towards realistic combinations.
- **Limited 3D Camera Movements:** The current camera control is restricted to 2D panning and zooming. Extending this to more complex 3D camera trajectories (e.g., orbiting, dollying) would require more sophisticated augmentation or synthetic data generation from rendering engines.
- **Colliding Bounding Boxes:** Overlapping bounding boxes for multiple objects can still lead to semantic interference. This suggests a need for more advanced attention modulation techniques that consider dynamic, adaptively segmented regions rather than fixed initial box regions, potentially through mask-based or instance-level guidance during diffusion.

Despite these limitations, Direct-a-Video lays a strong foundation for the next generation of highly controllable and flexible text-to-video synthesis systems, paving the way for more expressive and personalized AI-generated media.
