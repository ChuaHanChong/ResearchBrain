# An Real-Sim-Real (RSR) Loop Framework for Generalizable Robotic Policy Transfer with Differentiable Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> An iterative Real-Sim-Real (RSR) loop framework was developed to address the sim-to-real gap in robotic policy transfer. It combines differentiable simulation parameter tuning with an adaptive InfoGap loss to mitigate data collection bias, reducing KL divergence between real and simulated trajectories from over 16/36 to less than 1 on block-pushing tasks, enhancing real-world manipulation performance.

## Problem

- Policies trained in simulated environments often fail to generalize effectively to real-world robotic systems due to the "sim-to-real gap."
- Existing sim-to-real techniques, such as domain randomization and domain adaptation, often require manual parameter tuning, lack scalability, or struggle to capture real-world complexities comprehensively.
- Current simulator tuning methods that leverage real-world data can be limited by biases in data collection, an over-reliance on visual cues that do not directly reflect underlying physical dynamics, and narrow applicability to specific robotic systems.

## Method

- A **RSR** (==Real-Sim-Real==) loop framework iteratively refines both ==differentiable simulation parameters== and robotic policies through a continuous two-stage feedback mechanism.
- Physical parameters of the simulation environment are tuned using real-world data by minimizing a ==`L_physical` loss==, leveraging the gradient-based optimization capabilities of ==MuJoCo MJX==.
- An ==Adaptive InfoGap Loss== function is integrated into policy training to balance task completion with the collection of diverse and informative real-world data, actively mitigating data collection bias for subsequent iterations.

## Results

- The **RSR** framework systematically reduced the sim-to-real gap, decreasing the KL divergence between real and simulated trajectories from **16.3509/36.3168** to **0.9739/0.8384** (X/Y) in block pushing tasks by the **4th** iteration.
- Real-world robot trajectories for block pushing progressively improved, closely matching targets and simulated trajectories across RSR iterations, leading to successful task completion and reduced mean trajectory errors.
- The framework demonstrated generalizability and efficiency across tasks, successfully reducing yaw error in a more complex T-shaped block pushing scenario, but incorporating SSIM-based visual loss was found to introduce instability and was excluded.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative refinement through a continuous feedback loop between real-world data, differentiable simulator tuning, and policy training effectively and progressively reduces the sim-to-real gap.
> - An adaptive cost function (InfoGap Loss) that actively encourages the policy to gather informative real-world data is crucial for mitigating sampling biases and achieving robust simulator alignment.
> - Optimizing simulation based on physical states is more effective for stable sim-to-real transfer than incorporating visual appearance-based loss components, which can introduce instability and computational overhead.
