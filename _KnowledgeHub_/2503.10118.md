# An Real-Sim-Real (RSR) Loop Framework for Generalizable Robotic Policy Transfer with Differentiable Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> An iterative Real-Sim-Real (RSR) loop framework was developed to address the sim-to-real gap in robotic policy transfer. It combines differentiable simulation parameter tuning with an adaptive InfoGap loss to mitigate data collection bias, reducing KL divergence between real and simulated trajectories from over 16/36 to less than 1 on block-pushing tasks, enhancing real-world manipulation performance.

## Problem

- Policies trained in simulated environments often fail to generalize effectively to real-world robotic systems due to the "sim-to-real gap."
- Existing sim-to-real techniques, such as domain randomization and domain adaptation, often require manual parameter tuning, lack scalability, or struggle to capture real-world complexities comprehensively.
- Current simulator tuning methods that leverage real-world data can be limited by biases in data collection, an over-reliance on visual cues that do not directly reflect underlying physical dynamics, and narrow applicability to specific robotic systems.

## Method

- A **RSR** (==Real-Sim-Real==) loop framework iteratively refines both ==differentiable simulation parameters== and robotic policies through a continuous two-stage feedback mechanism.
- Physical parameters of the simulation environment are tuned using real-world data by minimizing a ==`L_physical` loss==, leveraging the gradient-based optimization capabilities of ==MuJoCo MJX==.
- An ==Adaptive InfoGap Loss== function is integrated into policy training to balance task completion with the collection of diverse and informative real-world data, actively mitigating data collection bias for subsequent iterations.

## Results

- The **RSR** framework systematically reduced the sim-to-real gap, decreasing the KL divergence between real and simulated trajectories from **16.3509/36.3168** to **0.9739/0.8384** (X/Y) in block pushing tasks by the **4th** iteration.
- Real-world robot trajectories for block pushing progressively improved, closely matching targets and simulated trajectories across RSR iterations, leading to successful task completion and reduced mean trajectory errors.
- The framework demonstrated generalizability and efficiency across tasks, successfully reducing yaw error in a more complex T-shaped block pushing scenario, but incorporating SSIM-based visual loss was found to introduce instability and was excluded.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative refinement through a continuous feedback loop between real-world data, differentiable simulator tuning, and policy training effectively and progressively reduces the sim-to-real gap.
> - An adaptive cost function (InfoGap Loss) that actively encourages the policy to gather informative real-world data is crucial for mitigating sampling biases and achieving robust simulator alignment.
> - Optimizing simulation based on physical states is more effective for stable sim-to-real transfer than incorporating visual appearance-based loss components, which can introduce instability and computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the "sim-to-real gap," a fundamental challenge in robotics where policies trained in simulated environments fail to generalize effectively to real-world robotic systems. Simulation offers advantages such as safety, cost-effectiveness, and rapid experimentation, but discrepancies in physics, sensor noise, and environmental uncertainties between simulated and real environments often lead to suboptimal performance upon real-world deployment.

Prior approaches to mitigate the sim-to-real gap include:
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** This technique enhances generalization by varying simulation parameters across a wide range during training. While effective, DR often requires manual parameter selection, may not scale with increasing problem complexity, and operates in an open-loop manner without real-world feedback.
- **Domain Adaptation:** This paradigm frames the sim-to-real problem as a transfer learning task, aiming to bridge the source (simulation) and target (real world) domains through feature alignment, often using adversarial or reconstruction-based methods. Challenges remain in extracting invariant features for robotics applications.
- **Neural-Augmented Simulation:** These methods utilize neural networks (e.g., LSTMs) to predict and correct discrepancies between simulated and real-world performance. However, they typically demand substantial real-world data and exhibit sensitivity to noise.
- **Simulator Tuning with Real-World Data:** More recent efforts involve using real-world data to refine simulators. Examples include Real-Sim-Real (RSR) transfer methods that construct task-relevant simulated environments and apply trained policies to the real world without further data, Bayesian methods (Bayessim) that build posterior distributions of simulation parameters, and approaches integrating vision-based foundation models with gradient-based optimization for pose reconstruction.
- **Differentiable Simulators:** These allow gradient-based optimization of simulation parameters (e.g., friction, mass) using real-world data, enabling the simulation environment to more accurately reflect real-world dynamics. Specific implementations involve comparing simulated states with real-world data to generate a loss function for parameter updates or creating fully differentiable pipelines linking observations to policies and state estimation.

Despite these advancements, existing methodologies often exhibit several limitations that this work aims to address:
- **Data Collection Bias:** Many methods overlook potential biases in real-world data collection, leading to suboptimal simulator tuning if data is unrepresentative or fails to cover critical regions.
- **Reliance on Visual Data:** Approaches solely relying on visual appearance or video data are susceptible to variations in lighting, camera angles, and sensor calibration. Visual data primarily captures pose information but may not provide sufficient insight into critical dynamic variables such (velocity, acceleration, thrust) for accurate simulation.
- **Limited Generalizability:** A number of current approaches are tailored to specific robotic systems or environments, which constrains their broader applicability.

The current work positions itself within the landscape of differentiable simulation and real-world data-driven simulator tuning. It aims to overcome the identified limitations by introducing a novel framework that emphasizes informative data collection and iterative refinement, thereby seeking to achieve robust and generalizable policy transfer across diverse scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for generalizable robotic policy transfer, specifically addressing the sim-to-real gap. The authors propose an iterative Real-Sim-Real (RSR) loop framework that refines simulation parameters and policy training to enhance real-world performance.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations of existing sim-to-real transfer techniques. While simulation is a valuable tool for training robotic policies, the discrepancies between simulated and real environments (the sim-to-real gap) hinder practical deployment. Existing methods, such as domain randomization and domain adaptation, have shown promise but often struggle with manual parameter tuning, scalability, or the comprehensive capture of real-world complexities. Differentiable simulators offer a pathway to automatically tune simulation parameters using real-world data, but the effectiveness of such approaches can be compromised by biases in data collection or an over-reliance on visual cues that may not directly reflect underlying physical dynamics.

Specifically, the key objectives driving this research are:
- **Iterative Simulation Parameter Refinement:** To develop a mechanism that continuously adjusts differentiable simulation parameters by leveraging real-world data, thereby progressively aligning the simulation with real-world conditions.
- **Biased Data Mitigation:** To design a cost function that actively encourages the collection of diverse and representative real-world data. This aims to minimize biases inherent in trajectory-based sampling and maximize the utility of each data point for accurate simulator tuning, particularly by exploring underrepresented regions of the real domain.
- **Comprehensive State Representation:** To consider both physical states and, initially, visual appearance in the cost function for simulator tuning. This objective sought a more holistic alignment, though the visual component was later de-emphasized due to observed challenges.
- **Integration with Reinforcement Learning:** To ensure the proposed data-collection strategy and cost function can be seamlessly integrated with established reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).
- **Broad Applicability:** To implement the framework on a versatile platform like MuJoCo MJX, ensuring compatibility with a wide array of robotic systems and environments to promote generalizability.
- **Demonstration of Effectiveness:** To experimentally validate the framework's capability in reducing the sim-to-real gap and achieving high task performance and generalizability across manipulation tasks involving both explicit and implicit environmental uncertainties.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is centered around a novel **Real-Sim-Real (RSR) Loop Framework**, which iteratively refines both the simulation environment parameters and the robotic policy through a two-stage feedback mechanism. The framework is designed to leverage real-world data for simulator tuning and policy training, aiming to reduce the sim-to-real gap.

**A. System Overview: The RSR Loop Framework**
The RSR framework operates in continuous cycles, consisting of two main feedback loops:
1. **Sim-Env Parameters Tuning Loop:** This loop adjusts the simulator's physical parameters using data collected from the real robot.
2. **Policy Training Loop:** This loop utilizes the refined simulator of the current iteration to train a new policy, incorporating an adaptive cost function designed to guide data collection for the next iteration.

The entire process is iterative (indexed by *k* for the RSR iteration) and outlined in Algorithm 1, encompassing three main steps per iteration:

**B. Simulation-Environment Parameter Tuning Process (Step 1)**
In this initial step, the objective is to align the simulated environment's dynamics with real-world observations.
- **Data Input:** Real-world data, denoted as `D_real^k`, is collected from the physical robot using the policy (`π_(k-1)`) trained in the previous iteration.
- **Optimization Target:** The simulation parameters `θ` (e.g., friction, mass, elasticity) are optimized to minimize the `L_physical` loss function. This loss quantifies the discrepancy between `D_real^k` and the corresponding simulated data `D_sim(θ)`.
- **Differentiable Simulation:** The process leverages a differentiable physics engine (MuJoCo MJX) to compute gradients of the physical loss with respect to `θ`.
- **Gradient-Based Update:** Parameters `θ` are iteratively updated using gradient descent (`θ ← θ - α∇_θ L(θ)`) until convergence. This ensures the simulator accurately reproduces the physical behaviors observed in the real world.

**C. Adaptive InfoGap Loss Construction (Step 2)**
Following simulator tuning, the next step involves training a new policy (`π_k`) within the updated simulation environment. A key contribution is the design of an **Adaptive InfoGap Loss function** that not only focuses on task completion but also guides the policy to collect informative real-world data for future iterations, mitigating data collection bias.
- **Motivation for InfoGap Loss:** Trajectory-based sampling for real-world data collection can introduce bias by over-representing regions already explored by the current policy. The InfoGap loss addresses this by balancing task completion with exploration of underrepresented regions.
- **Loss Function Structure:** The total loss for policy training `L(a_k,t)` is a combination of a nominal task completion cost `L_task(a_t)` (e.g., from PPO or SAC) and an adaptive sim-to-real loss `L_sr(a_k,t)`:
 `L(a_k,t) = L_task(a_t) + L_sr(a_k,t)`
- **`L_sr` (InfoGap Loss) Details:**
 `L_sr = -KL(p̂(D_real^k) || p̂(D_sim^(k-1))) * W_β(p̂(D_sim^k + D_t), p̂(D_sim^k))`
 - **First Term: `KL(p̂(D_real^k) || p̂(D_sim^(k-1)))`**
 - This term quantifies the sim-to-real gap at the beginning of iteration `k`. It measures the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the distribution of real-world data (`D_real^k`) and data propagated through the *untuned* simulator from the previous iteration (`D_sim^(k-1)`).
 - It remains constant throughout the policy training phase of a given iteration `k`. As the sim-to-real gap narrows, this term approaches zero.
 - **Second Term: `W_β(p̂(D_sim^k + D_t), p̂(D_sim^k))`**
 - This term uses the Wasserstein distance (`W_β`, also known as Earth Mover's Distance) to assess the impact of a newly generated data pair `D_t` (influenced by the policy's action `a_k,t`).
 - It measures the difference between the distribution of data in the *tuned* simulator (`D_sim^k`) and the distribution when `D_t` is added (`D_sim^k + D_t`).
 - This term encourages the policy to generate actions that produce "informative" data points—those that create larger discrepancies from the existing simulated distribution while being closer to the real-world distribution.
- **Adaptive Behavior:** When the sim-to-real gap (first term) is substantial, the `L_sr` term promotes exploratory actions that gather informative data. As the gap diminishes, `L_sr` approaches zero, allowing `L_task` to dominate, thereby shifting the policy's focus toward task completion.
- **Distribution Estimation:** Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) is employed to estimate probability density functions (`p̂`) from data samples.

**D. Data Collection Approaches (Step 3)**
After policy `π_k` is trained in the tuned simulator, it is deployed on the real robot to collect new real-world data (`D_real^(k+1)`). This data forms the input for the next RSR iteration's environment parameter tuning. The paper notes that common data collection methods include random sampling, grid sampling, and trajectory-based sampling, with the proposed framework explicitly addressing the biases inherent in trajectory-based collection through the InfoGap loss.

**E. Implementation Details**
- **Simulator:** The DISCOVERSE simulator, built upon the MuJoCo MJX engine, is used for physical modeling.
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed, implemented with the JAX computation library.
- **Robot Hardware:** Experiments are conducted using a 6-DOF AIRBOT Play robotic arm equipped with a Realsense D435i depth camera for visual perception.
- **Observation and Action Space:** Detailed definitions of the observation space (joint angles, end-effector coordinates, target/block positions, orientations for T-shaped block) and action space (6-dimensional vector for rotational displacements of joints) are provided.
- **Reward Setup:** Task-specific reward functions are defined, including distance-based rewards (`r_d`), end-effector guidance rewards (`r_ee`), and orientation rewards (`r_o` for T-shaped blocks), with adjustable weighting coefficients.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the RSR loop framework was evaluated through experiments on robotic manipulation tasks, specifically block-pushing scenarios.

**A. Block Pushing Experiment**
This experiment involved a 6-DOF robotic arm pushing a block to random target points. The performance was assessed by comparing real-world trajectories to simulated ones and analyzing the reduction in the sim-to-real gap across RSR iterations.
- **Initial Performance (1st PPO):** The policy initially trained solely in simulation ("1st PPO") exhibited a high KL divergence (16.3509 for X, 36.3168 for Y) between real and simulated trajectories. This indicated a substantial discrepancy between the simulation-trained policy and its behavior in the real world, where issues like low friction and gripper misalignment led to the block slipping rather than being pushed effectively.
- **RSR Iteration Improvement:** As the RSR loop progressed, the KL divergence systematically decreased. By the 4th RSR iteration, the KL divergence significantly reduced to 0.9739 for X and 0.8384 for Y (Table I). This reduction demonstrates that the iterative tuning process effectively made the simulator more representative of real-world dynamics and aligned the policy better with real-world behavior.
- **Trajectory Alignment:** Visual analysis of real-world trajectories (Fig. 3) confirmed this improvement. The "1st PPO" trajectory deviated considerably and failed to reach the target. Subsequent RSR-refined trajectories (2nd, 3rd, and 4th RSR) showed progressive accuracy improvements. By the 4th RSR iteration, the real-world trajectory closely matched the target and the simulated trajectory, indicating successful task completion.
- **Mean Trajectory Error:** Over three independent trials, the mean trajectory error in both X and Y directions consistently decreased with successive RSR iterations (Fig. 4). This trend further supports the framework's effectiveness in bridging the sim-to-real gap by refining simulation parameters and improving alignment between simulated and real robot movements.

**B. T-shaped Block Pushing Experiment**
To test the framework's robustness in more complex scenarios, the robot was tasked with pushing a T-shaped object, which introduced challenges due to its irregular geometry and orientation.
- **Yaw Error Reduction:** Similar to the cube-block experiment, the T-shaped block pushing task demonstrated improved performance with RSR iterations. The initial PPO policy exhibited a high yaw error. This error progressively reduced across the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th RSR iterations (Fig. 5), indicating enhanced alignment in object orientation control.
- **Validation of Efficiency:** The consistent improvement in performance, specifically the reduction in yaw error, across a task with more complex object geometry, validated the efficiency and adaptability of the proposed RSR pipeline for different types of manipulation tasks.

**C. Discussion on Incorporating Visual Loss**
The authors investigated the inclusion of a visual loss component, calculated using the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) between Gaussian-rendered simulation images and real-world images, as a multiplier to the sim-to-real loss.
- **Observation:** Experimental results (Fig. 6) indicated that incorporating this SSIM-based visual loss did not lead to improved performance; instead, it appeared to introduce instability into the adaptation process.
- **Reasons for Instability:** The authors suggest that SSIM, while capturing structural similarity, may not directly correlate with the physical parameters critical for policy learning. Visual discrepancies caused by factors unrelated to dynamics (e.g., lighting, reflections, camera noise) could mislead the optimization.
- **Computational Cost:** A practical limitation identified was the significant computational cost associated with rendering high-fidelity images for iterative environment parameter tuning, making it impractical for the current pipeline.
- **Conclusion:** Consequently, the visual loss component was not retained in the current framework, with the focus maintained on optimizing physical parameters that directly influence sim-to-real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic learning by proposing a Real-Sim-Real (RSR) loop framework designed to address the persistent sim-to-real gap, which remains a significant barrier to deploying simulation-trained policies on physical robots. The framework's core innovations, including an adaptive InfoGap loss function and iterative differentiable simulator parameter tuning, aim to enhance the reliability and efficiency of policy transfer.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap:** The experimental results demonstrate that the RSR framework effectively reduces the divergence between simulation and reality. This improvement allows policies trained in simulation to achieve higher task performance when deployed on real robots, as evidenced by the progressive reduction in KL divergence and trajectory errors.
- **Informative Data Collection:** A key contribution is the design of the adaptive InfoGap loss function. This function guides the policy to collect informative real-world data that is critical for accurate simulator tuning, mitigating the biases often associated with traditional trajectory-based sampling. This targeted data collection strategy enhances the efficiency of the tuning process.
- **Generalizability Across Tasks:** The framework's successful application to both a simple block-pushing task and a more complex T-shaped block manipulation task indicates its generalizability across different object geometries and manipulation challenges. This suggests broader applicability to various robotic tasks.
- **Compatibility and Versatility:** Built on the MuJoCo MJX platform, the framework is compatible with a wide range of robotic systems. Its design, which allows integration with standard reinforcement learning algorithms (like PPO and SAC), enhances its versatility and potential for adoption within the robotics community.
- **Advancement in Autonomous Robot Learning:** By providing a more robust and efficient pipeline for transferring policies from simulation to reality, this work contributes to the development of autonomous robots capable of learning and operating in diverse, previously unexplored environments with potentially reduced real-world data requirements and training time. This could accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robotic systems in various applications.

**Potential Impact:** The framework's capacity to facilitate smoother and more robust sim-to-real transfer could significantly impact areas such as:
- **Industrial Automation:** Faster and more reliable deployment of robotic solutions for manufacturing and logistics, reducing development costs and risks.
- **Service Robotics:** Enabling robots to adapt to varied and unstructured environments in healthcare, hospitality, or domestic settings with minimal retraining.
- **Exploration and Hazardous Environments:** Training policies in simulation for tasks in dangerous or inaccessible real-world locations, where real-world training is impractical or impossible.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges limitations, including the computational resource demands of the underlying simulation engine (MuJoCo MJX and JAX) and its current focus on explicitly tunable environmental effects. Future work aims to extend the framework to aerial robots, where implicit environmental factors such as wind resistance, ground effects, and turbulence need to be accounted for, and to adapt to these dynamic changes in real-time. Addressing these limitations could further broaden the framework's applicability and impact.
