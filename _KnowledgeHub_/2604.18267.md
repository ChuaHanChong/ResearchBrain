# MARCO: Navigating the Unseen Space of Semantic Correspondence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Politecnico di Torino and TU Darmstadt developed MARCO, a semantic correspondence model that leverages a single DINOv2 backbone to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on standard benchmarks while improving generalization to unseen keypoints and categories. The model operates with significantly fewer parameters and faster inference speeds than prior dual-encoder methods.

## Problem

- Existing dual-encoder semantic correspondence models (combining DINOv2 and diffusion models) are computationally intensive, slow, and possess a large number of parameters (e.g., approaching one billion).
- These models exhibit poor generalization capabilities, performing poorly on unseen keypoints or entirely novel object categories beyond their training data.
- Sparse keypoint supervision leads to models that often overfit to specific annotated landmarks, limiting both fine-grained spatial precision and broad applicability.

## Method

- **MARCO** employs a minimalist architecture based on a single, frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone, augmented with lightweight AdaptFormer modules for feature enrichment and a feature upsampling head for sub-patch resolution.
- A coarse-to-fine objective, utilizing a dynamically annealed Gaussian RBF kernel for the cross-entropy loss, guides the model to improve precise localization from broad regions to fine sub-pixel details.
- A dense self-distillation framework, leveraging an EMA teacher network, mutual nearest neighbors, Delaunay triangulation, and ground-truth anchoring, expands sparse keypoint supervision into dense, geometrically consistent pseudo-correspondences to enhance generalization.

## Results

- MARCO achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on SPair-71k and AP-10K, demonstrating significant gains in fine-grained precision (e.g., +5.3 PCK@0.01 on SPair-71k over Geo-SC) while being 3 times smaller and 10 times faster than prior dual-encoder methods.
- The model showed enhanced generalization on novel benchmarks, outperforming Jamais Vu by +5.1 PCK@0.10 on SPair-U (unseen keypoints) and by up to +10.2 PCK@0.10 on MP-100 (unseen categories, e.g., apparel items).
- Ablation studies confirmed that AdaptFormers, the upsampling head, the coarse-to-fine objective, and the dense self-distillation framework each contribute significantly to MARCO's performance and generalization, with self-distillation being critical for unseen keypoint performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art semantic correspondence can be achieved with a single, adapted DINOv2 backbone, obviating the need for computationally expensive dual-encoder architectures involving diffusion models.
> - A dynamic coarse-to-fine training objective, which gradually narrows the spatial supervision target, is effective for balancing stable region-level matching with precise sub-patch localization.
> - Generating dense, geometrically consistent pseudo-labels through self-distillation and filtering erroneous matches (Flow Anchoring) is crucial for transforming sparse supervision into a robust signal that improves generalization to unseen keypoints and categories.
