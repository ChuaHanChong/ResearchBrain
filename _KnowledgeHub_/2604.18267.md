# MARCO: Navigating the Unseen Space of Semantic Correspondence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Politecnico di Torino and TU Darmstadt developed MARCO, a semantic correspondence model that leverages a single DINOv2 backbone to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on standard benchmarks while improving generalization to unseen keypoints and categories. The model operates with significantly fewer parameters and faster inference speeds than prior dual-encoder methods.

## Problem

- Existing dual-encoder semantic correspondence models (combining DINOv2 and diffusion models) are computationally intensive, slow, and possess a large number of parameters (e.g., approaching one billion).
- These models exhibit poor generalization capabilities, performing poorly on unseen keypoints or entirely novel object categories beyond their training data.
- Sparse keypoint supervision leads to models that often overfit to specific annotated landmarks, limiting both fine-grained spatial precision and broad applicability.

## Method

- **MARCO** employs a minimalist architecture based on a single, frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone, augmented with lightweight AdaptFormer modules for feature enrichment and a feature upsampling head for sub-patch resolution.
- A coarse-to-fine objective, utilizing a dynamically annealed Gaussian RBF kernel for the cross-entropy loss, guides the model to improve precise localization from broad regions to fine sub-pixel details.
- A dense self-distillation framework, leveraging an EMA teacher network, mutual nearest neighbors, Delaunay triangulation, and ground-truth anchoring, expands sparse keypoint supervision into dense, geometrically consistent pseudo-correspondences to enhance generalization.

## Results

- MARCO achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on SPair-71k and AP-10K, demonstrating significant gains in fine-grained precision (e.g., +5.3 PCK@0.01 on SPair-71k over Geo-SC) while being 3 times smaller and 10 times faster than prior dual-encoder methods.
- The model showed enhanced generalization on novel benchmarks, outperforming Jamais Vu by +5.1 PCK@0.10 on SPair-U (unseen keypoints) and by up to +10.2 PCK@0.10 on MP-100 (unseen categories, e.g., apparel items).
- Ablation studies confirmed that AdaptFormers, the upsampling head, the coarse-to-fine objective, and the dense self-distillation framework each contribute significantly to MARCO's performance and generalization, with self-distillation being critical for unseen keypoint performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art semantic correspondence can be achieved with a single, adapted DINOv2 backbone, obviating the need for computationally expensive dual-encoder architectures involving diffusion models.
> - A dynamic coarse-to-fine training objective, which gradually narrows the spatial supervision target, is effective for balancing stable region-level matching with precise sub-patch localization.
> - Generating dense, geometrically consistent pseudo-labels through self-distillation and filtering erroneous matches (Flow Anchoring) is crucial for transforming sparse supervision into a robust signal that improves generalization to unseen keypoints and categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of semantic correspondence estimation focuses on establishing pixel-level matches between semantically equivalent object regions across different images. This task is foundational for various applications, including image editing, pose estimation, style transfer, and affordance understanding. A key challenge in this domain is the significant variability in pose, texture, and viewpoint that corresponding parts can exhibit, often compounded by the typical sparsity of available supervision in the form of keypoint annotations.

Recent advancements have seen the emergence of large-scale pre-trained vision models, particularly self-supervised encoders like DINOv2, which provide robust semantic alignment. Concurrently, diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion (SD), have demonstrated capabilities in providing rich local structure and spatial detail. The combination of these two types of models into dual-encoder architectures (e.g., Geo-SC, Jamais Vu) has become a prevalent approach for semantic correspondence. These models leverage DINOv2 for semantic features and diffusion models for fine-grained spatial information.

However, this trend has introduced several limitations. Dual-encoder architectures are computationally intensive, often approaching one billion parameters and requiring feature extraction from two separate encoders. A more critical issue, as identified by this work and concurrent research like Jamais Vu, is their observed poor generalization beyond the keypoints seen during training. This limitation manifests in degraded performance on novel keypoints or entirely unseen object categories, indicating a discrepancy between benchmark performance (often on in-domain, well-annotated keypoints) and the requirements of real-world applications, where queried points frequently do not match those present in the training data.

MARCO aims to address these limitations by developing a unified correspondence model that leverages DINOv2 as a primary backbone. It departs from the dual-encoder paradigm to enhance efficiency and focuses on improving both spatial precision and semantic generalization without relying on additional large models or external 3D geometric information. This work positions itself as a more efficient and generalizable alternative to current state-of-the-art methods, particularly those that depend on computationally heavy diffusion backbones or category-specific 3D canonical templates.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the MARCO research is to develop a semantic correspondence model that overcomes the limitations of existing dual-encoder architectures, specifically their computational burden and poor generalization capabilities. The authors identify a gap between the performance observed on standard benchmarks and the model's utility in real-world scenarios where queried points often diverge from those used in training.

The specific objectives and motivations for MARCO are detailed as follows:

1. **Achieve Higher Spatial Precision on Supervised Keypoints:** Existing methods, particularly those relying on sparse supervision, often struggle with fine-grained localization. The motivation is to enable the model to resolve finer subregions of objects (e.g., the pupil of an eye rather than just the general eye region) rather than merely aligning coarse semantic parts. This precision is intended to improve performance on standard benchmarks and enhance the utility of correspondence in tasks requiring accurate pixel-level alignment.

2. **Enhance Generalization to Unseen Keypoints and Categories:** A significant limitation of prior models is their tendency to overfit to the specific keypoints and categories encountered during training. This leads to a degradation in performance when presented with novel keypoint definitions or entirely new object classes. The authors are motivated to develop a model whose learned representation can transfer robustly to new landmark vocabularies and taxonomies, thereby expanding its applicability beyond strict in-domain evaluations. The observation that fine-tuning DINOv2 with sparse keypoints can cause its dense correspondence field to "contract around the annotated landmarks" motivates the need for mechanisms to preserve and extend the inherent global semantic structure of pre-trained encoders.

3. **Maintain Computational Efficiency and Compactness:** The reliance on dual-encoder architectures, combining DINOv2 with diffusion models, results in computationally expensive models (approaching one billion parameters) and slow inference speeds. A core motivation for MARCO is to achieve state-of-the-art performance with a minimalist architectural design, utilizing only a single DINOv2 backbone. This aims to produce a model that is significantly smaller and faster, making it more practical for deployment in resource-constrained environments or applications requiring high throughput.

To address these objectives, the authors propose a novel training framework. This framework is designed to simultaneously enhance fine-grained localization through a coarse-to-fine objective and expand sparse supervision into dense, semantically coherent correspondences via a self-distillation mechanism. This approach is motivated by the observation that, despite its limitations, the DINOv2 feature space contains reliable sparse correspondence cues that can be leveraged for self-supervision, without requiring predefined 3D structures or external depth models that might inherently tie the model to training categories or struggle with deformable objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MARCO's methodology builds upon a pre-trained DINOv2 backbone and introduces a novel training framework alongside minor architectural modifications to achieve fine-grained localization and improved generalization.

**Problem Statement:**
The task is defined as establishing pixel-level matches between semantically equivalent keypoints. Given source and target images $I_s, I_t$, a learned function $\Phi_\theta$ extracts lower-resolution feature maps $F_s, F_t$. For any source pixel $p_s$, the goal is to identify a target pixel $p_t$ representing the same landmark. Training is performed using sparse sets of ground-truth correspondences $E = \{ (p_{s_i}, p_{t_i}) \}$.

**Overview of MARCO:**
The method leverages DINOv2's semantic structure while enhancing its spatial consistency and localization abilities. This is achieved through architectural components and a novel training framework.

#### 3.1. Architecture (Sec. 3.1)

MARCO adopts a minimalist architectural design based on a frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone, with two additional components:
- **Adapter-based Feature Enrichment:** AdaptFormer modules are inserted into the higher layers (last 12 transformer layers) of the DINOv2 backbone. These adapters operate token-wise, consisting of a bottleneck with learnable down- and up-projections ($W_{down} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times d}, W_{up} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times D}$ where $d \ll D$). The adapter output is added residually to the MLP output of the transformer block: $x' = \text{MLP}(x_{self}) + x_{self} + A(x_{self})$. The backbone weights remain frozen; only the adapter matrices are learned, incurring minimal parameter overhead (less than 5%). This enriches high-level features for geometric awareness.
- **Feature Upsampling:** A lightweight upsampling layer is added to restore sub-patch resolution. DINOv2 features represent $14 \times 14$ image patches. This layer increases feature resolution by a factor of $\times 4$ using a $2 \times 2$ ConvTranspose followed by a $3 \times 3$ DepthwiseConv with GELU activation. This sub-patch refinement is designed to enhance localization accuracy without the computational overhead of additional CNN matchers or cross-attention blocks common in other methods.

#### 3.2. Coarse-to-Fine Objective for Precise Localization (Sec. 3.2)

To enhance spatial precision, MARCO replaces the unstable soft-argmax coordinate regression with a distributional matching objective.
- **Similarity Map and Gaussian RBF Kernel:** For each source keypoint $p_{s_i}$, a dense similarity map $S(p_{s_i}, u) = \langle \hat{F}_s[p_{s_i}], \hat{F}_t[u] \rangle$ is computed against the upsampled target feature grid $\hat{F}_t$. The target distribution is defined by a Gaussian RBF kernel $G_\sigma(u; p_{t_i})$ centered at the ground-truth keypoint $p_{t_i}$.
- **Cross-Entropy Loss:** The predicted probability map (softmax of $S$) is supervised to match this Gaussian target distribution using a cross-entropy (CE) loss: $L_{sup} = - \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i=1}^K \sum_{u \in \hat{\Lambda}} G_\sigma(u; p_{t_i}) \log \text{softmax } S(p_{s_i}, u)$.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Annealing:** The bandwidth $\sigma$ of the Gaussian kernel is dynamically adjusted during training using a cosine annealing schedule: $\sigma(t) = \sigma_{min} + \frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{max} - \sigma_{min})(1 + \cos(\pi \frac{t}{T}))$. This schedule starts with a wide kernel (large $\sigma_{max}$) to promote stable region-level matching and gradually narrows it (to $\sigma_{min}$) to guide the model towards subpatch-accurate localization, balancing the trade-off between coarse and fine alignment.

#### 3.3. Dense Self-Distillation via Flow Anchoring (Sec. 3.3)

To address overfitting to sparse keypoints and improve generalization, a self-distillation framework is introduced. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher network ($\theta_T \leftarrow \beta \theta_T + (1 - \beta) \theta_S$) generates pseudo-labels for the student.
- **Estimating Dense Semantic Flow:**
 - The EMA teacher extracts upsampled features ($F_s^T, F_t^T$).
 - Mutual Nearest Neighbors (MNNs) are identified: $P_{MNN} = \{ (u, v) \mid \text{NN}_{s \to t}(u) = v \land \text{NN}_{t \to s}(v) = u \}$.
 - These MNNs are combined with ground-truth keypoints $E$ to form a seed set $P_{seed}$.
 - $P_{seed}$ is optionally restricted to pixels within object masks (obtained via SAM) or bounding boxes to filter background noise.
 - **Densification via Delaunay Triangulation:** A Delaunay triangulation $T$ is constructed over the source points in $P_{seed}$. For each triangle $\tau \in T$, a corresponding target triangle $\tau'$ is defined by its matched vertices. Piecewise-affine warps $W_{\tau \to \tau'}$ are estimated between these triangle pairs.
 - The union of these transformations forms a continuous piecewise-affine warp $\hat{W}$, which yields a dense displacement map $D(u) = \hat{W}(u) - u$.
- **Flow Clustering and GT Anchoring:**
 - Flow vectors $D(u)$ are clustered via k-means. The number of clusters $k$ is automatically determined by over-clustering and greedily merging clusters until the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) is maximized.
 - For each flow cluster $\Omega_n$, corresponding source $C_s^n$ and target $C_t^n$ regions are identified.
 - To filter out erroneous matches (e.g., due to symmetries or occlusions), only clusters whose regions contain a pair of ground-truth annotated keypoints ($p_{s_i} \in C_s^n \land p_{t_i} \in C_t^n$) are retained. These "anchored" clusters form the final set of reliable pseudo-labels $P_{self}$.
- **Self-Distillation Loss:** The student network is trained to reproduce these geometrically consistent pseudo-correspondences using an L2 regression loss: $L_{self} = \frac{1}{|P_{self}|} \sum_{(\hat{u}, \hat{v}) \in P_{self}} ||\text{soft-argmax}_{u \in \hat{\Lambda}}(S(\hat{u}, u)) - \hat{v}||^2_2$. This encourages the features to vary smoothly across the object surface and enhances global consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates MARCO's performance across standard in-domain benchmarks and newly proposed generalization benchmarks, assessing both precision and robustness to unseen data.

#### 4.1. Comparison on Standard Benchmarks

(Refer to Table 1 in the paper)
- **Overall Performance:** MARCO achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on SPair-71k, AP-10K (intra-species, cross-species, cross-family splits), and PF-PASCAL, while utilizing a single DINOv2 encoder.
- **Fine-Grained Precision:** Significant gains are observed at the challenging PCK@0.01 threshold.
 - On SPair-71k, MARCO achieves 27.0% PCK@0.01, surpassing Geo-SC by +5.3 percentage points.
 - On AP-10K, MARCO shows a +10.0 percentage point improvement over Geo-SC at PCK@0.01.
 - At PCK@0.10, MARCO reaches 87.2% on SPair-71k, exceeding Geo-SC by +4.0 points.
- **AP-10K Sub-splits:** MARCO outperforms prior methods across all AP-10K splits, with the largest improvement in the challenging cross-family scenario (+4.9 PCK@0.10).
- **PF-PASCAL:** Despite nearing saturation, MARCO demonstrates a +5.8 PCK@0.05 improvement, indicating consistent fine-grained accuracy.
- **Efficiency:** Compared to dual-encoder methods like Geo-SC and Jamais Vu, MARCO is approximately 3 times smaller (323M parameters vs. 950M parameters) and 10 times faster (8.3 FPS vs. 0.85 FPS on an RTX 4090).
- **Impact of Object Masks:** The use of object masks (e.g., from SAM) during training or inference has a negligible effect on MARCO's performance. When using bounding boxes derived from ground-truth keypoints instead of SAM masks, performance remains highly competitive (e.g., 26.6 PCK@0.01 and 86.7 PCK@0.10 on SPair-71k), suggesting that masks provide a mild foreground prior rather than a critical supervision signal.

#### 4.2. Generalization

(Refer to Tables 2 and 3 in the paper)
- **Overall Generalization Trend:** Unlike methods that trade in-domain strength for generalization (e.g., Geo-SC) or vice-versa (e.g., Jamais Vu), MARCO demonstrates improved performance in both in-domain accuracy and out-of-domain generalization.
- **SPair-U (Unseen Keypoints):**
 - MARCO achieves 67.5% PCK@0.10, outperforming Jamais Vu (62.4%) by +5.1 percentage points. This indicates stronger transferability to novel keypoint definitions within familiar categories.
- **MP-100 (Unseen Keypoints and Unseen Categories):**
 - **Human Face (Unseen Keypoints):** MARCO achieves 87.5% PCK@0.10, surpassing Jamais Vu by +2.0 points on a split with 68 novel, densely annotated facial landmarks.
 - **Apparel Items (Unseen Keypoints):** MARCO reaches 55.9% PCK@0.10, improving over Jamais Vu by +10.2 points.
 - **Home Furniture (Unseen Categories):** MARCO records 60.4% PCK@0.10, an improvement of +7.7 points over Jamais Vu.
 - **Animal Body (Unseen Categories):** MARCO achieves 42.3% PCK@0.10, gaining +3.0 points over Jamais Vu.
 - **Animal Face (Unseen Categories):** MARCO reaches 52.6% PCK@0.10, outperforming Jamais Vu by +4.9 points across more than 30 unseen animal species.
 - These results collectively indicate that MARCO's dense self-supervision enables robust generalization to both new landmark vocabularies and entirely novel semantic domains.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

(Refer to Table 4 in the paper and Supplementary Material)
- **Architectural Components:**
 - Lightweight adapters provide substantial accuracy improvements (e.g., +8.1 PCK@0.01, +23.0 PCK@0.10 on SPair-71k over frozen DINOv2).
 - The upsampling head further contributes significant gains (e.g., +12.6 PCK@0.01, +10.3 PCK@0.10), highlighting the importance of restoring sub-patch structure.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Objective:**
 - The annealing schedule for $\sigma$ successfully balances the trade-off between fine localization (small $\sigma$) and coarse accuracy (large $\sigma$), achieving performance comparable to the best fixed settings without their respective weaknesses.
- **Dense Self-Distillation:**
 - The absence of dense self-supervision leads to a severe degradation in generalization (-25.7 PCK@0.10 on SPair-U).
 - Using raw semantic flow partially recovers accuracy but is prone to errors.
 - The full pipeline, including mutual nearest neighbors, Delaunay-based densification, and Ground Truth (GT) anchoring, progressively improves performance on unseen keypoints (from 41.8% to 67.5% PCK@0.10 on SPair-U), by effectively creating reliable, dense pseudo-labels and filtering out geometrically inconsistent motions.
- **Broad Applicability:** The dense self-distillation loss can improve generalization even when applied to other state-of-the-art methods. For example, augmenting Geo-SC with MARCO's self-distillation improves its SPair-U PCK@0.10 from 56.9% to 63.4%, outperforming Jamais Vu.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MARCO research contributes to the field of semantic correspondence by demonstrating a model architecture and training framework that simultaneously addresses key limitations of prior approaches.

**Technical Advancements and Significance:**
- **Unified and Efficient Model:** MARCO establishes that a single, adaptable DINOv2 backbone can achieve state-of-the-art performance in semantic correspondence, obviating the need for computationally expensive dual-encoder architectures involving diffusion models. This significantly reduces model size and inference time, making advanced correspondence capabilities more accessible and practical.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The work provides a solution to the prevalent issue of poor generalization in semantic correspondence models. By moving beyond sparse keypoint supervision through its dense self-distillation framework, MARCO learns more robust and transferable representations. This allows it to perform reliably on both unseen keypoints within known categories and entirely novel object classes, a crucial step towards models that function effectively in diverse, open-ended visual environments.
- **Novel Training Framework:** The introduction of a coarse-to-fine objective for spatial precision, combined with a dense self-distillation strategy leveraging flow anchoring, represents a methodological advancement. This framework effectively transforms limited sparse supervision into a dense, semantically coherent training signal, addressing the representation collapse often seen when fine-tuning pre-trained encoders with sparse annotations.
- **Minimal Dependency on External Cues:** Unlike some concurrent works that rely on 3D canonical templates or external depth models, MARCO achieves its generalization capabilities directly from the evolving feature space, without requiring such category-specific or geometrically explicit external information.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Practical Applications:** The efficiency and generalization capabilities of MARCO can directly benefit applications such as image editing, pose estimation, and robotic manipulation, where accurate and adaptable pixel-level correspondences are critical. Its faster inference speed and smaller footprint make it suitable for real-time systems or deployment on devices with limited computational resources.
- **Research Direction:** This work suggests a promising research direction towards developing correspondence models that are both accurate and efficient. It highlights the potential of leveraging the latent semantic structure within foundation models like DINOv2 more effectively. The demonstrated broad applicability of the dense self-distillation loss indicates that this technique could be integrated into and improve other correspondence pipelines.
- **Benchmark for Generalization:** The introduction of a new, challenging benchmark based on MP-100 for evaluating generalization across unseen keypoints and categories provides a valuable tool for the research community. This benchmark can facilitate the development and rigorous testing of future correspondence models, pushing the field towards more generalizable solutions.
- **Reduced Supervision Reliance:** The research points towards a future where models can infer geometric relationships more directly from visual data at scale, potentially reducing the heavy dependence on sparse, human-annotated keypoints. This could accelerate progress in scenarios where dense annotations are impractical or unavailable.
