# iMaC: Translating Actions into Motion and Contact Images for Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An embodied world model, iMaC, translates abstract robot actions into dense, image-like controls
—motion and contact images—to improve future video prediction for robot policy evaluation.
This approach achieved strong positive correlations (0.833 to 0.956) between world-model-based
success estimates and real-world policy performance across six out of eight challenging manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing embodied world models struggle to reliably condition future video prediction on robot actions, particularly for fine-grained spatial consequences in manipulation tasks.
- Current action representations are often abstract (compact vectors) or merely visualizations, failing to directly control future robot body states or explicitly describe robot-scene interaction geometry.
- Evaluating robot policies in the real world is resource-intensive, time-consuming, difficult to reproduce consistently, and poses safety risks.

## Method

- **iMaC** integrates a multi-view ==image-to-video diffusion== transformer with novel action-derived control videos: Motion Images, Scene-to-Gripper Contact Images, and Robot-to-Scene Contact Images.
- Motion Images provide direct pixel-level guidance for the future robot appearance by rendering robot configurations derived from URDF and forward kinematics.
- Contact Images encode contact-relevant spatial relations between the future robot's motion and the observed scene using two streams of pointcloud-derived distance fields.
- A training-time rollout strategy mitigates exposure bias by using the model's own one-step flow clean estimate of the final frame from the previous chunk as the next reference during training.

## Results

- iMaC achieved competitive future-video prediction quality, demonstrating the best task-averaged Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), and Frechet Video Distance (FVD) against baselines.
- For six out of eight challenging real-robot manipulation tasks, world-model evaluations exhibited strong positive correlations (**0.833** to **0.956**) with real-world policy performance, reliably preserving the relative ranking of policies.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both motion images and contact images are crucial for precise grasp and accurate robot body state prediction, with internally predicted RGB-D states yielding more consistent contact geometry than external depth estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatially explicit, image-like controls derived from robot kinematics and scene geometry can significantly enhance an embodied world model's ability to accurately predict fine-grained contact-rich manipulation.
> - Directly encoding future robot motion and robot-scene contact geometry into the latent space of a diffusion model reduces the model's burden of inferring complex interactions from abstract action representations.
> - Training a world model on its own generated contexts (via training-time rollout) is crucial for mitigating exposure bias and enabling robust, long-horizon closed-loop policy evaluation.
