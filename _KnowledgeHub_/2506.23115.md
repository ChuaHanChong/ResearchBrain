# MoCa: Modality-aware Continual Pre-training Makes Better Bidirectional Multimodal Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> MoCa is a two-stage framework that transforms pre-trained causal Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into robust bidirectional multimodal embedding models by applying modality-aware continual pre-training on unlabeled data and heterogeneous contrastive fine-tuning. The MoCa-7B model achieved a state-of-the-art 71.5 average Precision@1 on the MMEB benchmark and strong performance on ViDoRe-v2, demonstrating improved generalization and scalability.

## Problem

- Many state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are designed with causal attention for generation, which is suboptimal for embedding tasks requiring comprehensive context understanding.
- Current multimodal embedding methods heavily rely on large, costly labeled image-text pairs, limiting scalability and preventing the effective use of vast amounts of readily available unlabeled multimodal data.
- Existing VLM-based embedding approaches often use a single contrastive objective on relatively narrow datasets, which can lead to models that overfit and struggle to generalize to diverse multimodal scenarios beyond simple image-caption pairs.

## Method

- MoCa adapts pre-trained causal VLMs to a ==bidirectional attention mechanism== by removing causal masks and applying mean pooling for embedding extraction.
- It introduces ==Modality-aware Continual Pre-training (MCPT)== with a joint denoising objective (Masked Language Modeling for text and Masked Autoencoding for images) on massive unlabeled multimodal data.
- The framework incorporates ==Heterogeneous Contrastive Fine-tuning (HCFT)== using a broad range of supervised data, including long-form multimodal documents, curated pairs, and text-only pairs, along with task-aware batching.

## Results

- MoCa-7B achieved a state-of-the-art average Precision@1 score of **71.5** on the Massive Multimodal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB), surpassing prior models like mmE5 (**69.8**) and VLM2Vec (**62.9**).
- MoCa-3B, a smaller model, achieved an average **67.5** on MMEB and **59.8** NDCG@5 on ViDoRe-v2, demonstrating that the framework enables smaller models to match or exceed larger baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of Modality-aware Continual Pre-training (MLM and MAE) and Heterogeneous Contrastive Fine-tuning (diverse data types and task-aware batching) to the overall performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming causal VLMs into bidirectional encoders via continual pre-training with joint reconstruction (MLM+MAE) on unlabeled data significantly enhances multimodal embedding quality and scalability.
> - Leveraging diverse heterogeneous data sources and task-aware batching during contrastive fine-tuning is crucial for improving model generalization and robustness across various multimodal tasks.
> - The benefits of large-scale unlabeled continual pre-training can enable smaller models (e.g., 3B) to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding larger models (e.g., 7B) trained with less comprehensive methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "MoCa" addresses critical limitations within the rapidly evolving field of multimodal embedding models, which are fundamental for tasks requiring joint understanding and interaction between vision and language. These models aim to represent inputs from different modalities (e.g., images and text) in a shared, semantically meaningful vector space, enabling diverse applications such as multimodal classification, visual question answering (VQA), and document retrieval.

Historically, multimodal embedding research has progressed from dual-encoder architectures like CLIP and ALIGN, which process modalities separately and align them via contrastive learning, to approaches that leverage powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as backbones. Recent methods, such as VLM2Vec and mmE5, exemplify this trend, fine-tuning pre-trained VLMs (e.g., Phi-V, LLaVA, mLLaMA) with contrastive objectives on image-text pairs to enhance cross-modal representation quality.

However, MoCa identifies three significant limitations inherent in many existing VLM-based multimodal embedding models:

1. **Suboptimal Causal Attention:** Many state-of-the-art VLMs are designed with causal (autoregressive) attention mechanisms, primarily optimized for generation tasks. When these VLMs are repurposed for embedding tasks, they inherit this causal bias. As studies in text embedding (e.g., BERT, LLM2Vec) and foundational multimodal models (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP) have shown, bidirectional attention generally yields superior embeddings because it allows the model to leverage full context from both sides of a token or patch. MoCa aims to explicitly adapt VLMs to a bidirectional attention mechanism, addressing this architectural mismatch for embedding purposes.
2. **Scalability Challenges with Labeled Data:** Contrastive learning, while effective for alignment, heavily relies on large, diverse, and high-quality labeled image-text pairs. Curating such datasets is resource-intensive and limits scalability, preventing the utilization of vast amounts of readily available *unpaired* or *unlabeled* multimodal data on the internet. This work seeks more scalable training paradigms that can effectively leverage unlabeled data.
3. **Limited Diversity in Training Objectives and Data:** Many existing approaches fine-tune VLMs with a single contrastive objective on relatively narrow datasets, typically short image-caption pairs. This can lead to models that overfit to specific data distributions and struggle to generalize to more complex or diverse real-world multimodal scenarios. There's a need for objectives and data that promote deeper semantic and contextual understanding beyond surface-level similarity.

MoCa directly positions itself to overcome these challenges. It draws inspiration from text-only models like LLM2Vec, which successfully adapted causal Large Language Models (LLMs) into bidirectional text encoders through Masked Language Modeling (MLM). MoCa extends this concept to the multimodal domain, recognizing that a simple MLM approach is insufficient for mixed-modality inputs and necessitates a modality-aware, joint objective. By proposing a novel two-stage framework that incorporates continual pre-training with joint reconstruction objectives and heterogeneous contrastive fine-tuning, MoCa pushes the boundaries of multimodal embedding quality, scalability, and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of MoCa is to develop a robust and scalable framework for transforming existing pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into highly effective bidirectional multimodal embedding models. This objective is driven by the desire to overcome the three critical limitations identified in current state-of-the-art approaches:

1. **Objective 1: Transition from Causal to Bidirectional Attention for Superior Embeddings.**
 - **Motivation:** Current VLM-based embedding models inherit causal attention from their generative VLM backbones. However, for embedding tasks, which require a comprehensive understanding of the entire input context to produce rich representations, bidirectional attention is fundamentally more suitable. Analogous to the advancements seen in text embedding models (e.g., BERT over GPT-style models), allowing each token/patch to attend to all others in the sequence can significantly improve representation quality and enable practical benefits like late chunking for long contexts. MoCa aims to achieve this architectural shift efficiently through continual pre-training.

2. **Objective 2: Enhance Scalability by Reducing Reliance on Labeled Paired Data.**
 - **Motivation:** The conventional approach of relying solely on contrastive learning with high-quality labeled image-text pairs poses a significant bottleneck for scalability. Curating diverse and accurate paired data is costly and time-consuming. Moreover, this approach cannot leverage the vast quantities of unlabeled or weakly-labeled multimodal data available. MoCa seeks a training paradigm that can effectively utilize massive amounts of *unlabeled* multimodal data, thereby enabling training on larger datasets without prohibitive annotation costs.

3. **Objective 3: Improve Generalization and Deeper Semantic Understanding through Diverse Training Objectives and Data.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing fine-tuning strategies often involve a single contrastive objective applied to a limited variety of image-caption pairs. This can lead to models that only learn surface-level alignments and struggle to generalize to novel or more complex multimodal scenarios. The goal is to encourage the model to exploit the rich cross-modal reasoning capabilities inherent in pre-trained VLMs and learn more robust, semantically richer alignments. This requires incorporating more diverse training objectives and a broader distribution of multimodal data beyond simple image-caption pairs.

By addressing these three objectives, MoCa aims to significantly advance the performance, scalability, and robustness of multimodal embedding models, making them more versatile and powerful for a wide array of downstream applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MoCa proposes a novel two-stage framework to transform pre-trained VLMs into effective bidirectional multimodal embedding models: (1) Modality-aware Continual Pre-training and (2) Heterogeneous Contrastive Fine-tuning. The chosen VLM backbone for MoCa is Qwen-2.5-VL, a strong generative VLM.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Modality-aware Continual Pre-training (MCPT)

The first stage is designed to adapt the VLM backbone from its original causal attention mechanism to a bidirectional one, while simultaneously enhancing its cross-modal reasoning capabilities using massive unlabeled data. This is achieved through a joint denoising objective that combines Masked Language Modeling (MLM) for text and Masked Autoencoding (MAE) for images.

- **Bidirectional Adaptation:** The core idea is to remove the attention causal masks from the VLM backbone, allowing each token or image patch to attend to all other elements in the input sequence, irrespective of their position. This forms a bidirectional VLM backbone, denoted as $f_{\text{bi},\theta}(\cdot)$. For embedding extraction, mean pooling is applied over the hidden states of this bidirectional encoder, as opposed to relying on the last (EOS) token in causal models.

- **Joint Reconstruction Objective:** The model is trained to simultaneously reconstruct masked textual and visual inputs based on their interleaved multimodal context.
 - **Masked Language Modeling (MLM):** For text tokens, a random subset is replaced with a special mask token. The model then predicts the original masked tokens using the full multimodal context (both visible text and image patches). The MLM loss is computed using cross-entropy, where the prediction for a masked token at position $i$ is based on the representation of the previous token $i-1$, aligning with autoregressive VLM training recipes. This encourages the model to leverage both intra-modal and cross-modal textual dependencies.
 - **Masked Autoencoding (MAE):** For image patches, a random subset is masked (replaced with Gaussian noise). A lightweight decoder, a shallow transformer initialized from a middle layer of the VLM backbone, is then used on top of the VLM encoder's hidden states to reconstruct the original image patches. The MAE loss is computed using Mean Squared Error (MSE). This objective forces the model to understand visual semantics from partial information and integrate it with textual context.
 - **Combined Loss:** The final MCPT objective is a weighted sum of the MLM and MAE losses: $L_{\text{MoCa}} = L_{\text{MLM}} + wL_{\text{MAE}}$. The weight $w$ (set to 0.5) balances the contribution of textual and visual reconstruction.

- **Training Data and Efficiency:** This stage leverages a diverse and massive corpus of approximately 30 billion tokens, including text-only data (DCLM), common image-text pairs (PixelProse, CC12M, RedCaps, MAmmoTH-VL-Instruct, MMEB training set), and document-level multimodal data (DocMatix, VisRAG, ColPali training set). Efficient implementation strategies like data parallelism and sequence packing (to balance compute costs across GPUs) are crucial for handling such large-scale data.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Heterogeneous Contrastive Fine-tuning (HCFT)

Following MCPT, the bidirectionally adapted embedding model undergoes a fine-tuning stage with a contrastive objective, utilizing a broad range of heterogeneous multimodal data to further align embeddings and improve generalization.

- **Contrastive Learning Objective:** The standard InfoNCE-like contrastive loss is applied, maximizing similarity between query-positive document pairs ($q, d^+$) while minimizing similarity with hard negative documents ($d^-$) and in-batch negatives ($N_{\text{in}}(z)$). Cosine similarity is used, scaled by a temperature hyperparameter $\tau$ (fixed at 0.03).
- **Diverse Data Sources:** A key differentiator from prior works, which primarily rely on image-caption pairs, is the use of varied and semantically rich data categories:
 - **Long-form Multimodal Pairs:** From datasets like VisRAG and ViDoRe, these include document-level inputs with both images and extended text. This is critical for developing models capable of complex cross-modal reasoning over long contexts, especially for document retrieval tasks.
 - **Curated Multimodal Pairs:** From benchmarks like MMEB and mmE5, providing high-quality alignments beyond simple captioning, covering a wider range of visual and textual contexts.
 - **Text-only Pairs:** Sampled from large-scale retrieval datasets like E5, which are vital for enhancing the model's ability to capture fine-grained semantic differences purely within the linguistic domain.
 In total, approximately 2 million contrastive training pairs are sampled across these categories.
- **Task-aware Batching:** To make in-batch negative sampling more effective, a technique called task-aware batching is employed. This ensures that all instances within a given training batch originate from the same task or dataset. This strategy prevents trivial negative samples (e.g., an image of a puppy and an arXiv paper screenshot being "negative") and promotes the learning of harder, more discriminative representations relevant to specific task distributions, thereby improving generalization.

By combining these two stages, MoCa systematically addresses the limitations: MCPT transforms the VLM into a powerful bidirectional encoder scalable on unlabeled data, and HCFT refines its cross-modal alignment and generalization through diverse, semantically rich supervised data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MoCa's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated on two prominent multimodal benchmarks: the Massive Multimodal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB) and the Visual Document Retrieval Benchmarks (ViDoRe-v2).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **MMEB Performance (Table 1):** MoCa consistently sets new state-of-the-art results on MMEB, a comprehensive benchmark comprising 36 tasks across classification, VQA, retrieval, and visual grounding.
 - MoCa-7B achieves an overall average Precision@1 score of **71.5**, significantly outperforming prior SOTA models like mmE5 (69.8) and VLM2Vec (62.9).
 - Remarkably, MoCa-3B, a smaller model, achieves an average score of **67.5**. This score not only surpasses most existing baselines (e.g., VLM2Vec-7B at 62.9, MMRet-7B at 64.1) but also demonstrates that a smaller model, when subjected to MoCa's continual pre-training, can match or even exceed the performance of larger models that only use contrastive learning.
 - The results clearly show the benefit of the full MoCa framework ("bidirectional + CPT + CL") compared to baseline Qwen-2.5-VL variants trained only with contrastive learning, whether using causal or bidirectional attention alone. The combination significantly boosts performance across all meta-task categories.

- **ViDoRe-v2 Performance (Table 2):** MoCa also demonstrates strong performance on document-level multimodal retrieval, evaluated by NDCG@5.
 - MoCa-3B achieves an average score of **59.8**, slightly outperforming MoCa-7B (58.8). Both models achieve competitive results, with MoCa-3B setting a new high compared to GME (59.3) and previous strong baselines like VisRAG-Ret (51.0) and mmE5 (50.5).
 - The slight outperformance of the 3B model over the 7B model on ViDoRe-v2 is attributed by the authors to the relatively smaller dataset size for this benchmark, where a smaller model might be less prone to overfitting than a larger one. However, across the broader MMEB, the 7B model shows consistent advantages, confirming the method's scalability with model size.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation studies systematically confirm the contribution of each component of the MoCa framework (Table 3).
- **Modality-aware Continual Pre-training (MCPT):**
 - Removing Masked Language Modeling (w/o MLM) leads to a performance drop (e.g., MMEB: 66.2 vs. 67.5 for MoCa-3B).
 - Removing Masked Autoencoding (w/o MAE) also causes a decline (e.g., MMEB: 66.8 vs. 67.5).
 - Disabling both MLM and MAE (i.e., no continual pre-training) results in the largest performance degradation (e.g., MMEB: 65.8 vs. 67.5), underscoring the joint effectiveness of these objectives in developing robust bidirectional embeddings.
- **Heterogeneous Contrastive Fine-tuning (HCFT):**
 - Removing text-only pairs from the contrastive fine-tuning data (w/o Text-only Pairs) leads to a slight drop (MMEB: 67.1), indicating their importance for maintaining strong language representations.
 - Excluding long-form document retrieval pairs (w/o Long-form Document Retrieval Pairs) severely impacts ViDoRe-v2 performance (45.7 vs. 59.8), demonstrating their critical role for document-level understanding.
 - Removing task-aware batching (w/o Task-aware Batching) also leads to a noticeable performance decline (MMEB: 67.2 vs. 67.5), confirming its role in encouraging learning of more discriminative representations and improving generalization.

#### 4.3. Data Scaling Effect for Continual Pre-training

The study investigates how the scale of data used during Continual Pre-training (CPT) impacts downstream performance. As the number of CPT steps (and thus training tokens) increases, the downstream MMEB performance consistently improves for both 3B and 7B models (Figure 3). Notably, the MoCa-3B model, after CPT on approximately 20B tokens, achieves performance on par with a 7B baseline model that was trained without CPT. This robust scaling behavior suggests that MoCa can effectively leverage larger amounts of unlabeled data to further boost performance.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Analysis of Continual Pre-training

An analysis of key hyperparameters for the CPT stage (Mask Ratio, MAE Loss Weight, Learning Rate) reveals optimal settings that maximize performance (Figure 4). Moderate mask ratios (e.g., 40% for MLM, 50% for MAE) strike a balance between contextual learning and information retention. An MAE loss weight of 0.5 is found to best balance visual and textual reconstruction objectives. A learning rate of 2e-6 ensures stable optimization, demonstrating that careful tuning is crucial for effectively adapting pre-trained VLMs.

In summary, MoCa's experimental results convincingly validate its design principles. The combination of modality-aware continual pre-training and heterogeneous contrastive fine-tuning effectively addresses the limitations of existing methods, leading to state-of-the-art performance and strong scalability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MoCa represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal embeddings, offering a robust and scalable framework that addresses critical limitations of prior approaches. Its key contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Pioneering Continual Pre-training for Bidirectional Multimodal Embeddings:** MoCa is the first work to systematically propose and demonstrate the effectiveness of continual pre-training (CPT) with unlabeled data specifically for adapting causal Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into powerful bidirectional multimodal embedding models. This is a crucial paradigm shift, moving away from simple fine-tuning and unlocking the full potential of VLMs for embedding tasks. The strong scalability observed with increasing CPT data and model sizes highlights its practical viability for developing even more capable models in the future.

- **Architectural Optimization for Embedding Tasks:** By explicitly transforming VLMs to use bidirectional attention and mean pooling, MoCa rectifies a fundamental architectural mismatch. While causal attention is excellent for generative tasks, bidirectional context is inherently superior for creating dense, semantically rich embeddings that capture global relationships within an input. This shift leads to higher-quality representations, as evidenced by the significant performance gains across diverse benchmarks.

- **Enhanced Scalability and Resource Efficiency:** The reliance on joint reconstruction objectives (MLM and MAE) during CPT allows MoCa to effectively leverage vast amounts of *unlabeled* multimodal data. This capability drastically reduces the dependency on expensive, high-quality labeled paired datasets, making the development of powerful multimodal embeddings more scalable and cost-effective. The ability of MoCa-3B to outperform larger 7B baselines purely through this pre-training strategy underscores its efficiency.

- **Robustness and Generalization through Data Diversity:** The heterogeneous contrastive fine-tuning stage, utilizing long-form multimodal documents, curated multimodal pairs, and text-only pairs, is vital for the model's generalization capabilities. By exposing the model to a wider range of data distributions and semantic complexities (from short captions to detailed documents, and purely linguistic nuances), MoCa learns more robust and discriminative embeddings that perform well across diverse downstream tasks, preventing overfitting to narrow data types. The introduction of task-aware batching further refines this by providing harder, more meaningful in-batch negative samples.

- **New State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving new state-of-the-art results on the challenging MMEB benchmark and strong performance on ViDoRe-v2 demonstrates MoCa's superior general multimodal embedding quality and its particular strength in visually rich document retrieval. These results set a new bar for the field and provide a strong foundation for future research.

**Potential Impact:**
The proposed MoCa framework has broad implications for artificial intelligence and information retrieval:
- **Improved Multimodal Retrieval:** MoCa's strength in document retrieval (ViDoRe-v2) suggests direct applications in enterprise search, scientific literature indexing, and other domains involving complex multimodal documents.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Understanding:** Better multimodal embeddings can power more accurate visual question answering systems, multimodal classification, and visual grounding applications, improving human-AI interaction.
- **Foundation for Future Models:** The insights from MoCa regarding continual pre-training, bidirectional adaptation, and data diversity can serve as a blueprint for developing the next generation of multimodal foundation models, especially those designed for embedding and retrieval rather than solely generation.
- **Accessibility of High-Quality Models:** By demonstrating effective training on unlabeled data, MoCa opens avenues for smaller research groups or companies with limited labeling budgets to develop high-quality multimodal embedding models.

**Future Directions:**
The authors identify several promising avenues for future exploration:
1. **Multimodal Expansion:** Extending CPT to incorporate even more diverse modalities beyond image and text, such as video, speech, or structured data, to build more generalized embeddings.
2. **Advanced Denoising Objectives:** Investigating more sophisticated denoising objectives or truly unified encoder-decoder architectures to potentially further improve the efficiency and representation quality of bidirectional multimodal models.
3. **Real-World Application Validation:** Evaluating MoCa on more complex and challenging real-world scenarios like multi-hop retrieval or advanced interleaved-input reasoning tasks to further validate its robustness and practical utility.

Overall, MoCa represents a significant step forward in making multimodal embedding models more powerful, scalable, and versatile, leveraging existing VLM capabilities through strategic adaptation and diverse data utilization.
