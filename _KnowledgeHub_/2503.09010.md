# HumanoidPano: Hybrid Spherical Panoramic-LiDAR Cross-Modal Perception for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanoidPano offers a hybrid spherical panoramic-LiDAR perception framework designed for humanoid robots, addressing challenges of self-occlusion and limited field-of-view. It generates accurate real-time Bird's Eye View semantic maps, surpassing prior methods on the 360BEV-Matterport dataset by up to 4.66 percentage points in mIoU, and was validated on a full-scale humanoid platform.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots' anthropomorphic design causes severe self-occlusion and limited sensor field-of-view, leading to perceptual data degradation.
- Dynamic joint movements in humanoids induce real-time drift in sensor alignment matrices, complicating robust multimodal data fusion.
- Panoramic vision, while offering a 360-degree FOV, introduces distortions like structural fragmentation and scale disparity, hindering 3D scene understanding.

## Method

- Develops **HumanoidPano**, a hybrid panoramic-LiDAR cross-modal perception framework featuring a three-stage hierarchical processing pipeline.
- Implements a ==Panoramic Distortion==-aware Attention (PD Attention) module, which uses Spherical Geometry-aware Constraints (SGC) and Spatial Deformable Attention (SDA) for distortion-regularized 360°-to-BEV fusion.
- Introduces a novel joint data augmentation method for panoramic images and BEV semantic maps, maintaining cross-view metric consistency to enhance model generalization.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the 360BEV-Matterport dataset, improving mIoU by up to **4.66** percentage points (**40.12%** vs. **35.46%** baseline) on the validation set.
- Demonstrated superior robustness to simulated camera disturbances (pitch and roll rotations), exhibiting less performance degradation compared to previous methods.
- Successfully deployed on the full-scale Tiangong humanoid robot, providing real-time, robust BEV semantic mapping for navigation in complex, unstructured environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating spherical geometry constraints directly into the attention mechanism (SGC) is crucial for accurately aligning panoramic features with depth information and effectively managing image distortions.
> - An adaptive spatial attention mechanism (SDA) can efficiently aggregate geometrically consistent object representations from highly distorted panoramic images for robust 3D scene understanding.
> - Designing perception systems with explicit consideration for humanoid morphological characteristics, such as dynamic self-occlusion, enhances system robustness and overall performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Environmental perception is a foundational component of robotics, with significant advancements driven by computer vision in fields such as autonomous driving and industrial automation. Current standard solutions in these domains often rely on rigid, occlusion-free sensor configurations, such as roof-mounted multi-sensor arrays in autonomous vehicles or fixed Eye-in-Hand systems for industrial robots. These systems benefit from sensor field-of-view (FOV) invariance maintained through mechanical design, ensuring consistent geometric relationships between sensors.

However, the application of these perception paradigms to humanoid robots presents unique challenges. Humanoid robots, designed with biomimetic structures to enhance mobility and interaction in human-centric environments, inherently suffer from severe self-occlusion due to their anthropomorphic limbs. For example, arm movements can obstruct chest-mounted cameras, and leg movements can block waist-mounted sensors, leading to persistent degradation of perceptual data. This structural paradox renders conventional stereo vision and multi-view sensing configurations, effective in other robotic applications, less suitable for humanoid platforms. Additionally, the morphological diversity of humanoids complicates the establishment of universal sensor placement standards, and data scarcity hinders data-driven model adaptation.

Panoramic vision offers a potential solution by providing a 360-degree FOV, addressing the limited FOV issue. However, spherical projection introduces its own set of problems: structural fragmentation of linear elements, cross-regional scale disparity, and depth-aware feature degradation. Existing decoders that rely on 2D offset prediction mechanisms often fail to model the coupled effects of distortion and scene depth inherent in panoramic images. Furthermore, multimodal fusion in humanoid contexts is challenging because automotive LiDAR-camera registration paradigms are inadequate due to geometric incompatibility between spherical projections and point clouds. Dynamic joint motions in humanoids also induce real-time drift in cross-modal geometric alignment matrices. The complex spatial mapping required for unified panoramic-Bird's Eye View (BEV) representations is also limited by a lack of geometrically rich training data, as conventional augmentation strategies struggle to preserve cross-view metric consistency.

This work addresses these specific limitations by developing a hybrid cross-modal perception framework tailored for humanoid robots. It integrates panoramic vision and LiDAR sensing, aiming to overcome self-occlusion and FOV limitations by establishing geometrically-aware modality alignment. By focusing on distortion-aware fusion and robust BEV semantic map generation, the research contributes to advancing embodied perception for humanoid robots, enabling more autonomous operation in unstructured environments. It positions itself as a methodology that redefines humanoid robotic perception by adapting algorithms to morphological characteristics, transforming structural constraints into design assets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent structural and operational challenges faced by humanoid robots in achieving comprehensive environmental perception. Humanoid robots are envisioned as general-purpose robots capable of operating in complex, unstructured human environments. However, their anthropomorphic design, while advantageous for mobility and interaction, creates significant perceptual hurdles:
- **Severe Self-Occlusion:** The articulation of limbs and torso can frequently block the field of view of onboard cameras or other sensors, leading to blind spots that hinder continuous environmental understanding. For example, an elbow joint might obstruct a chest-mounted camera during a manipulation task, or thigh movements could completely occlude waist-mounted sensors during navigation.
- **Limited Field-of-View (FOV):** Traditional camera setups on humanoid robots often have a narrow FOV, limiting the robot's awareness of its surroundings and making navigation or interaction in dynamic environments challenging.
- **Dynamic Sensor Placement:** The robot's body movements induce changes in sensor positions and orientations, leading to real-time drift in geometric alignment matrices crucial for multimodal data fusion. This makes static calibration insufficient for continuous operation.
- **Distortion in Panoramic Vision:** While panoramic cameras offer a 360° FOV, their spherical projection introduces significant visual distortions (e.g., fragmentation of linear elements, scale disparities, depth-aware feature degradation) that complicate traditional computer vision tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation.
- **Incompatibility of Existing Solutions:** Conventional robot perception systems designed for autonomous vehicles or industrial arms often rely on rigid sensor configurations and assumptions that do not hold for the dynamic and self-occluding nature of humanoid robots. Stereo vision, for instance, is less effective when baseline stability is compromised by body movements.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of the HumanoidPano project are:
- **Develop a novel hybrid cross-modal perception framework:** This framework aims to synergistically integrate panoramic vision and LiDAR sensing to overcome the limitations of self-occlusion and limited FOV.
- **Establish geometrically-aware modality alignment:** The system seeks to achieve seamless fusion of 360° visual context from panoramic cameras with precise depth measurements from LiDAR, specifically by addressing the geometric incompatibilities introduced by spherical projection and dynamic sensor movements.
- **Generate accurate real-time Bird's Eye View (BEV) semantic maps:** The framework is designed to process the fused panoramic-LiDAR data to produce dense, semantically rich BEV maps. This is formalized as the task of "Panoramic-LiDAR Cross-modal BEV Semantic Segmentation," aiming to interpret 360° visual context and 3D spatial awareness jointly under dynamic morphological constraints.
- **Enable advanced downstream navigation tasks:** The generated BEV semantic maps are intended to directly support autonomous navigation and other complex tasks for humanoid robots in unstructured and cluttered environments, without reliance on external infrastructure.
- **Introduce specific technical innovations:** These include Spherical Geometry-aware Constraints (SGC) to guide distortion-regularized sampling, Spatial Deformable Attention (SDA) for efficient 360°-to-BEV fusion with complete object representations, and Panoramic Augmentation (AUG) to enhance feature consistency during data augmentation across views.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HumanoidPano framework is designed as a hybrid cross-modal perception system to integrate panoramic vision and LiDAR data for humanoid robots. It follows a three-stage hierarchical processing pipeline: pixel-level semantic feature extraction from panoramic images, geometry-aware depth profiling using LiDAR point clouds, and cross-modal feature fusion via a geometrically grounded alignment mechanism.

**Overall Architecture:**
The system comprises three main components:
1. **Transformer-based panoramic image encoder and transformation module:** This component processes the input panoramic RGB image.
2. **Panoramic Distortion-aware Attention (PD Attention) module:** This is central to the framework, responsible for view transformation from the panoramic image space to the BEV space and for adaptive spatial feature extraction, guided by spherical geometry constraints.
3. **BEV decoder:** This module parses the projected feature map from the PD Attention module to predict the final semantic BEV map.

**Panoramic Distortion-aware Attention (PD Attention):**
This module is introduced to address the challenges posed by distorted panoramic images, specifically variations in object scales and distortions, and to facilitate the learning of real 3D spatial distributions. It incorporates two main mechanisms:

- **Spherical Geometry-aware Constraints (SGC):** SGC leverages the intrinsic properties of panoramic imaging to guide distortion-aware cross-modal alignment.
 - **Input:** BEV queries (Q) and 3D reference points (P). The 3D reference points are obtained by projecting a depth map (D) into a pseudo-point cloud, with irrelevant points masked out.
 - **Transformation:** The 3D Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) of the reference points are transformed into spherical coordinates (azimuthal angle Φ, polar angle Θ) using standard conversion formulas.
 - **Offset Prediction:** Spherical sampling offsets (∆Φ, ∆Θ) are predicted by a linear layer based on the BEV queries and spherical position encoding of the initial spherical reference points.
 - **Adjusted Spherical Locations:** These offsets are added to the initial spherical coordinates to generate adjusted spherical locations (Φ', Θ').
 - **2D Index Generation:** Finally, these adjusted spherical locations are used to generate 2D index values (I_h, I_w) on the panoramic image feature map. This process aims to align panoramic features with depth measurements while accounting for projection artifacts.

- **Spatial Deformable Attention (SDA):** SDA performs efficient 360°-to-BEV fusion using adaptive spatial attention.
 - **Feature Sampling:** The 2D indices (I) generated by SGC serve as reference points for the BEV queries. Features are sampled from the panoramic image feature map (F) at these reference points.
 - **Weighted Sum:** A linear layer predicts additional 2D offsets for refinement, and an attention weight (A_i) is assigned to each sampled point. The final output of SDA is a weighted sum of these sampled features, aggregating geometrically consistent object representations from the panoramic image.

**Panoramic-LiDAR Cross-Modal Perception:**
The framework adopts a panoramic-LiDAR approach for depth information, primarily due to the limitations of conventional structured light depth cameras in real-world humanoid applications (e.g., limited range). LiDAR, despite generating sparser depth maps, offers extended sensing capabilities crucial for navigation.
- **Data Integration:** Real-world data is collected using an Insta360 X4 panoramic camera and a Livox Mid360 LiDAR. Precise alignment is achieved through timestamp synchronization.
- **Coordinate Transformation:** LiDAR point cloud data is transformed into the panoramic camera's coordinate system using an extrinsic calibration matrix.
- **BEV Map Construction:** This transformed depth information facilitates the construction of BEV-based map masks and map heights, which are then used by the PD Attention mechanism to generate 3D spatial points and 2D index values, leading to the final BEV semantic segmentation map. The architecture is designed to accommodate LiDAR's sparsity through its geometric constraint mechanisms.

**Data Augmentation:**
To enhance model generalization and robustness, the research incorporates a novel data augmentation method applied jointly to the panoramic image space and the BEV space.
- **Panoramic Image Augmentation:** Includes random horizontal flipping and random mixing (linear combination) of panoramic images within the training set.
- **BEV Augmentation:** Similar random flipping and mixing are applied to the BEV semantic maps. A critical aspect is maintaining geometric consistency: flipping in BEV space corresponds to the panoramic flip, and random rotations are applied to preserve spatial coherence. Mixing in BEV space also contributes to semantic diversity while maintaining spatial alignment. This joint augmentation addresses the challenge of preserving cross-view metric consistency during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HumanoidPano was conducted through extensive experiments on a standard benchmark dataset, under noisy conditions, via ablation studies, and through real-world deployment on a humanoid robot.

**Evaluation Dataset and Metrics:**
The method was evaluated on the 360BEV-Matterport dataset, which provides panoramic RGB images (512×1024 resolution) paired with dense BEV semantic annotations for 20 navigation-critical categories. Synthetic depth maps, consistent with panoramic inputs, are also provided. Evaluation metrics included pixel-wise accuracy (Acc), pixel recall (mRecall), precision (mPrecision), and mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU).

**Quantitative Performance on 360BEV-Matterport Dataset:**
- **State-of-the-art performance:** HumanoidPano consistently surpassed previous state-of-the-art methods, including 360BEV, Trans4Map, and BEVFormer, on both the validation and test sets of the 360BEV-Matterport dataset.
- **Significant Improvements:**
 - **On the validation set (Table 1):** With a MiT-B0 backbone, HumanoidPano (with augmentation) achieved mIoU of 40.12%, surpassing 360BEV (35.46%) by 4.66 percentage points. Similar improvements were observed for MiT-B2 (44.54% vs. 41.42%) and MSCA-B backbones (46.35% vs. 45.04%).
 - **On the test set (Table 2):** With MiT-B0, HumanoidPano (with augmentation) reached an mIoU of 35.37%, exceeding 360BEV (31.81%) by 3.56 percentage points. Comparable improvements were noted for MiT-B2 (38.49% vs. 36.00%) and MSCA-B backbones (40.41% vs. 39.10%).
- These results indicate that HumanoidPano's architecture effectively leverages panoramic vision and LiDAR for accurate BEV semantic mapping.

**Robustness to Noisy Settings (Table 3):**
- The system's robustness was tested under simulated camera disturbances (pitch and roll rotations).
- In these noisy conditions, HumanoidPano demonstrated less performance degradation compared to 360BEV. For instance, with both pitch and roll disturbances, HumanoidPano (with augmentation) experienced a 22.40% drop in mIoU, while 360BEV showed a 24.78% drop. This suggests that HumanoidPano's ability to understand the 3D world through its geometric constraints makes it more resilient to sensor disturbances.

**Ablation Study (Table 4):**
An ablation study confirmed the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Spatial Deformable Attention (SDA):** Implementing SDA alone (without SGC and AUG) provided a notable improvement in mIoU (e.g., from 31.81% to 33.05% with MiT-B0), indicating its role in capturing spatial context and enhancing 3D world understanding.
- **Spherical Geometry-aware Constraints (SGC):** Adding SGC to SDA further improved performance (e.g., to 33.56% mIoU). This demonstrates SGC's effectiveness in building panoramic perception capability and adapting to distorted object structures.
- **Panoramic Augmentation (AUG):** The inclusion of data augmentation consistently provided additional performance gains (e.g., to 35.37% mIoU), confirming its role in enhancing data diversity, improving model robustness, and promoting generalization.

**Qualitative Results (Figure 6):**
Visual comparisons showed that HumanoidPano outperformed baseline methods in distorted panoramic regions, particularly on severely warped objects. This capability is critical for reliable panoramic perception in both humanoid and general embodied AI systems.

**Real-World Humanoid Robot Validation:**
- **Deployment:** HumanoidPano was successfully deployed and validated on Tiangong, a full-sized humanoid robot (1.65m height, 23 DoF) developed by the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center.
- **Hardware Integration:** The perception module, comprising an Insta360 X4 panoramic camera and Livox Mid-360 LiDAR, was integrated with minimal structural interference, ensuring continuous 360° sensing even during dynamic motions.
- **Operational Efficacy:** Evaluations in cluttered domestic environments demonstrated robust BEV semantic mapping during complex full-body interactions, such as doorway traversal and obstacle navigation.
- **Practical Impact:** The framework resolved system-level challenges in omnidirectional semantic perception for humanoids, providing reliable semantic guidance for downstream navigation modules in real-time. This marked the first reported realization of embodied panoramic-BEV perception on a full-scale humanoid platform without external infrastructure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HumanoidPano framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic perception, particularly for humanoid robots, with broad implications for future embodied intelligence systems.

**1. A New Paradigm for Embodied Perception:** The work establishes a novel paradigm that moves beyond adapting conventional robot perception systems to humanoids. Instead, it proposes a co-design approach where perception algorithms are specifically tailored to the unique morphological characteristics and constraints of humanoid robots. This 'morphology-aware perception' transforms structural limitations, such as self-occlusion and dynamic sensor displacement, into explicit design considerations, effectively converting potential weaknesses into assets for algorithm development.

**2. Overcoming Fundamental Humanoid Perception Challenges:** HumanoidPano directly addresses critical long-standing issues in humanoid robot perception, including severe self-occlusion, limited field-of-view, and the complexities of integrating multi-modal sensors on a dynamically moving platform. By synergistically fusing 360° panoramic vision with LiDAR's precise depth measurements through geometrically-aware alignment mechanisms, the system achieves a comprehensive environmental awareness that was previously unattainable with conventional setups.

**3. Enhanced 3D Scene Understanding and Semantic Mapping:** The framework's ability to generate accurate, real-time Bird's Eye View (BEV) semantic maps from distorted panoramic and sparse LiDAR data is a key contribution. This capability provides humanoid robots with a rich, interpretable representation of their surroundings, which is crucial for higher-level cognitive functions and decision-making. The proposed Spherical Geometry-aware Constraints (SGC) and Spatial Deformable Attention (SDA) are methodological innovations that enable robust and distortion-resilient feature extraction and fusion, enhancing the precision of environmental understanding.

**4. Real-World Applicability and Validation:** The successful deployment and validation of HumanoidPano on a full-scale physical humanoid robot, Tiangong, underscore its practical significance. This demonstration validates the system's robustness and efficiency in complex, unstructured indoor environments, showing its capability to support advanced downstream tasks like semantic map-based navigation. This is a critical step towards developing autonomous humanoid robots capable of operating effectively in human-centric spaces without reliance on external infrastructure or pre-mapped environments.

**5. Scalable Architectural Blueprint:** The HumanoidPano framework offers a scalable architectural blueprint for future embodied AI systems. By providing both theoretical foundations and implementation guidelines for morphology-aware perception, it can inspire and facilitate the development of next-generation human-centered robotic systems across various applications, from assistance in homes and workplaces to exploration in challenging terrains.

In summary, this research not only advances the technical capabilities of humanoid robots for environmental perception but also contributes to a deeper understanding of how to design perception systems that are intrinsically adapted to the physical embodiment of robots.
