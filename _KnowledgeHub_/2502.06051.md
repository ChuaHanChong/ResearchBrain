# Towards a Sharp Analysis of Offline Policy Learning for $f$-Divergence-Regularized Contextual Bandits

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCLA and UIUC provided a sharp theoretical analysis for offline policy learning in f-divergence-regularized contextual bandits, demonstrating an eΘ(ϵ⁻¹) sample complexity for reverse KL regularization under single-policy concentrability and for strongly convex f-divergences without any data coverage dependency. This work established the sufficiency and necessity of single-policy concentrability for KL-regularization and showed that strongly convex f-divergences inherently mitigate data distribution shift challenges.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggles to achieve optimal policies from static datasets due to data hunger and instability, often relying on policy regularization.
- Prior theoretical analyses for f-divergence-regularized offline RL yielded suboptimal eO(ϵ⁻²) sample complexity bounds or required strong "all-policy concentrability" for tighter eO(ϵ⁻¹) bounds.
- A critical open problem was determining the weakest data coverage conditions necessary for near-optimal offline learning with f-divergence-regularized objectives, particularly for reverse KL divergence.

## Method

- Proposed **KL-PCB**, an algorithm for KL-regularized contextual bandits, which integrates ==pessimistic reward estimation== using a confidence bonus to address uncertainty.
- Introduced f-CB, an algorithm for f-divergence-regularized contextual bandits with strongly convex f, which directly computes the policy from a least-squares reward estimate.
- Utilized novel analytical techniques, including a moment-based analysis for KL-regularization and exploiting the dual-Bregman perspective for strongly convex f-divergences, to derive sharper sample complexity bounds.

## Results

- KL-PCB achieved an eO(η D²π* ϵ⁻¹ log N_G(ϵ)) sample complexity for KL-regularized contextual bandits, confirming the eΘ(ϵ⁻¹) rate under "single-policy concentrability" and matching information-theoretic lower bounds.
- The f-CB algorithm for strongly convex f-divergences attained an eΘ(α⁻¹ηϵ⁻¹ log N_G(ϵ)) sample complexity, a key finding demonstrating independence from data coverage conditions.
- Empirical results across various bandit environments validated the n⁻¹ rate for sub-optimality and consistently supported the theoretical predictions on concentrability dependency for KL-regularization and coverage independence for strongly convex f-divergences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reverse KL-regularization can achieve an eΘ(ϵ⁻¹) sample complexity under a weaker "single-policy concentrability" condition, which is shown to be both sufficient and necessary.
> - Strongly convex f-divergences inherently offer a stronger regularization effect, enabling eΘ(ϵ⁻¹) sample complexity without any dependency on data coverage conditions.
> - Novel analytical techniques, including a moment-based lemma, effectively transform risk bounds from all-policy to single-policy concentrability dependencies for KL-regularization.
