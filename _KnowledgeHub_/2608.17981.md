# Recirculation

> [!summary] Summary
> Recirculation is a training-free, inference-time technique that leaks a mixture of activation from a deep transformer layer back into a shallow layer at each processing step, creating recurrence in both depth and input-step dimensions so a frozen LLM can track evolving belief states. On Gemma3, adaptive recirculation cuts perplexity 23% and lifts GSM8k accuracy 21%, with no weight changes and negligible generation latency.

## Problem

- Feedforward transformers process tokens in parallel, so state updates are bounded by model depth, and late-resolved deep-layer disambiguations remain inaccessible to shallow layers before response commitment.
- Chain-of-thought propagates state via natural-language tokens but is costly to train and better reserved for complex inference than basic within-step state tracking.
- Trained recurrent architectures and depth-only looped transformers either lack guaranteed indefinite state tracking or require costly training/fine-tuning, limiting applicability to off-the-shelf frozen models.

## Method

- **Recirculation** leaks a ==convex mixture== of deep-layer (source) activation into a shallow-layer (destination) after each step, unrolling recurrence across both depth and input-step dimensions, unlike depth-only looping.
- A ==renormalization function== rescales source activation to the destination's ==L2 norm== before mixing, and ramping attenuates the mixture coefficient alpha for early context tokens to avoid harm.
- **Adaptive recirculation** trains a ==lightweight MLP==, base LLM frozen, that maps token-specific source/destination embeddings to token-conditional, vector-valued ==mixture coefficients== alpha and beta, replacing fixed scalar hyperparameters.

## Results

- Across Gemma3 1B/4B/12B on ten language-modeling datasets, fixed recirculation cuts perplexity up to **35%** on the 12B model; adaptive recirculation reaches a **23.0%** mean reduction versus **8.5%** fixed and **21.6%** for full fine-tuning.
- On GSM8k with Gemma3 4B, adaptive recirculation raises pass@1 accuracy from **29.3%** to **35.5%** and pass@128 from **94.9%** to **96.0%**, an **8.8%** and **20.9%** error-rate reduction respectively.
- Recirculation improves instruction-following (**25-75%** error reduction on Gemma3 4B/12B) and Racing Thoughts contextualization accuracy, while its effect is nearly additive with temperature tuning, ruling out simple distribution-sharpening artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training-free architectural interventions can reveal a pretrained model's own design affordances, inductive biases already present in the weights, guiding cheaper adaptation than arbitrary architecture proposals.
> - Recurrence needs both depth and input-step dimensions, not depth alone as in looping, since only step-wise recurrence lets a single layer hold both current and next state.
> - Benefits concentrate on content-bearing tokens (adverbs, adjectives, verbs, plural nouns) and mid-context positions, suggesting gains stem from genuine belief-state propagation rather than uniform signal amplification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a fundamental limitation of current transformer-based LLMs: their ability to track evolving "belief states" over extended sequences. Traditional transformers operate in a feedforward manner, meaning that information propagation and state updates are bounded by the model's architectural depth. While transformers can learn various mechanisms for state tracking over finite sequence lengths, they can exhibit failures in coherence for multi-turn conversations, information gathering, and multi-agent interactions, often due to an inability for shallow layers to access contextualized representations established in deeper layers.

The paper distinguishes its proposed method, "recirculation," from several related areas:
- **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and State-Space Models (SSMs)**: These architectures are inherently designed for sequential state tracking, but many SSMs do not offer greater expressivity than transformers, and training fully recurrent transformers can be computationally costly due to parallelism restrictions.
- **Looped Transformers**: These approaches involve applying a set of shared transformer blocks multiple times, effectively increasing the model's depth without adding new parameters. While successful in improving expressivity and reasoning, looping is primarily a depth-recurrence mechanism. The paper argues that recirculation introduces recurrence in both depth and input steps, allowing the same layer to track state over time, conceptually distinct from simply making the network deeper.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Latent Thought Models**: These methods enhance model expressivity by allowing the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps, often in natural language or latent space. While also introducing a form of recurrence, the paper positions recirculation as a more fundamental state-tracking mechanism distinct from complex inference steps, operating at the level of internal representation mixing rather than explicit thought generation.
- **Activation Steering**: Recent work has shown that modulating latent representations can predictably alter model behavior. Recirculation can be viewed as an inference-time self-steering mechanism, where the model's own deep-layer contextualized activations guide shallower representations, rather than relying on external, static steering vectors.

By proposing a training-free, inference-time architectural modification, this work seeks to leverage existing model properties, suggesting a route to architectural evolution guided by a trained network's inherent characteristics, rather than arbitrary design choices followed by expensive retraining. It aims to improve a foundational aspect of LLMs (state tracking) with minimal computational overhead during generation, potentially enhancing all capabilities built upon it.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the state-tracking capabilities of off-the-shelf foundation models without requiring costly retraining or fine-tuning of the entire model. This enhancement aims to improve perplexity and accuracy across various generation and reasoning tasks.

The motivation stems from the observed limitations of feedforward transformer architectures in maintaining a consistent and evolving understanding of context, referred to as "state tracking." The paper illustrates this limitation using a "bank" polysemy example (Figure 1 and 2a). In this scenario, a model might initially encounter the word "bank" in a context implying a "river bank." While deeper layers successfully disambiguate the word to this meaning, shallower layers, which contribute to early response generation, may still operate on an ambiguous representation. This can lead to "contextualization errors" where the model later incorrectly interprets "bank" as a "financial institution" when asked about an ATM, despite having previously processed it in the "river bank" context.

This phenomenon, characterized as a "race" between response generation and internal semantic convergence, suggests that critical state information resolved in deep layers is not readily available to shallower layers or subsequent processing steps. Prior work has shown that "patching" deep, disambiguated activations back to shallow layers can significantly reduce such errors, indicating that the information exists but is not effectively utilized throughout the feedforward pass.

The core motivation for recirculation is to address this by introducing a specific form of recurrence that allows deep, contextualized representations to "leak" back to shallower layers. This process aims to enrich the representations in earlier layers, making the resolved state information available for subsequent processing, without disrupting the model's distribution or requiring extensive retraining. The hypothesis is that a small, controlled feedback mechanism can significantly improve the model's ability to maintain a coherent belief state over time, leveraging the residual stream's property of shared "blackboard" representations across layers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, "recirculation," introduces a form of recurrence into an off-the-shelf transformer architecture at inference time. The core idea is to leak activation from a deep layer (source) to a shallow layer (destination) after each processing step.

**Formalization of Recirculation:**
The method is formalized by mixing activation from a source layer ($s$) with a destination layer ($d$) at each update step ($t$):
$z^{t+1,t,d} = \alpha f(z^{t,t,s} | d, t) + \beta z^{t,t,d}$
where:
- $z^{i,j,l}$ is the residual stream output after computation of layer $l$ at unrolling step $i$ and input step $j$.
- $s$ and $d$ are source and destination layer indices, respectively.
- $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are mixture coefficients, with $\beta \equiv 1 - \alpha$ typically for a convex mixture.
- $f(.)$ is a renormalization function, primarily used to rescale the source activation vector to have the same L2 norm as the destination vector: $f(z|d,t) = \frac{||z^{t,t,d}||_2}{||z||_2} z$. This normalization helps prevent source activations from overwhelming destination activations due to norm growth across layers.

**Distinction from Looped Transformers:**
The paper critically distinguishes recirculation from looped transformers. While looped transformers unroll recurrence solely in depth (applying shared blocks multiple times to increase effective depth), recirculation unrolls in both depth and input steps. This means that in a looped transformer, a state $z(t+1)$ is always one layer deeper than $z(t)$. In recirculation, however, the same layer can hold both $z(t)$ and $z(t+1)$, allowing for indefinite state propagation within the same architectural depth. This sequential updating in recirculation precludes parallel processing during the prefill phase, unlike looped transformers. All experiments in the paper use a single additional iteration of recirculation.

**Experimental Setup and Evaluation:**

1. **Hyperparameter Sweeps:**
 - Initial feasibility was explored by sweeping the mixture coefficient $\alpha$, source layer $s$, and destination layer $d$.
 - Evaluations were conducted on Gemma3 1B PT model using perplexity on a subset of arXiv, PG19, and C4 datasets.
 - Optimal $(s, d)$ pairs were identified for Gemma3 1B, 4B, and 12B models (e.g., {11, 4} for 1B).

2. **Perplexity Evaluation:**
 - The chosen $(s, d)$ pairs and a fixed $\alpha = 0.15$ were used to evaluate perplexity reduction on ten diverse language modeling datasets for Gemma3 1B, 4B, and 12B PT models.
 - Various normalization schemes were also explored, finding that convex or non-convex mixtures with L2 norm ratio scaling were most robust. Ramping of $\alpha$ for early tokens was introduced for the 1B model to avoid initial performance harm.

3. **Analysis of Recirculation:**
 - **Robustness across Model Families:** Recirculation's effectiveness was tested on other model families (Ministral3, Pythia, Qwen3, Phi2, Gemma2, Gemma4) to assess generalizability.
 - **Recirculation vs. Temperature Tuning:** Compared perplexity reduction from recirculation against adjustments to the softmax temperature to rule out simple distribution sharpening/smoothing.
 - **Recirculation vs. Looping:** Qualitatively compared perplexity heatmaps of recirculation against inference-time looped transformers to highlight architectural differences and performance outcomes.
 - **Token-Specific Benefits:** Analyzed which tokens (by position and Part-of-Speech) benefit most from recirculation by selectively applying the mechanism.

4. **Generative Tasks Evaluation:**
 - **Instruction Following:** Evaluated accuracy on a custom "fruit/animal" instruction-following task, comparing baseline, perplexity-tuned recirculation, and task-specific hyperparameter-tuned recirculation.
 - **Contextualization:** Used the "Racing Thoughts" dataset (Lepori et al., 2025) to assess performance on questions requiring disambiguation over varying distractor sentence counts.
 - **Multiple-Choice and Single-Token Response Tasks:** Tested on standard benchmarks from the Open LLM Leaderboard (ARC, MMLU, Winogrande, BoolQ, PiQA, HellaSwag, Lambada).
 - **GSM8k:** Evaluated performance on grade-school math problems requiring chain-of-thought reasoning, using pass@1 and pass@128 metrics to assess capability sharpening and expansion.

5. **Adaptive Recirculation:**
 - Introduced methods to learn the mixing coefficients $\alpha$ and $\beta$ through light fine-tuning, rather than fixed values.
 - Six variants were explored: fixed scalars, learned constant scalars, learned conditional scalars (MLP mapping embeddings to scalars), learned constant vectors, learned conditional vectors (MLP mapping embeddings to vectors), and full LLM fine-tuning.
 - The "learned conditional vectors" scheme, dubbed "adaptive recirculation," trains a small MLP to generate token-specific vector-valued $\alpha$ and $\beta$ based on source and destination embeddings, while freezing the main LLM weights. This was evaluated on perplexity and downstream tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings:

1. **Perplexity Reduction:**
 - Recirculation consistently reduced perplexity across a wide range of language modeling datasets and Gemma3 model sizes (1B, 4B, 12B PT).
 - For the 12B model, perplexity reductions reached up to 35.40% (PG19) and 32.91% (Booksum/book). The 1B and 4B models also showed significant reductions, up to 16%.
 - Normalization of source activations was crucial for robust improvements across hyperparameter sweeps.
 - Optimal source and destination layers were consistently found, with source layers typically deeper than destination layers.

2. **Robustness and Generalizability:**
 - Recirculation showed qualitatively similar benefits across five diverse model families (Gemma3, Ministral3, Pythia, Qwen3, Phi2), indicating broad applicability, though the magnitude of gains varied.
 - The Gemma family (Gemma2, Gemma3, Gemma4) showed particularly pronounced gains, which the authors hypothesize might be due to its Peri-LN architecture or training optimization process.

3. **Distinction from Related Techniques:**
 - Recirculation's effect was not reducible to simple softmax temperature adjustments; combining recirculation with optimal temperature tuning yielded additive perplexity reductions.
 - Qualitative differences in performance heatmaps demonstrated that recirculation operates on distinct principles from looped transformers, with looping not consistently producing robust benefits for Gemma3 without training.

4. **Token-Specific Effects:**
 - Recirculation provided larger benefits at shorter lags (distance from the recirculated token), but measurable benefits persisted even at long lags (up to 256 tokens).
 - Early tokens in the context window (e.g., first 10 tokens) sometimes showed harm from recirculation in the 1B model, suggesting little state information to propagate initially.
 - Tokens with grammatical parts-of-speech such as adverbs, adjectives, and verbs showed the largest benefits, while fixed classes like numerals and determiners showed smaller effects. Plural nouns benefited more than singular nouns.

5. **Performance on Generative Tasks:**
 - **Instruction Following:** For Gemma3 4B and 12B IT models, recirculation reduced error rates by approximately 25% and 75% respectively on a simple in-context instruction-following task. Task-specific hyperparameter tuning yielded even greater gains.
 - **Contextualization:** Recirculation generally improved accuracy on the contextualization error dataset (Lepori et al., 2025) for Gemma3 1B and 4B IT models, particularly as the number of distractor sentences increased. However, performance on the 12B model for this task showed mixed results.
 - **Multiple-Choice and Single-Token Response:** Recirculation yielded modest improvements in accuracy on 6 out of 8 standard benchmark datasets (e.g., MMLU, ARC Easy, PiQA) for Gemma3 4B PT, with small decreases on the others.
 - **GSM8k (Chain-of-Thought Reasoning):** Recirculation significantly improved accuracy on grade-school math problems, increasing pass@1 (greedy decoding) and pass@128 scores, indicating support for both capability sharpening and expansion in extended generative responses.

6. **Adaptive Recirculation:**
 - Learning the mixture coefficients ($\alpha$, $\beta$) through a small MLP (adaptive recirculation) significantly enhanced performance compared to fixed coefficients.
 - The "learned conditional vectors" scheme achieved a mean 23.0% perplexity reduction for Gemma3 1B PT, relative to 8.5% for fixed recirculation. This performance was comparable to or slightly better than full LLM fine-tuning, but without modifying the original model weights.
 - On downstream tasks, adaptive recirculation showed promising, robust accuracy gains when tuned on an appropriate dataset (e.g., MMLU test set), leading to an 8.8% and 20.9% reduction in error rate for GSM8k pass@1 and pass@128, respectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in demonstrating that substantial improvements in LLM performance can be achieved through a training-free, inference-time architectural modification, "recirculation." This approach departs from typical research that focuses on costly full model training or fine-tuning, instead highlighting how a pretrained model's inherent properties can be leveraged for improvement.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Novel Recurrence Mechanism:** Recirculation introduces a specific type of recurrence, distinct from looped transformers and traditional recurrent networks, that allows for sustained state tracking within the same layers over sequential input steps.
- **Training-Free Enhancement:** The method works "out-of-the-box" on off-the-shelf foundation models, requiring no modification to the original model weights during initial implementation, making it computationally inexpensive to deploy during generation.
- **Improved State Tracking:** By enabling shallower layers to access contextualized representations from deeper layers, recirculation directly addresses a core limitation of feedforward transformers, leading to notable reductions in perplexity and enhancements in tasks requiring consistent contextual understanding and reasoning.
- **"Model-Design Affordances":** The research advocates for exploring "model-design affordances"—properties of a trained model that reveal pathways for architectural improvement. This philosophical contribution suggests that identifying how a pretrained model "wants" to be tweaked can guide more effective and less costly architectural evolutions.
- **Adaptive Recirculation:** The development of adaptive recirculation, where a small auxiliary network learns to dynamically modulate recirculation coefficients, further boosts performance without altering the core LLM weights, showcasing the potential for lightweight adaptation.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced LLM Capabilities:** By improving the foundational ability of state tracking, recirculation can bolster the coherence of multi-turn conversations, the accuracy of question-answering, and the robustness of complex reasoning tasks (e.g., chain-of-thought).
- **Cost-Effective Improvements:** The training-free and low-latency nature of recirculation during generation provides a practical and efficient method for enhancing existing LLMs without the prohibitive computational costs of retraining or extensive fine-tuning.
- **Guidance for Future Architecture Design:** The concept of model-design affordances could inspire new research directions, encouraging the exploration of intrinsic model properties to inform architectural innovations, potentially simplifying future training processes and reducing development cycles.
- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** Recirculation is presented as complementary to other common practices like looping, variable computation time, and coarser-grain recurrence, suggesting it could be integrated to achieve cumulative benefits.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** Optimal hyperparameters for recirculation (source/destination layers, coefficients) can be task-dependent, requiring some tuning, although perplexity-derived parameters often generalize.
- **Model Family Dependence:** While broadly applicable, the magnitude of benefits may vary across model architectures, with Gemma models showing particular compatibility.
- **Normalization Schemes:** The specific normalization of activation vectors requires further investigation for generalizability across different model families.
- **Prefill Phase Latency:** Recirculation requires token-by-token sequential processing during the prefill phase, which can be slow for large contexts. Blockwise recirculation is proposed as a potential mitigation.
- **Single Iteration:** The current work explores only one additional iteration of recirculation; multiple iterations could offer further benefits but need to be studied.
- **Single Recirculation Path:** Only one source-destination pair was used; exploring multiple simultaneous recirculation paths could be beneficial, especially if different paths convey distinct levels of abstraction.
- **Further Adaptation:** The adaptive recirculation methods are preliminary; more sophisticated approaches to learning conditional pathways and normalization could yield greater gains.

In conclusion, recirculation offers a promising and computationally efficient avenue for improving the contextual understanding and reasoning capabilities of large language models by intelligently leveraging their internal representations.
