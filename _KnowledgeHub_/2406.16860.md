# Cambrian-1: A Fully Open, Vision-Centric Exploration of Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Cambrian-1 presents a fully open, vision-centric exploration of multimodal LLMs, systematically investigating the impact of various visual representations and connector designs on performance. The research introduces the Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA) and a new vision-centric benchmark, CV-Bench, achieving state-of-the-art performance among open-source models and competitive results against leading proprietary MLLMs.

## Problem

- The disproportionate focus on the language model backbone in MLLMs has led to insufficient exploration of vision components and their systematic evaluation.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks exhibit biases, often relying more on LLM capabilities than true visual grounding, and lack sufficient size for robust vision-centric evaluation.
- Current MLLMs struggle with genuine visual grounding in real-world scenarios, potentially due to over-reliance on language-supervised visual models like CLIP which may introduce a "language shortcut".

## Method

- A ==vision-centric approach== to MLLM design is employed, systematically evaluating over 20 diverse vision encoders (self-supervised, strongly supervised, etc.) using ==MLLM instruction tuning== as an evaluation protocol.
- A novel **Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA)** is introduced, which uses ==learnable latent queries== aligned with specific feature map sub-regions and ==multi-layer cross-attention== to efficiently integrate high-resolution, multi-source visual information.
- A comprehensive instruction tuning dataset, **Cambrian-7M**, is curated using a ==targeted internet data collection engine== and careful data balancing, alongside optimized training recipes including ==two-stage fine-tuning== and unfreezing vision encoders.

## Results

- The Cambrian-1 family of MLLMs achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source models, demonstrating competitive results against leading proprietary models like GPT-4V and Gemini-Pro on various benchmarks.
- The Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA) consistently outperforms other aggregation methods, showing significant improvements in OCR and chart tasks while using only **576 visual tokens**, a fifth of competitors.
- CV-Bench, a new vision-centric benchmark, repurposes classic 2D and 3D vision tasks into a VQA format, providing **3.5 times** more manually-inspected examples than previous benchmarks and highlighting MLLM reliance on LLM capabilities in existing benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Two-stage instruction tuning and unfreezing the vision encoder during fine-tuning consistently improve MLLM performance.
> - While language-supervised vision encoders generally lead, the performance gap with self-supervised models can be significantly narrowed or bridged by increased instruction tuning data and unfreezing the vision backbone.
> - Combining multiple vision encoders improves performance across various benchmarks, and the Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA) efficiently integrates high-resolution visual information while maintaining a low visual token count.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has rapidly advanced, driven primarily by the remarkable scaling capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). Early pioneering MLLMs like LLaVA demonstrated strong performance by integrating visual encoders with pre-trained LLMs. However, a critical observation in the current landscape, as highlighted by this paper, is a disproportionate focus on enhancing the language model backbone while neglecting a systematic exploration of the **vision components**. Many MLLMs predominantly rely on language-supervised visual models like CLIP, which, despite their effectiveness, can introduce a "language shortcut" that compensates for deficiencies in truly effective visual representations. This raises concerns about the genuine "visual grounding" of these models in real-world scenarios.

Cambrian-1 directly addresses this imbalance by advocating for a **vision-centric approach** to MLLM design and evaluation. It positions MLLMs not just as a means to understand multimodal inputs, but also as a powerful and flexible **evaluation protocol for various visual representations**. Traditionally, visual representations are evaluated using saturated benchmarks like ImageNet-1K or COCO. Cambrian-1 proposes that the diverse perception challenges inherent in real-world scenarios, as encapsulated by visual question answering (VQA) tasks, offer a more robust and insightful evaluation framework for visual models. By systematically investigating a wide array of vision encoders—including self-supervised, strongly supervised, and other specialized models (e.g., depth, segmentation)—Cambrian-1 aims to bridge the gap between decades of visual representation learning research and the emerging MLLM paradigm. Its commitment to full openness (code, models, data, recipes) also positions it as a foundational "cookbook" to accelerate open research in multimodal AI, contrasting with the often opaque nature of state-of-the-art proprietary MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Cambrian-1 project are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Vision-Language Gap:** The core motivation is to address the disconnect between advancements in large language models and the comparatively underexplored design choices for vision components in MLLMs. The authors argue that while stronger LLMs can boost multimodal capabilities, the visual understanding component often remains insufficiently grounded in real-world sensory input.
- **Evaluating Visual Representations through MLLMs:** A key objective is to establish and demonstrate MLLM instruction tuning as a robust and flexible evaluation protocol for different visual representations. This moves beyond traditional, often saturated, vision benchmarks (like ImageNet-1K) to provide insights into how various vision encoders (self-supervised, strongly supervised, etc.) contribute to complex multimodal understanding.
- **Addressing Benchmark Limitations:** The paper critically examines existing MLLM benchmarks, identifying their biases (e.g., heavy reliance on LLM capabilities rather than true visual grounding) and limitations (e.g., insufficient size of vision-centric benchmarks). This motivates the introduction of a new, more robust vision-centric benchmark, CV-Bench.
- **Optimizing MLLM Design Principles:** The project aims to provide comprehensive insights into five key pillars of MLLM design: visual representations, connector design, instruction tuning data, instruction tuning recipes, and benchmarking. The goal is to identify optimal strategies and design choices within each pillar.
- **Enhancing Visual Grounding:** A practical motivation is to improve the accuracy of sensory grounding in MLLMs, which is crucial for their application in challenging real-world scenarios where current MLLMs may struggle despite general capability improvements.
- **Promoting Open Science:** A fundamental objective is to provide a fully open and comprehensive "cookbook" for instruction-tuned MLLMs. By releasing model weights, code, supporting tools, curated datasets, and detailed training/evaluation recipes, the authors aim to inspire and accelerate advancements in the broader multimodal systems and visual representation learning research communities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Cambrian-1’s methodology is structured around five interconnected "pillars" to systematically explore the MLLM design space:

- **Evaluating Visual Representations through MLLMs:**
 - **Comprehensive Encoder Study:** The study evaluates over 20 diverse vision encoders, categorizing them by supervision type (language-supervised like CLIP and SigLIP, self-supervised like DINOv2 and MAE, and others like SAM for segmentation or MiDaS for depth).
 - **MLLM as Evaluator:** MLLM instruction tuning (specifically, a 2-stage process of connector pre-training followed by joint LLM and connector fine-tuning) is used as the evaluation protocol for these encoders, assessing their performance across various multimodal benchmarks.
 - **Tuning Recipe Analysis:** Experiments explore the impact of training recipes, including the amount of adapter data (0M, 0.5M, 1.2M) and the decision to freeze or unfreeze the vision encoder during fine-tuning.
 - **Multi-Encoder Combination:** The approach investigates combining multiple vision encoders (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2, ConvNeXt) by concatenating their features to leverage their complementary strengths.

- **Benchmarking and CV-Bench Introduction:**
 - **Benchmark Analysis:** Existing MLLM benchmarks are critically analyzed by comparing MLLM performance with and without visual input to identify language biases. Benchmarks are clustered into "General," "Knowledge," "Chart & OCR," and "Vision-Centric" categories.
 - **CV-Bench Development:** To address the scarcity and limited size of existing vision-centric benchmarks, a new benchmark, CV-Bench, is introduced. It repurposes standard 2D (ADE20K, COCO) and 3D (Omni3D) vision benchmarks into VQA format to explicitly evaluate spatial relationships, object counting, depth order, and relative distance.

- **Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA): A New Connector Design:**
 - **Addressing Token Limitations:** To overcome the quadratic scaling of visual tokens with image resolution in traditional MLP projectors, SVA is proposed.
 - **Spatial Inductive Bias:** SVA introduces a set of learnable latent queries that interact with multiple vision features via cross-attention. A key innovation is aligning each query token with a specific sub-region of the feature maps, thus preserving spatial structure and reducing information loss during aggregation.
 - **Multi-Layer Aggregation:** SVA allows cross-attention to occur multiple times by inserting aggregation layers throughout the LLM layers, enabling repeated access and integration of uncompressed visual information.
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning:** The design incorporates hyperparameters (D for number of cross-attention layers, G for number of query groups) to modulate capacity.

- **Instruction Tuning Data Curation:**
 - **Data Collection:** A large pool of instruction-tuning data, Cambrian-10M (~9.8M data points), is amassed from various public sources, including existing VQA benchmarks, visual interaction data, and a small volume of language-only data.
 - **Targeted Data Engine:** To address scarcity in specific domains (e.g., science), a novel internet data collection engine is developed. This engine uses LLMs (like GPT-4 and GPT-3.5) to identify topics, fetch relevant web pages (e.g., Wikipedia), extract image-caption-text tuples, and generate high-quality, knowledge-based Q&A pairs.
 - **Data Curation and Balancing:** The collected data undergoes curation, including filtering by thresholding data points from single sources to mitigate unbalanced distributions and conducting experiments to determine optimal categorical data ratios (e.g., for General, OCR, Language, Science data). This process yields the higher-quality Cambrian-7M dataset.
 - **System Prompts:** To mitigate the "answer machine phenomenon" (models giving curt, benchmark-optimized responses), specific system prompts are incorporated into the training data to encourage more detailed and conversational outputs.

- **Instruction Tuning Recipes:** The paper uses pre-trained LLMs (Vicuna-1.5, LLaMA-3, Hermes-2-Yi) and visual encoders, fine-tuning the LLM and the proposed SVA connector. Experiments revisit debated aspects such as the necessity of a two-stage training process (connector pre-training vs. direct joint fine-tuning) and the impact of freezing vs. unfreezing the vision encoder during fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Cambrian-1 yields several significant findings across its design space exploration:

- **Benchmark Analysis and CV-Bench Impact:**
 - Many existing MLLM benchmarks (e.g., SQA-I, MMMU) show small performance gaps between vision-enabled and vision-disabled models, indicating a heavy reliance on LLM capabilities rather than true visual input.
 - Existing vision-centric benchmarks are often too small, leading to high variance in evaluation.
 - CV-Bench successfully repurposes classic 2D and 3D vision tasks into VQA format, creating a larger and more robust benchmark (2638 manually-inspected examples, 3.5x more than RealWorldQA).

- **Instruction Tuning Recipes:**
 - **Two-stage training is beneficial:** Pre-training the connector on adapter data before joint fine-tuning consistently improves performance across all domains, and more adapter data (e.g., 1.2M) further enhances results.
 - **Unfreezing the vision encoder is widely beneficial:** While computationally more intensive, allowing the vision encoder to be fine-tuned improves performance across most benchmarks, especially for self-supervised models on vision-centric tasks. Language-supervised models also generally benefit.

- **Evaluating Visual Representations:**
 - Language-supervised models (CLIP, SigLIP) generally outperform self-supervised and other vision models across all categories, with a significant lead in OCR and chart-related tasks, likely due to their training data containing more text-heavy content.
 - Higher-resolution models and ConvNet-based architectures (e.g., OpenCLIP ConvNeXt-XXL@1024) particularly enhance performance on OCR & chart and vision-centric benchmarks, demonstrating their suitability for fine-grained visual understanding.
 - **Bridging the SSL-Language Gap:** While language supervision offers strong advantages, the performance gap between self-supervised (e.g., DINOv2) and language-supervised models can be significantly narrowed, and even bridged in some vision-centric tasks, by increasing the amount of instruction tuning data and unfreezing the vision backbone during fine-tuning.
 - **Ensembling Vision Encoders:** Combining multiple vision encoders (including non-language-supervised models like DINOv2) consistently improves MLLM performance across various benchmarks, especially in vision-centric tasks, by leveraging their distinct representations.

- **Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA) Effectiveness:**
 - The SVA module, with its spatial inductive bias and multi-layer aggregation, consistently outperforms other aggregation approaches (e.g., simple concatenation, Resampler) across all benchmark categories.
 - It shows significant improvements in OCR & chart tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in aggregating and condensing high-resolution vision information while maintaining a low visual token count (576 tokens, ~1/5th of competitors).
 - Ablation studies confirm that increasing capacity (number of cross-attention layers D or groups G) and enabling multi-layer aggregation within the LLM further enhance performance.

- **Instruction Tuning Data Curation:**
 - **Data Engine Success:** The targeted internet data collection engine successfully generates large volumes of high-quality, reliable scientific data (161k samples), significantly increasing data diversity in underrepresented categories.
 - **Data Balancing and Ratio Importance:** Applying thresholds to data sources (e.g., 250k-350k points per source) and carefully balancing the ratios of different data types (General, OCR, Language, Science) are crucial for improving overall model performance. The curated Cambrian-7M dataset, despite being smaller than Cambrian-10M, outperforms it due to better balancing.
 - **System Prompts for Conversationality:** Incorporating explicit system prompts during instruction tuning successfully alleviates the "answer machine phenomenon," enabling models to provide more detailed and conversational responses without sacrificing benchmark performance.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - The final Cambrian-1 family of MLLMs (8B, 13B, 34B parameters), incorporating insights from all studies and utilizing the SVA with a four-encoder combination, achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source models.
 - Cambrian-1 demonstrates competitive performance compared to leading proprietary models (GPT-4V, Gemini-Pro, Grok-1.5) on numerous benchmarks, particularly excelling in OCR & chart and vision-centric tasks despite using significantly fewer visual tokens (576 vs. 2880).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Cambrian-1 project holds significant implications for the future of multimodal AI research and development:

- **Re-focusing on Vision-Centric MLLM Design:** By systematically demonstrating the importance of visual representation learning and proper integration within MLLMs, Cambrian-1 challenges the prevailing LLM-centric approach. It provides a compelling argument and empirical evidence for the necessity of robust sensory grounding, echoing the philosophical concept of "Nihil est in intellectu quod non sit prius in sensu." This will likely spur more research into novel vision architectures and pre-training objectives specifically tailored for multimodal integration.
- **A New Paradigm for Visual Representation Evaluation:** The paper's advocacy for using MLLMs as a flexible evaluation interface for vision models marks a shift from traditional, often saturated, benchmarks. CV-Bench, as a concrete example, demonstrates how classic computer vision tasks can be effectively repurposed to assess MLLMs' fundamental visual understanding, guiding the development of more effective visual representations for real-world applications.
- **Advancements in Connector Design:** The Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA) is a notable technical contribution that addresses a critical bottleneck in MLLMs: efficiently integrating high-resolution and multi-source visual information while managing token count. SVA's superior performance in tasks requiring fine-grained visual understanding (like OCR) will likely inspire new connector designs that prioritize spatial awareness and deep interaction between vision and language.
- **Pioneering Data Curation Strategies:** The comprehensive data collection and meticulous curation process for Cambrian-10M and Cambrian-7M, coupled with the innovative targeted internet data engine, offers a valuable blueprint for building high-quality, balanced multimodal instruction tuning datasets. This work underscores that quantity alone is insufficient; thoughtful data balancing and specialized data generation methods are paramount for robust MLLM training. The approach to alleviating the "answer machine phenomenon" via system prompts is also a crucial step towards more human-aligned conversational MLLMs.
- **Acceleration of Open Research:** The most impactful aspect of Cambrian-1 is its commitment to open science. By releasing model weights, comprehensive codebases, curated datasets, and detailed training/evaluation recipes, the project dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for researchers. This "open cookbook" approach will undoubtedly accelerate advancements in multimodal systems, foster reproducibility, and enable a broader community to build upon these foundations, fostering innovation that might otherwise be confined to proprietary settings.
- **Practical Applications:** The state-of-the-art performance of Cambrian-1, especially its proficiency in vision-centric tasks with efficient token usage, makes it a highly practical foundation for real-world applications requiring nuanced visual understanding, such as scientific document analysis, complex chart interpretation, and visually-grounded reasoning in diverse environments.

In conclusion, Cambrian-1 is a seminal contribution that not only pushes the boundaries of MLLM performance but also provides a comprehensive, open-source framework and empirical insights that will shape the future trajectory of multimodal AI research, emphasizing robust visual grounding and transparent development.
