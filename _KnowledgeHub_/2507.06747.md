# LOVON: Legged Open-Vocabulary Object Navigator

> [!summary] Summary
> LOVON is a framework enabling legged robots to perform long-horizon, open-vocabulary object navigation in complex environments by integrating LLM-based planning, open-vocabulary visual perception with motion blur filtering, and a Language-to-Motion Model. The system achieves high success rates in simulations and real-world scenarios while being adaptable across various robot platforms and significantly reducing training time.

## Problem

- Integrating high-level LLM planning with low-level perception and motion control remains challenging for legged robots performing complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Real-world robotic navigation faces issues such as visual instability (jittering, blur) caused by robot motion, temporary target occlusions, and difficulty grounding language instructions to robust robot actions in unstructured environments.
- Existing research often focuses on single robotic tasks or isolated components, lacking a unified system for robust, open-world, open-vocabulary object navigation by agile legged platforms.

## Method

- **LOVON** unifies an LLM-based ==Task Planner== for hierarchical decomposition of high-level instructions, an open-vocabulary visual detection model (YOLO-11) for object identification, and a Language-to-Motion Model (L2MM) for precise motion prediction and control.
- It incorporates a Laplacian Variance-Based Motion Blur Filtering technique to preprocess camera frames, improving detection reliability during robot locomotion by replacing blurred images with the last clear frame.
- A transformer-based L2MM predicts robot motion vectors and adaptive task states (mission and search states) based on multimodal inputs, guiding the robot through functional logic for task execution, object searching, and mission accomplishment.

## Results

- The Laplacian Variance-Based Motion Blur Filtering method, combined with a moving average filter, increased the qualified frame rate by **25%**, ensuring more stable visual input for object detection.
- LOVON achieved an average **1.00** Success Rate in Gym-Unreal simulations across diverse scenes (e.g., ParkingLot, UrbanCity, SnowVillage), matching state-of-the-art performance with significantly reduced training time (**1.5** hours vs. 360 hours for TrackVLA).
- Real-world experiments on Unitree Go2, B2, and H1-2 robots demonstrated successful open-world adaptation, multi-goal tracking, dynamic object following, and robustness to disturbances like object displacement or physical robot interference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mitigating motion blur during legged robot locomotion is crucial for robust real-time open-vocabulary object detection, with Laplacian Variance Filtering improving the qualified frame rate significantly.
> - A specialized Language-to-Motion Model (L2MM) can effectively translate multimodal inputs (language instructions, visual detection, internal states) into precise robot control and adaptive state transitions for complex tasks.
> - Integrating hierarchical LLM planning with robust perception and a dedicated motion model enables legged robots to perform complex, long-horizon, multi-goal tasks in unstructured environments with enhanced autonomy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper addresses the growing intersection of large language models (LLMs), open-vocabulary visual perception, and legged robotics for complex, long-horizon tasks in unstructured environments.

Recent advancements in LLMs have provided capabilities in understanding, reasoning, and planning for complex tasks, allowing for the decomposition of high-level instructions into executable strategies. Concurrently, open-vocabulary visual detection models have expanded the scope of object recognition beyond predefined categories, enhancing adaptability to diverse environments. Legged robots have also demonstrated increasing mobility and adaptability in complex terrains, surpassing the capabilities of traditional wheeled robots in rugged environments.

However, a notable gap exists in effectively integrating these distinct areas. Prior research often focuses on optimizing single robotic tasks (e.g., walking, short-range navigation) or on LLM-based planning and vision-based detection in isolation. Challenges persist in grounding language instructions to real-world robot actions, handling dynamic and unstructured environments, and maintaining stable perception during robot motion. Specifically, integrating open-vocabulary perception with legged robot mobility for sequential, long-horizon tasks remains largely unexplored. Existing visual tracking methods often struggle with real-time performance and robustness to dynamic camera motions (e.g., jittering from robot locomotion) or temporary occlusions.

LOVON aims to bridge this gap by proposing a unified framework that combines LLM-based hierarchical task planning with open-vocabulary visual detection and a language-to-motion model for precise motion prediction. It specifically addresses real-world issues like visual instability caused by robot movement and temporary target loss, which are critical for robust long-horizon navigation. By integrating these components, LOVON seeks to enable legged robots to perform complex object search and navigation missions in open-world settings, an area where comprehensive operational systems have been limited.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptable operating system, named LOVON, that enables legged robots to perform long-horizon, open-vocabulary object navigation tasks in dynamic, unstructured, and open-world environments.

The key motivations for pursuing this objective are:
- **Addressing Limitations of Traditional Methods:** Traditional robotic systems often struggle to effectively integrate high-level task planning with low-level perception and motion control, particularly for complex, long-range missions involving varied targets. This limits their applicability in real-world scenarios that demand adaptability.
- **Leveraging LLM Capabilities:** LLMs have shown significant progress in understanding complex instructions and generating task plans. The motivation is to harness these capabilities to allow robots to comprehend and execute multi-step, natural language commands for navigation tasks.
- **Enhancing Open-Vocabulary Perception:** Advanced open-vocabulary vision models can recognize a wide range of objects beyond predefined categories. Integrating this capability is motivated by the need for robots to identify and interact with diverse, previously unseen objects in dynamic environments without requiring extensive retraining.
- **Exploiting Legged Robot Mobility:** Legged robots possess superior mobility in complex terrains compared to wheeled robots. The motivation is to unlock the full potential of these platforms by enabling them to execute complex, long-horizon tasks in real-world, rugged environments, moving beyond single-task optimizations.
- **Overcoming Real-World Challenges:** Robotic navigation in real-world settings is fraught with challenges such as visual jittering due to robot motion, blind zones, and temporary target loss. The research is motivated by the need to develop specific solutions, like robust visual stabilization techniques and adaptive task execution logic, to ensure reliable task completion despite these disturbances.
- **Enabling Autonomous and Adaptive Behavior:** The goal is to create a system that allows robots to autonomously search for and navigate towards dynamically changing subgoals based on flexible mission objectives, adapting their behavior in response to environmental feedback.
- **Developing a Generalizable and Compatible System:** A practical motivation is to develop a system that is adaptable across various legged robot platforms, allowing for "plug-and-play" deployment and thus increasing its utility and applicability in diverse robotic research and industrial contexts.

In summary, the research aims to create a unified framework that integrates advanced cognitive (LLM planning) and perceptual (open-vocabulary vision) abilities with the agile mobility of legged robots, specifically addressing the technical challenges of robust execution in dynamic, unstructured environments for long-horizon object navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LOVON is designed as an innovative operating system that integrates three main components: large language models (LLMs) for hierarchical task planning, open-vocabulary visual detection models for perception, and a Language-to-Motion Model (L2MM) for precise motion prediction and control.

The system's pipeline begins with a human's long-horizon task instruction. This instruction is processed by an LLM-based Task Planner, which reconfigures it into basic mission instructions. These instructions are then passed to an Instruction Object Extractor (IOE) that identifies the target object. Concurrently, a video stream from the robot's camera is captured, and each image frame is preprocessed using a Laplacian Variance-Based Motion Blur Filtering technique to mitigate visual instability caused by robot movement. The filtered image and the extracted target object name are then fed into an object detection model (YOLO-11). The mission instruction, target object, its bounding box information (center position, width, height, confidence score), and the current mission and search states are all combined as inputs to the proposed L2MM. The L2MM then generates the robot’s control vector (linear and angular velocities) and updated feedback states (mission state, search state) for both the LLM and its own subsequent iterations, progressively completing the tasks.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Multimodal Input Processing:**
- **LLM Task Planner:** Takes system description, user's long-sequence task, and feedback from L2MM to generate specific mission instructions. This enables the decomposition and execution of multi-step tasks.
- **Instruction Object Extractor (IOE):** A two-layer transformer with a perception layer that maps the mission instruction to the specific object class recognized by the detection model.
- **Object Detection Model:** Utilizes a lightweight and efficient model (YOLO-11) to process RGB images and the target object class, outputting detected object information including predicted object name, confidence score, bounding box center coordinates, and bounding box width and height. A moving average filter is applied to smooth bounding box outputs for improved stability.

**B. Laplacian Variance-Based Motion Blur Filtering:**
- This preprocessing step addresses motion blur in captured frames, particularly at the start of robot locomotion.
- The RGB frame is converted to grayscale, and a Laplacian operator is applied to enhance high-frequency components.
- The variance of the Laplacian response is computed. If this variance falls below an empirically calibrated threshold ($T_{blur}=150$), the frame is classified as blurred and replaced by the last clear frame. This aims to ensure consistent and clear visual input for the object detection pipeline.

**C. Language-to-Motion Model (L2MM):**
- L2MM is a transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture.
- **Encoder Input:** Consists of concatenated sequences including previous and current mission instructions, predicted object, confidence, bounding box parameters ($O_{xy}, O_{wh}$), and current mission and search states, separated by special tokens [SEP].
- **Decoder Output:** The decoder has three distinct heads:
 - **Motion Vector Head:** Predicts the robot’s 3D motion vector ($V_m = [v_x, v_y, \theta]$), representing linear velocities along x and y axes and angular velocity.
 - **Mission State Head:** Predicts the mission state ($S_m$), indicating task completion (success) or active movement (running towards object).
 - **Search State Head:** Predicts the search state ($S_s$), indicating robot behavior when a target is lost (rotating left or right to search).
- **Loss Functions:** The model is trained using Mean Squared Error loss for the motion vector head and Cross-Entropy loss for the mission and search state heads.

**D. Robot Functional Logic for Task Execution:**
A set of functional rules guides the robot’s behavior based on the L2MM's outputs:
- **Execute New Mission:** Initiates a new task if the current instruction differs from the previous one.
- **Run to the Object:** Directs the robot towards a detected object using the predicted motion vector.
- **Search for Lost Object:** Automatically triggers a searching state (rotating left or right) if the target object is lost, using the search state feedback from L2MM.
- **Maintain the State:** Keeps the robot in its current state based on visual inputs until a transition is required.
- **Accomplish the Mission:** Transitions to a success state when the target object's bounding box size (Owh) meets a predefined success threshold, indicating the robot has approached it sufficiently.

**E. Dataset Preparation:**
A synthetic dataset of 1 million samples is generated using a pipeline involving a DeepSeek R1 LLM. This pipeline includes:
- **Detection Class Synonym Expansion:** Generating synonyms for predefined object classes to enhance generalization.
- **Instruction Variation:** Generating paraphrases of mission instructions to improve adaptability to diverse language.
- **Threshold Generation for Object Categories:** Adapting success thresholds for object detection to various object sizes.
The generated data is iteratively fed back to the LLM for refinement, ensuring diversity and reducing redundancy. This dataset generation process is rapid, taking less than 15 minutes to generate 1 million samples using a CPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations through simulations and real-world experiments on various legged robot platforms, demonstrating LOVON's capabilities.

**A. Performance of Motion-Blurred Frame Filtering:**
Experiments demonstrated that motion blur significantly impacts object detection confidence. The proposed Laplacian Variance-Based Motion Blur Filtering method was found to be effective. By setting a blur threshold of $T_{blur}=150$, the method improved the qualified frame ratio by approximately 15% across different object types and speeds (0.3, 0.5, 0.7 m/s). When integrated with a moving average filter (MAF) for smoothing detection confidence and replacing blurred frames with the last clear frame, the system achieved an overall 25% increase in the qualified frame rate. This indicates improved visual stability and reliability of the input to the object detection pipeline.

**B. Evaluation on Simulation Environments:**
LOVON was evaluated on the Gym-Unreal benchmark using metrics of Episode Length (EL) and Success Rate (SR) over 100 trials across four scenes: ParkingLot, UrbanCity, UrbanRoad, and SnowVillage.
- LOVON achieved a perfect Success Rate (SR) of 1.00 in ParkingLot, UrbanCity, and SnowVillage, and 0.99 in UrbanRoad. The average SR across all environments was 1.00.
- The average Episode Length (EL) was 500 in ParkingLot, UrbanCity, and SnowVillage, and 499 in UrbanRoad, resulting in an average EL of 499.75.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** LOVON demonstrated superior or comparable performance to several baselines. For instance, against EVT, LOVON achieved 500/1.00 (EL/SR) compared to EVT's 484/0.92 in ParkingLot. While the state-of-the-art TrackVLA also achieved 500/1.00 across all environments, LOVON accomplished this with a significantly shorter training time of 1.5 hours compared to TrackVLA's 360 hours, indicating substantial efficiency.

**C. Evaluation on Real-World Experiments:**
Real-world experiments were conducted on Unitree Go2, B2, and H1-2 legged robots, showcasing performance in four key areas:
- **Open-World Adaptation:** LOVON enabled robots to effectively navigate and interact with a wide range of objects of varying sizes (cars, people, bags) in unfamiliar environments.
- **Multi-Goal Tracking:** The LLM planner facilitated long-horizon object navigation by coordinating multiple subgoals, allowing the robot to track multiple objects sequentially over extended periods, even in complex settings.
- **Dynamic Tracking:** The system successfully followed moving objects (e.g., a person, akin to walking a dog) across diverse terrains including flat roads, spiral stairs, and wild grass, reliably completing tasks under dynamic conditions.
- **Robustness to Disturbances:** LOVON demonstrated robustness when faced with external disturbances. If a targeted object was displaced or the robot itself was physically disturbed (e.g., kicked), it quickly re-localized the object and continued the task. Examples included approaching a displaced chair after being kicked and tracking a sports ball on sandy terrain after it was kicked away.

**D. Ablation Study:**
- **Model Parameters:** The ablation study on L2MM model parameters indicated that base-sized models provided a good balance of performance. Smaller models exhibited higher standard deviation ($\sigma_v$) and average speed bias ($\epsilon_v$), while larger models showed marginal improvements at increased computational cost. Dataset size affected stability, with larger datasets improving consistency. The motion loss weight ($\beta=10$) was found to be critical; both too low and too high values degraded performance. The inclusion of the special token [SEP] was deemed essential for differentiating input components and correctly following speed instructions.
- **Filter Method and Number of States:** Experiments compared configurations with three states (running, searching-left, searching-right) versus four states (running, searching-left, searching-right, success) and the impact of frame filtering.
 - Case 1 (3 states, no filter): Inefficient, with frequent object loss and long search times (e.g., 100s for backpack at 4m).
 - Case 2 (4 states, no filter): Improved efficiency compared to Case 1 by allowing the robot to explicitly transition to a success state. However, object loss and shaking still occurred, leading to multiple search circles.
 - Case 3 (4 states, with frame filtering): Achieved optimal efficiency. The number of searching circles ($N_s$) reduced to 1 for difficult-to-track objects like backpacks and chairs. Search time ($T_s$) for the backpack at 4m was reduced from 100s (Case 1) to 22.25s, and for the chair from 43.50s to 21.73s, closely matching the performance for easily detectable objects like persons. This confirmed the effectiveness of both the additional mission state and the Laplacian variance filtering for robust object navigation, especially when targets are temporarily lost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of LOVON represents a significant advancement in robotic autonomy, particularly for legged robots operating in complex, unstructured environments. Its primary significance lies in the successful integration of advanced artificial intelligence components—large language models, open-vocabulary visual perception, and a specialized motion control model—into a cohesive, operational framework.

The potential impacts include:

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy and Versatility:** LOVON enables legged robots to move beyond pre-programmed tasks or limited object categories. By allowing robots to understand and execute long-horizon, open-vocabulary instructions, it opens up possibilities for autonomous missions in diverse real-world settings such as search and rescue, logistics, environmental monitoring, or assisting in domestic environments.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** The integration of the Laplacian Variance Filtering technique directly addresses a critical challenge of visual instability caused by robot motion. This enhancement significantly improves the reliability of object detection, making the system more robust to the inherent dynamics of legged locomotion and environmental changes. The adaptive functional logic for searching lost objects further ensures task completion under challenging conditions.
- **Efficient and Adaptable Task Execution:** The LLM-based task planner allows for dynamic decomposition of complex instructions and adaptation to varying mission objectives. This flexibility, combined with the L2MM's ability to generate precise motion vectors and state feedback, facilitates efficient task completion and self-correction during execution.
- **Generalizability and Plug-and-Play Capability:** The demonstrated compatibility across different legged robot platforms (Unitree Go2, B2, H1-2) highlights LOVON's "plug-and-play" feature. This generalizability reduces the engineering effort required for deploying advanced navigation capabilities on new robotic hardware, accelerating research and development in the field.
- **Reduced Training Overhead:** Achieving high performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods in simulation (e.g., TrackVLA) with significantly less training time (1.5 hours vs. 360 hours) suggests a more efficient learning paradigm. This could democratize access to advanced robotic capabilities by lowering the computational and data requirements for deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** LOVON contributes to the ongoing effort to create more intelligent and capable embodied AI systems. By effectively grounding high-level language commands into robust low-level robot actions and perceptions, it lays a foundation for future research in areas such as human-robot interaction, complex manipulation, and sophisticated multi-robot coordination. The iterative dataset generation process also points towards scalable methods for creating training data for complex robotic behaviors.

In conclusion, LOVON presents a practical and effective solution for enabling legged robots to navigate and interact with the world in a more intelligent, adaptive, and robust manner, bridging the gap between advanced AI models and real-world robotic deployment.
