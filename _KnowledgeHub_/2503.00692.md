# Learning Perceptive Humanoid Locomotion over Challenging Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology developed the Humanoid Perception Controller (HPC), an architecture integrating terrain perception with sensor denoising through a learned world model. This framework enabled a humanoid robot to autonomously traverse 2 kilometers of diverse outdoor terrains, including stairs and rough surfaces, while demonstrating superior velocity tracking and maintaining stability even under extreme sensor noise conditions.

## Problem

- Current humanoid locomotion systems lack robust external terrain perception and struggle with unstructured, rapidly changing real-world environments.
- Existing controllers cannot effectively account for erroneous or noisy exteroceptive sensor data, leading to instability on deformable surfaces and in complex scenarios.
- Bridging the 'sim-to-real gap' remains challenging, as simulating the full spectrum of real-world perception failures is difficult, limiting deployment into unpredictable environments.

## Method

- A Humanoid Perception Controller (HPC) is introduced, employing a two-stage teacher-student distillation framework to learn robust locomotion.
- A learned world model with a Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) is integrated to denoise noisy observations and infer privileged states, enhancing robust state estimation.
- Extensive domain randomization, including a sophisticated terrain perception noise model, is applied during simulation training to improve sim-to-real transfer and noise resilience.

## Results

- The HPC achieved superior simulated performance, exhibiting the lowest linear velocity tracking error (0.182) and angular velocity tracking error (0.237), and successfully navigating the highest mean terrain difficulty level (8.292) compared to baselines.
- The system demonstrated exceptional robustness against sensor noise, retaining 74.3% of its baseline terrain performance even under 200% noise intensity, significantly outperforming other architectures.
- The trained controller was successfully deployed on a physical humanoid robot, autonomously traversing approximately 2 kilometers of varied outdoor terrains, including stairs, cobblestone, and snow, without requiring external intervention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned world model with a variational information bottleneck effectively filters noise from exteroceptive perception and robustly estimates the robot's state, crucial for adapting to terrain variability.
> - Teacher-student distillation provides an efficient paradigm for transferring optimal behaviors from an oracle with privileged information to a student policy that operates on noisy, real-world-like observations.
> - Realistic modeling of diverse sensor noise and comprehensive domain randomization are essential for training humanoid policies that generalize effectively from simulation to real-world deployment without fine-tuning.
