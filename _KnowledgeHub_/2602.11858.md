# Zooming without Zooming: Region-to-Image Distillation for Fine-Grained Multimodal Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Ant Group developed "Zooming without Zooming" (ZwZ), a paradigm using Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I) to train multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for fine-grained perception. This approach internalizes "zooming" capabilities during training, enabling a single-pass inference that achieves competitive accuracy while being approximately 10 times faster than agentic "Thinking-with-Images" models.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often lack fine-grained perception, struggling to discern subtle details like tiny text or specific attributes embedded within complex visual contexts.
- Current solutions using iterative "zooming" or agentic tool-use ("Thinking-with-Images") suffer from high inference latency, rendering them impractical for real-time applications.
- Existing benchmarks for fine-grained perception are often limited in scope, task coverage, or difficulty, hindering robust evaluation of MLLM capabilities.

## Method

- Developed ==Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I)==, an automated pipeline that synthesizes high-veracity, fine-grained VQA data by generating QA pairs from micro-crops using powerful teacher models, then distilling this supervision to full images with visual (bounding box overlay) and textual grounding.
- Trained student MLLMs using reinforcement learning (==DAPO==) on these augmented full-image QA pairs, teaching them to intrinsically "gaze" at relevant micro-evidence from the global context in a single forward pass.
- Introduced ==ZoomBench==, a hybrid-annotated benchmark featuring diverse perceptual dimensions and a dual-view evaluation protocol to rigorously assess fine-grained acuity and quantify the "global-regional zooming gap."

## Results

- ZwZ models, trained with **74K** R2I samples, demonstrated leading performance on various fine-grained benchmarks, surpassing larger open-source MLLMs and achieving competitive accuracy with state-of-the-art closed-source models, while performing approximately **10 times** faster than agentic baselines.
- Region-to-Image Distillation yielded higher-quality, more efficient training data, with models trained on **74K** R2I samples outperforming those trained on significantly larger public datasets.
- On the novel ZoomBench, ZwZ-8B reduced the "zooming gap" to **15.26%**, the smallest among tested models, and exhibited increased relative attention coverage within annotated key regions, confirming improved fine-grained attention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The benefits of inference-time agentic "zooming" for fine-grained perception can be effectively internalized into an MLLM during training, enabling a single-pass inference that maintains accuracy without high latency.
> - Synthesizing fine-grained VQA data from micro-crops using powerful teacher models significantly improves data quality and reduces hallucination compared to direct global-to-global synthesis.
> - Visually augmenting training images with bounding box overlays for specific regions serves as crucial "privileged information," guiding the model's attention to learn intrinsic fine-grained perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, demonstrating impressive capabilities in broad visual understanding and reasoning. However, a significant challenge remains in **fine-grained perception**, where MLLMs often struggle to identify subtle details, tiny text, or specific attributes that are crucial for accurate understanding, especially when these minute pieces of evidence are embedded within a vast and complex global visual context. This issue, often described as the "needle in a haystack" problem, limits their applicability in scenarios requiring high precision.

Existing approaches to address this fine-grained perception gap primarily fall under the "Thinking-with-Images" paradigm. These methods equip MLLMs with agentic capabilities to iteratively "zoom in" on regions of interest, re-encode them, and then process the more focused visual information. While effective in reducing interference and allowing models to concentrate on specific areas, this iterative process incurs substantial **inference latency** due to repeated tool calls and visual re-encoding. This makes "Thinking-with-Images" models less suitable for real-time applications. Examples include DeepEyes, Thyme, and PixelReasoner.

This paper positions itself directly against this trade-off between accuracy and latency. It proposes a novel paradigm, "Zooming without Zooming," that seeks to **internalize the benefits of agentic zooming into a single forward pass** of an MLLM. Instead of performing zooming actions at inference time, the proposed Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I) method integrates this capability during the training phase.

The work also contributes to the landscape of **multimodal synthetic data generation**. Previous methods, like Genixer and Oasis, typically employ "global-to-global" synthesis, where teacher models generate QA pairs from full images. However, these methods can suffer from hallucination and poor perceptual grounding for fine details. The "Zooming without Zooming" paper tackles this by adopting a **region-centric synthesis strategy**, generating high-veracity QA pairs from micro-crops first, and then distilling this supervision back to the full image. This approach aims to produce higher-quality, fine-grained training data.

Furthermore, the paper addresses limitations in **fine-grained perception benchmarks**. Existing benchmarks often suffer from narrow task coverage, templated question styles, low difficulty for advanced MLLMs, or expensive manual construction. The introduction of **ZoomBench**, a hybrid-annotated benchmark with a dual-view evaluation protocol, fills this gap by providing a more rigorous and comprehensive tool for evaluating fine-grained acuity, including the quantification of the "global–regional zooming gap."

In essence, this research attempts to bridge the gap between the expressive power of MLLMs and the demanding requirements of fine-grained perception, offering an efficient, training-time solution that enhances performance without the computational overhead of complex inference-time agentic workflows. It also critically re-evaluates the necessity of agentic tool-use, distinguishing between information-gain and information-neutral actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations driving this research are:

- **To overcome the fine-grained perception limitations of current MLLMs:** MLLMs excel at general visual understanding but often struggle with small, decisive visual evidence (e.g., tiny text, subtle color variations, intricate object structures) that is easily obscured by the broader global context of an image. This "needle-in-a-haystack" problem is a significant bottleneck for real-world applications requiring high visual precision.
- **To eliminate the high inference latency associated with "Thinking-with-Images" models:** While agentic methods that iteratively zoom in on regions of interest during inference are effective, they incur substantial computational overhead. The repeated visual re-encoding and tool calls make these models too slow for real-time or low-latency applications. The authors are motivated to achieve the accuracy benefits of zooming without this performance penalty.
- **To internalize "zooming" capabilities into a single MLLM forward pass:** The core objective is to shift the "zooming" process from an inference-time tool-use paradigm to a training-time primitive. This means teaching the MLLM to intrinsically "gaze" at relevant micro-evidence from the full image, effectively enabling "zooming without explicitly zooming."
- **To develop a scalable and high-quality method for synthesizing fine-grained VQA data:** Manual annotation of fine-grained data is labor-intensive and expensive. The authors aim to create an automated pipeline that can generate high-veracity, perception-centric question-answer pairs from unlabeled image corpora, specifically targeting micro-crops where details are unambiguous.
- **To create a rigorous benchmark for fine-grained multimodal perception:** The lack of comprehensive, challenging, and analytically insightful benchmarks for fine-grained perception hinders proper evaluation. A key objective is to introduce ZoomBench, which features diverse perceptual dimensions, human-AI hybrid construction, and novel evaluation protocols (like dual-view analysis) to quantify models' abilities and identify limitations.
- **To critically analyze the utility of agentic tool-use in MLLMs:** The paper also aims to provide a clearer understanding of when "Thinking-with-Images" paradigms are truly necessary (e.g., for unpredictable information gain) and when their benefits can be distilled into a more efficient, single-pass model.

In essence, the motivation is to advance the state-of-the-art in MLLM perception by enabling efficient, precise, and human-like visual acuity without compromising on inference speed, thereby making fine-grained multimodal understanding more accessible for a wider range of real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research revolves around **Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I)**, a novel method for synthesizing fine-grained multimodal training data, complemented by the introduction of **ZoomBench** for rigorous evaluation.

#### 3.1. Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I)

The R2I method transforms the concept of "zooming in" from an inference-time tool into a training-time primitive. It's an automated and scalable pipeline designed to distill fine-grained, region-grounded supervision into full-image VQA instances.

1. **Setup and Goal:**
 - **Problem:** Decisive evidence for fine-grained perception often lies in small micro-regions (R) within a larger image (I), easily overwhelmed by global context.
 - **Goal:** Optimize a student MLLM by distilling the regional-view expertise of strong teacher models (operating on R) into the student's global-view predictions (on I).

2. **Zoom-in Synthesis on the Cropped Region:**
 - **Image Pool:** A diverse pool of high-resolution images (e.g., from SA-1B, LAION, MetaCLIP).
 - **Bounding Box Proposal (P(I)):** For images lacking annotations, an object recognition and segmentation system (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B to identify objects, followed by SAM3 for precise bounding boxes) is used to generate candidate bounding boxes {B_i}.
 - **Micro-crop Filtering:** Only regions R_i where the area ratio `Area(B_i) / Area(I) < τ` (e.g., τ = 0.1) are retained. This ensures that the evidence is "micro" and challenging to discern from a global view, directly targeting fine-grained perception.
 - **Question Generation (Q_R):** For each selected micro-region R, a powerful teacher model (Qwen3-VL-235B) acts as a question generator, proposing perception-centric questions that are strictly answerable from R alone. These questions focus on fine-grained cues like tiny text, symbols, subtle attributes, or small instance counts.
 - **High-Veracity Answer Generation (A):** Multiple teacher models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B and GLM-4.5V) are used as answer generators. For each question, four responses per teacher are sampled. A QA pair is retained only if a strict majority consensus (e.g., > 6/8 responses matching) is reached, minimizing hallucinations and ensuring high-quality pseudo-labels.

3. **Zoom-out Distillation to the Full Image:**
 - **Addressing Referential Ambiguity:** When distilling region-level QA pairs back to the full image I, a question Q that was unambiguous in R might become ambiguous in I (e.g., multiple similar objects). To resolve this, a **grounding transformation G(I, Q, B)** is applied.
 - **Augmented Training Pair (I', Q'):**
 - **Visual Grounding (I'):** The original bounding box B is directly overlaid onto the full image I to form I'. This provides a strong visual hint.
 - **Textual Grounding (Q'):** A spatial constraint (e.g., "Only focus on the objects inside the bounding box in the image to answer this question.") is appended to the question Q to form Q'.
 - **Difficulty Filtering:** The synthetic data is further filtered by a smaller multimodal model (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B), removing overly easy samples that the student model can already answer reliably.
 - **Student Model Training:** The student model (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B/7B/8B) is then trained on these augmented triplets (I', Q', A) using reinforcement learning (specifically DAPO, Deep Averaging Policy Optimization). The objective `max_θ E_(I',Q',A)~D_syn, bA~π_θ(·|I',Q') [r(bA, A)]` encourages the student policy `π_θ` to generate answers `bA` that receive high rewards `r` based on the ground truth `A`.
 - **Intuition:** The bounding box, although absent during inference, acts as "privileged information" during training. It forces the model's internal attention mechanisms to align with the micro-region R, thereby teaching it to intrinsically "gaze" at relevant details from the full image.

4. **General View (Algorithm 1):** The method is generalized as "tool-action distillation." A raw image `I` undergoes a "tool-call action" `f(·)` (e.g., zoom-in) to create an altered observation `bI`. A teacher `T` synthesizes QA pairs `(Q, A)` on `bI`. An "inverse transformation" `f^-1` maps `(bI, Q)` back to `(I, bQ)`, where `bQ` is the original question augmented for the full image context. This creates the distilled dataset `D_syn = {(I, bQ, A)}`.

#### 3.2. ZoomBench

To rigorously evaluate fine-grained acuity, the authors introduce ZoomBench, a challenging hybrid-annotated benchmark.

1. **Construction:**
 - **Teacher Model Generation:** Gemini-2.5-Pro is used as both the question and answer generator on cropped micro-regions, following the R2I pipeline.
 - **Mapping to Full Images:** Questions are mapped to full images *without* spatial grounding (bounding boxes) to create challenging perception tasks.
 - **Human-in-the-Loop Verification:** Human annotators (PhD-level researchers) verify the validity, difficulty, and correctness of the model-generated QA pairs against both full images and cropped regions. This significantly reduces manual labeling costs while maintaining high quality.
 - **Data Split:** Images and QA pairs are carefully partitioned to ensure no overlap between training data and benchmark data.
 - **Statistics:** Comprises 845 high-quality QA pairs (224 open-ended, 621 multiple-choice) with high-resolution images, spanning six fine-grained perceptual dimensions: Counting, OCR, Color, Structural Attributes, Material Attributes, and Object Identification.

2. **Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **Standard Evaluation:** Accuracy is reported for both multiple-choice and open-ended questions.
 - **Dual-View Evaluation:** Each instance is evaluated in two conditions:
 - **Global-View:** Model answers from the full image.
 - **Regional-View:** Model answers from the corresponding micro-cropped region (empirical upper bound).
 - **"Zooming Gap":** The difference between Regional-View and Global-View accuracy, quantifying the model's ability to locate and utilize evidence from the full image.
 - **Attention Map-Based Analysis:** Relative attention maps are computed to interpretably demonstrate how much the model's question-relevant visual attention concentrates within the annotated key regions. This measures "Relative-Attention Coverage Ratio."

This comprehensive methodology allows for the efficient creation of high-quality training data and a robust benchmark, enabling the development and thorough evaluation of MLLMs with enhanced fine-grained perception in a single forward pass.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate the effectiveness of the Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I) method and the utility of ZoomBench.

#### 4.1. Leading Performance Across Various Benchmarks

- **Overall Improvement:** Training MLLMs (ZwZ models) using only 74K of the synthetic R2I data yielded consistent and substantial performance gains across all evaluated benchmarks (Table 2, Figure 1).
- **ZwZ-8B Performance:** ZwZ-8B (an 8B parameter model) significantly improved over its Qwen3-VL-8B baseline (from 62.86 to 68.12 average score), with a particularly large gain on the proposed ZoomBench (37.87% to 58.11%).
- **Competitiveness with Larger Models:** ZwZ-4B and ZwZ-8B surpassed all comparable open-source models, including much larger ones like GLM-4.5V (108B), Qwen3-VL-235B, and Kimi-K2.5 (1T) on the average of general perception benchmarks. ZwZ models even demonstrated competitiveness with state-of-the-art closed-source models like Gemini-3-Flash. This suggests that the R2I data effectively distills sophisticated perceptual expertise into smaller models.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** Beyond perception-centric tasks, ZwZ models showed strong OOD performance on MMStar (general multimodal understanding) and BabyVision (visual reasoning), indicating that the learned ability to retrieve micro-evidence from cluttered inputs benefits broader cognitive tasks.

#### 4.2. Superiority Over Existing Open Datasets

- **Data Efficiency:** Ablation studies (Table 3) showed that ZwZ models, trained on just 74K R2I samples, outperformed models trained on various public datasets, including human-curated non-synthetic data (DeepEyes, Thyme-RL, TreeVGR-RL) and significantly larger synthetic datasets (Oasis 500K, MM-Self-Instruct 65K). This highlights the high quality and fine granularity of the R2I-synthesized data, proving that quality trumps sheer volume.
- **Outperforming Proxy Task-Based Data:** ZwZ models also achieved stronger and more generalizable performance compared to methods utilizing synthetic data generated from proxy tasks (e.g., AGILE, DiG, Image-Jigsaw) (Table 5).

#### 4.3. Exceeding "Thinking-with-Images" Models and Lower Latency

- **Accuracy without Iteration:** ZwZ (single-pass models) were competitive with, and often surpassed, strong agentic "Thinking-with-Images" systems like Pixel-Reasoner, DeepEyes, Thyme, and Mini-o3 (Table 4). ZwZ-8B achieved the best overall average accuracy among these methods.
- **Internalized Tool-Use Benefits:** ZwZ also outperformed Qwen3-VL with its official tool-use pipeline, demonstrating that R2I effectively internalizes the gains of tool-based zooming directly into the model's weights.
- **Significant Speedup:** Crucially, ZwZ achieved these accuracy gains with substantially lower inference costs, being approximately **10 times faster** than agentic and tool-use baselines (Figure 5). This confirms the success of "Zooming without Zooming" in achieving efficiency.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Insights

- **Effectiveness of R2I vs. Direct Synthesis:** R2I significantly outperformed "Direct Synthesis" (traditional global-to-global synthesis), confirming that generating supervision on micro-crops reduces teacher hallucination and provides higher-quality training signals (Table 6).
- **Importance of Visual Grounding:** Overlying bounding boxes directly onto the image ("R2I + bbox-in-image") was the most effective grounding strategy. It outperformed providing box coordinates as text ("bbox-in-question") or no bounding box ("no-bbox"). This suggests that visual bounding box overlays act as strong "privileged information" during training, forcing the model to learn a direct mapping between global context and specific micro-regions.

#### 4.5. Real-World Task Generalization

- ZwZ-8B consistently outperformed Qwen3-VL-8B on real-world tasks requiring strong multimodal grounding, including AIGC detection (LOKI, FakeCLUE) and GUI agents (ScreenSpot Pro, OSWorldG) (Figure 6). This indicates that improved fine-grained perception enhances foundational multimodal capabilities across diverse applications.

#### 4.6. Deeper Analysis on ZoomBench

- **Dual-View Evaluation (Quantifying Zooming Gap):**
 - Vanilla MLLMs exhibited a consistent and substantial "zooming gap" (Global-View vs. Regional-View accuracy), indicating that many failures stem from perceptual oversight rather than recognition limitations (Figure 7, Table 7).
 - Even very large models (e.g., GLM-4.5V, Kimi-K2.5) showed notable gaps, implying that scaling parameters alone doesn't resolve the fine-grained retrieval bottleneck.
 - ZwZ-8B **significantly narrowed this gap to only 15.26%**, the smallest among tested models, and its Global-View accuracy nearly matched the Regional-View performance of the vanilla Qwen3-VL-8B baseline.
 - **Dimension-wise Insights:** Counting was the most challenging dimension (even regionally). Structural and Material attributes showed the largest zooming gaps, suggesting high sensitivity to attention dilution. OCR and Identification had smaller gaps due to more salient cues.
- **Attention Map Coverage Analysis:**
 - ZwZ variants consistently achieved higher "Relative-Attention Coverage Ratio" within annotated key regions compared to their Qwen-VL baselines (Table 8, Appendix 12). This provides interpretability, showing that ZwZ models indeed learn to concentrate their attention on task-relevant micro-level evidence, directly linking to the narrowed zooming gap.

In summary, the findings strongly support the efficacy of Region-to-Image Distillation in endowing MLLMs with superior fine-grained perception without the computational burden of iterative inference-time tools. The ZwZ models set new benchmarks for efficiency and accuracy in this critical domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Zooming without Zooming" offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact on the development and application of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):

1. **Breaking the Latency Barrier for Fine-Grained Perception:** The most direct and impactful contribution is demonstrating that high-accuracy fine-grained perception can be achieved in MLLMs *without* the high inference latency inherent in iterative "Thinking-with-Images" or agentic tool-use paradigms. By internalizing the benefits of zooming into a single forward pass, ZwZ models are significantly faster (e.g., 10x speedup), making them practical for real-time applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, medical image analysis, and interactive AI assistants where quick responses are critical.

2. **Efficient and Scalable Data Synthesis for Fine-Grained Tasks:** The Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I) method provides a novel, automated, and scalable pipeline for generating high-veracity, perception-centric VQA data. This addresses the bottleneck of expensive and labor-intensive manual annotation for fine-grained tasks. The method's ability to create high-quality training signals from micro-crops, coupled with efficient difficulty filtering, can accelerate research and development in areas requiring nuanced visual understanding. This could lead to a proliferation of more capable MLLMs trained on readily available, high-quality synthetic data.

3. **Rethinking Agentic Workflows and Tool-Use in MLLMs:** The paper provides a valuable framework for understanding when agentic tool-use is truly necessary versus when its benefits can be distilled. By distinguishing between "information-gain actions" (where tools introduce genuinely new, unpredictable information, e.g., web search) and "information-neutral actions" (where tools merely reformat or reveal existing, predictable evidence, e.g., zooming, rotating), the authors argue for distillation in the latter case. This re-evaluation can guide future MLLM design, fostering a more efficient blend of internalized capabilities and external tool invocation. It suggests that many perceived needs for complex inference-time reasoning can be optimized during training.

4. **A Rigorous Benchmark for Fine-Grained Perception:** ZoomBench is a significant contribution to the evaluation landscape. Its hybrid-annotated, diverse, and challenging nature, coupled with the innovative dual-view evaluation protocol (quantifying the "zooming gap") and attention map analysis, provides a more insightful and comprehensive tool for diagnosing MLLM capabilities and limitations in fine-grained perception. This benchmark can drive future research by allowing researchers to clearly identify and address specific weaknesses in models.

5. **Enhanced General Multimodal Cognition:** The observed improvements in out-of-distribution generalization tasks (like visual reasoning and GUI agents) suggest that improved fine-grained perception is not merely task-specific but enhances fundamental multimodal capabilities. This implies that teaching models to reliably focus on and extract micro-evidence from global contexts strengthens their overall understanding and reasoning abilities, leading to more robust and versatile MLLMs.

6. **Path Towards More "Human-like" Visual Acuity:** By teaching MLLMs to intrinsically "gaze" at relevant details, the work moves closer to a more human-like visual processing paradigm, where a single glance can integrate both global context and fine-grained details. This could pave the way for MLLMs that are more intuitive and efficient in interacting with the visual world.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The paper itself highlights a promising future direction: developing a **unified and dynamic agent policy**. Such an agent would intelligently switch between (i) efficient single-pass inference with enhanced perception (as achieved by ZwZ), (ii) invoking external tools when necessary, and (iii) prioritizing information-gain tools over information-neutral ones. This adaptive approach would combine the best of both worlds: the efficiency of internalized skills and the open-world adaptability of agentic systems. Furthermore, extending R2I to encompass other perception-related tasks like spatial reasoning and multi-object perception by synthesizing data through corresponding tools (e.g., 3D grounding tools) is a clear next step.

In conclusion, "Zooming without Zooming" makes a strong case for a paradigm shift in how MLLMs handle fine-grained visual information. By converting an expensive inference-time operation into an efficient training-time distillation process, the research significantly advances the capabilities and practicality of MLLMs for a wide array of demanding applications, while also providing valuable insights into the architecture and training of future multimodal AI systems.
