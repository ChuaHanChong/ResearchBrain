# Advancing Humanoid Locomotion: Mastering Challenging Terrains with Denoising World Model Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents the Denoising World Model Learning (DWL) framework, which enables XBot humanoid robots to achieve robust, zero-shot sim-to-real locomotion on challenging terrains using only proprioceptive sensors. The system integrates advanced active ankle control with a deep learning approach that filters sensor noise and estimates terrain properties, demonstrating successful navigation on snowy ground, stairs, and irregular surfaces.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust locomotion on challenging, uneven, and unpredictable real-world terrains due to their complex dynamics and high degrees of freedom.
- Bridging the 'sim-to-real gap' remains a significant challenge, often requiring extensive real-world fine-tuning of policies trained in simulation.
- Traditional humanoid control methods often rely on perfect state information or external sensors, making them vulnerable to noisy proprioceptive sensors and partial observability.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Denoising== ==World Model== Learning (**DWL**) framework, a deep ==reinforcement learning== approach that processes noisy historical observations to estimate true robot state and terrain properties.
- Utilizes a novel Closed Kinematic Chain Ankle Mechanism on XBot humanoid robots, providing active 2-DoF ankle control for enhanced stability, weight distribution, and shock absorption.
- Employs extensive domain randomization, observation masking, and noise injection during simulation training to build robust policies that generalize effectively to the real world.

## Results

- The XBot robots demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, successfully navigating diverse challenging terrains including snowy inclines, stairs, and irregular surfaces without fine-tuning.
- The DWL framework with active ankle control achieved higher success rates, reduced body oscillation, and lower energy consumption compared to baseline methods.
- The system showed high robustness to disturbances, maintaining stable locomotion despite mass displacements, external pushes, and even partial motor failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Denoising World Model Learning (DWL) effectively compensates for noisy sensor data and IMU drift, allowing robust state and terrain estimation from proprioceptive sensors alone.
> - Combining an active 2-DoF Closed Kinematic Chain Ankle Mechanism with a learning-based controller significantly enhances stability and adaptability on irregular surfaces and during disturbances.
> - Extensive domain randomization and noise injection during simulation training enable zero-shot transfer of complex locomotion policies to physical humanoid robots without fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Humanoid robots represent one of the most challenging frontiers in robotics. With their human-like form, they hold immense potential for operating in environments designed for humans - from navigating stairs in office buildings to traversing rough outdoor terrain. However, their complex dynamics, high center of gravity, and multiple degrees of freedom make controlling them exceptionally difficult, particularly when faced with uneven or unpredictable surfaces.

Recent advances in reinforcement learning (RL) have shown promise for robot locomotion, but a significant challenge remains: bridging the "sim-to-real gap" - the difference between simulated environments used for training and the complex, noisy real world where robots must operate. Traditional approaches often require extensive fine-tuning when transferred from simulation to reality, limiting practical application.

This paper introduces a breakthrough approach - Denoising World Model Learning (DWL) - that enables humanoid robots to achieve robust locomotion across various challenging terrains. By combining innovative ankle control mechanisms with a deep learning framework that can filter out sensor noise and estimate terrain properties, the system achieves zero-shot transfer from simulation to reality, allowing robots to immediately function in the real world without additional training.

### 2. Humanoid Robot Design

The research utilizes two sizes of humanoid robots - XBot-S (1.2m tall, 38kg) and XBot-L (1.65m tall, 57kg) - designed with specific features to enhance locomotion capabilities:

A key innovation in the robots' design is the Closed Kinematic Chain Ankle Mechanism. Unlike traditional humanoid robots with single-axis ankles, this mechanism provides active 2-DoF (degrees of freedom) ankle control. This mechanical design enables:

1. Greater stability on uneven surfaces through active ankle adaptation
2. Enhanced weight distribution when traversing slopes
3. Better shock absorption when landing from steps or jumps

The importance of this ankle design becomes evident when comparing locomotion on irregular surfaces. The mechanism allows the foot to conform to the ground, providing greater surface contact area and stability. The robots are equipped with proprioceptive sensors including joint position/velocity sensors and IMUs (Inertial Measurement Units), but notably lack external sensors like cameras or LiDAR, making the control challenge more significant.

### 3. The Denoising World Model Learning Framework

The core contribution of this work is the Denoising World Model Learning (DWL) framework, which addresses the challenge of partial observability and noisy sensors in the real world.

The problem is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), acknowledging that real-world robots cannot directly access perfect state information. Instead, they must rely on noisy sensor readings that provide incomplete information about their true state and environment.

The DWL framework consists of several key components:

1. **Encoder**: A Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network that processes historical observations including joint positions/velocities, IMU readings, and commanded velocities.

2. **Actor**: Generates control actions (joint torques) based on the latent representation from the encoder.

3. **Decoder**: Reconstructs the robot's true state from the latent representation, including estimates of terrain properties.

4. **Critic**: Evaluates the value function using privileged information available only during training.

The training process employs three loss functions:
- **Denoising Loss**: Minimizes the error between predicted and actual states
- **Policy Gradient Loss**: Optimizes the agent's policy using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
- **Critic Loss**: Guides the critic to accurately evaluate state values

The framework addresses IMU drift, a common problem in real-world robotics. As shown in Figure 2, IMU measurements can accumulate error over time:

The DWL model learns to compensate for this drift, maintaining accurate estimates of the robot's orientation over time.

### 5. Training and Implementation Details

The DWL agent was trained entirely in simulation using domain randomization to enable zero-shot transfer to the real world. Key aspects of the training procedure include:

1. **Domain Randomization**: Critical parameters were randomized during training:
 - Friction coefficients (0.2-1.0)
 - Ground stiffness (5,000-20,000 N/m)
 - Added weights (0-10% of robot mass)
 - Motor gains (±20% variation)

2. **Observation Masking**: To simulate real-world sensor limitations, observations were randomly masked during training, forcing the agent to learn robust state estimation from partial information.

3. **Noise Injection**: Gaussian noise was added to sensor readings to simulate real-world sensor noise.

4. **Reward Function**: A composite reward function was designed with components for:
 - Velocity tracking (following commanded velocity)
 - Periodic reward (encouraging natural gait cycles)
 - Foot trajectory tracking (ensuring smooth foot movements)
 - Regularization terms (minimizing energy consumption and unnecessary movements)

The training environment included varied terrains such as flat ground, steps, slopes, and randomly generated irregular surfaces. This diversity ensures the agent develops strategies that generalize across terrain types rather than overfitting to specific scenarios.

### 6. Experimental Results

The trained DWL agent was directly deployed on the physical XBot robots without any fine-tuning, demonstrating successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The robots were tested on diverse challenging terrains:

1. **Snowy Inclined Ground**: The robot maintained stability despite low friction and uneven surface depression.
2. **Stairs**: Successfully navigated both ascending and descending stairs.
3. **Irregular Surfaces**: Traversed surfaces with random height variations.
4. **Deformable Terrain**: Maintained stability on surfaces that deform under pressure.

The DWL framework's ability to estimate terrain properties and adapt foot placement is demonstrated in Figure 9:

Comparative studies against baseline methods (PPO with Ankle Control and DWL without Ankle Control) showed the superiority of the combined DWL framework with active ankle control:

1. **Success Rate**: Higher success rates in traversing challenging terrains
2. **Stability**: Reduced body oscillation and more stable center of mass trajectory
3. **Energy Efficiency**: Lower overall energy consumption due to more efficient gait patterns
4. **Adaptation Speed**: Faster adaptation to unexpected terrain changes

### 7. Robustness to Disturbances

Beyond terrain challenges, the DWL framework demonstrated exceptional robustness to various disturbances:

1. **Mass Displacement**: The robot maintained stable locomotion even with weight asymmetrically distributed or with added mass of up to 15% of body weight.

2. **External Pushes**: As shown in Figure 8B, the robot could recover from moderate external pushes through coordinated ankle and whole-body strategies.

3. **Motor Failures**: The system showed degraded but continued function even when individual motors were disabled, demonstrating fault tolerance.

This robustness emerges from the DWL framework's ability to continuously estimate the robot's true state and adapt control strategies accordingly, rather than relying on pre-programmed responses to specific disturbances.

### 8. Significance and Future Work

The DWL framework represents a significant advance in humanoid robotics for several reasons:

1. **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: By enabling direct deployment from simulation to reality without fine-tuning, it dramatically reduces development time and costs.

2. **Minimal Sensor Requirements**: The system requires only proprioceptive sensors (joint encoders and IMUs), avoiding the complexity, cost, and processing requirements of external sensors like cameras or LiDAR.

3. **Generalization Across Robot Sizes**: The same framework was successfully deployed on both the smaller XBot-S and larger XBot-L robots with minimal adjustments, demonstrating scalability.

4. **Robust State Estimation**: The ability to accurately estimate the robot's state and terrain properties from noisy, partial observations is a fundamental advancement.

Future work will focus on several promising directions:

1. Incorporating visual perception to enhance long-range planning and obstacle avoidance
2. Expanding the behavioral repertoire to include manipulation tasks while maintaining balance
3. Developing more energy-efficient gaits for extended operation
4. Exploring transfer learning approaches to quickly adapt to new robotic platforms

This research demonstrates that combining advanced mechanical design (the active ankle mechanism) with sophisticated learning algorithms (the DWL framework) can overcome longstanding challenges in humanoid locomotion. The zero-shot sim-to-real capability represents a particularly important breakthrough, potentially accelerating the deployment of humanoid robots in practical applications from disaster response to industrial settings.
