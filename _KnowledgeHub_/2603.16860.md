# DreamPlan: Efficient Reinforcement Fine-Tuning of Vision-Language Planners via Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamPlan presents a framework for efficiently fine-tuning Vision-Language Model (VLM) planners for deformable object manipulation through an action-conditioned video world model. This approach nearly doubles the average success rate of VLM planners on challenging real-world tasks, achieving an average score of 0.60, and reduces inference time per decision from thousands of seconds to approximately 1.12 seconds.

## Problem

- VLM planners, despite strong semantic reasoning, lack task-specific physical understanding, leading to ineffective actions and low success rates in real-world manipulation, especially for deformable objects.
- Directly fine-tuning VLMs with real-world Reinforcement Learning (RL) is prohibitively expensive, unsafe, and extremely sample-inefficient.
- Existing high-fidelity simulators struggle to model complex deformable object dynamics, and integrating learned world models into online RL is computationally intensive due to repeated video generation.

## Method

- The framework utilizes a pre-trained VLM, **Qwen3-VL-8B**, which generates discrete, keypoint-based manipulation commands conditioned on visual inputs and a goal image.
- An ==action-conditioned video world model== (built on **CogVideoX-5B**) is trained on sub-optimal exploratory data, with enhanced conditioning via rendered robot arm trajectories and a focus on predicting object-only videos.
- An efficient reinforcement fine-tuning strategy employs ==Best-of-K sampling== to generate preference signals from world model rollouts, which are then used for policy updates via ==Odds Ratio Policy Optimization== (**ORPO**), decoupling expensive video generation from policy optimization.

## Results

- DreamPlan's action-conditioned world model reliably simulates deformable dynamics, generating physically plausible motions and achieving a higher PSNR of 26.25 for object-only predictions compared to 24.70 for full-scene predictions.
- VLM planners fine-tuned with DreamPlan achieved an average score of 0.60 on real-world deformable manipulation tasks (e.g., rope straightening, cloth folding), nearly doubling the performance of the strongest zero-shot baseline (Qwen3-VL-32B at 0.35) and showing absolute gains of 15-**40%** in success rates.
- The framework demonstrated superior inference efficiency, reducing decision time from thousands of seconds (for an explicit verification baseline) to approximately 1.12 seconds, while achieving better performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sub-optimal exploratory data collected by a zero-shot VLM is sufficient to train a robust action-conditioned video world model capable of accurately simulating complex deformable object dynamics.
> - Conditioning a video diffusion model with rendered robot arm trajectories and focusing on object-only prediction significantly improves the physical plausibility and accuracy of predicted deformations.
> - Decoupling world model inference from policy optimization through Best-of-K sampling and ORPO enables highly efficient reinforcement fine-tuning, overcoming the computational bottleneck of direct online RL with generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving deformable objects, requires both high-level semantic reasoning and a grounded understanding of physical dynamics. Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated strong semantic reasoning capabilities and promise as zero-shot planners, enabling robots to generate high-level plans or commands from visual and linguistic instructions. However, these models often lack task-specific physical understanding. When deployed in real-world environments, especially for contact-rich tasks with complex and unpredictable dynamics such as deformable object manipulation, VLM planners can exhibit compounding errors and low success rates because their generated actions, while semantically plausible, may be physically ineffective.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers a method to adapt these planners to specific task dynamics, thereby bridging the reality gap between VLM reasoning and physical execution. However, directly fine-tuning large VLMs through real-world robot interaction is often prohibitively expensive, unsafe, and extremely sample-inefficient due to the vast amounts of data required. Prior works have attempted to address this data challenge by using simulation-based training, but constructing high-fidelity and computationally efficient simulators for deformable objects remains a significant challenge, leading to a substantial sim-to-real gap.

More recently, learned world models, particularly video generation models, have been explored as visual planners or implicit simulators to generate synthetic experience for policy fine-tuning. These models learn predictive environment dynamics from interaction data. While existing action-conditioned visual models have shown utility in rigid-body interactions or simple deformations, they frequently struggle to realistically simulate the complex and highly nonlinear behaviors of deformable objects. Furthermore, many prior world-model-based RL approaches involve heavy world model inference within the RL loop, which can be computationally intensive, especially with slow diffusion-based video generation.

DreamPlan addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that leverages video world models to enable efficient reinforcement fine-tuning of VLM planners for deformable object manipulation. It distinguishes itself by:
- Focusing specifically on challenging deformable object manipulation tasks, where existing world models often falter.
- Training an action-conditioned video world model on sub-optimal exploratory data collected by a zero-shot VLM, demonstrating that such data is sufficient to capture complex deformable dynamics.
- Introducing an efficient reinforcement adaptation strategy that decouples computationally expensive video diffusion inference from policy optimization using a Best-of-K sampling strategy combined with Odds Ratio Policy Optimization (ORPO), thereby reducing the computational overhead compared to direct online RL in generative models.
- Injecting crucial physical grounding into VLMs without the need for large-scale, costly real-world data collection for RL.

This work contributes to the field by providing a method for integrating the high-level reasoning of VLMs with the fine-grained physical understanding necessary for real-world manipulation, particularly for objects with complex dynamics, in a data-efficient and computationally scalable manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current Vision-Language Model (VLM) planners in real-world robotic manipulation, especially concerning deformable objects. While VLMs excel at semantic reasoning and zero-shot planning, their inherent lack of task-specific physical understanding leads to significant challenges:

1. **Reality Gap for VLM Planners:** Pre-trained VLMs, despite their broad knowledge, are not explicitly grounded in real-world physics. This means their generated actions, though semantically coherent, can be physically ineffective, failing to account for how objects respond to contact, force, and gravity. This discrepancy is magnified in tasks involving deformable objects, where small variations in action can lead to substantially different and unpredictable topological deformations.
2. **Cost of Real-World Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Adapting VLM planners to specific task dynamics through traditional reinforcement learning typically demands extensive real-world interaction data. This process is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe for complex robotic systems, making large-scale deployment impractical.
3. **Limitations of Existing Simulation and World Models:** Current alternatives to real-world RL, such as high-fidelity simulators, struggle to accurately model the complex dynamics of deformable objects, leading to a persistent sim-to-real gap. Similarly, existing action-conditioned video world models are largely limited to simpler rigid-body interactions or rely on highly curated datasets, making them insufficient for realistically simulating the intricate deformations required for effective policy fine-tuning.
4. **Computational Inefficiency of Online World Model Integration:** Integrating learned world models directly into an online RL loop for policy optimization often involves repeated, computationally expensive video generation steps, particularly when using diffusion models, which hinders efficient policy adaptation.

To address these fundamental limitations, the key objectives of the DreamPlan framework are:

- **Efficiently Reinforcement Fine-Tune VLM Planners:** Develop a scalable method to imbue VLM planners with task-specific physical knowledge, enabling them to generate physically effective actions for deformable object manipulation.
- **Leverage Action-Conditioned Video World Models:** Construct an action-conditioned video generation model that implicitly captures complex real-world physics from limited exploratory interaction data, serving as a reliable virtual environment for policy optimization.
- **Overcome Deformable Object Modeling Challenges:** Train the world model to accurately predict plausible object deformations, even from sub-optimal data, by effectively conditioning it on low-level robotic actions and focusing on relevant object dynamics.
- **Decouple World Model Inference from Policy Optimization:** Introduce an efficient reinforcement adaptation strategy that minimizes the computational burden of repeated video generation during policy updates, while still providing physics-informed supervision. This involves transforming world-model evaluations into structured preference signals for an offline policy optimization method.
- **Improve Manipulation Success Rates:** Demonstrate a significant improvement in manipulation success rates for challenging deformable object tasks, thereby bridging the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level physical grounding without requiring extensive real-world data collection.

By achieving these objectives, DreamPlan aims to unlock the full potential of VLM planners for complex real-world robotic tasks, making their adaptation more practical and efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamPlan is a three-stage framework designed to efficiently fine-tune Vision-Language Model (VLM) planners for goal-conditioned deformable object manipulation using virtual rollouts generated by a video world model.

#### 3.1. A. VLM Planner for Deformable Manipulation

The framework utilizes a pre-trained VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) as the base planner, denoted as $\pi_{\theta}$, which generates actions conditioned on the current visual observation ($o_t$) and a goal image ($g$). To constrain the VLM's reasoning for deformable objects and simplify subsequent fine-tuning, a structured visual prompting strategy is employed.

1. **Keypoint Extraction:** Visual keypoints ($K_t$ from $o_t$ and $K_g$ from $g$) are extracted using SAM2, an object segmentation model. These keypoints are plotted onto the images and provided as visual context to the VLM.
2. **Discrete Action Representation:** Instead of regressing continuous pixel coordinates, the VLM outputs a discrete manipulation command. An action $a_t$ is defined as a pair of keypoints: a source keypoint $k^s_t \in K_t$ for grasping and a target keypoint $k^g_t \in K_g$ for placement. This discrete, keypoint-based representation grounds the planner's reasoning in meaningful object regions, reducing spatial ambiguity and facilitating optimization.

#### 3.2. B. Learning World Model for VLM Fine-Tuning

To avoid the prohibitive costs of real-world RL, an action-conditioned video world model, $W_{\phi}$, is learned to approximate the complex dynamics of deformable objects.

1. **Automated Data Collection:** Exploratory interaction data is collected by deploying the zero-shot VLM in the target environment. Although this initial data is often sub-optimal (dominated by failed or imperfect executions), it is demonstrated to be sufficient for training the world model by capturing diverse action-outcome causalities.
 - **Action Primitive:** Discrete keypoint-based commands (grasp and target locations) are unprojected into 3D Cartesian waypoints. A fixed motion sequence (home to grasp, gripper close, transport to target, gripper open, return to home) is executed by the robot arms, with smooth joint-space interpolation.
 - **Visual Grounding:** SAM2 is used to obtain accurate object masks for the current and goal scenes. Farthest Point Sampling extracts candidate points from these masks, which are then used by the VLM (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B) to select the specific arm and point pair for manipulation.
 - This automated pipeline collected 2056 trajectories in approximately four hours of robot interaction.
2. **Action-Conditioned Video World Model:**
 - **Backbone:** CogVideoX-5B (an image-to-video diffusion model) is used as the pretrained backbone.
 - **Action Conditioning:** To effectively condition the Video Diffusion Model (VDM) on low-level robotic actions and ensure focus on object deformation, robot arm configurations corresponding to the commanded action sequences are rendered into synthesized videos. These rendered action videos ($r_i$) are then injected into the VDM using a ControlNet-style architecture. This structural conditioning ensures pixel-level alignment between the action signals and the generated video, enabling accurate prediction of object evolution under specific robot actions. The model predicts noise ($\hat{\epsilon}$) for data $(x^i_t, t, c_i)$ using:
 $\hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_{\theta}(x^i_t, t, c_i) + \Delta_{\phi}(x^i_t, t, r_i)$, where $c_i$ is text and input image conditions, $\epsilon_{\theta}$ is the frozen pretrained backbone, $\Delta_{\phi}$ is the residual control branch, and $r_i = \text{render}(a^i_{0:H-1})$ is the rendered robot arm trajectory. The diffusion loss $L_{\text{diff}}$ is optimized:
 $L_{\text{diff}} = \mathbb{E}_{x^i_0,t,\epsilon} \left\| \epsilon - \hat{\epsilon}(x^i_t, t, c, r_i) \right\|^2_2$.
 - **Object-Only Prediction:** To reduce the burden of reconstructing irrelevant visual details (e.g., lighting, robot arms, background clutter), the model is trained to predict cropped object-only videos with a white background. This forces the VDM to concentrate on the deformation dynamics of the object itself.

#### 3.3. C. Reinforcement Fine-Tuning of VLM Planner with World Model

Once the world model $W_{\phi}$ is trained, policy adaptation is shifted entirely into the model's imagination, avoiding real-world interaction costs.

1. **Efficient Reward Construction (Best-of-K Sampling):** Direct online RL with repeated video diffusion inference is computationally expensive (approx. one minute per action sequence). To mitigate this, a two-stage scheme based on Best-of-K sampling is adopted:
 - For each training observation-goal pair $(o^i_0, g^i)$ from the collected data, a batch of $K$ candidate action sequences ($\{a^j_i\}_{j=1}^K$) is sampled from the VLM planner.
 - The world model $W_{\phi}$ is queried to predict the outcomes ($\{\hat{o}^j_i\}_{j=1}^K$) for these candidate actions.
 - A task-level objective (e.g., computed by GPT-4O comparing predicted rollout images with the goal image) is used to evaluate these predicted outcomes. The action that achieves the highest objective ($a^*$) is selected as the positive sample, and the remaining actions are treated as negative samples. This transforms the world model's physics-informed evaluation into structured preference signals.
2. **Odds Ratio Policy Optimization (ORPO):** These preference signals are then used to fine-tune the VLM planner. Odds Ratio Policy Optimization (ORPO), a preference-based fine-tuning method, is employed. ORPO directly contrasts positive and negative responses by optimizing a contrastive objective based on the log-likelihood ratio.
 $L_{\text{ORPO}} = \log \sigma \left( \log \pi_{\theta}(a^*|s_i) - \log \pi_{\theta}(a^-|s_i) \right)$, where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function, $s_i = (o_0, g_i)$, $a^*$ is the positive action, and $a^-$ represents sampled negative actions. This objective maximizes the odds of preferred actions over disfavored ones, providing stable and memory-efficient policy adaptation without requiring repeated world model queries during the optimization itself.

This integrated approach enables efficient VLM fine-tuning by leveraging the world model's predictive capabilities to provide physics-informed supervision, significantly reducing the reliance on costly real-world interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted investigate two primary questions: the reliability of video world models as verifiers for planning (Q1) and the effectiveness of the reinforcement learning framework in enabling VLM planners to internalize physical dynamics (Q2), along with an analysis of inference efficiency.

#### 4.1. A. Video World Models Serve as Reliable Verifiers (Q1)

1. **Qualitative Comparison of Video Generation:**
 - DreamPlan's action-conditioned world model was qualitatively compared against two image-to-video baselines: CogVideoX-5B-I2V and Wan2.2-I2V-A14B.
 - The baselines, conditioned via textual prompts, frequently failed to adhere to specified actions and generated physically inconsistent deformations.
 - In contrast, DreamPlan's world model, conditioned on rendered robot arm trajectories, consistently produced motion that aligned with the intended manipulation and exhibited physically plausible deformation behavior (Figure 3). This indicates its reliability as a verifier for VLM planner fine-tuning.
2. **Ablation on Object-Only Video Prediction:**
 - An ablation study was performed to assess the impact of training the VDM to predict object-only videos versus full-scene videos.
 - The object-only model, which concentrates on deformation dynamics by predicting cropped object videos on a white background, achieved a higher Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 26.25 compared to 24.70 for the full-scene prediction model (Table II). This suggests that focusing the world model on relevant object dynamics improves the accuracy of deformable dynamics reconstruction.

#### 4.2. B. World-Model RL Enhances VLM Planning (Q2)

1. **Overall Success Rate Improvements:**
 - The DreamPlan framework, applying RL fine-tuning on the Qwen3-VL-8B base model, demonstrated a substantial improvement in task success rates compared to zero-shot baselines (Figure 5).
 - Absolute gains in success rates ranged from 15% to 40% across all evaluated real-world tasks, highlighting the effectiveness of fine-tuning in enhancing the model's ability to execute complex manipulation commands.
2. **Quantitative Comparison with Zero-Shot Baselines:**
 - DreamPlan was quantitatively compared against leading pure zero-shot VLMs (Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen3-VL-32B, GPT-4o) on three challenging deformable object manipulation tasks: rope straightening, cloth folding, and toy arm repositioning.
 - DreamPlan achieved an average score of 0.60, nearly doubling the overall performance of the strongest zero-shot baseline, Qwen3-VL-32B (0.35), and significantly outperforming GPT-4o (0.28) (Table I).
 - On specific tasks:
 - **Rope Straightening:** DreamPlan achieved 0.60, doubling GPT-4o's performance (0.30).
 - **Toy Arm Repositioning:** DreamPlan reached 0.85, outperforming Qwen3-VL-32B (0.70) despite its smaller base model size.
 - These results suggest that pure zero-shot VLMs, which rely primarily on visual features, struggle with the physical grounding required for dynamic manipulation. DreamPlan effectively bridges this gap by internalizing physical dynamics through world model feedback, leading to physically viable strategies (Figure 6).

#### 4.3. C. Efficient Inference without World Model Sampling

1. **Inference Efficiency and Computational Cost Analysis:**
 - The efficiency of DreamPlan was contrasted against a sampling-based explicit verification baseline, where the VLM proposes N candidate actions, and the world model explicitly rolls out and verifies each one.
 - The explicit verification baseline, even with N=8 samples, achieved a lower average score (0.50) than DreamPlan (0.60).
 - Critically, the explicit verification method incurred severe computational overhead, requiring thousands of seconds per decision (e.g., 2605.56 seconds for N=8) and high TFLOPs.
 - In stark contrast, DreamPlan achieved superior performance (0.60 average score) while reducing the inference time by orders of magnitude, requiring only approximately 1.12 seconds per decision and significantly lower TFLOPs (Table III). This demonstrates that DreamPlan's ORPO-based approach, which decouples expensive video generation from policy optimization, is substantially more efficient for inference.

In summary, the findings indicate that DreamPlan's video world model reliably simulates deformable dynamics, and its world-model-guided reinforcement learning effectively enhances VLM planners' physical understanding, leading to significant performance improvements and high inference efficiency compared to zero-shot baselines and explicit sampling approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DreamPlan framework presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving deformable objects, by effectively bridging the gap between the semantic reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the necessity for grounded physical understanding.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to:

- **Enhance VLM Planners with Physical Grounding:** DreamPlan demonstrates that VLM planners, initially lacking task-specific physical knowledge, can be efficiently adapted to real-world physics. This enables them to generate actions that are not only semantically plausible but also physically effective, overcoming a critical limitation of zero-shot VLM deployment in complex environments.
- **Overcome Data Efficiency Challenges in RL:** By leveraging an action-conditioned video world model for virtual rollouts, DreamPlan drastically reduces the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe real-world data collection for reinforcement learning. This makes VLM fine-tuning for robotic tasks more scalable and practical, especially for deformable objects where real-world interaction data is difficult and expensive to acquire.
- **Advance Deformable Object Manipulation:** The framework specifically addresses the challenges of modeling and manipulating deformable objects, which have historically posed significant difficulties for robotic systems due to their complex, non-linear dynamics. By training a world model capable of predicting realistic deformations from sub-optimal data, DreamPlan contributes to broader applicability of robots in tasks like cloth folding, rope manipulation, and soft material handling.
- **Improve Computational Efficiency:** The proposed Best-of-K sampling strategy combined with Odds Ratio Policy Optimization (ORPO) provides a computationally efficient method for integrating world model-generated supervision into policy optimization. This addresses the high inference costs typically associated with diffusion-based video generation within an RL loop, making the fine-tuning process significantly faster and more resource-friendly.
- **Enable Offline Policy Adaptation:** The ability to perform reinforcement fine-tuning entirely offline within the "imagination" of a world model is impactful. It minimizes the need for continuous online interaction, accelerates the development cycle for robotic behaviors, and allows for thorough policy refinement in a controlled virtual environment.

The potential impact of DreamPlan extends to various domains within robotics and AI:

- **Broader Adoption of VLMs in Robotics:** By making VLM adaptation more feasible and effective for complex physical tasks, DreamPlan could accelerate the integration of large foundation models into real-world robotic systems, leading to more intelligent and versatile robots.
- **New Avenues for Robot Learning:** The demonstration that sub-optimal exploratory data is sufficient to train a robust video world model opens new avenues for data collection strategies and learning from diverse, less-curated datasets.
- **Applications in Logistics and Manufacturing:** Improved deformable object manipulation capabilities could have direct applications in industries requiring handling of fabrics, soft goods, or irregularly shaped items, potentially automating tasks previously reliant on human dexterity.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots capable of more dexterous and intelligent manipulation of a wider range of objects could enhance their utility and safety in human-centric environments.

Overall, DreamPlan represents a step towards creating robotic systems that combine high-level semantic intelligence with a deep understanding of physical reality, moving closer to robots that can reliably operate in the unstructured and dynamic environments of the real world.
