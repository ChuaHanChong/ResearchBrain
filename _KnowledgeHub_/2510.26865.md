# Do Vision-Language Models Measure Up? Benchmarking Visual Measurement Reading with MeasureBench

> [!summary] Summary
> BAAI's FlagEval Team created MeasureBench, a benchmark for evaluating vision-language models (VLMs) on reading visual measurements from various instruments. It reveals that current state-of-the-art VLMs struggle with precise numerical value extraction from gauges and scales, with the best model achieving 30.2% accuracy on real-world images, while demonstrating that reinforcement finetuning with synthetic data can improve performance and generalization.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit limitations in fine-grained perception, particularly in accurately mapping physical scales to numerical values on measurement instruments.
- Existing fine-grained evaluation benchmarks for VLMs rarely require models to interpret diverse measurement instruments (e.g., gauges, rulers, clocks) across various types and readout designs.
- The ability to precisely read measurement instruments is a critical unmet need for real-world applications in industrial, daily life, and embodied AI systems.

## Method

- The research introduces **MeasureBench**, a comprehensive benchmark with 2,442 ==image-question pairs== from 26 real-world and 16 synthetic instrument types, covering four readout designs (Dial, Digital, Linear, Composite).
- A modular and scalable data synthesis framework was developed using both 2D programmatic and 3D physical rendering to procedurally generate diverse, accurately annotated instrument images.
- Systematic evaluations of 18 contemporary VLMs were conducted, and reinforcement finetuning (RFT) on synthetic data was explored to enhance performance and generalization for this task.

## Results

- The highest-performing VLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieved only **30.2%** overall accuracy on the real-world MeasureBench dataset, with value reading identified as the primary bottleneck.
- Model performance did not monotonically increase with larger language backbone sizes; for example, Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** performed comparably or better than **32B** and **72B** variants, suggesting visual encoder limitations.
- Reinforcement finetuning (RFT) on synthetic data significantly boosted in-domain synthetic test accuracy (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-**3B** more than threefold increase) and led to notable improvements on out-of-distribution real-world images (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** improved from **14.6%** to **19.7%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs struggle significantly with precise value reading from measurement instruments, often failing on fine-grained visual cues, while their unit prediction capabilities are strong.
> - The task difficulty varies by readout design; digital displays are easiest (reliant on OCR), while dial, linear, and composite instruments pose greater challenges requiring accurate spatial grounding.
> - Reinforcement finetuning (RFT) with synthetically generated data can effectively improve VLM accuracy on measurement reading tasks, generalize to real-world images, and mitigate language model prior biases without negative transfer to general benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in complex reasoning tasks that integrate textual and visual information. Models such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro have achieved performance levels comparable to human experts on benchmarks like MMMU and Humanity’s Last Exam (HLE), which assess high-level understanding and reasoning.

Despite these developments, state-of-the-art VLMs exhibit limitations in fine-grained perception, particularly concerning low-level visual cues, precise geometry, and subtle visual changes. Existing fine-grained evaluation benchmarks, such as those for text reading, chart reasoning (e.g., Singh et al., 2019; Masry et al., 2022; Tang et al., 2025), and artificial low-level vision tests (e.g., BlindTest by Rahmanzadehgervi et al., 2024; SalBench by Dahou et al., 2025), primarily focus on recognition or conceptual understanding. These benchmarks rarely require models to map physical scales to specific numerical values, which is a common requirement in real-world scenarios involving measurement instruments.

A limited number of studies have addressed specific types of instrument reading, including clocks (Saxena et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2022), rulers (Matuzevičius, 2023; Pan et al., 2025), industrial gauges (Izquierdo-Domenech et al., 2025; Valente et al., 2025), and household meters (Van et al., 2025). However, these studies typically focus on a narrow range of instrument types or readout designs, limiting their generalizability.

This work aims to address this identified gap by introducing MeasureBench, a comprehensive benchmark for visual measurement reading. It seeks to evaluate VLMs on tasks that require fine-grained visual perception, light quantitative reasoning, and basic arithmetic operations across a diverse set of instruments and readout designs. By focusing on this specific domain, the research contributes to understanding the limitations of current VLMs in precise spatial grounding and visually grounded numeracy, which are critical for future embodied AI systems. Furthermore, the paper investigates the utility of synthetic data generation and reinforcement finetuning in improving VLM performance on such tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while humans effortlessly read measurement instruments, current vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with this task, even with advanced high-level reasoning capabilities. Accurate interpretation of instruments such as pressure gauges, thermometers, and clocks is essential for safety, efficiency, and decision-making in industrial settings, daily life, and for potential embodied AI systems. The ability to correctly map physical scales to numerical values requires fine-grained visual perception, a capability where current VLMs appear to exhibit limitations.

To address this challenge, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

- **Introduce a Comprehensive Benchmark:** The central objective is to present MeasureBench, a new benchmark specifically designed for visual measurement reading. This benchmark is intended to be comprehensive, covering a broad diversity of instrument types (26 real-world, 16 synthetic) and four distinct readout designs (Dial, Digital, Linear, Composite). It aims to provide a standardized evaluation platform for assessing VLM performance in this domain.
- **Develop an Extensible Data Synthesis Pipeline:** A significant objective is the creation of a data synthesis framework. This pipeline is designed to procedurally generate images of various gauges with controllable visual attributes, including pointers, scales, fonts, lighting, and clutter. This framework offers scalability in terms of both the variety of instrument types and the depth of variations within each type, enabling the generation of accurately annotated data for both evaluation and training purposes.
- **Conduct Standardized VLM Evaluation and Analysis:** The research aims to perform a systematic evaluation of contemporary proprietary and open-weight VLMs on MeasureBench. This evaluation is intended to quantify current model performance and, critically, to identify and analyze their specific failure modes. The analysis seeks to highlight underlying limitations in models' fine-grained spatial grounding and precise geometric reasoning capabilities.
- **Explore the Efficacy of Synthetic Data for Training:** A further objective is to investigate whether training with synthetically generated data can improve VLM performance on visual measurement reading tasks. This involves conducting preliminary experiments using reinforcement finetuning (RFT) over synthetic data to assess its impact on both in-domain synthetic subsets and out-of-distribution real-world images. This exploration aims to determine the potential for data curation to enhance VLM capabilities in this area.
- **Contribute Resources for Future Research:** By releasing the MeasureBench dataset and the associated data synthesis code, the authors aim to provide a valuable resource to the research community. This contribution is motivated by the desire to foster advancements in visually grounded numeracy and precise spatial perception for VLMs, ultimately bridging the gap between basic number recognition and the practical skill of interpreting measurements in the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces MeasureBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate vision-language models (VLMs) on visual measurement reading. The methodology encompasses benchmark construction, data synthesis, evaluation metrics, and experimental protocols.

**MeasureBench Overview:**
MeasureBench consists of two primary components: a diverse collection of instrument images with standardized annotations, and a data synthesis framework. Instruments are categorized into four readout designs based on visual appearance:
- **Dial:** Analog instruments with one or more pointers (e.g., ammeters, pressure gauges, clocks).
- **Digital:** Devices with electronic or mechanical digital displays (e.g., pulse oximeters, electricity meters).
- **Linear:** Instruments featuring linear scales without pointers (e.g., rulers, vernier calipers).
- **Composite:** Instruments that combine multiple readout designs (e.g., dial calipers, complex water meters).

The benchmark comprises 2,442 image–question pairs, with an emphasis on dial and linear instruments due to their fine-grained reading requirements.

**Real-world Subset Curation:**
A subset of 1,272 real-world images, covering 26 instrument types, was assembled from Google Image Search (permissive licenses), team member contributions, and a third-party vendor. Images were filtered for quality (e.g., blur, resolution, occlusion). Each image was annotated with its instrument type, readout design, candidate units, and a valid interval of reading values. The annotation process involved 10 qualified annotators, with each image independently labeled by one, verified by a second, and adjudicated by a third if disagreements arose. A final independent review ensured annotation accuracy.

**Data Synthesis Framework:**
A modular and scalable synthesis framework was developed, where each instrument type is treated as a generator. This framework randomizes various visual parameters while maintaining semantic validity:
- **Randomized Attributes:** Number and type of scales, measurement readout numbers, ranges/units, materials, lighting, backgrounds, and camera pose.
- **Back-ends:**
 - **2D programmatic rendering:** Utilizes LLMs to draft code based on prompt templates and constraints, leveraging libraries like Pillow, NumPy, and Matplotlib to create diverse layouts with fine control over fonts and geometry.
 - **3D physical rendering:** Employs existing Blender assets to generate photorealistic images. This involves randomizing backgrounds, instrument readings (e.g., pointer angles, scale ranges), and camera poses to reduce the sim-to-real gap. Specific procedures included asset retrieval, model normalization (e.g., aligning pointers to zero, mapping linear scales), contextual scene augmentation (adding objects for scales or rulers), automated pointer rotation control via Blender’s Python API, camera alignment for legibility, and realistic lighting through HDR environment maps or direct light sources.
For benchmarking, 1,170 synthetic images were generated, comprising 30 images for each of 39 distinct appearances across 16 instrument types.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Correctness is determined via interval matching for numeric values and unit prediction.
- **Answer Extraction:** Numeric values (integers, decimals, scientific notation, fractions) and time patterns (hh:mm[:ss]) are extracted from natural language outputs, typically after markers like "Answer:" or within \boxed{}.
- **Answer Matching:** Each ground-truth candidate specifies a numeric interval and acceptable unit substrings. A prediction is `value-correct` if the parsed number falls within the interval, and `unit-correct` if the parsed unit matches. A `fully-correct` prediction satisfies both. When multiple candidates exist, the best-scoring candidate is chosen (prioritizing `fully-correct`, then `value-correct`, then smaller relative error, then narrower interval).

**Evaluated Models:**
The study evaluated 18 contemporary VLMs, including 8 proprietary (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Mistral) and 10 open-weight models (Qwen-VL, InternVL3, LLaMA-4). All evaluations were conducted using the FlagEvalMM framework.

**Ancillary Experiments:**
- **Inference-time "Thinking" Analysis:** Investigated the effect of enabling "thinking" (generating reasoning tokens) on VLM performance and efficiency.
- **Prompt Sensitivity Analysis:** Compared "Specific" prompts (naming the instrument) with "General" prompts and the impact of adding a "precise answer" suffix.
- **Detailed Instructional Prompts:** Tested the influence of providing step-by-step reading instructions and in-context examples.
- **Reinforcement Finetuning (RFT):** Using the GRPO algorithm (Shao et al., 2024) and the verl library (Sheng et al., 2024), RFT was applied to Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B models. A rule-based reward function was designed, aligning with the evaluation method, giving positive rewards for correct value and unit as well as adherence to a specified output format. An alternative soft-margin reward variant was also explored. Training was performed on 3,900 synthetic images for 15 epochs.
- **Comparison with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** SFT (direct answer only; rationale + final answer) was compared against GRPO using the same synthetic dataset.
- **Generalization to General Benchmarks:** RFT-trained Qwen2.5-VL-7B was evaluated on MMMU, MMMU-Pro, MathVista, and TextVQA to assess potential negative transfer.
- **Comparison with Earlier Special-Purpose Systems:** Domain-specific computer vision systems (Shu et al., 2023; Reitsma et al., 2024) for gauge reading were evaluated on a relevant subset of MeasureBench to benchmark against general-purpose VLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLMs on MeasureBench yielded several findings regarding their capabilities and limitations in visual measurement reading:

- **Persistent Difficulty for Frontier VLMs:** Current VLMs demonstrate difficulty with instrument reading. The highest-performing model, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieved an overall accuracy of 30.2% on the real-world dataset and 26.3% on the synthetic dataset, indicating that this task remains challenging.

- **Value Reading as the Primary Bottleneck:** Models exhibited high accuracy (over 90%) in recognizing or inferring units for both real and synthetic images. In contrast, value accuracy was significantly lower (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro: 30.7% value accuracy on real images). This suggests that while VLMs possess strong OCR and object recognition capabilities for unit prediction, they struggle with the precise localization of pointers, ticks, and scales required for accurate numerical value estimation.

- **Varying Challenges by Readout Design:**
 - **Digital Displays:** These were the easiest, with models achieving up to 80.2% accuracy (Gemini 2.5 Pro on real images), likely due to reliance on OCR.
 - **Dial and Linear Instruments:** These remained challenging (typically 10-32% accuracy), as they necessitate accurate needle localization or reading tick marks under various visual conditions like clutter, highlights, and distortion.
 - **Composite Instruments:** These were the most challenging, often resulting in near 0% accuracy, as they require integrating information from multiple readout designs and performing numerical calculations.

- **Non-Monotonic Performance with Model Size:** Contrary to general expectations, larger VLM language backbones did not consistently lead to better fine-grained perception. GPT-5-Mini marginally outperformed GPT-5, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B performed comparably to or better than Qwen2.5-VL-32B and Qwen2.5-VL-72B. This suggests that without improvements in the visual encoder, increased language model size may not enhance fine-grained visual processing and can, in some cases, introduce biases (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B’s tendency to predict "10:10" for clock images).

- **Real vs. Synthetic Performance Gap:** Most VLMs generally achieved lower accuracy on synthetic images compared to real images, indicating that the synthetic scenes pose a genuine challenge rather than an easier abstraction. This drop was primarily attributed to lower value accuracy, while unit accuracies remained similar.

- **Limited Impact of "Thinking" and Prompt Engineering:**
 - **Inference-time Thinking:** Enabling "thinking" (generating reasoning tokens) yielded minimal improvement, and in some cases degraded performance, suggesting that extended text-based reasoning is not effective for enhancing the fine-grained visual perception required by this task.
 - **Prompt Sensitivity:** Explicitly naming the instrument type in prompts showed only a moderate positive impact on unit accuracy but negligible effect on overall accuracy. Adding a "precise answer" suffix also did not consistently improve performance.
 - **Detailed Instructions:** Providing step-by-step reading instructions or in-context examples resulted in very limited performance gains, further supporting the conclusion that the bottleneck lies in visual perception rather than a lack of procedural knowledge.

- **Systematic Fine-Grained Errors:** Case studies revealed that VLMs generally understand the task and can identify instruments and units. However, errors stemmed from minor perceptual mistakes, such as misinterpreting pointer positions (off by a minor tick) or incorrect interpretation of indicators (e.g., wrong minor-tick count, reading the wrong meniscus edge). The phenomenon of "error cancellation," where incorrect reasoning steps accidentally lead to a correct final answer, was also observed.

- **Effectiveness of Reinforcement Finetuning (RFT) with Synthetic Data:**
 - **In-Domain Improvement:** GRPO-based RFT on synthetic data significantly boosted in-domain synthetic test set accuracy (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B saw a more than threefold increase).
 - **Real-world Generalization:** RFT also led to notable improvements in accuracy on out-of-distribution real-world images (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved from 14.6% to 19.7%).
 - **Bias Mitigation:** RFT helped reduce the tendency of models to produce numerical outputs with spikes at round multiples of ten (a language model prior), making the output distributions smoother and more aligned with ground truth. Gains were most pronounced for simpler instrument types.
 - **Comparison to SFT:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on synthetic data, while improving in-domain performance, degraded accuracy on real-world images, indicating overfitting. GRPO-based RFT demonstrated better generalization by improving both synthetic and real-world performance.
 - **No Negative Transfer:** RFT training did not degrade performance on general benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista, TextVQA), indicating retention of general capabilities.

- **Inferior Performance of Earlier Special-Purpose Systems:** Domain-specific computer vision systems designed for gauge reading performed significantly worse than general-purpose VLMs on MeasureBench. These systems often failed due to poor generalization, exhibiting issues in gauge detection, pointer localization, or text recognition when presented with out-of-distribution data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of vision-language model (VLM) capabilities and limitations, particularly in the domain of fine-grained visual measurement reading. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Highlighting a Critical VLM Gap:** The introduction of MeasureBench rigorously demonstrates a persistent and significant limitation of current frontier VLMs: their struggle with fine-grained visual perception, precise spatial grounding, and accurate mapping of physical scales to numerical values. This identifies a concrete and challenging area for future VLM development, moving beyond high-level reasoning to precise visual interpretation.

- **Standardized Benchmark for Evaluation:** MeasureBench provides a comprehensive, standardized, and diverse benchmark for evaluating VLMs on a crucial task that integrates visual perception, quantitative reasoning, and basic arithmetic. Its dual nature, comprising both real-world and synthetically generated images across various instrument types and readout designs, offers a robust platform for comparative analysis and progress tracking.

- **Scalable Data Generation Resource:** The extensible 2D/3D data synthesis pipeline is a significant methodological contribution. It offers a scalable, controllable, and cost-effective means of generating accurately labeled data for instrument reading. This pipeline can be instrumental for:
 - **Controlled Experimentation:** Enabling researchers to systematically vary visual parameters (e.g., lighting, clutter, pointer angles) to test VLM robustness and identify specific failure modes.
 - **Training Data Augmentation:** Providing a rich source of training data that can mitigate the challenges and expense associated with real-world data collection and annotation, potentially accelerating model development.

- **Advancing VLM Training Methodologies:** The preliminary experiments with reinforcement finetuning (RFT) using synthetic data demonstrate a viable path for improving VLM performance in this domain. The observed gains, particularly the mitigation of language-model prior biases (e.g., "10:10" clock bias, spikes at round numbers), suggest that targeted data curation and training strategies can enhance visual groundedness. The comparison with supervised fine-tuning further emphasizes the importance of generalization in training approaches.

- **Guiding Future VLM Architecture and Representation Research:** The findings, especially the limited impact of increased model size or extended reasoning on fine-grained perception, indicate that the bottleneck may lie in current VLM architectures and their visual encoding schemes. This research implicitly calls for the development of improved visual representations and model designs that can genuinely reason from detailed visual cues and exhibit stronger generalization across novel instrument types and conditions.

- **Relevance for Embodied AI and Real-World Applications:** Accurate instrument reading is a foundational capability for intelligent systems operating in physical environments, such as industrial automation, smart homes, robotics, and healthcare. By improving VLM performance in this area, the work contributes directly to the development of more capable, reliable, and trustworthy embodied AI systems that can interact with and interpret the physical world.

- **Community Resource:** The release of MeasureBench and its associated code serves as a valuable public resource, fostering collaborative research and encouraging the wider scientific community to engage with and build upon these identified challenges and solutions.

In summary, this work provides a comprehensive assessment of VLM limitations in a practical, fine-grained visual task and offers methodological advancements that pave the way for more visually precise and numerically accurate AI systems.
