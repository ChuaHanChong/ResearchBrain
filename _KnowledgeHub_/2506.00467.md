# SST: Self-training with Self-adaptive Thresholding for Semi-supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SST framework introduces an efficient and robust approach to semi-supervised learning that leverages Self-Adaptive Thresholding (SAT) for high-quality pseudo-label selection. This method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on large-scale datasets like ImageNet-1K while dramatically reducing annotation costs and computational overhead.

## Problem

- The prohibitive cost and labor intensity of acquiring sufficient high-quality annotated data for deep learning models.
- Limitations of fixed confidence thresholds in pseudo-labeling, which often lead to either too few useful pseudo-labels or the inclusion of too many inaccurate ones.
- Existing dynamic thresholding methods (e.g., FlexMatch, FreeMatch) are computationally intensive due to frequent updates and can amplify confirmation bias by using non-converged models to generate pseudo-labels.

## Method

- SST employs an ==iterative self-training framework== with a novel ==Self-Adaptive Thresholding (SAT)== mechanism that generates class-specific thresholds.
- SAT updates these thresholds sparingly (once per training cycle) using a well-trained, high-performance model from the previous cycle, drastically reducing computational cost and mitigating ==confirmation bias==.
- The framework is implemented as ==Super-SST== (offline pseudo-labeling) and ==Semi-SST== (combining offline SAT-filtered pseudo-labels with online EMA-Teacher pseudo-labeling, also filtered by SAT).

## Results

- Semi-SST-ViT-Huge achieved state-of-the-art Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, reaching **80.7%** with **1%** labeled data and **84.9%** with **10%** labeled data.
- Super-SST significantly reduces threshold updates (e.g., **3** cycles compared to over **1,000,000** for FlexMatch/FreeMatch) and demonstrates faster runtime (**0.1s/iteration** on ImageNet-1K).
- Semi-SST-ViT-Huge with **10%** labeled ImageNet-1K data achieved **84.9%** Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the **84.8%** of a fully-supervised DeiT-III-ViT-Huge model trained on **100%** labeled data, indicating a tenfold reduction in annotation costs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Updating pseudo-label selection thresholds infrequently with a converged, high-performance model is critical for reducing confirmation bias and computational overhead in semi-supervised learning.
> - Dynamically generated, class-specific thresholds (SAT) effectively balance the quantity and quality of pseudo-labels, adapting better to model learning progress than static thresholds.
> - Robust semi-supervised learning methods can achieve equivalent or superior performance to fully-supervised models with significantly less labeled data, leading to substantial reductions in annotation costs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in modern machine learning: the prohibitive cost and labor intensity of acquiring sufficient high-quality annotated data for supervised learning, especially for deep neural networks that thrive on large datasets. Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a critical paradigm to mitigate this issue by effectively leveraging a small amount of labeled data alongside a large volume of unlabeled data.

The broader SSL landscape is primarily dominated by two families of methods:
1. **Consistency Regularization:** Approaches like Mean Teacher, UDA, and MixMatch encourage models to produce consistent predictions for unlabeled samples under various perturbations (e.g., data augmentations).
2. **Pseudo-labeling and Self-training:** Methods such as Pseudo-Label, Noisy Student, FixMatch, and Semi-ViT iteratively assign "pseudo-labels" to unlabeled data and use these to augment the training set, refining the model's performance over cycles. Self-training is a broader framework that incorporates pseudo-labeling.

While recent advancements, particularly with methods like Semi-ViT and Noisy Student, have shown significant promise, a persistent challenge in pseudo-labeling-based SSL is the accurate selection of high-quality pseudo-labels. Many methods rely on **fixed thresholds**, which are static confidence scores used to filter pseudo-labels. This rigidity often leads to either:
- **Too few pseudo-labels:** If the threshold is too high, many potentially useful unlabeled samples are discarded.
- **Too many inaccurate pseudo-labels:** If the threshold is too low, noisy or incorrect pseudo-labels are incorporated, leading to **confirmation bias**, where the model reinforces its own mistakes during training, ultimately hindering performance.

In response to the limitations of fixed thresholds, more sophisticated **dynamic or self-adaptive thresholding techniques** have been proposed, notably FlexMatch and FreeMatch. These methods aim to adjust thresholds more flexibly, often class-wise or based on global statistics. However, the authors identify critical shortcomings in these advanced methods:
- **Computational Intensity:** FlexMatch and FreeMatch update thresholds at every training iteration (potentially millions of updates), making them computationally expensive and time-consuming.
- **Confirmation Bias Amplification:** They update thresholds using models that are *still in training* rather than fully converged, high-accuracy models. This means early-stage models, which are less accurate, generate more unreliable pseudo-labels, exacerbating confirmation bias and leading to suboptimal performance.

The "SST: Self-training with Self-adaptive Thresholding" paper positions itself as a direct evolution from these prior works, specifically addressing the efficiency and confirmation bias issues inherent in existing dynamic thresholding approaches. By proposing an "effective and efficient" SSL framework, SST aims to set a new standard for pseudo-label selection, bridging the gap between performance and computational cost while enhancing robustness against inaccurate labels. This work fits into the growing trend of making SSL more practical and scalable for real-world, large-scale applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of the Self-training with Self-adaptive Thresholding (SST) framework are multifaceted and directly tackle the limitations observed in prior semi-supervised learning (SSL) methodologies.

The core motivations stem from the inherent challenges of existing pseudo-labeling and self-training methods:
1. **Fixed Threshold Limitations:** Many SSL methods rely on static confidence thresholds to select pseudo-labels. This approach is rigid and struggles to adapt to the evolving learning dynamics of a model, leading to either an insufficient quantity of pseudo-labels or the inclusion of low-quality, noisy ones.
2. **Computational Burden of Dynamic Thresholds:** While recent methods like FlexMatch and FreeMatch introduced more flexible, dynamic thresholding, they typically update these thresholds at every training iteration. This process is deemed "time-consuming, computationally intensive, and potentially unnecessary," especially given the large number of iterations in deep learning training.
3. **Confirmation Bias Amplification:** A significant concern is that both fixed and existing dynamic thresholding methods tend to introduce more noisy labels during the early stages of training. When these methods predict unlabeled data and update thresholds based on models that are still learning (i.e., not yet converged), they inevitably generate inaccurate pseudo-labels. This creates a detrimental feedback loop where the model reinforces its own mistakes ("confirmation bias"), ultimately degrading overall performance.

To overcome these pressing issues, the authors propose the SST framework, with its central **Self-Adaptive Thresholding (SAT)** mechanism, driven by the following key objectives:

- **Accurate and Adaptive Pseudo-label Selection:** To develop a mechanism (SAT) that can accurately and adaptively adjust class-specific thresholds throughout the learning process. This adaptability is crucial for ensuring both the high quality and sufficient quantity of selected pseudo-labeled data.
- **Mitigation of Confirmation Bias:** To design a pseudo-labeling strategy that actively reduces the risks of inaccurate pseudo-labels and confirmation bias. Specifically, SAT aims to prevent the model from prematurely incorporating low-confidence pseudo-labels, thereby reducing error reinforcement.
- **Enhanced Efficiency:** To create an SSL framework that is not only effective but also highly efficient, addressing the computational overhead of existing dynamic thresholding methods. This involves minimizing the frequency and cost of threshold updates.
- **Improved Overall Model Performance:** By achieving better pseudo-label quality and quantity, and mitigating bias, the ultimate goal is to significantly enhance the overall performance (e.g., accuracy) of deep learning models in semi-supervised settings.
- **Generalization and Scalability:** To demonstrate that the proposed SST framework is effective across various neural network architectures (CNNs and Transformers) and diverse datasets, showcasing its broad applicability and ability to scale with model capacity.
- **Reduction of Annotation Costs:** To enable models to achieve competitive, or even superior, performance with a drastically smaller amount of labeled data compared to fully-supervised methods, thereby reducing human annotation costs by a significant margin.

In essence, the authors are motivated to build a more robust, efficient, and performant SSL framework that can unlock the full potential of unlabeled data, making advanced AI technologies more accessible and cost-effective for practical applications where labeled data is scarce or expensive.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is the **Self-training with Self-adaptive Thresholding (SST)** framework, designed for effective and efficient semi-supervised learning. The SST framework is implemented in two variations: **Super-SST** and **Semi-SST**, and its central innovation is the **Self-Adaptive Thresholding (SAT)** mechanism.

The methodology involves an iterative training process, outlined in the following steps:

1. **Initialization:**
 - The model parameters ($\theta$) are initially trained using only the available, small set of original labeled data. This uses a standard Cross-Entropy (CE) loss to establish a strong baseline or "checkpoint" for subsequent self-training cycles.

2. **Prediction:**
 - After the initial training phase (or at the beginning of each cycle), the *well-trained, high-performance model* (from the previous cycle) is used to predict probabilities for all available unlabeled data. This yields an $N_u \times N_c$ probability matrix, where $N_u$ is the number of unlabeled samples and $N_c$ is the number of classes.

3. **Self-Adaptive Thresholding (SAT) Mechanism:**
 - This is the pivotal component that differentiates SST. Unlike fixed thresholds or per-iteration dynamic thresholds, SAT adaptively generates class-specific thresholds ($\tau_j$) based on the model's current learning progress. The SAT mechanism consists of four sub-steps:
 - **Sorting:** For each class $j$, all predicted probabilities from the unlabeled data are sorted in descending order.
 - **Cutoff Filtering:** A predefined cutoff value $C$ (e.g., 0.5) is applied. Only probabilities *higher* than $C$ are retained for each class. This step ensures that only higher-confidence predictions contribute to the threshold calculation, thereby enhancing pseudo-label quality.
 - **Average Calculation:** The average of the *filtered* probabilities is computed for each class $j$. This provides a class-specific measure of the model's confidence for high-probability predictions.
 - **Threshold Scaling:** The calculated average for each class is then multiplied by a scaling factor $S$ to derive the final class-specific threshold ($\tau_j$). This factor allows for fine-tuning the aggressiveness of pseudo-label selection.
 - **Key Distinction:** Crucially, SAT updates these thresholds *once per training cycle* (e.g., only a few times over the entire training process), using a model that has undergone full training in the previous cycle. This significantly reduces computational overhead and, by leveraging a more accurate model, mitigates the confirmation bias prevalent in methods that update thresholds more frequently with less converged models.

4. **Pseudo-label Selection:**
 - For each unlabeled sample, its highest predicted confidence ($\hat{p}_{ui}$) and corresponding pseudo-label ($\hat{y}_{ui}$) are determined.
 - A sample $x_{ui}$ is selected as a pseudo-labeled data point only if its confidence $\hat{p}_{ui}$ exceeds its respective class-specific threshold $\tau_{\hat{y}_{ui}}$. This ensures that only high-confidence and reliable pseudo-labels are chosen.

5. **Data Combination:**
 - The original labeled data is combined with the newly selected, high-confidence pseudo-labeled data. This expanded dataset serves as the "labeled" training set for the next optimization step.

6. **Model Optimization:**
 - **Super-SST:** The model is re-optimized using standard Cross-Entropy (CE) loss on the combined labeled data. In this variation, the selected pseudo-labels are treated as equally reliable as human-labeled data for training purposes. The training itself is purely supervised on this augmented dataset.
 - **Semi-SST:** This variation integrates the Super-SST's offline pseudo-labeling with an **EMA-Teacher** model, enabling dynamic *online* pseudo-labeling. The student model is trained on a total loss function ($\mathcal{L}$) comprising:
 - CE loss on the offline combined labeled data (from step 5).
 - Weighted CE loss on online pseudo-labeled data. For online pseudo-labeling, the teacher model (whose parameters are an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's parameters) generates pseudo-labels for weakly-augmented unlabeled data. These online pseudo-labels are *also filtered by the SAT mechanism* to maintain quality, before being used to train the student on strongly-augmented versions of the same data. This dual pseudo-labeling mechanism (offline and online, both SAT-filtered) leverages the strengths of both approaches.

7. **Iterative Training:**
 - Steps 2 through 6 are repeated for a predetermined number of cycles (e.g., 6 cycles as shown in Algorithm 1) until the model's performance converges. Each cycle allows the model to progressively learn from a larger and more refined set of pseudo-labeled data.

This iterative approach, with the crucial SAT mechanism, allows SST to adaptively grow the labeled dataset with high-quality pseudo-labels while maintaining computational efficiency and explicitly addressing the issue of confirmation bias by updating thresholds with more stable, better-performing models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation of SST (Super-SST and Semi-SST) demonstrates its state-of-the-art performance, remarkable efficiency, generalization, and scalability across a diverse range of benchmarks and architectures.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness, Generalization, and Scalability

- **Superior Performance:** Both Super-SST and Semi-SST consistently outperform their respective supervised and semi-supervised baselines across eight datasets, including large-scale ImageNet-1K, Food-101, iNaturalist, and smaller datasets like CIFAR-100/10, SVHN, and STL-10.
- **Significant Accuracy Gains:** The methods achieve relative Top-1 accuracy improvements ranging from 0.7% to 20.7% with only 1% labeled data, and 0.2% to 12.1% with 10% labeled data. These improvements are particularly pronounced in low-data regimes, highlighting their data efficiency.
- **Architectural Robustness:** SST's benefits extend to both Convolutional Neural Networks (ConvNeXt-T) and Transformer-based architectures (ViT-Small, ViT-Base, ViT-Huge), demonstrating strong generalization capabilities irrespective of the underlying model design.
- **Scalability:** The performance gains are more substantial with larger models like ViT-Huge, indicating that SST scales effectively with increased model capacity, allowing bigger models to better leverage unlabeled data.

#### 4.2. State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance

- **ImageNet-1K Benchmark:** Semi-SST-ViT-Huge achieves leading results on ImageNet-1K SSL benchmarks (without external data), reaching 80.7% Top-1 accuracy with only 1% labeled data and 84.9% with 10% labeled data. This sets new SOTA records in these competitive settings.
- **Direct Comparison:** On ImageNet-1K with 100K labeled data, Super-SST (using ResNet-50) surpasses FlexMatch and FreeMatch by 6.27% and 4.99% respectively, demonstrating significant performance lead over prominent dynamic thresholding methods.
- **Classical SSL Benchmarks:** Semi-SST achieved the lowest mean error rate of 8.99% across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and STL-10, securing the top rank in a Friedman test (p-value of 3.11 × 10⁻²⁵), followed closely by Super-SST (10.82% mean error rate).

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Cost-Performance Ratio

- **Drastically Reduced Threshold Updates:** Super-SST requires an astonishingly low number of threshold updates (e.g., 3 cycles means 3 updates) compared to FlexMatch and FreeMatch which perform over 1,000,000 updates. This is a crucial distinction contributing to efficiency and reduced confirmation bias.
- **Faster Runtime:** Super-SST achieves a significantly faster runtime of 0.1 seconds per iteration on ImageNet-1K, outperforming FixMatch (0.4s), FlexMatch (0.6s), and FreeMatch (0.4s).
- **Reduced GPU Hours:** On smaller datasets (CIFAR-100, STL-10), Super-SST and Semi-SST achieve 60x to 120x speedups in GPU hours compared to FreeMatch and SoftMatch, while simultaneously delivering superior performance.
- **Optimal Resource Utilization:** Super-SST offers an excellent balance of high performance with low GPU memory usage and moderate GPU days, making it highly practical. Semi-SST achieves maximum accuracy with slightly higher resource consumption.

#### 4.4. Annotation Cost Reduction & Performance Disparity

- **Tenfold Cost Reduction:** Semi-SST-ViT-Huge achieves 84.9% Top-1 accuracy using only 10% of ImageNet-1K labeled data. This performance is notably *superior* to the fully-supervised DeiT-III-ViT-Huge model, which achieves 84.8% with 100% labeled data. This signifies a tenfold reduction in human annotation costs while achieving equivalent or better performance, drastically narrowing the gap between semi-supervised and fully-supervised learning.
- **Knowledge Distillation for Deployment:** Huge-to-small knowledge distillation experiments (ViT-Huge teacher to ViT-Small student) further demonstrate the ability to train compact, high-performance models for practical deployment. A distilled Super-SST-ViT-Small on 10% data achieves 80.3% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the fully-supervised DeiT-ViT-Small on 100% data (79.8%).

#### 4.5. Robustness to Real-World Challenges

- **Class Imbalance and Label Noise:** Experiments on Clothing-1M (noisy labels, class imbalance) and iNaturalist (extreme class imbalance) show that Super-SST achieves strong robustness, significantly outperforming supervised baselines and approaching fully-supervised performance with limited clean labels.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **SAT Parameter Influence (C and S):** Experiments on ImageNet-1K (1% labels, ViT-Small) clarify the role of the cutoff value (C) and scaling factor (S) in SAT. Lower C values (0.3, 0.5) lead to faster and higher accuracy, while moderate S values (0.8, 0.9) provide optimal performance, balancing the influx of pseudo-labels with their quality.
- **SAT vs. Fixed Thresholding (FTH):** SAT consistently outperforms FTH methods in Top-1 accuracy for both Super-SST and Semi-SST. SAT's dynamic adjustment of class-average thresholds better balances the quantity and accuracy of selected pseudo-labels, adapting to the model's evolving confidence, unlike static FTH methods which often lead to sub-optimal selection (either too many noisy or too few high-quality pseudo-labels).

In summary, the results unequivocally establish SST as a highly effective, efficient, and robust SSL framework, capable of achieving SOTA performance while dramatically reducing annotation costs and computational resources, marking a significant advancement in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "SST: Self-training with Self-adaptive Thresholding" framework introduces several significant advancements that have far-reaching implications for both academic research in semi-supervised learning (SSL) and practical applications of deep learning.

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Novelty in Dynamic Thresholding:** The core innovation lies in the Self-Adaptive Thresholding (SAT) mechanism. Unlike prior dynamic thresholding methods that update thresholds frequently with models still in training, SAT updates thresholds sparingly (once per training cycle) using a well-trained, high-performance model. This fundamentally alters the pseudo-label selection process, offering a more stable and less biased approach.
- **Mitigation of Confirmation Bias:** By designing SAT to leverage converged models for pseudo-label generation and selection, the framework effectively addresses the critical issue of confirmation bias. This represents a significant step forward in making self-training more robust and reliable.
- **Enhanced Understanding of Pseudo-label Quality-Quantity Trade-off:** The ablation studies thoroughly investigate the impact of SAT's parameters (cutoff value C and scaling factor S) and demonstrate how SAT dynamically balances the quality and quantity of pseudo-labels. This contributes to a deeper understanding of this fundamental trade-off in SSL.
- **Flexible Framework:** The SST framework's ability to integrate with both supervised (Super-SST) and semi-supervised (Semi-SST, leveraging EMA-Teacher) algorithms showcases its flexibility and potential as a foundational component for future SSL research.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact and Applications

- **Drastic Reduction in Annotation Costs:** The most striking practical implication is the demonstrated tenfold reduction in human annotation costs. Achieving comparable or superior performance (e.g., 84.9% Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with 10% labeled data versus 84.8% with 100% labeled data) means that deploying high-performance AI models becomes feasible for organizations with limited labeling budgets.
- **Accessibility to AI in Data-Scarce Domains:** This cost reduction and performance efficiency are critical for domains where labeled data is inherently scarce, expensive, or sensitive. Examples include:
 - **Medical Imaging:** Diagnosing rare diseases, segmenting tumors, where expert annotations are extremely costly and time-consuming.
 - **Scientific Research:** Analyzing specialized scientific data where labeling requires domain-specific expertise.
 - **Industrial Inspection/Defect Detection:** Automating quality control in manufacturing, where obtaining labeled images of rare defects is challenging.
 - **New Product Development:** Rapidly prototyping AI features for new products without extensive initial data collection.
- **High Efficiency for Large-Scale Deployment:** The remarkable efficiency of SST in terms of fewer threshold updates and faster runtime translates directly into lower computational resource requirements (GPU days, memory). This makes it a highly attractive solution for large-scale enterprise applications and scenarios where energy consumption or processing time is a critical factor.
- **Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** The demonstrated effectiveness in handling class imbalance and label noise (on iNaturalist and Clothing-1M) further enhances SST's applicability to imperfect, real-world datasets, which rarely conform to perfectly clean and balanced distributions.
- **Enabling Compact, High-Performance Models:** The successful application of knowledge distillation from large teacher models (ViT-Huge) to smaller student models (ViT-Small) within the SST framework opens avenues for deploying lightweight, high-performance models on edge devices or in resource-constrained environments, making AI more ubiquitous.

#### 5.3. Broader Societal Impact (as acknowledged by authors)

- While advancing AI capabilities, the authors responsibly acknowledge a potential negative societal impact. The significant reduction in the need for human annotation services could lead to job displacement or reduced income for individuals who rely on these services. This highlights the importance of considering the socio-economic implications alongside technological advancements and potentially exploring reskilling initiatives.

In conclusion, SST represents a significant leap forward in semi-supervised learning. By ingeniously combining self-training with an efficient and robust self-adaptive thresholding mechanism, it not only pushes the boundaries of performance but also makes high-quality deep learning more accessible, cost-effective, and practical for a wide array of real-world applications. The framework lays strong groundwork for future research in adaptive pseudo-labeling and optimization strategies.
