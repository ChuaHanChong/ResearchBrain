# NestDex: Nested Policy Learning with Copilot Assisted Teleoperation for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> NestDex is a nested policy-learning framework for dexterous manipulation. During teleoperation, the operator drives the arm while a single-DoF clutch regulates proprioceptive inner hand skills selected by a vision-language model, so fingers need not be specified directly. Collected demonstrations train a separate outer visuomotor policy using compact hand-action VAE latents, reaching 100% collection success and up to 100% autonomous task success.

## Problem

- Multi-finger hands need coordinated motion across many joints, so operators must specify arm targets and fine contact behaviour simultaneously during every demonstration.
- Glove and vision-based dexterous teleoperation systems give expressive finger control but still leave all hand-arm coordination burden on the human throughout multi-stage tasks.
- Skill-based hierarchical systems keep selectors and low-level skills inside the deployed controller, and high-dimensional hand joint targets make behaviour cloning hard from few demonstrations.

## Method

- ==Copilot-assisted teleoperation==: a reversible single-DoF clutch maps to a normalised skill-progress index driving a ==proprioceptive inner hand policy== trained on multi-view retargeted hand demonstrations with ==Huber-penalised vector retargeting==.
- ==Vision-language skill selector== reads the wrist-camera image plus numbered skill descriptions and activates an inner policy at startup or after full reversal, enabling skill reuse across task stages.
- ==Hand-action variational autoencoder (H-VAE)== compresses 20-DoF hand commands into 10-dimensional latents as ==behaviour-cloning== targets for a ==DINOv3==-based outer visuomotor ==Transformer==, keeping arm commands in joint space.

## Results

- Copilot collection hits **100%** demonstration success on all six tasks versus AnyTeleop's **0-75%**, and cuts time per successful demonstration, e.g. **41.37 s** versus **88.88 s** on Bottle Disposal.
- Outer policies from copilot data with H-VAE reach **100%**, **75%**, **90%**, **100%** across four tasks, beating no-H-VAE copilot (**65-85%**) and AnyTeleop-trained policies (**20-75%**).
- Bottle-grasp ablation: fixed-command replay succeeds **3/10**, closed-loop no-ensemble **7/10**, closed-loop with temporal ensembling **9/10**, which also cuts P95 executed jerk **2.30x**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting control authority, human for task-level arm motion and learned skills for fine contact, can be a data-collection tool rather than a permanent deployed architecture component.
> - Proprioception-only skills conditioned on joint positions and efforts adapt contact configurations to object geometry implicitly, without any vision or object identity input.
> - Compressing correlated high-dimensional actuator commands into low-dimensional latents makes behaviour cloning tractable from limited demonstrations; open-loop replay of one successful trajectory is brittle.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation, involving multi-fingered robot hands, holds potential for sophisticated interaction with the physical world. However, developing autonomous policies for such tasks is often hampered by the challenge of collecting high-quality demonstration data. Imitation learning, a common paradigm for robot control, relies on consistent and complete human demonstrations. For dexterous tasks, operators must simultaneously coordinate multi-joint arm movements with intricate, contact-rich finger behaviors, a process that is both complex and time-consuming.

Existing dexterous teleoperation systems often provide operators with increasingly granular control over the robot hand, necessitating high operator skill and continuous attention to both arm and hand kinematics. This complexity contrasts with parallel-jaw grippers, where hand control is typically reduced to a single opening coordinate. The difficulty in data collection restricts the complexity and number of tasks for which dexterous manipulation policies can be learned.

This work addresses this data collection bottleneck by proposing a nested policy-learning framework. Rather than requiring operators to directly specify full finger trajectories, NestDex introduces a division of labor where learned hand skills assist the demonstration process. This approach aligns with broader trends in hierarchical and skill-based policy learning, where complex tasks are decomposed into reusable sub-skills. However, unlike many hierarchical methods where these skills and selectors remain part of the deployed system, NestDex uses these "inner" policies purely for data collection. The final "outer" policy, trained on the collected demonstrations, then operates autonomously without the assisting inner policies. Furthermore, the work explores the use of compact latent action representations, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), to simplify the high-dimensional action space of dexterous hands, a strategy also pursued in reinforcement learning and imitation learning to improve policy learning efficiency and stability. NestDex positions itself as a practical pipeline that leverages automation during data collection to facilitate subsequent autonomous learning, bridging the gap between human teleoperation and fully autonomous dexterous robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind NestDex is to mitigate the inherent difficulty of collecting consistent and complete demonstrations for dexterous manipulation tasks. The high dimensionality of robot arm-hand systems requires simultaneous coordination of numerous joints, placing a substantial burden on human operators during teleoperation. This burden leads to unreliable and inefficient data collection, which in turn limits the performance of imitation learning algorithms.

The key objectives of the NestDex framework are:

1. **Reduce Operator Burden during Demonstration Collection:** By offloading fine-grained dexterous hand control to learned policies, NestDex aims to allow the operator to focus primarily on task-level arm motion and overall task progression, rather than micromanaging individual finger movements. This is intended to simplify the teleoperation interface.
2. **Improve Demonstration Reliability and Efficiency:** The framework seeks to enhance the consistency and success rate of collected demonstrations, leading to higher quality training data for autonomous policies. This includes reducing the time and effort required to obtain a successful demonstration.
3. **Enable Effective Autonomous Policy Learning from Assisted Demonstrations:** The core objective is to demonstrate that demonstrations collected with the copilot-assisted approach can successfully train a separate, fully autonomous visuomotor policy capable of controlling both the arm and hand without the assistance of the inner policies at deployment.
4. **Facilitate Generalization and Reuse of Hand Skills:** The system aims to allow individual dexterous hand skills to be learned once and then reused across different stages of various complex tasks, as well as adapting to varying contact conditions for different objects.
5. **Investigate the Utility of Compact Hand-Action Representations:** To address the challenge of learning high-dimensional hand control, an objective is to evaluate whether a compact latent action space, derived from a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), can improve the learning efficiency and performance of the autonomous outer policy.
6. **Evaluate the Benefits of Online, Contact-Aware Execution and Temporal Ensembling:** The research seeks to quantify the advantages of allowing inner policies to adapt to real-time contact changes and the smoothing effect of temporal ensembling on executed commands, contributing to more robust and fluid manipulation.

These objectives collectively aim to provide a practical and effective pipeline from human-assisted data collection to autonomous dexterous manipulation, thereby addressing a fundamental bottleneck in robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NestDex proposes a nested policy-learning framework that separates demonstration collection from autonomous policy deployment.

**A. Inner Policy Learning**
This stage focuses on learning reusable dexterous hand skills.
1. **Hand Skill Demonstration Collection:** Human operators demonstrate specific dexterous hand skills (e.g., grasping, pinching) using a camera-based hand retargeting system. Multi-view camera observations are used to reconstruct 3D human-hand poses, reducing depth ambiguity and occlusions. These poses are then retargeted to the follower dexterous robot hand using a vector-based formulation, adapted from AnyTeleop, that minimizes a Huber penalty to robustly match human hand vectors to robot hand vectors while penalizing deviations from the previous robot hand configuration to ensure temporal smoothness. During this process, the robot hand's joint positions and efforts are recorded to form skill-specific demonstration trajectories.
2. **Inner Policy Training:** For each collected hand skill, a proprioceptive inner policy is trained. This policy is a Transformer network that takes a history of recent hand states (joint positions and efforts) as input and predicts an action chunk of future hand joint positions. The training uses a sliding window approach on the collected trajectories, pairing proprioceptive observations with corresponding action chunks. A single grasp inner policy is trained across demonstrations involving multiple distinct objects (e.g., scallion toy, water bottle, radish toy, paper cup) to encourage adaptation to contact dynamics rather than object identity.

**B. Copilot Assisted Teleoperation**
During complete-task demonstration collection, the human operator controls the robot arm and regulates the active inner hand policy.
1. **Clutch Guided Inner-Policy Execution:** The operator directly teleoperates the follower robot arm by mapping leader-arm joint positions. Concurrently, a single-degree-of-freedom (DoF) clutch on the leader system controls the "skill progress" (`p_t`) of the active inner hand policy. This progress is a normalized scalar that maps to a target execution index (`s_in_t`) within the inner policy's training trajectories. The robot hand's current execution index (`r_in_t`) tracks this target, advancing or retracting one step per control cycle. When advancing, the inner policy is queried with the latest proprioceptive history, and its ensembled predictions (using temporal ensembling, as described in Preliminaries, to smooth transitions) are executed. This closed-loop execution allows the hand motion to adapt to real-time contact changes. Reversing the clutch allows the operator to retract the skill, enabling corrections.
2. **Vision-Language Policy Selection:** A pre-trained vision-language agent dynamically selects the appropriate inner policy (e.g., "Tongs Grasp," "Button Press") based on the current wrist-camera image and a list of skill descriptions. This selection occurs at startup and when the operator fully reverses the active policy to its starting point (`r_in_t = 0`), allowing for sequential execution of different hand skills throughout a multi-stage task.

**C. Outer Policy Learning**
After collecting complete task demonstrations using the copilot assistance, a separate, autonomous outer visuomotor policy is trained.
1. **Hand Variational Autoencoder (H-VAE) Training:** To manage the high dimensionality (20 DoF) of dexterous hand commands, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is trained specifically for hand joint-position commands. The H-VAE encodes these commands into a compact, lower-dimensional latent action space (10-dimensional in experiments) and then reconstructs the full 20-DoF command. During outer policy training, the posterior mean of the encoded latent action is used as the target. Arm commands remain in their original joint space.
2. **Outer Policy Training:** The autonomous outer policy is a visuomotor Transformer network. It receives a wrist-camera image and the robot's state (arm and hand joint positions and efforts) and predicts an action chunk comprising arm commands and the compact hand latent actions from the H-VAE. This policy is trained using behavior cloning on the full-task demonstrations collected with copilot assistance. Visual embeddings are derived from a DINOv3 model pre-trained on a large visual dataset (LVD-1689M). During deployment, the outer policy predicts action chunks, which are then processed by temporal ensembling. The resulting arm commands are sent directly to the robot arm, while the predicted hand latent actions are decoded by the H-VAE to generate the final hand joint-position commands.

**Experimental Setup:** All experiments use a leader-follower platform with 7-DoF Piper Nero arms and 20-DoF five-finger WujiHand I dexterous hands. Inner policies run at 100 Hz. The evaluation covers six dexterous manipulation tasks, ranging from single-arm to dual-arm scenarios (e.g., Tongs Transfer, Toast Preparation). Metrics include demonstration collection success rate, time per successful demonstration, and autonomous outer-policy success rate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of NestDex focused on six dexterous manipulation tasks, comparing its performance in demonstration collection and subsequent autonomous policy learning against a baseline (AnyTeleop) and assessing various system components.

**A. Reliable and Efficient Demonstration Collection (Q1)**
- **Comparison with AnyTeleop:** NestDex demonstrated a higher success rate and reduced time per successful demonstration across all six evaluated tasks compared to AnyTeleop. For some complex tasks like "Tongs Transfer," "Toast Preparation," and "Binder Filing," AnyTeleop yielded no successful demonstrations (0% success rate), while NestDex achieved a 100% success rate. For "Bottle Disposal," "Dual-Object Transfer," and "Ingredient and Pot Transfer," NestDex maintained 100% success rates with significantly lower average collection times (e.g., 41.37s vs. 88.88s for Bottle Disposal).
- **Conclusion:** The separation of arm teleoperation from dexterous hand motion generation, facilitated by inner policies, improved both the reliability and efficiency of collecting complete-task demonstrations.

**B. From Copilot to Autonomy (Q2) and Benefit of H-VAE (Q3)**
- **Autonomous Policy Learning (Q2):** Outer policies trained exclusively on copilot-collected demonstrations achieved high success rates across the evaluated tasks (ranging from 60% to 100%). When compared directly, policies trained on copilot demonstrations consistently outperformed those trained on AnyTeleop demonstrations for tasks where both methods yielded data. For tasks where AnyTeleop failed to produce any successful demonstrations, NestDex-trained policies still enabled successful autonomous execution.
- **H-VAE Contribution (Q3):** Incorporating the Hand Variational Autoencoder (H-VAE) for hand-action representation consistently improved the success rates of the autonomous outer policies. For example, in "Tongs Transfer," success rates increased from 65% (without H-VAE) to 100% (with H-VAE). Similarly, "Bottle Disposal" saw an increase from 60% to 75%, "Dual-Object Transfer" from 80% to 90%, and "Ingredient and Pot Transfer" from 85% to 100%.
- **Conclusion:** Copilot-assisted demonstrations are effective for training autonomous policies. The compact latent action space provided by the H-VAE aids outer-policy learning by simplifying the high-dimensional hand command space, allowing the policy to focus on task-relevant coordination patterns.

**C. Online Execution and Contact Behaviour (Q4 and Q5)**
- **Online Execution (Q4):** A study on the water-bottle grasp compared three execution conditions: fixed-command replay, closed-loop without temporal ensembling, and closed-loop with temporal ensembling.
 - **Robustness to Contact:** Fixed-command replay had a success rate of 3/10 trials, whereas closed-loop execution (with or without ensembling) significantly improved robustness (7/10 and 9/10 successes, respectively). This indicates that real-time adaptation to proprioceptive feedback is crucial for contact-rich tasks.
 - **Smoothness (Temporal Ensembling):** Closed-loop execution without temporal ensembling exhibited 2.30 times greater executed-command P95 jerk (a measure of motion abruptness) compared to closed-loop execution with temporal ensembling. This smoothing effect was observed across different filter windows and did not increase execution duration. Furthermore, successful trials without ensembling sustained greater post-closure effort, suggesting more abrupt contact interactions.
- **Contact-Conditioned Adaptation (Q5):** The same grasp inner policy, trained on demonstrations with four different objects, produced distinct hand configurations tailored to each object during execution. The policy achieved this without receiving any visual or identity information about the objects, relying solely on evolving joint positions and efforts. This indicates the policy's ability to adapt its motion based on contact-derived proprioceptive feedback rather than simply replaying a fixed trajectory.
- **Conclusion:** Closed-loop prediction, responding to proprioceptive history, is essential for robust dexterous manipulation in the presence of contact variations. Temporal ensembling further enhances this by providing substantially smoother command execution. The inner policies demonstrate contact-conditioned adaptation across learned object geometries.

**D. Skill Selection and Reuse Across Task Stages (Q6)**
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Observations during "Toast Preparation" and "Binder Filing" tasks showed that the vision-language policy selector successfully activated the appropriate inner hand skills for different task stages. It also demonstrated the ability to reactivate previously used skills when needed later in the task (e.g., returning to "Tongs Grasp" in Toast Preparation, or "Paper Pinch" in Binder Filing).
- **Conclusion:** The vision-language selector effectively reuses a compact set of learned hand skills across multiple stages of complex, multi-skill tasks, streamlining the demonstration collection process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NestDex presents a significant advancement in the field of dexterous robot manipulation by directly addressing the long-standing challenge of collecting high-quality, comprehensive demonstrations. The core contribution is its nested policy-learning framework, which strategically places learned hand skills within the demonstration collection loop. This innovation offers several impactful implications:

Firstly, **it substantially reduces the operator burden** associated with teleoperating multi-DoF dexterous robot hands. By automating fine-grained finger coordination, NestDex allows human operators to concentrate on high-level task progression and arm movements, transforming complex dexterous teleoperation into a more manageable "copilot-assisted" experience. This simplification makes it feasible to collect data for tasks that were previously too difficult or time-consuming to demonstrate reliably.

Secondly, the framework **improves demonstration reliability and efficiency**, leading to cleaner and more consistent training data. The empirical results demonstrate that NestDex can collect successful demonstrations for tasks where traditional retargeting methods (like AnyTeleop) fail, and it does so with greater speed. This directly tackles the data bottleneck, which is often a limiting factor in the performance of imitation learning policies.

Thirdly, NestDex provides a **practical and effective pathway from assisted data collection to fully autonomous manipulation.** The collected demonstrations are shown to be sufficient for training independent visuomotor policies that control both the robot arm and hand without relying on the assisting inner policies during deployment. This confirms the utility of the "collection-time only" assistance paradigm.

Fourthly, the research highlights the **importance of compact action representations** (via H-VAE) for high-dimensional dexterous hands. By encoding hand joint commands into a lower-dimensional latent space, the learning problem for the outer policy becomes more tractable, leading to improved autonomous task success rates. This finding has broader implications for learning control policies for complex robot systems.

Finally, the work emphasizes the **critical role of online, contact-aware execution and temporal ensembling** for robust and smooth dexterous manipulation. The ability of inner policies to adapt to real-time proprioceptive feedback improves robustness to contact variations, while temporal ensembling ensures fluid and less abrupt movements. This ensures that the learned skills are not brittle but reactive and physically compliant. The demonstrated reuse of contact-conditioned hand skills across different objects and task stages further underscores the framework's adaptability.

The potential impact of NestDex is broad, extending beyond research to practical applications in domains requiring complex object interaction, such as logistics, healthcare, domestic assistance, and advanced manufacturing. By making dexterous manipulation more accessible to train and deploy, NestDex paves the way for robots to perform a wider range of contact-rich and intricate tasks, bringing general-purpose manipulation closer to realization.
