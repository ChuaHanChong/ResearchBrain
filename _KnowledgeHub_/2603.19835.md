# FIPO: Eliciting Deep Reasoning with Future-KL Influenced Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The FIPO algorithm introduces a method for dense credit assignment in large language model reinforcement learning through discounted Future-KL divergence. This approach enabled a Qwen2.5-32B-Base model to significantly extend its average chain-of-thought reasoning length from 4,000 to over 10,000 tokens and improve Pass@1 accuracy on AIME 2024 by 6.0%.

## Problem

- Existing GRPO-style reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms for LLMs use outcome-based rewards with coarse-grained, uniform credit assignment across all tokens in a trajectory.
- This coarse credit assignment leads to "length stagnation," limiting the depth of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning models can achieve for complex multi-step tasks.
- Alternative PPO-based methods for granular advantage estimation often require auxiliary critic models or pre-trained value models, introducing additional complexity or external knowledge priors.

## Method

- **FIPO** integrates ==discounted Future-KL divergence== into a ==GRPO==-style policy update, providing dense, token-level ==credit assignment== by capturing the causal influence of a token on its subsequent trajectory.
- It formulates a ==dense advantage== that re-weights tokens based on an iteratively refined Future-KL metric, which quantifies cumulative policy deviation for the remainder of the trajectory.
- The method includes extreme value filtering and a ==soft decay window== for Future-KL estimation to ensure training stability.

## Results

- FIPO improved Pass@1 accuracy on AIME 2024 to **56.0%** (**6.0%** over DAPO baseline) and on AIME 2025 to **43.0%** (**5.0%** over DAPO baseline), matching o1-mini performance on AIME 2024.
- The algorithm enabled models to overcome "length stagnation," extending the average chain-of-thought length from approximately **4,000** tokens to over **10,000** tokens, correlating with improved accuracy.
- Qualitative analysis revealed an evolution of reasoning behavior from superficial planning to "systematic deep reasoning" characterized by compute-heavy strategies and multi-pass symbolic re-derivation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sophisticated reasoning, including self-reflection and systematic verification, can be elicited from clean base models without prior long-CoT synthetic data, purely through algorithmic improvements in RL.
> - A dense advantage formulation, typically associated with PPO, can be effectively realized within the efficient, value-network-free GRPO framework, challenging the necessity of complex critic models for granular credit assignment.
> - FIPO's structured increase in Policy KL divergence and smooth rise in policy entropy indicate stable exploration and progressive specialization, fostering sustained growth in reasoning depth.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research paper addresses a challenge within the field of large language model (LLM) reasoning, specifically concerning the efficacy of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms in eliciting complex, multi-step chain-of-thought (CoT) behaviors. Recent advancements in LLMs, such as OpenAI's o-series, Google's Gemini series, and DeepSeek's R-series, have demonstrated enhanced reasoning capabilities through the allocation of greater computational resources during inference and large-scale reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). However, the specific algorithmic details enabling these breakthroughs, particularly how RL catalyzes the emergence of deep reasoning, remain largely proprietary.

In parallel, the open-source community has invested efforts into reproducing and scaling similar algorithms. Works like DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization) have replicated GRPO-style (Group Relative Policy Optimization) training, which is a value-network-free approach. A core limitation identified in these GRPO-style methods is their reliance on outcome-based rewards (ORM) that provide coarse-grained, uniform credit assignment across all tokens in a trajectory. This uniform reward signal has been observed to lead to a "length stagnation," limiting the depth of reasoning that models can achieve.

Some alternative approaches, such as Open-Reasoner-Zero, VC-PPO, VAPO, and T-PPO, have resorted to the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework for more granular advantage estimation. However, these PPO-based methods often require auxiliary critic models or pre-trained value models, which can introduce additional complexity or external knowledge priors, potentially confounding the evaluation of the policy optimization algorithm itself.

This work, FIPO (Future-KL Influenced Policy Optimization), positions itself by aiming to overcome the limitations of coarse-grained credit assignment *within* the efficient GRPO framework. It seeks to achieve a dense advantage formulation without the need for a critic model or reliance on pre-trained value models, thereby enhancing the reasoning depth of base models that initially exhibit no long-CoT tendencies. This approach aims to bridge the gap between the efficiency of GRPO and the performance of PPO on complex reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reinforcement learning algorithm that can effectively elicit deep and extended reasoning capabilities in large language models by addressing the limitations of coarse-grained credit assignment in existing GRPO-style training methods.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations within the current landscape of LLM reinforcement learning:

1. **Reasoning Bottlenecks from Coarse Credit Assignment**: Current GRPO-style RL algorithms, while effective for scaling, rely on outcome-based rewards (ORM). These rewards assign a uniform advantage to every token within a generated trajectory, regardless of its importance to the overall reasoning process. This "coarse-grained credit assignment" means critical logical pivots and trivial tokens are treated identically. The authors contend that this structural constraint prevents the model from identifying and reinforcing the specific steps that drive correct, complex logic.

2. **Performance Ceiling and Length Stagnation**: As a consequence of coarse credit assignment, models trained with standard GRPO-style methods exhibit a "performance ceiling." Their reasoning trajectories tend to plateau at intermediate lengths, limiting their ability to solve more difficult tasks that require extensive, multi-step logical derivations. The uniform reward signal does not sufficiently highlight the specific tokens responsible for successful long-chain reasoning, hindering the model's convergence towards such paths.

3. **Limitations of Existing Solutions**: While some approaches revert to the PPO framework to achieve granular advantage estimation, these often introduce additional complexities. PPO typically requires a separate critic model to estimate state-value functions, increasing computational overhead. Furthermore, some PPO-based methods rely on value models pre-trained on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data with long-CoT examples. The authors argue that such pre-training introduces an "external knowledge prior," making it difficult to ascertain whether performance gains are due to the policy optimization algorithm itself or inherited from the pre-trained value model.

4. **Unlocking Inherent Reasoning Potential**: The motivation includes demonstrating that the inherent reasoning potential of base models can be unlocked through improved credit assignment within an efficient RL framework, specifically GRPO, without external CoT data or complex auxiliary models.

In response to these motivations, FIPO aims to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To integrate "discounted Future-KL divergence" into the policy update mechanism of a GRPO-style algorithm.
- To formulate a "dense advantage" that re-weights tokens based on their influence on subsequent trajectory behaviors, thereby providing finer-grained credit assignment.
- To enable models, starting from a clean base model (Qwen2.5-32B-Base) without prior exposure to long-CoT synthetic data, to break through length stagnation and generate significantly longer chain-of-thought reasoning.
- To demonstrate that establishing dense advantage formulations is a viable and effective path for evolving ORM-based GRPO algorithms to unlock deep reasoning capabilities, without the need for complex critic models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FIPO (Future-KL Influenced Policy Optimization) is a reinforcement learning algorithm designed to introduce dense credit assignment into GRPO-style policy updates. The methodology is built upon analyzing token-level probability shifts and iteratively refining a "Future-KL" metric to modulate advantage functions.

The approach integrates the following key components:

1. **Probability Shift (∆ log p)**:
 The method begins by leveraging previous findings that the magnitude and direction of the probability shift (∆ log p) during RLVR updates serve as indicators of improved reasoning. This token-level shift is defined as:
 `∆ log p_t = log π_θ(o_t | q, o_<t) - log π_θ_old(o_t | q, o_<t)`
 where `π_θ` is the current policy and `π_θ_old` is the old policy. A positive `∆ log p_t` indicates increased likelihood of token `o_t`, suggesting reinforcement, while a negative value indicates suppression. This term is considered the atomic unit for credit assignment, capturing the instantaneous policy drift.

2. **Future-KL Estimation**:
 To capture the causal influence of a token `o_t` on its subsequent trajectory, FIPO introduces `FutureKL_t`, initially defined as the cumulative signed probability shift from the current step `t` to the end of the sequence `T`:
 `FutureKL_t = Σ_{k=t to T} ∆ log p_k`
 This sum provides a sample-based estimate of the KL divergence restricted to the future horizon, quantifying the cumulative deviation of the current policy from the reference policy for the remainder of the trajectory.

 To address observed training instability arising from highly volatile importance ratios, two refinements are applied:
 - **Extreme Value Filtering (Masking)**: A binary filter `M_k` is introduced to mask tokens whose importance ratio exceeds a Dual-Clip threshold `c` (typically `c ≥ 10`). This prevents excessively high importance ratios from propagating into the recursive sum, thereby mitigating gradient explosion. The refined formulation becomes:
 `FutureKL_t = Σ_{k=t to T} M_k · ∆ log p_k`
 - **Soft Decay Window**: To account for the diminishing causal dependency of `o_t` on distant future tokens, a discount factor `γ ∈ (0, 1]` is applied. `γ` is parameterized as `γ = 2^(-1/τ)`, where `τ` controls the effective horizon or "half-life" of the future supervision. This focuses credit assignment on the more immediate and certain parts of the reasoning chain. The final Future-KL formulation is:
 `FutureKL_t = Σ_{k=t to T} M_k · γ^(k-t) · ∆ log p_k`

3. **Future-KL Re-weighted Advantage with Clipping**:
 The calculated `FutureKL_t` is used to modulate the standard group relative advantage `A_hat_t` (derived from GRPO) into a modified advantage `A_tilde_t`. This modulation involves two steps:
 - **Exponential Mapping**: `f_t = exp(FutureKL_t)` transforms the log-space sum into a multiplicative importance weight, reflecting the policy's preference for the generated future.
 - **Influence Weight Clipping**: The weight `f_t` is constrained to `[1 - ϵ_flow, 1 + ϵ_fhigh]` to prevent excessive variance in gradient estimates.
 The modified advantage is then calculated as `A_tilde_t = A_hat_t · f_t`. Functionally, `f_t > 1` magnifies updates for successful future trajectories, while `f_t < 1` attenuates them for less favored ones. For stability, `f_t` is reset to 1 for tokens with negative advantages (`A_hat_t < 0`) that also have excessively large importance ratios.

4. **Target Loss (FIPO Objective)**:
 The re-weighted advantage `A_tilde_t` is integrated into a policy optimization objective similar to DAPO, which uses asymmetric clipping and a token-level policy gradient loss. The FIPO objective function is:
 `J_FIPO(θ) = E_[(q,a)∼D, {o_i}∼π_θ_old] [ (1 / Σ |o_i|) Σ_{i=1 to G} Σ_{t=1 to |o_i|} min(r_i,t * f_i,t * A_hat_i,t, clip(r_i,t, 1 - ϵ, 1 + ϵ) * f_i,t * A_hat_i,t) ]`
 where `r_i,t` is the importance ratio and `G` is the number of sampled outputs.

**Experimental Settings**:
- **Base Model**: Qwen2.5-32B-Base, selected for its lack of prior exposure to long-CoT synthetic data, ensuring the emergence of reasoning is purely RL-driven.
- **Baseline**: DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization).
- **Training Framework**: VeRL (Sheng et al., 2025) and DAPO codebase.
- **Dataset**: Publicly released DAPO-17K dataset, focused on mathematical reasoning.
- **Hyperparameters**: Global batch size of 512, 16 responses per prompt. Learning rate `1e-6`. For FIPO, `τ=32` and `Future-KL` weight clipped within `[1, 1.2]` for the 32B model. Maximum response length 20,480 tokens with an overlong penalty. Mini-batch size was increased from DAPO's 32 to 64 for enhanced training stability.
- **Evaluation**: AIME 2024 and AIME 2025 benchmarks, reporting Pass@1 (average over 32 samples), Cons@32 (majority vote), and Pass@32 (at least one correct answer). Inference uses temperature 1.0 and top-p 0.7.
- **Computational Optimization**: A chunk-based memory-efficient algorithm was implemented for `Future-KL` calculation to manage the `O(L^2)` memory complexity for long trajectories, making training feasible on modern GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using FIPO on the Qwen2.5-32B-Base model demonstrated several findings regarding performance, reasoning length, and training dynamics.

**1. Performance on Reasoning Benchmarks:**
- **AIME 2024**: FIPO achieved a Pass@1 accuracy of 56.0% (converging from a peak of 58.0%), which represents a 6.0% improvement over the DAPO baseline (50.0%). This also surpassed DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Math-32B (~47.0%) and matched o1-mini (~56.0%).
- **AIME 2025**: FIPO similarly improved Pass@1 accuracy to 43.0% compared to DAPO's 38.0%.
- **Consistency (Cons@32)**: FIPO improved consistency on AIME 2024 to 73.0% from DAPO's 60.0%.
- **Coverage (Pass@32)**: Modest improvements were observed, with FIPO achieving 83.0% on AIME 2024 compared to DAPO's 80.0%. The authors attributed the limited gains in Pass@32 to the inherent challenge of expanding the absolute problem-solving scope of LLMs solely through reinforcement learning.

**2. Overcoming Length Stagnation and Eliciting Deep Reasoning:**
- FIPO enabled models to overcome the "length stagnation" observed in baselines. The average chain-of-thought length extended from approximately 4,000 tokens (where DAPO plateaued) to over 10,000 tokens.
- This expansion was observed across all length-related percentiles (Min, Q25, Mean, Median, Q75), indicating a fundamental shift in the model's problem-solving strategy rather than isolated outlier generations.
- A strong positive correlation was identified between increased response length and improved model accuracy across different training stages. This suggests that FIPO effectively translates extended sequence length into genuine reasoning depth.
- Qualitative analysis revealed an evolution of reasoning behavior:
 - Initial Stage: "Superficial planning" with template-like outlines.
 - DAPO Convergence/Early FIPO: "Linear execution" with single-pass CoT.
 - Intermediate FIPO: "Emergent self-reflection," where the model proactively verifies results using alternative methodologies.
 - Late FIPO: "Systematic deep reasoning," characterized by compute-heavy strategies, multi-pass symbolic re-derivation, and granular arithmetic verification. This aligns with inference-time scaling in advanced proprietary models.

**3. Training Dynamics and Stability:**
- **Rewards vs. Advantages**: While DAPO maintained higher mean raw training rewards (partially due to FIPO incurring higher overlong penalties for its extended responses), FIPO exhibited a consistent upward trajectory in "response length weighted mean relative advantage." This indicated that positive samples under FIPO were becoming significantly more substantive, fostering sustained growth in reasoning.
- **Policy Drift and Exploration**: FIPO showed a steady and structured increase in Policy KL divergence, signifying a progressive and specialized policy shift. Concurrently, FIPO maintained a smooth and sustained rise in policy entropy, indicating continuous and stable exploration of a broader reasoning space where longer CoT paths increasingly correspond to correct solutions. In contrast, DAPO exhibited volatile fluctuations in Policy KL and noisy oscillations in entropy.
- **Gradient Stability**: FIPO’s Gradient Norm remained low and consistent throughout training, suggesting fine-grained and numerically stable updates. DAPO, however, displayed highly volatile fluctuations and frequent spikes in its gradient norm, indicating a less stable search process.

**4. Ablation Studies (Qwen2.5-7B-Math Model)**:
- **Extreme Value Filtering**: The filtering mechanism for extreme importance sampling ratios was crucial for training stability and optimal performance. Without filtering, higher clipping fractions and suboptimal performance were observed, especially for the 7B model.
- **Influence Weight Clipping**: For the 7B model, a balanced clipping range (`[0.8, 1.2]`) for the influence weight was more effective than a one-sided range (`[1.0, 1.2]`). The latter, while encouraging exploration, led to sustained high entropy and degraded performance for the smaller model, suggesting it benefits more from converging to a specific, high-confidence reasoning manifold rather than aggressive broad exploration.
- **Effective Horizon of Decay Rate (τ)**: An intermediate value of `τ=32` provided the best balance. Smaller `τ` (e.g., 8) led to myopic guidance and premature entropy collapse, while larger `τ` (e.g., 256) introduced excessive volatility and higher entropy, which the 7B model struggled to accommodate for optimal performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FIPO carries several significances and potential impacts on the field of large language model reasoning:

1. **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for LLMs**: FIPO offers a method to address the long-standing challenge of coarse-grained credit assignment in outcome-based reward (ORM) reinforcement learning, which is a known limitation for inducing complex reasoning behaviors in LLMs. By providing dense, token-level supervision through Future-KL divergence, FIPO refines how models learn from sparse terminal rewards.

2. **Unlocking Inherent Reasoning Capabilities**: A key impact is the demonstration that sophisticated reasoning, including spontaneous self-reflection and systematic verification, can be elicited from clean base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B-Base) that have no prior exposure to long chain-of-thought (CoT) synthetic data. This suggests that the capacity for deep reasoning can be fostered directly through algorithmic improvements in RL rather than solely relying on pre-training with carefully curated CoT datasets or external knowledge.

3. **Enhancing GRPO-Style Algorithms**: FIPO demonstrates that a dense advantage formulation, typically associated with PPO and its reliance on value networks, can be effectively realized within the more efficient, value-network-free GRPO framework. This challenges the prevailing assumption that complex critic models are indispensable for granular credit assignment, thereby broadening the applicability and potential performance ceiling of GRPO-style algorithms for complex tasks.

4. **Breaking Through Reasoning Length Stagnation**: The ability of FIPO to significantly extend the average chain-of-thought length from approximately 4,000 to over 10,000 tokens is critical. This breakthrough in length stagnation directly enables models to tackle more complex mathematical reasoning tasks, as evidenced by the performance gains on AIME benchmarks. Longer, higher-quality reasoning chains can lead to more robust and accurate solutions.

5. **Improved Performance on Challenging Benchmarks**: The empirical results on AIME 2024 and AIME 2025 demonstrate practical improvements in LLM reasoning capabilities, outperforming strong baselines like DAPO and competitive proprietary models such as DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Math-32B and o1-mini in specific contexts.

6. **Open-Source Contribution**: The commitment to open-sourcing the complete training system (built on the `verl` framework) and configuration recipes is a significant contribution. It provides the research community with transparent, reproducible, and scalable tools to further investigate and advance large-scale RL for LLMs, accelerating future research and development.

7. **Future Research Directions**: The work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - **Efficiency Optimization**: Exploring methods to make the elicited long reasoning paths more computationally efficient for deployment.
 - **Task Generalization**: Validating FIPO's effectiveness and the elicited reasoning behaviors across a broader range of open-ended or less structured domains beyond mathematics.
 - **Data and Model Scaling**: Investigating FIPO's scalability on larger-scale and more diverse datasets, as well as its efficacy when applied to models that have already undergone supervised fine-tuning or distillation with long-CoT data.
 - **Performance Gap Analysis**: Further understanding the persistent performance gap between self-trained RL models and models benefiting from direct distillation from larger teacher models.

Overall, FIPO represents a methodical step forward in harnessing reinforcement learning to develop more capable and robust reasoning abilities in large language models by addressing a fundamental limitation in credit assignment.
