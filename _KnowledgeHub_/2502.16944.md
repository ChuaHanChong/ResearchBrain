# Pretrain Value, Not Reward: Decoupled Value Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Decoupled Value Policy Optimization (DVPO) streamlines reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) for large language models by pretraining a global value model offline, which then guides policy optimization without further updates. This method matches or exceeds the performance of traditional RLHF approaches while reducing GPU memory usage by 30-40% and training time by 30-45%.

## Problem

- Traditional RLHF pipelines suffer from instability due to online critic training (critic drift) and high computational demands from concurrently loaded models.
- Existing methods often lack fine-grained token-level credit assignment, relying on sentence-level rewards that can lead to high variance and limit precise preference learning.
- The two-stage process of training a reward model and then a value model from fixed human preferences is informationally redundant and complex when no new rewards are introduced.

## Method

- Introduce Decoupled Value Policy Optimization (**DVPO**), which pretrains a ==Global Value Model== (GVM) offline to function as a universal, fixed critic.
- Train the GVM using Temporal Difference (TD) learning to capture policy-conditioned action-value functions across diverse trajectories and freeze its parameters.
- Optimize the policy using a modified PPO objective, leveraging the fixed GVM for stable advantage estimates, thereby decoupling actor and critic learning dynamics.

## Results

- DVPO achieved performance on par with or superior to PPO, DPO, ReMax, and GRPO on various LLM benchmarks (e.g., MT-Bench, Alpaca-Eval, Arena-Hard) for both base and instruction-tuned models.
- The method reduced GPU memory usage by 30-**40%** and training time by 30-**45%** compared to PPO, along with faster convergence rates (e.g., **75%** fewer interaction steps).
- The pre-trained GVM provided more robust and generalizable value estimates, outperforming PPO's online critic, especially under distribution shifts, and enabled finer-grained token-level feedback for policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretraining a value model directly from a fixed human preference dataset is informationally equivalent to the conventional reward model-critic training pipeline.
> - Decoupling value learning from policy optimization mitigates critic drift, enhancing the stability and predictability of policy updates.
> - A policy-agnostic, pre-trained Global Value Model can provide more accurate, finer-grained (token-level) and generalizable supervision than dynamically trained critics or models relying solely on sentence-level rewards.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The alignment of large language models (LLMs) with human preferences is a significant challenge in natural language processing. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has become a standard methodology for shaping LLM behavior, enabling models to be more helpful, accurate, and safe. Traditional RLHF frameworks, such as those based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), typically involve a multi-stage process. Initially, a reward model (RM) is trained using human preference data to provide a scalar reward for generated text. Subsequently, this reward model is used to supervise either an online-trained critic (value function) in an actor-critic setup or to indirectly estimate values through extensive trajectory sampling, as seen in methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), ReMax, and GRPO.

The existing RLHF paradigms, particularly those employing actor-critic methods, often encounter several challenges. Online critic training can lead to instability due to critic drift, where the value function shifts during joint training with the policy. Furthermore, these methods are computationally intensive, often requiring the simultaneous loading of multiple models (policy, critic, reference, and reward models), which increases GPU memory consumption and training time. Sampling-based methods, while often more efficient in terms of memory, may suffer from high variance and typically discard token-level credit assignment, instead providing only sentence-level rewards. This limitation can hinder the policy's ability to learn fine-grained preferences.

This work positions itself within this landscape by proposing an alternative approach that addresses these computational and stability issues. It challenges the conventional two-stage process (reward modeling followed by online value estimation) by positing that, in scenarios where no new ground-truth rewards are introduced during policy training, directly pretraining a value model is informationally equivalent and can be more stable and efficient. The paper seeks to simplify the RLHF pipeline by decoupling value learning from policy optimization, thereby offering a more streamlined and robust method for aligning LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to simplify and stabilize the reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) process for large language models (LLMs) by directly pretraining a value model rather than first training a reward model and then deriving value estimates online. This approach aims to reduce the computational complexity and instability often associated with current RLHF methodologies.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several key observations and challenges in the existing RLHF landscape:

- **Informational Redundancy in Value Estimation:** In standard RLHF, once human preference data is collected, no new ground-truth reward signals become available during the subsequent policy optimization phase. The current pipeline typically involves two steps: first, training a reward model (RM) from this fixed preference data, and second, using this RM to either train an online critic or estimate values via trajectory sampling. The authors argue that this two-stage process is informationally redundant. If the reward signal is fixed, then learning a value model online from a pretrained reward model adds no fundamentally new information compared to directly pretraining a value model from the same initial preference data. The process of training a reward model and then deriving a value model from it is presented as informationally equivalent to directly pretraining a value model.
- **Addressing Critic Drift and Instability:** Traditional actor-critic methods in RLHF, such as PPO, train the policy (actor) and value function (critic) simultaneously. This joint training can lead to critic drift, where the value function becomes unstable due to its interdependence with the evolving policy, making policy updates less reliable. By decoupling value learning and freezing the value model, the authors aim to eliminate this "moving target" problem, leading to more stable and predictable policy updates.
- **Mitigating Computational Overhead:** The concurrent training of multiple models (policy, critic, reference model, and reward model) in methods like PPO significantly increases computational resource consumption, particularly GPU memory and training time. The proposed approach seeks to reduce this overhead by requiring only a single, offline-pretrained value model (Global Value Model - GVM) to guide policy optimization, thereby simplifying the training setup and reducing the number of active models during the RL phase.
- **Enabling Fine-Grained Credit Assignment:** Sampling-based methods like DPO often operate on sentence-level rewards, discarding the ability to assign credit at the token level. This can result in high variance and instability during training, as all tokens are treated as equally contributing to the overall score. The motivation is to provide a more nuanced, token-level supervision signal through a pretrained value model, allowing the policy to learn finer-grained preferences and achieve more effective training.
- **Exploiting Policy-Invariance of Rewards:** In open-ended language generation tasks, the intrinsic quality or "return" of a generated response (e.g., its correctness or alignment with preferences) is often largely independent of the specific policy that produced it. This "policy-invariance" suggests that a value function could be amortized into a single, global model, pretrained once on diverse trajectories and then reused across different policies.

In summary, the core objective is to move towards a "policy-only optimization" framework guided by a single, stable, and offline-pretrained value model, thereby offering a more efficient and robust alternative to existing RLHF pipelines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Decoupled Value Policy Optimization (DVPO), comprises two main stages: training a Global Value Model (GVM) offline and then using this fixed GVM to guide policy optimization.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting

The task of sequence generation in natural language processing is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). A response `y` consists of `T` tokens `[y_1..., y_T]`. Given a prompt `x` and a prefix `y_<t>`, the language model predicts the next token `π_θ(· | [x, y_<t>])`. The state `s_t` is defined as `[x, y_<t>]`, and the action `a_t` is `y_t`. Human feedback provides a sentence-level ground-truth reward `r(x, y)`. This sentence-level reward is transformed into token-level rewards `r(s_t, a_t)` by assigning 0 to intermediate steps and the full sentence reward `r(x, y)` to the final step, leading to a cumulative return `G_t = γ^(T-t) * r(x, y)`. DVPO relies on an offline dataset `D` of `(τ_i, s_i_t, a_i_t, G_i_t)` tuples, representing state-action trajectories, returns, and policy behaviors, with no new ground-truth rewards collected during training.

#### 3.2. Training a Global Value Model (GVM)

The initial stage involves learning a policy-conditioned action-value function `Q_ϕ(τ, s, a)` from the offline dataset `D`. This GVM is designed to be universal and generalize across different policies, avoiding the need for online adaptation or iterative re-learning. Instead of conditioning on explicit policy parameters, the GVM is conditioned on trajectories `τ` sampled from various policies. These trajectories implicitly capture policy traits such as stylistic tendencies or domain expertise.

The GVM, `Q_ϕ(τ, s, a)`, is trained using Temporal Difference (TD) learning. The target return `G_t` for a given state-action pair `(s_t, a_t)` is calculated as `G_t = r(s_t, a_t) + γQ_ϕ(τ, s_t+1, a_t+1)`. The training objective `L_GVM(ϕ)` minimizes the squared difference between the predicted `Q_ϕ(τ, s_t, a_t)` and this TD target, ensuring the value function accurately reflects both immediate and future rewards. The GVM can be trained from returns derived from either existing reward models or directly from pairwise preference data (e.g., assigning +1 to a preferred response and -1 to a rejected response). Once trained, the GVM's parameters are frozen.

#### 3.3. Decoupled-Value Policy Optimization

After the GVM is trained and its parameters `ϕ` are fixed, it serves as a universal critic to guide policy updates. This decouples the learning dynamics of the actor (policy) and critic (value model).

The policy `π_θ(a | s)` is optimized using a clipped Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) objective:
`L_PPO(θ) = E[min(r_t(θ) * A_hat_t, clip(r_t(θ), 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ) * A_hat_t)]`,
where `r_t(θ)` is the importance sampling ratio `π_θ(a_t|s_t) / π_θ_old(a_t|s_t)`, and `ϵ` is a clipping parameter.

The advantage function `A_hat_t` is crucial for policy updates and is defined using the fixed GVM: `A_hat_t = Q_ϕ(τ, s_t, a_t)`. This static advantage definition provides a stable signal for policy improvement without requiring dynamic value updates. This decoupled approach eliminates the "moving target" problem and allows for efficient reuse of the offline dataset, as no new environment rewards are collected.

#### 3.4. Equivalence of Reward-Critic Training and Value Pretraining

The paper provides a theoretical argument for the informational equivalence between the conventional reward model (RM) + online critic training pipeline and the direct pretraining of a value model (GVM), given a fixed preference dataset and no new reward signals during training.

- **Preliminaries:** An RM `R_ϕ(s, a)` approximates the true reward `r(s, a)` with error `ϵ_R`, and a GVM `Q_ψ(s, a)` approximates the true action-value function `Q_π(s, a)` with error `ϵ_Q`.
- **Equivalence Lemma:** The core claim is formalized by Lemma 3.1. It states that if `R_ϕ` and `Q_ψ` both approximate the ground-truth reward/value functions within bounded errors (`ϵ_R`, `ϵ_Q`), and no new rewards are observed during training, then the induced policy gradients from a reward-derived value function (`∇_θ J_R(π_θ)`) and from a directly pretrained global value model (`∇_θ J_Q(π_θ)`) are approximately equivalent. The difference between these gradients `∥∇_θ J_R(π_θ) - ∇_θ J_Q(π_θ)∥` is bounded by `κ(ϵ_R, ϵ_Q)`, which approaches zero as `ϵ_R` and `ϵ_Q` approach zero.
- **Convergence Corollary:** This equivalence implies that if policy updates are regularized (e.g., with KL-clipping as in PPO) and the GVM's approximation error is bounded, DVPO inherits the same monotonic improvement guarantees as PPO. This suggests that a fixed, pretrained GVM can guide policy learning effectively without introducing the practical issues of critic drift.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations of DVPO were conducted across two primary settings: a Base setting (fine-tuning SFT from a base model) and an Instruction setting (fine-tuning from a pre-aligned instruction-tuned model). Baselines included SFT, PPO, ReMax, GRPO, and DPO. Evaluation metrics comprised MT-Bench, Arena-Hard, Alpaca-Eval, and GPT-4o-based win rates on a held-out test set.

#### 4.1. Performance in Base Setting

In the Base setting, using LLaMA3-3B-Base and LLaMA3-8B-Base models, DVPO consistently demonstrated superior optimization performance compared to SFT and achieved results comparable to or slightly exceeding standard PPO, ReMax, and GRPO. For instance, with LLaMA3-8B, DVPO improved MT-Bench scores by 0.2, Arena-Hard by 1.5, and Alpaca-Eval by 1.74 points relative to SFT. For LLaMA3-3B, improvements were even more pronounced, with a 0.51 increase on MT-Bench, a 5-point increase on Arena-Hard, and a 4.14-point increase on Alpaca-Eval. The GPT-4o-judged win rates on the held-out test set further confirmed DVPO's advantage over SFT. These results indicate that the pre-trained GVM provides a sufficiently robust and high-quality supervision signal to guide policy optimization, leading to performance levels competitive with complex actor-critic frameworks.

#### 4.2. Performance in Instruction Setting

When applied to pre-aligned models (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 and Llama3-8B-Instruct), DVPO significantly enhanced performance across all benchmarks. For Mistral-7B-Instruct, DVPO achieved a 0.19 improvement on MT-Bench, a 12.1% increase in win rate on Arena-Hard, and a 10.32% improvement on Alpaca-Eval compared to the original instruction-tuned model. It also outperformed DPO, ReMax, GRPO, and PPO, demonstrating its effectiveness in scenarios where a strong base model is further aligned. This suggests that the GVM can effectively and reliably guide the optimization of already capable policy models.

#### 4.3. Role of Finer-Grained Feedback Signals

The study found that the finer-grained token-level supervision provided by the GVM contributed to superior performance compared to reward-only methods (e.g., ReMax, GRPO) that provide only sentence-level feedback. GVM, trained with TD-learning over prefixes, assigns distinct values to different parts of a response, allowing the policy model to learn more precise preferences. Decisive reasoning tokens receive high values, while misleading continuations receive low values, which is illustrated with a case study in the appendix (Figure 3).

#### 4.4. GVM Evaluation and Comparison

- **GVM vs. ScalarRM:** On the RewardBench, GVM's aggregated token-level values, when converted to response-level rewards, showed comparable overall performance to a Scalar Reward Model (ScalarRM) trained on the same data. However, GVM demonstrated better generalization on "Chat-Hard" subsets, whereas ScalarRM performed better on "Chat (easy)" subsets. This indicates that GVM's TD-learned token-level value estimation provides more accurate supervision for complex problems.
- **GVM vs. Actor-Critic Value Model:** GVM significantly outperformed the value model (critic) from a PPO actor-critic setup on in-distribution data (Ultrafeedback testset) and exhibited greater robustness under distribution shifts (HH-RLHF dataset). This suggests that GVM's policy-agnostic pretraining on large-scale preferences leads to more accurate and generalizable value estimates compared to a critic that is tightly coupled to and adapts with an evolving policy.

#### 4.5. Computational Efficiency

DVPO demonstrated notable improvements in computational efficiency. Compared to standard PPO, DVPO reduced GPU memory usage by approximately 30-40% and training time by 30-45%. For example, with LLaMA3-3B, DVPO reduced memory from 41.43 GB to 27.5 GB and time per step from 23.00s to 13.00s (a 1.76x speedup). This efficiency gain is attributed to DVPO only needing to load and activate the policy model during the RL phase, with the GVM being frozen. DVPO also converged faster, requiring approximately 75% of the interaction steps compared to PPO (e.g., LLaMA3-8B DVPO peaked at 800 steps vs. PPO at 1250 steps), which is attributed to the warm-starting effect of the pre-trained GVM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Decoupled Value Policy Optimization (DVPO) carries several implications for the field of large language model (LLM) alignment through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

- **Simplification and Stabilization of RLHF:** DVPO offers a streamlined and potentially more stable alternative to conventional RLHF pipelines. By demonstrating that pretraining a global value model (GVM) is informationally equivalent to the reward model-critic training paradigm when no new reward signals are available, the framework removes the need for iterative, online critic updates. This decoupling addresses issues such as critic drift and the "moving target" problem, which contribute to instability in actor-critic methods, thus potentially making RLHF more robust and reliable.

- **Improved Computational Efficiency:** A significant impact of DVPO is its substantial reduction in computational resource requirements. The empirical results indicate 30-40% less GPU memory usage and 30-45% faster training times compared to PPO. This efficiency gain, stemming from activating only the policy model during the RL phase and utilizing a frozen GVM, makes advanced LLM alignment techniques more accessible. Lower computational costs can broaden the participation in RLHF research and development, allowing for experimentation with larger models or datasets, or simply reducing the barrier to entry for smaller research groups.

- **Competitive or Superior Performance:** Despite its simplified architecture and reduced computational footprint, DVPO demonstrated performance on par with or surpassing state-of-the-art RLHF methods across multiple benchmarks (MT-Bench, Alpaca-Eval, Arena-Hard). This indicates that the proposed decoupled approach does not compromise model quality and can effectively guide LLMs to align with human preferences. The finer-grained, token-level feedback provided by the GVM, as opposed to sentence-level rewards, is highlighted as a factor contributing to these performance gains.

- **Reconceptualization of RLHF:** The work suggests a reframing of RLHF as primarily a policy-only optimization problem, guided by a single, pretrained value model. This shift in perspective could influence future research directions, encouraging exploration of more sophisticated offline value learning techniques and reducing the emphasis on complex, coupled actor-critic dynamics in language model alignment.

- **Generalizability of Value Models:** The finding that a policy-agnostic GVM outperforms an online-trained actor-critic value model, particularly under distribution shifts, suggests that carefully pretrained value models can offer more robust and generalizable estimates of response quality. This has implications for developing more universal "critics" that can be applied across various policies or tasks.

- **Limitations and Future Work:** While promising, the paper also acknowledges limitations. DVPO assumes adequate coverage in the offline preference data and that the GVM can approximate token-level returns within bounded error. Furthermore, its guidance is fixed; thus, it may not adapt optimally in highly non-stationary settings or if new human/environment feedback becomes available during training. The authors propose extending DVPO to a semi-online regime by periodically refreshing the GVM with new data as a direction for future work. Additionally, like all RLHF methods, DVPO inherits potential biases from the underlying reward-preference data, which remains an open challenge.

In conclusion, DVPO represents a substantial contribution to efficient and stable LLM alignment, offering a practical and theoretically supported method that could simplify and accelerate the development of more helpful and harmless AI systems.
