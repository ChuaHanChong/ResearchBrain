# Co-Evolving Latent Action World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Co-Evolving Latent Action World Models (CoLA-World) introduces a unified framework that jointly trains latent action models with pre-trained video-generation world models. The method addresses representational collapse through a warm-up phase, enabling a synergistic co-evolution that improves both latent action quality and world model prediction performance.

## Problem

- Existing two-stage pipelines for integrating Latent Action Models (LAMs) with video generative world models suffer from redundant training efforts, as both the LAM's Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) and the world model perform next-observation prediction.
- The latent action space learned in the first stage of prior methods is static, preventing it from adapting or refining as the world model gains deeper understanding, thereby limiting the world model's ultimate performance.
- Direct joint training of a freshly initialized LAM with a powerful pre-trained world model is unstable and leads to representational collapse in the latent action space, where the world model disregards uninformative latent action signals.

## Method

- **CoLA-World** proposes a unified, ==one-stage training pipeline== that directly co-evolves a ==Latent Action Model (LAM)== alongside a powerful, pre-trained ==OpenSora-based== world model.
- A critical ==warm-up phase== is introduced where the pre-trained world model is frozen, allowing only the LAM to update. The world model acts as a "knowledgeable tutor," guiding the LAM's initial learning and preventing ==representational collapse==.
- Following warm-up, the entire system (LAM and world model) is unfrozen and trained ==end-to-end==, enabling a ==synergistic co-evolution== where both components mutually reinforce each other's representations and improve concurrently.

## Results

- CoLA-World successfully prevents representational collapse, maintaining a healthy and diverse latent action codebook throughout training and adaptation, unlike prior two-stage methods.
- The framework consistently matches or surpasses two-stage baselines in video prediction quality across various metrics (e.g., FVD) and datasets, demonstrating superior sample efficiency.
- CoLA-World shows enhanced robustness and adaptability to real-action interfaces and out-of-distribution environments, leading to higher success rates in visual planning tasks like "Upright Block" on RoboDesk.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct joint training of a freshly initialized latent action model with a pre-trained video generative world model is unstable and leads to representational collapse of the latent action space.
> - A dedicated warm-up phase, where the world model acts as a static tutor to guide the latent action model's initial learning, is crucial for stabilizing the training process and aligning latent action representations.
> - A synergistic co-evolution, where both the world model and latent action model refine each other, results in a more robust and higher-performing system than sequential, decoupled training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist artificial intelligence agents capable of operating across diverse environments and embodiments is a prominent objective in the field of AI. A core component of this vision is the "world model," an internal simulator that enables an agent to plan and learn through imagination. Recent advancements in large-scale video generative models, such as those based on diffusion models, have presented them as candidates for universal world models due to their capacity to capture complex world physics and dynamics from extensive pretrained knowledge.

However, a fundamental challenge persists in adapting these generative models into *controllable* world models: the inherent heterogeneity of action spaces across different domains. Real actions, ranging from continuous robotic torques to discrete game inputs, make direct fine-tuning of a video generative model for universal control impractical.

Latent Action Models (LAMs) have emerged as a method to address this challenge. LAMs infer abstract actions directly from visual observations, providing a unified, embodiment-agnostic interface for controlling a world model. This approach facilitates the pretraining of general-purpose world models conditioned on a universal latent action space. Existing methodologies for integrating LAMs with world models typically employ a two-stage paradigm: first, a LAM (comprising an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM)) is trained on action-free videos. Subsequently, the IDM is frozen to provide latent action labels for training a separate, often higher-capacity, world model.

This two-stage approach introduces several issues. Primarily, both the FDM within the LAM and the independently trained world model perform the redundant task of next-observation prediction. Secondly, the world model is constrained to a fixed latent action space determined by the initially trained LAM, preventing co-adaptation and refinement of latent actions as the world model improves.

This work, CoLA-World, directly addresses these limitations by proposing a synergistic, one-stage joint training paradigm. It challenges the conventional two-stage approach by attempting to replace the FDM with the more powerful world model and train them concurrently. While conceptually appealing, prior attempts at such joint training have encountered representational collapse. CoLA-World therefore aims to overcome this instability, establishing a new framework that allows latent action learning and world modeling to co-evolve.

Within the broader landscape of related work, this research differentiates itself from prior latent action learning methods (e.g., FICC, LAPO, Genie, UniSkill, AC-LAM, MVP-LAM) by leveraging a pretrained video generation model to co-evolve latent action learning and world modeling rather than treating the generative model as an auxiliary tool or training from scratch with simpler architectures. It also extends beyond latent-action-based world models (e.g., AdaWorld, Motus, AD3, PreLAR, LAWM) that typically maintain a decoupled latent action learning phase or rely on fixed latent action labels. Furthermore, while other works focus on finetuning pretrained video generation models as world models by adding action conditioning (e.g., AVID, IRASim, DWS, Vid2World, EnerVerse-AC), most assume a pre-specified action space, whereas CoLA-World introduces controllability through learnable, co-evolving latent actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to successfully implement, for the first time, a synergistic joint training framework for a latent action model (LAM) and a pretrained video-generation-based world model. This framework, termed CoLA-World, seeks to overcome the challenges associated with directly integrating these components, particularly the issue of representational collapse observed in naive joint training attempts.

The motivation behind pursuing this objective is multi-faceted:

1. **Elimination of Redundancy:** Current two-stage paradigms involve training a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) as part of the LAM and then a separate, often larger, world model to perform next-observation prediction. This constitutes a redundant learning effort, as both models aim to simulate future states. Joint training seeks to consolidate this into a single, efficient process where the world model itself serves as the dynamics predictor.
2. **Enabling Co-adaptation:** In two-stage approaches, the latent action space is fixed after the initial LAM training, preventing it from adapting as the world model develops. This static nature can bottleneck the world model's potential. The motivation is to allow the latent action space and the world model to co-evolve, enabling the latent actions to become more informative and the world model to develop a more precise understanding of how these actions translate into environmental changes.
3. **Improved Model Design and Training Efficiency:** A unified, end-to-end framework is conceptually simpler and promises greater training efficiency by avoiding redundant training phases and allowing for integrated optimization.
4. **Leveraging Pretrained Knowledge:** The research is motivated by the potential of powerful, large-scale video generative models (like OpenSora) to serve as backbones for world models. Joint training aims to harness the rich prior knowledge embedded in these pretrained models while making them interactively controllable through learned latent actions.
5. **Resolving Instability of Joint Training:** Initial experiments revealed that naively training the IDM and a pretrained world model together leads to representational collapse in the latent action space. A key motivation is to identify and resolve the underlying cause of this fragility, thereby enabling stable and effective joint learning.
6. **Establishing a Virtuous Cycle:** The core hypothesis is that a powerful, evolving world model can act as a "knowledgeable tutor," providing richer gradients to guide the LAM towards higher-quality latent actions. Concurrently, a continuously improving LAM offers a more precise and adaptable control interface to the world model. This creates a mutually reinforcing "co-evolution cycle" that drives overall system performance.
7. **Enhancing Performance and Adaptability:** The ultimate motivation is to achieve superior performance in both video simulation quality and downstream applications such as visual planning, as well as improved adaptability to diverse real-action control interfaces, compared to existing two-stage methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of CoLA-World centers on replacing the Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) in a Latent Action Model (LAM) with a powerful, pretrained video generation model, which then functions as the world model, and training these components jointly in an end-to-end fashion. This approach aims to address the limitations of the conventional two-stage pipeline.

**Core Idea and Initial Challenge:**
The conceptual shift involves using the world model (WM) directly as the FDM, allowing gradients from the world model's prediction objective to flow back to the Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) and its associated latent action representation. However, preliminary experiments demonstrated that a direct, naive joint training of a freshly initialized IDM with a pretrained world model leads to representational collapse. Specifically, the Vector Quantization (VQ) codebook for latent actions exhibited zero utilization and entropy, with one code dominating, indicating a failure to learn diverse or informative actions. This collapse was observed even when the IDM was initialized from a pretrained state. The authors hypothesized this fragility arises from a misalignment between the from-scratch IDM and the highly performant pretrained world model, where the latter quickly disregards uninformative action signals.

**Proposed Solution: CoLA-World with Warm-up Phase:**
To mitigate this instability, CoLA-World introduces a critical **warm-up phase** before engaging in full joint training.
1. **Warm-up Phase:** During this initial phase, the pretrained OpenSora world model is kept frozen. The system's loss (flow matching loss, detailed below) is backpropagated only through the action conditioning modules and the LAM components (IDM and VQ quantizer). This allows the IDM to "catch up" and align its representations with the frozen, powerful world model, guided by its structured gradients. Figure 3 illustrates that sufficient warm-up steps lead to stable and healthy codebook metrics (utilization, max usage, entropy), preventing subsequent collapse.
2. **Joint Training (Co-evolution) Phase:** Following the warm-up, the OpenSora world model is unfrozen, and all components of the system (IDM, VQ quantizer, action conditioning modules, and the OpenSora backbone) are updated simultaneously via end-to-end gradient flow. This phase facilitates the synergistic co-evolution, where the improving world model provides better gradients to the LAM, and the improving LAM offers a more precise control interface to the world model.

**Model Instantiation Details:**
- **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):** Implemented as a 12-layer Spatial-Temporal Transformer (ST-Transformer) with 768 hidden dimensions and 12 attention heads. It takes a video clip ($o_t, o_{t+1}$) as input and predicts latent action $z_t$.
- **Latent Action Representation:** The output of the IDM is subjected to Vector Quantization (VQ) to produce discrete latent actions. These are represented as two 32-dimensional action tokens chosen from a codebook of 32 entries, resulting in 1024 possible latent action choices. A bottleneck is applied to this space to enforce compact encoding of meaningful changes. VQ loss and commitment loss are included to regularize the IDM and codebook.
- **World Model (WM):** The backbone is a pretrained OpenSora model (v1.2) with approximately 1.2 billion parameters. The original temporal transformer blocks in OpenSora are modified with causal masks to prevent future information leakage, essential for dynamics modeling.
- **Latent Action Conditioning:** Latent actions extracted by the IDM are integrated into the OpenSora model using Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN). A new, from-scratch self-attention network processes the sequence of latent actions to produce contextualized embeddings. These embeddings are then projected via an MLP into action-specific scale, shift, and gate parameters. These parameters are fused with the diffusion timestep modulation parameters and applied at each LayerNorm layer within all OpenSora blocks, conditioning the video denoising process. The weights of the action attention blocks are initialized to zero to ensure stable training.

**Training Objective and Data:**
The system is optimized using a flow matching loss objective, which trains the model to predict the velocity vector required to denoise the video latent representations. During inference, step-wise classifier-free guidance is applied. The training data comprises a large-scale, action-free mixture of human egocentric and robot manipulation videos, including datasets like Open X-Embodiment (OXE), AgiBot, Something-Something V2, Ego4D, and others.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings supporting the efficacy and benefits of the CoLA-World framework:

1. **Resolution of Joint Training Fragility:**
 - Direct joint training of a freshly initialized IDM with a pretrained world model (OpenSora) was observed to lead to rapid representational collapse of the latent action space, with the VQ codebook exhibiting zero utilization and entropy (Figure 2, gray curve).
 - The proposed warm-up strategy successfully stabilizes the training process. With adequate warm-up steps (e.g., 8K), the latent action codebook metrics (utilization, maximum usage, entropy) remain healthy throughout subsequent joint training, demonstrating that the warm-up phase effectively aligns the from-scratch LAM with the pretrained world model (Figure 3, dark blue curve).

2. **Performance of Jointly Learned LAM and World Model:**
 - **Latent Action Quality:** CoLA-World yields a competitive latent action space, achieving linear probing losses on par with or superior to the two-stage baseline on several robotics datasets, including out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks like LIBERO (Table 5). While initial probing differences can be subtle, later analysis shows this LAM is more robust.
 - **World Model Simulation Performance:** CoLA-World (WARM8K + E2E52K) consistently matches or surpasses the two-stage method (LAM30K + WM30K) in action-conditioned video prediction across in-distribution (OXE, EgoCentric, AgiBot) and out-of-distribution (LIBERO) datasets (Table 1). Notably, CoLA-World demonstrated more pronounced improvements on the FVD metric, indicating enhanced temporal coherence and realism in generated videos.
 - **Data Efficiency:** The CoLA-World WARM8K + E2E30K model, with a substantially smaller total training budget, achieved performance comparable to or better than the fully trained LAM30K + WM30K two-stage model, particularly on OOD data. This suggests that the synergistic training leads to faster convergence and better utilization of data.

3. **Evidence for Synergistic Co-evolution:**
 - **Evolving World Model as a Better Tutor:** Ablation studies showed that the LAM's linear probing loss on LIBERO decreased faster when the world model was allowed to co-evolve during E2E training (CoLA-World) compared to when the world model remained frozen (PURE WARMUP). This indicates that as the world model refines its understanding of dynamics, it provides increasingly informative and causally sound gradients to the LAM (Figure 4a).
 - **Evolving LAM as a Better Control Interface:** The world model achieved substantially higher video generation quality when paired with a dynamically improving LAM during E2E training compared to a frozen LAM after warm-up. This suggests that an adaptable LAM provides a progressively more precise control interface, unlocking the world model's full predictive potential (Figure 4b).
 - These findings collectively confirm the presence of a virtuous cycle where the improving world model and LAM mutually reinforce each other.

4. **Adaptation for Real-Action-Based Simulation:**
 - CoLA-World demonstrated superior adaptability when finetuned to novel, real-action control interfaces on LIBERO and RoboDesk datasets. Even with a smaller training budget (WARM8K + E2E30K), CoLA-World outperformed the more extensively trained two-stage baseline (LAM30K + WM30K) in real-action-based video prediction, especially on the FVD metric (Table 2).
 - **Codebook Dynamics Analysis (Figure 5):** Quantitative analysis of VQ codebook metrics during adaptation revealed that the two-stage method suffered from severe representational collapse when using an adapter to translate real actions. Codebook utilization plummeted, and max usage spiked, indicating that the adapter mapped most real actions to a few dominant latent codes. In contrast, CoLA-World maintained a healthier and more diverse latent action space under these conditions, attributed to the robustness forged by co-evolution. This robustness allows CoLA-World to better generalize from ideal ground-truth latent actions to imperfect adapter outputs.

5. **Visual Planning Performance:**
 - The adapted CoLA-World models exhibited a clear advantage in solving control tasks via visual planning on the RoboDesk VP² benchmark (Table 3). CoLA-World (WARM8K+E2E30K) achieved higher success rates across multiple tasks compared to both the two-stage baseline and AdaWorld, translating its superior simulation quality into more effective control.

6. **Scalability and Generalizability:**
 - **Model Size:** Increasing the world model's capacity (by using more OpenSora DiT blocks) during E2E training led to a consistent decrease (improvement) in LAM probing loss, indicating that larger world models effectively "tutor" the LAM to learn more expressive representations (Figure 6).
 - **Training Data Size:** Increasing batch size (representing more data exposure) resulted in consistent improvements in both LAM probing loss and world model video prediction performance (Table 6).
 - **Backbone Agnosticism:** The joint training paradigm demonstrated similar performance benefits when using a different video generation backbone (Wan2.1) instead of OpenSora, suggesting its architectural universality (Table 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoLA-World introduces a novel and robust paradigm for the integration of latent action learning with pretrained video-generation-based world models. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling a Previously Untapped Paradigm:** This work is presented as the first successful realization of synergistic joint training between a latent action model (LAM) and a pretrained video generative model acting as a world model. By resolving the issue of representational collapse through a critical warm-up phase, CoLA-World opens a new avenue for developing generalist world models that was previously deemed non-trivial and prone to failure.

2. **Improved Performance and Efficiency:** The framework demonstrably outperforms traditional two-stage methods in key metrics, including video simulation quality (e.g., FVD), data efficiency, and downstream visual planning success rates. This suggests that the co-evolutionary approach leads to more capable and practical world models. The improved data efficiency implies that high-performing world models can be trained with fewer computational resources or less data, accelerating research and development in this domain.

3. **Fundamental Advancement in World Model Design:** CoLA-World establishes a virtuous cycle where the world model acts as a knowledgeable tutor for the LAM, and the LAM, in turn, provides a more precise and adaptable control interface for the world model. This creates a deeply coupled and intrinsically consistent system, fostering better alignment between action representation and environmental dynamics. This conceptual shift offers a more coherent approach to learning controllable simulators.

4. **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** The jointly trained latent action space demonstrates greater robustness against representational collapse during adaptation to real-world, out-of-distribution control interfaces. This resilience, contrasted with the fragility of two-stage baselines, is crucial for practical applications where real-world actions may be noisy or imperfectly translated into latent commands. This enhanced adaptability is a key step towards creating versatile generalist agents.

5. **Foundation for Generalist AI Agents:** By providing a method to effectively control powerful video generative models through learned latent actions, CoLA-World contributes a significant building block towards the creation of universal, general-purpose world models. Such models are central to the vision of generalist AI agents capable of planning and learning through imagination across a multitude of tasks and environments without explicit pre-specification of action spaces.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The establishment of this paradigm opens several future research avenues, including evaluating learned latent actions in multimodal (vision-language-latent-action) settings for manipulation policy training, scaling the framework to even larger video datasets to develop more foundational world models with broader adaptability, and exploring more computationally efficient video generation backbones to mitigate resource demands. The demonstrated scalability with model and data size further supports its potential for developing increasingly powerful world models.
