# Succeed or Learn Slowly: Sample Efficient Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning for Mobile App Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Noah's Ark Lab and University College London introduced Succeed or Learn Slowly (SoLS) with Successful Transition Replay (STR), an off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm for sample-efficient fine-tuning of foundation models in mobile app control. This framework achieved a 51.3% success rate on a challenging AndroidWorld benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o-based and other RL methods while demonstrating 5-60x faster inference times.

## Problem

- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to mobile app control is challenging due to sparse/delayed rewards, large action spaces, and computationally expensive simulations (4-5 seconds per step).
- Existing solutions are either costly (large language model prompting) or struggle with generalization to out-of-distribution tasks (Supervised Fine-Tuning).
- Standard policy gradient updates from negative advantage values can inadvertently degrade model performance by increasing probabilities for semantically related but inappropriate tokens.

## Method

- Developed **SoLS**, a novel off-policy actor-critic algorithm that employs an ==asymmetric policy update== mechanism, learning aggressively from positive advantages while conservatively handling negative ones.
- Introduced Successful Transition Replay (STR), an experience replay buffer that selectively stores and prioritizes successful timesteps to maximize learning efficiency from rare successful interactions.
- Fine-tuned a Llama-3-8B-Instruct model using LoRA adapters, with text-only UI tree descriptions and action history as input for reduced token count and faster inference.

## Results

- SoLS-STR achieved the highest success rate of **51.3%** on an 80-task subset of AndroidWorld, outperforming all GPT-4o-based methods (e.g., UGround-**7B** at **43.8%**) and other RL baselines (e.g., DigiRL-STR at **38.8%**).
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster inference times of approximately **0.9** seconds per step, making it 5 to 60 times quicker than GPT-4o-based approaches.
- Both SoLS's asymmetric policy updates and STR independently contributed to performance gains, with STR proving crucial for sample efficiency across various RL algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An asymmetric policy update, reinforcing successful behaviors directly and constraining updates for unsuccessful ones, prevents policy degradation while enhancing learning in sparse reward settings.
> - Prioritizing and replaying successful individual transitions significantly boosts sample efficiency, especially in environments with high simulation costs and sparse rewards.
> - Smaller, fine-tuned foundation models, when combined with specifically designed RL algorithms, can achieve superior performance and significantly higher efficiency compared to large, proprietary model prompting approaches.
