# A Real-to-Sim-to-Real Approach to Robotic Manipulation with VLM-Generated Iterative Keypoint Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> The IKER framework enables robotic manipulation by using Vision-Language Models to generate iterative, visually-grounded keypoint rewards, facilitating robust real-to-sim-to-real policy transfer. This approach allows robots to perform complex multi-step tasks, recover from disturbances, and adapt their strategies in dynamic, unstructured environments.

## Problem

- Current VLM-robotics approaches often lack the ability to provide precise 3D (SE(3)) control, hindering their application in fine-grained manipulation tasks.
- Designing effective reward functions for complex, long-horizon robotic tasks is a time-consuming and difficult process, often creating a bottleneck for reinforcement learning in robotics.
- Robotic systems struggle to adapt to dynamic, open-world environments, requiring flexible task specifications that can account for implicit steps, uncertainties, and real-time environmental changes.

## Method

- The Iterative Keypoint Reward (IKER) framework uses Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate visually-grounded, Python-based reward functions that specify target keypoint locations for objects.
- A robust real-to-sim scene transfer process leverages 3D mesh reconstruction (BundleSDF) and 6D object pose estimation (FoundationPose) to create faithful simulated environments from real-world scenes.
- Reinforcement learning policies are trained in the IsaacGym simulator using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with extensive domain randomization, enabling effective sim-to-real transfer for deployment on physical robots.

## Results

- IKER (keypoint-based) significantly outperforms object pose-based methods when VLM-generated reward functions are used, achieving 0.7 success on 'Shoe Place' compared to 0.3 for pose-based methods, indicating better VLM reasoning for keypoints.
- The framework demonstrates superior performance in multi-step tasks compared to open-loop planners like VoxPoser, successfully completing complex tasks such as 'pushing a shoe box then placing shoes' due to its iterative replanning capability.
- The robot exhibits robust capabilities, including spontaneous recovery from human disturbances, on-the-fly strategy adjustments based on suboptimal outcomes, and re-planning when initial approaches become infeasible, showcasing human-like intelligence in adaptive problem-solving.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs struggle to directly specify SE(3) object poses for rewards but excel at reasoning about relative Cartesian positions of keypoints, simplifying complex 3D control into more manageable spatial relationships.
> - Iterative VLM-generated feedback, combined with a closed-loop system, enables continuous refinement of task specifications, leading to robust multi-step task chaining, spontaneous error recovery, and on-the-fly strategy adjustments.
> - Domain randomization is critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap, significantly improving real-world robotic manipulation performance by making policies robust to unmodeled physical dynamics and perception inaccuracies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses critical challenges at the intersection of robotic manipulation, reinforcement learning (RL), and large Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It operates within several active research fronts:

- **VLM-driven Robotics and Task Specification**: The field of robotics is rapidly moving towards using natural language instructions to guide complex behaviors, leveraging the vast world knowledge encoded in VLMs. Previous works have explored VLMs for high-level task decomposition [e.g., Ahn et al., Huang et al. (2022)], generating affordance maps [e.g., Huang et al. (2023), Liu et al.], and even low-level control. This paper distinguishes itself by focusing on the **precision and adaptability of VLM-generated task specifications for 3D manipulation**. While many VLMs excel at semantic understanding, their ability to specify exact 3D target locations and dynamically adapt to environmental changes remains a significant hurdle. IKER attempts to bridge this gap by grounding VLM output in precise 3D keypoints, allowing for fine-grained SE(3) control.
- **Reward Function Engineering**: Designing effective reward functions for complex, long-horizon robotic tasks is notoriously difficult and time-consuming, often requiring extensive manual tuning. This "reward engineering" bottleneck is a major impediment to scaling RL in robotics. Recent efforts have explored using LLMs/VLMs to generate reward functions [e.g., Yu et al., Ma et al. (2023)], but many of these have limited real-world applicability or are constrained to specific scenarios. IKER advances this by demonstrating a robust VLM-generated reward function (IKER itself) that is visually grounded, iterative, and proven effective on diverse real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Real-to-Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Training complex robotic policies directly in the real world is often impractical due to safety, cost, and data efficiency issues. Simulation offers a scalable alternative, but policies trained in simulation often struggle with deployment on physical robots due to the "sim-to-real gap." The "real-to-sim-to-real" paradigm aims to leverage the strengths of both. This paper contributes by integrating high-fidelity scene reconstruction (real-to-sim) with robust policy training techniques like domain randomization (sim-to-real) within a VLM-driven framework. Unlike some approaches that focus on robustifying imitation learning (e.g., RialTo), this work leverages the simulation for *training RL policies from scratch* based on VLM-generated rewards, bypassing the need for human demonstrations.
- **Multi-step and Adaptive Manipulation**: Open-world tasks rarely involve single, isolated actions. They often require sequences of steps, dynamic adjustments, and error recovery. Many existing VLM-robotics pipelines operate in an open-loop fashion, lacking mechanisms to incorporate real-time feedback and adapt plans. IKER directly addresses this by incorporating an "iterative refinement mechanism" where the VLM updates the task specification based on the robot's ongoing interaction with the environment, enabling capabilities like multi-step chaining, spontaneous error recovery, and on-the-fly strategy adjustments. The comparison with VoxPoser highlights the advantage of IKER's closed-loop, iterative approach.

In summary, this work positions itself at the forefront of enabling more intelligent and autonomous robots by tackling the critical issues of flexible task specification, automated reward design, and reliable sim-to-real transfer through a novel, iterative VLM-keypoint-RL integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation of this research stems from the inherent difficulties in deploying robots for manipulation tasks in unscripted, open-world environments. The authors identify several key challenges that their work aims to address:

1. **Challenging Task Specification in Open-World Environments**: Traditional robotic systems often rely on rigid, pre-defined instructions that struggle to account for the implicit steps, uncertainties, and variations encountered in dynamic real-world settings. As illustrated by the "shoe box on the rack" example, a robot might need to perform implicit sub-tasks (like clearing space) before achieving the primary goal. The objective is to enable flexible and adaptive task objectives that can align with nuanced human intentions and evolve dynamically.
2. **Lack of Precise 3D Control with VLMs**: While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown immense potential in interpreting natural language and encoding rich world knowledge, existing VLM-robotics approaches often lack the capability to specify precise target locations and orientations in 3D (SE(3) space). This limitation hinders their application in complex manipulation tasks requiring fine-grained control. A primary objective is to leverage VLMs to specify precise 3D manipulation targets.
3. **Inability to Adapt to Environment Changes**: Many current VLM-driven robotic systems operate with open-loop plans, meaning they generate a sequence of actions without a robust mechanism to incorporate real-time feedback from the environment. This makes them brittle in dynamic scenarios where unexpected events or changes necessitate re-planning or adjustment. The motivation is to create a closed-loop system that can continuously refine task specifications based on environmental feedback.
4. **Feasibility of Real-World Training**: Training complex reinforcement learning policies directly on physical robots is often impractical due to safety concerns, high data requirements, and time inefficiency. Simulation offers a scalable training ground, but transferring policies from simulation to the real world remains a significant hurdle due to the "sim-to-real gap." The objective is to establish a robust and efficient real-to-sim-to-real pipeline that enables policy training in simulation for real-world deployment.

To address these objectives, the authors introduce **Iterative Keypoint Reward (IKER)**. The motivation behind IKER is to serve as a visually grounded, Python-based reward function that acts as a dynamic task specification. By leveraging VLMs to generate and refine these reward functions based on spatial relationships between keypoints and commonsense priors, IKER aims to enable robots to perform multi-step tasks, exhibit spontaneous error recovery, and make on-the-fly strategy adjustments in dynamic, real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named IKER, adopts a novel **real-to-sim-to-real loop** to enable robotic manipulation with VLM-generated, iterative keypoint-based rewards. The methodology can be broken down into three main stages: Iterative Keypoint Reward Generation, Real-to-Sim Scene Transfer, and Sim-to-Real Policy Deployment.

#### 3.1. Iterative Keypoint Reward (IKER) Generation

- **Core Idea**: IKER is a visually grounded, Python-based reward function that serves as a dynamic task specification. It leverages the ability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to interpret natural language instructions and visual observations to generate executable code that defines the desired state of keypoints in the environment.
- **VLM Role**: A VLM (specifically GPT-4o) is prompted with the human language instruction, the current RGB observation overlaid with numerical markers for keypoints, and a history of previous observations and generated reward functions. This "execution history" is crucial for enabling iterative refinement and multi-step reasoning.
- **Code Generation**: The VLM is instructed to generate Python code. This code defines:
 - `object_to_interact`: The object the robot should manipulate in the current step.
 - `keypoint_indices_to_interact`: The keypoints associated with the target object.
 - `grasp_mode`: A boolean indicating whether to grasp or push the object.
 - `final_keypoint_coordinates`: Precise 3D target locations for the keypoints of the interacting object, expressed relative to other keypoints (including static environment keypoints). This allows the VLM to perform arbitrary, precise calculations using current keypoint locations, simplifying SE(3) control into Cartesian space reasoning.
 - `done`: A boolean flag indicating task completion.
- **Reward Function Design**: Once the VLM-generated code predicts the target keypoint locations, a scalar reward function `f_i` is computed for Reinforcement Learning. It comprises several terms:
 - `r_dist`: Gripper-to-object distance reward to encourage approaching the target object.
 - `r_dir`: Direction reward to guide movement towards target keypoint locations.
 - `r_align`: Alignment reward to drive keypoints close to their target locations.
 - `r_bonus`: Success bonus for maintaining keypoints within a threshold for a duration.
 - `r_penalty`: Penalties for undesirable actions (excessive movement, dropping object, excessive force).

#### 3.2. Real-to-Sim Scene Transfer

- **3D Mesh Reconstruction**: To create a faithful simulated environment, 3D meshes of all manipulable objects (e.g., shoes, books) are generated. This is achieved by capturing video footage of each object from multiple angles, which is then used by **BundleSDF** for accurate 3D mesh reconstruction. Static environment elements (e.g., tables, racks) are captured as point clouds to create their meshes. Once created, object meshes can be reused across scenarios.
- **Object Pose Estimation**: In the real-world scene, **FoundationPose** is used to estimate the 6D poses (position and orientation) of all objects. This allows for precise placement of the reconstructed meshes in the simulation, mirroring the real-world setup.
- **Keypoint Generation**: Keypoints are automatically identified based on the generated meshes. For manipulable objects, keypoints are placed at their extremities along their axes. For static objects, keypoints are uniformly distributed across their surfaces. A filtering process removes redundant or overlapping keypoints. Critically, the VLM is not explicitly given object-keypoint associations but must infer them from the visual input.

#### 3.3. Sim-to-Real Policy Deployment

- **RL Policy Training**: Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies are trained in the **IsaacGym** simulator using the **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** algorithm.
 - **State Space**: The policy's input state `s_t` includes the gripper's end-effector pose, the pose of the currently manipulated object, the set of observed object keypoints, and their corresponding target positions derived from the IKER reward function. This approach avoids direct raw RGB-D input by using pre-processed pose and keypoint information.
 - **Action Space**: The action space `a_t` is defined as relative changes in the gripper's 6D end-effector pose (translation `∆p_e` and rotation `∆r_e`).
 - **Architecture**: An actor-critic architecture with a shared MLP backbone is used.
- **Domain Randomization (DR)**: To bridge the sim-to-real gap, extensive domain randomization is applied during training. This involves randomizing object properties (friction, mass, restitution, compliance, geometry), object initial positions, gripper locations, and grasp poses. This exposure to varied conditions makes the learned policies more robust to real-world discrepancies.
- **Real-World Deployment**: The trained RL policy `π_i` is directly deployed on a physical **XArm7** robot.
 - **Control**: Inverse kinematics computes joint angles from the policy's end-effector pose commands. The policy operates at 10Hz.
 - **Perception**: **FoundationPose** continues to provide real-time object pose estimates, which are used to compute the keypoint locations for the policy's state input.
 - **Grasping**: For real-world grasping, **AnyGrasp** is used to detect appropriate grasp poses, a more sophisticated approach than the heuristic grasps used in simulation.

This holistic approach allows for efficient training in simulation while ensuring robust transfer to the real world, guided by an intelligent, adaptive task specification generated by VLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the efficacy of the IKER framework across various robotic manipulation tasks, highlighting its precision, adaptability, and robustness in real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Policy Training with IKER for Single-Step Tasks

- **Keypoints vs. Object Pose for Reward Functions**: The paper compares IKER (keypoint-based) against a conventional object pose-based method for constructing reward functions.
 - **Human-Annotated Rewards**: When reward functions are manually defined (human-annotated), both IKER and the pose-based method achieve high and comparable success rates in simulation (e.g., Shoe Place: 0.945 for IKER, 0.938 for Pose). This indicates that both representations can effectively capture target locations for RL training when precisely specified.
 - **VLM-Generated (Automatic) Rewards**: When VLMs automatically generate reward functions, IKER significantly outperforms the pose-based approach. For Shoe Place, IKER achieves 0.7 success, while the pose-based method only reaches 0.3. Similar trends are observed across Shoe Push, Stowing Push, and Stowing Reorient tasks. This critical finding suggests that VLMs struggle to directly reason about and specify rotations in SE(3) space (as required by object poses), but they perform much better when the task is simplified to reasoning about relative Cartesian positions of keypoints. Keypoints effectively reduce the challenge from SE(3) to R3 for the VLM.
- **Sim-to-Real Performance**: A slight but notable drop in success rate is observed from simulation to the real world (e.g., IKER Shoe Place: 0.945 in sim vs. 0.8 in real; Shoe Push: 0.871 in sim vs. 0.850 in real). This indicates that while domain randomization helps, factors like inaccuracies in real-world environment reconstruction, perception errors (e.g., FoundationPose inaccuracies), and unmodeled physical dynamics still contribute to a performance gap.
- **Failure Modes**: Common failures include the VLM sometimes selecting incorrect keypoints or failing to use all available keypoints for precise alignment. Discrepancies between heuristic grasps in simulation and **AnyGrasp** predictions in the real world, as well as complex physical dynamics during non-prehensile tasks (like pushing), also contribute to failures. The authors suggest that more in-context examples for VLMs and improved dynamic parameter estimation could mitigate these.

#### 4.2. Iterative Replanning for Multi-Step Tasks

- **Multi-Step Task Chaining**: The framework is evaluated on a complex multi-step task: pushing a shoe box to clear space, then placing a pair of shoes on a rack. This requires sequential actions and adaptive planning.
- **Comparison with VoxPoser**: IKER is compared against VoxPoser, an existing VLM-driven system for generating motion plans. IKER consistently outperforms VoxPoser across all stages of the multi-step task.
 - **IKER's Advantage**: IKER's success (e.g., 8/10 for the first step, 4/10 overall success) stems from its **closed-loop, iterative replanning** capability. The VLM can re-evaluate the scene after each action and generate a refined reward function for the next step.
 - **VoxPoser's Limitations**: VoxPoser, being an open-loop planner, struggles with the dynamic nature of multi-step tasks. Its failures are attributed to issues like imprecise pushing distances, reliance on simple heuristic grasping, and lack of collision avoidance, which lead to subsequent task failures.

#### 4.3. Robustness, Adjusting Plans, and Re-Planning

A significant qualitative finding is IKER's ability to exhibit human-like intelligence in adapting to unforeseen circumstances:

- **Disturbance Recovery**: When a human perturbs the robot during a task (e.g., moving a shoe the robot is placing), IKER enables the robot to recover by re-grasping and successfully completing the task.
- **On-the-Fly Strategy Adjustments**: If an intermediate outcome is suboptimal (e.g., a shoe is placed too far from its pair), the VLM can predict a corrective action (e.g., pushing the shoe closer) to achieve the desired state.
- **Re-planning for Infeasibility**: If an initial plan becomes infeasible (e.g., a book is too large for a direct grasp, leading to grasp failure), the VLM can propose an alternative strategy (e.g., re-grasping the book using the environment) to complete the task. This demonstrates advanced problem-solving capabilities derived from the iterative VLM feedback.

#### 4.4. Effect of Domain Randomization (DR)

- **Impact on Performance**: DR is shown to be crucial for real-world performance. While it slightly reduces simulation success rates (e.g., Shoe Place: 0.964 without DR vs. 0.945 with DR), it dramatically improves real-world performance (Shoe Place: 0.5 without DR vs. 0.8 with DR; Push: 0.2 without DR vs. 0.7 with DR).
- **Benefits of DR**: DR makes policies robust to sim-to-real gaps by exposing them to varied physical properties and initial conditions, preventing issues like collisions with the environment or objects slipping during manipulation that occurred without DR.

In essence, the findings validate that VLM-generated iterative keypoint rewards, combined with a robust real-to-sim-to-real pipeline, enable robots to perform complex, adaptive, and resilient manipulation tasks in dynamic, open-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IKER framework represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between high-level human intent and low-level robotic control in unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Enabling Flexible and Adaptive Robotic Manipulation**: By introducing Iterative Keypoint Reward (IKER), the paper provides a novel method for dynamic task specification. This moves beyond rigid, pre-programmed instructions, allowing robots to tackle tasks with implicit steps and adapt to unforeseen circumstances. This flexibility is crucial for deploying robots in real-world settings that are inherently uncertain and dynamic, such as homes, warehouses, or service industries.
- **Automating Reward Function Design**: The ability of VLMs to generate and refine reward functions automatically, based on visual and linguistic input, is a major breakthrough. This substantially reduces the burden of manual reward engineering, a notorious bottleneck in reinforcement learning. By transforming a complex, intuitive human goal into an executable Python-based reward function, IKER simplifies the development cycle for new robotic skills.
- **Precise 3D Control with VLMs**: The paper effectively addresses a key limitation of prior VLM-robotics approaches: the lack of precise 3D spatial reasoning. By utilizing keypoints and allowing the VLM to reason about their relative Cartesian positions, IKER enables fine-grained SE(3) control. This makes VLMs more practical for manipulation tasks requiring high accuracy, like placing objects precisely or reorienting them in constrained spaces.
- **Robust Real-to-Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The successful implementation of a real-to-sim-to-real loop, integrating state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction (BundleSDF, FoundationPose) with robust RL training (IsaacGym, Domain Randomization) and real-world deployment (AnyGrasp), provides a validated blueprint for developing and deploying complex robotic skills efficiently. This approach mitigates the safety and cost concerns of real-world training while ensuring generalization.
- **Human-like Problem Solving and Error Recovery**: The demonstrated capabilities of multi-step task execution, spontaneous error recovery, and on-the-fly strategy adjustments are highly impactful. These are hallmarks of intelligent behavior and critical for robots to operate autonomously in unscripted environments. A robot that can recover from a human disturbance or replan when its initial approach fails is far more valuable and reliable.
- **Advancing VLM-Robotics Integration**: This work pushes the boundaries of how VLMs can be integrated into robotic systems, moving beyond high-level planning to direct influence on the reward signal for low-level policy learning. It highlights the potential for VLMs to imbue robots with commonsense reasoning and adaptability.

**Potential Impact**:
The framework has the potential to accelerate the development of general-purpose robotic manipulators. It could lead to:
- **More Autonomous Home Robots**: Capable of handling diverse household chores with minimal pre-programming.
- **Flexible Industrial Automation**: Robots that can adapt to changes in product layouts or unexpected events on assembly lines.
- **Enhanced Service Robotics**: Robots that can perform complex tasks in dynamic human environments, such as assisting in healthcare or logistics.
- **Reduced Development Costs**: By automating reward design and leveraging simulation, the framework could significantly lower the barrier to entry for developing new robotic applications.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges several limitations, which also point to avenues for future research:
- **Object Mesh Generation**: The current need to capture objects from all views for 3D mesh reconstruction is time-consuming. Future work could integrate methods for single-image mesh generation to streamline this process.
- **Dynamic Parameter Modeling**: The real-to-sim transfer does not explicitly account for accurate dynamic parameters of objects, which can still lead to discrepancies (e.g., object slipping during pushes). Incorporating system identification techniques could improve realism.
- **Complex Multi-Object Interactions**: The current evaluation primarily focuses on single-object manipulation at each stage. Extending the framework to handle more intricate multi-object interactions and cooperative manipulation would be a crucial next step.

In conclusion, IKER makes a substantial contribution to creating more intelligent, versatile, and robust robotic systems capable of operating effectively in the complexities of the real world, largely by harnessing the power of VLMs for adaptive task specification.
