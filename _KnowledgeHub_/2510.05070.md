# ResMimic: From General Motion Tracking to Humanoid Whole-body Loco-Manipulation via Residual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Amazon FAR researchers developed ResMimic, a two-stage residual learning framework that transforms general motion tracking into precise humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation. The system achieved a 92.5% average task success rate in simulation, significantly outperforming baselines, and enabled a Unitree G1 robot to carry heavy and irregularly shaped objects using whole-body contact in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Achieving robust, precise, and expressive whole-body loco-manipulation in humanoids remains a challenge, with existing methods often focusing on locomotion or isolated manipulation.
- Learning from human demonstrations faces an 'embodiment gap,' where direct retargeting of human motions to robots leads to issues like floating contacts or object penetrations.
- Current General Motion Tracking (GMT) policies can reproduce diverse human motions but lack object awareness, making them unsuitable for precise interaction tasks with physical objects.

## Method

- **ResMimic** introduces a two-stage residual learning framework that decouples general motion tracking from object-specific interaction, leveraging ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== for training.
- Stage I involves training a General Motion Tracking (GMT) policy on large-scale human-only motion capture data to provide a robust, task-agnostic backbone for human-like whole-body movements.
- Stage II trains a Residual Refinement policy that learns to output small, task-specific corrections to the GMT policy's actions, incorporating object observations and using specialized reward engineering (e.g., point-cloud-based object tracking, contact reward) and a virtual object force curriculum for robust interaction.

## Results

- ResMimic achieved an average task success rate of **92.5%** in sim-to-sim evaluations (IsaacGym to MuJoCo), dramatically outperforming 'Train from Scratch' (**0%**) and 'Base Policy + Fine-tune' (**7.5%**) while converging significantly faster (1300 vs. 4500+ iterations).
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid demonstrated expressive whole-body loco-manipulation, including carrying heavy (**4.5**-**5.5** kg) and irregularly shaped objects using stable whole-body contact, and reacting robustly to external perturbations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the virtual object controller was essential for stabilizing early training, and the contact reward was critical for learning human-like whole-body interaction strategies, improving sim-to-real robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging a pre-trained General Motion Tracking policy as a strong prior for human-like movement significantly improves training efficiency and robustness for complex humanoid tasks.
> - A residual learning approach effectively adapts the general motion prior to specific object-aware loco-manipulation tasks, outperforming direct fine-tuning methods by preserving base capabilities while learning precise corrections.
> - Specialized reward functions (point-cloud object tracking, contact reward) and training curricula (virtual object force) are crucial for stabilizing learning, promoting robust whole-body contact strategies, and enabling successful interaction with heavy and irregular objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is rapidly advancing, driven by the promise of robots that can operate in human-centric environments, performing complex tasks requiring both locomotion and manipulation. However, achieving robust, precise, and expressive whole-body loco-manipulation remains a grand challenge.

**Current Landscape & Gaps:**
- **Locomotion vs. Manipulation:** Much prior work has focused either on robust bipedal locomotion (e.g.,) or isolated tabletop manipulation (e.g.,), but not the seamless, coordinated *whole-body loco-manipulation* that combines movement and interaction.
- **Human Imitation & Embodiment Gap:** Learning from human demonstrations is attractive because humans naturally exhibit coordinated full-body control. However, directly retargeting human motions to robots faces a significant "embodiment gap." Differences in kinematics, dynamics, and physical dimensions between humans and robots often lead to issues like floating contacts or object penetrations (Figure 2), making direct imitation difficult for precise interaction tasks.
- **General Motion Tracking (GMT):** Recent advances in GMT policies (e.g.,) have enabled humanoids to reproduce diverse human motions with high fidelity. These policies are trained on large-scale human-only datasets. However, a major limitation is their *lack of object awareness*. They can mimic human *movements* but cannot naturally interact with *physical objects* in a task-specific, precise manner.
- **Task-Specific Designs:** Existing humanoid loco-manipulation approaches often rely on highly task-specific designs (e.g., staged controllers, handcrafted data pipelines). While effective for narrow tasks, these approaches lack scalability, generality, and often struggle with whole-body expressiveness and complex contact.
- **Foundation Models in Robotics:** Breakthroughs in foundation models (e.g., GPT-4) and their application in robotics (e.g., π0, LBM for manipulation) have demonstrated the power of pre-training on large-scale data followed by post-training (finetuning or adaptation). This paradigm addresses data scarcity and improves robustness and generalization. However, this powerful pre-train–finetune paradigm remains *largely unexplored* for humanoid *whole-body control*.

**ResMimic's Contribution to the Landscape:**
ResMimic strategically positions itself at the intersection of these challenges and opportunities. It directly addresses the limitations of GMT policies by extending them to object-aware loco-manipulation, and it introduces a generalizable framework to overcome the scalability issues of task-specific designs. Crucially, it pioneers the application of a two-stage pre-train/residual-learning paradigm to humanoid whole-body control. By leveraging a pre-trained GMT policy as a robust prior for human-like movement, ResMimic effectively decouples the general motion generation from the task-specific, precise object interaction. This approach aligns with the success of foundation models, demonstrating how a powerful general model can be efficiently adapted for specialized, demanding robotic tasks. It represents a significant step towards creating more versatile, autonomous, and physically intelligent humanoid robots capable of complex, human-like interaction with their environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of ResMimic is to enable **precise and expressive humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation** in a robust and efficient manner. This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the current limitations in the field:

- **Transformative Applications:** Humanoid robots possess the unique ability to combine locomotion and manipulation in a coordinated, human-like manner. This "whole-body loco-manipulation" capability holds immense promise for transformative applications in daily services (e.g., home assistance) and industrial operations (e.g., warehouse tasks), particularly by leveraging existing human-centric infrastructure. Realizing this potential is a primary driver.
- **Addressing Precision and Expressiveness Gaps:** Current humanoid control methods struggle with the demanding requirements of loco-manipulation, which necessitate high precision, rich whole-body contacts, and expressive motion. Existing approaches often sacrifice one for the other (e.g., precise manipulation with limited expressiveness, or expressive motion without object awareness).
- **Overcoming the Embodiment Gap:** While learning from human demonstrations is a natural paradigm, directly retargeting human motions to humanoids introduces significant challenges. Contact locations and relative object poses in human data often do not translate perfectly to robots, leading to simulation artifacts like floating contacts or penetrations, which impede learning.
- **Leveraging General Motion Tracking (GMT) while enhancing Object Awareness:** Recent GMT policies can effectively reproduce diverse human motions, but they are inherently *unaware of manipulated objects*. A key motivation is to build upon these robust human-like motion priors without needing to re-learn fundamental motor skills from scratch for every new task.
- **Achieving Scalability and Generality:** Existing loco-manipulation methods are often highly task-specific, requiring extensive engineering for each new task. There is a strong need for a unified, efficient, and precise framework that can generalize across a variety of loco-manipulation scenarios, reducing development overhead.
- **Applying the Foundation Model Paradigm to Humanoids:** The success of pre-training and finetuning in other AI domains (NLP, vision, general robotics manipulation) highlights a powerful paradigm for resolving data scarcity and improving robustness. A central motivation for ResMimic is to explore and apply this paradigm, particularly the concept of residual learning, to the challenging domain of humanoid whole-body control, where it has been largely unexplored.
- **Key Insight: Shared Motions vs. Task-Specific Corrections:** The authors' core insight is that many whole-body motions (like balance, stepping, reaching) are shared across tasks. Only the *fine-grained object interaction* requires task-specific adaptation. This observation directly motivates the proposed residual learning paradigm, where a stable motion prior (from GMT) is augmented with lightweight, task-specific adjustments for object interaction.

In summary, the key objectives are to develop a framework that is general, efficient, precise, and robust enough to enable humanoids to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks by effectively combining generalized human motion with context-aware object interaction, leveraging modern AI paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ResMimic introduces a **two-stage residual learning framework** designed to efficiently transfer human-object interaction data into humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation policies. This approach is rooted in the insight that while broad human motion can be generally mimicked, precise object interaction demands specific corrections. Both stages utilize Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for training.

**A. Two-Stage Residual Learning Framework:**
The framework decouples general motion tracking from object-specific interaction.
1. **Stage I: General Motion Tracking (GMT) Policy ($\pi_{GMT}$):**
 - **Purpose:** To serve as a task-agnostic backbone controller generating human-like whole-body movements. It provides a robust prior for fundamental humanoid skills.
 - **Input:** Robot proprioception (joint angles, velocities, root orientation/angular velocity, recent action history) and reference human motion (root translation/orientation, joint positions, including future frames for anticipation).
 - **Dataset:** Trained solely on large-scale *human-only* motion capture (MoCap) datasets (e.g., AMASS, OMOMO), comprising over 42 hours of diverse human motion. Inapplicable motions (e.g., stair climbing) are filtered. Kinematic retargeting (GMR) maps human motions to humanoid reference motions.
 - **Training Strategy:** Single-stage RL in simulation, without privileged information. Optimized to maximize a motion tracking reward ($r_m$).
 - **Reward and Domain Randomization:** The motion tracking reward is a sum of task, penalty, and regularization terms, similar to TWIST. Extensive domain randomization is applied during training to enhance sim-to-real transfer robustness.

2. **Stage II: Residual Refinement Policy ($\pi_{Res}$):**
 - **Purpose:** To refine the coarse actions from the GMT policy, incorporating object awareness and enabling precise, task-specific manipulation. This policy learns the "corrections" needed for object interaction.
 - **Input:** Full state information: robot proprioception ($s_r$), object state ($s_o$), and *both* human reference motion ($\hat{s}_r$) and *object reference state* ($\hat{s}_o$).
 - **Output:** A residual action ($\Delta a_{res}$) that is added to the GMT policy's output ($a = a_{gmt} + \Delta a_{res}$). The action space is the same as GMT (target joint angles).
 - **Reference Motions:** Collected using a MoCap system that simultaneously records human and object motions. Human motion is retargeted via GMR, and object motion is used directly as reference.
 - **Training Strategy:** Single-stage RL with PPO.
 - **Network Initialization:** The final layer of the PPO actor is initialized with a small gain factor (Xavier uniform initialization), encouraging initial residual actions to be close to zero, as the GMT policy already provides a good base.
 - **Virtual Object Force Curriculum:** To stabilize early training, especially with noisy reference trajectories or heavy objects, a PD controller applies virtual forces/torques to drive the object towards its reference. The gains of this controller are gradually decayed, forcing the learned policy to take over autonomously. This addresses issues like accidental object knocking or instability due to kinematic retargeting imperfections.
 - **Reward and Early Termination:**
 - **Combined Reward:** The residual policy maximizes the sum of the motion tracking reward ($r_m$, reused from GMT training) and a new object reward ($r_o$).
 - **Point-cloud-based Object Tracking Reward ($r_o$):** Instead of simple pose differences, this reward measures the difference between sampled point clouds on the current and reference object meshes. This provides a smoother reward landscape, naturally accounts for both translation and rotation, and eliminates the need for manual weight tuning.
 - **Contact Reward ($r_c$):** Explicitly encourages correct physical interactions. Oracle contact information (which robot links should contact the object) is derived from the human-object interaction reference. The reward is higher when expected contacts occur with appropriate forces. This promotes whole-body contact strategies over relying solely on end-effectors, improving real-world deployability.
 - **Early Termination:** Episodes terminate if: (i) unintended ground contact, (ii) substantial deviation from motion reference, (iii) object mesh deviates beyond a threshold, or (iv) required body-object contact is lost for too long.

**B. Deployment:**
During real-world deployment, the combined policy (base GMT + residual refinement) is used for loco-manipulation control. It supports both "blind" deployment (without real-time object state input, relying on planned object reference) and "non-blind" deployment (with real-time MoCap-based object state input for reactive behavior).

This two-stage approach effectively combines the broad motor skills learned from vast human motion data with precise, object-aware control, facilitated by specialized reward engineering and training curricula for efficiency and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ResMimic's effectiveness was rigorously evaluated through extensive simulation studies and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, addressing four key research questions:

**A. Sim-to-Sim Evaluation (IsaacGym to MuJoCo Transfer):**
Policies were trained in IsaacGym and evaluated in MuJoCo, which is considered a better proxy for real-world physics.

- **Q1: GMT alone is insufficient but a strong prior.**
 - The pre-trained GMT policy (Base Policy) deployed directly to loco-manipulation tasks achieved a very low average task success rate (SR) of 10%. While it excelled at joint tracking, it performed poorly on object tracking ($E_o$) and overall task completion due to its lack of object awareness. This confirms the necessity of task-specific adaptation for object manipulation.
- **Q2: GMT as a base significantly improves efficiency and effectiveness.**
 - **ResMimic (using GMT as base)** achieved an average SR of 92.5%, compared to **0% for "Train from Scratch"** policies.
 - ResMimic converged significantly faster, requiring only an average of 1300 training iterations, whereas "Train from Scratch" required 4500 iterations and still failed to solve the tasks in MuJoCo.
 - ResMimic also demonstrated substantially lower object tracking error ($E_o$), motion tracking error ($E_m$), and joint tracking error ($E_j$) across all tasks, highlighting its superior performance and the importance of leveraging a pre-trained GMT foundation for robustness against sim-to-sim gaps.
- **Q3: Residual learning outperforms direct fine-tuning.**
 - **ResMimic (residual learning)** achieved an average SR of 92.5%, dramatically outperforming **"Base Policy + Fine-tune,"** which managed only a 7.5% SR.
 - Fine-tuning struggled because the original GMT policy architecture could not incorporate additional object observations, limiting its ability to learn robust object-interaction behaviors under randomized object poses. Furthermore, fine-tuning tended to overwrite the generalization capabilities of the base GMT policy.
 - Sim-to-sim comparison (Figure 5) showed that fine-tuned policies that sometimes succeeded in IsaacGym failed entirely in MuJoCo, underscoring the superiority of residual learning as a more robust and generalizable adaptation strategy.

**B. Real-World Evaluation (Unitree G1 Humanoid):**
ResMimic demonstrated its capabilities on a real Unitree G1 robot (Figure 1), showcasing:

- **Expressive Carrying Motions:** The robot performed complex motions like kneeling to pick up a box and carrying it on its back, exhibiting expressive whole-body movement.
- **Beyond Manipulation Interaction:** The humanoid successfully sat down on a chair and stood up, maintaining balance and contact with the environment, demonstrating interaction beyond simple object manipulation.
- **Heavy Payload Carrying with Whole-Body Contact:** The robot successfully carried a 4.5 kg box, significantly exceeding the G1's wrist payload limit (around 2.5 kg). This highlights the system's ability to leverage *whole-body contact* for stability and load distribution, a crucial human-like strategy.
- **Generalization to Irregular Heavy Objects:** The robot could lift and carry chairs weighing 4.5 kg and 5.5 kg, demonstrating instance-level generalization to novel, non-box geometries without specific retraining for these objects.
- **Qualitative Baselines Comparison (Figure 6):** In real-world tests, ResMimic succeeded where baselines (Base Policy, Train from Scratch, Base Policy + Fine-tune) largely failed, due to issues like object unawareness or catastrophic failures from the sim-to-real gap.
- **Non-Blind Deployment (MoCap-based object state):** The robot demonstrated reactive capabilities, manipulating objects from random initial poses, performing consecutive tasks, and showing robust behavior against external perturbations (Figure 4).

**C. Ablation Studies:**

- **Effect of the Virtual Object Controller (Figure 7):** The virtual object controller was crucial for stabilizing early training. Without it, policies frequently failed due to initial imperfections (e.g., hand-object penetrations) in reference motions, leading the policy to retreat. The curriculum-based virtual forces allowed the policy to overcome these initial challenges and converge to precise manipulation strategies.
- **Effect of the Contact Reward (Figure 8):** The contact reward proved essential for promoting human-like whole-body contact strategies. Without it, the humanoid often defaulted to less stable, wrist/hand-only lifting, which failed in sim-to-real transfer. With the contact reward, the policy learned to engage the torso and arms, mirroring human demonstrations and significantly improving sim-to-sim and sim-to-real robustness.

In summary, ResMimic delivers substantial gains in task success, training efficiency, motion fidelity, and robustness. Its real-world performance on complex loco-manipulation tasks, including heavy and irregular objects, conclusively demonstrates its superiority over strong baselines and validates the proposed two-stage residual learning framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ResMimic represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly for whole-body loco-manipulation. Its potential impact spans both research methodology and practical applications:

- **Pioneering a Generalizable Paradigm for Humanoid Control:** The work successfully applies the "pre-train, then adapt" paradigm, analogous to foundation models in other AI domains, to the highly challenging area of humanoid whole-body control. This demonstrates that robust, general-purpose policies can be built upon a strong prior (GMT) and efficiently adapted for specific, complex tasks via residual learning. This approach offers a powerful alternative to purely task-specific or end-to-end learning methods that struggle with data efficiency and generalization.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Robustness:** By leveraging large-scale human motion data for the base GMT policy, ResMimic significantly improves training efficiency and reduces the need for costly, specific robotic interaction data. The residual learning framework, coupled with innovations like the point-cloud object tracking reward, contact reward, and virtual object controller, enhances robustness to real-world complexities, including sim-to-real gaps, imperfect reference data, and heavy payloads.
- **Unlocking Complex Loco-Manipulation Capabilities:** ResMimic enables humanoids to perform truly versatile loco-manipulation tasks, such as carrying heavy objects using whole-body contact, interacting with irregularly shaped objects, and performing expressive, coordinated movements. This moves beyond simple locomotion or static manipulation to dynamic, context-aware interaction, a crucial step for real-world utility.
- **Practical Applicability in Human-Centric Environments:** The ability to handle heavy and irregularly shaped objects, combined with expressive and robust motion, makes humanoids controlled by ResMimic far more capable for tasks in warehouses, logistics, and potentially daily service environments. These are areas where humanoids, with their human-like form factor, can leverage existing infrastructure and adapt to diverse scenarios.
- **Accelerating Future Research:** The commitment to releasing the GPU-accelerated simulation infrastructure, sim-to-sim evaluation prototype, and motion data is a vital contribution to the research community. This will lower the barrier to entry for other researchers, enable standardized benchmarking, and foster rapid innovation in humanoid loco-manipulation.
- **Foundation for More Autonomous Humanoids:** By demonstrating that humanoids can learn and adapt complex physical interaction skills effectively, ResMimic paves the way for increasingly autonomous robots that can perform intricate tasks without constant human teleoperation or extensive re-engineering. This could fundamentally change how humans interact with and delegate tasks to robots.

In conclusion, ResMimic's novel two-stage residual learning framework and its demonstrated success in both simulation and the real world represent a significant leap forward. It not only addresses critical technical challenges in humanoid control but also establishes a scalable and robust paradigm that could accelerate the deployment of intelligent humanoids in a wide array of real-world applications.
