# ResMimic: From General Motion Tracking to Humanoid Whole-body Loco-Manipulation via Residual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Amazon FAR researchers developed ResMimic, a two-stage residual learning framework that transforms general motion tracking into precise humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation. The system achieved a 92.5% average task success rate in simulation, significantly outperforming baselines, and enabled a Unitree G1 robot to carry heavy and irregularly shaped objects using whole-body contact in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Achieving robust, precise, and expressive whole-body loco-manipulation in humanoids remains a challenge, with existing methods often focusing on locomotion or isolated manipulation.
- Learning from human demonstrations faces an 'embodiment gap,' where direct retargeting of human motions to robots leads to issues like floating contacts or object penetrations.
- Current General Motion Tracking (GMT) policies can reproduce diverse human motions but lack object awareness, making them unsuitable for precise interaction tasks with physical objects.

## Method

- **ResMimic** introduces a two-stage residual learning framework that decouples general motion tracking from object-specific interaction, leveraging ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== for training.
- Stage I involves training a General Motion Tracking (GMT) policy on large-scale human-only motion capture data to provide a robust, task-agnostic backbone for human-like whole-body movements.
- Stage II trains a Residual Refinement policy that learns to output small, task-specific corrections to the GMT policy's actions, incorporating object observations and using specialized reward engineering (e.g., point-cloud-based object tracking, contact reward) and a virtual object force curriculum for robust interaction.

## Results

- ResMimic achieved an average task success rate of **92.5%** in sim-to-sim evaluations (IsaacGym to MuJoCo), dramatically outperforming 'Train from Scratch' (**0%**) and 'Base Policy + Fine-tune' (**7.5%**) while converging significantly faster (1300 vs. 4500+ iterations).
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid demonstrated expressive whole-body loco-manipulation, including carrying heavy (**4.5**-**5.5** kg) and irregularly shaped objects using stable whole-body contact, and reacting robustly to external perturbations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the virtual object controller was essential for stabilizing early training, and the contact reward was critical for learning human-like whole-body interaction strategies, improving sim-to-real robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging a pre-trained General Motion Tracking policy as a strong prior for human-like movement significantly improves training efficiency and robustness for complex humanoid tasks.
> - A residual learning approach effectively adapts the general motion prior to specific object-aware loco-manipulation tasks, outperforming direct fine-tuning methods by preserving base capabilities while learning precise corrections.
> - Specialized reward functions (point-cloud object tracking, contact reward) and training curricula (virtual object force) are crucial for stabilizing learning, promoting robust whole-body contact strategies, and enabling successful interaction with heavy and irregular objects.
