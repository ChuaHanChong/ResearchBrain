# Grounding Large Language Models In Embodied Environment With Imperfect World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The GLIMO framework, developed by researchers at UCSD, enables large language models (LLMs) to effectively learn and generalize for embodied tasks by fine-tuning them with experiences generated from imperfect world models. This approach allows open-source LLMs like LLaMA-3 to achieve significant performance gains, including up to 2.04x task completion improvement and outperforming GPT-4 in specific embodied environments.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) lack direct physical world experience, leading to difficulties in basic physical reasoning and execution of embodied tasks.
- Existing methods for physically grounding LLMs often rely on the costly and unsafe assumption of access to perfect world models or simulators.
- Generating diverse and high-quality instruction datasets for fine-tuning LLMs is a labor-intensive, unscalable process that limits generalizability to new robotics tasks.

## Method

- ==GLIMO== introduces a framework to fine-tune LLMs using embodied experiences sampled from =='imperfect world models'== (simulators).
- An ==automated LLM agent-based data generator== autonomously navigates imperfect environments, interprets experiences, and transforms them into diverse, high-quality instruction tuning datasets.
- A ==two-stage fine-tuning approach with LoRA== is employed to specialize LLMs on grounded embodied experiences while mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enhancing generalization.

## Results

- Fine-tuned open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-3) achieved significant task completion improvements of **2.04x**, **1.54x**, and **1.82x** across Agent World, Urban Driving, and Multi-Agent benchmarks, respectively.
- The fine-tuned LLaMA-3 models surpassed GPT-4, outperforming it by **51.7%** in Agent World and **84.5%** in Urban Driving task completion percentage.
- The approach enhanced zero-shot generalization capabilities in out-of-distribution QA accuracy, with fine-tuned models largely preserving general language modeling capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can effectively learn useful, domain-invariant knowledge from imperfect world models that transfers to more complex, real-world environments, challenging the necessity of perfect simulators.
> - Automated LLM agents can generate high-quality, diverse instruction data through self-guided sampling, iterative refining, and specific QA formats (rationale, counterfactual, episodic memory), significantly reducing manual effort.
> - Training with data predominantly from imperfect world models can act as an effective regularization technique, improving LLM generalization and robustness, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) with robotics and embodied AI represents a rapidly evolving and critical area of research. LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in common-sense reasoning, complex planning, and rich knowledge recall, leading to their application in diverse robotics domains such as self-driving cars, autonomous drones, and robotic manipulation. However, a significant challenge remains: LLMs, primarily trained on vast text corpora, inherently lack direct experience with the physical world's nuances. This deficiency often results in failures when confronted with basic physical reasoning tasks or when executing actions that require a deep understanding of environmental dynamics, task-related constraints, and the real-world consequences of their actions.

Previous approaches to physically grounding LLMs in robotics typically fall into two categories:
1. **Prompting-based methods:** These involve providing LLMs with structured instructions, decomposing problems, or allowing self-refinement through iterative interaction. While effective in small-scale, predefined environments, "heavy prompting" often overfits, suffers from hallucination, struggles with time-aware actions, and lacks scalability for open-ended real-world tasks because it doesn't alter the model's core weights.
2. **Instruction Tuning:** This approach involves fine-tuning LLMs with question-answering data to teach them new capabilities, mitigate hallucinations, and improve tool usage or physical world understanding. Recent work, such as Embodied Experiences from World Model (E2WM) by Xiang et al. (2023), has applied instruction tuning by sampling embodied experiences from "world models" (grounded simulators) to fine-tune LLMs.

GLIMO directly addresses several key limitations of these existing paradigms:
- **Reliance on perfect world models:** Previous instruction tuning methods for embodied tasks often assume access to a "perfect" world model capable of generating high-fidelity, safe, and cost-effective experiences. In reality, directly sampling from real-world environments is costly and unsafe (e.g., autonomous driving), and even advanced simulators often present a "sim2real gap" due to imperfect modeling.
- **Scalability of data generation:** Generating diverse and high-quality instruction datasets for fine-tuning has traditionally required extensive human annotation or manual design of downstream tasks for specific applications. This process is labor-intensive, unscalable, and limits generalizability to new robotics tasks.
- **Generalization across environments:** The distribution shift between simulated (imperfect) and real (perfect) environments can cause LLMs to learn spurious correlations, hindering their ability to generalize.

By proposing to learn from *imperfect* world models and automating the instruction data generation process using an LLM agent, GLIMO attempts to bridge these gaps. It aims to make the process of physically grounding LLMs more efficient, safer, and scalable, ultimately pushing towards more robust and generalizable AI agents for real-world robotics. This work represents a significant step towards leveraging the vast knowledge of LLMs in practical, dynamic, and physically constrained environments without incurring the prohibitive costs or safety risks associated with real-world data collection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind GLIMO stems from the inherent challenge of grounding Large Language Models (LLMs) in physical reality for robotics applications. Despite their linguistic prowess, LLMs lack direct physical experience, leading to difficulties in basic physical reasoning and execution of embodied tasks.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Relaxing the Perfect World Model Assumption:** To overcome the cost and safety concerns of acquiring embodied experiences from real or perfectly simulated environments, GLIMO aims to enable LLMs to learn effectively from readily available, low-cost, and interactive "imperfect world models" (e.g., simulators). The central hypothesis is that LLMs can learn useful, domain-invariant knowledge from these imperfect models that transfers to more complex, real-world (or "perfect") environments, despite distribution shifts.
2. **Automating High-Quality and Diverse Instruction Data Generation:** To address the scalability and manual effort required for instruction tuning, the research seeks to develop an automated LLM agent-based framework. This framework should be capable of autonomously navigating an imperfect world model, interpreting embodied experiences, and transforming them into a high-quality, diverse instruction tuning dataset in a scalable manner, without human intervention for specific task design.
3. **Enhancing LLM Generalization and Robustness:** The project aims to improve the performance of open-source LLMs on embodied tasks in target environments (perfect world models) by fine-tuning them with data generated from imperfect ones. This includes enhancing their ability to understand environment dynamics, anticipate action consequences, and generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

In essence, GLIMO seeks to provide a practical and scalable solution for physically grounding LLMs in robotics by leveraging readily available simulated environments and intelligent, autonomous data generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GLIMO (Grounding Large language model with Imperfect world MOdel) framework introduces a novel learning paradigm to fine-tune LLMs for embodied tasks using experiences sampled from imperfect world models. The core idea is to enable LLMs to learn domain-invariant knowledge from accessible proxy models (like simulators) and transfer it to real-world environments, thereby mitigating cost and safety concerns.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The paper formulates the problem as minimizing the loss of an LLM, $f_\theta(x)$, when trained on a dataset $\hat{D}$ sampled from an imperfect world model $\hat{\tau}$, with the goal of optimizing its performance in a real-world (perfect) environment $\tau$. This explicitly acknowledges the distribution shift between $\hat{\tau}$ and $\tau$ and aims to learn policies that generalize despite this shift. Two primary environments are used for validation: "Agent World" (a 2D strategic puzzle game) and "Urban Driving" (based on the CARLA simulator), each with imperfect and perfect versions to study the distribution shift.

#### 3.2. Generating Grounded Embodied Experiences with LLM Agents

The cornerstone of GLIMO is an **LLM agent-based data generator** that autonomously samples experiences from the imperfect world model and transforms them into instruction tuning datasets. This replaces manual annotation with an intelligent, scalable process. The LLM agent framework comprises:

- **Agent Integration:**
 - **Tool Module:** Allows the LLM to interact with the environment (e.g., object detection, collision detection) to acquire necessary information for decision-making.
 - **Memory Module:** A customizable module that retains past actions and observations, incorporating human prior knowledge about relevant historical information.
 - **Skill Library:** Encodes necessary low-level skills for the LLM to act in the physical world, used via function calling (e.g., `lane_change(lane_id)`).
- **Iterative Self-Refining Module:** The LLM agent iteratively executes a plan and evaluates its success. It compares the current plan with prior experiences, learns from environmental feedback, and refines its decisions. This process uses prompt engineering techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), ReAct, and Reflexion to facilitate reasoning, reduce error accumulation, and generate "n reasoning data" with voting for ground truth consistency. The goal is to improve the LLM’s performance on frequently accessed states and ensure temporal consistency.
- **Diverse Question-Answering Instruction Seeds:** A rich set of question-answering templates is used to generate instruction data across different temporal contexts, enhancing diversity and quality. These templates specifically aim for:
 - **Rationale Enhancement:** Encouraging the LLM to generate explanations for its decisions, promoting chain-of-thought fine-tuning and robust strategies.
 - **Counterfactual Reasoning:** Training the LLM to consider alternative outcomes and develop a deeper understanding of causality and consequences.
 - **Episodic Memory Question Answering:** Querying pre-recorded embodied experiences to assess the LLM's spatial and temporal understanding of a scenario (e.g., "Where is the altar you encountered most recently?").

#### 3.3. Retrieval-Augmented Experiences (RAG)

GLIMO incorporates a RAG pipeline to allow the LLM to self-reflect and self-improve by leveraging past experiences and promoting exploration. The framework retrieves previous related embodied experiences (tagged with strategy and outcome, e.g., "firstly get the yellow keys, open the door with the key, grab the hammers"), embeds these tags, and uses them to generate instruction data. This provides diverse, high-quality few-shot examples, especially useful in domains with abundant expert demonstrations. The LLM also labels the "surprising score" of instruction data for weighted sampling, prioritizing difficult samples. Domain-specific models (e.g., MTR transformer for urban driving scenarios) can also be used for retrieval.

#### 3.4. Annotation Generation and Filtering

The pipeline automatically transforms embodied experiences into grounded instruction data formats. Inspired by Self-Instruct, it starts with a small set of task templates and iteratively generates new instructions and instances, filtering out low-quality or similar generations. This self-refining, bootstrapping process, combined with constant feedback from simulators, ensures data diversity and quality. Filtering techniques include asking the LLM to determine novelty based on tags and using domain-specific models (e.g., VAE reconstruction loss for urban driving) to evict "easy" (in-distribution) samples, focusing on "hard negative data mining."

#### 3.5. Training

A two-stage fine-tuning approach is employed:
1. **Stage 1:** Improves the base model's instruction following capabilities using a general conversational QA dataset (e.g., Chat-QA), specializing in tabular data and text retrieval.
2. **Stage 2:** Fine-tunes the model with the GLIMO-generated grounded embodied experiences. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) techniques with 8-bit quantization are used to save computation resources and prevent catastrophic forgetting, often combined with Kullback-Leibler divergence regularization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate GLIMO across two distinct embodied environments: Agent World (a 2D strategic puzzle game) and Urban Driving (a CARLA-based self-driving simulator), each with imperfect and perfect versions. The evaluations focus on task completion rates, environment understanding (via QA accuracy), generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, and ablation studies on the framework's components.

1. **Performance Boosts for Open-Source LLMs:** GLIMO successfully fine-tunes strong open-source LLMs such as LLaMA-3 (8B and 70B), LLaMA-2-13B, and OPT-13B. The fine-tuned models achieve significant performance improvements:
 - **Task Completion:** Improvements of **2.04×, 1.54×, and 1.82×** were observed across three different benchmarks (Agent World, Urban Driving, Multi-Agent), respectively, compared to their base models.
 - **Competition with SOTA Closed-Source Models:** The fine-tuned LLaMA-3 models demonstrated the capability to **compete with or surpass GPT-4**, outperforming it by a large margin of **51.7% in Agent World** and **84.5% in Urban Driving** environments in task completion percentage (Figure 6a).
 - **Environment Understanding (QA Accuracy):** Fine-tuned models, especially LLaMA-3 variants, significantly improved their accuracy on environment-specific QA datasets, beating GPT-4 in both Agent-QA and Driving-QA. This demonstrates a better understanding of fine-grained environmental dynamics.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalization (OOD Scenarios):** The approach shows considerable improvements in OOD QA accuracy, indicating enhanced zero-shot generalization capabilities to unfamiliar scenarios, a crucial aspect for real-world deployment.

2. **Role of Imperfect World Model Data:**
 - Experiments exploring the ratio of imperfect to perfect world model data for training revealed that while increasing the proportion of perfect data enhances performance, the gains diminish rapidly beyond a ratio of 0.25.
 - Crucially, training with data predominantly from the imperfect world model actually **improves the generalization capability of LLMs, particularly in OOD scenarios**. This suggests that imperfect data can act as an effective "regularization" technique, mitigating overfitting common in LLM supervised fine-tuning (Figure 5).

3. **Ablation Studies:**
 - The ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of GLIMO's core components (Figure 7).
 - **Self-guided sampling and iterative refining** were found to significantly improve the quality of embodied experiences and, consequently, task success rates.
 - **Counterfactual QA and Episodic QA** formats markedly improved the reasoning capabilities of the fine-tuned LLMs by exposing them to novel states and hypothetical scenarios, leading to better exploration and successful attempts.
 - The **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) module** also played a vital role in enhancing successful attempts over prompt iterations.

4. **Data Quality and Annotating LLM:**
 - The quality of the instruction datasets is directly influenced by the capability of the LLM used for annotation. GPT-4 consistently achieved the optimal data annotation quality compared to LLaMA-3 8B and 70B, which in turn outperformed smaller models (Table 2). This highlights the importance of using powerful LLMs as annotating agents.

5. **Scaling Performance and Catastrophic Forgetting:**
 - Performance consistently improved as the parameter number of the base LLaMA-2 models increased (from 7B to 70B), indicating the scalability and potential of the approach with larger computation budgets (Table 1).
 - The fine-tuned models showed only a slight increase in perplexity on general language modeling tasks (PILE dataset), demonstrating that GLIMO largely **preserves the generality and language modeling capabilities** of the LLMs, successfully mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

In summary, GLIMO effectively addresses the physical grounding challenge for LLMs by leveraging imperfect simulators and an intelligent, automated data generation pipeline. It significantly boosts the performance and generalization abilities of open-source LLMs, allowing them to perform competitively or even surpass state-of-the-art closed-source models like GPT-4 in embodied tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GLIMO framework represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and the practical application of Large Language Models in robotics. Its contributions address several critical bottlenecks currently limiting the widespread deployment of LLM-powered agents in real-world scenarios.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **learn effectively from imperfect world models**. This relaxation of the "perfect simulator" assumption is a game-changer for robotics. Real-world data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and often unsafe. Perfect simulators are equally difficult and costly to develop. By demonstrating that LLMs can derive valuable, domain-invariant knowledge from simpler, more accessible simulators, GLIMO opens the door to **more cost-efficient and safer training paradigms**. This has profound implications for industries like autonomous driving, where extensive real-world testing is prohibitive.

Secondly, the **automated LLM agent-based data generator** is a major step towards scalable instruction tuning. Manual human annotation and task-specific dataset design are labor-intensive and hinder the application of LLMs to diverse, open-ended robotics tasks. GLIMO's self-refining, diverse instruction generation (including rationale, counterfactual, and episodic memory QA), and retrieval-augmented generation modules provide a blueprint for autonomously creating high-quality, task-relevant training data. This drastically **reduces the human effort required**, making it feasible to continually adapt and expand LLM capabilities for new environments and tasks.

The **empirical results** showcasing substantial performance boosts for open-source LLMs (LLaMA-3) to levels competitive with or exceeding GPT-4 are highly impactful. This demonstrates that advanced embodied reasoning capabilities are not exclusive to proprietary, massive models, but can be achieved with fine-tuning techniques on smaller, open-source alternatives. This democratizes access to powerful AI agents for robotics research and development, fostering innovation across a broader community. The observed improvement in **zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios** further underscores the robustness of the learned policies, which is vital for real-world robotics where unexpected situations are common. The finding that imperfect data can act as a "regularizer" to improve generalization is a counter-intuitive yet valuable insight for future model training strategies.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotics Development:** Researchers and developers can prototype and train LLM-driven robots more rapidly and economically using readily available simulators.
- **Enhanced Safety:** By learning from imperfect simulators, LLMs can be pre-trained in various hazardous scenarios without physical risk, leading to safer deployment in real-world applications.
- **Broader Accessibility of AI:** The ability to achieve high performance with open-source LLMs makes advanced AI capabilities more accessible to smaller research groups and companies.
- **More Generalizable Agents:** The framework pushes towards agents that can better transfer knowledge across varied, non-identical environments, which is a key step for truly autonomous and adaptable robots.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge key limitations, primarily the exclusive reliance on **simulated environments** for evaluation. While highly controlled and valuable for initial validation, real-world complexity often introduces unforeseen challenges. Additionally, the current LLMs accept **text-only inputs**, whereas real-world robotics inherently requires **multimodal perception** (vision, audio, tactile). The proposed future extension to multimodal LLMs represents the next logical and crucial step to fully realize GLIMO's potential in complex physical environments.

In conclusion, GLIMO offers a practical, scalable, and effective method for physically grounding LLMs in embodied environments. By strategically leveraging imperfect simulators and intelligent automated data generation, it paves the way for more robust, adaptable, and deployable AI agents in a wide range of robotics applications, addressing fundamental challenges in the field.
