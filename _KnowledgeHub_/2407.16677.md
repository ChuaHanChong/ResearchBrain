# From Imitation to Refinement -- Residual RL for Precise Assembly

> [!summary] Summary
> ResiP, a method developed at MIT's Improbable AI Lab, integrates imitation learning with reinforcement learning to enable robots to perform high-precision assembly tasks by adding real-time, closed-loop corrections to a base policy. This approach achieves near-perfect success rates on complex manipulation tasks in simulation and improves real-world performance for vision-based policies by up to 30 percentage points.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Behavior Cloning (BC) policies, especially with action chunking, exhibit performance saturation and unreliability for high-precision robotic assembly tasks due to distribution shift and a lack of real-time, closed-loop control.
- Directly fine-tuning complex BC architectures with reinforcement learning (RL) is often unstable or impractical due to large action spaces and potential catastrophic forgetting.
- Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) improves over BC but still saturates and lacks reactivity for precision-critical moments, falling short of expert-level performance.

## Method

- A pre-trained, ==frozen Behavior Cloning== (BC) policy acts as a long-horizon trajectory planner, providing coarse action sequences.
- **ResiP** uses a small, lightweight ==residual Gaussian MLP policy==, trained with ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==, to provide high-frequency, per-timestep, closed-loop corrections to the base BC plan, leveraging privileged state information in simulation.
- A ==sim-to-real transfer== pipeline leverages re-rendered synthetic data from the RL-trained state-based teacher combined with a small set of real-world demonstrations to train a vision-based student policy.

## Results

- ResiP boosted success rates on assembly tasks in simulation, such as `round table` from **12% (Diffusion Policy) to 94%** and `peg-in-hole` from **5% to 99%**, demonstrating superior precision.
- The method exhibited greater robustness to dynamic disturbances, experiencing only a **12% performance drop** under force perturbations compared to **19-26%** for chunk-based BC methods.
- Real-world deployment showed vision-based student policies co-trained with synthetic and real data achieved **50-60% success** on the `one leg` task, outperforming real-only policies (**20-30%**) and exhibiting increased robustness to visual variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-precision robotic manipulation requires frequent, real-time, closed-loop corrections, which open-loop action chunking in Behavior Cloning fundamentally lacks.
> - Augmenting a frozen, pre-trained BC policy with a small, specialized residual RL policy offers a stable and effective way to inject reactivity and overcome distribution shift without directly fine-tuning complex BC models.
> - Combining extensively randomized synthetic data, generated by re-rendering successful trajectories from an RL-trained teacher, with a small amount of real-world data significantly enhances the visual robustness and real-world performance of vision-based policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of imitation learning (Behavior Cloning, BC) and reinforcement learning (RL) for robotic manipulation, addressing the significant challenge of enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks with high precision and reliability.

**Current Landscape Challenges:**
1. **Behavior Cloning (BC) Limitations:** BC has gained popularity for its ease of teaching, allowing robots to learn new tasks from human demonstrations. Recent advancements, such as diffusion models (e.g., Diffusion Policy) and action chunking (predicting sequences of future actions), have enabled BC to tackle long-horizon and complex behaviors. However, a critical limitation emerges in tasks requiring high precision, such as assembly. The authors observe that BC performance saturates with increasing data, often failing due to:
 - **Distribution Shift:** As the robot policy executes actions, it may encounter states slightly different from the training demonstrations. These small deviations compound, leading the policy into "out-of-distribution" states where it performs suboptimally or fails.
 - **Lack of Closed-Loop Control (due to action chunking):** While action chunking helps with long horizons by effectively reducing the planning horizon, it involves executing a sequence of actions open-loop. This open-loop execution prevents the robot from making real-time, fine-grained adjustments based on immediate sensor feedback, which is crucial for precision tasks. The paper argues that chunked BC policies act more like "trajectory planners" than reactive controllers.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Challenges:** RL is a standard solution for overcoming distribution shifts by allowing policies to explore and learn in states encountered during execution. However, directly fine-tuning modern BC architectures (like diffusion models or action-chunked policies) with RL presents new challenges:
 - **Instability:** The iterative refinement of diffusion models and the large, high-dimensional action spaces introduced by action chunking (e.g., 8-fold increase in action dimensionality) can make standard RL algorithms unstable or require significant architectural modifications.
 - **Forgetting:** Fine-tuning large pre-trained models with RL can lead to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned capabilities.
3. **Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) Limitations:** DAgger, an interactive imitation learning approach, addresses distribution shifts by iteratively collecting expert demonstrations in states where the learned policy struggles. While improving performance over pure offline BC, DAgger still saturates and lacks reactivity for the same reason as chunked BC: its reliance on open-loop execution of action chunks.
4. **Existing Residual Learning:** Residual learning, where a learned component corrects the output of a base model (analytical or learned), is a proven technique in robotics. However, existing residual frameworks often don't explicitly address the combination of a low-frequency, open-loop "planner" with a high-frequency, closed-loop "corrector" in the context of complex, chunked BC policies.

**This Work's Contribution to the Landscape:**
This paper introduces **ResiP (Residual for Precise Manipulation)**, a novel approach that effectively combines the strengths of BC and RL to overcome these limitations. It bridges the gap by:
- Leveraging chunked BC for long-horizon planning, treating it as a "trajectory planner."
- Introducing a lightweight, RL-trained *residual policy* that provides high-frequency, closed-loop corrections to the base BC plan, thereby injecting reactivity and mitigating distribution shift.
- Designing a stable RL training pipeline that avoids the instability issues associated with directly fine-tuning complex BC models.
- Demonstrating a practical sim-to-real transfer pipeline, an essential aspect for real-world deployment.

In essence, this research provides a robust and data-efficient method to make robot skills learned via imitation more reliable and precise, particularly for challenging assembly tasks, by strategically integrating the complementary strengths of BC and RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable robots to reliably perform long-horizon, high-precision manipulation tasks, such as assembly, which remain a significant challenge in robotics. The authors are primarily motivated by the observed limitations of state-of-the-art Behavior Cloning (BC) methods when applied to such tasks.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

1. **Address the Unreliability of BC for Precision Tasks:** The authors observe that despite recent advances making BC easy to teach, it often results in unreliable performance for tasks requiring high precision. This unreliability stems from two critical factors:
 - **Compounding Errors from Distribution Shift:** As a BC policy operates in the real world, it inevitably encounters states slightly different from its training data. These small deviations can accumulate, leading to significant errors and task failure, especially in precise steps like insertions.
 - **Lack of Closed-Loop Corrective Control:** The prevalent use of action chunking in modern BC (predicting a sequence of future actions to be executed open-loop) sacrifices real-time reactivity. During critical moments (e.g., "bottleneck states" like insertions), the policy cannot make immediate adjustments to compensate for disturbances or minor inaccuracies in its planned actions. This makes chunked BC policies behave more like open-loop "planners" rather than dynamic "controllers."
2. **Overcome Performance Saturation of BC and DAgger:** The research aims to surpass the observed performance saturation of BC methods, where increasing the volume of demonstrations does not lead to a proportionate increase in success rates for precision tasks. Similarly, while DAgger mitigates distribution shift, it also suffers from performance saturation due to its inherent open-loop, chunked execution.
3. **Devise a Stable and Effective RL Fine-Tuning Approach:** While Reinforcement Learning (RL) is known for its ability to learn robust, reactive policies and mitigate distribution shifts, directly applying RL to fine-tune complex BC architectures (like diffusion models or action-chunked policies) is often unstable or impractical due to large action spaces and iterative policy generation. A core motivation is to find a way to incorporate RL's benefits without these drawbacks.
4. **Retain BC's Ease of Teaching and Long-Horizon Capabilities:** The authors want to improve reliability without sacrificing the advantages of BC, namely the ease of learning complex, long-horizon behaviors from a relatively small number of demonstrations. The solution should augment, not replace, these core strengths.
5. **Enable Practical Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Beyond simulation, the ultimate goal is to deploy these robust policies on physical robots. This necessitates a robust sim-to-real pipeline that can bridge the reality gap, allowing policies trained with privileged state information in simulation to operate effectively from sensory observations (e.g., RGB images) in the real world.

In summary, the key objective is to develop a "simple yet effective method, ResiP," that can transform BC-trained trajectory "planners" into reliable and reactive controllers by augmenting them with closed-loop, per-timestep corrections learned through stable reinforcement learning, all while maintaining the benefits of learning from demonstrations and enabling real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ResiP (Residual for Precise Manipulation) method is a three-component pipeline designed to transition from imitation learning to robust, precise assembly, with a clear path for sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The task is framed as a discrete-time sequential decision-making problem. The robot receives observations (o_t) corresponding to the underlying state (s_t) and outputs an action (a_t). The action space comprises 6-DoF end-effector poses and gripper commands. The state space includes robot configuration and object poses. Task completion is defined by a sparse reward signal (1 for successful assembly, 0 otherwise).

**B. Base Policy Learning via Behavior Cloning (BC)**
1. **Data Collection:** A small dataset of 50 demonstrations (D_sim) is collected in simulation by teleoperating the robot for each task. Each trajectory contains state-action pairs.
2. **Base Policy Training:** A base policy (π_base) is trained using Behavior Cloning on D_sim. The Diffusion Policy (DP) architecture is chosen, known for its strong BC performance.
3. **Action Chunking:** Consistent with state-of-the-art BC, action chunking is employed. The policy predicts an action chunk of T_a future actions (e.g., T_a = 32), but only a subset (T_exec, e.g., T_exec = 8) is executed open-loop. This design enables long-horizon control but, as identified by the authors, sacrifices reactivity. The base policy effectively acts as a "trajectory planner" operating at a coarse temporal resolution.

**C. Reactive Control via ResiP (Residual Reinforcement Learning)**
This is the core innovation where the frozen BC policy is augmented with a reactive, closed-loop residual component.
1. **Architecture:** The pre-trained, chunked π_base remains *frozen*. A small, single-step **residual Gaussian Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) policy** (π_res) is introduced.
2. **Residual Observation:** For each timestep *within* the base policy's action chunk, the residual policy receives an observation (s_res) consisting of the current full simulation state (robot proprioception and object poses) concatenated with the *base policy's predicted action for that specific timestep* (s_res_t+i = [s_t+i, a_base_t+i]).
3. **Corrective Action:** π_res then predicts a corrective action (a_res_t+i) at each control step (e.g., ~10Hz).
4. **Combined Action:** The final action executed in the environment is the sum of the base action and the residual correction: a_t+i = a_base_t+i + a_res_t+i.
5. **Training:** π_res is trained using **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, an on-policy RL algorithm, with sparse task-completion rewards.
 - **Key Design Choice:** The per-timestep correction (instead of chunk-level correction) is crucial for reactivity.
 - **Stability:** The residual policy uses orthogonal initialization with a small gain factor on the final layer, ensuring initial corrections are small and centered around zero. This design constrains corrections to be local adjustments, aiding stable RL training and preventing large, destabilizing policy updates.
 - **Observation Space for RL:** The simulation training for π_res uses privileged state information (end-effector pose/velocity, gripper state, and 6-DoF poses of all parts).

**D. Sim-to-Real Transfer (Policy Distillation)**
To deploy the policies on a physical robot using visual input, a teacher-student distillation approach is used.
1. **Teacher Policy:** The state-based, RL-trained ResiP (π_res) acts as the teacher policy (π_teacher).
2. **Synthetic Data Generation:** A dataset of successful trajectories (D_synth) is collected by running π_teacher from diverse initial object poses in simulation.
3. **Realistic Re-rendering:** D_synth (containing state information) is converted into trajectories with realistic sensory observations (RGB images) by re-rendering them using IsaacSim. This process allows for extensive visual variations (object colors, textures, lighting, camera perspectives) not easily achievable with standard augmentations or real-world data collection. This rendered dataset is denoted D_synth-render.
4. **Real-World Data Integration:** A small set of real-world demonstrations (D_real, 10-40 trajectories) is collected directly on the physical robot, providing RGB observations without ground-truth pose information.
5. **Student Policy Training:** A vision-based student policy (π_student) is trained using the Diffusion Policy architecture, equipped with a ResNet18 vision encoder pre-trained on robotic manipulation data (R3M). π_student is trained with the same BC loss as in simulation, but on the *combined dataset* D_real ∪ D_synth-render. This co-training balances domain coverage from synthetic data with real-world fidelity from actual demonstrations.

This comprehensive methodology allows ResiP to leverage the strengths of both imitation learning and reinforcement learning, producing highly precise and robust robotic skills while providing a scalable pathway to real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation highlights several key findings regarding the limitations of existing BC methods and the significant advantages of ResiP, both in simulation and real-world deployment.

1. **Behavior Cloning (BC) Performance Saturation:**
 - State-of-the-art BC methods, particularly the Diffusion Policy (DP) architecture, struggle with high-precision tasks. For example, on the `round table` assembly task, DP achieves only a 12% success rate with 50 demonstrations.
 - Crucially, performance saturates with increasing data. Even with 10,000 demonstrations, DP's success rate on `round table` only reaches 56%, and on `one leg`, it plateaus at around 80% with 100,000 demonstrations. This demonstrates a fundamental limitation of offline BC for precision-critical tasks.
 - MLP-based BC without chunking (MLP-S) consistently fails across most tasks, reinforcing the importance of chunking for long-horizon control, even if it introduces reactivity issues.

2. **DAgger Limitations:**
 - DAgger, which addresses distribution shift through online data collection, performs better than pure BC, reaching 71% success on `round table`.
 - However, DAgger also plateaus, falling significantly short of expert-level performance. This indicates that merely addressing distribution shifts without true closed-loop control is insufficient for precision.

3. **ResiP's Superior Performance and Reactivity:**
 - ResiP achieves remarkably high success rates across the task suite, starting from the same initial 50 demonstrations. For instance, it boosts `round table` success from 12% (DP) to 94% and `one leg` from 54% (DP) to 98%.
 - The most striking improvement is seen in the `peg-in-hole` task, which requires extreme precision (0.2mm clearance), where ResiP achieves 99% success compared to 5% for DP. This unequivocally demonstrates ResiP's effectiveness for tasks demanding precise local corrections.
 - **Failure Analysis:** Qualitative analysis shows DP failures are primarily due to small imprecisions (e.g., misalignment before insertion). ResiP effectively mitigates these by making subtle, real-time corrections (e.g., sideways translations to align before applying downward force), completely eliminating "Peg Precision" failures in some cases.
 - **Emergent Behaviors:** ResiP exhibits novel, more robust behaviors not present in the original demonstrations, such as alternative grasping strategies and more decisive recovery actions.

4. **Robustness to Dynamic Disturbances:**
 - Under random force perturbations, chunk-based methods (DP, DP-DAgger, ResiP-C) experience significant performance drops (19-26 percentage points).
 - In contrast, ResiP, with its per-timestep corrections, maintains much greater robustness, with only a 12% performance drop. This confirms that closed-loop control is vital for handling unexpected disturbances and achieving real-world robustness.

5. **Training Stability and Efficiency:**
 - ResiP demonstrates stable RL training, largely due to its architecture that constrains corrections to local adjustments and operates on a small residual network, avoiding the instability issues of directly fine-tuning complex BC models or handling large action spaces.
 - Alternative RL methods (PPO-C, IDQL) show more limited improvements and encounter training instabilities.

6. **Impact of Closed-Loop Control (Ablation):**
 - The ablation study comparing ResiP (per-timestep corrections) with ResiP-C (chunk-level corrections) shows that chunk-level corrections lead to significantly slower learning and lower asymptotic performance (92% for ResiP-C vs. 98% for ResiP). This highlights the critical role of frequent, real-time corrections for precision tasks.

7. **Sim-to-Real Deployment and Limitations:**
 - **Improved Real-World Performance:** Co-training vision-based student policies with a combination of synthetic (re-rendered from ResiP teacher) and real-world demonstrations (Real+Sim) significantly outperforms policies trained on real data alone (Real-Only). Real+Sim achieves 50-60% success on `one leg` compared to 20-30% for Real-Only policies.
 - **Visual Robustness:** Real+Sim policies show greater robustness to unseen visual variations (e.g., changing part colors from white to black) compared to Real-Only policies, which catastrophically fail.
 - **Distillation Bottleneck:** A performance gap remains between the state-based ResiP teacher (98%) and the vision-based student (73%) in the real world. This gap is attributed more to limitations in the *policy distillation process* itself rather than the visual modality or sim-to-real gap, as increasing synthetic data only marginally improves it.

In conclusion, ResiP successfully overcomes critical limitations of BC and RL, delivering highly reliable and precise robotic manipulation for assembly tasks in simulation and demonstrating promising real-world transfer capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "From Imitation to Refinement – Residual RL for Precise Assembly" carries significant implications for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence, offering a practical and robust pathway toward more capable robotic systems.

1. **Enabling High-Precision Robotic Manipulation:** The most immediate impact is the significant leap in performance for precision-critical tasks. By addressing the fundamental unreliability of Behavior Cloning (BC) in scenarios requiring tight tolerances and real-time adjustments, ResiP makes robot learning applicable to a broader range of industrial and domestic assembly tasks. This could accelerate automation in manufacturing, logistics, and even assistive robotics, where delicate and precise interactions are paramount.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Imitation Learning and Reinforcement Learning:** The paper provides an elegant and stable solution to integrate the strengths of BC and RL. BC's ease of teaching long-horizon behaviors from demonstrations is preserved, while RL's capacity for robustness, reactivity, and overcoming distribution shifts is effectively leveraged. This hybrid approach offers a paradigm that bypasses the challenges of direct RL fine-tuning on complex BC architectures, which has been a major bottleneck.

3. **Addressing Core Limitations of Current Robot Learning Methods:**
 - **Overcoming Performance Saturation:** The demonstration that BC performance saturates even with vast amounts of data is a critical insight. ResiP offers a clear method to break through this ceiling, demonstrating that closed-loop, reactive control is essential beyond mere data volume for precise tasks.
 - **Mitigating Distribution Shift:** By allowing the residual policy to learn online corrections in encountered states, ResiP effectively combats the compounding errors that arise from distribution shift, leading to more robust execution in varied environments.
 - **Injecting Reactivity into Open-Loop Plans:** The concept of augmenting a low-frequency trajectory "planner" (chunked BC) with a high-frequency, closed-loop "corrector" (RL-trained residual) is a powerful design pattern. This allows robots to execute complex plans while retaining the agility to adapt to unexpected dynamics or minor misalignments in real-time.

4. **Data Efficiency and Stability in RL Training:** ResiP achieves high performance with a remarkably small number of expert demonstrations (e.g., 50), making it a data-efficient approach. Furthermore, the residual framework, by constraining corrections to local adjustments around a frozen base policy, promotes stable RL training, a significant practical advantage over often-unstable direct RL fine-tuning methods.

5. **Practical Sim-to-Real Transfer Pipeline:** The demonstrated sim-to-real pipeline, which combines re-rendered synthetic data from the RL-trained teacher with a small amount of real-world data, offers a viable and scalable strategy for deploying complex skills on physical robots. This is crucial for real-world adoption, as training solely in reality is often prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. The ability to enhance visual robustness with randomized synthetic data is particularly impactful for real-world generalization.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper openly discusses current limitations, particularly the performance gap in policy distillation from state-based teachers to vision-based students, and the local nature of corrections under very high initial randomness. These identified challenges create clear and exciting avenues for future research, including:
 - Developing more effective online learning mechanisms for real-world policy distillation.
 - Exploring techniques for discovering and correcting large-scale execution errors beyond local adjustments.
 - Improving sim-to-real transfer techniques for vision-based policies, perhaps with more sophisticated domain randomization or adaptive methods.
 - Incorporating inductive biases for broader initial state distributions.

In essence, ResiP contributes a robust and intelligent framework that pushes the boundaries of what robots can reliably achieve in precise manipulation, laying groundwork for future autonomous systems capable of performing intricate tasks with human-like dexterity and adaptability.
