# HEXAR: a Hierarchical Explainability Architecture for Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> HEXAR is a plug-in hierarchical explainability framework for modular robots: specialised component explainers (LLM-based, causal, LIME, templated) each cover one module, an explainer selector picks and aggregates them per query. On a TIAGo home-assistant robot across 180 scenario-query variations, HEXAR beats end-to-end and all-components baselines on root cause identification, incorrect-fact avoidance, and runtime.

## Problem

- Module-specific explainability methods (navigation, manipulation, task planning) are evaluated in isolation and can't answer general cross-module 'why' questions users actually ask.
- Monolithic system-wide approaches feed all logs into one centralised LLM, discarding the modularity of robot architectures and any module-tailored explanation technique.
- Prior proposals for modular explainability architectures remain theoretical or partial, lacking a concrete orchestration mechanism connecting explainers to components and to each other.

## Method

- **HEXAR**: ==hierarchical architecture== pairing a set of ==specialised component explainers==, each a function over query and context returning natural language, with an ==explainer selector== module.
- Explainer selector implements a ==two-stage select-then-aggregate== process, using heuristics on skill failure/plan validity plus an ==LLM classifier== to route queries to the right component explainer(s).
- Five ==component explainers== instantiated per skill using distinct techniques: ==LLM-based== planner and navigation explainers, a ==causal counterfactual model== for ask-human-for-help, ==LIME== for pizza recommender, ==templated== for text-to-speech.

## Results

- HEXAR reaches **97%** mean root cause identification and **93%** combined explanation accuracy, versus **73%/66%** for end-to-end and **92%/67%** for all-components baselines.
- HEXAR shows lowest incorrect-fact rate at **7%** versus **28%** (end-to-end) and **32%** (all-components), with Cochran's Q and McNemar tests confirming significance across all three metrics.
- HEXAR runs in a mean **1.73** seconds versus **7.86s** (end-to-end) and **10.05s** (all-components), while selecting the correct component explainer in **179 of 180** cases (**99.44%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Routing a query to one specialised, correctly-selected explainer beats both a single monolithic LLM and naively aggregating every explainer's output.
> - Aggregating irrelevant component outputs injects incorrect facts even when the relevant explanation is correct, showing more context can actively hurt explanation quality.
> - A plug-in explainability layer that reuses existing modular robot interfaces (logs, topics) can add heterogeneous explanation techniques without modifying the underlying application.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The increasing complexity of robotic software systems, often structured as modular architectures, has amplified the demand for explainable decision-making. This demand aligns with the broader field of eXplainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), seeking to enhance human understanding and trust in autonomous systems.

Existing explainability approaches in robotics typically fall into two main categories:
- **Module-specific explanations:** Many efforts focus on providing explanations for individual robot modules or functionalities, such as addressee selection, navigation, manipulation, motion planning, or task planning. These approaches often employ diverse techniques, including attention maps, feature importance (e.g., LIME), Visual Language Models (VLM), or model-based contrastive explanations (e.g., PDDL plans). While effective for their specific modules, these methods are often evaluated in isolation and may not adequately address end-users' queries regarding high-level robot behaviors, which typically involve multiple interacting components. End-users often lack detailed knowledge of the robot's internal architecture and pose general "Why" questions, rather than queries directed at specific modules.
- **System-wide (monolithic) explanations:** Some approaches attempt to explain the behavior of an entire robotic system, often leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to process robot logs and diagnostic information, or assuming a single behavior tree representation for decision processes. Examples include frameworks like REFLECT, RONAR, and RACCOON, which aim to provide comprehensive summaries or explanations of system failures or actions. However, these monolithic approaches may not fully exploit the inherent modularity of robotic architectures. They often rely on a single, centralized explanation generation mechanism, potentially limiting the specialization and effectiveness of explanations for different types of modules that might benefit from tailored techniques. Some theoretical works have advocated for modular and hybrid models in explainability but have not fully implemented or validated such architectures in practice, grappling with challenges of orchestration, component connectivity, and data management.

HEXAR positions itself within this landscape by addressing a critical gap: the need for a hierarchical, modular explainability framework that can generate system-level explanations by orchestrating diverse, specialized explanation techniques. It aims to bridge the gap between isolated module-specific explanations and generalized monolithic approaches, leveraging the modularity of robot architectures to provide more accurate, relevant, and efficient explanations for complex robotic behaviors. This approach aligns with calls for hybrid and modular XAI architectures in robotics that integrate various explanation generation mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the efficacy of a hierarchical explanation system in accurately explaining complex and modular robotic systems, particularly when compared to monolithic alternatives. This objective is directly articulated by the research question: "Is a hierarchical explanation system, composed of specialised explainer modules orchestrated by a selector module, better at accurately explaining complex and modular robotic systems than equivalent monolithic systems?"

The motivation for this research stems from several challenges inherent in developing and deploying complex robotic systems:

- **Complexity of Robotic Architectures:** Modern robotic software systems are inherently complex, comprising numerous interconnected modules that perform diverse functionalities and interact through various interfaces. While end-to-end learning approaches exist, modular architectures remain prevalent due to their ability to provide structured, aggregated, and semantically meaningful intermediate information channels, which are crucial for understanding and debugging.
- **User Understanding and Trust:** For autonomous robots to be widely adopted and trusted, their decision-making processes and behaviors must be understandable to human users. This includes the ability to explain why a robot acted in a certain way, why it failed to achieve a goal, or why it made a particular choice.
- **Limitations of Existing Explainability Approaches:**
 - **Module-Centric Explanations:** Current explainability efforts often focus on individual robot modules. While useful for developers, end-users typically lack knowledge of the robot's internal architecture and are more interested in explanations of high-level behaviors that may involve multiple modules. They tend to ask general "Why" questions (e.g., "Why did the robot not go to the kitchen?") rather than module-specific queries.
 - **Monolithic System-Wide Explanations:** While some system-wide approaches exist (e.g., using centralized LLMs on robot logs or single behavior trees), they often do not fully exploit the modularity of robotic architectures. These methods may employ a single explanation generation technique across the entire system, which may not be optimal for explaining the diverse functionalities of different modules. There is an argument for modular architectures and hybrid models in robotics XAI.
- **Need for Tailored Explanations:** Different robot modules (e.g., navigation, task planning, interaction) may require distinct explanation techniques to effectively convey their internal logic or failure modes. A system that can dynamically select the most appropriate explainer for a given query and context would likely yield more accurate and relevant explanations.

By developing HEXAR, the researchers aim to provide a framework that addresses these motivations by offering a flexible, plug-in, and hierarchical approach to explanation generation, leveraging specialized explainers tailored to specific robot modules and orchestrating them through an intelligent selector.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces HEXAR, a hierarchical explainability architecture, and evaluates its performance against two baseline approaches in a specific robot assistance use case.

#### 3.1. HEXAR Framework Design

HEXAR is designed as a plug-in add-on to existing modular robot architectures. It consists of two primary components:

1. **Specialised Component Explainers (E):** These are individual modules responsible for generating explanations for one or more specific robot modules.
 - Each explainer `e` is defined by a function `f_e(q, C)` that takes a user query `q` and a context vector `C` and returns a natural language explanation `x_e`.
 - Component explainers observe a subset of robot events (`V_e`) and can employ diverse underlying explanation generation techniques (e.g., counterfactual reasoning, feature importance, templated responses, LLM-based reasoning, causal models).
 - They can also be designed to invoke other component explainers if a query requires understanding dependencies between modules.
 - A mapping (`M`) exists from robot modules to explainers, allowing flexibility (one explainer for multiple modules, multiple explainers for one module, or no explainer if not needed).

2. **Explainer Selector (s):** This component orchestrates the component explainers. Upon receiving a user query `q`, it performs three sequential functions:
 - **Selection:** It uses a selection function `select(q, V_s)` to identify and select an appropriate subset of component explainers (`E_s ⊆ E`). The selection logic can be heuristic (e.g., prioritizing failed modules) or classifier-based (e.g., using an LLM to interpret the query).
 - **Context Provision:** It computes a context vector (`C`) from the query `q` and observed events `V_s` and passes it to the selected explainers. This context can include task information, time windows, etc.
 - **Aggregation:** If multiple explainers are selected (`|E_s| > 1`), the selector aggregates their individual explanations `{x_e}` into a single, coherent natural language explanation `x` (e.g., using an LLM for summarization).

HEXAR leverages existing information flows (`V`) within the robot system, such as diagnostic logs or inter-module communication, without requiring modifications to the core robot application architecture.

#### 3.2. Implementation in a Home Assistant Use Case

To evaluate HEXAR, the researchers implemented it on a **TIAGo robot** performing assistive tasks in a home environment. The robot's capabilities were structured around five modules:
- **Task Planner:** Orchestrates high-level tasks.
- **Navigation:** Enables movement between rooms.
- **Text to Speech:** Handles verbal communication.
- **Ask Human for Help:** Manages interaction for assistance.
- **Pizza Recommender:** Suggests recipes based on ingredients.

For each of these modules, a specialized component explainer was implemented using distinct explanation generation mechanisms:
- **Component Explainer Planner:** Utilizes an LLM prompted with human instructions, generated plans, grounding errors, skill statuses, and the user query to explain task planning logic and issues.
- **Component Explainer Text to Speech:** Employs a simple, template-based strategy to explain timeout failures due to excessively long utterances.
- **Component Explainer Navigation:** Uses an LLM to process filtered ROS2 Nav2 logs, trained with examples of failure and suboptimal navigation scenarios.
- **Component Explainer Ask Human for Help:** Adapts a causal model approach to generate counterfactual explanations ("Y occurred because X=x. If X=x\*, Y\* would have occurred instead") and uses an LLM to convert these into natural language. It also provides templated explanations for specific negative conditions (e.g., high variance in human detection).
- **Component Explainer Pizza Recommender:** Employs LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) to identify the most relevant ingredients influencing a pizza recommendation.

The **Explainer Selector** was implemented as a two-stage process:
1. It first checks the robot's last plan for failed skill executions or an invalid plan. If detected, the corresponding component explainer (e.g., planner explainer for invalid plans) is automatically selected.
2. If no failure or invalid plan is found, an LLM processes the user's query to determine the most relevant component explainer.
This specific implementation typically selects only a single component explainer, simplifying the aggregation step. The context vector `C` includes the task, skill sequence, return statuses, plan validity, and time window. All LLM operations within HEXAR and the application used the Phi4 model (14B parameters) with greedy decoding (temperature=0) to ensure deterministic outputs.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Procedure

- **Metrics:**
 - **Root Cause Identification:** Binary (1 if ground truth root cause identified, 0 otherwise).
 - **Presence of Incorrect Facts:** Binary (1 if explanation contains false information, 0 otherwise).
 - **Combined Explanation Accuracy:** Binary (1 if root cause identified AND no incorrect facts, 0 otherwise).
 - **Runtime:** Time taken to compute the explanation (in seconds).
 - **Component Explainer Selection Accuracy:** (for HEXAR) Proportion of correctly identified component explainers.
- **Baselines:**
 - **End-to-End Baseline:** A single LLM parses all relevant logs and diagnostic information (the same information available to HEXAR) to generate an explanation in response to a query. This represents monolithic LLM-based approaches without specialized explainers or a selector.
 - **All Components Baseline:** A modification of HEXAR where *all* component explainers are triggered. An LLM then aggregates their outputs into a single explanation, prompted to select only relevant information. This baseline isolates the effect of specialized explainers from the explainer selector.
- **Test Cases:**
 - 20 distinct scenarios were identified in the home assistant use case, covering seven broad categories of situations requiring explanations (e.g., agent error, inability, unforeseen circumstances, social norm violation) across the five robot modules.
 - These scenarios were generated by modifying robot parameters (e.g., timeout in text-to-speech) or environmental conditions (e.g., obstacles, human refusal).
 - 60 task-specific scenario instances were created by varying user task instructions (3 variations per scenario).
 - For each instance, three types of explanation-seeking queries (generic, task-contextual, problem-specific) were formulated, resulting in 180 scenario-query variations.
 - All 60 scenarios were executed on the TIAGo robot and recorded as `rosbag` files. The same 180 `rosbag` executions were used for evaluating HEXAR and both baselines.
- **Annotation:** 540 explanations (180 for each of the three methods) were independently labeled by three co-authors. Annotators followed pre-discussed criteria for metrics, with low disagreement rates (0.93% for root cause identification, 1.30% for incorrect facts). A majority vote determined the final label.
- **Hardware:** Explanations were generated on a system with an 11th Gen Intel Core i5-11400H CPU, 16GB RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080 GPU (12GB memory).
- **Statistical Analysis:** Cochran’s Q test and pairwise McNemar tests with Holm correction were performed to assess statistical significance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HEXAR against the End-to-End and All Components baselines yielded the following results across 180 scenario-query variations:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Across Metrics

- **Root Cause Identification:**
 - HEXAR achieved a mean root cause identification rate of 97% (σ² = 0.16).
 - The All Components baseline achieved 92% (σ² = 0.27).
 - The End-to-End baseline achieved 73% (σ² = 0.45).
 - HEXAR demonstrated significantly higher root cause identification than both baselines (p < 0.001). The All Components baseline also significantly outperformed the End-to-End baseline (p < 0.001). This suggests that specialized explainers contribute to better root cause identification, and selecting a single relevant explainer further improves it.

- **Presence of Incorrect Facts:**
 - HEXAR achieved the lowest mean score (best) for incorrect facts at 7% (σ² = 0.26).
 - The End-to-End baseline had 28% (σ² = 0.45).
 - The All Components baseline had the highest score (worst) at 32% (σ² = 0.47).
 - HEXAR showed significantly lower rates of incorrect facts compared to both baselines (p < 0.001). There was no statistically significant difference between the two baselines for this metric. This indicates that including irrelevant information, either processed by a centralized LLM or aggregated from multiple explainers, tends to introduce erroneous details.

- **Combined Explanation Accuracy:** This metric combines root cause identification and absence of incorrect facts.
 - HEXAR achieved the highest mean accuracy at 93% (σ² = 0.26).
 - The All Components baseline scored 67% (σ² = 0.47).
 - The End-to-End baseline scored 66% (σ² = 0.48).
 - HEXAR demonstrated significantly higher combined explanation accuracy than both baselines (p < 0.001). The performance of the two baselines was not statistically significantly different. This highlights HEXAR's ability to provide both correct and factually accurate explanations.

- **Runtime:**
 - HEXAR exhibited the shortest mean runtime at 1.73 seconds (σ² = 1.66).
 - The End-to-End baseline took 7.86 seconds (σ² = 2.04).
 - The All Components baseline took the longest at 10.05 seconds (σ² = 1.62).
 - HEXAR was significantly faster than both baselines.

#### 4.2. Component Explainer Selection Accuracy (HEXAR)

- HEXAR's explainer selector achieved an accuracy of 99.44%, correctly identifying the appropriate component explainer in 179 out of 180 cases.
- One instance of incorrect selection occurred in a scenario where the planner missed a step, and the system wrongly selected the navigation component explainer instead of the planning component explainer.

#### 4.3. Explanation Accuracy Per Robot Module

When comparing combined explanation accuracy across the five robot modules:
- HEXAR generally maintained strong performance across all modules.
- The End-to-End baseline performed competitively in modules where its component explainers were LLM-based (e.g., navigation, planning) or where explanations were relatively simple (e.g., pizza recommender, text to speech). However, it struggled with more complex skills like "ask human for help," where a tailored causal model provided superior root cause identification.
- The All Components baseline showed poorer performance in most categories, with the exception of planning and pizza recommender. This was primarily attributed to the planner component sometimes introducing non-existent issues in the high-level plan, which were then incorporated into the final aggregated explanations, reducing accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and evaluation of HEXAR present several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of explainable robotics:

- **Addressing the Explanability Gap in Modular Robotics:** HEXAR directly confronts the challenge of explaining complex, modular robotic systems by proposing a hierarchical framework. This bridges the divide between narrowly focused module-specific explanations and often generalized, less precise monolithic system-wide approaches. By leveraging the inherent modularity of robot architectures, HEXAR allows for tailored explanation mechanisms to be applied where they are most effective.

- **Improved Explanation Quality and Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that HEXAR significantly outperforms both monolithic and aggregated baseline approaches in terms of accurately identifying root causes, avoiding the inclusion of incorrect information, and producing explanations more quickly. This enhanced accuracy and efficiency are critical for practical applications, as unclear or erroneous explanations can degrade user trust and hinder effective human-robot collaboration.

- **Flexibility and Heterogeneity of Explanation Techniques:** HEXAR's plug-in architecture supports the integration of diverse explanation generation techniques (e.g., LLM-based reasoning, causal models, feature importance, templated responses). This flexibility allows developers to select or design the most appropriate explanation method for each specific robot module or functionality, optimizing the quality and relevance of explanations without enforcing a single, universal approach. This approach encourages hybrid models for XAI in robotics.

- **Foundation for Transparent Autonomous Systems:** By providing a robust framework for generating accurate and context-aware explanations, HEXAR lays a foundation for building more transparent and trustworthy autonomous robotic systems. Enhanced transparency can lead to better understanding of robot capabilities and limitations, easier debugging, and improved human-robot interaction in various domains, from industrial automation to home assistance.

- **Guidance for Future XAI Architecture Design:** The study validates the benefits of a hierarchical and selective approach to explanation generation. This provides valuable insights and a concrete architectural blueprint for future research and development in explainable robotics, guiding how complex systems can be designed to be explainable from the outset.

- **Potential for Advanced Robotic Capabilities:** Beyond just explanations, the authors suggest that HEXAR could be extended to support proactive capabilities such as replanning, reactive failure recovery, prevention of issues, and the generation of corrective action recommendations for users. This indicates a potential pathway for explainability to evolve from a diagnostic tool into an integral part of autonomous decision-making and human-robot collaboration.

In summary, HEXAR offers a practical and empirically validated solution to a critical problem in robotics, advancing the state-of-the-art in explainable AI for complex, modular systems and contributing to the development of more understandable and reliable robots.
