# Reshaping Reasoning in LLMs: A Theoretical Analysis of RL Training Dynamics through Pattern Selection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Hong Kong conducted a theoretical and empirical analysis of reinforcement learning (RL) training dynamics in large language models (LLMs), revealing that RL primarily reshapes reasoning by optimizing the selection of existing reasoning patterns rather than altering their intrinsic success rates. The study characterizes two distinct convergence regimes for verifiable rewards (RLVR) and explains why internal feedback (RLIF) can lead to initial performance gains followed by degradation.

## Problem

- Despite empirical success, the precise mechanisms and training dynamics through which reinforcement learning enhances LLM reasoning remain largely opaque.
- There was a lack of comprehensive understanding of how granular token-level changes translate to higher-level reasoning pattern shifts during RL training.
- The distinct influences of different reward structures, such as verifiable rewards versus internal feedback, on LLM reasoning dynamics were not fully characterized.

## Method

- Conducted systematic empirical analysis of RL training in LLMs at both reasoning-pattern and ==token levels==, observing dynamic shifts across various models and datasets.
- Developed a formal mathematical framework that models the reasoning process as a two-stage operation: pattern selection followed by answer generation, assuming stable intrinsic pattern success rates.
- Theoretically analyzed the training dynamics and derived optimal policies for two common reward types: Verifiable Reward (RLVR) and Reinforcement Learning with Internal Feedback (RLIF).

## Results

- RLVR consistently leads to stable and continuous performance improvement, with models shifting towards adopting reasoning patterns that possess intrinsically higher success rates.
- RLIF exhibits unstable training dynamics, showing initial performance improvement followed by potential degradation, failing to specialize in more effective reasoning patterns.
- Token-level analysis revealed that RL training sparsely optimizes less than **10%** of critical tokens, indicating a selective modification of probabilities that reshapes reasoning pattern distributions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL's primary impact on LLM reasoning is to reweight the *selection* of existing reasoning patterns, as the intrinsic success rates of individual patterns remain stable during training.
> - RLVR training exhibits two distinct convergence regimes: rapid convergence when the base model is already high-quality, and a slow 'entanglement stage' when suboptimal patterns initially perform better than the overall accuracy.
> - RLIF optimizes for output confidence rather than correctness, leading to initial accuracy improvements but a 50% theoretical probability of converging to the least accurate reasoning path long-term.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have led to their remarkable success in complex reasoning tasks, from mathematics to programming, with reinforcement learning (RL) serving as a pivotal technique for enhancing these capabilities. Models like Gemini 2.5, Qwen3, and DeepSeek-R1 exemplify this trend, demonstrating human-like performance on challenging logical problems. However, despite RL's empirical effectiveness, the precise mechanisms and training dynamics through which it reshapes LLM behavior remain largely opaque. This opacity presents a critical challenge for further development, reliable deployment, and theoretical understanding of advanced AI systems.

Previous research efforts have attempted to demystify RL in LLMs from various angles. Some studies have compared pass@k performance, questioning whether RL truly elicits novel reasoning patterns or merely refines existing ones. Others have investigated the role of policy entropy, suggesting that RL-based methods can reduce entropy and link it to model performance. A notable line of inquiry has shown that RL primarily optimizes a sparse subset of "critical tokens," implying a targeted rather than a holistic modification of the model's output generation. Yet, despite these valuable insights, a comprehensive understanding of the *entire training process*—how these granular token-level changes translate to higher-level reasoning pattern shifts and how different reward structures influence these dynamics—has been lacking.

This paper directly addresses this gap by providing a systematic empirical and theoretical analysis of RL training dynamics in LLMs. It moves beyond isolated observations to offer a cohesive explanation, distinguishing itself by integrating both fine-grained token-level analysis with higher-level reasoning-pattern-level observations throughout the training trajectory. Furthermore, it develops a formal mathematical framework to theoretically model these dynamics for two prevalent RL reward types: verifiable reward (RLVR) and model's internal feedback (RLIF). By doing so, it provides a much-needed bridge between empirical phenomena and theoretical principles, contributing significantly to the foundational understanding of how RL refines LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to elucidate the training dynamics of reinforcement learning in Large Language Models, particularly regarding their reasoning capabilities. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive explanation of this complex process through a dual approach: rigorous empirical analysis and robust theoretical modeling.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical knowledge gap. While RL has demonstrated remarkable success in elevating LLMs' performance on challenging logical tasks, the underlying mechanisms governing its training dynamics remain largely unclear. Understanding *how* RL achieves these improvements—at what level (tokens, patterns) and under what conditions—is crucial for several reasons:
1. **Advancing Theoretical Understanding:** A deeper theoretical grasp of RL's impact on LLMs can inform the design of more effective and principled training algorithms.
2. **Improving Practical Applications:** Insights into training dynamics can help practitioners diagnose issues, optimize hyperparameters, and predict model behavior, especially for different reward structures.
3. **Enhancing Interpretability:** Unpacking the internal workings of RL-enhanced LLMs can improve their interpretability, making their reasoning processes more transparent and trustworthy.

Specifically, the paper sets out to:
- Empirically analyze RL training at both reasoning-pattern and token levels to observe dynamic shifts.
- Develop a theoretical framework that models the reasoning process and the training dynamics of RL.
- Theoretically analyze two common reward types: verifiable reward (RLVR) and model's internal feedback (RLIF), characterizing their distinct convergence behaviors.
- Validate theoretical predictions through controlled experiments and numerical simulations.

By addressing these objectives, the authors seek to bridge the gap between observed successes of RL in LLMs and the fundamental understanding of *why* and *how* these successes occur.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a comprehensive methodology that integrates empirical experimentation with a formal theoretical framework to analyze RL training dynamics in LLMs.

#### 3.1. Empirical Exploration

The empirical phase is structured to provide a systematic, multi-level analysis of RL training:

- **Training Procedure Overview:** The authors first compare the overall performance trajectories of models trained with two typical reward functions: Verifiable Reward (RLVR), where rewards are based on correctness, and Reinforcement Learning with Internal Feedback (RLIF), which uses intrinsic model-derived signals (specifically, negative average KL divergence between a uniform distribution and the model's next-token distribution). They use Qwen2.5-3B as the base model and train it on the MATH dataset, keeping other settings consistent.
- **Reasoning-Pattern Level Analysis:** To understand performance disparities, responses from the base model are collected and categorized into distinct reasoning patterns using GPT-4o, based on keywords and logical structure. These classifications are then used to analyze the distribution and intrinsic success rates of these patterns throughout the RL training process for both RLVR and RLIF. This analysis is conducted at regular intervals (e.g., every 20 steps) to observe dynamics. Additional experiments validate these findings across different models (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct) and datasets (GSM8K, AIME, AMC23, OlympiadBench).
- **Token-Level Analysis:** Inspired by prior work, the authors conduct a token-level analysis to uncover the granular mechanisms driving reasoning pattern changes. They sample responses from the base model for various datasets (GSM8K, MATH, AIME). For these identical responses, they compare the probability ranks of individual tokens in both the base model and RL-enhanced models. This helps determine how many tokens experience rank shifts during training, indicating which parts of the generation process are actively optimized.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Characterization of RL Training Dynamics

Building upon the empirical insights, the authors develop a mathematical framework:

- **Abstract Reasoning Process:** Reasoning is formalized as a two-stage process: (1) selecting a reasoning pattern `r` given a question `q` (`π(r|q)`), and (2) generating an answer `a` based on the chosen pattern (`π(a|r,q)`). The core assumption (Assumption 5.1) states that the success rate for each reasoning pattern `r_i` to provide a correct answer remains constant during training, implying that RL primarily optimizes the pattern selection stage.
- **Policy Parameterization and Optimization:** A simplified "tabular policy parameterization" is adopted, where each output (token or reasoning pattern) has its own trainable logit, allowing for analytical tractability. The optimization objective is the standard RL objective with a KL divergence regularization term to prevent excessive deviation from the base policy. The analysis proceeds by examining the policy gradient in the small learning rate limit, using gradient flow to model parameter evolution over time.
- **Optimal Policy Derivation:** The paper derives the optimal policy for both RLVR and RLIF under a general autoregressive policy, demonstrating how `π_opt(r|q)` is related to the reference policy and the reasoning path reward `R(r)`. For RLVR, `R(r)` is the pattern's success rate; for RLIF, it's the confidence of the final answer distribution.
- **RLVR Training Dynamics Analysis:** The theoretical analysis characterizes two distinct convergence regimes for RLVR, depending on the base model's initial reasoning quality:
 - **Regime 1 (Efficient Convergence):** When the base model's overall accuracy exceeds the success rate of all non-optimal patterns, RLVR rapidly guides the model to select the optimal reasoning pattern.
 - **Regime 2 (Slow Convergence/Entanglement Stage):** When a suboptimal pattern has a higher success rate than the initial overall accuracy, the model undergoes an "entanglement stage," where optimization towards the optimal pattern is significantly slowed before eventually transitioning to Regime 1 dynamics.
- **RLIF Theoretical Explanation:** The paper theoretically explains RLIF's observed behavior: initial performance improvement, followed by potential degradation. It shows that for a "well-trained LLM" (where the correct answer has the highest probability), the accuracy derivative at initialization is positive. However, it also proves that there's a 50% chance the model converges to the *least* accurate reasoning path in the long run because RLIF optimizes for confidence (low entropy), not necessarily correctness.

#### 3.3. Case Studies and Numerical Simulations

To validate the theoretical findings, the authors present:
- **Case Studies for RLVR:** Illustrating the rapid convergence (Regime 1) and the entanglement phase (Regime 2) with simulated pattern dynamics.
- **Numerical Simulations for RLIF:** Conducting multiple training simulations with uniformly distributed pattern success rates to confirm the probability of initial performance gains and the likelihood of converging to degraded states.

This multi-faceted approach, combining empirical evidence with mathematical rigor, provides a robust understanding of the intricate training dynamics of RL in LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, encompassing both empirical observations and theoretical derivations, which collectively shed light on how RL reshapes reasoning in LLMs.

#### 4.1. Empirical Findings

1. **Distinct Performance Trajectories of RLVR and RLIF:**
 - **RLVR (Verifiable Reward):** Consistently leads to stable and continuous performance improvement on tasks like MATH, with the model's accuracy gradually converging to a higher level.
 - **RLIF (Internal Feedback):** Exhibits unstable training dynamics. While it initially improves performance in early stages, continued training can lead to a performance drop, potentially resulting in a model worse than the original base model.
2. **Reasoning Pattern Dynamics:**
 - **RLVR:** Models trained with RLVR consistently shift towards adopting reasoning patterns that possess intrinsically higher success rates, thereby explaining the overall accuracy improvement.
 - **RLIF:** Fails to specialize in more effective reasoning patterns, contributing to its unstable and potentially degrading performance.
 - **Stable Intrinsic Pattern Success Rates:** A crucial observation is that the intrinsic success rate of *individual* reasoning patterns remains remarkably stable throughout the training process for both RLVR and RLIF. This suggests that RL primarily reweights the *selection* of patterns rather than fundamentally altering the patterns themselves.
3. **Sparse Critical Token Optimization:** Token-level analysis reveals that RL primarily optimizes a sparse subset of critical tokens. In most cases, less than 10% of tokens experience a rank shift during training. This indicates that RL selectively modifies the probabilities of a small fraction of tokens, which in turn reshapes the distribution of reasoning patterns, ultimately affecting overall model performance.

#### 4.2. Theoretical Findings

The empirical observations motivated a theoretical framework and subsequent analyses:

1. **Optimal Policy Characterization:**
 - **RLVR:** The optimal policy increases the probability of selecting reasoning patterns `r` that have a higher success rate `p*(r)`. In the limit of small β (a hyperparameter controlling KL divergence), the policy deterministically selects the pattern with the highest success rate.
 - **RLIF:** The optimal policy optimizes for the "confidence" of the final answer distribution (negative average log-likelihood of answers given a reasoning pattern), rather than direct correctness. This means RLIF aims for low-entropy distributions over answers, which doesn't guarantee accuracy.
2. **Two Distinct Convergence Regimes for RLVR:**
 - **Regime 1 (Efficient Convergence):** If the base model's initial overall accuracy `ACC_ref` is already higher than the success rate of all suboptimal reasoning patterns, RLVR leads to rapid and efficient convergence to the optimal reasoning pattern `r*`. The convergence time is polynomial in 1/ϵ (the desired accuracy gap from 1).
 - **Regime 2 (Slow Convergence / Entanglement Stage):** If a suboptimal reasoning pattern `r'` has a higher success rate than the initial `ACC_ref`, the model first enters an "entanglement stage." During this period, the optimization process for `r*` is significantly slowed, potentially requiring a super-exponentially long training time `T0` before `ACC_t` surpasses `p*(r')`, allowing it to transition into Regime 1 dynamics. This highlights the importance of base model quality for RLVR efficiency.
3. **RLIF's Initial Improvement and Long-term Degradation:**
 - **Initial Accuracy Gains:** For well-trained LLMs where the correct answer has the highest probability, the theoretical analysis shows that the derivative of overall accuracy at the initial training step is positive with probability 1 (as the number of reasoning patterns |R| approaches infinity). This provides a theoretical justification for RLIF's observed early performance improvements.
 - **Potential Long-term Degradation:** However, the analysis also reveals that with a probability of 0.5, the RLIF policy converges to the reasoning pattern `r` that *minimizes* `p*(r)`. This is because RLIF optimizes for confidence, and a very confident (low entropy) but incorrect reasoning path can receive a high reward, leading to convergence to the least accurate strategy. This aligns with the empirical observation of performance degradation with continued RLIF training.

#### 4.3. Validation

Case studies and numerical simulations extensively validate these theoretical predictions, illustrating the rapid vs. entanglement phases of RLVR and confirming the probabilistic behavior of RLIF's initial gains and potential degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in understanding the intricate mechanisms through which reinforcement learning influences the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models. By offering a unified view that connects empirical observations with rigorous theoretical explanations, the paper provides both foundational insights and practical guidance for the field.

**Theoretical Significance:**
- **Bridging Empirical and Theoretical Gaps:** The study successfully bridges a critical gap in LLM research by moving beyond anecdotal observations of RL's success to provide a systematic, multi-level analysis supported by mathematical proofs. This enhances the scientific rigor and interpretability of LLM development.
- **Mechanistic Understanding of Reward Types:** By theoretically dissecting RLVR and RLIF, the paper offers a fundamental understanding of *why* different reward structures lead to vastly different training dynamics and outcomes. This clarifies the specific signals each reward type optimizes for (correctness vs. confidence).
- **Characterization of Convergence Behaviors:** The identification of distinct convergence regimes for RLVR (rapid vs. entanglement-driven slow convergence) based on base model quality provides a nuanced theoretical model for predicting and explaining RL training efficiency. This is a crucial insight for optimizing complex, non-convex learning landscapes.
- **Explaining RLIF Paradoxes:** The theoretical explanation for RLIF's dual behavior—initial improvement followed by potential degradation—resolves an apparent paradox observed in practice. It highlights the inherent risk of optimizing purely for internal confidence without external grounding in correctness.

**Practical Impact:**
- **Informed RL Training Strategies:** Practitioners can leverage these findings to make more informed decisions when applying RL to LLMs. For RLVR, understanding the two convergence regimes implies that a high-quality base model is essential for rapid and efficient optimization. For weaker base models, engineers should anticipate an initial "entanglement stage" and potentially adjust training schedules or base model pre-training.
- **Careful Reward Model Design:** The insights into RLIF underscore the perils of relying solely on internal feedback for complex reasoning tasks. It reinforces the importance of designing reward models that genuinely correlate with desired external metrics (like correctness) rather than just internal consistency or confidence. This could lead to hybrid reward approaches or more sophisticated internal feedback mechanisms.
- **Diagnostics for Training Issues:** The analysis provides diagnostic tools. If an RLVR model is converging slowly, it might be stuck in an "entanglement stage," prompting investigation into the base model's initial reasoning patterns. If an RLIF model degrades after initial gains, the theoretical framework explains *why* this might be happening.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The formal two-stage reasoning framework and the analytical techniques employed can serve as a foundation for future theoretical work on LLM reasoning. This could include analyzing more complex reasoning patterns, alternative reward functions, or different policy architectures. The identified limitations (interpretability of LLM-identified patterns, framework complexity, specific base model assumptions) also explicitly point towards avenues for continued research.

In summary, this paper not only deepens our understanding of how RL contributes to LLM reasoning but also provides actionable insights for developing more robust, efficient, and reliable LLM training methodologies.
