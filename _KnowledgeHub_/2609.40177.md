# Social-WM: Safety-Aware Latent World Models for Robot Social Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Social-WM is a latent world-model planner for robot social navigation, trained offline from egocentric RGB video. It extends a LeWM-style predictor over frozen DINOv2 tokens with an inverse-dynamics head supervised on the motion the robot actually achieved, not the commanded motion. It then rejects proposed action chunks whose imagined commanded-versus-achieved gap is large. On Social-HM3D it reaches 63.77% success with 21.67% human collisions.

## Problem

- Future-aware social navigation methods like Falcon predict pedestrian trajectories explicitly, which relies on human-state prediction rather than learning safety consequences directly from egocentric RGB experience.
- NavThinker's action-conditioned world model only feeds imagined features into an online-trained DD-PPO policy and never uses imagined transitions to judge whether a candidate action is executable.
- Standard inverse dynamics recovers the recorded command and ignores that obstacles or pedestrians can block or limit motion, so the commanded action and the realized action silently diverge.

## Method

- **Safety-aware latent world model**: frozen ==DINOv2-small== patch tokens pooled to 16 latents, with a ==six-layer transformer predictor== trained by teacher-forced, multi-step open-loop and regularizer losses on the futures actually observed.
- ==Realizable inverse dynamics==: a head that maps the latent state and the latent transition difference to the realized action derived from odometry, not the nominal command, trained jointly with the prediction losses.
- Propose-imagine-evaluate-select planner: a goal-independent ==CVAE== samples eight action chunks and the world model rolls them out. A threshold on the commanded-versus-realized residual sets admissibility, then a position-goal or image-goal cost picks the chunk.

## Results

- On Social-HM3D: **63.77%** success, **94.37%** personal-space compliance and **21.67%** human collisions, versus NavThinker's **59.46%** success and **39.09%** collisions, a **44.6%** relative collision reduction.
- In zero-shot transfer to Social-MP3D it keeps the highest success rate (**54.15%**) and lowest human collisions (**23.34%**). CVAE planning runs at **51 ms** per step versus **139 ms** for LeWM-style MPC.
- Ablation: the realizable inverse-dynamics loss alone cuts collisions from **44.97% to 42.12%**, and adding discrepancy-based safety evaluation brings them to **31.33%**. The residual separates blocked from free transitions at **0.961** AUROC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The gap between commanded and realized action is a free self-supervised safety label. Training on futures that actually occurred turns blocked executions into constraints the model can learn.
> - Using a world model as a feasibility checker on candidate plans, rather than as feature input to a policy, can give large safety gains from offline data alone.
> - Short-horizon reasoning about whether actions can be executed reduces collisions but not timeouts. Persistent blockages need route-level replanning or spatial memory on top of local world-model planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot that shares space with pedestrians must anticipate whether its command can actually be carried out when pedestrians or obstacles constrain its motion. A forward command may work in open space, but near a person the same command may be stopped or constrained. Social-WM imagines the result of a proposed command. It then checks whether the motion in that imagined future matches the command.

The method separates the **nominal action**, which is the command issued, from the **realizable action**, which is the motion that actually occurs. The authors present this gap as a safety signal, not merely execution noise. In simulation, Social-WM improves goal-reaching and social-safety results over the closest comparison, but it does not improve every efficiency measure.

Each of the two scenarios shows a ground-truth observation sequence (T=0–7) above a Social-WM predicted-future sequence. The predicted latent states are visualized using nearest-neighbor dataset observations, not exact reconstructed frames. In the top-down views, the solid purple line is the proposed nominal trajectory and the dashed orange line is the inverse-dynamics-predicted realizable trajectory. The yellow dot is the pedestrian, the large yellow circle is the safety region, and the black circle is the robot's start. This is a qualitative visualization, not a quantitative safety test.

### 2. From command to achievable motion

Social-WM learns from RGB sequences. After a command, it predicts the **actual observed next frame's latent representation**, rather than a hypothetical future in which the command was fully executed. It also trains inverse dynamics, the model that infers the action associated with a state transition. This model learns to predict motion recovered from consecutive robot poses, not simply the command that was issued.

At deployment, Social-WM proposes action chunks, imagines their future latent transitions, and estimates their realizable actions. It treats a large nominal–realizable discrepancy as a reason to reject a candidate. It then chooses among the admissible candidates based on goal progress and executes only the first action before replanning.

This differs from [[2603.15359|NavThinker]], which already uses action-conditioned imagined futures for social navigation. Social-WM's distinction is therefore not simply that it predicts the future. Instead, it explicitly asks whether the imagined motion is consistent with the proposed command, and it uses that discrepancy to screen action chunks.

The training data include a keep-out gate that rejects commands entering pedestrians, producing blocked or constrained examples in 18.1% of transitions. The model receives no explicit human states or keep-out signals as inputs, but it learns from safety-shaped experience.

### 3. Does the discrepancy help?

Success rate (SR) is the fraction of episodes that reach the goal. SPL also accounts for path efficiency. Personal Space Compliance (PSC) measures compliance with pedestrians' personal space. H-Coll is the fraction of episodes involving a human collision.

The ablation uses a fixed image-subgoal/MPC comparison on Social-HM3D, with results changing in stages:

- **Latent-world-model baseline:** 51.33% SR and 44.97% H-Coll.
- **Adding realizable inverse-dynamics training, without safety evaluation:** 53.08% SR and 42.12% H-Coll.
- **Adding explicit discrepancy-based safety evaluation:** 56.08% SR and 31.33% H-Coll.

The comparison supports complementary benefits from the training objective and from its use in planning. It does not establish that the discrepancy is the only cause of the improved safety.

Image-subgoal setting with the MPC planner. SR, SPL, PSC, and H-Coll are listed for each configuration: Latent WM 51.33, 26.49, 90.26, 44.97; + Realizable ID 53.08, 30.16, 90.34, 42.12; Social-WM 56.08, 32.72, 91.34, 31.33.

On the standard final-position-goal Social-HM3D comparison, Social-WM and NavThinker report:

MetricSocial-WMNavThinkerSR63.77%59.46%PSC94.37%89.91%H-Coll21.67%39.09%SPL49.62%55.00%

Social-WM therefore improves success and the two reported social-safety measures, but its SPL is lower than NavThinker's. The authors also report the best SR and lowest H-Coll for Social-WM in zero-shot Social-MP3D transfer, without retraining or adaptation.

### 4. Safety is local

Social-WM changes short-horizon decision-making by checking predicted realizability before execution. The ablation supports using the nominal–realizable distinction during planning. These results come from Habitat simulation, not a real-robot deployment. They support a promising learned safety signal, not a guarantee against collision. The remaining failures include timeouts when a pedestrian persistently blocks a route, because short-horizon safety reasoning does not provide long-horizon route replanning.
