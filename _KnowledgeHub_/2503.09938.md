# PanoGen++: Domain-Adapted Text-Guided Panoramic Environment Generation for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> PanoGen++ generates realistic, diverse panoramic environments for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks by integrating domain-specific knowledge into pre-trained diffusion models. This approach leads to improved performance of VLN agents, including a 2.44% increase in success rate on the R2R benchmark and enhanced metrics on R4R and CVDN.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents suffer from limited generalization capabilities due to the scarcity of diverse training environments, relying on finite datasets like Matterport3D.
- Existing data augmentation techniques and basic generative models (e.g., PanoGen) lack the domain-specific adaptations required to produce environments that accurately reflect visual characteristics and spatial relationships necessary for effective VLN training.
- Fully fine-tuning large pre-trained diffusion models for domain adaptation is computationally expensive, limiting accessibility for researchers with constrained compute resources.

## Method

- Introduces **PanoGen++**, a framework that integrates pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models with domain-specific fine-tuning for generating panoramic environments.
- ==Applies Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== to efficiently adapt the Stable Diffusion model to the VLN domain, focusing on attention layers using a VLN-specific image-text dataset.
- Employs two distinct environment generation methods: masked image inpainting and recursive image outpainting, which are then used to augment VLN agent training.

## Results

- Improved success rate on the Room-to-Room (R2R) test leaderboard by **2.44%** compared to state-of-the-art approaches.
- Achieved a **0.63%** improvement on the Room-for-Room (R4R) validation unseen set and a **0.75**-meter enhancement in goal progress on the Cooperative Vision-and-Dialog Navigation (CVDN) validation unseen set.
- Generated panoramic environments exhibit high visual fidelity and semantic coherence, enhancing the training of VLN agents and demonstrating versatility for both indoor and outdoor scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating domain-specific knowledge into pre-trained diffusion models significantly improves the realism and utility of generated environments for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks.
> - Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, specifically LoRA, effectively adapt large generative models like Stable Diffusion to specialized domains (VLN environments) with reduced computational cost.
> - A combination of masked image inpainting and recursive image outpainting provides flexible strategies for generating diverse panoramic environments, balancing consistency with original scenes and creating entirely novel ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is an embodied AI task that requires agents to navigate through environments following natural language instructions. A persistent challenge in this field is the limited availability of diverse training environments, which restricts the generalization capabilities of navigation agents. PanoGen++ addresses this limitation by generating realistic, diverse panoramic environments specifically tailored for VLN tasks.

This paper, authored by researchers from Tianjin University, Harbin Institute of Technology, and the Defense Innovation Institute, introduces a novel framework called PanoGen++ that builds upon previous environment generation methods. By incorporating domain-specific knowledge into pre-trained diffusion models, PanoGen++ creates panoramic environments that significantly enhance the performance of VLN agents across multiple benchmarks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Background and Motivation

Vision-and-Language Navigation requires agents to interpret natural language instructions and navigate through 3D environments accordingly. Current VLN datasets, such as Room-to-Room (R2R), Room-for-Room (R4R), and Cooperative Vision-and-Dialog Navigation (CVDN), are built upon a limited number of 3D scans from the Matterport3D dataset. This limitation creates a challenge for training models that can generalize to unseen environments.

Previous approaches to address this data scarcity include:

1. **Data augmentation methods**: Techniques like EnvDrop, REM, and EnvEdit modify existing environments or combine them in novel ways.
2. **Basic generative approaches**: PanoGen, a predecessor to this work, uses text-to-image generation but lacks domain-specific adaptations.

Despite these advances, there remains a gap in generating environments that accurately reflect the visual characteristics and spatial relationships needed for effective VLN training. PanoGen++ aims to fill this gap by incorporating domain-specific knowledge into the generation process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Methodology

PanoGen++ integrates pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models with domain-specific fine-tuning to create panoramic environments tailored for VLN tasks. The framework consists of several key components:

1. **Data preparation**: The framework starts by discretizing panoramic images from the Matterport3D dataset into sub-views and generating textual descriptions for each using BLIP-2, a vision-language model.

2. **Domain adaptation**: The framework fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion model using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), a parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique that focuses on modifying attention layers in the U-Net architecture.

3. **Environment generation**: PanoGen++ employs two distinct methods for generating panoramic environments:
 - Masked image inpainting
 - Recursive image outpainting

4. **Agent training**: The generated environments are incorporated into the training of VLN agents, where a portion of the original observations is replaced with the generated panoramic environments.

#### 3.2. Domain Adaptation Techniques

A core contribution of PanoGen++ is its approach to domain adaptation, which enables the generation of environments that are specifically suited for VLN tasks. This adaptation process involves:

1. **Creation of a VLN-specific dataset**: The authors construct a dataset of image-text pairs by extracting sub-view images from the Matterport3D dataset and generating descriptions using BLIP-2.

2. **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)**: Rather than fine-tuning the entire diffusion model, which would be computationally expensive, PanoGen++ uses LoRA to efficiently adapt the Stable Diffusion model to the VLN domain.

3. **Attention layer modification**: The adaptation focuses on the attention layers in the U-Net architecture, which helps the model learn the specific visual characteristics and spatial relationships found in indoor navigation environments.

The impact of different LoRA rank values on the model performance is carefully analyzed, with the authors finding that a rank of 64 provides the optimal balance between performance and computational efficiency, as shown in the ablation studies.

#### 3.3. Environment Generation Approaches

PanoGen++ implements two distinct approaches for generating panoramic environments:

1. **Masked Image Inpainting**:
 - The framework retains a center crop from each sub-view of a panorama
 - The surrounding areas are masked
 - The model inpaints the masked areas using the corresponding text descriptions
 - This approach preserves some original visual information while generating new content

2. **Recursive Image Outpainting**:
 - Starts with generating a single sub-view based on a text description
 - Iteratively outpaints adjacent areas by shifting the generation window
 - Uses text descriptions for each adjacent sub-view
 - The generated sub-views are stitched together to form a complete panorama

Each approach has its advantages: inpainting maintains more consistency with the original environments, while outpainting allows for more creative generation of entirely new scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

PanoGen++ was evaluated on three standard VLN benchmarks: R2R, R4R, and CVDN. The results demonstrate significant improvements over existing methods:

1. **Room-to-Room (R2R)**:
 - 2.44% increase in success rate on the test leaderboard compared to state-of-the-art approaches

2. **Room-for-Room (R4R)**:
 - 0.63% improvement on the validation unseen set

3. **Cooperative Vision-and-Dialog Navigation (CVDN)**:
 - 0.75-meter enhancement in goal progress on the validation unseen set

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Applications and Impact

The PanoGen++ framework has several important applications and implications for the field:

1. **Addressing data scarcity**: By generating diverse and realistic training environments, PanoGen++ helps overcome the limited availability of 3D scans for VLN tasks.

2. **Improving agent generalization**: Training on a wider variety of environments enhances an agent's ability to navigate effectively in unseen settings.

3. **Parameter-efficient adaptation**: The use of LoRA makes the adaptation of large generative models more accessible to researchers with limited computational resources.

4. **Cross-domain applications**: While focused on indoor navigation, the approach shows promise for outdoor settings as well (as demonstrated in Figure 5).

#### 5.2. Conclusion

PanoGen++ represents a significant advancement in generating training environments for Vision-and-Language Navigation tasks. By incorporating domain-specific knowledge into pre-trained diffusion models through parameter-efficient fine-tuning, the framework creates panoramic environments that enhance the performance of VLN agents across multiple benchmarks.

The results demonstrate that domain adaptation is crucial for generating environments that are both realistic and relevant for navigation tasks. The two generation approaches—masked image inpainting and recursive image outpainting—provide flexibility in how new environments are created, allowing researchers to balance between maintaining consistency with original environments and generating entirely novel scenes.

Future directions suggested by the authors include incorporating more diverse datasets, integrating multiple domain adaptation modules, and exploring more advanced text-to-image generation models. As the field of embodied AI continues to advance, approaches like PanoGen++ will play a critical role in enabling agents to learn generalizable navigation skills through diverse training experiences.
