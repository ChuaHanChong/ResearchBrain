# Chain-of-Zoom: Extreme Super-Resolution via Scale Autoregression and Preference Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> The Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) framework enables existing single-image super-resolution (SISR) models to achieve extreme magnifications up to 256x. It employs a scale autoregression strategy combined with multi-scale-aware textual prompts, generated by a Vision-Language Model (VLM) fine-tuned with Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO), to maintain high perceptual quality and fidelity.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art SISR models are limited to fixed, relatively small upscaling factors (e.g., 4x) and collapse when pushed to extreme magnifications.
- Training separate deep learning models for each extreme magnification factor (e.g., 16x, 32x) is computationally prohibitive due to memory and time constraints.
- At very high magnifications, low-resolution inputs provide sparse visual cues, making it challenging for SR models to generate plausible and semantically coherent high-frequency details without hallucinating unreasonable outputs.

## Method

- The ==Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) framework== factorizes the super-resolution task into an ==autoregressive chain of intermediate scale-states==, repeatedly applying any off-the-shelf SR backbone trained for a fixed, small upscaling factor.
- ==Multi-scale-aware textual prompts==, generated by a Vision-Language Model (VLM) conditioned on two previous image scales, guide the SR process at each zoom step, compensating for sparse visual information.
- The prompt-extraction VLM is fine-tuned using ==Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a composite reward function to align generated prompts with human preferences and reduce implausible hallucinations at extreme scales.

## Results

- CoZ achieves superior perceptual quality at extreme magnifications (up to **256x**) compared to Nearest Neighbor interpolation and direct one-step SR, as evidenced by better no-reference quality scores (NIQE, MUSIQ, MANIQA-pipal, CLIPIQA).
- GRPO fine-tuning effectively guides the VLM to produce prompts that reduce implausible hallucinations and yield statistically higher human preference scores for both image and text quality in user studies.
- The framework demonstrates model-agnosticism and robustness, maintaining good performance when integrated with different existing SR backbones like an open-source OSEDiff model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extreme super-resolution can be effectively achieved by decomposing the task into an autoregressive sequence of smaller magnification steps, allowing a single SR backbone to be reused without retraining.
> - Semantic guidance from multi-scale-aware textual prompts, derived from Vision-Language Models, is critical for synthesizing coherent and plausible details when visual cues become highly sparse at high magnifications.
> - Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback, specifically GRPO, can successfully fine-tune VLM behavior to generate prompts that are more aligned with human aesthetic and semantic preferences, mitigating hallucinations in generative vision tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Single-Image Super-Resolution (SISR) is a field within generative modeling focused on reconstructing high-resolution (HR) images from low-resolution (LR) inputs. Historically, SISR approaches evolved from interpolation or regression methods, which often produced blurry results, to more sophisticated techniques leveraging generative models, particularly diffusion models. These modern generative prior-based methods broadly fall into two categories: those that frame SR as an inverse problem during inference using pre-trained generative models, and those that integrate data consistency into the model's training objective for faster inference.

While current state-of-the-art diffusion-based SR models can generate photo-realistic images efficiently at the specific magnification factors they were trained on, they encounter a significant limitation: their performance degrades substantially, or they "collapse," when required to magnify images far beyond their trained scale. This constraint necessitates retraining or developing new models for different extreme magnification factors, which is computationally expensive and inefficient.

This work addresses this scalability bottleneck by proposing a model-agnostic framework that enables existing SR models to achieve extreme magnifications without additional training. It draws inspiration from multi-scale image generation techniques, which synthesize larger images through successive refinement stages, and autoregressive models, which predict tokens sequentially. However, unlike prior autoregressive SR methods that operate on pixels, this work proposes autoregressing over "scale-states." Additionally, it incorporates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for textual conditioning, a recent trend in diffusion-based SR, and utilizes Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF), specifically Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO), to align these textual prompts with human preference, a technique previously applied in vision-language tasks like dialogue refinement and segmentation, but novel for prompt extraction in super-resolution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable existing single-image super-resolution models to effectively generate images at significantly higher resolutions than they were originally trained for, without requiring extensive retraining. This addresses a fundamental scalability challenge in SISR, where models typically fail or produce artifacts when pushed beyond their design parameters.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Scalability Bottleneck:** Modern SR models, while highly effective at their trained scale factors, lack robustness when faced with extreme magnification demands. This necessitates retraining new models for each desired high magnification, which is inefficient and resource-intensive. The researchers aim to overcome this by allowing flexible, arbitrary-scale super-resolution using existing, well-trained models.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Directly training SR models for extremely high magnification factors (e.g., 16×, 32×) is often computationally prohibitive due to memory and time constraints. By extending the capabilities of pre-trained, lower-magnification models (e.g., 4× SR models) to much higher factors, the framework offers a more resource-efficient pathway to extreme resolutions.
- **Information Sparsity at Extreme Scales:** At very high magnifications, the visual cues from the original low-resolution input become increasingly sparse. This can lead the SR model to generate implausible details or hallucinations. The researchers are motivated to augment the super-resolution process with rich semantic guidance to mitigate this issue.
- **Semantic Coherence and Human Preference:** To provide effective guidance in the face of sparse visual information, the text prompts used for conditioning need to be semantically aligned with the image content and human aesthetic preferences. Therefore, a specific objective is to develop a mechanism for generating multi-scale-aware text prompts that are refined through a process aligned with human judgment.

In summary, the work seeks to develop a general, model-agnostic framework that can extend the practical applicability of current SR models to extreme magnifications while maintaining high perceptual quality and fidelity, primarily through an autoregressive scaling strategy augmented with preference-aligned textual guidance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ), a model-agnostic framework that decomposes the extreme super-resolution problem into an autoregressive sequence of intermediate scale-states, enhanced by multi-scale-aware text prompts.

#### 3.1. Intermediate Scale-State Modeling

CoZ frames the generation of a target high-resolution image ($x_H$) from an input low-resolution image ($x_L$) as a sequence of intermediate scale-states ($x_0, x_1..., x_n$), where $x_0 = x_L$ and $x_n = x_H$. Each consecutive state ($x_i$) is a magnified version of the previous one ($x_{i-1}$) by a fixed scale factor ($s$).

To address the loss of high-frequency details that occurs if relying solely on a Markov chain assumption ($p(x_i|x_{i-1})$), CoZ introduces latent variables $c_i$ in the form of text embeddings. These text prompts are designed to supplement the diminishing visual information at higher magnifications. Crucially, to prevent hallucinations from incorrect text guidance, the prompt extraction is made "multi-scale aware," meaning the VLM generates prompts by conditioning on not just the current scale-state ($x_{i-1}$) but also the coarser previous state ($x_{i-2}$). This leads to an AR-2 (AutoRegressive-2) modeling of the image generative process:
$p(x_i|x_{i-1}, x_{i-2}) = \int p(x_i|x_{i-1}, x_{i-2}, c_i)p(c_i|x_{i-1}, x_{i-2})dc_i$.

The overall objective is to maximize the likelihood of the joint distribution of all image scale-states and prompts. This objective is decomposed into three terms: an initial super-resolution step ($L_{init}$), subsequent super-resolution steps ($L_{SR}$), and prompt prediction steps ($L_{VLM}$).

- **Next $x_i$ prediction ($L_{SR}$):** The super-resolution backbone model $f_{\theta}$ is responsible for predicting $x_i$ given $x_{i-1}$ and the prompt $c_i$. The dependency on $x_{i-2}$ is implicitly handled through the multi-scale-aware prompt $c_i$. The training objective for this part is to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted HR image and the ground truth, consistent with how many SR backbones are typically trained. The framework is designed to be model-agnostic, allowing various existing SR models (e.g., a 4× diffusion SR model like OSEDiff) to be used as the backbone without retraining.
- **Next $c_i$ prediction ($L_{VLM}$):** A Vision-Language Model (VLM) $p_{\phi}$ is used to generate the multi-scale-aware text prompts $c_i$ conditioned on $x_{i-1}$ and $x_{i-2}$. The VLM generates tokens autoregressively, and its objective is to maximize the log-likelihood of these tokens, typically achieved by minimizing the negative log-likelihood (cross-entropy loss) during pre-training.

#### 3.2. Inference Procedure

During inference, starting from the LR image $x_L = x_0$:
1. A prompt $c_1$ is sampled from $p_{\phi}(c_1|x_0)$.
2. The first HR image $x_1$ is sampled from $p_{\theta}(x_1|x_0, c_1)$.
3. For subsequent steps ($i \ge 2$), a multi-scale-aware prompt $c_i$ is sampled from $p_{\phi}(c_i|x_{i-1}, x_{i-2})$.
4. The next HR image $x_i$ is sampled from $p_{\theta}(x_i|x_{i-1}, c_i)$.
This recursive process allows for progressive magnification. For SR backbones that operate on fixed input/output dimensions, a fixed-size window is cropped from the previous HR image and resized before feeding it to the SR model, enabling local super-resolution. Full image super-resolution can be achieved through tiling methods.

#### 3.3. Training Multi-Scale-Aware Prompt Extraction using RL (GRPO)

To ensure text prompts provide semantically coherent and human-preferred guidance, especially when visual evidence is scarce, the prompt-extraction VLM is fine-tuned using a Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) pipeline based on Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO).

The fine-tuning process involves:
- **Reward Function:** For each zoom step $i$, the VLM generates a candidate prompt $c_i$ from multi-scale image crops ($x_{i-2}, x_{i-1}$). A composite reward function $R(c_i)$ is computed, consisting of three components:
 - **Critic Preference Reward ($R_{critic}$):** A stronger critic VLM (InternVL2.5-8B in experiments) scores the candidate prompt in the context of the multi-scale image crops. This score is rescaled to to approximate human preference.
 - **Phrase-Exclusion Reward ($R_{phrase}$):** A reward of 1 is given if the prompt does not contain predefined "blacklisted" phrases (e.g., "first image," "second image") that are meaningless to the SR model; otherwise, 0.
 - **Repetition Penalty ($R_{rep}$):** A negative reward (up to -1) is applied based on the fraction of repeated n-grams in the prompt, encouraging conciseness and relevance.
- **GRPO Update:** The weighted sum of these rewards ($R(c_i) = w_{critic}R_{critic} + w_{phrase}R_{phrase} + w_{rep}R_{rep}$) forms the signal that drives the GRPO update. GRPO iteratively fine-tunes the prompt-extraction VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct in experiments) to generate prompts that maximize this reward, thereby aligning textual guidance with human preferences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted experiments comparing Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) against nearest neighbor interpolation, one-step direct SR, and other baseline methods at various magnification factors, using OSEDiff (with Stable Diffusion 3.0 or v2.1 backbones) and S3Diff as backbone SR models.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Comparison

Qualitative results (Figure 5, Figures 11-16) demonstrate that nearest neighbor interpolation and direct one-step SR methods rapidly degrade in quality at higher magnification scales, producing blurry images and severe artifacts. In contrast, CoZ variants consistently produce images with better perceptual quality and sharper details, even at extreme magnifications (up to 256×). The visual clarity of textures, wrinkles, and fine structures is notably superior with CoZ. The incorporation of VLM-generated prompts within CoZ is observed to significantly contribute to the generation of realistic details without introducing semantic hallucinations, especially as the original visual signal becomes increasingly sparse.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison

Due to the absence of ground-truth images for extreme magnifications, evaluation was conducted using no-reference perceptual metrics: NIQE (Natural Image Quality Evaluator, lower is better), MUSIQ (Multi-Scale Image Quality, higher is better), MANIQA (Multi-dimension Attention Network for No-Reference Image Quality Assessment, higher is better), and CLIPIQA (CLIP for Assessing Image Quality, higher is better).

- **Comparison across CoZ variants and baselines (Table 1):**
 - At lower scales (4×), the differences between direct SR and CoZ variants are minimal.
 - At higher scales (64×, 256×), CoZ consistently outperforms nearest neighbor interpolation and direct one-step SR across all metrics.
 - CoZ with VLM-generated prompts generally achieves superior performance compared to CoZ with null prompts or DAPE (Degradation-Aware Prompt Extractor) prompts, particularly at extreme magnifications. This supports the assertion that VLM-generated prompts compensate for the diminishing visual conditioning.
- **Comparison with additional baseline methods (Table 2):**
 - Arbitrary-scale SR methods (LIIF) and other diffusion-based SR methods (SeeSR, S3Diff) show degraded performance at high magnifications.
 - Applying CoZ to a pre-trained S3Diff model (S3Diff + CoZ) demonstrates significant performance improvements across all metrics at higher scales compared to S3Diff alone, reinforcing CoZ's efficacy as a general framework.
- **CoZ with open-source OSEDiff (Table 4):**
 - Experiments using the open-source OSEDiff with a Stable Diffusion v2.1 backbone further validate CoZ's robustness, showing consistent performance improvements at higher magnifications when integrated with CoZ and the GRPO fine-tuned VLM.
- **Critic VLM comparison (Table 5):**
 - Results show that CoZ with VLM prompts fine-tuned using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the critic VLM also yields strong performance, similar to using InternVL2.5-8B, indicating robustness to critic model choice.

#### 4.3. GRPO for VLM and Preference Alignment

- **GRPO Training Rewards (Figures 6, 7):** During GRPO training, the phrase exclusion reward consistently converges to 1.0 (no blacklisted phrases), and the repetition penalty converges to 0.0 (no repetitions) early in training. The critic reward, reflecting human preference, gradually increases throughout the training process. This trend is consistent across different critic VLMs, demonstrating the robustness of the GRPO pipeline in aligning prompt generation with specified preferences.
- **Preference Alignment (Figure 8, 10):** User studies (Mean Opinion Score - MOS tests) indicate that GRPO fine-tuning significantly improves human preference alignment for both image generation quality and text generation quality. The fine-tuned VLM-generated prompts lead to samples with reduced unwanted hallucinations and more suitable textual descriptions compared to base VLMs or those only conditioned on multi-scale images without GRPO.

#### 4.4. Runtime Analysis

Runtime analysis (Table 3) shows that the super-resolution (SR) phase of CoZ scales linearly with the number of recursions, as the same backbone model is reused. The prompt extraction (PE) phase also scales, with VLM-based prompt extraction being the most computationally intensive part due to the VLM's processing time. However, CoZ still enables extreme magnification at feasible inference speeds, especially compared to the alternative of training entirely new models for each scale.

#### 4.5. Overlapping Regions Consistency

Cosine similarity measurements of overlapping patches across different magnifications (Table 6) indicate high consistency between adjacent regions, even at 256× magnification, suggesting that the autoregressive process maintains spatial coherence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of single-image super-resolution, primarily by addressing the long-standing scalability gap of existing models. Its model-agnostic nature allows for the repurposing of any pre-trained SR backbone, turning it into an engine capable of extreme magnifications (e.g., 256×) without requiring costly retraining. This offers a resource-frugal and flexible approach to image enhancement, moving beyond the limitations of fixed-scale training.

The introduction of multi-scale-aware text prompts, derived from a VLM fine-tuned with a GRPO-based RLHF pipeline, is a notable contribution. This mechanism provides essential semantic guidance to the SR process, particularly when the visual information from the original low-resolution image becomes sparse at extreme magnifications. By aligning these prompts with human preference, CoZ mitigates the risk of implausible hallucinations and enhances the perceptual quality and fidelity of the super-resolved outputs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Practical Applicability:** CoZ could broaden the real-world utility of super-resolution in diverse applications, from enhancing consumer photographs and legacy media to improving critical details in medical imaging, satellite surveillance, and scientific visualization, where arbitrary and extreme magnifications are often desired.
- **Resource Efficiency:** By extending the utility of existing models, CoZ reduces the computational and data requirements associated with training new models for specific high magnification factors, making advanced super-resolution more accessible.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a foundation for exploring more sophisticated "learned zoom policies," domain-specific reward functions for specialized tasks, and adaptive backbone selection strategies to further optimize extreme super-resolution.

**Limitations and Potential Negative Impacts:**
- **Error Accumulation:** The autoregressive nature of CoZ, involving repeated application of the SR backbone, inherently carries the risk of error accumulation over multiple iterations, which could potentially degrade image quality at the highest magnifications.
- **Computational Bottleneck:** While efficient compared to retraining, the iterative nature and the VLM-based prompt extraction for each zoom step (especially for tiled full-image SR) introduce a computational overhead during inference, which could be a bottleneck for real-time applications.
- **Misinformation and Ethical Concerns:** The ability to generate highly photorealistic details from low-resolution inputs raises concerns regarding misinformation, deepfakes, and the unauthorized reconstruction or enhancement of sensitive visual data, which necessitates careful consideration of ethical implications and responsible deployment.
