# Chain-of-Zoom: Extreme Super-Resolution via Scale Autoregression and Preference Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> The Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) framework enables existing single-image super-resolution (SISR) models to achieve extreme magnifications up to 256x. It employs a scale autoregression strategy combined with multi-scale-aware textual prompts, generated by a Vision-Language Model (VLM) fine-tuned with Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO), to maintain high perceptual quality and fidelity.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art SISR models are limited to fixed, relatively small upscaling factors (e.g., 4x) and collapse when pushed to extreme magnifications.
- Training separate deep learning models for each extreme magnification factor (e.g., 16x, 32x) is computationally prohibitive due to memory and time constraints.
- At very high magnifications, low-resolution inputs provide sparse visual cues, making it challenging for SR models to generate plausible and semantically coherent high-frequency details without hallucinating unreasonable outputs.

## Method

- The ==Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) framework== factorizes the super-resolution task into an ==autoregressive chain of intermediate scale-states==, repeatedly applying any off-the-shelf SR backbone trained for a fixed, small upscaling factor.
- ==Multi-scale-aware textual prompts==, generated by a Vision-Language Model (VLM) conditioned on two previous image scales, guide the SR process at each zoom step, compensating for sparse visual information.
- The prompt-extraction VLM is fine-tuned using ==Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a composite reward function to align generated prompts with human preferences and reduce implausible hallucinations at extreme scales.

## Results

- CoZ achieves superior perceptual quality at extreme magnifications (up to **256x**) compared to Nearest Neighbor interpolation and direct one-step SR, as evidenced by better no-reference quality scores (NIQE, MUSIQ, MANIQA-pipal, CLIPIQA).
- GRPO fine-tuning effectively guides the VLM to produce prompts that reduce implausible hallucinations and yield statistically higher human preference scores for both image and text quality in user studies.
- The framework demonstrates model-agnosticism and robustness, maintaining good performance when integrated with different existing SR backbones like an open-source OSEDiff model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extreme super-resolution can be effectively achieved by decomposing the task into an autoregressive sequence of smaller magnification steps, allowing a single SR backbone to be reused without retraining.
> - Semantic guidance from multi-scale-aware textual prompts, derived from Vision-Language Models, is critical for synthesizing coherent and plausible details when visual cues become highly sparse at high magnifications.
> - Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback, specifically GRPO, can successfully fine-tune VLM behavior to generate prompts that are more aligned with human aesthetic and semantic preferences, mitigating hallucinations in generative vision tasks.
