# Reliability-Asymmetric Spacecraft Autonomy: Co-Designing a Capable Learned GNC Stack with a Verified, Adaptation-Aware Runtime Shield

> [!summary] Summary
> The AMPLE-GNC autonomy system for deep-space missions co-designs a learned guidance, navigation, and control stack with a formally verified runtime shield to achieve both high capability and certifiable safety. It demonstrated over 90% reduction in Deep Space Network contacts and enabled 97.8% recovery from actuator sign faults in simulations, robustly handling unmodeled dynamics within a real-time budget.

## Problem

- Deep-space missions require high onboard autonomy due to communication latency, but also demand certifiable safety for high-consequence failures.
- Learned autonomy offers capability but lacks robust verifiability for flight, while traditional rule-based autonomy is certifiable but brittle and lacks generalization.
- Reconciling the transient 'unsafe' behavior of online-adapting controllers with worst-case runtime safety shields often leads to premature safe-holds, preventing recovery.

## Method

- Introduces AMPLE-GNC, a three-tier GNC stack based on a 'reliability asymmetry' principle, where formally verified components bound less verifiable learned components.
- Employs a deployed edge commander using fine-tuned language models with grammar-constrained decoding to provide hard output validity for natural language commands.
- Develops a Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)-based fault-adaptive controller that learns system identification to feed an analytic control law, enabling recovery from unmodeled actuator faults.
- Integrates a formally verified runtime shield (using Lustre and Kind 2 model checker) to monitor safety invariants, coupled with a split-conformal recovery-deadline certificate to permit bounded transient excursions during adaptation.

## Results

- The RMA controller achieved **97.8%** recovery for actuator sign faults and **94.4%** for continuous-gain faults, outperforming fault-unaware PD (**0%**) and end-to-end deep RL (**0%**) baselines on the settled gate.
- The adaptation-aware runtime assurance allowed the recovering RMA controller to remain **94.5%** autonomous, leading to a **90.3%** reduction in DSN contacts under nominal conditions and **98.3%** under stress, with the verified shield incurring **0.02%** control loop overhead.
- The commander achieved **84%** planner-executable rate for PDDL+ actions and **48.0%** exact match generalization on a de-leaked test set, meeting flight memory constraints (277 MB int5) on a Raspberry Pi 5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'reliability asymmetry' principle effectively bridges the capability-certifiability gap by strategically bounding capable, opaque learned components with simpler, formally verifiable ones.
> - A hybrid architecture, combining learned system identification (via GRU) with an analytic control law, can significantly outperform end-to-end learned policies for robust fault adaptation.
> - A statistical, distribution-free conformal recovery-deadline certificate provides a method for licensing transient 'unsafe' behavior during adaptation, resolving the tension between adaptive control and worst-case runtime safety monitors.
