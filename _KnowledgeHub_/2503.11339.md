# Contextual Similarity Distillation: Ensemble Uncertainties with a Single Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at TU Delft developed Contextual Similarity Distillation (CSD), a single-model approach for uncertainty quantification that distills the epistemic uncertainty of deep ensembles. CSD demonstrated competitive performance in distribution shift detection and proved effective as an exploration signal in challenging reinforcement learning tasks while maintaining high computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Reliable uncertainty quantification (UQ) is essential for robust deep learning and reinforcement learning systems, especially in safety-critical applications.
- Current UQ methods, such as Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) and Deep Ensembles, are often computationally expensive or intractable for large, modern neural networks.
- There is a need for an efficient, single-model approach that can provide uncertainty estimates comparable to resource-intensive ensemble methods.

## Method

- ==Contextual Similarity Distillation== (**CSD**) reinterprets the problem of estimating ensemble variance as a structured supervised regression task, inspired by Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory.
- It trains a single 'contextualized regression model' to predict the approximate NTK-based kernel similarities between inputs, which allows it to infer the ensemble's predictive variance.
- At inference, the model takes an input as its own context, enabling a single forward pass to estimate the uncertainty, thereby avoiding the computational overhead of training or querying multiple models.

## Results

- Achieved competitive distribution shift detection, often outperforming traditional UQ methods like Monte Carlo Dropout and Bayesian Neural Networks, and performing on par with smaller deep ensembles.
- CSD variants utilizing data augmentations or unlabeled target-domain data as context showed substantial performance gains, frequently surpassing large deep ensembles (ENS(**15**)).
- Demonstrated robust and effective exploration in VizDoom reinforcement learning environments, reliably finding goals across various difficulty levels and performing competitively against established exploration strategies like Random Network Distillation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The predictive variance of an infinite deep ensemble, grounded in Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory, can be framed and approximated as a supervised regression problem.
> - A single neural network can be trained to learn contextualized similarities, enabling it to efficiently estimate the ensemble-like uncertainty for arbitrary inputs in a single forward pass.
> - The method inherently allows for the integration of unlabeled data or data augmentations as context, which can significantly refine uncertainty estimates in practice.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) has emerged as an indispensable component of robust and reliable deep learning and reinforcement learning (RL) systems, particularly as these systems are deployed in real-world, safety-critical applications. The ability to assess the reliability of a model's predictions is crucial for tasks ranging from out-of-distribution (OOD) detection and anomaly identification (e.g., in medical diagnostics) to enabling efficient exploration and ensuring stability in offline RL.

Traditionally, the research landscape for UQ in deep neural networks has been dominated by two main paradigms:
- **Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs):** These methods aim to place a prior distribution over network weights and infer a posterior distribution, thereby capturing epistemic uncertainty. While theoretically well-motivated, full Bayesian inference is often computationally intractable for large modern neural networks, necessitating coarse approximations or costly sampling methods (e.g., Markov Chain Monte Carlo, Variational Inference).
- **Deep Ensembles:** A more practical and empirically successful approach involves training multiple neural networks with different random initializations and averaging their predictions. The variance or diversity among ensemble members serves as a reliable proxy for epistemic uncertainty. However, the primary drawback of deep ensembles is their computational expense, as they require training and evaluating several distinct models, a burden that scales linearly with the ensemble size. This cost becomes prohibitive for very large models or in data-intensive applications like RL, where millions of interactions might be required.

This paper positions itself directly at the intersection of these challenges. It aims to bridge the gap between the computational efficiency of single-model approaches and the reliability of ensemble-based uncertainty estimates. The authors achieve this by leveraging recent theoretical advancements in understanding the learning dynamics of wide neural networks, specifically the **Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK)**. The NTK describes how a wide neural network behaves as a linear model under gradient descent, and crucially, how an infinite ensemble of randomly initialized networks trained under these conditions can be characterized by a Gaussian Process (GP), known as the NTK GP. While the NTK GP provides an analytical expression for ensemble variance, direct computation involves inverting potentially massive Gram matrices, rendering it impractical.

Contextual Similarity Distillation (CSD) directly addresses this practical intractability. It seeks to approximate this theoretically grounded ensemble variance with a single forward pass of a single model. By doing so, it attempts to "distill" the essence of ensemble uncertainty into a computationally efficient form, offering a principled alternative to resource-intensive existing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a novel, efficient, and principled single-model approach for uncertainty quantification in deep neural networks that can provide reliable uncertainty estimates comparable to or superior to those obtained from deep ensembles, but at a significantly lower computational cost.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** Modern deep learning models are increasingly large and complex, making the training and inference of multiple ensemble members (or computationally expensive Bayesian approximations) impractical for real-world deployment. The authors are motivated to find a method that yields high-quality uncertainty estimates with just a single model.
- **Leveraging Theoretical Insights:** The recent breakthroughs in Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory have provided a deeper understanding of how wide neural networks learn and how their ensembles behave. The authors are motivated to translate these theoretical insights into a practical, scalable algorithm that can directly approximate the analytically derived ensemble variance, without incurring the computational costs of direct NTK calculations.
- **Enhancing Exploration in Reinforcement Learning:** A significant application domain for uncertainty quantification is reinforcement learning, particularly in sparse-reward or high-dimensional environments where efficient exploration is critical. By providing a reliable and efficient uncertainty signal, the proposed method aims to improve the performance of RL agents by guiding them towards uncertain but potentially rewarding states.
- **Improving Out-of-Distribution Detection:** Reliable uncertainty estimates are fundamental for identifying inputs that deviate from the training distribution. The authors aim to develop a method that can effectively detect such distribution shifts, which is vital for safety and robustness in deployed AI systems.
- **Exploiting Unlabeled Data:** Many existing UQ methods struggle to leverage unlabeled data effectively for refining uncertainty estimates. A specific motivation for CSD is its inherent ability to incorporate unlabeled target-domain data or data augmentations, potentially leading to more robust uncertainty estimations in real-world scenarios.

In essence, the authors are motivated to provide a "principled and scalable alternative" to existing UQ methods, particularly deep ensembles, thereby making reliable uncertainty quantification more accessible for large-scale deep learning and reinforcement learning applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Contextual Similarity Distillation (CSD) is a novel approach that reinterprets the problem of estimating ensemble variance as a structured supervised regression task. Its methodology is built upon the theoretical insights of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) and its connection to Gaussian Processes (GPs).

**Theoretical Foundation:**
The core theoretical insight derives from the fact that in the limit of infinite layer widths, wide neural networks trained by gradient descent are well-described by their Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK). Under these conditions, an infinite ensemble of randomly initialized networks converges to a Gaussian Process (GP), where the predictive variance is given by:
`V[f(x, θ∞)] = Θ(x, x) - Θ(x, X)Θ(X, X)⁻¹Θ(X, x)`
Here, `Θ(x, x')` is the NTK, `X` is the training data, and `Y` are the labels. The term `Θ(x, X)Θ(X, X)⁻¹Θ(X, x)` represents the "confidence" gained by observing the training data, while `Θ(x, x)` represents a prior uncertainty term. The challenge lies in computing the inverse of the Gram matrix `Θ(X, X)⁻¹`, which is computationally prohibitive for large datasets.

**Single-Query Variance Estimation:**
The authors' breakthrough insight is to recognize that the complex term `Θ(x, X)Θ(X, X)⁻¹Θ(X, x_t)` can be obtained as the result of a standard supervised regression problem. If a single neural network `g(x, θ̃_t)` (with the same architecture as the original `f`) is trained on the dataset `X` with a specific label function `Y_xt(X) = Θ(X, x_t)` (where `x_t` is a fixed test query point), and assuming small function initialization, its post-training function `g_xt(x, θ̃_∞)` will approximate precisely `Θ(x, X)Θ(X, X)⁻¹Θ(X, x_t)`. Thus, the ensemble variance for a specific query `x_t` can be efficiently computed as:
`V[f(x_t, θ∞)] = Θ(x_t, x_t) - g_xt(x_t, θ̃_∞)`
This eliminates the need for explicit Gram matrix inversion or training an actual ensemble.

**Contextualized Similarity Distillation (CSD) for Arbitrary Queries:**
The single-query approach is limited as it requires training a new model for each query point. To overcome this, CSD proposes a **contextualized regression model** `g(x, c, θ̃_t)`. Here, `c` serves as a context variable, allowing the model to learn a family of regression solutions. The model is trained to predict `Θ(X, c)` for various `c` values. At inference time, to estimate the variance for an input `x`, the context variable `c` is set to `x` itself. The variance is then approximated as:
`V[f(x, θ∞)] ≈ Θ(x, x) - g(x, x, θ̃_∞)`
This generalizes the single-query idea, allowing a single trained model to estimate variance for any input `x`.

**Practical Implementation with Deep Neural Networks:**
To make CSD feasible for deep learning, the authors introduce several algorithmic design choices:
1. **Parametrization of `g(x, c, θ̃_∞)`:** The model `g` is parametrized as an inner product between a feature vector `ϕ(x, θ̃_f)` and a context vector `ψ(c, θ̃_c)`, i.e., `g(x, c, θ̃_∞) = ϕ(x, θ̃_f)ᵀψ(c, θ̃_c)`. This allows for efficient computation across mini-batches of `x` and `c` without individual forward passes for each pair.
2. **Approximation of NTK Prior:** The full NTK `Θ(x, x')` can still be computationally intensive. CSD approximates it using **partial gradients with respect to only the last layer weights** (`Θ_L(x, x')`). This is justified by the fact that the last layer often plays a significant role in determining network behavior.
3. **Loss Function:** The model `g` is trained using a standard **squared loss** (`L(θ̃_t) = ½ (g(x_i, c_i, θ̃_t) - Θ_L(x_i, c_i))²`), minimizing the difference between its predictions and the approximate kernel similarities.
4. **Context Data `C` Choices:**
 - **Reuse Training Set:** The simplest approach where `c_i` are sampled directly from the training data `X`.
 - **Data Augmentations:** Context data can be generated by applying data augmentations to the training samples. This allows the model to learn about similarities in perturbed versions of inputs, enhancing robustness.
 - **Unlabeled Target Domain Data:** When available, unlabeled data from the target test distribution can be used as context, allowing the model to refine its uncertainty estimates specifically for that domain. This is a unique advantage over many UQ methods.

While grounded in theory, CSD introduces practical approximations (e.g., partial gradients for NTK, contextualization for generalization) that might slightly deviate from the exact NTK GP dynamics. However, the empirical results demonstrate that these approximations yield highly effective performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of CSD focuses on two main application areas: distribution shift detection and reinforcement learning exploration. Across these diverse benchmarks, CSD consistently demonstrates strong performance, often competitive with or superior to established baselines, while maintaining its single-model efficiency.

#### 4.1. Distribution Shift Detection

The authors evaluated CSD on various image classification datasets (FashionMNIST, MNIST, KMNIST, NotMNIST) under distribution shifts. Models were trained on one dataset (in-distribution, ID) and evaluated on others (out-of-distribution, OOD), as well as perturbed versions of the ID dataset. The quality of uncertainty estimates was assessed using threshold-independent metrics: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (AUROC), Area Under the Precision-Recall curve for In-Distribution (AUPR-IN), and Out-of-Distribution (AUPR-OUT) samples.

- **Competitive Performance:** Even the baseline CSD (using only training data as context) achieved highly effective distribution shift detection, surpassing many traditional UQ methods like Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD), Bayesian Neural Networks (BNN-MCMC, BNN-Laplace), and performing competitively with Random Network Distillation (RND) and smaller Deep Ensembles (ENS(3)).
- **Superiority with Context Data:** Crucially, CSD variants that incorporated additional context data showed significant improvements:
 - **CSD-Aug. (with data augmentations):** Consistently outperformed most baselines and the standard CSD, indicating that enriching the context with augmented data effectively refines uncertainty estimates.
 - **CSD-OOD. (with unlabeled target-domain data):** Demonstrated the highest performance, often outperforming even large deep ensembles (ENS(15)). This highlights a key advantage of CSD: its ability to leverage readily available unlabeled data from the target domain to improve uncertainty awareness without needing labels.

#### 4.2. Exploration in VizDoom Reinforcement Learning

CSD was evaluated as an exploration signal in challenging visual navigation tasks within the VizDOOM environment, characterized by high-dimensional observations and sparse rewards. CSD's uncertainty estimates were integrated as an intrinsic reward for a Rainbow-based agent.

- **Robust Exploration:** CSD proved to be a highly effective exploration strategy. In the various "MyWayHome" VizDoom environments (Dense, Sparse, Very Sparse, reflecting increasing difficulty), CSD was "able to find the goal across all seeds and environments," a robust performance not matched by all baselines.
- **Strong Competitive Standing:** While Random Network Distillation (RND) performed most competitively, CSD demonstrated superior overall reliability and consistent performance across all difficulty levels compared to other baselines like Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Bootstrapped Q-Networks (BDQN+P), and Information-Directed Sampling (IDS-C51). This confirms CSD's utility in guiding efficient exploration in complex, sparse-reward RL settings.

Overall, the empirical results strongly support the claims of CSD as a principled, scalable, and effective method for uncertainty quantification, demonstrating its competitive performance across diverse applications and its unique capability to leverage unlabeled data for enhanced uncertainty estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Contextual Similarity Distillation represents a significant advancement in the field of uncertainty quantification for deep learning and reinforcement learning, offering a compelling alternative to existing methods. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key advantages:

- **Scalability and Computational Efficiency:** The primary and most impactful contribution of CSD is its ability to estimate ensemble-like predictive variance with a *single model and a single forward pass*. This directly addresses the computational bottleneck of deep ensembles, making reliable uncertainty quantification feasible for increasingly large neural networks and computationally demanding real-world applications where training and deploying multiple models is impractical.
- **Principled Theoretical Foundation:** Unlike many heuristic or empirical single-model uncertainty estimation methods, CSD is firmly grounded in the established theoretical framework of the Neural Tangent Kernel. This principled derivation lends credibility to its uncertainty estimates, suggesting that it approximates a well-defined form of epistemic uncertainty (that of an infinite deep ensemble).
- **Leveraging Unlabeled Data and Augmentations:** CSD's design allows it to naturally incorporate unlabeled data from the target domain or through data augmentations to refine its uncertainty estimates. This is a unique and powerful capability, as unlabeled data is often abundant, and its effective utilization can lead to more robust and accurate uncertainty awareness in practical scenarios, particularly where distribution shifts are common. This opens new avenues for self-supervised uncertainty learning.
- **Enhanced Reinforcement Learning Exploration:** The empirical success of CSD in guiding exploration in challenging, sparse-reward VizDoom environments is highly significant for the field of RL. Efficient and reliable uncertainty signals are crucial for tackling the exploration-exploitation dilemma in complex, high-dimensional spaces, potentially enabling the development of more sample-efficient and robust RL agents for real-world tasks.
- **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated in OOD detection and RL, the core principle of CSD is generalizable to a wide array of deep learning tasks requiring uncertainty awareness, including active learning, robust decision-making, and anomaly detection in various domains.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several promising avenues for future research. This includes exploring its application in other challenging RL settings, such as model-based RL and offline RL, where accurate uncertainty estimates are paramount for safe and efficient learning. Further investigation into more sophisticated data augmentation techniques specifically designed for UQ within the CSD framework could also yield further improvements.

In conclusion, CSD provides a principled, scalable, and computationally efficient solution to a critical problem in modern AI. By offering a single-model approach that rivals or surpasses the performance of deep ensembles, it positions itself as a transformative tool for integrating robust uncertainty quantification into real-world deep learning and reinforcement learning systems.
