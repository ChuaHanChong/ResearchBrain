# Foundational Automatic Evaluators: Scaling Multi-Task Generative Evaluator Training for Reasoning-Centric Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> Salesforce AI Research developed Foundational Automatic Reasoning Evaluators (FARE), a family of generative models trained on a large-scale, multi-task (2.5 million samples) dataset using iterative rejection sampling supervised finetuning. These evaluators achieve best-in-class performance in objective correctness across diverse reasoning domains and enhance downstream LLM performance in tasks like reranking and reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Existing LLM evaluation methods, including human assessment and LLM-as-a-judge approaches, are either non-scalable, slow, expensive, or prone to biases and lack fine-grained, objective criteria for complex reasoning domains.
- Open-source specialized evaluators typically focus on one or two tasks with small data scales, limiting their generalization and utility across diverse evaluation needs and domains.
- Developing efficient and stable multi-task, multi-domain evaluators that can provide robust reward signals for LLM alignment and serve as verifiers in agentic systems remains a challenge.

## Method

- A ==multi-task==, multi-domain training dataset of 2.5 million samples, curated from existing high-quality data and strategically generated synthetic data for reasoning-centric tasks (pairwise, step-level, verification, single rating across math, code, tool-use, etc.) was compiled.
- Iterative Rejection Sampling Supervised Finetuning (RS-SFT), a stable and efficient method combining online learning principles with SFT, was employed to train generative evaluators at scale.
- The Foundational Automatic Reasoning Evaluators (FARE) are developed as generative models that produce natural language critiques and judgments, optimized for both accuracy and efficient, low-latency output.

## Results

- FARE-**20B** achieves best-in-class performance on core evaluation benchmarks, surpassing **70B**+ specialized judges on JudgeBench (64.29 vs. 60.00) and outperforming RL-trained evaluators like StepWiser-**7B** on ProcessBench by 6.1 points.
- In downstream applications, FARE-**20B** demonstrates near-oracle reranking performance, significantly outperforming **70B**+ judges by up to 21 absolute points on challenging math benchmarks.
- Utilized as a verifier in GRPO training, FARE-**20B** boosts downstream policy model accuracy by nearly 11 absolute points (from **34.3%** to **45.2%**), indicating improved LLM alignment and faster convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A data-centric approach, leveraging a large-scale, multi-task, multi-domain dataset, can yield highly effective generative evaluators that surpass larger and more complex RL-trained or specialized models.
> - Iterative Rejection Sampling SFT provides a stable and efficient training paradigm for evaluators, enabling scalable development while avoiding the computational demands and brittleness of online reinforcement learning methods.
> - Foundational evaluators like FARE can act as strong initializations, allowing for efficient adaptation to specialized domains through continual finetuning with minimal new data, significantly reducing the cost of developing domain-specific evaluators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement and widespread adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs) have created a parallel demand for equally scalable and robust methods to evaluate their outputs. This paper directly addresses this critical need by proposing a new paradigm for training *foundational automatic evaluators*.

Historically, LLM evaluation has evolved from human-centric assessment to LLM-as-a-judge (prompting powerful LLMs like GPT-4 to act as evaluators), and then to finetuned specialized evaluators. The authors correctly highlight the limitations of these approaches:
- **Human Evaluation:** Gold standard but expensive, slow, and non-scalable, bottlenecking rapid LLM development cycles.
- **Prompted LLMs as Judges:** Scalable but prone to biases (e.g., positional, length, self-preference) and often lack fine-grained, objective criteria, especially in complex reasoning domains.
- **Finetuned Specialized Evaluators:** While better than prompted approaches, much of the prior work in the open-source community has focused on training evaluators for one or two specific tasks (e.g., pairwise comparison) and at relatively small data scales. This leads to task-specific models that struggle with generalization across diverse evaluation needs and domains.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Recent methodological advancements like RLVR (e.g., for process reward models) show promise but are computationally demanding, have brittle training pipelines, and often struggle to scale to large datasets and multiple tasks.

This work explicitly positions itself to *bridge the gap* between early foundational evaluators (which showed generalization with data scaling via offline SFT/DPO) and the more recent methodological advances (like RLVR). The key insight is that large-scale, data-driven development, combined with a stable and efficient training approach, can produce superior multi-task, multi-domain evaluators that challenge even larger, specialized, or RL-trained models. The authors specifically aim to address the neglect of "large-scale, data-driven development" in favor of new methodologies within the open-source evaluation community.

Furthermore, the paper underscores the increasing importance of evaluators beyond simple benchmark judging. They are becoming integral to:
- **LLM Alignment:** Providing generative reward signals for preference optimization during RL training (e.g., RLHF, DPO).
- **Inference-time Scaling:** Acting as verifiers or critics for reranking multiple model responses to improve quality.
- **Agentic Systems:** Assessing complex agent reasoning, tool-use, and multi-agent workflows, where evaluation quality is a known bottleneck.

By developing "Foundational Automatic Reasoning Evaluators (FARE)," the authors aim to create a versatile and robust family of models capable of meeting these diverse and demanding requirements across the entire LLM development ecosystem, with a particular focus on complex reasoning tasks where objective correctness is crucial.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind this research stems from the pressing need for automatic evaluators that are both **scalable** and **versatile** to keep pace with the rapid evolution of LLMs. As LLMs become more integrated into various applications, from complex reasoning to autonomous agents, the demands on evaluation systems have grown significantly. The authors identify several critical gaps and needs that drive their work:

1. **Scalable Evaluation for LLM Development:** The continuous training, finetuning, and deployment of LLMs necessitate efficient and high-quality evaluation at scale, far beyond what manual human annotation can provide.
2. **Multi-Task Evaluation Capability:** Different stages of LLM development and deployment require different evaluation abilities (e.g., pairwise comparison for alignment, step-level analysis for debugging, verification for factual correctness, single rating for overall quality). Existing open-source evaluators often specialize in one or two tasks, limiting their utility.
3. **Multi-Domain Generalization:** LLMs are increasingly applied across diverse domains (math, code, tool-use, general reasoning, safety). Evaluators must perform effectively across these varied contexts, especially in "reasoning-centric settings" where subtle errors can be difficult to detect.
4. **Addressing Limitations of Current Approaches:**
 - **Data Scarcity:** Open-source evaluators often lack training at large data scales, hindering their generalization.
 - **Methodological Complexity:** While methods like RLVR offer promise, their computational intensity and training fragility make them difficult to scale and widely adopt for multi-task scenarios.
 - **Efficiency:** Many evaluation settings, like inference-time reranking or real-time RL rollout verification, demand low latency. Long "chain-of-thought" (CoT) evaluators, while effective, can be inefficient.

Based on these motivations, the authors set forth the following key objectives:

- **Data Curation at Scale:** To overcome data limitations, curate a **large-scale (2.5 million samples), multi-task, multi-domain training dataset** explicitly emphasizing reasoning-centric settings. This involves combining existing high-quality data with strategically generated synthetic data to cover five distinct evaluation tasks (pairwise, step-level, reference-free verification, reference-based verification, single rating) and multiple domains.
- **Develop a Scalable and Stable Training Paradigm:** Introduce and validate **iterative rejection sampling supervised finetuning (RS-SFT)** as a computationally stable and efficient method for training evaluators at scale. This approach aims to leverage the benefits of online learning (finetuning on policy-generated correct responses) while avoiding the distribution shifts of teacher-model SFT and the computational demands/brittleness of full RL.
- **Train Best-in-Class Foundational Evaluators:** Develop **Foundational Automatic Reasoning Evaluators (FARE)**, a family of models (FARE-8B and FARE-20B), that are both well-rounded (multi-task, multi-domain) and high-performing.
- **Rigorous Assessment:** Conduct comprehensive evaluations across both **static benchmarks** and **real-world downstream applications** to demonstrate FARE's superior accuracy, robustness, and practical utility. This includes assessing performance as inference-time rerankers, verifiers in RL training, and as strong initializations for domain-specific continual finetuning.
- **Design for Efficiency:** Explicitly design FARE to avoid generating excessively long chain-of-thoughts or reference answers, prioritizing efficient and low-latency evaluation outputs without compromising accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a data-centric and efficient training methodology to create their Foundational Automatic Reasoning Evaluators (FARE). Their approach is characterized by large-scale data curation and an iterative, semi-online supervised finetuning technique.

#### 3.1. Data Curation: 2.5 Million Multi-Task, Multi-Domain Samples

Recognizing the critical role of data, the authors curate a substantial dataset of 2.5 million training samples, segmented across five evaluation tasks and multiple reasoning-focused domains. This dataset is a mix of:

- **Existing Data (56.8%):** Sourced from proven datasets used for evaluator and preference finetuning. This includes human- and frontier LLM-annotated data for tasks like step-level, single-rating, and pairwise evaluation, covering chat, code, and safety domains. A particular focus is placed on modern (2024+) datasets. Preference finetuning datasets are converted into pairwise evaluation samples. For domains with objective answers (e.g., math), verification data is created by labeling correct/incorrect responses. Hand-crafted evaluation rubrics are developed for consistency.
- **Synthetic Data (43.2%):** Generated to address gaps in existing data, particularly for underrepresented tasks like verification, reasoning-centric pairwise comparisons, and challenging frontier datasets.
 - **Programmatic Error Injection:** Applied to structured data like tool-use and function-calling. Errors (e.g., type errors, syntax errors) are programmatically injected into correct function calls to generate diverse pairwise and verification samples.
 - **Generate-then-Grade:** Leveraging 12 unique generator models from 6 model families (both weak and strong), responses are sampled (up to 20 per question) for challenging seed datasets with verifiable ground-truth answers (e.g., from recent math and reasoning tasks). These responses are then graded against the ground truth, and grouped into correct/incorrect sets to form verification and pairwise samples. This ensures diversity in model responses, improving FARE's generalization.

The dataset covers a task distribution of 33.0% pairwise, 24.0% step-level, 18.4% reference-based verification, 13.1% reference-free verification, and 11.4% single rating. Domain-wise, math constitutes the largest portion (35.7%), followed by chat (19.8%), general reasoning (16.0%), tool use (11.7%), code (11.3%), and safety (5.5%).

#### 3.2. Model Training: Iterative Rejection Sampling Supervised Finetuning (RS-SFT)

The core training methodology for FARE is a semi-online iterative rejection sampling SFT, which combines the benefits of online learning with the stability and efficiency of SFT.

- **Policy Model (π_θ):** The evaluator is trained as a generative model that takes an input `x` (evaluation protocol, question, and responses) and outputs a natural language critique `c` and a final judgment `j`.
- **Initialization:** The policy model (π_θ_0) is initialized from existing post-trained LLMs: Qwen3-8B-Base (for FARE-8B) and gpt-oss-20B (for FARE-20B).
- **Iterative Process:** For each training step `t`:
 1. **Rollout:** The current policy model (π_θ_t) generates `K=4` responses for each input `x_i,t` in a rollout batch. A temperature of 0.9 is used for sampling.
 2. **Rejection Sampling:** Each of the `K` generated responses is compared against the ground-truth judgment `j*i,t`. Only correct responses are kept. If multiple correct responses are generated for an input, one is randomly chosen. Inputs with no correct responses are discarded. This forms the dataset `D_t` for the current SFT update.
 3. **Policy Update:** The policy weights `θ` are updated via SFT using `D_t`, starting from `θ_t`. This maximizes the log-likelihood of generating correct evaluation outputs.
- **Advantages of RS-SFT:** This approach avoids the problematic distribution shifts common in imitation learning (where a teacher model's outputs are used directly) and is more computationally stable and efficient than online RL-based methods, enabling scaling to millions of samples.
- **Batch Composition:** Rollout batches are sampled from the curated dataset, ensuring the task mixture mirrors the global dataset composition.
- **Inclusion of Direct Judgment Data:** A fixed percentage (40% for FARE-8B, 60% for FARE-20B) of `D_t` is converted into "direct judgment" samples where the critique `c` is omitted, and the model is prompted only for `j`. This helps isolate the judgment training signal and allows for faster inference when critiques are not needed.
- **Per-Batch Continuous Curriculum Learning:** Within each `D_t`, samples are sorted in descending order of "pass percentage" (number of correct generations out of K). This curriculum prioritizes easier samples first, benefiting certain tasks like step-level evaluation.
- **Base Models:** FARE-8B uses Qwen3-8B-Base (cold-started with SFT data from Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct to mitigate over-training issues in the original Qwen3-8B). FARE-20B uses gpt-oss-20B. Training uses batch size 128 and a constant learning rate of 1e-6.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Methodology

FARE's performance is assessed through a dual approach:

- **Core Benchmarks:** Evaluated on 7 challenging static benchmarks covering diverse aspects:
 - **Reasoning:** JudgeBench, ReasoningJudgeBench, PPE Correctness.
 - **Bias and Robustness:** RM-Bench.
 - **Tool-use:** When2Call.
 - **Step-level Error Identification:** ProcessBench.
 - **Reference-based Verification:** VerifyBench.
 Performance is measured using consistent-accuracy for pairwise tasks, F1-score for ProcessBench, and accuracy for VerifyBench. Comparisons are made against existing foundational evaluators, RL-trained judges, and strong specialized judges.
- **Downstream Applications:** To demonstrate real-world utility, FARE is tested in three practical settings:
 - **Reward Model for Inference-Time Scaling:** Used as a reranker for 10 candidate responses from various generators on JETTS benchmarks (MATH, CHAMP, MBPP+, BigCodeBench).
 - **Verifier for GRPO Training:** Integrated as a reference-based verifier in Generative Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization (GRPO) training on the WebInstruct-Verified dataset. Its impact is measured by the downstream performance of the trained policy model on a suite of reasoning benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, MATH-500, Minerva-Math, OlympiadBench, AIME24).
 - **Initialization for Continual Finetuning:** FARE-20B is continually finetuned with a small dataset (15K samples) for code evaluation, creating FARE-20B-Code, and evaluated on CodingJudgeBench tasks (code generation, repair, test-case quality).

This comprehensive methodology, from data creation to training and multi-faceted evaluation, aims to establish FARE as a robust, efficient, and highly capable family of automatic evaluators.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that FARE, trained with a large-scale data-driven approach and iterative rejection sampling SFT, achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse evaluation tasks and practical applications.

#### 4.1. Core Benchmarks

- **Pairwise Evaluation (Table 1):**
 - **Best-in-Class Performance:** FARE models consistently outperform comparably sized and many larger baseline evaluators.
 - **FARE-8B:** Challenges specialized RL-trained models (e.g., J1-8B, RM-R1-14B) on JudgeBench (13.71 and 6.57 absolute points improvement, respectively).
 - **FARE-20B:** Sets a new standard for open-source evaluators, surpassing 70B-sized judges (like J1-70B and EvalPlanner-70B) despite having 3.5x fewer total parameters. It achieves 64.29 on JudgeBench compared to J1-70B's 60.00.
 - **Reasoning & Tool-Use:** FARE excels in discerning objective correctness across various reasoning domains and demonstrates strong performance in tool-calling evaluation (When2Call), crucial for agentic workflows.
 - **Robustness:** Shows general robustness to stylistic biases (RM-Bench).

- **Step-Level Evaluation (Table 2):**
 - **Leading Generative Critic:** FARE-8B is the best small-sized generative critic model, outperforming RL-trained specialized evaluators like StepWiser-7B by 1.6 points on ProcessBench.
 - **FARE-20B:** Achieves exceptional performance, outperforming the specialized Qwen2.5-Math-72B-PRM by 6.1 points and nearly matching GPT-5. It particularly excels on the most challenging splits (OlympiadBench and OmniMATH), beating StepWiser-7B by 5.6 points and Qwen2.5-Math-72B-PRM by an average of 7.3 points. This highlights FARE's ability to identify subtle step-level errors.

- **Reference-Based Verification (Table 3):**
 - **Superior Verifiers:** Both FARE-8B and FARE-20B outperform all reported baselines on VerifyBench-Hard, a challenging verification task.
 - **FARE-20B:** Beats the next best model, GPT-4o, by a significant 12.5 absolute points on VerifyBench-Hard.

- **Scaling Inference-Time Compute (Table 4):**
 - **Enhanced Performance with Self-Consistency:** Applying self-consistency with 32 responses (SC@32) generally improves FARE's performance across most splits, widening the performance gap against baselines. For example, FARE-20B shows a 3.3-point improvement on MMLU-Pro with SC@32.
 - **Efficiency Advantage:** FARE-8B with SC@32 still beats DeepSeek-GRM-27B + MetaRM, demonstrating that smaller FARE models can compete effectively with larger, more complex systems.

#### 4.2. Downstream Evaluation

- **Reward Model for Inference-Time Scaling (Figure 3, Table 10):**
 - **Near-Oracle Reranking:** FARE exhibits best-in-class reranking performance. FARE-20B achieves near-oracle reranking performance on MATH, significantly outperforming 70B+ judges like SFR-Judge-70B and Skywork-Critic-70B by 14 and 21 absolute points, respectively.
 - **Cross-Domain Reranking:** FARE-20B is the *only judge* capable of improving performance over greedy generation across *all generators and benchmarks* tested, showcasing its broad utility. FARE-8B matches 70B judges in math and surpasses similar-sized judges in coding.

- **Verifier for GRPO Training (Figure 4):**
 - **Significant Downstream Gains:** Using FARE-20B as a verifier in GRPO training leads to substantial improvements in the downstream policy model's performance, from 34.3% to 45.2% accuracy, an almost 11 absolute point gain.
 - **Improved Convergence & Quality:** This translates to a 14.1% relative gain over typical string-matching verifiers and 9.4% over the General-Verifier baseline. These gains are consistent across both natural language and mathematical reasoning domains, indicating FARE's effectiveness in providing high-quality reward signals for complex reasoning outputs. The authors also note FARE-20B enabled competitive performance with significantly fewer gradient updates, indicating faster convergence.

- **Initialization for Domain-Specific Continual Finetuning (Figure 5):**
 - **Efficient Adaptation:** FARE serves as a strong initialization for domain-specific tasks. By continually finetuning FARE-20B with only 15K code-specific samples, FARE-20B-Code is created.
 - **Outperforms Larger Models:** FARE-20B-Code outperforms even gpt-oss-120B on average in the challenging CodingJudgeBench, particularly excelling in the difficult 'test case quality' task, where it improves over gpt-oss-20B by 11.67 absolute points (a 65% relative improvement). This demonstrates FARE's adaptability and efficiency in learning new evaluation nuances with minimal additional data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis (Appendix D)

- **Direct Judgment Proportion:** The optimal proportion of direct judgment data varies by model size (40% for FARE-8B, 60% for FARE-20B). For gpt-oss-20B-based FARE-20B, removing the intermediate CoT during direct judgment improves performance.
- **Continuous Curriculum:** Improves ProcessBench performance significantly, with minimal impact on pairwise tasks.
- **Critique Generation:** Direct judgment (without critique) improves FARE-8B performance but can degrade FARE-20B, suggesting a strong prior for CoT in its gpt-oss-20B base.
- **Positional Bias Robustness:** Emerges with data scaling, as demonstrated by increasing consistency during training (Figure 6).

In summary, FARE establishes a new benchmark for open-source generative evaluators, showcasing that a robust data strategy combined with a stable SFT approach can yield highly effective, multi-task, and multi-domain evaluation capabilities, often surpassing larger and more complex models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Foundational Automatic Reasoning Evaluators (FARE) by Salesforce AI Research represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM evaluation, with far-reaching implications across the AI research and development landscape.

#### 5.1. Elevated State-of-the-Art for Open-Source Evaluators

FARE-8B and FARE-20B set new benchmarks for open-source generative evaluators. Their ability to challenge or even surpass larger, specialized, and RL-trained models (e.g., FARE-20B beating 70B+ judges and matching GPT-5 on certain tasks) is a substantial achievement. This democratizes access to high-quality evaluation tools, enabling more researchers and developers to build and refine LLMs without relying solely on expensive, proprietary API-based evaluators.

#### 5.2. Validation of Data-Centric and Efficient Training

The work strongly validates the power of a large-scale, multi-task, multi-domain data curation strategy combined with a relatively simple yet stable training method (iterative rejection sampling SFT). This paradigm offers a compelling alternative to computationally intensive and often brittle online RL-based training. It suggests that focusing on data quality and diversity, alongside efficient SFT, can be a more scalable and robust path for developing foundational evaluators, potentially shifting research focus in this area. The emphasis on efficiency (compact CoTs, low latency) is critical for practical deployment.

#### 5.3. Enhanced LLM Development Cycle

FARE's capabilities promise to accelerate and improve various stages of the LLM development lifecycle:
- **Faster and More Reliable RL Training:** As a verifier in GRPO training, FARE significantly boosts downstream model performance and convergence speed. This can lead to more effective and efficient alignment of LLMs.
- **Improved Inference-Time Quality:** FARE's near-oracle reranking performance on challenging benchmarks like MATH enables LLMs to produce significantly better outputs without requiring architectural changes or retraining. This is a crucial "inference-time scaling" mechanism for real-world applications.
- **Better Agentic Systems:** The strong performance in tool-use evaluation is vital for the development and deployment of complex AI agents that require accurate assessment of their planning and execution, a known bottleneck in current agentic research.

#### 5.4. Facilitating Domain-Specific Adaptation

The demonstration that FARE can be continually finetuned with minimal domain-specific data (e.g., 15K code samples) to achieve state-of-the-art performance (outperforming gpt-oss-120B in code evaluation) highlights its utility as a powerful foundational model. This reduces the burden of data collection for new application areas, making high-quality, specialized evaluation more accessible.

#### 5.5. Addressing Core Evaluation Challenges

FARE directly addresses long-standing issues in automatic evaluation, such as positional biases (by showing robustness emerges with data scaling) and the need for fine-grained error detection (through strong step-level evaluation performance). Its multi-task nature ensures flexibility, capable of handling diverse evaluation protocols.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The paper opens several avenues for future work, including further exploration of direct judgment prompting, investigating optimal cold-start SFT strategies for over-trained base models, and applying FARE to an even wider array of emerging LLM tasks and domains. The rigorous benchmarking also provides a strong baseline for future evaluator models to compete against.

In essence, FARE is not just another evaluator; it represents a foundational shift towards truly versatile and high-performing automatic evaluation, grounded in scalable data and efficient training, making it an indispensable tool for the next generation of LLM research and applications.
