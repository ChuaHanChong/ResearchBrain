# CoSTA$\ast$: Cost-Sensitive Toolpath Agent for Multi-turn Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> CoSTA* is a training-free agent for multi-turn image editing. An LLM decomposes the instruction into a subtask tree that prunes a tool dependency graph, then A* search picks a toolpath on the small subgraph. Each step balances cost and quality, and a VLM checks each output and updates the tool's scores after failures. It reaches 0.94 accuracy versus 0.73 for the best baseline, with a controllable cost-quality trade-off.

## Problem

- LLM-only planners hallucinate and lack accurate knowledge of tool capabilities and costs, so they pick suboptimal tools and produce fragile paths over long multi-turn editing horizons.
- Classical search such as A* or MCTS can find optimal toolpaths but explores too expensively over large graphs of heavy models such as diffusion tools.
- Existing image-editing agents ignore cost, support few subtasks (fewer than 12 tools) and lack multimodal image-plus-text editing, so users cannot control the quality-cost trade-off.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical planning==: an LLM decomposes the instruction into a ==subtask tree==, which restricts the ==tool dependency graph== (tools linked when one's output is a valid input to another) to a small searchable subgraph.
- ==Cost-sensitive A* search==: ==heuristic h(x)== combines tool benchmark accuracy and runtime through a ==trade-off coefficient alpha==, and ==actual-execution term g(x)== accumulates real cost and quality from completed subtasks.
- ==VLM feedback loop==: each subtask output is quality-checked, and a failure updates that tool's cost and quality so A* ==backtracks== and tries alternatives; it also switches between image and ==text-in-image tools== across 24 subtasks.

## Results

- On a new **121**-task benchmark with 1 to 8 subtasks, CoSTA* reaches **0.94** overall accuracy versus **0.73** GenArtist, **0.63** CLOVA, **0.62** VisProg, **0.59** MagicBrush and **0.56** InstructPix2Pix.
- Accuracy stays near **0.93** to **0.95** as tasks grow, while baselines degrade sharply; at 7-8 image subtasks CoSTA* scores **0.95** against **0.61** for GenArtist and **0.38** to **0.46** for the rest.
- Ablations: adding g(x) feedback lifts accuracy on 35 high-risk tasks from **0.798** to **0.923**, and multimodal tool support lifts text-editing accuracy from **0.48** to **0.93** versus image-only tools.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Split planning by strength: let the LLM do coarse subtask commonsense, and let graph search do precise tool selection on the pruned space, which cuts exploration cost.
> - Published benchmark numbers give usable priors for search heuristics, and runtime execution feedback then corrects those priors where a tool underperforms on the actual input.
> - A single tunable coefficient exposes a Pareto frontier of cost versus quality; also, CLIP similarity tracks human judgment poorly on multi-step edits (Spearman 0.59), so human evaluation is needed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work fits into the rapidly evolving research landscape of:

- **Text-to-Image Generation and Editing:** The paper addresses a known limitation of current text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion and DALL-E 3: their difficulty in handling complex, multi-turn image editing tasks. This problem is a significant hurdle in the practical application of these models for real-world image manipulation scenarios.
- **Large Language Model (LLM) Agents:** It builds upon the trend of using LLMs as agents that can decompose complex tasks, select appropriate tools, and orchestrate workflows. Several recent works, such as GenArtist, DialogGen, and SmartEdit, explore this paradigm for image editing. CoSTA* contributes by focusing on cost-efficiency and incorporating prior knowledge about tool performance.
- **Neuro-Symbolic AI:** CoSTA* embodies a neuro-symbolic approach by combining the strengths of neural networks (LLMs for planning, vision-language models for evaluation) with symbolic methods (graph search using A*). This approach is gaining traction in AI as a way to overcome the limitations of purely data-driven methods and improve reasoning and generalization capabilities.
- **Cost-Sensitive AI:** A key innovation of the paper is its focus on cost-sensitivity. This aligns with a growing awareness in the AI community of the need to develop algorithms that are not only accurate but also efficient and resource-aware. This is particularly relevant for tasks involving computationally expensive models like diffusion models.
- **Tool Use and Orchestration:** The work relates to the broader area of AI agents that can select and use external tools to accomplish tasks. This is a key capability for building more general-purpose AI systems that can adapt to new situations and leverage the vast array of existing AI tools.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the paper are:

- **Develop a cost-efficient agent for multi-turn image editing:** The authors aim to create an agent (CoSTA*) that can effectively handle complex image editing tasks that require a sequence of adjustments, while minimizing the computational cost and time involved.
- **Combine the strengths of LLMs and graph search:** The motivation is to leverage the high-level planning capabilities of LLMs with the optimality guarantees of graph search algorithms (A*) to find the best toolpath.
- **Address the limitations of existing image editing agents:** Existing agents often lack cost-sensitivity, rely on expensive exploration, or struggle with multimodal tasks involving both image and text manipulation. CoSTA* seeks to overcome these limitations.
- **Create a novel benchmark for multi-turn image editing:** The authors recognize the lack of suitable benchmarks for evaluating multi-turn image editing agents. They aim to introduce a new, challenging benchmark that covers a range of complexities and modalities.
- **Enable user control over the quality-cost trade-off:** The authors want to provide users with the ability to balance the quality of the edited image with the computational cost, by providing a control parameter.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoSTA* employs a three-stage hierarchical planning strategy:

- **Subtask Tree Planning:** An LLM is used to decompose the given image editing task into a subtask tree. The LLM leverages its knowledge of common sense and task dependencies to identify the necessary subtasks and their relationships. The nodes in the tree represent subtasks, and the edges represent dependencies between them.
- **Tool Subgraph Construction:** The subtask tree is then used to prune a graph of available AI tools, called the Tool Dependency Graph (TDG). The TDG represents the dependencies between tools, where an edge between two tools indicates that the output of the first tool is a valid input to the second. The subtask tree effectively reduces the search space for the A* algorithm.
- **A* Search for Optimal Toolpath:** An A* search algorithm is applied to the pruned tool subgraph to find the most cost-efficient toolpath that satisfies the user's desired quality-cost trade-off. The A* algorithm uses a heuristic function that combines both the cost and quality of each tool on each subtask. The cost and quality metrics are obtained from prior knowledge, benchmark evaluations, and real-time feedback from a vision-language model (VLM).

The approach also incorporates:

- **Model Description Table (MDT):** A table that lists all available AI models (tools) along with their supported tasks, input requirements, and output formats.
- **Benchmark Table:** A table that stores the benchmark performance and cost of each tool for various subtasks. This information is used to initialize the heuristic function for the A* search.
- **Real-time Feedback:** A vision-language model (VLM) is used to evaluate the output of each subtask. If a subtask fails, the tool's cost and quality are updated, and the A* search can recover and explore alternative paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's main findings include:

- **CoSTA* outperforms state-of-the-art image editing models and agents:** Experimental results on the novel multi-turn image editing benchmark show that CoSTA* achieves better cost-quality trade-offs than existing methods, pushing the Pareto frontier.
- **CoSTA* is more accurate on complex, multimodal tasks:** The A* search integration and the diverse toolset enable CoSTA* to handle intricate workflows and multimodal tasks involving both image and text manipulation more effectively.
- **Cost-sensitive A* search enables flexible quality-cost trade-offs:** By adjusting the parameter α, users can control the balance between quality and cost, adapting to different preferences and budgets.
- **Real-time feedback improves path selection and execution robustness:** The VLM-based feedback mechanism allows CoSTA* to recover from failures and dynamically adjust its toolpath, leading to more reliable results.
- **Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of real-time feedback and multimodality support:** Removing either of these components significantly degrades performance, highlighting their contribution to CoSTA*'s effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this work are:

- **Advances the state-of-the-art in multi-turn image editing:** CoSTA* provides a novel and effective approach for tackling complex image editing tasks, overcoming the limitations of existing models and agents.
- **Promotes cost-efficient AI development:** The focus on cost-sensitivity is important for practical deployment and scalability of AI systems, particularly in resource-constrained environments.
- **Offers a valuable framework for combining LLMs and symbolic methods:** The neuro-symbolic approach of CoSTA* demonstrates the benefits of integrating neural networks with graph search algorithms, providing a potential blueprint for other AI applications.
- **Provides a new benchmark for multi-turn image editing:** The novel benchmark created by the authors will facilitate future research in this area, allowing for more rigorous evaluation and comparison of different methods.
- **Enables new applications in image manipulation:** CoSTA* can potentially be used in various applications, such as content creation, image restoration, and accessibility tools for visually impaired users.
- **Highlight responsible development and deployment:** The inclusion of an "Impact Statement" discussing the potential misuse of image manipulation systems and advocating for safeguards like provenance tracking and bias mitigation demonstrates a commitment to responsible AI development.
