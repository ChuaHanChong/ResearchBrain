# Humanoid Parkour Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A humanoid robot policy was developed that allows the Unitree H1 to perform a diverse range of parkour skills, including jumping and leaping over gaps, using only onboard vision. The system achieves robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without relying on explicit motion priors or complex reward engineering.

## Problem

- Applying learning-based methods to humanoid robots for dynamic locomotion and parkour is challenging due to inherent instability, high-dimensional control, and the typical reliance on extensive motion priors or complex reward engineering.
- Existing humanoid locomotion methods often lack generalizability, requiring task-specific tuning or pre-programmed movements for different obstacles.
- Achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for agile humanoid behaviors is difficult due to sensory noise, latency, and hardware constraints.

## Method

- A three-stage reinforcement learning framework combines forward parkour training from planar walking, massive parallel oracle parkour policy training with auto-curriculum, and visual perception distillation.
- Fractal noise applied to terrain heights during training encourages diverse gaits and foot lifting without needing explicit "feet air time" reward terms or motion references.
- A teacher-student distillation approach (DAgger) transfers knowledge from an oracle policy (trained with perfect terrain information) to a student policy that processes noisy depth images from a simulated real-world camera.

## Results

- The policy enabled a Unitree H1 robot to jump onto 0.42m platforms and leap over 0.8m gaps, and navigate various stairs, hurdles, and slopes using onboard vision.
- Policies trained with fractal noise consistently outperformed those using "feet air time" rewards in simulation across challenging parkour skills, achieving higher success rates and greater distances.
- The learned leg-centric parkour policy demonstrated robustness to external control of the robot's arms, maintaining balance and stable locomotion even when upper limbs were teleoperated.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training with fractal noise terrain effectively replaces explicit "feet air time" rewards or motion references, leading to more diverse and robust emergent gaits for humanoids.
> - Onboard depth vision is crucial for enabling agile parkour skills, allowing the humanoid to autonomously perceive and adapt to diverse obstacles.
> - Efficient sim-to-real transfer for complex whole-body control policies benefits significantly from initializing the student policy with an expert (oracle) policy and leveraging multi-GPU accelerated distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged locomotion, particularly for humanoid robots, stands at the intersection of robotics, control theory, and artificial intelligence. Historically, impressive demonstrations of robot agility, such as Boston Dynamics' Atlas, relied heavily on meticulous, hand-engineered motion planning and control, often specific to a predefined sequence of obstacles. While yielding visually stunning results, these approaches suffer from a lack of generalizability, require extensive human effort for each new task, and are typically limited to controlled lab environments due to the high cost and complexity of hardware.

The broader research landscape has seen a significant shift towards learning-based methods, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), which offers the promise of more adaptable and generalizable robot behaviors. This paradigm has achieved remarkable success with quadruped robots, enabling them to walk robustly on diverse terrains, climb stairs, and even perform "parkour" maneuvers in complex environments (e.g., *Robot Parkour Learning* by Zhuang et al., 2023, and *Extreme Parkour with Legged Robots* by Cheng et al., 2023, both cited by the authors). These quadruped advancements often leverage large-scale simulation for training and sophisticated sim-to-real transfer techniques.

However, applying these learning-based methods to humanoid robots presents unique and substantially greater challenges:
- **Higher Instability:** Humanoids, with their bipedal nature and high center of gravity, are inherently less stable than quadrupeds, making robust balance control more difficult.
- **Whole-Body Coordination:** Agile humanoid locomotion demands complex coordination of not just the legs but also the arms and torso for balance and momentum, leading to a much higher-dimensional control problem.
- **Data Scarcity and Motion Priors:** Many existing learning-based humanoid locomotion methods still rely on significant "motion priors" (e.g., human motion capture data, predefined gaits) or explicit reward engineering (e.g., "feet air time") to guide the learning process. While helpful, these can limit the robot's ability to discover novel, more optimal, or diverse maneuvers.
- **Perception and Latency:** Real-time perception of complex terrains and rapid decision-making are critical, but sensory processing introduces latency, which can destabilize an already sensitive system.
- **Hardware Constraints:** The power throughput and actuator capacity of electric humanoid robots are often pushed to their limits during dynamic tasks like parkour, necessitating proactive action smoothness and damage mitigation.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by attempting to develop a *general* parkour system for humanoids that operates fully onboard, without external support, and critically, *without relying on extensive motion priors or task-specific reward engineering*. It aims to bridge the gap between impressive, but pre-programmed, humanoid demonstrations and the highly agile, adaptable, and autonomous behaviors seen in learning-based quadruped robots, pushing the frontier of "athletic intelligence" for embodied systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **end-to-end vision-based whole-body-control parkour policy for humanoid robots** that can overcome a diverse set of challenging obstacles without any motion prior or complex reward engineering.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Achieving General Humanoid Agility:** To enable humanoid robots to perform dynamic, human-like parkour maneuvers (e.g., jumping onto platforms, leaping over gaps, hurdling, navigating various slopes and stairs) as a benchmark for "athletic intelligence." This moves beyond the current state-of-the-art for learning-based humanoids, which is largely limited to robust walking on flat or gently uneven terrains.
- **Reducing Reliance on Motion Priors and Complex Reward Engineering:** A significant motivation is to simplify the learning process. The authors challenge the conventional need for reward terms that explicitly encourage foot raising (e.g., "feet air time") or reference trajectories, proposing a more implicit and generalizable approach.
- **Unified Policy for Diverse Skills:** To train a single locomotion network capable of executing a wide array of parkour skills, rather than relying on separate controllers or task-specific engineering for each maneuver. This promotes efficiency and adaptability.
- **Robustness and Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To ensure the learned policy is robust enough to transfer directly from simulation to a real-world humanoid robot (Unitree H1) without further fine-tuning. This is crucial for practical deployment and accelerates development cycles.
- **Autonomous Skill Selection:** To demonstrate that the robot can autonomously perceive its surroundings (via onboard vision) and select appropriate parkour skills to overcome encountered obstacles.
- **Foundation for Mobile Manipulation:** To explore the robustness of the locomotion policy to external influences, specifically by testing if the robot can maintain balance and perform parkour while its arm actions are overridden, paving the way for future mobile manipulation tasks.
- **Addressing System Challenges:** To explicitly tackle the inherent challenges of agile humanoid locomotion, including balancing diverse skill learning, integrating egocentric perception efficiently, mitigating system latency, and operating within hardware power constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a comprehensive three-stage training framework for learning an end-to-end vision-based whole-body control parkour policy, primarily using Reinforcement Learning (RL) and a sim-to-real distillation approach.

#### 3.1. Training Forward Parkour from Planar Walking (Stage 1 & 2)

- **Unified Architecture:** The core policy uses a Gate Recurrent Unit (GRU) and an MLP for the actor, with an MLP-based state estimator that predicts base linear velocity from proprioception and last action.
- **Fractal Noise for General Gait Learning:** To enable the robot to lift its feet naturally and learn diverse gaits without explicit "feet air time" rewards or motion references, the authors introduce **fractal noise** to the height field of the terrain during training. This continuously varied terrain encourages the robot to adapt and raise its feet dynamically.
- **Auto Command Mechanism:** For straight parkour tracks, a common issue is the loss of turning ability. The authors design an auto-command mechanism that initializes the robot's orientation randomly and guides it towards the parkour track using yaw commands. This ensures the policy retains general walking and turning capabilities.
- **Reward Function Design:** The policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a reward function comprising:
 - **Task Rewards:** Encouraging linear and angular velocity tracking and minimizing energy consumption.
 - **Regularization Rewards:** Penalizing high torque, joint acceleration, joint velocity, and foot contact. Torque penalties are weighted by joint-specific `kp` factors to distribute the penalty more evenly across motors.
 - **Safety Rewards:** Penalizing unnecessary arm and waist movements, encouraging feet separation, and promoting smooth action rates. Importantly, **no motion reference is used.**
- **Massively Parallel Oracle Parkour Policy Training (Curriculum Learning):**
 - **Diverse Terrain Generation:** 10 types of parkour obstacles (e.g., jump up/down, leap, stairs, hurdles, slopes, discrete/wave terrains) are generated in IsaacGym, with 40 columns and 10 rows, allowing for different difficulties.
 - **Auto-Curriculum:** The robot starts on easier terrain and progressively moves to more difficult terrain upon successful task completion (moving >3/4 distance of sub-terrain). Failure (falling or stopping <1/2 distance) leads to an easier terrain.
 - **Scandots for Oracle Perception:** The "oracle" policy, which has full access to simulation terrain data, uses "scandots" (a sparse representation of terrain height) as its exteroceptive input. This is computationally efficient in IsaacGym.
 - **Novel Safety Reward Terms:**
 - **Virtual Obstacle Penetration Penalty:** For tasks like leaping, a "virtual obstacle" is defined around the robot's body. Penetration into this virtual obstacle (computed as penetration depth multiplied by velocity of penetration points) is heavily penalized (Equation 1). This prevents exploiting dangerous edges and promotes safer, more robust maneuvers.
 - **Footstep Reward:** For tasks like stairs, a reward (Equation 2) is given based on the negative logarithm of the x-offset between the robot's foot and a recommended stepping point. This encourages accurate foot placement without explicit footstep planning.
- **Domain Randomization:** To facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, extensive domain randomization is applied to various physical parameters (added mass, center of mass, friction, motor strength) and sensor properties (proprioception/depth latency, depth camera FoV, position, orientation).

#### 3.2. Distilling Visual Perception from Scandots (Stage 3)

- **Teacher-Student Distillation (DAgger):** The core idea is to distill the knowledge from the "oracle" policy (trained with perfect scandot information) into a "student" policy that uses real-world visual input (depth images). DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) is employed.
- **Initialize Student from Teacher:** The student policy's recurrent (GRU) and MLP modules are initialized with weights from the trained oracle parkour policy. Only the scandot encoder is replaced with a randomly initialized Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) designed to process depth images. This significantly speeds up convergence.
- **Simulating Real-World Depth Noise:** To bridge the gap between pristine simulation depth data and noisy real-world sensor data, the raw rendered depth images in simulation are corrupted with simulated noise patterns from an Intel RealSense D435i camera (depth clipping, multi-level Gaussian noise, random artifacts). Correspondingly, real-world depth images are pre-processed using built-in RealSense filters (depth clipping, hole filling, spatial/temporal smoothing).
- **Multi-Process Distillation Acceleration:** Recognizing that depth image rendering in IsaacGym is computationally expensive, the DAgger distillation process is accelerated using 4 Nvidia 3090 GPUs. One GPU acts as a trainer, while three others function as collectors, generating student trajectories and labeling them with teacher actions. Data is exchanged via a shared filesystem. The objective for distillation is the L1 norm between student and teacher actions.

#### 3.3. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment

- The distilled policy model is directly transferred to the Unitree H1 robot.
- The robot's onboard Intel Core i7 CPU handles the policy execution, with the visual encoder running at 10Hz and the rest of the network at 50Hz.
- Communication between processes and with motors uses ROS2 and Cyclone DDS.
- Torque limits are enforced by clipping target joint positions based on motor capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling evidence for the effectiveness and robustness of their proposed framework through both simulation and real-world experiments.

- **Effectiveness of Fractal Noise (vs. "Feet Air Time"):** In comparative simulation tests across 10 terrain types, the policy trained with fractal noise terrain consistently achieved higher success rates and average moving distances in challenging parkour skills (Jump up, Leap, Stairs up, Hurdle) compared to policies trained with the commonly used "feet air time" reward term. This demonstrates that fractal noise provides a superior and less restrictive inductive bias for learning diverse and robust gaits, allowing for more freedom in downstream whole-body control tasks.
- **Crucial Role of Onboard Vision:** Real-world quantitative experiments on the Unitree H1 robot confirm that onboard depth vision is indispensable for agile parkour. The parkour policy, utilizing vision, significantly outperformed a "blind walking policy" and the Unitree H1's built-in MPC controller in difficult tasks (e.g., jumping up to 0.42m platforms, leaping over 0.8m gaps, stairs, hurdles). This highlights the robot's ability to autonomously perceive and select appropriate skills based on visual input.
- **Criticality of Expert Policy Initialization and Multi-GPU Acceleration:** Ablation studies on the distillation process revealed:
 - Distilling from a randomly initialized student policy resulted in near-zero success rates, struggling even to maintain balance.
 - Distilling from the oracle policy but with only one GPU achieved only half the performance of the full 4-GPU accelerated method within the same 24-hour training window. This underscores the necessity of initializing the student policy from an expert and leveraging massive parallelization (432 million transitions in 24 hours vs. 4.147 million for 1-GPU) for efficient and high-quality policy distillation, especially given the computational cost of depth rendering.
- **Emergent Whole-Body Control Behavior:** Observations of locomotion behavior demonstrated that the policy, despite regularization terms discouraging arm and waist movement, still exhibited meaningful whole-body coordination. For example, during high jumps, the robot's arms dynamically swung to maintain balance, showcasing emergent, task-relevant behaviors.
- **Robustness to Upper Limb Control Override:** A significant finding for future applications is the policy's robustness to external control of the upper limbs. Even when a human operator teleoperated the robot's arms (creating an out-of-distribution scenario), the humanoid maintained balance and stable locomotion. This suggests that the learned leg-centric parkour policy is sufficiently robust to allow for concurrent mobile manipulation tasks without significant disruption to stability.
- **Achieved Performance Metrics:** The policy enabled the Unitree H1 robot to successfully jump onto 0.42m platforms, leap over 0.8m gaps, overcome various hurdles, run at 1.8m/s in the wild, and walk robustly on diverse terrains (slopes, stairs, discrete, wave, tilted ramps).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a substantial advancement in the field of humanoid robot locomotion and embodied AI, with several significant implications:

- **Pioneering General Humanoid Agile Locomotion:** By demonstrating a unified policy capable of performing diverse and challenging parkour skills without motion references, this work pushes the boundaries of autonomous agile locomotion for humanoids. It moves beyond the traditional limitations of either pre-programmed movements or learning methods constrained to flat surfaces, bringing humanoids closer to operating effectively in complex, unstructured real-world environments.
- **Simplified Policy Design and Scalability:** The successful use of fractal noise instead of complex reward terms like "feet air time" or explicit motion priors simplifies the reward engineering process for complex humanoid tasks. This simplification is critical for scaling up learning to even more diverse behaviors and for making the development of new humanoid skills more efficient.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Robustness:** The achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across a wide range of obstacles (10 types) demonstrates a high degree of generalizability and robustness. This is a crucial step towards deploying humanoids in real-world scenarios that demand adaptability to unforeseen terrain variations.
- **Enabling Mobile Manipulation:** The demonstrated robustness of the locomotion policy to overridden arm actions is a key enabling factor for future mobile manipulation tasks. It implies that humanoids can potentially perform complex dexterous manipulations while simultaneously navigating challenging terrains, without needing to completely re-learn or compromise their locomotion stability. This opens doors for applications in logistics, disaster response, construction, or even assistance in human environments, where both mobility and interaction are required.
- **Advancing Athletic Intelligence:** The paper contributes to the broader quest for "athletic intelligence" in robots, showcasing how deep reinforcement learning combined with robust simulation and distillation techniques can unlock highly dynamic and intelligent physical behaviors. It provides a blueprint for developing embodied agents that can perceive, decide, and execute complex actions in dynamic environments.
- **Practical Framework for Sim-to-Real:** The detailed methodology, particularly the multi-stage training, the domain randomization, and the accelerated distillation process, offers a practical and effective framework for achieving sim-to-real transfer for complex whole-body control systems, addressing critical challenges like visual noise and computational efficiency.

While acknowledging limitations such as reliance on manually constructed terrain for training, the demonstrated capabilities lay a strong foundation for future research, including generalization to unseen terrains and tighter integration with more complex manipulation skills. The work marks a significant stride towards creating truly versatile and agile humanoid robots.
