# Humanoid Parkour Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A humanoid robot policy was developed that allows the Unitree H1 to perform a diverse range of parkour skills, including jumping and leaping over gaps, using only onboard vision. The system achieves robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without relying on explicit motion priors or complex reward engineering.

## Problem

- Applying learning-based methods to humanoid robots for dynamic locomotion and parkour is challenging due to inherent instability, high-dimensional control, and the typical reliance on extensive motion priors or complex reward engineering.
- Existing humanoid locomotion methods often lack generalizability, requiring task-specific tuning or pre-programmed movements for different obstacles.
- Achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for agile humanoid behaviors is difficult due to sensory noise, latency, and hardware constraints.

## Method

- A three-stage reinforcement learning framework combines forward parkour training from planar walking, massive parallel oracle parkour policy training with auto-curriculum, and visual perception distillation.
- Fractal noise applied to terrain heights during training encourages diverse gaits and foot lifting without needing explicit "feet air time" reward terms or motion references.
- A teacher-student distillation approach (DAgger) transfers knowledge from an oracle policy (trained with perfect terrain information) to a student policy that processes noisy depth images from a simulated real-world camera.

## Results

- The policy enabled a Unitree H1 robot to jump onto 0.42m platforms and leap over 0.8m gaps, and navigate various stairs, hurdles, and slopes using onboard vision.
- Policies trained with fractal noise consistently outperformed those using "feet air time" rewards in simulation across challenging parkour skills, achieving higher success rates and greater distances.
- The learned leg-centric parkour policy demonstrated robustness to external control of the robot's arms, maintaining balance and stable locomotion even when upper limbs were teleoperated.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training with fractal noise terrain effectively replaces explicit "feet air time" rewards or motion references, leading to more diverse and robust emergent gaits for humanoids.
> - Onboard depth vision is crucial for enabling agile parkour skills, allowing the humanoid to autonomously perceive and adapt to diverse obstacles.
> - Efficient sim-to-real transfer for complex whole-body control policies benefits significantly from initializing the student policy with an expert (oracle) policy and leveraging multi-GPU accelerated distillation.
