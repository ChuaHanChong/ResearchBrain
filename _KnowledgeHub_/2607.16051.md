# Loop the Loopies!

> [!summary] Summary
> A new scaling methodology, the "Loopie Recipe," enables recurrent Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language models to achieve competitive or superior performance under identical pre-training compute budgets. This approach culminated in Loopie models demonstrating gold-medal level performance on the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and International Physics Olympiad (IPhO) 2025 problems without external tools.

## Problem

- Looped Transformers historically faced a "compute-accounting issue," where recurrent steps significantly increased pre-training compute cost, often negating parameter efficiency benefits under fixed budgets.
- Effective integration of recurrent computation with modern Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures was an open challenge for competitive large language model (LLM) scaling.
- Achieving advanced, frontier-level reasoning abilities in LLMs, specifically on complex academic competitions like IMO and IPhO, remained a difficult goal without reliance on external tools.

## Method

- Developed the "**Loopie** Recipe," a hardware-aware scaling procedure that optimizes throughput by using a ==layer-loop== recurrence (R=2) to reduce stored layers, reinvesting memory headroom into larger microbatch sizes and increased model capacity.
- Introduced scalable looped ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== language models (**Loopie**-20B-A2B, **Loopie**-6B-A0.6B) based on a decoder-only architecture with a specific ==layer-loop== recurrence pattern.
- Implemented a multi-stage pre-training and post-training pipeline, featuring a novel ==Supervised Pre-Training (SPT)== stage and ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)==, to imbue strong reasoning abilities.

## Results

- Loopie-20B-A2B surpassed a compute-matched vanilla 30B-A3B MoE Transformer in pre-training performance after **600 billion tokens**, with higher average throughput (**261.53 TFLOPS/s** vs. **189.65 TFLOPS/s**).
- Loopie-20B-A2B achieved gold-medal thresholds on IMO 2025 (**35 points**) and IPhO 2025 (**20.3 points**) without external tools, utilizing extensive test-time scaling pipelines.
- In post-training, Loopie-20B-A2B showed competitive or superior performance against larger models (Nemotron 3 Nano, Nemotron Cascade 2) on benchmarks like MMLU (**81.28**), ARC-Challenge (**93.52**), BBH (**82.28**), and IFEval (**84.72**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recurrent computation, when combined with optimized scaling and a "layer-loop" pattern, can become compute-competitive, surpassing vanilla Transformers under the same pre-training compute budget.
> - The "Loopie Recipe" effectively translates reduced activation memory from recurrence into increased training throughput and model capacity, enabling more efficient resource utilization.
> - Supervised Pre-Training (SPT), applying SFT-style loss at pre-training scale, can imbue broad reasoning and task-following capabilities while preserving general knowledge and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Loop the Loopies!" is situated within the field of large language model (LLM) development, specifically focusing on the architectural design of Transformers. The paper addresses the re-emergence of "looped Transformers," also known as Universal Transformers, as an alternative to traditional depth scaling in neural networks. Instead of stacking numerous distinct layers, looped Transformers repeatedly apply a shared model or layer across recurrent steps, a concept related to parameter sharing.

Prior research has indicated that recurrent computation is beneficial for complex problems, with looped Transformers demonstrating strong empirical performance in domains such as language modeling, algorithmic learning, and abstract reasoning. These models have shown capabilities in in-context learning and data-fitting tasks, and can implement multi-step gradient descent with fewer parameters than non-recurrent architectures. Studies on tasks like ARC-AGI, Sudoku, and Maze suggest that recurrence provides an inductive bias for compositional generalization, rule discovery, and inductive reasoning.

Recent advancements in scaling looped Transformers include models like Ouro (1.4B and 2.6B parameters with four recurrent steps) and Huginn (3.5B parameters with 32 recurrent steps), which have demonstrated parameter efficiency. However, a central challenge in scaling looped Transformers has been the "compute-accounting issue." Looping a model *N* times during pre-training approximately multiplies the pre-training compute cost by *N*. This implies that a looped model should be evaluated against non-looped models trained under the *same pre-training compute budget*, rather than solely on parameter count. Previous work suggested that under fixed-FLOP comparisons, looped models might have less non-recurrent capacity, indicating that recurrence primarily improved parameter efficiency but not necessarily compute efficiency.

Furthermore, modern frontier LLMs increasingly utilize Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, such as Qwen3, Kimi K2.5, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4, and MiniMax-M2, to expand total model capacity while maintaining a relatively small number of active parameters per token. This makes MoE models efficient under training and inference compute constraints. Therefore, integrating recurrent computation with MoE architectures is crucial for competitive scaling.

This paper aims to bridge these gaps by developing looped MoE LLMs that can compete with or surpass vanilla Transformers under equivalent pre-training compute budgets, specifically integrating with MoE architectures and demonstrating strong reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether looped Transformers can achieve performance equivalent to or superior to vanilla Transformers when trained under an identical pre-training compute budget. This addresses a fundamental challenge in the scalability of recurrent architectures, where increased computational cost due to looping often outweighs the benefits of parameter efficiency in a fixed-compute scenario.

The motivation behind this objective is multifaceted:
- **Addressing the Compute-Accounting Issue**: The authors aim to overcome the historical hurdle that looping a model *N* times during pre-training incurs an *N*-fold increase in computational cost. They propose that for recurrent Transformers to be a viable scaling strategy for large language models, they must demonstrate competitive performance against non-looped models when total pre-training compute is held constant. This perspective aligns with established language model scaling laws which prioritize total training FLOPs rather than parameter count alone.
- **Compatibility with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Architectures**: Modern large language models increasingly adopt MoE architectures for their ability to achieve high total parameter counts with sparse activation, which is efficient for both training and inference. A significant motivation for this work is to integrate recurrent computation effectively within an MoE framework, rather than being limited to dense Transformer backbones. This integration is posited as essential for future scaling to trillion-parameter models.
- **Developing a Compute-Matched Scaling Strategy**: The research is motivated by the hypothesis that recurrence can become compute-competitive if it is paired with an appropriate trade-off among stored width, stored depth, and recurrent depth. This led to the formulation of the "Loopie Recipe," a hardware-aware scaling procedure designed to optimize performance under a fixed pre-training compute budget.
- **Achieving Advanced Reasoning Abilities**: Beyond general language modeling, a key motivation is to develop models capable of advanced, frontier-level reasoning. This is specifically demonstrated through the ambition to achieve gold-medal performance on highly challenging academic competitions such as the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Physics Olympiad (IPhO) without the aid of external tools. This represents a rigorous test of the models' intrinsic problem-solving capabilities.

The paper outlines three main contributions:
1. The introduction and validation of the "Loopie Recipe" for compute-matched scaling in looped Transformers, demonstrated through extensive ablation studies.
2. The successful development and training of scalable looped MoE language models, specifically Loopie-20B-A2B and Loopie-6B-A0.6B.
3. The implementation of a large-scale post-training pipeline, including a novel supervised pre-training stage, to imbue Loopie with strong reasoning abilities, leading to gold-medal level performance in the IMO and IPhO.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves designing and training a series of looped Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Transformers, dubbed the Loopie series, through a multi-stage process that prioritizes compute-matched scaling and targeted post-training for reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.1. Architecture

Loopie models are based on a decoder-only Mixture-of-Experts Transformer architecture, similar to the Qwen3-MoE family, maintaining comparable attention mechanisms and sparsity patterns. The core architectural distinction is the adoption of a "layer-loop" recurrence pattern. In contrast to "model-loop" (where the entire Transformer stack is repeated), layer-loop applies each layer recurrently multiple times before passing its output to the subsequent layer (e.g., Layer 1 → Layer 1 → Layer 2 → Layer 2 → Layer 3 → Layer 3). This design is motivated by observed empirical scaling benefits, improved infrastructure-friendly execution due to better locality for parameter and gradient reuse, and a more coherent parameter-sharing pattern at adjacent effective depths. The models utilize two loop steps (R=2), selected based on ablation studies indicating that the marginal return of additional recurrence steps decays rapidly under fixed compute budgets, with R=2 providing the optimal trade-off between iterative refinement, throughput, and compute-matched scaling.

#### 3.2. Loopie Recipe: Compute-Matched Scaling

The "Loopie Recipe" is a hardware-aware scaling procedure designed to match models by *realized pre-training cost* (optimizer-step wall-clock time) rather than theoretical FLOPs. It comprises three steps:
1. **Construct Recurrent Seed Model**: Starting from a non-recurrent MoE reference (e.g., Qwen3-30B-A3B), the number of stored layers is halved, and the recurrent depth is set to R=2. This approximately preserves the leading-order Transformer block compute while substantially reducing activation memory.
2. **Utilize Memory Headroom**: The reduced activation memory allows for doubling the per-device microbatch size while proportionally halving the number of gradient-accumulation steps. This enhances microbatch efficiency.
3. **Reinvest Efficiency into Capacity**: The measured training efficiency gain from the larger microbatch size is reinvested into increasing model capacity (hidden dimension *D* and number of stored layers *L*). The final architecture is chosen by selecting the configuration whose measured end-to-end optimizer-step time most closely matches the non-recurrent reference. For Loopie-20B-A2B, the process started from a Qwen3-like 30B-A3B model (D=2048, L=48) and resulted in a model with D=2304, L=27, R=2.

#### 3.3. Pre-Training

Pre-training is conducted in two stages using the Megatron-LM framework with the Qwen3 tokenizer:
1. **Stage 1: Multi-Epoch High-Quality Pre-Training**: Both Loopie-20B-A2B and Loopie-6B-A0.6B are trained on Nemotron-CC-v2-HQ (approximately 570B unique tokens) for four epochs, totaling 2.28T tokens. This multi-epoch approach on high-quality data is chosen for its effectiveness over single-pass training on larger, lower-quality corpora.
2. **Stage 2: High-Quality Annealing**: A further 1.26T tokens are used, composed of a diverse mixture of high-quality data. This mixture includes Nemotron-pre-training-SFT-v1 (SFT-style data spanning STEM, academic, code, math, reasoning), Nemotron-pre-training-Specialized-v1 (STEM reasoning, scientific/cross-domain coding, synthetic Wikipedia/math textbooks), Nemotron-pre-training-Code-v2 (GitHub, synthetic code QA, dialogues), Nemotron-CC-v2-HQ-Synthetic (rephrased web), Nemotron-CC-v2.1-HQ (recent web, translated), and Nemotron-CC-Math-v1 (high-quality math corpus).
Training utilizes AdamW optimizer with a warmup-stable-only learning rate schedule, a global batch size of 1024, and a sequence length of 8192. Model parameters are initialized using specific Gaussian scales (d⁻¹/² for embeddings/LM head, (2.5d)⁻¹/² for block sub-layers).

#### 3.4. Post-Training

Post-training further refines Loopie's capabilities through two main stages:
1. **Supervised Pre-Training (SPT)**: This novel regime applies a supervised, target-token-only loss function (like SFT) but at the optimization scale of traditional pre-training (large global batch sizes, long sequence lengths, multi-trillion token budgets). SPT aims to acquire broad task-following, reasoning, coding, and tool-use capabilities over 2T tokens, while simultaneously preserving and enhancing general knowledge, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting typically observed in conventional SFT.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Applied to mathematical and coding tasks from the Guru-RL corpus, RL uses an optimizer based on Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO). This includes asymmetric clipping (Clip-Higher) and dynamic filtering of prompts to focus on those yielding non-degenerate policy gradients. Rewards are computed via rule-based answer equivalence for math and sandboxed unit-test execution for code. Training progresses through two context-length stages (32K then 64K tokens) until validation performance saturates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, scalability, and reasoning capabilities of the Loopie models.

#### 4.1. Compute-Matched Performance

Comparison between Loopie-20B-A2B and a compute-matched vanilla 30B-A3B MoE Transformer, reproduced from Qwen3, shows that Loopie initially lags during early pre-training. However, after approximately 600 billion tokens of pre-training, Loopie-20B-A2B surpasses the baseline and maintains a consistent advantage. This suggests that the Loopie Recipe, by integrating recurrent computation and optimized scaling, can lead to stronger final performance under the same pre-training compute budget compared to allocating compute to a larger vanilla Transformer. The measured average throughput for Loopie-20B-A2B (261.53 TFLOPS/s) exceeded that of the Qwen3-30B-A3B (189.65 TFLOPS/s) under optimal configurations in Megatron-LM benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Scalability

A scaling ladder comprising four non-recurrent MoE baselines (ranging from 0.15B to 1B parameters) and their corresponding compute-matched Loopie models was established. Across all four scales, Loopie models consistently outperformed their vanilla counterparts in average downstream benchmark accuracy. Critically, the performance gap did not diminish as model size increased, indicating that the Loopie design and recipe maintain their advantage across a meaningful scaling range and are not merely small-model artifacts.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

An ablation study on Loopie-6B-A0.6B demonstrated the specific contribution of the "layer-loop" recurrence schedule. A variant of Loopie-6B-A0.6B with the layer-loop pattern removed, but preserving the overall computation budget, showed substantially lower downstream performance. This indicates that the specific ordering of recurrent computation, rather than just the presence of recurrence, is a significant factor in achieving high compute efficiency and performance.

#### 4.4. Post-Training Results

- **Comparison with Similarly Sized MoE Reasoning Models**: Loopie-20B-A2B, pre-trained on 3.5T tokens, demonstrated competitive or superior performance against Nemotron 3 Nano and Nemotron Cascade 2 (both trained on 25T tokens). Loopie achieved 81.28 on MMLU (exceeding Nemotron 3 Nano 80.52 and Nemotron Cascade 2 81.22) and 93.52 on ARC-Challenge (outperforming Nemotron 3 Nano by 1.56 points). It also substantially surpassed both Nemotron models on BBH (82.28 vs. 68.76 and 75.86) and IFEval (84.72 vs. 77.05 and 79.21). This suggests higher token efficiency. In mathematics benchmarks, Loopie achieved 92.09 on AIME 2024 and 94.21 on AMC, ranking second among the compared models, and tied Qwen3-30B-A3B Thinking with 83.75 on AIME 2025.
- **Comparison with Compact Reasoning Models**: Loopie-6B-A0.6B generally outperformed other listed compact reasoning models across knowledge, general, code, and math benchmarks.

#### 4.5. International Olympiad Performance

- **International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) 2025**: Using an extensive generate–verify–refine test-time scaling pipeline (up to 16 iterations with 64 candidate solutions and verifications), Loopie-20B-A2B achieved a total score of 35 points on the 2025 IMO problems, which met the gold-medal threshold.
- **International Physics Olympiad (IPhO) 2025**: Employing a similar generate–vote–critic test-time scaling strategy, Loopie-20B-A2B scored 20.3 points on the IPhO problems, exceeding the gold-medal threshold of 19.7 points. These results were achieved without the use of external tools.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Loop the Loopies!" offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the development of large language models and the broader field of artificial intelligence:

#### 5.1. Validation of Recurrent Computation as a Scalable LLM Axis

A key significance of this work is its demonstration that recurrent computation, when carefully integrated with architectural design and training efficiency, can serve as a practical and effective scaling axis for large language models. By directly addressing the "compute-accounting issue," the Loopie series challenges the prior notion that parameter scaling always outperforms recurrent depth scaling under fixed compute budgets. This opens avenues for exploring recurrent architectures not just for parameter efficiency, but for overall computational efficiency and performance gains.

#### 5.2. Introduction of the Loopie Recipe and Enhanced Training Efficiency

The novel "Loopie Recipe" provides a principled, hardware-aware methodology for compute-matched scaling. This approach effectively converts memory headroom, achieved by reducing stored depth with layer-loop recurrence, into increased per-device microbatch size and, subsequently, reinvests the measured throughput gains into additional model capacity. This method offers a practical blueprint for optimizing resource utilization in large-scale pre-training, leading to more performant models for a given computational investment.

#### 5.3. Advanced Reasoning Capabilities and Olympiad-Level Performance

The achievement of gold-medal level performance on the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and International Physics Olympiad (IPhO) problems without external tools represents a notable advancement in the intrinsic reasoning capabilities of large language models. This level of performance on highly complex, multi-step, abstract reasoning tasks suggests that the combination of the Loopie architecture and its large-scale post-training pipeline (including supervised pre-training and reinforcement learning) has enabled the model to develop sophisticated problem-solving skills. This could accelerate research into AI systems capable of human-level scientific and mathematical reasoning.

#### 5.4. Supervised Pre-Training (SPT) as an Effective Post-Training Paradigm

The proposed Supervised Pre-Training (SPT) method, which applies SFT-style objectives at PT-scale optimization, appears to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. Unlike conventional SFT, SPT reportedly improves both general-knowledge (pre-training) metrics and reasoning metrics simultaneously, without observed overfitting over extended training durations. If widely applicable, SPT could provide a more robust and efficient pathway for aligning and specializing large base models for diverse tasks without compromising their foundational knowledge.

#### 5.5. Scalability and Compatibility with MoE Architectures

The successful scaling of Loopie to MoE models like Loopie-20B-A2B demonstrates the compatibility of recurrent computation with state-of-the-art sparse architectures. As MoE models become increasingly prevalent in frontier LLMs, this research indicates that the benefits of recurrence can be leveraged within these architectures, potentially leading to even more powerful and efficient models.

Future work could explore the impact of Loopie on a broader range of post-training tasks, systematically investigate inference-time compute optimizations, and examine its synergy with other emerging architectural advancements.
