# DrivingGPT: Unifying Driving World Modeling and Planning with Multi-modal Autoregressive Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> DrivingGPT unifies driving world modeling and end-to-end planning into one autoregressive transformer over a multimodal driving language of interleaved discrete image and action tokens, trained via standard next-token prediction. It surpasses SVD-based diffusion baselines on video generation and a ResNet-50+MLP planner baseline on NAVSIM, reaching PDMS 82.4.

## Problem

- Driving world models rely on video diffusion architectures that specialize in visual generation but lack flexibility to natively incorporate other modalities like action sequences.
- Diffusion-based world modeling and end-to-end trajectory planning remain separate, non-differentiable pipelines, blocking integrated model-based planning.
- Diffusion decoders lack historical context per frame, causing temporal inconsistency, object hallucination, and quality degradation over long video generation horizons.

## Method

- A ==multimodal driving language== interleaves ==VQ-VAE== image tokens with frame-wise ==relative-action tokens== (translation, lateral shift, yaw) quantized via component-wise binning into a shared vocabulary.
- **DrivingGPT**, a ==Llama-style autoregressive transformer== with frame-wise ==1D rotary embeddings==, models this unified token sequence via ==cross-entropy next-token prediction==, jointly performing world modeling and planning.
- ==Frame-to-frame relative action parameterization== (instead of absolute multi-step trajectories) prevents the causal model from trivially copying privileged future-action information from history.

## Results

- On navtest video generation, DrivingGPT (trained from scratch) beats SVD fine-tuned on navtrain: FVD **142.61** vs **227.54** and FID **12.78** vs **24.03**.
- On NAVSIM planning, DrivingGPT reaches PDMS **82.4** versus **77.8** for a ResNet-50+MLP baseline and **24.2** for constant velocity, with NC **98.9** and EP **79.7**.
- Ablations show copying history actions instead of predicting them tanks PDMS to **53.5**, and dropping rotary position embeddings for action tokens drops PDMS from **82.4** to **65.3**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating heterogeneous modalities (vision, action) as tokens in one shared vocabulary lets a single autoregressive model natively unify world modeling and planning without separate pipelines.
> - Data quality dominates data quantity for driving-language modeling: 104k curated NAVSIM sequences outperformed 651k uniform nuPlan sequences.
> - Position information must be applied per-modality (not zeroed for actions) for autoregressive control tokens to carry real signal instead of being ignored by attention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: autonomous driving, world models, and large-scale multimodal sequence modeling.

**Autonomous Driving:** The ultimate goal of the paper is to contribute to robust and intelligent autonomous driving systems. Traditionally, autonomous driving pipelines are modular, involving separate components for perception, prediction, and planning. End-to-end driving models, which directly map sensor inputs to driving actions, represent a significant paradigm shift, aiming for simplicity and potentially better learning of complex driving behaviors. DrivingGPT pushes the boundaries of end-to-end learning by integrating more fundamental capabilities.

**World Models:** World models are a core concept in AI, particularly in reinforcement learning and robotics, where an agent learns to simulate the environment's dynamics. In autonomous driving, world models predict future states (e.g., visual observations, agent positions) given current states and actions. The current landscape of driving world models is heavily dominated by **video diffusion models** (e.g., SVD, GAIA-1, Drive-WM). These models excel at generating high-fidelity visual outputs, leveraging the success of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and more recently, diffusion models for image and video synthesis. However, the authors identify a critical limitation: diffusion models are primarily designed for visual generation and struggle with seamless, differentiable integration of multiple modalities like control signals (actions) and do not naturally support end-to-end differentiable planning within their framework. This work directly addresses this gap.

**Autoregressive Transformers and Sequence Modeling:** The paper draws heavily from the success of **Large Language Models (LLMs)**, which are autoregressive transformers trained on vast text corpora for "next-token prediction." This paradigm has been extended beyond text to other modalities, including image generation (e.g., LlamaGen, VAR), video prediction (e.g., VideoPoet, Emu3), and even sequential decision-making in robotics (e.g., Decision Transformer, RT-1/2). DrivingGPT leverages this powerful general-purpose architecture. By treating visual observations and driving actions as "tokens" in a unified "driving language," the paper extends the LLM paradigm to the highly complex, real-time, and safety-critical domain of autonomous driving. This positions DrivingGPT as a pioneer in applying a pure autoregressive, multimodal transformer approach to simultaneously perform world modeling and planning in driving, distinct from previous efforts that might combine autoregressive elements with diffusion decoders (like GAIA-1).

In essence, DrivingGPT challenges the prevalent diffusion-based approach for driving world models by proposing a unified autoregressive transformer framework. It argues that this architecture is inherently better suited for integrating multimodal data and enabling end-to-end differentiable planning, thus offering a promising path towards more holistic and intelligent autonomous driving systems, reflecting a broader trend of "foundation models" in AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the DrivingGPT research are clearly articulated in the paper:

- **Address Limitations of Existing Driving World Models:** The prevailing driving world models largely rely on video diffusion models. While effective for visual generation, these models inherently struggle with:
 - **Multi-modal Integration:** They lack the flexibility to seamlessly incorporate and generate other crucial modalities, particularly action sequences, alongside visual outputs. This makes truly end-to-end, action-conditioned simulation and planning challenging.
 - **Differentiable Planning Integration:** Achieving full end-to-end integration of driving planning within the diffusion model framework remains a significant technical hurdle, limiting the potential for differentiable model-based planning (where a planner can be optimized by backpropagating through the world model).
 - **Temporal Consistency and Long Horizon Generation:** Diffusion models often face challenges in maintaining long-term temporal consistency in generated videos and are typically trained for fixed, shorter video lengths.

- **Leverage the Power of Autoregressive Transformers:** In contrast to diffusion models, autoregressive transformers, with their "next-token prediction" training objective, have demonstrated exceptional capabilities across diverse sequential and multimodal tasks, including language, image, and video generation, and sequential decision-making. The authors are motivated by their natural ability to handle sequential data and integrate multiple modalities.

- **Unify World Modeling and Planning:** The core objective is to formulate both visual driving world modeling (predicting future observations) and end-to-end driving trajectory planning (predicting future actions) as a *single, unified sequence modeling problem*. This aims to simplify the architecture, enable joint learning, and facilitate a more coherent understanding of driving dynamics.

- **Develop a Multi-modal Driving Language:** To achieve the unification, a key motivation is to design a "multi-modal driving language" where both visual information (from camera feeds) and driving actions are represented as discrete tokens and interleaved within a single sequence. This "language" becomes the input and target for the autoregressive transformer.

- **Pave the Way for Differentiable Model-Based Planning:** By creating a unified, differentiable model that can simulate future states and generate optimal actions, the research aims to lay a foundational "stepping stone" for advanced differentiable model-based planning approaches in autonomous driving, which are considered crucial for achieving human-level physical intelligence.

In summary, DrivingGPT is motivated by the desire to overcome the limitations of current driving world models by adopting the powerful and flexible autoregressive transformer paradigm, thereby enabling a unified, end-to-end approach to both simulating the driving world and planning actions within it.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DrivingGPT's methodology revolves around framing the complex autonomous driving problem as a multimodal sequence generation task, analogous to large language models. This is achieved through a novel "multi-modal driving language" and a Llama-like autoregressive transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Driving as Next Token Prediction

The authors formulate driving as a partially observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the goal is to learn both an end-to-end policy $\pi(a_t|o_t)$ (planning) and an observation space transition function $P(o_{t+1}|o_t, a_t)$ (world modeling). DrivingGPT aims to unify these two challenges into a single sequence modeling task, where the transformer predicts the next token in an interleaved sequence of observations and actions.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Driving Language Construction

The cornerstone of DrivingGPT is the conversion of continuous driving data into a discrete "driving language":

- **Observation Tokenization:**
 - **Input:** Front-camera images $o_i \in R^{3 \times H \times W}$ (resized to 288x512).
 - **Process:** A pre-trained VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder) is used to encode images into discrete "image tokens" $z_i = \{z_{ij}\}$ of a fixed shape (e.g., H/S x W/S, where S is the spatial downsample rate, leading to 576 or 2304 tokens per image). VQ-VAE effectively compresses the visual information into a discrete codebook, making it suitable for transformer processing. An ablation study identified LlamaGen's VQ-VAE as optimal for this task.
 - **Vocabulary:** The image tokens draw from a vocabulary size of 16384 (the VQ-VAE codebook size).

- **Action Tokenization:**
 - **Input:** Continuous driving trajectories. Unlike most planners that predict a long horizon absolute trajectory, DrivingGPT focuses on *frame-to-frame relative actions*. This means predicting the change in longitudinal translation ($\Delta x$), lateral translation ($\Delta y$), and yaw rotation ($\Delta \theta$) between the current and next timestep ($T_{t+1 \to t}$). This relative formulation is crucial for the causal nature of next-token prediction, preventing the model from simply copying future privileged information.
 - **Process:** Each of the three continuous action components ($\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta \theta$) is first clamped between its 1st and 99th percentile values. Then, these clamped values are uniformly quantized into discrete bins (M bins).
 - **Vocabulary:** Each action component (x, y, $\theta$) has its own set of bins, leading to a total action vocabulary size of 3 * M, where M is 128 (384 total action tokens). This allows for different granularities for different action types.

- **Unified Visual-Action Sequence Modeling:**
 - The tokenized image ($z_t$) and action ($q_t$) tokens are interleaved chronologically to form a unified driving sequence: $z_1, q_1, z_2, q_2, \ldots, z_T, q_T$.
 - The total vocabulary for this driving language is the sum of the image and action vocabularies (16384 + 384 = 16768).

#### 3.3. DrivingGPT Architecture and Training

- **Model Architecture:** A Llama-like autoregressive transformer is employed. This architecture is known for its effectiveness in language modeling and its ability to handle long sequences with causal attention masks (meaning predictions for a given token can only attend to previous tokens in the sequence).
- **Positional Embeddings:** Frame-wise 1D rotary embeddings are applied to *both* image and action tokens. An ablation study demonstrates the critical importance of applying positional embeddings to action tokens for planning performance.
- **Training Objective:** The model $p_{\theta}$ learns to predict the next token in the unified sequence using a standard cross-entropy loss: $\sum_{t} -\log(p_{\theta}(x_t|x_{<t}))$. This single objective implicitly trains the model for both:
 - **World Modeling:** Predicting future image tokens ($p_{\theta}(z_t|z_{<t}, q_{<t})$).
 - **End-to-End Planning:** Predicting future action tokens ($p_{\theta}(q_t|z_{\le t}, q_{<t})$).
- **Data and Parameters:** Trained on large-scale nuPlan (16 frames at 10Hz) and NAVSIM (12 frames, 4 history + 8 future, at 2Hz) datasets. AdamW optimizer, learning rate $10^{-4}$, 100k iterations, batch size 16. Token-level dropout (0.1) and random horizontal flip augmentation are used.

#### 3.4. Trajectory Integration (Inference)

Since the model predicts frame-to-frame relative actions, these discrete action tokens must be converted back into continuous absolute driving trajectories for evaluation. This is done by first "unbinning" the discrete action tokens into continuous $\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta \theta$ values. These relative transformations are then converted into 2D transformation matrices and consecutively multiplied to obtain the absolute pose (and thus trajectory) at each future timestep.

#### 3.5. Optional Video Refinement (Supplementary)

For higher resolution and improved temporal consistency in *generated videos* (distinct from the core joint modeling), the supplementary material mentions an optional post-processing step where a fine-tuned SVD decoder is used, conditioned on the image tokens generated by DrivingGPT. This is specifically for visual quality enhancement, not part of the primary world modeling and planning loop of DrivingGPT itself.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DrivingGPT demonstrates strong performance across both its core objectives: action-conditioned video generation (world modeling) and end-to-end trajectory planning.

#### 4.1. World Modeling: Action-Conditioned Video Generation

- **Superior Visual Quality:** DrivingGPT, trained from scratch, significantly outperforms strong diffusion-based baselines like SVD and CogvideoX on the NAVSIM `navtest` dataset (Table 1). It achieves lower Frechet Video Distance (FVD) and Frechet Inception Distance (FID), indicating higher visual quality and realism in generated videos. Even when SVD is fine-tuned on the same `navtrain` data, DrivingGPT maintains a clear lead (FVD: 142.61 vs. 227.54; FID: 12.78 vs. 24.03).
- **Long Video Generation:** A key advantage of the autoregressive approach is its ability to generate long-duration videos by leveraging historical information. DrivingGPT excels in generating coherent sequences up to 64 frames (32 seconds) without significant quality degradation, unlike SVD which shows a marked drop in performance for longer sequences (Table 2, Figure 3). This suggests better temporal consistency and understanding of long-term dynamics.
- **Mitigation of Object Hallucination:** Unlike diffusion models that sometimes suffer from objects suddenly appearing or disappearing due to lack of historical context, DrivingGPT's autoregressive nature leads to superior consistency and reduced object hallucination (Figure 4).

#### 4.2. End-to-End Planning

- **Strong Planning Performance:** DrivingGPT achieves impressive results on the challenging NAVSIM benchmark, outperforming a simple yet robust ResNet-50 + MLP baseline (Table 3). DrivingGPT achieves a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 82.4%, significantly higher than the baseline's 77.8%. It shows strong scores across critical sub-metrics like "No At-Fault Collision" (NC: 98.9%), "Time-to-Collision" (TTC: 94.9%), and "Ego Progress" (EP: 79.7%).
- **Qualitative Planning Examples:** Figure 5 showcases DrivingGPT's ability to handle complex driving scenarios such as unprotected left turns, large curvature turns, and merging into traffic, and even generating trajectories that appear better than human demonstrations.
- **Ablation Study: Learning vs. Copying:** A crucial ablation demonstrates that DrivingGPT genuinely *learns* driving behaviors rather than merely copying or extrapolating historical actions. Replacing any of the predicted action components (longitudinal, lateral, or yaw) with copied historical values leads to a substantial drop in planning performance (Table 5). Copying longitudinal actions results in the most severe degradation, particularly in scenarios involving acceleration from a stop.
- **Ablation Study: Training Data Curation:** Data quality is shown to be more critical than raw quantity for planning performance. Models trained on the higher-quality, curated NAVSIM dataset (104k sequences) significantly outperform those trained on a much larger but uniformly sampled nuPlan dataset (651k sequences) (Table 6). This suggests that general driving data may contain much "low information gain" content.
- **Ablation Study: Positional Embedding for Actions:** Applying 1D rotary embeddings to *both* image and action tokens is critical for strong planning performance. Omitting positional embeddings for action tokens leads to a considerable drop in PDMS (Table 7), highlighting the importance of encoding temporal position for action understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DrivingGPT represents a significant step forward in autonomous driving research, particularly in unifying key functionalities within a single, coherent framework. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Unified AI for Autonomous Driving:** The most profound significance lies in successfully unifying driving world modeling (visual prediction) and end-to-end planning (action prediction) into a single, multimodal autoregressive transformer. This unification simplifies the architecture, promotes joint learning of perception and control, and moves away from complex, modular pipelines. This paradigm shift could lead to more robust and adaptable autonomous driving systems.
- **Leveraging Foundational AI Models:** By demonstrating the efficacy of the "next-token prediction" paradigm, borrowed from LLMs, in a complex multimodal domain like autonomous driving, DrivingGPT reinforces the idea that general-purpose foundation models can be highly effective across diverse AI tasks. It opens doors for applying even larger and more sophisticated transformer models to real-world control problems.
- **Overcoming Diffusion Model Limitations:** The work provides a compelling alternative to diffusion models for driving world modeling, addressing their inherent limitations in multimodal integration, temporal consistency, and action conditioning. The superior long-video generation capabilities and reduced object hallucination highlight the advantages of the autoregressive approach for continuous, dynamic environments.
- **Enabling Differentiable Model-Based Planning:** By creating a unified model that performs both simulation and planning in a differentiable manner, DrivingGPT lays a crucial "stepping stone" for future research into differentiable model-based planning. This could allow for direct optimization of planning policies by backpropagating gradients through the learned world model, potentially leading to more optimal and efficient driving strategies.
- **Improving Data Efficiency and Quality:** The finding that data quality is more important than sheer quantity for planning performance has significant implications for future dataset curation and training strategies in autonomous driving. It suggests a need for more diverse, challenging, and information-rich driving scenarios to train truly intelligent agents.
- **Future Research Directions:** DrivingGPT sets a new baseline and opens several exciting avenues for future research, including:
 - Incorporating more sensor modalities (LiDAR, radar, IMU) into the unified driving language.
 - Expanding the scope of the world model to include more complex environmental dynamics and agent interactions.
 - Exploring techniques for closed-loop evaluation and deployment of such unified models in real-world scenarios.
 - Investigating the interpretability and safety guarantees of these large, black-box models in safety-critical applications.

In conclusion, DrivingGPT offers a novel and highly promising architecture for autonomous driving, demonstrating the power of multimodal autoregressive transformers to unify complex tasks. Its success suggests a future where autonomous vehicles might be controlled by sophisticated "driving language models" that understand and interact with the world through a seamless integration of perception and action.
