# PolyFlow: Safe and Efficient Polytope-Constrained Flow Matching with Constraint Embedding and Projection-free Update

> [!summary] Summary
> PolyFlow, from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, enables flow-based generative models to strictly satisfy polytope constraints through a discrete-time formulation and a projection-free architecture. The system achieves 100% constraint satisfaction and maintains high distributional fidelity, demonstrating superior efficiency and adaptability to dynamic constraints in tasks like robot locomotion.

## Problem

- Standard flow matching models lack inherent mechanisms for enforcing safety constraints, leading to unsafe trajectories due to fitting and numerical integration errors.
- Existing post-hoc correction methods for constrained generation often incur high computational overhead and can distort learned flow dynamics.
- Intrinsic constraint embedding methods struggle with mathematical difficulty, scalability, and generalization to complex constraint geometries.

## Method

- A discrete-time flow formulation is introduced with theoretical guarantees that if conditional flows are interior safe, the marginal expectation field is also interior safe.
- **PolyFlow** utilizes a projection-free architecture that ensures all generated states remain within a convex safety polytope via a Ray Shooting operator and a learned gating factor.
- A Constraint Encoding Block processes linear inequality constraints, fusing their embeddings with the model's latent representation to adapt to dynamic and state-dependent constraints.

## Results

- PolyFlow achieved a **100%** safety rate across various tasks, including 2D maze navigation and Gym tasks, consistently outperforming baselines that failed to guarantee absolute safety.
- It demonstrated competitive or superior distributional quality (MMD, 2-Wasserstein) and higher rollout returns compared to unconstrained and other constrained baselines.
- The method exhibited significantly higher inference efficiency, being substantially faster than projection-based methods, and adapted zero-shot to dynamic, state-dependent constraints in quadrupedal locomotion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The discrete-time formulation allows for theoretical guarantees that aggregating safe conditional flows results in a safe marginal flow, ensuring recursive safety.
> - Embedding constraints directly into the flow architecture using a projection-free update rule, inspired by Frank-Wolfe, achieves strict safety without the computational cost of iterative projections.
> - The Ray Shooting operator provides a differentiable and flexible mechanism to steer the flow toward any point on the polytope boundary, preventing 'zig-zagging' and enabling smooth, safe trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Flow-based generative models have demonstrated capabilities in areas such as image generation, video synthesis, and decision-making tasks. This has motivated their application in physical systems like scientific discovery, trajectory planning, and robotic control. However, deploying these models in safety-critical physical domains introduces a fundamental challenge: adherence to strict safety and constraint requirements. In such applications, constraint violations can lead to hardware damage or system failure, contrasting with the less critical outcomes of artifacts in image generation.

Standard flow matching models lack inherent mechanisms for enforcing safety constraints. Issues like model fitting errors, which cause learned flows to deviate from ground truth, and numerical integration errors during sampling (especially with large integration steps), can result in trajectories entering unsafe regions. This challenge is further complicated when training data itself may contain unconstrained or unsafe examples, while safety constraints are required during generation.

Existing methods for constrained generation typically fall into two categories:
- **Post-hoc Correction:** These approaches enforce constraints by modifying trajectories during the sampling phase. Examples include projection-based methods (e.g., projecting unsafe states back to the feasible region) and Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based methods (e.g., introducing auxiliary control inputs to ensure forward invariance of the safety set). While these methods offer some mitigation of violations and can scale to complex constraints, they often incur substantial computational overhead due to iterative optimization (e.g., solving Quadratic Programming problems) and can disrupt the learned flow dynamics, potentially distorting the target distribution or trapping the sampling process in local optima.
- **Intrinsic Constraint Embedding:** These methods integrate constraints directly into the flow dynamics or model architecture. Examples include reflection-based models, Riemannian flow approaches, and Gauge/Mirror map methods. However, these methods often face limitations such as mathematical difficulty in deriving terms for complex geometries, limited scalability, reliance on carefully designed mappings, or the introduction of additional approximation errors.

A persistent challenge in this field is achieving a balance between distribution matching fidelity and strict constraint satisfaction while maintaining computational efficiency. This work aims to address this gap by proposing a framework that embeds hard constraints into the flow architecture without compromising distributional quality or incurring significant computational cost.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the PolyFlow research are:
1. **Guarantee Strict Constraint Satisfaction:** To develop a generative model framework that can strictly satisfy arbitrary, time-varying polyhedral constraints, eliminating violations that are unacceptable in safety-critical applications.
2. **Enhance Computational Efficiency:** To reduce the computational overhead associated with constraint enforcement, particularly the expensive iterative projection or optimization solvers used in many existing post-hoc correction methods, thereby enabling faster inference.
3. **Maintain High Distributional Fidelity:** To ensure that while enforcing strict safety, the model can still accurately match the underlying data distribution and generate high-quality, smooth trajectories.

These objectives are motivated by the inherent limitations of current flow-based generative models when applied to physical systems:
- **Unreliability of Standard Flow Matching:** Standard flow matching often generates unsafe trajectories even when both source and target distributions are within a feasible region. This arises from:
 - **Fitting Error:** The neural network's approximation of the ground-truth vector field can cause the flow to drift into unsafe regions.
 - **Numerical Integration Error:** The discretization required for sampling continuous-time ODEs introduces numerical errors. Large integration steps, desired for fast inference, can push states across safety boundaries.
- **Inefficiency and Distortion of Post-hoc Corrections:** Current methods that apply post-hoc corrections (e.g., projection, reflection) are computationally expensive, hindering real-time applications. Furthermore, these external interventions can interfere with the learned flow dynamics, potentially distorting the learned distribution and trapping the sampling process in local optima.
- **Scalability and Generalization Issues with Intrinsic Methods:** While intrinsic methods integrate constraints, they often struggle with scalability and generalization to complex geometries.

PolyFlow proposes a novel philosophy: embedding constraints directly into the flow definition and model architecture, rather than treating them as an afterthought, to overcome these limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PolyFlow introduces a framework for polytope-constrained flow matching built on two core components: a discrete-time flow formulation and a projection-free architecture.

#### 3.1. Discrete-Time Flow Formulation and Safety

The work reformulates the problem from continuous-time Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) to a discrete-time flow framework.
- **Discrete-Time Flow Definition:** The flow $\psi$ is defined by a sequence of vector fields $\{u_t\}$ over discrete time steps $T = \{0, 1, \dots, T-1\}$, where $\psi_{t+1}(x) = \psi_t(x) + u_t(\psi_t(x))$.
- **Interior Safe Discrete-Time Flow:** A flow is defined as interior safe if, for any initial state $x_0$ within the feasible set $X_c$ (a compact and convex set), all subsequent states $\psi_t(x_0)$ remain within $X_c$. This implies recursive safety: if $x_t \in X_c$, then $x_{t+1} \in X_c$.
- **Discrete-Time Flow Matching:** The Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) framework is adapted for discrete dynamics. A parameterized vector field $u_\theta$ is trained to approximate the marginal expectation field $\tilde{u}_t(x) = E_{z \sim p_{Z|t}(\cdot|x)}[u_t(x|z)]$ by minimizing a CFM objective.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:**
 - **Discretization Error Bound (Theorem 4.4):** The work demonstrates that the 2-Wasserstein distance between the true and approximate probability paths (generated by $\tilde{u}_t$) accumulates recursively and is bounded by a term related to the Lipschitz continuity of $\tilde{u}_t$ and the matching error. This justifies using $\tilde{u}_t$ as a proxy for discrete generation, with approximation errors remaining bounded.
 - **Safety Preservation (Theorem 4.5):** It is proven that if the conditional flow is interior safe (e.g., constructed using linear interpolation between endpoints $x_0, x_1 \in X_c$), then the marginal expectation field $\tilde{u}_t(x)$ is also interior safe. This means that aggregating safe conditional flows yields a safe marginal flow.

#### 3.2. PolyFlow: Safe Discrete-Time Flow Matching Architecture

To ensure strict safety for the parameterized network $u_\theta$ and avoid post-hoc projections, PolyFlow adopts a projection-free strategy inspired by the Frank-Wolfe algorithm.
- **Projection-Free Update Rule:** The core idea is to parameterize the update vector $u_\theta(x_t, t)$ such that the next state $x_{t+1}$ is guaranteed to remain within the convex safety polytope $X_c$. Instead of directly selecting vertices as in standard Frank-Wolfe, PolyFlow decouples the update into a learned direction and a bounded step magnitude:
 $u_\theta(x_t, t) = \gamma_\theta(x_t, t) \cdot (\text{RS}(x_t, d_\theta(x_t, t)) - x_t)$
 where:
 - $d_\theta(x_t, t)$ is an unconstrained direction vector predicted by a neural network.
 - $\text{RS}(x_t, d_\theta)$ is the **Ray Shooting operator**. This operator computes the intersection point ($z^*$) between the boundary $\partial X_c$ and a ray originating at $x_t$ in the direction of $d_\theta$. For polyhedral constraints ($Ax \le b$), this involves finding the maximum step size $\lambda^*$ such that $x_t + \lambda^* d_\theta$ remains feasible for all constraints.
 - $\gamma_\theta(x_t, t) \in$ is a learned scalar gating factor (output via a sigmoid activation) that controls the step length along the segment from $x_t$ to $z^*$.
- **Guaranteed Safety:** Because $x_t \in X_c$, $z^* \in \partial X_c$, and $\gamma_\theta \in$, the update $x_{t+1} = (1 - \gamma_\theta)x_t + \gamma_\theta z^*$ is a convex combination. Due to the convexity of $X_c$, $x_{t+1}$ is guaranteed to remain within $X_c$.
- **Advantages of this Parameterization:**
 - **Differentiability:** The ray-boundary intersection calculation is differentiable with respect to $x_t$ and $d_\theta$, allowing for end-to-end training.
 - **Flexibility:** Predicting a continuous direction $d_\theta$ allows the model to target any point on the polytope boundary, preventing "zig-zagging" behavior and enabling smooth trajectories.
 - **Projection-Free:** Eliminates the need for expensive iterative projection solvers during inference.
- **Practical Implementation:**
 - **Initial Sampling:** The initial distribution support must be within $X_c$. This is achieved by uniformly sampling within the Chebyshev ball of $X_c$, which is found by solving a Linear Programming problem once during pre-computation.
 - **Model Structure:** A standard DiT (Diffusion Transformer) backbone is augmented. For constrained dimensions, a **Constraint Encoding Block** (using Transformer blocks without positional encoding for permutation invariance) processes the set of linear inequality constraints ($Ax \le b$). The resulting constraint embeddings are fused with the DiT's latent representation via cross-attention. A **Ray Shooting Block** then takes this fused representation to predict $d_\theta$ and $\gamma_\theta$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PolyFlow's performance was evaluated across various constrained generation tasks against several baselines, including projection-based (FlowTrunc), CBF-based (SafeFlow, SafeDiffuser variants: RoSD, ReSD, TVSD), and intrinsic mapping methods (GaugeFlow). Metrics included Safety Rate, distributional quality (MMD, 2-Wasserstein, KL Divergence), trajectory smoothness (Curvature, Acceleration), and inference efficiency (TotalTime, StepTime).

#### 4.1. Safe Planning in 2D Maze Navigation

- **Safety Performance (Q1):** Both PolyFlow-attn and PolyFlow-mlp achieved a 100% safety rate, guaranteeing strict constraint satisfaction. In contrast, all baselines failed to achieve absolute safety, even with extensive sampling steps, and their safety significantly collapsed in low-step comparisons (N=10). CBF-based methods struggled with complex mazes due to the need for super-ellipse approximations and potential infeasibility of the safety controller with many constraints.
- **Generational Quality (Q2):** PolyFlow-mlp achieved competitive performance across distributional metrics, outperforming even the unconstrained Flow baseline. Other methods like ReSD and FlowTrunc exhibited oscillations and trajectory points clustered near obstacle boundaries, indicating "local trap" issues from aggressive post-hoc corrections.
- **Inference Efficiency (Q2):** PolyFlow's projection-free architecture demonstrated efficiency. At 10 steps, PolyFlow-mlp (0.58s) was substantially faster than SafeFlow (7.558s) and nearly matched unconstrained Flow (0.324s). The MLP-based constraint encoder proved more efficient than the attention-based one, reducing total time by approximately 72%.

#### 4.2. Safe Control for Gym Tasks (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah)

- **Overall Performance (Q1, Q2):** PolyFlow exhibited strong performance, achieving 100% constraint satisfaction during generation. It recorded the highest rollout returns and lowest inference latency across all tasks. It also yielded the best fit to the expert distribution in four out of five tasks.
- **Safety-Return Trade-off:** PolyFlow achieved a favorable trade-off between safety and task performance, securing the lowest constraint violation magnitude during rollouts while maintaining high task returns (Figure 5). Constrained methods, including PolyFlow and SafeFlow, occasionally outperformed the unconstrained Flow baseline in task return. This was hypothesized to be because enforcing safety constraints acts as "hard guidance," steering the model toward safer data modes that correlate with higher rewards.
- **Constraint Violations in Rollouts:** While constraints were enforced on generated states (rather than directly on executed actions), PolyFlow significantly reduced the magnitude and duration of minor violations that occurred during physical execution, outperforming baselines.

#### 4.3. Safe Control for Quadrupedal Locomotion (Unitree Go2)

- **Dynamic and Complex Constraints (Q3):** PolyFlow was evaluated in a challenging quadrupedal locomotion task where action space constraints (friction cones) were highly state-dependent and time-varying due to the robot's movement. The friction coefficient was also dynamically randomized during rollouts.
- **Adaptation:** The constraint embedding mechanism allowed PolyFlow to achieve strong constraint satisfaction and zero-shot adaptation to these dynamic safety requirements, as visualized by ground reaction forces remaining within the shifting friction cone boundaries (Figure 4, 10).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Necessity of Constraint Embedding:** For tasks with static constraints (Maze2d, HalfCheetah), the model performed well even without explicit constraint embedding, suggesting it could implicitly learn fixed boundaries. However, for the Unitree Go2 task with dynamic, state-dependent constraints, explicit constraint encoding was essential, and the "w/o embed" baseline showed a significant performance drop.
- **Coupling between Weight and Direction Predictor:** Decoupling the gating factor $\gamma_\theta$ from the direction predictor $d_\theta$ showed only marginal differences in performance, indicating that $\gamma_\theta$ can infer directional context implicitly from shared latent states.
- **Ray Shooting Operator:** The "Hard" (exact minimization) ray shooting operator yielded superior performance and strict safety compared to smoothed approximations (Softmin, Boltzmann). This was particularly evident in tasks where optimal actions lie on constraint boundaries, as precise boundary estimation is crucial. The Boltzmann variant failed to ensure strict safety.
- **OT-Based Batch Coupling:** Incorporating Optimal Transport (OT) data coupling within training batches improved rollout performance, aligning with theoretical findings on reducing discretization approximation error.
- **Integration Steps:** PolyFlow maintained high reward returns even with very low sampling steps (N=2) and consistently achieved a perfect safety rate (R=1) across all tested step sizes, demonstrating robustness to the number of integration steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PolyFlow represents a contribution to the field of constrained generative modeling by addressing the critical trade-off between safety, efficiency, and generative quality. Its primary significance lies in its ability to provide **strict, provable safety guarantees** (100% constraint satisfaction during generation) by design, eliminating constraint violations that are unacceptable in safety-critical physical systems. This is achieved through a novel combination of a discrete-time flow formulation and a projection-free architecture that embeds constraints directly into the model dynamics.

The **computational efficiency** of PolyFlow is another key impact. By avoiding expensive iterative projection or optimization solvers during inference, PolyFlow significantly reduces latency, making it suitable for real-time applications such as robotic control and autonomous driving. This efficiency is achieved without compromising distributional fidelity or trajectory smoothness, as demonstrated by its competitive generative quality metrics and superior rollout returns compared to existing baselines. The ability to handle **dynamic and complex constraint formulations** (e.g., time-varying friction cones in quadrupedal locomotion) further broadens its applicability.

The work's impact extends to:
- **Reliable Autonomous Systems:** Enabling more reliable deployment of generative models in robotics, aerospace, and other domains where safety is paramount.
- **Accelerated Research:** Providing a robust and efficient baseline for future research into constrained generative models, potentially inspiring new architectures and theoretical developments.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By offering inherent safety, it could reduce the need for extensive safety validation and hardware testing cycles in certain applications.

Despite these advancements, PolyFlow has **limitations**. Currently, it requires constraints to be formulated as **convex polytopes**. Additionally, the framework necessitates that the **initial distribution resides within the feasible region**, which may incur some pre-computation overhead. Future work could explore generalizing PolyFlow to handle non-convex constraint geometries, possibly through techniques like convex decomposition, or by developing methods that relax the initial distribution requirement while maintaining safety guarantees. Extending its applicability to equality constraints through null-space projection or dimensionality reduction is also a potential avenue.
