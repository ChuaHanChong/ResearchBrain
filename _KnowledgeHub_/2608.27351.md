# Understanding Evolution Strategies for LLM Reasoning: Broader Reasoning Coverage than GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper studies Evolution Strategies as an LLM reasoning post-training paradigm against GRPO. ES perturbs parameters, evaluates rollouts, and aggregates z-score-normalized reward-weighted perturbations. Theory links verifier-projected Jensen-Shannon population diversity to Pass@K; empirically ES raises Pass@1 while beating GRPO at large K without entropy collapse, and its huge parameter drift is functionally sparse, not catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- ES optimization behavior for LLM reasoning is understudied, so its advantage scope versus mainstream backprop-based post-training such as GRPO remains undefined.
- GRPO-style policy gradients suffer entropy collapse, narrowing reasoning coverage and lowering large-K repeated-sampling accuracy relative to the base model.
- Prior work blames ES parameter drift for catastrophic forgetting, but that evidence used one model, one task, and a small training set, confounding forgetting with overfitting.

## Method

- Theoretical diversity-coverage analysis: three lemmas plus a proposition tie ==Fisher-induced perturbation diversity== and ==verifier-projected Jensen-Shannon divergence== to population success and center-model ==Pass@K==.
- Sequential mixed training compositions, ==ES-then-GRPO== and ==GRPO-then-ES==, split one update budget across stages to combine ==GRPO=='s Pass@1 strength with ==ES=='s Pass@K coverage.
- ==Magnitude-thresholded checkpoint analysis== zeroes updates below a threshold to isolate ==functional sparsity==, paired with hyperparameter study of ==z-score normalization==, perturbation scale, population size, ==one- versus two-point estimators==.

## Results

- On Llama-3.2-3B GSM8K post-training, ES averages **45.9/75.8/80.4** Pass@1/16/32 versus GRPO **47.1/72.7/77.0** and base **44.1/74.0/78.6**; GRPO drops below base on Pass@16 and Pass@32 in **15 of 18** comparisons.
- ES sits **40.7-44.1 times** farther from initialization than GRPO, yet **77.6-93.0 percent** of nonzero updates fall under **1.5e-3**, and zeroing them leaves target-task Pass@1 broadly stable.
- Population scaling: at update **300**, N equal **16** lands within **0.01** of the N equal **64** reward for 1.5B and 3B models, with the gap shrinking from **0.0352** at 0.5B to **0.0030**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameter-space population search and token-level policy gradients occupy different points on the Pass@1 versus Pass@K frontier, so they compose rather than substitute.
> - Whole-model L2 drift is a poor proxy for functional change; large movement can be dominated by reward-irrelevant diffusion that cancels without harming held-out capability.
> - Zeroth-order intuitions from supervised tuning do not transfer: antithetic two-point estimation loses its variance benefit when responses are regenerated autoregressively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language model (LLM) reasoning has seen the emergence of various post-training paradigms aimed at enhancing model capabilities. Among these, reinforcement learning (RL) methods, particularly those leveraging verifiable rewards (RLVR) like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have become mainstream for optimizing LLMs based on scalar feedback from generated responses. Concurrently, Evolution Strategies (ES) have recently gained attention as an alternative post-training approach, primarily recognized for its memory efficiency and parallelizability, as it avoids backpropagation.

However, despite the practical advantages of ES in terms of computational resources, its fundamental optimization behavior and effectiveness compared to established gradient-based methods like GRPO remained underexplored. Prior research on GRPO-style RL methods has indicated potential drawbacks, such as "entropy collapse," where the policy distribution becomes overly concentrated, leading to reduced reasoning-path diversity and, in some cases, a degradation of Pass@K performance (the probability of finding a correct answer within K samples) relative to the base model. This suggests that while GRPO can improve single-sample accuracy (Pass@1), it may do so by narrowing the range of successful reasoning modes.

Furthermore, the impact of ES on pretrained capabilities, specifically its susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting, has been a subject of debate. Observations of substantial parameter drift under ES, attributed by some prior work to catastrophic forgetting, required more systematic verification across diverse tasks and training conditions. Lastly, the practical deployment of ES necessitated a clearer understanding of how hyperparameters and estimator designs influence its stability and scalability, particularly with increasing LLM size.

This paper addresses these gaps by systematically investigating the dynamics and mechanisms of ES in LLM reasoning post-training. It aims to define ES's advantages, understand its relationship with catastrophic forgetting, and provide guidelines for its effective application, thereby positioning ES more definitively within the landscape of LLM post-training paradigms beyond merely a memory-efficient alternative.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper identifies three primary research questions (RQs) that motivate its investigation into Evolution Strategies for LLM reasoning:

**RQ1: Does ES exhibit the same post-training characteristics as GRPO, particularly regarding reasoning coverage?**
- **Motivation:** Mainstream RL methods like GRPO have been observed to improve single-sample accuracy (Pass@1) but can suffer from "entropy collapse," where the model's policy distribution becomes too narrow. This often leads to a decrease in Pass@K (the probability of finding a correct solution within K attempts) for larger K values, even below the base model's performance. This phenomenon indicates a reduction in reasoning-path diversity, making it harder to discover correct but less probable solutions through repeated sampling. The motivation for this RQ is to determine whether ES, with its population-based parameter-space exploration, can avoid this narrowing behavior and maintain or even improve broader reasoning coverage, thereby better exploiting the inherent reasoning capabilities of pretrained LLMs.

**RQ2: Does ES necessarily cause catastrophic forgetting?**
- **Motivation:** Prior work has noted that ES can induce substantial parameter drift—large movements in the model's weight space—and has sometimes linked this drift to catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses previously acquired capabilities on held-out tasks. However, the evidence supporting this link has been limited to specific tasks and small training sets, making it difficult to differentiate broad capability forgetting from overfitting to the training data. This RQ aims to systematically examine the extent and distribution of ES-induced parameter changes, identify which specific updates contribute to performance gains, and critically assess whether large-scale parameter movement inherently leads to a loss of pretrained capabilities across a range of tasks.

**RQ3: What hyperparameter settings and estimators make ES effective and scalable?**
- **Motivation:** The practical application and widespread adoption of any optimization strategy depend on understanding its sensitivity to hyperparameters and the efficiency of its underlying mechanisms. For ES, crucial aspects such as reward normalization techniques, the optimal perturbation scale, and the required population size for stable and effective optimization need to be characterized, especially as LLM scale increases. Additionally, the choice of estimator (e.g., one-point versus two-point) for gradient approximation, which can significantly impact variance and computational cost, needs to be evaluated in the context of reasoning tasks involving stochastic autoregressive response generation. This RQ seeks to provide practical guidance for configuring ES for robust and scalable LLM post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of theoretical analysis and extensive empirical evaluation to address its objectives.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Comparator (GRPO)

The paper defines the objective function as maximizing the expected verifier reward $F(\theta) = E_{x \sim D_{train}} E_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} [r(x, y)]$. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is selected as the primary gradient-based comparator to ES, as both methods optimize this objective using verifier rewards but through fundamentally different mechanisms.
- **GRPO:** Samples a group of $G$ responses for each prompt, computes response-level advantages based on the group's mean and standard deviation, and uses a PPO-style clipped objective to backpropagate these advantages. This process supervises token-level probabilities. The paper highlights GRPO's tendency towards "entropy collapse," where the policy distribution concentrates, potentially reducing response diversity.
- **ES:** Optimizes $F(\theta)$ through parameter-space evaluations. It samples $N$ perturbations ($\epsilon_i$), evaluates the perturbed models ($\theta + \sigma\epsilon_i$), obtains rewards ($R_i$), and aggregates reward-weighted perturbations ($z_i\epsilon_i$) to estimate an update direction. The standard one-point estimator $\hat{g}_{raw}^{ES} = \frac{1}{N\sigma} \sum R_i \epsilon_i$ is used, with rewards typically standardized (z-score normalization).

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis for RQ1: ES Population Diversity and Pass@K

To demonstrate how ES population diversity can support Pass@K, the paper introduces several theoretical lemmas:
- **Lemma 1 (Perturbations induce policy diversity):** Quantifies how ES parameter perturbations ($\sigma\epsilon_i$) translate into policy-level diversity, showing that the expected local policy displacement is proportional to $\sigma^2 \text{tr } I_x(\theta)$, where $I_x(\theta)$ is the prompt-conditioned Fisher information.
- **Lemma 2 (Policy diversity improves correct-answer discovery):** Establishes that sampling across multiple policies in an ES population (one sample per member) is at least as likely to yield a correct answer as drawing the same number of samples from a single policy with the same average success rate. This is formalized using Jensen–Shannon (JS) diversity of success probabilities.
- **Lemma 3 (Reward weighting improves success under positive correlation):** Shows that if the weights assigned to population members are positively correlated with their success probabilities, the reward-weighted policy mixture will have a higher success probability than the uniform average.
- **Proposition 1 (ES updates can improve Pass@K):** Lays out sufficient conditions under which the ES-updated model achieves higher Pass@K than the pre-update model. These conditions involve ensuring that verifier-relevant heterogeneity improves population coverage, reward-aligned selection produces a sufficient margin over the initial policy, and the center update preserves this margin.

#### 3.3. Empirical Evaluation for RQ1: Comparison of ES, GRPO, and Sequential Compositions

- **Experimental Settings:** Two main settings are used:
 - **Easy Setting:** Post-training Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models for two epochs on GSM8K.
 - **Hard Setting:** Post-training DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B for one epoch on DeepScaleR.
- **FLOP Matching:** ES and GRPO configurations are FLOP-matched (estimated model FLOPs) by using $N=32$ perturbation directions for ES and $G=8$ responses per prompt for GRPO, ensuring comparable computational budgets.
- **Metrics:** Performance is primarily evaluated using Pass@K (for K=1, 16, 32) and Maj@K (majority vote accuracy). Token-level entropy is also monitored to assess policy concentration.
- **Sequential Compositions:** Two sequential training strategies are developed and evaluated: ES→GRPO (ES first, then GRPO) and GRPO→ES (GRPO first, then ES) under the same total update budget, to explore potential complementary strengths.

#### 3.4. Analysis for RQ2: Parameter Drift, Functional Sparsity, and Forgetting

- **Quantifying Parameter Drift:** Whole-model movement is measured using the relative $L_2$ distance, $D_{rel}(\theta, \theta_0) = \frac{\|\theta - \theta_0\|_2}{\|\theta_0\|_2}$.
- **Update Magnitude Sparsity:** The distribution of parameter changes is analyzed by quantifying the fraction of nonzero updates whose magnitudes fall within specific thresholds ($s_\tau$).
- **Magnitude-Thresholded Checkpoints:** Experiments are conducted by creating ablated checkpoints where small-magnitude updates (within a threshold $\tau$) are set to zero (i.e., parameters reset to base model values), to assess their contribution to target-task performance.
- **Location of Largest Updates:** The specific layers and parameter types where the largest ES and GRPO updates occur are identified and compared.
- **Held-Out Evaluation:** Performance on a suite of held-out benchmarks (e.g., GPQA, MBPP, CSQA, Countdown) is used to assess the preservation of pretrained capabilities and evaluate catastrophic forgetting.

#### 3.5. Study for RQ3: Hyperparameter Settings and Estimator Designs

- **Reward Normalization:** The impact of z-score normalization for population rewards is compared against no normalization in ES training.
- **Perturbation Scale ($\sigma$):** The general role of $\sigma$ in balancing exploration and stability is discussed.
- **Population Size Scaling:** The effect of varying population size ($N \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$) is investigated across different model scales (Qwen2.5-{0.5, 1.5, 3}B-Instruct) to determine if smaller populations suffice for larger models.
- **Estimator Choice (One-point vs. Two-point):** An ablation study compares the performance of one-point ES with the antithetic two-point estimator (algebraically identical to two-point zeroth-order optimization) on a reasoning task (GSM8K). A diagnostic test is performed to analyze the covariance required for paired subtraction to reduce variance, particularly for stochastic autoregressive evaluations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the characteristics, impact, and optimal configurations of Evolution Strategies for LLM reasoning.

#### 4.1. RQ1: ES Exhibits Broader Reasoning Coverage Than GRPO

- **Theoretical Support:** The theoretical analysis (Lemmas 1-3, Proposition 1) suggests that ES's population-based parameter perturbations induce policy diversity, which can increase the probability of finding correct answers through repeated sampling. Reward weighting can exploit this heterogeneity, and under specified conditions, this population-level advantage can transfer to the updated center policy, leading to Pass@K improvements.
- **Empirical Performance:**
 - **Pass@K Advantage:** Empirical results (Figure 2, Tables 2 and 3) consistently demonstrate that ES improves Pass@1 and achieves higher Pass@K (Pass@16 and Pass@32) than GRPO. While GRPO often showed larger average Pass@1 gains, these were frequently accompanied by a decline in Pass@K performance relative to the base model, particularly in the Easy Setting (15 out of 18 comparisons for Pass@16 and Pass@32). In contrast, ES improved all three metrics (Pass@1, Pass@16, Pass@32) across both Easy and Hard settings.
 - **Entropy Stability:** Unlike GRPO, which exhibited substantial token-level entropy collapse and corresponding Pass@K degradation during training, ES maintained relatively stable entropy levels and finished above the base model on Pass@16 and Pass@32 (Figure 2). This indicates ES largely avoids the policy concentration issues observed in GRPO-style optimization.
- **Sequential Compositions:** The sequential training strategies (ES→GRPO and GRPO→ES) introduced additional non-dominated points on the Pass@1–Pass@K Pareto fronts (Figure 3). ES→GRPO, for instance, achieved the highest Pass@32 on the Hard-Setting math average while retaining much of GRPO’s Pass@1 gain. This suggests that combining the strengths of both methods can yield new trade-offs.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Large Parameter Drift Does Not Necessarily Indicate Broad Forgetting

- **Greater Parameter Drift:** Quantitatively, ES-trained models exhibited significantly greater whole-model parameter drift, with relative $L_2$ distances 40.7 to 44.1 times larger than GRPO-trained models across the four evaluated LLMs (Table 4). This confirms prior observations of ES's distinct parameter geometry.
- **Functional Sparsity of Updates:** Despite perturbing all parameters, the task-relevant effects of ES were concentrated in a sparse subset of larger-magnitude updates. Magnitude-thresholding experiments revealed that preserving only a small fraction of the largest-magnitude updates (e.g., 7.0–22.4% of nonzero updates above a threshold of $1.5 \times 10^{-3}$) was sufficient to retain most of the target-task Pass@1 performance (Figure 4, Tables 11 and 12). The largest ES updates were predominantly found in LayerNorm weights and attention projections, in contrast to GRPO’s largest changes in token embeddings and the language model head.
- **Preservation of Held-Out Capabilities:** The study found that ES generally preserved held-out performance, and in some cases, improved it. Across the Easy Setting models, ES yielded positive average changes for all five held-out task metrics (Pass@1, Pass@16, Pass@32, Maj@16, Maj@32), while GRPO often resulted in negative changes for Pass@16 and Pass@32 (Table 10). In the Hard Setting, ES achieved higher average Pass@32 on held-out non-mathematical benchmarks than GRPO (Table 5) and exceeded both the base model and GRPO in average Maj@16 and Maj@32 (Table 9). These findings suggest that substantial whole-model parameter drift under ES does not necessarily lead to broad catastrophic forgetting, differentiating these results from previous reports that may have conflated forgetting with training-set overfitting.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Effective Hyperparameter Settings and Estimators for ES Scalability

- **Z-score Reward Normalization:** Z-score normalization of population rewards was identified as a key ingredient for effective ES training, leading to consistently higher mean training rewards compared to no normalization after initialization (Figure 1c, item 1). This indicates improved reward-guided optimization.
- **Population-Size Scaling with Model Scale:** The required population size for effective ES optimization was observed to decrease as the pretrained model scale increased. For larger models (1.5B and 3B Qwen2.5-Instruct), a population size of $N=16$ yielded performance comparable (within 0.01 smoothed reward) to $N=64$, whereas for the 0.5B model, $N=32$ was required to achieve similar performance (Table 6, Figure 5). This suggests that larger models may have a denser distribution of task-improving perturbations around their pretrained weights.
- **One-Point vs. Two-Point Estimator:** For reasoning tasks involving autoregressive response generation, the two-point estimator (common in zeroth-order optimization) provided no advantage over the one-point estimator in terms of training reward or held-out performance (Figure 1c, item 3). Diagnostic tests (Table 13) revealed that regenerating autoregressive responses at perturbed parameter points substantially weakens the covariance between positive and negative evaluations, making raw paired subtraction less effective at variance reduction compared to fixed-batch supervised objectives or fixed-rollout evaluations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the understanding of Evolution Strategies as a post-training paradigm for Large Language Models, offering several significant implications:

- **Distinct Optimization Paradigm:** The findings position ES not merely as a memory-efficient alternative to gradient-based methods like GRPO, but as a distinct optimization paradigm with unique advantages. Its capacity to maintain broader reasoning coverage (higher Pass@K) while improving single-sample accuracy (Pass@1), without exhibiting the entropy collapse observed in GRPO, highlights its potential for more robust and diverse reasoning capabilities in LLMs.
- **Reassessment of Catastrophic Forgetting:** The demonstration that substantial whole-model parameter drift does not necessarily lead to broad catastrophic forgetting challenges prior conclusions. This finding is crucial for developing LLM fine-tuning strategies that balance performance gains on new tasks with the preservation of existing capabilities, particularly in continual learning scenarios. The identified functional sparsity of updates suggests that performance gains are driven by specific, impactful changes rather than widespread functional modifications.
- **Practical Guidance for ES Deployment:** The study provides empirical guidelines for effectively deploying ES, including the importance of z-score reward normalization, the observation that larger models can operate effectively with smaller population sizes, and the unsuitability of the two-point estimator for reasoning tasks due to the nature of autoregressive generation. These practical insights can facilitate the adoption and optimization of ES for LLM post-training.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for future research. Further exploration of ES's ability to exploit correct paths with low base-policy probabilities could lead to more innovative reasoning enhancements. Deeper investigations into the long-term effects of parameter drift on previously acquired capabilities in continual learning, and the potential of parameter-efficient updates to manage beneficial exploration while suppressing harmful drift, are also indicated. The identification of specific parameter types (e.g., LayerNorm and attention projections) as sites of significant ES updates could guide architectural design or targeted fine-tuning strategies.

Overall, this research enhances the understanding of ES dynamics, clarifies its advantages over a prominent RL method, mitigates concerns regarding catastrophic forgetting under specific conditions, and provides practical recommendations for its application, thereby advancing the field of LLM reasoning post-training.
