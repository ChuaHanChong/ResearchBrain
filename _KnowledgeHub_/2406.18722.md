# Towards Open-World Grasping with Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Leveraging Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), Open World Grasper (OWG) enables zero-shot robotic grasping from open-ended language instructions in cluttered environments. It achieved an 80.8% mIoU for open-ended grounding and robust real-world grasping performance, reaching 50.0% success for unseen objects in clutter.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) used in robotics inherently lack direct perception and physical grounding, limiting their reasoning by external vision modules that cannot provide low-level spatial understanding crucial for complex grasping.
- Existing robotic grasping approaches often struggle to generalize to unseen objects or varied natural language instructions, frequently operating in an open-loop manner without incorporating environmental feedback.
- Traditional vision models fail to convey intricate spatial relationships or low-level physical properties (e.g., precise 3D geometry, contact physics) crucial for successful grasping in cluttered, contact-rich scenarios.

## Method

- ==The Open World Grasper (OWG)== pipeline integrates Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), specifically GPT-4v, with off-the-shelf instance segmentation and 4-DoF grasp synthesis models.
- It employs a three-stage closed-loop process: open-ended referring segmentation using visual markers, grounded grasp planning to propose multi-step actions for obstacle removal, and LVLM-ranked grasp generation considering potential contacts.
- OWG utilizes extensive visual prompting by drawing information (e.g., segmented masks, numeric IDs, grasp rectangles) directly onto input images, enabling the LVLM to perform zero-shot grounding, planning, and ranking for complex manipulation tasks.

## Results

- Open World Grasper (OWG) achieved an **80.8%** mIoU for open-ended grounding, outperforming the second-best baseline (SoM) by **27.7%** and showing particular strength in semantic and affordance-based queries.
- In real-world robot experiments, OWG consistently outperformed baselines, achieving up to **83.3%** success in isolated seen object scenarios and **50.0%** in challenging cluttered unseen object scenarios.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the criticality of grasp planning (**22%** success rate drop without it) and grasp ranking (**8%** success rate drop without it) for robust performance in cluttered scenes, alongside the reference image as the most important visual prompt component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LVLMs can effectively bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding from language and low-level physical-geometric reasoning needed for grasping by jointly processing visual and linguistic information.
> - Sophisticated visual prompting strategies are crucial for communicating complex spatial and geometric information (e.g., segmented masks, object IDs, grasp candidates) to general-purpose LVLMs, enabling them to orchestrate multi-stage robotic tasks without fine-tuning.
> - Integrating planning (multi-step action sequences) and contact-aware grasp ranking within an LVLM-driven pipeline significantly improves robustness and success rates in highly cluttered and occluded environments.
