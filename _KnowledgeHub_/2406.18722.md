# Towards Open-World Grasping with Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Leveraging Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), Open World Grasper (OWG) enables zero-shot robotic grasping from open-ended language instructions in cluttered environments. It achieved an 80.8% mIoU for open-ended grounding and robust real-world grasping performance, reaching 50.0% success for unseen objects in clutter.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) used in robotics inherently lack direct perception and physical grounding, limiting their reasoning by external vision modules that cannot provide low-level spatial understanding crucial for complex grasping.
- Existing robotic grasping approaches often struggle to generalize to unseen objects or varied natural language instructions, frequently operating in an open-loop manner without incorporating environmental feedback.
- Traditional vision models fail to convey intricate spatial relationships or low-level physical properties (e.g., precise 3D geometry, contact physics) crucial for successful grasping in cluttered, contact-rich scenarios.

## Method

- ==The Open World Grasper (OWG)== pipeline integrates Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), specifically GPT-4v, with off-the-shelf instance segmentation and 4-DoF grasp synthesis models.
- It employs a three-stage closed-loop process: open-ended referring segmentation using visual markers, grounded grasp planning to propose multi-step actions for obstacle removal, and LVLM-ranked grasp generation considering potential contacts.
- OWG utilizes extensive visual prompting by drawing information (e.g., segmented masks, numeric IDs, grasp rectangles) directly onto input images, enabling the LVLM to perform zero-shot grounding, planning, and ranking for complex manipulation tasks.

## Results

- Open World Grasper (OWG) achieved an **80.8%** mIoU for open-ended grounding, outperforming the second-best baseline (SoM) by **27.7%** and showing particular strength in semantic and affordance-based queries.
- In real-world robot experiments, OWG consistently outperformed baselines, achieving up to **83.3%** success in isolated seen object scenarios and **50.0%** in challenging cluttered unseen object scenarios.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the criticality of grasp planning (**22%** success rate drop without it) and grasp ranking (**8%** success rate drop without it) for robust performance in cluttered scenes, alongside the reference image as the most important visual prompt component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LVLMs can effectively bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding from language and low-level physical-geometric reasoning needed for grasping by jointly processing visual and linguistic information.
> - Sophisticated visual prompting strategies are crucial for communicating complex spatial and geometric information (e.g., segmented masks, object IDs, grasp candidates) to general-purpose LVLMs, enabling them to orchestrate multi-stage robotic tasks without fine-tuning.
> - Integrating planning (multi-step action sequences) and contact-aware grasp ranking within an LVLM-driven pipeline significantly improves robustness and success rates in highly cluttered and occluded environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly moving towards enabling agents to operate in unstructured, open-ended environments, a significant departure from traditional industrial settings with highly constrained and predictable conditions. A core challenge in this "open-world" paradigm is robust object grasping guided by natural language instructions, which requires robots to not only perceive objects but also understand the nuances of human intent, context, and physical interactions.

Historically, robotic grasping research has focused on two main areas: generating stable grasp poses from visual data (e.g., RGB-D) and segmenting target objects. Recent advancements have introduced language guidance, where models learn to generate grasps conditioned on text. However, many of these approaches are supervised and struggle to generalize to unseen objects or highly varied natural language instructions (e.g., CROG). They often operate in an "open-loop" fashion, attempting a single grasp without incorporating environmental feedback or planning for complex scenarios like occlusions.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-3/4 and Llama has revolutionized AI by demonstrating emergent general knowledge, commonsense reasoning, and semantic understanding from web-scale data. This led to a surge of LLM-based approaches in robotics, primarily for high-level task planning (e.g., SayCan-IM, ProgPrompt). LLMs can decompose complex instructions into sequences of primitive actions. However, a notorious limitation of LLMs is their lack of inherent "world grounding"—they cannot directly perceive or reason about the physical state of the environment. To overcome this, LLM-based systems often rely on external, often specialized, vision and action models as APIs. This modularity creates a bottleneck: the LLM's reasoning is constrained by the quality and expressiveness of the visual interface. For instance, traditional object detectors might identify objects but fail to convey intricate spatial relationships (e.g., an object blocking another) or low-level physical properties crucial for grasping, such as precise 3D geometry or contact physics.

The emergence of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), such as GPT-4V, QWEN-VL, and InstructBLIP, marks a significant shift. By integrating visual understanding and language generation into a unified architecture, LVLMs can directly incorporate perceptual information into their semantic knowledge base. Preliminary explorations (e.g.,) have shown that LVLMs, especially when combined with segmentation models and clever "visual prompting" techniques (e.g., Set-of-Mark), can exhibit extraordinary open-ended visual grounding capabilities and even complex reasoning akin to chain-of-thought.

This paper positions itself precisely at this frontier. It acknowledges the limitations of both end-to-end supervised methods (poor generalization) and modular LLM-based systems (visual grounding bottleneck). By proposing Open World Grasper (OWG), the authors aim to leverage the inherent multimodal reasoning capabilities of LVLMs to bridge the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level physical interaction. This work introduces a zero-shot, integrated pipeline that not only grounds open-ended language instructions to visual entities but also reasons about complex scene configurations (e.g., clutter, occlusions) to plan multi-step grasping actions and refine grasp poses based on contact reasoning. This holistic approach, powered by LVLMs, represents a significant step towards truly capable and adaptable robotic manipulation in diverse, human-centric environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable robotic grasping system capable of executing "open-world grasping" tasks from "open-ended language instructions." This entails combining high-level contextual and semantic understanding with low-level physical-geometric reasoning, all in a manner applicable to arbitrary, previously unseen scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from two critical observations regarding the limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Bottlenecks of LLM-centric Robotics:** While Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive planning and reasoning capabilities in robotic contexts, they inherently lack direct perception and physical grounding. When LLMs are paired with external vision models, their reasoning capacity becomes constrained by the quality and expressiveness of the visual grounding provided by these separate modules. This means LLMs cannot access or reason about low-level spatial understanding (e.g., object shapes, precise 3D geometry, contact physics, embodiment constraints), which is crucial for successful grasping in complex, contact-rich scenarios.
2. **Emergent Capabilities of LVLMs:** Modern Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) offer a promising solution to these limitations. By unifying visual and linguistic processing, LVLMs are implicitly grounded and can jointly reason about both semantics and geometry. Preliminary studies suggested that LVLMs, especially when combined with segmentation models and suitable visual prompting mechanisms, can unlock powerful open-ended visual grounding and complex reasoning abilities.

Therefore, the authors are motivated to explore and demonstrate that LVLMs can effectively overcome the grounding and low-level reasoning limitations faced by LLM-only approaches. Their goal is to create a zero-shot grasping pipeline that can perform in real-world cluttered scenes, respond to diverse language queries (including open-vocabulary descriptions, referring expressions, and affordances), and plan multi-step actions to achieve a successful grasp, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in intelligent robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, named **Open World Grasper (OWG)**, is an integrated pipeline that leverages the capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) – specifically GPT-4v – in conjunction with off-the-shelf segmentation and grasp synthesis models. The core idea is to visually prompt the LVLM to orchestrate a multi-stage grasping process, enabling zero-shot grounding, planning, and contact-aware grasp ranking in cluttered, open-ended environments. OWG operates in a closed-loop fashion, allowing for re-planning and recovery from initial failures.

The OWG pipeline decomposes the open-world grasping task into three subsequent stages:

#### 3.1. Open-Ended Referring Segmentation

This initial stage aims to identify and segment the target object from an RGB-D observation based on a free-form natural language instruction.
1. **Instance Segmentation:** Given an RGB image ($I_t$), an off-the-shelf 2D instance segmentation model (e.g., SAM) is first applied to generate pixel-level masks ($M_{1:N}$) for all visible objects in the scene.
2. **Visual Marker Overlay:** A new image ($I^m_t$) is created by overlaying these masks with unique numeric IDs as visual markers. This step is crucial for exploiting the LVLM's Optical Character Recognition (OCR) capabilities and providing a grounded reference system for its subsequent reasoning.
3. **VLM Grounding:** The LVLM ($F_{ground}$) is then prompted with the original RGB image ($I_t$), the marked image ($I^m_t$), and the natural language instruction ($T$). The prompt guides the VLM to identify the numeric ID corresponding to the target object ($n^*$) based on the instruction. This enables the VLM to ground abstract language queries (e.g., "something a child would play with," "the flashlight behind the blue cereal box") to specific visual instances in the scene. A "reference image" (original image without markers) is also provided in the prompt, which proved crucial for performance in cluttered scenes.

#### 3.2. Grounded Grasp Planning

Once the target object ($n^*$) is identified, this stage leverages the LVLM's visual reasoning to determine the optimal sequence of actions to make the target graspable.
1. **VLM Planning Query:** The marked image ($I^m_t$) and the identified target object's ID ($n^*$) are fed to the LVLM ($F_{plan}$). The VLM is prompted to act as a planner, proposing a sequence of actions (e.g., "remove [object ID]", "pick [object ID]") that maximize the chance of successfully grasping the target.
2. **Obstacle Reasoning:** The LVLM is expected to reason about potential obstructions. If $n^*$ is blocked by neighboring objects, the VLM's plan will prioritize removing these blocking objects first by grasping and relocating them.
3. **Closed-Loop Operation:** OWG takes the first action suggested by the plan ($\tilde{n} = p_1$). This allows the system to operate in a closed-loop, re-evaluating the scene and generating a new plan after each executed action until the target object ($n^*$) is successfully grasped.

#### 3.3. Grasp Generation and Ranking

After determining which object ($\tilde{n}$) needs to be grasped in the current step of the plan, this stage focuses on synthesizing and selecting the best grasp pose.
1. **Grasp Proposal Generation:** The segmentation mask ($M_{\tilde{n}}$) of the chosen object $\tilde{n}$ is used to isolate it within the RGB-D observation ($\tilde{I}_t = I_t \odot M_{\tilde{n}}, \tilde{D}_t = D_t \odot M_{\tilde{n}}$). A pre-trained 4-DoF grasp synthesis model (e.g., GR-ConvNet) then generates a set of candidate grasp poses ($G_{1:K}$) for this isolated object. The 4-DoF representation, which can be drawn as 2D rectangles, is chosen to be visually interpretable by the VLM.
2. **Grasp Visual Prompting:** A small region of interest ($c_{\tilde{n}}$) around the bounding box of the segmented object $\tilde{n}$ is cropped from the original image. The generated grasp proposals ($G_{1:K}$) are then drawn as 2D grasp rectangles within this cropped image and annotated with their own unique numeric IDs ($c'_{\tilde{n}}$).
3. **VLM Grasp Ranking:** This marked cropped image ($c'_{\tilde{n}}$) is fed to the LVLM ($F_{rank}$). The VLM is prompted to rank the grasp proposals based on potential contacts with neighboring objects or the object's own geometry, providing a nuanced understanding beyond what a pure grasp quality predictor might offer. For instance, it can avoid grasps that would collide with a nearby object or select a more stable grasp point given the object's shape.
4. **Execution:** The grasp pose ranked highest by the VLM ($G'_1$) is selected and executed by the robot. The loop then continues, updating the observation and potentially generating a new plan or moving to the next object in the existing plan.

A key aspect of this methodology is the extensive use of **visual prompting**, where visual information (segmented masks, numeric IDs, grasp rectangles) is explicitly "drawn" onto the input images to make it perceivable and understandable by the LVLM. The system emphasizes **zero-shot** capabilities, meaning it does not require fine-tuning on specific robot demonstration data or task-specific datasets, relying instead on the pre-trained knowledge of the foundation models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments to evaluate OWG's performance across open-ended grounding, simulated robot grasping, and real-world robot grasping, along with detailed ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Open-Ended Grounding in Cluttered Scenes

- **Dataset:** A custom subset of the OCID-VLG test split was used, manually annotated for a broad range of grasping instructions, including object attributes, spatial relations, visual relations, semantic relations, affordances, and multi-hop queries.
- **Baselines:** Compared against CLIP-based visual prompting techniques (ReCLIP, RedCircle, FGVP) and an open-source LVLM (QWEN-VL-2), as well as a GPT-4v-based method (SoM).
- **Results:** OWG (using GPT-4v) achieved an average mIoU of **80.8%**, significantly outperforming the second-best approach (SoM at 53.1%) by a **27.7% margin**. OWG demonstrated particular strength in semantic and affordance-based queries (76.9% and 93.8% respectively), which are crucial for human-robot interaction but often challenging for other models.
- **Failure Modes:** Identified two primary failure modes: a) the LVLM confusing the target description with another visually or semantically similar object, and b) correct reasoning about object location but selecting an incorrect numeric ID.

#### 4.2. Open-World Grasping Robot Experiments

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted in both Gazebo simulation (50 trials/scenario) and on a real UR5e robot (6 trials/scenario) with Robotiq grippers. Scenes involved 5-15 objects, categorized into "isolated" (scattered) and "cluttered" (tightly packed). Trials included "seen" and "unseen" objects/queries to test generalization.
- **Baselines:** CROG (end-to-end supervised referring grasp synthesis) and SayCan-IM (LLM-based zero-shot planning with external modules).
- **Results:**
 - **CROG** struggled with unseen data, showing poor generalization (e.g., 0% success in real-world cluttered unseen objects).
 - **SayCan-IM** showed better zero-shot generalization but was limited by its external vision models, particularly in cluttered scenes (e.g., 16.6% in real-world cluttered seen/unseen).
 - **OWG** consistently outperformed both baselines across all scenarios and test splits. It showed strong immunity to seen/unseen objects due to its zero-shot nature. In simulation, OWG achieved **78.0% (isolated seen) to 66.0% (cluttered unseen)**, significantly higher than baselines. In real-world experiments, OWG achieved **83.3% (isolated seen) to 50.0% (cluttered unseen)**, demonstrating robust performance in challenging real-world clutter.
 - **Failure Decomposition:** OWG consistently reduced error rates in both grounding and grasping stages compared to baselines, indicating its integrated approach improves overall task success.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Instance Segmentation Bottleneck:**
 - Using **Ground-Truth Masks** for the segmentor, OWG achieved **86.6% mIoU** in grounding.
 - With **SAM**, performance dropped to **75.8% mIoU**.
 - With **ViLD-RPN** and **UOIS**, performance was **72.9%** and **71.1% mIoU** respectively.
 - Conclusion: While SAM performs best among off-the-shelf segmentors, the segmentor remains a bottleneck, with potential for further improvement if segmentation quality increases.
- **Visual Prompt Components:**
 - The **reference image** (original image alongside marked one) was the *most critical* component, with a mIoU drop from 86.6% to 23.2% without it. This highlights its role in providing context for the LVLM in highly cluttered scenes.
 - Overlaying numeric IDs, using high-resolution images, and mask filling were also important, showing decreasing impact.
 - Surprisingly, marking **bounding boxes** led to a 12% mIoU *drop*, possibly due to visual clutter introduced by many boxes in dense scenes.
 - **Self-consistency** and **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)** prompting improved performance by ~16% and ~10% respectively, by ensembling responses and enforcing step-by-step reasoning.
- **Grasp-Related Ablations (Simulation Success Rates):**
 - **Without Grasp Planning:** Success rate dropped by **22%** in cluttered scenes (from 68.0% to 46.0%). This highlights planning's crucial role in handling occlusions and multi-step tasks.
 - **Without Grasp Ranking:** Success rate dropped by **8%** in cluttered scenes (from 68.0% to 60.0%). While less critical than planning, ranking still provided a valuable boost by refining grasp choices based on contact reasoning.
 - **Without Both:** Performance dropped drastically in cluttered scenes (from 68.0% to 42.0%), demonstrating that both components are essential for robustness and recovery in complex environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Towards Open-World Grasping with Large Vision-Language Models" marks a significant step forward in the field of intelligent robot manipulation, particularly for applications requiring human-robot interaction in unstructured environments.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Bridging High-Level Semantics and Low-Level Physics:** The most significant contribution of OWG is its successful demonstration of how Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) can effectively bridge the long-standing gap between high-level semantic understanding (from language) and low-level physical-geometric reasoning (for grasping). Previous approaches often struggled with this integration, either lacking grounding (LLMs) or generalizable semantic understanding (traditional vision models). OWG shows that LVLMs, via clever visual prompting, can jointly perform these complex inferences.
2. **Advancing Zero-Shot Generalization:** OWG significantly pushes the boundaries of zero-shot robotic manipulation. By leveraging the pre-trained, web-scale knowledge of foundation models, it achieves superior performance on unseen objects, attributes, and language instructions compared to supervised methods and even previous LLM-based zero-shot approaches. This generalizability is paramount for deploying robots in real-world, diverse scenarios without extensive retraining.
3. **Robustness in Clutter and Multi-Step Planning:** The demonstrated improvements in cluttered scenes, both in simulation and hardware, are highly impactful. The LVLM's ability to reason about occlusions, plan multi-step actions (e.g., removing blocking objects), and refine grasp poses based on potential contacts directly addresses major challenges in robotic grasping. This closed-loop approach, enabled by the VLM's re-evaluation capabilities, is crucial for robust operation in dynamic and unpredictable environments.
4. **Novel Use of Visual Prompting:** The paper highlights the power and effectiveness of sophisticated visual prompting strategies for communicating complex spatial and geometric information (segmentation masks, object IDs, grasp candidates) to general-purpose LVLMs like GPT-4v. This methodology offers a powerful paradigm for future robot learning, allowing foundation models to be integrated into robotic pipelines without prohibitive fine-tuning.
5. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** OWG sets a strong foundation for future research in embodied AI. It demonstrates that LVLMs are not just for language generation or image captioning, but can act as central orchestrators for complex physical tasks, understanding both "what to do" (semantics) and "how to do it" (physical interaction).

**Potential Impact:**

- **More Capable Service Robots:** OWG's advancements could lead to more capable domestic and industrial service robots that can better understand natural human commands and operate autonomously in varied, non-engineered settings.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** The ability to interpret open-ended instructions and adapt to cluttered environments will facilitate more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and home assistance.
- **Reduced Training Data Requirements:** The strong zero-shot performance implies that future robot systems might require significantly less domain-specific training data, accelerating development and deployment cycles.
- **New Research Directions:** This work opens up exciting avenues for research into more sophisticated VLM prompting schemes, integration with 6-DoF grasp detectors, and methods for grounding more complex relational reasoning (e.g., "grasp the object I just pointed to, avoiding the one on its left").

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Error Cascading:** As a modular approach, OWG's performance is affected by the underlying segmentation and grasp synthesis models. Improvements in these components would directly benefit OWG.
2. **4-DoF Grasp Limitation:** Currently, OWG uses 4-DoF grasps for visual communication with GPT-4v, which limits grasping to a single view. Future work aims to integrate 6-DoF grasp detectors and explore new prompting schemes to represent and rank more complex grasp information visually.
3. **Complex Relationship Grounding:** Despite significant improvements, LVLMs still struggle with grounding highly complex object relationships. Future directions include more sophisticated prompting strategies beyond simple marker overlays, and potentially "instruct-tuning" LVLMs on grasp-related data to enhance their understanding of subtle visual cues and relations.

Overall, this paper presents a compelling vision for the future of robot manipulation, where powerful multimodal foundation models act as the brain of intelligent robots, enabling them to perceive, reason, and act in increasingly complex and human-like ways.
