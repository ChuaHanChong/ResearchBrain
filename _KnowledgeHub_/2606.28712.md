# J-LAW: Joint Localization and Actionable World Modeling via Coupled Latent Factor Graphs

> [!summary] Summary
> J-LAW unifies metric localization and action-conditioned world modeling by introducing a coupled latent factor graph that jointly optimizes metric poses and latent world states. This approach reduces latent prediction drift by 71-94% and improves long-horizon stability compared to open-loop and pose-only methods, while creating an actionable map grounded in metric localization.

## Problem

- Autonomous agents require both accurate localization (SLAM) and a predictive understanding of their environment (world modeling) for effective navigation and planning.
- Traditional SLAM provides globally consistent geometric maps but lacks direct predictive capabilities regarding the consequences of actions.
- Action-conditioned world models (e.g., JEPAs) offer actionable latent dynamics for planning but typically lack global metric consistency and suffer from accumulating drift over time.

## Method

- **J-LAW** introduces a ==coupled factor graph== that jointly estimates metric poses, latent world states, and latent landmark embeddings, establishing bidirectional links between metric and latent representations.
- It incorporates a ==pose-conditioned latent encoder== and a ==pose-to-latent coupling factor==, enabling mutual constraint and refinement between localization and world modeling.
- An ==alternating block coordinate descent== optimization strategy is employed to robustly solve the ill-conditioned joint estimation problem, effectively integrating various probabilistic factor terms for observations, predictions, motion, and loop closures.

## Results

- Factor-graph correction consistently reduced latent Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) by **71-94%** compared to open-loop rollout, demonstrating substantial improvement in short-to-medium horizon consistency.
- J-LAW with alternating block coordinate descent achieved a latent RMSE of **0.1383**, outperforming direct joint optimization (**0.1664**) by **16.9%** and a latent-only baseline (**0.1595**) by **13.3%**.
- The bidirectional coupling provided superior long-horizon drift stability, with J-LAW's endpoint latent drift increasing by only **11%** compared to **38%** for pose-only methods over long trajectories, and adaptive coupling reduced pose RMSE by **29-42%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional coupling between metric poses and latent world states within a factor graph allows for mutual refinement, where improvements in one domain enhance the other, leading to a more consistent and actionable world representation.
> - Direct joint optimization of metric and latent variables in a coupled system is prone to degeneracy, necessitating a robust alternating block coordinate descent approach for stable and superior performance.
> - Latent loop closure mechanisms, combined with robust kernels, can effectively reduce drift and improve global consistency in action-conditioned latent spaces, similar to how they function in traditional SLAM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous agents operating in complex environments requires both an understanding of the agent's position within its surroundings (localization) and the ability to predict future environmental states in response to actions (world modeling). Traditionally, these two problems have been addressed by distinct research paradigms.

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems focus on estimating the agent's metric poses and constructing a geometric map of the environment, such as a point cloud or occupancy grid. SLAM systems are known for achieving global consistency, particularly through loop closure mechanisms that correct accumulated drift. However, the maps generated by classical SLAM are primarily descriptive and lack inherent predictive capabilities; they do not directly inform an agent about the consequences of its actions. Recent extensions in SLAM, such as those leveraging factor-graph smoothing, enhance efficiency and accuracy but generally do not integrate predictive models directly into the mapping process. Some predictive spatial mapping approaches have explored how visual next-state prediction tasks can implicitly lead to metric-accurate cognitive maps, suggesting a link between prediction and spatial understanding.

In parallel, action-conditioned world models, particularly Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), focus on learning compact latent dynamics that can predict future observations based on current states and actions. These models are designed to support planning by providing an "actionable" representation of the environment. While JEPAs excel at learning stable latent dynamics and have been extended to various applications, including video representation learning and real-time control, they typically operate in a representation that lacks global metric consistency. Open-loop rollout of these models accumulates drift, and they generally do not incorporate mechanisms for global consistency corrections like loop closure. Previous JEPA research often focuses on local dynamics without explicitly grounding the latent space in a globally consistent metric framework.

This work, J-LAW, aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It proposes a unified framework that combines the global consistency and metric grounding of SLAM with the predictive and actionable capabilities of world models. By jointly optimizing both metric poses and latent world states within a coupled factor graph, J-LAW seeks to create an environmental representation that is simultaneously metric for accurate localization and actionable for effective planning, thereby addressing the limitations of existing standalone approaches. The research positions itself as a development that enforces consistency without requiring retraining of the predictive model, contrasting with methods that might learn implicit metric properties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for J-LAW stems from the perceived disconnect between two fundamental requirements for mobile agents: accurate localization and predictive world modeling. Existing solutions, SLAM and action-conditioned world models (specifically JEPAs), address these needs separately, leading to complementary but incomplete capabilities.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **To develop a joint formulation that unifies localization and action-conditioned prediction.** Classical SLAM provides metric poses and geometric maps that are globally consistent but non-predictive. Conversely, JEPAs offer actionable latent dynamics for planning but suffer from accumulated drift and lack global consistency. The research aims to integrate these two perspectives into a single estimation problem.
2. **To establish bidirectional coupling between metric poses and latent world states.** A central goal is to create a mechanism where improvements in localization contribute to a more accurate world model, and concurrently, a consistent world model aids in refining localization. This mutual constraint is intended to enhance the performance of both components beyond what either could achieve independently.
3. **To create an actionable map grounded in metric localization.** The research seeks to define an environmental map not as a purely geometric construct (e.g., point cloud) but as a set of learned latent embeddings. These embeddings, living in the same latent space as the world states, would be directly usable for planning tasks, while also being constrained by the agent's metric poses.
4. **To enable loop closure mechanisms that benefit both metric trajectory and predictive latent rollout.** In SLAM, loop closures correct geometric drift. In JEPAs, such mechanisms are typically absent. A key objective is to introduce loop closure factors that simultaneously improve the global consistency of the metric pose graph and regularize the action-conditioned latent predictions, thereby correcting drift in both spatial and predictive domains.
5. **To address the challenges of joint optimization.** Directly optimizing a combined objective for both metric poses and latent states can lead to ill-conditioned problems or degenerate solutions. Therefore, an objective is to develop a robust optimization strategy that effectively handles the coupled nature of the variables while ensuring stable and accurate convergence.

By achieving these objectives, J-LAW aims to overcome the limitations of current autonomous systems by providing a comprehensive world representation that supports both precise navigation and intelligent, action-aware planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

J-LAW proposes a **coupled factor graph** that jointly estimates metric poses, latent world states, and latent landmark embeddings. The core idea is to introduce a bidirectional link between the metric and latent representations.

**A. Coupled State Representation:**
The system maintains a coupled state `S` over a trajectory of length `T`, comprising:
- `X_1:T = {x_1, ..., x_T}`: Metric poses, represented as elements of SE(3).
- `Z_1:T = {z_1, ..., z_T}`: Latent world states, residing in a lower-dimensional Euclidean space `R^d`.
- `M_1:K = {m_1, ..., m_K}`: Latent landmark embeddings, also in `R^d`, forming the actionable map.

A critical component is the **pose-conditioned latent encoder `E_θ(o_t, x_t)`**, which generates the latent representation `z_t` from a visual observation `o_t` and the current metric pose `x_t`. This encoder explicitly grounds the latent state in metric localization, a departure from standard JEPAs where the encoder typically ignores pose information.

**B. Joint Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) Objective:**
The coupled state `S` is estimated by minimizing a joint MAP objective function composed of six probabilistic factor terms:
1. **Latent Observation Factor (`Φ_obs`):** This factor connects each latent state `z_t` to its corresponding pose-conditioned encoding `E_θ(o_t, x_t)`. It penalizes deviations between the current latent estimate and the visually and geometrically grounded latent representation, using a robust kernel `ρ` for stability.
 `Φ_obs = Σ_t ρ(||z_t - E_θ(o_t, x_t)||^2_Σo)`
2. **Action-conditioned Latent Prediction (`Φ_pred`):** This factor enforces consistency in the latent space by ensuring that successive latent states `z_t+1` are predictable from the current latent state `z_t` and action `a_t` via a learned latent predictor `P_θ(z_t, a_t)`, typical of JEPA-style models.
 `Φ_pred = Σ_t ||z_t+1 - P_θ(z_t, a_t)||^2_Σp`
3. **Metric Motion Model (`Φ_mot`):** This is a standard odometry factor, constraining consecutive metric poses `x_t+1` based on a metric motion model `g(x_t, a_t)`, which can be derived from IMU preintegration or kinematics.
 `Φ_mot = Σ_t ||x_t+1 ⊟ g(x_t, a_t)||^2_Σg`
4. **Pose–Latent Coupling Factor (`Φ_couple`):** This is the central bidirectional bridge. It connects the latent state `z_t` to a learned decoder `h_θ(x_t, M)` that predicts an expected latent state from the metric pose `x_t` and the latent map `M`. This factor enables mutual constraint: refined poses improve latent estimates, and consistent latent estimates feed back to refine poses.
 `Φ_couple = Σ_t ||z_t - h_θ(x_t, M)||^2_Σc`
5. **Latent Loop Closure (`Φ_loop`):** When a previously visited location is revisited, this factor pulls the corresponding latent states `z_i` and `z_j` towards consistency. A robust kernel is used to mitigate the impact of false loop detections.
 `Φ_loop = Σ_(i,j)∈L ρ(||z_i - z_j||^2_Σl)`
6. **Latent Landmark Observation (`Φ_lmk`):** This factor associates observed latent states `z_t` with existing latent map landmarks `m_k`, thereby making the map actionable by grounding it in the learned latent space.
 `Φ_lmk = Σ_(t,k)∈O ||z_t - m_k||^2_Σm`

**C. Optimization Strategy: Alternating Block Coordinate Descent:**
Directly optimizing the full objective (3) over all variables `X`, `Z`, and `M` simultaneously was empirically found to be ill-conditioned, leading to degenerate solutions where `z_t` and `h_θ(x_t)` move towards each other without improving either estimate. To address this, J-LAW employs an alternating block coordinate descent approach:
1. **Pose Block Optimization (fix Z, M; optimize X):** With the latent states `Z` and map `M` fixed, the coupling factor `Φ_couple` acts as a regularizer, pulling each pose `x_t` towards the location whose decoded latent matches the current `z_t`. This sub-problem includes `Φ_couple`, `Φ_mot`, and a metric pose-graph loop closure `Φ_X_loop`.
2. **Latent Block Optimization (fix X; optimize Z, M):** With the poses `X` fixed, the latent sub-problem optimizes `Z` and `M`. The `Φ_couple` factor here pulls `z_t` towards the latent state predicted by the fixed `x_t`. This block includes `Φ_obs`, `Φ_pred`, `Φ_couple`, `Φ_loop`, and `Φ_lmk`.

Each block is optimized using L-BFGS with strong Wolfe line search. The process iterates for 5-8 outer rounds. This decomposition ensures that the coupling factor acts as a unidirectional regularizer within each block, preventing degeneracy while still enabling bidirectional information flow across iterations.

**D. Training and Inference Procedure:**
The overall system operates in three phases:
1. **Pretraining:** The latent encoder `E_θ` and predictor `P_θ` are initially trained using standard JEPA loss functions, without incorporating pose conditioning (i.e., `x_t` is set to a positional encoding or zero).
2. **Pose Grounding:** The coupling decoder `h_θ` is then trained on a dataset where ground-truth poses are available (e.g., from SLAM or motion capture). The objective is to minimize the difference between the observed latent state `z_t` and the latent state predicted by `h_θ` from the known pose `x_t`.
3. **Alternating Inference:** At test time, the pretrained `E_θ`, `P_θ`, and `h_θ` models are frozen. The coupled factor graph is then solved using the alternating block coordinate descent procedure described above.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of J-LAW was conducted on two real-world datasets: PushT and WildGS. The experiments aimed to assess the ability of the coupled factor graph to reduce latent drift, improve consistency, and compare different optimization and coupling strategies.

**A. PushT Dataset Experiments (LeWM Latents):**
A series of experiments on PushT, using frozen LeWorldModel (LeWM) latents, focused on quantifying the benefits of graph correction and loop closure in the latent space.
- **Drift Reduction:** Factor-graph correction consistently reduced latent Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) by 71–94% compared to open-loop rollout (e.g., from 1.401 to 0.167 for "FG no-loop" in Stage 1). This indicates that the factor graph significantly improves the short- and medium-horizon consistency of latent predictions.
- **Loop Consistency vs. Accuracy Trade-off:** True loop closures were found to improve global trajectory consistency, reducing loop error by up to 98%, albeit at a modest cost to pointwise latent RMSE. This confirms an inherent trade-off between strict consistency and local accuracy.
- **Robustness to False Loops:** The use of uncertainty-weighted and Cauchy robust kernels for loop factors improved resilience to false loop detections. For instance, in Stage 3, robust false-loop mitigation resulted in an RMSE of 0.1285, compared to 0.1863 without such weighting. This suggests that robust weighting makes latent loop closure more practical in real-world scenarios.

**B. WildGS Dataset Experiments (J-LAW Coupled Ablation):**
Experiments on WildGS scenes focused on a 3-way ablation study comparing different configurations:
- **Latent-only FG:** Optimizes only `Z`, without explicit pose or coupling factors.
- **Pose-only:** Optimizes only `X`, with latents `z_t` entirely derived from `h_θ(x_t)`.
- **J-LAW Coupled (Alternating):** The full proposed J-LAW system using alternating block coordinate descent.
- **J-LAW Coupled (Joint L-BFGS):** The full J-LAW system but with direct joint optimization of all variables.

The results (Table II) showed:
- **Alternating vs. Joint Optimization:** Alternating block coordinate descent was superior to direct joint L-BFGS optimization. J-LAW alternating achieved a latent RMSE of 0.1383, which was 16.9% lower than J-LAW joint L-BFGS (0.1664) and 13.3% lower than the latent-only baseline (0.1595). The poor performance of joint L-BFGS confirmed the hypothesis that direct joint optimization leads to degenerate solutions.
- **Pose-only Performance:** Pose-only achieved the lowest average latent RMSE (0.1202) on short trajectories because it solves a simpler sub-problem where latents are fully determined by the pose-to-latent decoder `h_θ(x_t)`. However, it lacks the ability to jointly refine latents based on visual observations.

**C. Long-Horizon Drift Stability:**
Further analysis on WildGS investigated endpoint latent drift over varying trajectory lengths (H).
- **Drift Comparison:** Pose-only's endpoint drift increased by 38% from H=48 to H=512 (2.25 to 3.11). In contrast, J-LAW alternating's drift increased by only 11% over the same range, and notably, it crossed below Pose-only at H=192 and H=512 (2.33 vs. 3.11 at H=512). This demonstrated that the bidirectional coupling in J-LAW provides long-horizon drift stability, as the latent observation factor `Φ_obs` anchors latents to visual evidence, mitigating the impact of accumulating pose errors.

**D. Adaptive Coupling Confidence:**
The research explored an IRLS-style adaptive confidence scheme for the coupling weight `λ_c`.
- **Pose Estimation Improvement:** Adaptive coupling significantly reduced pose RMSE by 29–42% across all horizons compared to fixed coupling. This indicates that the reliability of `h_θ(x_t)` varies, and an adaptive weighting scheme is more effective for pose estimation.
- **Latent Accuracy:** Despite improvements in pose estimation, latent RMSE did not improve with adaptive coupling. This suggests that the core limitation for latent accuracy might be the quality of the coupling decoder `h_θ` itself rather than the coupling weight strategy.

**Summary of Findings:**
The experiments indicate that J-LAW's graph-based correction substantially reduces latent drift (71-94%). Latent loop closures improve global consistency significantly (up to 98% reduction in loop error). Alternating block coordinate descent is crucial for effective optimization, outperforming direct joint methods and leading to better latent RMSE. The bidirectional coupling mechanism provides enhanced long-horizon drift stability compared to pose-only methods. Finally, adaptive confidence weighting for the coupling factor demonstrably improves pose estimation, highlighting the variable reliability of the pose-to-latent mapping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The J-LAW framework offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the fields of robotics, computer vision, and autonomous systems:

1. **Unified World Representation:** J-LAW addresses a fundamental limitation in autonomous systems by merging two historically distinct paradigms: global metric localization (SLAM) and predictive world modeling (JEPAs). This integration yields a map that is simultaneously metric (for precise pose estimation and navigation) and actionable (for learning-based planning and decision-making). Such a unified representation could lead to more intelligent and robust autonomous agents capable of both accurate self-localization and proactive behavior generation.

2. **Enhanced Consistency and Drift Stability:** By introducing bidirectional coupling between metric poses and latent states within a factor graph, J-LAW allows for mutual refinement. Better localization informs the world model, and a consistent world model helps improve localization. This bidirectional flow of information, particularly when combined with latent loop closure, contributes to significantly reduced latent prediction drift over long horizons compared to open-loop world models and provides greater stability than pose-only systems. This improved long-term consistency is crucial for prolonged autonomous operation in dynamic environments.

3. **Actionable Map for Planning:** The concept of "latent landmarks" that exist in the same space as the latent world states provides a directly actionable map. Unlike traditional geometric maps (e.g., point clouds) that require additional processing for planning, J-LAW's latent map can be immediately used by planning algorithms that operate on latent representations, streamlining the perception-to-action pipeline for mobile robots.

4. **Robust Optimization Strategy:** The development and validation of alternating block coordinate descent as an effective optimization strategy for the coupled factor graph is significant. It addresses the practical challenges of ill-conditioned joint optimization, demonstrating that carefully designed decomposition can lead to superior performance compared to direct joint approaches, which were shown to degrade results. This methodological insight is valuable for other complex, coupled estimation problems.

5. **Improved Real-world Applicability:** The incorporation of robust kernels for loop closure and adaptive coupling confidence mechanisms enhances the system's robustness to noise and uncertainty inherent in real-world data. These features contribute to safer and more reliable operation by mitigating the negative effects of false loop detections and varying model reliability.

Future work outlined, such as tighter end-to-end training of the coupling module and more principled loop-confidence estimation, suggests avenues for further enhancing the fidelity and reliability of the coupled representation. Ultimately, J-LAW represents a step towards creating autonomous systems that possess a holistic understanding of their environment, combining precise spatial awareness with predictive intelligence for complex tasks.
