# Task Reconstruction and Extrapolation for $π_0$ using Text Latent

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Text Latent Interpolation (TLI), a method for manipulating internal representations of Vision-Language-Action models, which improved the π0 model's extrapolation ability on a new skill recombination benchmark from 9% to 83% success rate. The research further identified "spatial overfitting" as a prevalent limitation where VLAs associate object names with fixed spatial locations rather than abstract identities.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limited out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, struggling to recombine learned sub-skills for novel tasks.
- It was unclear if VLAs possess truly composable internal representations of skills or primarily overfit to specific demonstration trajectories.
- The absence of a dedicated benchmark for evaluating VLA capabilities in composing known motion primitives into novel task sequences hindered progress.

## Method

- The concept of "Text Latent" was introduced, defined as task-specific vectors derived by averaging hidden states of text tokens across ==transformer layers== during task execution.
- ==Text Latent Interpolation (TLI)== was developed to enable skill recombination, smoothly transitioning the model's internal "intent" by ==linearly interpolating text latents== in the ==residual stream==.
- A new benchmark, `libero-ood`, was created featuring tasks that demand the recombination of previously observed motion primitives into novel, extrapolated sequences.

## Results

- Injecting extracted text latent into the π0 model's residual stream restored task success rates from 11-**28%** (with blank prompts) to 81-**94%** for known tasks.
- Text Latent Interpolation (TLI) increased the π0 model's success rate on the `libero-ood` benchmark from a baseline of **9%** to **83%**.
- Experiments revealed that "spatial overfitting" is a common VLA failure mode, causing models to prioritize an object's training-time location over its current position in new environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models internalize individual, composable skill representations, but typically fail to autonomously combine them for extrapolated tasks.
> - The identified "text latent" vectors effectively encapsulate task-specific knowledge, capable of reconstructing complex robot behaviors even without explicit language prompts.
> - A pervasive limitation, termed "spatial overfitting," was discovered, indicating that VLAs often map object names to fixed training locations instead of achieving true object-centric understanding.
