# DextrAH-RGB: Visuomotor Policies to Grasp Anything with Dexterous Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> DextrAH-RGB presents the first successful sim2real transfer of an end-to-end RGB-based visuomotor policy for complex, dexterous grasping tasks, effectively overcoming the limitations of depth cameras. The system achieves competitive grasping performance and faster cycle times in real-world scenarios, while demonstrating robustness to diverse lighting conditions.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous manipulation policies often rely on depth cameras, which are limited by issues with translucent/reflective objects, direct sunlight interference, and difficult-to-simulate geometric distractors.
- A lack of robust, end-to-end RGB-based visuomotor policies capable of complex, contact-rich dexterous grasping that can successfully transfer from simulation to the real world.
- The need for generalizable and reactive policies that can grasp a wide variety of novel objects with unseen geometry and textures, moving beyond systems requiring precise object models.

## Method

- A two-stage training pipeline is employed: first, a state-based teacher policy is trained in simulation using PPO with extensive Automatic Domain Randomization (ADR) for physics and observation parameters.
- The teacher policy is then distilled into an RGB-based student policy using an online DAgger approach, leveraging NVIDIA Isaac Lab's ray-traced rendering for photorealistic images and extensive visual domain randomization.
- The policy utilizes geometric fabrics as a robust and safe control framework, where the RL policy outputs parametric forces/targets for joint-level PD controllers, providing a strong inductive bias for dexterous grasping.

## Results

- DextrAH-RGB (stereo) achieved state-of-the-art or competitive grasping success rates on 11 diverse real-world objects, outperforming several prior works and performing comparably to depth-based DextrAH-G on many items.
- The system demonstrated significant robustness to lighting, showing no substantial performance difference between controlled indoor lighting and challenging High-Dynamic Range (direct sunlight) conditions, where depth cameras typically fail.
- DextrAH-RGB achieved significantly faster bin packing cycle times (1-2 seconds faster on average) compared to DextrAH-G (e.g., 8.22s vs. 10.66s), while experiencing a slight trade-off in reliability (10-14% lower success rate).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity RGB rendering combined with extensive visual and physical domain randomization enables robust sim2real transfer for complex, contact-rich dexterous grasping using only commodity cameras, without requiring depth information.
> - Stereo vision significantly enhances policy performance in simulation due to the implicit depth cues, although its real-world benefits were less pronounced, suggesting other generalization errors might dominate.
> - Teacher-student distillation using KL-divergence for action imitation, coupled with a finetuned ResNet-18 encoder and an auxiliary object position prediction head, is effective for training robust RGB-based policies for dexterous tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation, particularly grasping diverse objects with multi-fingered robot hands, remains a formidable challenge in robotics. Historically, approaches ranged from classical methods based on optimizing analytic grasp metrics, often requiring precise object models, to data-driven methods leveraging large grasp datasets. More recently, reinforcement learning in simulation has shown immense promise due to the ability to scale experience in dynamic environments and techniques like domain randomization for sim2real transfer.

However, a significant limitation of much of the existing vision-based robotic manipulation work, especially those using simulation, has been its reliance on depth cameras. While depth information simplifies geometry inference, IR-based depth cameras suffer from several issues: they struggle with translucent and reflective objects, are susceptible to direct sunlight interference, and introduce geometric distractors that are hard to simulate realistically at scale. Many state-of-the-art dexterous grasping policies, including the closely related DextrAH-G, rely on depth input.

DextrAH-RGB directly addresses these limitations by demonstrating the first successful sim2real transfer of an *end-to-end RGB-based policy* for complex, dynamic, and contact-rich tasks like dexterous grasping with a robot arm-hand system. This represents a significant shift from depth-centric to RGB-centric perception, offering a more generalizable and robust solution that is less sensitive to environmental lighting conditions and material properties. The paper pushes the frontier of visuomotor policies by showing that high-fidelity RGB rendering in simulation, combined with advanced policy training and robust control, can achieve performance competitive with, or even exceeding, depth-based counterparts in certain aspects. This work contributes to the growing trend of leveraging rich visual information directly for control, paving the way for more anthropocentric and adaptable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DextrAH-RGB is to enable a dexterous robot arm-hand system to grasp a wide variety of objects **end-to-end from RGB image input**, overcoming the limitations of prior work that often relied on depth maps or object poses.

The specific motivations driving this research are:

- **Overcoming Depth Camera Limitations:** The inherent weaknesses of IR-based depth cameras (issues with translucent/reflective objects, direct sunlight, and difficult-to-simulate geometric distractors) necessitate an alternative visual input modality for robust real-world deployment. RGB cameras are ubiquitous and not subject to these specific limitations, offering a more versatile sensory input.
- **Achieving End-to-End Visuomotor Control:** Many existing systems factorize the grasping problem, avoiding direct RGB-to-action policies. This often leads to non-reactive behaviors that struggle with disturbances, partial observability, and novel objects. The goal is to develop a continuously reactive policy that directly maps raw RGB pixels to robot actions.
- **Demonstrating Robust Sim2Real Transfer with RGB:** While sim2real transfer has seen progress, successfully transferring RGB-based policies for highly complex, contact-rich tasks like dexterous grasping has been an open challenge. The paper aims to prove the viability of such transfer, specifically leveraging photorealistic rendering and advanced domain randomization techniques.
- **Improving Generality and Reactivity:** The ultimate aim is to create a system that can generalize to novel objects with unseen geometry, texture, and lighting conditions, and react quickly to dynamic changes in the environment, moving beyond the static grasp configurations common in classical methods.
- **Leveraging High-Fidelity Simulation:** The motivation includes showcasing the capabilities of modern simulation platforms (like NVIDIA Isaac Lab with ray-traced tiled rendering) to generate sufficiently realistic RGB data to train robust vision-based policies for real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DextrAH-RGB employs a sophisticated two-stage training pipeline leveraging reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning, geometric fabrics, and advanced simulation techniques:

**A. Geometric Fabrics and Fabric-Guided Policies (FGPs):**
Central to the control system are geometric fabrics, second-order dynamical systems that provide a safe, reactive, and stable control framework. These generalize classical mechanics and allow desired robot behaviors to be embedded as geometric or forcing terms. Key behaviors encoded include collision avoidance (self and environmental), a kinematic redundancy resolution to a nominal "elbow-out, fingers-curled" posture, and goal-reaching in the palm's 6-DoF pose and a reduced 5-dimensional PCA space for the hand. The RL policy outputs parametric forces/targets for this fabric controller, which then generates modulated accelerations for the robot's joint-level PD controllers. This fabric controller, adopted directly from DextrAH-G, provides a strong inductive bias for dexterous grasping and ensures hardware safety.

**B. State-based Teacher FGP Training:**
Due to the sample inefficiency of RL with high-dimensional visual inputs, an RGB-based policy is not trained from scratch. Instead, a **teacher Fabric-Guided Policy (FGP)** is first trained in simulation using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** The teacher uses an asymmetric actor-critic framework. The critic receives privileged (perfect) state observations, while the actor (teacher policy) receives a noisy subset of these observations. This prevents the teacher from relying on unrealistically accurate state estimates, facilitating better distillation.
- **Architecture:** The teacher policy network consists of a 512-unit LSTM layer followed by two 512-unit MLP layers, with dense skip connections around the LSTM for stability.
- **Observations:** Input includes noisy robot joint states, fingertip/palm positions/velocities, object pose, object goal, object one-hot encoding, previous FGP action, and fabric states. The critic additionally receives joint torques and fingertip/palm forces.
- **Reward Function:** A simplified reward function guides learning, including terms for hand-object proximity, moving the object to a freespace goal, lifting the object, and a regularization penalty to keep fingers open.
- **Automatic Domain Randomization (ADR):** ADR is extensively used during teacher training to progressively increase task and environmental difficulty. This involves randomizing physics parameters (friction, restitution, joint stiffness/damping, object mass, disturbance forces), observation noise/bias for robot and object states, and reward coefficients. This curriculum-based randomization is crucial for learning robust policies that transfer to the real world.
- **Key Training Settings:** Departures from DextrAH-G include annealing PD velocity targets, scaling FGP velocity/acceleration inputs (forcing reliance on recurrency for position-only dynamics), integrating fabric differential equations over two timesteps, and an earlier activation of object disturbance wrenches, all aimed at promoting faster and more reactive policies.

**C. RGB Student FGP Training:**
The trained teacher FGP is then distilled into an **RGB-based student FGP** using an online version of DAgger.
- **Input:** The student policy receives proprioceptive data (robot joint states and velocities) and either one (monocular) or two (stereo) RGB images. No object state information is provided.
- **Photorealistic Rendering:** NVIDIA Isaac Lab's ray-traced tiled rendering is utilized to generate high-fidelity RGB images efficiently at scale.
- **Visual Domain Randomization:** To create realistic and diverse scenes for sim2real transfer, extensive visual domain randomization is applied: random HDRI backgrounds, randomization of material properties (albedo tint, roughness, metallic, specularity) for the robot, table, and objects, and randomization of textures (binding random everyday object textures to meshes). Data augmentations like random background, color jitter, and motion blur are also applied.
- **Student Architecture (Stereo):** The student architecture features a stereo encoder (Figure 7a). Each 320x240 RGB image is fed into a pre-trained ResNet-18 encoder (last two layers removed). The resulting feature vectors are projected, reshaped into 128 tokens, and then passed into a two-layer transformer. The transformer uses a learnable `[embed]` token and employs a cross-attention mechanism where tokens from one image can attend to tokens from the other image and the `[embed]` token (inspired by DUSt3R for implicit depth inference). The `[embed]` token's output forms a 64-dimensional stereo embedding, which is concatenated with proprioceptive input and fed into a 512-unit LSTM, followed by a multi-layered MLP. An auxiliary head predicts the 3D object position. The ResNet encoder is finetuned, with weights cast to bf16 for memory efficiency.
- **Loss Function:** The student is supervised jointly on an imitation loss (KL-divergence between student and teacher action distributions) and an auxiliary loss (L2 norm of predicted vs. ground truth object position). KL-divergence is preferred over L2 for action imitation due to its sensitivity to dimensions with lower variance, leading to better performance.
- **Episode Termination:** Student training includes early episode termination if the object is held in the air for 2 seconds, focusing learning on the crucial grasping phase.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Architecture Ablations (Simulation):**
- **Stereo vs. Monocular:** Stereo policies consistently outperformed monocular ones, with a clear performance boost in success rate (0.89 vs 0.83 normalized to teacher) and a reduction in positional error (2.5 cm vs 3.5-3.3 cm), highlighting the benefit of implicit depth cues from stereo.
- **Transformer Impact:** Including the transformer module enhanced stereo policy performance, confirming its role in effectively processing visual information, likely for improved implicit stereo matching.
- **Encoder Training:** Finetuning the pre-trained ResNet-18 encoder yielded the most performant policies, outperforming frozen or scratch-trained encoders. This suggests the importance of leveraging pre-learned representations while adapting them to the specific task.

**B. Real-World Experiments:**
The policies were deployed on a Kuka LBR iiwa arm with an Allegro Hand v4, equipped with two Intel Realsense D415 cameras. The system runs at 60 Hz for FGP and camera nodes.

- **Single Object Grasping Assessment:**
 - Evaluated on 11 objects from common datasets (e.g., Yale-CMU-Berkeley), each in five different poses.
 - DextrAH-RGB (stereo) generally outperformed DextrAH-RGB (mono) across most objects (e.g., Pitcher: 80% vs 40%; Cheezit: 80% vs 60%).
 - DextrAH-RGB achieved state-of-the-art performance for most objects, being competitive with or exceeding several prior works like DexDiffuser and ISAGrasp. While DextrAH-G (depth-based) occasionally achieved higher rates on specific objects, DextrAH-RGB demonstrated strong overall performance without depth input's limitations.

- **Bin Packing Assessment:**
 - The robot continuously grasped 36 different objects one-at-a-time and deposited them into a bin. Metrics tracked: Consecutive Successes (CS), Cycle Time (CT), and Success Rate (SR).
 - **Speed (CT):** DextrAH-RGB significantly improved upon cycle time, being 1-2 seconds faster on average over DextrAH-G (e.g., DextrAH-RGB stereo (no HDR) at 8.22s vs DextrAH-G at 10.66s). Some grasps completed in 4 seconds, close to human cycle times.
 - **Reliability (CS, SR):** The increased speed came with a slight trade-off in reliability. CS was lower by 2-3 objects, and SR decreased by 10-14% on average compared to DextrAH-G. The authors attribute this to the high variance of sim2real transfer and the difficulty of accurately predicting transfer success.
 - **Robustness to Lighting (HDR):** Crucially, DextrAH-RGB showed no substantial difference in performance between controlled indoor lighting and High-Dynamic Range (HDR) conditions (direct natural sunlight), where depth cameras typically fail. This empirically proves its robustness to varying lighting, a key advantage over depth-based methods.
 - **Stereo vs. Mono in Real World:** Performance discrepancies between DextrAH-RGB (stereo) and (mono) were less striking in the real world compared to simulation, suggesting that real-world generalization error might dominate the inherent advantages of stereo in this context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DextrAH-RGB represents a significant leap forward in dexterous robot manipulation, particularly for real-world applications. Its core significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering End-to-End RGB-based Sim2Real Transfer:** This work is a pioneering demonstration of robust sim2real transfer for end-to-end RGB-based policies in complex, contact-rich dexterous grasping tasks. This achievement validates the feasibility of training highly capable visuomotor policies entirely in simulation using only commodity RGB cameras, moving away from specialized depth sensors.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Environmental Conditions:** By successfully leveraging RGB input, DextrAH-RGB intrinsically overcomes major limitations of depth cameras, such as sensitivity to reflective/translucent objects and adverse lighting conditions (e.g., direct sunlight). Its demonstrated robustness in HDR environments is a critical step towards deploying robots in diverse, unstructured, and anthropocentric real-world settings.
- **Scalability and Generalizability:** The simulation-only training approach, combined with extensive visual and physical domain randomization, allows for highly scalable data generation. This enables the policy to generalize to novel objects with unseen geometry, texture, and lighting conditions in the real world, reducing the need for explicit object models or pre-scanned environments.
- **Foundation for Future Dexterous Skills:** DextrAH-RGB provides a strong foundation for developing more complex and diverse dexterous manipulation skills. The ability to learn reactive, continuous control directly from pixels opens doors for broader applications beyond simple grasping, such as in-hand reorientation, tool use, and dexterous assembly. It can also serve as a source of data or a benchmark for larger, foundational policies.
- **Advancement in Simulation Fidelity and Usage:** The success of DextrAH-RGB highlights the increasing fidelity and utility of modern simulation environments (like NVIDIA Isaac Lab) capable of photorealistic ray-traced rendering. This reinforces the paradigm of simulation as a powerful tool for developing advanced robotic AI.
- **Potential for Faster and More Agile Robots:** The demonstrated improvement in cycle time during bin packing suggests that RGB-based policies can enable faster, more agile grasping behaviors. While a slight trade-off in reliability was observed, further scaling and refinement could lead to policies that are both fast and highly reliable.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Dexterity Limitations:** The use of a PCA action space, inherited from DextrAH-G, focuses on grasping and inherently limits the full dexterity of the multi-fingered hand.
- **Hardcoded Behaviors:** Behaviors like collision avoidance with the table are hardcoded into the geometric fabric, which can hinder grasping smaller objects close to the surface. Learning these behaviors through sensory input would be more desirable.
- **Two-Stage Training:** The teacher-student distillation pipeline is cumbersome. Future work could explore single-stage, end-to-end RL for RGB policies.
- **Lack of Functional Grasping:** The current policy does not prioritize functional grasps (e.g., grasping a pot by its handle), focusing instead on stable prehensile grasps.
- **Single Object Scenario:** The policies are currently limited to handling one object at a time, restricting their use in cluttered environments.

Despite these limitations, DextrAH-RGB sets a new benchmark for RGB-based dexterous grasping policies trained entirely in simulation. Its ability to perform competitive grasping in diverse lighting conditions and generalize to novel objects marks a significant step towards truly autonomous and adaptable robots.
