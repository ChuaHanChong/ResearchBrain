# Resolving the Identity Crisis in Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Qualcomm AI Research developed DISCO, an annotation-free reinforcement learning framework that resolves the "identity crisis" in multi-human text-to-image generation. DISCO improves facial diversity, counting accuracy, and identity consistency both within and across generated images, achieving over 90% unique face accuracy and global identity spread on complex multi-human prompts while maintaining image quality.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art text-to-image models struggle to generate distinct human identities in multi-person scenes, leading to duplicate faces, merged identities, or miscounted individuals.
- Existing reinforcement learning applications in generative models have not explicitly addressed human identity diversity, especially across multiple generated samples for the same prompt.
- Optimizing for intra-image diversity alone can result in a "global diversity collapse," where duplicate identities are merely distributed across different generated samples, and direct diversity optimization is susceptible to "reward hacking."

## Method

- The DISCO framework fine-tunes flow-matching text-to-image models using ==Group-Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) to optimize for identity diversity without annotations.
- A compositional reward function integrates intra-image diversity (penalizing facial similarity within an image), group-wise diversity (discouraging identity repetition across samples for a prompt), count control (ensuring correct number of individuals), and quality/alignment (HPSv3 score).
- A single-stage curriculum learning strategy gradually introduces more complex multi-human prompts (2-7 people) to stabilize training and improve generalization for specialist models.

## Results

- On the DiverseHumans dataset, DISCO(Flux) achieved **92.4%** Count Accuracy (up from **70.8%** for baselines), **98.6%** Unique Face Accuracy (UFA) (up from **48.2%**), and **98.3%** Global Identity Spread (GIS) (up from **50.5%**).
- DISCO maintained high UFA and Count Accuracy even for scenes with 6-7 individuals, where baseline methods showed sharp degradation, and its HPS quality scores and MLLM Action scores were maintained or improved.
- The framework demonstrated graceful generalization to out-of-distribution prompts (8-10 individuals) and improved general text-to-image capabilities on the GenEval2 benchmark, showing enhanced object presence, counting, and positional understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for identity diversity requires addressing both intra-image distinctiveness and group-wise identity spread to prevent duplicates from merely migrating across generated samples.
> - Reward hacking, such as generating fewer people to simplify diversity, must be explicitly countered with count control mechanisms within the reward function.
> - Curriculum learning is crucial for stable convergence and generalization when fine-tuning specialist models for complex multi-human generation tasks, gradually increasing prompt complexity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-image generation models have advanced significantly, primarily driven by developments in diffusion models and flow-based training schemes such as rectified flow and flow matching. These models have demonstrated an ability to produce images with high realism and adherence to textual prompts. Despite these advancements, a persistent challenge remains in scenarios involving the generation of multiple human subjects within a single image or across a set of images for the same prompt. Current state-of-the-art methods frequently exhibit what the authors term an "identity crisis," wherein they produce duplicate faces, merge identities, or miscount individuals. This limitation, which has been previously noted in literature, affects the realism and practical utility of these models.

Existing text-to-image systems primarily focus on generating aesthetically pleasing and realistic human figures but do not explicitly address the diversity of identities, particularly as the number of individuals and scene complexity increase. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a methodology to optimize non-differentiable objectives in generative models, such as prompt adherence, aesthetic quality, and human preferences. Algorithms like Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have been applied to flow-matching models to enhance stability and sample efficiency. However, prior RL applications in this domain have not explicitly targeted human identity diversity, either within a single generated image or across a collection of generations for an identical prompt. This paper aims to address this specific limitation within the evolving landscape of generative AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work is the observed "identity crisis" in state-of-the-art text-to-image models when generating scenes with multiple humans. This crisis manifests as:
- **Duplicate faces**: The model generates individuals with visually similar or identical facial features within a single image.
- **Merged identities**: Distinct individuals specified in the prompt are rendered with indistinguishable or conflated identities.
- **Miscounting individuals**: The generated image contains an incorrect number of people compared to the prompt's specification.

These issues undermine the realism of generated images and restrict their utility in various applications. The paper highlights several areas where these failures pose a significant constraint:
- **Synthetic data generation**: For training models in tasks such as group photo personalization, consistent character generation for narratives, and creating educational content.
- **Narrative media and storytelling**: Inconsistent character identities hinder coherent storytelling.
- **Simulation environments**: For research in social interaction, accurate and diverse representations of individuals are necessary.

The authors identified several observations that guided their approach:
1. **Intra-image diversity alone is insufficient**: Optimizing for facial diversity exclusively within a single image can lead to a "global diversity collapse." In this scenario, duplicate identities are not eliminated but merely distributed across different generated samples for the same prompt, maintaining an overall lack of identity spread.
2. **Reward hacking**: Direct optimization for diversity can be susceptible to "reward hacking," where the model finds shortcuts to satisfy the reward function without producing genuinely diverse or realistic results. Examples include generating fewer people than requested or producing unnatural grid-like face arrangements that technically meet diversity metrics but lack realism.
3. **Curriculum learning for stability**: Specialist models, which are often optimized for specific aesthetics, exhibit improved convergence stability and generalization when complex multi-human prompts are introduced gradually through structured curriculum learning.

Based on these challenges and observations, the key objective of this research is to develop an annotation-free reinforcement learning framework, named DISCO (Reinforcement with Diversity Constraints), that directly optimizes for identity diversity in multi-human text-to-image generation. Specifically, DISCO aims to:
- Penalize facial similarity within individual images.
- Discourage identity repetition across groups of generated samples for the same prompt.
- Enforce accurate counting of individuals as specified in the prompt.
- Preserve the overall visual fidelity and alignment with the text prompt.
- Achieve these objectives in a scalable manner without requiring real-world annotated data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DISCO fine-tunes flow-matching text-to-image models by employing Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), guided by a compositional reward function and a single-stage curriculum learning strategy.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Flow-GRPO

The denoising process of text-to-image generation is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). Given a text prompt, the policy learns to generate successive latents. A terminal reward is computed on the final generated image. GRPO enables training by sampling a group of trajectories for each prompt and computing group-normalized advantages, which guide policy updates while controlling drift from a reference policy via a KL divergence term. This approach allows flexible optimization over non-differentiable objectives.

#### 3.2. Reward Signal

The core of DISCO is a compositional reward function designed to address the identity crisis from multiple angles. This reward combines four distinct components, each bounded within and weighted by coefficients (α, β, γ, ζ).

- **Facial Embeddings**: To evaluate identity, each generated image is processed by the RetinaFace detector (with a confidence threshold of 0.7) to identify bounding boxes around faces. Each face crop is then encoded into a d-dimensional embedding using the ArcFace encoder. Cosine similarity is used to measure identity similarity between these embeddings.

- **Intra-Image Diversity (r_d_img)**: This component penalizes facial similarity *within* a single generated image. If an image contains two or more faces, the reward is calculated as `1 - max(s(f_i,j, f_i,k))`, where `s` is the cosine similarity between any two distinct face embeddings `f_i,j` and `f_i,k` in image `i`. This formulation aims to eliminate the most similar pair of faces, preventing duplicates. If fewer than two faces are detected, a default reward of 0.5 is assigned.

- **Group-wise Diversity (r_d_grp)**: This component addresses identity repetition *across a group* of samples generated for the same prompt. It uses a "counterfactual remove-one" statistic. For each image `x_i` in a group `G`, the average pairwise similarity `S_G` of all faces across the entire group is computed. Then, the average pairwise similarity `S_G-i` is re-computed after hypothetically removing faces from `x_i`. The contribution `∆_i = S_G - S_G-i` indicates if `x_i` increases (negative `∆_i`) or decreases (positive `∆_i`) group diversity. This `∆_i` is then passed through a sigmoid function `σ(-λ * ∆_i)` (with `λ=5`) to map it to a reward. This mechanism explicitly encourages images that contribute to the overall diversity of the group, preventing identity duplicates from simply migrating across samples.

- **Count Control (r_c_img)**: To prevent the model from generating an incorrect number of people (a form of reward hacking), this component rewards images that precisely match the target number of individuals specified in the prompt (`N_target`). A reward of 1 is given if the detected face count `m_i` equals `N_target`, and 0 otherwise.

- **Quality/Alignment (r_q_img)**: To mitigate visual artifacts (such as "grid" arrangements of faces) and ensure overall perceptual quality and prompt adherence, the HPSv3 score is incorporated as a reward. This score is normalized to the range. Beyond preventing artifacts, the HPSv3 reward also reinforces the base model’s ability to follow fine-grained compositional instructions related to attributes like clothing and hairstyles for individual persons.

#### 3.3. Single-Stage Curriculum Learning

To manage the increasing complexity of multi-human generation with more individuals, DISCO employs a single-stage curriculum. Training starts by sampling prompts predominantly describing simpler scenarios (2-4 people), and then gradually anneals the sampling distribution towards a uniform selection over the full range of complexities (2 to N_max people) as training progresses. This annealing is controlled by an exponential weighting parameter `λ_t` and a curriculum duration `t_curriculum`. This strategy is particularly beneficial for stabilizing the training of specialist models.

#### 3.4. DISCO Algorithm

The overall algorithm iteratively samples prompts (guided by the curriculum), generates a group of trajectories, extracts facial embeddings, computes the compositional rewards, calculates group-normalized advantages, and updates the model parameters using GRPO.

#### 3.5. Datasets

- **Training Data**: A dataset of 30,000 prompts, generated by GPT-5, depicting group scenes with 2-7 people in diverse social contexts, settings, and activities.
- **DiverseHumans Testset**: A new test set of 1,200 prompts (200 prompts each for 2-7 people), categorized by four types of diversity tags (no instruction, general "diverse faces," single ethnicity, individual ethnicity assignments). This dataset is designed to evaluate generalization.
- **MultiHuman-TestBench (MHTB)**: An existing benchmark for general multi-human generation, including people performing actions.

#### 3.6. Metrics

- **Count Accuracy**: Percentage of images with the exact number of people specified.
- **Unique Face Accuracy (UFA)**: Percentage of images where all depicted individuals have visually distinct identities (cosine similarity < 0.5).
- **Global Identity Spread (GIS)**: Ratio of total unique identities (clustered across the dataset) to the total prompted identities, measuring diversity across the test set.
- **HPSv2**: Assesses image quality and prompt/image alignment.
- **MLLM Action scores**: Measures alignment with textual action descriptions, as per MHTB.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DISCO demonstrates substantial improvements in multi-human generation, particularly in resolving the identity crisis.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Scores

- **DiverseHumans Dataset**: DISCO exhibited significant gains across key metrics. DISCO(Flux) achieved 92.4% Count Accuracy, an increase from Flux-Dev's 70.8%. Its Unique Face Accuracy (UFA) reached 98.6% (compared to Flux-Dev's 48.2%), and Global Identity Spread (GIS) was 98.3% (versus 50.5%). DISCO(Krea) also showed considerable improvements. DISCO(Flux) outperformed proprietary models such as GPT-Image-1 (UFA 85.1%, GIS 89.8%) and Gemini-Nanobanana in overall identity-related metrics. Performance remained robust even with increased complexity (6-7 individuals), where baseline models showed degradation. Crucially, HPS quality scores and MLLM Action scores were maintained or improved, indicating that identity-focused optimization did not compromise overall generation quality or prompt adherence.
- **MultiHuman-TestBench**: Results on this independent dataset corroborated findings, with DISCO(Flux) achieving 86.6% Count Accuracy and 94.3% UFA, confirming the generalizability of the approach.
- **Performance vs. Number of People**: DISCO maintained consistently high UFA and Count Accuracy even for scenes with 6-7 individuals, whereas baseline methods showed a sharp decline. HPS scores remained competitive across all configurations, indicating quality preservation.
- **Diversity Tags**: DISCO maintained uniformly high UFA (97.7-99.7%) and GIS (98.5-100%) across all four types of diversity tags (no tag, general diversity, single ethnicity, individual ethnicity assignments), demonstrating robustness to varying prompt formulations, unlike baseline models which struggled with complex individual assignments.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples illustrated DISCO's ability to generate distinct individuals with varied facial features, age, and appearance, eliminating the repetitive faces observed in state-of-the-art baseline models. The generated scenes maintained coherence and visual appeal, offering natural demographic diversity within groups.

#### 4.3. Generalizability and Compositional Control

- **Out-of-Distribution Evaluation**: When evaluated on prompts requesting 8-10 individuals, beyond the training range, DISCO demonstrated graceful generalization, maintaining high Unique Face Accuracy where baseline models degraded significantly.
- **Beyond Facial Diversity**: Although trained solely on facial identity diversity, DISCO improved clothing and body-type variation as a byproduct, suggesting the model learns a broader concept of inter-person distinctiveness. Further fine-tuning with a CLIP-based clothing similarity reward yielded additional improvements in clothing diversity.
- **General Text-to-Image Performance (GenEval2)**: DISCO's RL fine-tuning did not degrade general text-to-image capabilities; instead, it improved performance across object presence, counting, positional, and verb understanding attributes on the GenEval2 benchmark, especially at higher prompt complexity levels.
- **Compositional Control**: The HPSv3 reward component ensured that DISCO faithfully followed fine-grained per-person compositional prompts specifying clothing style and hairstyle, even without explicit training for such attributes.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the contribution of each DISCO component:
- Intra-image diversity alone improved UFA but caused Global Identity Spread to decline, as duplicate identities spread across samples.
- Group-wise diversity effectively addressed this global identity spread collapse.
- Count control was essential to prevent reward hacking, such as generating fewer people to simplify diversity.
- HPS quality control successfully mitigated visual artifacts like unnatural "grid" arrangements of faces.
- Curriculum learning proved critical for the stable convergence of specialist models by gradually increasing prompt complexity.

#### 4.5. User Preference Study

A user study indicated that DISCO's automated metrics (mean and max pairwise cosine similarity) align highly with human perception of identity diversity in generated images.

#### 4.6. Computational Analysis

DISCO achieved superior generation quality compared to open-source baselines while maintaining identical inference times, offering a balance between performance and computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DISCO addresses a fundamental limitation in text-to-image generation: the inability to consistently produce distinct human identities in multi-person scenes. The primary significance of this work lies in:

- **Novel Application of Reinforcement Learning**: DISCO is presented as one of the first methods to leverage reinforcement learning for directly optimizing identity diversity in text-to-image models, specifically targeting both intra-image and cross-sample uniqueness.
- **Resolution of the "Identity Crisis"**: The framework effectively resolves the issue of duplicate faces, merged identities, and miscounting individuals, which has been a persistent challenge for advanced generative models.
- **Annotation-Free and Scalable Solution**: DISCO achieves its performance improvements without requiring extensive real-world annotated data or auxiliary spatial/semantic annotations, making it a scalable and practical solution.
- **Enhanced Realism and Utility**: By improving the diversity and consistency of generated human identities, DISCO enhances the realism and practical utility of text-to-image models across various applications. This includes synthetic data generation for training other AI models, consistent character design in narrative media, and creating more realistic simulation environments.
- **Demonstrated Generalizability**: The reward framework's ability to generalize beyond facial diversity to other attributes like clothing and body-type, coupled with its improvement of general compositional control, suggests a broader impact on the robustness and versatility of generative models.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work establishes cross-sample diversity as a critical axis for addressing identity collapse and lays a foundation for future directions, including identity-aware personalized generation, consistent video generation, and explicit optimization for demographic fairness.
