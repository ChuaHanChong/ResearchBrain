# Reward Reasoning Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs) introduce explicit chain-of-thought reasoning before generating a reward, allowing for adaptive utilization of test-time compute. This approach achieves competitive accuracy on reward modeling benchmarks and improves best-of-N inference for large language models.

## Problem

- Current reward models apply a nearly uniform computational budget across all inputs, leading to suboptimal performance on complex queries and inefficiency for simple ones.
- Existing reward models often lack an inherent reasoning mechanism to accurately assess the quality of responses for intricate tasks, where the correct reward is not immediately apparent.
- There is an ineffective utilization of test-time compute to enhance reward model performance, preventing adaptive effort proportional to query complexity.

## Method

- Proposes ==Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs)== which utilize a Transformer-decoder backbone to autoregressively generate a ==chain-of-thought reasoning process== followed by a final reward.
- Develops a ==self-evolving reinforcement learning framework==, using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), that trains RRMs to acquire reasoning capabilities based on a simple rule-based reward function (correct vs. incorrect judgment), without explicit human-annotated reasoning traces.
- Implements ==multi-response rewarding strategies==, including ELO rating systems and knockout tournaments, to apply pairwise-trained RRMs to scenarios with multiple candidate responses, leveraging majority voting for enhanced robustness.

## Results

- Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs) achieved **98.6%** accuracy in the 'Reasoning' category of RewardBench, consistently outperforming 'DirectJudge' models.
- RRM-32B (voting@5) reached **83.0%** on MMLU-Pro, **91.8%** on MATH, and **64.3%** on GPQA for best-of-N inference, surpassing baseline models.
- RRMs successfully guided LLM post-training via reinforcement learning on unlabeled data and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with RRM-annotated preferences, improving downstream performance on benchmarks like Arena-Hard.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit chain-of-thought reasoning within reward models enhances their performance, particularly on complex evaluation tasks.
> - Reward models can self-evolve sophisticated reasoning capabilities through a simple rule-based reinforcement learning signal, without requiring human-annotated reasoning traces.
> - Adaptive utilization of test-time compute (both parallel and sequential scaling) in reward models directly translates to improved accuracy and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically pertaining to their alignment and refinement through post-training techniques. The broader landscape of LLM development has shifted from merely scaling pre-training resources to emphasizing sophisticated post-training methods, particularly Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or verifiable rewards (RLVR).

**Positioning within LLM Alignment:**
- **RLHF vs. RLVR:** The authors highlight that while RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) excels in tasks with verifiable answers (e.g., mathematical reasoning), its application is limited by the intractability of verification for general-domain queries. RLHF, which uses a *reward model* as a proxy for human preference, offers broader applicability. This paper is squarely focused on improving this critical reward model component of RLHF.
- **Reward Model Types:** Existing reward models are broadly categorized into scalar reward models (predicting a single numerical score) and generative reward models (producing natural language feedback, often akin to "LLM-as-a-Judge"). The paper positions Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs) as an advancement over both, particularly the latter. While generative reward models offer interpretability, they often lack the adaptive computational allocation that complex queries demand.

**Addressing the "Inference-Time Scaling" Gap:**
The core innovation of RRMs directly tackles the "inference-time scaling" problem. Traditional reward models apply a uniform computational budget regardless of query complexity. This is inefficient for simple queries and insufficient for complex ones. Prior work on inference-time scaling for LLMs has explored dynamic adjustments of computational resources (e.g., multi-sampling, horizon-based scaling), but these have not been systematically applied to *reward models themselves*. This paper bridges that gap by proposing a reward model that can internally "think" or "reason" for longer when faced with challenging evaluation tasks, mimicking human cognitive processes.

**Novelty in Reasoning Integration:**
The paper introduces a novel approach to integrate explicit chain-of-thought reasoning into the reward model itself. Unlike previous methods where reasoning might be part of the *response generation* by the LLM being evaluated, RRMs perform this reasoning *before generating the reward*. Crucially, they achieve this *without requiring explicit reasoning traces as training data*, differentiating it from supervised fine-tuning approaches. This self-evolution of reasoning capabilities through reinforcement learning in a rule-based environment is a significant methodological contribution to the field of AI safety and alignment.

In essence, this work pushes the frontier of reward modeling by making them more adaptive, robust, and interpretable, thereby enhancing the efficacy of post-training alignment techniques and leading to more intelligent and reliable LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs) stems from a significant open challenge in the field of large language models: the **ineffective utilization of test-time compute to enhance reward model performance**. Current reward models, whether scalar or generative, tend to apply a nearly uniform computational budget across all inputs, regardless of their complexity. This inflexibility leads to several issues:

- **Suboptimal Performance on Complex Queries:** For intricate tasks requiring nuanced analysis or multi-step reasoning, existing reward models often lack the computational capacity or inherent reasoning mechanism to accurately assess the quality of responses. The "correct" reward is not immediately apparent, necessitating a deeper cognitive process.
- **Inefficiency for Simple Queries:** Conversely, for straightforward queries where the preferred response is obvious, allocating the same fixed computational resources is wasteful.
- **Lack of Adaptability:** To serve as truly general-purpose evaluators, reward models should ideally be capable of adapting their "effort" or "thinking" to the specific demands of a query, much like humans do.

To address these challenges, the **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Introduce Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs):** To propose a novel class of reward models specifically designed to execute a deliberate, explicit reasoning process (chain-of-thought) *before* generating the final reward. This enables adaptive utilization of test-time compute.
2. **Develop a Self-Evolving Training Framework:** To create a reinforcement learning framework that fosters reward reasoning capabilities in RRMs without relying on explicit human-annotated reasoning traces. This is crucial given the scarcity and cost of such detailed reasoning data for reward evaluation.
3. **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** To empirically show that RRMs achieve superior performance on diverse reward modeling benchmarks across various domains, including reasoning, general knowledge, safety, and human preference alignment.
4. **Validate Adaptive Compute Utilization:** To provide concrete evidence that RRMs can adaptively exploit additional test-time compute (through parallel and sequential scaling) to further improve reward accuracy on complex tasks.
5. **Show Practical Utility:** To illustrate the effectiveness of RRMs in practical applications, specifically in guiding best-of-N inference and facilitating LLM post-training (e.g., through RL and DPO), demonstrating their value as a robust feedback mechanism.
6. **Analyze Reasoning Patterns:** To investigate and characterize the distinct reasoning patterns developed by RRMs, providing insights into how the reinforcement learning framework guides models to acquire effective evaluation strategies.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to advance the state-of-the-art in reward modeling, making them more capable, efficient, and versatile tools for aligning and enhancing large language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs) by reframing reward modeling as a reasoning task. Their methodology involves a specific model architecture, a novel training framework, and flexible multi-response rewarding strategies.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Input Representation

- **Backbone:** RRMs utilize the Qwen2 Transformer-decoder architecture as their foundational backbone, a common choice for LLMs due to its strong performance.
- **Task Formulation:** The reward modeling task is formulated as a text completion problem. This means the RRM takes a query and candidate responses as input and autoregressively generates an output text.
- **Output Structure:** The distinctive feature of the RRM's output is its structure: it first produces a *long chain-of-thought reasoning process* and *then* generates the final reward (judgment). This explicit reasoning phase is designed to leverage test-time compute adaptively.
- **Input Format:** Each RRM input consists of a query and *exactly two* candidate responses (Assistant 1 vs. Assistant 2). This pairwise comparison format is central to its operation.
- **System Prompt:** A detailed system prompt, adapted from the RewardBench repository, guides the RRM's analysis. It instructs the model to systematically evaluate responses based on criteria like instruction fidelity, helpfulness, accuracy, harmlessness, and level of detail. Crucially, it also explicitly cautions against common biases (e.g., response order or length) and requires a justified decision in a specific format (`\boxed{Assistant 1}` or `\boxed{Assistant 2}`), with no ties allowed. This structured prompting encourages a deliberate reasoning process.

#### 3.2. Model Training with Reinforcement Learning (Reward Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning)

- **Challenge:** A key challenge is the lack of explicit reasoning traces for reward assessment in existing datasets. Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) would require such data.
- **Solution: Self-Evolved Reasoning:** To overcome this, the authors develop a reinforcement learning framework that encourages RRMs to *self-evolve* their reasoning capabilities within a rule-based reward environment.
- **Reward Function:** A simple, rule-based reward function is defined: `R = +1` if the RRM selects the correct response, and `-1` otherwise. This means the reward signal directly evaluates the RRM's final judgment accuracy, *not* the quality of its generated reasoning trace. Despite its simplicity, this signal is shown to be effective in supervising the development of complex reasoning patterns.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used for training, implemented with the verl library. This algorithm is well-suited for policy optimization in RL.
- **Base Models:** Deepseek-R1 distilled models (1.5B, 7B, and 32B Qwen variants) serve as the base models for RRMs.
- **Training Data:** A diverse dataset of approximately 420K preference pairs is compiled, including:
 - Skywork-Reward data.
 - Synthesized pairs from the Tülu 3 prompt dataset with responses generated by Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen models and preferences annotated by GPT-4o.
 - Synthesized pairs from verifiable question-answer sources (WebInstruct-verified, Skywork-OR1, Big-Math-RL, DAPO-Math), where Deepseek-R1 models generate responses and a rule-based verifier assigns correct/incorrect labels, forming correct-incorrect preference pairs. Intermediate thinking steps are removed from responses.

#### 3.3. Multi-Response Rewarding Strategies

Although RRMs are trained on pairwise comparisons, the authors develop strategies to apply them to scenarios with multiple candidate responses:
- **ELO Rating System:** For applications requiring full rankings of `n` candidates, RRMs are used in a round-robin tournament structure (pairwise comparisons between all candidates). Win-loss records are converted into ELO ratings. This provides `O(n^2)` comparisons but can be reduced by sampling. The ratings can then serve as rewards for RLHF.
- **Knockout Tournament:** For identifying the single best response among `n` candidates, a knockout tournament bracket is designed. Candidates are paired, and RRMs determine the preferred response, with winners advancing. This requires `n-1` pairwise comparisons, leading to `O(n)` complexity and `O(log(n))` sequential rounds, making it computationally more efficient than ELO for `best-of-N` selection.
- **Majority Voting Integration:** Both strategies can be combined with majority voting. For each pairwise comparison, RRMs are sampled multiple times, and the majority vote determines the pairwise result, enhancing robustness and leveraging additional test-time compute.

This comprehensive methodological approach allows RRMs to learn sophisticated evaluation behaviors, adapt their computational effort, and be applied effectively in various LLM alignment and inference scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness and versatility of Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs) across various evaluation scenarios and their ability to adaptively utilize test-time compute.

#### 4.1. Agreement with Human Preference (Reward Modeling Benchmarks)

- **Superior Performance:** RRMs achieved competitive performance on established reward modeling benchmarks (RewardBench and PandaLM Test).
 - **RewardBench:** RRM-32B attained a remarkable accuracy of 98.6% in the "Reasoning" category, showcasing its strength in complex logical evaluations.
 - **Comparison to DirectJudge:** Critically, RRMs consistently outperformed "DirectJudge" models (trained on the same data but without explicit reasoning), particularly in reasoning tasks. This highlights the substantial performance gains attributable to the explicit reasoning process.
- **Benefits of Majority Voting:** Applying majority voting (e.g., voting@16) further boosted RRM performance on both benchmarks, demonstrating that increased test-time compute translates into higher accuracy.

#### 4.2. Reward-Guided Best-of-N Inference

- **Outperformance on PPE:** RRMs surpassed all baseline models (Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2, GPT-4o) in selecting the best response from a set of candidates on Preference Proxy Evaluations (PPE) datasets (MMLU-Pro, MATH, GPQA).
 - RRM-32B (voting@5) achieved 83.0% on MMLU-Pro, 91.8% on MATH, and 64.3% on GPQA, outperforming all baselines.
- **Adaptive Selection:** The knockout tournament strategy effectively guided LLMs to perform best-of-N sampling, showcasing RRM's utility in practical inference scenarios.
- **Binary Preference Classification:** In standard binary preference classification (Frick et al. protocol), RRMs maintained strong performance, consistently outperforming baseline reward models and instruction-tuned LLMs, even against significantly larger models like J1-Llama-70B. RRM-32B (voting@5) achieved state-of-the-art accuracy across all three PPE benchmarks.

#### 4.3. Post-Training with RRM Feedback

- **Reinforcement Learning with Unlabeled Data:** RRMs were successfully used as reward models to post-train Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B on unlabeled WebInstruct queries using GRPO. The downstream performance on MMLU-Pro and GPQA steadily improved throughout training, demonstrating RRM's effectiveness in guiding RL-based LLM optimization, a role traditionally dominated by scalar reward models.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Qwen2.5-7B models post-trained with DPO, using preference labels annotated by RRMs (RRM-7B and RRM-32B), showed significant performance improvements on the Arena-Hard benchmark compared to the base model. The model trained with RRM-32B labels achieved the highest Arena-Hard score (55.4), validating RRMs as a source of high-quality supervision signals for DPO.

#### 4.4. Scaling Test-Time Compute

- **Parallel Scaling (Number of Pairwise Comparisons):** Increasing the number of pairwise comparisons (e.g., in ELO or knockout tournaments) consistently improved best-of-N performance on MATH for both RRM-7B and RRM-32B, indicating that RRMs effectively utilize dynamic compute budgets.
- **Majority Voting:** Majority voting further enhanced performance, confirming it as an effective method to translate increased test-time compute into accuracy gains.
- **Rewarding Strategies Comparison:** ELO rating consistently slightly outperformed the knockout tournament strategy in accuracy, but the knockout tournament offered a more efficient `O(n)` complexity with only slightly lower performance, demonstrating a valuable efficiency-performance trade-off.
- **Sequential Scaling (Thinking Budgets):** Longer chain-of-thought "thinking budgets" consistently improved output accuracy on RewardBench across all RRM model sizes (7B, 14B, 32B). This crucial finding confirms that RRM's reasoning capabilities can be scaled through additional sequential computation, offering a flexible way to improve performance without necessarily increasing model size or inference passes.

#### 4.5. Scaling RRM Training Compute

- **Model Size:** Larger RRM model sizes (7B, 14B, 32B) consistently led to performance gains on RewardBench, showcasing positive scaling properties.
- **Training Duration:** Tracking RRM-7B performance on RewardBench throughout training revealed steady improvements across all domains without signs of overfitting, validating the robustness of the reinforcement learning framework.

#### 4.6. Reward Reasoning Pattern Analysis

- **Distinct Reasoning:** Compared to a standard Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B model, RRM-32B demonstrated a significantly greater overall utilization of explicit reasoning patterns.
- **Iterative and Reflective:** RRM-32B exhibited more "transition" (e.g., "alternatively"), "reflection" (e.g., "wait," "think again"), and "comparison" patterns. This suggests an iterative approach involving self-reflection, comparative analysis, and considering multiple perspectives.
- **Less Direct Breakdown:** In contrast, the baseline model showed a higher frequency of "breakdown" patterns, indicating a more direct, monolithic problem-solving approach rather than an iterative comparative judgment.
- **Enhanced Instruction Following:** Case studies confirmed RRM-32B's ability to iteratively apply reasoning for more thorough and in-depth comparisons, leading to enhanced instruction following and more accurate comparative judgments.

In summary, the findings robustly support the core claims of the paper: RRMs achieve state-of-the-art reward modeling performance, effectively leverage adaptive test-time compute, serve as high-quality feedback mechanisms for LLM alignment, and develop distinct, more sophisticated reasoning patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Reward Reasoning Models (RRMs) marks a significant advancement in the field of large language models and their alignment, with wide-ranging potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced LLM Alignment and Safety:** Reward models are the cornerstone of RLHF, which is critical for aligning LLMs with human preferences, values, and safety guidelines. By making reward models more accurate, adaptable, and robust through explicit reasoning, RRMs directly contribute to the development of safer, more helpful, and more honest LLMs. This can reduce undesirable model behaviors like hallucinations, bias, and refusal to follow instructions.

2. **More Efficient and Effective Post-Training:**
 - **Improved RLHF/RLAIF:** RRMs provide higher-fidelity reward signals, which directly translates to more effective reinforcement learning from human feedback (or AI feedback, RLAIF). The demonstration of RRMs guiding RL on *unlabeled data* is particularly impactful, as it reduces the reliance on expensive human-annotated answers, making large-scale LLM training more feasible and cost-effective.
 - **Better Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** The success of DPO using RRM-annotated preference data highlights their utility in alternative alignment techniques, providing high-quality supervision without direct human involvement. This can streamline the DPO process and yield stronger aligned models.
 - **Advanced Best-of-N Inference:** The superior performance of RRMs in guiding best-of-N inference means that LLMs can produce higher-quality outputs with greater confidence by intelligently selecting the best from multiple generated candidates. This directly improves the usability and perceived quality of LLM-powered applications.

3. **Adaptive Compute for Sustainable AI:** The core innovation of adaptively leveraging test-time compute for reward models is crucial for efficient and sustainable AI. Instead of uniform, often excessive, computation, RRMs can allocate resources proportional to task complexity. This can lead to:
 - **Reduced Inference Costs:** Simple queries can be evaluated quickly, saving computational resources.
 - **Improved Performance-Cost Trade-offs:** Developers can dynamically balance desired accuracy with available compute budgets, optimizing for different application scenarios.
 - **Generalizable Efficiency:** The principles of sequential and parallel scaling demonstrated for RRMs could inspire similar adaptive compute strategies for other components of LLM systems.

4. **Advancing Interpretability and Transparency:** By forcing the reward model to produce an explicit chain-of-thought reasoning process, RRMs offer a degree of interpretability that scalar reward models lack. This "thinking process" can provide insights into *why* a particular response was preferred, which is invaluable for debugging, understanding model limitations, and building trust in AI systems. The analysis of reasoning patterns further contributes to our understanding of how these models arrive at their judgments.

5. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Learning from Imperfect Reasoning:** Further exploration of how simple rule-based rewards can elicit complex reasoning without explicit reasoning supervision.
 - **Complex Multi-Response Strategies:** Developing even more sophisticated and efficient multi-response evaluation strategies beyond ELO and knockout tournaments.
 - **Integration with Other LLM Components:** Applying adaptive reasoning principles to other parts of the LLM pipeline, such as content generation, fact-checking, or planning modules.
 - **Robustness to Adversarial Inputs:** Investigating how explicit reasoning can make reward models more robust to adversarial or subtly misleading inputs.

By open-sourcing their code and models, the authors directly contribute to accelerating research in the LLM post-training community, enabling other researchers and practitioners to build upon their foundational work. Overall, RRMs represent a significant step towards more intelligent, efficient, and aligned large language models.
