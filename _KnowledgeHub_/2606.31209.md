# Long-term Traffic Simulation via Structured Autoregressive Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong introduced RosettaSim, a unified framework for long-horizon traffic simulation, and Retrieval-based Traffic Evaluation (RTE), a novel evaluation protocol. RosettaSim achieves state-of-the-art performance in long-term traffic flow realism and competitive short-term simulation, demonstrating the transferability of large language models' structural priors to complex, non-linguistic sequence modeling.

## Problem

- A performance trade-off exists when jointly modeling agent population dynamics (scene generation) and interaction-aware motion in long-term traffic simulations.
- Existing evaluation protocols for long-term traffic simulations are unreliable due to strict one-to-one agent correspondence requirements or statistical biases from global comparisons.
- The application of large-scale sequence models (LLMs) to traffic simulation has been limited, lacking a deeper justification for their structural modeling capabilities beyond linguistic supervision.

## Method

- **RosettaSim**, a unified framework, formulates long-term traffic simulation as ==conditional sequence generation==, handling parallel motion updates for existing agents and ==autoregressive generation== of new agents using a modified LLM.
- The framework leverages pretrained LLMs, primarily by ==freezing most layers== and fine-tuning specific components, based on the hypothesis that LLMs possess transferable ==structural priors== for sequences with ==Zipfian distributions==.
- **Retrieval-based Traffic Evaluation (RTE)** is proposed as a context-aware evaluation protocol that retrieves semantically similar real-world scenarios for simulated segments and uses a combined ==Realism Meta Metric F1 (RMM-F1)== for behavior and traffic flow realism.

## Results

- RosettaSim achieved a new state-of-the-art RMM-F1 score of **0.7624** for long-term traffic simulation, substantially outperforming baselines in traffic flow realism and maintaining stable performance over extended horizons.
- On standard 8-second WOSAC benchmarks, RosettaSim achieved a competitive composite score of **0.7833**, outperforming prior short-term methods not explicitly optimized with closed-loop reinforcement learning.
- Ablation studies showed that pretrained LLM backbones provided transferable structural awareness, with fine-tuning only the last layer achieving performance comparable to fully fine-tuned models (**0.7648** vs **0.7658**) and demonstrating rapid adaptation, while larger LLMs did not yield further performance gains for this specific task.
- RTE metrics exhibited significantly higher Pearson correlations (**r=0.825** and **r=0.791**) with standard short-term WOSAC metrics compared to log-based alternatives, validating its efficacy in reflecting simulation quality without strict one-to-one matching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models' architectural inductive biases and statistical priors, particularly their capacity to model structured, Zipfian-distributed sequences, are transferable and beneficial for complex, non-linguistic tasks like interactive traffic simulation.
> - LLMs can effectively model structured traffic dynamics with minimal fine-tuning, demonstrating similar attention locality patterns for motion data as for text, suggesting a transfer of fundamental sequence understanding.
> - The Retrieval-based Traffic Evaluation (RTE) framework provides a robust and context-aware evaluation for long-term traffic simulations, successfully mitigating the statistical biases observed in traditional metrics or global histogram comparisons.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Interactive traffic simulation serves as a world model fundamental to the scalable and safe development of autonomous driving systems. The existing research landscape for traffic simulation can be broadly categorized by its temporal horizon.

**Short-term Traffic Simulation:** Previous works, such as those referenced, have demonstrated proficiency in simulating traffic for short durations, typically less than 8 seconds. These models often focus on accurately predicting interaction-aware motions within a fixed set of agents present in the scene. Advancements in this area have included framing simulation as next-token prediction, enhancing models with reinforcement learning, or developing sophisticated tokenization strategies.

**Long-term Traffic Simulation:** Simulating traffic over extended horizons, akin to trip-level scenarios, introduces additional complexities. A central challenge is the modeling of sustained multi-agent interactions compounded by dynamic token cardinality, meaning agents continuously enter and exit the scene. Recent efforts have approached long-term simulation by reframing it as a joint modeling problem, integrating both scene generation (agent injection) and motion generation within a unified rollout process.

**Unresolved Challenges in Long-term Simulation:** Despite progress, two fundamental challenges persist:
1. **Performance Trade-off:** There is an observed trade-off when jointly modeling agent population dynamics (which focuses on spatial distributions and when/where new agents appear) and interaction-aware motion (which emphasizes temporal consistency of agent movements). Existing methods that jointly optimize these objectives often struggle to adequately balance the two tasks. This can lead to models that underperform specialized short-term models in motion generation, as illustrated by SceneStreamer against UniMM.
2. **Lack of Reliable Evaluation Protocols:** Standard evaluation metrics, such as those from the Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge (WOSAC), rely on strict one-to-one agent correspondence with ground truth. This makes them unsuitable for dynamic long-term rollouts where agents enter and exit. Current workarounds involve comparing rollout histograms against entire validation sets. However, this global matching approach can introduce statistical bias and yield misleading scores, as the aggregated distribution of an entire validation set is not context-aware (e.g., a fast-moving scenario might be unfairly penalized for not matching an overall distribution dominated by slow-moving traffic).

**The Role of Large-Scale Sequence Models (LLMs):** Concurrently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated substantial capabilities in modeling long-range dependencies across homogeneous sequences (e.g., natural text) and heterogeneous modalities (e.g., vision, robotics control). However, their application to traffic simulation has been limited. Existing approaches generally use LLMs as conditioning tools for controllable simulation or scene generation, or by drawing analogies to next-token prediction paradigms without providing a deeper justification for the transferability of LLMs' structural modeling capabilities to traffic dynamics, particularly for long-horizon scenarios. This leaves an open question regarding the effectiveness and underlying reasons for applying large-scale sequence models to traffic dynamics without relying on linguistic supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research presented in this paper aimed to address the aforementioned challenges in long-term traffic simulation.

**Primary Objectives:**
1. To introduce a unified framework for long-horizon traffic simulation that effectively models both multi-agent interaction-aware motion and dynamic agent population changes (agent entry and exit) within a single autoregressive process.
2. To propose a reliable and context-aware evaluation protocol specifically designed for long-term traffic simulations, overcoming the limitations of existing metrics.

**Motivation for the Unified Modeling Framework:**
The authors hypothesize that the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) in traffic simulation lies not in their linguistic semantics, but in their inherent ability to model structured, compositional sequences autoregressively. This hypothesis is supported by several observations:
- **Statistical Priors:** Empirical analysis revealed that the frequency distribution of discretized traffic motion tokens closely mirrors the statistical properties of natural language, specifically following Zipf’s law (Figure 2). This statistical alignment suggests that LLMs, having been trained under similar regimes to model such distributions, could offer a highly optimized structural prior and initialization manifold for traffic dynamics.
- **Transferability of Attention Mechanisms:** Probing experiments conducted using fully and partially frozen LLMs demonstrated rapid adaptation to motion generation tasks. This indicated that LLMs effectively process traffic dynamics as structured sequential data with long-range dependencies, akin to learning a "foreign language." Further investigation showed that LLMs exhibit similar attention locality when processing both language and motion data separately, providing additional evidence for this transferability.
- **Mitigating Performance Trade-offs:** Existing approaches often face a performance degradation when attempting to jointly optimize scene generation and motion generation. By leveraging the structural priors from pretrained LLMs, the authors aimed to develop a unified framework that could inherently manage flexible token lengths and complex structural dependencies, thereby mitigating this trade-off.

**Motivation for the Retrieval-based Evaluation Protocol:**
The motivation for developing a new evaluation protocol stemmed from the limitations of current methods:
- **Inapplicability of Standard Metrics:** Standard metrics (e.g., WOSAC) require strict one-to-one agent matching, which is fundamentally incompatible with long-term simulations where agents dynamically enter and exit.
- **Bias in Existing Workarounds:** Workarounds that compare aggregated rollout histograms against entire validation sets suffer from severe statistical bias. For example, comparing a simulation of a high-speed scenario to a global distribution predominantly composed of low-speed scenarios would unfairly penalize the realistic high-speed behavior. This lack of context-awareness could lead to misleadingly high scores for trivial or degenerate policies. The authors aimed to address this by introducing a method that could provide grounded, context-aware reference anchors for evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **RosettaSim**, a unified framework for long-horizon traffic simulation, and **Retrieval-based Traffic Evaluation (RTE)**, a novel evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. RosettaSim: Unified Modeling Framework

RosettaSim formulates long-term traffic simulation as a conditional sequence generation task where the number of active agents dynamically changes over an extended horizon. It decomposes scene generation into two primary, interconnected processes:

**a. Parallel Motion Update:**
- Given the current traffic scene ($S_t$) and map context (C), this module predicts the future states ($\tilde{S}_{t+1}$) of all $N_t$ existing agents simultaneously.
- The scene encoder (E), based on pretrained transformer layers from prior work, encodes the agent-centered scene context into agent tokens ($a_{1:N_t}^t$). These tokens are dimension-aligned with the LLM via an MLP.
- A set of learnable motion tokens ($mo_{1:N_t}$) interacts with the agent tokens via self-attention within a pretrained LLM ($f_\theta$) to retrieve motion context.
- Agent-wise sinusoidal position embeddings are added to both agent and motion tokens to encode agent identities.
- The updated motion tokens are then projected to motion logits via an MLP, which are used to update motions in parallel.

**b. Autoregressive Agent Generation:**
- Conditioned on the motion-updated intermediate scene ($\tilde{S}_{t+1}$), this process dynamically determines the final next scene ($S_{t+1}$) by handling the spawning of new agents.
- The current scene and motion tokens ($\{a_{1:N_t}, mo_{1:N_t}\}$) serve as a contextual prompt.
- Two additional topological-aware tokens are introduced as contextual input:
 - **Map tokens ($m_{1:M_t}$):** Extracted from a pretrained map encoder within E, encoding fine-grained road network geometry.
 - **Layout tokens ($l_{1:N_t}$):** Derived from the intermediate state $\tilde{S}_{t+1}$ by tokenizing agent positions into a set of discretized grid indices ($g_i$) and orientations into discretized yaw indices ($y_i$). These are embedded and combined with sinusoidal position embeddings.
- Each newly spawned agent is represented by three tokens: type ($c_j$), grid ($g_j$), and yaw ($y_j$).
- During inference, the agent type is first sampled autoregressively ($c_j \sim P(\text{mlp}_{\text{type}}(f_\theta(\text{<SOS>} + \text{SinPE}(j)))$), followed by grid ($g_j$) and yaw ($y_j$) tokens conditioned on the sampled type and previous tokens. A special `<EOS>` token signals the cessation of agent spawning.
- **Training:** RosettaSim employs a one-stage training paradigm using simple cross-entropy losses. The input sequence `seq` concatenates agent, motion, map, layout, and spawn tokens, with `label` corresponding to ground-truth motion and spawn tokens, padded where necessary. Most LLM layers are frozen, with only specific motion heads and adapter layers being trainable.
- **Inference Heuristics:** To compensate for the lack of a dedicated continuous offset prediction head, certain heuristics are applied:
 - A newly decoded grid position is snapped to the closest lane centerline if within 3 meters.
 - If a newly spawned agent overlaps with existing agents, the model samples an `<EOS>` token to halt generation.
 - Agents exiting the observable map boundary or generating physically unrealistic motion are removed from the scene context.

#### 3.2. Retrieval-based Traffic Evaluation (RTE)

RTE is designed to provide context-aware evaluation for long-term simulations by mitigating statistical bias.

- **Retrieval Mechanism:** For a given segment of a simulated long-term rollout ($S_{0:T}$), it is divided into short-term segments ($S_{t:t+\tau}$). Each segment is encoded into a latent representation ($z = [z_{\text{object}}, z_{\text{map}}]$) using a pretrained Scene VAE encoder ($E_\phi$). A database of encoded validation log segments ($ \hat{z}$) is pre-built.
- **Similarity Measurement:** For each simulated segment, the top-K most semantically similar real-world scenarios are retrieved from the database based on the Wasserstein distance ($W_2$) between their latent representations: $ \text{similarity}(z, \hat{z}) = W_2(z_{\text{object}}, \hat{z}_{\text{object}}) + \lambda \cdot W_2(z_{\text{map}}, \hat{z}_{\text{map}})$. These retrieved scenarios serve as grounded reference anchors.
- **Realism Meta Metric F1 (RMM-F1):** The overall quality of long-term simulation is assessed using RMM-F1, calculated as the harmonic mean (with $\beta=1$) of two dimensions:
 1. **Behavior Realism:** Aggregates WOSAC-like metrics (kinematic, interactive, and map-based fidelity) computed between the generated segments and their retrieved real-world references. Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence is used for collision and offroad likelihoods to improve correlation.
 2. **Traffic Flow Realism:** Evaluates macro-level population dynamics, such as agent entry/exit counts (#Enter, #Exit), against the global log distribution, utilizing JS divergence instead of NLL-likelihood to avoid penalizing sparse distributions.
- Metrics are computed over sliding evaluation windows, with results averaged across windows and then across distinct rollout scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Short-term Traffic Simulation Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Comparison:** RosettaSim demonstrated competitive performance on the standard 8-second WOSAC benchmark, achieving a composite score of 0.7833 (Table 1). It outperformed all existing methods designed for short-term simulation except those explicitly optimized with expensive closed-loop reinforcement learning or fine-tuning. Among simulators capable of dynamic agent insertion, RosettaSim established a new state-of-the-art.
- **LLM Adaptability:**
 - **Transferable Structural Awareness:** An ablation study showed that even when the LLM backbone was fully frozen, RosettaSim outperformed a baseline method, indicating that the pretrained internal representations of the LLM possess transferable structural awareness for traffic dynamics (Table 2). Fine-tuning only the last transformer layer achieved a composite score (0.7648) comparable to fully fine-tuned models (0.7658), suggesting that pretrained knowledge requires minimal alignment.
 - **Contribution of Prior:** A comparison against a randomly initialized Qwen-0.5B revealed that architectural inductive biases alone accounted for 63% of the performance gain over the baseline, while the pretrained statistical prior contributed the remaining 37% (Figure 5).
 - **Impact of LLM Architecture:** Modern LLMs significantly outperformed older architectures like GPT-2 in rapid adaptation (Table 3). Qwen2.5-0.5B achieved the best performance, surpassing larger models like Llama-3.2, hypothesized to be due to its extensive multilingual pre-training fostering generalized sequence modeling.
- **Why LLMs Adapt:**
 - **Zipfian Distribution:** Experiments demonstrated that disrupting the Zipfian distribution of motion tokens significantly degraded performance (Figure 7), validating the hypothesis that LLM effectiveness is linked to their ability to model such data distributions.
 - **Attention Locality:** Qwen2.5-0.5B exhibited similar attention locality patterns for text and motion data across layers (Figure 6), suggesting a metaphorical transfer of language understanding mechanisms to motion tokens.
- **Scaling LLMs:** Increasing LLM model size (within the Qwen2.5 series) did not yield further performance improvements in the frozen setting; performance even dropped at 3B (Table 4). When fine-tuning the last layer, performance converged across different model sizes and genres (Table 5, 6). This suggests that the structural "grammar" of traffic dynamics can be sufficiently captured by smaller architectures, and advanced reasoning capabilities of larger models offer diminishing returns for motion generation.

#### 4.2. Long-term Traffic Simulation Performance

- **RTE Efficacy:** RTE-based metrics exhibited significantly higher Pearson correlations with standard short-term WOSAC metrics (r=0.825 and r=0.791) compared to log-based metrics (r=0.742 and r=0.726) (Figure 8, left). This indicated that RTE accurately reflects simulation quality even without strict one-to-one matching and mitigated the bias observed in log-based Kinematic metrics (which showed negative correlation). RTE's hyperparameters demonstrated robust stability (Figure 8, right).
- **Overall Performance:** RosettaSim achieved a new state-of-the-art performance on the comprehensive RMM-F1 metric (0.7624) for long-term traffic simulation (Table 7).
 - **Traffic Flow Realism:** RosettaSim substantially outperformed the autoregressive baseline, InfGen, in Traffic Flow Realism (0.7846 vs. 0.7637), validating its ability to manage dynamic agent population density and maintain global flow consistency over extended horizons.
 - **Behavior Realism:** RosettaSim demonstrated strong performance in Behavior Realism, particularly excelling in Kinematic metrics (Table 9), indicating superior generation of physically realistic and smooth trajectories. While some baselines showed higher overall interactive scores, this was attributed to their lower agent densities resulting from a lack of continuous spawning.
- **RTE Justifications:**
 - **Exclusion of specific metrics:** RTE was excluded from Traffic Flow metrics like #Enter, #Exit, D_enter, and D_exit because it can misleadingly indicate "improvement" for non-generative baselines as agents naturally leave the scene without replenishment (Figure 11). Moreover, D_enter and D_exit metrics were found to be flawed, rewarding mode collapse to boundary states rather than true generative diversity (Appendix H.3).
 - **Harmonic Mean:** The use of the harmonic mean for RMM-F1 was justified to prevent models from "gaming" the score by prioritizing one dimension (e.g., high density) while neglecting another (e.g., collision avoidance) (Appendix F.2).
- **Retrieval Model Faithfulness:** Evaluation confirmed the retrieval model's high accuracy (averaging 0.90 and 0.85 in different settings) in finding semantically similar real-world scenarios, validating the foundation of the RTE framework (Appendix F.1).
- **Performance Stability:** RosettaSim maintained a more stable performance trajectory over extended horizons compared to baselines. While its pointwise behavior scores might slightly decrease due to active density increase, its temporal stability outperformed InfGen (which decayed) and non-generative methods (which drastically dropped) (Figure 12).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of autonomous driving and broader AI research:

**1. Advancement in Long-term Traffic Simulation:** RosettaSim provides a unified framework that effectively addresses the complex challenges of long-horizon traffic simulation, specifically the joint modeling of multi-agent behaviors and dynamic population dynamics (agent entry and exit). This is a crucial step towards developing more realistic and scalable world models for autonomous driving, enabling more comprehensive training, testing, and validation scenarios than previously possible with short-term simulators.

**2. Novel Application of Large Language Models:** The research empirically demonstrates that the architectural inductive biases and statistical priors of LLMs, particularly their capacity to model structured, Zipfian-distributed sequences, are transferable and beneficial for complex, non-linguistic tasks like interactive traffic simulation. This finding opens new avenues for applying LLMs in domains beyond natural language processing and offers a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms that contribute to their emergent generalization capabilities. It also informs the design of future large-scale sequence models for diverse AI applications.

**3. Development of Reliable Evaluation Metrics:** The introduction of the Retrieval-based Traffic Evaluation (RTE) framework provides a robust, context-aware, and reliable protocol for assessing the fidelity of long-horizon traffic simulations. By moving beyond the limitations of strict one-to-one agent matching and biased global comparisons, RTE enables more accurate benchmarking and fosters more meaningful advancements in simulation quality. This contributes to a more rigorous scientific foundation for evaluating the realism and safety of autonomous driving systems.

**4. Insights into LLM Transferability and Scalability:** The extensive ablation studies on LLM adaptation and scaling offer valuable insights. The finding that smaller LLMs can effectively capture the "grammar" of traffic dynamics, and that performance plateaus or even drops with larger models in certain settings, suggests that for structured, non-linguistic sequences, general sequence modeling capabilities and distributional consistency are more critical than the vast semantic knowledge typically associated with larger models. This could guide future research in selecting and adapting LLMs for domain-specific applications.

**5. Enhanced Safety and Development Cycle for Autonomous Vehicles:** By facilitating the creation of more realistic and stable long-term traffic simulations, RosettaSim can accelerate the development cycle of autonomous vehicles. It enables engineers to test perception, prediction, and planning systems in a wider range of dynamic and complex scenarios, potentially leading to safer and more robust autonomous driving technologies.
