# One Language-Free Foundation Model Is Enough for Universal Vision Anomaly Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent YouTu Lab introduced UniADet, a universal vision anomaly detection framework that operates without a language encoder, simplifying the process by directly learning decoupled decision weights from a vision foundation model. This approach achieves superior performance, reducing learnable parameters by factors of 300x to 10,000x and demonstrating the first instance of a few-shot method surpassing full-shot state-of-the-art on benchmarks like Real-IAD.

## Problem

- Traditional anomaly detection (AD) demands extensive, scenario-specific "normal" image training, hindering adaptability and scalability in open-world settings.
- Existing zero-shot/few-shot AD methods leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are complex, requiring elaborate prompt engineering, adaptive modules, and intricate training strategies.
- VLM-based approaches face limitations in generality, particularly in transferring to pure vision foundation models, and often suffer from learning conflicts due to shared decision weights across diverse visual features.

## Method

- UniADet directly learns "normal" and "anomaly" ==decision weights== from frozen vision foundation model features, eliminating the need for a language encoder and textual prompts.
- It employs ==Decoupling Classification and Segmentation (DCS)==, using separate learned weights for image-level anomaly classification and pixel-level segmentation to mitigate learning conflicts.
- The framework also utilizes ==Decoupling Hierarchical Features (DHF)==, learning distinct weights for each hierarchical feature layer to fully leverage multi-scale information without interference.
- For few-shot scenarios, a ==multi-scale normal memory bank== generates additional anomaly scores, which are then fused with the zero-shot predictions for enhanced performance.

## Results

- UniADet achieved superior performance across **14** diverse datasets, with its DINOv3 integration outperforming VLM-based methods like Bayes-PFL by **7%** P-AUPR in pixel-level segmentation on industrial benchmarks.
- The framework significantly reduced learnable parameters by factors of **3**00x** to 10,000x** and demonstrated faster inference speeds, showcasing its efficiency.
- In a few-shot setting, **4-shot** UniADet became the first method to surpass the performance of existing full-shot state-of-the-art anomaly detection systems on challenging datasets like Real-IAD.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The language encoder's role in VLM-based AD is essentially to derive decision weights, which can be directly learned in a data-driven manner, making language components expendable.
> - Shared decision weights between image-level and pixel-level tasks, or across different hierarchical feature layers, create learning conflicts that hinder optimal performance.
> - Decoupling these weights across tasks and feature hierarchies allows for optimized decision boundaries at each level, fully leveraging the foundation model's diverse representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Anomaly Detection (AD) is a critical task in computer vision with widespread applications, ranging from quality control in manufacturing (e.g., detecting surface defects) and medical imaging analysis (e.g., identifying lesions) to more abstract tasks like discovering hybrid subspecies in biological sciences. Traditionally, AD methods necessitate extensive training on large datasets of "normal" images specific to each application scenario. This "full-shot" training paradigm presents significant challenges in terms of adaptability, scalability, and privacy, particularly in "open-world" scenarios where anomalies are diverse, novel, and cannot be exhaustively predefined.

To address these limitations, the research community has increasingly shifted towards "zero-shot" and "few-shot" anomaly detection. These paradigms aim to identify and segment anomalies without prior training or with very limited normal examples from the target domain, respectively. Recent advancements in this area have largely leveraged the power of large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP. VLM-based approaches typically rely on "textual prompts" to encode the semantics of "normal" and "anomaly" concepts, which are then used to guide the visual features extracted by the model. Methods like WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, Bayes-PFL, and AdaptCLIP have demonstrated impressive performance by integrating visual knowledge into textual prompts, designing elaborate adaptive modules, or employing complex training strategies.

However, the authors of this paper critically examine the prevailing VLM-centric approach. They argue that current VLM-based methods often suffer from several drawbacks:
1. **Complex prompt engineering:** Crafting effective textual prompts can be challenging and domain-specific.
2. **Elaborate adaptation modules:** Many methods introduce additional learnable parameters and modules to adapt VLMs, increasing model complexity.
3. **Convoluted training strategies:** The need for specific training strategies further limits flexibility and generality.
Crucially, these issues inherently limit their ability to easily transfer to purely visual foundation models (eFMs) like DINOv2 or DINOv3, which lack a language encoder. This paper challenges the fundamental assumption that a language encoder is necessary for universal anomaly detection, posing a crucial, unexplored question in the field. By demonstrating that a language-free foundation model can achieve, and even surpass, the performance of VLM-based methods, this work represents a significant departure from current trends and opens a new avenue for research in universal AD. It suggests that the impressive generalization capabilities of self-supervised vision-only foundation models might be sufficient, and potentially superior, for anomaly detection tasks without the added complexity of language.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **universal anomaly detection framework (UniADet)** that is exceptionally simple, general, and effective, capable of identifying and segmenting anomalies in open and dynamic scenarios under zero- and few-shot paradigms, without requiring dataset-specific fine-tuning.

The motivation for this objective stems from a critical re-evaluation of the current state of universal AD:
1. **Addressing limitations of VLM-based AD:** The authors observe that existing VLM-based zero-shot and few-shot AD methods, despite their progress, are bogged down by complexities such as elaborate prompt engineering, the need for intricate adaptive modules, and convoluted training strategies. These complexities restrict their flexibility, generality (especially to pure vision foundation models), and computational efficiency.
2. **Rethinking the role of language encoders:** The core insight and motivation for UniADet is the realization that in VLM-based AD, the language encoder primarily serves to derive "decision weights" for anomaly classification and segmentation. The authors hypothesize that these "normal" and "anomaly" embeddings (weights) can be learned directly and efficiently through a data-driven process, effectively rendering the language encoder and all associated textual components unnecessary. This fundamentally questions the necessity of language in universal AD.
3. **Enhancing generality and flexibility:** By removing the language dependency, the goal is to create a framework that can seamlessly adapt to a variety of powerful foundation models, including purely visual ones like DINOv2 and DINOv3, thereby expanding its applicability beyond vision-language models like CLIP.
4. **Improving efficiency and reducing complexity:** A key motivation is to significantly reduce model complexity and resource consumption (parameters and inference time) associated with VLM adaptation, ultimately leading to a more parameter-efficient and faster AD solution.
5. **Mitigating learning conflicts:** The authors also identify that existing methods often share decision weights across global image tokens and local patch tokens, and across multi-level hierarchical features. They hypothesize that these different feature manifolds lead to learning conflicts, motivating the need for decoupled learning mechanisms.

In essence, the paper aims to demonstrate that "one language-free foundation model is enough" by stripping down the complexities of current methods and focusing on directly learning task-specific, decoupled decision weights, thereby achieving superior performance with remarkable simplicity and efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **UniADet (Universal vision Anomaly Detection)** framework is designed to be embarrassingly simple, general, and effective by fundamentally rethinking the role of language models in AD and introducing key decoupling principles.

#### 3.1. Rethinking VLMs for Anomaly Detection

The foundational premise of UniADet is the observation that in typical VLM-based AD methods (e.g., those using CLIP), the text encoder's primary function is to generate a two-class weight matrix (W) that embeds "normal" and "anomaly" semantics. This matrix then interacts with visual features (global image tokens `x_q` and local patch tokens `F_q`) to derive anomaly scores and maps through similarity computations.
The authors' breakthrough insight is that if the language encoder's role is merely to derive these decision weights, then these weights (`W`) can be **directly learned** rather than being indirectly derived through text prompts and a language encoder. This simple change allows for the complete removal of the text encoder and any associated textual components (e.g., prompt engineering, learnable prompt embeddings), as well as task-specific learnable visual parameters (`θ_v`) if the chosen vision foundation model is powerful enough. This simplifies the anomaly scoring process to direct similarity computation between learned weights and frozen features from a foundation model:
`ˆY_z = Softmax(<W, F_q>/τ)` for pixel segmentation
`ˆy_z = Softmax(<W, x_q>/τ)` for image classification

#### 3.2. Decoupling Classification and Segmentation (DCS)

Existing VLM-based AD methods often use a *shared weight* `W` for both image-level anomaly classification (global `x_q`) and pixel-level anomaly segmentation (local `F_q`). The authors argue that global image tokens and local patch tokens represent fundamentally different manifolds of visual information. Forcing them to share a single decision weight introduces a learning conflict, suboptimal for both tasks.
To address this, UniADet proposes **decoupling** the classification and segmentation tasks. Instead of a single shared `W`, two independent, specialized weights are learned: `W_cls` for global image classification and `W_seg` for local pixel segmentation. This allows each task to learn its optimal decision boundary without interference, thus resolving the conflict arising from disparate feature manifolds.

#### 3.3. Decoupling Hierarchical Features (DHF)

Further extending the decoupling principle, UniADet recognizes that visual features extracted from different layers (or blocks) of a foundation model encode varying semantics and reside in different feature manifolds. Most existing methods, however, tend to share the same weights across these multi-scale hierarchical features.
UniADet proposes to **decouple hierarchical features** by learning layer-specific weights. For each `l`-th layer, separate weights `W_cls^l` and `W_seg^l` are learned for classification and segmentation, respectively. This complete decoupling ensures that anomaly detection at each layer benefits from its own optimized decision boundary, leveraging the rich, complementary information available at different scales without internal conflicts.

#### 3.4. UniADet with Few-Shot Normal Images

While the decoupled zero-shot framework is powerful, some anomalies are defined relative to specific normal references (e.g., a missing component). To extend UniADet to a few-shot setting, inspired by memory bank approaches (e.g., PatchCore, WinCLIP), a multi-scale normal memory is incorporated.
Given `k` (e.g., 1, 2, or 4) normal images, their local patch features from multiple scales are stored in a memory bank `M^l`. For a query image, few-shot anomaly scores (`ˆY_f^l`) are computed by finding the cosine distance to the nearest neighbor in the memory bank for each patch. These multi-scale few-shot predictions are then aggregated (simple average) and fused with the zero-shot predictions (`ˆY_z`) using a balance factor `λ_f`. A similar fusion strategy is applied for image-level anomaly scores.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference

- **Training:** UniADet optimizes the decoupled weights (`W_cls^l` and `W_seg^l`) using standard loss functions: cross-entropy loss for classification and a combination of Focal and Dice losses for segmentation.
- **Class-Aware Augmentation (CAA):** To enhance robustness, especially to multi-scale context, a novel Class-Aware Augmentation is introduced. This includes:
 - **Grid Mosaic:** Normal images are augmented by mosaicking with other normal images of the same category. Anomalous images can be mosaicked with a mix of normal and anomalous images to maintain class ratio and contextual consistency.
 - **Grid Cropping:** Random patches are cropped. For anomalous images, only patches containing anomaly regions are selected, ensuring relevant supervisory signals.
- **Inference:** For few-shot scenarios, the fused predictions (Eqs. 11 and 12) are used. For zero-shot, Eq. 8 is used, often fused with the maximum value of the anomaly map. The framework operates training-free on target datasets once the decoupled weights are learned from an auxiliary dataset.

In summary, UniADet's methodology hinges on a profound simplification: directly learning decision weights, coupled with a systematic decoupling strategy across tasks (classification vs. segmentation) and feature hierarchies. This is further bolstered by a memory-bank mechanism for few-shot learning and class-aware augmentation for robust training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation of UniADet across 14 diverse datasets (6 industrial, 8 medical) covering both zero-shot and few-shot settings yielded several significant findings:

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Generalization (Industrial and Medical Domains)

- **Superiority over VLM-based SOTA:** UniADet, even when utilizing CLIP as the foundation model, substantially outperforms state-of-the-art language-dependent methods like AdaptCLIP in image anomaly classification (e.g., 91.6% vs. 89.6% I-AUROC on industrial domain mean). This directly validates the core hypothesis that complex language prompts and encoders are unnecessary.
- **Leveraging Pure Vision Foundation Models:** The language-free nature of UniADet enables seamless integration with powerful pure vision foundation models like DINOv2 and DINOv3. When using DINOv3, UniADet achieves absolute leading performance in pixel-level anomaly segmentation on industrial datasets, surpassing Bayes-PFL by a significant margin (e.g., 7% P-AUPR, from 40.2% to 47.4%). This highlights the fine-grained representational capabilities of self-supervised vision models, which are crucial for precise segmentation tasks.
- **Robustness on Challenging Datasets:** UniADet demonstrates exceptional robustness on the large-scale Real-IAD industrial benchmark, achieving 82.5% I-AUROC and 43.1% P-AUPR with DINOv2, outperforming SOTA methods by approximately 8% and 13%, respectively.
- **Strong Cross-Domain Generalization (Medical):** When trained solely on an industrial auxiliary dataset (VisA) and directly applied to medical anomaly detection, UniADet consistently outperforms most SOTA methods. With DINOv3, it achieves 91.7% P-AUROC and 63.2% P-AUPR on medical pixel-level segmentation, significantly exceeding Bayes-PFL. This strongly confirms the robust cross-domain generalization of the language-free framework.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Complexity

- **Parameter Efficiency:** UniADet achieves drastic reductions in learnable parameters. Compared to Bayes-PFL and AdaptCLIP, UniADet reduces learnable parameters by factors of 10,000x and 300x, respectively (only 0.002M learnable parameters, primarily the decoupled weights).
- **Faster Inference Speed:** The framework also achieves the best inference speed, with an image processing time of 15.7ms (with CLIP) and 41.9ms (with DINOv3), showcasing its computational efficiency. Crucially, extending from zero-shot to one-shot adds almost no additional parameters or inference time.

#### 4.3. Few-Shot and Full-Shot Comparisons

- **SOTA Performance with Minimal Resources:** In the one-shot setting, UniADet with a single pure vision foundation model (e.g., DINOv3) achieves superior performance (e.g., 95.6% I-AUROC and 98.5% P-AUROC on industrial domain mean), outperforming methods leveraging VLMs. This underscores the efficiency of the decoupling and memory bank strategies.
- **Competitive with Model Ensembles:** UniADet with a single foundation model (DINOv2) achieves performance comparable to or better than ensemble methods like UniVAD (which combines multiple powerful models like RAM, Grounding DINO, SAM, CLIP, and DINOv2), highlighting its architectural and computational efficiency.
- **Outperforming Full-Shot SOTA for the First Time:** Remarkably, with just a few (e.g., 4) training-free normal images, UniADet surpasses earlier full-shot methods like UniAD. Furthermore, on the challenging Real-IAD dataset, 4-shot UniADet (90.3% I-AUROC) outperforms the current best *full-shot* Dinomaly (89.3% I-AUROC). This is the *first time* a few-shot method has surpassed full-shot SOTA, demonstrating the powerful potential of foundation models for few-shot anomaly detection in open-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Decoupling Classification and Segmentation (DCS):** Introducing independent weights for image classification and pixel segmentation significantly improves both image- and pixel-level performance, validating that decoupling mitigates learning conflicts arising from distinct feature manifolds.
- **Decoupling Hierarchical Features (DHF):** Extending decoupling to layer-specific weights across all hierarchical features further enhances performance, confirming that full decoupling unlocks the foundation model's potential by addressing conflicts within and across layers.
- **Class-Aware Augmentation (CAA):** The class-aware augmentation strategy leads to further performance gains, while class-agnostic augmentation degrades performance. This confirms that maintaining semantic consistency across augmented contexts is vital for robust AD learning.
- **Impact of Hierarchical Features:** Performance generally improves with the integration of more hierarchical features from different blocks, validating their rich and complementary information for comprehensive AD, especially fine-grained pixel-level segmentation benefiting from low-level details in shallower blocks.

In summary, the main findings conclusively demonstrate UniADet's superior performance, remarkable parameter efficiency, and broad generality, challenging established norms in universal vision anomaly detection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "One Language-Free Foundation Model Is Enough for Universal Vision Anomaly Detection" carries significant implications for the field of computer vision, particularly for anomaly detection, and holds substantial potential impact:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Universal Anomaly Detection:** The most profound significance of this work is its fundamental challenge to the prevailing reliance on vision-language models (VLMs) and language encoders for universal anomaly detection. By demonstrating that a "language-free" framework can achieve, and often surpass, the performance of complex VLM-based methods, the paper advocates for a simpler, more direct approach. This could inspire a paradigm shift, prompting researchers to explore the untapped potential of pure vision foundation models and refocus efforts on core visual mechanisms rather than linguistic interfaces for AD.

2. **Unlocking the Full Potential of Pure Vision Foundation Models:** UniADet's design allows for seamless integration with powerful self-supervised pure vision models like DINOv2 and DINOv3. The superior performance observed with these models, especially for pixel-level segmentation, highlights that their inherent fine-grained representational capabilities might be more directly suited for anomaly localization tasks than language-supervised models. This opens new avenues for leveraging the rapidly evolving landscape of vision-only foundation models for AD.

3. **Remarkable Simplicity and Efficiency:** The "embarrassingly simple" nature of UniADet, characterized by its minimal learnable parameters (orders of magnitude fewer than SOTA methods) and fast inference speeds, is a critical practical advantage. This simplicity reduces computational burden, memory requirements, and development complexity, making universal AD more accessible, deployable, and environmentally friendly. It simplifies the deployment process in real-world applications where resource constraints or real-time performance are crucial.

4. **Enhanced Generality and Flexibility:** By decoupling the framework from language, UniADet gains unprecedented generality, capable of adapting to various foundation models without requiring model-specific architectural changes or re-engineering of prompts. This flexibility ensures that the framework can evolve with advancements in general visual representation learning, rather than being tied to the slower pace of VLM development or the complexities of adapting them.

5. **Breaking Performance Ceilings:** The achievement of few-shot UniADet surpassing state-of-the-art *full-shot* methods on challenging datasets like Real-IAD is a landmark result. This demonstrates an exceptional level of generalization and efficiency, suggesting that for many real-world scenarios, collecting vast amounts of normal training data for specific tasks might become less necessary. This finding can significantly reduce the data annotation burden and cost for new AD applications.

6. **Broad Practical Applications:** The demonstrated effectiveness across diverse industrial and medical domains underscores UniADet's immediate practical utility. From manufacturing quality control to medical diagnosis (e.g., tumor detection, lesion segmentation, polyp segmentation), a highly accurate, generalized, and efficient anomaly detection system can lead to improved product quality, earlier disease detection, and enhanced operational efficiency. The training-free nature on target domains is particularly valuable for privacy-sensitive sectors like healthcare.

In conclusion, UniADet offers a compelling vision for universal anomaly detection that is rooted in simplicity, efficiency, and the direct exploitation of powerful vision features. Its significance lies in redefining the landscape of AD research, pushing the boundaries of generalization, and providing a robust, practical solution for complex real-world challenges.
