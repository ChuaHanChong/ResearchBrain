# TableVerse: A Large-scale Tabletop Dataset with Real-world Grounded Layouts for Generalizable Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TableVerse presents an automated Real2Sim pipeline that transforms unstructured internet images into 100,000 high-fidelity, physically consistent, simulation-ready tabletop environments for robotic manipulation research. This system achieved a 0.0% scene collision rate and superior MLLM-evaluated layout and geometry fidelity compared to existing 3D scene reconstruction methods.

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale, high-fidelity, and physically plausible scene data hinders the development of generalizable robotic manipulation policies.
- Existing automated 3D scene generation methods often produce physically implausible, geometrically colliding, or overly simplistic layouts, failing to capture real-world clutter.
- A significant 'reality gap' exists between synthetic data and the complex, cluttered environments robots need to operate in, limiting the robustness of learned policies.

## Method

- An automated ==Real2Sim pipeline== converts unscripted internet media into high-fidelity, simulation-ready tabletop environments, preserving metric scales, object topologies, and contact mechanics.
- A ==Layout-Consistent Collision Rectification (LCCR)== framework addresses mesh interpenetrations by disentangling objects while preserving macroscopic layouts, followed by physics-based stabilization.
- The **TableVerse**-100K Dataset, comprising 100,000 unique, physically consistent, and instruction-annotated scenes, is constructed using an ==MLLM-driven curation workflow==.

## Results

- TableVerse achieved a **0.0%** scene collision rate, an absolute improvement compared to baselines like MIDI (**90.0%**), SAM3D (**81.0%**), and SceneMaker (**72.0%**).
- The pipeline consistently outperformed baselines in MLLM-based GPT-Scores, scoring higher in Layout Fidelity (**7.14**), Visual Quality (**7.08**), and Geometry Quality (**7.03**).
- The LCCR module reduced the scene collision rate from **79.0%** (direct alignment) to **0.0%**, demonstrating its effectiveness in eliminating volumetric overlap, with subsequent physics simulation ensuring stable resting states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deterministic reconstruction from real-world observations offers a more effective path to generating physically accurate and manipulation-ready scene layouts than imaginative or procedural methods.
> - Combining geometric collision rectification with physics-based stabilization is crucial for transforming raw reconstructed scenes into stable, simulation-ready environments.
> - Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) can be integrated into an automated workflow for scalable, high-quality data curation, annotation, and task generation, including complex spatial relation predicates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalizable robotic manipulation policies requires substantial quantities of physics-ready simulation data that accurately reflect the characteristics of real-world environments. Existing methods for automated 3D scene generation typically fall into two categories: vision-driven reconstruction and language-conditioned layout synthesis.

Within the vision-driven domain, single-view methods like MIDI, SAM3D, and SceneMaker have demonstrated capabilities in reconstructing 3D scenes from images. However, these methods often struggle with dense occlusion, lack precise metric constraints, and frequently produce severe mesh interpenetrations in cluttered environments, rendering the generated scenes unsuitable for stable physics simulation. These issues stem from a failure to balance geometric fidelity with physical feasibility.

Concurrently, language-conditioned paradigms, which use large language models (LLMs) to synthesize scenes based on text queries or symbolic graphs, often produce overly simplistic and sparse layouts. These methods typically lack continuous geometric grounding and suffer from significant scale errors, failing to capture the complex, dense clutter, vertical stacking, and composite asset structures prevalent in real human environments. Datasets generated by these methods, such as TO-Scene and MesaTask-10K, exhibit limitations like simplistic "click-and-drop" mechanisms or an "over-idealized neatness bias," which do not mirror the chaotic distributions of real-world tabletops.

The field of 6-DoF grasp synthesis and robotic motion planning, which is crucial for generating manipulation demonstrations, has also seen advancements. Modern dense architectures map point clouds to 6-DoF grasp spaces, and GPU-accelerated motion planners enable efficient trajectory generation. However, these synthesis pipelines remain constrained by the quality and realism of the input scene layouts, often bottlenecked by the "spatial hallucinations" or over-simplified arrangements produced by previous generation methods.

TableVerse addresses these limitations by introducing a novel "Real2Sim" pipeline. Rather than generating layouts imaginatively or procedurally, it focuses on deterministic reconstruction from unstructured, in-the-wild image data. This approach aims to bridge the gap between synthetic data and real-world complexity by producing high-fidelity, physically consistent, and manipulation-ready tabletop environments. The work positions itself as a scalable data foundation to foster robust, highly generalizable visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation, moving beyond the simplistic and often physically implausible scenes generated by prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the limitations imposed on generalizable robotic manipulation policies by the scarcity of large-scale, high-fidelity, and physically plausible scene data. The existing automated synthesis methods for generating scene layouts are motivated by the need for abundant training data but frequently produce outputs that suffer from physical implausibility, severe geometric collisions, and a failure to accurately represent the dense clutter and complex topologies characteristic of actual human environments. This creates a significant "reality gap" for training robotic manipulation systems.

The motivation for TableVerse stems from the observation that conventional approaches catastrophically fail when confronted with unstructured, in-the-wild real-world data, proving incapable of reconstructing the intricate arrangements common in human-occupied spaces. To address this, the authors aim to shift the paradigm from imaginative layout generation to deterministic reconstruction.

The key objectives are threefold:

1. **Develop an Automated Real2Sim Pipeline:** To create a fully automated, observation-driven framework, termed the TableVerse Pipeline, that can seamlessly convert unscripted internet media directly into high-fidelity, simulation-ready environments. This pipeline is designed to restore accurate metric scales, authentic topologies, and verified mechanical stability.
2. **Introduce a Layout-Consistent Collision Rectification (LCCR) Framework:** To devise a geometric rectification module capable of disentangling intersecting meshes. This framework is motivated by the fact that direct deployment of reconstructed layouts often results in severe mesh interpenetrations, which current physics engines cannot handle gracefully, leading to unstable or "explosive" simulations. The LCCR framework is tightly coupled with a physics-based resting-state stabilization step to ensure mechanically stable environments.
3. **Construct and Release a Large-Scale Dataset:** To leverage the complete automated pipeline to create the TableVerse-100K Dataset. This dataset is motivated by the need for a massive, physically consistent corpus comprising 100,000 unique tabletop environments. These environments are intended to be paired with interactive manipulation trajectories, capturing diverse asset compositions, realistic spatial distributions, and high-quality demonstrations to establish a scalable data foundation for future research in generalizable robotic manipulation tasks.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to provide a robust and scalable data solution that enables the training of robotic policies capable of operating effectively in complex, cluttered real-world settings, thereby advancing the field of generalizable robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TableVerse methodology involves a fully automated Real2Sim pipeline that transforms unstructured internet media into interactive, simulation-ready tabletop environments. The core of the approach is a deterministic perception-to-physics workflow designed to preserve authentic real-world metric scales, object topologies, and contact mechanics.

The comprehensive system workflow comprises four sequential stages:

#### 3.1. Instance-level Object Extraction and Generation

This stage processes a single-view real-world image to identify and reconstruct individual objects.

- **Open-Vocabulary Hybrid Object Detection:** To detect objects in a scene, the Seed-1.8 model is employed for direct open-vocabulary detection on the tabletop. Objects are classified as either "regular objects" or "composite objects" (containers with nested contents). Non-meshable substances are treated as regular single entities. For interior item clusters, the detector provides a single bounding box and an instance count. SAM2 is then used to extract precise instance segmentation masks from these bounding boxes. The system uses a detailed text prompt for Seed-1.8 to guide detection, filter out human elements, detect the tabletop, and define rules for composite object contents.
- **Composite Object Generation:** To handle occlusions and ensure physical validity for composite objects, SAM3D reconstructs 3D meshes for both the container and its nested contents as separate entities. These individual assets are then assembled in an isolated MuJoCo free-fall simulation, where the contents are "dropped" into the container. This physics-guided assembly ensures valid physical contacts and independent structural manipulation properties.
- **Object Position and Pose Extraction:** Depth Anything 3 is utilized to extract scene point clouds from the monocular input. The dominant plane normal of the segmented table or floor surface mask is used to estimate the absolute gravity vector and align the scene into a canonical coordinate system. Object-specific point cloud segments are then isolated via SAM2 masks, followed by statistical and radius-based denoising. A coarse-to-fine alignment procedure involves a continuous search over the z-axis rotation to find the optimal initial heading using Chamfer distance, followed by Iterative Closest Point (ICP) registration to regress the final 6-DoF poses and dimensions. All 3D meshes undergo approximate convex decomposition via CoACD to prevent MuJoCo simulation artifacts.

#### 3.2. Layout-Consistent Collision Rectification (LCCR)

This module addresses mesh interpenetrations, which are common after initial registration and can lead to unstable physics simulations.

- **Phase 1: Hierarchical Contact Grouping and Grounding:** Objects in physical contact are clustered (treating composite assets as single entities). All groups are grounded by translating their lowest vertices to the table plane (z=0). Objects are projected onto the horizontal plane, and those with a significant 2D area overlap (≥ 50%) are merged into hierarchical groups to preserve vertical stacking. Pairs with < 50% overlap are treated as disjoint horizontal entities.
- **Phase 2: Radial Graph Construction and Horizontal Rectification:** A proximity-driven radial scene graph is constructed where the most central group is the root. Non-root groups are sorted by their distance to the root. An inside-out, sequential expansion workflow rigidly translates each group outward along its radial directional unit vector until it clears all previously rectified boundaries (Equations 1-3). This process eliminates lateral overlap while preserving the macroscopic azimuthal arrangement.
- **Phase 3: Vertical Disentanglement and Physics Stabilization:** Vertical interpenetrations within high-overlap groups are resolved. If a 3D intersection is detected, the object with the smaller horizontal footprint is translated upward along the z-axis until it is disjoint from the supporting mesh. After geometric rectification, the collision-free layout is imported into the MuJoCo physics engine for a brief forward simulation under gravity, allowing objects to settle naturally into a mechanically stable resting state.

#### 3.3. TableVerse-100K Dataset Construction and Curation

Leveraging the automated pipeline, the TableVerse-100K Dataset is generated.

- **Scale and Sourcing:** The dataset comprises 100,000 unique, physically consistent, and instruction-annotated tabletop environments, containing nearly 1 million individual object instances spanning over 35,000 semantic categories. Data is sourced from unstructured internet images, with automated visual filtering to isolate valid tabletop scenes.
- **MLLM-Driven Curation and Annotation:** Gemini 2.5 Pro is used in an automated workflow (Figure 5) to filter out degenerate layouts and enrich metadata. For each candidate scene, a composite image (Front View + Top-Down View) is generated as a multi-view visual prompt. The MLLM performs a seven-fold evaluation and annotation:
 - **(A) Usability Gate:** Filters scenes with extreme regional partitions or non-tabletop entities.
 - **(B) Severe Label Error Check:** Identifies and corrects clearly wrong labels.
 - **(C) Label Confidence Calibration:** Assigns a confidence score for each instance.
 - **(D) Physical Property Prediction:** Infers mass and articulated hinge structures.
 - **(E) Quality Scoring:** Assigns a score (1-10) based on object count/diversity, geometric plausibility, graspability, and layout clarity.
 - **(F) Object Relabeling:** Provides visual-only category names and distinguishing phrases.
 - **(G) Pick & Place Task Generation:** Synthesizes manipulation commands with 10 distinct spatial relation enums, ensuring physical feasibility and daily-life plausibility.
- **Domain Randomization:** Over 1,700 high-resolution texture maps from professional platforms are dynamically applied to tabletop surfaces during simulation initialization to enhance visual diversity and robustness against overfitting.

#### 3.4. Task-Conditioned Trajectory Generation

This framework translates high-level manipulation instructions into continuous, executable robotic trajectories.

- **Top-Down Prioritized 6D Grasp Synthesis:** GraspGen predicts candidate 6D grasp poses for the target object. A top-down prioritization filter (Equation 4) selects high-quality poses based on alignment with the world vertical axis. A multi-stage relaxation arbitration loop (Appendix D.1) is used to ensure grasp availability, progressively relaxing strict directional constraints if no valid poses are found.
- **Relation-Constrained Placement and Motion Planning:** To fulfill spatial placement predicates (e.g., "on," "in"), target placement poses are sampled within localized bounding regions derived from the reference asset’s geometry (Appendix D.2). Candidate poses are verified for sufficient clearance against surrounding assets using an Axis-Aligned Bounding Box module (Equation 5). Collision-free joint-space trajectories are then optimized and executed using the GPU-accelerated cuRobo motion planner, following a six-phase manipulation strategy. The planner utilizes a hybrid collision-representation hierarchy, preferentially using high-fidelity meshes but falling back to AABBs for topologically degenerate assets (Appendix D.3).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TableVerse focused on demonstrating its capability in producing simulation-ready scenes with accurate metric scales and physical fidelity, comparing it against alternative single-view 3D scene reconstruction methods and performing an ablation study on the LCCR module.

#### 4.1. Setup

- **Implementation Details:** The pipeline integrates Seed-1.8 for object detection, SAM2 for instance segmentation, SAM3D for 3D mesh reconstruction, and Depth Anything 3 for metric point cloud extraction. Gravity-alignment transformation is applied for physical consistency, and CoACD performs convex decomposition on meshes. Gemini 2.5 Pro is used for multi-dimensional scene evaluation, filtering, and task annotation.
- **Baselines:** TableVerse was compared against MIDI, SAM3D, and SceneMaker. To ensure a fair comparison and isolate 3D layout evaluation, all baselines were provided with the exact instance masks generated by the TableVerse pipeline. Due to their limitations, baselines only received masks for outermost containers, omitting nested objects.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance was assessed using:
 - **Scene Collision Rate (%) ↓:** The percentage of scenes with volumetric mesh interpenetration before physics relaxation.
 - **GPT-Score:** A multi-dimensional MLLM evaluation (1-10) with sub-dimensions:
 - **Layout Fidelity (LF) ↑:** Spatial layout and scale alignment.
 - **Visual Quality (VQ) ↑:** Texture and rendering naturalness.
 - **Geometry Quality (GQ) ↑:** Preservation of correct categories, shapes, and structural details.
 - **Average Rank (Avg. Rank) ↓:** Mean cross-method preference ranking.
- **Test Set:** A test set of 100 unscripted tabletop samples curated from raw internet images was used, covering diverse scenarios like dense layouts, vertical stacking, nested assets, low resolution, and cluttered backgrounds.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with Alternative Methods

The quantitative results, summarized in Table 1, indicate that TableVerse exhibited superior performance across all evaluation dimensions when compared to MIDI, SAM3D, and SceneMaker.

- **Scene Collision Rate:** TableVerse achieved an absolute 0.0% Scene Collision Rate, indicating no volumetric mesh interpenetrations in its generated scenes prior to physics relaxation. In contrast, MIDI showed a 90.0% collision rate, SAM3D 81.0%, and SceneMaker 72.0%. These high collision rates for baselines signify that their generated environments are largely unsimulable without further rectification.
- **GPT-Score:** TableVerse consistently outperformed baselines in the MLLM-based GPT-Score sub-dimensions:
 - **Layout Fidelity (LF):** TableVerse scored 7.14, substantially higher than MIDI (5.81), SAM3D (5.73), and SceneMaker (4.90). This indicates superior preservation of object-to-object spatial relationships, scale, orientation, and position.
 - **Visual Quality (VQ):** TableVerse achieved 7.08, compared to MIDI (5.08), SAM3D (5.44), and SceneMaker (5.48), demonstrating better rendering and texture naturalness.
 - **Geometry Quality (GQ):** TableVerse scored 7.03, outperforming MIDI (4.83), SAM3D (5.47), and SceneMaker (4.85), reflecting accurate preservation of object categories, shapes, and structural details.
- **Average Rank:** TableVerse secured the top average cross-method ranking at 1.38, indicating a consistent preference over all other methods, which ranked significantly lower (MIDI 2.73, SAM3D 2.67, SceneMaker 3.22).

Qualitative analysis (Figure 7) showed that baselines struggled with dense clutter and complex backgrounds, leading to compromised layout fidelity and structural vulnerabilities. For instance, SceneMaker degraded under dense clutter, and SAM3D lacked rigid metric alignment and physical boundary constraints, leading to catastrophic collision rates. MIDI also suffered from pervasive interpenetrations and failed to resolve fine-grained geometric structures. TableVerse, by contrast, eliminated physical anomalies and achieved high fidelity in layout, visual quality, and geometric recovery.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the LCCR module was conducted using the same 100 test tabletop environments under three configurations: Direct alignment, Direct + LCCR, and Direct + LCCR + Simulation (the full pipeline).

- **Scene Collision Rate:**
 - Direct alignment yielded a 79.0% collision rate, indicating that raw outputs from perception noise are largely unsimulable (Figure 8a).
 - Direct + LCCR achieved a 0.0% collision rate, demonstrating the LCCR algorithm's effectiveness in eliminating volumetric overlap geometrically (Figure 8b).
 - Direct + LCCR + Simulation (Ours) also maintained a 0.0% collision rate, while further refining the scene (Figure 8c).

The results confirmed that LCCR successfully disentangles meshes, but pure rigid translations can leave artificial micro-gaps or floating assets. The final forward MuJoCo simulation phase was shown to be crucial for settling objects under gravity, closing these micro-gaps, and establishing contact-validated, physics-ready digital twins.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TableVerse project presents a significant contribution to the fields of robotic manipulation and simulated environment generation. Its primary significance lies in the development of a fully automated, observation-driven Real2Sim pipeline that can convert unstructured, in-the-wild internet media into high-fidelity, simulation-ready tabletop environments. This addresses a critical bottleneck in robotic research: the lack of large-scale, physics-ready simulation data that accurately reflects the complexity and clutter of real human environments.

The potential impacts are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Generalizable Robotic Manipulation:** By providing a dataset of unprecedented scale (TableVerse-100K with 100,000 unique scenes, nearly 1M object instances, and 35K semantic categories) and realism, TableVerse can significantly advance the development of generalizable visuomotor policies. Traditional datasets often feature simplistic or synthetically generated layouts, which limits the robustness and transferability of learned policies to real-world, cluttered scenarios. TableVerse's emphasis on diverse asset compositions, realistic spatial distributions, and physically grounded scenes offers a more robust foundation for training.
- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** The pipeline's ability to deterministically reconstruct scenes with accurate metric scales, authentic topologies, and verified mechanical stability directly from real-world images helps bridge the "reality gap." This reduces the need for manual scene design and overcomes the physical implausibility and severe collision issues prevalent in previous automated synthesis methods.
- **Novel Collision Rectification:** The introduction of the Layout-Consistent Collision Rectification (LCCR) framework is a key methodological advancement. Its ability to elegantly disentangle intersecting meshes while preserving macroscopic spatial layouts, followed by physics-based stabilization, is critical for generating truly simulation-ready environments where objects behave predictably. This specific component has implications for any system aiming to reconstruct cluttered 3D scenes for physical simulation.
- **Automated Data Curation and Annotation:** The MLLM-driven (Gemini 2.5 Pro) curation and annotation pipeline streamlines the creation of high-quality, metadata-rich datasets. This automated approach, including usability gates, property prediction, quality scoring, relabeling, and task generation, is highly scalable and reduces human intervention, paving the way for even larger and more diverse datasets in the future.
- **Enhanced Domain Randomization:** The extensive domain randomization through dynamically applied texture maps further enhances the dataset's utility by increasing visual diversity. This is crucial for training policies that are robust to variations in appearance and less prone to overfitting.
- **Facilitating Future Research:** The release of the TableVerse-100K dataset and the underlying methodologies provides a valuable benchmark and resource for the robotics and computer vision communities. It encourages research into more robust perception, planning, and control algorithms for complex manipulation tasks.

Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges some limitations, primarily related to the performance of SAM3D with low-resolution or heavily occluded objects, which can lead to inaccurate 3D model generation. The time-consuming nature of 3D model generation for entire scenes is also noted, indicating an area for future optimization. Addressing these could further enhance the pipeline's efficiency and fidelity, expanding its impact.
