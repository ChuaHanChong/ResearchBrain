# Sampling-Based Optimization with Parallelized Physics Simulator for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Tartu developed a model-based planning framework that uses a parallelized physics simulator for bimanual manipulation. Their system achieves high success rates on complex, contact-rich tasks in cluttered environments, demonstrating superior generalization compared to learning-based methods, and operates in real-time with successful sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- End-to-end learning policies for bimanual manipulation struggle with generalization to novel scenarios, especially in cluttered environments with static obstacles.
- Existing learning-based approaches often require extensive data collection and can exhibit brittleness when faced with variations in object placement or task constraints.
- Prior model-based planning methods have not been extensively applied to contact-rich bimanual tasks or were too slow for real-time control, often being task-specific and difficult to generalize.

## Method

- **MPPI-Bimanual** — a sampling-based optimization framework using a customized ==Model Predictive Path Integral Control (MPPI)== algorithm with a ==GPU-accelerated MuJoCo (MJX)== physics simulator.
- A ==Quadratic Program (QP)== projects sampled joint velocities onto a feasible set, enforcing smoothness and ==jerk-bounded trajectories== in parallel for all samples.
- Task-specific, ==phase-dependent algebraic cost functions== encode desired behaviors and constraints, guiding the manipulators through complex interactions and object handling.

## Results

- Achieved **nearly 100% success** for ball lifting and transporting and up to **95%** for a handover task in obstacle-rich environments, qualitatively outperforming a state-of-the-art learning-based baseline in generalization.
- Demonstrated real-time performance with computation times consistently **under 100ms per step** on commodity GPUs for all tasks, even with large batch sizes (e.g., **<60ms for 1750 samples**).
- Successfully executed a bimanual handover task in a real-world setting through robust sim-to-real transfer, facilitated by real-time object pose synchronization via motion capture.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based planning with a high-fidelity, parallelized physics simulator offers a robust and generalizable alternative to purely learning-based methods for bimanual manipulation in cluttered, contact-rich tasks.
> - Integrating a Quadratic Program for trajectory smoothing into a sampling-based optimizer effectively enforces joint derivative constraints, allowing for robust exploration with higher noise levels and improving overall trajectory quality.
> - Successful sim-to-real transfer for complex bimanual tasks can be achieved by meticulous environmental synchronization and real-time state updates from external sensing, tightly coupling the physical and simulated worlds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dual-arm manipulation is an active area of robotics research, driven by the goal of achieving human-level dexterity for complex tasks like assembly, collaborative object handling, and interaction with unstructured environments. The ability to coordinate two robotic arms offers capabilities, such as stabilizing large objects and applying opposing forces, that single manipulators cannot achieve.

Historically, the dominant paradigm for bimanual manipulation has been end-to-end learning, encompassing approaches based on imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL). These methods have demonstrated success in learning high-dimensional policies by directly mapping perception inputs to motor commands and by implicitly handling complex contact physics, thereby avoiding explicit modeling of arm-object interactions. However, a significant and widely acknowledged limitation of these learning-based approaches is their difficulty in generalizing to novel scenarios, particularly in cluttered environments. They often require extensive data collection and can exhibit brittleness when faced with variations in object placement, environmental clutter, or task constraints not present in the training data. Furthermore, state-of-the-art IL methods, like those based on diffusion or flow-matching, can have high inference times, and neural network predictions frequently struggle with hard constraints such as collision avoidance.

In contrast to learning-based methods, model-based planning approaches offer an alternative. Previous model-based methods, such as gradient-based trajectory optimization, have been applied to bimanual tasks but are often task-specific and difficult to generalize to complex settings involving obstacles or making/breaking contacts. Sampling-based optimization methods are more general, capable of handling arbitrary tasks if a tractable world model is available for rollout. However, prior sampling-based methods have not been extensively applied to contact-rich tasks like ball-lifting or have been slower than desired for real-time control.

This work positions itself as a robust alternative to purely learning-based methods, specifically addressing their generalization challenges in cluttered, contact-rich bimanual tasks. It proposes a model-based planning framework that leverages a high-fidelity, GPU-accelerated physics simulator. By doing so, the authors aim to overcome the brittleness and generalization limitations of learning-based approaches while achieving real-time performance and facilitating practical sim-to-real transfer, potentially also providing a mechanism for generating synthetic expert data for learning-based systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robust, generalizable, and real-time capable framework for autonomous bimanual manipulation in complex and cluttered environments. This goal is primarily motivated by the identified limitations of current predominant approaches, particularly end-to-end learning-based methods.

The key motivations are detailed as follows:
- **Addressing Generalization Challenges in Cluttered Environments:** Existing end-to-end learning policies often struggle to generalize to novel scenarios, especially when static obstacles or minor environmental variations are introduced. This brittleness severely impedes their deployment in dynamic real-world settings. The authors are motivated to develop an alternative paradigm that can inherently adapt to such variations by dynamically reacting to the current state of the world.
- **Handling Complex Contact Interactions:** Bimanual manipulation frequently involves intricate contact forces between manipulators and objects, as well as between the manipulators themselves and the environment. Explicitly modeling these interactions is challenging, and learning-based methods often bypass it, but this can lead to difficulties when interactions deviate from training data. The paper aims to demonstrate that real-time, model-based planning with a ground-truth physics world model can effectively handle these challenging contact tasks.
- **Achieving Real-time Performance:** For practical applications, robotic manipulation systems must operate in real-time. The authors seek to develop an optimization framework that can rapidly plan and execute complex bimanual motions within stringent time constraints, even on commodity hardware.
- **Enabling Reliable Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Bridging the gap between simulation and real-world deployment remains a significant hurdle in robotics. The research is motivated to establish a clear methodology for successful sim-to-real transfer, leveraging specific features of the chosen physics simulator.

Based on these motivations, the paper outlines four specific contributions (objectives):
1. To introduce a novel sampling-based optimizer capable of solving complex bimanual manipulation tasks.
2. To provide the first demonstration of sampling-based optimization successfully tackling significantly more complicated variants of tasks from the PerAct$^2$ benchmark, which are specifically designed to be challenging for existing learning-based methods due to the presence of obstacles and complex interactions.
3. To present evidence of the method's real-time performance on commodity GPUs and detail a methodology that enables successful sim-to-real transfer.
4. To conduct a rigorous statistical analysis to quantify the sample complexity and robustness of the proposed approach, thereby providing a clear understanding of its performance characteristics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a sampling-based optimization framework that utilizes a GPU-accelerated physics simulator as its predictive world model. This approach is built upon a customized Model Predictive Path Integral Control (MPPI) algorithm, tailored for bimanual manipulation.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The bimanual planning problem is formulated as an optimization over a horizon H, aiming to minimize a cost function $c(\theta_k, \dot{\theta}_k, x_k)$ which encodes task requirements and constraints (e.g., collision avoidance). The system dynamics $\theta_{k+1}, x_{k+1} = f(\theta_k, \dot{\theta}_k, x_k)$ are implicitly defined by a MuJoCo physics engine acting as the world model. Joint motion smoothness is explicitly enforced by bounding derivatives up to the jerk level ($r = \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$). The optimization simultaneously solves for both arm motions, allowing the automatic discovery of coordination strategies.

**B. Sampling-Based Optimizer (Alg. 1):**
The customized MPPI algorithm iteratively refines a distribution of control inputs:
1. **Initialization:** A Gaussian distribution, defined by a mean ($\nu_m$) and covariance ($\Sigma_m$), is initialized to represent the search space for joint velocity inputs.
2. **Sampling:** In each iteration, a batch of $n$ candidate bimanual joint velocity sequences ($\tilde{\dot{\theta}}_j$) is drawn from the current Gaussian distribution.
3. **Quadratic Program (QP) Based Smoothing:** To address potential noisiness and enforce smoothness, these raw samples are projected onto a feasible set defined by joint derivative constraints (up to jerk level). This projection is formulated as a Quadratic Program (QP) (Eq. 2-3), which minimizes the difference between the sampled and projected velocities while satisfying the derivative bounds. This QP is solved for all samples in parallel on GPUs, ensuring smooth and jerk-bounded trajectories and aiding in robust exploration by allowing a higher noise level in the initial covariance matrix.
4. **Parallel Rollouts and Cost Evaluation:** The smoothed joint velocity sequences are then used to perform parallel "rollouts" in the GPU-accelerated MuJoCo (MJX) physics simulator. This simulates the future states of the manipulators and the environment. Each simulated outcome is subsequently evaluated using a task-specific cost function.
5. **Distribution Update:** An "Elite Set" ($n_e$ samples with the lowest costs) is selected. This elite set is then used to update the mean ($\nu_{m+1}$) and covariance ($\Sigma_{m+1}$) of the Gaussian distribution (Eq. 4a-4d), using a learning rate ($\eta$) and a temperature parameter ($\beta$). This process iteratively converges the distribution towards optimal control sequences.
6. **Receding Horizon Control:** After a set number of iterations, the algorithm returns the best joint velocity sequence. The first few steps of this optimal sequence are executed on the manipulators, and the planning process is re-invoked in a receding horizon fashion. The updated $\nu_M$ and $\Sigma_M$ are used to warm-start the next planning cycle.

**C. Task-Specific Cost Functions:**
The method relies on carefully designed algebraic cost functions that encode desired behaviors and constraints for each task phase. Examples of costs provided include:
- **Collision Cost ($C_c$):** A combination of a barrier function to prevent reaching infeasible regions and a conventional penetration penalty. For ball manipulation, collision between manipulators and ball is removed during the move phase to allow for contact forces.
- **Joint Deviation Cost ($C_{\theta}$):** Penalizes deviation from a home joint position.
- **End-effector Alignment Costs:** E.g., Vertical Alignment ($C_z$) for tray moving, and Planar Alignment ($C_{yz}$) for ball lifting and handover, to ensure coordinated end-effector positions.
- **Relative Velocity Cost ($C_v$):** Encourages coordinated motion by forcing relative velocity between end-effectors to be perpendicular to their relative position, especially crucial when holding rigid objects.
- **Position and Orientation Costs:** Phase-specific costs to guide end-effectors and/or objects (tray, ball) to desired grasp locations, target positions, and orientations ($C_{p,pick}, C_{r,pick}, C_{p,tray}, C_{r,tray}$, etc.).
- **End-effector Distance Cost ($C_l$):** Maintains a fixed distance between end-effectors, important for gripping and carrying objects.
These individual costs are combined using phase-dependent weights ($w_{pick}, w_{move}$, etc.) to form a total cost function for each task.

**D. Implementation Details:**
The algorithm was implemented in JAX, leveraging the GPU-accelerated MJX physics engine. A simulation time-step of 0.1s was chosen for balance between accuracy and planning time.

**E. Experimental Setup for Sim-to-Real:**
A high-fidelity sim-to-real transfer was achieved by synchronizing the physical and simulated environments. Two UR5e manipulators with Robotiq grippers were used. An OptiTrack motion capture system provided real-time pose data of dynamic objects (obstacles, ball, tray) to a control laptop. This data was then used to continuously update the Mocap body features within the MuJoCo simulation, ensuring that the simulation accurately reflected the real-world state.

**F. Evaluation Metrics:**
The performance of the approach was quantified using:
- **Success Rate:** Percentage of successful runs out of total experiments, with failure conditions including collisions, task incompletion within 120s, or object drops (for ball-lifting).
- **Computation Time:** Mean and standard deviation of planning time per step.
- **Task Time:** Mean and standard deviation of total time to complete a task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative results from simulations and real-world experiments across three challenging bimanual manipulation tasks.

**A. Qualitative Results:**
- **Task Demonstrations:** The method successfully demonstrated complex bimanual behaviors in obstacle-rich environments for:
 - Lifting and transporting a ball over a barrier.
 - Moving a tray to a goal position while navigating between wall-like obstacles.
 - Performing a hand-over task between two manipulators amidst multiple static obstacles.
- **Real-world Validation:** The hand-over task was successfully demonstrated in a real-world setting, as evidenced by accompanying videos.
- **Comparison with Learning-Based Baseline:** A qualitative comparison was performed with a state-of-the-art learning-based method on a ball-lifting task modified with an obstacle. While the baseline method could partially adapt by navigating around the obstacle, it consistently failed to lift the ball. In contrast, the proposed sampling-based approach successfully lifted and transported the ball while avoiding the obstacle. Over 10 trials with perturbed obstacle positions, the proposed method succeeded 6 times, compared to 2 times for the baseline, demonstrating superior adaptability and robustness in novel, cluttered scenarios. This comparison also indicated the generalizability of the proposed approach to different manipulators (Franka-Panda).

**B. Quantitative Results (Statistical Analysis):**
The method's performance was evaluated over 20 runs for each task, varying the batch size (number of samples in Alg.1) from 250 to 2000.
- **Success Rate:**
 - **Ball lifting and transporting:** Achieved nearly 100% success for batch sizes of 1250 and above.
 - **Tray moving:** Maintained a success rate above 80% up to batch size 500, experienced a drop at 750, but recovered with larger batch sizes.
 - **Handover task:** Showed a similar trend, peaking at 95% success with a batch size of 1750.
 - In general, larger batch sizes correlated with higher success rates across all tasks.
- **Task Time:**
 - For the ball lifting and transporting and tray moving tasks, the task completion time generally decreased with increasing batch sizes, suggesting that more samples allowed for finding more efficient trajectories.
 - The handover task exhibited only marginal improvement in task time with larger batch sizes.
- **Computation Time:**
 - As expected, computation time increased with batch size due to the increased number of parallel simulations.
 - Crucially, the computation time remained below 100ms for all tasks and batch sizes tested, confirming its real-time applicability on commodity GPUs. For instance, the handover task achieved its peak success rate with a computation time just under 60ms.
- **Benchmarking Context:** The achieved success rates are comparable to those reported for the best learning approaches on simpler, free-space variants of the tasks. Furthermore, the computation time is lower than several baselines referenced in.

**C. Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
The experimental setup demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer, facilitated by tightly coupling the real and simulated environments. Real-time object pose data from an OptiTrack system was seamlessly integrated into the MuJoCo simulation, which continuously updated the Mocap body features, thereby synchronizing the virtual and physical workspaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for complex bimanual tasks in challenging, unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing the Generalization Gap:** The work directly challenges the critical limitation of generalization in end-to-end learning methods by demonstrating that model-based planning, coupled with a high-fidelity physics simulator, can effectively solve complex bimanual tasks in novel and cluttered environments. This robustness to environmental variations is crucial for deploying robots in real-world settings that are inherently dynamic and unpredictable.
- **Enhanced Robustness for Contact-Rich Tasks:** The customized MPPI, with its embedded Quadratic Program for jerk-bounded trajectory generation and carefully designed cost functions, enables the system to discover and execute coordinated, collision-free motions, even when intricate contact interactions are involved. This is particularly impactful for tasks requiring fine manipulation and object handling.
- **Practical Viability and Real-Time Operation:** The demonstration of real-time performance (computation times under 100ms) on commodity GPUs makes the proposed framework practically viable for immediate deployment. This efficiency allows for reactive control, which is essential for dynamic environments.
- **Proven Sim-to-Real Transfer Methodology:** The successful implementation of sim-to-real transfer, achieved through meticulous environmental alignment and real-time state synchronization via motion capture, provides a robust pathway for developing and testing complex manipulation strategies in simulation before transferring them to physical robots. This reduces development time and resources.
- **Expanding the Scope of Bimanual Capabilities:** By tackling significantly more challenging variants of benchmark tasks (e.g., ball lifting through an obstacle course), the research pushes the boundaries of what bimanual robots can achieve autonomously, moving beyond free-space manipulation to scenarios with tight constraints and interactions.
- **Foundation for Hybrid AI Approaches:** The generated optimal trajectories from this model-based framework offer a valuable source of "expert data." This opens avenues for future work, such as distilling these trajectories into policy networks for warm-starting optimizers in novel scenarios or integrating the approach into hybrid Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) frameworks. Such hybrid approaches could combine the planning capabilities of model-based methods with the learning power of model-free reinforcement learning, potentially leading to even more advanced and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Generalizability to Diverse Hardware:** The demonstrated adaptability of the method to different manipulator types (UR5e and Franka-Panda) suggests a broad applicability across various robotic platforms.

In conclusion, this work offers a compelling alternative to prevalent learning-based approaches for bimanual manipulation, particularly excelling in generalization, robustness, and real-time performance within cluttered environments. Its contributions lay a foundation for more capable and practical robotic systems in diverse applications.
