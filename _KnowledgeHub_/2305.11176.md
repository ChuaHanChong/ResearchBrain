# Instruct2Act: Mapping Multi-modality Instructions to Robotic Actions with Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Instruct2Act is a zero-shot framework that prompts an LLM to write Python programs for tabletop manipulation. The programs call perception APIs (SAM for segmentation, CLIP for retrieval) and robot skill primitives. One retrieval design handles text, image and pointing instructions. With no training, it matches the learning-based VIMA at 84.4% average success on VIMABench.

## Problem

- Code-as-policy approaches like CaP depend on what perception APIs offer and struggle with longer, complex commands because generated policy code demands high precision.
- Learning-based instruction-following policies need large task-specific datasets and training, and they degrade under distribution shift such as novel objects, placements or tasks.
- Off-the-shelf vision foundation models used zero-shot give incomplete or incorrect masks (shadows, tiny fragments, redundant masks), and prior systems rarely unify language, visual and pointing instructions.

## Method

- ==LLM code generation==: prompts combine third-party library imports, APIs organized by robot-system hierarchy, and full-logic ==in-context examples== plus 'think step by step'. The LLM outputs one main() Python function.
- Perception via ==SAM== plus ==CLIP== APIs: SAM masks become crops, CLIP embeddings are matched to text or image queries. Gray-threshold and closing pre-processing, plus opening, size filtering and ==NMS== post-processing, clean the masks.
- ==Unified instruction modality manager==: a shared CLIP retrieval handles text, image-crop and pointing queries (cursor clicks prompt SAM). ==Hungarian matching== aligns scene-level targets. An ==environment cache== stores crops and coordinate mappings for PickPlace actions.

## Results

- On VIMABench L1, Instruct2Act with multimodal instructions averages **84.4%**, equal to VIMA-20M and far above DT, Gato and Flamingo (about **41-46%**). It wins Task 05 (**72.0** vs **59.5**) and Task 17 (**85.2** vs **47.5**).
- Processing modules matter: with none, success is **51.0%**. Mask post-processing lifts it to **83.0%**, and adding image pre-processing reaches **84.1%**. Pre-processing alone gives **50.0%**, no gain.
- Larger foundation models help: SAM Base/Large/Huge scores **75.1/82.7/84.1%** success. CLIP B-16/L-14/H-14 scores **70.0/76.5/84.1%**. Performance shows minimal fluctuation across L1-L3 generalization levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Composing frozen foundation models through readable APIs removes data collection and training, and any upgrade to the perception models raises action accuracy at no extra cost.
> - Prompt parts complement each other: API definitions give flexibility and reasoning, while in-context examples give structure. Together they yield accurate, human-readable code. Either alone yields errors or missing logic.
> - Zero-shot perception needs cheap domain-specific cleanup: mask post-processing gave the biggest gain (51% to 83%). Pre-processing with untuned parameters can hurt, so raw foundation-model outputs should not be trusted blindly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Instruct2Act" is situated at the forefront of several rapidly evolving research areas: Large Language Models (LLMs), visual foundation models, and language-driven robotics. The confluence of these fields has given rise to a new paradigm where high-level human instructions can be directly translated into robotic actions.

Historically, developing general-purpose robotic systems has been a significant challenge due to the complexities of perception, planning, and control in dynamic environments. Traditional approaches often required extensive feature engineering, explicit programming for each task, or vast amounts of task-specific training data for learning-based policies. The emergence of LLMs (e.g., GPT-3, LLaMA, ChatGPT) revolutionized natural language understanding and generation, showcasing remarkable zero-shot generalization. Concurrently, powerful visual foundation models like CLIP (for image-text alignment) and SAM (Segment Anything Model for universal segmentation) have provided robust, generalized perception capabilities without task-specific fine-tuning.

Prior work has explored bridging LLMs with visual models (e.g., Visual ChatGPT, VISPROG, ViperGPT) for visual reasoning and image manipulation, and some have ventured into language-driven robotics. Projects like CLIPORT and Perceiver-Actor incorporated CLIP for semantic understanding in manipulation. Say-Can demonstrated zero-shot concept grounding using PaLM. More recently, CaP (Code as Policies) explored generating robot-centric policy code directly from LLMs, while PaLM-E presented an embodied multimodal language model. VIMA introduced a large-scale benchmark for multimodal prompt-conditioned robot manipulation, often relying on extensive training.

Instruct2Act distinguishes itself by explicitly aiming for a **general-purpose, zero-shot robotic system** that *leverages off-the-shelf foundation models* (SAM and CLIP) for perception and *LLMs for generating executable Python programs* that orchestrate these models and robotic primitives. Unlike many learning-based policies (e.g., VIMA), Instruct2Act bypasses the need for large-scale robot interaction data for training or fine-tuning. Instead, it frames the problem as LLM-driven code generation, integrating pre-trained vision models as modular APIs. This approach offers a unique balance: utilizing the high-level reasoning and generalization of LLMs with the robust, pre-trained perceptual capabilities of visual foundation models, while minimizing the cost and complexity of data collection and model training inherent in many end-to-end learning systems. It seeks to overcome the limitations of prior code-generation approaches, such as CaP, by focusing on generating decision-making actions rather than direct, potentially error-prone, low-level policy codes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of Instruct2Act is to develop a flexible and general-purpose robotic manipulation system that can interpret diverse multi-modal instructions (natural language and visual) and translate them into sequential robotic actions. The authors explicitly ask: "Could we build a ChatGPT-like robotic systems that support robotic manipulation, visual goal-reaching, and visual reasoning?" This encapsulates their ambition to create a highly autonomous and adaptable robotic agent.

The key motivations driving this research are:
- **Leveraging Foundation Model Capabilities:** Inspired by the unprecedented progress of LLMs in natural language understanding and zero-shot generalization, and the robust perception capabilities of visual foundation models like SAM and CLIP, the authors seek to combine these strengths for robotics.
- **Addressing Challenges in General-Purpose Robotics:** Developing a robot capable of executing complex tasks in dynamic environments requires sophisticated perception, planning, and control. Integrating these technologies without task-specific training remains a significant hurdle.
- **Reducing Data and Training Overhead:** Existing learning-based robotic policies often necessitate extensive data collection and training processes, which are costly and time-consuming. Instruct2Act aims to provide a zero-shot solution that requires minimal adaptation and no fine-tuning of the core models.
- **Enhancing Flexibility and Interpretability:** The framework strives for a system that is flexible in accommodating various instruction modalities and task demands. By generating human-readable Python code, the approach also aims to increase the interpretability of the robot's decision-making process, a crucial aspect for debugging and trust.
- **Improving Accuracy in Complex Tasks:** The paper notes that prior LLM-driven approaches (like CaP) sometimes struggle with longer and more complex commands due to the high precision requirements of direct policy code. Instruct2Act attempts to mitigate this by generating intermediate "decision-making actions" that orchestrate foundation models, thereby reducing the direct burden on the LLM for low-level control precision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Instruct2Act employs a neuro-symbolic approach, using a Large Language Model (LLM) as a high-level planner and code generator, which then invokes off-the-shelf visual foundation models and robotic primitive APIs to execute tasks. The core methodology can be broken down into several interconnected components:

#### 3.1. LLM as the Robotic Driver

The LLM (primarily OpenAI's `text-davinci-003` and LLaMA-Adapter for verification) is central to Instruct2Act. It acts as a "robotic driver" by generating Python programs that define the perception, planning, and action loop.
- **Prompt Engineering:** To guide the LLM, a meticulously designed prompt is used. This prompt includes:
 - **Third-party Library Imports:** Information about standard Python libraries (e.g., `numpy`, `PIL`) that might be needed for calculations.
 - **API Definitions:** A comprehensive set of API definitions, explicitly categorized by their functionality (e.g., File IO, Core Modules -> Perception/Action, Connect to Robotic Hardware). These APIs abstract complex functionalities like image acquisition, object segmentation, classification, coordinate mapping, and robot actuation (e.g., `GetObsImage()`, `SAM()`, `CLIPRetrieval()`, `Pixel2Loc()`, `PickPlace()`, `RobotExecution()`).
 - **In-Context Examples:** A few full-logical code examples demonstrating how these APIs can be combined to solve specific robotic tasks (e.g., "Put X into Y," "Rotate Z degrees," "Rearrange to scene"). These examples serve to teach the LLM the desired output format and logical flow.
 - **Chain-of-Thought Prompting:** The prompt includes "Think step by step" to encourage better reasoning and "only implement the `main()` function" to reduce verbose output.
- **Code Generation and Execution:** Given a task instruction, the LLM generates a Python function in string format. This code is then executed by a Python interpreter. The system can detect errors (e.g., missing brackets) and prompt the LLM for regeneration.
- **Advantages:** This LLM-driven approach offers flexibility (prompts can be adjusted), zero-shot capability (no training data needed, direct use of foundation models), and interpretability (human-readable Python code).

#### 3.2. Perception with Off-the-Shelf Foundation Models

For understanding the environment, Instruct2Act leverages two powerful visual foundation models:
- **Segment Anything Model (SAM):** Accessed via the `SAM()` API, it performs universal object segmentation on the input image, generating masks for all potential objects.
- **CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training):** Accessed via the `CLIPRetrieval()` API, it is used for classifying and identifying objects. After SAM provides object masks, `ImageCrop()` extracts individual object crops. These crops are then encoded by CLIP's image encoder, and their features are compared with text queries (from language instructions) or image queries (from visual instructions) to localize the intended object.

To mitigate issues arising from the zero-shot application of these models (e.g., incompleteness, incorrectness, shadows), Instruct2Act incorporates **processing modules**:
- **Image Pre-processing:** A gray threshold filter followed by a morphological closing operation is applied to the input image to reduce the effect of shadows, which can confuse segmentation.
- **Mask Post-processing:** After SAM's output, morphological opening (dilation then erosion) is applied to eliminate small holes or unconnected gaps within masks. Filters based on mask size remove irrelevant outputs (e.g., tables or background patterns), and Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) reduces redundant masks for a single object.

#### 3.3. Flexible Instruction Modality Manager

Instruct2Act is designed to handle various input modalities:
- **Pure-Language Instruction:** The LLM directly interprets descriptive sentences (e.g., "Put the green and purple polka dot block into the green container"). CLIP's text encoder converts object descriptions into feature vectors for retrieval.
- **Language-Visual Instruction:** Instructions include placeholders for images (e.g., "Put <dragged_obj> into <base_obj>"). The LLM identifies these placeholders, and the corresponding image crops (stored in an environment cache) are sent to CLIP's image encoder for object retrieval. For scene-level understanding, the Hungarian algorithm matches objects between target and observed scenes.
- **Pointing-Language Enhanced Instruction:** When language or image descriptions are insufficient, users can click on target objects in the observation image. These click points serve as "point prompts" for SAM, guiding its segmentation.

#### 3.4. Action Construction and Execution

Once objects are perceived and identified, their pixel locations are mapped to robot coordinates using a pre-defined transformation matrix (`Pixel2Loc()`). The LLM then identifies the appropriate robot action (e.g., `PickPlace()`, `RearrangeActions()`) based on the instruction and context. These actions are parameterized with the calculated locations and executed by a low-level controller via the `RobotExecution()` API.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Instruct2Act was conducted on six representative meta-tasks from the VIMABench simulator, covering simple object manipulation, visual goal-reaching, and visual reasoning. The tasks were assessed across three generalization levels (L1 placement, L2 combinatorial, L3 novel object) to test the framework's robustness to varying distributions.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison

- **Zero-shot Superiority:** Instruct2Act, operating in a zero-shot manner without fine-tuning, achieved comparable average performance to state-of-the-art *learning-based* methods like VIMA-20M (84.4% success rate for Instruct2Act-Multi vs. 84.4% for VIMA-20M on L1 tasks).
- **Multi-step Task Advantage:** For complex, multi-step tasks (e.g., Task 05: Rearrange then restore; Task 17: Pick in order then restore), Instruct2Act significantly outperformed VIMA-20M. For instance, on Task 17, Instruct2Act-Multi achieved 85.2% compared to VIMA-20M's 47.5%. This is attributed to the strong generalization of large visual models and the powerful reasoning capacity of LLMs.
- **Modality Impact:** Multimodal instructions generally led to better performance than single-modal (pure language) instructions, providing more comprehensive information to the model. Instruct2Act-Multi achieved 84.4% average success rate on L1 tasks, while Instruct2Act-Single scored 86.7% for Task 01 and 94.6% for Task 03.
- **Generalization Across Levels:** The method consistently achieved positive results across L1, L2, and L3 generalization tasks, exhibiting less performance fluctuation compared to other baselines. This indicates better distribution shifting robustness due to the integration of foundation models.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Prompt Elements:**
 - **API Definitions Only:** The LLM could generate reasonable logic but often produced unnecessary actions or incomplete code (e.g., missing return statements). However, it demonstrated surprising reasoning, like inferring the need for `SaveFailureImage()` even when not explicitly in examples.
 - **In-Context Examples Only:** The LLM generated highly structured code, similar to examples, but lacked functional understanding, leading to generic comments or incorrect inferences (e.g., "SAM was mistakenly inferred as Semantic Affinity Module"). It couldn't generate new logic beyond examples.
 - **Combined (API Definitions + In-Context Examples):** This combination yielded the most accurate, human-readable, and semantically rich Python code, demonstrating the synergistic effect of both prompt components. API style prompts provided flexibility and reasoning, while in-context examples provided structure.
- **Processing Modules:** The image pre-processing and mask post-processing modules were critical.
 - Without any processing, the success rate dropped significantly to 51.0%.
 - Mask post-processing alone boosted the average success rate to 83.0%.
 - Adding image pre-processing further improved it to 84.1%.
 - Image pre-processing alone showed no improvement, suggesting the importance of both components and potentially optimized parameters.

#### 4.3. Additional Foundational Models & LLM Comparison

- **Larger Foundation Models:** Experiments replacing SAM's backbone (Base, Large, Huge) and CLIP's backbone (B-16, L-14, H-14) consistently showed that larger, more powerful foundation models led to better performance, indicating scalability and potential for future improvements.
- **LLaMA-Adapter vs. ChatGPT:** While ChatGPT (`text-davinci-003`) was used for large-scale experiments, Instruct2Act also demonstrated plausible performance with the open-source LLaMA-Adapter (72.5% SR on Task 01 initially, improvable to 85.5% with basic filtering and multiple trials), suggesting the approach is not solely reliant on commercial LLMs.

#### 4.4. Flexibility and Robustness

Instruct2Act exhibited strong flexibility and robustness:
- **Human Intervention:** The LLM could interpret instructions like "I cancel this task. Stop!" or "That instruction is wrong, use this one," demonstrating adaptive behavior.
- **Missing Characteristics/Synonym Replacement:** It maintained grounding ability even with minor misspellings or missing object characteristics and handled synonym replacements (e.g., "Rotate" with "Spin").
- **Third-party Library Extension:** The LLM successfully integrated `numpy` to convert degrees to radians for robot actions, showcasing its ability to leverage external libraries for calculations, given appropriate context in the prompt.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Instruct2Act represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent, adaptable, and general-purpose robotic systems. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable **zero-shot approach** for complex robotic manipulation tasks by effectively orchestrating off-the-shelf, pre-trained foundation models with Large Language Models.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By eliminating the need for extensive task-specific data collection and model fine-tuning, Instruct2Act dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for developing new robotic capabilities. Any improvements in underlying foundation models (LLMs, SAM, CLIP) automatically benefit the system without additional cost.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The framework's ability to perform well across various tasks and generalization levels (L1, L2, L3) suggests a strong capacity for open-vocabulary and novel object handling, which is crucial for real-world deployment where unforeseen scenarios are common.
- **Flexible Human-Robot Interaction:** The support for multi-modal instructions (pure language, language-visual, pointing-language) makes human-robot interaction more intuitive and adaptable to diverse user preferences and task complexities.
- **Interpretability:** The LLM's output in the form of human-readable Python code offers a higher degree of transparency compared to black-box end-to-end neural policies, aiding in debugging and building trust in autonomous systems.
- **Robustness to Variations:** The demonstrated robustness to variations in instructions (e.g., misspellings, synonyms, human intervention) highlights the potential for more forgiving and user-friendly robotic interfaces.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its strengths, Instruct2Act has notable limitations:
- **High Computational Cost:** Relying on multiple large foundation models (LLM, SAM, CLIP) incurs significant computational overhead, making real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms challenging.
- **Limited Action Primitives:** The current system is constrained by the basic action primitives (e.g., Pick and Place) provided by the VIMABench simulator. Expanding to more intricate and continuous actions would require extending the API set.
- **Simulation-Only Evaluation:** The method has only been validated in a simulation environment. Transitioning to real-world applications would introduce new complexities, such as noise in perception, precise control in physical environments, and safety considerations.

Future work will likely focus on addressing these limitations, including optimizing the computational efficiency of foundation model inference, expanding the repertoire of robotic skills and primitives, and, critically, validating the approach in real-world robotic setups. The success of Instruct2Act underscores the immense potential of integrating powerful generative AI with embodied systems, paving the way for truly intelligent and versatile robots.
