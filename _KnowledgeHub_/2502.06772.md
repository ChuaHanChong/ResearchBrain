# ReasonFlux: Hierarchical LLM Reasoning via Scaling Thought Templates

> [!summary] Summary
> ReasonFlux presents a hierarchical framework for Large Language Model reasoning that leverages a structured thought template library and hierarchical reinforcement learning to plan optimal reasoning paths. The approach achieved 91.2% accuracy on the MATH benchmark, outperforming OpenAI o1-preview by 6.7%, and 56.7% accuracy on AIME 2024, surpassing o1-preview by 27%, while demonstrating cost-efficient training with 8 GPUs.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex multi-step reasoning tasks like mathematical Olympiads, requiring fine-grained search and overcoming token sampling randomness.
- Existing deliberate search (e.g., Tree of Thoughts) and reward-model-guided methods are computationally expensive, lack generalizability, and face exploration-exploitation trade-offs.
- Current template-based reasoning methods struggle to integrate multiple knowledge pieces effectively and face scalability issues with large template libraries due to simple retrieval mechanisms.

## Method

- A structured and compact ==thought template library== of approximately **500** high-level mathematical reasoning strategies was constructed, meticulously organized with metadata for precise retrieval.
- ==Hierarchical reinforcement learning== was employed to train a =="navigator" LLM== to plan optimal, generalizable sequences of thought templates (trajectories) based on problem analysis.
- An ==adaptive inference scaling system== was designed, allowing for multi-round, iterative interaction between the navigator and an inference LLM to dynamically select and instantiate templates, refining reasoning steps based on feedback.

## Results

- ReasonFlux-32B achieved **91.2%** accuracy on the MATH benchmark, outperforming OpenAI o1-preview by **6.7%**, and **56.7%** accuracy on AIME 2024, surpassing o1-preview by **27%**.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization across diverse mathematical benchmarks, including OlympiadBench and Chinese College Entrance Mathematics Exam, consistently outperforming comparable models.
- ReasonFlux-32B was trained efficiently using only **8** NVIDIA A100 GPUs and generates interpretable reasoning structures via its planned template trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structuring a knowledge base into high-level thought templates with detailed metadata allows for more precise and scalable retrieval, improving reasoning efficiency.
> - Hierarchical reinforcement learning can effectively train LLMs to plan optimal sequences of abstract reasoning steps (template trajectories), guiding the problem-solving process.
> - Dynamically adapting the depth and breadth of reasoning (e.g., number of templates, interaction rounds) based on problem complexity leads to a superior exploration-exploitation trade-off during inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have led to remarkable progress in complex reasoning tasks, with models demonstrating human-level or even superhuman performance in certain domains. Models like OpenAI's O1, Google's Gemini-2.0, and DeepSeek-V3 are at the forefront, often employing "slower, more deliberate" thought processes to enhance accuracy by leveraging increased inference time. However, the paper highlights that extremely challenging tasks, such as those found in mathematical Olympiads (e.g., AIME, OlympiadBench) and complex code generation (LiveCodeBench), continue to pose significant challenges. These problems demand a more fine-grained search through vast solution spaces and intricate multi-step reasoning, where the inherent randomness of token sampling can hinder efficiency.

Current research efforts to enhance LLM reasoning on such complex problems broadly fall into two categories:
- **Deliberate Search Methods:** Approaches like Tree of Thoughts (ToT) and Graph of Thoughts (GoT) allow LLMs to explore multiple reasoning paths and self-evaluate. While effective, they are computationally intensive, especially with many reasoning steps or large search spaces, and often rely on manually designed search strategies, limiting their generalization.
- **Reward-Model-Guided Methods:** These leverage reward models (Outcome Reward Models - ORM, Process Reward Models - PRM) to assess reasoning quality. Methods like Best-of-N and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) use these models to guide the search for optimal paths. Similar to deliberate search methods, they often incur high computational costs and rely on instance-level or step-level rewards, which may not capture higher-level cognitive processes. They also struggle with the exploration-exploitation trade-off during inference scaling.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** This paradigm aims to mitigate hallucinations and enhance factual accuracy by retrieving relevant information from external knowledge sources. Recent works like Buffer of Thought (BoT) and SuperCorrect have explored the use of "thought templates" within RAG to guide reasoning. However, existing template-based methods may struggle with complex problems requiring the integration of multiple templates or diverse information pieces, and face scalability challenges as template libraries grow due to reliance on simple embedding similarity search.

ReasonFlux directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel hierarchical reasoning framework that goes beyond simple retrieval or exhaustive search. It aims to achieve more efficient and precise search of reasoning paths by:
1. **Structuring Knowledge:** Instead of unstructured text, it uses a carefully curated and structured thought template library, allowing for more targeted retrieval.
2. **Hierarchical Control:** It employs a hierarchical reinforcement learning approach to learn optimal "template trajectories," essentially planning a high-level reasoning strategy rather than optimizing individual tokens or steps. This simplifies the search space.
3. **Adaptive Inference Scaling:** It designs a new inference system that dynamically retrieves and instantiates templates, allowing for adaptive scaling based on problem complexity and balancing exploration and exploitation more effectively.

By focusing on high-level thought processes and their systematic application, ReasonFlux positions itself as a significant advancement over existing methods, promising to unlock superior performance on highly challenging reasoning tasks while maintaining computational efficiency and offering greater interpretability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ReasonFlux stems from the observed limitations of current large language models (LLMs) when tackling exceptionally complex reasoning problems, particularly in mathematics. While frontier LLMs have made strides in emulating deliberate thought, they still struggle with tasks demanding a fine-grained, structured search through vast solution spaces and delicate, multi-step logical progression. Existing inference-time strategies, such as Tree of Thoughts or Monte Carlo Tree Search, are often computationally expensive and lack generalizability due to their reliance on manual design or instance/step-level rewards. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods, while promising, face challenges in effectively integrating multiple knowledge pieces and scaling template-based reasoning for intricate problems.

Against this backdrop, ReasonFlux sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Optimize Reasoning Search Space:** To move beyond exhaustive or random search, the framework aims to structure and simplify the search for optimal reasoning paths, making the process more efficient and targeted.
2. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Mathematical Reasoning Capabilities:** The central goal is to significantly enhance LLMs' ability to solve complex mathematical problems, specifically aiming to surpass the performance of powerful, larger proprietary models like OpenAI o1-preview and DeepSeek V3 on challenging benchmarks like MATH and AIME.
3. **Develop a Cost-Efficient Solution:** A practical objective is to achieve high performance with relatively modest computational resources, as demonstrated by training the ReasonFlux-32B model with only 8 GPUs. This addresses the accessibility and scalability concerns associated with training and deploying extremely large models.
4. **Introduce a Structured and Generic Thought Template Library:** To provide a robust foundation for reasoning, the project aims to build a comprehensive library of high-level thought templates that can generalize across similar or relevant problem types.
5. **Enable Hierarchical Learning for Strategic Planning:** The framework seeks to empower a base LLM to learn and plan optimal "template trajectories" for complex problems, moving from abstract problem analysis to a sequence of high-level reasoning steps. This involves training the model to "navigate" the solution process.
6. **Design an Adaptive Inference Scaling System:** To ensure efficient and robust problem-solving, an objective is to create a system that can dynamically select and instantiate relevant thought templates during inference, thereby achieving a better exploration-exploitation trade-off.
7. **Promote Explainable Reasoning:** A crucial objective is to generate reasoning structures that are more transparent and understandable than those produced by black-box LLMs, enhancing trust and enabling easier analysis of problem-solving paths.

In essence, ReasonFlux is motivated by the need for a more intelligent, structured, and resource-efficient approach to complex reasoning, aiming to bridge the gap between LLMs' raw computational power and their ability to strategically navigate challenging problem spaces.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReasonFlux introduces a novel hierarchical LLM reasoning framework built upon three core innovations: a structured template library, hierarchical reinforcement learning for trajectory optimization, and an adaptive inference scaling system.

#### 3.1. Constructing Structured Thought Template Library

Recognizing the limitations of unstructured RAG, ReasonFlux begins by creating a **structured and compact thought template library** (`D_temp`).
- **Content:** This library contains approximately 500 high-level thought templates, curated from diverse and challenging mathematical reasoning problems found in datasets like MATH and self-curated Chinese high-school competition-level data.
- **Creation:** Gemini-2.0, a powerful LLM, was used to summarize and extract high-level problem-solving strategies and common patterns from solutions.
- **Structure:** Each template (`T_i`) is meticulously structured with specific metadata and content fields:
 - `T_nam`: Name of the template (e.g., "Trigonometric Substitution with Sum-to-Product Identities").
 - `T_tag`: Set of keywords for retrieval (e.g., {"Substitution Method", "Trigonometric Substitution"}).
 - `T_des`: Description of the underlying principle and applicable scenarios.
 - `T_sco`: Defines the scope or problem types it addresses.
 - `T_a`: Sequence of detailed application steps.
 - `T_exa`: Set of examples demonstrating its application.
- **Purpose:** This structured organization, combined with rich metadata, enables more precise and targeted retrieval compared to traditional embedding similarity searches on unstructured text, addressing scalability and relevance issues.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning on Thought Template Trajectory

To effectively utilize the template library, ReasonFlux employs a hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) process to train a "navigator" LLM.
- **Structure-based Fine-tuning:** A base LLM (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, denoted `π`) is initially fine-tuned on a knowledge-intensive dataset (`D_train`, 15K samples derived from `D_temp`). The objective is to maximize the likelihood of generating the correct description and scope given a template's name and tags. This process (`L_struct`) instills a foundational understanding of the templates' structure and utility, resulting in `π_struct`.
- **Preference Learning on Thought Template Trajectory:**
 - `π_struct` acts as a "navigator," analyzing an input problem (`x`) to abstract its core concepts.
 - Based on this analysis, it configures a "thought template trajectory" (`T_traj`), which is a sequence of high-level reasoning steps, each associated with a specific template from the library.
 - An independent "inference LLM" (`π_inf`, also Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) then instantiates these retrieved templates with problem-specific details to solve a series of simpler sub-problems.
 - **Reward Signal:** The quality of a generated `T_traj` is measured by a "trajectory reward" (`R(T_traj)`), defined as the average accuracy achieved by `π_inf` when guided by `T_traj` across a set of problems similar to the original input (`X_sim`). This rewards generalizable trajectories.
 - **Optimization:** This reward signal is used to construct optimization pairs (`D_pair`), where `R(T+traj) > R(T-traj)`. A preference learning loss function (`L_TTR`) is then used to further refine `π_struct` (now ReasonFlux), enabling it to learn how to plan optimal and generalizable template trajectories.

#### 3.3. Inference Scaling with Scaling Thought Templates

After the hierarchical RL process, the optimized `π_struct` becomes ReasonFlux. This model then operates within a novel **inference scaling system** through a multi-round, iterative interplay with `π_inf`.
- **Problem Analysis and Trajectory Configuration:** Given an input problem `x`, ReasonFlux analyzes it to extract core mathematical concepts (`a(x)`) and configures an optimal template trajectory (`T*_traj`), a dynamic plan consisting of a sequence of steps, each linked to a specific template name and tags.
- **Template Retrieval:** ReasonFlux uses these template names and tags to efficiently retrieve the most relevant structured templates (`T_rag`) from `D_temp`.
- **Instantiated Reasoning:** ReasonFlux instructs `π_inf` to instantiate each step in `T*_traj` with its corresponding retrieved template and problem-specific details, generating concrete reasoning steps (`ŝ_i`).
- **Iterative Feedback and Refinement:** The instantiated step `ŝ_i` is then evaluated and analyzed by ReasonFlux. Based on this feedback (`δ_i`), ReasonFlux can refine the current trajectory (`T*_traj`), potentially adjusting subsequent steps or retrieving alternative templates. This iterative loop mimics human problem-solving, where intermediate results inform further planning.

This hierarchical and iterative approach allows ReasonFlux to dynamically adapt its reasoning strategy to problem complexity, transcending the limitations of traditional inference methods and achieving a more balanced exploration-exploitation trade-off.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReasonFlux-32B demonstrates significant advancements in mathematical reasoning capabilities, establishing new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on several challenging benchmarks. The key findings are:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance in Mathematical Reasoning:**
 - **MATH Benchmark:** Achieved an accuracy of 91.2%, surpassing powerful proprietary models like OpenAI o1-preview (85.5%) by 6.7% and current SOTA open-source LLMs in its parameter range.
 - **AIME 2024 Benchmark:** Demonstrated extraordinary reasoning with 56.7% accuracy, significantly outperforming o1-preview (44.6%) by 27% and DeepSeek-V3 (39.2%) by 45%. Notably, it matched the performance of the proprietary OpenAI o1-mini.
 - **AMC 2023 Benchmark:** Maintained a top-tier position with 85.0% accuracy, outperforming other open-source LLMs and achieving performance comparable to proprietary models.
 - **Generalization Across Diverse Benchmarks:** ReasonFlux-32B also showed strong performance on OlympiadBench (63.3%, surpassing DeepSeek-V3 by 14%) and the Chinese College Entrance Mathematics Exam (Gaokao En 2023) (83.6%, surpassing o1-mini by 7%). This consistent performance, despite using a template library primarily constructed from publicly available datasets, highlights its robust generalization ability.

- **Cost-Efficient Training:** The ReasonFlux-32B model, despite achieving SOTA results, was trained with only 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, indicating remarkable cost-efficiency compared to the vast resources typically required for training and fine-tuning high-performing LLMs.

- **Generalization to Different Base Models:** The ReasonFlux framework consistently showed significant improvements across various base models (1.5B, 7B, and 32B parameter levels). This indicates that the proposed hierarchical reasoning and template-augmented approach is broadly applicable and can enhance the reasoning accuracy of diverse LLMs, often allowing a smaller ReasonFlux model to outperform a larger base model.

- **High Generalization Ability of Structured Template Library:** Experiments on the MATH benchmark demonstrated that using the structured template library as in-context examples significantly improves the reasoning accuracy of different base LLMs compared to direct reasoning (e.g., +27.5% for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). This validates the quality and effectiveness of the curated templates, enabling smaller LLMs to even surpass larger models in direct reasoning mode.

- **Explainable Reasoning Structures:** The planned template trajectories generated by ReasonFlux provide more interpretable and explainable reasoning structures compared to other powerful models like DeepSeek-R1 and o3-mini. This transparency aids in understanding the model's problem-solving process.

- **Adaptive Inference Scaling Laws:** ReasonFlux adaptively scales the number of retrieved templates and the rounds of interaction (interplay) between the planning and inference LLMs with increasing problem complexity. This demonstrates its ability to dynamically allocate computational resources and adjust its reasoning depth based on the perceived difficulty of the input problem.

- **Superior Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** When compared to traditional reasoning strategies like Best-of-N and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), ReasonFlux maintained a consistently lower and more stable "exploration-exploitation cost" across varying levels of problem difficulty. This indicates that its structured template library and hierarchical reasoning effectively refine the search space, leading to more efficient identification of correct reasoning paths and a better balance between exploring new solutions and exploiting known strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReasonFlux represents a significant leap forward in empowering Large Language Models with enhanced reasoning capabilities, particularly for complex mathematical problems. Its impact is multifaceted:

- **Pushing Performance Boundaries with Efficiency:** By achieving new state-of-the-art results on challenging mathematical benchmarks like MATH and AIME, ReasonFlux demonstrates that it's possible to reach top-tier performance with significantly fewer parameters (32B) and training resources (8 GPUs) compared to some proprietary frontier models. This is crucial for democratizing access to high-performance AI reasoning and reducing the environmental and financial costs associated with large model development.

- **Novel Paradigm for LLM Reasoning:** The introduction of hierarchical LLM reasoning via scaling thought templates is a novel conceptual contribution. It moves beyond purely auto-regressive generation or brute-force search methods, proposing a more "human-like" approach that involves strategic planning at a high level (template trajectories) and iterative refinement through interaction between a planner and an executor LLM. This could inspire new architectural designs and training methodologies for reasoning-focused LLMs.

- **Structured Knowledge Integration:** The emphasis on a meticulously structured and generic thought template library highlights the power of organized knowledge for LLMs. This approach offers a more precise and scalable alternative to unstructured RAG, potentially leading to breakthroughs in how external knowledge is curated, retrieved, and applied in complex AI systems.

- **Improved Interpretability and Trust:** The generation of explainable reasoning structures through planned template trajectories is a vital step towards more transparent and trustworthy AI. Understanding "how" an LLM arrives at a solution, rather than just the solution itself, is critical for debugging, education, and deployment in sensitive domains.

- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The consistent performance across diverse math benchmarks (competition, Olympiad, standardized exams) and the ability to generalize across different base models underscore the robustness and wide applicability of the ReasonFlux framework. This suggests its potential beyond mathematics to other domains requiring structured, multi-step reasoning, such as scientific discovery, engineering problem-solving, or complex code generation.

- **Better Resource Management (Exploration-Exploitation):** The demonstrated superior exploration-exploitation trade-off signifies a more intelligent and efficient use of computational resources during inference. This capability to adaptively scale reasoning depth based on problem complexity is key for practical deployment, ensuring high accuracy without unnecessary computational overhead.

In sum, ReasonFlux lays a foundational stone for the next generation of reasoning-capable LLMs. By combining structured knowledge, hierarchical learning, and adaptive inference, it offers a blueprint for building more intelligent, efficient, generalizable, and transparent AI systems capable of tackling the most challenging intellectual problems. Its impact could extend to diverse fields, accelerating scientific discovery, automating complex problem-solving, and fostering a deeper understanding of AI's cognitive processes.
