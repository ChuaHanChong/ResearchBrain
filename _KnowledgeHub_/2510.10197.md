# Don't Just Fine-tune the Agent, Tune the Environment

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces ENVIRONMENT TUNING, a training paradigm that enhances large language model (LLM) agents for complex, multi-turn tool-use tasks by tuning the learning environment rather than solely the agent. It significantly improves agent performance on in-distribution benchmarks (e.g., boosting Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct from 7.00% to 36.92% on BFCL V3) and demonstrates robust generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and direct reinforcement learning approaches.

## Problem

- Developing LLM agents for complex, multi-turn tool-use tasks faces data scarcity, as high-quality human-annotated datasets are rare and labor-intensive to create.
- Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods often lead to agents that overfit static data and fail to generalize effectively to out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) struggles with multi-turn tasks due to complex environments, the 'cold-start' problem, long interaction chains, sparse rewards, and training instability.

## Method

- ENVIRONMENT TUNING orchestrates agent learning through a four-stage ==structured curriculum== that progressively builds skills from syntactic correctness to advanced problem-solving.
- Actionable Environment Augmentation modifies the environment's feedback, transforming generic error messages into detailed, corrective hints to guide the agent during exploration.
- A Fine-Grained Progress Reward provides dense, turn-by-turn learning signals, calculated as the average success rate across all turns, to address the sparse reward problem in long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- On the BFCL V3 multi-turn benchmark, ENVIRONMENT TUNING boosted Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct from **7.00%** to **36.92%**, surpassing proprietary models like Gemini 2.5 Pro and demonstrating superior performance over direct RL baselines.
- The method substantially enhanced out-of-distribution generalization, with Llama-3.1-**8B**-Instruct's Web Search score improving from **1.00%** to **15.00%** on BFCL V4, while SFT baselines often exhibited performance collapse.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of each component: the structured curriculum increased performance by **19.50%** over direct GRPO, actionable environment augmentation provided over **20%** improvement in challenging scenarios, and fine-grained rewards enabled stable learning where binary rewards led to training failure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tuning the learning environment through structured feedback and progressive curricula is more effective for training robust LLM agents than solely fine-tuning the agent on static data.
> - Combining curriculum learning with actionable environment augmentation and dense rewards significantly stabilizes reinforcement learning, preventing common issues like catastrophic forgetting and gradient explosion in complex multi-turn tasks.
> - Providing targeted, corrective feedback within the environment allows agents to infer inter-tool dependencies and internal tool constraints dynamically, fostering better generalization to unseen scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in developing Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents capable of performing complex, multi-turn tool-use tasks. The broader research landscape for LLM agents has seen significant advancements, particularly in areas like intricate reasoning, planning, and tool interaction, leading to applications in software engineering, computer use, and web browsing. However, much of this success has been observed in single-turn tasks (e.g., mathematical reasoning), where large, structured datasets, automated verifiers, and advanced algorithms are more readily available.

The transition of these agents to dynamic, multi-turn interaction settings, which more closely resemble real-world problems, introduces several critical challenges that current paradigms struggle to resolve:
- **Data Scarcity (C1):** High-quality, human-annotated multi-turn tool-use datasets are rare and labor-intensive to create. For instance, the Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard (BFCL) V3 multi-turn dataset contains only 800 samples.
- **Complex Environments (C2):** Multi-turn tool use often requires agents to navigate intricate and diverse tool ecosystems, spanning multiple domains and involving numerous distinct tools, necessitating sophisticated orchestration capabilities.
- **Long Interaction Chains (C3):** Success depends on consistent performance across many interaction turns, where a single error can lead to overall task failure, making credit assignment difficult.

Existing training paradigms primarily fall into two categories:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on synthetic data:** While this approach attempts to mitigate data scarcity, it often leads to agents that overfit to static, synthetic traces and fail to generalize effectively to real-world, unseen scenarios.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Although RL offers the potential for improved generalization through online interaction and exploration, it faces significant practical hurdles. The complexity of environments (C2) can lead to a "cold-start" problem, where an unproficient agent struggles to explore the vast action space, becoming trapped in cycles of low-quality rollouts. Furthermore, long interaction chains (C3) contribute to training instability and susceptibility to performance collapse.

This research aims to introduce a new training paradigm that specifically addresses the intersection of data scarcity, generalization, and learning stability in multi-turn tool-use contexts, moving beyond the limitations of existing SFT and traditional RL approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method for training high-quality agents for complex, multi-turn tool-use tasks under extreme data scarcity, while ensuring both robust generalization and learning stability.

The motivation for this objective stems from the identified limitations of current approaches:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The prohibitive cost and effort associated with creating high-quality, human-annotated multi-turn tool-use datasets necessitate a training paradigm that can effectively learn from a very limited number of problem instances (e.g., 400 samples used in this study).
- **Overcoming Generalization Issues in SFT:** Supervised fine-tuning, particularly when relying on synthetically generated data, has demonstrated a tendency to produce agents that perform well on in-distribution data but exhibit significant performance degradation on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks. The motivation is to foster agents that learn underlying problem-solving principles rather than merely memorizing patterns from static datasets.
- **Mitigating RL's Cold-Start and Instability Problems:** Traditional reinforcement learning, while conceptually suitable for online interaction, struggles in complex, sparse-reward, long-horizon multi-turn environments. The "cold-start" problem prevents initial learning, and the compounding effects of errors over long interaction chains lead to unstable training and frequent performance collapse. The motivation is to engineer the learning environment and process to provide more informative signals and a structured learning path, thereby stabilizing and accelerating RL.
- **Enabling Learning without Expert Trajectories:** A key motivation is to allow agents to learn complex behaviors directly from problem instances, reducing or eliminating the reliance on pre-collected expert trajectories, which are costly and difficult to obtain.

By addressing these specific challenges, the research seeks to enable agents to acquire sophisticated multi-step behaviors from scratch, even in the absence of expert demonstrations, leading to more robust and data-efficient LLM agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed **ENVIRONMENT TUNING**, is a novel training paradigm designed to address the challenges of data scarcity, generalization, and stability in multi-turn tool-use scenarios. It orchestrates the learning process through three complementary principles: a structured curriculum, actionable environment augmentation, and fine-grained progress rewards. The approach shifts from trajectory-based imitation to dynamic, environment-based exploration.

The core components of ENVIRONMENT TUNING are:

1. **Structured Curriculum RL Training (Section 3.2):**
 This four-stage curriculum is designed to progressively build an agent's skills from foundational to advanced capabilities, while maintaining training stability.
 - **Stage 1: Mastering Syntactic and Schematic Correctness:** The initial stage focuses on enabling the agent to produce well-formed outputs with valid tool calls, establishing a reliable foundation. Rewards are task-agnostic and based on turn-by-turn counters:
 - `C_correct`: number of tool calls with correct name and valid arguments.
 - `C_error`: number of calls with correct name but invalid arguments.
 - `C_format`: number of turns violating required XML-like format.
 These are combined into a `Format Reward (R_format = (N - C_format)/N)` and `Tool Call Reward (R_tool = C_correct / (C_correct + C_error))`, where `N` is total turns. The final reward `R_Stage1` encourages active tool use through an indicator `I_tool`.
 - **Stage 2: Basic Learning with Augmented Feedback:** The curriculum transitions to task-oriented reasoning using the "Base" split of the BFCL benchmark. This stage introduces two key mechanisms: the Fine-Grained Progress Reward and Actionable Environment Augmentation.
 - **Stage 3: Advanced Learning on Complex Scenarios:** The agent is exposed to the full training dataset, including "Missing Parameters," "Missing Functions," and "Long-Context" splits. This stage aims to train the agent to handle ambiguity, recognize functional gaps, and perform information retrieval from noisy contexts, leveraging the Progress Reward and Actionable Environment Augmentation.
 - **Stage 4: Alignment with the Evaluation Environment:** The final stage prepares the agent for real-world evaluation. While still using the full dataset and Progress Reward, the Actionable Environment Augmentation is disabled. This compels the agent to generalize its learned policies and rely on internal reasoning for standard error messages, as would occur during evaluation.
 - **Stage Transition Rule:** Transitions between stages occur when validation accuracy has plateaued and its gradient norm remains stable, ensuring mastery of current skills and readiness for increased complexity.

2. **Actionable Environment Augmentation (Section 3.3):**
 This mechanism modifies the environment's feedback to provide detailed, corrective hints upon failure, transforming ambiguous error messages into constructive learning signals.
 - **Discovering Inter-Tool Dependencies:** Rather than relying on pre-constructed dependency graphs, the environment provides hints that guide the agent to infer relationships between tools. For example, an "Invalid airport code[s]:..." message prompts the agent to use another tool to find correct codes.
 - **Revealing Internal Tool Constraints:** Pedagogical feedback is provided to explain why an action failed, moving beyond generic error codes. For instance, "Paths are not allowed. Specify only file/directory name..." clarifies tool-specific protocol.
 - **Implementation:** An LLM-driven workflow using an "Augmentation Generator" (to produce enhanced feedback) and a "Constitutional Judge" (to validate feedback against solution leakage and exploration constraints) is employed for semi-automated generation of augmentations (Appendix B). This workflow requires only a small number of domain-specific few-shot examples for bootstrapping.

3. **Fine-Grained Progress Reward (Section 3.4):**
 To overcome the problem of sparse, binary rewards in long-horizon tasks, a dense, turn-by-turn learning signal is introduced.
 - **Turn-based Evaluation:** Success is evaluated at the end of each turn based on two criteria: the correctness of the resulting environment state (for actions modifying the environment) and the execution result of the agent’s chosen action (for actions returning values).
 - **Reward Calculation:** A turn `t` is successful (`r_t = 1`) only if both state evaluation (`r_state_t`) and execution result evaluation (`r_exec_t`) are correct. Otherwise, `r_t = 0`. The total `Progress Reward (R_P)` for an episode is the average success rate across all turns: `R_P = (1/T) * sum(r_state_t * r_exec_t)`. This formulation enables learning from partially successful attempts.

**Agent Training Details (Section D.2):**
The agent's policy `π_θ` is trained using an adapted Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm. This implementation includes enhancements such as a decoupled clipping mechanism (`ϵ_low = 0.2`, `ϵ_high = 0.28`) and a KL-divergence penalty (`β = 0.1`) to ensure stable and effective exploration. The advantage estimate `A_hat_t` is computed without a critic network by normalizing rewards within a batch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, generalization, and stability of agents trained with ENVIRONMENT TUNING.

**Results on In-Distribution Multi-Turn Tool Use (BFCL V3, Table 1):**
- **Significant Performance Improvement for Base Models:** ENVIRONMENT TUNING demonstrated substantial gains when applied to base models. For instance, it increased Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's performance from 7.00% to 36.92% on the BFCL V3 multi-turn benchmark. This score surpassed several open-source baselines (BitAgent-8B, Arch-Agent-7B) and the proprietary Gemini 2.5 Pro model.
- **Effective Enhancement of SFT-Tuned Models:** The method also improved models that had already undergone Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). ToolACE-2-Llama-3.1-8B saw an increase of 9.19%, reaching 47.18%, which exceeded Gemini 2.5 Pro. Similarly, watt-tool-8B achieved an 18.50% improvement, elevating its score to 54.34%, outperforming several proprietary models including o3 and GPT-4o.
- **Superiority over Direct RL:** ENVIRONMENT TUNING consistently and substantially outperformed the ToolRL baseline, which applies a direct GRPO algorithm. While ToolRL yielded only modest gains or even performance degradation on some SFT-tuned models (e.g., -4.24% on ToolACE-2-Llama-3.1-8B), ENVIRONMENT TUNING provided larger and more stable improvements across all tested models.

**Results on Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Agentic Tasks (BFCL V4, τ^2-bench, ACEBench Agent, Table 2):**
- **SFT Baselines Collapse on OOD Tasks:** SFT-tuned models, such as xLAM-2-8b-fc-r (which achieved 70.50% on BFCL V3), exhibited a notable performance collapse on OOD tasks like BFCL V4 Web Search, dropping to 5.00%. This pattern was observed across other OOD benchmarks, indicating a limitation of SFT-based approaches in generalizing beyond their training distribution.
- **ENVIRONMENT TUNING Fosters Robust Generalization:** In contrast, ENVIRONMENT TUNING improved OOD generalization. It enhanced Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct's Web Search score from 1.00% to 15.00%, suggesting that the method teaches general problem-solving principles rather than dataset-specific patterns.
- **Enhanced Generalization for Proficient Models:** The method further improved the OOD capabilities of already proficient models. ToolACE-2-Llama-3.1-8B's Web Search score increased from 9.00% to 14.00%, and its average ACEBench Agent score rose from 8.34% to 15.00%.
- **Advantage over Direct RL on Complex OOD Tasks:** The framework demonstrated stronger and more stable generalization compared to the ToolRL baseline on complex OOD benchmarks. For instance, on ACEBench Agent, ENVIRONMENT TUNING boosted ToolACE-2-Llama-3.1-8B's score to 15.00%, whereas direct RL resulted in a performance drop to 6.65%.

**Ablation Studies (Section 4.3):**
- **Effect of Structured Curriculum:** A direct GRPO training baseline on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct yielded minimal gains (17.42%) over the base model (7.00%). In contrast, the full four-stage curriculum provided a steady improvement path, reaching 36.92%, an increase of 19.50% over the direct GRPO baseline.
- **Effect of Actionable Environment Augmentation:** Training dynamics showed that the augmented environment consistently led to more stable learning curves across all data splits. It provided performance improvements of over 20% in challenging scenarios such as "Missing Parameters" and "Missing Functions."
- **Effect of Fine-Grained Progress Reward:** While the performance difference was subtle for simpler tasks (Stage 2, Base data), the necessity of a denser reward signal became evident in complex splits (Stage 3). Binary rewards led to training failure and near-zero performance on "Missing Parameters" and "Missing Functions" splits, whereas the Progress Reward facilitated stable and effective learning.

**Training Dynamics (Section E.1 and E.2):**
- **Stage 1 Mastery:** Training dynamics showed rapid mastery of syntactic correctness in Stage 1, with steep increases in Format Reward and Tool Call Reward, and a reduction in wasteful interaction rounds.
- **Stability of Curriculum:** Throughout the full four-stage curriculum, the gradient norm remained stable, preventing the catastrophic gradient explosions often associated with long-horizon RL. This stability was crucial for sustained learning and consistent performance improvement on validation sets.
- **Instability of Single-Stage RL:** An experiment directly fine-tuning Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with single-stage RL showed initial performance gains but a sharp decline in accuracy after approximately 70 steps, coinciding with an explosion in the gradient norm, empirically confirming training instability without the proposed curriculum.
- **KL Loss Coefficient:** An ablation study demonstrated that a higher KL loss coefficient (β=0.1) was crucial for maintaining policy entropy and preventing performance collapse, which occurred with smaller or no KL penalties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Don’t Just Fine-tune the Agent, Tune the Environment" introduces a new paradigm for training LLM-based agents that carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Paradigm Shift in Agent Training:** The research proposes a fundamental shift from supervised fine-tuning on static, pre-collected trajectories to a dynamic, environment-driven exploration approach. This moves beyond imitation learning towards a more active, interaction-based learning process, which can lead to agents with more robust and adaptable behaviors.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** A key significance is the demonstration that sophisticated multi-turn tool-use capabilities can be learned from a minimal dataset (only 400 problem instances), and without requiring expert demonstrations. This addresses the critical challenge of data scarcity in complex real-world agentic tasks, making agent development more accessible and less resource-intensive.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The method significantly enhances out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, overcoming the performance collapse often observed in SFT-based approaches when encountering unseen scenarios. By learning directly through guided interaction with the environment, agents acquire more general problem-solving principles rather than overfitting to specific training patterns. This leads to more robust agents capable of handling novel situations.
- **Stabilized Reinforcement Learning:** The structured curriculum, actionable environment augmentation, and fine-grained progress rewards collectively address the inherent challenges of traditional RL in complex, long-horizon tasks, specifically the "cold-start" problem, training instability, and sparse rewards. By providing dense, informative feedback and a progressive learning path, ENVIRONMENT TUNING makes RL a more viable and effective training strategy for multi-turn agent tasks.
- **Practicality and Scalability:** The semi-automated, LLM-driven workflow for generating actionable environment augmentations reduces the manual engineering effort typically required for designing feedback for every error case. By leveraging few-shot examples, the approach is designed to be practical and generalizable to new domains, facilitating broader adoption.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for future investigation, including the development of fully automated mechanisms for curriculum generation and feedback provisioning, as well as extending the ENVIRONMENT TUNING paradigm to more complex, multi-modal agentic scenarios.
- **Advancing Real-World LLM Agent Deployment:** By enabling the training of robust, adaptable, and data-efficient agents, this research contributes to accelerating the deployment of LLM-based agents in real-world applications where data is scarce and tasks are complex and dynamic.
