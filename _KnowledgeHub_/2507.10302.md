# DisCo: Towards Distinct and Coherent Visual Encapsulation in Video MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> DisCo introduces a visual encapsulation method for video Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that generates semantically distinct and temporally coherent visual tokens. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across various video understanding benchmarks while significantly improving token efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing visual encapsulation methods for video MLLMs often produce semantically indistinct tokens, leading to redundancy and increased computational load.
- Current approaches struggle with temporal incoherence, failing to consistently track specific entities or actions across video frames.
- The inefficient use of visual tokens hinders the LLM's comprehensive understanding of dynamic video content and increases inference costs.

## Method

- A ==Visual Concept Discriminator (VCD)== employs GPT-4 for semantic concept extraction and uses bipartite matching with contrastive and matching losses to align visual token groups with distinct semantic concepts.
- A ==Temporal Focus Calibrator (TFC)== module ensures consistency by supervising each visual token to maintain focus on its corresponding semantic concept across all video frames through a ==frame-level focus alignment loss==.
- The method integrates these modules into a ==two-stage training scheme==: initial vision-text alignment on dense captions, followed by instruction tuning with diverse video QA data.

## Results

- DisCo achieves new state-of-the-art performance on multiple video question-answering benchmarks, including MVBench (**68.2%**), STAR (**77.7%**), and EgoSchema (**72.2%**) when integrated with InternVideo2-HD.
- It demonstrates enhanced token efficiency, with a **16**-token DisCo model outperforming a **64**-token traditional resampler baseline on MVBench, reducing token count by **75%**.
- Qualitative evaluations show improved correctness, stronger temporal coherence, and richer detail in video captions and conversational responses compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly enforcing semantic distinctiveness and temporal coherence in visual tokens is crucial for improving video MLLM understanding beyond simple token compression.
> - Leveraging large language models like GPT-4 to curate non-overlapping semantic concepts provides effective supervision for differentiating visual tokens.
> - Using feature centroids to anchor temporal alignment enables more robust and consistent tracking of objects or actions across variable video segments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically addressing critical challenges in extending their capabilities from static images to dynamic video content. MLLMs have revolutionized vision-language learning, demonstrating impressive understanding abilities across various open-world tasks. While initial breakthroughs were primarily in image MLLMs, the field has rapidly expanded to video MLLMs, enabling applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and AIGC.

The core challenge in video MLLMs lies in effectively "encapsulating" the vast and temporally complex visual information from videos into a format that Large Language Models (LLMs) can optimally process. The paper identifies two dominant visual connector paradigms:
- **Linear Projectors:** These are widely used due to their simplicity and ability to preserve local visual details. However, when applied to videos, they suffer from **semantic indistinctness** (redundant representation of the same object across frames) and **temporal incoherence** (failure to model consistent object presence and dynamics across time). Downsampling or compressing video patches, while improving efficiency, does not fundamentally resolve these issues due to the local nature of projection.
- **Resamplers:** These methods, often based on cross-attention mechanisms (e.g., Q-Former), compress video patches into a fixed, smaller set of visual tokens, implicitly attempting temporal modeling and reducing the input token count. While more token-efficient, the paper argues that current resamplers still exhibit semantic indistinctness (multiple tokens focusing on the same instance, neglecting others) and temporal incoherence (tokens failing to consistently track instances across frames).

DisCo directly tackles these limitations by introducing a novel visual encapsulation method that explicitly aims for **semantically distinct** and **temporally coherent** visual tokens. It builds upon the promising structure of resamplers, providing explicit guidance for their cross-attention mechanisms. By achieving distinct and coherent representations, DisCo addresses a fundamental bottleneck in video MLLMs, allowing the LLM to process higher-quality, less redundant, and more semantically organized visual information, thereby enhancing overall video understanding and reasoning capabilities. This work represents a significant step towards more efficient and effective video MLLMs, moving beyond mere token compression to semantic and temporal quality control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "DisCo" paper is to develop a novel visual encapsulation method that generates **semantically distinct** and **temporally coherent** visual tokens for video MLLMs. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Semantic Indistinctness:** Current visual encapsulation methods, particularly linear projectors and even existing resamplers, often produce redundant visual tokens. For instance, the same object appearing in multiple frames or being captured by several tokens within a single frame leads to repetitive semantic information, increasing computational load and potentially confusing the LLM. DisCo aims to ensure each visual token represents a unique and non-overlapping semantic concept from the video.
2. **Overcoming Temporal Incoherence:** Videos are inherently temporal, but existing methods struggle to maintain consistent focus on specific entities across frames. A token might attend to an object in one frame but lose track of it in subsequent frames, hindering the LLM's ability to understand dynamic events, actions, and temporal relationships. DisCo seeks to calibrate the temporal focus of visual tokens, ensuring they consistently track their designated semantic instances throughout the video.
3. **Enhancing LLM Comprehension:** By providing visual tokens that are both distinct and coherent, DisCo aims to significantly improve the quality of visual representations fed to the LLM. This higher quality input is expected to enable LLMs to more accurately and comprehensively comprehend video content, leading to better performance in various video understanding tasks, including question-answering, captioning, and conversational AI.
4. **Improving Token Efficiency:** Semantic indistinctness implies inefficient token usage. If multiple tokens convey the same information, many tokens are essentially wasted. By reducing this redundancy, DisCo aims to achieve higher token efficiency, meaning fewer tokens can represent the same or even more complete visual cues, thereby mitigating training and inference costs.
5. **Leveraging Resampler Potential:** The authors recognize the inherent promise of cross-attention mechanisms in resamplers for flexible remodeling of visual cues. The motivation is to unlock this potential by explicitly guiding the resampler's attention towards distinct semantics and coherent temporals, which is currently lacking in their design.

In essence, DisCo's motivation is to move beyond superficial token compression to a more intelligent and structured visual encapsulation that directly supports the nuanced understanding capabilities required by video MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DisCo proposes a novel visual encapsulation method, building upon the resampler architecture commonly found in video MLLMs, and introducing two key modules: a Visual Concept Discriminator (VCD) and a Temporal Focus Calibrator (TFC).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The underlying structure for video MLLMs typically involves:
- **Visual Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) processes video frames (sampled as $T$ frames $X = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^T$) to extract deep video features $V = \{v_i \in R^{n \times c}\}_{i=1}^T$.
- **Resampler:** A component (e.g., Q-Former) uses learnable query embeddings $X_q = \{q_i\}_{i=1}^N$ to interact with video features $V$ via cross-attention. This interaction generates a set of visual tokens $X_v = \{v_i\}_{i=1}^N$, representing encapsulated visual information.
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** The LLM processes these visual tokens $X_v$ alongside text instructions $X_i$ to generate natural language answers $X_a$, trained by minimizing negative log-likelihood.

#### 3.2. Visual Concept Discriminator (VCD) Module

The VCD module addresses semantic indistinctness by explicitly aligning different visual tokens with distinct semantic concepts.
1. **Grouping Visual Tokens:** The $N$ visual tokens from the resampler are reorganized into $N_g$ groups, $\{ \hat{v}_i \}_{i=1}^{N_g}$, with each group containing $N/N_g$ tokens. This grouping facilitates aligning multiple visual features to a single, more abstract semantic concept.
2. **Semantic Concept Extraction:** GPT-4 is leveraged to extract distinct words or phrases from the video caption, each representing a specific instance (e.g., "person," "pan," "sink"). These $M$ semantic concepts are then converted into text embeddings $\{ \hat{t}_j \}_{j=1}^M$ using the resampler’s text processing branch. The prompt for GPT-4 is carefully designed to avoid extracting repetitive instances.
3. **Bipartite Matching:** To establish a one-to-one correspondence, a bipartite matching algorithm (specifically, the Hungarian algorithm) is used to pair each visual token group $\hat{v}_i$ with a unique semantic concept $\hat{t}_{\hat{\sigma}(i)}$ by minimizing cosine distance. This ensures each visual token group focuses on a distinct element. If $N_g \neq M$, min($N_g$, $M$) pairs are formed.
4. **Pairwise Loss Functions:** After matching, two loss functions guide the learning:
 - **Visual-Semantic Pairwise Contrastive (VSC) Loss ($L_{vsc}$):** Encourages matched visual-semantic pairs to be similar and dissimilar to unmatched pairs.
 - **Visual-Semantic Matching (VSM) Loss ($L_{vsm}$):** Uses a cross-entropy loss with an MLP to predict if a given visual-semantic pair is a match or not.
5. **Global Tokens:** A set of global tokens (not part of the grouped, matched tokens) is introduced to capture comprehensive video information that might not be covered by the specific semantic concepts, ensuring the integrity of visual cues.

#### 3.3. Temporal Focus Calibrator (TFC) Module

The TFC module ensures temporal coherence by explicitly supervising each visual token to consistently focus on its corresponding semantic concept across every video frame.
1. **Focused Feature Extraction:** From the resampler's cross-attention maps between visual tokens and ViT-extracted video features, frame-wise focused features $a_t^i$ are extracted for each token group $i$ and frame $t$. This is done by averaging attention maps across cross-attention layers and pooling along spatial dimensions.
2. **Frame-level Focus Alignment (FFA) Loss ($L_{ffa}$):** This contrastive loss enforces temporal consistency. For each visual token group $i$, its frame-wise focused features $a_t^i$ are pulled closer to the group's feature centroid $\bar{a}_i$ (average of $a_t^i$ across all frames) and pushed away from the feature centroids of other token groups. The feature centroid provides a more robust temporal reference, especially when objects temporarily disappear in some frames.

#### 3.4. Training Scheme

The training comprises two stages, similar to standard MLLM training:
1. **Stage 1: Vision-Text Alignment:** This stage uses a large dataset of visual dense captions to align DisCo's visual tokens with the LLM. The VCD and TFC modules are fully integrated here, with the total loss being a weighted sum: $L_{stage1} = L_{llm} + \lambda_{vsc}L_{vsc} + \lambda_{vsm}L_{vsm} + \lambda_{ffa}L_{ffa}$. During this stage, the visual encoder is frozen, the resampler is updated, and the LLM is fine-tuned using LoRA.
2. **Stage 2: Instruction Tuning:** The model is further fine-tuned using diverse image and video caption and question-answer data to equip it with strong instruction-following capabilities.

DisCo is designed as a plug-and-play module compatible with existing resampler-based video MLLM frameworks, demonstrated with ST-LLM and InternVideo2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DisCo demonstrates significant advancements in video understanding across multiple benchmarks, particularly in generating high-quality visual tokens and improving model efficiency.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Video Question-Answering (QA):** DisCo consistently outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods across a wide array of video QA benchmarks, including:
 - **Short video benchmarks** like STAR and PerceptionTest (focused on fine-grained visual details).
 - **Long video benchmarks** like EgoSchema and MLVU (stressing complex temporal relationships).
 - **Comprehensive benchmarks** like MVBench and VideoMME (covering diverse video QA tasks).
 - For instance, DisCo integrated with InternVideo2-HD achieved 68.2% on MVBench, 77.7% on STAR, and 72.2% on EgoSchema (subset), surpassing the baseline InternVideo2-HD (66.3%, 75.7%, 67.0% respectively) and other SOTA models.
- **Video Conversation:** DisCo also shows superior performance on video conversation benchmarks like VideoChatGPT-Bench, improving metrics such as Correctness of Information, Detail Orientation, Context Understanding, Temporal Understanding, and Consistency, leading to higher average scores.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Improvements

- Qualitative examples (Fig. 4) highlight DisCo's ability to achieve:
 - **Better correctness:** Accurately identifying object colors or water temperature.
 - **Stronger temporal coherence:** Precisely describing sequential actions (e.g., "add more wood to the fire," "arm circles").
 - **Richer details in video captioning:** Providing more sufficient and fine-grained descriptions of video content.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Major Components:** Both the Visual Concept Discriminator (VCD) and Temporal Focus Calibrator (TFC) modules independently contribute significant performance improvements. Adding VCD alone to InternVideo2-HD improved MVBench from 66.3% to 67.6%, and TFC alone to 67.1%. Their combined effect (DisCo) yielded the best result (68.2%). The gains were confirmed to originate from the module designs, not just additional training data, as direct fine-tuning (SFT) of the baseline with the same data yielded smaller improvements.
- **Token Efficiency:** DisCo significantly enhances token efficiency. A DisCo model using only 16 visual tokens outperformed a traditional resampler with 64 tokens on MVBench, demonstrating its potential to achieve comparable or better performance with 75% fewer tokens. This translates to substantial mitigation of training and inference costs, with DisCo introducing less than 5% overhead in training time for the same token count.
- **VCD Key Designs:**
 - **Number of Visual Token Groups ($N_g$):** An optimal balance is found (e.g., $N_g=16$ for InternVideo2) where too few groups limit semantic detail, and too many groups lead to insufficient tokens per group.
 - **Global Tokens:** The inclusion of global tokens is crucial for capturing comprehensive video information, preventing performance decline.
 - **Loss Functions:** Both VSC and VSM losses are necessary for optimal performance, with neither alone achieving the best results.
 - **Semantic Instance Extraction:** Using an "unoverlapped" semantic instance extraction method (as guided by GPT-4 prompts) is superior to extracting all instances, further validating the importance of semantic distinctiveness.
 - **Visualization (Fig. 6a):** Shows that resampler tokens often redundantly focus on common instances while ignoring others, whereas DisCo ensures distinct semantics and comprehensive coverage.
- **TFC Key Designs:**
 - **Feature Centroids:** Using feature centroids for contrastive learning in the FFA loss (instead of only frame-wise features) consistently yields performance improvements, indicating a more robust reference for temporal alignment.
 - **Visualization (Fig. 6b):** Demonstrates that traditional resamplers fail to consistently track instances across frames, while DisCo effectively maintains attention on the target object (e.g., "pot") throughout the video.
- **Caption Quality:** DisCo demonstrates adaptability to varying caption qualities (dense vs. short captions), consistently yielding performance gains regardless of the source, highlighting its robustness.
- **Loss Weight Ablations:** Optimal performance is achieved when VSC, VSM, and FFA loss weights are all set to 1.0.
- **Comparison with Other Token Compression Methods:** DisCo achieves comparable performance to TokenPacker and DeCo while using significantly fewer visual tokens (64 vs. 400/256) and demonstrating superior training and inference efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DisCo represents a significant advancement in the field of video Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications for both research and practical applications.

1. **Fundamental Improvement in Visual Representation:** The core contribution of DisCo lies in its ability to generate visual tokens that are both semantically distinct and temporally coherent. This is a fundamental improvement over previous encapsulation methods, which suffered from redundancy and inconsistency. By providing higher-quality visual inputs, DisCo enables LLMs to develop a more accurate, nuanced, and comprehensive understanding of video content.
2. **Enhanced Video Understanding and Reasoning:** The improved visual representations directly translate to superior performance across a diverse range of video understanding tasks, including question-answering, detailed video captioning, and conversational AI. This will lead to more intelligent and reliable video MLLMs capable of complex temporal reasoning and fine-grained visual comprehension.
3. **Increased Efficiency and Scalability:** DisCo dramatically improves token efficiency, allowing models to achieve better or comparable performance with significantly fewer visual tokens. This reduction in token count directly mitigates the computational burden during both training and inference, making video MLLMs more accessible, scalable, and environmentally friendly. This efficiency is critical for deploying MLLMs in resource-constrained environments or for processing extremely long videos.
4. **Plug-and-Play Adaptability:** DisCo's design as a plug-and-play module for existing resampler-based video MLLMs makes it highly versatile. This ease of integration means that researchers can readily adopt DisCo to enhance their current MLLM frameworks without extensive architectural redesigns, accelerating further research and development in the field.
5. **Broader Impact on AI Applications:** The advancements brought by DisCo have a wide-reaching potential impact on various real-world applications:
 - **Robotics:** More precise perception of dynamic environments, better understanding of complex actions, and improved human-robot interaction.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Enhanced scene comprehension, more accurate prediction of events, and safer navigation.
 - **Content Generation (AIGC):** Generating more contextually relevant, temporally consistent, and semantically rich video content.
 - **Video Surveillance and Analysis:** More accurate event detection and anomaly recognition.
 - **Education and Accessibility:** Generating detailed descriptions of video content for visually impaired individuals or for educational purposes.

By providing a more principled approach to visual encapsulation, DisCo sets a new standard for how video information should be processed for MLLMs. It paves the way for future research into even more sophisticated visual representations that can unlock the full potential of multimodal AI for understanding the dynamic world.
