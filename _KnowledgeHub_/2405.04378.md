# Splat-MOVER: Multi-Stage, Open-Vocabulary Robotic Manipulation via Editable Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> Splat-MOVER introduces a robotics framework enabling open-vocabulary, multi-stage manipulation by using an editable Gaussian Splatting 3D scene representation. It integrates semantic and grasp affordance features, along with real-time scene updates, to achieve superior grasping success and successfully complete complex multi-stage tasks on a physical robot where existing NeRF-based methods could not operate.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with open-world manipulation, particularly in dynamic, unstructured environments, often lacking the ability to use natural language commands without extensive prior training on specific objects.
- Existing "smart map" methods, largely based on Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), suffer from slow training/rendering and limited editability, making them unsuitable for multi-stage tasks where the environment changes after robot interaction.
- Many current methods lack explicit understanding of grasp affordances, requiring significant human input or demonstrations to specify precise and stable grasp locations on objects.

## Method

- **ASK-Splat:** An editable Gaussian Splatting representation that distills both language semantic features (from CLIP) and grasp affordance features (from VRB) into the 3D scene for comprehensive understanding.
- **SEE-Splat:** A real-time scene-editing module that localizes target objects, masks their associated Gaussians, applies transformations to reflect robot interactions, and uses 3D Gaussian infilling to dynamically update the scene.
- **Grasp-Splat:** A grasp generation module that takes initial grasp proposals from GraspNet and re-ranks them based on the embedded grasp affordance knowledge from ASK-Splat, ensuring stable and human-like grasps.

## Results

- ASK-Splat successfully encoded grasp affordance knowledge with SSIM values ranging from 0.592 to 0.681 across different scenes, demonstrating effective generalization of affordance understanding.
- Splat-MOVER consistently outperformed baselines in single-stage tasks, achieving higher grasp success rates (e.g., 100% for a saucepan vs. 40% for LERF-TOGO) and significantly higher affordance-aligned grasp rates (e.g., 95% AGSR for a power drill vs. 0% for LERF-TOGO).
- Splat-MOVER uniquely completed all four complex multi-stage manipulation tasks (Cooking, Chopping, Cleaning, Workshop) on a physical robot with pick-and-place success rates ranging from 50% to 100%, a capability not supported by existing NeRF-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting Gaussian Splatting with distilled semantic and grasp affordance features provides a rich, actionable 3D scene representation that is efficient and editable, overcoming limitations of prior NeRF-based approaches.
> - Dynamic, real-time scene editing is crucial for enabling robots to execute complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks, as it allows the robot's internal model to continuously adapt to environmental changes.
> - Embedding grasp affordance knowledge directly into the 3D scene significantly improves the quality and intuitiveness of generated grasps, reducing the reliance on external guidance for stable interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the critical challenge of **open-world robotic manipulation**, a long-standing goal in robotics where robots must operate in unstructured, dynamic environments using natural language commands, without prior training on specific objects or tasks. This field generally branches into two main approaches: "smart policy" methods and "smart map" methods.

**"Smart policy" methods** typically involve training large, end-to-end visuomotor policies (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment, Octo, OpenVLA) using vast datasets and computational resources, often transferring web knowledge to robotic control. While powerful for zero-shot generalization, these methods can be data-hungry, computationally intensive for training, and may lack explicit 3D scene understanding or the ability to easily update their internal representation of the environment. Splat-MOVER explicitly positions itself as *not* a "smart policy" but rather a "smart map" approach.

**"Smart map" methods**, to which Splat-MOVER belongs, focus on creating rich 3D scene representations that embed features from vision-language foundation models (VLMs), which then interface with traditional manipulation planning stacks. Previous works in this category primarily utilized **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs)**, such as LERF-TOGO and F3RM. NeRFs enabled high-fidelity scene representations and language grounding, allowing robots to identify objects based on semantic attributes. However, NeRFs suffer from several limitations:
1. **Slow training and rendering times:** Making real-time operation challenging.
2. **Difficulty with dynamic scenes:** NeRFs are not easily edited to reflect changes in the environment, which is crucial for multi-stage tasks where object poses change after robot interaction.
3. **Dependence on human input for grasping:** Many NeRF-based methods still require external guidance (e.g., from an LLM or human demonstrations) to specify *where* to grasp an object.

The emergence of **Gaussian Splatting (GSplat)** has provided a significant technological leap over NeRFs, offering real-time rendering speeds and greater flexibility for dynamic scene editing. Recent works have started embedding semantic features into 3D Gaussian Splatting, similar to how it was done with NeRFs, but these typically haven't focused on complex robotic manipulation tasks or grasp affordances.

Splat-MOVER innovates by:
- **Leveraging Gaussian Splatting:** Overcoming the speed and editability limitations of NeRFs.
- **Integrating Grasp Affordance:** Beyond just semantic understanding, it distills *grasp affordance* features (from models like VRB) directly into the 3D scene representation. This is a novel integration for GSplat in robotic manipulation, reducing the need for explicit human guidance on grasp locations.
- **Enabling Multi-Stage Manipulation:** The core novelty lies in its ability to dynamically edit the 3D scene representation in real-time to reflect object movements, a capability critical for tasks involving sequences of actions where the environment changes. This differentiates it from existing "smart map" baselines that are largely confined to single-stage tasks.

In essence, Splat-MOVER pushes the frontier of "smart map" approaches by combining the efficiency and editability of Gaussian Splatting with comprehensive semantic *and* affordance understanding, specifically tailored to support complex, multi-stage robotic tasks in open-world settings. It builds upon foundational concepts of 3D feature fields but significantly advances their applicability and robustness for practical robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of Splat-MOVER is to develop a modular robotics stack that facilitates **open-vocabulary, multi-stage robotic manipulation** through the innovative use of **editable Gaussian Splatting scene representations**.

The motivation behind Splat-MOVER stems from several critical limitations in existing open-world robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Beyond Spatial and Semantic Understanding:** While identifying objects and their locations is foundational, robots often need to know *how* to interact with objects – specifically, the best places to grasp them. This necessitates understanding **grasp affordances** (i.e., the possibilities for action that an object offers), which existing semantic-only 3D scene representations often lack.
2. **Handling Dynamic Environments for Multi-Stage Tasks:** Most real-world manipulation tasks involve a sequence of actions where the robot modifies the environment (e.g., picking up an object changes its position). For the robot to effectively plan subsequent stages, its internal 3D model of the scene must be **continually updated in real-time** to reflect these changes. Current NeRF-based methods are not amenable to such dynamic scene editing, severely limiting their applicability to multi-stage tasks.
3. **Reducing Reliance on Human Input:** Existing language-guided manipulation methods often require humans or large language models (LLMs) to specify precise grasp locations or rely on extensive human demonstrations. A key motivation for Splat-MOVER is to embed sufficient "common-sense" knowledge about grasping directly into the scene representation, thereby reducing the need for such external guidance.
4. **Leveraging Advances in 3D Scene Representation:** The recent advent of Gaussian Splatting offers significant advantages over Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) in terms of training speed, real-time rendering, and inherent editability. The authors are motivated to harness these capabilities to address the aforementioned challenges in robotic manipulation.

To achieve its primary objective and address these motivations, Splat-MOVER sets out to:
- **Develop ASK-Splat:** A Gaussian Splatting representation that distills both semantic features (from CLIP) and grasp affordance features (from VRB) directly into the 3D scene, providing comprehensive spatial, semantic, and affordance understanding.
- **Create SEE-Splat:** A real-time scene-editing module that can visualize and implement the effects of robot interactions, transforming the ASK-Splat scene to reflect object motions and creating a "digital twin" of the evolving environment.
- **Design Grasp-Splat:** A grasp generation module that utilizes the rich semantic and affordance information from ASK-Splat and the dynamic scene updates from SEE-Splat to propose stable, affordance-aligned grasps for open-world objects, suitable for multi-stage tasks.

Ultimately, Splat-MOVER aims to provide a robotic system capable of executing complex, sequential tasks with minimal human intervention by integrating robust 3D scene understanding, dynamic scene updating, and intelligent grasp planning within an efficient framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Splat-MOVER is structured as a modular robotics stack comprising three interconnected modules: ASK-Splat, SEE-Splat, and Grasp-Splat. Its operation involves an initial scanning/training phase followed by real-time execution during manipulation.

**Overall Workflow:**
1. **Pre-scanning Phase:** A brief scanning period where RGB images of the workspace are collected from multiple viewpoints. These posed images are used to train the ASK-Splat model.
2. **Task Specification:** A natural language prompt describes a multi-stage manipulation task.
3. **Operational Phase:** Splat-MOVER uses its modules to localize objects, plan grasps, execute actions, and dynamically update the scene for subsequent stages.

**Detailed Modules:**

#### 3.1. ASK-Splat: Affordance-and-Semantic-Knowledge Gaussian Splatting

ASK-Splat is the core 3D scene representation, building upon Gaussian Splatting to embed two crucial types of information:
- **Language Semantics:** It distills 2D CLIP features (from MaskCLIP) into the 3D scene. To maintain real-time rendering speed, a small autoencoder compresses the high-dimensional CLIP features (e.g., 512-1024 dimensions) into a lower-dimensional latent space (l=3). Each 3D Gaussian is augmented with a semantic feature parameter ($f_s \in \mathbb{R}^l$). The autoencoder and GSplat semantic features are optimized simultaneously (or sequentially for better localization) using reconstruction (MSE) and cosine-similarity losses. This allows generating 3D semantic heatmaps based on open-vocabulary text prompts.
- **Grasp Affordances:** ASK-Splat embeds object-specific grasp affordances, distilling them from a vision-affordance foundation model (VRB, trained on human-object interaction datasets like EPIC-KITCHENS). Each Gaussian is augmented with a parameter $\beta \in$ representing its graspability score. This is optimized using an MSE loss against the ground-truth 2D affordance heatmaps rendered from the 3D Gaussians. This enables identifying graspable regions on objects.

The training of ASK-Splat takes approximately 25 minutes on an NVIDIA 3090 GPU, following a one-time data processing step.

#### 3.2. SEE-Splat: Scene-Editing-Enabled Gaussian Splatting

SEE-Splat enables real-time editing of the ASK-Splat scene, crucial for multi-stage tasks. It has three main components:
- **Semantic Localization:** Given a natural language query, SEE-Splat leverages ASK-Splat to generate a semantic similarity map, identifying relevant Gaussians. Localization accuracy can be enhanced by including geometric or visual properties in the prompt.
- **Gaussian Masking:** A mask is generated for the identified object by thresholding the semantic relevancy. This creates a sparse point cloud of the relevant Gaussians (their means). To achieve photorealistic rendering, this sparse cloud is densified by identifying neighboring Gaussians within a 7D feature space (3D position + RGB color + semantic score) using an efficient KD-tree query.
- **Editing the Gaussians:** SEE-Splat applies transformations (e.g., rigid, non-rigid) to the masked and densified Gaussians representing the manipulated object. These transforms can be generated by heuristics, an LLM, a human, or computed from sensor observations. This updates the spatial and geometric attributes of the Gaussians, effectively creating a "digital twin" of the evolving scene.
- **3D Gaussian Infilling:** To counteract visual and geometric artifacts (like "holes") that appear when objects are moved, SEE-Splat performs 3D Gaussian infilling by introducing new Gaussians with similar attributes in the missing regions, ensuring photorealistic rendering of the edited scene.

#### 3.3. Grasp-Splat: Affordance-Aligned Grasp Generation

Grasp-Splat is responsible for proposing grasp configurations, leveraging the understanding from ASK-Splat and the updated scene from SEE-Splat:
- **Initial Grasp Proposal:** It takes the dense point cloud of the target object (generated by SEE-Splat) as input to a pre-trained deep-learned model, GraspNet, which estimates 6D grasp poses for a parallel-jaw gripper along with quality scores.
- **Affordance-Aligned Re-ranking:** Recognizing that GraspNet's proposals might not always be ideal (e.g., not localized to a handle), Grasp-Splat introduces a grasp metric $\nu: SE(3) \to \mathbb{R}$. This metric computes the grasp affordance at a specified grasp pose by querying ASK-Splat's embedded affordance information. Candidate grasps from GraspNet are then re-ranked based on these affordance scores, prioritizing grasps that align with human-like, stable grasp regions (e.g., object handles).
- **Multi-Stage Integration:** For multi-stage tasks, the entire Splat-MOVER stack is iteratively applied. Task stages can be specified by a human or an LLM. For each stage, ASK-Splat identifies the object and target location, SEE-Splat updates the scene, and Grasp-Splat proposes and refines grasps.
- **Motion Planning:** Standard off-the-shelf motion planners (e.g., MoveIt) are used for point-to-point motion planning for the robot arm, utilizing the extracted point cloud and mesh from the dynamically updated ASK-Splat/SEE-Splat scene for collision detection.

This integrated approach allows Splat-MOVER to reason about and execute complex manipulation sequences in dynamic, open-world environments by continuously adapting its scene understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Splat-MOVER's effectiveness was rigorously evaluated through hardware experiments on a Kinova Gen3 robot equipped with a Robotiq parallel-jaw gripper, comparing its performance against two recent baseline methods: LERF-TOGO and F3RM* (an implementation of F3RM using GraspNet). The experiments focused on four single-stage and four multi-stage, open-vocabulary manipulation tasks across various real-world scenes (Kitchen, Cleaning, Workshop).

#### 4.1. ASK-Splat Representation Quality

- **Qualitative Assessment:** Visualizations demonstrated that ASK-Splat successfully encodes grasp affordance knowledge, with rendered affordance heatmaps closely matching those predicted by the VRB foundation model. These heatmaps accurately highlight regions amenable to grasping (e.g., the handle of a saucepan). Semantic similarity maps generated by ASK-Splat effectively localized objects based on natural language queries, even at novel viewpoints.
- **Quantitative Assessment (SSIM for Affordance):** The Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) was used to quantify the similarity between VRB-generated and ASK-Splat-rendered affordance maps. SSIM values ranged from 0.592 (Workshop scene, with out-of-distribution objects like a power drill) to 0.681 (Meal scene). This indicates good generalization of affordance knowledge by ASK-Splat, even for objects not explicitly in the VRB training distribution, suggesting its robustness in open-world scenarios.

#### 4.2. Single-Stage Manipulation Performance

For four single-stage pick-and-place tasks (saucepan, knife, cleaning spray, power drill), Splat-MOVER consistently outperformed baselines:
- **Grasping Success Rate and Affordance-Aligned Grasp Rate (AGSR):** Splat-MOVER achieved significantly higher grasping success rates and AGSR for the saucepan, knife, and cleaning spray. For example, for the saucepan, Splat-MOVER achieved 100% grasp success and 60% AGSR, compared to LERF-TOGO's 40% grasp success and 5% AGSR, and F3RM*'s 30% grasp success and 30% AGSR.
- **Power Drill Task Nuance:** While LERF-TOGO achieved a perfect 100% grasp success rate for the power drill, its AGSR was 0%, meaning it consistently grasped the object outside its intended affordance region (e.g., the top part, not the handle). In contrast, Splat-MOVER achieved a 95% grasp success rate with a 95% AGSR, demonstrating its ability to generate not just successful, but *sensible and stable* grasps in the affordance region.
- **Underlying Reason:** Splat-MOVER's superior performance in the first stage is attributed to the distilled grasp affordance knowledge in ASK-Splat, which encodes human-like common sense about where to grasp. Baselines like LERF-TOGO and F3RM* either require explicit human/LLM guidance or rely on human demonstrations, which may not generalize as effectively or provide as consistent affordance-aligned grasps.
- **Pick-and-Place Success (Stage 1):** Splat-MOVER also generally achieved higher pick-and-place success rates for the first stage, with place rates for LERF-TOGO not evaluated due to lack of functionality. Even for challenging tasks like placing a saucepan on an electric burner (60% place success for Splat-MOVER), it demonstrated robust performance.

#### 4.3. Multi-Stage Manipulation Performance

This is where Splat-MOVER demonstrated its unique and most significant capability:
- **Unique Multi-Stage Capability:** LERF-TOGO and F3RM* are not designed for multi-stage manipulation as their underlying NeRF representations cannot be easily edited to reflect changes in the scene. Consequently, these baselines could not be evaluated for the full multi-stage tasks.
- **Splat-MOVER's Success:** Splat-MOVER successfully completed all four multi-stage tasks (Cooking, Chopping, Cleaning, Workshop), achieving pick-and-place success rates ranging from 50% to 100% across the various stages. For example, in the Cooking task, Stage 1 achieved 100% pick and 60% place, followed by Stage 2 with 50% pick and 80% place. In the Cleaning task, Splat-MOVER achieved 90% pick and 83.33% place in Stage 1, followed by 70% pick and 100% place in Stage 2. This unequivocally demonstrates the effectiveness of SEE-Splat in updating the scene representation and enabling sequential task execution.
- **Grasp-Splat Quality (Qualitative):** Visual comparisons of proposed grasps showed that Grasp-Splat, by leveraging affordance knowledge, generated more intuitive and stable grasps (e.g., on the handle of a saucepan or the middle of a power drill) compared to raw GraspNet or baselines requiring explicit guidance.
- **Scene Editing Effectiveness:** SEE-Splat's 3D Gaussian infilling effectively removed visual artifacts (e.g., holes in a table after an object was moved), maintaining photorealistic and coherent scene representations throughout the dynamic task execution.

In summary, Splat-MOVER significantly advances open-vocabulary manipulation by providing a robust, real-time, and affordance-aware system capable of handling complex multi-stage tasks, a feat not possible with existing NeRF-based baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Splat-MOVER represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for real-world, open-vocabulary, and multi-stage tasks. Its core significance lies in the novel integration of several advanced techniques to address long-standing challenges in robotics:

1. **Enabling Multi-Stage Manipulation:** This is arguably the most impactful contribution. By introducing a real-time, editable 3D scene representation through SEE-Splat, Splat-MOVER uniquely allows robots to dynamically update their understanding of the environment after each manipulation action. This capability is fundamental for executing complex, sequential tasks that inherently change the scene, moving beyond the limitations of single-stage manipulation common in previous "smart map" approaches. This opens doors for robots to perform more intricate chores, assembly, or cooking tasks.

2. **Affordance-Aware Grasping:** The distillation of grasp affordance features into ASK-Splat significantly enhances the robot's ability to plan effective and intuitive grasps. By incorporating common-sense human knowledge about where to grasp objects, Splat-MOVER reduces the reliance on external human intervention (e.g., specifying grasp locations) or extensive human demonstrations. This makes the system more autonomous, adaptable, and robust to novel objects, as it can infer stable grasp regions.

3. **Leveraging Gaussian Splatting for Robotics:** By adopting Gaussian Splatting (GSplat) over Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), Splat-MOVER capitalizes on GSplat's advantages in real-time rendering speed and inherent editability. This makes the "smart map" approach much more practical for dynamic robotic applications where scene updates and fast perception are crucial. The concept of a "digital twin" of the evolving environment, facilitated by SEE-Splat, is powerful for visualization, planning, and potentially simulation.

4. **Modular and Extensible Architecture:** The modular design of ASK-Splat, SEE-Splat, and Grasp-Splat provides flexibility. Each component can potentially be improved independently or integrated with other advanced techniques. For instance, the grasp generation module could incorporate more sophisticated physics simulations, or the scene editing could be enhanced with better object tracking.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Increased Autonomy:** Splat-MOVER pushes towards more autonomous robots that can interpret high-level language commands, understand their environment, adapt to changes, and execute complex tasks without continuous human supervision.
- **Broader Applications:** This technology has significant implications for various domains, including domestic robotics, industrial automation, logistics, and hazardous environment operations, where robots need to interact with diverse objects and perform multi-step procedures.
- **Advancement in 3D Scene Understanding:** The work demonstrates a powerful paradigm for embedding rich, actionable information (semantics + affordance) into efficient 3D representations, influencing future research in 3D vision and perception for robotics.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By leveraging foundation models and minimizing the need for task-specific demonstrations or explicit human guidance, Splat-MOVER could reduce the development time and cost associated with deploying robots for new manipulation tasks.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors transparently discuss several limitations, which also highlight directions for future impact:
- **Generalization of Affordance Models:** The effectiveness of distilled affordances depends on the underlying foundation model (e.g., VRB, trained on kitchen tasks). Future work aims for more diverse, cross-environment affordance models.
- **Grasp Affordance in SE(3):** Current grasp affordance is primarily spatial; extending it to incorporate orientation-dependent grasp quality would improve planning.
- **Initial Scanning Phase:** The need for a one-time scanning phase could be eliminated by integrating fast online Gaussian Splatting.
- **Robustness to Dynamic Events:** SEE-Splat currently doesn't model unexpected events like slippage or complex physics. Integrating real-time object pose tracking and physics-based simulation would enhance robustness and realism.
- **Integration with Generalist Policies:** The current motion planning uses off-the-shelf MoveIt. A compelling future direction is to integrate Splat-MOVER with robust generalist robot policies (like OpenVLA or Octo) to combine Splat-MOVER's global scene understanding and digital twin capabilities with the closed-loop control and disturbance robustness of these policies, creating a truly superior robotic manipulation system.

In conclusion, Splat-MOVER represents a substantial contribution to open-world robotic manipulation by successfully integrating editable Gaussian Splatting with comprehensive semantic and affordance knowledge to enable multi-stage tasks. Its modular design and proven hardware performance mark it as a significant milestone towards creating more capable, autonomous, and versatile robotic systems.
