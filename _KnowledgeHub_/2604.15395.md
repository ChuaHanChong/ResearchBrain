# Foundation Models in Robotics: A Comprehensive Review of Methods, Models, Datasets, Challenges and Future Research Directions

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive review by researchers from multiple institutions systematically maps the evolving landscape of Foundation Models (FMs) in robotics, categorizing approaches across six criteria, tracing their five-phase evolution, and identifying key challenges and future directions. This work provides a structured understanding of how FMs are transforming robot design, learning, and deployment practices.

## Problem

- The rapidly evolving field of Foundation Models (FMs) in robotics lacked a holistic and systematically structured analysis.
- A need existed to delineate the historical evolution, current trends, and diverse methodological approaches of FMs across various robotic tasks and application domains.
- Understanding the landscape of datasets, open challenges, and promising future research directions for FMs in robotics was crucial for guiding further development.

## Method

- A ==systematic literature review== was conducted across six major scientific databases, ultimately selecting 435 articles for in-depth analysis.
- The research evolution was delineated into ==five distinct phases==, providing a historical perspective on the integration and advancement of Foundation Models in robotics.
- A highly-granular, ==multi-criteria (six distinct criteria) taxonomic investigation== classified methods based on ==FM types==, ==neural network architectures==, ==learning paradigms==, ==learning stages==, ==robotic tasks==, and ==application domains==.

## Results

- The review categorized **435 articles** based on a six-criteria taxonomy, providing detailed comparative analyses of FM types, neural network architectures, learning paradigms, and their application to specific robotic tasks and domains.
- **Five evolutionary phases** of robotic FM research were identified, highlighting milestone models such as RT-1, PaLM-E, and GR00T N1, and demonstrating a shift towards embodied, multi-modal, and general-purpose agents.
- A comprehensive overview of publicly available datasets was compiled, revealing a lack of essential tactile sensing data and failure/recovery data, while emphasizing the growth of massive cross-embodiment and vision-language-action datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic FM research has evolved through five distinct phases (2018-present), progressing from integrating native NLP/CV models to achieving multi-sensory generalization and real-world deployment.
> - Four primary Foundation Model types (LLMs, VFMs, VLMs, VLAs) are utilized, each contributing to different levels of robotic intelligence, from high-level cognitive planning to embodied physical execution.
> - The field faces critical bottlenecks including physical-world data scarcity (especially for tactile sensing and failure data), the embodiment gap, and computational latency, necessitating advancements in hardware-agnostic architectures and integrated multi-sensory intelligence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is undergoing a shift from specialized, single-task robotic systems to adaptive, multi-function, general-purpose agents capable of operating in complex, dynamic, and open-world environments. This evolution is driven by advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML), particularly the emergence of Foundation Models (FMs).

Traditional robotic control largely relies on explicit mathematical modeling and programming, which are effective in structured environments but lack adaptability. Machine Learning methods offer higher adaptability and efficiency in complex environments but often require extensive, computationally expensive datasets. Foundation Models, large-scale neural networks trained on vast, heterogeneous datasets, represent a paradigm shift by offering multi-modal understanding, long-horizon planning, and cross-embodiment generalization. They serve as a versatile base for developing specialized applications without training from scratch, providing advantages such as improved transferability, reusable representations, enhanced semantic understanding, support for sim-to-real transfer, and multi-modal integration. However, FMs in robotics also present challenges, including inference latency, computational cost, limited real-time deployability, lack of physical grounding, data scarcity, safety risks, and interpretability issues.

This review situates itself within a growing body of literature examining FMs in robotics. While other surveys have explored specific aspects such as general-purpose robots (Hu et al., 2023), robotic manipulation (Xu et al., 2024c), embodied AI (Ma et al., 2024c; Sapkota et al., 2025), robotic autonomy (Jang et al., 2024), component replacement (Kawaharazuka et al., 2024), broad applications (Firoozi et al., 2025), robot learning (Xiao et al., 2025b), and real-world deployment (Tayyab Khan & Waheed, 2025), many are noted for their relatively narrow scope, limited analysis criteria, or narrative-style discussion.

This work aims to provide a more holistic, systematic, and in-depth analysis. It distinguishes itself by:
- Delineating the research evolution into five distinct phases, offering a historical perspective.
- Conducting a highly-granular, multi-criteria (six distinct criteria) taxonomic investigation of the literature, including FM types, neural network architectures, learning paradigms, learning stages, robotic tasks, and application domains.
- Providing methodical comparative analysis and critical insights for each criterion.
- Compiling a thorough report on publicly available datasets.
- Offering a comprehensive and hierarchical discussion of current challenges and future research directions.

By adopting a structured review methodology and broad analytical scope, this paper seeks to offer a more comprehensive mapping and analysis of the robotic FM research landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the recognition of Foundation Models (FMs) as a driving force behind a transformative shift in robotics. The field is moving towards more adaptive, multi-functional, and general-purpose robotic agents capable of operating in complex and dynamic real-world environments. FMs offer unprecedented capabilities in multi-modal understanding, reasoning, long-horizon planning, and cross-embodiment generalization, fundamentally reforming robot design, learning, programming, and deployment practices.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this study are outlined as follows:

1. **Delineate Research Evolution:** To systematically trace the historical development of FMs in robotics, identifying distinct research phases. This includes an initial phase (2018–2021) of integrating native Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV) models, followed by grounded planning with Vision-Language (VL) representations (2021–2022), the emergence of embodied Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies (2022–2023), advancements in memory, autonomous task composition, and Web-to-robot transfer (2023–2024), and the current frontier of multi-sensory generalization and real-world deployment (2024–present). This objective aims to provide a structured understanding of the field's progression and the milestone models within each phase.

2. **Conduct Holistic and Granular Taxonomic Investigation:** To perform an in-depth and multi-criteria classification of existing robotic FM approaches. This comprehensive taxonomy examines six key aspects:
 - **Employed FM types:** Categorizing models based on their input-output modalities (e.g., Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs)).
 - **Underlying neural network architectures:** Analyzing the dominant architectural choices (e.g., Transformers, State-Space Models (SSMs), Diffusion Models, Convolutional and hybrid encoders, Graphical models).
 - **Adopted learning paradigms:** Investigating the diverse learning techniques (e.g., pre-training, self-supervised learning, fine-tuning, domain adaptation, imitation learning, reinforcement learning, in-context/prompt learning, world model learning, generative learning).
 - **Learning stages of knowledge incorporation:** Examining at which point in the learning process knowledge is integrated (e.g., pre-training, offline fine-tuning, online adaptation, continuous learning).
 - **Major robotic tasks:** Identifying the core robotic functionalities addressed by FMs (e.g., perception, planning, navigation, manipulation, human-robot interaction).
 - **Main real-world application domains:** Cataloging the practical deployment areas (e.g., agentic mobility, industrial manipulation, service robots, medical robots).
 For each criterion, the study aims to provide comparative analysis and critical insights into the various categories of approaches.

3. **Report on Publicly Available Datasets:** To compile a thorough overview of the datasets and benchmarks utilized for training and evaluating FM-based solutions across the identified robotic tasks. This objective addresses the crucial need for data in FM development and highlights resource availability.

4. **Discuss Challenges and Future Research Directions:** To identify and analyze current open challenges inherent in the application of FMs to robotics, and to propose promising future research avenues. This forward-looking objective aims to guide subsequent research efforts towards addressing existing limitations and fostering further advancements in the field.

These objectives collectively aim to provide a comprehensive resource for researchers and practitioners, mapping the current state of FMs in robotics and guiding future development towards more capable and reliable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employed a systematic literature review methodology to identify, map, and analyze the relevant research in robotic Foundation Models (FMs). This approach was designed to ensure comprehensiveness and relevance of the selected works, comprising several iterative steps:

1. **Scope and Objectives Formulation:**
 - The primary goal was to review literature on robotic FMs applied to various core robotic tasks, specifically perception, reasoning, planning, navigation, manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
 - The focus was on identifying recent advancements and impactful contributions.
 - Specific objectives included categorizing methods by multiple criteria, cataloging datasets, and outlining current challenges and future directions.

2. **Literature Search:**
 - To ensure broad coverage, the search strategy involved querying six major scientific databases: IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar, Scopus, DBLP, arXiv, and Web of Science.
 - The search queries combined targeted keywords such as "foundation model," "robotics," "vision-language-action," "large language model," "visual foundation model," "robotic foundation model," "robot," "manipulation," "navigation," "human-robot interaction," and "embodied" using Boolean operators ("AND," "OR," "NOT").
 - A temporal filter was applied, primarily focusing on publications within the last five years (post-2020, up to 2026 for arXiv preprints), with exceptions for seminal earlier studies. An example Scopus query was provided, demonstrating the combination of keywords and filters.
 - Multiple iterative search steps were conducted, with keyword refinements to enhance relevance.
 - Reference lists of identified articles were also manually analyzed to uncover additional pertinent studies.

3. **Screening:**
 - **Initial Exclusion:** The first step involved removing duplicate records, non-English papers, and articles without full-text access.
 - **Title and Abstract Review:** Articles were then selected or excluded based on their titles and abstracts to filter out irrelevant works.
 - **Full-Text Review:** A detailed full-text review was performed on the remaining articles. Inclusion criteria for this stage required studies to:
 - Focus on approaches for implementing robotic tasks (perception, planning, navigation, manipulation, human-robot interaction) where an FM is a key algorithmic component.
 - Demonstrate substantial theoretical and/or experimental contributions.
 - **Prioritization:** Preference was given to research published in prominent robotics and AI/ML venues.

Following this systematic process, a total of 435 articles were selected for in-depth analysis and were included as references in the report. Bibliometric analytics, such as article types and popular venues, were also compiled.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The report provides a comprehensive overview of Foundation Models (FMs) in robotics, structured around their evolution, categorization, and the landscape of datasets, challenges, and future directions.

#### 4.1. Robotic FM Research Evolution

The study delineates the evolution of robotic FM research into five phases:
1. **Phase 1 (2018–2021): Integration of native NLP and CV models.** Early efforts focused on incorporating off-the-shelf NLP and CV models to enhance perception (e.g., object tracking, language grounding), with robot behavior still governed by conventional controllers.
2. **Phase 2 (2021–2022): Grounded planning with Vision-Language (VL) representations.** This phase saw the integration of multimodal embeddings to link visual input with natural language commands for task sequencing, leveraging broader access to aligned image-text data.
3. **Phase 3 (2022–2023): Embodied Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies.** A shift towards end-to-end training emerged, where FMs processed vision, language, and task context to output action sequences directly, driven by large-scale robot-demonstration datasets (e.g., RT-1, Gato).
4. **Phase 4 (2023–2024): Memory, autonomous task composition, and Web-to-robot transfer.** Systems gained capabilities for long-horizon planning, world-state tracking, and autonomous skill discovery, using both structured robot trajectories and unstructured web-scale corpora (e.g., PaLM-E, RT-2).
5. **Phase 5 (2024–Present): Multi-sensory generalization and real-world deployment.** Recent advancements focus on robust generalist robots operating in unstructured real-world environments, supporting multimodal inputs (vision, language, touch, force, proprioception), and real-time adaptation (e.g., GR00T N1, Gemini robotics).

Key FMs introduced throughout these phases include BERT, ViT, GPT-3, CLIP, DINO, OWL-ViT, BLIP, SayCan, RT-1, Gato, PaLM-E, SAM, RT-2, Eureka, AutoRT, and RFM-1, each contributing distinct innovations in language processing, visual perception, multimodal understanding, or end-to-end action generation.

#### 4.2. Taxonomic Investigation of Robotic FM Methods

The report categorizes robotic FM methods based on six criteria:

- **Foundation Model Types:**
 - **LLMs:** Primarily used for high-level cognitive task planning and reasoning, translating natural language to robot actions (e.g., SayCan, Code-as-Policies). Strengths include robust instruction translation and generalization, but limitations involve lack of embodiment, hallucinations, and latency.
 - **VFMs:** Provide generalized, high-quality visual representations (e.g., SAM for segmentation, DINOv2 for features). Advantages include improved transfer learning, open-world perception, and robustness, but challenges include domain specificity and incomplete physical dynamics modeling.
 - **VLMs:** Combine vision and language for semantic world understanding and intuitive HRI (e.g., CLIP for visual grounding, VoxPoser for affordances). They offer rich semantic information and enhanced generalization but struggle with precise action definition and semantic grounding.
 - **VLAs:** Native robotic models integrating multi-modal understanding with direct physical execution for embodied task execution (e.g., RT-1, PaLM-E, GR00T N1). They offer end-to-end simplicity and robust generalization but demand large-scale training data and face operational latency.

- **Neural Network Architectures:**
 - **Transformers:** Widely used for sequence modeling, capturing long-range dependencies in multimodal inputs (e.g., ViTs for vision, text transformers for planning, multimodal transformers for VLA policies). Strengths are long-range dependency modeling and architectural homogenization, but limitations include high computational complexity and discrete tokenization.
 - **State-Space Models (SSMs):** Adopted for real-time control and long-horizon reasoning due to linear scaling complexity and stable memory (e.g., RoboMamba).
 - **Diffusion Models:** Generate robot behaviors by reversing a denoising process, producing diverse and uncertainty-aware action samples (e.g., Diffusion Policy). Advantages include multimodal feature modeling and trajectory-level decision making, but limitations involve sampling latency and lack of built-in safety.
 - **Convolutional and Hybrid Encoders:** CNNs provide robust low-level feature extraction, often combined with transformers for broader context (e.g., EfficientNet in RT-1). They offer zero-shot generalization and noise robustness but can suffer from latency and loss of fine details.
 - **Graphical Models:** Enable structured relational reasoning by modeling environments as interconnected entities (e.g., Scene Graphs in ConceptGraphs, GRID). They provide combinatorial generalization and increased explainability, but face computational overhead and dynamic topology challenges.

- **Learning Paradigms:**
 - **Pre-training:** Learning broad world understanding from massive datasets (e.g., PaLM-E, Octo). Offers data efficiency and zero-shot generalization.
 - **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Generating supervisory signals from unlabeled data (e.g., MAE, CLIP, DINOv2). Leads to massive scalability and sample efficiency.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Adapting pre-trained FMs to specific tasks or robots with smaller, in-domain datasets (e.g., LoRA-based tuning in OpenVLA). Improves precision and domain adaptation.
 - **Domain Adaptation (DA):** Bridging sim-to-real gaps by adjusting models to new target domains (e.g., DrEureka, ReBot). Reduces real-world data needs and enhances robustness.
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** Learning from expert demonstrations (e.g., Behavioral Cloning in RT-1, In-context IL in ICRT). Eliminates reward engineering and reduces training data, but depends on data quality.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Agents learn through trial-and-error interaction with environments (e.g., LLM-guided reward design in Eureka, World-model RL in RoboDreamer). Enables self-improvement and generalization but is sample-inefficient.
 - **In-Context/Prompt Learning (ICL):** Adapting FMs at inference time via demonstrations or instructions in the prompt (e.g., SayCan, ReAct-style prompting). Facilitates generalization to novel settings without weight updates.
 - **World Model (WM) Learning:** Predicting environmental changes in response to actions, encoding causal relations (e.g., Dreamer-style agents, RoboDreamer). Incorporates "imagined" experiences and models physics but is prone to hallucinations and cumulative errors.
 - **Generative Learning (GL):** Synthesizing data, plans, or action sequences (e.g., Diffusion Policy, generative video synthesis). Increases zero-shot generalization and handles multimodality but faces sim-to-real gaps.

- **Learning Stages:**
 - **Pre-training:** First, computationally intensive step to learn general-purpose representations from massive, diverse, often multi-modal datasets (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, GR00T N1).
 - **Offline Fine-tuning:** Adjusting FM knowledge to specific application domains or tasks using minimal additional training data (e.g., Octo, OpenVLA with LoRA adapters).
 - **Online Adaptation:** Real-time adjustments of robot behavior during deployment to maintain performance in dynamic, unforeseen conditions (e.g., TTA-Nav, LLM-based ReAct prompting).
 - **Continuous Learning:** Incrementally acquiring new skills and refining existing ones over the long term, without catastrophic forgetting (e.g., LOTUS, Self-Improving Embodied FMs).

- **Robotic Tasks:**
 - **Perception:** FMs enhance environmental understanding through open-vocabulary recognition, 3D semantic mapping, pose estimation, affordance prediction, contact-centric perception, and long-term object tracking (e.g., Grounding DINO, SAM, ConceptGraphs).
 - **Planning:** FMs bridge high-level reasoning and low-level control, enabling language-driven task decomposition, neuro-symbolic reasoning, multi-modal policy generation, execution-time validation, and multi-robot coordination (e.g., SayCan, Code-as-Policies, AutoTAMP).
 - **Navigation:** FMs provide spatial common-sense knowledge, enabling open-world navigation, cross-embodiment transfer, semantic spatial grounding, instruction-following policies, and end-to-end navigation (e.g., VLMaps, GNM, DriveGPT-4).
 - **Manipulation:** FMs facilitate translation from semantic instructions to precise physical movements, offering increased generalization, improved robustness, and enhanced embodiment (e.g., RT-1, OpenVLA, Diffusion Policy, VoxPoser).
 - **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** FMs enable semantic, human-like reasoning, conversational policy alignment, reciprocal social tuning, and active alignment/mitigation for intuitive and safe interaction (e.g., TidyBot, DRAGON, RoboVQA).

- **Application Domains:**
 - **Agentic Mobility:** FMs enable autonomous driving and navigation with semantic understanding, cross-embodiment generalization, and real-time efficiency (e.g., GNM, DriveGPT-4).
 - **Industrial Manipulation:** FMs redefine automation with flexible, general-purpose agents for unstructured manufacturing, offering zero-shot generalization and simplified transfer (e.g., RFM-1, OpenVLA).
 - **Supply Operations:** FMs provide flexibility and intelligence for dynamic logistics tasks, supporting sustainable planning and multi-agent coordination (e.g., DeepFleet).
 - **Service Robots:** FMs enable robots to navigate domestic environments and interpret natural language instructions for personalized assistance (e.g., TidyBot, ELLMER).
 - **Medical Robots:** FMs bridge clinical knowledge and robotic execution for high-stake tasks, enhancing surgical perception and decision-making (e.g., Surgical-DINO, DARES).
 - **Cognitive Agrisystems:** FMs enable adaptive intelligence for field operations, reasoning through environmental variability for tasks like harvesting (e.g., HarvestFlex).
 - **Crisis Agents:** FMs reform disaster response with autonomous reasoning in unpredictable and hazardous environments for tasks like hazard detection (e.g., SafeGuard ASF).
 - **Maritime Robotics:** FMs bolster autonomous maritime systems with enhanced perception and reasoning in challenging aquatic environments (e.g., UnderwaterVLA, MarineInst).
 - **Space Robotics:** FMs provide strong priors and zero-shot generalization for off-world operations under extreme conditions and data scarcity (e.g., SAM for crater detection).

#### 4.3. Public Datasets

The review identifies numerous public datasets (Tables 8-12) categorized by primary robotic task (perception, planning, navigation, manipulation, HRI). Key observations include:
- A focus on **massive cross-embodiment datasets** (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, AgiBot World) to foster generalist policies.
- Emphasis on **vision-language-action capturings** (e.g., BridgeData V2, CALVIN) to integrate perception, reasoning, and control.
- Movement towards **high-fidelity, real-world capturing settings** (e.g., Ego4D, DROID) to address the sim-to-real gap.
- Incorporation of **human reasoning aspects** (e.g., RoboVQA, TEACh) for common-sense and interactive capabilities.
- Critical **lack of tactile sensing data**, which is essential for dexterous manipulation.
- **Lack of failure and recovery data**, hindering the development of robust error-handling models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The emergence of Foundation Models (FMs) has instigated a significant transformation in robotics, moving the field from rigid, task-specific solutions towards adaptive, multi-sensory, and generalist agents. This paradigm shift holds substantial significance for the future of robotics, promising unprecedented capabilities in multi-modal understanding and reasoning, long-horizon planning, and cross-embodiment generalization. The current review systematically maps this evolving landscape, highlighting both the advancements achieved and the persistent challenges that shape the trajectory of future research.

**Current Significance:**
FMs have already demonstrated their capacity to revolutionize core robotic functionalities:
- **Enhanced Autonomy:** Robots can now interpret complex, high-level natural language commands, decompose them into executable plans, and adapt to unstructured, dynamic environments with a degree of flexibility previously unattainable.
- **Improved Generalization:** Pre-trained FMs exhibit strong zero-shot and few-shot generalization capabilities, allowing robots to perform tasks with novel objects, in unseen environments, and across different embodiments with reduced training data.
- **Richer Interaction:** In human-robot interaction, FMs enable more intuitive, natural, and context-aware communication, including conversational interfaces, understanding of human intent, and adaptive responses.
- **Cross-Domain Application:** The versatility of FMs has facilitated their application across diverse real-world domains, from agentic mobility and industrial manipulation to medical and space robotics, showcasing their potential to solve complex problems in various sectors.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
Addressing the identified challenges is critical for fully realizing the potential of FMs in robotics. Future research is poised to impact the field significantly in several key areas:

1. **Architectural Evolution for Universal Robotics:**
 - **Heterogeneous Action Spaces:** Developing embodiment-agnostic action spaces and hardware-specific modulation components will enable truly universal FMs that can seamlessly operate across diverse robotic platforms, from manipulators to quadrupedal robots and drones. This will reduce development costs and accelerate deployment.
 - **Sophisticated Action Sequence Tokenization:** Advancements in tokenization methods that capture continuous action dynamics more precisely will lead to finer-grained control and enhanced dexterity, particularly for complex manipulation tasks.
 - **Diffusion and Flow-Based Action Modeling:** Equipping robots with the ability to predict action consequences in continuous latent spaces through flow and diffusion models will enable more robust and diverse trajectory generation, moving beyond average motions that can lead to failures.

2. **Multi-modal Embodied Intelligence:**
 - **Integrated Tactile, Proprioception, and Force Control:** Holistic integration of tactile, proprioceptive, and force feedback with vision and language is crucial for achieving human-level dexterity, especially in contact-rich tasks, soft object manipulation, and delicate assembly.
 - **Auditory Feedback Exploitation:** Leveraging auditory signals, which require fewer computational resources, can provide complementary information for event detection, environmental understanding, and reliable operation in noisy or visually occluded environments.

3. **Reasoning and Long-Horizon Autonomy:**
 - **Long-Horizon Memory Frameworks:** Developing FMs with extended memory capabilities, such as structured summaries of past interactions and latent-graph memory formalisms, will allow robots to overcome catastrophic forgetting and plan effectively for multi-stage, long-duration missions without repeating past failures.
 - **Hierarchical Semantic Representations:** Reasoning on hierarchical abstractions (e.g., scene graphs) will simplify decision-making in cluttered environments, reduce computational load, and lead to more robust task planning at varying levels of detail.

4. **World Foundation Models:**
 - **Physics-informed Generative Models:** Advancing world models to generate physically plausible data with accurate physics (gravity, friction, deformation) will significantly reduce the sim-to-real gap, enabling robots to learn and act more reliably from synthetic experiences.
 - **Action-Conditioned Scenario Generation:** Creating interactive cognitive engines capable of generating action-conditioned scenarios, especially for cross-embodiment and "long-tail" situations, will alleviate the data scarcity problem and allow for robust training across multiple hardware platforms.

5. **Safety and Verification:**
 - **Adaptive Safety Measures:** Moving towards an integrated approach where safety constraints are embedded directly into the FM's training and reasoning loops will enable robots to proactively predict and mitigate physical risks before action execution.
 - **Formal Verification Techniques:** Developing modular, runtime-based formal verification frameworks, including neuro-symbolic integration and automated specification mining, is essential to ensure that FM-based robotic systems are not only high-performing but also predictable, reliable, and safe for deployment in high-stakes human-robot interaction environments.

In conclusion, the report underscores that while FMs offer unprecedented reasoning capabilities, the critical bottlenecks of physical-world data scarcity, the embodiment gap, and computational latency persist. Future research that prioritizes hardware-agnostic architectures, multi-sensory integration, advanced reasoning, physics-informed world models, and robust safety mechanisms will be instrumental in bridging the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical grounding. This will ultimately accelerate the development of truly autonomous, reliable, and intelligent robotic systems capable of navigating and interacting with the complexities of the real world.
