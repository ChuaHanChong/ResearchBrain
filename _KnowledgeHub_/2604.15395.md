# Foundation Models in Robotics: A Comprehensive Review of Methods, Models, Datasets, Challenges and Future Research Directions

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive review by researchers from multiple institutions systematically maps the evolving landscape of Foundation Models (FMs) in robotics, categorizing approaches across six criteria, tracing their five-phase evolution, and identifying key challenges and future directions. This work provides a structured understanding of how FMs are transforming robot design, learning, and deployment practices.

## Problem

- The rapidly evolving field of Foundation Models (FMs) in robotics lacked a holistic and systematically structured analysis.
- A need existed to delineate the historical evolution, current trends, and diverse methodological approaches of FMs across various robotic tasks and application domains.
- Understanding the landscape of datasets, open challenges, and promising future research directions for FMs in robotics was crucial for guiding further development.

## Method

- A ==systematic literature review== was conducted across six major scientific databases, ultimately selecting 435 articles for in-depth analysis.
- The research evolution was delineated into ==five distinct phases==, providing a historical perspective on the integration and advancement of Foundation Models in robotics.
- A highly-granular, ==multi-criteria (six distinct criteria) taxonomic investigation== classified methods based on ==FM types==, ==neural network architectures==, ==learning paradigms==, ==learning stages==, ==robotic tasks==, and ==application domains==.

## Results

- The review categorized **435 articles** based on a six-criteria taxonomy, providing detailed comparative analyses of FM types, neural network architectures, learning paradigms, and their application to specific robotic tasks and domains.
- **Five evolutionary phases** of robotic FM research were identified, highlighting milestone models such as RT-1, PaLM-E, and GR00T N1, and demonstrating a shift towards embodied, multi-modal, and general-purpose agents.
- A comprehensive overview of publicly available datasets was compiled, revealing a lack of essential tactile sensing data and failure/recovery data, while emphasizing the growth of massive cross-embodiment and vision-language-action datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic FM research has evolved through five distinct phases (2018-present), progressing from integrating native NLP/CV models to achieving multi-sensory generalization and real-world deployment.
> - Four primary Foundation Model types (LLMs, VFMs, VLMs, VLAs) are utilized, each contributing to different levels of robotic intelligence, from high-level cognitive planning to embodied physical execution.
> - The field faces critical bottlenecks including physical-world data scarcity (especially for tactile sensing and failure data), the embodiment gap, and computational latency, necessitating advancements in hardware-agnostic architectures and integrated multi-sensory intelligence.
