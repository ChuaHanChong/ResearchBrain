# DIVA-GRPO: Enhancing Multimodal Reasoning through Difficulty-Adaptive Variant Advantage

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at CAS and Kuaishou Technology developed DIVA-GRPO, an RL framework that mitigates reward sparsity and advantage vanishing in MLLMs by adaptively generating problem variants. The framework enables 7B-scale models to achieve state-of-the-art multimodal reasoning performance, often on par with 72B models and proprietary systems, alongside significant training speedups.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with 'long-chain reasoning' due to heterogeneous modalities and the need for stepwise problem-solving.
- Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), effective for reasoning, suffers from reward sparsity and advantage vanishing (zero or excessively large relative advantages) when problems are too easy or too difficult.
- Existing solutions to reward sparsity and advantage vanishing often introduce biases, waste valuable data, or provide misaligned optimization signals.

## Method

- Developed a dynamic ==difficulty assessment== mechanism that iteratively recalibrates problem difficulty based on the model's empirical accuracy.
- Introduced a difficulty-adaptive variant generation strategy to create semantically consistent problem variations (e.g., perturbations, paraphrases, reasoning hints) tailored to the assessed difficulty.
- Implemented a robust advantage estimation method that balances normalized local and global advantages, scaled by difficulty, and includes Reward-Range-Based (RRB) Rescaling to prevent overestimation from minor reward differences.

## Results

- DIVA-GRPO-**7B** achieved state-of-the-art performance among **7B**-scale models across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks, with an average score of 54.58.
- The **7B**-scale model matched or surpassed the performance of significantly larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-**72B**, GPT-4o) on several benchmarks, showing an average 8.23-point accuracy gain over its Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** backbone.
- The framework delivered substantial training speedups (**1.76x** to **2.55x** end-to-end wall-clock time) and maintained stable, informative advantage signals throughout training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ensuring sufficient variance in the within-group reward distribution of responses is fundamental for generating clear and informative optimization signals in GRPO.
> - Dynamically adapting problem difficulty to keep the model at approximately 50% accuracy maximizes the informativeness of the advantage signal.
> - Combining diverse feedback from original problems and difficulty-adjusted variants, with careful normalization and weighting, stabilizes and enhances policy optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in integrating textual and visual information for complex reasoning tasks such as visual question answering (VQA) and multimodal logical reasoning. The overarching goal in this area is to enable MLLMs to perform "long-chain reasoning," a challenging feat due to the heterogeneous nature of text and visual modalities, which often requires careful observation and stepwise problem-solving.

Existing research has explored several avenues to enhance MLLM reasoning. Multimodal chain-of-thought (CoT) approaches, including techniques like Video-of-Thought, Det-CoT, and Grounded-RL, aim to improve reasoning by decomposing problems and leveraging structured strategies. Complementary to these, reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks have emerged as powerful tools for fine-tuning MLLMs. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) are widely used for alignment tasks, while Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has gained prominence for advancing long-chain reasoning by combining rewards with group-relative advantage estimation, significantly boosting both alignment and reasoning efficiency.

However, the authors identify a critical challenge when directly applying GRPO to MLLMs: **advantage vanishing** and **reward sparsity**. Advantage vanishing occurs when a problem is either too easy or too difficult for the current model, leading to scenarios where all generated responses are either entirely correct or entirely incorrect. In such cases, the relative advantage calculated by GRPO becomes zero or excessively large, providing noisy or non-existent optimization signals and thus slowing down learning and destabilizing training. Reward sparsity, on the other hand, refers to the scarcity of positive feedback, especially for difficult problems or in the early stages of training, making it hard for the model to learn effective reasoning paths.

The paper critically reviews existing solutions to these issues, categorizing them into three main types, each with its own limitations:
1. **Sample enhancement and expansion:** Enlarges the problem space by adding prompts or generating variants. However, it often exacerbates advantage vanishing due to poor control over difficulty distribution and can introduce biases.
2. **Selective sample utilization:** Prioritizes effective samples or discards unhelpful ones to improve efficiency. This approach, however, risks underutilizing valuable data, limiting exposure to complex problems, and reducing data diversity.
3. **Indirect reward design:** Provides finer-grained feedback signals to mitigate sparsity. Yet, these indirect rewards might not perfectly align with the ultimate task objectives, potentially guiding the model towards suboptimal optimization directions.

DIVA-GRPO thus positions itself as a novel solution that directly tackles the "fundamental question" overlooked by prior work: "for a given problem, how can we ensure that the within-group reward distribution of responses exhibits enough variance to yield clear optimization signals for each response?" By addressing this gap, DIVA-GRPO aims to provide a more robust and efficient GRPO-based training paradigm for MLLMs, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for DIVA-GRPO stems from the inherent limitations of current Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) frameworks when applied to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). While GRPO is effective for long-chain reasoning, it critically suffers from:

1. **Reward Sparsity:** Especially for difficult problems or early in training, MLLMs often generate few correct reasoning paths, leading to a scarcity of positive rewards. This makes it challenging for the model to discern effective actions and learn from sparse feedback.
2. **Advantage Vanishing:** This occurs when a given problem is either too easy or too hard for the current MLLM. If all generated responses are correct, or all are incorrect, the within-group variance of rewards becomes negligible. Consequently, the relative advantage calculated by GRPO approaches zero, yielding no meaningful gradient signal for optimization. This issue becomes more pronounced as training progresses, and the model's capabilities evolve, leading to unstable and inefficient learning.

Existing solutions, as discussed in the previous section, offer only partial remedies. Sample expansion without difficulty awareness can worsen advantage vanishing. Selective sampling wastes valuable data. Indirect reward design risks misaligning optimization goals. The authors identify a crucial oversight: the lack of a mechanism to dynamically ensure sufficient variance in reward distributions within a group of responses, regardless of problem difficulty.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the DIVA-GRPO research are:

- **To propose a novel framework, DIVA-GRPO, that dynamically adjusts the difficulty distribution of problem variants.** This aims to ensure that for any given problem, the model always encounters a range of difficulties that generate informative positive and negative feedback, thereby mitigating reward sparsity and advantage vanishing.
- **To develop a dynamic difficulty assessment mechanism.** This mechanism must adaptively gauge the model's current proficiency on a problem to guide the generation of appropriately difficult variants.
- **To introduce a difficulty-adaptive variant generation strategy.** This strategy should create semantically consistent problem variations (e.g., perturbing images/text, adding reasoning hints) that are tailored to the assessed difficulty, maintaining the original answer while adjusting the challenge level.
- **To design a robust advantage estimation method that integrates local and global perspectives.** This involves combining advantages computed from the original problem's responses (local) with those from its generated variants (global), balanced through normalization and difficulty-weighted scaling.
- **To present a reward-range-based advantage rescaling method.** This aims to prevent an overestimation of advantages from minor reward differences, ensuring that optimization signals accurately reflect significant performance gains.
- **To empirically demonstrate the superiority of DIVA-GRPO.** The method should show significant improvements in both training efficiency and multimodal reasoning performance across diverse benchmarks, particularly for 7B-scale models.

Ultimately, DIVA-GRPO seeks to provide a principled, stable, and efficient reinforcement learning framework that enables MLLMs to master complex, long-chain reasoning tasks by continuously providing salient optimization signals throughout the training process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DIVA-GRPO introduces a sophisticated, multi-pronged approach to address reward sparsity and advantage vanishing in GRPO-based MLLM training. Its core methodology revolves around dynamically adjusting problem difficulty and leveraging a refined advantage calculation mechanism.

#### 3.1. Difficulty Assessment of Samples

A foundational element of DIVA-GRPO is its dynamic difficulty assessment. Unlike static difficulty labels, problem difficulty is treated as an evolving metric tied to the model's current capability.
- **Dynamic Assessment:** The difficulty of a problem is iteratively assessed based on the empirical accuracy (α) of the model's historical rollouts for that problem. If most rollouts are correct, the problem is deemed easy; if most fail, it's considered difficult.
- **Recalibration:** This assessment is recalibrated at every training epoch, ensuring it adapts as the model's performance improves or degrades.
- **Update Rule:** The difficulty `D` is updated using the formula: `D_new = clip(D_old + η * (0.5 - α), D_min, D_max)`. Here, `η` is a learning rate, and the `(0.5 - α)` term drives the difficulty down when accuracy is high (α > 0.5) and up when accuracy is low (α < 0.5). `clip` ensures difficulty stays within predefined bounds (`D_min`, `D_max`). This rule is designed to keep the perceived difficulty at a level where the model achieves around 50% accuracy, thus maximizing the informativeness of the advantage signal (as theoretically justified in Appendix C).

#### 3.2. Difficulty-Adaptive Variant Generation

Once a problem's difficulty `D_q` is assessed, DIVA-GRPO adaptively generates a set of semantically consistent variants `V_q`, ensuring each variant preserves the original ground-truth answer while adjusting its challenge level.
- **Simpler Problems (D_q < D_mid):** For problems that are relatively easy, variants are generated by perturbing both the text and the image. Textual variants (`T_q^(i)`) involve rephrasing, restructuring, or introducing slight linguistic perturbations. Image variants (`I_q^(i)`) are created through functions like rotation, Gaussian noise, salt-and-pepper noise, speckle noise, or blurring. Stronger or multiple perturbations increase difficulty. Additionally, textual information can be embedded directly into images to shift modality dominance.
- **Moderate Problems (D_q ≈ D_mid):** For moderately difficult problems, the focus is on diversifying expression without drastically changing difficulty. Variants consist of semantically equivalent textual paraphrases (`T_q^(i)`), allowing the model to generalize to different linguistic formulations.
- **Difficult Problems (D_q > D_mid):** For hard problems, the approach is to provide partial reasoning guidance. Variants incorporate sequences of intermediate reasoning steps (`R_q^(i)`), generated and verified by a closed-source model (e.g., GPT-o3). These hints help the model make progress, ensuring that even challenging problems yield meaningful advantage signals and prevent gradient vanishing.

#### 3.3. Difficulty-Weighted and Normalized Advantage Balancing

This component refines the advantage calculation by considering both local and global reward signals and adapting them based on difficulty.
- **Local and Global Advantages:**
 - **Local advantage (`A_local`):** Computed for each original problem based on its `k` rollouts, using the standard GRPO formula.
 - **Global advantage (`A_global`):** Computed across all responses in the variant set (`V_q`), which includes `m` variants * `k` rollouts.
- **Local-Global Imbalance Mitigation:** Global advantages, based on a larger sample pool, tend to dominate. To address this, batch-level z-score normalization is applied separately to `A_local` and `A_global` (`A_tilde_local`, `A_tilde_global`). This ensures both signals are comparable in magnitude and contribute effectively to training.
- **Difficulty-Weighted Scaling:** To encourage the model to tackle harder problems, the normalized advantages (`A_tilde`) are rescaled using a difficulty coefficient. For each response `y_i` associated with variant `q^(i)`:
 `A_hat(y_i | q^(i)) = exp(k * (D_q^(i) - D_q_bar) * sgn(A_tilde(y_i))) * A_tilde(y_i)`
 Here, `D_q^(i)` is the difficulty of variant `q^(i)`, `D_q_bar` is the group-wise mean difficulty, and `k` is a sensitivity parameter. This formula amplifies correct answers for variants harder than the average, while penalizing incorrect answers more heavily for variants easier than the average. This adaptive weighting stabilizes optimization and aligns feedback with actual problem difficulty. Theoretical analysis in Appendix B supports that this strategy reduces gradient variance while maintaining unbiasedness.

#### 3.4. Reward-Range-Based Advantage Rescaling (RRB-Rescaling)

This method tackles situations where a small reward range within a rollout group can lead to exaggerated advantage values, misrepresenting the actual learning gain.
- **Problem:** Standard z-score normalization might overamplify minor reward differences (e.g., between "correct formatting" and "fully correct" if both are positive but close).
- **Solution:** Introduce `Delta_r_q = (max(R_q) - min(R_q)) / R_max`, representing the actual reward variability relative to the maximum possible range. The rescaled advantage is then `A_hat_range(y_i) = Delta_r_q * A_tilde(y_i)`.
- **Effect:** This ensures that the magnitude of the advantage truly reflects the variability in rewards, preventing misleading optimization signals from tightly clustered rewards. RRB-Rescaling is compatible with any GRPO-based framework.

In summary, DIVA-GRPO constructs a dynamic learning environment where the model is continuously challenged with appropriately difficult variants, and the feedback signals are meticulously balanced and weighted to ensure stable, efficient, and targeted policy optimization for enhanced multimodal reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DIVA-GRPO, conducted on six diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, yielded compelling results that validate its effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness (RQ1)

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** DIVA-GRPO-7B achieved state-of-the-art performance among 7B-scale models across all six challenging benchmarks: MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, OlympiadBench, WeMath, and MMK12test. It delivered a consistently strong average score of 54.58.
- **Comparison to Larger Models:** Notably, DIVA-GRPO's performance on MathVista, MathVerse, and WeMath was on par with the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B model. It also substantially outperformed several open-source base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-32B, InternVL2.5-VL-78B) and even proprietary systems like GPT-4o and Claude 3.7-Sonnet, showcasing significant efficiency and cost-effectiveness at a smaller parameter count.
- **Improvement Over Backbone:** Compared to its backbone model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, DIVA-GRPO demonstrated an impressive average accuracy gain of 8.23 points across all benchmarks, underscoring its clear advantages over existing multimodal training frameworks.
- **Training Dynamics:** Visualizations of training dynamics (Figure 5) illustrated that DIVA-GRPO effectively maintained stable and informative advantage signals across varying difficulty levels as training progressed. In early stages, advantage distributions were concentrated, but as training advanced, the distribution expanded, ensuring meaningful signals even for high-difficulty samples, preventing collapse into "all-correct" or "all-wrong" states.

#### 4.2. Completeness and Component Ablation (RQ2)

- **Necessity of Components:** An ablation study on core components (Adaptive Variant Generation, Difficulty Weighting, RRB-Rescaling, and Global-Local Balance) revealed that removing any single component consistently led to a decrease in performance. The full DIVA-GRPO model achieved the highest accuracy, confirming that all components contribute complementary gains and are essential for optimal performance.
- **Superiority in Training:** DIVA-GRPO also demonstrated superior performance in terms of training speed and stability compared to other GRPO-based methods like GRPO, GSPO, and DAPO throughout the training process (Figure 3a).
- **Scalability:** Further experiments on Qwen3-VL-4B and 8B backbones confirmed that DIVA-GRPO's improvements generalize across different model sizes and architectures, showing average gains of 2.5 and 4.2 points over GRPO, respectively.

#### 4.3. Generalization of RRB-Rescaling (RQ3)

- **Broader Applicability:** The Reward-Range-Based Advantage Rescaling (RRB-Rescaling) component was evaluated independently by integrating it into a standard GRPO framework without other DIVA-GRPO modifications. This demonstrated that RRB-Rescaling alone improved average accuracy from 60.23% to 62.17% across benchmarks, enhancing both stability and performance in a general GRPO setting (Table 3, Figure 3b). This confirms its generalizability beyond DIVA-GRPO.

#### 4.4. Training Efficiency and Stability (RQ4)

- **Significant Speedup:** DIVA-GRPO achieved substantial speedups in training steps: 1.90x when ensuring a constant number of sampled sentences (N=1) and 2.55x when ensuring consistent input data requirements (N=3).
- **End-to-End Time Efficiency:** Even accounting for the time overhead of generating variants, DIVA-GRPO delivered end-to-end wall-clock time speedups of 1.76x (for N=1) and 1.56x (for N=3). This highlights its practical efficiency, making training faster and potentially more resource-effective.
- **Stable Advantage Signals:** The method consistently maintained stable and informative advantage signals throughout training, preventing the performance plateaus often seen in other GRPO methods when problems become too easy or hard.

In summary, DIVA-GRPO successfully addresses the critical challenges of reward sparsity and advantage vanishing in MLLM training. It achieves leading performance with smaller models, significantly accelerates training, and demonstrates robust generalization, positioning it as a highly effective and practical framework for advancing multimodal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DIVA-GRPO represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in enhancing their long-chain reasoning capabilities through reinforcement learning. Its contributions have several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Overcoming Core RL Limitations in MLLMs:** The paper directly tackles the pervasive problems of reward sparsity and advantage vanishing in GRPO-based MLLM training. These issues are fundamental roadblocks to stable and efficient learning, especially as models mature and exhibit varying proficiencies across tasks. By providing a principled solution, DIVA-GRPO enables MLLMs to learn more effectively from diverse multimodal data.

2. **Enhanced Reasoning Performance and Stability:** By ensuring a continuous stream of informative optimization signals, DIVA-GRPO significantly boosts the reasoning accuracy of MLLMs across a wide array of complex mathematical and logical tasks. The dynamic difficulty adjustment and balanced advantage estimation lead to more stable training, preventing performance plateaus and enabling models to push the boundaries of their reasoning abilities.

3. **Improved Training Efficiency and Cost-Effectiveness:** Achieving state-of-the-art performance with 7B-scale models, comparable to or surpassing much larger models and even proprietary systems, is a critical highlight. This efficiency means that high-performance multimodal reasoning can be achieved with fewer computational resources and a shorter training time. This has a profound impact on research accessibility, allowing more institutions and researchers to develop and experiment with advanced MLLMs, reducing the barrier to entry for cutting-edge AI research. The observed speedups in training steps and wall-clock time directly translate into reduced operational costs and faster development cycles.

4. **Generalizable Framework:** DIVA-GRPO is presented as a generalizable framework for improving reinforcement learning in MLLMs. Its core mechanisms, such as adaptive difficulty assessment and robust advantage estimation, are not overly specific to particular tasks or architectures. The demonstrated generalization of RRB-Rescaling to standard GRPO further underscores that individual components of DIVA-GRPO can benefit other GRPO-style training frameworks, fostering broader adoption and impact.

5. **Implications for Adaptive Learning and Curriculum Design:** The dynamic difficulty assessment mechanism offers a sophisticated form of curriculum learning, where the model is continuously challenged with problems at an appropriate difficulty level. This adaptive approach has broader implications for how AI models can learn more effectively and efficiently, not just in MLLMs but potentially in other complex RL domains.

6. **Bridging the Gap to Commercial Systems:** By enabling open-source 7B models to approach the performance of larger, proprietary commercial systems, DIVA-GRPO contributes to democratizing advanced AI capabilities. This reduces reliance on black-box models and promotes greater transparency and reproducibility in AI development.

In conclusion, DIVA-GRPO provides a robust, efficient, and theoretically sound solution to critical challenges in MLLM reinforcement learning. Its impact extends beyond mere performance gains, influencing the accessibility, stability, and future development trajectories of multimodal reasoning systems.
