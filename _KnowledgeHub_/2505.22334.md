# Advancing Multimodal Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning with Cold Start

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed a two-stage Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipeline to enhance multimodal reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models (MLLMs). Their 7B parameter model achieved an average score of 55.66 on multimodal mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming existing open-source MLLMs and some larger closed-source models.

## Problem

- The prevailing assumption that "aha moment" patterns in MLLMs are solely emergent properties of reinforcement learning (RL) and reliably correlate with reasoning improvement required re-evaluation.
- A lack of robust and systematically validated two-stage training methodologies (SFT + RL) for effectively enhancing complex multimodal reasoning in MLLMs.
- The need for improved state-of-the-art performance in open-source MLLMs on challenging multimodal reasoning tasks, particularly at more accessible parameter scales.

## Method

- A two-stage post-training process combining ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as a "cold start"== to establish foundational reasoning, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) to refine capabilities.
- Exploration of various ==multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data construction strategies== during SFT, including distillation from larger models, reflection, caption-enhancement, and self-critic CoT, to equip models with structured reasoning patterns.
- Utilization of the ==GRPO (Group-Normalized Policy Optimization) algorithm== in the RL stage, employing an accuracy-based reward function to activate and optimize multimodal reasoning.

## Results

- The SFT+RL pipeline consistently outperformed both SFT-only and RL-only approaches across challenging multimodal mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- The **7B** parameter model achieved a state-of-the-art average score of **55.66**, representing a **+6.19 point** improvement over the base model, and surpassed all other open-source 7B MLLMs, along with some larger closed-source models like GPT-4o (**51.82**) and Skywork R1V-38B (**51.95**).
- The **3B** parameter model achieved an average score of **50.84**, a **+10.84 point** improvement over its base, demonstrating performance competitive with or superior to several existing 7B models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Aha moment" patterns, often associated with self-correction, exist in MLLMs prior to RL training and do not consistently correlate with genuine reasoning improvement, sometimes leading to ineffective or hallucinatory reflections.
> - A strong Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) cold start, particularly with high-quality CoT data distilled from more capable teacher models, is foundational and directly correlates with better final reasoning performance after RL.
> - MLLMs can learn structural reasoning *formats* even from unjudged or incorrect CoT data, suggesting the importance of reasoning structure beyond the correctness of individual steps for initial training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated within the highly dynamic and competitive landscape of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs), particularly concerning their reasoning capabilities. Recent breakthroughs in LLMs, such as OpenAI's GPT series, have showcased remarkable chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, often attributing some of these advanced problem-solving strategies to emergent properties from reinforcement learning (RL) – specifically the concept of an "aha moment" where models self-correct.

The work directly builds upon and critically re-evaluates findings from influential text-only models like DeepSeek-R1, which demonstrated the emergence of "aha moments" during RL and the effectiveness of combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as a "cold start" with subsequent RL for enhancing reasoning. While these concepts have been explored in text-only domains, their systematic application and validation within the more complex multimodal realm, where models must process and integrate both visual and textual information, have been comparatively underexplored.

Several concurrent works have attempted to advance MLLM reasoning using various SFT and RL techniques. Some have focused on "Zero RL" (RL without a cold start), claiming that "aha moments" can emerge in MLLMs through this method and that it can even outperform SFT+RL combinations. Wei et al. critically engage with these prevailing assumptions. They challenge the direct correlation between the presence of "aha moment" patterns and genuine reasoning improvement in MLLMs, presenting empirical evidence that these patterns can exist prior to RL training and do not necessarily indicate enhanced problem-solving. This observation directly contradicts claims by some concurrent works (e.g., MM-Eureka, VLAA-Thinker, R1-Zero's "aha moment" in visual reasoning).

By systematically studying the impact of SFT as a cold start for RL in MLLMs, this paper positions itself as a crucial investigation into optimal training paradigms. It seeks to establish a more robust and effective methodology for MLLM reasoning, providing empirical guidance that contrasts with or refines the understanding presented in other contemporary research. The paper's contribution is therefore not just an incremental improvement but a foundational re-evaluation of how MLLMs acquire and refine complex reasoning skills, emphasizing the importance of a structured, two-stage training approach over emergent, less controllable phenomena.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to significantly **advance the multimodal reasoning capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models (MLLMs)**. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and research questions:

1. **Re-evaluating the "Aha Moment" in MLLMs:** A primary motivation stems from challenging the prevailing assumption that "aha moment" patterns—where models exhibit self-correction through reflection—are solely emergent properties of reinforcement learning (RL) in MLLMs and inherently correlate with improved reasoning. The authors observe that these patterns can exist in MLLMs even *prior* to RL training and, critically, do not always indicate genuine reasoning improvement, sometimes leading to ineffective or hallucinatory reflections. This motivates an empirical investigation to understand the true prevalence and effectiveness of such patterns.

2. **Developing a Robust Two-Stage Training Paradigm:** Motivated by the limitations of relying solely on emergent "aha moments" and the successes observed in text-only LLMs (like DeepSeek-R1), the authors aim to propose and validate a **two-stage training methodology**. This involves an initial **supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase serving as a "cold start"** to equip the model with structured chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning patterns, followed by a **reinforcement learning (RL) phase** to further refine and activate these capabilities. The objective is to demonstrate that this combined approach is more effective and consistent than SFT-only or RL-only methods.

3. **Systematic Investigation of Cold Start Strategies:** To optimize the proposed two-stage approach, the authors are motivated to conduct a comprehensive empirical study on **how different cold start strategies during SFT affect downstream RL performance in the multimodal domain**. This involves exploring various methods of constructing high-quality, reasoning-rich CoT data for the initial SFT phase, such as different distillation techniques, reflection-based CoT, caption-enhanced CoT, and self-critic CoT.

4. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance in Open-Source MLLMs:** Ultimately, the researchers aim to develop open-source MLLMs that achieve **state-of-the-art performance** on challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks, particularly at accessible parameter scales (3B and 7B). This objective not only validates their proposed training paradigm but also provides practical guidance and high-performing models for the broader research community, potentially narrowing the performance gap between smaller and larger (including closed-source) models.

In essence, the research is driven by a desire to move beyond potentially misleading emergent behaviors towards a more principled, data-driven, and systematically optimized approach for cultivating advanced reasoning in MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology lies in a **two-stage post-training process** designed to enhance multimodal reasoning in MLLMs, carefully integrating Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as a "cold start" with subsequent Reinforcement Learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Cold Start (Supervised Fine-Tuning)

The first stage, "cold start," focuses on equipping the base MLLM with strong foundational reasoning abilities through SFT. The authors argue that merely relying on RL to induce new reasoning strategies is insufficient.
- **Base Models:** The experiments are conducted using Qwen2.5-VL models at 3B and 7B parameter scales.
- **Training Datasets:** A diverse dataset of 50k examples is curated from established open-source resources, including Geometry3K, GeoQA, MathVista, We-Math, and other datasets covering chart understanding, scientific QA, and geometric reasoning.
- **Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Data Construction:** The key innovation in this stage is the exploration of various methods to generate structured CoT reasoning patterns via distillation, serving as diverse SFT cold start strategies:
 - **Distilled-CoT:** Leveraging larger, more capable teacher models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-32B) to generate CoT responses from a seed dataset using rejection sampling. This aims to distill high-quality reasoning pathways.
 - **Reflection-CoT (v1):** Combining incorrect responses with corrected ones, interspersed with reflective phrases like "Wait, perhaps we could consider it from a different perspective. Let’s re-evaluate..." This attempts to embed a self-correction mechanism.
 - **Reflection-CoT (v2):** Specifically collecting *correct* responses that *naturally exhibit* "aha moment"-like reflection patterns from a powerful teacher model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B), to directly test the efficacy of such patterns.
 - **Caption-CoT:** Encouraging detailed visual focus by first generating a caption for the image, then concatenating it with the correct reasoning response.
 - **Self-Critic-CoT:** Implementing an iterative reasoning structure where the model generates a draft, critically evaluates it with detailed comments, and then produces a refined final output, mimicking human self-correction.
- **SFT Training Details:** The `ms-swift` framework is used for SFT over 3 epochs with a learning rate of 1x10⁻⁵.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning

Following the SFT cold start, the second stage employs RL to further activate and refine the model's multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- **Algorithm:** The **GRPO (Group-Normalized Policy Optimization)** algorithm is utilized. GRPO is chosen for its computational efficiency, as it eliminates the need for a separate value model by directly using group-normalized rewards to estimate advantages.
- **Objective Function:** The GRPO objective maximizes the policy by sampling groups of responses and estimating advantages based on the difference between an individual response's reward and the group's average reward, normalized by the standard deviation. A KL divergence constraint is included to prevent excessive deviation from a reference policy.
- **Reward Function:** An accuracy reward is used, assigning +1 for a correct final answer and 0 for an incorrect one.
- **RL Training Details:** The `EasyR1` framework is adopted for GRPO training, with 2 training episodes and a learning rate of 1x10⁻⁶. The same dataset used in the cold start phase is employed for RL.

#### 3.3. Evaluation and Ablation Studies

- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Models are rigorously evaluated on four prominent multimodal mathematical reasoning benchmarks: MathVision, MathVista, MathVerse, and We-Math. These benchmarks cover diverse problem types (geometry, charts, tables) and varying difficulty levels.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Models are prompted to place answers in a designated box, and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is used to evaluate correctness against ground truth.
- **Ablation Studies:** Extensive ablation studies are conducted on Qwen2.5-VL-3B to systematically investigate:
 - The comparative effectiveness of SFT-only, RL-only, and SFT+RL.
 - The impact of different cold-start CoT data construction strategies (e.g., Distilled-CoT from 7B vs. 32B teachers, Caption-CoT, Reflection-CoT, Self-Critic-CoT).
 - Directly revisiting the "aha moment" by comparing training on Reflection-CoT (v2) data versus randomly selected distilled data.
 - The influence of SFT data quality (correct, wrong, or unjudged responses) on final performance.

This comprehensive methodology, combining structured data creation, a two-stage training pipeline, and thorough empirical validation through ablation studies, forms a robust approach to understanding and enhancing MLLM reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, challenging existing assumptions and demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed methodology:

1. **Challenging the "Aha Moment" Narrative:**
 - The paper empirically demonstrates that "aha moment" patterns (reflective phrases like "re-evaluate") already exist in MLLMs *before* any RL training.
 - Crucially, the presence or increased frequency of these patterns does *not* necessarily correlate with improved reasoning capabilities. In some cases (e.g., MM-EUREKA, VLAA-Thinker), while the frequency of "aha moments" increased with GRPO, the associated accuracy actually *dropped* (Figure 2).
 - Case studies further illustrate that these reflective patterns can be ineffective, misleading, or even introduce hallucinations rather than improving solution quality (Appendix D.1).
 - A targeted ablation study showed that training on "aha moment"-infused data (Reflection-CoT v2) *underperformed* models trained on randomly selected correct data (Figure 4), directly undermining the idea that these patterns intrinsically lead to advanced reasoning.

2. **Superiority of the SFT+RL Two-Stage Approach:**
 - Extensive experiments consistently show that the combined SFT-based cold start followed by RL significantly outperforms both SFT-only and RL-only approaches across challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks (Table 3).
 - For the 3B model, SFT alone improved the average score from 40.00 (base) to 49.46, while RL alone achieved 48.79. The combination reached 50.84.
 - Similarly, for the 7B model, SFT alone improved the average from 49.47 to 53.62, RL alone to 55.10, and the combination achieved the highest score of 55.66.
 - This highlights that SFT provides a strong foundation by equipping the model with structured reasoning patterns, which RL then effectively refines and activates.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Open-Source MLLMs:**
 - The models trained with the proposed SFT+RL paradigm achieve state-of-the-art performance among open-source MLLMs at both 3B and 7B parameter scales (Figure 1, Table 1).
 - The 7B model achieved an average score of 55.66, representing a substantial +6.19 point improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (49.47). It surpassed all other open-source 7B models and even outperformed some larger closed-source models like GPT-4o (51.82) and Skywork R1V-38B (51.95).
 - The 3B model achieved an average score of 50.84, a remarkable +10.84 point improvement over its base (40.00). Notably, this 3B model's performance was competitive with, or even superior to, several existing 7B models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VLAA-Thinker-7B).

4. **Impact of Cold Start Strategies and Data Quality:**
 - The choice of cold-start SFT data construction strategy significantly impacts final performance (Table 4). Distillation from a stronger teacher model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) yielded the best results for both SFT and subsequent RL. This implies that the quality and scale of the teacher model are crucial.
 - A strong positive correlation was observed between the performance of the cold start (SFT) stage and the final model performance after RL, emphasizing the foundational importance of high-quality initial training.
 - Intriguingly, the ablation studies on SFT data quality (Table 5) revealed that even training on *unjudged* or *incorrect* CoT data still led to improvements over the base model, particularly after subsequent RL. While correct data yielded the best results, this finding suggests that models can learn structural reasoning *format* independently of the correctness of individual reasoning steps, underscoring the importance of reasoning structure.

5. **Increase in Effective Rank:**
 - Post-training (SFT and RL) consistently led to an increase in "Effective Rank" for both 3B and 7B models across all benchmarks (Table 2). Effective Rank correlates with the amount of knowledge a model comprehends, indicating that the training process successfully injected additional relevant knowledge into the models, thereby enhancing their understanding and reasoning capabilities.

In summary, the paper decisively argues for a structured SFT+RL approach as superior for MLLM reasoning, debunks the simplistic correlation of "aha moments" with improved ability, and delivers new state-of-the-art results for open-source models, especially highlighting the impressive gains at smaller parameter scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "Advancing Multimodal Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning with Cold Start" carry substantial significance and potential impact for the development and understanding of MLLMs:

1. **Reframing MLLM Reasoning Development:** The most profound impact is the **critical re-evaluation of emergent "aha moments"**. By demonstrating that these reflective patterns often exist pre-RL and do not necessarily correlate with enhanced reasoning, the paper shifts the focus from passively waiting for emergent properties to a more deliberate and structured approach. This challenges a prevalent narrative in recent MLLM research and calls for more rigorous analysis of "emergent" behaviors.

2. **Providing Practical and Scalable Training Guidance:** The research offers **clear, empirically-backed practical guidance** for building advanced multimodal reasoning models. The consistent superiority of the two-stage SFT+RL pipeline over individual components establishes it as a robust and effective training paradigm. This is particularly valuable for the open-source community, where optimizing training efficiency and effectiveness for state-of-the-art results is crucial.

3. **Narrowing the Performance Gap for Smaller Models:** The remarkable performance of the 3B model, which achieved results competitive with or even surpassing several 7B models, demonstrates the **potential for scalable training strategies to make advanced MLLM reasoning more accessible**. This is a significant step towards democratizing high-performance AI, allowing researchers and developers with limited computational resources to build capable models. It suggests that architectural scale is not the only determinant of performance, and sophisticated training regimens can unlock latent capabilities in smaller models.

4. **Highlighting the Importance of Structured CoT Data:** The extensive ablation studies on various CoT data construction strategies underscore the **critical role of high-quality SFT data as a "cold start."** The finding that a strong SFT foundation directly correlates with better downstream RL performance emphasizes that investing in well-structured, reasoning-rich supervision is paramount. The unexpected observation that models can learn reasoning *format* even from unjudged or incorrect data is also impactful, suggesting new avenues for data augmentation and training, where the structure of thought might be as important as its immediate correctness.

5. **Advancing Multimodal Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks:** By achieving new state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks like MathVision, MathVista, MathVerse, and We-Math, the paper **raises the bar for MLLM capabilities** in complex mathematical reasoning involving visual inputs. This pushes the frontier of what MLLMs can achieve and provides strong baselines for future research.

6. **Contribution to Open Science:** The public availability of the code fosters **reproducibility and accelerates further research and development** in the field. This commitment to open science allows others to build upon these findings, validate the methodology, and explore new directions, maximizing the overall impact of the work.

In conclusion, Wei et al.'s work is a pivotal contribution that not only delivers concrete performance improvements but also critically informs the scientific understanding of how complex reasoning emerges and can be effectively cultivated in multimodal AI systems, paving the way for more reliable and efficient MLLM development.
