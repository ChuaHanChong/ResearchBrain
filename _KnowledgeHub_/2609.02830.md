# Toward Robust LiDAR Semantic Segmentation for Real-World Deployment: Evaluation under Coarse Labels, Adverse Conditions, and Domain Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified evaluation protocol judges deployment readiness of LiDAR semantic segmentation across three axes: safety-oriented coarse-label taxonomies, eight synthetic sensor corruptions, and unadapted cross-dataset transfer, with runtime measured on an embedded Jetson AGX Orin. Ten retrained models spanning point, projection, sparse-convolution and fusion families show clean-benchmark rankings poorly predict safety-critical accuracy, corruption robustness, or domain generalization.

## Problem

- Standard benchmarks score in-distribution accuracy on clean scans with fine taxonomies, penalizing safety-irrelevant confusions and leaving real deployment readiness essentially unmeasured.
- Existing protocols each cover one axis only: corruption robustness, coarse labels, or domain transfer, so no unified view of accuracy-robustness-generalization trade-offs exists.
- Prior evaluations ignore embedded compute limits, reporting throughput on datacenter GPUs rather than the latency budget an autonomous platform actually imposes.

## Method

- Coarse-label pillar adopts the ==COLA taxonomy==, merging classes into seven safety-functional groups so intra-group confusions are forgiven while cross-group errors remain penalized.
- Robustness pillar evaluates on Robo3D's ==nuScenes-C== and ==SemanticKITTI-C==, eight corruptions in atmospheric, geometric and sensor groups, scored by ==mean Corruption Error== against a ==MinkowskiNet== baseline.
- Domain-generalization pillar trains on nuScenes or SemanticKITTI and tests unadapted on ==ParisLuco3D==, isolating appearance/scene shift from combined sensor shift; all models retrained under one unified schedule.

## Results

- Coarse labels lift every model but unevenly: FRNet tops fine-grained SemanticKITTI mIoU at **66.0%** yet reaches only **83.3%** coarse, dragged by **55.6%** on living-being versus MinkowskiNet's **83.5%**.
- All methods degrade under corruption; best mCE reaches **99.5%** (KPConv, FRNet), while HARP-NeXt pairs top clean **77.1%** mIoU with **105.7%** mCE at **14.1 FPS** on Jetson AGX Orin.
- Cross-domain transfer collapses: best ParisLuco3D scores are **32.5%** (nuScenes source) and **33.3%** (SemanticKITTI source), with CENet and FRNet dropping to near-zero **2.9%** and **3.4%** under sensor shift.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggregate benchmark accuracy hides safety-critical failure; per-category scores on vulnerable road users should gate deployment decisions rather than headline mIoU.
> - Architecture determines failure mode: voxel and point representations preserve geometry under shift, while range-image projections overfit source sensor sampling patterns.
> - Robustness and throughput trade off sharply, so efficiency must be measured on the target embedded hardware, not on datacenter GPUs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

LiDAR-based semantic segmentation is a fundamental perception component for autonomous vehicles and mobile robots. Recent advancements in deep learning have enabled numerous methods to achieve high performance on established benchmarks such as SemanticKITTI and nuScenes. These benchmarks typically evaluate models on clean data, using fine-grained label taxonomies within single-domain settings and standard train/validation/test splits.

However, the authors identify a gap between this benchmark-driven progress and the requirements for real-world deployment. Autonomous systems operate in complex and variable environments, necessitating perception models that exhibit robustness to sensor degradation, generalize across diverse environments, adhere to safety-critical label semantics, and operate efficiently on embedded hardware. Existing evaluation protocols have addressed some of these aspects individually: the Robo3D benchmark focuses on corruption robustness, the COLA framework on coarse-label evaluation and cross-dataset comparison, and various studies on domain generalization. Despite these efforts, a unified evaluation framework that assesses all these dimensions concurrently has been absent.

This paper positions itself by proposing a comprehensive evaluation protocol designed to bridge this gap. It aims to provide a more holistic assessment of deployment readiness by integrating safety-oriented label semantics, robustness to sensor degradations, cross-domain generalization, and computational efficiency into a single, structured framework. This unified approach moves beyond fragmented evaluations, offering a more complete view of how models perform under conditions relevant to real-world autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the deployment readiness of LiDAR semantic segmentation models for real-world autonomous systems. The authors are motivated by the observation that despite strong performance on conventional benchmarks, current evaluation practices do not adequately reflect the challenges encountered in practical applications.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed through the three complementary pillars of their proposed evaluation protocol:

- **Motivation for Coarse-Label Evaluation:** Standard benchmarks use fine-grained label taxonomies (e.g., distinguishing between different types of vehicles), which can lead to strict evaluations that penalize semantically reasonable predictions from a safety and operational perspective. For instance, misclassifying a "car" as a "truck" might significantly reduce Intersection-over-Union (IoU) scores in fine-grained evaluation, but could have minimal impact on autonomous driving decisions (e.g., similar avoidance behavior). The objective is to assess performance under label groupings aligned with autonomous driving safety priorities, analyzing how label granularity affects different methods and to what extent benchmark outcomes are influenced by annotation detail. This aims to provide insights into semantic consistency and safety relevance.

- **Motivation for Robustness Evaluation under Real-World LiDAR Corruptions:** In real-world environments, LiDAR sensors are subject to various degradations (e.g., fog, rain, motion blur, sensor noise) that affect point cloud quality. Current benchmarks often rely on clean data, which does not represent these adverse conditions. The objective is to systematically measure model performance under eight types of synthetic LiDAR corruptions designed to emulate realistic atmospheric, geometric, and sensor-induced degradations. This evaluation aims to characterize architectural-dependent robustness, quantify performance degradation, and assess the trade-off between robustness and inference speed on embedded hardware, crucial for real-time operation.

- **Motivation for Domain Generalization Evaluation:** Autonomous vehicles operate across diverse geographical locations and environmental conditions. Models trained on one dataset may not perform reliably in a new, unseen domain without adaptation, due to shifts in sensor characteristics, environmental appearance, and scene composition. The objective is to evaluate the ability of models trained on a source dataset to operate effectively on a previously unseen target dataset without any adaptation. This measures the extent of performance degradation under cross-domain variability and assesses the inherent robustness of methods to distribution shifts, which is critical for scalable deployment.

Additionally, an overarching objective is to measure **inference speed on embedded hardware (Jetson AGX Orin)** alongside other metrics. This directly reflects the computational and latency constraints of real-world autonomous platforms, providing a practical dimension to deployment readiness that is often overlooked in academic benchmarks.

Through these objectives, the research seeks to expose discrepancies between benchmark performance and real-world deployment readiness, and to establish a protocol for more practically grounded evaluation of LiDAR semantic segmentation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes a unified evaluation protocol to assess LiDAR semantic segmentation models across three dimensions pertinent to real-world deployment, complemented by computational efficiency measurements. A set of representative state-of-the-art methods, encompassing point-based, projection-based, sparse convolution-based, and fusion-based architectures, were selected for evaluation. These included KPConv, WaffleIron, PTv3, SalsaNext, CENet, FRNet, Minkowski, Cylinder3D, SPVCNN, and HARP-NeXt. To ensure a fair comparison, all models were retrained from scratch using a unified training protocol on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. This protocol involved the AdamW optimizer, WarmupCosine scheduler, and a combination of CrossEntropy, Lovász-Softmax, and Boundary loss (the latter for projection-based methods only). Standard point cloud augmentations (rotation, flipping, scaling) were applied, along with instance-level augmentations (PolarMix, Instance CutMix) for SemanticKITTI to address class imbalance for rare categories. Inference speed was measured on both an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU for reference and an embedded NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin to reflect deployment constraints, encompassing both data pre-processing and network inference.

The evaluation protocol is structured as follows:

- **A. Coarse-Label Evaluation for Safety Assessment:**
 - **Framework:** The COarse-LAbel (COLA) framework was adopted.
 - **Taxonomy:** Fine-grained class labels from nuScenes and SemanticKITTI were grouped into seven coarse, safety-oriented categories: Driveable Ground, Other Ground, Structure, Vehicle, Nature, Living Being, and Object. This re-categorization ensures that segmentations are evaluated based on their functional relevance for autonomous navigation.
 - **Metrics:** Performance was assessed using both fine-grained mIoU (mIoU\_FG) and coarse-label mIoU (mIoU\_COLA). Inference speed on the NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU was also reported.
 - **Analysis:** The study analyzed the performance change when transitioning from fine- to coarse-grained taxonomies to understand the impact of label granularity and reveal underlying semantic consistency.

- **B. Robustness Under Real-World LiDAR Corruptions:**
 - **Corrupted Datasets:** Models trained on clean data were evaluated on nuScenes-C and SemanticKITTI-C from the Robo3D benchmark, which include synthetic corruptions. No fine-tuning on corrupted data was performed to assess out-of-distribution robustness.
 - **Corruption Types:** Eight distinct corruption types were categorized into three groups:
 - **Atmospheric:** Fog, Wet Ground, Snow (emulating environmental effects on laser propagation).
 - **Geometric:** Motion Blur, Beam Missing (simulating motion dynamics and sensor beam dropouts).
 - **Sensor:** LiDAR Crosstalk, Incomplete Echo, Cross-Sensor (representing internal sensor artifacts or hardware variations).
 - **Metric:** The mean Corruption Error (mCE) was used as the primary robustness metric, normalized against MinkowskiNet as a baseline.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Inference speed was measured on the Jetson AGX Orin. Real-time operation was defined as less than 100 ms per scan for SemanticKITTI (>10 FPS) and less than 50 ms per scan for nuScenes (>20 FPS), corresponding to the acquisition frequencies of the respective LiDAR sensors.

- **C. Domain Generalization Evaluation:**
 - **Protocol:** A single-source to single-target protocol was used, where models were trained on a source dataset and evaluated directly on an unseen target dataset without any adaptation.
 - **Datasets:** nuScenes and SemanticKITTI served as source datasets, and ParisLuco3D was used as the target dataset. ParisLuco3D was selected due to its differences in geographic environment, acquisition conditions, and scene composition compared to the source datasets.
 - **Evaluation Scenarios:**
 - **nuScenes (source) → ParisLuco3D (target):** This scenario evaluates generalization under appearance and scene shifts, with identical LiDAR sensor characteristics (Velodyne HDL-32E) for both datasets.
 - **SemanticKITTI (source) → ParisLuco3D (target):** This scenario introduces an additional sensor shift (Velodyne HDL-64E for SemanticKITTI vs. Velodyne HDL-32E for ParisLuco3D), alongside appearance and scene shifts, presenting a more challenging generalization task.
 - **Metric:** Evaluation was performed using the native fine-grained label taxonomy of each source dataset, providing per-class IoU scores that reflect the full impact of domain shift.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation protocol revealed distinct performance characteristics and trade-offs among the evaluated LiDAR semantic segmentation methods across the three dimensions.

**A. Coarse-Label Evaluation:**
All methods exhibited a consistent improvement in mIoU when transitioning from fine-grained (mIoU\_FG) to coarse-label (mIoU\_COLA) evaluation on both nuScenes and SemanticKITTI. This indicated that many fine-grained misclassifications were within broader semantic categories and did not always reflect safety-critical failures.

- **On nuScenes:** Point-based methods, particularly PTv3, achieved high coarse-label performance (87.8% mIoU\_COLA), leading across key categories like vehicle and living-beings. Fusion-based HARP-NeXt also performed strongly (86.7%) while demonstrating high throughput (100 FPS). Point-based methods, however, incurred high computational costs (e.g., PTv3 at 4.1 FPS), limiting real-time applicability.
- **On SemanticKITTI:** WaffleIron, Minkowski, and SPVCNN showed high coarse-label performance (e.g., WaffleIron at 87.4%). Notably, FRNet, despite having the highest fine-grained mIoU (66.0%), obtained a comparatively lower coarse-label performance (83.3%) due to a lower score on the safety-critical "living-being" category (55.6%). In contrast, voxel-based methods like Minkowski and SPVCNN achieved substantially higher living-being scores (83.5% and 83.2%, respectively). This suggests that high overall fine-grained accuracy does not guarantee robust performance on all safety-critical classes. Fusion-based HARP-NeXt offered a balance between strong performance (86.2%) and high efficiency (76.9 FPS). The results underscored that while models are generally resilient to label granularity changes, performance on safety-critical classes can vary significantly across architectures, with implications for real-world deployment.

**B. Robustness Under Real-World LiDAR Corruptions:**
All methods experienced performance degradation under the tested corruptions, with sensor-related corruptions proving particularly challenging across the board. A clear robustness-efficiency trade-off was observed.

- **On nuScenes-C:** Sparse convolution-based methods demonstrated strong robustness to geometric corruptions (MinkowskiNet), attributed to their spatial aggregation within voxelized representations. FRNet achieved the lowest mCE among non-baseline methods (103.7%) and exhibited improved robustness across atmospheric and sensor corruptions, suggesting that retaining point-level features beyond projected representations contributes to resilience. HARP-NeXt balanced high clean mIoU (77.1%) with competitive robustness (105.7% mCE) and a real-time inference speed of 14.1 FPS on the Jetson AGX Orin, faster than other high-performing methods.
- **On SemanticKITTI-C:** The robustness gap was more pronounced. Point-based methods like KPConv achieved the lowest mCE (99.5%) and highest atmospheric corruption average, indicating resilience to point density and geometric structure perturbations. FRNet also achieved 99.5% mCE and the best geometric group average, but at a low inference speed (2.5 FPS). SalsaNext provided high throughput (9.2 FPS) but suffered substantial degradation. HARP-NeXt, while efficient (8.3 FPS), showed greater degradation on SemanticKITTI-C compared to nuScenes-C, suggesting sensitivity to fine-grained geometric structure affecting its fusion-based approach. The denser point clouds of SemanticKITTI led to increased sensitivity to geometric and sensor degradations. The findings indicate that current state-of-the-art methods often overfit clean data and that improving robustness for noisy real-world scenarios remains an open challenge.

**C. Domain Generalization Evaluation:**
Domain generalization proved challenging, indicating current approaches are insufficient for reliable real-world deployment.

- **nuScenes (source) → ParisLuco3D (target):** Despite identical sensor configurations, overall performance was limited (Minkowski and SPVCNN leading at 32.5% and 32.1% mIoU, respectively). This indicated that appearance and scene variations alone significantly degrade performance. Sparse convolution-based methods generally performed best, suggesting geometry-preserving voxel representations offer improved robustness to domain shift. Small or rare classes (e.g., bicycle, traffic cone) were poorly segmented across methods.
- **SemanticKITTI (source) → ParisLuco3D (target):** This scenario, introducing both sensor and scene shifts, was more challenging. Sparse convolution-based methods, Minkowski (33.3% mIoU) and SPVCNN (31.5% mIoU), again led. HARP-NeXt achieved a modest 18.8% mIoU, but notably outperformed projection-based CENet and FRNet which showed near-zero performance. This was attributed to HARP-NeXt's architectural design (depth-wise separable convolutions) fostering more channel-specific representations. Interestingly, KPConv and Cylinder3D showed better generalization from SemanticKITTI, suggesting their reliance on local geometric structure benefited from its richer geometric supervision. The results underscore that generalization is influenced by sensor type, environmental context, and architectural design, highlighting the need for sensor-invariant architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of LiDAR semantic segmentation by providing a unified evaluation protocol that moves beyond conventional benchmark metrics to assess deployment readiness for real-world autonomous systems. The work has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Exposing Gaps in Current Evaluation Practices:** The findings reveal a disconnect between high performance on standard, clean benchmarks and actual reliability in deployment scenarios. Models that achieve top rankings on fine-grained metrics may underperform on safety-critical classes, exhibit substantial degradation under adverse conditions (corruptions), or fail to generalize across different domains. This highlights that existing evaluation protocols provide an incomplete view of model suitability for practical applications.

- **Establishing a Comprehensive Evaluation Framework:** The proposed protocol integrates crucial dimensions—safety-oriented label semantics, robustness to corruptions, domain generalization, and computational efficiency on embedded hardware—into a single, reproducible framework. This provides a more holistic and practically grounded method for assessing LiDAR semantic segmentation models, encouraging researchers to consider real-world constraints from the outset.

- **Guiding Future Model Design:** The detailed analysis of architectural characteristics and their performance across various challenging conditions offers insights for future model development. For instance, the superior performance of voxel-based methods in certain robustness and generalization tasks, or the trade-offs observed with projection-based and fusion-based methods, can inform the design of more resilient and efficient architectures. Specifically, the challenges identified in segmenting safety-critical "living-being" classes and the limited robustness to sensor-related corruptions and domain shifts indicate areas requiring focused architectural innovation and training strategies (e.g., sensor-invariant designs, advanced data augmentation).

- **Promoting Realistic Benchmarking:** By providing a reference protocol for more practically grounded evaluation, this work can foster a shift in the research community towards more realistic benchmarking. This could lead to the development of models that are not only accurate in ideal conditions but also robust, generalizable, and efficient enough for reliable integration into autonomous platforms.

- **Informing Deployment Decisions:** For industries developing autonomous vehicles and mobile robots, the protocol offers a more rigorous method to assess and select perception models. It allows practitioners to quantify trade-offs (e.g., between accuracy, robustness, and speed) directly relevant to their deployment constraints, thereby improving safety and reliability in operational systems.

In summary, this research provides a critical step towards bridging the gap between academic benchmarks and real-world deployment challenges in LiDAR semantic segmentation. Its significance lies in both exposing the limitations of current practices and offering a structured path forward for developing and evaluating more robust and deployable autonomous perception systems.
