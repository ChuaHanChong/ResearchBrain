# DexFormer: Cross-Embodied Dexterous Manipulation via History-Conditioned Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> DexFormer, developed by researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and Wuhan University, introduces a history-conditioned Transformer policy that achieves zero-shot cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation by implicitly inferring hand morphology from temporal observation-action histories. The single, morphology-agnostic policy demonstrates superior grasping success rates and real-world adaptability across diverse and unseen robotic hands.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation policies typically require extensive training and tuning for each new multi-fingered robotic hand due to embodiment variability in kinematics, inertias, and dynamics.
- Existing approaches struggle to achieve truly generalist policies, often relying on explicit kinematic models, retargeting, or extensive real-world data collection for each specific hand.
- The high cost and time investment in adapting control strategies for different hand designs hinder the widespread adoption of multi-fingered robots.

## Method

- A history-conditioned Transformer architecture is employed, using a finite window of past observation-action pairs to implicitly infer a hand's morphology and dynamics during execution.
- A shared canonical D_F-dimensional finger action space is defined, enabling a single policy to control heterogeneous hands with varying degrees of freedom through an embodiment-specific embedding operator.
- Large-scale procedural randomization generates diverse morphological variants of canonical hands during training, combined with a distributed training framework for efficient learning of a generalizable policy.

## Results

- DexFormer achieved an average combined grasping success rate of 77.50% across canonical and 32 unseen hand variants, significantly outperforming LSTM (58.72%) and GRU (44.29%) baselines.
- Incorporating a 5-step observation-action history generally improved zero-shot performance on unseen LEAP and Allegro hands, confirming the utility of temporal context for morphology inference.
- The policy successfully transferred to a real-world LEAP hand, including variants with physically removed finger joint structures, demonstrating practical viability and adaptability to unforeseen morphological changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit inference of hand characteristics from temporal context is effective, allowing a single policy to adapt to unknown morphologies without explicit identifiers or separate per-embodiment components.
> - Training with a broad distribution of procedurally randomized hand embodiments is crucial for achieving robust zero-shot generalization to unseen and heterogeneous robotic hands.
> - A shared action space, combined with low-level embodiment-specific controllers, provides a scalable method to unify control across fundamentally different robotic hand designs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation, involving multi-fingered robotic hands, is a critical yet profoundly challenging area in robotics due to the high degrees of freedom (DoF), complex contact physics, and the inherent variability across different robotic hand designs. The fundamental problem DexFormer tackles is **embodiment variability**: the fact that different dexterous hands possess distinct kinematics, inertias, actuator responses, and contact dynamics. This variability typically necessitates training separate policies for each hand or employing complex adaptation mechanisms, making the deployment of dexterous robots labor-intensive and expensive.

The existing landscape of addressing the sim-to-real gap and cross-embodiment transfer can be broadly categorized into three paradigms:
1. **System Identification (SysID):** This involves meticulously fitting simulator parameters to real hardware. While effective, SysID is specific to each embodiment and limited by the chosen model parameterization, making it non-scalable for diverse hardware.
2. **Domain Randomization:** Policies are trained over a wide distribution of physical and morphological parameters, aiming for the real world to appear as merely another sample from the training distribution. Approaches like those by OpenAI have shown remarkable success in sim-to-real dexterous manipulation. However, randomization alone does not provide an explicit mechanism for dynamic compensation at test time.
3. **Residual or Compensatory Models:** These methods learn models for unmodeled dynamics, often from real-world interaction data, to bridge the sim-to-real gap. While effective in improving robustness, they still require embodiment-specific real data collection, hindering zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer.

Within the more specific field of **cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation**, prior works have explored:
- **Static grasp pose generation:** These open-loop methods struggle with disturbances and real-time contact changes.
- **Embodiment-aware closed-loop controllers:** Some approaches explicitly encode hand kinematic graphs (e.g., GET-ZERO) or use retargeting mapping to a shared action space (e.g., Yuan et al.). These, however, depend on explicit kinematic modeling or retargeting, which can be difficult to scale across fundamentally different hand types.

DexFormer distinguishes itself by proposing a novel approach that synergistically combines the strengths of large-scale domain randomization with history-conditioned learning, drawing inspiration from **test-time adaptation** techniques like Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) and LocoFormer. Unlike RMA, which learns a separate adaptation module, or prior cross-embodiment methods that rely on explicit kinematic models, DexFormer trains a *single, morphology-agnostic policy* directly conditioned on temporal observation-action histories. This allows the policy to *implicitly infer* embodiment-specific morphology and dynamics *on the fly*, without explicit identifiers or separate decoder heads.

By constructing a broad morphology distribution through procedural randomization of canonical hands and training a transformer-based policy on this diverse set, DexFormer aims to acquire a generalizable manipulation prior. This positions DexFormer as a significant step towards achieving truly generalist dexterous manipulation policies that can adapt zero-shot to previously unseen and heterogeneous robotic hands. It pushes the boundary beyond merely robust sim-to-real transfer for a single robot or specific adaptations, moving towards "foundation-style models" for robotic manipulation that can generalize across an entire family of embodiments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DexFormer stems from the inherent challenge of **embodiment variability** in dexterous manipulation. Current robotic systems typically require extensive training and tuning for each new multi-fingered hand embodiment, making development and deployment costly and time-consuming. This is because distinct hands possess unique kinematics, inertias, actuator responses, and contact dynamics, meaning a control strategy effective for one hand may fail drastically on another. The authors identify this as a major barrier to achieving general-purpose robotic manipulation.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of the DexFormer project are:

1. **Develop a unified, morphology-agnostic policy:** The central goal is to create a single, end-to-end policy capable of controlling diverse dexterous hands without needing separate policies, explicit morphology identifiers, or per-embodiment decoder heads.
2. **Achieve zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer:** The policy should be able to generalize immediately to unseen canonical hands (e.g., Allegro, LEAP, RAPID) and their variants, eliminating the need for retraining or fine-tuning when deploying on a new hand.
3. **Leverage temporal context for implicit inference:** The policy should infer morphology and dynamics on the fly from historical observations. This implicit adaptation mechanism is crucial for handling the unknown characteristics of new embodiments during deployment.
4. **Establish a scalable training framework:** By combining large-scale procedural morphology randomization with distributed reinforcement learning, the authors aim to create a scalable foundation for learning generalist dexterous manipulation policies.
5. **Demonstrate practical viability:** Validate the approach through extensive simulation experiments and, crucially, through real-world deployment on physical dexterous hands, showcasing the policy's ability to transfer from simulation to reality.

In essence, DexFormer seeks to revolutionize dexterous manipulation by offering a truly generalizable and adaptive control solution that dramatically reduces the engineering overhead associated with different robotic hand designs, thereby accelerating the adoption of multi-fingered robots in complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexFormer introduces a novel methodology centered on a **history-conditioned Transformer** architecture, designed to enable cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation by implicitly inferring hand morphology and dynamics.

**Problem Formulation:** The problem is framed as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The robotic hand must grasp and manipulate objects, with each episode sampling an object and a hand embodiment. Crucially, the policy receives observations but *does not* explicitly observe the embodiment identity. The objective is to maximize discounted reward over the joint distribution of embodiments and objects, forcing the agent to infer morphology-dependent dynamics through temporal interaction.

**Shared Action Space:** To accommodate heterogeneous hands with varying DoFs, a **canonical D_F-dimensional finger action space** is defined. Joints with similar anatomical functions (e.g., thumb abduction, index flexion) are mapped to fixed indices. An embodiment-specific embedding operator `P_e` maps the hand's native `d_e`-dimensional command into this canonical space, zero-padding unused dimensions for lower-DoF hands. This canonical finger action is combined with the robotic arm's delta pose (`a_A`) to form a high-level action. This high-level action is then consumed by embodiment-specific low-level controllers, which ignore zero-padded dimensions.

**Embodiment Generation:** To train a generalist policy, a diverse set of hand embodiments is crucial. The authors start with **canonical hands** (Allegro, LEAP, RAPID) and then procedurally generate `N_e` **randomized variants** for each. This randomization perturbs morphology-related physical parameters such as link lengths, masses, and inertias, while preserving the kinematic graph topology and actuation structure. This creates a "morphology-rich" training set (`E_train`), exposing the policy to a continuum of physical realizations and promoting robustness.

**History-Conditioned Transformer (DexFormer Architecture):**
The core of the policy is a Transformer encoder. At each timestep `t`, a **finite history window `h_t`** (comprising past observation-action pairs and the current observation) is provided as input.
1. **Tokenization:** Observations for each timestep within the history window are embedded to create a sequence of `H` temporal tokens.
2. **Transformer Encoder:** This sequence is fed into a stack of Transformer layers. **Positional encodings** are added to capture temporal order. A **causal mask** is applied to the self-attention mechanism, ensuring that the computation for any token only considers preceding tokens, preventing information leakage from the future.
3. **Action Head:** The final embedding, corresponding to the current timestep `t`, serves as a compact summary of the history and implicitly encodes morphology and dynamics. This embedding is then passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) action head to produce the action distribution.

**Observation Space:** The policy's observation `o_t` is comprehensive, including:
- Object quaternion (`o_OC`) and target object pose (`o_OT`).
- Previous action (`a_t-1`).
- Hand joint positions (`o_JP`) and velocities (`o_JV`).
- Fingertip and palm states (`o_FT`), contact forces (`o_FC`).
- Object pointcloud (`o_V`) obtained via a ray-casting pipeline. This rich observation space allows the policy to gather sufficient information for implicit inference.

**Reward Design:** A composite reward function `r_t` is used to guide learning, comprising five terms:
- `r_P`: Penalizes large actions and action changes for smooth control.
- `r_D`: Encourages the end-effector to approach the object.
- `r_M`: Rewards grasping the object, based on contact forces.
- `r_T`: Guides object pose tracking *under contact*.
- `r_S`: Guides object pose tracking *without contact*.

**Parallelism for Cross-Embodiment Training:** A custom distributed training framework is developed to handle the morphological diversity efficiently in Isaac Lab. Due to Isaac Lab's requirement for similar kinematic topology within a GPU, randomized variants of the *same* canonical embodiment are grouped on a single GPU. *Morphologically distinct canonical embodiments* (e.g., LEAP, Allegro, RAPID families) are assigned to *different* GPUs. **Distributed Data Parallel (DDP)** is employed, where rollouts and forward passes are computed locally on each GPU, but gradients are synchronized via an all-reduce primitive during backpropagation, ensuring a single shared policy across all devices. This setup maximizes morphological diversity while maintaining computational efficiency. **Automatic Domain Randomization (ADR)** is also used to progressively increase noise levels in the simulation based on policy performance, further enhancing robustness.

**Real-World Evaluation:** For real-world validation, a LEAP hand on a Franka arm is used. Two Intel RealSense D435 cameras provide depth data, fused via ICP to generate point-cloud observations consistent with simulation. An **offline distillation** approach is used for sim-to-real transfer, training a student MLP network from expert demonstrations recorded from the trained DexFormer policy. This allows for stable and efficient deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted extensively in simulation and validated in the real world, demonstrate the significant capabilities of DexFormer.

1. **Superior Performance over Baselines (Q1, Q2):** DexFormer consistently and substantially outperforms recurrent neural network baselines (LSTM and GRU) in grasping success rate. Across canonical LEAP, Allegro, and RAPID hands, as well as 32 unseen variants of each, DexFormer achieved an average combined success rate of 77.50%, compared to 58.72% for LSTM and 44.29% for GRU. This definitively shows DexFormer's effectiveness and strong **zero-shot generalization** across heterogeneous hands.

2. **Benefit of Temporal Context (Q3):** A comparison between DexFormer policies with a single history step and a 5-step history window confirmed the importance of temporal context. Incorporating a 5-step observation-action history generally improved zero-shot performance for LEAP and Allegro hands, underscoring that implicit morphology inference benefits from a temporal understanding of interaction dynamics. While RAPID hand showed a minor decrease in canonical zero-shot, the overall trend supports the value of historical context.

3. **Impact of Embodiment Diversity (Q4):** Ablation studies on the number of distinct training embodiments revealed that increasing morphological diversity in the training set (from 25 to 50 to 100 variants per canonical hand) significantly improves zero-shot grasping performance on unseen hands. This finding validates the hypothesis that exposure to a wide range of embodiments during training is crucial for learning a truly generalizable cross-embodiment policy.

4. **Scalability with Parallelism (Q5):** Analyzing the effect of environment-level parallelism, the results showed that increasing the number of parallel environments per GPU generally improved performance for LEAP and Allegro hands. This indicates that larger-scale training benefits the learning process. A noted performance drop for the RAPID hand in this ablation was attributed to potential imbalances in morphology statistics during training, suggesting avenues for further optimization in distributed training protocols.

5. **Robustness to Morphological Impairments (Q6):** Experiments involving locking individual joints demonstrated DexFormer's structured degradation patterns, reflecting the anatomical importance of different degrees of freedom. Distal flexion joints (PIP/DIP) and low-leverage MCP abduction joints caused mild performance reductions, whereas basal flexion and thumb CMC joints, critical for stable contact and opposition, led to more pronounced drops. Importantly, no compensatory gain was observed from joint removal, and extended execution windows (4 seconds) allowed for partial compensation even with impaired morphology, highlighting the policy's adaptive nature.

6. **Real-World Validation:** DexFormer demonstrated practical viability through successful real-world deployment on a LEAP hand mounted on a Franka arm. After offline distillation, the policy successfully lifted a cube to a target position. Qualitative evaluations on various objects (e.g., orange fruit, red mug, yellow cube, polyhedra) showed varying success rates, indicating robustness for common objects while revealing challenges for more complex geometries. Crucially, the distilled policy also exhibited adaptability when tested on real-world LEAP hand variants with physically removed finger joint structures (1, 2, or 3 DoFs), reinforcing its ability to handle unforeseen morphological changes without retraining.

Overall, the findings strongly support the central premise that a history-conditioned Transformer, trained on a diverse set of procedurally randomized embodiments, can achieve robust zero-shot cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation, transferring effectively from large-scale simulation to real-world robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DexFormer represents a significant advancement in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation, offering a scalable and generalizable solution to the long-standing challenge of embodiment variability. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Bridging the Generalization Gap in Robotics:** The most immediate impact is the successful demonstration of a single policy that can control heterogeneous dexterous hands zero-shot. This capability fundamentally reduces the need for costly and time-consuming per-embodiment policy training, retraining, or complex explicit kinematic modeling. It pushes robotics closer to the ideal of truly "generalist robots" that can adapt to different physical forms.

2. **Foundation Models for Manipulation:** By combining large-scale procedural randomization with a history-conditioned Transformer, DexFormer lays a foundation for developing "foundation-style models" in dexterous manipulation. Such models, broadly trained on diverse embodiments and tasks, could serve as powerful pre-trained agents that can be fine-tuned or adapted for new specific tasks and previously unseen hardware with minimal effort. This paradigm shift could accelerate research and deployment of advanced robotic systems.

3. **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** The implicit inference of morphology and dynamics from temporal context is a powerful mechanism. It allows the policy to adapt online to the specific characteristics of an unknown hand, including physical impairments or minor structural variations, without explicit system identification modules. This significantly enhances the robustness and versatility of dexterous manipulation systems in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.

4. **Scalable Training Paradigm:** The proposed distributed training framework, which intelligently groups similar embodiments on GPUs while synchronizing a single policy, provides a scalable approach for generating large, morphologically diverse training datasets and efficiently learning from them. This methodology can be extended to other domains requiring robust generalization across varying robot morphologies.

5. **Practical Deployment in Diverse Scenarios:** The successful real-world evaluation, even with a distilled policy and on physically altered hand variants, indicates the practical viability of DexFormer. This suggests that advanced dexterous manipulation could be deployed on a wider range of robotic platforms and in various application scenarios, from industrial assembly to service robotics, without the prohibitive costs of custom policy development for each hand.

6. **Future Research Directions:** DexFormer opens up exciting avenues for future research. Addressing limitations such as object-level generalization (by training on more diverse object collections), architectural improvements for higher-DoF hands, and more sophisticated distributed training strategies (e.g., mixed buffer for diverse kinematic topologies) can further enhance the capabilities of cross-embodiment policies. The work encourages a shift towards learning paradigms that prioritize large-scale, morphologically diverse training to achieve unprecedented levels of generalization in robotic control.
