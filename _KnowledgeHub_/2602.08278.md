# DexFormer: Cross-Embodied Dexterous Manipulation via History-Conditioned Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> DexFormer, developed by researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and Wuhan University, introduces a history-conditioned Transformer policy that achieves zero-shot cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation by implicitly inferring hand morphology from temporal observation-action histories. The single, morphology-agnostic policy demonstrates superior grasping success rates and real-world adaptability across diverse and unseen robotic hands.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation policies typically require extensive training and tuning for each new multi-fingered robotic hand due to embodiment variability in kinematics, inertias, and dynamics.
- Existing approaches struggle to achieve truly generalist policies, often relying on explicit kinematic models, retargeting, or extensive real-world data collection for each specific hand.
- The high cost and time investment in adapting control strategies for different hand designs hinder the widespread adoption of multi-fingered robots.

## Method

- A history-conditioned Transformer architecture is employed, using a finite window of past observation-action pairs to implicitly infer a hand's morphology and dynamics during execution.
- A shared canonical D_F-dimensional finger action space is defined, enabling a single policy to control heterogeneous hands with varying degrees of freedom through an embodiment-specific embedding operator.
- Large-scale procedural randomization generates diverse morphological variants of canonical hands during training, combined with a distributed training framework for efficient learning of a generalizable policy.

## Results

- DexFormer achieved an average combined grasping success rate of 77.50% across canonical and 32 unseen hand variants, significantly outperforming LSTM (58.72%) and GRU (44.29%) baselines.
- Incorporating a 5-step observation-action history generally improved zero-shot performance on unseen LEAP and Allegro hands, confirming the utility of temporal context for morphology inference.
- The policy successfully transferred to a real-world LEAP hand, including variants with physically removed finger joint structures, demonstrating practical viability and adaptability to unforeseen morphological changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit inference of hand characteristics from temporal context is effective, allowing a single policy to adapt to unknown morphologies without explicit identifiers or separate per-embodiment components.
> - Training with a broad distribution of procedurally randomized hand embodiments is crucial for achieving robust zero-shot generalization to unseen and heterogeneous robotic hands.
> - A shared action space, combined with low-level embodiment-specific controllers, provides a scalable method to unify control across fundamentally different robotic hand designs.
