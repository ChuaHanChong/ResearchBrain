# On-Policy Context Distillation for Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> On-Policy Context Distillation (OPCD) enables language models to internalize transient in-context knowledge by training student models on their own generations guided by a context-conditioned teacher using reverse KL divergence. This approach yields improved task accuracy, better out-of-distribution generalization, and effective knowledge transfer across model sizes, overcoming limitations of off-policy distillation.

## Problem

- In-context knowledge in large language models (LLMs) is transient, requiring repeated 're-learning' from prompts, which leads to inefficiencies and increased computational overhead.
- Existing off-policy context distillation methods suffer from exposure bias, as models are trained on teacher-generated data but generate their own sequences during inference.
- Minimizing forward Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence in off-policy methods can lead to mode-covering behavior, causing student models to produce overly broad distributions or 'hallucinations'.

## Method

- Introduces ==On-Policy Context Distillation (OPCD)==, a framework that trains a student model on its own generated responses to mitigate exposure bias.
- Utilizes a ==context-conditioned teacher model== to guide the student's learning by minimizing ==reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence==, promoting mode-seeking behavior.
- Applies OPCD to two main scenarios: experiential knowledge distillation (internalizing strategies from model-generated solutions) and system prompt distillation (embedding optimized prompt behaviors into model parameters).

## Results

- OPCD consistently achieved superior task accuracy across mathematical reasoning and text-based games, outperforming off-policy distillation (e.g., Qwen3-8B on Math reached **79.7%** with OPCD vs. **78.5%** for off-policy distillation).
- Demonstrated enhanced out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, better preserving OOD capabilities compared to off-policy methods, with observed improvements of approximately **4 points** in OOD task accuracy for safety distillation.
- Enabled effective cross-size distillation, allowing smaller student models (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B) to successfully internalize experiential knowledge from larger teachers (Qwen3-8B), often outperforming direct in-context usage for smaller models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing reverse KL divergence encourages mode-seeking behavior, prompting the student to focus on high-probability regions of the teacher's context-aware distribution, thus preventing mode-averaging.
> - On-policy learning, where the student trains on its own generations, aligns training conditions with inference, leading to more robust models with better out-of-distribution generalization.
> - A fixed, frozen teacher model in teacher-student distillation provides a more stable and consistent learning signal, generally outperforming self-distillation where the teacher's weights evolve.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in in-context learning, allowing them to adapt their behavior based on information provided in the input prompt without requiring parameter updates. This involves prepending instructions, few-shot demonstrations, or retrieved documents to steer model behavior. However, this in-context knowledge is temporary; insights generated or retrieved during a session are not retained once the context is reset, necessitating re-learning from the prompt in subsequent interactions.

Context distillation emerged as an approach to address this transience by training a student model to emulate the behavior of a context-conditioned teacher. The objective is to compress contextual information into the student model's parameters, enabling it to reproduce context-aware behavior without requiring the explicit context at inference time. Existing context distillation methods commonly rely on off-policy training, employing forward Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence minimization on a fixed dataset. This off-policy approach presents limitations, including exposure bias—where the student is trained on teacher-generated or ground-truth data but must generate its own autoregressive sequences during inference. Furthermore, forward KL minimization tends to promote mode-covering behavior, which can lead the student model to allocate probability mass to all teacher-generated tokens. This can result in "hallucinations" or overly broad distributions, particularly when the student model lacks the capacity to fully represent the teacher's complex, context-aware distribution.

This work integrates principles from on-policy distillation, which seeks to mitigate exposure bias by training student models on their own generated trajectories, often utilizing reverse KL divergence to foster mode-seeking behavior. By adapting this paradigm, the research aims to address the limitations of prior context distillation methods and provide a more effective mechanism for LLMs to internalize transient in-context knowledge into their permanent parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that enables language models to internalize transient in-context knowledge into their permanent parameters more effectively than existing approaches. This addresses the limitation that valuable insights derived from prompts or retrieved documents are typically lost after a single interaction, requiring the model to process the same context repeatedly.

The motivation behind developing On-Policy Context Distillation (OPCD) stems from several observed shortcomings of current context distillation techniques:
1. **Transient Nature of In-Context Knowledge:** While LLMs exhibit strong in-context learning, the knowledge gained from prompts is not permanently stored. This necessitates re-providing context for each new task or session, leading to computational overhead and potential inefficiencies. The aim is to make this knowledge persistent within the model's parameters.
2. **Limitations of Off-Policy Training:** Existing context distillation methods primarily use off-policy training with forward KL divergence. This creates a mismatch, known as exposure bias, between the training regime (where the student sees teacher-generated or ground-truth sequences) and the inference regime (where the student generates its own sequences autoregressively). This mismatch can lead to performance degradation.
3. **Drawbacks of Forward KL Divergence:** Minimizing forward KL divergence encourages mode-covering, meaning the student attempts to match all modes of the teacher's distribution. For complex, context-aware teacher distributions, a less capable student model may struggle to cover all modes accurately, potentially leading to "hallucinations" or diluted probability distributions. The research is motivated by the potential benefits of reverse KL divergence, which promotes mode-seeking behavior, allowing the student to focus on the teacher's high-likelihood regions.
4. **Need for Experiential Knowledge Internalization:** Language models often generate elaborate solution traces, or "experiences," during problem-solving. There is a motivation to consolidate this valuable experiential knowledge, accumulated from historical solutions, directly into the model's parameters to allow for progressive self-improvement without requiring the full historical context at inference time.
5. **Efficiency of System Prompt Integration:** System prompts are used to steer LLM behavior for specialized tasks, but their inclusion at inference time increases computational overhead and latency, especially for longer prompts. The objective is to internalize the beneficial behaviors encoded in optimized system prompts, enabling models to operate with these behaviors inherently without explicit prompting.
6. **Maintaining Out-of-Distribution Capabilities:** Traditional distillation methods sometimes lead to catastrophic forgetting, where the student model loses its generalization abilities on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks. A key objective is to develop a distillation method that not only improves in-distribution performance but also preserves or enhances OOD capabilities.
7. **Enabling Cross-Size Distillation:** There is a motivation to investigate if smaller student models can effectively internalize complex knowledge from larger, more capable teacher models, thereby enabling the deployment of more efficient models with enhanced performance.

By addressing these points, the research seeks to provide a robust framework for knowledge internalization in LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, On-Policy Context Distillation (OPCD, illustrated in Figure 1), aims to internalize in-context knowledge into a student model's parameters by combining principles from on-policy distillation with context distillation. The core idea is to train a context-free student model, $\pi_\theta$, to mimic the behavior of a context-conditioned teacher model, $\pi_{teacher}$, such that the student can replicate context-aware behavior without explicitly receiving the context $c$ at inference time.

**Formalization and Loss Function:**
Given an input $x$, OPCD minimizes the divergence between the student distribution $\pi_\theta(\cdot | x)$ and the teacher distribution $\pi_{teacher}(\cdot | c, x)$, where the teacher has access to the guiding context $c$. The method specifically optimizes the reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, as opposed to the forward KL divergence used in prior context distillation techniques. The sequence-level divergence is decomposed into a sum of token-level divergences.

The loss function is defined as:
$L(\theta) = E_{(x,c)\sim D, y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} \left[ \frac{1}{|y|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y|} D_{KL} (\pi_\theta(\cdot | x, y_{<t})\|\pi_{teacher}(\cdot | c, x, y_{<t})) \right]$

The token-level reverse KL divergence is computed via:
$D_{KL} (\pi_\theta(\cdot | x, y_{<t})\|\pi_{teacher}(\cdot | c, x, y_{<t})) = \sum_{y'_t \in V} \pi_\theta(y'_t | x, y_{<t}) \left(\log \pi_\theta(y'_t | x, y_{<t}) - \log \pi_{teacher}(y'_t | c, x, y_{<t})\right)$

In implementation, the summation for the analytic KL divergence is approximated by restricting it to the top-k tokens predicted by the student model (V_top-k).

**On-Policy Sampling Mechanism:**
The key aspect of OPCD is its on-policy sampling strategy. During training, the student model $\pi_\theta$ generates its own complete response trajectories $y$ *without* access to the context $c$. This addresses exposure bias by ensuring the student learns from the distribution of sequences it will generate at inference time. The reverse KL divergence then encourages mode-seeking behavior:
- If the student generates a token that the context-conditioned teacher considers highly probable, the student is encouraged to increase the probability of that token.
- Conversely, if the student assigns a high probability to a token that the teacher considers unlikely, this behavior is suppressed.
This mechanism guides the student $\pi_\theta$ to align its generation trajectory with the context-aware teacher $\pi_{teacher}$, thereby internalizing the context $c$ into its parameters.

**Algorithm 1 (OPCD Training Pseudocode):**
1. **Initialization:** Input training data $D = \{(x, c)\}$, student LLM $\pi_\theta$, teacher LLM $\pi_{teacher}$.
2. **Loop:** For each batch $(x, c)$ sampled from $D$:
 - **On-policy rollout:** Sample a response $y$ from the student model $\pi_\theta(\cdot | x)$ (without context $c$).
 - **Compute token-level reverse KL:** For each token position $t$ in $y$, calculate $D_{KL}^{(t)}$ using the student's probability for $y'_t$ and the teacher's probability for $y'_t$ (where the teacher is conditioned on $c, x, y_{<t}$).
 - **Calculate total loss:** Aggregate $D_{KL}^{(t)}$ over the trajectory $y$.
 - **Update student model:** Minimize $L(\theta)$ to update the student's parameters $\theta$.
3. **Return:** The trained student model $\pi_\theta$.

**Teacher Model Configurations:**
The framework offers flexibility in teacher model selection:
- **Teacher-Student Distillation ($\pi_{teacher} \neq \pi_\theta$):** The teacher can be a larger, more capable model, or a model initialized with the same weights but kept frozen or periodically updated. This is the default configuration.
- **Self-Distillation ($\pi_{teacher} = \pi_\theta$):** The teacher and student share the same underlying model weights and are updated simultaneously. The distinction lies in the teacher seeing $[c; x]$ while the student only sees $x$.

**Evaluation Tasks and Setup:**

1. **Experiential Knowledge Distillation:**
 - **Concept:** Extracting transferable "experiential knowledge" from historical solution traces (without ground-truth labels) and consolidating it into the model's parameters.
 - **Stages:**
 - **Experiential Knowledge Extraction:** Model generates solution traces for problems, then produces experiential knowledge learned from these traces.
 - **Experiential Knowledge Accumulation:** Knowledge from different problems is combined to form a composite context $c$.
 - **Experiential Knowledge Consolidation:** OPCD is applied to internalize this knowledge into the student's weights.
 - **Datasets:** DAPO-Math-17K (English math problems), Frozen Lake and Sokoban (TextArena text-based games).
 - **Training Setup:** 300 experiential knowledge contexts constructed from validation data. Two settings:
 - *Test-time experiential knowledge distillation:* Random selection of knowledge contexts.
 - *Filtered experiential knowledge distillation:* Knowledge contexts scored and highest-scoring one selected.
 - **Models:** Qwen3-8B (teacher for math/student), Qwen3-4B (student), Qwen3-1.7B (teacher/student for Frozen Lake), Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (teacher/student for Sokoban).
 - **Training Details:** 50 steps, batch size 128, top-256 vocabulary tokens for KL, learning rate search [1e-6, 5e-6]. Max response length: 16384 tokens for math, 5 rounds of 1024 tokens for text games.

2. **System Prompt Distillation:**
 - **Concept:** Internalizing beneficial behaviors encoded in optimized system prompts, eliminating the need for explicit prompting during deployment.
 - **Datasets:** Medical (MedMCQA) and Safety (Tweet Eval, Hatecheck, Ethos) tasks, using system prompts from MetaSPO.
 - **Models:** Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct.
 - **Training Details:** 50 steps, batch size 128, top-256 vocabulary tokens for KL, learning rate search [1e-6, 5e-6]. Max response length: 512 tokens.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **In-distribution performance:** Accuracy on test splits (math: 1000 samples, text games: 128 samples, system prompts: 500 samples).
- **Out-of-distribution (OOD) performance:** Prompt-level strict accuracy on IF-Eval benchmark.
- **Baselines:** Base model (no context, no distillation), In-Context (base model with context prepended), and Context Distillation (off-policy, forward KL minimization).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of On-Policy Context Distillation (OPCD) across various tasks and configurations.

**Experiential Knowledge Consolidation (Tables 1 & 2):**
- OPCD consistently outperformed the off-policy context distillation baseline and, in several cases, surpassed the performance of the original model when experiential knowledge was provided directly in context.
- For test-time experiential knowledge consolidation (Table 1), OPCD on Qwen3-8B for math achieved 79.7% accuracy, compared to 78.5% for context distillation and 77.6% for in-context. On Frozen Lake with Qwen3-1.7B, OPCD achieved 26.5% versus 22.9% for context distillation.
- Similar trends were observed for filtered experiential knowledge consolidation (Table 2), with OPCD showing higher accuracies across math, Frozen Lake, and Sokoban tasks.
- Beyond in-distribution accuracy, OPCD better preserved out-of-distribution (OOD) capabilities, evidenced by higher IF-Eval scores compared to the context distillation baseline (e.g., 81.7% vs 81.2% for Qwen3-8B math; 67.1% vs 65.1% for Qwen3-1.7B Frozen Lake).

**System Prompt Distillation (Tables 3 & 4):**
- OPCD generally outperformed the off-policy context distillation baseline in test accuracy across different models and tasks (medical and safety).
- For medical system prompt distillation (Table 3), Llama-3.1-8B-Ins achieved 76.7% with OPCD versus 75.2% with context distillation. Llama-3.2-3B-Ins reached 76.3% with OPCD versus 71.0% with context distillation.
- On safety system prompt distillation (Table 4), OPCD demonstrated robust performance. For Llama-3.1-8B-Ins, OPCD achieved 79.6% compared to 77.2% for context distillation. For Llama-3.2-3B-Ins, OPCD achieved 83.1% which was comparable to 83.3% of context distillation, while significantly higher than its in-context performance.
- The research noted that on-policy training through OPCD provided more stable improvements during the training process compared to off-policy context distillation.

**Effect of Model Size (Figure 2):**
- OPCD consistently improved the test accuracy of smaller Qwen3 models (1.7B, 4B) when distilling experiential knowledge from a frozen Qwen3-8B teacher.
- Conversely, directly injecting teacher-generated experiential knowledge into the context of smaller models (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B or Qwen3-4B) sometimes resulted in performance degradation. This suggests that the alignment between the experiential knowledge and the consuming model is critical.

**On-Policy Context Distillation Mitigates Forgetting (Figure 3, Tables 1 & 2):**
- OPCD exhibited superior in-distribution performance and maintained OOD performance, surpassing the off-policy baseline in OOD tasks. For example, on the Frozen Lake OOD IF-Eval benchmark, OPCD achieved approximately 2% higher scores than the context distillation baseline.
- When distilling a safety system prompt, OPCD maintained OOD performance on a medical test dataset, outperforming the off-policy baseline by approximately 4 percentage points, while also achieving higher in-distribution performance on the safety task.

**Teacher-Student Distillation vs. Self-Distillation (Table 5):**
- The teacher-student distillation configuration (using a frozen teacher) significantly outperformed the self-distillation configuration (where the model acts as both teacher and student and is continuously updated).
- For Sokoban, teacher-student OPCD achieved 53.9% accuracy, whereas self-distillation achieved 18.8%. Similar differences were observed for the medical task.
- Teacher-student distillation also demonstrated more stable training dynamics, while self-distillation showed tendencies to diverge, attributed to the high variance of an evolving teacher in an RL-like setting.

**Importance of Learning from Experiential Knowledge (Table 6):**
- The research highlighted the necessity of explicitly extracting structured "experiential knowledge" from raw traces. Simply prepending raw solution traces from previous problems as context degraded performance.
- In contrast, extracting and then prepending the learned experiential knowledge led to higher validation accuracy than the original model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of On-Policy Context Distillation (OPCD) presents several significant contributions to the field of large language models and their application:

1. **Permanent Knowledge Internalization:** OPCD offers a method for language models to internalize transient in-context knowledge directly into their permanent parameters. This addresses a fundamental limitation of in-context learning, where valuable insights are typically lost once the conversational context is reset. By making knowledge persistent, OPCD can reduce the need for repetitive context provision, potentially leading to more efficient and autonomous LLMs.

2. **Improved Performance and Generalization:** The consistent outperformance of OPCD over baseline methods, including traditional off-policy context distillation, indicates its effectiveness in improving task accuracy across diverse domains like mathematical reasoning, text-based games, and domain-specific tasks. Furthermore, its ability to better preserve out-of-distribution (OOD) capabilities suggests improved generalization and reduced catastrophic forgetting, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

3. **Efficiency Gains:** By internalizing experiential knowledge and system prompt behaviors, OPCD can reduce the computational overhead and latency associated with prepending lengthy contexts or optimized prompts at inference time. This efficiency gain can be particularly valuable for high-throughput applications and resource-constrained environments.

4. **Enabling Experiential Learning and Self-Improvement:** The framework for experiential knowledge distillation allows models to learn from their own historical solution traces. This capability for self-accumulation of experience could pave the way for more adaptive and progressively improving models that continuously refine their understanding and problem-solving strategies without direct human supervision for every new piece of information.

5. **Effective Cross-Size Distillation:** The demonstration that smaller student models can effectively internalize complex experiential knowledge from larger teacher models has significant implications for model deployment. It enables the creation of smaller, more efficient models that retain the specialized capabilities of their larger counterparts, facilitating wider deployment and reduced operational costs.

6. **Mitigation of Exposure Bias and Mode-Covering Issues:** By adopting an on-policy training strategy with reverse KL divergence, OPCD directly addresses known challenges like exposure bias and the mode-covering behavior associated with forward KL divergence. This methodological advancement contributes to more robust and accurate knowledge transfer.

7. **Foundational Work for Continual Learning:** The ability to consolidate experiential knowledge opens avenues for future research in continual accumulation of knowledge. This could lead to models that continuously learn and adapt in dynamic environments, acquiring new skills and information over their operational lifespan.

In summary, OPCD represents a methodological advancement in knowledge distillation for LLMs, offering a more effective, stable, and generalizable approach to internalize context-dependent behaviors. Its impact could extend to enabling more capable, efficient, and continually learning AI systems.
