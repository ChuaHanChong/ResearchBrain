# When Vision Transformers Outperform ResNets without Pre-training or Strong Data Augmentations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Google Research and UCLA identified that Vision Transformers and MLP-Mixers converge to sharp local minima when trained from scratch, hindering their performance compared to ResNets. Employing Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) enabled these convolution-free architectures to achieve state-of-the-art ImageNet accuracy (e.g., ViT-B/16 at 79.9% top-1, surpassing ResNet-152's 79.3%) and improved robustness without large-scale pre-training or strong data augmentations.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) and MLP-Mixers typically require extensive pre-training or strong data augmentations to match Convolutional Neural Networks (ConvNets).
- When trained from scratch with standard preprocessing, ViTs and MLP-Mixers often exhibit lower accuracy and are prone to converging to sharp local minima.
- These architectures can suffer from optimization challenges like high sensitivity to hyperparameters and poor "trainability" as indicated by NTK condition numbers.

## Method

- Analyzed the ==loss landscape geometry== of ViTs and MLP-Mixers, quantifying sharpness via ==Hessian eigenvalues== and assessing trainability using ==Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) condition numbers==.
- Implemented **Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)**, an optimization strategy that guides models towards flatter minima by explicitly minimizing loss over a neighborhood of parameters.
- Conducted comprehensive empirical evaluation by training various ViT, MLP-Mixer, and ResNet models from scratch on ImageNet using only basic data preprocessing.

## Results

- ViT-B/16 with Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) achieved **79.9%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, surpassing ResNet-152 (**79.3%**) trained under similar conditions (from scratch, basic preprocessing).
- SAM led to a **9.9%** absolute increase in ViT-B/16 accuracy on ImageNet-C (from **46.6%** to **56.5%**) and a **15.0%** increase for Mixer-B/16 (from **33.8%** to **48.8%**), significantly boosting robustness.
- Loss landscape analysis confirmed that SAM drastically reduced the dominant Hessian eigenvalue (λmax) for ViT-B/16 (from **738.8** to **20.9**) and Mixer-B/16 (from **1644.4** to **22.5**), indicating a much smoother loss landscape.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Transformers and MLP-Mixers trained from scratch with basic preprocessing converge to extremely sharp local minima and exhibit poor trainability, leading to suboptimal generalization.
> - Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) effectively smooths the loss landscapes of these models, fundamentally improving their generalization and robustness.
> - The benefits of SAM are distinct from strong data augmentations; SAM directly reduces the worst-case sharpness of the loss landscape, unlike augmentations which can sometimes increase it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep learning for computer vision has witnessed a significant shift towards general-purpose neural architectures, notably Vision Transformers (ViTs) and MLP-Mixers, moving away from hand-wired features or strong inductive biases prevalent in Convolutional Neural Networks (ConvNets) like ResNets. This trend aims to develop unified knowledge bases capable of generating versatile representations across different data modalities, potentially simplifying model deployment.

Despite their theoretical appeal and success in Natural Language Processing, ViTs and MLP-Mixers have historically required substantial computational resources and large datasets for training, often through large-scale pre-training (e.g., on ImageNet-21k or JFT-300M) or the application of numerous strong data augmentations (e.g., AutoAugment, RandAugment, Mixup, CutMix). When trained from scratch on ImageNet with standard data preprocessing, these architectures typically yield lower accuracies compared to similarly sized ResNets. Existing literature also indicates that the training of ViTs and MLP-Mixers can be sensitive to initialization and hyperparameters, and may exhibit optimization-related issues such as spiking gradients. This dependency on extensive data and complex training schemes poses challenges for their broader adoption and efficiency. The current work positions itself within this landscape by investigating methods to improve the data efficiency and generalization of these convolution-free architectures, aiming to enable their competitive performance without these external dependencies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the training and generalization characteristics of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and MLP-Mixers, particularly when they are trained without large-scale pre-training or strong data augmentations. The authors hypothesize that optimization-related problems, such as convergence to sharp local minima, contribute to the observed performance gap between these architectures and ConvNets like ResNets under data-constrained or standard training regimes.

The specific motivations and objectives include:
- **Analyzing Loss Landscape Geometry:** To understand the optimization challenges, the paper aims to characterize the loss landscapes of converged ViTs and MLP-Mixers. This involves visualizing the loss landscapes and quantifying their sharpness using metrics such as the dominant Hessian eigenvalue (λmax) and average flatness (L_N_train), and evaluating trainability via the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) condition number (κ). The expectation is that these architectures might converge to sharp minima, which are known to correlate with poor generalization.
- **Improving Data Efficiency and Generalization:** Based on the insights from loss landscape analysis, the paper seeks to identify and apply improved learning algorithms that can guide ViTs and MLP-Mixers towards flatter, better-generalizing minima. This aims to reduce their reliance on massive datasets or aggressive data augmentation strategies.
- **Achieving Competitive Performance:** A key objective is to demonstrate that ViTs can outperform or achieve performance comparable to ResNets of similar size and throughput, specifically when trained from scratch on ImageNet using only basic Inception-style data preprocessing, without resorting to large-scale pre-training or strong data augmentations. Similarly, the goal is to bring MLP-Mixers to a performance level on par with ResNets under these conditions.
- **Understanding Intrinsic Model Changes:** The research also aims to delve into the intrinsic properties of the models (e.g., weight norms, active neuron sparsity, attention maps) that change as a result of optimizing for smoother loss landscapes, to provide a deeper understanding of the mechanisms behind improved generalization.
- **Comparing with Existing Regularization Methods:** To contextualize the proposed approach, the study seeks to compare its effects with those of strong data augmentations, exploring similarities and differences in their impact on loss geometry and generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach involving theoretical analysis of loss landscapes, application of a sharpness-aware optimization technique, and extensive empirical evaluation across various tasks and model architectures.

#### 3.1. Initial Investigation of Loss Landscapes

The authors began by training ResNet-152, ViT-B/16, and Mixer-B/16 from scratch on ImageNet using conventional Inception-style preprocessing. They then analyzed the loss landscapes of the converged models using several techniques:
- **Visualization:** 2D projections of the cross-entropy loss landscapes were generated using the "filter normalization" method to visually assess sharpness.
- **Hessian Eigenvalue Analysis:** The dominant eigenvalue (λmax) of the Hessian matrix was computed to quantitatively measure the worst-case curvature around the local minima.
- **Average Flatness (L_N_train):** This metric measured the training error under Gaussian perturbations on model parameters, providing an estimate of the average flatness of the converged region.
- **Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) Condition Number:** The condition number (κ) of the NTK was approximated to evaluate the "trainability" of the architectures, indicating the effectiveness of gradient descent optimization.

#### 3.2. Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)

Based on the observation that ViTs and MLP-Mixers converge to sharp local minima, the authors adopted Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) as the core optimization strategy. SAM explicitly seeks to find a solution whose entire neighborhood has low losses, rather than focusing solely on a single point.
- **Objective Function:** SAM formulates a minimax objective: `min_w max_‖ε‖_2≤ρ L_train(w + ε)`, where ρ defines the size of the neighborhood ball.
- **Approximation:** An efficient first-order approximation `ε̂(w) = ρ∇_w L_train(w) / ‖∇_w L_train(w)‖_2` is used for the inner maximization.
- **Gradient Update:** The model parameters `w` are then updated using the sharpness-aware gradient `∇_w L_train(w)|_(w+ε̂(w))`.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** The SAM perturbation strength ρ was tuned for each model and task, with different architectures favoring different ρ values.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Training Regime:** All models (ResNets, ViTs, MLP-Mixers) were trained from scratch on ImageNet with basic Inception-style preprocessing. The standard training pipelines for ResNet and ViT were followed, while for MLP-Mixer, strong augmentations were replaced with Inception-style preprocessing.
- **Model Architectures:** A range of ResNet, ViT (S/B with different patch resolutions), and MLP-Mixer (S/B with different patch resolutions) models were evaluated.
- **Performance Metrics:**
 - **Accuracy:** Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet validation set, ImageNet-ReaL, and ImageNet V2.
 - **Robustness:** Accuracy on ImageNet-R (renditions) and ImageNet-C (common corruptions), averaged across 19 corruptions and five levels.
 - **Throughput:** Measured in images per second per core.
- **Comparative Studies:**
 - **Optimizer Comparison:** SAM was applied on top of both AdamW (standard for Transformers) and momentum SGD, to assess its generality.
 - **SAM vs. Strong Augmentations:** Experiments compared SAM's impact with a regime incorporating strong data augmentations (mixup and RandAugment) on ImageNet and its subsets, and their combination.
 - **Adversarial Training:** SAM was integrated into adversarial training frameworks using a three-level minimax objective.
 - **Contrastive Learning:** SAM was combined with supervised contrastive learning.
 - **Transfer Learning:** Models pre-trained on ImageNet were fine-tuned on downstream tasks (CIFAR-10/100, Flowers, Pets) to assess transferability.
 - **Scaling Training Data:** Models were trained on ImageNet subsets (1/2, 1/4, 1/10) to evaluate performance under data scarcity.

#### 3.4. Analysis of Intrinsic Model Properties

The study further investigated the internal changes in models after SAM optimization:
- **Hessian Breakdown:** The Hessian λmax was analyzed for individual network components (e.g., embedding layer, MSA/token MLP, MLP/channel MLP) to identify critical architectural parts contributing to sharpness.
- **Weight and Activation Norms:** The L2 norms of model parameters and post-activation values were tracked to understand regularization effects.
- **Active Neurons Sparsity:** The percentage of active GELU neurons was measured to examine changes in network activation patterns.
- **Attention Map Visualization:** Attention maps of ViTs were visualized to observe potential improvements in interpretability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings regarding the optimization and generalization of convolution-free vision architectures:

#### 4.1. Sharp Local Minima and Poor Trainability of ViTs and MLP-Mixers

- **Sharpness:** Visualizations of loss landscapes and quantitative measures (Hessian λmax, L_N_train) confirmed that ViTs and MLP-Mixers converge to extremely sharp local minima when trained with basic preprocessing. Their λmax values were orders of magnitude larger (e.g., ViT-B/16: 738.8, Mixer-B/16: 1644.4) compared to ResNet-152 (179.8).
- **Overfitting:** MLP-Mixers, despite having fewer parameters than some ViTs, exhibited smaller training errors but significantly worse test accuracy, suggesting a higher propensity to overfit. Removing softmax from ViT's attention further exacerbated sharpness and reduced accuracy.
- **Trainability:** Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) analyses revealed that ViTs and MLP-Mixers suffer from worse trainability, characterized by divergent NTK condition numbers (e.g., ViT-B/16: 14468.0), in contrast to the stable NTK of ResNets (e.g., 2801.6).

#### 4.2. SAM Improves Generalization by Smoothing Loss Landscapes

- **Smoother Landscapes:** The application of SAM resulted in significantly smoother loss landscapes for both ViTs and MLP-Mixers, as evidenced by visualizations and drastically reduced Hessian λmax values (e.g., ViT-B/16-SAM: 20.9, Mixer-B/16-SAM: 22.5) and lower L_N_train.
- **Accuracy Improvements:** SAM substantially improved top-1 accuracy on ImageNet. ViT-B/16 saw an increase from 74.6% to 79.9% (+5.3%), and Mixer-B/16 from 66.4% to 77.4% (+11.0%). ResNet-152, with its inherent inductive biases, received a smaller improvement of 0.8% (from 78.5% to 79.3%). Improvements were greater for models with larger capacity and longer patch sequences.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** SAM led to even larger improvements in robustness metrics. On ImageNet-C, ViT-B/16 improved by 9.9% (from 46.6% to 56.5%), and Mixer-B/16 by 15.0% (from 33.8% to 48.8%). These were 21.2% and 44.4% relative improvements, respectively, exceeding relative clean accuracy gains.
- **Optimizer Compatibility:** SAM proved effective even with momentum SGD, improving ViT-B/16 accuracy from 71.5% (SGD alone) to 79.1%, significantly closing the gap with AdamW+SAM (79.9%).

#### 4.3. ViTs Outperform ResNets without Pre-training or Strong Augmentations

- With SAM optimization, ViT-B/16 achieved 79.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, surpassing ResNet-152 (79.3%), despite having comparable parameters and throughput. Similar advantages were observed across various ViT sizes and on other clean accuracy benchmarks (ImageNet-ReaL, ImageNet V2) and robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-C). For instance, ViT-B/16-SAM (56.5%) outperformed ResNet-152-SAM (52.2%) on ImageNet-C. MLP-Mixers also became competitive with ResNets after SAM.

#### 4.4. Intrinsic Model Changes after SAM

- **Smoother Components:** SAM consistently reduced the λmax of all network components, with shallower layers (especially the initial embedding layer) showing the largest reductions.
- **Increased Weight Norms:** Post-SAM, the L2 norms of model parameters and post-activation values generally increased, suggesting that standard weight decay alone may not be sufficient for regularization and that SAM's regularization mechanism is distinct.
- **Sparser Active Neurons:** SAM significantly reduced the proportion of active GELU neurons, particularly in the first few layers of MLP-Mixer-B/16, pushing them towards sparser states. ViTs already exhibited high sparsity (<10% active neurons in most layers), indicating potential for network pruning.
- **Improved Attention Maps:** ViT models optimized with SAM showed visually more interpretable attention maps compared to those trained with vanilla AdamW.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Strong Augmentations

- **Generalization:** SAM benefited ViT-B/16 and Mixer-B/16 more than strong data augmentations (mixup + RandAugment), especially when training data was scarce (e.g., +7.6% for ViT-B/16-SAM vs. ViT-B/16-AUG on 1/10 ImageNet).
- **Loss Landscape Impact:** While both SAM and strong augmentations increased training error (indicating regularization) and improved average flatness (L_N_train), strong augmentations surprisingly *enlarged* the worst-case Hessian λmax, making the landscape sharper in specific directions, unlike SAM which explicitly reduces it.
- **Linearity:** SAM-trained models demonstrated a property of "linearity" (lower prediction error between interpolated training data) similar to that explicitly encouraged by mixup augmentation.

#### 4.6. Scaling Training Set Size

- ViTs and MLP-Mixers showed a more rapid decline in accuracy than ResNets on smaller ImageNet subsets. However, SAM significantly mitigated this performance drop, providing larger absolute improvements on smaller datasets. For instance, on 1/4 ImageNet, SAM boosted ViT-B/16 by 14.4% and Mixer-B/16 by 25.6%. ViT-B/16-SAM achieved performance comparable to ResNet-152-SAM with only half the ImageNet training data. SAM also offered improvements even when models were pre-trained on ImageNet-21k.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and advancement of convolution-free neural architectures, particularly Vision Transformers and MLP-Mixers, with several significant implications:

- **Reduced Resource Dependency:** The most direct impact is demonstrating that ViTs and MLP-Mixers can achieve competitive or superior performance to ResNets without relying on massive pre-training datasets or complex strong data augmentation strategies. This significantly reduces the data, computational, and hyperparameter tuning demands previously considered necessary, making these powerful architectures more accessible and efficient for researchers and practitioners.
- **Advancing General-Purpose Architectures:** By addressing the training difficulties of ViTs and MLP-Mixers, the work supports the broader vision of moving towards general-purpose neural architectures. It suggests that architectural design is less confounded by training strategies than previously thought, allowing for a clearer assessment of the intrinsic capabilities of these models.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent and substantial improvements in clean accuracy and, especially, robustness to common corruptions and renditions indicate that optimizing for smoother loss landscapes is a principled approach to developing more reliable and generalizeable vision models. This has direct benefits for real-world applications where data distribution shifts and noisy inputs are common.
- **Insights into Optimization and Generalization:** The detailed analysis of loss landscape geometry, Hessian eigenvalues, NTK condition numbers, and intrinsic model changes provides valuable theoretical and empirical insights into why certain architectures generalize better and how optimization strategies can influence this. The discovery of increased neuron sparsity and more interpretable attention maps after SAM opens new avenues for architectural design and analysis.
- **Flexibility in Optimizer Choice:** The finding that SAM can enable momentum SGD to train Transformers effectively, largely bridging the gap with adaptive optimizers like Adam, offers greater flexibility in training methodology and potentially reduces the sensitivity to specific optimizers.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies limitations, such as the 2x computational overhead of SAM and its diminishing returns on very large datasets. These limitations highlight areas for future work, including developing more computationally efficient sharpness-aware optimizers and strategies tailored for extreme-scale pre-training.

In summary, this work provides a rigorous analysis and a practical solution to enhance the data efficiency, generalization, and robustness of Vision Transformers and MLP-Mixers, fundamentally altering the perception of their training requirements and potential capabilities.
