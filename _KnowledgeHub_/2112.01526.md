# MViTv2: Improved Multiscale Vision Transformers for Classification and Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Facebook AI Research introduces MViTv2, an enhanced Multiscale Vision Transformer architecture that refines the pooling attention mechanism with decomposed relative positional embeddings and residual pooling connections. This approach achieves new state-of-the-art results across image classification on ImageNet-1K, object detection on COCO, and video recognition on Kinetics and Something-Something-v2 benchmarks.

## Problem

- Standard Vision Transformers (ViTs) face quadratic computational and memory complexity challenges with high-resolution inputs, limiting their application in tasks like object detection and video understanding.
- Developing a unified architecture that can efficiently achieve high performance across diverse visual recognition tasks (image classification, object detection, video recognition) remains a challenge.
- The initial MViTv1's pooling attention, while efficient, had opportunities for improvement in terms of shift-invariance and optimizing information flow within its attention blocks.

## Method

- Develops a refined ==Multiscale Vision Transformer (MViTv2)== with a hierarchical design that progressively reduces spatial/temporal resolution while increasing channel depth, similar to CNNs.
- Introduces ==decomposed relative positional embeddings==, separating positional encoding into height, width, and temporal components, to improve shift-invariance and reduce memory complexity for high-resolution inputs.
- Incorporates a ==residual pooling connection== that adds the pooled query tensor directly to the attention block's output, improving information flow and facilitating the training of deep pooling attention networks.

## Results

- Achieved **88.8%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with ImageNet-21K pre-training and **512x512** resolution, setting a new state-of-the-art.
- Attained **55.8** APbox on COCO object detection using Cascade Mask R-CNN, outperforming Swin-B by **+2.5** APbox with less computation.
- Established new state-of-the-art benchmarks for video recognition, including **86.1%** on Kinetics-400 and **73.3%** on Something-Something-v2, demonstrating strong temporal modeling capabilities and up to **3.9x** faster training on COCO with improved components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Refinements to the pooling attention mechanism, specifically decomposed relative positional embeddings and residual pooling connections, significantly boost performance and efficiency in hierarchical Vision Transformers.
> - A single, unified multiscale Transformer architecture can effectively serve as a state-of-the-art backbone across diverse visual tasks, from image classification to dense video understanding, minimizing task-specific architectural changes.
> - Pooling attention offers a superior accuracy-to-computation trade-off compared to global or windowed attention, proving crucial for efficient processing of high-resolution inputs in complex vision tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of two dominant paradigms in computer vision: **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)** and **Vision Transformers (ViTs)**. Historically, CNNs (e.g., ResNet, VGGNet) have been the backbone for most visual recognition tasks due to their simplicity and efficacy. However, the advent of ViTs, particularly after Dosovitskiy et al.'s seminal "Vision Transformer" paper in 2020, demonstrated that transformer architectures, originally designed for natural language processing, could achieve competitive, and often superior, performance in image classification.

The broader research landscape for ViTs quickly expanded to address their limitations, especially their quadratic computational and memory complexity with respect to input resolution, which makes them challenging for high-resolution tasks like object detection and dense video understanding. Two main strategies emerged to mitigate this:
1. **Local Attention/Window Attention:** As seen in models like Swin Transformer, this approach partitions the input into non-overlapping windows and computes self-attention locally within each window, reducing complexity.
2. **Pooling Attention:** Introduced by the earlier MViTv1, this method downsamples key and value tensors (and optionally queries) before computing self-attention, enabling efficient processing of multiscale features.

MViTv2 builds directly upon the "pooling attention" strategy of its predecessor, MViTv1, which was initially successful for video tasks. The current work positions itself as a refinement and generalization of MViT, aiming to create a **unified architecture family** that can achieve state-of-the-art results across a wider spectrum of visual tasks including image classification, object detection, and video recognition, without specialized architectural modifications for each domain. This contrasts with approaches that might tailor architectures more specifically or struggle to scale efficiently across different resolutions and input types. By demonstrating superior performance and efficiency across these diverse tasks, MViTv2 pushes the frontier towards more generalized and versatile vision backbones, reducing the need for task-specific architectural design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the MViTv2 research can be summarized as follows:

- **Develop a Unified and General Vision Backbone:** The overarching goal is to present MViT (specifically MViTv2) as a single, hierarchical architecture capable of excelling across various visual recognition tasks, including image classification, object detection, and video recognition. This aims to simplify architectural design and foster reusability in the field.
- **Improve Upon MViTv1's Pooling Attention Mechanism:** While MViTv1 introduced pooling attention, the authors were motivated to enhance its performance and efficiency. They specifically aimed to address limitations related to shift-invariance and information flow within the attention blocks.
- **Address Computational and Memory Challenges of ViTs:** High-resolution inputs in tasks like object detection and video understanding lead to quadratic complexity in standard ViTs. A key motivation was to refine the pooling attention mechanism to efficiently handle these high-dimensional inputs, making ViTs more practical for such applications.
- **Compare Pooling Attention with Other Efficiency Mechanisms:** The authors sought to empirically compare their pooling attention strategy (and its improvements) against popular alternatives like "window attention" (e.g., from Swin Transformer) in terms of accuracy-computation trade-offs.
- **Instantiate a Family of MViTv2 Models:** To demonstrate scalability and broad applicability, the research aimed to create and evaluate MViTv2 models of varying sizes (Tiny to Huge), providing a spectrum of complexity and performance suitable for different resource constraints.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** A crucial objective was to set new benchmarks and achieve state-of-the-art results on widely-used datasets across all targeted domains (ImageNet, COCO, Kinetics, Something-Something-v2) using a simple and effective architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MViTv2 builds upon the Multiscale Vision Transformer (MViT) architecture, characterized by its hierarchical structure that progressively reduces spatial/temporal resolution while increasing channel depth, similar to CNNs. The core innovation of MViT is its "Pooling Attention" mechanism, which efficiently downsamples query (Q), key (K), and value (V) tensors within self-attention blocks to manage computational complexity. MViTv2 introduces two main empirical upgrades to this mechanism and adapts the architecture for diverse tasks.

#### 3.1. Core Architectural Upgrades (Improved Pooling Attention)

1. **Decomposed Relative Positional Embeddings:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard absolute positional embeddings lack shift-invariance, and joint relative embeddings can be computationally expensive for high-resolution inputs (scaling quadratically).
 - **Approach:** MViTv2 incorporates relative positional embeddings, which depend only on the relative distance between tokens. To alleviate complexity, these embeddings are **decomposed** into separate components along the height, width, and temporal (for video) axes ($R_h$, $R_w$, $R_t$). This reduces the number of learned embeddings from $O(TWH)$ to $O(T+W+H)$, making it highly efficient, especially in early, high-resolution stages. This positional information is added to the attention score calculation: $\text{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \text{Softmax}((QK^\top + E^{(\text{rel})})/\sqrt{d})V$, where $E^{(\text{rel})}_{ij} = Q_i \cdot R_{p(i),p(j)}$. By default, only the query (Q) term interacts with relative positional embeddings to balance accuracy and efficiency.

2. **Residual Pooling Connection:**
 - **Motivation:** Pooling attention, especially when keys and values are heavily downsampled, might lose fine-grained information or hinder information flow.
 - **Approach:** A simple residual connection is introduced by adding the pooled query tensor (Q) directly to the output sequence (Z) of the attention block: $Z := \text{Attn}(Q, K, V) + Q$. This connection helps compensate for the effects of pooling strides, ensures better information flow, and facilitates the training of deep pooling attention networks without significant computational overhead.

#### 3.2. Adaptation for Object Detection

- **FPN Integration:** MViTv2's inherent hierarchical, multiscale feature generation naturally integrates with Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) for object detection. The four stages of MViTv2 provide multiscale feature maps that FPN uses to construct rich semantic features at all scales for detection heads (e.g., Mask R-CNN, Cascade Mask R-CNN).
- **Hybrid Window Attention (Hwin):** Recognizing the strengths of both pooling and window attention, MViTv2 proposes Hwin. While pooling attention is used by default in the backbone, Hwin is optionally incorporated, particularly in later stages. Hwin performs local attention within windows for most blocks but employs global connections (via pooling attention or full attention) in the last blocks of the final three stages to ensure global context is captured before feeding into FPN. This provides a balance between local detail and global understanding.
- **Positional Embedding Handling:** For varying input resolutions common in object detection, positional embeddings are initialized from ImageNet pre-training weights (e.g., 224x224) and then interpolated to match the target detection input sizes.

#### 3.3. Adaptation for Video Recognition

- **Spatiotemporal Extension:** The MViTv2 architecture extends seamlessly to video. The patchification stem projects input into 3D space-time cubes, and pooling operators handle spatiotemporal feature maps. Crucially, the decomposed relative positional embeddings incorporate a temporal component ($R_t$).
- **Inflation Initialization:** When pre-training from 2D image models (e.g., ImageNet), convolutional layers for the 3D video task are initialized using an "inflation" strategy: 2D weights are copied to the center frame of the 3D convolution, and other temporal weights are set to zero. Spatial relative embeddings are initialized from pre-trained weights, while temporal embeddings are initialized to zero.

#### 3.4. MViT Architecture Variants

The authors instantiate MViTv2 in five scales (Tiny, Small, Base, Large, Huge) by adjusting parameters like channel width, number of blocks, and number of attention heads across its four stages. This provides a spectrum of models for different computational budgets, ranging from 4.7 GFLOPs (Tiny) to 120.6 GFLOPs (Huge) for image classification.

#### 3.5. Training Regimes

MViTv2 utilizes robust and high-performing training recipes adapted from previous state-of-the-art methods.
- **Image Classification (ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-21K):** Trained for 300 epochs (or 90 for IN-21K pre-training) with AdamW optimizer, linear warm-up, cosine learning rate decay, and strong augmentations (Mixup, Cutmix, Random Erasing, RandAugment, Stochastic Depth, Label Smoothing). Fine-tuning for higher resolutions (e.g., 384x384, 512x512) is also employed.
- **Object Detection (COCO):** Backbones are pre-trained on ImageNet, then fine-tuned on COCO using Mask R-CNN or Cascade Mask R-CNN frameworks in Detectron2. Standard 3x schedule (36 epochs) and a stronger 50-epoch schedule with large-scale jittering are used.
- **Video Recognition (Kinetics, Something-Something-v2, AVA):** Models are trained from scratch or fine-tuned from ImageNet/Kinetics pre-trained weights. Standard video augmentation techniques (e.g., temporal sampling, spatial cropping) are applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MViTv2 demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across all evaluated visual recognition benchmarks, showcasing the efficacy of its improved architecture and unified design.

#### 4.1. Image Classification on ImageNet-1K

- **Significant Improvement over MViTv1:** MViTv2-S achieves 83.6% accuracy, surpassing MViTv1-B-16 (83.0%) with 10% fewer FLOPs. MViTv2-B reaches 84.4%, a 1.0% improvement over MViTv1-B-24, while being lighter. This validates the effectiveness of the decomposed relative positional embeddings and residual pooling connections.
- **Outperformance against Other Transformers:** MViTv2 consistently outperforms other leading ViTs like DeiT and Swin Transformer, especially at larger scales. For instance, MViTv2-B (84.4%) beats DeiT-B and Swin-B by 2.6% and 1.1% respectively, often with fewer FLOPs and parameters.
- **State-of-the-Art Accuracy:** With ImageNet-21K pre-training and higher resolution (512x512) testing, MViTv2-H achieves an impressive 88.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, setting a new state-of-the-art without bells-and-whistles.

#### 4.2. Object Detection on COCO

- **Superior Performance with Mask R-CNN:** MViTv2 backbones consistently outperform CNN (ResNet, ResNeXt) and other Transformer backbones (PVT, Swin, ViL, MViTv1). For example, MViTv2-B achieves 51.0 APbox, outperforming Swin-B by +2.5 APbox, with less computation and smaller model size.
- **State-of-the-Art with Cascade Mask R-CNN:** Using Cascade Mask R-CNN and a strong training recipe, MViTv2-L achieves 55.8 APbox. With SoftNMS and multi-scale testing, MViTv2-L reaches 58.7 APbox, surpassing Swin-L (58.0 APbox) even without the improved HTC++ detector.

#### 4.3. Video Recognition on Kinetics, Something-Something-v2, and AVA

- **Kinetics-400/-600/-700:** MViTv2 models significantly improve upon MViTv1 when trained from scratch (e.g., MViTv2-S: 81.0% vs. MViTv1: 78.4% on K400). With ImageNet-21K pre-training, MViTv2-L achieves 86.1% on K400, 87.9% on K600, and 79.4% on K700, establishing new state-of-the-art benchmarks.
- **Something-Something-v2 (SSv2):** MViTv2-B achieves 70.5% top-1 accuracy, outperforming Swin-B (69.6%) even with less pre-training data and fewer resources. MViTv2-L further boosts this to 73.3%. This highlights MViTv2's strong temporal modeling capabilities.
- **AVA Action Detection:** MViTv2 models consistently achieve higher mAP than MViTv1 counterparts. MViTv2-L reaches 34.4 mAP (with IN-21K and K700 pre-training), setting a new state-of-the-art for spatiotemporal action localization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Attention Mechanisms:** Pooling attention demonstrates a better accuracy/computation trade-off compared to global or windowed attention, especially for high-resolution tasks. Hybrid window attention (Hwin) also performs well, often complementing pooling attention effectively.
- **Positional Embeddings:** Decomposed relative positional embeddings provide crucial shift-invariance and achieve comparable accuracy to joint relative embeddings but with significantly lower memory consumption (e.g., 3.9x faster training on COCO) and higher throughput. Absolute positional embeddings offer only marginal gains over no positional embeddings.
- **Residual Pooling Connection:** The residual pooling connection is critical, yielding accuracy gains of up to +1.4 APbox on COCO when combined with full Q pooling, validating its role in improving information flow.
- **Pre-training Effects:** Large-scale ImageNet pre-training (especially IN-21K) consistently boosts performance across all tasks, particularly for larger MViTv2 models and video recognition, where it helps overcome overfitting from training on smaller datasets.
- **Efficiency:** MViTv2 models often show higher throughput (images/sec) and lower peak memory usage during training and inference compared to competitors like Swin Transformer, indicating better practical efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MViTv2 paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of computer vision, with substantial potential impact:

- **Towards Unified Vision Architectures:** The most profound impact of MViTv2 lies in its demonstration that a single, generalized hierarchical architecture can achieve state-of-the-art performance across highly diverse visual recognition tasks (image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and video recognition) with minimal task-specific modifications. This pushes the field closer to developing universal backbones for vision, simplifying research and deployment.
- **Improved Efficiency and Practicality for ViTs:** By refining the "pooling attention" mechanism with decomposed relative positional embeddings and residual pooling connections, MViTv2 successfully addresses the quadratic complexity challenge of standard Vision Transformers. This makes ViTs significantly more computationally efficient and memory-friendly for high-resolution tasks, broadening their practical applicability in real-world systems.
- **New State-of-the-Art Benchmarks:** MViTv2 sets new records on multiple highly competitive and widely-used benchmarks (ImageNet, COCO, Kinetics, SSv2, AVA). These superior performance figures serve as strong baselines and motivate further research and development in these areas.
- **Architectural Innovation:** The specific architectural improvements, particularly the *decomposed relative positional embeddings* and *residual pooling connections*, are valuable contributions that can be adopted or adapted by other Transformer-based models. They offer efficient ways to incorporate spatial and temporal context while managing computational overhead.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The authors' commitment to open-sourcing their code and models (via GitHub and PyTorchVideo) is crucial. This enables other researchers and developers to easily reproduce results, build upon the MViTv2 architecture, and integrate it into their own applications, accelerating progress in the broader AI community.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** As a highly performant and generalized vision backbone, MViTv2 has the potential to enhance a wide array of vision-related applications, including:
 - **Healthcare:** Improved accuracy in medical image analysis (e.g., cancer detection, disease diagnosis from X-rays or MRI).
 - **Manufacturing:** Enhanced visual inspection for quality control and defect detection.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** More robust object detection and video understanding for self-driving cars, drones, and robotics.
 - **Security and Surveillance:** Better person re-identification and anomaly detection.
 However, the authors prudently acknowledge the **potential negative societal impact** of advanced vision technologies if misused, particularly in surveillance systems that could infringe on privacy. This highlights the ongoing ethical considerations in AI research and deployment.

In conclusion, MViTv2 represents a significant step forward in making Vision Transformers more generalized, efficient, and powerful, solidifying their role as a leading architecture for diverse and challenging visual understanding tasks.
