# MaMi-HOI: Harmonizing Global Kinematics and Local Geometry for Human-Object Interaction Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at South China University of Technology developed MaMi-HOI, a framework for 3D Human-Object Interaction generation that harmonizes global kinematics with local geometry by addressing 'Geometric Forgetting'. This framework enabled physically plausible interactions with precise surface contact and natural human motion, reducing trajectory end-point error by over 50% and improving downstream task success rate by 6.02 percentage points on long-term tasks.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion models for 3D Human-Object Interaction (HOI) generation struggle to balance natural human motion with precise physical contact, leading to 'Geometric Forgetting'.
- Joint generation methods often produce artifacts like object floating or interpenetration due to implicitly entangled states, lacking explicit control over global trajectories.
- Waypoint-guided approaches treat spatial constraints as soft guidance, causing deep networks to lose fine-grained object geometry and sacrifice contact accuracy for overall semantic continuity.

## Method

- The **MaMi-HOI** framework augments a diffusion backbone with a ==dual-adapter paradigm== (==GAPA== and ==KHA==) to reconcile macro-level kinematic fluidity with micro-level spatial precision.
- A **Geometry-Aware Proximity Adapter (GAPA)** explicitly re-injects dense object geometry via ==distance-aware cross-attention== and applies ==residual snapping corrections== to ensure precise contact.
- A **Kinematic Harmony Adapter (KHA)** implicitly modulates the generative flow using a ==spatial-aware copy mechanism== and ==zero-linear injection== to maintain kinematically natural global motion and adherence to sparse waypoints.

## Results

- **MaMi-HOI** achieved the **lowest errors** for start, end, and intermediate waypoints on the FullBodyManipulation dataset, demonstrating strict adherence to spatial conditions.
- The model recorded the **best FID** for human motion quality and the highest F1 score and Contact Percentage while maintaining low penetration scores for interaction quality.
- In long-term tasks within 3D scenes, MaMi-HOI reduced trajectory end-point error by over **50%** and improved the downstream task success rate by **6.02 percentage points** compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 'Geometric Forgetting' is a core problem in HOI diffusion models, where fine-grained geometric details crucial for accurate physical contact are lost in deeper network layers.
> - A synergistic, coarse-to-fine approach, explicitly separating global kinematic control and local geometric refinement, effectively overcomes the limitations of single-stream HOI generation models.
> - Hard-wiring geometric locality into attention mechanisms (GAPA) and applying zero-linear injection for kinematic modulation (KHA) provide robust inductive biases for high-fidelity HOI synthesis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic 3D Human-Object Interactions (HOI) is a foundational task with applications spanning embodied artificial intelligence, virtual reality, and autonomous robotics. A key challenge in this field involves synthesizing dynamically evolving sequences where human motion is precisely coordinated with object manipulation, maintaining physical engagement.

Recent advancements have seen the successful application of diffusion models to HOI generation. Current methodologies in this area generally fall into two categories:
1. **Joint generation methods**: These implicitly entangle human and object states within single-stream diffusion models, often leveraging mechanisms like cross-modal attention. While effective in producing diverse semantic behaviors and short-term plausibility, they frequently struggle with adhering to strict physical constraints, leading to artifacts such as objects floating or penetrating the human body. These methods often lack explicit control over global trajectories, limiting their use in navigation tasks.
2. **Waypoint-guided methods**: These approaches introduce sparse object states as constraints to provide spatial grounding, bridging high-level planning with low-level synthesis. However, they typically treat spatial constraints as soft guidance. As network depth increases, strong semantic features tend to dominate, overshadowing fine-grained spatial cues. This results in models "forgetting" precise object geometry, sacrificing contact accuracy for overall semantic continuity.

Consequently, existing approaches often fail to achieve a simultaneous balance between kinematically natural motion and precise physical contact. This work addresses this specific limitation by identifying "Geometric Forgetting" as a core problem and proposing a hierarchical framework designed to reconcile these often conflicting requirements. It seeks to bridge the gap between generating natural human motions and ensuring accurate, physically plausible object contact within complex interaction sequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a framework capable of generating realistic 3D Human-Object Interactions that simultaneously exhibit both natural human motion and precise physical contact with objects.

The motivation for this work stems from an observed limitation in current diffusion-based HOI generation models, which the authors term "Geometric Forgetting." Through probing experiments, the authors found that as the depth of diffusion models increases, the network's alignment with high-level semantic features (e.g., the kinematic trajectory for lifting) strengthens. Concurrently, however, its sensitivity to fine-grained object geometry (e.g., surface boundaries, detailed shape) suffers a substantial degradation. This phenomenon leads to generated interactions that appear semantically plausible but are locally inaccurate, characterized by issues such as hand-object interpenetration or floating artifacts. The authors hypothesize that in standard single-stream diffusion transformers, semantic features tend to overshadow geometric features, causing precise geometric information crucial for valid contact to be diluted.

To counteract this "Geometric Forgetting," the key objectives are:
1. **Re-inject fine-grained geometric details**: Explicitly reintroduce dense object geometry into the model's processing pipeline to ensure accurate contact.
2. **Maintain global kinematic naturalness**: Prevent aggressive local geometric enforcement from disrupting the overall fluidity and naturalness of the human motion.
3. **Harmonize conflicting goals**: Design a framework that effectively balances these two often opposing requirements (local precision vs. global naturalness) without compromising either.
4. **Extend generation capabilities**: Enable the generation of long-term interaction sequences with complex trajectories, bridging the gap between global navigation and high-fidelity manipulation in 3D scenes.

The authors propose a "Retrieve-and-Harmonize" paradigm to achieve these objectives, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior methods that either sacrifice contact accuracy for naturalness or impose rigid constraints leading to stiff, unnatural movements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, MaMi-HOI, augments a diffusion backbone with a dual-adapter paradigm to reconcile macro-level kinematic fluidity with micro-level spatial precision. The overall system takes text descriptions, object geometry, and sparse waypoints as input to synthesize HOI sequences.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

- **Human Motion**: Represented as `H ∈ R^(T × Dh)`, where each frame `h_t` includes root translation, linear joint velocities, and 6D continuous rotations. The SMPL-X model reconstructs mesh vertices.
- **Object Motion**: Represented as `O ∈ R^(T × 12)`, with each frame `o_t` containing global centroid and a flattened relative rotation matrix. Vertices are recovered via rigid transformation.
- **Object Geometry (BPS)**: Basis Point Set (BPS) is used to capture fine-grained geometric details. It comprises `N = 1024` fixed points within a unit sphere, with directional vectors to their nearest neighbors on the object surface, forming `G ∈ R^(N × 3)`. This is encoded into a single `1 × 256` global geometry vector which is broadcast across time to form `Ĝ ∈ R^(T × 256)`.
- **Input Conditions**: Three conditions guide the diffusion:
 - Text (`T`): Encoded by a frozen CLIP model into `c_text`.
 - Spatial Waypoints (`S`): A masked sequence `S ∈ R^(T × (12+Dh))` providing 2D object positions at sparse intervals (e.g., every 30 frames) and a final 3D position, with unconstrained frames zero-padded.
 - Geometry Embedding (`Ĝ`): The broadcasted BPS feature for fine-grained contact modeling.
- **Conditional Diffusion Model**: The core is a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM). The clean joint state `x_0 = [H, O]` is progressively noised. A denoiser network `ε_θ` learns to predict the noise given the noisy state `x_t`, timestep `t`, and the multi-modal conditioning `C = {c_text, S, Ĝ}`. Training minimizes an MSE objective between predicted and actual noise.

#### 3.2. Geometry-Aware Proximity Adapter (GAPA)

GAPA is designed to explicitly counteract "Geometric Forgetting" by re-injecting dense object geometry and enabling precise contact via residual snapping corrections. It acts as a terminal retrieval mechanism.
- **Distance-Aware Cross-Attention**: GAPA employs a Distance-Aware Cross-Attention module (Figure 4). It projects motion features (`F_mot`) and object BPS embeddings (`F_obj`) into a latent geometric space to obtain coordinate representations (`P_mot`, `P_obj`).
- **Distance-Biased Attention Scoring**: The relative difference vector `δ_ij` between feature pairs is computed. The squared Euclidean distance `∥δ_ij∥²` is incorporated as a negative bias into the attention scoring function `S_ij = (Q_i K_j^⊤ / √d) - γ ⋅ ∥δ_ij∥²`, where `γ` is a learnable scalar. This biases attention towards immediate contact geometry.
- **Residual Snapping Correction**: Geometric context is computed by aggregating values `V_j` along with a function of the relative difference `ψ(δ_ij)`. This context is projected to form a residual field `∆F_geo`, which is then added to the original motion features: `F_refined = F_mot + ∆F_geo`. This non-destructive refinement preserves semantic motion while adding precise adjustments to snap end-effectors onto the object surface. GAPA architecturally hard-wires a geometric inductive bias, providing direct, fine-grained correction cues.

#### 3.3. Kinematic Harmony Adapter (KHA)

KHA addresses the issue that aggressive local enforcement by GAPA can disrupt global dynamics and lead to unnatural, stiff motion. It acts as a parallel resonance module that implicitly modulates the generative flow to ensure the skeleton actively accommodates spatial objectives.
- **Spatial-Aware Copy Mechanism**: KHA mirrors the main Diffusion Transformer's architecture as a parallel branch. It accepts the same motion inputs but is conditioned on a spatial embedding derived from sparse waypoints `S` fused with the timestep embedding. This specialization allows KHA to extract control-specific features for trajectory adherence, while the main backbone focuses on intrinsic motion distribution.
- **Zero-Linear Injection**: Each KHA layer is connected to the corresponding main branch layer via a zero-initialized linear layer `Z(⋅)`. The injection is `F_main^(l) ← F_main^(l) + Z(F_kha^(l))`. This mechanism ensures the model's initial distribution is preserved at the start of training, gradually allowing KHA to inject meaningful spatial modulations that guide generated features to satisfy waypoints while maintaining kinematic coherence.

#### 3.4. Synergistic Integration

MaMi-HOI integrates GAPA and KHA in a complementary fashion. KHA establishes a kinematically plausible global posture, reducing the deformation burden for GAPA's terminal fine-tuning. This coarse-to-fine synergy allows the framework to balance physical accuracy and biological naturalness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments on two datasets: FullBodyManipulation and 3D-FUTURE.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **FullBodyManipulation Dataset (Table 1)**:
 - **Condition Matching**: MaMi-HOI achieved the lowest errors for start (`T_s`), end (`T_e`), and intermediate (`T_xy`) waypoints, outperforming HOI-Dyn and other baselines. This indicates strict adherence to input waypoints.
 - **Human Motion Quality**: The model achieved the best Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and R-precision, surpassing CHOIS. This suggests generated motions are both distributionally closer to real dynamics and semantically consistent, with KHA contributing to harmonizing the global motion manifold.
 - **Interaction Quality**: MaMi-HOI recorded the highest F1 score (`C_F1`) and Contact Percentage (`C%`). Notably, it maintained a lower Penetration Score (`P_hand`) compared to CHOIS and HOI-Dyn. This demonstrates GAPA's effectiveness in resolving fine-grained physical conflicts by achieving high contact with minimal interpenetration.
- **3D-FUTURE Dataset (Table 2)**: This dataset tested generalization to unseen object geometries.
 - MaMi-HOI maintained superiority in spatial alignment, showing the lowest Condition Matching errors.
 - It generated more realistic motions (better FID) than MDM, Lin-OMOMO, and CHOIS.
 - While its penetration score was slightly higher than CHOIS, it achieved a noticeably higher Contact Percentage (`C%`) and R-precision (`R_prec`). The authors interpret this as MaMi-HOI actively attempting to satisfy contact constraints on complex unseen shapes, leading to more plausible overall interaction despite the domain gap, whereas baselines might avoid contact to minimize penetration. The average hand-to-object surface distance was also significantly lower for MaMi-HOI (6.05 mm vs. CHOIS's 9.59 mm) on active interaction frames (Appendix E.6), supporting the claim of tighter physical engagement.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Figure 5)

Visual comparisons with CHOIS demonstrated that MaMi-HOI produced physically plausible interactions with precise surface contact and coherent whole-body dynamics. CHOIS frequently suffered from severe hand-object penetration, floating artifacts, and inharmonious body movements (e.g., unnatural spine curvature), indicating a disconnection between local goals and global kinematics. MaMi-HOI's dual-adapter framework successfully mitigated these artifacts.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

- **Removing KHA**: Led to a degradation in human motion quality, evidenced by an increase in FID and larger ground truth differences, confirming its role in ensuring global kinematic coherence and realism.
- **Removing GAPA**: Primarily impaired spatial precision, resulting in increased condition matching errors and slightly higher hand-object penetration. This validated GAPA's criticality for fine-grained geometric alignment.
- **Full Model**: The complete MaMi-HOI model achieved an optimal balance across all metrics, underscoring the complementary nature of both adapters. A further analysis (Table 8, Appendix E.4) showed KHA excelled in trajectory adherence, while GAPA improved local contact.

#### 4.4. Application in 3D Scenes (Figure 6, Table 4)

MaMi-HOI was deployed in complex 3D environments (Replica dataset) through a three-stage pipeline (instruction parsing, global planning, interaction synthesis).
- **Visualizations**: Demonstrated coherent agent behaviors, such as naturally dragging a clothes stand or performing a coordinated full-body push on a large box, adapting posture to object structure and environment.
- **Long-term Synthesis**: Quantitative evaluation on long-term tasks (Table 4) showed MaMi-HOI significantly reduced trajectory end-point error (`T_e`) by over 50% compared to CHOIS and maintained a high Contact Percentage, indicating reliable performance over extended temporal horizons in complex scenarios.
- **Downstream Task Success Rate (TSR)**: MaMi-HOI improved the heuristic TSR by 6.02 percentage points over the baseline (Table 9, Appendix E.5), demonstrating enhanced practical utility for macroscopic object transport tasks requiring both target reaching and stable physical engagement.

#### 4.5. Dataset-Wide Probe for Geometric Forgetting (Table 6, Appendix E.1)

A systematic feature-probing experiment confirmed the "Geometric Forgetting" phenomenon in baseline models, where fine-grained BPS information was nearly absent in deeper layers. In contrast, MaMi-HOI maintained and even increased geometric alignment across layers, preserving a distinct geometric signal within its deep latent representations, thereby supporting the efficacy of the proposed adapters in counteracting this issue.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the field of 3D Human-Object Interaction generation by explicitly identifying and addressing "Geometric Forgetting," a critical bottleneck in achieving high-fidelity interactions. The paper demonstrates that relying solely on implicit feature entanglement within diffusion models is insufficient for simultaneously harmonizing fluid whole-body dynamics with strict physical contact constraints.

The proposed MaMi-HOI framework offers a novel "Retrieve-and-Harmonize" paradigm. By introducing the Geometry-Aware Proximity Adapter (GAPA), the work directly tackles the loss of geometric detail in deeper network layers, enabling precise, sub-centimeter contact. Complementarily, the Kinematic Harmony Adapter (KHA) ensures that this local precision does not compromise global kinematic naturalness, preventing stiff or unnatural motions. This dual-adapter approach effectively balances two often conflicting requirements, leading to interactions that are both physically accurate and biologically plausible.

The significance and potential impact of MaMi-HOI include:
- **Advancement in HOI Realism**: The framework generates motions with high contact precision and naturalness, setting a new benchmark for realistic 3D HOI synthesis.
- **Bridging Global and Local Control**: Its ability to extend generation capabilities to long-term tasks with complex, non-linear trajectories, bridging the gap between global navigation and high-fidelity manipulation in 3D scenes, is crucial for embodied AI applications.
- **Applications in Virtual Environments**: The improved realism and controllability have direct positive implications for virtual reality, gaming, and the creation of immersive digital content, potentially reducing manual animation effort.
- **Synthetic Data Generation**: MaMi-HOI can be used to generate high-quality synthetic data for training other machine learning models in embodied AI and robotics, where realistic human-object interactions are essential.
- **Novel Architectural Inductive Bias**: GAPA's distance-aware cross-attention mechanism introduces an efficient way to hard-wire geometric locality directly into the attention mechanism, which could inspire future architectural designs for handling spatial constraints.

Current limitations include reliance on standard SMPL-X topology, which restricts finger-level resolution, potentially omitting high-frequency articulatory details. Future work aims to integrate high-fidelity hand models and explore multi-agent scalability, further enhancing the realism and complexity of generated interactions. The authors state that, to their knowledge, the work focuses on generic interaction physics and does not have specific negative societal consequences related to misleading content, common in some generative models.
