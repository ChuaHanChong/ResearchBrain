# CLAW: Learning Continuous Latent Action World Models via Adversarial Latent Regularization

> [!summary] Summary
> CLAW, developed by researchers from the University of Chicago, Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago, and Argonne National Laboratory, introduces a self-supervised framework for jointly learning continuous latent action representations and environment dynamics directly from action-free videos. It utilizes adversarial latent regularization and a diffusion-based world model, achieving improved performance in imitation learning from observation and goal-directed planning across various simulated and real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- World models often rely on explicit action labels, limiting generalization and scalability from abundant unlabeled video data.
- Latent action models struggle to learn semantically meaningful continuous representations without information leakage (encoding future observations) or representation collapse.
- Existing methods for latent action learning often involve discretization or require some action-labeled data, which can limit fine-grained control and data efficiency.

## Method

- **CLAW** is an end-to-end ==self-supervised== framework that jointly trains a Latent Action Model (LAM) and a ==diffusion==-based ==World Model== from action-free videos.
- The LAM infers continuous, L2-normalized latent action representations from consecutive observations, while the diffusion World Model predicts future frames conditioned on these latent actions and initial observations.
- A novel adversarial latent regularization strategy, incorporating a gradient reversal layer and scheduled weighting, is used to prevent the LAM from encoding future visual information into the latent actions.

## Results

- CLAW achieved stronger imitation-learning-from-observation (ILfO) performance, outperforming baselines on Crafter (e.g. Wood Collection) and OGBench, and in real-world robot manipulation pretraining, notably on the long-horizon 'Push and Cover Duck' task.
- Achieved higher success rates in visual planning tasks, outperforming baselines on 7 out of 10 tasks across VP2, Crafter, and Procgen environments, indicating effective planning capabilities through the learned world model.
- Qualitatively, CLAW's retrieved nearest-neighbor latent actions consistently showed similar end-effector motion across varied visual contexts, suggesting meaningful action disentanglement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial regularization effectively discourages information leakage from future observations into latent action representations, promoting the learning of pure action semantics.
> - Constraining continuous latent actions to a unit sphere helps structure the latent space, making action representations more semantically meaningful and consistent.
> - End-to-end training of the Latent Action Model and a diffusion-based World Model allows for reciprocal supervision, leading to more robust and controllable learned dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the domain of model-based reinforcement learning and robotics, specifically concerning the development of "world models." World models are predictive models that capture how an agent's actions affect its environment, enabling capabilities such as planning, control, and policy optimization. Historically, these models have often relied on explicit action labels, which introduces limitations regarding generalization across different robotic embodiments and scalability with the large volume of unlabeled video data available.

Recent research efforts have focused on "latent action world models," which aim to infer action representations directly from visual observations. This approach allows training without explicit action labels and offers the potential for improved transferability across diverse tasks and agent morphologies. However, a significant challenge in this area is learning latent actions that are semantically meaningful—meaning they consistently represent similar effects across different contexts. Without proper regularization, latent action models can suffer from "information leakage," where the latent representation encodes future observational content rather than the action itself, or "representation collapse," where the latent actions become uninformative. Prior methods have attempted to mitigate these issues through techniques such as quantization (e.g., VQ-VAE based discretization in Genie and LAPO) or variational constraints (e.g., AdaWorld). Some approaches also incorporate limited action-labeled data to provide supervision.

CLAW contributes to this landscape by introducing an end-to-end self-supervised framework that aims to overcome the challenges of learning semantically meaningful continuous latent action representations and robust environment dynamics concurrently, without relying on action labels or quantization. It proposes a novel adversarial latent regularization strategy and integrates diffusion-based video generation to enhance the structure and utility of the learned representations, supporting both imitation learning from observation and goal-directed planning. This positions CLAW as an advancement in enabling robots to learn complex behaviors from observation alone, promoting data efficiency and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Develop an end-to-end self-supervised framework for joint learning:** The core objective is to create a unified system that simultaneously learns a world model and continuous latent action representations. This framework is designed to operate solely from action-free videos, eliminating the need for explicit action labels or annotations during training.
- **Learn semantically meaningful continuous latent action representations:** A significant motivation is to ensure that the learned latent actions are structured and carry semantic meaning. This means that transitions with similar effects should map to proximate points in the latent space, regardless of specific visual contexts or embodiments. Such meaningful representations are crucial for grounded action transfer and downstream policy learning with minimal supervision.
- **Model rich, predictive environment dynamics:** The framework aims to learn a world model that accurately predicts future environment states (visual observations) conditioned on inferred latent actions. This predictive capability is foundational for planning and evaluating potential actions in imagination.
- **Address information leakage and representation collapse:** A critical challenge in latent action learning is preventing the latent action representation from encoding future observation content (information leakage) or becoming uninformative (representation collapse). The research seeks to develop mechanisms to counteract these degenerate solutions.
- **Support diverse downstream tasks:** Once trained, the learned latent action world model is intended to be versatile, supporting two key robotic learning paradigms:
 - **Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO):** Enabling agents to learn policies by imitating behaviors observed in action-free videos.
 - **Goal-directed Planning:** Facilitating the generation of sequences of latent actions that guide the agent towards specified goals within the learned environment dynamics.
- **Demonstrate competitive or superior performance:** The motivation extends to showing that the proposed CLAW framework achieves performance levels comparable to or exceeding existing methods across a range of tasks, environments, and robotic embodiments, both quantitatively and qualitatively.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the limitations of current approaches, which often rely on explicit action labels, struggle with learning truly meaningful latent actions, or employ discrete action spaces that can limit fine-grained control. By addressing these aspects, CLAW aims to leverage the abundance of unlabeled video data to enable more scalable, generalizable, and data-efficient robotic learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLAW (Continuous Latent Action World Models via Adversarial Latent Regularization) is a fully end-to-end self-supervised framework that jointly trains a Latent Action Model (LAM) and a diffusion-based World Model (WM) from action-free videos. The framework formulates the problem as learning the observation transition dynamics $P(o_{i+1} | o_i, z_i)$, where $z_i$ is a learned latent variable representing the action.

The methodology consists of three main components:

#### 3.1. Latent Action Model (LAM)

- **Function:** The LAM is an inverse dynamics model designed to infer a continuous latent action representation $z_i$ from consecutive observations $(o_i, o_{i+1})$.
- **Architecture:** It employs a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone to process image patches from the input frames. These patches are augmented with both spatial and temporal positional encodings to maintain structural and temporal context. A learnable action token is concatenated with the spatio-temporal patch tokens.
- **Output:** The ViT encoder processes this sequence, and the final representation of the action token is projected into a compact $R^n$ continuous latent action vector. This vector is then $\ell_2$-normalized, constraining it to lie on the unit sphere, which is intended to promote a structured latent space.
- **Training:** The LAM is trained end-to-end with the World Model.

#### 3.2. Diffusion-based World Model

- **Function:** This model represents the environment dynamics and predicts a sequence of future frames $\{o_j\}_{j=i+1}^{i+c+1}$ given an initial observation frame $o_i$ and a sequence of latent action embeddings $\{z_j\}_{j=i}^{i+c}$ inferred by the LAM.
- **Architecture:** It utilizes a U-ViT backbone, a transformer architecture that operates on image patches. The input to the model includes the diffusion timestep embedding $t_\tau$, latent action tokens $\{z_j\}$, conditioning frame patches $o_i$, and noisy patches from the target observations $\{x_j\}$.
- **Causal Attention Mask:** To ensure causal consistency and prevent information leakage from future observations or actions, a modified causal attention mask is applied. This mask restricts each noised target token to attend only to other tokens within the same frame, tokens from the initial conditioning frame, and action tokens corresponding to transitions up to the timestep of the noised target frame. This ensures that predictions are based on valid action-conditioned dynamics.
- **Training Objective:** The world model is trained to optimize a score network $s_\phi(x_t, t, z_t, o_i)$ to predict the noise $\epsilon$ added during the forward diffusion process. This minimizes the mean squared error between the predicted noise and the true noise (Equation 1). The process uses a variance-preserving stochastic differential equation to progressively add Gaussian noise to target frames.

#### 3.3. Adversarial Information Regularization

- **Motivation:** To prevent the LAM from taking a shortcut by simply encoding future observation $o_{i+1}$ into $z_i$, which would bypass learning true action semantics.
- **Mechanism:** During end-to-end training, the world model is presented with two types of inputs for predicting $o_{i+1}$:
 1. The initial observation $o_i$ and the latent action $z_i$.
 2. Only the latent action $z_i$.
 A gradient reversal layer is applied between the latent action $z_i$ and the first layer of the world model when only $z_i$ is provided as input (without $o_i$). This layer scales and inverts the gradients, effectively discouraging the LAM from encoding future visual information into $z_i$ because accurate predictions from $z_i$ alone are penalized. Simultaneously, the world model's accurate predictions based on both $o_i$ and $z_i$ encourage $z_i$ to encode useful action information.
- **Weight Scheduling:** The strength of this adversarial signal is controlled by a gradually increasing weight $\delta$, following a sigmoidal schedule (Equation 2). This schedule initially prioritizes accurate predictions but progressively increases the pressure on the LAM to avoid future information leakage.

#### 3.4. Downstream Applications

Once trained, the CLAW model can be used in two modes:
- **Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO):** Latent actions extracted from expert videos using the LAM serve as targets for a behavior cloning policy. During inference, predicted latent actions are mapped to executable actions via nearest-neighbor retrieval from a small, action-annotated reference set.
- **Goal-directed Planning:** Model Predictive Control (MPC) with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is used to optimize sequences of latent actions through the learned world model to reach a goal observation. Similar to ILfO, latent actions are mapped to true actions via nearest-neighbor retrieval for execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates CLAW across 14 tasks in 5 simulated environments and 3 real-world robotic tasks, comparing it against baselines such as AdaWorld, LAPO, and LAPA.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Learned Latent Action Representation

- **Latent Action Policy Learning (Imitation Learning from Observation - ILfO):**
 - **Setup:** Latent actions are extracted from action-free expert videos using the trained LAM to train a behavior cloning policy. During inference, predicted latent actions are mapped to executable actions via nearest-neighbor lookup in a small reference set.
 - **Environments:** Crafter (Greedy Wood Collection, Single Wood Collection) and OGBench (Open Drawer, Close Drawer, Push Button).
 - **Results (Table 1):** CLAW consistently achieves stronger performance than all baselines across Crafter tasks, with higher success rates and average wood collected. On OGBench, CLAW shows improved performance in "Open Drawer" and "Close Drawer" compared to baselines, and competitive performance in "Push Button." These results suggest CLAW's latent representations capture environment interaction dynamics for downstream policy learning effectively.
- **Real-world Latent Action Pretraining:**
 - **Setup:** A latent action world model is trained on human video (Something-Something V2). The pretrained LAM infers latent actions from action-free robot demonstration videos, which are then used to pretrain an ACT robot policy. The vision encoder is frozen, and the policy is fine-tuned on limited action-labeled robot demonstrations for specific tasks.
 - **Environments:** Real-world tabletop manipulation tasks (Push Duck, Cover Duck, Push and Cover Duck).
 - **Results (Table 3):** All latent action pretraining methods, including CLAW, demonstrate improved performance over training from scratch. CLAW achieves the strongest overall performance on the long-horizon "Push and Cover Duck" task, indicating improved modeling of compositional behaviors.
- **Closest Action Retrieval:**
 - **Setup:** Qualitatively evaluates the latent space structure by retrieving L2 nearest-neighbor latent actions for a given query transition and visualizing the corresponding frame transitions.
 - **Results (Figure 2, Figures 6-10):** CLAW's retrieved nearest neighbors exhibit similar end-effector motion despite variations in non-action information (e.g., object identity, scene color). This suggests that CLAW learns latent actions that capture meaningful behavioral structure rather than superficial visual similarities, in contrast to baselines which often retrieve visually similar scenes regardless of action semantics.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of the Dynamics Model

- **Visual Planning:**
 - **Setup:** MPC-CEM is used to plan sequences of latent actions through the learned world model towards a goal observation. Latent actions are mapped to true actions via nearest-neighbor retrieval.
 - **Environments:** VP2 (Robosuite, RoboDesk), Crafter, and Procgen (Climber, Jumper, Ninja).
 - **Results (Table 4, Table 2):** CLAW achieves the highest success rate on four out of six VP2 tasks. In Crafter and Procgen, CLAW achieves the best performance on three out of four tasks and the highest average success rate overall. The results indicate that CLAW's structured latent action representation supports effective planning, although specific tasks (e.g., Green and Blue Button in VP2) presented challenges, potentially due to partial occlusion or camera viewpoint limitations.
- **Action Transfer:**
 - **Setup:** Qualitatively evaluates the ability to transfer actions by extracting a sequence of latent actions from a source video and applying it to an initial observation from a different target video, then comparing the predicted rollout.
 - **Results (Figure 3, Figures 12-14):** CLAW's predicted rollouts demonstrate that transferred actions induce end-effector motion closely matching the source behavior while adapting to the target scene's object shapes and positions. This suggests that CLAW successfully disentangles actions from non-action observations and learns robust, plausible dynamics.
- **Controllability (Appendix C.1):**
 - **Setup:** Evaluated using the $\Delta_t$PSNR metric, which measures how strongly generated rollouts depend on conditioning actions by comparing predictions from inferred vs. random latent actions.
 - **Results (Table 7):** CLAW exhibits higher $\Delta_t$PSNR values compared to a variant without adversarial regularization, indicating stronger action influence and improved controllability of the world model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLAW framework presents a significant contribution to the fields of robotic learning and world modeling, primarily through its novel approach to jointly learning continuous latent action representations and environment dynamics in a self-supervised manner.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **learn directly from action-free videos**, which is crucial for scalability, as it bypasses the need for costly and labor-intensive action labeling. This allows for leveraging vast amounts of readily available video data (e.g., human activity videos, robot play data) to train foundational models for robot behavior.

A key technical advancement is the **adversarial latent regularization strategy**. This mechanism effectively addresses the persistent challenge of "information leakage," where latent action models tend to encode future observations rather than pure action semantics. By promoting semantically meaningful continuous latent actions, CLAW ensures that the learned representations are structured and generalize better across different contexts and embodiments. The use of continuous latent actions, as opposed to discrete ones, may facilitate finer-grained control and improved performance in various tasks.

The **end-to-end training of the Latent Action Model and world model** is another important aspect, as it allows for reciprocal supervision, where improvements in one component benefit the other. This integrated approach can lead to more coherent and robust latent action world models.

The demonstrated capabilities of CLAW in **imitation learning from observation (ILfO)** and **goal-directed planning** highlight its practical utility. For ILfO, it offers a pathway for robots to learn new skills from observing human or other agent behaviors without requiring explicit action demonstrations. For planning, the learned dynamics enable agents to evaluate and optimize action sequences in imagination, a fundamental requirement for intelligent autonomous systems. The results showing improved performance in real-world latent action pretraining suggest that CLAW can enhance sample efficiency and transferability in practical robotics applications.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Reduced Data Annotation Burden:** By eliminating the need for action labels, CLAW can accelerate the development of robotic systems by enabling learning from readily available, large-scale, unlabeled video datasets.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Transfer:** The learning of semantically meaningful latent actions can lead to policies that generalize more effectively across different tasks, environments, and robot morphologies, reducing the need for task-specific retraining.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** CLAW contributes to the development of more capable embodied AI agents that can learn and reason about their interactions with the world from rich visual data, moving towards more human-like cognition in robotics.
- **Improved Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** By providing robust and controllable world models, CLAW can advance model-based reinforcement learning, which is known for its data efficiency.

**Limitations:**
The paper identifies two main limitations. First, the current world model generates future observations based solely on the most recent frame and latent action, lacking an explicit memory of past generations. This could lead to inconsistencies in long-horizon predictions or partially observable environments. Incorporating explicit memory mechanisms is suggested as a direction for future work. Second, while CLAW eliminates the need for action labels during world model training, a small set of labeled ground-truth action data remains necessary to map the inferred latent actions to executable controls for real-world deployment. This implies that full autonomy from observation alone is not yet achieved for direct execution.
