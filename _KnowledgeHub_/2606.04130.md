# CLAW: Learning Continuous Latent Action World Models via Adversarial Latent Regularization

> [!summary] Summary
> CLAW, developed by researchers from the University of Chicago, Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago, and Argonne National Laboratory, introduces a self-supervised framework for jointly learning continuous latent action representations and environment dynamics directly from action-free videos. It utilizes adversarial latent regularization and a diffusion-based world model, achieving improved performance in imitation learning from observation and goal-directed planning across various simulated and real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- World models often rely on explicit action labels, limiting generalization and scalability from abundant unlabeled video data.
- Latent action models struggle to learn semantically meaningful continuous representations without information leakage (encoding future observations) or representation collapse.
- Existing methods for latent action learning often involve discretization or require some action-labeled data, which can limit fine-grained control and data efficiency.

## Method

- **CLAW** is an end-to-end ==self-supervised== framework that jointly trains a Latent Action Model (LAM) and a ==diffusion==-based ==World Model== from action-free videos.
- The LAM infers continuous, L2-normalized latent action representations from consecutive observations, while the diffusion World Model predicts future frames conditioned on these latent actions and initial observations.
- A novel adversarial latent regularization strategy, incorporating a gradient reversal layer and scheduled weighting, is used to prevent the LAM from encoding future visual information into the latent actions.

## Results

- CLAW achieved stronger performance in imitation learning from observation (ILfO) tasks, consistently outperforming baselines on Crafter tasks (e.g., higher success rates in Wood Collection) and showing improvements on OGBench.
- Demonstrated enhanced real-world latent action pretraining for robot manipulation, particularly achieving the strongest overall performance on the long-horizon "Push and Cover Duck" task.
- Achieved higher success rates in visual planning tasks, outperforming baselines on 7 out of 10 tasks across VP2, Crafter, and Procgen environments, indicating effective planning capabilities through the learned world model.
- Qualitatively, CLAW's retrieved nearest-neighbor latent actions consistently showed similar end-effector motion across varied visual contexts, suggesting meaningful action disentanglement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial regularization effectively discourages information leakage from future observations into latent action representations, promoting the learning of pure action semantics.
> - Constraining continuous latent actions to a unit sphere helps structure the latent space, making action representations more semantically meaningful and consistent.
> - End-to-end training of the Latent Action Model and a diffusion-based World Model allows for reciprocal supervision, leading to more robust and controllable learned dynamics.
