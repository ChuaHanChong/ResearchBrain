# One-to-Two Acting: A Novel Framework for Single-arm Agent Action Expansion to Dual Arms

> [!summary] Summary
> ExS2D is a hierarchical framework that upgrades a single-arm manipulation agent to dual-arm execution without any bimanual demonstrations. A fine-tuned MLLM generates precedence-structured subtasks, a mask-guided CLIPort grounds each into pick-place poses, and an MLLM coordinator allocates actions to arms via cost heuristics plus synchronized RRT* collision checking. It cuts average simulation execution steps by 54.4% at comparable success rate.

## Problem

- Dual-arm policies depend on bimanual datasets whose teleoperated collection is expensive, latency-prone, or hardware-heavy, and often assume static setups or extra domain engineering.
- Existing dual-arm policies use predefined rigid strategies that cannot adapt to real-time changes such as arm malfunctions or irregular task conditions.
- Mature single-arm agents generate strictly sequential actions, ignoring inter-action dependency, spatial relations, and arm synchronization, so parallelizable rearrangement tasks run inefficiently.

## Method

- **VL-SubGen** fine-tunes an ==MLLM== (LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) with two caption losses: a scene-description perception loss and a demonstration-derived task-decomposition loss, weighted into one objective.
- **SA-Map** grounds subtasks by querying ==OWL-ViT== for a bounding box, refining it to a ==SAM== mask, and masking the observation so ==CLIPort=='s pick/place Q-maps focus on task-relevant regions.
- **P-DCoord** partitions subtasks into precedence levels whose predecessors are complete, greedily ranks arm assignments by movement plus transfer distance cost, then verifies candidates with synchronized ==RRT*== collision checking.

## Results

- Macro-average across four simulated tasks: **2.86** steps versus the single-arm CLIPort baseline's 6.27, a **54.4%** reduction, with success rate **87.36%** versus 88.57% (1.22% drop).
- **ExS2D** beats dual-arm planners LLM+MAP (70.22% SR, 3.43 steps) and RoCo (73.78% SR, 3.29 steps) on both success and step count.
- Ablations: removing mask guidance drops SR **17.15% to 70.21%**; removing precedence reasoning drops SR to 63.09% and raises cost to 2.24; oracle upper bound reaches **92.57%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parallelism can be recovered post hoc: a dependency-level decomposition plus cost-ranked allocation converts sequential single-arm primitives into concurrent dual-arm execution without new demonstrations.
> - Segmentation masks that suppress irrelevant scene regions matter more than expected for affordance grounding, and adaptive masks clearly beat fixed ones.
> - The gains are bounded by an open-loop, planar pick-place abstraction and by upstream subtask prediction, whose token accuracy degrades as instruction horizon lengthens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation increasingly seeks to enhance the capabilities and efficiency of robotic systems, particularly through the use of multi-arm configurations. Dual-arm manipulators offer significant advantages in terms of throughput, versatility, and the ability to perform complex bimanual coordination for tasks such as tabletop rearrangement. However, scaling dual-arm policies in practice presents considerable challenges. A primary obstacle is the high cost and difficulty associated with collecting high-quality bimanual demonstration datasets for training. Many existing dual-arm approaches frequently rely on such datasets, or necessitate extensive domain engineering and strong assumptions for reliable arm coordination. Furthermore, these methods often employ predefined rigid strategies, which limit their flexibility and adaptability to real-time changes or task irregularities.

In contrast, single-arm robotic agents have matured considerably, benefiting from abundant single-arm samples for supervision and offering readily reusable low-level action primitives. These agents have demonstrated versatility and scalability, particularly when combined with Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level planning and contextual understanding. However, a fundamental limitation of current single-arm agents is their design for sequential single-action generation. This sequential operation restricts their efficiency, especially for rearrangement tasks that could be executed simultaneously by multiple manipulators. While some methods extend single-arm planning to dual-arm settings, they often fall short of enabling simultaneous multi-object manipulation because they do not adequately account for inter-action dependencies, spatial relationships, or synchronized coordination between arms.

This work addresses the gap between the mature capabilities of single-arm agents and the underdeveloped, data-hungry nature of many dual-arm systems. It proposes a framework to upgrade single-arm agents to dual-arm execution without requiring bimanual datasets. By doing so, it aims to leverage existing single-arm manipulation skills, reduce the cost and effort of data collection, and enable efficient, parallel execution for tasks that traditionally suffer from the sequential limitations of single-arm policies. This approach is positioned as a method to overcome the reliance on explicit environment representations or domain-specific descriptions, moving towards more perception-driven, end-to-end dual-arm manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a hierarchical dual-arm manipulation policy capable of performing coordinated bimanual tasks using supervision derived solely from single-arm data, thereby eliminating the need for costly bimanual demonstrations. The work is motivated by the recognized benefits of dual-arm manipulation, such as improved throughput and versatility through parallel execution, juxtaposed with the practical difficulties of training and deploying such systems.

Specifically, the authors identify and aim to address three key challenges:

1. **Subtask Structuring:** Long-horizon task instructions are often underspecified, making it difficult for robotic systems to automatically derive a complete and temporally consistent set of atomic subtasks. The objective is to enable the system to automatically decompose complex human instructions into structured subtasks, explicitly capturing their temporal precedence and dependencies. This is crucial for planning parallel execution.

2. **Action Grounding:** Each derived subtask needs to be translated into precise, executable action primitives (e.g., pick-place poses) that are consistent with the current scene geometry. This requires robust object grounding, particularly in the presence of visual ambiguity and a dynamic environment. The motivation here is to reliably map abstract subtasks to concrete, low-level robotic actions.

3. **Dual-Arm Coordination:** After subtasks are identified and grounded into actions, these actions must be efficiently and safely allocated across two manipulators. This allocation must respect the derived precedence constraints, avoid collisions between the arms and with the environment, and ideally minimize execution effort to maximize efficiency. The objective is to achieve synchronized, collision-free dual-arm execution that exploits parallelism whenever possible.

By tackling these challenges, the authors aim to develop a framework that can transform single-arm operational capabilities into a dual-arm system, thereby offering increased efficiency for tasks amenable to parallel execution, enhancing the versatility of robotic manipulators, and significantly reducing the data collection burden typically associated with bimanual robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose ExS2D (Extending Single-Arm Agent Actions to Dual Arms), a hierarchical framework designed to translate single-arm supervision into coordinated dual-arm execution. The framework comprises three main modules: VL-SubGen for subtask generation, SA-Map for action grounding, and P-DCoord for dual-arm coordination.

**A. Vision-Language Subtask Generation (VL-SubGen)**
This module is responsible for predicting a structured sequence of subtasks (\(\hat{S}_t\)) from multimodal input. It employs a fine-tuned Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that takes visual observations (\(o_t\)), an environment description (\(e_t\)), and a task instruction (\(l_t\)) as input. To enhance the MLLM's ability to generate precise subtasks, it is trained using two complementary caption-based supervision signals:
- **Static Spatial Understanding:** The MLLM is trained to generate an informative scene description (\(e_t\)) from a single observation (\(o_t\)), fostering an understanding of object attributes and spatial relations. This is supervised via a perception loss (\(L_{perc}\)).
- **Dynamic Action Understanding:** The MLLM is supervised to infer the induced subtask sequence (\(S_t\)) from demonstration sequences (\(o_{1:T}\)), enabling it to capture temporal dependencies and action semantics based on visual changes. This is supervised via a task decomposition loss (\(L_{task}\)).
The total loss for VL-SubGen is a weighted combination: \(L = \lambda L_{perc} + L_{task}\). This joint training aims to integrate spatial grounding with action-level reasoning for reliable subtask planning.

**B. Subtask-Guided Action Mapping (SA-Map)**
The SA-Map module implements the action policy (\(\pi_{act}\)), translating each predicted subtask (\(s_i\)) into an executable pick-place action (\(a_i = (T_{pk_i}, T_{pl_i})\)). The approach combines a subtask-conditioned vision-language signal with CLIPort.
- **Initial Grounding:** For a given subtask, a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically OWL-ViT, processes a cropped observation and a textual query to predict an object-level bounding box. This bounding box is then refined into a fine-grained segmentation mask using the Segment Anything Model (SAM).
- **CLIPort Integration:** The generated mask guides CLIPort's attention, producing spatially focused probability maps that highlight task-relevant regions for pick and place operations. CLIPort, pre-trained with oracle actions, computes the optimal pick pose (\(T_{pk_i}\)) and subsequent place transformation (\(T_{pl_i}\)) using its learned spatial Q-maps.
- **Mask-Guided Refinement:** To handle multiple objects or sequential actions within a subtask, a localized crop around the initial pick/place target is re-queried with the VLM. The resulting bounding box is again processed by SAM to generate a refined object mask. This mask is applied to the observation, selectively preserving the target object's geometry while suppressing irrelevant scene content, and CLIPort is re-applied to infer subsequent pick-place actions. This refinement aims to ensure robust localization for multiple objects.

**C. Precedence-aware Dual-Arm Coordinator (P-DCoord)**
P-DCoord realizes the allocation policy (\(\pi_{alloc}\)) by using an MLLM-driven allocator to reason over the precedence constraints within the subtask sequence (\(S_t\)).
- **Precedence Reasoning:** The MLLM, conditioned on the environment description and observation, partitions \(S_t\) into a sequence of ready sets (\(\{L_k\}\)). Each \(L_k\) contains subtasks whose predecessors have been completed, allowing them to be executed in parallel.
- **Action Allocation:** For the current ready set (\(L_t\)), grounded actions are provided by SA-Map. A greedy-based strategy enumerates candidate arm assignments for these actions.
- **Heuristic Cost Estimation:** A heuristic cost is calculated for each candidate assignment, comprising a movement cost (Euclidean distance from current end-effector pose to pick pose) and a transfer cost (distance from pick to place pose). This provides an approximation of execution effort.
- **Collision Checking and Planning:** Based on cost estimation, promising dual-arm assignments are selected. For each candidate, a synchronized RRT* planner generates dual-arm trajectories. These trajectories are discretized and checked for arm-arm and arm-environment collisions.
- **Fallback Mechanism:** If all candidate dual-arm assignments are deemed infeasible due to collisions or other constraints, the system defaults to asynchronous execution and replans the allocation after resetting the arm.

The overall framework aims to minimize cumulative execution cost while respecting subtask precedence and exploiting dual-arm parallelism. The implementation details specify fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B for VL-SubGen, using CLIPort with OWL-ViT and SAM for SA-Map, and employing a pretrained Qwen3-VL as an external reasoner for P-DCoord.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated ExS2D through both language-conditioned simulation tasks and real-robot experiments.

**A. Quantitative Results (Simulation)**
Experiments were conducted on four language-conditioned simulation tasks from the Raven Bench. The performance was compared against a single-arm baseline (CLIPort) and two MLLM-based dual-arm planners (LLM+MAP, RoCo).
- **Efficiency:** ExS2D reduced the average execution steps by 54.4% compared to the single-arm CLIPort baseline.
- **Success Rate:** It maintained a comparable success rate to CLIPort, with only a 1.22% average decrease.
- **Comparison to Dual-Arm Planners:** ExS2D attained higher success rates with fewer steps than LLM+MAP and RoCo, demonstrating a more favorable balance between efficiency and success. Specifically, on tasks with strong temporal dependencies (e.g., "Stacking of Blocks in Zone"), ExS2D's precedence-aware allocation showed a clear advantage.

**B. Ablation Study**
Ablation experiments were performed to quantify the contribution of each ExS2D module:
- **VL-SubGen Reasoning Robustness:** The study showed that token accuracy consistently decreased as subtask sequence length increased for all variants. However, the full VL-SubGen (with both \(L_{perc}\) and \(L_{task}\)) maintained the highest robustness. Removing either \(L_{perc}\) or \(L_{task}\) led to a degradation in performance, with removing \(L_{task}\) causing the steepest drop, indicating the necessity of both supervision signals for stable reasoning.
- **SA-Map Masking Effect:** Removing mask guidance ("w/o Mask") resulted in a 17.15% decrease in success rate (SR), highlighting the importance of suppressing irrelevant regions for reliable action grounding. Using a fixed mask ("w/ Fixed Mask") partially recovered performance, indicating the benefit of adaptive semantic masking. The full SA-Map achieved a 87.36% SR and 2.02 Cost.
- **P-DCoord Allocation Effect:** "Without Precedence Reasoning" reduced the SR to 63.09% and increased the execution cost, confirming the critical role of dependency-aware coordination. "With Fixed Allocation" improved SR to 80.21% but incurred a higher cost, suggesting that while static assignment can complete tasks, it sacrifices efficiency due to reduced parallelism. The full model demonstrated the best trade-off, and an oracle setting (with perfect upstream predictions) showed a potential SR of 92.57%, suggesting further headroom for improvement.

**C. Further Analysis**
- **Model Scale:** Token accuracy for VL-SubGen improved with larger model scales, with Qwen2.5VL-7B performing best among the tested MLLM variants.
- **Inference Time:** Analysis of inference time decomposition showed that motion planning (Motion) dominated the overall time cost, while VL-SubGen and P-DCoord contributed a smaller, though non-negligible, overhead.

**D. Real-Robot Experiments**
ExS2D was tested on four real-world dual-arm tasks using Elephant Pro 630 robots.
- **Success Rates:** The framework achieved an average success rate of 60.51% across the four tasks.
- **Data Efficiency:** Crucially, these results were obtained using only few-shot single-arm samples for training (image-action pairs), and *zero* bimanual demonstrations or dual-arm datasets.
- **Complex Tasks:** Tasks requiring temporal dependencies (e.g., placing an object before covering it with a lid) and bimanual coordination (e.g., towel folding) were successfully accomplished, demonstrating the framework's reliability in such scenarios.
- **Affordance Visualization:** Visualizations confirmed that ExS2D could localize task-relevant regions and generate executable actions even with limited data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ExS2D presents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, primarily by addressing a significant bottleneck in dual-arm system deployment: the prohibitive cost and complexity of collecting bimanual demonstration data.

The primary significance lies in its capacity to enable **dual-arm manipulation from single-arm supervision**. This approach effectively bridges the gap between the mature, data-rich domain of single-arm robotics and the data-scarce realm of bimanual tasks. By leveraging abundant single-arm datasets, ExS2D offers a pathway to upgrade existing single-arm capabilities to dual-arm execution, thereby **substantially reducing the data collection burden and associated costs** for developing bimanual robotic systems. This has the potential to accelerate research and development in dual-arm manipulation and lower the entry barrier for practical applications.

The framework's hierarchical design, encompassing subtask decomposition, mask-guided action grounding, and precedence-aware coordination, contributes to **improved efficiency and robustness** in task execution. The demonstrated 54.4% reduction in average execution steps in simulation highlights its ability to exploit dual-arm parallelism effectively, leading to **increased throughput** for rearrangement tasks. The explicit reasoning over temporal dependencies and collision avoidance further enhances the reliability of the system, particularly in complex scenarios involving multiple objects and coordinated actions.

Furthermore, the integration of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for both high-level subtask generation and low-level coordination tasks, combined with visual grounding via Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and segmentation models (SAM), represents an **advancement in perception-driven, end-to-end manipulation**. This reduces reliance on predefined strategies or explicit environmental representations, offering a more flexible and adaptive approach to task execution.

While the current system primarily supports planar pick-place actions and operates in an open-loop execution paradigm, its success in real-robot experiments with zero bimanual data is a strong indicator of its practical viability. Future advancements could extend this framework to include closed-loop control, incorporate richer dexterous skills, and generalize to multi-arm scenarios, thereby unlocking capabilities for a wider range of complex manipulation tasks and further solidifying its impact on embodied AI and robotics.
