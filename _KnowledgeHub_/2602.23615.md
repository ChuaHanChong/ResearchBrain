# Annotation-Free Visual Reasoning for High-Resolution Large Multimodal Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanjing University developed HART (High-resolution Annotation-free Reasoning Technique), a post-training framework that enables Large Multimodal Models to perform visual reasoning on high-resolution images without bounding box annotations. HART introduces a self-verification mechanism and Advantage Preference Group Relative Policy Optimization (AP-GRPO) to address reward misspecification, achieving up to 62.4% accuracy on MME-RealWorld-Lite and 46.9% correct grounding on TreeBench, often outperforming much larger models.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with high-resolution visual inputs due to quadratically scaling visual tokens, computational burdens, and information loss from downsampling.
- Existing annotation-free reinforcement learning methods for visual grounding suffer from 'reward misspecification,' where correct final answers do not guarantee accurate intermediate localization, hindering robust grounding.
- The reliance on costly human-annotated bounding boxes for supervised visual grounding methods makes scaling to diverse, high-resolution datasets impractical.

## Method

- HART introduces a closed-loop framework where LMMs ==self-verify localization==: models predict ROIs from a downsampled image, then answer the same question using only high-resolution crops of those ROIs.
- ==Advantage Preference Group Relative Policy Optimization (AP-GRPO)== is employed, a refined RL strategy that dynamically weights samples based on grounding faithfulness, ensuring rewards accurately reflect localization quality.
- A subsequent ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage== further enhances the model's general high-resolution reasoning capabilities by providing full image context for overall performance improvement.

## Results

- HART-7B achieved an overall accuracy of **62.4%** on the MME-RealWorld-Lite benchmark, surpassing its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (**42.3%**) and larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B (**43.7%**).
- On the out-of-distribution TreeBench dataset, HART-7B secured an open-source state-of-the-art accuracy of **43.7%** and demonstrated superior grounding performance with **46.9%** correct grounding (IoU > 0.3).
- HART consistently outperformed baselines across various multimodal benchmarks, including MMStar, V* Bench, HR-Bench-4K, and HR-Bench-8K, showing strong adaptability to diverse visual scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward misspecification is a critical issue in annotation-free visual grounding, with pilot experiments showing models like InternVL3-8B having incorrect grounding in 90.0% of cases even with correct answers.
> - The self-verification mechanism, where a model must answer a question correctly using only cropped high-resolution ROIs, effectively creates a causal dependency between localization and reasoning correctness, removing the need for explicit bounding box annotations.
> - Dynamically adjusting reward weights and KL penalties based on the faithfulness of grounding, as in AP-GRPO, can effectively steer reinforcement learning to prioritize and learn accurate localization policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has demonstrated significant progress in visual understanding and image-text interleaved reasoning across various complex real-world tasks, such as object detection and visual question answering. However, a notable limitation of current LMMs is their diminished performance when confronted with high-resolution visual inputs. This degradation is attributed to the quadratic increase in the number of image tokens with higher resolutions, which introduces substantial redundancy and irrelevant information, exceeding the models' visual-token capacity. Common approaches to mitigate this include imposing maximum pixel constraints, which can inadvertently lead to the loss of critical visual information.

To address the challenges posed by high-resolution inputs, existing research has explored visual grounding—a mechanism inspired by human visual processing, where attention is directed to key regions of interest (ROIs) within an image before reasoning. This process reduces computational load by focusing on relevant visual information. Within this paradigm, two primary lines of research have emerged for optimizing visual grounded reasoning models:
1. **Direct Optimization with Visual Annotations:** Some methods enhance localization capabilities by fine-tuning models on auxiliary visual annotations, such as ground-truth bounding boxes. While effective in directly improving grounding accuracy, these approaches are resource-intensive due to the high cost of human-annotated labels.
2. **Annotation-Free Optimization with Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Recent efforts leverage RL to jointly optimize grounding and reasoning without requiring additional annotated data. These methods typically use the correctness of the final answer as a reward signal. A critical issue identified with these annotation-free RL approaches is "reward misspecification," where a model can receive a positive reward for a correct final answer even if its underlying localization (grounding) was inaccurate. This disconnect can hinder the direct optimization of grounding performance and lead to suboptimal reasoning pathways.

This paper's proposed method, High-resolution Annotation-free Reasoning Technique (HART), positions itself to bridge the gap in annotation-free RL-based visual grounding. It specifically targets the reward misspecification problem by introducing a mechanism that enables LMMs to self-verify their localization results. By doing so, HART aims to achieve direct optimization of grounding capabilities in high-resolution LMMs without external visual supervision, thereby enhancing their performance on perception-heavy tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an annotation-free framework that enables Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to effectively process high-resolution visual inputs for reasoning tasks. This framework aims to allow LMMs to focus on, and critically, self-verify, key regions of visual inputs without relying on costly external visual supervision in the form of bounding box annotations.

The motivation for HART stems from several identified limitations in the current landscape of LMMs and visual grounding techniques:

1. **Challenges with High-Resolution Inputs:** Current LMMs face a significant performance degradation when processing high-resolution images. The number of visual tokens grows quadratically with resolution, leading to increased computational complexity, redundancy, and the inclusion of irrelevant information. While some models impose maximum pixel constraints, this often results in the loss of crucial fine-grained visual details necessary for complex reasoning tasks.
2. **Limitations of Existing Visual Grounding Models:**
 - **Dependency on Costly Annotations:** Visual grounded reasoning, a promising strategy inspired by human perception, often relies on explicit supervision from ground-truth bounding box annotations to train models to identify relevant regions. The collection of such precise grounding labels is expensive and time-consuming, limiting the scalability and applicability of these methods.
 - **Reward Misspecification in Annotation-Free RL:** Approaches that use reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize visual grounding without direct bounding box annotations typically use the correctness of the final answer as the sole reward signal. However, a significant problem, termed "reward misspecification," arises: a model might generate a correct final answer despite localizing an incorrect or irrelevant region. This means the positive reward does not accurately reflect good grounding behavior, leading to a suboptimal or even negatively optimized grounding policy.
3. **Empirical Evidence of Reward Misspecification:** The authors conducted pilot experiments using prominent LMMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL3-8B) on the Visual CoT dataset, which provides ground-truth bounding boxes. These experiments empirically confirmed the prevalence of reward misspecification, finding that 36.5% of correct answers from Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 63.8% from InternVL3-8B were accompanied by incorrect grounding. This finding highlights a clear need for a mechanism that more accurately links final answer correctness to the quality of visual grounding.
4. **Lack of Direct Grounding Optimization without Annotations:** Given the above challenges, a key open question in the field is how to directly optimize an LMM's grounding capabilities in the absence of explicit visual annotations. Current annotation-free methods indirectly optimize grounding, leading to the identified misspecification issues.

HART is motivated by the necessity to overcome these limitations by proposing a novel closed-loop framework. This framework aims to enable LMMs to internally assess and refine their visual grounding by making the correctness of the final answer more directly dependent on the accuracy of the localized regions, thus fostering a more reliable and interpretable reasoning process for high-resolution visual tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HART (High-resolution Annotation-free Reasoning Technique) is designed as a closed-loop framework for post-training Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to enhance high-resolution visual reasoning without external visual annotations. The methodology integrates a novel reinforcement learning strategy with supervised fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Closed-Loop Framework for Self-Verification

HART introduces a two-step reasoning process during the training phase, which serves as a self-verification mechanism for visual grounding:

1. **Region of Interest (ROI) Prediction:** Given a down-sampled full input image ($I_f$) and a textual question ($q$), the model is first prompted to identify and output the coordinates of key ROIs relevant to the question. This output is in a bounding-box format.
2. **Reasoning based on Cropped Regions:** Subsequently, these predicted ROIs ($I_s$) are cropped from the *original high-resolution image*. Crucially, the *original full image ($I_f$) is deliberately withheld* from the model at this stage. The model is then instructed to answer the *same question ($q$)* based solely on these cropped sub-images ($I_s$).

This closed-loop design ensures that for the model to provide a correct final answer in the second step, its initial localization of ROIs must have been accurate enough to capture all necessary visual information. This setup creates a stronger causal dependency between localization correctness and response correctness, allowing the reward signal (from the second step) to more accurately reflect grounding quality. The authors empirically show that this procedure reduces the proportion of correct answers with incorrect localization (e.g., a 15.0% decrease for Qwen2.5-VL-7B).

#### 3.2. Advantage Preference Group Relative Policy Optimization (AP-GRPO)

To optimize the model's policy within this self-verification framework, HART proposes Advantage Preference Group Relative Policy Optimization (AP-GRPO), a reinforcement fine-tuning strategy that modifies the standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

1. **Candidate Response Generation and Reward Assignment:** For a given question and the cropped sub-images, AP-GRPO generates a group of $G$ candidate responses {o\_i} and assigns binary rewards {r\_i}, where $r_i=1$ for a correct answer and $r_i=0$ for an incorrect one.
2. **Dynamic Weighting for Optimization:** Unlike vanilla GRPO, which weights all samples equally, AP-GRPO introduces dynamic scaling factors, $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$, to prioritize samples with correct grounding. The optimization objective $J_{AP-GRPO}(\theta)$ is defined as:
 $J_{AP-GRPO}(\theta) = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^{G} \left( \mu_1 \frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\theta_{old}}(o_i|q)} A_i - \mu_2 D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta} || \pi_{ref}) \right)$
 where:
 - $\mu_1 = 1 + k(r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_i\}_{i=1}^G))$: This factor assigns higher weights to responses with higher advantages, especially for correct answers. The scaling factor $k$ is a hyperparameter (set to 0.6 in experiments).
 - $\mu_2 = \beta \left( 1 - k \left| r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_i\}_{i=1}^G) \right| \right)$: This factor dynamically adjusts the KL penalty coefficient. It reduces the KL penalty when the grounding is correct (indicated by a correct answer with a high advantage), thereby allowing for larger deviations from the reference model and more aggressive policy updates in beneficial directions.
3. **Theoretical Justification:** The paper provides a theoretical proposition (Proposition 2) indicating that AP-GRPO effectively reduces the negative impact of reward misspecification observed in prior studies. Specifically, it shows that AP-GRPO's gradient is equivalent to the baseline gradient minus a term associated with incorrect localization leading to a correct answer, effectively reducing updates in such misspecified cases. This means answer correctness becomes a more accurate reflection of perception quality.

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

Following the RL stage with AP-GRPO, HART incorporates an additional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase. In this stage, the original, full-resolution image is made visible to the model. This SFT phase aims to further enhance the model's general high-resolution reasoning capabilities, building upon the improved grounding learned during the RL phase. A clean dataset separation is used, with $D_{SFT}$ for SFT and $D_{RL}$ for RL. The loss function for SFT is a standard cross-entropy loss.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The Transformer Reinforcement Learning (TRL) framework is used for distributed training. Qwen2.5-VL-7B serves as the base model. A binary reward function evaluates answer correctness for all reinforcement fine-tuning methods. The training uses a randomly sampled 10K examples from the MME-RealWorld training set for $D_{RL}$ and the remainder for $D_{SFT}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate HART across various high-resolution visual understanding benchmarks, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art open-source and private LMMs, as well as other post-training and visual grounded reasoning methods.

#### 4.1. Pilot Experiments on Reward Misspecification

Initial experiments on the Visual CoT dataset demonstrated the reward misspecification problem in baseline LMMs. For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 36.5% of correct answers were associated with incorrect localization. For InternVL3-8B, this figure was 63.8%. When the HART framework (specifically the closed-loop self-verification process) was applied, the proportion of correct answers with incorrect localization decreased by 15.0% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 7.9% for InternVL3-8B, indicating that the framework effectively forces a stronger correlation between correct answers and accurate grounding.

#### 4.2. Overall Answer Accuracy

- **MME-RealWorld-Lite (In-Distribution):** HART-7B achieved an overall accuracy of 62.4%, surpassing all compared open-source general models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B), post-training methods (GRPO+SFT at 58.1%, MGPO+SFT at 60.5%), and visual grounded reasoning models (Pixel-Reasoner-7B at 49.7%, DeepEyes-7B at 53.2%). HART also outperformed several larger-scale open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 43.7%) and private models (e.g., GPT-4o-mini at 36.4%, Gemini-1.5-Pro at 38.2%, GPT-4o at 45.2%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 51.6%). Significant improvements were observed in specific challenging tasks, such as +26.0% on Remote Sensing and +27.7% on Perception-Autonomous Driving compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- **TreeBench (Out-of-Distribution):** HART-7B achieved an accuracy of 43.7%, establishing a new open-source state-of-the-art on this benchmark. It outperformed the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (37.0%), other post-training paradigms (GRPO+SFT at 38.5%, MGPO+SFT at 40.3%), and existing visual grounded reasoning models (DeepEyes-7B at 37.5%, Pixel-Reasoner-7B at 39.0%). HART's performance on TreeBench was competitive with some larger-scale open-source and private models (e.g., slightly below Gemini-2.5-Flash at 45.9% and GPT-4o at 46.9%).
- **Other Multimodal Benchmarks (MMStar, V\* Bench, HR-Bench-4K/8K):** HART-7B consistently showed improvements across these benchmarks. For instance, on HR-Bench-8K (average resolution 5,727 × 4,430), HART-7B achieved 71.9% accuracy, significantly outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (61.0%) and InternVL3-7B (62.0%), indicating strong adaptability to different resolutions and fine-grained visual understanding.
- **Robustness Across Base Models:** When applied to InternVL3-8B as an additional base model, HART also achieved improved performance compared to the original model, suggesting robust training behavior.

#### 4.3. Grounding Performance

Evaluation of grounding accuracy on TreeBench and Visual CoT datasets, using intersection over ground-truth (coverage threshold of 0.3), demonstrated the effectiveness of AP-GRPO.
- AP-GRPO achieved 75.4% correct grounding on TreeBench, representing a +25.2% improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- On Visual CoT, AP-GRPO achieved 77.7% correct grounding, a +11.7% improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
These results confirm that AP-GRPO directly optimizes visual grounding capabilities without relying on external bounding box annotations. Figure 3 further illustrates AP-GRPO's improved grounding performance across a range of Intersection over Union (IoU) thresholds.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of both the RL (AP-GRPO) and SFT stages within HART. The combination yielded considerably higher accuracy than either stage alone. The method also demonstrated robustness to different values of the hyperparameter $k$ in AP-GRPO, consistently improving performance.

#### 4.5. Visualization Results

Visual comparisons (Figure 4) indicated that HART-7B reliably identifies critical regions in complex images, such as focusing on the correct man in a scene, leading to more accurate reasoning outcomes compared to baseline models like InternVL3-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

#### 4.6. Training Cost

HART incurred a higher training time per step (46.0 s/step) compared to simpler baselines like GRPO (21.0 s/step) and MGPO (30.7 s/step) due to its closed-loop feedback structure. This increased computational cost is presented as a trade-off for the improved performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces HART, a novel framework that contributes to the advancement of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by addressing critical limitations in handling high-resolution visual inputs. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling High-Resolution Visual Reasoning without Annotation Burden:** HART directly tackles the problem of LMM performance degradation on high-resolution images by providing a mechanism for effective visual grounding. Crucially, it achieves this without requiring expensive and labor-intensive human-annotated bounding box labels for training. This annotation-free approach significantly lowers the barrier for developing and deploying high-resolution LMMs, making the technology more accessible and scalable for diverse applications.

2. **Addressing Reward Misspecification in RL for Visual Grounding:** The paper identifies and empirically quantifies the prevalent issue of "reward misspecification" in existing annotation-free reinforcement learning methods, where correct answers do not always imply accurate visual grounding. HART's closed-loop framework and the proposed AP-GRPO strategy directly mitigate this problem. By compelling the model to reason solely on its predicted regions of interest, HART ensures a stronger causal link between grounding accuracy and the final answer's correctness, leading to more faithful and effective policy updates for localization.

3. **Enhanced Model Capabilities and Generalization:** The consistent and significant performance improvements demonstrated across a wide array of challenging high-resolution benchmarks (e.g., MME-RealWorld-Lite, TreeBench, HR-Bench-4K/8K) indicate that HART enhances both fine-grained visual understanding and reasoning capabilities of LMMs. Its effectiveness on out-of-distribution datasets like TreeBench suggests improved generalization. The ability to post-train various base models further highlights the robustness and broad applicability of the framework.

4. **Improved Interpretability of Reasoning Pathways:** By explicitly identifying and focusing on key regions of interest, HART provides more explainable reasoning pathways. This interpretability can be valuable for understanding how LMMs arrive at their conclusions, fostering greater trust and enabling debugging in critical applications.

5. **Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** By enabling LMMs to selectively focus on relevant visual information from high-resolution inputs, HART effectively reduces redundant computations. This approach helps overcome the inherent maximum pixel constraints of many LMMs, thereby enhancing their efficiency in processing large images.

6. **Broadening Real-World Applications:** The enhanced capability of LMMs to perform accurate visual reasoning on high-resolution data has considerable implications for real-world scenarios. This includes applications in domains such as:
 - **Remote Sensing:** Analyzing satellite imagery for environmental monitoring or urban planning.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Interpreting detailed road scenes and identifying small obstacles or distant signs.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Assisting in the diagnosis by accurately localizing anomalies in high-resolution scans.
 - **Optical Character Recognition (OCR) in the Wild:** Extracting and understanding text from complex, high-resolution images.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** HART establishes a flexible foundation for further exploration in the joint optimization of grounding and reasoning capabilities in LMMs. The authors note future work will involve scaling up training datasets and model sizes, indicating a path towards even more powerful and broadly capable LMMs.

In summary, HART represents a significant step towards more robust, efficient, and interpretable LMMs capable of mastering complex, high-resolution visual tasks without the prohibitive cost of extensive manual annotation.
