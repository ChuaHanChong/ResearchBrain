# Annotation-Free Visual Reasoning for High-Resolution Large Multimodal Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanjing University developed HART (High-resolution Annotation-free Reasoning Technique), a post-training framework that enables Large Multimodal Models to perform visual reasoning on high-resolution images without bounding box annotations. HART introduces a self-verification mechanism and Advantage Preference Group Relative Policy Optimization (AP-GRPO) to address reward misspecification, achieving up to 62.4% accuracy on MME-RealWorld-Lite and 46.9% correct grounding on TreeBench, often outperforming much larger models.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with high-resolution visual inputs due to quadratically scaling visual tokens, computational burdens, and information loss from downsampling.
- Existing annotation-free reinforcement learning methods for visual grounding suffer from 'reward misspecification,' where correct final answers do not guarantee accurate intermediate localization, hindering robust grounding.
- The reliance on costly human-annotated bounding boxes for supervised visual grounding methods makes scaling to diverse, high-resolution datasets impractical.

## Method

- HART introduces a closed-loop framework where LMMs ==self-verify localization==: models predict ROIs from a downsampled image, then answer the same question using only high-resolution crops of those ROIs.
- ==Advantage Preference Group Relative Policy Optimization (AP-GRPO)== is employed, a refined RL strategy that dynamically weights samples based on grounding faithfulness, ensuring rewards accurately reflect localization quality.
- A subsequent ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage== further enhances the model's general high-resolution reasoning capabilities by providing full image context for overall performance improvement.

## Results

- HART-7B achieved an overall accuracy of **62.4%** on the MME-RealWorld-Lite benchmark, surpassing its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (**42.3%**) and larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B (**43.7%**).
- On the out-of-distribution TreeBench dataset, HART-7B secured an open-source state-of-the-art accuracy of **43.7%** and demonstrated superior grounding performance with **46.9%** correct grounding (IoU > 0.3).
- HART consistently outperformed baselines across various multimodal benchmarks, including MMStar, V* Bench, HR-Bench-4K, and HR-Bench-8K, showing strong adaptability to diverse visual scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward misspecification is a critical issue in annotation-free visual grounding, with pilot experiments showing models like InternVL3-8B having incorrect grounding in **90.0%** of cases even with correct answers.
> - The self-verification mechanism, where a model must answer a question correctly using only cropped high-resolution ROIs, effectively creates a causal dependency between localization and reasoning correctness, removing the need for explicit bounding box annotations.
> - Dynamically adjusting reward weights and KL penalties based on the faithfulness of grounding, as in AP-GRPO, can effectively steer reinforcement learning to prioritize and learn accurate localization policies.
