# E-TTS: A New Embodied Test-Time Scaling Framework for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> E-TTS (Embodied Test-Time Scaling) presents a framework for robotic manipulation that unifies reasoning and action scaling through history-aware iterative refinement. This approach boosts task success rates by up to 33.14% in simulation and 26.62% in real-world scenarios across diverse Vision-Language-Action models without requiring retraining.

## Problem

- Prior embodied Test-Time Scaling (TTS) methods predominantly focus on action scaling, overlooking the critical role and coupling of reasoning in generating effective actions for complex manipulation tasks.
- Existing embodied TTS approaches often operate in an open-loop manner, failing to leverage historical context or provide iterative feedback for refinement, limiting their effectiveness in sequential, long-horizon tasks.
- The need for more robust and adaptable robotic manipulation policies that can improve performance at inference time without costly data collection or retraining.

## Method

- A ==Reasoning-Action Joint Sampling== module generates multiple coupled reasoning and action candidates, utilizing a history buffer to provide temporal context.
- Dual vision-language verifiers (one for reasoning, one for action) evaluate the validity, coherence, and feasibility of sampled joint units, enabling adaptive online selection.
- A feedback-guided iterative refinement mechanism analyzes rejected candidates, generates structured textual feedback, and incorporates it into subsequent sampling rounds for closed-loop error correction.

## Results

- Achieved performance improvements of up to **33.14%** in simulation and **26.62%** in real-world scenarios, with an average gain of **13.52%** across diverse VLA models and tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of joint reasoning-action scaling, feedback-guided refinement, historical context via a buffer, and joint scoring for improved success rates.
- Demonstrated a favorable efficiency-performance trade-off, achieving a **150%** relative improvement in success rate with only a **46.6%** increase in latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal performance in complex robotic manipulation requires jointly scaling both high-level reasoning and low-level actions, rather than focusing solely on the action space.
> - Integrating historical context and a closed-loop iterative refinement mechanism with explicit feedback enables robots to learn from errors and exhibit self-correction behaviors at inference time.
> - A modular, plug-and-play test-time scaling framework can effectively enhance various Vision-Language-Action policies without requiring costly retraining or additional expert data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the evolving field of robotic manipulation, specifically at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and Test-Time Scaling (TTS). VLA models aim to integrate visual perception, language understanding, and decision-making capabilities within a single framework to enable robots to perform complex tasks based on natural language instructions. Recent advancements in VLA models have increasingly incorporated reasoning components to externalize internal cognitive processes before generating concrete actions, enhancing performance in complex embodied environments. Examples include E-CoT, RAD, ThinkAct, $\pi^{0.5}$, Embodied-R1, EMMA-X, and MolmoAct. Most of these models primarily rely on scaling dataset and model sizes during training.

Test-Time Scaling (TTS) is a methodology that allocates additional computation during the inference phase to improve model performance without requiring further data collection or retraining. This approach has gained traction in fields like computer vision and natural language processing, often employing strategies such as parallel scaling (generating multiple candidates for aggregation), sequential scaling (iterative refinement), or hybrid methods (e.g., Tree-of-Thought). For embodied tasks, where real-world data collection is expensive and latency is not always a critical constraint (e.g., object rearrangement), TTS offers a promising avenue for achieving more robust execution.

Early attempts to extend TTS to the embodied domain, such as Hume, RoboMonkey, RoVer, and TACO, have focused predominantly on scaling within the action space. These methods augment VLA models with mechanisms to re-evaluate and refine action proposals or use reward models for external evaluation of action candidates. However, these prior works often overlook two key aspects intrinsic to robotic manipulation: the tight coupling between high-level reasoning and low-level actions, and the sequential, long-horizon nature of embodied tasks, which necessitates the utilization of historical context and iterative feedback. E-TTS differentiates itself by addressing these limitations through a unified framework that jointly scales reasoning and action, incorporates history-aware verification, and implements a closed-loop iterative refinement mechanism. This positions E-TTS as an advancement in making VLA models more robust and adaptable in real-world robotic manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a novel framework, E-TTS (Embodied Test-Time Scaling), that enhances the performance of robotic manipulation policies by unifying reasoning and action scaling through history-aware iterative refinement. The motivation for developing E-TTS stems from two main challenges identified in existing embodied test-time scaling approaches for robotic tasks:

1. **Limited focus on reasoning scaling:** While incorporating a reasoning component has proven effective in improving policy performance in VLA models, existing embodied TTS methods have largely concentrated on scaling only the action space. This creates a misalignment where high-level planning (reasoning) is not subject to the same rigorous test-time scrutiny and refinement as low-level actions. In complex manipulation tasks, a coherent and valid reasoning process is fundamental to generating effective actions. Neglecting to scale reasoning can lead to suboptimal performance, as even well-executed actions based on flawed reasoning may fail the task. The authors contend that both reasoning and action are intertwined and should be jointly scaled for optimal results.

2. **Inadequate utilization of historical information and lack of iterative feedback:** Embodied tasks are inherently long-horizon and sequential decision-making processes. Actions at any given timestep are influenced by and, in turn, influence, the trajectory of the task. Relying solely on current observations for action scaling is insufficient because it overlooks the rich contextual information available from past interactions and decisions. Prior methods largely operate in an open-loop manner, lacking mechanisms to integrate historical context for candidate evaluation or to generate iterative feedback for refinement. This limitation restricts their effectiveness in complex scenarios where temporal consistency and learning from past errors are crucial for successful task completion.

To address these challenges, the authors propose E-TTS with the following specific objectives:
- To enable **joint reasoning-action scaling**, treating reasoning and action as a coupled unit to be sampled and evaluated together, ensuring consistency between high-level planning and low-level execution.
- To leverage **historical context** by incorporating a history buffer that stores past reasoning-action pairs and observations, allowing verifiers to model temporal dependencies and make more informed decisions.
- To establish a **closed-loop iterative refinement mechanism** by generating feedback for rejected candidates. This feedback guides subsequent sampling rounds, allowing the model to learn from its errors and progressively refine its reasoning and actions, improving inference efficiency and environmental adaptability.
- To design E-TTS as a **modular and plug-and-play framework** that can be seamlessly integrated with various VLA models without requiring additional expert data collection or retraining, thus offering broad applicability.

By tackling these motivations, E-TTS aims to improve the robustness, success rate, and adaptability of robotic manipulation policies, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks where traditional TTS methods fall short.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

E-TTS is presented as a modular and plug-and-play Embodied Test-Time Scaling framework designed to unify reasoning and action scaling for robotic manipulation. The framework operates through a history-aware iterative refinement process utilizing vision-language verifiers.

The E-TTS framework consists of four main modules:

1. **Reasoning-Action Joint Sampling:**
 - Unlike prior TTS methods that typically scale only actions, E-TTS aims to identify the optimal $\langle$reasoning, action$\rangle$ pair.
 - Given a task instruction ($I$) and current observation ($O_t$), a general robot policy $\pi_\theta(c_t, a_t | O_t, I)$ is used.
 - At each timestep $t$, the framework first samples $M$ reasoning candidates ($c_i^t$). For each reasoning candidate, $N$ corresponding action candidates ($a_{i,j}^t$) are generated.
 - These form joint units $J_{i,j}^t = (c_i^t, a_{i,j}^t)$ and joint sample batches $B_i^t = \{J_{i,j}^t\}_{j=1}^N$.
 - **History Buffer ($H_t$):** To account for the sequential nature of embodied tasks, a dynamic history buffer $H_t = \{J_{t-K}, O_{t-K}, \ldots, J_{t-1}, O_{t-1}\}$ stores the $K$ most recent selected reasoning-action pairs and observations. This buffer provides historical context for subsequent verification.

2. **History-aware Verification and Selection:**
 - A dual-verifier mechanism evaluates the sampled joint units.
 - **Reasoning Verifier ($V_c$):** This module assesses the validity, coherence, and groundedness of candidate reasoning samples. It utilizes a vision-language foundation model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) in a zero-shot manner.
 - For each $J_{i,j}^t$, $V_c$ generates a confidence score $S_{i,j}^c = V_c(H_t, J_{i,j}^t, O_t, I)$.
 - It employs specialized processing for three reasoning types:
 - **Textual Reasoning:** Purely linguistic content (e.g., subtasks from Embodied-R1). $V_c$ assesses logical consistency and task relevance.
 - **Multimodal Reasoning:** Integrates visual perception (e.g., object detection bounding boxes, gripper positions from E-CoT) and textual understanding. Visual perception is rendered directly onto the image as visual prompts to preserve spatial details.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** Involves spatial relationships (e.g., trajectory keypoints from MolmoAct). These are transformed into visual prompts (overlaid on observations), and representative examples are provided to $V_c$ to learn valid spatial grounding.
 - Efficiency Optimization: The reasoning verifier is deployed with a vLLM engine, PagedAttention, and prefix-sharing strategy to reduce inference latency.
 - **Action Verifier ($V_a$):** This module evaluates the low-level feasibility and task relevance of candidate actions. It uses LLaVA-7B as its backbone, trained on a preference dataset of 90k paired successful and failed policy rollouts from SimplerEnv and LIBERO.
 - $V_a$ predicts a scalar score $S_{i,j}^a = V_a(J_{i,j}^t, O_t, I)$ for each action in a joint pair.
 - **Adaptive Online Joint Selection:**
 - The final joint score $S_{i,j}^t$ for each pair $(c_i^t, a_{i,j}^t)$ is computed by multiplying the normalized scores: $S_{i,j}^t = S_{i,j}^c \times \hat{S}_{i,j}^a$. This ensures both coherent reasoning and feasible actions receive high scores.
 - An $\epsilon$-greedy policy balances exploration and exploitation:
 - With probability $\epsilon$, a random reasoning-action pair is sampled for exploration.
 - Otherwise, the pair with the highest joint score ($J_{i,j^*}^t$) is chosen.
 - A confidence threshold $\eta$ is applied: if $S_{i,j^*}^t > \eta$, the action is executed. If not, the refinement mechanism is triggered.

3. **Feedback-Guided Iterative Refinement:**
 - If no candidate pair in a joint batch meets the confidence threshold, the reasoning verifier (acting as a feedback advisor) analyzes the failure causes for the rejected samples.
 - It generates structured textual feedback ($F_i^t$) describing reasoning flaws and offering actionable corrections, prompted by guided questions.
 - This feedback is then incorporated into the original instruction prompt ($I_{i+1}^t = \text{Concat}(I, F_i^t)$) for subsequent sampling rounds.
 - This closed-loop process allows the model to learn from errors, iteratively improving reasoning coherence and action feasibility until a high-score solution is found or a maximum batch limit is reached.

The modular architecture allows components to be independently enabled, disabled, or combined, making E-TTS adaptable to various tasks and VLA architectures without additional training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across four benchmarks, six environments, three embodiments, and four base VLA models (E-CoT, MolmoAct, $\pi^{0.5}$, Embodied-R1) demonstrated consistent performance improvements, addressing five key research questions:

**Q1: Generality across various manipulation methods and reasoning types:**
- **Overall Improvement:** E-TTS consistently improved performance, achieving up to a 33.14% increase in simulation and 26.62% in real-world scenarios, with an average gain of 13.52%.
- **E-CoT (Multimodal Reasoning):** On SimplerEnv WidowX, E-TTS increased the average success rate of E-CoT from 6.67% to 39.81%. It showed a 50.91% improvement over RoboMonkey, with only a 7.8% increase in execution time.
- **MolmoAct (Spatial Reasoning):** On SimplerEnv (Google Robot), E-TTS boosted MolmoAct's average success rates from 71.05% to 80.00% (Visual Matching) and 65.68% to 77.77% (Variant Aggregation). On LIBERO, it raised the average success rate from 85.57% to 90.78%. On LIBERO-Plus, improvements were consistent across various perturbation dimensions, demonstrating enhanced robustness.
- **$\pi^{0.5}$ (Textual Reasoning):** On VLABench, E-TTS consistently improved $\pi^{0.5}$ across most tracks and tasks, with average success rates increasing. Notable gains were observed in multi-step spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., select_poker from 0.46 to 0.63). E-TTS also achieved a better average success rate compared to TACO.
- **Embodied-R1 (Textual Reasoning):** On SimplerEnv WidowX, E-TTS improved Embodied-R1's average success rate from 39.8% to 44.9%.
- These results collectively confirm E-TTS's ability to enhance diverse manipulation methods regardless of their intermediate reasoning types.

**Q2: Benefits of specific embodied designs (joint scaling, feedback, history):**
- **Ablation Studies on SimplerEnv WidowX (E-CoT base):**
 - `w/o feedback`: Significant performance drop, indicating feedback's importance.
 - `w/o reasoning scaling` and `w/o action scaling`: Substantially degraded performance, showing both components are necessary.
 - `w/o joint scoring`: Poor performance, highlighting the need for combined evaluation of reasoning and action.
 - `w/o history buffer`: Reduced success rate, confirming the value of historical context.
 - `w/o $\epsilon$-greedy`: Lower performance, underscoring the importance of balancing exploration and exploitation.
- **Parameter analysis:** Moderate $\eta$ (acceptance threshold) and small $\epsilon$ (exploration probability) provided the best balance. Increasing history length $K$ improved success up to $K=10$ but dropped for longer histories. These ablations validate the motivations behind E-TTS's design choices.

**Q3: Efficiency-performance trade-off:**
- An optimal scaling configuration yielded a 150% relative improvement in success rate with only a 46.6% increase in latency.
- Compared to RoboMonkey, E-TTS achieved a 51.5% relative gain in average success rate with a marginal 7.8% increase in execution time. This indicates a favorable efficiency-performance trade-off due to system-level optimizations and efficient sampling.

**Q4: Optimal trade-off between reasoning and action scaling:**
- Experiments with a fixed computational budget (60-step sampling limit) showed that over-prioritizing either reasoning or action scaling led to suboptimal performance.
- Peak performance was achieved at an approximately 1:1 ratio, indicating that both reasoning and action scaling are indispensable and should be balanced.

**Q5: Performance in real-world environments:**
- **Real-robot experiments:** Using a Franka Research 3 arm across four manipulation tasks (pick-and-place, long-horizon, deformable objects), E-TTS boosted MolmoAct-Finetuned's average success rate from 22.13% to 48.75%.
- **Emergent Self-Correction:** The robot exhibited self-correction behaviors (e.g., re-attempting a missed grasp) without specific training for such recovery, attributed to the VLM's pretrained knowledge and E-TTS's history-aware, feedback-guided mechanism.

In summary, the findings demonstrate that E-TTS significantly and consistently enhances robotic manipulation performance across various models, tasks, and environments by effectively integrating reasoning and action scaling, utilizing historical context, and employing iterative refinement, all without requiring additional training data or retraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The E-TTS framework presents several significant contributions and holds potential for broad impact in the field of robotic manipulation:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Success Rates:** The most immediate impact is the consistent and substantial improvement in task success rates, observed across diverse simulated benchmarks (up to 33.14%) and real-world scenarios (up to 26.62%). This directly addresses the critical need for more reliable robot performance in complex, open-ended environments. By jointly scaling reasoning and actions and incorporating iterative refinement, E-TTS makes VLA models more robust to uncertainties and errors that commonly arise during real-world task execution.

2. **Increased Adaptability and Generalization:** E-TTS is designed as a modular and plug-and-play framework, enabling seamless integration with various existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., E-CoT, MolmoAct, $\pi^{0.5}$, Embodied-R1) and different robot embodiments without requiring additional expert data collection or retraining of the base policy. This eliminates the costly and time-consuming process of gathering new datasets for performance improvement, making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible and adaptable to novel tasks and environments. The demonstrated improvements across diverse tasks and perturbed environments (LIBERO-Plus) indicate stronger generalization.

3. **Addressing Core Challenges in Embodied AI:** The work directly tackles two fundamental limitations of prior embodied test-time scaling methods: the oversight of reasoning scaling and the underutilization of historical context and iterative feedback. By introducing joint reasoning-action scaling and a history-aware, closed-loop refinement mechanism, E-TTS provides a more comprehensive and ecologically valid approach to test-time performance enhancement for sequential embodied tasks. The emergent self-correction behaviors observed in real-robot experiments highlight the framework's ability to imbue robots with greater autonomy in handling unexpected situations.

4. **Optimal Resource Allocation for Test-Time Scaling:** The empirical analysis on the trade-off between reasoning and action scaling offers valuable insights for future research, indicating that a balanced allocation of computational resources (approximately a 1:1 ratio) is crucial for maximizing performance. This guidance can inform the design of more efficient TTS strategies in resource-constrained robotic systems.

5. **Practical Applicability for Latency-Insensitive Tasks:** While TTS inherently trades inference time for performance, E-TTS demonstrates a favorable trade-off (e.g., a 150% relative success rate increase for a 46.6% latency increase). For a wide range of latency-insensitive embodied tasks (e.g., object rearrangement, assembly), the gains in reliability and success rate outweigh the marginal increase in computation, making E-TTS a viable and practical solution for deploying more capable robots.

In conclusion, E-TTS represents a step forward in making robotic manipulation more reliable, intelligent, and adaptable. Its modularity and strong empirical performance across various settings suggest its potential to serve as a foundational component for developing next-generation, high-performance robotic systems that can operate effectively in complex and unstructured environments. Future work on optimizing its latency for highly dynamic tasks could further expand its applicability.
