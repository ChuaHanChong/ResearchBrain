# Active Hierarchical Imitation and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework combines active learning with hierarchical imitation and reinforcement learning to enhance robot learning from human demonstrations in continuous state-action spaces. This approach, particularly with a reward-based active learning strategy, achieved improved task success rates and reduced human expert effort in 3D maze navigation.

## Problem

- Obtaining sufficient high-quality human demonstrations for robot learning is labor-intensive and costly.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) often suffers from poor sample efficiency, especially in continuous, sparse-reward environments.
- Prior Hierarchical Imitation and Reinforcement Learning (HIRL) work was primarily limited to simplified 2D discrete environments and did not extensively leverage direct human demonstrations for continuous tasks.

## Method

- An **Active Hierarchical Imitation** and ==Reinforcement Learning== (HIRL) framework is developed for continuous state-action spaces, using a two-level hierarchy with a DAgger-trained high-level meta-controller and a DDPG-pre-trained low-level controller.
- Three active learning methods (noise-based, multi-policy, reward-based) are proposed to select informative states for demonstration or manage data in the experience replay buffer.
- These methods are designed to reduce the physical and mental effort required from human experts during the training process for complex tasks like 3D maze navigation.

## Results

- DAgger+Reward-based Active Learning demonstrated the highest task success rates and significantly reduced expert demonstration costs to achieve higher performance.
- A human user study revealed that DAgger+Reward-based Active Learning led to significantly lower perceived physical demand and effort from participants, alongside higher perceived task success.
- Conversely, DAgger+Multi-Policy Active Learning resulted in lower success rates and increased perceived physical demand and effort for human demonstrators.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical structures enable effective learning of complex tasks in continuous state and action spaces when combined with interactive imitation learning.
> - Judicious application of active learning can significantly reduce the expert cost and improve learning efficiency for robot training from human demonstrations.
> - The effectiveness of active learning strategies varies, with reward-based active learning proving superior in both objective performance and human perception of the training process compared to multi-policy or vanilla DAgger.
