# A2Eval: Agentic and Automated Evaluation for Embodied Brain

> [!summary] Summary
> A2Eval introduces an agentic framework for autonomously evaluating embodied vision-language models, addressing issues of benchmark redundancy, skewed coverage, and high computational costs. This system notably reduces evaluation expenses by 77% and achieves a 3.4x to 4.6x speedup, producing model rankings that align more closely with human judgment.

## Problem

- Current embodied VLM evaluation relies on static, expert-defined, and manually annotated benchmarks, leading to a "broken evaluation ecosystem."
- Existing benchmarks exhibit significant sample duplication (up to 92%) and skewed distributions of task types, causing coverage imbalance and evaluation redundancy.
- Manual evaluation processes are prohibitively costly, consuming over 3200 GPU hours for a single model and distorting model rankings due to overfitting on overrepresented tasks.

## Method

- **A2Eval** proposes a ==two-agent framework== (Data Agent and Eval Agent) to automate the entire evaluation lifecycle, from benchmark construction to model assessment.
- The ==Data Agent== autonomously induces unified capability dimensions and constructs a compact, balanced benchmark through multi-agent collaboration and ==diversity-aware sampling==.
- The ==Eval Agent== automates model assessment by synthesizing and validating executable inference and scoring logic in an ==isolated sandbox environment==, eliminating manual pipeline bottlenecks.

## Results

- Reduced evaluation suite size by **85%** (from **24,519** to **3,781** examples) across **10** benchmarks, while inducing **8** balanced capability dimensions.
- Achieved a **77%** reduction in computational costs and a **3.4x to 4.6x** speedup in model evaluation across various model sizes.
- Demonstrated high alignment with human judgment for dimension assignments (Cohen's **κ = 0.78**) and improved human-centric model rankings (Spearman's **ρ = 0.85**) compared to original benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Benchmark curation can be framed as an optimization problem to maximize capability coverage and diversity while minimizing redundancy and human effort.
> - Autonomous multi-agent collaboration can effectively identify and define comprehensive and independent capability dimensions for embodied AI.
> - Semantic diversity-aware sampling enables significant benchmark compression (85%) while maintaining representational completeness and improving cross-dimension balance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The evaluation of embodied vision-language models (VLMs) is a critical component in the advancement of Embodied AI. Current evaluation practices primarily rely on static, expert-defined, and manually annotated benchmarks. While a wide array of embodied reasoning benchmarks exist, such as Where2Place, VSI-Bench, RefSpatial, RoboSpatial, EmbSpatial, OmniSpatial, ERQA, COSMOS, BLINK, and EgoSchema, their proliferation has introduced significant challenges.

The paper identifies three systemic issues within this existing "broken evaluation ecosystem":
- **Coverage Imbalance & Redundancy:** Existing benchmarks exhibit substantial sample duplication (up to 92% similarity) and skewed distribution of task types. This leads to repetitive assessments and an incomplete evaluation of model capabilities. For instance, critical reasoning capacities like physical causality (PhysCaus) are underrepresented, while easier-to-annotate tasks are oversampled.
- **Ranking Distortion:** The imbalance and redundancy can lead to models overfitting to overrepresented tasks. This results in distorted leaderboards, where models appear to perform well due to strengths in specific, oversampled areas, while potentially possessing critical weaknesses in others. Such distortions can misguide research directions.
- **Prohibitive Evaluation Cost:** The process of evaluating models across numerous redundant samples and manually constructing inference and scoring logic for each benchmark is computationally and financially expensive. A single model evaluation can consume over 3200 GPU hours, which hinders iterative development cycles for embodied AI research.

Related work in automated benchmark construction, such as Code2Bench and OK-Bench, has focused on generating new, domain-specific evaluation sets with mechanisms for contamination control and reproducibility. However, these methods typically aim to create novel benchmarks rather than consolidating and optimizing existing, heterogeneous ones.

A2Eval differentiates itself from this existing landscape by proposing an agentic framework that directly addresses the challenges of redundancy, fragmented coverage, and high computational costs in *existing* embodied VLM benchmarks. It introduces automatic capability induction with balanced sampling across domains, redundancy-aware benchmark consolidation, and a unified, end-to-end data and evaluation pipeline. This approach represents a paradigm shift from manual, expert-driven processes to an autonomous, agent-driven system for benchmark curation and evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind A2Eval stems from the identified inefficiencies and biases in the current embodied VLM evaluation ecosystem. The labor-intensive "expert definition + manual annotation" paradigm is costly, slow, and produces potentially misleading results, thereby impeding research and development in Embodied AI. The authors frame benchmark curation as an optimization problem: maximizing capability coverage and diversity while minimizing redundancy and human effort.

The key objectives of this work are:

- **To address Coverage Imbalance and Redundancy:** By moving away from subjective expert definitions, the research aims to autonomously infer meaningful capability dimensions. This enables the construction of a balanced and compact evaluation suite through diversity-aware sampling, substantially reducing benchmark size and redundant computation. This objective directly seeks to improve the comprehensiveness and fairness of evaluations.
- **To correct Ranking Distortion:** By building balanced benchmarks that accurately reflect a model's capabilities across various dimensions, A2Eval aims to mitigate systematic ranking biases. The goal is for model rankings to align more closely with human judgments of embodied competence, preventing models from appearing high-ranked due to strengths in overrepresented tasks while neglecting critical weaknesses.
- **To reduce Prohibitive Evaluation Costs:** A core objective is to decrease the computational and human effort overhead associated with current evaluation practices. This involves compressing evaluation suites, speeding up the evaluation process, and automating the generation of evaluation pipelines. The aim is to enable faster, more frequent, and more affordable iterative development cycles for embodied VLMs.
- **To resolve the Manual Pipeline Bottleneck:** The manual construction of inference and scoring logic for each new benchmark is identified as a significant constraint. A2Eval seeks to automate this process by developing an Eval Agent that synthesizes and validates executable evaluation pipelines, enabling fully autonomous and high-fidelity assessment without manual intervention.
- **To establish a new standard for embodied assessment:** Ultimately, the work aims to introduce a framework that offers high-fidelity, low-cost evaluation, serving as a more effective "compass" for navigating advancements in Embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

A2Eval is structured as a two-agent framework designed to automate the entire evaluation lifecycle, from benchmark construction to model assessment. The framework consists of a Data Agent and an Eval Agent, which operate collaboratively.

#### 3.1. Data Agent

The Data Agent is responsible for discovering unified capability dimensions and constructing a compact, balanced benchmark. Its workflow is divided into two stages:

##### 3.1.1. Dimension Induction

This stage involves iterative collaboration among three specialized agent roles:
- **Proposer (A_p):** This agent generates candidate capability dimensions by analyzing existing benchmark information, incorporating feedback from the Reviewer, and drawing from a shared memory of historical proposals and critiques. This iterative refinement process helps avoid previously rejected configurations and addresses identified issues.
- **Reviewer (A_r):** This agent evaluates the Proposer's candidate dimensions based on criteria such as conceptual redundancy, coverage comprehensiveness relative to domain requirements, and dimension balance. The Reviewer's critiques, which may suggest merging or refining dimensions, are fed back to the Proposer.
- **Assigner (A_a):** This agent validates dimension balance by performing actual example assignment and diversity-aware sampling. It reports balance statistics to the Reviewer, providing empirical evidence for further dimension refinement.

These agents communicate through a shared memory, enabling collective reasoning until a stable and optimal set of capability dimensions is established. The process iterates until the proposed dimensions stabilize.

##### 3.1.2. Benchmark Construction

After validating the dimension set, the Assigner constructs the benchmark through a two-stage process:
- **Dimension Assignment:** An ensemble of $N_v$ voter agents independently analyzes each example from the source benchmarks. Each voter assigns relevant dimensions based on its interpretation. The final dimension assignment for an example is determined by majority consensus among the voters. Examples are then aggregated into per-dimension pools ($E_{d_k}$).
- **Diversity-Aware Sampling:** To eliminate intra-dimension redundancy while preserving semantic diversity, a clustering-based selection method is applied. For each dimension $d_k$:
 - Examples are embedded into a semantic space by concatenating visual (CLIP features, averaged for video frames) and textual (sentence-transformers) embeddings.
 - If the number of examples in a dimension is less than a predefined cluster count $K$ (set to 500), all examples are retained.
 - Otherwise, the examples are clustered into $K$ groups, and the example closest to each cluster centroid is selected. This ensures that the retained examples sparsely yet strategically cover the semantic manifold, removing redundancy without sacrificing representational completeness.
 - By applying a uniform $K$ across dimensions, cross-dimension balance is achieved while maximizing internal diversity within each dimension. The final benchmark ($E_s$) is the union of these sampled sets, along with their balance statistics.

#### 3.2. Eval Agent

The Eval Agent automates the evaluation process on the benchmark constructed by the Data Agent. It comprises two specialized roles and interacts with a sandbox executor:

- **Evaluator (A_e):** This agent synthesizes model inference logic. It generates executable Python code that loads target models, processes test inputs from the benchmark, and produces model predictions. The code is iteratively refined based on diagnostic feedback (e.g., runtime errors, API mismatches) from the sandbox executor.
- **Scorer (A_s):** Given the validated inference logic and model predictions, this agent synthesizes executable scoring logic. This code computes evaluation metrics based on the test cases and model outputs. Similar to the Evaluator, the Scorer iteratively refines its code based on diagnostic feedback from the sandbox executor until reliable execution and metric consistency are achieved.
- **Sandbox Executor (X):** This component executes the agent-generated code (inference and scoring logic) in an isolated environment. It provides diagnostic feedback when errors occur, facilitating the iterative refinement process by the Evaluator and Scorer.

Once both inference logic ($F_e$) and scoring logic ($F_s$) are validated, the complete evaluation pipeline is executed on the final benchmark ($E_s$) to generate comprehensive assessment results for the target model across all induced capability dimensions.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup Details

- **Agent Models:** GPT-4o was used for the Assigner agent, while Gemini 3 Pro powered the Proposer, Reviewer, and Eval Agents.
- **Source Benchmarks:** Ten established embodied vision-language reasoning benchmarks were used: COSMOS, ERQA, Where2Place, VSI-Bench, OmniSpatial, EgoSchema, BLINK, RefSpatial, RoboSpatial, and EmbSpatial.
- **Evaluated Models:** A diverse set of 13 vision-language models were assessed, including variants from Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL-3.5, Qwen3-VL series, and GPT-5 Mini.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of A2Eval involved extensive experiments across 10 benchmarks and 13 models, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving evaluation efficiency, fidelity, and rationality.

#### 4.1. Unified Dimensions and Balanced Benchmark

- **Induced Dimensions:** The Data Agent autonomously induced eight capability dimensions for embodied visual-language reasoning (Table 1): Perception & Object Grounding (PercepObj), Scene & Action Understanding (SceneAct), Spatial & Geometric Reasoning (SpatGeo), Quantitative & Numerical Reasoning (QuantNum), Affordance & Function Reasoning (AffdFunc), Physical & Causal Reasoning (PhysCaus), Decision & Task Planning (DecPlan), and Dynamic Scene Reasoning (DynScene).
- **Benchmark Compression:** From an initial pool of 24,519 examples across source benchmarks, the Data Agent constructed a compact benchmark containing 3,781 examples, achieving an 85% reduction in sample count.
- **Coverage and Diversity:** The original benchmarks exhibited skewed distributions across dimensions, with substantial overlap. The sampled A2Eval benchmark showed a significantly more balanced coverage across the induced dimensions (Figure 5). UMAP visualizations confirmed that the selected subset sparsely yet strategically covers the full semantic manifold, indicating that comprehensive capability coverage was maintained despite redundancy removal (Figure 3, 6).

#### 4.2. Quality of Induced Capability Dimensions

- **Comprehensiveness:** The induced eight dimensions systematically decompose the cognitive requirements for embodied agents, spanning perception, reasoning, and planning. Only 19 out of 24,519 source examples were classified as "Other," suggesting comprehensive coverage of the embodied domain by the taxonomy (Table 2).
- **Independence:** The dimensions were distinguished by underlying reasoning mechanisms rather than superficial properties, ensuring they captured distinct cognitive operations (e.g., SpatGeo vs. PercepObj).

#### 4.3. Benchmark Validity and Rationality

- **Human–Agent Agreement:** An agreement study with 5 domain experts showed strong consistency between automatic dimension assignments and human-validated annotations. Average Cohen’s κ was 0.78, and inter-annotator agreement was 0.80, indicating a high alignment of the induced dimensions with human judgment (Table 9).
- **Ranking Fidelity under Compression:** Despite an 85% reduction in evaluation volume, the model rankings from the A2Eval constructed benchmark ($B_e$) showed strong alignment with those from all original benchmarks ($B$), with a Spearman’s ρ of 0.94 and Kendall’s τ of 0.81 (Table 3).
- **Capability-Aware Ranking via Human Alignment:** The A2Eval benchmark demonstrated higher alignment with human-centric rankings (Spearman’s ρ = 0.85, Kendall’s τ = 0.72) compared to the original benchmark's correlation with human rankings (ρ = 0.83, τ = 0.64). This indicated that A2Eval produces a more rational, capability-aware ordering that better reflects human-perceived embodied competence.
- **Case Study:** The case of Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct versus Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct illustrated that A2Eval corrected a ranking distortion. While the original benchmark ranked Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct higher, A2Eval reversed this order to align with human preference. This was attributed to A2Eval's rebalancing of under-tested critical reasoning capabilities, revealing significant performance gaps not apparent in the biased original benchmarks (Table 3, 7).
- **Ablation Study:** The ablation study on sampling strategies confirmed that both agentic dimension awareness (via multi-agent voting) and diversity-aware sampling (via clustering) were essential. The combined approach achieved the best human alignment (ρ = 0.85) while maintaining reasonable consistency with the original benchmark (ρ = 0.94) (Table 4).

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency

A2Eval significantly reduced evaluation costs:
- **Suite Compression:** Compressed evaluation suites by 85%.
- **Cost Reduction:** Reduced overall computational costs by 77%.
- **Speedup:** Achieved a consistent 3.4x to 4.6x speedup across various model sizes. For instance, the evaluation time for Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking decreased from 412.9 to 89.4 hours (a 4.6x speedup) (Table 5).

#### 4.5. Eval Agent Fidelity

- **Overall Fidelity:** The Eval Agent achieved an average fidelity of 96.9% for its complete agent-generated evaluation pipeline (including both inference and scoring logic) when compared to reference implementations (Table 6).
- **Inference Fidelity:** The inference logic generated by the Eval Agent showed an average fidelity of 93.6%, measured by normalized edit distance against reference model outputs (Table 10).
- **Scoring Fidelity:** The scoring logic demonstrated an average fidelity exceeding 97% based on relative score difference compared to reference scoring functions (Table 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

A2Eval presents a novel framework for automated and agentic evaluation of embodied vision-language models, addressing several critical limitations of current evaluation practices.

- **Improved Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** By compressing evaluation suites by 85% and reducing computational costs by 77% with a 4.6x speedup, A2Eval significantly lowers the barriers to comprehensive model evaluation. This efficiency gain can accelerate iterative research and development cycles in Embodied AI, making advanced model development more accessible. The reduction in computational resources may also contribute to a positive environmental impact by lowering energy consumption.
- **Enhanced Evaluation Quality and Trustworthiness:** The framework mitigates the issues of benchmark redundancy, coverage imbalance, and ranking distortion. The Data Agent's ability to autonomously induce capability dimensions and construct balanced benchmarks leads to more comprehensive and fair assessments. The demonstrated high alignment with human judgment (Spearman’s ρ = 0.85) suggests that A2Eval produces more rational and reliable model rankings, which can guide research directions more accurately than evaluations based on biased datasets.
- **Scalability and Automation:** By decoupling assessment from static expert labels and automating the synthesis and validation of evaluation pipelines, A2Eval offers a scalable solution for the rapidly evolving field of embodied intelligence. This agentic approach reduces the need for manual intervention, making the evaluation process more robust and adaptable to new models and tasks.
- **Setting a New Standard:** The combination of high fidelity, low cost, and improved human alignment positions A2Eval as a new standard for embodied assessment. It provides a more dependable "compass" for navigating advancements in embodied AI, ensuring that progress is measured against a more robust and representative set of capabilities.

Overall, A2Eval offers a systematic solution to long-standing challenges in embodied VLM evaluation, with the potential to foster more principled and efficient development in Embodied AI.
