# Foundational World Models Accurately Detect Bimanual Manipulator Failures

> [!summary] Summary
> A method for real-time failure detection in bimanual manipulator robots leverages a probabilistic world model within the latent space of a pretrained vision foundation model, achieving high accuracy in simulated and real-world cable manipulation tasks with fewer parameters than comparative learning-based approaches. This system effectively differentiates between nominal and anomalous behaviors by utilizing the world model's uncertainty as a failure indicator.

## Problem

- Deploying bimanual manipulators faces challenges from anomalous failures that can cause damage, safety issues, or degrade performance in operational environments.
- Defining and detecting all possible failure modes in the vast, high-dimensional visual and proprioceptive state spaces of bimanual robots is computationally infeasible and impractical.
- Existing anomaly detection methods struggle with the complexity, high dimensionality, and real-time processing demands of modern robotic data, often being unsuitable or unreliable under distribution shifts.

## Method

- A ==probabilistic, history-conditioned world model== (WM) is trained exclusively on nominal robot trajectories within the compressed latent space of NVIDIA's ==Cosmos Tokenizer==, a pretrained vision foundation model.
- The WM learns to predict future latent feature maps and their distributions, enabling it to quantify ==uncertainty== during expected nominal operation.
- Two ==non-conformity scores== are derived from the WM for failure prediction (intrinsic uncertainty and prediction error), and calibrated using ==conformal prediction== to set failure thresholds solely from nominal data.

## Results

- In simulated Push-T tasks, the world model's uncertainty effectively distinguished nominal from anomalous behaviors, consistently increasing for out-of-distribution inputs.
- On a real-world bimanual cable manipulation dataset, the proposed world model methods outperformed competing learning-based approaches, achieving a **3.8%** higher failure detection rate with approximately **20x fewer** trainable parameters.
- WM uncertainty correlated with impending or ongoing failures, demonstrating rapid increases before events like cable drops, indicating early detection capabilities even before visible failure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World model uncertainty, specifically the predicted standard deviations over future latents, serves as a more robust indicator of anomalous behavior than a simple prediction error.
> - Integrating pretrained vision foundation models allows the world model to operate in a compressed latent space, significantly reducing trainable parameters and computational load while maintaining strong performance.
> - The world model can detect subtle anomalous conditions, such as an impending cable drop, by integrating visual, proprioceptive, and action data, revealing deviations even before visible failure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of visuomotor robotic systems, particularly bimanual manipulators, in operational environments presents challenges due to the potential for anomalous failures. Such failures can degrade performance, inflict damage, or compromise safety. Bimanual manipulators operate within expansive state spaces encompassing high-dimensional visual data and proprioceptive signals. Explicitly pre-defining all possible failure modes within these complex state spaces is largely impractical. This research addresses the challenge of anomaly detection in such systems by leveraging recent advancements in machine learning, specifically foundation models and world models.

The paper situates its approach within the context of established anomaly detection and out-of-distribution (OOD) detection techniques. Traditional methods, such as control charts and hypothesis testing, are noted for their simplicity but are often unsuitable for high-dimensional, non-stationary, and multimodal robotic data. Ensemble-based statistical methods offer some improvements but still primarily estimate uncertainty from limited data distribution knowledge.

Representation learning methods, including autoencoders and embedding-based techniques, aim to discover feature spaces where nominal and anomalous states are more easily distinguishable. While flexible and model-agnostic, their reliability can diminish under distribution shifts. Distribution modeling approaches, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), autoregressive models, and normalizing flows, explicitly learn nominal data distributions, but they can assign high likelihoods to anomalous data and are computationally demanding.

More modern approaches have explored the use of world models, which learn system dynamics from training data and can provide insights into confidence or epistemic uncertainty for OOD detection. The paper also acknowledges nascent efforts involving large language models (LLMs) for semantic anomaly detection, which, despite offering generalization and interpretability, face challenges related to prompt sensitivity, latency, and potential for hallucination.

This work specifically advances the application of world models by integrating them with pretrained vision foundation models. By operating within the compressed latent space of a model like NVIDIA’s Cosmos Tokenizer, the proposed method aims to achieve robust failure detection with reduced computational resources and parameter counts compared to alternative learning-based approaches, addressing a critical need for scalable and reliable monitoring of complex robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and reliable method for detecting anomalous failures in bimanual manipulator robots during runtime. This objective is motivated by several challenges inherent in deploying such systems in high-stakes environments:

1. **Safety and Reliability:** Robotic systems are increasingly deployed in scenarios where failures can lead to significant delays, property damage, or pose risks to human safety. For bimanual manipulators, which perform intricate, coordinated tasks (e.g., assembly, tool use, deformable object handling), small errors can cascade into significant failures. Detecting these anomalies in real-time is crucial for ensuring safe and consistent operation.

2. **Complexity of State Spaces:** Bimanual robots generate vast amounts of high-dimensional data, including multiple high-resolution camera feeds and extensive proprioceptive signals. Explicitly defining every conceivable failure mode within this expansive state space is practically infeasible. A method is needed that can identify deviations from "nominal" behavior without requiring an exhaustive enumeration of "failure" behaviors.

3. **Efficient Data Processing:** The high data rates from modern robotic sensors make real-time processing for anomaly detection computationally challenging. An efficient representation of high-dimensional behavioral sequences is required to enable timely identification of anomalies.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a specific approach centered on leveraging pretrained foundation vision models. Their core idea is to train a probabilistic, history-informed world model within the compressed latent space generated by such a foundation model. This world model is designed to learn only nominal system dynamics, thereby minimizing prediction uncertainty during expected operation. Deviations from nominal behavior are then expected to result in increased uncertainty from the model.

The research's specific contributions, as outlined by the authors, are threefold:

1. **Development of a Probabilistic World Model:** Proposing a VAE-based world model trained within the latent space of NVIDIA’s pretrained Cosmos Tokenizer. This approach aims to reduce the trainable parameter count, making the model more efficient.

2. **Introduction of Failure Prediction Methods:** Presenting two distinct methods for failure prediction based on the world model: (i) an intrinsic VAE uncertainty estimate, and (ii) an empirical forecast error. These are benchmarked against existing anomaly detection techniques.

3. **Creation of a Novel Dataset:** Introducing the Bimanual Cable Manipulation dataset, which provides labeled nominal and failure trajectories from real-world bimanual robot manipulators engaged in a data center maintenance task, offering a challenging testbed for evaluation.

By fulfilling these objectives, the research aims to provide a practical and scalable solution for real-time failure detection in complex robotic systems, thereby contributing to their safer and more reliable deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on training a probabilistic, history-conditioned world model (WM) within the latent space of a pretrained vision foundation model and subsequently utilizing its uncertainty estimates as non-conformity scores within a conformal prediction framework.

**A. Training the World Model**

1. **Input Processing:** The world model's input consists of a historical sequence of visual observations, proprioceptive states, and actions.
 - **Visual Encoding:** Raw images are first encoded using NVIDIA’s Cosmos Tokenizer, a pretrained vision autoencoder specialized for manipulator images, which generates compressed latent feature maps.
 - **Feature Fusion:** These latent visual feature maps are then combined with learned projections of historical proprioceptive states and action embeddings.
2. **Model Architecture:** A transformer-based sequence model processes these fused latent features.
3. **Prediction:** The transformer is trained to predict distributions over future latent feature maps. These distributions include standard deviations, which quantify the uncertainty of the future prediction.
4. **Training Data:** The WM is trained exclusively on pairs of historical context (h_t) and subsequent state (s_t+1) sampled from nominal trajectories. The training process continues until validation loss no longer improves. The objective is to minimize uncertainty when the robot exhibits nominal behavior.
5. **Loss Function:** The total loss (L) during training is a weighted sum of four components:
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L_recon):** Combines a perceptual loss (L_V) to maximize perceptually accurate reconstructions of future images across all camera views in pixel space, and a Mean Squared Error (L_MSE) term for the proprioceptive state.
 - **Latent Reconstruction Loss (L_z_recon):** Minimizes the MSE between the predicted and ground truth latent feature maps (z and ˆz).
 - **KL Divergence (L_KL):** Regularizes the latent distribution over future states, ensuring it remains close to a standard normal distribution (zero mean, unit standard deviation).
 - **Negative Log Likelihood (L_NLL):** Encourages the ground truth latent state to be likely under the learned latent distribution.
6. **Curriculum Learning:** Training begins with a one-step prediction objective. Every 16 epochs, the autoregressive prediction horizon is doubled, gradually extending to a maximum of 32 steps. This strategy aims to stabilize training and enable the model to capture longer-term dynamics.

**B. Non-conformity Scores**

To translate world model predictions into actionable failure detection, scalar non-conformity scores are computed, quantifying the deviation from nominal behavior. The study investigates two primary scores derived from the world model and seven baseline methods:

1. **WM Uncertainty (Proposed):** Defined as the average of the standard deviations across all dimensions of the predicted 3D tensor of normal distributions over future latents. Higher values indicate greater uncertainty.
2. **WM Prediction Error (Proposed):** Calculated as the discrepancy between the world model’s predicted next-step states and the actual observed states, computed as Mean Squared Error in the latent space (L_z_recon).
3. **logpZO:** The negative log-probability under a Zero-order (ZO) normalizing flow model trained on nominal latent trajectories. Low probabilities indicate OOD samples.
4. **AE Reconstruction Error:** The Mean Squared Error between the input state and its reconstruction by an autoencoder trained solely on nominal states.
5. **AE sim(z, z_safe):** The Mean Squared Error (similarity measure) in latent space between a test embedding and the nearest embedding from a known "safe" set of nominal data.
6. **SPARC:** Measures the smoothness of a trajectory by the arc length of the Fourier magnitude spectrum of sequential states. Jerkier (anomalous) trajectories have longer arc lengths.
7. **PCA K-means:** Involves PCA-based dimensionality reduction followed by K-means clustering. The score is the distance from the nearest cluster centroid, a method that requires both nominal and failure data for training.
8. **Random:** A baseline that assigns uniform random scores, serving as a lower bound for classification performance.

**C. Conformal Prediction**

Conformal prediction (CP) is employed to calibrate failure thresholds for each non-conformity score:

1. **Score Smoothing:** For each trajectory, the raw non-conformity score sequence is convolved with a uniform triangular filter (length 50) to smooth high-frequency variations, emphasizing recent samples. The maximum value of this smoothed sequence is taken as the trajectory-level statistic.
2. **Threshold Fitting:** Thresholds are determined using only a held-out set of nominal trajectories, without exposure to any failure data. A trajectory is flagged as anomalous if its statistic exceeds the (1 - α) quantile of the nominal calibration distribution, providing a theoretical false alarm rate of at most α.
3. **Bias Reduction:** A delete-d jackknife procedure with 32 random permutations is used. In each permutation, thresholds are fitted on half of the held-out nominal trajectories and evaluated on the other half, with the resulting thresholds then averaged.
4. **Assumptions and Caveats:** The validity of CP guarantees relies on the exchangeability of calibration and test data. While time series data is often temporally correlated, the use of a summary statistic per trajectory mitigates this, requiring only trajectory-level exchangeability. The paper acknowledges that changing environmental conditions or hardware wear can shift data distributions, potentially misestimating error rates for long-term deployments.

**D. Datasets**

1. **Push-T Dataset (Simulated):**
 - **Task:** An end-effector agent pushes a blue T-shaped object into a green T-shaped slot.
 - **Data:** 1028 nominal rollouts for training, 128 for validation. 512 nominal and 2048 failure rollouts for evaluation (512 each across four distinct failure modes: T-object color change to orange or green, T-object friction reduced to half or zero).
 - **State:** 25fps, 128x128x3 single-view RGB video; (x,y) position of end-effector as proprioceptive state.
2. **Bimanual Cable Manipulation Dataset (Real-World, Novel):**
 - **Task:** A WR1 mobile manipulator robot performs data center maintenance, specifically plugging in cables. The primary failure mode is the cable being dropped.
 - **Data:** 83 nominal trajectories for training/validation, 7 held-out nominal for CP threshold fitting, 7 held-out nominal and 9 held-out failure trajectories for evaluation.
 - **Collection:** Data collected using a WR1 robot in a data center, operated by a remote technician via a low-latency, secured network.
 - **State:** Trajectories are 18 seconds to 3 minutes long. Features four synchronized ~60fps, 1280x720x3 RGB video feeds (head, chest, left gripper, right gripper cameras); a 52-dimensional proprioceptive state vector; and a 41-dimensional action vector at each timestep. Only gripper camera views were used in this work.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the efficacy of foundation world models for detecting bimanual manipulator failures.

**A. Push-T Dataset Performance**
On the simulated Push-T environment, the world model (WM) uncertainty scores demonstrated a consistent increase for out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs compared to nominal trajectories. This effect was observed for both visual anomalies (changes in T-object color) and alterations to the environmental dynamics (modified friction coefficients). The method, which processes both visual and kinematic data, effectively distinguished between nominal and anomalous behaviors across these varied OOD modes, with the 90% conformal prediction threshold clearly separating the score distributions.

**B. Bimanual Cable Manipulation Dataset Performance**
Evaluation on the newly introduced Bimanual Cable Manipulation dataset demonstrated the comparative performance of the proposed methods against several baselines:

- **Superiority of WM Methods:** The proposed WM-based methods consistently outperformed the competing learning-based approaches (logpZO, AE reconstruction error, AE sim(z, z_safe)) and statistical methods (SPARC, PCA K-means). This was observed despite the WM requiring approximately one-twentieth of the trainable parameters (569.7k) compared to the next-best learning-based approach (around 10M), while still achieving a 3.8% higher failure detection rate.
- **WM Uncertainty vs. Prediction Error:** The WM uncertainty score was found to be a more reliable non-conformity indicator than the WM prediction error. This is attributed to the model's distributional uncertainty providing a more robust signal of off-manifold inputs compared to a single-outcome prediction error, which might be low by chance even for anomalous inputs.
- **Conformal Prediction Coverage:** While conformal prediction was employed, the empirical coverage on the evaluation set did not consistently match the presumed (1-α) quantile (85% in this case). This discrepancy is hypothesized to be due to the relatively small size of the total nominal dataset, which could lead to biases in the randomly selected fitting and evaluation subsets, preventing strict exchangeability.

**C. Correlation of WM Uncertainty with Anomalous Failures**
An analysis of unseen failure cases in the Bimanual Cable Manipulation dataset revealed that the WM uncertainty metric correlates with impending or ongoing anomalous failures. Periods when the robot was not holding a cable (and thus could not drop it) were associated with the lowest WM uncertainty. Conversely, uncertainty rapidly increased prior to the cable being dropped, even when the grippers visually appeared to be grasping the cable. This suggests that the world model leverages proprioceptive state and action sequences in conjunction with visual information to identify deviations from nominal behavior, indicating that internal dynamics or manipulation attempts that precede visible failure states contribute to increased uncertainty.

**D. Runtime Performance**
All evaluated methods, including the deep learning-based WM approaches, operated at a speed sufficient for real-time execution in the robotic setting, achieving throughput comfortably above 9Hz. Statistical baselines (SPARC, PCA K-means) were the fastest. Among learning-based methods, those operating directly in latent space (e.g., AE sim(z, z_safe)) were faster than those requiring reconstruction to pixel space (e.g., AE reconstruction error). While WM-based methods were comparatively slower than statistical methods, their speed was still adequate for deployment, and they offer additional benefits such as enabling planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a method for detecting anomalous failures in bimanual manipulator robots using a probabilistic world model trained exclusively on nominal task completion data. The primary significance of this work lies in its contribution to enhancing the safety and reliability of complex robotic deployments.

The findings indicate that world model uncertainty, when integrated with a pretrained vision foundation model and calibrated via conformal prediction, serves as a reliable indicator of anomalous behavior. This enables effective discrimination between nominal and failure trajectories in real-time, specifically demonstrated in a challenging real-world bimanual robot manipulation scenario (data center cable manipulation).

The approach addresses key challenges in robotic anomaly detection:
- **Handling High-Dimensionality:** By operating within the compressed latent space of a vision foundation model, the method efficiently processes high-dimensional visual and proprioceptive data, overcoming the computational burden associated with raw sensor streams.
- **Implicit Failure Definition:** The methodology bypasses the impracticality of explicitly defining every failure mode by learning the distribution of nominal behavior and detecting deviations, thus offering a more scalable solution.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** The utilization of pretrained foundation models allows the world model to achieve strong performance with a significantly reduced number of trainable parameters, making it more resource-efficient than comparable learning-based alternatives. This efficiency is critical for deploying advanced perception and monitoring systems on robotic hardware with potentially constrained computational resources.

The ability to accurately detect failures in real-time, with guarantees on false alarm rates through conformal prediction, paves the way for safer and more robust deployment of manipulator robots in environments where reliability is critical and failures can have substantial consequences. This can lead to reduced downtime, prevention of equipment damage, and improved human safety in industrial, service, and potentially hazardous applications.

**Limitations identified by the authors include:**
- **Conformal Prediction Assumptions:** The formal assumptions regarding exchangeability for conformal prediction are violated with time-series data, although empirical results suggest effectiveness.
- **Benign Distribution Shifts:** The method may flag false errors in response to benign environmental changes (e.g., background color changes) that represent OOD inputs but are not actual failures.
- **Tokenizer Biases:** The inherited biases from the pretrained vision tokenizer are currently unknown.

**Future work suggested by the authors includes:**
- Exploring longer and more compact historical representations to enhance the detection of long-term task-progression failures.
- Conducting feature importance analysis to quantify the contributions of visual and proprioceptive data to the WM uncertainty metric.
- Extending the approach to fully autonomous manipulation policies.
- Investigating the use of the WM for simultaneous failure detection and correction through optimization procedures aimed at minimizing WM uncertainty, potentially improving the robustness of bimanual manipulators.
