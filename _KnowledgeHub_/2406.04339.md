# RoboMamba: Efficient Vision-Language-Action Model for Robotic Reasoning and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboMamba introduces an efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for robotic reasoning and manipulation by leveraging the Mamba State Space Model. It achieves up to 7 times faster inference speeds and acquires manipulation skills by fine-tuning only 0.1% of its parameters, while improving manipulation success rates by up to 7.0% on 'seen' tasks in simulation.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM-based robotic VLA models often exhibit insufficient reasoning capabilities for complex robotic scenarios, such as long-horizon planning or affordance understanding.
- Traditional Transformer-based MLLMs incur high computational costs due to quadratic attention complexity, making them impractical for real-time robotic control and expensive to fine-tune.
- Acquiring low-level robotic manipulation skills typically requires extensive fine-tuning of a large proportion of the MLLM's parameters, demanding significant computational resources.

## Method

- **RoboMamba** integrates a pre-trained ==CLIP vision encoder== with a ==Mamba (State Space Model)== Large Language Model and a small, dedicated ==MLP policy head== for ==6-DoF end-effector pose prediction==.
- A ==two-stage training pipeline== is employed: first, ==cross-modal alignment== and ==instruction co-training== for general and robotic-related reasoning (freezing CLIP encoder, fine-tuning Mamba LLM); second, efficient ==manipulation fine-tuning==.
- During manipulation fine-tuning, all parameters of the pre-trained RoboMamba (vision encoder, Mamba LLM) are frozen, with only the simple, ==0.1% parameter policy head== being updated to learn action skills.

## Results

- RoboMamba achieves state-of-the-art manipulation success rates in simulation, improving 'seen' tasks by **7.0%** and 'unseen' tasks by **2.0%** over the previous best methods.
- It demonstrates significantly faster inference, up to **7 times** quicker than LLaMA-AdapterV2 and ManipLLM, and acquires manipulation skills by fine-tuning only **0.1% (3.7M)** of its parameters in dozens of minutes.
- The model exhibits superior robotic-related reasoning on the RoboVQA dataset and competitive performance on general VQA benchmarks, while also showing improved hallucination reduction on the POPE benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mamba's linear complexity and strong context-aware reasoning make it an effective and efficient architectural backbone for multimodal robotic VLA models, overcoming Transformer's computational limitations.
> - Developing strong, dedicated robotic-related reasoning capabilities in an MLLM is foundational and directly correlates with improved manipulation accuracy and generalization.
> - Once an MLLM possesses sufficient reasoning abilities, the acquisition of specific low-level manipulation skills can be achieved with extremely minimal fine-tuning (e.g., updating only ~0.1% of parameters in minutes).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "RoboMamba" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), State Space Models (SSMs), and Robotic Manipulation. The dramatic scaling of data and model parameters has propelled LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, LLaMA) to exhibit remarkable reasoning and generalization in Natural Language Processing (NLP). This success has inspired the development of MLLMs (e.g., BLIP-2, LLaVA, Flamingo), which extend LLMs' capabilities to understand and generate responses based on multimodal inputs, primarily vision and language.

Within robotics, MLLMs are being increasingly leveraged to imbue robots with human-like understanding and interaction capabilities. Current research in robotic MLLMs broadly falls into two categories:
1. **High-level Task Planning:** Models that interpret natural language instructions and visual scenes to generate abstract task plans (e.g., Palm-E, VoxPoser).
2. **Low-level Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Models that directly predict robot actions, such as SE(3) end-effector poses, conditioned on visual and linguistic inputs (e.g., RT-1, RoboFlamingo, ManipLLM).

RoboMamba specifically targets the second category, VLA models, while simultaneously addressing two critical challenges identified in existing MLLM-based robotic VLA models:
1. **Insufficient Reasoning:** Pre-trained MLLMs often lack the specific reasoning capabilities required for complex robotic scenarios, such as long-horizon planning, understanding affordances, or predicting future states.
2. **High Computational Costs:** Attention-based LLMs, with their quadratic complexity concerning sequence length, incur significant computational overhead during both fine-tuning and inference. This makes them impractical for real-time robotic control, which demands rapid responses.

This paper makes a novel contribution by being "the first attempt to introduce Mamba to address critical challenges in robotics." Mamba, a recently proposed State Space Model (SSM), offers linear inference complexity and impressive capabilities in non-trivial sequence modeling, making it a highly attractive alternative to the computationally expensive Transformer architecture prevalent in most LLMs. By integrating Mamba into a VLA framework, RoboMamba aims to overcome the efficiency bottleneck without sacrificing, and indeed enhancing, the reasoning abilities crucial for robust robotic manipulation. It positions itself as an efficient, end-to-end solution that balances sophisticated reasoning with real-world deployment practicalities, differentiating itself from prior MLLM-based VLA models that either face efficiency issues or require extensive parameter fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for robotic reasoning and manipulation that addresses the limitations of existing MLLM-based approaches.

The core motivations driving the development of RoboMamba are:

1. **Enhancing Robotic Reasoning for Complex Tasks:** Existing VLA models, while capable of basic tasks, often struggle with complex robotic reasoning, such as understanding long-horizon plans, generating appropriate sub-tasks, and handling nuanced semantic information from visual scenes. The authors seek to empower a VLA model with superior "visual common sense and robotic-related reasoning" abilities.

2. **Overcoming Computational Inefficiency:** Traditional MLLMs, built on Transformer architectures, suffer from high computational costs due to their attention mechanisms, which scale quadratically with sequence length. This makes fine-tuning and inference expensive and often too slow for real-time robotic applications where quick, responsive actions are critical. The motivation is to find a more "efficient" architectural backbone that can maintain strong reasoning while drastically reducing computational overhead.

3. **Leveraging State Space Models (SSMs):** Inspired by the promising capabilities of Mamba, a State Space Model (SSM) with linear inference complexity and strong context-aware reasoning, the authors are motivated to explore if this novel architecture can serve as the backbone for an efficient and powerful robotic VLA model. This involves investigating how Mamba can integrate visual and linguistic inputs to generate both high-level reasoning outputs and low-level actions.

4. **Achieving Cost-Effective Manipulation Skill Acquisition:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate that once a VLA model possesses strong reasoning capabilities, it can acquire low-level manipulation skills (like SE(3) pose prediction) with "minimal fine-tuning parameters and time." This challenges the prevalent paradigm of extensive end-to-end fine-tuning of large MLLMs for robotic tasks, aiming for a more resource-efficient and practical training strategy.

In summary, the authors aim to build a VLA model that is both intellectually capable (strong reasoning) and practically viable (efficient fine-tuning and inference), explicitly using Mamba as the core enabling technology to achieve this balance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboMamba's methodology revolves around integrating a vision encoder with the Mamba Large Language Model (LLM) and a specialized policy head, trained through a meticulously designed two-stage pipeline to achieve both robust reasoning and efficient manipulation capabilities.

#### 3.1. RoboMamba Architecture

The model architecture is composed of three main components:
- **Vision Encoder:** A pre-trained CLIP (e.g., ViT-Large) encoder extracts visual features from input images. The authors specifically avoid ensemble techniques to maintain efficiency, opting for a single, straightforward backbone.
- **Cross-Modal Connector:** A Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) acts as a projection layer, converting the extracted visual features into the language embedding space compatible with the Mamba LLM.
- **Mamba Language Model:** The core language model is based on Mamba, a State Space Model (SSM) with a selective scan mechanism (S6). Mamba is chosen for its context-aware reasoning and linear computational complexity, crucial for efficient real-time operation in robotics. Text prompts are tokenized and concatenated with the visual embeddings before being fed into Mamba.
- **Policy Head:** For action prediction, a simple policy head consisting of two separate MLPs is attached to the global output token from Mamba (derived via a pooling operation). These MLPs are designed to predict the 6-DoF end-effector pose (3D position $a_{pos}$ and 3x3 rotation matrix $a_{dir}$). For grasping tasks, gripper status is added, resulting in 7-DoF control. This policy head is intentionally small, comprising only ~0.1% of the total model parameters.

#### 3.2. Training Pipeline (Two Stages)

The training strategy is divided into two distinct stages:

**Stage 1: General and Robotic-Related Reasoning**
This stage aims to equip RoboMamba with comprehensive reasoning abilities, covering both general visual understanding and specific robotic contexts.
- **Stage 1.1: Alignment Pre-training:**
 - **Objective:** To align visual tokens with Mamba's language embedding space.
 - **Method:** The vision encoder and Mamba LLM parameters are frozen. Only the MLP projection layer is updated.
 - **Data:** LLaVA-LCS 558K dataset (image-text pairs) is used for this cross-modal alignment.
- **Stage 1.2: Instruction Co-training:**
 - **Objective:** To impart visual common sense and "enhanced generalization capabilities" for high-level robotic tasks.
 - **Method:** The CLIP encoder remains frozen. The projection layer and the entire Mamba LLM are fine-tuned.
 - **Data:** A co-training strategy is employed, combining:
 - **General Instruction Data:** Datasets like LLaVA mixed instruction (655K), ShareGPT4V-SFT, or LLaVA-Next. This mitigates hallucination and enhances general scene understanding.
 - **Robotic Instruction Data:** Randomly sampled image-text paired training samples from the RoboVQA dataset (e.g., 300K samples in main experiments). This includes tasks like long-horizon planning, success classification, affordance prediction, past description, and future prediction.
 - **Loss:** Cross-entropy loss is used for supervising language model outputs.

**Stage 2: Robot Manipulation Fine-tuning**
This stage focuses on efficiently equipping RoboMamba with low-level action prediction skills.
- **Objective:** To enable SE(3) pose prediction with minimal computational cost and parameter updates.
- **Method:** Crucially, *all parameters of the pre-trained RoboMamba (vision encoder, projection layer, and Mamba LLM) are frozen*. Only the newly introduced, simple MLP policy head is fine-tuned.
- **Data:** A custom dataset of 10K end-effector pose predictions is collected using the SAPIEN simulation environment with articulated objects from PartNet-Mobility. The Franka Emika Panda robot with a suction gripper is used. Data includes both "seen" and "unseen" categories for evaluating generalization.
- **Loss:** Position loss ($L_{pos}$) uses L1-norm, and direction loss ($L_{dir}$) uses an arccos formulation based on rotation matrices, following previous work.
- **Key Finding/Insight:** The authors discover that with sufficient reasoning capabilities from Stage 1, RoboMamba can acquire manipulation skills with "extremely efficient fine-tuning"—requiring only a few dozen minutes and updating a mere 0.1% of the model's total parameters.

In essence, RoboMamba prioritizes building a strong, efficient reasoning foundation with Mamba, then leverages this foundation to quickly and cheaply add specific manipulation skills via a small, dedicated policy head, rather than re-training or extensively fine-tuning the entire large model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on RoboMamba demonstrate its superior performance in both reasoning and manipulation tasks, particularly highlighting its efficiency gains.

#### 4.1. Reasoning Capabilities

- **General Reasoning:** RoboMamba, using a 2.7B Mamba LLM, achieves competitive performance on various general VQA and MLLM benchmarks (VQAv2, OKVQA, GQA, VizWiz, POPE, MME, MMBench, MM-Vet). Notably, it shows satisfactory results on the POPE benchmark for hallucination reduction, which is critical for reliable robotic actions. While some scores are slightly below larger MLLMs (7B), RoboMamba prioritizes efficiency and still delivers strong overall performance.
- **Robotic-Related Reasoning:** RoboMamba significantly outperforms baselines like LLaMA-AdapterV2 and TinyLLaVA on the RoboVQA validation set. It achieves superior BLEU-1 to BLEU-4 scores (e.g., 42.8 BLEU-4), indicating advanced capabilities in long-horizon planning, affordance generation, past/future prediction, and other robotic-specific reasoning tasks.
- **Efficiency in Reasoning:** RoboMamba achieves inference speeds up to 7 times faster than LLaMA-AdapterV2 and ManipLLM, attributing this to the content-aware reasoning and linear complexity of the Mamba language model.

#### 4.2. Manipulation Capabilities

- **Simulation Performance:** In the SAPIEN simulation, RoboMamba achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) manipulation success rates. It demonstrates a 7.0% improvement on "seen" tasks and a 2.0% improvement on "unseen" tasks compared to the previous SOTA, ManipLLM. This highlights its effectiveness and stability in predicting action poses and its strong generalization ability to novel objects.
- **Efficiency in Manipulation:** A key finding is that RoboMamba acquires manipulation skills with exceptional efficiency. Its simple policy head, comprising only 0.1% (3.7M parameters) of the total model parameters, is fine-tuned in "a few dozen minutes." This is significantly more efficient than RoboFlamingo (35.5% / 1.8B parameters updated) and ManipLLM (0.5% / 41.3M parameters updated), while still achieving better performance and 3 times faster inference speed than existing VLA models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of LLM:** Mamba-2.7B demonstrates significant improvements in reasoning (common sense and robotic-related) compared to other linear-complexity LLMs like RWKV-3B, validating Mamba's suitability and the effectiveness of the training strategy.
- **Impact of Reasoning on Manipulation:** The ablation study confirms that an MLLM's reasoning ability is directly proportional to its manipulation accuracy. Models with stronger reasoning (e.g., RoboMamba-2.7B) perform better in manipulation. Furthermore, including robotic instruction data during the co-training phase (Stage 1.2) significantly enhances the model's generalization ability to unseen manipulation objects. The study explicitly states: "fine-tuning an MLLM to learn robot skills does not require extensive resources; it only requires that the MLLM possesses strong robotic-related reasoning abilities."
- **Impact of Policy Head Design:** The results show that the specific design and parameter count of the policy head have minimal impact on manipulation accuracy once the core reasoning capabilities are established. This reinforces the efficiency argument, indicating that a complex policy head is not necessary for skill acquisition after robust reasoning is in place.

#### 4.4. Real-world Experiments

- Qualitative real-world experiments with a Franka Emika robotic arm demonstrate RoboMamba's ability to perform various robotic downstream tasks. This includes accurate long-horizon planning, discriminative and generative affordance understanding, past and future action prediction, and successful low-level pose prediction for interacting with household objects. The model's ability to reduce hallucination contributes to more reliable planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboMamba represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics, offering both theoretical novelty and practical implications.

#### 5.1. Pioneering Mamba in Robotics

This work is the "first attempt to introduce Mamba to address critical challenges in robotics." By successfully integrating the Mamba State Space Model (SSM) as the core language model, RoboMamba demonstrates that linear-complexity architectures can effectively handle the complex sequence modeling demands of multimodal robotic tasks. This opens up a new frontier for efficient, context-aware sequence modeling in embodied AI, potentially inspiring further research into SSMs for other robotic applications.

#### 5.2. Breakthrough in Computational Efficiency

One of the most impactful contributions is the drastic reduction in computational cost. RoboMamba achieves:
- **Faster Inference:** 3-7 times faster inference speeds compared to existing MLLM-based VLA models. This is crucial for real-time robotic control, enabling more responsive and dynamic interactions in physical environments.
- **Highly Efficient Fine-tuning:** Manipulation skills are acquired by fine-tuning only 0.1% of the model's total parameters in a matter of "dozens of minutes." This is a significant improvement over previous methods that require updating a much larger proportion of the LLM or its adapters. This makes the adaptation of VLA models to new manipulation tasks far more accessible and less resource-intensive.

#### 5.3. Novel Paradigm for Skill Acquisition

The paper's central finding—that "once RoboMamba possesses sufficient reasoning capabilities, it can acquire pose prediction skills with minimal cost"—introduces a new paradigm for training VLA models. It suggests a decoupling of high-level reasoning and low-level action learning, where strong, general reasoning (especially robotic-specific reasoning) is foundational, and specific manipulation skills can be "plugged in" with minimal additional training. This challenges the need for extensive end-to-end fine-tuning of massive MLLMs for every new manipulation task, potentially accelerating the development and deployment of robotic systems.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Robotic Intelligence and Reliability

RoboMamba exhibits superior robotic-related reasoning abilities, including long-horizon planning, robust affordance understanding, and accurate past/future prediction. The improved performance on hallucination benchmarks (POPE) is particularly significant for robotics, as it reduces the likelihood of a robot generating nonsensical or unsafe actions. This leads to more reliable and intelligent robotic behavior in complex, unstructured environments.

#### 5.5. Broadening Accessibility to Advanced Robotics

The increased efficiency in both inference and training lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, allowing advanced VLA capabilities to be deployed on more modest hardware or within tighter computational budgets. This could democratize access to sophisticated robotic intelligence.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The limitations (e.g., current reliance on a 2.7B LLM for certain complex reasoning tasks) provide clear avenues for future work. The authors propose adapting the framework to more advanced linear-complexity LLMs and integrating 3D point cloud and temporal (4D) data, which are crucial for more robust and physically grounded robotic understanding and action. This work lays a strong foundation for the next generation of efficient and intelligent embodied AI systems.
