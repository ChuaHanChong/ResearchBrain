# Mem-World: Memory-Augmented Action-Conditioned World Models for Persistent Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Mem-World is a memory-augmented multi-view action-conditioned world model for robot manipulation. Its core, W-VMem, anchors historical wrist-view observations to 4D surfels and retrieves geometry-aware relevant history frames for future prediction, fixing occlusion-driven forgetting. It raises real-world correlation by 14.5% and boosts long-horizon success rate from 58% to 72%.

## Problem

- Wrist-camera occlusion and rapid egocentric motion leave current observations insufficient, causing autoregressive world models to forget or hallucinate previously seen scene details.
- Fixed-window or stride-based history sampling (Interactive World Simulator, Ctrl-World) uses joint-pose similarity as an indirect proxy, missing visibility constraints from end-effector occlusion.
- Navigation-oriented memory retrieval (FOV overlap, static 3D anchors) assumes static scenes, violated in manipulation where the scene is partially dynamic and the arm causes structured occlusions.

## Method

- W-VMem: a 4D wrist-view-centered surfel-indexed memory extending VMem with timestep and manipulated-object flags, anchoring historical observations to temporally evolving surface elements.
- Grounding future wrist views: forward kinematics plus fixed camera-to-end-effector transform converts predicted joint actions into future wrist-camera poses for surfel rendering.
- Geometry-aware retrieval scoring: renders surfels per-timestep from the future wrist pose, scores by visibility, task relevance, and temporal decay, then applies NMS-based top-K selection for non-redundant context.

## Results

- On curated occlusion/motion replay trajectories, Mem-World beats Ctrl-World and Cosmos Predict 2.5, e.g. wrist-view PSNR 19.21 vs 17.34 and object consistency 0.524 vs 0.476.
- Ablation against short-term and stride memory shows W-VMem improves wrist-view SSIM to 0.680 versus 0.614 (stride) and 0.526 (short-term).
- Policy evaluation correlation with real-world success rate reaches r=0.97 for Mem-World versus r=0.85 for Ctrl-World; synthetic data fine-tuning raises average success rate from 58% to 72%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal-aware, viewpoint-specific surfel rendering (not joint static rendering) is needed once scene elements move, since static-scene memory assumptions break under manipulation.
> - Restricting memory updates to the most task-relevant view (wrist) rather than all views preserves meaningful temporal association, avoiding memory saturation from wide-FOV cameras.
> - A world model whose imagined rollouts correlate strongly with real deployment outcomes can double as both a policy evaluator and a synthetic data engine for policy improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Action-conditioned world models represent a rapidly evolving area within robot learning, offering a method to generate action-consistent video trajectories. These models are typically built upon pre-trained video diffusion backbones and fine-tuned with action conditioning using large-scale robotics datasets. This approach aims to provide scalable alternatives to extensive and costly real-world experimentation, effectively serving as virtual environments for policy development and evaluation.

In robot manipulation, world models frequently employ a multi-view setup, integrating external third-person cameras with a wrist-mounted camera. This configuration is intended to capture both global scene context and fine-grained hand-object interactions. However, this multi-view approach, particularly the use of a wrist camera, introduces a significant challenge: the wrist camera undergoes rapid egocentric motion and is often occluded by the end-effector or manipulated objects. As a result, the current observation may provide only partial information about the scene state, leading autoregressive world models to exhibit "forgetting" or "hallucination" of objects that were visible in earlier frames, a problem that intensifies in long-horizon rollouts.

Existing research has attempted to mitigate these issues with varying degrees of success. Approaches like Interactive World Simulator address limited temporal context by expanding the conditioning window size to include adjacent historical frames; however, this method does not fundamentally resolve the underlying problem of inconsistent scene representation. Ctrl-World retrieves historical frames based on joint-pose similarity, but this is an indirect measure of contextual relevance. It may not adequately account for visibility constraints imposed by occlusions or prioritize viewpoints offering comprehensive scene coverage. Furthermore, memory retrieval mechanisms developed for navigation-oriented world models, which use camera field-of-view overlap or static 3D anchors, operate under assumptions of static scenes. These assumptions are often violated in robot manipulation environments, where scenes are partially dynamic and the robot arm itself causes structured occlusions. This paper identifies a gap in the current landscape: the lack of a robust solution for persistent, long-horizon multi-view rollouts in dynamic robot manipulation scenarios that effectively addresses occlusions and rapid camera motion.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a multi-view action-conditioned world model capable of generating persistent future observations for robot manipulation tasks. This addresses the challenge that current world models frequently fail to maintain scene consistency over long horizons, particularly in dynamic manipulation environments characterized by rapid wrist-camera movements and recurrent end-effector occlusions. These environmental factors often lead to visual inaccuracies such as the forgetting or hallucination of scene elements that were previously observed.

The motivation for this work stems from the understanding that a single, current observation is often insufficient to accurately predict future views in complex manipulation scenarios. There is a need for a mechanism that can effectively recall informative historical context, especially in a manner that is both geometry-aware and responsive to temporal dynamics. The authors specifically aim to overcome the limitations of existing memory retrieval strategies, which either rely on insufficient temporal context or indirect proxies for relevance that do not account for physical visibility or dynamic scene changes.

The specific objectives of Mem-World include:
- **Generating consistent rollouts**: To produce more temporally consistent video trajectories for long-horizon robot manipulation tasks, even when frequent end-effector occlusions and complex wrist camera motions occur.
- **Enabling reliable policy evaluation**: To allow world models to serve as effective simulators for evaluating diverse robot policies. This involves ensuring that the predicted rollouts accurately reflect the real-world performance of these policies, thereby improving the correlation between simulated and real-world outcomes.
- **Supporting policy improvement**: To facilitate the generation of useful synthetic data that can be used to fine-tune and improve robot policies, offering a scalable alternative to gathering extensive real-world training data.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to enhance the reliability and utility of world models in accelerating robot learning and development.

### 3. Methodology and approach

Mem-World is proposed as a memory-augmented multi-view action-conditioned world model designed to generate persistent robot manipulation rollouts. Its architecture integrates two main components: a wrist-view-centered 4D surfel memory module (W-VMem) and a multi-view action-conditioned world model. The overall pipeline operates autoregressively: the world model predicts future observations conditioned on retrieved history frames and future actions, and these predicted observations are then used to update the W-VMem for subsequent retrieval cycles.

The core innovation is W-VMem, which extends the concept of surfel-indexed view memory (VMem) by anchoring historical wrist-view observations to temporally evolving surface elements. This extension specifically addresses the challenges inherent in dynamic robot manipulation scenes.

The methodology addresses three key challenges in adapting surfel-indexed memory to manipulation tasks:

1. **Grounding future wrist views from robot actions**:
 - For the fixed third-person cameras, future poses are constant.
 - For the wrist camera, future poses are derived from the robot's joint-space actions. Forward kinematics are used to obtain future end-effector poses, and then a known fixed geometric transformation is applied to derive the corresponding wrist-camera poses in the robot base frame.
 - These camera poses are then transformed into the reference frame of the point cloud, which is typically estimated using a calibrated third-person camera.

2. **Time-aware Surfel Definition for Manipulation**:
 - Unlike VMem's assumption of a static scene, W-VMem augments each surfel with temporal and task-relevance attributes to account for dynamic manipulation.
 - Each surfel $s_k$ is defined as $(p_k, n_k, r_k, t_k, m_k)$, where:
 - $p_k, n_k, r_k$ are the 3D position, surface normal, and radius, respectively.
 - $t_k$ is a set of timesteps indicating when the surfel was created and updated, capturing temporal dynamics.
 - $m_k \in \{0, 1\}$ is a binary flag denoting whether the surfel belongs to a manipulated object. This flag is determined by using a vision-language model to identify task-relevant object categories from text instructions and initial observations, followed by a segmentation model to segment these objects and assign the flag to associated surfels.

3. **Memory Initialization and Updating**:
 - **Initialization**: An initial set of surfels is constructed from the first frame of all three camera views, providing a comprehensive initial scene observation.
 - **Updating**: In subsequent timesteps, only the wrist-view frame is used to update the surfel representation. This design choice is based on two rationales:
 - The wrist view offers the most manipulation-centric observations, capturing fine-grained changes and contact states in the active interaction region.
 - Restricting updates to the wrist view preserves the temporal association between surfels and the moving wrist camera, preventing global third-person views from repeatedly refreshing surfels and blurring their observation history.

**Geometry-aware Memory Retrieval**:
The memory reading module (Algorithm 1, steps 4-6) is responsible for retrieving relevant historical frames.
- **Average Future Wrist Pose**: An average future wrist camera pose ($\bar{c}_w$) is computed from the action chunk provided by the policy.
- **Individual Surfel Rendering**: Surfels from *each historical timestep* are rendered individually from the predicted $\bar{c}_w$. This differs from VMem, which renders all surfels jointly, and is crucial for capturing wrist-view-dependent temporal dynamics associated with distinct interaction stages.
- **Surfel Scoring**: Each rendered surfel at a given timestep $t$ is assigned a score (Equation 2) based on three factors:
 - **Geometric visibility**: Dependent on surfel depth ($d_s$), normal ($n_s$), and its alignment with the average future wrist-camera viewing direction ($\bar{v}_w$).
 - **Task relevance**: Encoded by the `is_manipulated` flag ($m_s$).
 - **Temporal recency**: A temporal decay term gives higher weights to more recently observed surfels, considering the latest timestep $T$, temporal half-life $H$, and minimum decay factor $\lambda_{min}$.
- **Selection**: Non-maximum suppression (NMS) is applied to the candidate observations based on their scores to reduce redundancy and encourage broader scene coverage among the selected top-K historical observations. These selected observations then serve as conditioning inputs for the world model's future prediction.

The world model itself is built upon Ctrl-World. It is fine-tuned on a dataset where original temporal context is replaced with geometry-related context retrieved by the training-free W-VMem. The training process specifically fine-tunes the temporal attention layers of the world model and upweights the loss term associated with wrist-view predictions.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate Mem-World's capabilities in generating consistent rollouts, its reliability for policy evaluation, and its effectiveness in supporting policy training.

#### 4.1. World Model Consistency Evaluation

- **Baselines and Setup**: Mem-World was compared against Cosmos Predict 2.5 (fine-tuned for single-view) and Ctrl-World. A curated set of 34 "memory-stressing" trajectories from the DROID dataset was used, featuring frequent end-effector occlusions and complex wrist-camera motions. Rollouts consisted of 20 prediction steps, equivalent to 20-second trajectories.
- **Metrics**: Evaluation included computation-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and an object-level consistency metric based on DINOv2 feature similarity of SAM3-segmented manipulated objects.
- **Results**:
 - Mem-World demonstrated superior performance across all metrics for both third-view and wrist-view cameras compared to the baselines (Table 1).
 - The improvements were notably significant for the wrist-view camera, which presents challenges due to rapid egocentric motion and frequent occlusions.
 - Qualitative results (Fig. 2) illustrated Mem-World's ability to maintain scene details and object appearance persistently, such as a lid remaining recoverable after transient occlusion and a black snack consistently regenerated despite back-and-forth camera motion.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study of Memory Retrieval Method

- **Baselines**: The study compared W-VMem against "Short-term memory" (N adjacent frames, as in Interactive World Simulator) and "Stride memory" (fixed stride sampling, as in Ctrl-World and EVAC). A base model was trained with randomly sampled context to ensure a fair comparison of retrieval methods.
- **Results**:
 - W-VMem consistently outperformed both short-term and stride memory strategies across all consistency metrics (Table 2).
 - Qualitative results (Fig. 3) indicated that W-VMem's geometry-aware retrieval effectively preserved consistent object appearances, preventing distortion or disappearance of objects (e.g., a spoon) during rollouts, unlike the other methods which provided less informative context.

#### 4.3. World Model for Policy Evaluation

- **Task Design**: Five manipulation tasks with varying temporal complexities were designed, including short-horizon tasks (e.g., "pick cube to the plate") and long-horizon tasks (e.g., "pick 2 objects to 2 targets") that involved sequential subtasks and complex loop-like wrist-camera motions.
- **Comparison**: Policies ($\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$) were evaluated in both real-world environments and in Mem-World and Ctrl-World simulations, starting from identical initial observations. Policies and world models were post-trained on a custom environment to adapt to visual domain shifts.
- **Results**:
 - Policy success rates in Mem-World exhibited a strong Pearson correlation (r = 0.97, p < 0.001) with their real-world counterparts across the five tasks (Fig. 4). While absolute success rates were generally lower in simulation, the gap was relatively consistent.
 - Ctrl-World showed a weaker correlation (r = 0.85, p < 0.01), primarily due to significant performance degradation on long-horizon tasks. This suggested that its stride-memory design struggled to maintain scene consistency, leading to poorer alignment with real-world outcomes (Fig. 6, e.g., severe distortion of an object like a fork preventing grasping). Mem-World improved the Pearson correlation by 14.5% compared to Ctrl-World.

#### 4.4. World Model for Policy Training

- **Setup**: The $\pi_{0.5}$ policy was used to generate synthetic trajectories with Mem-World in initial observations with unseen object arrangements (200 trajectories per task). A subset of 20-30 successful trajectories (human-annotated) was selected for policy fine-tuning.
- **Results**:
 - Fine-tuning the $\pi_{0.5}$ policy on these Mem-World-generated successful trajectories resulted in an increase in the average real-world success rate on the three long-horizon tasks from 58% to 72% (Fig. 5). This indicates the utility of Mem-World in generating high-quality synthetic data for improving policy performance.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The introduction of Mem-World, specifically through its W-VMem component, represents an advancement in addressing a persistent limitation of action-conditioned world models for robot manipulation: maintaining long-term scene consistency in dynamic environments. By integrating a 4D wrist-view-centered surfel-indexed memory that enables geometry-aware retrieval of historical observations, Mem-World effectively mitigates the issues of forgetting and hallucination caused by frequent end-effector occlusions and rapid wrist-camera motion.

The findings demonstrate that Mem-World produces more temporally consistent video rollouts than prior methods and widely adopted memory mechanisms, particularly under challenging manipulation scenarios. This enhanced consistency is critical for the reliability of simulated environments.

Furthermore, the research establishes that Mem-World provides a more dependable platform for policy evaluation. The strong correlation between policy success rates in Mem-World simulations and real-world performance (a 14.5% improvement in Pearson correlation over Ctrl-World) suggests that it can serve as a more accurate and trustworthy simulator. This reliability can significantly reduce the need for extensive real-world testing, thereby accelerating the development cycle of robot policies.

The demonstrated capability of Mem-World to generate useful synthetic data for policy training further underscores its potential impact. By improving the average real-world success rate of policies on long-horizon tasks through synthetic post-training, Mem-World offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative to real-world data collection, which is often resource-intensive and time-consuming. This ability to act as a "data engine" could democratize access to diverse training data, fostering innovation in robot learning.

Potential future work, as acknowledged by the authors, includes enhancing multi-view consistency to improve rollout quality when the initial wrist-view frame is limited or occluded. Additionally, incorporating physics-aware signals, such as contact or force information, could further increase the physical fidelity of imagined rollouts, preventing physically implausible outcomes (e.g., successful grasps without proper contact). These directions suggest ongoing development towards increasingly robust and physically grounded simulation environments for robotic applications, ultimately contributing to more reliable and generalizable robot learning.
