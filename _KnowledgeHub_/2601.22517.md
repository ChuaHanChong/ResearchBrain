# RoboStriker: Hierarchical Decision-Making for Autonomous Humanoid Boxing

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboStriker introduces a hierarchical framework for autonomous humanoid boxing, enabling a Unitree G1 robot to learn complex tactical behaviors by decoupling high-level strategy from low-level physical execution. The system achieves superior competitive performance and stable physical interaction in simulation, along with demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- High-dimensional continuous action spaces and strict physical constraints in humanoid control conflict with the diverse exploration needed for multi-agent strategic learning.
- Non-stationarity from evolving opponent policies in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) self-play destabilizes dynamically fragile humanoid systems.
- Existing MARL methods often lack physical feasibility for embodied robots, while current embodied control excels at single-agent tasks but lacks strategic co-evolution capabilities.

## Method

- A hierarchical, three-stage framework decouples high-level strategic reasoning from low-level physical execution to manage complex dynamics.
- Physically plausible boxing motions are first learned via a robust motion tracker and then distilled into a continuous, topologically constrained latent motion space.
- ==Latent-Space Neural Fictitious Self==-Play (LS-NFSP) is introduced for stable competitive co-evolution within this bounded latent space, augmented by behavioral warm-up and Adversarial Motion Priors.

## Results

- LS-NFSP achieved a **100.0%** win rate against direct action-space control agents and outperformed other latent-space self-play methods by up to **31.48%** in cross-play tournaments.
- Agents demonstrated higher offensive landing rates (**0.685** ± **0.03**) and engagement rates (**0.824** ± **0.02**), coupled with superior base stability (**0.942** ± **0.01**) and smoother torque control.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of behavioral warm-up for overcoming initial instability and Adversarial Motion Priors for generating effective and physically authentic strikes, with successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling balance maintenance from tactical exploration via a bounded latent manifold is a prerequisite for competitive learning in high-dimensional humanoid combat.
> - A topologically constrained latent space can semantically cluster motion primitives, allowing for smooth, stable interpolation between diverse behaviors like moving and striking.
> - Integrating policy averaging from Neural Fictitious Self-Play stabilizes competitive embodied MARL, resolving policy cycling and enabling robust strategy evolution in physically dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous humanoid robots that can perform complex, dynamic, and contact-rich tasks represents a critical frontier for embodied intelligence. Recent breakthroughs have significantly advanced humanoid capabilities in areas such as locomotion and agile maneuvers, enabling robots to execute impressive single-agent behaviors in passive, predefined environments. These successes often rely on sophisticated motion tracking and physics-based control techniques, as evidenced by the cited works from the authors' own group (e.g., Yin et al., 2025; Zeng et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025).

However, the research landscape has largely struggled to extend these single-agent capabilities to multi-agent competitive scenarios, where robots must engage in strategic co-adaptation under real-time physical constraints. Tasks like boxing introduce a fundamental shift: instead of merely generating predefined motions, the robot must reason strategically, react to an opponent's unpredictable actions, and maintain physical stability in high-dimensional, contact-rich dynamics.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) offers a principled framework for strategic interaction, having achieved grandmaster-level success in abstract domains like Go (Silver et al., 2017) and StarCraft II (Vinyals et al., 2019). Yet, its direct application to embodied humanoid control is hampered by severe challenges:
1. **High-dimensional Continuous Action Spaces:** Humanoid control involves many degrees of freedom, making policy exploration difficult and prone to physically invalid motions.
2. **Contact Dynamics and Physical Constraints:** Maintaining balance, respecting joint limits, and managing complex contact forces are paramount, but unconstrained MARL exploration often leads to catastrophic physical failures.
3. **Non-stationarity in Self-Play:** MARL often relies on self-play, where opponent policies continuously evolve. This non-stationarity can destabilize fragile humanoid systems, leading to training collapse.

Existing approaches typically address these challenges in isolation:
- **Game-theoretic MARL methods (e.g., Neural Fictitious Self-Play - NFSP):** While effective in abstract games, these lack inductive biases for physical feasibility, leading to unrealistic or unstable behaviors when directly applied to robotics.
- **Embodied control frameworks (e.g., DeepMimic, Adversarial Motion Priors - AMP):** These excel at learning robust single-agent motor skills through imitation and adversarial regularization, ensuring physical plausibility and human-like movement. However, they are not designed for strategic co-evolution or opponent-aware adaptation.

RoboStriker directly tackles these dual contradictions inherent to embodied MARL: the tension between physical feasibility and diverse exploration, and between strategic evolution and system stability. It seeks to bridge the gap between abstract strategic reasoning in MARL and the demanding physical realities of humanoid robotics. By proposing a hierarchical framework that explicitly decouples high-level strategy from low-level physical execution and introduces mechanisms to stabilize competitive learning, RoboStriker positions itself as a crucial advancement in making sophisticated multi-agent physical interaction a reality for humanoid robots. It builds upon the group's prior work in universal motion tracking and latent skill representation, extending these foundations to the complex domain of competitive physical tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RoboStriker stems from the significant challenges faced when attempting to imbue humanoid robots with human-level competitive intelligence and physical agility in dynamic, contact-rich tasks such as boxing. The authors identify two fundamental, domain-agnostic contradictions that characterize competitive humanoid systems and motivate their research:

1. **Physical Feasibility vs. Non-stationary Learning:** Humanoid control operates in a high-dimensional, continuous action space subject to strict physical constraints (e.g., contact stability, joint limits, underactuated dynamics). However, effective strategy exploration in a multi-agent setting requires diverse and often aggressive behaviors. Unconstrained policy exploration frequently leads to physically invalid motions and loss of balance, creating an "embodied cold-start problem" where agents cannot develop competitive strategies without first maintaining stable physical stance. Conversely, overly restrictive controllers stifle strategic learning.
2. **Strategy Evolution vs. System Stability:** Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) through self-play relies on continually evolving opponent policies, which introduces severe non-stationarity during training. While this dynamic is essential for discovering competitive strategies, humanoid systems are dynamically fragile. Small shifts in opponent behavior can destabilize balance and contact, causing training collapse. This creates a tension between the need for competitive strategy evolution and the requirement for stable embodied interaction.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the RoboStriker project are:

- **To formally characterize the intrinsic contradictions in embodied MARL:** Specifically, to highlight the trade-off between physical feasibility and non-stationary learning, and the conflict between strategy evolution and system stability, using humanoid competition as a concrete example.
- **To propose a hierarchical framework that decouples high-level strategic reasoning from low-level physical execution:** The goal is to create a structured approach that allows for stable evolution of dynamic combat behaviors by separating the complex physics of motion from the strategic decisions.
- **To learn a comprehensive repertoire of physically grounded boxing skills:** This involves creating a robust foundation of fundamental motions that are both human-like and physically feasible for the robot.
- **To distill these skills into a structured, bounded latent motion space:** This latent space will serve as a compact and physically safe strategy search space, abstracting away the low-level motor control complexity and enabling efficient high-level policy learning.
- **To develop a novel multi-agent training mechanism, Latent-Space Neural Fictitious Self-Play (LS-NFSP), that stabilizes competitive evolution within this latent space:** This mechanism aims to resolve the non-stationarity issue by allowing agents to learn competitive tactics in a confined, physically plausible action space.
- **To demonstrate superior competitive performance and robustness in simulation:** The framework should enable the emergence of tactical boxing behaviors that outperform existing baselines.
- **To achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer:** This is a crucial objective to validate the practical applicability of the learned policies on physical humanoid robots.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to provide a principled pathway for scaling MARL from abstract games to physically grounded robotic systems, pushing the boundaries of autonomous physical interaction for humanoids.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboStriker employs a hierarchical, three-stage framework to enable fully autonomous humanoid boxing by decoupling high-level strategic reasoning from low-level physical execution, as illustrated in Figure 2. This structured decision-making process is designed to address the challenges of high-dimensional contact dynamics and non-stationary learning in embodied MARL.

**Stage I: Learning Expertise Motion Tracker (Section 3.2, Appendix A)**
The first stage focuses on establishing a robust low-level controller capable of faithfully tracking diverse humanoid boxing motions while ensuring physical stability.
- **Motion Data:** A diverse dataset of human boxing motions (46 clips, 14 mins total) is collected from professional boxers using an Xsens motion capture system, then augmented via mirroring and retargeted to the Unitree G1 humanoid morphology.
- **Motion Tracker (π_trk):** A DeepMimic-based tracking policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This policy takes a robot's proprioceptive state (s_self) and a reference motion goal (s_ref) as input, and outputs target joint positions.
- **Reward Function (r_trk):** A comprehensive reward function encourages high-fidelity imitation of reference motions, including terms for global root position/orientation, relative body link pose, linear/angular velocities, and regularization for action rate, joint limits, and self-collisions.
- **Stability Mechanisms:** Early termination conditions (e.g., pose collapse, end-effector violation) are implemented to prevent exploration of unstable or physically unfeasible states, accelerating stable skill acquisition.

**Stage II: Motion Encoding via Topological Latent Distillation (Section 3.3, Appendix B)**
The second stage compresses the diverse repertoire of physically plausible motions learned in Stage I into a continuous, bounded latent space, which will serve as the strategic action space for higher-level control.
- **Teacher-Student Distillation:** A teacher-student framework is used, where the pre-trained motion tracker (π_trk) acts as the teacher.
- **Components:** An encoder (E_ϕ), a decoder (D_ψ), and a state-conditioned latent prior (P_ξ) are jointly optimized.
 - **Encoder (E_ϕ):** Maps observations (robot state and reference motion) to a distribution of latent codes (z_t), modeled as a diagonal Gaussian.
 - **Decoder (D_ψ):** Trained to reconstruct the teacher's actions (a_t) conditioned on the robot's proprioceptive state and the latent code (z_t).
 - **State-Conditioned Latent Prior (P_ξ):** Models valid latent transitions from the current proprioceptive state and regularizes the encoder via KL-divergence, preventing posterior collapse.
- **Topological Constraint:** Crucially, latent codes are normalized onto a unit hypersphere (S_d-1) (Eq. 8). This topological regularization ensures that the latent space is compact and continuous, restricting motion commands to a physically plausible set and preventing out-of-distribution execution by the decoder. This bounded latent space becomes the effective strategy space.

**Stage III (a): Behavioral Warm-Up with Adversarial Priors (Section 3.4, Appendix C)**
To overcome the instability of direct competitive self-play from scratch, a behavioral warmup stage is introduced.
- **Objective:** Agents learn basic tactical competence by striking a stationary opponent that maintains a stable standing stance.
- **Residual Policy (π_θ):** A learnable residual policy is defined to output residual latent commands (∆z_t) over a fixed behavioral prior from Stage II. The final latent action is z_t = Normalize(∆z_t + z^p_t).
- **Goal-Oriented Observation (s_goal):** The policy receives goal-oriented spatial information, including relative positions of ego agent's fists to opponent's torso (offensive target) and opponent's fists to ego agent's torso (defensive threat).
- **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP):** To ensure human-like motion quality and style-consistency, an AMP discriminator (C) is employed. This discriminator distinguishes between agent-generated motion transitions and reference motion capture data, providing a style-consistency reward (r_style) to regularize policy training.
- **Hybrid Reward:** The learning objective maximizes a hybrid return combining task rewards (r_task, encouraging facing alignment, velocity, distance, and hit) and the AMP-derived style reward (r_style). Reward scheduling phases in task reward as training progresses.

**Stage III (b): Latent-Space Neural Fictitious Self-Play (LS-NFSP) (Section 3.5, Appendix D)**
Building on the warmup, competitive co-evolution is enabled using Latent-Space Neural Fictitious Self-Play.
- **Core Principle:** Adapts Neural Fictitious Self-Play (NFSP) to operate over the structured latent action space (Z).
- **Dual-Policy System:** Each agent maintains:
 - **RL Policy (π_RL_z):** An on-policy actor trained via PPO on an RL dataset (D_RL) to learn an approximate best response to the opponent's *mixed strategy*.
 - **Average Policy (π̄_z):** A supervised learning network trained from a reservoir buffer (D_SL) to imitate the agent's historical best-response behaviors, acting as a stable strategic anchor.
- **Mixed Strategy:** During interaction, agents select actions based on an exploration parameter η, sampling from a mixture of the average policy and the RL policy (σ = η · π̄_z + (1 − η) · π_RL_z).
- **Expanded Reward (r_com):** The reward function is expanded to include competitive terms like strike force reward (based on contact forces), defensive penalties for being hit, and terminal rewards for winning/losing the bout.
- **Theoretical Grounding (Section 3.6, Appendix E):** The authors formally state assumptions (bounded continuous latent space, well-defined/bounded payoffs, approximate best-response updates) and provide a proof sketch that under these conditions, LS-NFSP dynamics in this continuous zero-sum game approach an approximate Nash Equilibrium, ensuring convergence stability.

The entire framework is implemented in Isaac Lab on the NVIDIA Omniverse platform, leveraging large-scale parallel simulation (4,096 environments) and domain randomization for robustness and efficient data collection on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot with 29 degrees of freedom.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RoboStriker demonstrates its superior performance and robustness in autonomous humanoid boxing, validating the hierarchical framework and its components.

**1. Superior Competitive Performance (Cross-Playing Evaluation):**
- **Overall Dominance:** In an 8x8 cross-play tournament (Table 1), LS-NFSP consistently achieved higher win rates against all baselines.
- **Latent Space vs. Raw Action Space:** LS-NFSP achieved a perfect 100.0% win rate against the "29Dof Action-Space SP" agent (which directly controls raw joint targets). This crucial result confirms that decoupling balance maintenance from tactical exploration via a latent manifold is a fundamental prerequisite for competitive learning in high-dimensional humanoid combat.
- **NFSP Advantage:** LS-NFSP significantly outperformed other latent-space self-play methods like "Fictitious SP" (68.52% win rate against Ours) and "Naive SP" (76.24% win rate against Ours), proving that the integration of NFSP's policy averaging effectively mitigates policy cycling and produces more robust best responses.
- **Necessity of Competition:** The substantial win rate against the "PPO-Only" baseline (84.47%) underscores that competitive interaction is indispensable for the emergence of sophisticated combat behaviors.

**2. Enhanced Tactical and Physical Proficiency:**
- **Tactical Metrics (Table 2, 3a):** LS-NFSP demonstrated significantly higher tactical proficiency:
 - **Offensive Landing Rate (η_hit):** 0.685 ± 0.03 (compared to 0.142 for 29Dof Action-Space SP, and 0.420 for Fictitious SP).
 - **Engagement Rate (ER):** 0.824 ± 0.02 (compared to 0.315 for 29Dof Action-Space SP, and 0.650 for Fictitious SP). These metrics confirm LS-NFSP's ability to engage opponents effectively and land powerful strikes.
- **Physical Metrics (Table 2):** LS-NFSP achieved superior physical stability and smoother control:
 - **Base Stability (BOS):** 0.942 ± 0.01 (compared to 0.418 for 29Dof Action-Space SP), indicating excellent upright posture maintenance under competitive perturbations.
 - **Torque Smoothness (TS_τ):** 0.930 ± 0.150 (compared to 7.452 for 29Dof Action-Space SP), signifying more hardware-friendly execution with reduced high-frequency oscillations in motor commands.

**3. Validation of Hierarchical Components (Ablation Studies - Table 3):**
- **Warm-Up Training:** The "SP w/o Warmup" variant collapsed, achieving a negligible η_hit of 0.050. This confirms the critical role of the behavioral warmup stage in overcoming reward sparsity and initial learning instability, providing agents with basic tactical competence before intense competition. The "Static-Target Specialist" (warmup-only policy) also showed significantly lower performance than full LS-NFSP.
- **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP):** The "LS-NFSP w/o AMP" variant showed reduced tactical efficiency (η_hit = 0.490) despite a decent engagement rate. This indicates that without AMP, the agent could approach the opponent but executed erratic and ineffective strikes, lacking physical authenticity (visualized in Figure 4). This highlights AMP's necessity for producing both strategically dominant and physically authentic movements.

**4. Analysis of the Learned Latent Space:**
- **Structured Manifold:** A t-SNE visualization of the 32-dimensional latent manifold (Figure 3) revealed a highly structured topological arrangement.
- **Semantic Clustering:** Clear separation was observed between "pure Move" and "pure Strike" clusters.
- **Compositional Nature:** "Move+Strike" embeddings occupied an intermediate space, acting as a "topological bridge." This demonstrates the latent space's ability to disentangle stable locomotion from offensive maneuvers while allowing for seamless, stable interpolation between them, enabling dynamic attacks while moving without discontinuity or instability.

**5. Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
- The abstract and introduction claim "sim-to-real transfer," and Figure 1 provides real-world clips of humanoid boxing using RoboStriker, showcasing agile, contact-rich punches and defenses under physical constraints. This visual evidence supports the practical applicability of the framework beyond simulation.

In summary, the results overwhelmingly support that RoboStriker's hierarchical design, particularly its bounded latent strategy space and LS-NFSP training, effectively addresses the long-standing challenges of physical feasibility and learning stability in embodied multi-agent reinforcement learning for dynamic humanoid tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboStriker represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, particularly for multi-agent physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Resolution of Fundamental Embodied MARL Challenges:** The work formally characterizes and effectively resolves the intrinsic contradictions that have historically hindered the application of MARL to humanoid robots: the tension between physical feasibility and non-stationary learning, and the conflict between strategy evolution and system stability. By systematically decoupling high-level strategic reasoning from low-level physical execution, RoboStriker provides a blueprint for stable and robust learning in dynamically fragile robotic systems.

2. **A Generalizable Framework for Embodied Competition:** While demonstrated on humanoid boxing, the hierarchical structure, the concept of a topologically constrained latent skill space, and Latent-Space Neural Fictitious Self-Play (LS-NFSP) offer a "general recipe" for embodied multi-agent competition. This framework has the potential to be applied to a wide array of other complex, contact-rich, and dynamic multi-robot tasks, such as wrestling, collaborative manipulation, or competitive sports beyond boxing.

3. **Scalability of MARL to Physical Systems:** RoboStriker provides a principled pathway to scale Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning from abstract game domains, where it has achieved grandmaster-level performance, to physically grounded robotic systems. This bridges a critical gap, demonstrating how sophisticated strategic intelligence can be integrated with the complexities of real-world physics and robot morphology.

4. **Advanced Humanoid Capabilities:** The framework enables humanoid robots to exhibit sophisticated and human-like competitive behaviors. The emergence of tactical boxing maneuvers, such as slip-and-counter tactics, along with high engagement and landing rates, signifies a leap forward in the cognitive and physical capabilities of autonomous humanoids. The robustness and stability metrics (high BOS, low TS_τ) confirm that these complex behaviors are executed reliably and safely.

5. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to physical Unitree G1 humanoid robots is a crucial indicator of the method's practical viability. This demonstrates that policies trained in simulation with RoboStriker's framework are sufficiently robust and generalizable to operate effectively in the real world, reducing the need for extensive real-robot experimentation.

6. **Insights into Latent Skill Representation:** The detailed analysis of the learned latent space, revealing semantic clustering and topological connectivity between different motion primitives (Move, Strike, Move+Strike), provides valuable insights. This compositional nature of the latent space enables smooth, dynamic transitions between behaviors without instability, paving the way for more flexible and adaptable robot control architectures.

7. **Contribution to Robotics and AI Research:** RoboStriker pushes the boundaries of several key research areas:
 - **Humanoid Control:** By enabling complex, reactive, and strategic behaviors in dynamic contact.
 - **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning:** By addressing its inherent challenges in embodied settings and establishing a stable learning paradigm.
 - **Motion Synthesis and Imitation Learning:** By leveraging and extending advanced motion tracking and adversarial prior techniques to competitive scenarios.

In conclusion, RoboStriker signifies a major step towards making autonomous humanoid robots truly intelligent and agile competitors in physically interactive environments. Its innovative hierarchical approach and principled integration of state-of-the-art techniques lay a robust foundation for future research in embodied AI, with potential far-reaching implications for robotics applications ranging from hazardous tasks to human-robot collaboration and entertainment.
