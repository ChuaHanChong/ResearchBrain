# MuM: Multi-View Masked Image Modeling for 3D Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> MuM introduces a self-supervised learning objective for 3D vision, leveraging multi-view masked image modeling to extract geometric visual representations from unlabeled data. The framework outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods like DINOv3 and CroCo v2 on various geometric tasks while requiring approximately 30 times less computational resources for training.

## Problem

- Current self-supervised learning methods primarily learn semantic features, often failing to capture geometric understanding essential for 3D vision tasks.
- Existing geometric self-supervised approaches, such as CroCo, face limitations in data sampling, requiring significant view overlap, and struggle to scale beyond two views.
- Developing computationally efficient methods for learning strong geometric features from unlabeled multi-view data remained a challenge.

## Method

- Introduces a multi-view masked image modeling objective that uniformly masks patches across an arbitrary number of views from the same scene.
- Utilizes a ==Vision Transformer== encoder for per-view feature extraction and a lightweight multi-view decoder with alternating frame-wise and global attention for inter-frame communication.
- Trains by reconstructing original pixel values for masked patches, enabling flexible data sampling without strict co-visibility requirements.

## Results

- MuM surpasses DINOv3 and CroCo v2 in 3D reconstruction tasks, achieving higher AUC@30 on CO3Dv2 (**71.5%** vs. DINOv3 **66.9%**) and lower error in point cloud estimation (e.g., DTU Accuracy 3.7).
- Demonstrates superior performance in dense feature matching, with an EPE of 10.2 pixels and robustness of **94.2%** on MegaDepth-1500, outperforming DINOv3 (EPE 17.1, R **87.2%**).
- Achieves these results with approximately 30 times less training compute and data compared to DINOv3, while maintaining competitive performance on relative pose estimation and surpassing CroCo v2 on monocular tasks like depth and normal estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symmetric masking across all views, combined with a pixel reconstruction objective, provides a robust and scalable method for learning geometric features.
> - Alternating frame-wise and global attention within a lightweight decoder effectively enables inter-frame communication and geometric reasoning without needing a heavy model.
> - Specializing a self-supervised objective for geometric understanding allows for substantial reductions in training compute and data compared to general-purpose semantic SSL models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on MuM is situated at the intersection of self-supervised learning (SSL) and 3D computer vision, aiming to bridge a crucial gap in current visual representation learning.

**Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Evolution:** The paper begins by recognizing SSL's role as a cornerstone of modern visual representation learning, particularly when scaled to large datasets. A dominant paradigm within SSL is **Masked Autoencoding (MAE)**, exemplified by models like MAE. These models learn by reconstructing partially masked images, an objective that produces powerful general-purpose features.

**The Semantic-Geometric Divide:** A central theme is the distinction between *semantic understanding* and *geometric reasoning*. While state-of-the-art SSL models like **DINOv3** (a successor in the DINO family) have achieved remarkable performance in extracting rich semantic features, they are not inherently optimized for geometric tasks. DINOv3's reliance on self-distillation and billions of images, coupled with specific heuristics, makes it computationally demanding and primarily yields semantic-biased features, despite its recent use as a feature encoder in some 3D vision pipelines.

**The Predecessor: CroCo:** An important precursor to MuM is **Cross-View Completion (CroCo)**, and its successor CroCo v2. CroCo specifically tailored the MAE paradigm for 3D understanding by introducing a *cross-view* objective: reconstructing a heavily masked view while conditioning on an unmasked reference view from the same scene. This approach attempts to imbue geometric understanding by enforcing consistency across views. However, CroCo suffers from significant limitations:
1. **Fragile Data Sampling:** It relies on substantial overlap between view *pairs*, making data sampling challenging and often requiring ground-truth geometry for tasks like co-visibility segmentation.
2. **Limited Scalability:** The two-view setup with one unmasked reference view does not easily generalize to an arbitrary number of views.

**MuM's Contribution to the Landscape:** MuM directly addresses these limitations by proposing a more flexible and scalable multi-view MAE objective. It extends MAE to an *arbitrary number of views* from the same scene, *uniformly masking all views*, rather than relying on a single unmasked reference. This symmetric approach, combined with inter-frame attention in a lightweight decoder, simplifies data sampling and is inherently more robust to viewpoint variations.

**Integration with Multi-view Geometry:** The paper also contextualizes MuM within the broader effort to replace traditional, hand-crafted multi-view geometry methods with learning-based approaches. It references recent advances in neural network architectures for tasks like image matching, pose estimation, and especially transformer architectures like VGGT and MapAnything that jointly process arbitrarily many frames for full scene attribute prediction. MuM aims to provide the foundational geometric features necessary for such advanced multi-view pipelines. By offering a computationally efficient way to learn geometric features, MuM provides a compelling alternative to computationally heavy semantic SSL models for 3D vision tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of MuM are clearly articulated, stemming from the identified challenges and gaps in the existing self-supervised learning landscape for 3D vision:

1. **Learn Features Tailored for 3D Vision:** The primary objective is to develop a self-supervised learning strategy that specifically learns *geometric features* conducive to 3D vision tasks. This contrasts with many prevailing SSL methods like DINOv3, which excel at semantic understanding but often fall short in geometric reasoning.

2. **Overcome Limitations of CroCo:** MuM is motivated to improve upon CroCo's approach to cross-view completion. Specifically, it seeks to:
 - **Enable Flexible Data Sampling:** Address CroCo's "fragile data sampling" issue, which arises from its reliance on substantial overlap between view pairs. MuM aims for a system robust to varying co-visibility and viewpoint changes, without requiring access to ground-truth geometry for complex objectives.
 - **Generalize to Arbitrary Views:** Extend the cross-view concept beyond just two views, making it applicable to an arbitrary number of images from a scene in a scalable manner.

3. **Achieve Computational Efficiency:** A significant motivation is to develop a method that provides strong geometric performance while being substantially more computationally efficient than state-of-the-art semantic encoders like DINOv3. The authors aim to make high-quality geometric feature learning more accessible, requiring less training compute and data.

4. **Simplify the Self-Supervised Learning Objective:** The paper emphasizes a desire for a "simple SSL objective." This simplicity translates into a more straightforward architectural design and training framework compared to the complex student-teacher setups and specific heuristics (like Sinkhorn-Knopp centering) found in some other SSL methods.

**Summary of Main Contributions:** The paper explicitly lists its main contributions as:
- Extending masked image modeling to an arbitrary number of views within a simple, scalable training framework specifically tailored for 3D vision.
- Conducting a comprehensive comparison of various models through dense feature matching using a linear probe.
- Validating MuM's effectiveness as a feature extractor on key 3D vision tasks, including feedforward reconstruction, feature matching, and relative pose estimation, where it outperforms DINOv3 and CroCo v2.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MuM's methodology builds upon the masked autoencoding (MAE) paradigm but innovates by extending it to a multi-view context with a symmetric and scalable design.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition and Learning Objective

The core idea is to apply masked image modeling to a sequence of `n` images, `I = (I_1..., I_n)`, from the same 3D scene.
- **Uniform Masking:** Unlike CroCo, which uses an unmasked reference view, MuM uniformly masks a subset of patches in *all* input images `I_i` with a fixed ratio of `γ_i = 0.75`. This creates a partially masked sequence `˜I = (˜I_1..., ˜I_n)`.
- **Pixel Reconstruction Target:** The network `ϕ_θ` is trained to predict the original (unmasked) pixel values for the masked patches. The target representation `f(I_i)` simply normalizes the pixel values within a patch by its mean and standard deviation.
- **Loss Function:** The loss `L(θ)` is the sum of squared differences between the predicted and ground-truth normalized pixel values, applied only to the masked patches across all views: `L(θ) = ∑_{i=1}^{n} ||M_i * (ϕ_θ(˜I_i) - f(I_i))||^2`.
- **Flexibility and Robustness:** This uniform masking and pixel reconstruction objective allows for simple and flexible data sampling. It gracefully degrades to the standard MAE objective if only a single view is available, and, crucially, does not require strict co-visibility or a designated reference frame, unlike CroCo.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture

MuM employs an encoder-decoder Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, similar to MAE and CroCo, but with a critical modification for multi-view processing.
- **Encoder:** A ViT-L (Vision Transformer-Large) backbone (width 1024, depth 24, 16 heads) processes each input image independently after patchification and masking. It extracts features only from the visible patches.
- **Mask Tokens:** After the encoder, learnable mask tokens are appended to represent the positions of the originally masked patches.
- **Multi-View Decoder:** The key innovation lies here. A lightweight ViT-B (Vision Transformer-Base) decoder (width 768, depth 12, 12 heads) jointly processes the representations from all views.
 - **Alternating Attention Blocks:** The decoder consists of `L=6` alternating attention blocks:
 1. **Frame-wise Attention:** Attention is restricted *within* each individual view, allowing for local feature refinement.
 2. **Global Attention:** Attention is allowed *across all views*, enabling inter-frame communication and geometric reasoning by connecting corresponding patches.
 - **Symmetric Design:** This alternating attention mechanism is applied symmetrically across all frames, avoiding the need to designate any single "reference frame," which simplifies the architecture and improves robustness.
- **Prediction Head:** A final linear prediction head maps the decoder's token embeddings back to the pixel space, regressing the normalized RGB values for each patch.
- **Modern ViT Choices:** The architecture incorporates modern ViT enhancements like axial RoPE (Rotary Position Embeddings).

#### 3.3. Training Procedure

The training of MuM is designed for large-scale multi-view data and computational efficiency.
- **Duration:** MuM is trained for 500,000 steps, which takes approximately three days on 64 A100 GPUs. This is significantly less compute (roughly 30x less) than DINOv3.
- **Optimizer and Scheduler:** AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate scheduler and a linear warmup of 25,000 steps. A base learning rate of `1 × 10^-4` is used, scaling up to `2.4 × 10^-3` for a global batch size of 6144.
- **Dynamic Sequence Length:** For each batch, the number of views per scene (`n`) is randomly sampled uniformly between 2 and 24.
- **Batch Sampling:** The system samples as many scenes as possible per batch without exceeding 96 total frames per GPU.
- **Image Preprocessing:** Images are resized to 256x256, and random horizontal mirroring is applied per frame.
- **Training Data:** A diverse collection of 3D datasets is used, totaling approximately 20 million individual frames. This mix includes outdoor (3DStreetView, MegaScenes, MegaDepth), indoor (ARKitScenes, RealEstate10K, ScanNet++, Hypersim), and object-centric (WildRGBD, CO3D, DL3DV10K, BlendedMVS) datasets, comprising both real and synthetic data. Critically, a 10% probability is included to sample from ImageNet-1K, enabling the model to also learn from single-view data and semantic contexts. The sampling scheme is robust, not strictly requiring co-visibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MuM is evaluated extensively on a wide range of downstream tasks, comparing its performance primarily against DINOv3 (ViT-L) and CroCo v2 (ViT-L), and also MAE for some tasks. The results consistently highlight MuM's superior geometric understanding and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Summary

- **Geometric Tasks (n ≥ 2 views):** MuM consistently outperforms both DINOv3 and CroCo v2 across multi-view and two-view 3D tasks. This is a significant finding given that MuM is trained with substantially less compute and data than DINOv3.
- **Monocular/Semantic Tasks (n = 1 view):** On tasks like image classification and semantic segmentation, which favor stronger semantic inductive biases, MuM performs below DINOv3. However, it still generally outperforms CroCo v2, indicating a reasonable balance despite its geometric focus.

#### 4.2. Multi-view Tasks (n > 2): Feedforward 3D Reconstruction

- **Frozen Encoder Setting:** MuM features, when used as a frozen encoder for downstream multi-view ViT architectures (inspired by VGGT), demonstrate strong 3D understanding.
 - **Camera Pose Estimation:** MuM achieves higher AUC@30 (better) on CO3Dv2 (71.5% vs. DINOv3 66.9%, CroCo v2 58.2%), Re10K (50.8% vs. DINOv3 36.7%, CroCo v2 27.7%), and MegaDepth (73.0% vs. DINOv3 59.3%, CroCo v2 60.7%).
 - **Point Cloud Estimation:** MuM shows lower (better) median accuracy and completeness on DTU (Accuracy 3.7, Completeness 1.6) and ETH3D (Accuracy 0.8, Completeness 0.8), outperforming DINOv3 and CroCo v2.
- **Distillation Finetuning:** When the full MuM architecture is finetuned by distilling a strong teacher model like VGGT, it significantly improves over random initialization across camera pose and point cloud estimation, showcasing its adaptability and strong pre-training base.

#### 4.3. Two-view Tasks (n = 2): Matching

- **Dense Feature Matching (Linear Probe):** A linear probe on frozen MuM features yields the best performance for dense image matching.
 - **MegaDepth-1500:** MuM achieves the lowest EPE (10.2 pixels) and highest robustness (94.2%), significantly outperforming DINOv3 (EPE 17.1, R 87.2%) and CroCo v2 (EPE 27.3, R 75.7%).
 - **ScanNet-1500:** MuM (EPE 27.9, R 78.9%) also surpasses DINOv3 (EPE 27.3, R 77.2%) and CroCo v2 (EPE 39.0, R 66.6%).
- **RoMa Integration:** When MuM replaces the frozen encoder in the RoMa dense matcher pipeline, it consistently improves matching performance.
 - **MegaDepth-1500:** MuM yields lower 100-PCK scores (better) at all thresholds (e.g., 12.5 at 1px, 4.0 at 3px, 2.6 at 5px) compared to DINOv3 and CroCo v2.
 - **ScanNet-1500:** Similar improvements are observed on ScanNet-1500.

#### 4.4. Two-view Tasks (n = 2): Relative Pose Estimation

- Through finetuning, MuM performs comparably or better than CroCo v2 and significantly outperforms DINOv3 on relative pose estimation across MegaDepth, Re10K, and BlendedMVS datasets (higher AUC@ is better). For instance, on MegaDepth, MuM achieves 47.0% AUC@10° compared to DINOv3's 32.5% and CroCo v2's 30.0%.

#### 4.5. Single-view Tasks (n = 1)

- **Monocular Depth Estimation:** MuM (RMSE 0.41 on NYUd, 3.89 on KITTI) performs better than CroCo v2 and MAE but trails DINOv3 (RMSE 0.34 on NYUd, 2.63 on KITTI).
- **Surface Normal Estimation:** Similar to depth, MuM outperforms CroCo v2 and MAE but is surpassed by DINOv3 in recall and RMSE metrics.
- **Semantic Recognition (Image Classification, Semantic Segmentation):** DINOv3 leads significantly due to its explicit semantic objective. However, MuM (mIoU 30.2% on ADE20K with logistic regression, 26.0% with k-NN) still outperforms CroCo v2 and MAE in semantic segmentation, though it slightly lags MAE in ImageNet-1K classification accuracy. This confirms MuM's stronger geometric bias.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **SSL Objective:** A multi-view reformulation of MAE significantly improves matching performance (EPE reduced from 18.7 to 12.5) with computational efficiency (3x faster than DINOv2). Applying multi-view to DINOv2 yielded only minor gains.
- **Architectural Choices:**
 - Longer sequence lengths (up to 24 frames) improve matching.
 - The 75% masking ratio (from MAE) is optimal.
 - An *unmasked reference view* (a la CroCo) *degrades* performance compared to MuM's symmetric masking.
 - Inter-frame communication (global attention) is crucial and most effective when placed in the decoder.
 - Axial RoPE positional embeddings are superior.
 - A normalized pixel reconstruction objective is highly effective.
- **Feature Layers:** Deeper layers in the ViT-L encoder provide progressively better representations for dense matching, with the final layer yielding the best results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MuM represents a significant step forward in self-supervised learning for 3D computer vision. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Geometric Self-Supervised Learning:** MuM successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of learning *robust geometric representations* from unlabeled data. While semantic understanding has seen rapid advancements through models like DINOv3, geometric reasoning, crucial for 3D tasks, often lagged. MuM demonstrates that a simplified multi-view masked autoencoding objective can effectively bridge this gap.

2. **Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** One of the most impactful aspects of MuM is its efficiency. By achieving state-of-the-art geometric performance with approximately 30 times less training compute and data than DINOv3, MuM makes high-quality geometric feature learning more accessible to a wider academic and industrial community. This democratizes the development of advanced 3D vision systems, as fewer resources are needed for pre-training.

3. **Simplicity and Scalability:** The symmetric, arbitrary multi-view masking objective, combined with a lightweight decoder featuring alternating frame-wise and global attention, offers a clean and scalable solution. This design simplifies data sampling (no need for fragile co-visibility heuristics or designated reference frames) and enhances robustness to diverse scene conditions and viewpoint variations. It lays a foundation for processing very long sequences of images from a scene.

4. **Boost to 3D Vision Pipelines:** MuM provides a powerful, ready-to-use feature encoder that can be seamlessly integrated into existing and future 3D vision pipelines. Its demonstrated superiority across fundamental 3D tasks like feedforward reconstruction, dense image matching, and relative pose estimation suggests it will become a go-to backbone for various applications, from robotic navigation and augmented reality to large-scale 3D mapping and content creation. Its integration into pipelines like RoMa and VGGT-inspired architectures directly showcases this potential.

5. **Inspiring Future Research:** The paper explicitly outlines limitations and interesting future directions.
 - **Hybrid Models:** Investigating the combination of MuM's strong geometric understanding with the semantic power of DINO-like self-distillation objectives could lead to even more comprehensive and versatile visual representations.
 - **Scaling Pre-training:** Further scaling pre-training, despite current computational limits, is a natural next step to push performance boundaries.
 - **Zero-Shot Matching:** The qualitative analysis of attention maps hinting at direct correspondence information opens avenues for developing zero-shot matching techniques directly from MuM's internal representations, similar to ZeroCo.
 - **New Architectures:** The success of alternating attention in the decoder could inspire new multi-view transformer architectures for other 3D processing tasks.

In conclusion, MuM represents a practical and highly effective solution for learning geometric features in a self-supervised manner. Its efficiency, scalability, and strong empirical performance position it as a significant contribution that will likely influence the development of future 3D computer vision systems and research in geometric representation learning.
