# MuM: Multi-View Masked Image Modeling for 3D Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> MuM introduces a self-supervised learning objective for 3D vision, leveraging multi-view masked image modeling to extract geometric visual representations from unlabeled data. The framework outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods like DINOv3 and CroCo v2 on various geometric tasks while requiring approximately 30 times less computational resources for training.

## Problem

- Current self-supervised learning methods primarily learn semantic features, often failing to capture geometric understanding essential for 3D vision tasks.
- Existing geometric self-supervised approaches, such as CroCo, face limitations in data sampling, requiring significant view overlap, and struggle to scale beyond two views.
- Developing computationally efficient methods for learning strong geometric features from unlabeled multi-view data remained a challenge.

## Method

- Introduces a multi-view masked image modeling objective that uniformly masks patches across an arbitrary number of views from the same scene.
- Utilizes a ==Vision Transformer== encoder for per-view feature extraction and a lightweight multi-view decoder with alternating frame-wise and global attention for inter-frame communication.
- Trains by reconstructing original pixel values for masked patches, enabling flexible data sampling without strict co-visibility requirements.

## Results

- MuM surpasses DINOv3 and CroCo v2 in 3D reconstruction tasks, achieving higher AUC@30 on CO3Dv2 (**71.5%** vs. DINOv3 **66.9%**) and lower error in point cloud estimation (e.g., DTU Accuracy 3.7).
- Demonstrates superior performance in dense feature matching, with an EPE of 10.2 pixels and robustness of **94.2%** on MegaDepth-1500, outperforming DINOv3 (EPE 17.1, R **87.2%**).
- Achieves these results with approximately 30 times less training compute and data compared to DINOv3, while maintaining competitive performance on relative pose estimation and surpassing CroCo v2 on monocular tasks like depth and normal estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symmetric masking across all views, combined with a pixel reconstruction objective, provides a robust and scalable method for learning geometric features.
> - Alternating frame-wise and global attention within a lightweight decoder effectively enables inter-frame communication and geometric reasoning without needing a heavy model.
> - Specializing a self-supervised objective for geometric understanding allows for substantial reductions in training compute and data compared to general-purpose semantic SSL models.
