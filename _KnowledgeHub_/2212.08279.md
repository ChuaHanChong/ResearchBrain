# Werewolf Among Us: A Multimodal Dataset for Modeling Persuasion Behaviors in Social Deduction Games

> [!summary] Summary
> Werewolf Among Us is the first multimodal persuasion-modeling dataset: 199 transcribed videos of 4-6 player One Night Ultimate Werewolf and Avalon games from Ego4D and YouTube, with 26,647 utterance-level labels over six persuasion strategies plus per-player voting outcomes. Benchmark models fuse a text encoder, five-utterance context, and a frozen video transformer, raising average F1 and predicting votes above chance.

## Problem

- Prior persuasion corpora are text-only, drawn from online forums or scripted crowdworker chats, so non-verbal cues like gesture, gaze and pointing are entirely absent.
- Existing resources cover one-on-one or asynchronous exchanges, never naturalistic real-time group discussion where several speakers compete to reshape each other's beliefs.
- Deduction-game research models game state or agent play alone, leaving no resource linking utterance-level persuasion strategies to the resulting social decision outcomes.

## Method

- A six-tactic annotation scheme - Identity Declaration, Accusation, Interrogation, Call for Action, Defense, Evidence - labeled per utterance as independent binary tasks, reaching Krippendorff alpha above 0.6 per class.
- Strategy predictors built on ==BERT==, ==RoBERTa== and multi-task ==MT-BERT== encoders, extended with a context variant concatenating k prior utterances and a video variant fusing frozen ==MViT-B-24== Kinetics features through a Tanh fusion layer.
- A late-fusion model linearly blending context and video probability outputs, plus a logistic-regression deduction model over a 26-dim vector of voter/candidate strategy distributions and voter starting role.

## Results

- Combining context and video gives the best average F1 on both sources: **59.4** versus **56.2** for BERT on Ego4D, and **71.3** versus **70.4** for RoBERTa on YouTube.
- Video features alone add about **0.8** points to BERT on both sources, with the largest gains on Accusation, Interrogation and Call for Action; context length five is the empirical optimum.
- GPT-3-175B five-shot reaches only **49.6** average F1 on YouTube, far below fine-tuned models; vote deduction gets **32.7** F1 and **54.7** AUC against **28.6** and **50.0** for random.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-verbal signal helps most for strategies with visible correlates - accusing, questioning, rallying - so modality gains concentrate in specific label classes rather than spreading uniformly.
> - Text transfers across data sources far better than video: YouTube-trained text models beat Ego4D-only models, while visual features fail to generalize across domains.
> - Large web video collections can pre-train social-behavior models cheaply, but off-the-shelf prompted LLMs remain well short of supervised fine-tuning on nuanced interaction labels.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper contributes to the fields of natural language processing, computer vision, and human-computer interaction, specifically focusing on the computational modeling of persuasion.

Prior research in persuasion modeling has predominantly centered on textual dialogue corpora. These efforts have involved analyzing argument persuasiveness and identifying persuasion strategies in online forums such as Reddit and crowdfunding platforms (Yang et al., 2019; Chen and Yang, 2021; Atkinson et al., 2019), as well as in simulated 1-on-1 dialogues conducted via platforms like Amazon Mechanical Turk (Wang et al., 2019; Chawla et al., 2021). These existing approaches are characterized by their reliance on text-based data, often lacking real-time, multi-speaker conversational dynamics.

The present work identifies several gaps in this landscape. First, there is a recognized limitation in the analysis of persuasive behaviors during naturalistic group discussions, where real-time interaction and complex conversational structures are prevalent. Second, existing models largely overlook the multimodal nature of human social interaction. While verbal communication (language and audio) is typically considered, non-verbal cues such as gestures, gaze behavior, and other visual signals, which are integral to understanding persuasion, have received less attention in this context. Third, there is a scarcity of resources that explicitly connect persuasion strategies to their downstream effects, particularly how these strategies influence deduction outcomes in social interactive settings.

In related areas, multimodal social interaction research has addressed topics like multimodal sentiment analysis (Kaur and Kautish, 2022) and identifying conversational partners in egocentric video (Grauman et al., 2022; Bara et al., 2021). However, these studies generally do not focus on the higher-level social behaviors associated with persuasion within complex group dynamics. Computational modeling of social deduction games has primarily focused on game theory, AI agent development, and predicting game states (Nakamura et al., 2016; Serrino et al., 2019; Chuchro, 2022; Braverman et al., 2008; Bi and Tanaka, 2016). While some work has explored predicting game outcomes from player behaviors like speaking and interrupting (Chittaranjan and Hung, 2010), and recent advancements have shown human-level performance in games like Diplomacy using language models (Bakhtin et al.), these efforts typically do not integrate a multimodal understanding of persuasive dialogue in group settings.

This paper positions itself as the first to introduce a multimodal dataset for persuasion modeling, specifically captured in naturalistic, multi-player social deduction games. It aims to bridge the identified gaps by incorporating both verbal and non-verbal communication cues in real-time group interactions and by exploring the relationship between these cues, persuasion strategies, and game outcomes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the computational understanding of human persuasion behaviors, which is considered a foundational element for developing sophisticated conversational agents. The broader motivation stems from the recognition that an automatic system capable of understanding persuasion strategies and inferring human belief states could enhance human-computer interaction and facilitate collaborative decision-making processes.

More specifically, the paper is motivated by and aims to address several limitations observed in the existing research landscape:

1. **Limited Scope of Persuasion Modeling Modalities:** Current persuasion modeling efforts are largely confined to analyzing textual dialogue. The authors argue that this approach is insufficient because visual signals, which encompass non-verbal communication cues such as gestures and gaze, are critical for a comprehensive understanding of human persuasive behaviors in real-world interactions. A key objective is therefore to introduce multimodal data that includes visual components.

2. **Lack of Naturalistic Group Discussion Data:** Prior datasets for persuasion modeling primarily involve online text platforms or simulated 1-on-1 dialogues. These settings do not fully capture the complexity, intensity, and real-time nature of persuasive behaviors that occur during naturalistic group discussions with multiple participants. The motivation is to capture these behaviors in a setting that more closely mirrors daily social interactions.

3. **Absence of Link between Persuasion Strategies and Deduction Outcomes:** Existing language technologies often lack resources that demonstrate how the application of specific persuasion strategies impacts the outcomes of social interactions, particularly in contexts requiring deduction. A core objective is to investigate this relationship, thereby contributing to models that can predict the effects of persuasive communication.

To address these motivations, the paper introduces a novel multimodal benchmark dataset specifically designed for modeling persuasive behaviors in multi-player social deduction games. The specific objectives for evaluating this dataset and the proposed models include:

- **Demonstrating the Efficacy of Multimodal Cues and Context:** To conduct extensive experiments illustrating how both dialogue context (prior utterances) and visual signals (video data) contribute to improving the accuracy of persuasion strategy prediction.
- **Assessing Model Generalization Capabilities:** To explore the ability of computational models, particularly language models, to generalize in persuasion modeling tasks across different data sources (e.g., professionally collected vs. publicly available videos) and across different types of social deduction games (e.g., Werewolf vs. Avalon), which possess distinct rules and strategic requirements.
- **Investigating the Impact of Persuasion Strategies on Game Outcomes:** To analyze the role that identified persuasion strategies play in predicting the deduction game outcomes, specifically whether a player votes for another player. This objective aims to provide insights into the human deduction process within these social games.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to provide a foundational dataset and initial benchmarks that can propel the development of more sophisticated and context-aware conversational AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a multimodal dataset and employs several computational approaches to model persuasion behaviors and predict game outcomes.

### 4. Data Collection

The dataset, named "Werewolf Among Us," comprises 199 dialogue transcriptions and corresponding video recordings from multi-player social deduction games. Data was sourced from two distinct origins to evaluate model generalization:
- **Ego4D Dataset:** A subset of the Ego4D Social dataset (Grauman et al., 2022) provided 7.3 hours of third-person video, capturing 40 games of "One Night Ultimate Werewolf" and 8 games of "The Resistance: Avalon." Third-person videos were selected to ensure all participants were visible.
- **YouTube Videos:** Approximately 14.8 hours of video were retrieved from YouTube by searching for "one night ultimate werewolf" and "ultimate werewolf" keywords. A total of 151 completed game clips were selected, maintaining the same game setup as the Ego4D data and having visible game outcomes.

The collection process received Institutional Review Board approval.

### 5. Data Annotation

The collected data underwent a multi-stage annotation process:
- **Video Annotation:** Annotators marked the start and end times of each game, identified the starting and ending roles for each player, and recorded the voting outcome of each participant.
- **Transcription:** Automatic transcription was initially performed using rev.com. Annotators then manually reviewed and corrected the alignment of videos and transcripts, as well as errors in speaker names and text.
- **Persuasion Strategy Annotation:** Utterance-level persuasion strategies were annotated based on six predefined categories, inspired by prior psychology and NLP studies:
 - **Identity Declaration:** Stating one's role or identity.
 - **Accusation:** Claiming another player has a specific identity or behavior.
 - **Interrogation:** Asking questions about identity or behavior.
 - **Call for Action:** Encouraging players to perform a game action.
 - **Defense:** Defending oneself or another player against accusations or justifying arguments.
 - **Evidence:** Providing game-related facts or information.
 A "No Strategy" label was used for utterances not fitting these categories. Annotators were trained using a subset of dialogues, achieving a Krippendorff's alpha greater than 0.6 for each category, indicating substantial inter-annotator agreement. The task was formulated as multi-label, meaning an utterance could be assigned multiple strategies. The dataset includes 5,815 utterances from Ego4D and 20,832 from YouTube, with varying distributions of "No Strategy" labels (49.2% for Ego4D, 37.9% for YouTube) and an imbalanced distribution across specific strategies (e.g., "Accusation," "Interrogation," "Defense" being most frequent).

### 6. Persuasion Strategy Prediction

The task was framed as a binary classification problem for each of the six persuasion strategies, with non-strategic utterances receiving negative labels across all categories.
- **Models:** Experiments utilized pre-trained language models, BERT (Devlin et al., 2018) and RoBERTa (Liu et al., 2019), as text encoders, and a pre-trained Vision Transformer, MViT-B-24 (Fan et al., 2021), as a fixed video encoder. A multi-task BERT (MT-BERT) model (Chawla et al., 2021) served as an additional baseline.
- **Approaches:**
 - **Base:** Uses only the current utterance text.
 - **Base + C (Context):** Incorporates *k* previous utterances concatenated with the current utterance. An ablation study determined *k=5* as optimal.
 - **Base + V (Video):** Fuses text representation (from current utterance) with visual representation (extracted from a corresponding video segment) by concatenation, followed by a linear fusion layer and a classifier. Video segments are localized by utterance timestamps, with a minimum 2-second window, and 32 frames are sampled and processed with three square crops per frame (left, center, right).
 - **Base + C + V (Context + Video):** A late fusion approach combining the probability predictions of the Base+C and Base+V models.
- **Training:** All models were trained for 10 epochs using cross-entropy loss and the AdamW optimizer, with hyper-parameters selected via grid search. Experiments were run with three random seeds, and average scores with standard deviations were reported.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** F1 score for each category, average F1 score across all categories, and Joint Accuracy (where all categories must be predicted correctly).
- **GPT-3 Inference:** The `text-davinci-002` engine was tested in zero-shot, one-shot, and five-shot settings using predefined prompt templates.
- **Generalization Experiments:**
 - **Data Domain Generalization:** Models trained on YouTube data were tested on Ego4D Werewolf data, both without and with fine-tuning on Ego4D training data.
 - **Game Domain Generalization:** Models trained on Ego4D Werewolf data were tested on Ego4D Avalon data, to assess performance across games with differing rules and strategy distributions.

### 7. Game Outcome Deduction

This task aimed to predict whether a specific player (voter) would vote for another player (candidate).
- **Data:** A merged dataset from Ego4D Werewolf and YouTube was used, split into train/val/test sets (2741/427/827 samples). Due to single-vote per player, the data was imbalanced (20.4% positive samples).
- **Input Features:** A 26x1 vector was constructed, combining a 7x1 vector representing the voter's persuasion strategy distribution, a 7x1 vector for the candidate's strategy distribution, and a 12x1 one-hot vector indicating the voter's starting role.
- **Model:** A logistic regression model was employed, trained with weighted binary classification loss to address class imbalance.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** F1 score and Area Under the Curve (AUC).

### 8. Main Findings and Results

Results are reported separately for the two predictive tasks: persuasion strategy prediction and game outcome deduction.

#### 8.1. Persuasion Strategy Prediction

- **Impact of Context:** Incorporating textual context consistently improved persuasion strategy prediction performance across all baseline models. An optimal context length of 5 previous utterances was empirically determined. Longer context lengths could sometimes reduce performance for strategies reliably predicted from the current utterance.
- **Impact of Visual Signals:** Video features generally enhanced performance. For the BERT model, an average F1 increase of 0.8% was observed on both Ego4D and YouTube datasets. RoBERTa showed a smaller gain of 0.2% on the YouTube dataset. The inclusion of video features particularly benefited the prediction of "Accusation," "Interrogation," and "Call for Action," suggesting a correlation with non-verbal cues such as pointing or gesturing. The `BERT + C + V` and `RoBERTa + C + V` models, combining context and video, achieved the highest overall average F1 scores.
- **GPT-3 Inference:** GPT-3 achieved a non-trivial Joint Accuracy (e.g., 52.0% on YouTube data zero-shot) compared to a majority baseline (38.8%). Performance improved with additional examples in one-shot and five-shot settings but remained inferior to fine-tuned models.
- **Data Domain Generalization:** Models trained on the larger YouTube dataset performed better on the Ego4D Werewolf test set than models trained solely on Ego4D, even without fine-tuning. Further fine-tuning on Ego4D data led to additional performance gains. This indicated that the text modality exhibited a relatively small domain gap between the two data sources, suggesting the utility of leveraging large online video collections for pre-training. However, incorporating video representation did not consistently improve generalization, and sometimes decreased performance, indicating a larger domain gap in the visual modality (e.g., due to differences in camera angles, lighting).
- **Game Domain Generalization:** Models trained on Ego4D Werewolf data demonstrated "decent performance" when applied to Ego4D Avalon data, despite significant differences in game rules and strategy distributions. The inclusion of additional context offered marginal improvements and occasionally compromised performance for certain models in this cross-game setting.

#### 8.2. Game Outcome Deduction

- The logistic regression model for predicting voting outcomes achieved an F1 score of 32.7% and an AUC of 54.7%, outperforming random prediction (F1 28.6%, AUC 50.0%).
- A baseline model utilizing only persuasion strategy embeddings achieved an F1 of 32.2% and an AUC of 54.6%, indicating that persuasion strategy information was more influential than player role information for predicting voting intent.
- Analysis of logistic regression weights revealed that for predicting a positive vote (voter votes for candidate), the candidate's behaviors (strategies) had a greater influence than the voter's. For predicting a negative vote (voter does not vote for candidate), the "Evidence" strategy from the candidate was identified as particularly important for clearing suspicion, aligning with the intuition that players trust those providing more information.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the fields of social computing and AI:

**Novel Multimodal Dataset:** The introduction of "Werewolf Among Us" represents the first multimodal dataset specifically designed for persuasion modeling in naturalistic, multi-player social deduction games. This addresses a recognized gap in existing resources, which have largely focused on textual, 1-on-1, or simulated interactions. The dataset's inclusion of dialogue transcriptions, video recordings, utterance-level persuasion strategy annotations, and game outcome annotations provides a comprehensive resource for future research.

**Advancement in Persuasion Modeling:** The empirical findings demonstrate the utility of integrating both conversational context and visual signals for predicting persuasion strategies. The observed performance improvements, particularly from video features for specific strategy types, suggest that multimodal approaches can lead to a more nuanced understanding of human persuasive behaviors than text-only models.

**Insights into Social Dynamics:** By analyzing the relationship between persuasion strategies and game outcomes, the research provides computational insights into the human deduction process in social interactions. The finding that a candidate's behaviors, especially providing "Evidence," significantly influence voting decisions offers empirical support for common psychological theories of persuasion in group settings.

**Facilitation of Future Research:** The public release of the dataset, code, and models establishes a new benchmark for developing advanced computational models of persuasion. This resource can encourage further exploration into multimodal representation learning, joint learning across modalities, and the integration of audio cues, which were identified as areas for future work.

**Potential for Applied Systems:** The ability to automatically understand human persuasion strategies and their impact has implications for developing more sophisticated conversational agents. Such agents could engage in more proactive, context-aware, and effective human-computer interaction, supporting collaborative decision-making in various domains, from virtual assistants to educational tools and professional negotiation systems.

**Ethical Considerations:** The authors acknowledge the dual-use potential of persuasion modeling, highlighting that persuasion skills can be applied for both constructive (e.g., pitching ideas, advocacy) and detrimental (e.g., deception, manipulation) purposes. To mitigate risks of misuse, the study underwent institutional review board approval, ensured participant consent, de-identified personally identifiable information, and implemented a mandatory online agreement for dataset users, prohibiting malicious applications. This proactive approach to ethical guidelines is a noteworthy aspect of the work.
