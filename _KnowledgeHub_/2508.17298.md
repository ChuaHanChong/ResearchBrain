# Explain Before You Answer: A Survey on Compositional Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey charts the recent trajectory of Compositional Visual Reasoning (CVR) from 2023 to 2025, introducing a five-stage taxonomy to explain its evolution and distinct advantages over monolithic approaches. The work systematically reviews over 260 papers, identifying key benefits such as enhanced interpretability and robustness, while also outlining persistent open challenges and future research directions for the field.

## Problem

- Traditional monolithic visual reasoning models struggle to replicate human-like intelligence, often relying on superficial correlations and lacking explicit mechanisms for decomposition and relational understanding.
- These models face limitations such as reliance on dataset biases, diminishing returns with increased reasoning complexity, and poor generalization to novel compositions.
- Existing literature lacked a dedicated and comprehensive synthesis of the rapidly expanding compositional visual reasoning paradigm, particularly recent advancements from 2023-2025.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic review of over 260 papers from leading AI venues between 2023 and 2025, focusing on methods emphasizing explicit intermediate "thought" steps and structured decomposition.
- The paper introduces a ==unified five-stage taxonomy==, tracing CVR's evolution from prompt-enhanced language-centric methods to ==unified agentic Vision-Language Models (VLMs)==.
- It catalogues over ==60 benchmarks== and evaluation metrics and identifies persistent open challenges with corresponding future research directions for the field.

## Results

- The survey formally defines CVR, robustly articulates its benefits, and presents a five-stage evolutionary roadmap for the field's architectural and methodological progression.
- It catalogs a comprehensive list of over **60** benchmarks and their corresponding evaluation metrics designed to probe various dimensions of compositional visual reasoning.
- The paper identifies **six** core challenges (e.g., LLM reasoning limitations, hallucinations, data scalability, benchmark limitations) and proposes actionable future research directions to advance CVR capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compositional Visual Reasoning (CVR) offers advantages over monolithic approaches, including better cognitive alignment with human reasoning, enhanced interpretability, improved generalization, and reduced data requirements.
> - The field has evolved through distinct architectural stages, progressing from LLM-centric approaches with external vision tools to unified VLM architectures that integrate perception and inference for explicit intermediate thought processes.
> - Current benchmarks and evaluation protocols are often insufficient, lacking explicit assessment of intermediate reasoning steps, quantified difficulty levels, and comprehensive diagnostic insights.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Compositional Visual Reasoning (CVR) has rapidly emerged as a critical frontier in multimodal AI, aiming to bridge the gap between machine perception and human-like cognitive abilities. This survey positions itself as a crucial and timely contribution by addressing a significant void in the existing literature. While numerous surveys have covered broader topics such as monolithic vision-language models, general multimodal reasoning, or specific aspects like visual question answering (VQA) robustness and neurosymbolic frameworks, none have provided a dedicated and comprehensive synthesis of the *rapidly expanding* compositional visual reasoning paradigm, particularly the advancements made from 2023 to 2025.

The authors explicitly highlight that earlier surveys often focus on monolithic, end-to-end approaches that directly map visual and textual inputs to answers without explicit intermediate reasoning steps. These methods, while achieving impressive results in general multimodal understanding, struggle with the inherently compositional and multi-step nature of human visual reasoning, exhibiting limitations such as reliance on dataset biases, diminishing returns with increased reasoning complexity, and incompatibility with human-like cognitive processes.

This paper fills this critical gap by systematically reviewing over 260 papers from leading AI venues, focusing specifically on methods that emphasize explicit intermediate "thought" steps and structured decomposition. It provides a unique historical roadmap of the field, tracing the paradigm shifts from prompt-enhanced language-centric pipelines to unified agentic Vision-Language Models (VLMs). By offering a unified taxonomy and a critical outlook on challenges and future directions, this survey distinguishes itself as a foundational reference for understanding and advancing CVR as a distinct and rapidly evolving research area. It not only chronicles the evolution but also articulates the "why" behind CVR, emphasizing its advantages in cognitive alignment, semantic fidelity, robustness, interpretability, and data efficiency, thereby providing a strong theoretical and practical justification for this research direction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the inherent limitations of traditional monolithic visual reasoning models in replicating human-like intelligence. Humans effortlessly decompose complex visual scenes, ground intermediate concepts, and perform multi-step logical inference. Existing monolithic models, however, often rely on superficial correlations, struggle with complex multi-hop reasoning, and lack explicit mechanisms for decomposition and relational understanding, leading to issues like dataset bias exploitation and poor generalization. The authors posit that scaling up data and compute alone does not proportionally enhance capabilities for tasks demanding complex reasoning, necessitating a paradigm shift.

Motivated by these cognitive insights and the shortcomings of monolithic approaches, researchers have turned to Compositional Visual Reasoning (CVR). CVR explicitly incorporates structured reasoning steps guided by visual perception, making intermediate thought processes visible and enabling step-by-step inference.

The key objectives of this comprehensive survey are to:
1. **Formalize Definitions:** Introduce core definitions for visual reasoning, monolithic visual reasoning, and compositional visual reasoning to establish a clear conceptual framework.
2. **Justify CVR's Advantages:** Articulate the benefits of compositional approaches from multiple perspectives, including cognitive alignment with human reasoning, enhanced semantic and relational understanding, improved generalization and robustness, greater transparency and interpretability, modular reuse, reduction of language biases and hallucinations, and lower data requirements with improved efficiency.
3. **Trace CVR's Evolution:** Outline a five-stage roadmap that captures the recent progression of CVR paradigms, from early prompt-enhanced language-centric methods to unified agentic VLM architectures. For each stage, the survey aims to describe architectural designs, strengths, and limitations.
4. **Catalog Benchmarks and Metrics:** Provide a comprehensive catalog of over 60 benchmarks and their corresponding evaluation metrics that probe various dimensions of compositional visual reasoning.
5. **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** Distill key insights from the surveyed literature, identify persistent open challenges (e.g., limitations of LLM-based reasoning, hallucinations, data scalability, tool integration, and benchmark limitations), and outline promising future research directions.
6. **Serve as a Foundational Reference:** Offer a unified taxonomy, historical roadmap, and critical outlook to serve as a foundational reference for the field and inspire the next generation of CVR research.

In essence, the survey aims to systematically examine "why, what, and how" compositional visual reasoning is developing, providing a structured understanding of its current state and future potential.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic and comprehensive methodology to survey the burgeoning field of compositional visual reasoning. Their approach is characterized by a specific scope, a multi-faceted organizational framework, and an analytical depth designed to distill key insights and future directions.

**Scope of Review:** The survey covers a vast body of recent literature, reviewing over 260 papers published between January 2023 and May 2025. This tight timeframe highlights the rapid acceleration of research in CVR and emphasizes the survey's currency. The papers were drawn from top AI venues, including major conferences in computer vision (CVPR, ICCV, ECCV), machine learning (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR), and natural language processing (ACL, EMNLP, AAAI), along with highly cited arXiv preprints, ensuring coverage of influential and cutting-edge research. The focus is specifically on "large-model-enhanced methods applied to 2D image-based modalities," deliberately excluding video-based or 3D reasoning frameworks to maintain a clear scope. A key criterion for inclusion was the emphasis on "explicit intermediate 'thought' steps prior to answer generation."

**Organizational Framework:** The survey is structured around four central research questions that guide the narrative:
1. Why is compositional visual reasoning necessary?
2. What are the major reasoning architectures and paradigms of compositional visual reasoning?
3. What are the benchmarks and evaluation metrics?
4. What are the current limitations and bottlenecks?

To address the second question, a novel **five-stage paradigm shift** is introduced, charting the evolution of CVR models:
- **Stage I: Prompt-Enhanced Language-Centric Methods:** Characterized by LLMs decomposing tasks and synthesizing answers, leveraging VLMs primarily for perception, with reasoning occurring largely in the language space.
- **Stage II: Tool-Enhanced Large Language Models:** LLMs acting as central planners, dynamically invoking external vision tools via structured calls (e.g., code snippets).
- **Stage III: Tool-Enhanced Vision-Language Models:** VLMs taking over the planning role, directly perceiving raw images and coordinating external tools, which reduces information loss and enables visual feedback.
- **Stage IV: Chain-of-Thought Reasoning VLMs:** Unified architectures integrating perception and inference in a single forward pass, explicitly revealing intermediate thought processes. This stage is further divided into prompt-enhanced, RL-enhanced, and visually grounded CoT reasoning VLMs.
- **Stage V: Unified Agentic Vision-Language Models:** The latest evolution, where models autonomously plan, adapt, imagine, and execute sequences of decisions in iterative feedback loops, incorporating higher-order cognitive mechanisms.

**Benchmark and Evaluation Analysis:** For the third research question, the authors systematically catalog over 60 benchmarks and datasets, categorizing them into types such as General VQA, Relational Scene Graph Reasoning, Synthetic and Diagnostic Evaluation, Knowledge-Based, Image-Based Text, High-Resolution, Multidisciplinary, and Hallucination & Robustness Evaluation. They also discuss various evaluation metrics, including accuracy-based, grounding and segmentation metrics, rationale and text quality, task-specific, behavioral metrics, and efficiency/cost.

**Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** Drawing on the comprehensive review, the final section synthesizes key insights, pinpoints six core challenges limiting CVR (LLM reasoning limitations, hallucinations, bias toward deductive reasoning, data limitations, tool integration bottlenecks, and benchmark limitations), and proposes concrete future research directions for overcoming these hurdles.

This systematic, multi-layered approach allows the survey to provide a structured, in-depth, and critical analysis of the current landscape of compositional visual reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey distills several key findings and offers a comprehensive overview of the compositional visual reasoning landscape:

**Advantages of Compositional Visual Reasoning:**
The paper robustly argues that CVR offers significant advantages over monolithic approaches:
- **Cognitive Alignment:** Mimics human-like task decomposition and flexible reasoning.
- **Semantic and Relational Understanding:** Enables explicit modeling of deep semantic structures and object-centric relationships.
- **Generalization and Robustness:** Promotes systematic generalization to novel compositions and reduces reliance on dataset biases.
- **Transparency and Interpretability:** Generates explicit intermediate outputs (e.g., bounding boxes, rationales), enhancing understanding and debugging.
- **Modular Reuse:** Allows components to be reused across tasks, reducing retraining needs.
- **Reduced Biases and Hallucinations:** Enforces explicit grounding of intermediate steps in visual evidence, mitigating linguistic biases and factual inconsistencies.
- **Lower Data Requirements and Efficiency:** Modular structure reduces the need for massive datasets and enables few-shot or zero-shot generalization.

**Five-Stage Paradigm Shift in CVR:**
The survey's core contribution is its five-stage taxonomy, outlining the architectural and methodological evolution:
1. **Prompt-Enhanced Language-Centric Methods (Stage I):** Initial approaches where LLMs decompose complex visual questions into sub-questions, which VLMs answer, and then the LLM synthesizes the final response in language space. These methods are lightweight but have weak visual grounding and fixed prompting logic.
2. **Tool-Enhanced Large Language Models (Stage II):** LLMs function as central planners, dynamically invoking external vision tools (e.g., object detectors, OCR) via structured calls. This provides more flexibility but is limited by the LLM's access to visual information primarily through textual descriptions, leading to potential information loss.
3. **Tool-Enhanced Vision-Language Models (Stage III):** VLMs, rather than LLMs, serve as planners, directly receiving raw images and coordinating external visual tools. This direct perception reduces semantic loss, enables more accurate action planning, and facilitates visual feedback. Challenges include computational demands and reliance on external tool accuracy.
4. **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning VLMs (Stage IV):** This stage marks a move towards unified architectures that integrate perception and inference in a single forward pass, generating explicit intermediate thought processes without relying on external tools. Sub-categories include prompt-enhanced, RL-enhanced (for systematic reasoning and generalization), and visually grounded CoT (coupling reasoning steps with visual grounding information).
5. **Unified Agentic Vision-Language Models (Stage V):** The latest and most advanced stage, where VLMs act as autonomous agents, actively planning, adapting, imagining, and executing sequences of decisions in iterative feedback loops. These models incorporate higher-order cognitive mechanisms like memory, goal-driven exploration of informative regions, and even internal visual imagination, demonstrating robust performance on complex, high-resolution, and compositional tasks.

**Benchmarks and Evaluation:**
The survey identifies a wide array of over 60 benchmarks, categorized by their focus (e.g., general VQA, relational reasoning, knowledge-based, high-resolution, hallucination detection), and details various evaluation metrics. However, a critical finding is the **limitations of current evaluation protocols**:
- Lack of explicit evaluation for *intermediate reasoning steps*, leading to an incomplete assessment of reasoning quality and consistency.
- Absence of quantified *reasoning difficulty levels*, hindering fine-grained analysis of model capabilities.
- Need for *comprehensive diagnostic insights* to differentiate reasoning capabilities effectively.

**Key Challenges and Future Directions:**
The authors distill six major challenges:
- **Limitations of LLM-Based Reasoning:** LLMs lack an internal "world model" for simulating visual/physical dynamics, limiting their ability to perform causal or counterfactual inference and often resorting to System 1 heuristic reasoning.
- **Hallucinations:** Even in compositional models, misalignment between language and visual content can lead to plausible but ungrounded or factually incorrect outputs.
- **Bias Toward Deductive Reasoning:** Over-reliance on formal, logical deduction makes models brittle to noisy or biased inputs, overlooking inductive or abductive reasoning.
- **Data Limitations and Scalability Challenges:** Scarcity of diverse, multi-step, and grounded visual programs, high annotation costs, and noise from synthetic data generation limit progress.
- **Tool Integration and Architectural Bottlenecks:** Issues with tool awareness, computational overhead, information bottlenecks from vision encoders, and fragmented pipelines hinder robust integration.
- **Benchmark Limitations and Evaluation Contamination:** Current benchmarks overestimate capabilities due to in-distribution data, linguistic shortcuts, and inadequate evaluation of intermediate reasoning steps.

To address these, the survey proposes future directions such as integrating explicit **world models**, developing **human-in-the-loop interactive frameworks**, embracing **diverse reasoning paradigms** (inductive, abductive, analogical), improving **hybrid data construction strategies**, fostering **more integrated architectures**, and developing **nuanced evaluation protocols** that assess intermediate reasoning steps and difficulty levels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds substantial significance for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in multimodal reasoning, and promises to have a notable impact on future research and development.

First, its primary contribution is the provision of a **foundational and comprehensive reference** for Compositional Visual Reasoning. By systematically reviewing over 260 papers published in a critical two-and-a-half-year period (2023-2025), the authors have meticulously mapped the contours of a rapidly evolving domain. This synthesis is invaluable for new researchers seeking to understand the field, as well as for seasoned experts aiming to keep abreast of the latest advancements.

Second, the introduction of a **unified five-stage taxonomy and historical roadmap** for CVR paradigms is a major conceptual contribution. This framework offers a clear and coherent narrative of the field's evolution, from early, loosely coupled systems to sophisticated, agentic architectures. This structured understanding helps to categorize diverse methodologies, highlight architectural shifts, and clarify the progression of research, preventing fragmentation and fostering a shared understanding across the community.

Third, the survey **articulates the "why" behind CVR** with compelling arguments centered on cognitive alignment, robustness, interpretability, and efficiency. By clearly outlining the advantages over monolithic approaches, the paper reinforces the necessity and value of pursuing compositional methods, potentially encouraging more researchers to adopt this paradigm. This foundational justification can guide the development of AI systems that are not only more capable but also more trustworthy and human-centric.

Fourth, the **identification of critical open challenges and detailed future directions** is perhaps the most impactful aspect. The authors pinpoint specific weaknesses in current CVR systems—such as the lack of internal world models in LLMs, persistent hallucination issues, over-reliance on deductive reasoning, data limitations, and architectural integration bottlenecks. More importantly, they propose concrete pathways forward, including the integration of explicit world models for causal inference, human-in-the-loop supervision, exploration of inductive and abductive reasoning, development of hybrid data engines, and creation of more integrated and tool-aware architectures. These actionable insights provide a clear research agenda, stimulating and guiding future investigations toward addressing the most pressing problems.

Finally, by critiquing the **limitations of current benchmarks and evaluation protocols** and advocating for more nuanced, difficulty-aware, and step-level assessment methods, the survey pushes for higher standards in model validation. This call for improved evaluation is crucial for accurately gauging progress, preventing inflated performance claims, and ensuring that future models genuinely achieve robust and generalizable visual reasoning capabilities.

In conclusion, "Explain Before You Answer" is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a strategic document that maps the past, defines the present, and illuminates the future of compositional visual reasoning. Its comprehensive scope, clear taxonomy, and forward-looking analysis are poised to inspire and shape the next generation of research in multimodal AI, ultimately contributing to the development of intelligent systems that can reason about the visual world with human-like proficiency and understanding.
