# DoReMi: Grounding Language Model by Detecting and Recovering from Plan-Execution Misalignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute developed DoReMi, a framework that grounds Large Language Models (LLMs) in robotic control by enabling immediate detection and recovery from plan-execution misalignments. The system achieves this by having LLMs generate explicit constraints for planned actions, which are then continuously monitored by a Vision Language Model (VLM) for real-time validation, leading to significantly higher task success rates and reduced execution times across diverse robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks compared to prior delayed-feedback approaches.

## Problem

- Current hierarchical robotic systems using LLMs for high-level planning are fragile and lack robustness to common plan-execution misalignments caused by environmental perturbations, imperfect controllers, or sensor noise.
- Existing feedback mechanisms for LLM-based robots, such as 'plan-level feedback,' are inefficient as they only trigger re-planning *after* an entire skill step has completed, leading to wasted effort if a failure occurs mid-execution.
- Feedback from Vision Language Models (VLMs) in prior works often provides open-ended and ambiguous scene descriptions, making it difficult for LLMs to precisely identify and address specific execution failures.

## Method

- **DoReMi** employs a novel ==two-stage framework== where an LLM first plans the next high-level skill and then generates explicit, natural language ==constraints== that must be satisfied during that skill's execution.
- During low-level skill execution, a ==Vision Language Model (VLM)== continuously monitors the environment by querying the LLM-generated constraints as precise 'Yes/No' questions, providing real-time, ==execution-level feedback==.
- Upon immediate detection of a constraint violation by the VLM, the current low-level skill is aborted, and the LLM is promptly invoked for re-planning, receiving specific information about the violated constraint to facilitate targeted recovery.

## Results

- DoReMi consistently achieved higher success rates and significantly shorter execution times across various robot arm manipulation and humanoid robot locomotion/manipulation tasks compared to baselines like SayCan and Inner Monologue.
- In complex tasks like 'Stack blocks in order' and humanoid 'Prepare-food', DoReMi demonstrated superior recovery from critical failures and perturbations, outperforming methods that relied on delayed or ambiguous feedback.
- The DoReMi-FT variant, using a VLM fine-tuned with a small dataset, further improved VLM accuracy and generalized well to unseen objects and tasks, achieving high success rates (**90%** for 'Prepare-food-real', **80%** for 'Stack-real') in real-world perturbed environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can be leveraged not only for high-level task planning but also to generate explicit, natural language constraints that define expected states during skill execution.
> - Vision Language Models (VLMs) can serve as precise, continuous 'constraint detectors' by answering specific 'Yes/No' questions derived from LLM-generated constraints, enabling real-time monitoring.
> - Immediate detection of plan-execution misalignments, followed by rapid abortion of the current action and LLM-driven re-planning, significantly improves task success rates and efficiency compared to delayed, step-completion feedback mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotic control has become a rapidly expanding area of research. LLMs, with their vast semantic knowledge and powerful reasoning capabilities, are increasingly being leveraged to provide high-level task planning for embodied agents, decomposing complex instructions into executable sequences of skills. This approach, often termed "hierarchical robotics," stands in contrast to "end-to-end" methods (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Perceiver-Actor) which directly map sensory inputs to low-level robot actions. While end-to-end models have shown progress, they typically require massive datasets and can struggle with the high-frequency control demanded by complex robot dynamics (e.g., legged robots). Hierarchical approaches, by offloading high-frequency control to specialized low-level policies (e.g., reinforcement learning controllers), offer a more practical path for complex long-horizon tasks.

However, a critical challenge in this hierarchical paradigm is "grounding" the abstract textual plans generated by LLMs to the uncertain realities of the physical world. Prior works, such as SayCan and Inner Monologue, have demonstrated LLMs' ability to generate feasible plans. Yet, a major limitation persists: these systems often assume the flawless execution of each low-level skill. In reality, environmental perturbations (e.g., an object being knocked over), sensor noise, or imperfect low-level controllers can cause the robot's actual execution to deviate significantly from the high-level plan.

Existing feedback mechanisms in LLM-based planning often involve "plan-level feedback," where the LLM is prompted for re-planning only *after* a complete skill step has finished (e.g., ReAct, Reflexion, Inner Monologue). While an improvement over open-loop planning, this delayed feedback can be highly inefficient. For instance, if a robot accidentally drops an object mid-transport, waiting until it reaches the destination to realize the failure leads to wasted time and effort. Furthermore, feedback provided by Vision Language Models (VLMs) in previous works (e.g., Inner Monologue's scene descriptions) can be open-ended and ambiguous, making it difficult for the LLM to precisely identify the misalignment.

DoReMi directly addresses this critical gap. It shifts the paradigm from delayed, ambiguous, plan-level feedback to *immediate, precise, execution-level feedback*. By enabling the LLM to not only plan the next action but also generate explicit constraints for that action, and then continuously monitoring these constraints with a VLM, DoReMi provides a more robust and efficient mechanism for grounding language models in dynamic physical environments. This work pushes the boundaries of LLM applications in robotics by making them active participants in monitoring and correcting real-time execution, thus moving towards more reliable and autonomous embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind DoReMi stems from the inherent fragility of current hierarchical robotic systems that leverage LLMs for high-level planning. Despite LLMs' impressive reasoning capabilities and common-sense knowledge, their textual plans operate in an idealized domain. When these plans are translated into low-level actions by robotic controllers in the physical world, numerous factors can lead to discrepancies:
- **Environmental Perturbations:** Unexpected changes in the environment, such as an object being moved or knocked over by external forces.
- **Imperfect Controller Design:** Low-level robot controllers are rarely perfect and can introduce errors, like an object slipping from a gripper or inaccurate placement.
- **Sensor Noise/Errors:** Imperfect perception can lead to misinterpretations of the environment.

These "plan-execution misalignments" are common and can occur at any point during a task. Traditional approaches, which either operate open-loop or only provide feedback at the completion of an entire plan step, are highly inefficient or outright fail when such misalignments occur mid-skill. For example, if a robot is carrying a box to a table and drops it halfway, continuing to the table before realizing the box is gone is a significant waste of time and effort. The system needs to react *immediately* to such events, much like a human would.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the DoReMi framework are:
1. **Enable Immediate Detection of Misalignment:** Develop a mechanism to detect deviations between the high-level plan and the low-level execution *as they happen*, rather than waiting for a skill to complete.
2. **Facilitate Timely Recovery:** Upon detecting a misalignment, allow the system to rapidly abort the current, futile action and re-plan an appropriate recovery strategy.
3. **Leverage LLMs for Both Planning and Supervision:** Extend the role of LLMs beyond mere high-level planning. Harness their understanding of the physical world to generate explicit "constraints" that define the expected state during the execution of a given skill.
4. **Utilize VLMs for Precise, Continuous Monitoring:** Employ Vision Language Models as "constraint detectors" that can continuously and precisely verify the satisfaction of these LLM-generated constraints through visual input, providing clear "Yes/No" feedback.

In essence, DoReMi aims to make LLM-driven embodied agents more robust, efficient, and intelligent by providing them with an active, real-time "internal monologue" that monitors execution quality and enables rapid adaptation to unexpected failures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DoReMi proposes a novel two-stage framework for immediate detection and recovery from plan-execution misalignments. The ingenuity lies in leveraging LLMs in a dual role and employing VLMs for continuous, precise monitoring.

**A. High-Level Planning Stage (LLM's Dual Role):**
At the outset of a task, and whenever re-planning is necessary, an LLM is invoked.
1. **Next Skill Planning:** Following established practices, the LLM (Vicuna-13B or GPT-4) generates the next high-level skill (e.g., "Pick up the yellow box," "Go to the gray table") based on the high-level task instruction, the available basic skill set, and historical information (previously executed steps, current state). This is done via few-shot in-context learning.
2. **Constraint Generation:** This is DoReMi's critical innovation. Immediately after selecting the next skill, the LLM is prompted *again* to generate explicit, natural language "constraints" that *must be satisfied* during the execution of that specific skill. These constraints are derived from the LLM's vast encoded understanding of the physical world and common sense.
 - **Example:** If the LLM plans "Pick up the yellow box," a corresponding constraint might be "[Constraint: The robot is holding the yellow box]".
 - **Example:** If the LLM plans "Go to the gray table" *after* picking up a box, a composite constraint might be "[Constraint: The robot is holding the yellow box]".
 - This dual role of the LLM allows it to define not just *what* to do, but also *what state is expected* during the execution of that action. The generated constraints are specific and enable a binary (Yes/No) check.

**B. Low-Level Skill Execution Stage (VLM as Constraint Detector):**
Once the LLM has planned a skill and its associated constraints, the agent begins executing the low-level skill.
1. **Continuous Monitoring:** Throughout the entire execution period of the low-level skill, a Vision Language Model (VLM) (BLIP-2 in this case) acts as a "constraint detector." It continuously queries the environment for visual information (from first-person or third-person cameras).
2. **Precise Constraint Checking:** The VLM is fed the LLM-generated constraints in the form of specific "Yes/No" questions (e.g., "Question: Is the robot holding a fish? Answer:"). This is a crucial distinction from general scene descriptions, as it focuses the VLM on precise, actionable information and reduces ambiguity. The low latency of VLM (sub-0.1 seconds) allows for frequent checks (every ∆t = 0.2 seconds).
3. **Immediate Detection and Re-planning:**
 - If the VLM detects that *any* of the active constraints are violated (i.e., the VLM answers "No" to a constraint query), it signifies a plan-execution misalignment.
 - Upon this immediate detection, the execution of the current low-level skill is *aborted*.
 - The LLM is then immediately invoked for re-planning. Crucially, the prompt for re-planning includes information about the *specific constraint violation*, allowing the LLM to generate a recovery plan that directly addresses the detected issue.

**VLM Fine-tuning for Complex Scenarios (DoReMi-FT):**
For tasks with very complex scenes, the authors found that zero-shot VLM performance could diminish. To enhance robustness, they propose fine-tuning the pre-trained BLIP-2 model using a small, task-specific dataset (e.g., 128 image-text pairs for fruit objects). This fine-tuning, performed using the parameter-efficient LoRA method, significantly improves the VLM's accuracy and its generalization capabilities to unseen objects, backgrounds, and even new tasks, as demonstrated in the experiments.

This methodology creates a tightly integrated feedback loop where the LLM's semantic understanding is grounded by precise, continuous visual monitoring, enabling proactive and efficient recovery from real-world execution failures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DoReMi was rigorously evaluated across a diverse range of tasks involving both robot arm manipulation and complex humanoid robot locomotion and manipulation, incorporating various forms of environmental disturbances and imperfect controllers.

**A. Robot Arm Manipulation Tasks:**
- **Tasks:** "Pick and Place" (with random object drops during transport) and "Stack blocks in order" (with noise in placement positions and random drops).
- **Baselines:** SayCan (no feedback), CLIPort (repeats failed step), Inner Monologue (IM) (feedback at step completion with ambiguous scene descriptions), IM-Oracle (IM with perfect, but impractical, feedback).
- **Results (Table I):**
 - **SayCan** consistently failed in disturbed environments due to its lack of any re-planning mechanism.
 - **CLIPort and Inner Monologue (IM)** showed some improvement in simple "Pick and Place" by repeating steps or re-planning at step end. However, they were significantly less efficient (longer execution times) because they waited for the entire skill to complete before reacting.
 - In the more complex "Stack blocks in order" task, especially with higher noise and drop probabilities, CLIPort and IM struggled to recover from critical failures like collapsing stacks or incorrect placements. CLIPort's simple repetition was insufficient, and IM's ambiguous scene descriptions made intelligent recovery difficult.
 - **DoReMi** consistently achieved higher success rates and notably shorter execution times across all disturbance levels. Its ability to immediately detect a dropped object or a collapsed stack and re-plan (e.g., re-picking a fallen block) proved far more efficient and robust.

**B. Humanoid Robot Tasks:**
- **Tasks:** "Obstacle-avoidance" (with unknown obstacles), "Move-box" (with random box drops), "Prepare-food" (collecting multiple food items with pick/drop failures), and real-world versions of "Prepare-food" and "Stack-real". These tasks further tested the framework's generalizability and real-world applicability on a complex 20-DOF humanoid robot.
- **Baselines:** Similar to robot arm tasks, with the addition of **DoReMi-FT** (DoReMi with fine-tuned VLM).
- **Results (Table II):**
 - **SayCan and IM** again demonstrated significant performance degradation and high failure rates in the presence of disturbances, mirroring the robot arm experiments. IM's delayed feedback and reliance on general VLM scene descriptions were insufficient for the dynamic and often nuanced challenges of humanoid locomotion and manipulation.
 - **DoReMi** (with zero-shot VLM) showed substantial improvements in success rates compared to SayCan and IM across all simulated humanoid tasks.
 - **DoReMi-FT** (with a VLM fine-tuned on a small dataset) achieved the highest success rates, often closely matching or even *exceeding* the IM-Oracle baseline in terms of task completion time. This highlights the framework's efficiency gains from immediate recovery, as IM-Oracle, despite perfect feedback, still suffered from the *delay* inherent in plan-level re-planning.
 - **Real-world experiments** ("Prepare-food-real" and "Stack-real") further validated DoReMi-FT's robustness, achieving high success rates (90% and 80% respectively) in physically perturbed environments, where baselines struggled significantly.
 - **VLM Fine-tuning Effectiveness (Table III & IV, Figure 6):** The ablation studies showed that zero-shot VLM performance decreased with scene complexity (e.g., "Prepare-food"). However, fine-tuning BLIP-2 with a small dataset (128 image-text pairs) drastically improved VLM accuracy and enabled strong generalization to unseen objects, backgrounds, and tasks, confirming the practicality and transferability of DoReMi-FT.

In summary, DoReMi's main finding is its ability to create significantly more robust and efficient LLM-driven embodied agents by implementing an "execution-level feedback" loop. This is achieved through the LLM's novel dual role in generating both plans and precise constraints, coupled with continuous, VLM-based monitoring for immediate detection and recovery from misalignments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DoReMi represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly in bridging the gap between high-level AI reasoning and reliable physical robot execution. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability for Embodied AI:** The primary impact of DoReMi is the creation of more robust and reliable robotic systems. By enabling immediate detection and recovery from plan-execution misalignments, DoReMi addresses a fundamental challenge in real-world robotics: dealing with uncertainty, noise, and unexpected events. This capability is crucial for deploying LLM-powered robots in unstructured, dynamic environments where perfect execution cannot be guaranteed.

2. **Increased Efficiency and Reduced Task Completion Time:** The ability to abort a futile action and re-plan immediately, rather than waiting for a full skill step to complete, leads to significant gains in efficiency. As demonstrated by the shorter task completion times, DoReMi minimizes wasted effort, making robots faster and more productive in complex, multi-step tasks. This efficiency is vital for practical applications where time is a critical factor.

3. **Novel Application and Grounding of Large Language Models:** DoReMi expands the utility of LLMs beyond traditional planning. By compelling LLMs to generate explicit "constraints" that define expected execution states, the framework introduces a novel way for LLMs to "supervise" their own plans in the physical world. This represents a deeper form of grounding, where the LLM's abstract knowledge is concretely linked to real-time sensory feedback, fostering a more intelligent and self-correcting autonomous agent.

4. **Practical and Scalable Feedback Mechanism:** The use of VLMs as "constraint detectors" provides a general, visual-semantic mechanism for monitoring execution. Unlike manually designed success detectors or ambiguous scene descriptions, DoReMi's VLM queries are precise, binary, and derived directly from the LLM's contextual understanding. The demonstrated effectiveness of fine-tuning VLMs with small datasets further enhances the practicality and scalability of this approach, allowing it to adapt to new objects and environments with minimal effort.

5. **Stepping Stone Towards General-Purpose Robots:** As robots move beyond controlled factory settings to more complex human environments, their ability to reason, adapt, and recover from failures becomes paramount. DoReMi contributes a core architectural component for developing truly general-purpose robots that can handle unforeseen circumstances, learn from mistakes, and operate more autonomously. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in areas like logistics, domestic assistance, and elder care.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** DoReMi opens several avenues for future research. This includes exploring more sophisticated methods for LLM-generated constraints (e.g., temporal constraints, safety constraints), integrating other modalities beyond vision for constraint detection (e.g., haptic, audio), and developing adaptive frequencies for constraint checking based on task criticality. It also highlights the potential for deeper interplay between high-level cognitive models (LLMs) and low-level reactive control systems.

In conclusion, DoReMi's contribution is not just an incremental improvement but a fundamental advancement in how LLMs are integrated into embodied AI. By enabling robots to detect and recover from misalignments *immediately* and *intelligently*, this work paves the way for a new generation of more capable, reliable, and truly autonomous robotic systems that can operate effectively in the unpredictable real world.
