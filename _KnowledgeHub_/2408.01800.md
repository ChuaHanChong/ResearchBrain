# MiniCPM-V: A GPT-4V Level MLLM on Your Phone

> [!summary] Summary
> The OpenBMB team introduces MiniCPM-V, an efficient multimodal large language model designed for on-device deployment that achieves GPT-4V-level performance. The 8.5-billion-parameter MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5 scored 65.1 on OpenCompass, surpassing GPT-4V-1106 (63.5), and demonstrated efficient operation on mobile phones with optimized inference speeds up to 8.2 tokens/s and reduced hallucination rates.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are excessively large and computationally demanding, requiring deployment on expensive cloud servers.
- This cloud-centric dependency restricts MLLM application in critical real-world scenarios such as mobile devices, offline environments, and privacy-sensitive use cases.
- The high resource demands lead to significant operational costs, energy consumption, and privacy concerns, hindering widespread practicality.

## Method

- Developing a series of compact Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), **MiniCPM-V**, optimized for on-device deployment while maintaining high performance.
- Employing a three-component architecture: a ==SigLIP visual encoder==, a ==Perceiver Resampler== compression layer, and a Llama3-Instruct 8B base LLM, alongside an ==Adaptive Visual Encoding== strategy for high-resolution images.
- Implementing a multi-stage training process, including ==pre-training==, ==supervised fine-tuning==, and ==RLAIF-V (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback)== to enhance trustworthiness and reduce hallucination.

## Results

- MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5 (**8.5B**) achieved an OpenCompass score of **65.1**, surpassing GPT-4V-1106 (**63.5**) and other larger open-source MLLMs.
- Demonstrated robust OCR capabilities, scoring **725** on OCRBench and performing comparably to GPT-4V-1106 on TextVQA and DocVQA.
- Achieved efficient on-device deployment with a **5GB** memory footprint and up to **8.2 tokens/s** decoding throughput on mobile phones (e.g., Xiaomi 14 Pro) via quantization and NPU acceleration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The concept of "Moore's Law for MLLMs" is empirically validated, showing that GPT-4V level performance can be achieved with significantly reduced model sizes (e.g., 8.5B parameters).
> - Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF-V) effectively reduces MLLM hallucination rates while simultaneously improving overall general capabilities.
> - Efficient multilingual multimodal understanding for over 30 languages can be achieved by leveraging strong base LLM multilingual capabilities with minimal targeted multilingual supervised fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has dramatically transformed the AI landscape, showcasing impressive capabilities in understanding, reasoning, and interacting with multimodal data. However, the prevailing challenge in the field is the immense computational burden and parameter size associated with most cutting-edge MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4V, Gemini Pro). These models typically require deployment on high-performing cloud servers, severely limiting their applicability in crucial real-world scenarios such as mobile, offline, energy-sensitive, and privacy-protective environments. This reliance on cloud infrastructure also contributes to significant energy consumption and carbon emissions.

Against this backdrop, "MiniCPM-V" positions itself at the forefront of a critical emerging trend that the authors metaphorically term "Moore's Law for MLLMs." This "red line" trend, illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper, indicates a rapid decrease in the model sizes required to achieve performance levels comparable to state-of-the-art proprietary models like GPT-4V. Simultaneously, the "blue line" represents the steady increase in the computational capacity of end-side devices, including mobile phones and personal computers. The convergence of these two trends signifies that high-performance MLLMs are becoming increasingly feasible for on-device deployment.

MiniCPM-V serves as a direct instantiation and a representative example of this "Moore's Law for MLLMs." It directly addresses the critical need for efficient, lightweight MLLMs that can operate locally. The paper highlights MiniCPM-V's ability to achieve "GPT-4V level performance" at significantly reduced model sizes (e.g., 8.5 billion parameters for MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5), which is considerably smaller than many high-performing open-source counterparts (e.g., Yi-VL 34B, LLaVA-NeXT-Yi-34B). By focusing on an optimal balance between performance and efficiency, MiniCPM-V directly competes with both larger proprietary models (by matching or exceeding performance at a fraction of the size) and other open-source efforts in efficient MLLMs (e.g., Phi-3-vision, Mini-Gemini, MobileVLM), often outperforming them on comprehensive benchmarks.

The work contributes to the broader landscape by demonstrating that miniaturization of advanced AI capabilities is not only possible but also practical, aligning with a historical pattern of technological development. It paves the way for a new generation of ubiquitous AI applications that are more accessible, private, and sustainable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the MiniCPM-V project is to develop a series of highly efficient Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that can be effectively deployed and run on end-side devices, such as mobile phones, personal computers, vehicles, and robotics, without compromising on performance.

This ambitious goal is driven by several critical motivations, stemming from the limitations of current state-of-the-art MLLMs:

- **High Computational Costs and Resource Demands:** Existing large MLLMs possess a massive number of parameters and require extensive computation, necessitating deployment on expensive, high-performing cloud servers. This incurs significant operational costs, energy consumption, and carbon emissions.
- **Restricted Application Scope:** The cloud-centric nature of most MLLMs severely limits their applicability in real-world scenarios. This includes:
 - **Mobile Devices:** Direct integration into smartphones is hindered.
 - **Offline Environments:** Lack of stable network connectivity prevents usage.
 - **Energy-Sensitive Scenarios:** High power demands make them unsuitable for battery-powered devices.
 - **Privacy and Security Concerns:** Processing sensitive data in the cloud raises privacy and security issues for both personal and industrial users.
- **Lack of Practicality:** The cumulative effect of these limitations means that despite their impressive capabilities, current MLLMs are "far from being practical in real-world applications" for a broad user base.

In light of these challenges, the MiniCPM-V team is motivated by the significant advantages that efficient, end-side deployable MLLMs can offer:

- **Broader Usage Scope:** Enabling AI capabilities on ubiquitous devices enhances accessibility.
- **Improved Computation Efficiency:** Local processing reduces latency and resource consumption.
- **Robust Offline Functionality:** Ensures consistent performance even without internet access.
- **Enhanced Privacy and Security:** Local data processing keeps sensitive information on the device.

Specifically, for the latest iteration, MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5, the team set out to achieve several notable features:

- **Strong Performance:** To surpass or perform comparably to proprietary models like GPT-4V-1106, Gemini Pro, and Claude 3 on comprehensive benchmarks like OpenCompass.
- **Advanced Visual Perception:** To develop robust OCR capabilities and support for 1.8 million-pixel high-resolution image perception at any aspect ratio.
- **Trustworthy Behavior:** To significantly reduce hallucination rates, making the model more reliable for sensitive applications.
- **Multilingual Support:** To enable multimodal understanding and interaction in over 30 languages.
- **Efficient End-side Deployment:** To integrate advanced optimization techniques that ensure practical and responsive performance on mobile phones and other edge devices.

Ultimately, MiniCPM-V aims to serve as a beacon for the promising trend of developing powerful MLLMs that are simultaneously compact and efficient, unlocking a wider spectrum of real-world AI applications in the near future.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core philosophy behind MiniCPM-V is to strike an optimal balance between performance and efficiency, a crucial objective for real-world applications. This is reflected in its architectural design, training strategies, and deployment optimizations.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

MiniCPM-V's architecture comprises three main components: a visual encoder, a compression layer, and a Large Language Model (LLM).

- **Visual Encoder:** The paper employs SigLIP SoViT-400m/14, a state-of-the-art vision transformer, to process input images.
- **Compression Layer:** This layer is responsible for efficiently reducing the dimensionality of visual tokens. It utilizes a Perceiver Resampler structure with a single cross-attention layer. This design is critical for efficiency, compressing potentially thousands of visual tokens into a manageable number (e.g., 64 for V1/V2, 96 for V2.5), significantly reducing subsequent LLM computation.
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** For MiniCPM-V 1.0 and 2.0, a custom 2B MiniCPM base LLM is used. For the latest MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5, the powerful Llama3-Instruct 8B serves as the foundation.

A key innovation in the visual encoding is the adoption of **Adaptive Visual Encoding**, inspired by LLaVA-UHD. This method addresses the challenges of high-resolution images with diverse aspect ratios:
- **Image Partition:** High-resolution images are divided into multiple "slices" to better match the ViT's pre-training resolution and aspect ratio. An ideal number of slices is calculated based on image size and ViT's native resolution, and a score function selects the optimal (rows, columns) partition. To handle cases where the ideal slice number is prime, the system considers partitions based on N-1, N, and N+1 slices. The approach supports up to 1.8 million pixels (e.g., 1344×1344).
- **Slice Encoding:** Each slice is proportionally resized to fit the ViT's pre-training area, and 2D positional embeddings are interpolated to adapt to the slice's ratio. Crucially, the *original full image* is also included as an additional slice to provide holistic contextual information to the MLLM.
- **Spatial Schema:** Special tokens (`<slice>`, `</slice>`, `\n`) are added to indicate the relative positions of slices within the full image, aiding spatial reasoning.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy

The model undergoes a meticulously designed three-phase training process:

1. **Pre-training:** This phase aims to align the visual modules with the LLM's input space and establish foundational multimodal knowledge.
 - **Stage-1 (Warm-up):** The randomly initialized compression layer is trained with frozen visual encoder and LLM. Resolution is set to 224x224, using 200M English and Chinese image captioning data.
 - **Stage-2 (Resolution Extension):** The visual encoder is trained to extend its input resolution to 448x448, using another 200M image captioning data. Other parameters remain frozen.
 - **Stage-3 (Adaptive Visual Encoding):** Both the compression layer and visual encoder are trained with the adaptive visual encoding strategy to accommodate high-resolution, arbitrary aspect ratio inputs. This stage incorporates additional OCR data alongside image captioning data to enhance text recognition capabilities.
 - **Caption Rewriting:** An auxiliary LLM, fine-tuned on GPT-4 annotated samples, rewrites low-quality web-sourced captions into higher-quality question-answer pairs, improving training stability.
 - **Data Packing:** To enhance memory and computational efficiency, multiple samples are packed into fixed-length sequences, accelerating pre-training by 2-3 times.
 - **Multilingual Generalization:** Leveraging the strong multilingual capabilities of the base LLM (Llama3), the model is primarily pre-trained on English and Chinese multimodal data. A lightweight but high-quality multilingual supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase then generalizes its multimodal understanding to over 30 languages, significantly reducing the reliance on extensive low-resource language multimodal data.

2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** This phase focuses on learning richer knowledge and human-like interaction capabilities from high-quality, human-annotated or strong-model-generated datasets. All model parameters are unlocked during this phase.
 - **Data Strategy:** SFT data is divided into two parts and sequentially concatenated. Part-1 (e.g., COCO, VQA, GQA) bolsters basic recognition with shorter responses. Part-2 (e.g., LLaVA-Instruct, ShareGPT4V, text-only LLM datasets) enhances the model's ability to generate detailed responses and follow complex instructions. MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5 specifically integrates 2M Cauldron data for knowledge augmentation and 90K multilingual data for conversation.

3. **RLAIF-V (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback - Visual):** This crucial phase addresses the common hallucination problem in MLLMs by aligning model behaviors with AI/human feedback.
 - **Response Generation:** The policy model generates multiple (e.g., 10) diverse responses for a given instruction using high-temperature sampling.
 - **Feedback Collection (Divide-and-Conquer):** An open-source MLLM (OmniLMM 12B or LLaVA-NeXT-Yi 34B) acts as an "AI judge." Each generated response is broken down into atomic claims (by Llama-3 8B). These claims are converted into yes/no questions, and the AI judge verifies their correctness. The response score is based on the number of invalid claims. This "divide-and-conquer" approach makes feedback collection more scalable and robust for open-source models.
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** The collected high-quality AI feedback is used to create preference pairs (one preferred, one less preferred response). The DPO algorithm then optimizes the model directly on this preference dataset (6K pairs from 3K unique images), enhancing trustworthiness.

#### 3.3. End-side Deployment Optimization

Recognizing the severe resource constraints of mobile devices (limited memory, slower CPUs/GPUs), the team integrates a suite of optimization techniques:

- **Quantization:** The fp16 model (16-17GB) is quantized to 4-bit (Q4_K_M mode) using the GGML framework, reducing its memory footprint to approximately 5GB, making it feasible for mobile phones.
- **Memory Usage Optimization:** Instead of loading the Visual Encoder (ViT) and LLM simultaneously, they are loaded sequentially. ViT is loaded for visual encoding, then its memory is released before the LLM is loaded for text and visual token processing. This prevents frequent memory paging/swapping, significantly improving image processing speed.
- **Compilation Optimization:** Direct compilation of the models on the target devices (e.g., Android phone, Mac M1) yields better instruction set architecture consistency, resulting in notable reductions in encoding latency and improvements in decoding throughput.
- **Configuration Optimization:** An automatic parameter search algorithm is employed to dynamically determine optimal `llama.cpp` configurations (e.g., CPU core allocation) for various end-side devices, further maximizing inference speed.
- **NPU Acceleration:** For devices equipped with Neural Processing Units (NPUs), MiniCPM-V leverages them specifically for visual encoding (using QNN backend for ViT), while the LLM still uses the `llama.cpp` backend. This capitalizes on NPUs' efficiency for AI tasks, drastically reducing visual encoding time.

This holistic approach, from architecture and training data to model alignment and deployment, allows MiniCPM-V to achieve a unique balance of high performance and on-device efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MiniCPM-V series models, particularly MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5, demonstrates impressive capabilities across various benchmarks and practical deployment scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance on General Multimodal Benchmarks

MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5, with 8.5 billion parameters, achieves an OpenCompass score of 65.1. This is a significant finding, as it **outperforms proprietary state-of-the-art models** such as GPT-4V-1106 (63.5), Gemini Pro (62.9), and Claude 3 Opus (57.7) on this comprehensive benchmark suite. Furthermore, it **substantially surpasses leading open-source MLLMs** of comparable or even larger sizes, including Idefics2-8B (by 7.9 points), Cambrian-34B, LLaVA-NeXT-Yi-34B, and Yi-VL-34B. The earlier MiniCPM-V 2.0 (2.8B parameters) also shows strong performance (54.5), outperforming other 2B-3B models and rivaling some Llama3-based 8B MLLMs. This establishes MiniCPM-V as a top-tier performer in the compact MLLM category.

#### 4.2. OCR Capability

MiniCPM-V models exhibit **strong OCR capabilities**, demonstrating proficiency in scene-text, document, and screenshot understanding. MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5 scores 725 on OCRBench, 76.6 on TextVQA, and 84.8 on DocVQA. These scores not only **outperform all tested open-source MLLMs** ranging from 1.7B to 34B parameters but also **perform comparably to proprietary models** like GPT-4V-1106 and Gemini Pro. Qualitative examples in the paper showcase its ability to accurately transcribe articles, convert complex tables to Markdown, and comprehend code logic from images.

#### 4.3. Trustworthy Behavior (Low Hallucination)

A critical finding is MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5's **reduced hallucination rates**. On the Object HalBench, it achieves lower hallucination rates (10.3% response-level / 5.0% mention-level) compared to GPT-4V-1106 (13.6% / 7.3%). The ablation study confirms that the RLAIF-V technique effectively reduces hallucination without sacrificing general capabilities; in fact, it slightly improves the overall OpenCompass score by 0.6 points. This indicates a more reliable and trustworthy model for real-world applications where factual accuracy is paramount.

#### 4.4. Multilingual Support

Leveraging the multilingual generalization approach from VisCPM, MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5 successfully **extends its multimodal capabilities to over 30 languages**. It outperforms Yi-VL 34B and Phi-3-vision-128k-instruct on multilingual LLaVA Bench evaluations across various languages (e.g., German, French, Japanese, Korean, Spanish). An ablation study highlights the efficiency of this approach, showing over 25 points of improvement in LLaVA Bench scores across multiple languages by using less than 0.5% of the total SFT data for multilingual training. This broad language support makes the model highly valuable for a global user base.

#### 4.5. Efficient End-side Deployment

The deployment optimizations are highly effective in making MiniCPM-V practical for mobile devices:
- **Memory Footprint:** 4-bit quantization reduces the model's memory requirement from 16-17GB (fp16) to approximately 5GB, fitting within typical mobile phone RAM limits.
- **Inference Speed:** On a Xiaomi 14 Pro (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3), initial unoptimized CPU performance was slow (64.2s encoding latency, 1.3 tokens/s decoding throughput). Through sequential application of optimizations:
 - Memory optimization reduced image processing time from 45.2s to 31.5s.
 - Compilation optimization dramatically cut encoding latency to 17.0s and boosted decoding to 3.2 tokens/s.
 - Configuration optimization further improved decoding throughput to an impressive 8.2 tokens/s, which **surpasses typical human reading speed**.
 - NPU acceleration, specifically for visual encoding, further reduced visual encoding time from 3.7s to 1.3s, bringing it close to real-time.
- **Cross-Device Performance:** The model operates efficiently on various end-side devices including Xiaomi 14 Pro, vivo X100 Pro, and Mac M1, delivering acceptable latency and throughput. The NPU on the Xiaomi 14 Pro enables it to achieve encoding speeds comparable to a Mac M1.

#### 4.6. Adaptive Visual Encoding Effectiveness

The adaptive visual encoding strategy allows MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5 to effectively handle high-resolution inputs (1.8M pixels) with extreme aspect ratios (e.g., 10:1). The model accurately recognizes fine-grained details and OCR contents from such challenging inputs, demonstrating its robust visual perception.

In summary, the main findings underscore that MiniCPM-V successfully achieves its goal of providing GPT-4V-level performance in a compact, efficient, and trustworthy package, deployable directly on consumer-grade end-side devices, thus empirically validating the "Moore's Law for MLLMs" trend.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MiniCPM-V series, particularly MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5, represents a significant leap forward in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications and potential impacts across various domains.

#### 5.1. Democratization and Accessibility of Advanced AI

The most immediate and impactful significance is the **democratization of advanced MLLM capabilities**. By demonstrating that GPT-4V level performance can be achieved with significantly fewer parameters (8.5B compared to unstated, but undoubtedly much larger, proprietary models) and deployed efficiently on consumer-grade mobile phones, MiniCPM-V lowers the barrier to entry for developing and utilizing cutting-edge AI. This shifts powerful multimodal AI from being a cloud-exclusive service to an on-device utility, making it accessible to a much broader user base, regardless of internet connectivity or access to high-end cloud infrastructure.

#### 5.2. Unlocking New Application Scenarios

The ability to run MLLMs directly on end-side devices unlocks a vast spectrum of previously constrained or impractical applications:
- **Enhanced Mobile AI:** Imagine more intelligent personal assistants, real-time visual search, on-device content creation, and assistive technologies for the visually impaired, all running seamlessly on a smartphone without reliance on cloud servers.
- **Offline Functionality:** Critical for use in remote areas, during travel, or in situations with unreliable internet, enabling consistent AI performance.
- **Privacy and Security:** Local processing of sensitive user data, such as images and personal information, greatly enhances privacy and reduces the risk of data breaches associated with cloud transmission and storage. This is particularly crucial for enterprise applications and sensitive personal data.
- **Edge AI and Robotics:** Extends advanced multimodal understanding to drones, autonomous vehicles, industrial robots, and smart home devices, where low latency, energy efficiency, and offline capability are paramount.
- **Sustainability:** Reduced reliance on large data centers for inference can lead to lower overall energy consumption and a smaller carbon footprint for AI services.

#### 5.3. Validation of "Moore's Law for MLLMs"

The paper's conceptualization and empirical validation of "Moore's Law for MLLMs" is a crucial contribution. It provides a forward-looking perspective, suggesting that the trend of achieving higher performance with smaller model sizes will continue. This provides a clear roadmap and optimistic outlook for the future development of AI, pushing researchers to innovate in model architecture, training efficiency, and hardware-software co-design. MiniCPM-V stands as a strong proof-of-concept for this trajectory.

#### 5.4. Setting New Benchmarks for Efficiency and Performance

MiniCPM-V sets a new standard for efficient MLLMs. Its ability to outperform larger open-source models and even compete with proprietary giants on comprehensive benchmarks while maintaining a compact size and efficient on-device performance will undoubtedly spur further research and competition in this space. The detailed methodology, including adaptive visual encoding, sophisticated training stages, RLAIF-V for trustworthiness, and comprehensive deployment optimizations, offers valuable insights and techniques for the broader research community.

#### 5.5. Fostering Open-Source Innovation

The decision to open-source the MiniCPM-V models and the underlying techniques is significant. It encourages collaboration, allows other researchers and developers to build upon this work, and accelerates the collective progress towards more capable and deployable AI. This open approach can lead to rapid advancements and diversification of on-device AI applications.

#### 5.6. Addressing Critical AI Challenges

By explicitly tackling hallucination through RLAIF-V and achieving high multilingual proficiency, MiniCPM-V addresses two critical challenges for real-world AI adoption. Trustworthiness is essential for high-stakes applications, while multilingual support ensures global applicability and inclusivity.

In conclusion, MiniCPM-V is not merely another MLLM; it is a pioneering effort that redefines the practical boundaries of multimodal AI. Its significance lies in making state-of-the-art capabilities widely accessible, private, and efficient, thereby catalyzing innovation in on-device AI and shaping the future trajectory of the entire field.
