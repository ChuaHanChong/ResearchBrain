# Thinking with Visual Abstract: Enhancing Multimodal Reasoning via Visual Abstraction

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT), a novel paradigm that transforms complex visual inputs into simplified visual abstracts (e.g., sketches) to enhance Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) reasoning. This approach achieves an average performance gain of 16.72% over the GPT-4o baseline and significantly reduces computational costs, requiring only 27.53% of the runtime and 10.00% of the tokens compared to tool-augmented methods.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle to effectively reason with the inherent richness and often redundant nature of visual inputs.
- Existing reasoning paradigms, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and tool-augmented methods, add more information, increasing computational complexity, potential for hallucination, and overall costs.
- There is a lack of cognitively inspired paradigms that actively reduce redundant visual information, unlike how humans use abstract thinking to simplify complex inputs.

## Method

- The ==Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT) paradigm== converts raw images into simplified ==visual abstracts== (e.g., sketches, edge maps) using various methods, including pixel-based and deep learning-based techniques.
- MLLMs are prompted with both the original image and its visual abstract, encouraging a more concentrated visual reasoning mechanism by directing models to focus on essential visual elements.
- The methodology integrates the ==visual abstract== into a simple prompt template alongside the original query, explicitly instructing the MLLM to leverage both inputs for optimal performance.

## Results

- VAT achieved an average performance gain of **16.72%** over the GPT-4o baseline, outperforming Chain-of-Thought (CoT) by **3.56 percentage points** on diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- The approach significantly improved efficiency, requiring only **27.53%** of the runtime and **10.00%** of the token cost compared to tool-augmented methods like Visual Sketchpad.
- OpenSketch style visual abstracts consistently yielded the highest overall improvements, effectively preserving essential geometric and semantic information while filtering noise for object-centric perception and relation reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstract visual cues, derived from complex images, effectively enhance MLLM visual reasoning capabilities by filtering redundant information and highlighting salient features.
> - VAT offers substantial efficiency gains over existing methods, significantly reducing both runtime and token consumption by utilizing low-cost input tokens rather than expensive generated outputs.
> - The paradigm demonstrates generality across various MLLM architectures and scales, with larger models like GPT-4o showing more pronounced performance improvements, indicating an emergent capacity for abstract reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress, demonstrating impressive capabilities across a spectrum of multimodal tasks including visual perception, reasoning, and grounding. However, a persistent challenge lies in the inherent richness and often redundancy of visual inputs, which can overwhelm models and hinder effective reasoning.

Existing approaches to enhance MLLM reasoning primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Explicit Verbal Thinking:** Methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and its multimodal variants encourage LLMs to generate verbose, step-by-step intermediate reasoning rationales in natural language. While these improve performance and interpretability, they increase the volume of information and computational complexity, and can sometimes amplify issues like hallucination.
2. **Tool-Augmented Reasoning:** These approaches equip MLLMs with external tools (e.g., for object detection, segmentation, or visual grounding) and integrate their textual or visual outputs into the context. While powerful, these methods also add complexity, external dependencies, and can be computationally costly due to the generation and processing of additional concrete visual information.

This paper identifies a critical gap: unlike these "explicit thinking" paradigms that add more information, humans often employ "abstract thinking" to simplify complex or lengthy inputs, extracting only essential features. The research landscape thus lacks a robust, cognitively inspired paradigm that *reduces* redundant visual information rather than adding to it.

"Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT)" directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel reasoning paradigm for MLLMs that emulates human abstract visual cognition. By converting raw images into simplified visual abstracts (e.g., sketches), VAT aims to filter out noise and emphasize salient structural, conceptual, and relational cues. This approach is positioned as a **complementary alternative** to CoT (focusing on visual abstraction rather than verbal elaboration) and a **more efficient alternative** to tool-using agents (avoiding the generation of costly, concrete visual traces). The work leverages recent advancements in deep learning-based sketch representation, which allows for the abstraction and compression of visual context while retaining crucial semantic information, thus building upon established techniques in visual understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT) stems from observing human cognitive strategies when processing complex visual information. Humans tend to employ abstract thinking to simplify verbose or intricate messages, transforming them into concise, essential representations. The authors note that while images convey rich detail, they often contain redundant information that can detract from an MLLM's reasoning performance. Existing explicit thinking paradigms, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and tool-augmented methods, exacerbate this issue by introducing even more verbose intermediate steps or additional visual/textual information, thereby increasing complexity and potential for hallucination.

Against this backdrop, the **key objective** of this research is to introduce Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT) as a novel reasoning paradigm that prompts MLLMs with visual abstracts instead of explicit verbal thoughts or elaborate guidance. The core idea is to encourage a more concentrated visual reasoning mechanism by reducing redundant visual information and directing models to focus on essential visual elements.

Specifically, the paper aims to investigate the following questions:
1. **Enhancement of Reasoning:** Whether visual abstracts can effectively enhance the visual reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. This tests the fundamental hypothesis that abstract visual cues are beneficial.
2. **Task Applicability:** To identify which types of multimodal reasoning tasks are most amenable to VAT and which might not benefit as much, or even be hindered. This explores the practical scope and limitations of the paradigm.
3. **Impact of Abstraction Types:** To understand how different forms or "styles" of visual abstraction (e.g., edge detection, semantic sketches) influence the effectiveness of VAT. This delves into the mechanics of what constitutes a useful visual abstract.

In essence, the motivation is to align MLLM visual reasoning more closely with human cognitive processes, leading to more effective and efficient performance by simplifying the visual input rather than augmenting it with more explicit details.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT) paradigm centers on transforming complex visual inputs into simplified yet semantically rich visual abstracts. This process is designed to mimic human cognitive abstraction, guiding MLLMs to focus on essential visual elements for reasoning.

**Core Concept of VAT:**
The methodology introduces a visual abstraction `V_a` as a complementary input to the MLLM. For a given multimodal query `Q = [I; T]` (where `I` is the input image and `T` is the input text), VAT transforms the image `I` into `V_a` using a function `T: I → V_a`. The MLLM then reasons over a joint space of language and visual abstraction (`L ∪ V_a`), attending to both the original image and its abstract counterpart. This contrasts with CoT (`Q → (T_chain, A)`) which relies on verbal intermediate steps `T_chain`, and tool-augmented reasoning `(Q, V_c) → A` which uses concrete visual traces `V_c` from external tools.

**Visual Abstract Transformation:**
The paper explores various methods for generating visual abstracts, ranging from simple code-based conversions to advanced deep learning-based semantic sketch generation. These methods are designed to preserve different types of visual essence:
- **Direct Extraction (Pixel-based):**
 - **Canny Edge Detection:** Extracts prominent edges.
 - **Image Binarization:** Converts images to binary maps, emphasizing silhouettes.
- **Freehand Sketch Conversion (Deep Learning-based):** These models are trained to generate sketches that capture both geometric and semantic information, filtering out irrelevant details. Examples include:
 - **PhotoSketch:** Focuses on boundary-like drawings, capturing outlines and salient inner edges.
 - **Contour Style:** Emphasizes object contours.
 - **Anime Style:** Provides artistic, stylized abstractions.
 - **OpenSketch:** Aims to preserve geometric details (depth, shape) and rich semantic information, often yielding the most effective results.

**Prompting Strategy:**
The visual abstract (`<sketch of images>`) is incorporated into a simple prompt template alongside the original query (`<question>`) and the input image. The prompt explicitly instructs the MLLM to utilize both the original image and the visual abstract, emphasizing the abstract's role in highlighting spatial, structural, relational, and conceptual features. The instruction encourages the model to fully leverage the sketches to achieve optimal performance, guiding its reasoning without generating verbose intermediate steps.

**Experimental Setup and Evaluation:**
To validate VAT, experiments were conducted across a diverse set of MLLMs and multimodal reasoning benchmarks:
- **Models:** Proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0) and open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MiniCPM-V 2.6) were used to assess generality across scales and architectures.
- **Benchmarks:** A comprehensive suite of tasks covering different reasoning capabilities:
 - **Object-Centric Perception:** MME (existence, count, color), BLINK (count), HallusionBench (illusion), CoSpace (DIR-Obj).
 - **Object Relation Reasoning:** A newly constructed "Odd-One-Out" benchmark, and BLINK (semantic/visual correspondence).
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** MME (position), BLINK (spatial), CoSpace (DIR-Rec, ROT-Ang, ROT-Dif).
 - **Commonsense Reasoning:** MME (commonsense reasoning), BLINK (functional correspondence).
- **Metrics:** Accuracy (ACC) for most tasks, and SUM(ACC, ACC+) for MME following its original paper, where ACC+ denotes correctness for both sub-questions.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Type of Visual Abstract:** Investigated the performance impact of Canny, Binary, PhotoSketch, Contour, Anime, and OpenSketch.
 - **Prompting Formats:** Compared "Image-only," "Vis.Abs-only," "VAT w/o Vis.Abs" (repeating the original image), and "VAT (Best)" (full VAT setup).
 - **Efficiency:** Measured token consumption cost and runtime against CoT and Visual Sketchpad.
 - **Compatibility:** Explored combining VAT with CoT for complex tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate that Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT) significantly empowers Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) across a variety of visual perception and reasoning tasks, offering notable advantages in both performance and efficiency.

**Overall Performance Gains:**
- VAT achieves an impressive average gain of **16.72%** over the GPT-4o baseline (using the best visual abstract type), outperforming CoT's average gain of 13.16%.
- Across other models, VAT consistently surpasses both CoT and the visual-augmented concrete reasoning method (Scaffold). For example, on Gemini-2.0, VAT achieves the top performance on nine out of thirteen tasks.

**Efficiency Improvements:**
- **Time-efficiency:** VAT requires only 27.53% of the runtime compared to tool-augmented methods like Visual Sketchpad, and 67.62% of the runtime compared to CoT.
- **Cost-effectiveness:** VAT significantly reduces token costs, consuming only 10.00% of the tokens used by Visual Sketchpad. Compared to CoT, VAT shows only a 34.72% increase in token consumption while yielding higher accuracy, primarily by using low-cost input tokens rather than expensive generated tokens.

**Impact of Visual Abstract Types (Ablation Study):**
- **OpenSketch** style visual abstracts consistently yield the highest overall improvements for GPT-4o, followed closely by PhotoSketch. These styles are effective because they largely preserve essential geometric details (depth, shape) and rich semantic information while filtering noise.
- Even simpler pixel-based methods like Canny edge detection or binarization contribute noticeable gains, highlighting that the core benefit of VAT lies in information reduction and focusing on salient features.
- Different tasks exhibit preferences for specific abstract styles; for instance, BLINK-spatial tasks benefit from simpler binary or PhotoSketch abstractions, which reduce visual information most aggressively.

**Impact of Prompting Formats (Ablation Study):**
- Prompting formats that include visual abstracts, whether used alone ("Vis.Abs-only") or alongside the original image ("VAT (Best)"), consistently achieve the strongest performance across tasks. This confirms that the visual abstract itself, and not just the two-image input format, is key to VAT's effectiveness.

**Task Specificity and "Why VAT Works":**
- VAT is particularly effective in **object-centric perception** and **object relation reasoning** tasks, where it helps MLLMs isolate core object features, highlight contours, and understand structural relationships by blurring background clutter.
- For highly reasoning-intensive tasks like spatial reasoning and commonsense reasoning, while VAT still provides improvements over the baseline, its average advantages over CoT are relatively limited. This suggests that these tasks may still benefit from explicit verbal processing. However, VAT still assists by reducing visual redundancy.
- The effectiveness stems from the "visually intangible form" of abstracts, which filters redundant information and extracts essential conceptual, contoured, and structural features.

**Generality Across MLLMs:**
- While all investigated MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-32B) benefit from VAT, the extent of improvement varies. GPT-4o exhibits the most substantial gains (15.33%), followed by Gemini-2.0 Pro (5.51%), and then Qwen2.5-VL-32B (2.03%). This disparity is attributed to the larger models' ability to better adapt to and integrate abstract thinking patterns due to their scale, extensive training data, and sophisticated architectures.
- Smaller open-source models show more nuanced results, where VAT excels in visually intensive perception tasks, while CoT might still be more effective for verbal logical inference due to their pretraining biases and capacity constraints.

**Compatibility with CoT:**
- VAT can be complementary to CoT in specific knowledge-intensive visual reasoning tasks (e.g., visual planning tasks in CoSpace, commonsense reasoning in MME). In these scenarios, combining both paradigms can elicit both verbal concrete and visual abstract thinking, leading to further performance gains. However, this combination is not universally beneficial, as the added complexity of CoT can sometimes conflict with VAT's emphasis on simplicity.

**Failure Cases:**
- VAT's performance can degrade when the original image is already simple, offering little room for beneficial simplification.
- It may also fail if critical fine-grained visual details, essential for accurate comprehension (e.g., counting small, densely packed objects), are inadvertently removed during the abstraction process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT) represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), offering a novel and highly effective paradigm for enhancing their visual reasoning capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Novel Cognitively-Inspired Reasoning Paradigm:** VAT introduces a fundamentally new approach to MLLM reasoning, directly inspired by human abstract thinking. Unlike prevailing methods that either add verbose verbal rationales (Chain-of-Thought) or integrate concrete visual information from external tools, VAT focuses on *reducing* redundant visual detail to emphasize essential elements. This shift in perspective opens up a new avenue for designing more intuitive and effective reasoning strategies for AI.

2. **Significant Performance Enhancement:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated by VAT across diverse tasks and various MLLM architectures (particularly for advanced models like GPT-4o) highlight its practical utility. Achieving an average gain of 16.72% over GPT-4o, surpassing CoT, indicates a robust improvement in MLLMs' ability to perform conceptual, structural, and relational reasoning tasks.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** A critical impact of VAT is its superior efficiency. By leveraging low-cost input tokens for visual abstracts instead of generating expensive intermediate verbal or concrete visual outputs, VAT drastically reduces both token consumption and runtime. This makes MLLM reasoning more computationally feasible, sustainable, and scalable for real-world applications, especially in environments with resource constraints or high-throughput demands.

4. **Deeper Understanding of MLLM Capabilities:** The varying degrees of benefit observed across different MLLM scales (e.g., GPT-4o vs. Qwen2.5-VL) provide valuable insights into the emergent abstract reasoning capabilities of larger models. It suggests that advanced MLLMs are not merely processing explicit information but are capable of interpreting and leveraging abstract representations, moving closer to human-like cognitive processes. This encourages further research into how MLLMs develop and utilize such high-level cognitive functions.

5. **Complementary to Existing Paradigms:** VAT's proven compatibility with CoT for knowledge-intensive tasks showcases its potential to be integrated into hybrid reasoning frameworks. This allows for a flexible approach where models can simultaneously employ visual abstract thinking and verbal explicit reasoning, drawing on the strengths of each paradigm to tackle complex multimodal problems.

6. **Future Research Directions:** This work lays a foundation for exploring a broader spectrum of cognitively inspired reasoning paradigms. It encourages further investigation into:
 - **Diversity of Abstracts:** Developing more sophisticated and task-adaptive visual abstraction techniques.
 - **Generality Beyond MLLMs:** Exploring the applicability of abstract thinking concepts to general language models.
 - **Real-world Embodied AI:** Testing VAT in dynamic, real-world scenarios beyond current benchmarks, which could unlock new possibilities for AI in robotics, autonomous systems, and human-computer interaction.

In conclusion, Visual Abstract Thinking offers a powerful, efficient, and cognitively aligned method for enhancing MLLM performance. Its immediate impact lies in improving current MLLM applications, while its long-term potential resides in reshaping how we design and understand AI's reasoning capabilities, drawing closer parallels to human intelligence. The authors explicitly state that the work is "less likely to pose significant risks of negative societal impact," aiming to improve AI's utility and efficiency responsibly.
