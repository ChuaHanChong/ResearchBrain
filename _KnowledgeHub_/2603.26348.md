# Reflect to Inform: Boosting Multimodal Reasoning via Information-Gain-Driven Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> VRE (Visual Re-Examination) is a self-evolving post-training framework that teaches a multimodal LLM to pause mid-reasoning and re-attend to its own image tokens, with no extra visual inputs or teacher model. Difficulty-aware rollouts plus a Reflection Information Gain filter build cold-start data, then GRPO with a reflection reward and a post-RL self-distillation loop lift Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 7.4 points on V*-Bench while cutting hallucination.

## Problem

- Long-form multimodal generation drifts off image evidence onto textual priors, so prior models hallucinate exactly when late-stage visual re-verification of fine details matters most.
- Explicit interventions (injecting high-resolution patches, overlaying visual markers, interleaving new visual tokens) disrupt the autoregressive input pipeline, need heuristic design, and add heavy compute overhead.
- Neither explicit nor naive implicit re-looking specifies what evidence to retrieve, so re-examination degenerates into self-paraphrasing; teacher distillation instead imports alien visual biases.

## Method

- ==Difficulty-aware partitioning== splits samples by pass rate over eight rollouts into ==stable==, ==unstable== and ==intractable==, then synthesizes ==Elaborative==, ==Corrective== and ==Recheck reflections== by re-feeding each trace to the same policy.
- A **Reflection Information Gain** criterion with ==rejection sampling== keeps only traces adding new visual evidence; ==homologous reconstruction== reuses the model's own visual backbone to keep perception and reasoning aligned.
- ==Cold-start SFT== then ==GRPO== with format, LLM-judged accuracy and reflection rewards, followed by ==post-RL self-distillation== that revisits discarded hard prompts to build the next SFT round.

## Results

- On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VRE gains **+7.4** on V*-Bench (**83.8** overall), **+5.1** ChartQA, **+7.5** OCRBench_v2 zh, beating tool-using Thyme (**82.2**) and DeepEyesV2 (**81.8**) without tools.
- Ablation: SFT alone reaches **80.1** V*-Bench but only **+0.4** MathVerse; adding RLVR jumps MathVerse to **52.5**; iterative SFT tops out at **53.1** and **71.2** MathVista.
- Rejection-sampling stats: all-correct queries rise **30.8% to 58.1%**, all-wrong halves **12.7% to 5.7%**; the policy solves **68.1%** of previously unsolvable hard queries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-generated traces stay on the policy's own manifold: lower KL than GPT-5-distilled reflections, faster RL convergence, no alien perception bias imported from a stronger teacher.
> - Format imitation is not grounding: SFT installs the reflection template but only a reward on information gain removes redundant no-gain paraphrasing and unlocks reasoning gains.
> - RLVR has a usable window, not a monotone curve; cap it near peak and consolidate gains with self-distillation rather than training longer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has demonstrated capabilities in vision-language problem-solving. These models integrate visual and textual information to generate responses, exhibiting strong performance in various tasks. However, a persistent challenge in MLLMs is the phenomenon of "visual drift," where models, particularly during long-form generation, progressively shift their attention away from image evidence and rely more on textual priors. This often leads to ungrounded reasoning and hallucinations, especially in tasks requiring late-stage visual re-verification or fine-grained detail extraction.

Existing approaches to mitigate visual drift often involve explicit visual interventions, such as dynamic injection of high-resolution patches, superimposing visual markers, or interleaving new visual tokens. While these methods can be effective, they typically disrupt the standard autoregressive input pipeline, introduce complex heuristic designs, and incur substantial computational overhead. An alternative line of research suggests that MLLMs possess a latent capability for implicit visual refocusing, allowing them to re-attend to original visual tokens without external additions. However, these implicit paradigms often lack specific guidance on which visual evidence to retrieve, frequently leading to redundant textual self-paraphrasing rather than capturing missing visual clues.

Furthermore, post-training optimization, including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), has become a standard practice for enhancing reasoning capabilities in large language models. While RLVR has shown promise in structured domains like mathematics and code generation, its direct application to MLLMs often leads to performance degradation due to over-optimization and "multimodal reward hacking." This involves models generating verbose textual illusions to game the verification system while neglecting actual visual input, and can also lead to catastrophic forgetting of foundational visual capabilities.

This work contributes to addressing these limitations by proposing a framework that aims to transform the latent, unreliable implicit visual re-examination capability into a robust and learnable policy. It seeks to achieve this without architectural changes, additional visual inputs, or external teacher models, thus avoiding the computational overhead of explicit interventions and the potential biases of strong-to-weak distillation from proprietary models. The research positions itself as a self-evolutionary approach that allows MLLMs to autonomously improve their image-grounded reasoning, thereby enhancing perceptual reliability and reducing hallucinations, particularly in complex, long-chain reasoning scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Visual Drift and Hallucinations in Long-Form Multimodal Generation:** The authors identify a recurring failure mode in MLLMs, termed "visual drift," where models progressively detach from visual evidence as generation length increases. This reliance on textual priors leads to ungrounded reasoning and severe hallucinations. The motivation is to counteract this fundamental instability, especially in tasks demanding late-stage visual re-verification, such as extracting fine-grained text or deducing intricate spatial layouts.

2. **Activating Latent Implicit Visual Re-Examination:** Through preliminary attention analysis, the research observes that MLLMs possess a "latent capability for late-stage visual verification," which they term "Implicit Visual Re-Examination." This capability is present but not consistently activated, manifesting as occasional, prompt-dependent re-allocation of attention to original image tokens. The objective is to transform this sporadic and unreliable ability into a robust and learnable policy for purposeful visual introspection.

3. **Developing a Self-Evolving Training Framework:** Current methods often resort to explicit visual interventions or strong-to-weak distillation from proprietary models. The authors are motivated to explore an alternative path: enabling MLLMs to self-evolve. This involves designing a framework where the model autonomously learns *when* to initiate an implicit visual look-back and *how* to extract missing visual evidence, without relying on external visual inputs, architectural changes, or stronger teacher models that might introduce distribution shifts or inductive biases.

4. **Ensuring Actionable Visual Information Through Information Gain:** A critical bottleneck in both explicit and naive implicit paradigms is the lack of strict guidance on *what* specific visual evidence to retrieve. Without a principled supervision signal, re-look behavior can degenerate into redundant self-paraphrasing. The objective is to transform unguided introspection into a purpose-driven mechanism by making visual information actionable through an "Information Gain" criterion. This criterion ensures that reflections introduce genuine, corrective visual evidence.

5. **Mitigating Post-Training Pitfalls in MLLMs:** While reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has been effective in other domains, its application to MLLMs can lead to "multimodal reward hacking" and "catastrophic forgetting" of visual capabilities. The motivation is to design an RLVR approach that fundamentally prevents textual reward hacking and ensures that intermediate reasoning steps remain anchored to the image, promoting authentic, image-grounded deduction.

In summary, the key objective is to propose and implement Visual Re-Examination (VRE), a self-iterative framework that enables MLLMs to autonomously and reliably perform visual introspection during long-form reasoning. This framework aims to combat visual drift and hallucinations by activating and guiding the model's latent visual re-attention capabilities through an evidence-driven, information-gain-guided, and perception-reasoning aligned self-training process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is Visual Re-Examination (VRE), a self-iterative training framework designed to strengthen implicit visual re-attention during long-form reasoning. VRE does not require architectural changes, additional visual inputs, or external teacher models. Instead, it promotes iterative self-improvement through a closed-loop paradigm comprising three primary phases.

#### 3.1. Reflection Data Construction for Cold-Start SFT

This initial phase aims to elicit and standardize the model's latent visual reflection behavior using its own distribution.

1. **Difficulty-Aware Sample Partitioning:**
 - Given a multimodal sample (image `i`, question `q`, ground-truth answer `a_s`), `N` independent rollout trajectories are generated from the base MLLM (`π_θ`).
 - A "pass rate" (`P_pass(s)`) is calculated for each sample, representing the proportion of correct answers among the `N` rollouts. This metric serves as a proxy for the model's intrinsic competence.
 - Samples are partitioned into three regimes:
 - **Stable (`P_pass(s) = 1`):** All rollouts are correct. These samples establish format priors and preserve mastered abilities.
 - **Intractable (`P_pass(s) = 0`):** All rollouts fail. These are discarded to avoid forcing imitation of capabilities beyond the model's intrinsic capacity, distinguishing VRE from strong-to-weak distillation.
 - **Unstable (`0 < P_pass(s) < 1`):** Inconsistent performance, indicating the model's capability boundary. These queries are considered ideal for strengthening implicit visual re-examination.

2. **Information-Gain-Driven Reflection Data Synthesis:**
 - Rollout trajectories from the Unstable and Stable regimes are categorized into `T_stable(s)`, `T_right_unstable(s)`, and `T_wrong_unstable(s)`.
 - A standardized response format is enforced, separating initial reasoning from a `reflection` block and a final `answer` block.
 - To synthesize the `reflection` block, the model's initial reasoning trace (`τ_img`) is concatenated with the original multimodal input and re-fed into the *same policy model* for a second inference pass. This "homologous reconstruction" ensures perception-reasoning alignment and prevents perceptual discrepancies from external knowledge sources.
 - Three types of reflection steps are synthesized based on an Information Gain criterion, ensuring reflections introduce additional visual evidence, corrective grounding, or robustness verification:
 - **Elaborative Reflection (`τ ∈ T_stable(s)`):** Encourages broader visual exploration and supplementary evidence grounding to enhance confidence and coherence.
 - **Corrective Reflection (`τ ∈ T_wrong_unstable(s)`):** Explicitly identifies prior visual misinterpretations, localizes erroneous regions, and regrounds reasoning on correct visual evidence.
 - **Recheck Reflection (`τ ∈ T_right_unstable(s)`):** Enforces deliberate visual re-attention to verify that no cues were misperceived or overlooked, improving robustness.

3. **Cold-Start SFT:**
 - High-quality VRE trajectories (those with correct final predictions) form a filtered supervision set (`D_cold`).
 - The base MLLM is fine-tuned on `D_cold` to obtain the cold-start model (`π^(0)_SFT`), which is endowed with basic VRE capability.

#### 3.2. Closed-Loop RLVR and SFT

This phase iteratively refines the model's reflection capabilities using reinforcement learning.

1. **RL Data Construction:**
 - The `π^(0)_SFT` model performs dynamic rejection sampling on training queries.
 - Only reflection-formatted trajectories passing verifiable outcome checks are retained.
 - Intractable queries and already-mastered cases are discarded. The remaining "unsettled queries" (showing partial correctness, instability, or reflection sensitivity) form a focused RL data pool.

2. **GRPO and Reward Evaluation:**
 - The policy is optimized on the RL data using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which maximizes relative advantage while constraining KL divergence against the Cold-Start model.
 - A comprehensive reward function `R(τ_i)` is formulated as a weighted combination of three components:
 - **Format Reward (`R_form`):** A penalty-based component (0, -1, or -2) that enforces structural integrity (e.g., correct tag balance, non-empty/meaningful reflection, reasonable answer length).
 - **Accuracy Reward (`R_acc`):** A binary reward (0 or 1) determined by an LLM-based judge, evaluating the semantic correctness of the final answer against ground truth.
 - **Reflection Reward (`R_refl`):** A binary reward (0 or 1) determined by an LLM-based scorer, evaluating the quality of the `reflection` block based on whether it introduces meaningful, information-gain visual evidence.

3. **Post-RL Self-Distillation:**
 - After RL, the updated policy (`π^(0)_RL`) is obtained.
 - To address potential reward overfitting or degradation from prolonged RL, an iterative refinement phase is introduced. The `π^(0)_RL` model revisits previously discarded "hard prompts" and generates new reasoning traces.
 - Rejection sampling retains high-quality traces, constructing an updated SFT dataset (`D_new`).
 - Fine-tuning the `π^(0)_RL` model on `D_new` yields `π^(1)_SFT`, closing the self-evolution loop and consolidating gains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VRE framework was evaluated across a diverse suite of multimodal benchmarks, demonstrating consistent improvements in reasoning accuracy and perceptual reliability.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Competitive Performance at 7B Scale:** VRE, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B as its base model, achieved high performance among 7B-scale models without relying on explicit tool invocation or architectural modifications.
- **High-Resolution Perception and Document Understanding:** VRE obtained an 83.8% Overall score on V*-Bench, outperforming tool-augmented reasoning models like Thyme (82.2%) and DeepEyesV2 (81.8%). It showed substantial absolute gains over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B: +7.4% on V*-Bench, +5.1% on ChartQA, and +7.5% on OCRBench_v2. These results indicate that implicit visual re-examination effectively addresses visual drift without external cropping or zooming tools.
- **Mathematical and Multi-Disciplinary Reasoning:** VRE achieved 71.2 on MathVista and 53.1 on MathVerse, with absolute gains of +3.0% and +3.9% over the base model, respectively. Consistent improvements were also observed on WeMath (+6.6% to 68.7), MathVision (+2.8%), LogicVista (+2.8%), and MMMU (+0.9%). The 7B-parameter VRE model narrowed, and in some cases bridged, performance gaps with larger closed-source proprietary systems.

#### 4.2. Mechanism Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Efficacy of Cold-Start SFT:** Initial SFT provided immediate and substantial gains on perception-heavy and structured understanding tasks (e.g., V*-Bench, ChartQA, OCRBench_v2), confirming its role in establishing the `reflection` format. However, gains on complex reasoning tasks (e.g., MathVerse +0.4%) were marginal, suggesting SFT alone is insufficient for deep logical error correction.
- **Impact of RLVR:** The integration of RLVR significantly boosted reasoning capabilities. MathVerse saw a +2.9% improvement (from 49.6 to 52.5), and V*-Bench reached 83.8%. This validated that `Reflection Rewards` drive active utilization of visual evidence for logical self-correction, enabling the model to solve complex problems that SFT alone could not.
- **Necessity of Post-RL SFT:** The full VRE pipeline, including Post-RL SFT, provided consistent stability and further performance boosts across all benchmarks (e.g., MathVerse to 53.1, MathVista to 71.2). This stage helped salvage "hard prompts" from RL exploration, consolidating the policy and expanding the model’s cognitive boundary.
- **Visual Attention Re-allocation:** Attention heatmaps showed that while the base model exhibited "visual decay" (attention to image features monotonically fading with reasoning length), VRE demonstrated a "visual attention resurgence." Specifically, within the `reflection` block, attention weights on visual tokens spiked significantly, indicating active re-examination of visual evidence.
- **Introspective Visual Re-Examination:** Qualitative analysis of spatial attention revealed that during reflection, the model performed a targeted search-and-lock operation, shifting attention precisely to relevant spatial coordinates (e.g., focusing on a specific object like a dustpan). This confirmed that VRE identifies missing information, spatially re-grounds specific entities, and extracts correct attributes to rectify hallucinations.
- **Evolution of Reflection Paradigms:** Tracking the distribution of reflection types (Elaborative, Corrective, Recheck, No Gain) across training stages showed that after Cold-Start SFT, "No Gain" reflections were frequent. However, after RLVR and Post-RL Self-Distillation, the proportion of "No Gain" drastically decreased, validating the `Reflection Reward` in enforcing tangible visual evidence extraction. The distribution of gainful reflection types adapted to task difficulty, with Corrective reflections prominent in tasks requiring elusive detail or complex logic, Recheck behaviors in data-intensive tasks, and Elaborative reflections in OCR-heavy contexts.
- **Distributional Affinity (KL Divergence):** Comparing VRE's self-generated cold-start SFT data with GPT-5-distilled reflections, VRE exhibited significantly lower Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. This suggested that training on its own native probability manifold resulted in organically integrated and structurally compatible reflection capabilities, avoiding reasoning patterns alien to the base model.
- **Information Gain (Conditional Entropy):** VRE maintained a higher average conditional entropy (1.08) in its reflection blocks compared to the Semantic-back model (0.66). This indicated that VRE successfully breaks "textual inertia" by introducing novel, non-redundant visual details, serving as a proxy for genuine information gain.
- **Trajectory Distribution and Boundary Breakthrough:** Analysis of rollout trajectory outcomes showed that the proportion of "All Correct" queries surged from 30.8% (cold-start) to 58.1% (Post-RL SFT), while "All Wrong" queries more than halved (12.7% to 5.7%). Crucially, the Post-RL SFT model successfully generated at least one correct trajectory in 68.1% of queries that the cold-start model had initially failed on completely, demonstrating a "Boundary Breakthrough."
- **Training Dynamics and Stability:** During GRPO, the reward score exhibited rapid ascent and convergence around 50 steps, attributed to the high distributional affinity of self-evolution data. The average response length stabilized around 600 tokens, indicating efficient visual evidence extraction and successful mitigation of "reward hacking" through verbose self-repetition. The Post-RL SFT loss curve remained stable, suggesting smooth consolidation of exploratory gains into parametric memory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Visual Re-Examination (VRE) framework introduces a method for enhancing multimodal reasoning in MLLMs by addressing the critical problem of "visual drift" and associated hallucinations without relying on costly external interventions or proprietary model distillation. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Autonomous Self-Improvement:** VRE enables MLLMs to autonomously develop and refine their visual introspection capabilities through a self-iterative training strategy. This paradigm shift from external guidance to internal self-evolution could lead to more robust and adaptable MLLMs, reducing dependence on human annotation or expensive teacher models for continuous improvement.
- **Enhanced Perceptual Reliability and Reduced Hallucinations:** By consistently reactivating and guiding the model's latent visual re-attention, VRE grounds long-form reasoning trajectories more firmly in image evidence. This directly contributes to higher perceptual reliability, significantly reducing the occurrence of ungrounded reasoning and hallucinations, which are major limitations of current MLLMs.
- **Parameter-Efficient Reasoning:** The framework demonstrates substantial performance gains across diverse and challenging benchmarks (e.g., mathematical reasoning, high-resolution perception, document understanding) using a 7B-parameter model. This parameter efficiency suggests that deeper cognitive reasoning can be unlocked within existing MLLM architectures without the need for brute-force scaling, making advanced multimodal AI more accessible.
- **Improved Long-Chain Reasoning:** VRE's ability to trigger context-aware re-examination and correct prior misinterpretations is particularly impactful for tasks requiring extended, multi-step visual reasoning. This can lead to MLLMs that are more capable of tackling complex, real-world problems involving intricate visual analysis and logical deduction.
- **Novel Approach to Multimodal RL:** The introduction of "Reflection Information Gain" as a central criterion for data curation and reward formulation offers a solution to the challenges of "multimodal reward hacking" and "catastrophic forgetting" in MLLM post-training. By explicitly rewarding the extraction of novel visual evidence and penalizing redundancy, VRE provides a pathway for safe and continuous RL optimization in multimodal contexts.
- **Advancement in Understanding MLLM Mechanisms:** The detailed analysis of attention dynamics, spatial re-grounding, and the evolution of reflection paradigms offers insights into how MLLMs can be trained to introspect and self-correct visually. This mechanistic understanding contributes to the broader research in explainable and reliable AI systems.

In conclusion, VRE presents a methodology that could significantly advance the capabilities of MLLMs, making them more reliable, robust, and capable of complex, image-grounded reasoning by fostering an intrinsic ability for visual self-verification.
