# MapNav: A Novel Memory Representation via Annotated Semantic Maps for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> MapNav proposes Annotated Semantic Maps (ASMs) as a novel memory representation for Vision-and-Language Navigation, substituting historical frames with compact, semantically enriched, and textually annotated top-down maps. The system achieves state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE, RxR-CE, and real-world environments, simultaneously reducing memory footprint by up to 99.9% and processing time by 79.5%.

## Problem

- Existing continuous Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods rely on sequences of historical egocentric observations (e.g., RGB frames), leading to substantial storage and computational overhead.
- Raw historical frames lack structured and semantically rich understanding of the agent's past trajectory and the environment, which hinders effective spatial reasoning and path planning.
- Many conventional map representations are not inherently interpretable by general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs), limiting the models' ability to leverage their pre-trained knowledge for spatial reasoning.

## Method

- ==Introduces Annotated Semantic Maps (ASMs)== as a novel, compact, and semantically enriched top-down memory representation that replaces traditional historical observation frames.
- Develops a dynamic system to construct and update the ASM incrementally using egocentric RGB-D and pose data, mapping physical obstacles, explored regions, agent position, trajectory, and semantic object locations.
- Augments semantic regions in the map with explicit textual labels (e.g., "chair", "plant"), making the map directly interpretable by Vision-Language Models for end-to-end action prediction.

## Results

- MapNav (with ASM and 2 historical RGB frames) surpassed previous state-of-the-art methods that use all historical frames, achieving improvements of **2.5%** SR and **1.3%** SPL for R2R-CE, and **8.8%** SR and **6.5%** SPL for RxR-CE.
- The system achieved a constant memory footprint of 0.17MB, representing a **99.9%** reduction compared to frame-based methods (up to 276MB for 300 steps), and reduced the average processing time per step by **79.5%** (from 1.22s to 0.25s).
- Demonstrated superior performance in real-world environments across diverse scenes and instruction types, with notable gains of up to **30%** in Success Rate for semantic instruction following tasks compared to baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured, semantically enriched map representation with explicit textual annotations can effectively replace raw historical observation frames, significantly improving efficiency without sacrificing performance.
> - Explicitly grounding semantic information in the map with natural language labels enables the Vision-Language Model to better leverage its pre-trained linguistic knowledge for spatial reasoning and attention guidance.
> - By generating navigation actions directly in natural language and employing a robust pattern matching system, the architecture is simplified, eliminating the need for a separate action decoder and enhancing robustness to linguistic variations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) constitutes a crucial area within Embodied AI and multimodal understanding. It involves enabling autonomous agents to interpret natural language instructions and navigate complex, often previously unseen, environments. Traditional navigation tasks often rely on simpler inputs like visual data or predefined waypoints, whereas VLN demands a more sophisticated integration of language comprehension and visual perception to handle nuanced instructions and dynamic scenes.

Current VLN methodologies generally fall into discrete or continuous navigation paradigms. Discrete environment methods typically abstract navigation space into graph structures, often utilizing benchmarks like MP3D. While successful in these structured settings, they exhibit limitations in representing the continuous nature of real-world environments. Continuous navigation research, facilitated by simulators such as Habitat and benchmarks like R2R-CE and RxR-CE, addresses this by employing low-level action spaces (e.g., forward movement, rotation) or waypoint-based navigation.

A common challenge across existing continuous environment navigation methods is their reliance on historical robot observations (e.g., sequences of RGB frames) as spatio-temporal context for decision-making. This approach contributes to substantial storage requirements and computational overhead. Furthermore, historical frames, while providing raw visual data, often lack a structured and semantically rich understanding of the agent's past trajectory and the environment.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have significantly impacted VLN development, enabling the use of large-scale pre-trained models and VLN-specific pre-training. While these models offer increased flexibility and reasoning capabilities, many still depend on prompt engineering or retain the historical frame-based memory representation, perpetuating the aforementioned efficiency and structured understanding limitations.

Map representations have been explored in VLN to enhance environmental understanding and navigation performance. These include topological maps for viewpoint capture, value maps for waypoint selection, and semantic maps for object-level information. However, many conventional map representations are not inherently interpretable by general-purpose VLMs, requiring additional processing or limiting the VLM's ability to leverage its pre-trained knowledge.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the memory representation problem in continuous VLN. It aims to introduce a novel memory structure, the Annotated Semantic Map (ASM), that can effectively replace traditional historical frames. By doing so, MapNav seeks to reduce computational and storage demands, while simultaneously providing a VLM-interpretable, structured understanding of the environment, thereby enhancing navigation performance and robustness. The proposal of a new memory representation method contributes to the foundational architectural components of future VLN systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel end-to-end Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) model, termed MapNav, which incorporates an innovative memory representation mechanism. The primary motivation stems from observed limitations in current continuous environment VLN approaches.

Specifically, existing methods frequently rely on storing and processing sequences of historical egocentric observations, such as RGB frames, to provide spatio-temporal context for navigation decisions. This strategy presents several challenges:
1. **High Storage and Computational Overhead:** Accumulating historical frames for an entire trajectory significantly increases memory consumption and computational demands, particularly as trajectories lengthen. This scaling issue can impede the deployment of VLN agents in resource-constrained or real-time applications.
2. **Lack of Structured Understanding:** Raw historical frames, while rich in visual detail, do not inherently provide a structured, high-level understanding of the environment. Agents must implicitly infer semantic objects, obstacle layouts, and explored regions from these raw pixels, which can be inefficient and prone to errors. This lack of structured memory can hinder effective spatial reasoning and path planning.
3. **Limited VLM Interpretability:** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are powerful in integrating visual and linguistic information, directly feeding them sequences of raw historical images might not fully leverage their potential for structured spatial reasoning if the visual input lacks explicit semantic organization.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the MapNav project are:

- **Develop a Novel Memory Representation (Annotated Semantic Map - ASM):** Introduce ASM as an alternative to historical frames. The ASM is designed to be a compact, top-down, semantically enriched representation of the environment.
- **Enable Dynamic Map Construction and Update:** Design a system capable of constructing a base semantic map from egocentric RGB-D and pose data at the start of an episode and updating it incrementally at each timestep. This ensures real-time environmental awareness and accurate mapping of physical obstacles, explored areas, agent position, trajectory, and semantic object locations.
- **Integrate Explicit Textual Annotations:** Enhance the semantic map by automatically identifying key regions (e.g., objects, rooms) and augmenting them with explicit natural language labels. This aims to transform abstract semantic information into clear, linguistically grounded navigation cues, making the map directly interpretable and leverageable by VLMs.
- **Integrate ASM into an End-to-End VLM Framework:** Design MapNav as an end-to-end model that takes the current RGB observation, the generated ASM, and the natural language instruction as inputs, and directly outputs executable navigation actions (e.g., "move forward", "turn left") in natural language format.
- **Demonstrate Performance and Efficiency:** Empirically validate that MapNav, leveraging ASM, can achieve state-of-the-art navigation performance in diverse simulated and real-world continuous environments, while significantly reducing memory consumption and processing time compared to historical frame-based methods.
- **Facilitate Reproducibility and Future Research:** Release the ASM generation source code and a comprehensive dataset to provide valuable resources for the research community, fostering further advancements in memory representation for VLN.

These objectives collectively aim to establish ASM as a foundational memory representation, bridging the gap between raw perceptual data and high-level semantic understanding required for intelligent embodied navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MapNav framework is designed as an end-to-end Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based system for continuous Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN-CE). Its core innovation lies in leveraging Annotated Semantic Maps (ASM) as a structured memory representation, replacing traditional historical observation frames.

#### 3.1. Task Definition

The agent operates in continuous 3D environments, guided by natural language instructions ($I$). At each timestep $t$, it receives a first-person RGB observation ($X_t$) and predicts a low-level continuous action ($a_{t+1}$) from a defined action repertoire (e.g., move forward, turn left, turn right, stop). The task concludes when the agent determines it has reached the target destination specified by the instruction.

#### 3.2. MapNav Agent Architecture

The MapNav agent integrates current visual observations, the generated ASM, and the natural language instruction to directly predict navigation actions. The overall architecture consists of several key stages:

##### 3.2.1. Annotated Semantic Maps (ASM) Generation

This is a central component of MapNav, providing the novel memory representation. The process involves:
- **Semantic Map Initialization and Update:** A multi-channel tensor map $M$ (dimensions $C \times W \times H$) is initialized at the episode's start, with the agent positioned at the center. $C$ includes foundational channels (physical obstacles, explored regions, agent's current position, historical locations) and $n$ channels for distinct object categories.
- **Point Cloud Transformation and Projection:** At each timestep, egocentric RGB-D data from the agent's observation is transformed into 3D point clouds. These point clouds are then projected onto a 2D plane to create a top-down view.
- **Semantic Segmentation and Alignment:** A semantic segmentation module (Mask2Former) processes the RGB image to generate semantic masks for objects. These masks are aligned with the projected 3D point cloud data to accurately map object-specific semantic information into the corresponding channels of the semantic map.
- **Textual Annotation (ASM Specific):** This is the distinguishing feature of ASM.
 - **Semantic Region Identification:** Connected component analysis is performed on each object-specific channel of the semantic map to identify distinct semantic regions.
 - **Centroid Computation and Text Placement:** For each identified semantic region exceeding a minimum area threshold, its geometric centroid is computed. Explicit textual labels (e.g., "chair", "plant", "bed") are placed at these centroids, transforming abstract semantic regions into linguistically grounded spatial information. This linguistic augmentation aims to make the map directly interpretable by the VLM's pre-trained knowledge.
- **Dynamic Update:** The ASM is continuously updated at each timestep, reflecting newly explored areas, object detections, and the agent's changing pose and trajectory.

##### 3.2.2. Inputs Encoding

MapNav utilizes an architecture based on LLaVA-Onevision, which leverages a large VLM.
- **Visual Encoder:** A shared SigLIP vision encoder processes both the current RGB observation frame ($X_t$) and the ASM ($M_t$). This encoder extracts initial visual embeddings for both inputs.
- **Modality-Specific Projectors:** The visual features from $X_t$ and $M_t$ undergo parallel spatial-aware transformations (via a patch merge function) and are then projected into the language model's embedding space using distinct two-layer MLP projectors ($P_{obs}^{mlp}$ and $P_{map}^{mlp}$) with GELU activation.
- **Multimodal Representation:** The projected observation features ($E_t$) and map features ($E_{M_t}$) are concatenated with special task-structuring tokens (e.g., `[TASK]`, `[OBS]`, `[MAP]`) to form a unified multimodal representation. This representation serves as input to the VLM.

##### 3.2.3. Action Prediction

MapNav employs an end-to-end action prediction strategy where the VLM directly generates navigation commands in natural language text.
- **VLM Inference:** The concatenated multimodal representation and the natural language instruction are fed into the VLM (Qwen2-7B-Instruct backbone). The VLM leverages its generative capabilities to output a natural language response specifying the next action.
- **Pattern Matching System:** A robust pattern matching system parses the VLM's natural language output into discrete executable actions. This system utilizes case-insensitive regular expressions to recognize various synonymous expressions for fundamental actions: `P_FORWARD`, `P_TURN-LEFT`, `P_TURN-RIGHT`, `P_STOP`. This approach simplifies the architecture by eliminating the need for a separate action decoder and enhances robustness to linguistic variations.

#### 3.3. Experimental Details

- **Dataset Construction:** A comprehensive dataset of approximately 1 Million step-wise training pairs was created using a hybrid strategy:
 - **Expert Demonstrations:** Ground truth trajectories from R2R and RxR datasets (~300k pairs).
 - **Interactive Learning (DAgger):** Data collected by the pre-trained model interacting with the environment (~200k pairs), enhancing robustness to agent errors.
 - **Collision Recovery Samples:** Specialized data (~25k pairs) to teach obstacle avoidance and recovery, addressing scenarios rarely present in expert trajectories.
 - **Co-training:** Integrated with ~600k samples from general vision-language tasks (VQA, multi-image, video-based) to enhance VLM's general visual understanding.
- **Simulated Environments:** Evaluated on the VLN-CE benchmark in Habitat, using the `val-unseen` splits of R2R (1,839 episodes) and RxR (1,517 episodes) datasets.
- **Real-world Environments:** Experiments conducted across five diverse indoor environments (office, conference room, lecture hall, living room, tea room) with 50 instructions, encompassing both simple (basic actions) and semantic (object-oriented navigation) instructions.
- **Metrics:** Navigation Error (NE), Oracle Success rate (OS), Success Rate (SR), Success weighted Path Length (SPL), and normalized Dynamic Temporal Wrapping (nDTW). SPL is highlighted as the primary metric.
- **Implementation:** Trained on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for approximately 30 hours. Used Google's SigLIP-so400M as the vision encoder, Qwen2-7B-Instruct as the language model, and Mask2Former for semantic segmentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated MapNav's performance across simulated and real-world environments, as well as its efficiency and the contribution of its core components through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Methods

- **Simulated Environment (R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen splits):**
 - **Single-frame RGB comparison:** MapNav (w/ ASM + Cur. RGB) demonstrated substantial improvements over baselines using only single-frame RGB observations. Compared to NaVid (Cur. RGB), MapNav (w/o ASM + Cur. RGB) improved R2R SR by 14.1% and SPL by 15.7%, and RxR SR by 6.9% and SPL by 5.4%. The integration of ASM further enhanced performance, with MapNav (w/ ASM + Cur. RGB) showing improvements of 23.5% in SR and 26.5% in SPL for R2R over NaVid (Cur. RGB).
 - **ASM vs. All Historical Frames:** MapNav (w/ ASM + Cur. RGB) achieved performance comparable to NaVid (All Frames), which utilizes all historical RGB frames, indicating ASM's effectiveness as a memory representation for large models.
 - **Superior Performance with Limited Historical Frames:** When MapNav incorporated only two historical RGB frames (MapNav(w/ ASM + Cur. RGB + 2 His. RGB)), it surpassed previous SOTA methods that used all historical frames. Specifically, it achieved improvements of 2.5% in SR and 1.3% in SPL for R2R, and 8.8% in SR and 6.5% in SPL for RxR, compared to NaVid (All RGB Frames).

- **Real-World Environment (Diverse Scenes):**
 - MapNav (w/ ASM + Cur. RGB) consistently outperformed WS-MGMAP and Navid (both using all historical frames) in both Success Rate (SR) and Navigation Error (NE) across simple and semantic instruction following tasks in various real-world settings (Meeting Room, Office, Lecture Hall, Tea Room, Living Room).
 - Notable gains were observed in semantic instruction following tasks, such as improving SR by 30% in the Lecture Hall and Living Room compared to Navid, demonstrating strong semantic understanding in practical scenarios.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Efficiency Analysis:**
 - **Memory Consumption:** MapNav exhibited a constant memory footprint of 0.17MB, irrespective of trajectory length. In contrast, Navid's frame-based approach showed linear scaling, reaching 276MB for 300 navigation steps. This represents a substantial reduction in memory requirements.
 - **Average Processing Time:** MapNav reduced the average processing time per step by 79.5% (from 1.22 seconds for Navid to 0.25 seconds), due to processing only the current RGB frame and the compact ASM, rather than all historical frames.

- **Effect of ASM:**
 - A comparative analysis of different map variants (RGB-only, original top-down map, semantic map without textual annotations, and ASM) revealed a clear performance hierarchy.
 - The RGB-only baseline performed lowest. Moderate improvements were observed with the original and semantic map variants.
 - ASM consistently outperformed all other map configurations across all evaluated metrics, highlighting the benefits of semantic enrichment and explicit textual annotations for spatial reasoning.

- **Impact of Different Training Dataset Compositions:**
 - Increasing the number of R2R training samples (from 100K to 300K) led to performance improvements.
 - Integrating DAgger-generated samples resulted in the most significant performance gains, emphasizing the importance of interactive learning.
 - Including the RxR dataset provided modest additional improvements, particularly for diverse linguistic instructions.
 - The final configuration, incorporating specialized collision-aware training data, yielded slightly better performance across metrics, contributing to robustness.

- **Effect of Varying Numbers of Historical RGB Frames:**
 - The most substantial performance improvement was observed upon the initial integration of ASM with a single current RGB frame, significantly boosting SR (27% to 36%), SPL (23% to 34%), OS (41% to 50%), and reducing NE (7.2m to 5.2m).
 - Adding a small number of historical RGB frames (1 or 2) yielded incremental, modest improvements.
 - The gains plateaued with four historical RGB frames, indicating that ASM efficiently captures most of the necessary spatial information, making extensive temporal accumulation of raw frames less critical. This validates ASM as an efficient alternative.

- **VLM Attention Visualization:**
 - Analysis of attention heatmaps demonstrated that when processing ASMs, the VLM (Vicuna-13B) exhibited concentrated attention distributions (peak attention > 0.8) precisely aligned with labeled semantic objects and navigation-relevant areas (e.g., trajectory lines).
 - In contrast, attention patterns were diffuse for original top-down maps (peak < 0.3) and semantic maps (peak < 0.4), indicating limited semantic understanding without textual annotations.
 - This visualization confirmed that ASM's explicit textual annotations and structured trajectory representation effectively guide the VLM's attention, facilitating better grounding of navigational features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MapNav research introduces a notable advancement in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by proposing Annotated Semantic Maps (ASMs) as a novel memory representation. This work addresses several critical limitations of existing VLN methodologies and presents implications for the future development of embodied AI agents.

#### 5.1. Novel Memory Representation and Enhanced Spatial Understanding

The introduction of ASMs directly replaces the reliance on sequences of historical RGB frames, which is a common practice in VLN. This shift provides a compact, structured, and semantically rich memory representation. By integrating explicit textual labels directly onto a top-down semantic map, MapNav enables Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to leverage their pre-trained language understanding capabilities for spatial reasoning. This direct linguistic grounding allows for more intuitive interpretation of the environment's semantics (e.g., identifying "chair" or "bed" directly from the map) and aids in developing a structured spatial understanding that can be difficult to derive from raw visual observations alone.

#### 5.2. Significant Efficiency Gains

One of the most immediate impacts of MapNav is its contribution to efficiency. The research demonstrates a constant memory footprint (0.17MB) for ASMs, regardless of trajectory length, which is a substantial improvement over traditional frame-based methods that exhibit linear memory scaling (e.g., 276MB for 300 steps). Concurrently, MapNav achieves a considerable reduction in average processing time (79.5% faster), making VLN systems more scalable and suitable for deployment on resource-constrained platforms or in real-time applications where computational overhead is a critical factor.

#### 5.3. State-of-the-Art Performance in Diverse Environments

MapNav demonstrates superior navigation performance, achieving state-of-the-art results in both complex simulated environments (R2R-CE, RxR-CE) and diverse real-world settings. Its robust performance, especially in tasks involving semantic instructions, indicates its ability to interpret and act upon nuanced language commands effectively. The successful transfer of performance from simulation to real-world scenarios further validates the practical applicability and generalization capabilities of the ASM-based approach.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Future Embodied AI Research

By providing an efficient and semantically rich memory representation, MapNav establishes a foundation for future advancements in embodied AI. The explicit textual grounding in ASMs can potentially simplify the integration of VLMs into more complex tasks beyond navigation, such as interactive navigation, object manipulation, and human-robot collaboration, where a shared understanding of semantic context is crucial. The ability to abstract environmental details into a VLM-interpretable map can lead to more generalized and capable agents.

#### 5.5. Contribution to Reproducibility and Community Resources

The commitment to releasing the ASM generation source code and a comprehensive dataset (1 Million step-wise samples) is a significant contribution to the research community. This action fosters reproducibility of the presented results and provides valuable resources that can accelerate further research and development in VLN and related fields, lowering the barrier for other researchers to build upon this work.

In conclusion, MapNav introduces a paradigm shift in how embodied agents store and utilize environmental memory for navigation. By prioritizing structured, semantically annotated maps that are directly consumable by VLMs, the research offers a path towards more efficient, intelligent, and deployable navigation systems, with implications for a broader range of embodied AI applications.
