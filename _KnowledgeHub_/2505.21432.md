# Hume: Introducing System-2 Thinking in Visual-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Hume introduces a dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that integrates deliberative 'System-2 thinking' into robot control through value-guided action selection and cascaded denoising. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world robot platforms, particularly improving robustness and failure recovery in complex manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Generalist robot policies, often relying on reactive 'System-1-like' thinking, struggle with the nuances, dexterity, and fallibility inherent in real-world manipulation.
- Applying 'System-2 thinking' (deliberate reasoning) to robotics faces challenges due to the semantic ambiguity of robot actions and the need for high-frequency, real-time control.
- Existing dual-system robot architectures often do not perform effective 'thinking and reasoning' to genuinely guide lower-level action prediction, limiting their true deliberative capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces **Hume**, a dual-system ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model composed of a low-frequency System 2 (VLM backbone) for high-level deliberation and a high-frequency System 1 (lightweight visuomotor policy) for reactive control.
- Enables value-guided System-2 thinking by augmenting the VLM with an action denoising head to generate multiple action candidates and a novel value-query head to estimate their state-action values for best-of-N selection.
- Implements cascaded action denoising, where System 2 provides long-horizon, partially denoised action chunks that System 1 then continuously refines for precise, real-time execution.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.6%** on the LIBERO benchmark, securing first rank, and demonstrated significant improvements on SimplerEnv benchmarks (e.g., **+32.5%** over baseline on WidowX tasks).
- Attained high success rates in real-world zero-shot deployments, including **91%** on WidowX, **87%** on Franka, and excelled on complex tasks with the AgiBot G-1 humanoid robot (e.g., **88%** for 'Fold Shorts').
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of value-guided thinking (e.g., **-78%** real-world performance drop without it) and cascaded denoising (e.g., **-19%** real-world performance drop without System 1 refining System 2's output).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deliberative, value-guided System-2 thinking can be effectively integrated into Vision-Language-Action models for physical robot control, extending principles from Large Language Models.
> - A dual-system architecture with asynchronous, cascaded action denoising effectively balances the 'slowness' of deliberation with the 'fastness' required for real-time robotic control.
> - Explicit action evaluation via value functions and best-of-N selection significantly enhances robot robustness and enables recovery from failures in complex real-world tasks.
