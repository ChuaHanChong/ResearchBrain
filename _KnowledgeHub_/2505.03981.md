# X-Reasoner: Towards Generalizable Reasoning Across Modalities and Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework combining supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning enables vision-language models to develop generalizable reasoning capabilities through text-only training, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multimodal benchmarks like MMMU and MathVista while demonstrating successful transfer to specialized domains like medicine.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal models require expensive task-specific datasets and training
- Limited understanding of whether reasoning skills can generalize across modalities and domains

## Method

- **X-Reasoner** ==two-stage post-training approach== using text-only data on vision-language models
- Combines ==supervised fine-tuning using distilled long chain-of-thoughts== with ==reinforcement learning using verifiable rewards==

## Results

- Outperformed existing state-of-the-art models on multiple multimodal benchmarks (MMMU, MMMU-Pro, MathVista)
- Achieved new state-of-the-art results on medical benchmarks after domain-specific fine-tuning
- Ablation studies confirmed genuine multimodal reasoning rather than relying on text-based shortcuts

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mathematical reasoning trained on text serves as an effective "anchor domain" for developing generalizable reasoning capabilities
> - Text-only training can improve multimodal reasoning without requiring multimodal training data
> - Domain-specific fine-tuning further enhances performance in specialized areas like medicine

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper addresses several important trends and gaps in the current AI research landscape:

- **Multimodal Reasoning:** The paper directly addresses the growing interest in extending reasoning capabilities of AI models beyond text to include other modalities like vision. It acknowledges the recent progress in proprietary multimodal models (e.g., OpenAI's o3) but emphasizes the need for open-source research in this area.

- **Generalizability:** A key theme is the generalizability of reasoning skills. Much of the existing work in training reasoning models focuses on specific tasks or domains. This paper investigates whether reasoning skills learned from general-domain text data can transfer to other modalities and specialized domains like medicine.

- **Text-Only Training for Multimodal Tasks:** A significant contribution is the exploration of text-only training methods for improving multimodal reasoning. This is a departure from the common practice of training models directly on multimodal datasets, which can be expensive and task-specific.

- **Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning:** The paper leverages reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards as a method for improving reasoning capabilities. This is aligned with the growing interest in using RL to train LLMs and VLMs for various tasks, including reasoning.

- **Medical AI:** The paper highlights the application of the proposed model to the medical domain. This is a relevant area of research, as AI has the potential to significantly impact healthcare.

In essence, this work connects several active research areas and attempts to bridge the gap between text-based reasoning models and the more complex demands of multimodal and domain-specific reasoning. The open-source nature of this work enables reproducibility and future contributions from the research community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Primary Objective:** The central objective is to determine if reasoning capabilities can be generalized across different modalities (e.g., from text to vision) and domains (e.g., from general knowledge to medicine).

- **Underlying Question:** The core question the paper seeks to answer is: "Is reasoning generalizable across modalities and domains?"

- **Motivation:**
 - The observation that proprietary models (e.g., o3) exhibit strong multimodal reasoning.
 - The limitations of existing open-source research, which primarily focuses on text-only reasoning within limited domains.
 - The practical advantages of using general-domain text-only training data:
 - Compute efficiency compared to multimodal training.
 - Abundance of general-domain text data.
 - Verifiability of text-based reasoning data, allowing for easier reward signal generation.
 - The desire to avoid the cost and complexity of curating large, task-specific multimodal datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper's methodology revolves around a two-stage post-training approach applied to a vision-language model (VLM):

- **Base Model:** The starting point is an instruction-tuned VLM, specifically Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Distilled Long Chain-of-Thoughts (CoTs):**
 - The VLM is fine-tuned on general-domain text data (OpenThoughts-114k).
 - The training data includes long CoT reasoning traces distilled from a larger model (DeepSeek-R1).
 - A "forced-exiting" mechanism is implemented to mitigate "endless thinking" issues where the model fails to terminate its responses.

- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):**
 - The model is further trained using reinforcement learning.
 - The reward signal is based on the accuracy of the model's answers to mathematical textual questions.
 - The GRPO algorithm is used, with enhancements like a higher upper clipping threshold, token-level policy gradient loss, and reduced KL penalty.

- **X-REASONER and X-REASONER-MED:**
 - The model resulting from the two-stage training is named X-REASONER.
 - To further improve performance in the medical domain, X-REASONER is further fine-tuned on medical text data, creating X-REASONER-MED.

- **Evaluation:**
 - The models are evaluated on a range of benchmarks, including text-only and multimodal tasks, across general and medical domains.
 - Metrics include accuracy, average accuracy over multiple runs, majority-vote accuracy, and pass@n accuracy.
 - An ablation study is conducted to ensure that performance gains are not solely due to solving text-solvable examples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **General-Domain Text-Based Training Works:** The key finding is that text-only post-training, particularly the combination of SFT with long CoTs and RLVR, can significantly improve the reasoning capabilities of VLMs across both modalities and domains.

- **SFT and RL Complement Each Other:** SFT is effective for learning reasoning patterns, while RL helps to refine accuracy and generalization.

- **Mathematics as an "Anchor Domain":** Training on mathematical questions proves to be particularly effective for promoting generalizable reasoning.

- **X-REASONER Achieves State-of-the-Art Results:** X-REASONER outperforms existing state-of-the-art models (including those trained with multimodal data) on several challenging multimodal benchmarks (MMMU, MMMU-Pro, MathVista).

- **Domain-Specific Fine-Tuning Enhances Performance:** X-REASONER-MED, which is fine-tuned on medical text data, achieves new state-of-the-art results on medical benchmarks.

- **Genuine Multimodal Reasoning:** The ablation study confirms that X-REASONER is not simply relying on text-based shortcuts but is genuinely integrating visual information into its reasoning process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Demonstration of Generalizability:** The paper provides strong evidence that reasoning skills learned from general-domain text can be effectively transferred to other modalities and specialized domains.

- **Efficient Training Strategy:** The proposed text-only training approach offers a more efficient alternative to training models directly on expensive and task-specific multimodal datasets.

- **Advancement of Multimodal AI:** The findings contribute to the advancement of multimodal AI by demonstrating a viable path towards building models with more general and robust reasoning capabilities.

- **Potential Applications in Healthcare:** The success of X-REASONER and X-REASONER-MED in the medical domain suggests the potential for developing AI-powered tools that can assist healthcare professionals in diagnosis, treatment planning, and other tasks.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper highlights several avenues for future research, including:
 - Exploring the use of larger models and different base architectures.
 - Investigating the transfer of reasoning skills to more complex tasks like open-ended generation and interactive dialogue.
 - Continuing to explore the interplay between general-domain training and domain-specific fine-tuning.

Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of AI by demonstrating the potential for generalizable reasoning across modalities and domains through a carefully designed text-only training approach. The results have implications for the development of more efficient, robust, and versatile AI models, particularly in areas like multimodal AI and medical AI.
