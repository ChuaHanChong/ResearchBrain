# Multi-Agent Guided Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Multi-Agent Guided Policy Optimization (MAGPO) introduces an iterative framework that couples a centralized, autoregressive "guider" policy with decentralized "learner" policies, ensuring their alignment to bridge powerful centralized coordination with practical decentralized execution. The method achieved state-of-the-art results among CTDE approaches, outperforming baselines on 32 out of 43 tasks and matching fully centralized methods on a subset of these across diverse environments.

## Problem

- Existing Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) methods often underutilize centralized information, primarily via value functions, without directly coordinating agent actions during training.
- Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student (CTDS) frameworks suffer from scalability issues due to exponentially growing joint action spaces for the teacher, and an "imitation gap" caused by observation and policy asymmetries.
- Many cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods lack theoretical guarantees for monotonic policy improvement, leading to less stable and predictable learning outcomes.

## Method

- Introduces an iterative framework that couples a centralized, autoregressive "guider" policy (with global state access) with decentralized "learner" policies (with local observations).
- The guider is trained using a Policy Mirror Descent (**PMD**) framework to maximize an RL objective, while the learners are updated by minimizing KL divergence to the guider, ensuring alignment and monotonic improvement.
- Employs mechanisms like double clipping on the guider's policy ratio and guider backtracking to dynamically constrain the guider's exploration, ensuring its learned coordinated strategies remain realizable by the decentralized learners.

## Results

- MAGPO consistently outperformed all CTDE baselines on **32** out of **43** tasks and surpassed even fully centralized CTCE methods on **20** tasks across 6 diverse multi-agent environments.
- It demonstrated a considerable performance advantage over Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student (CTDS) approaches, effectively addressing the imitation gap, especially where centralized policies were not easily decentralizable.
- The framework exhibited competitive sample efficiency and maintained performance robustness even under reduced decentralized actor capacities, indicating its practical applicability for deployment with computational constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Principled alignment of a powerful centralized "guider" with decentralized "learner" policies can effectively bridge the gap between strong centralized coordination (CTCE) and practical decentralized execution (CTDE).
> - Actively constraining the centralized guider's policy to stay aligned with the decentralized learners throughout training is critical for mitigating the "imitation gap" inherent in CTDS frameworks, ensuring that coordinated strategies are decentralizably realizable.
> - The tuning of alignment strength (e.g., hyperparameter δ) between the guider and learner is crucial, as tighter constraints help in tasks where centralized policies are hard to decentralize, while looser constraints allow more freedom for complex coordination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is a framework designed for solving complex problems involving multiple interacting agents, such as autonomous driving and robot swarm coordination. Two fundamental challenges in MARL are the exponential growth of the joint action space, which impedes scalability, and the necessity for decentralized execution under conditions of partial observability, which complicates the learning process for individual agent policies.

A widely adopted paradigm to address these challenges is Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE). In CTDE, agents benefit from privileged global information during training to stabilize learning, but must execute actions independently based solely on their local observations at deployment. Historically, CTDE methods have largely fallen into two categories: value-based approaches, which use a centralized value function to guide decentralized utility functions (e.g., VDN, QMIX), and policy-based approaches, which leverage centralized value functions to inform decentralized policies (e.g., COMA, MAPPO). While these "vanilla CTDE" methods have demonstrated empirical success, they often utilize global information primarily through value functions, potentially underutilizing the full potential of centralized training for direct coordination.

Parallel advancements in single-agent Reinforcement Learning (RL) within Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) have explored similar concepts. Asymmetric actor-critic methods employ a critic with full state access during training while the actor operates on partial observations. Another paradigm is teacher-student learning, where a teacher policy, trained with privileged information, directly supervises a student policy operating under partial observability, facilitating the transfer of action-level knowledge.

The teacher-student paradigm has recently been extended to MARL, giving rise to the Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student (CTDS) framework. In CTDS, a centralized teacher policy, with access to global state, generates joint actions to guide coordinated exploration. Decentralized student policies are then trained to imitate these teacher actions. While CTDS aims to leverage centralized coordination more effectively, it introduces new challenges, including scalability issues due to the teacher operating in an exponentially growing joint action space, and a fundamental "imitation gap." This gap arises from observation asymmetry (teacher uses global state, students use local observations) and policy asymmetry (teacher's joint policy versus students' factorized, decentralized policies), which can lead to suboptimal performance as students may not be able to replicate the teacher's coordinated strategies.

Other related MARL paradigms include Heterogeneous Agent Reinforcement Learning (HARL), which updates agents sequentially and provides theoretical guarantees but lacks parallelism and parameter sharing benefits, and Centralized Training with Centralized Execution (CTCE). CTCE methods, exemplified by transformer-based approaches like MAT and Sable, can achieve state-of-the-art performance by treating the multi-agent problem as a single-agent problem with a large action space, offering strong coordination and theoretical guarantees. However, CTCE methods are often impractical for real-world scenarios requiring decentralized execution.

This work, Multi-Agent Guided Policy Optimization (MAGPO), positions itself as a framework that seeks to bridge the gap between the powerful coordination capabilities of CTCE and the practical necessity of CTDE. It aims to address the limitations of existing CTDE (underutilization of centralized information) and CTDS (scalability, imitation gap) by developing a principled, theoretically-grounded method that leverages centralized guidance effectively while ensuring decentralized deployability and offering monotonic policy improvement guarantees.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel MARL framework, Multi-Agent Guided Policy Optimization (MAGPO), that effectively leverages centralized training for coordinated policy learning while ensuring that the resulting policies are practical for decentralized execution under partial observability.

The motivation for MAGPO stems from several identified limitations of existing MARL paradigms:

1. **Underutilization of Centralized Training in Vanilla CTDE:** Many existing CTDE methods primarily use centralized information for value function estimation (e.g., centralized critics), but do not directly coordinate agent actions during training. This can lead to agents struggling with inter-agent coordination signals, requiring extensive exploration to converge on effective joint behaviors. The paper illustrates this with a "CoordSum" example where independent agents struggle to reach a target sum without explicit coordination.

2. **Scalability and Imitation Gap in Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student (CTDS) Frameworks:** While CTDS aims to provide stronger supervision by directly imitating a centralized teacher, it faces significant challenges:
 - **Scalability:** Learning a centralized teacher policy over the joint action space becomes computationally intractable as the number of agents increases due to the exponential growth of the action space.
 - **Observation Asymmetry:** The teacher often relies on privileged global information (e.g., full state) that is unavailable to the student policies, which must act based only on local observations. This mismatch prevents students from faithfully reproducing the teacher's behavior.
 - **Policy Asymmetry/Space Mismatch:** The teacher's policy operates in a joint, expressive action space, while student policies are factorized and decentralized. Coordinated patterns encoded in the teacher's policy may not be representable by the decentralized student policies, leading to an "imitation gap" and suboptimal performance. The CoordSum example further highlights this, showing how a centralized teacher's stochastic, coordinated strategy might fail to distill into independent decentralized policies.

3. **Lack of Theoretical Guarantees:** Many existing CTDE methods lack formal guarantees of monotonic policy improvement, making their learning process less stable or predictable.

MAGPO is motivated to overcome these limitations by introducing a principled design that:
- Integrates centralized guidance more deeply into the training process than vanilla CTDE, allowing for scalable, coordinated exploration.
- Explicitly aligns the centralized guidance with decentralized policies throughout training, mitigating the imitation gap and ensuring deployability under partial observability, unlike prior CTDS approaches.
- Provides theoretical guarantees of monotonic policy improvement.

The overall goal is to offer a theoretically grounded and practically deployable solution that combines the benefits of centralized coordination with the constraints of decentralized execution in MARL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MAGPO (Multi-Agent Guided Policy Optimization) proposes a framework that integrates a centralized, autoregressive "guider" policy with decentralized "learner" policies, ensuring their alignment throughout training. The methodology is structured around an iterative four-step procedure and grounded in theoretical guarantees.

**Formulation:**
The problem is framed as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP) within the CTDE paradigm.
- **Guider Policy:** A centralized policy, μ, has an autoregressive structure, meaning agents act sequentially conditioned on previous agents' actions: μ(a|s) = μ_i1(a_i1|s)μ_i2(a_i2|s, a_i1)...μ_in(a_in|s, a_i1:n-1). This structure allows for coordinated exploration using global state access during training.
- **Learner Policy:** A decentralized policy, π, is factorized, implying agents act independently based on their local observations: π(a|s) = Product_j=1^n π_ij(a_ij|s).

**Iterative Four-Step Procedure:**
MAGPO iteratively updates both policies:
1. **Data Collection:** The current guider policy, μ_k, is rolled out in the environment to collect trajectories. This enables coordinated exploration.
2. **Guider Training:** The guider policy, μ_k, is updated to ˆμ_k by maximizing an RL objective using the Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) framework. Specifically, this involves maximizing the expected Q-value of the guider while regularizing with a KL divergence to the previous guider policy: ˆμ_k = arg max_μ {η_k⟨Q_μk(s, ·), μ(·|s)⟩ − D_KL(μ(·|s), μ_k(·|s))}. PMD is a general policy gradient framework that can subsume algorithms like PPO and TRPO.
3. **Learner Training:** The decentralized learner policy, π_k, is updated to π_k+1 by minimizing the KL divergence D_KL(π, ˆμ_k). This step aims to align the decentralized learner with the improved centralized guider.
4. **Guider Backtracking:** The guider policy for the next iteration, μ_k+1, is reset to the newly updated learner policy, π_k+1. This ensures that the guider does not diverge too far into policy spaces unrepresentable by the decentralized learners, thereby mitigating the imitation gap.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
- **Monotonic Policy Improvement (Theorem 4.1):** MAGPO provides a theoretical guarantee that the expected cumulative return of the joint learner policies, V_ρ(π_k+1), will monotonically improve over iterations (V_ρ(π_k+1) ≥ V_ρ(π_k)). This is attributed to the guider identifying an improved policy via PMD, and the learner then projecting this improvement into the decentralized policy space through KL minimization.
- **Sequential Advantage Decomposition (Corollary 4.2):** The learner updates in MAGPO can be interpreted as sequential advantage-based updates. While this is similar to methods like HARL, MAGPO allows for simultaneous updates of all agent policies, facilitating parallel training and enabling parameter sharing, which are beneficial for scalability.

**Practical Implementation:**
The practical implementation of MAGPO is based on the GPO-clip framework, adapted for the multi-agent sequential setting.
- **Guider Update (Equation 9):** The guider policy (parameterized by ϕ) is optimized using a PPO-style objective. This objective includes two key modifications:
 - **Double Clipping (clip(·, ϵ, δ)):** This function, controlled by hyperparameter δ, clips the policy ratio to ensure the guider does not drift too far from the learner, specifically bounding the ratio between guider and learner policies within (1/δ, δ).
 - **Mask Function (m_ijt(δ)):** This function ensures that a KL regularization loss (D_KL(μ_ij_ϕ, π_ij_θ)) is only applied when the policy ratio constraint (1/δ, δ) is violated, encouraging alignment.
 - The advantage estimate (ˆA_t) is computed using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
- **Learner Update (Equation 10):** The learner policy (parameterized by θ) is updated with a dual objective:
 - **Behavior Cloning:** Minimizing KL divergence D_KL(π_ij_θ, μ_ij_ϕ) between the learner and the guider, directly promoting imitation.
 - **RL Auxiliary Term:** An additional PPO-style RL objective, weighted by λ, helps the learner directly maximize return from collected trajectories. This term also allows the learner to "counter-supervise" the guider, helping it discover more decentralizable update directions.
- **Parameter Sharing:** In the practical implementation, all learners share parameters and are updated jointly and in parallel, consistent with a MAPPO-style approach, to enhance efficiency and generalization.

The hyperparameters δ and λ are crucial for MAGPO's performance. δ regulates the guider's allowed deviation from the learner, influencing the strictness of the alignment. λ controls the contribution of the RL auxiliary loss, impacting how much the learner directly optimizes its own return versus imitating the guider.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MAGPO was empirically evaluated across 43 tasks in 6 diverse environments, including CoordSum, Level-based Foraging (LBF), MaConnector, Multi-agent Particle Environment (MPE), Robotic Warehouse (RWARE), and The StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge in JAX (SMAX). It was compared against two CTCE methods (Sable, MAT), two CTDE baselines (MAPPO, HAPPO), and a vanilla CTDS implementation.

**Overall Performance:**
- MAGPO consistently demonstrated strong performance, achieving state-of-the-art results among CTDE methods.
- It matched or surpassed fully centralized approaches (CTCE methods) on a subset of tasks despite adhering to decentralized execution constraints.
- Quantitatively, MAGPO outperformed all CTDE baselines on 32 out of 43 tasks and surpassed all baselines (including CTCE methods) on 20 out of 43 tasks.
- Aggregated sample-efficiency curves across environments (Figure 2) showed MAGPO's competitive learning speed and final performance.
- The probability of improvement analysis (Figure 3) indicated statistically significant improvements over other baselines in various environments.

**Comparison with CTDS:**
- MAGPO exhibited a significant performance advantage over the CTDS baseline, particularly in the CoordSum and RWARE domains. This suggests that in these environments, the CTCE teacher policies learned strategies that were not effectively decentralizable, and direct policy distillation (as in CTDS) proved ineffective. MAGPO's explicit alignment mechanisms address this limitation.

**Ablation Studies and Discussions:**
- **Bridging CTCE and CTDE:** MAGPO's performance was shown to be dependent on the capability of the underlying CTCE method used as its guider. For instance, on tasks where Sable outperformed MAT, MAGPO with Sable as the guider performed better, and vice-versa. This illustrates MAGPO's role in allowing advancements in centralized coordination (CTCE) to directly benefit decentralized deployment (CTDE).
- **Effect of the Ratio δ:** The hyperparameter δ, which regulates the guider's deviation from the learner, had a notable impact on performance.
 - A smaller δ (stricter constraint) improved performance on tasks like CoordSum-5x20-80, where the centralized guider might otherwise learn non-decentralizable policies.
 - A larger δ (more freedom) was beneficial on tasks like medium-4ag-hard, where the guider's optimal policy was more directly imitable, and tight restrictions hindered learning. This emphasizes the importance of tuning δ based on task structure and imitation feasibility.
- **Effect of RL Auxiliary Loss λ:** The RL auxiliary loss in the learner update, controlled by λ, was found to improve performance when properly tuned. It contributes to better utilization of collected data and can help stabilize learning by enabling the learner to "counter-supervise" the guider towards more decentralizable update directions. In contrast, applying this auxiliary loss to CTDS showed limited benefit, likely because the large teacher-student gap in CTDS makes the collected data highly off-policy for the student.
- **Observation Asymmetry:** The paper acknowledges that observation asymmetry (guider sees global information, learners see partial local observations) is critical. MAGPO addresses this by controlling the divergence between guider and learner via δ, similar to single-agent guided policy optimization. The potential benefit of providing additional privileged information to the guider was also noted.
- **Model Capacity:** In an experiment simulating deployment compression (reducing decentralized actor capacity at evaluation), MAGPO consistently outperformed CTDS across all evaluated actor capacities. The performance gap widened with decreasing actor capacity, indicating that MAGPO's mechanism for aligning the teacher with decentralized realizability improves robustness to model compression for deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MAGPO represents a significant contribution to cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning by offering a principled and practical solution for decentralized multi-agent learning under partial observability.

Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging CTCE and CTDE Paradigms:** MAGPO effectively addresses a fundamental challenge in MARL by creating a framework that can leverage the powerful coordination capabilities of Centralized Training with Centralized Execution (CTCE) methods while adhering to the real-world constraints of Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE). This allows for the co-development of both paradigms, where advancements in CTCE can directly translate into improved decentralized solutions.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing CTDE and CTDS Methods:**
 - **Enhanced Centralized Guidance:** Unlike "vanilla CTDE" methods that primarily use centralized critics, MAGPO introduces a centralized guider policy for direct action coordination, leading to more effective utilization of centralized training.
 - **Mitigation of the Imitation Gap:** MAGPO directly confronts the "imitation gap" problem faced by Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student (CTDS) frameworks. By constraining the centralized guider to remain aligned with the decentralized learners throughout training, it ensures that the coordinated strategies learned by the guider are realizable by the decentralized policies, which CTDS methods struggle with due to observation and policy asymmetries.
 - **Scalability and Parallelism:** Despite its sophisticated guidance mechanism, MAGPO enables parallel updates and benefits from parameter sharing among agents, addressing scalability concerns that plague some other methods like HARL.

3. **Theoretical Guarantees for Monotonic Improvement:** The provision of a theoretical guarantee for monotonic policy improvement enhances the reliability and predictability of MAGPO's learning process, a feature often absent in many empirical MARL algorithms.

4. **Strong Empirical Performance:** MAGPO's consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art CTDE baselines and its competitiveness with fully centralized (CTCE) methods across a diverse suite of 43 tasks in 6 environments highlight its practical effectiveness and robustness.

5. **Robustness to Model Capacity Constraints:** The demonstrated ability of MAGPO to degrade gracefully under severe model capacity constraints for decentralized actors is particularly impactful for real-world deployments, where computational and latency limitations often necessitate compact policies.

In summary, MAGPO offers a robust, theoretically grounded, and empirically validated framework that advances the state of the art in cooperative MARL. Its ability to reconcile powerful centralized coordination with practical decentralized execution makes it a significant step towards deploying sophisticated multi-agent intelligence in complex, real-world systems.
