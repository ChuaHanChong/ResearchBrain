# Multi-Agent Guided Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Multi-Agent Guided Policy Optimization (MAGPO) introduces an iterative framework that couples a centralized, autoregressive "guider" policy with decentralized "learner" policies, ensuring their alignment to bridge powerful centralized coordination with practical decentralized execution. The method achieved state-of-the-art results among CTDE approaches, outperforming baselines on 32 out of 43 tasks and matching fully centralized methods on a subset of these across diverse environments.

## Problem

- Existing Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) methods often underutilize centralized information, primarily via value functions, without directly coordinating agent actions during training.
- Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student (CTDS) frameworks suffer from scalability issues due to exponentially growing joint action spaces for the teacher, and an "imitation gap" caused by observation and policy asymmetries.
- Many cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods lack theoretical guarantees for monotonic policy improvement, leading to less stable and predictable learning outcomes.

## Method

- Introduces an iterative framework that couples a centralized, autoregressive "guider" policy (with global state access) with decentralized "learner" policies (with local observations).
- The guider is trained using a Policy Mirror Descent (**PMD**) framework to maximize an RL objective, while the learners are updated by minimizing KL divergence to the guider, ensuring alignment and monotonic improvement.
- Employs mechanisms like double clipping on the guider's policy ratio and guider backtracking to dynamically constrain the guider's exploration, ensuring its learned coordinated strategies remain realizable by the decentralized learners.

## Results

- MAGPO consistently outperformed all CTDE baselines on **32** out of **43** tasks and surpassed even fully centralized CTCE methods on **20** tasks across 6 diverse multi-agent environments.
- It demonstrated a considerable performance advantage over Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student (CTDS) approaches, effectively addressing the imitation gap, especially where centralized policies were not easily decentralizable.
- The framework exhibited competitive sample efficiency and maintained performance robustness even under reduced decentralized actor capacities, indicating its practical applicability for deployment with computational constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Principled alignment of a powerful centralized "guider" with decentralized "learner" policies can effectively bridge the gap between strong centralized coordination (CTCE) and practical decentralized execution (CTDE).
> - Actively constraining the centralized guider's policy to stay aligned with the decentralized learners throughout training is critical for mitigating the "imitation gap" inherent in CTDS frameworks, ensuring that coordinated strategies are decentralizably realizable.
> - The tuning of alignment strength (e.g., hyperparameter δ) between the guider and learner is crucial, as tighter constraints help in tasks where centralized policies are hard to decentralize, while looser constraints allow more freedom for complex coordination.
