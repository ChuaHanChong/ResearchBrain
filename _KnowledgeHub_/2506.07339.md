# Real-Time Execution of Action Chunking Flow Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Real-Time Chunking (RTC) is an inference-time algorithm developed by Physical Intelligence and UC Berkeley that enables smooth, asynchronous execution of action chunking policies for high-latency diffusion- and flow-based Vision-Language-Action models. By treating policy execution as an inpainting problem with soft masking, RTC achieves robust performance and high task throughput on both simulated and real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, even with significant inference delays.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models incur high inference latency, hindering their effective deployment in real-time robotic control.
- Existing action chunking methods, while improving temporal consistency, lead to issues like pauses, mode-jumping, and jerky movements at chunk boundaries due to latency.
- Naive asynchronous strategies or simple smoothing techniques (e.g., temporal ensembling) can exacerbate latency issues or produce invalid actions, especially with multi-modal action distributions.

## Method

- **RTC** formulates asynchronous policy execution as an ==inpainting== task, leveraging generative models (diffusion/flow) to maintain continuity between action chunks.
- It employs gradient-based guidance (ΠGDM with a crucial clipping mechanism) integrated into the learned velocity field, aligning newly generated chunks with actions already committed from the previous chunk.
- A novel soft masking technique, using exponentially decaying weights across chunk boundaries, provides a stronger and smoother guidance signal to ensure continuous strategy execution without requiring retraining of the base VLA policy.

## Results

- RTC demonstrated remarkable robustness to inference delays in simulation, consistently outperforming all baselines and maintaining high task success rates even when **50%** of the prediction horizon was delayed.
- On real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, RTC achieved the best average task throughput and complete robustness to injected latencies up to +**200ms**, while synchronous inference degraded linearly and other baselines failed entirely.
- The method enabled faster task completion, higher success rates, and smoother execution, significantly reducing retries in precision-sensitive real-world manipulation tasks such as 'light candle' and 'bed making'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The challenge of high-latency policy execution in real-time robotics can be effectively addressed by reframing it as an inpainting problem, leveraging the inherent capabilities of diffusion and flow models.
> - Soft masking, with its gradual weighting scheme, is crucial for achieving superior cross-chunk continuity and preventing discontinuities or 'strategy switching' compared to binary hard masking.
> - An inference-time-only algorithm can robustly mitigate severe latency in complex Vision-Language-Action models, making them practical for demanding real-time physical tasks without requiring costly model retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical and pervasive challenge in the field of embodied AI: the reconciliation of sophisticated, high-latency models with the stringent real-time demands of physical world interaction.

Modern AI systems, particularly **embodied agents** like robots, are increasingly expected to operate autonomously and interact dynamically with their environments. Recent advancements, driven by large-scale deep learning, have led to the development of powerful **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. These models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and the authors' own `π` series, leverage pre-trained vision-language model backbones to achieve impressive generalization capabilities in robot manipulation. However, their effectiveness comes at a cost: high inference latency. With billions of parameters, these models are computationally intensive, requiring powerful hardware and incurring significant delays between observation and action. Unlike chatbots or image generators, where delay might only lead to user annoyance, latency in physical systems can have severe consequences, from task failure to safety hazards.

To mitigate this, **action chunking** has emerged as a partial solution. In this paradigm, the model predicts a sequence of future actions (a "chunk") for each inference call, which are then executed sequentially. This improves temporal consistency compared to single-step prediction. However, action chunking does not fully resolve the latency problem. If the time required to generate the next chunk (`δ`) exceeds the controller's sampling period (`∆t`), the robot either pauses, leading to inefficient execution and altered dynamics, or it exhibits "mode-jumping" and jerky movements at chunk boundaries due to discontinuous transitions between successive, independently generated action chunks. Existing naive asynchronous strategies often exacerbate this issue, while simple smoothing techniques like temporal ensembling can produce invalid actions, especially when dealing with multi-modal action distributions.

The "Real-Time Chunking (RTC)" method proposed in this paper directly tackles these limitations. It positions itself as a crucial **inference-time algorithm** designed to enable **smooth, asynchronous execution** of action chunking policies, specifically those based on **diffusion or flow models**. Unlike approaches that focus on merely *speeding up* inference (e.g., consistency policy, streaming diffusion, parallel decoding), RTC operates by enabling the system to *think while moving*, effectively working around the inherent latency of complex VLAs. It leverages the inherent strengths of generative models—their ability to perform inpainting and follow guidance—to maintain continuity between action chunks while incorporating new observations. This work thus bridges a critical gap, allowing powerful but slow VLAs to be deployed effectively in dynamic, real-time robotic applications, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in dexterous and generalist robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the fundamental conflict between the computational demands of state-of-the-art generalist AI models, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLAs), and the real-time operational requirements of physical robotic systems.

The **key objectives** of this paper are to:

1. **Address the latency problem in VLAs for real-time control:** Modern VLAs are characterized by high computational latency, making them inherently slow for applications demanding rapid, continuous responses, such as controlling a robot. The authors aim to develop a solution that enables these powerful models to operate effectively in real-time environments despite their inherent slowness.
2. **Overcome limitations of existing action chunking:** While action chunking partially addresses temporal consistency by predicting sequences of actions, it introduces problems like pauses, mode-jumping, and jerky movements at chunk boundaries due to latency and lack of coordination between successive chunks. The research seeks to eliminate these discontinuities.
3. **Enable smooth, asynchronous policy execution:** The goal is to allow the robot to continuously execute actions from a current chunk while the VLA simultaneously generates the subsequent chunk. This asynchronous operation is crucial for maintaining fluid movement and responsiveness to environmental changes.
4. **Maintain temporal consistency and reactivity:** The new system must ensure that the transition between chunks is smooth and that the policy can still incorporate new observations to react to dynamic changes, rather than merely executing a pre-planned sequence without updates.
5. **Develop an inference-time solution:** A significant objective is to create an algorithm that can be applied "out of the box" to any existing diffusion- or flow-based VLA without requiring any re-training or modification of the underlying policy, thus making it broadly applicable and cost-effective.
6. **Improve task throughput and success rates:** Ultimately, the system should lead to faster task completion and higher success rates, particularly in precise and dynamic manipulation tasks, even in the presence of considerable inference delays.

In essence, the authors are motivated to unlock the full potential of large, generalist visuomotor policies by making them truly viable for demanding real-time physical applications, ensuring that robots can perform complex tasks smoothly, efficiently, and robustly, even when operating with computational delays inherent to current state-of-the-art AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core contribution of this paper is **Real-Time Chunking (RTC)**, a novel inference-time algorithm that enables smooth, asynchronous execution of action chunking policies, specifically targeting diffusion- and flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The methodology hinges on treating the asynchronous execution problem as an **inpainting task**.

The approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Asynchronous Execution Paradigm:** The fundamental idea is to overlap the execution of the current action chunk with the generation of the next. When an inference call is initiated for a new chunk (`A_new`), a certain number of actions (`d`, the inference delay) from the *previous* chunk (`A_prev`) will already be committed for execution by the time `A_new` becomes available. This creates an "inpainting" opportunity.

2. **Inpainting with Flow Matching (or Diffusion):** RTC leverages the inherent capabilities of iterative denoising frameworks like diffusion models and conditional flow matching. The algorithm integrates a **gradient-based guidance term** into the learned velocity field (`v`) at each denoising step (Equation 1). This guidance term encourages the newly generated chunk to align with known information.
 - **Pseudoinverse Guidance (ΠGDM):** The specific guidance mechanism builds upon existing training-free image inpainting algorithms, adapting ΠGDM (Equation 2) to the action space. This involves estimating the final denoised action chunk (`c_A_1_t`) and computing a vector-Jacobian product to steer the generation towards a target (`Y`), which represents the actions from `A_prev` that overlap with the new chunk.
 - **Guidance Weight Clipping (β):** A crucial empirical modification introduced by the authors is the clipping of the guidance weight (the `min` term in Equation 2). This prevents the guidance weight from becoming infinite at the initial denoising steps (`τ=0`), which was found to cause instability and divergence in generated action chunks, particularly with the low number of denoising steps typically used in control problems.

3. **Soft Masking for Cross-Chunk Continuity:** This is a key innovation for RTC, addressing the issue of weak guidance signals that can still lead to "strategy switching" or discontinuities even with inpainting. Instead of a simple binary mask (`W`) that only "freezes" the first `d` actions, soft masking employs **real-valued weights** (Equation 5):
 - The first `d` actions of the new chunk (those that will be executed from `A_prev` before `A_new` is ready) are given a weight of **1** (fully frozen).
 - The last `s` actions of the new chunk (those that extend beyond the previous chunk's horizon) are given a weight of **0** (freshly generated).
 - The intermediate actions (from `d` to `H-s-1`) receive **exponentially decaying weights** from 1 to 0. This "soft" transition allows the policy to consider a broader segment of the previous chunk for continuity, while acknowledging increasing uncertainty further into the future. This provides a stronger and smoother guidance signal, effectively enforcing a consistent strategy across chunk boundaries.

4. **Real-Time Chunking System (Algorithm 1):** The entire system is orchestrated via an algorithm that manages concurrency between the main controller thread and a background inference thread:
 - **`GETACTION`:** The main controller calls this function at a fixed interval (`∆t`) to consume an action and provide the latest observation.
 - **`INFERENCELOOP`:** This runs asynchronously in a background thread. It monitors the progress of execution, forecasts the next inference delay (`d`) based on a buffer of past delays, and determines the actual execution horizon (`s`). Crucially, it extracts the relevant unexecuted part of the `A_prev` to guide the `GUIDEDINFERENCE` call.
 - **`GUIDEDINFERENCE`:** This function performs the core inpainting. It takes the flow policy, current observation, the remaining part of `A_prev`, the estimated delay `d`, and the execution horizon `s`. It constructs the soft mask `W` (Equation 5) and then iteratively denoises the action chunk while applying the ΠGDM guidance with the computed `W` and the clipping `β`. Once `A_new` is ready, it replaces `A_prev` for subsequent actions.

A significant advantage of RTC is its **inference-time-only nature**. It does not require any re-training of the base VLA policy, making it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing diffusion- or flow-based models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Real-Time Chunking (RTC) was thoroughly evaluated across both a novel simulated benchmark and challenging real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, demonstrating its superior performance, robustness to latency, and efficiency compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Simulated Benchmark (Kinetix Simulator)

A new benchmark of 12 highly dynamic and stochastic environments in the Kinetix simulator was introduced. These tasks, involving force-based control and requiring closed-loop corrections due to added Gaussian noise, were designed to highlight the challenges of inference delay. RTC was compared against RTC (hard masking), Bidirectional Decoding (BID), Naive Async, and Temporal Ensembling (TE).

- **Robustness to Inference Delay:** RTC demonstrated remarkable robustness to increasing inference delays (Figure 5, right). It consistently outperformed all baselines, with the performance gap widening significantly as delays increased from 0 to 4 controller timesteps (where H=8, d=4 is 50% of the prediction horizon). This shows RTC's ability to maintain high success rates even when facing substantial computational lag.
- **Effect of Soft Masking:** The ablation study clearly showed that **soft masking is superior to hard masking**, particularly at lower inference delays. Soft masking's exponentially decaying weights provided a more effective and continuous guidance signal, preventing mode-jumping and improving overall task success.
- **Leveraging Execution Horizon:** RTC was better able to exploit shorter execution horizons (s) for faster closed-loop corrections, resulting in strictly increasing performance with decreasing execution horizon (Figure 5, left). This indicates that RTC's continuity across chunks allows it to react more quickly to new information.
- **Baseline Performance:** Temporal Ensembling (TE) performed poorly across the board, even at zero delay, underscoring its inability to handle multi-modal action distributions where averaging can lead to invalid actions. Bidirectional Decoding (BID) showed some robustness but consistently underperformed RTC while requiring significantly more compute (sampling batches of 64 chunks vs. RTC's single chunk generation).

#### 4.2. Real-World Results (Bimanual Manipulation)

RTC was deployed on a real bimanual robot system using the `π0.5` VLA policy (H=50, ∆t=20ms) across 6 challenging dexterous manipulation tasks, including mobile manipulation. The experiments specifically injected additional latency (+0ms, +100ms, +200ms) to simulate varying computational loads and network conditions.

- **Superior Task Throughput and Robustness to Delay:** RTC achieved the **best average task throughput** (tasks completed per minute) at all tested inference delays (Figure 6, right). Crucially, RTC showed **complete robustness to injected delay**, with its throughput remaining stable even at +200ms latency. In stark contrast, synchronous inference degraded linearly with increased delay, experiencing visible pauses. Both Temporal Ensembling variants (sparse and dense) **failed entirely** at +100ms and +200ms of injected latency, causing such severe oscillations that the robot's protective stop mechanisms were triggered, rendering them unusable for practical deployment under high latency.
- **Improved Task Completion Speed and Success:** Beyond just mitigating delays, RTC enabled faster task completion than synchronous inference even when pauses were factored out (Figure 6, top, left). This indicates that RTC contributes to fewer mistakes and less retrying behavior. For the highly precision-sensitive "light candle" task (which did not allow retries), RTC showed a significant advantage in final score, reflecting a higher overall success rate. Similar improvements were observed in complex tasks like "bed making," where the policy often struggles with pillow manipulation.
- **Computational Overhead:** While RTC incurs a latency increase per denoising step (2.5x compared to vanilla `π0.5` due to backpropagation), the overall benefits in robustness and performance far outweigh this overhead. BID, a competing method, was found to have 2.3 times the latency of RTC for inferior performance in simulation.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that RTC is a highly effective solution for real-time robotic control with high-latency VLAs. It provides smooth, robust, and performant execution, making complex manipulation tasks achievable even in the face of significant inference delays.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Real-Time Execution of Action Chunking Flow Policies" paper introduces Real-Time Chunking (RTC), a significant advancement with profound implications for the practical deployment of sophisticated AI models in physical systems.

- **Addresses a Core Bottleneck:** RTC directly tackles one of the most pressing challenges in embodied AI: the inherent tension between the high computational cost of powerful Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and the real-time demands of physical robots. By providing a robust, inference-time solution to latency-induced discontinuities, RTC makes state-of-the-art VLAs truly viable for dynamic and interactive real-world applications.
- **Enables Generalist Robot Capabilities:** The ability to execute complex, multi-step actions smoothly and responsively under real-world latency allows VLAs to demonstrate their full potential for generalizable robot manipulation. Tasks requiring precision, dexterity, and continuous interaction (like lighting a match) become feasible even with significant delays.
- **Training-Free Implementation:** A major strength of RTC is that it operates purely at inference time, requiring no modifications or re-training of existing diffusion- or flow-based VLA policies. This drastically lowers the barrier to adoption, allowing researchers and practitioners to immediately enhance their pre-trained models with real-time capabilities without incurring immense retraining costs.
- **Improved Reliability and Throughput:** The demonstrated robustness to inference delays and the resulting increase in task throughput and success rates translate directly into more reliable and efficient robot operation. This is crucial for real-world applications where robots need to perform tasks consistently and without failure, reducing downtime and operational costs.
- **New Research Direction:** By posing asynchronous action chunking as an inpainting problem and leveraging generative models in a novel way for control, the paper opens up new avenues for research at the intersection of generative AI, real-time systems, and robotics.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Deployment of Advanced Robotics:** RTC can significantly accelerate the deployment of advanced AI-powered robots in diverse fields such as manufacturing, logistics, service industries (e.g., healthcare, elder care), and household automation. Robots capable of performing complex, human-like tasks smoothly and reliably will become more widespread.
- **Safer and More Natural Human-Robot Interaction:** Smoother, more responsive robot movements reduce the risk of accidents and create a more natural, predictable interaction experience for humans. This is vital for collaborative robotics and applications where robots operate in close proximity to people.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By providing an accessible method to overcome latency issues without extensive re-engineering, RTC can empower a broader community of researchers and developers to deploy and experiment with advanced robot learning policies, even on less optimized hardware or with higher network latencies.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** This work lays a foundation for future embodied AI systems that can integrate increasingly complex, large-scale models into real-time physical agents. It highlights the importance of intelligent inference-time algorithms alongside model architecture and training improvements.
- **New Benchmarking Standards:** The introduction of the Kinetix simulated benchmark, specifically designed for highly dynamic and stochastic tasks, encourages the development and evaluation of real-time control methods in more challenging scenarios, pushing the field beyond quasi-static tasks.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge some limitations, including the computational overhead introduced by RTC compared to direct policy sampling, its applicability restricted to diffusion- and flow-based policies, and the need for evaluation in even more dynamic real-world settings (e.g., legged locomotion, which is present in simulation but not real-world experiments). Future work could focus on optimizing RTC's computational efficiency, extending its applicability to other policy architectures, and deploying it in scenarios with even higher degrees of real-time dynamic challenges.
