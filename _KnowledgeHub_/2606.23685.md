# LaST-HD: Learning Latent Physical Reasoning from Scalable Human Data for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> LaST-HD, a framework from Peking University and collaborators, enables robots to learn manipulation skills from scalable human-hand demonstrations by aligning human and robot actions within a shared latent physical reasoning space. This approach, supported by a novel data collection glove and progressive training, improves generalization to unseen scenarios and allows for rapid adaptation with minimal human input.

## Problem

- Developing generalist robot manipulation policies is challenging due to the high cost and inefficiency of collecting large-scale, high-quality robot demonstration datasets.
- The 'embodiment gap' between human hands and robot manipulators hinders direct transfer of human manipulation knowledge, as existing methods often overlook the alignment of underlying physical dynamics.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on extensive robot data and lack robust mechanisms for internalizing and generalizing from diverse human interaction priors effectively.

## Method

- Introduces **LaST-HD**, a VLA framework that learns morphology-agnostic actions by aligning human and robot demonstrations within a ==shared latent physical reasoning space==, based on an action-conditioned world model.
- Developed the Out-of-Lab (OOL) Glove, a low-cost, high-fidelity motion-capture system for scalable collection of human-hand interaction data, providing precise, retargetable keypoint supervision for various robot morphologies.
- Employs a progressive mixed-to-human training recipe, combining initial mixed human-robot co-training with post-training human-hand online correction to enhance generalization and enable rapid adaptation to new environments.

## Results

- LaST-HD (Mix-HD) achieved a **0.68** average success rate across six real-world tasks using 50 robot and 50 OOL Glove demonstrations, comparable to the **0.73** success rate of a model trained solely on 100 robot demonstrations.
- Demonstrated improved generalization to unseen scenarios with adaptation: **41%** average success for unseen positions, **58%** for unseen objects, and **68%** for unseen backgrounds, outperforming baselines.
- Enabled rapid adaptation through human-hand online correction, achieving **100%** success for 'Unseen Background' with just 20 human-hand trajectories (approximately 20 minutes of collection).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning human and robot actions in a latent physical reasoning space, which captures the physical consequences of interactions rather than just kinematics, is crucial for robust cross-embodiment transfer.
> - High-fidelity human-hand data, collected efficiently with the OOL Glove, can effectively supplement or even outperform equivalent robot data when correctly aligned, demonstrating efficient transfer of interaction priors.
> - A progressive training approach that includes online human-hand correction using the OOL Glove allows for rapid adaptation and policy refinement in novel or failure-prone scenarios with minimal additional data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Developing generalist manipulation policies for robots operating in unstructured environments is a significant challenge in robotics. Recent advancements, particularly those driven by large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have led to the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as a paradigm for robotic control. However, scaling these VLA models often necessitates extensive robot demonstration datasets, which are resource-intensive to collect due to hardware overhead and labor.

Human hand demonstrations offer a direct and data-rich alternative, providing diverse physical interaction priors that could facilitate the transfer of human manipulation experience to robots. Prior work in human-to-robot transfer has primarily focused on kinematic retargeting, which involves mapping human hand poses to robotic joint spaces, or morphological translation. Other approaches extract visual representations or object-centric trajectory priors from human videos to guide robot learning. Within the VLA domain, leveraging human hand data for open-world generalization has gained attention, with methods exploring joint co-training to learn shared representations across embodiments.

A limitation of many existing methods is their sensitivity to data scale or their tendency to overlook the alignment of underlying physical dynamics between human and robot manipulation, often focusing more on geometric or kinematic correspondences. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a method that explicitly aligns human-hand and robot demonstrations within a shared latent physical reasoning space. This approach moves beyond purely kinematic or visual alignment to focus on the common physical principles governing manipulation, aiming to provide a more robust and scalable solution for incorporating human expertise into robot learning. The work positions itself as an extension of "reasoning-before-acting" VLA models, utilizing latent physical reasoning as an intermediate interface for morphology-agnostic action learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges associated with developing generalist robot manipulation policies. Current VLA models, despite their potential, are hindered by the prohibitive cost and inefficiency of collecting large-scale, high-quality robot demonstration datasets. In contrast, human interaction data, particularly human hand demonstrations, represent a vast and accessible source of manipulation knowledge. However, directly transferring this knowledge to robots is complicated by the inherent "embodiment gap"—differences in kinematics, morphology, and physical dynamics between human hands and robot manipulators.

Existing human-to-robot transfer methods have not fully addressed this embodiment gap at the level of underlying physical dynamics. Methods based on kinematic retargeting often provide rigid mappings that do not account for dynamic alignment. Co-training approaches, while learning shared representations, can be sensitive to data volume or may not explicitly align the physical reasoning processes essential for robust manipulation in varied environments. This gap limits the extent to which robots can effectively internalize and generalize from human manipulation experience.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:
- **To introduce a novel human-to-robot action learning paradigm (LaST-HD):** This paradigm aims to extend existing "reasoning-before-acting" VLA models by incorporating an intermediate interface for morphology-agnostic action learning.
- **To align human-hand and robot demonstrations in a shared latent physical reasoning space:** This objective aims to overcome the embodiment gap by focusing on the common physical consequences of actions, thereby enabling robust transfer of manipulation skills. The approach seeks to move beyond merely mimicking human kinematics by instead synthesizing unified latent targets that represent shared physical dynamics.
- **To develop a low-cost, high-fidelity human-hand data collection system (OOL Glove):** This system is designed to capture native human-hand interactions with the environment, providing precise, retargetable keypoint supervision that is applicable across different robot morphologies, from grippers to dexterous hands. This addresses the need for scalable and quality human data.
- **To establish a progressive mixed-to-human training recipe:** This recipe combines mixed human-robot co-training with human-hand online correction. The goal is to enhance generalization to novel objects, scenes, and positions, and to enable rapid adaptation to new environments using minimal additional human data.

In essence, the research seeks to unlock the full potential of human-hand data for robot learning by developing a comprehensive system that includes a novel architectural approach for latent physical reasoning alignment, a specialized data collection tool, and an efficient training methodology.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LaST-HD framework is designed to facilitate robot action learning from human-hand data through latent physical reasoning. It comprises a VLA model architecture, a human-to-robot latent alignment strategy, a specialized human-hand data collection system (OOL Glove), and a progressive mixed-to-human training recipe.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The problem is formulated as probabilistic sequential decision making, where a policy $\pi_\theta$ predicts an action chunk given a language instruction and visual observation. The action space is embodiment-specific, ranging from 14-16 DoF for dual-arm grippers to 54 DoF for dual-arm dexterous hands.

LaST-HD adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) VLA architecture based on Janus-Pro, following a "reasoning-before-acting" paradigm. A SigLIP-Large vision encoder extracts visual features from 384x384 observations. The VLA backbone, a repurposed DeepSeek-LLM 1.5B (24-layer decoder-only transformer), consists of two components:
- **Reasoning Expert:** Autoregressively predicts a sequence of compact latent states to model task-relevant physical dynamics.
- **Action Expert:** Predicts the robot action chunk using flow-matching.
Latent reasoning knowledge is transferred from the reasoning expert to the action expert via a shared attention mechanism, providing an interface for morphology-agnostic physical reasoning priors.

#### 3.2. Human-to-Robot Latent Alignment

To address the embodiment gap, LaST-HD employs a latent alignment strategy. An action-conditioned world model, fine-tuned on an unpaired mixed dataset of OOL Glove-collected human and real-robot demonstrations, serves as the alignment bridge. This world model takes visual observations as input and incorporates continuous action chunks via cross-attention to guide future generation. Features are extracted from the deepest U-Net layer at the final denoising step, which are hypothesized to capture predictive physical dynamics and provide domain-invariant representations. These spatiotemporal features are then projected to the LaST-HD latent dimension using an MLP-based aligner, flattened, and compressed into a fixed number of latent tokens via adaptive average pooling. These resulting tokens explicitly supervise LaST-HD's reasoning expert as ground truth. This approach focuses on encoding the physical consequences of interactions, which are shared across embodiments, rather than solely relying on appearance-based future-frame predictions.

#### 3.3. Human-Hand Data Collection (Out-of-Lab (OOL) Glove)

The OOL Glove is a low-cost, low-latency, and scalable motion-capture glove designed for collecting high-fidelity human-hand data. It features an ultra-lightweight design (<100g per glove) to minimize interference with natural hand motion. The system uses six compact IMU modules to track 21 hand-wrist keypoints (20 hand, 1 wrist) within a unified hand-centric coordinate frame. It operates at over 200 Hz with less than 10 ms latency and achieves sub-millimeter average RMS position error per keypoint.

During data collection, users perform manipulation tasks directly in the physical environment, with synchronized multimodal trajectories captured, including language instructions, visual observations (from wrist-mounted and head/chest-mounted cameras), and precise hand-wrist states. The native human-hand kinematics are preserved, offering high precision compared to vision-based tracking and direct action recording compared to exoskeletons. The collected data is then mapped into a unified hand-centric representation, allowing gripper commands to be derived from fingertip distances and dexterous hand joint angles to be solved through inverse-kinematics retargeting. This reusability across different robot morphologies contributes to data scalability.

#### 3.4. Mixed-to-Human Training Recipe

A progressive mixed-to-human training recipe activates LaST-HD's potential:

- **Stage 1: Mixed Human-Robot Co-training.**
 First, the action-conditioned world model is trained on mixed human-hand and robot trajectories and then frozen. This world model provides morphology-agnostic latent supervision for LaST-HD. The LaST-HD VLA model is then optimized on both human-hand and robot trajectories. The action expert learns robot actions via a flow matching loss ($L_{act}$), while the latent reasoning expert is supervised by the aligned latent targets from the world model using a cosine similarity loss ($L_{latent}$). The overall training objective is $L_{loss} = L_{act} + \lambda L_{latent}$. This stage aims to inject human priors and improve generalization.

- **Stage 2: Human-Hand Online Correction Post-training.**
 After co-training, LaST-HD is deployed, and policy rollouts identify failure-prone states. Instead of traditional robot teleoperation, human-hand corrective demonstrations are collected at these failure states using the OOL Glove. The world model remains frozen. LaST-HD is then post-trained for 1-2 epochs using balanced replay (sampling equally from previous data and the DAgger buffer of new human corrections) to incorporate new knowledge while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. This stage aims for rapid adaptation and improved accuracy in novel environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LaST-HD was conducted across six real-world tasks and three robot embodiments (Galaxea R1 Lite with grippers, Tianji Marvin with grippers, and Tianji Marvin with a WUJI dexterous hand).

#### 4.1. In-Domain Setting

In-domain evaluation compared LaST-HD against baselines ($\pi_{0.5}$, Cosmos-Policy, LaST$_0$) trained on 100 real-robot demonstrations. Two variants of LaST-HD were assessed: one trained on 100 robot demonstrations and another (LaST-HD (Mix-HD)) trained on 50 robot and 50 OOL Glove demonstrations.
- Both LaST-HD variants achieved the highest average success rates across the six tasks (LaST-HD: 0.73, LaST-HD (Mix-HD): 0.68) compared to baselines (e.g., $\pi_{0.5}$: 0.62, LaST$_0$: 0.63).
- LaST-HD demonstrated proficiency in multi-step tasks such as "Sort Fruits" (95% success) and "Put Items to Bag and Zip" (80% success). The performance disparity with baselines increased in high-DoF dexterous manipulation tasks.
- LaST-HD (Mix-HD) maintained performance comparable to LaST-HD on four of six tasks, suggesting that OOL Glove demonstrations provide effective supervision without degrading the robot's inherent capabilities.

#### 4.2. Generalization Setting

Generalization was evaluated under three scenarios: unseen positions, objects, and backgrounds. Models were assessed in a zero-shot setting and with adaptation using additional human-hand data.
- **Zero-shot evaluation:** All methods, including LaST-HD (Mix-HD), experienced substantial drops in success rates when applied to unseen scenarios without further fine-tuning (e.g., $\pi_{0.5}$ dropped to 12% average for unseen position).
- **Adaptation with human data:** LaST-HD (w/ unseen HD), trained with 100 in-domain robot demonstrations plus 60 human-hand demonstrations per unseen scenario, significantly outperformed baselines.
 - **Unseen Position:** Achieved 41% average success (compared to LaST$_0$ (w/ unseen HD) at 33%), indicating that human data aids in expanding the policy's physical exploration space.
 - **Unseen Object:** Achieved 58% average success (outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ zero-shot by 22% and LaST$_0$ (w/ unseen HD) by 9%), demonstrating robust semantic understanding and adaptive contact patterns.
 - **Unseen Background:** Achieved 68% average success, suggesting improved robustness to novel visual contexts through low-cost human-hand data and the proposed training recipe.

#### 4.3. Human-Hand Online Correction

The effectiveness of human-hand online correction was evaluated incrementally on the "Sort Fruits" task using varying amounts of OOL Glove data (10, 20, 60 trajectories, with 60 trajectories taking approximately 20 minutes to collect).
- Policy performance improved monotonically with increased human-hand correction data.
- With 20 human-hand trajectories, the success rate reached 100% for the "Unseen Background" scenario.
- With 60 human-hand trajectories, "Unseen Object" reached 100% success, and "Unseen Position" improved from 60% to 80%.
This indicates that the method, combined with OOL Glove data collection, enables rapid adaptation in novel scenarios, with latent alignment improving learning efficiency from human demonstrations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies on the dual-arm "Sort Fruits" task (generalization success rates) examined key design choices.
- **Effect of latent alignment strategies:**
 - Removing latent reasoning ("W/o Latent") resulted in a performance drop to 60%, indicating that action-level co-training alone is insufficient.
 - Using future SigLIP image features as latent targets ("SigLIP") yielded 63% success.
 - Using world-model latent targets without action conditioning ("WM-only") achieved 66%.
 - LaST-HD's approach (action-conditioned world-model latents) achieved 73%, demonstrating its superiority by capturing temporal cues and physical consequences, leading to more task-relevant and embodiment-agnostic physical reasoning (supported by attention map analysis).
- **Effectiveness of OOL Glove data:**
 - OOL Glove data (60 demonstrations) achieved 73% success, outperforming vision-based "Bare-hand" demonstrations (63%) and "UMI" data (65%), indicating higher-quality interaction data.
 - For equivalent collection time, OOL Glove data (73%) significantly outperformed real-robot data (12 trajectories, "Real-12", 60%), and was comparable to 5x more real-robot data (60 trajectories, "Real-60", 75%). This suggests efficient transfer of human interaction data via latent alignment.
 - Ablation on camera placement indicated that placing the wrist camera near the thumb-index web space (73%) outperformed a palm-view setup (67%).
- **Effect of world-model denoising steps:** Denoising steps of 2, 5, and 10 for latent target extraction yielded similar success rates (0.73, 0.72, 0.76), with 2 steps chosen for efficiency.
- **Effect of LaST-HD's shared latent length:** While a longer latent length (16 tokens) showed the highest success rate (0.78), a length of 4 tokens (0.73) was adopted for balancing performance and inference latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LaST-HD framework presents a contribution to the field of robot manipulation by addressing the challenge of efficiently leveraging human-hand data for robot learning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Bridging the Human-Robot Embodiment Gap:** By focusing on aligning underlying physical dynamics in a shared latent reasoning space, rather than solely on kinematics or visual appearances, LaST-HD offers a more robust mechanism for transferring human manipulation knowledge to robots. This is a fundamental step towards enabling robots to internalize complex human interaction priors.
- **Scalable and High-Fidelity Data Collection:** The introduction of the OOL Glove provides a low-cost, high-fidelity system for capturing human-hand interactions. This addresses a critical bottleneck in robot learning by offering a scalable alternative to laborious robot teleoperation, with data collection reported to be 4-5 times faster. The retargetable nature of the collected data further enhances its utility across diverse robot morphologies.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptation:** The progressive mixed-to-human training recipe, combining co-training with human-hand online correction, demonstrably improves robot generalization to novel objects, scenes, and positions. Furthermore, the ability for rapid adaptation to new environments or failure states, using minimal human corrections (e.g., over 90% accuracy with 20 minutes of data), represents a practical advancement for robot deployment in real-world, unstructured settings.
- **Advancement of VLA Models:** LaST-HD extends the "reasoning-before-acting" paradigm in VLA models by demonstrating the efficacy of latent physical reasoning for cross-embodiment learning. This architectural contribution could inform future designs of generalist robot policies.

**Potential Impact:**
This research has the potential to accelerate the development of more capable, adaptable, and general-purpose robots by making robot learning more accessible and less dependent on expensive robot-specific data. By democratizing the data collection process through low-cost human demonstrations, it could enable a broader range of applications and foster rapid iteration in robotic skill development.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The paper notes that latent reasoning in LaST-HD is not yet real-time, which could be a practical limitation for some applications. Future work aims to explore fast-slow system designs or further compress the latent space to improve efficiency. Additionally, the current system relies on heuristic mappings for hand-to-robot retargeting for dexterous hands, suggesting a future direction for learnable retargeting models. Further improvements are also targeted for specific challenges such as fluid manipulation (requiring fluid-aware perception) and precise dual-arm coordination.
