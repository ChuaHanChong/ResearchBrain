# Embodied-Navigator: Point, Think, Memorize, and Align for Efficient Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> TAMP-Nav is a VLM-based vision-language navigation framework that reformulates 3D action prediction as 2D pixel pointing projected via depth into SLAM waypoints, pairs sparse Chain-of-Thought triggered only at key topological nodes with a compressed Anchor-Trajectory Memory, and aligns both via a Two-Level GRPO combining trajectory- and step-level rewards, reaching 66.2% SR on R2R-CE using only 90k trajectories.

## Problem

- Prior VLN methods map observations to atomic actions or regress raw 3D coordinates, forcing 2D-pretrained VLMs into unnatural geometric transformations that cause spatial hallucination and poor sample efficiency.
- Existing chain-of-thought navigators trigger reasoning every step or at fixed intervals, creating a rigid tradeoff between decision quality and inference latency rather than adapting to scene complexity.
- Current memory schemes either retain full visual history, causing attention dilution and overflow, or discard/forget past observations, losing key spatio-temporal information over long horizons.

## Method

- **Pixel-to-3D Action Formulation**: the VLM selects a multi-view image and outputs a 2D pixel that is projected via depth and camera intrinsics into a 3D waypoint executed by a low-level ==SLAM== controller.
- **Selective Reasoning with Anchor-Trajectory Memory**: ==CoT== is triggered only at mined key topological nodes stored as high-fidelity Explicit Anchors, while intervening path segments compress into lightweight Space-Time Indicator tokens via ==RoPE==-encoded position, time, and yaw.
- **Two-Level GRPO**: multi-branch rollouts sample candidate actions per step and full trajectories, superimposing Z-score-normalized global outcome rewards (success, SPL, reasoning density) with fine-grained local step rewards via annealed guided sampling.

## Results

- TAMP-Nav achieves state-of-the-art **66.2%** SR / **58.8%** SPL on R2R-CE and **65.7%** SR / **72.4%** nDTW on RxR-CE val-unseen, beating DualVLN and NavFoM while using only **90k** training trajectories.
- Ablations show Pixel-to-3D beats a NavFoM-style metric-waypoint head by **24.8/25.2 SR points**, local GRPO advantages add **4.1/4.2 points** over trajectory-only advantage, and full AT-Mem beats full-history retention by **4.3/4.6 points**.
- On long-horizon trajectories (>12.5m) TAMP-Nav reaches **49.8%** SR versus **41.9%** for DualVLN and **30.9%** for StreamVLN, and real-world zero-shot deployment hits **60.0%** SR versus **53.0%** and **49.0%** for those baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating 3D control as 2D pixel pointing lets a VLM exploit its native 2D pretraining prior instead of learning geometry from scratch, a transferable trick for any 2D-pretrained model facing 3D action spaces.
> - Reasoning density should be an emergent, reward-shaped policy choice rather than a fixed schedule: RL-trained selective CoT nearly matches dense CoT accuracy (66.2% vs 66.8%) using only 26.3% of the reasoning calls.
> - Separating high-fidelity semantic anchors from lightweight geometric/temporal indicators lets memory scale to long horizons without the attention dilution or information loss that plagues uniform or full-history retention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation, which involves agents interpreting natural language instructions to navigate complex environments, has seen advancements with the introduction of Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Existing methodologies in VLM-based embodied navigation typically operate by mapping observations to low-level atomic actions (e.g., "turn left") or by regressing 3D spatial coordinates. However, these approaches face limitations due to VLMs being primarily pre-trained on 2D image-text pairs. This fundamental mismatch often leads to difficulties in spatial reasoning, manifested as geometric hallucinations and reduced sample efficiency.

Furthermore, integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has shown promise in enhancing high-level planning. However, current models often employ rigid reasoning schedules, triggering CoT at every step or fixed intervals. While this can improve decision quality, it introduces significant inference latency, creating a trade-off between navigation performance and computational efficiency.

Memory management for long-horizon navigation tasks also presents a challenge. Approaches that retain full historical visual features are prone to attention dilution and memory overflow. Conversely, indiscriminately discarding past observations or using simple forgetting mechanisms can lead to the loss of critical information necessary for robust navigation. Additionally, many existing architectures lack explicit spatio-temporal encoding for historical information, hindering the agent's ability to maintain a clear understanding of its past trajectory. This research addresses these three technical bottlenecks to improve the efficiency and robustness of VLM-based embodied navigation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to overcome the identified technical bottlenecks in VLM-based embodied navigation by proposing a unified framework named TAMP-Nav (Think, Align, Memorize, and Point). The motivations behind each component of TAMP-Nav are detailed below:

- **Addressing the Geometric Gap in Action Formulation:**
 - **Motivation:** Current VLN methods force VLMs, predominantly pre-trained on 2D data, to learn complex 3D geometric transformations for atomic actions or 3D coordinate regression. This results in spatial hallucinations and low sample efficiency, as it misaligns with the VLM's inherent 2D visual capabilities.
 - **Objective:** To bridge this geometric gap by decoupling high-level visual prompting from low-level 3D execution. The goal is to allow the VLM to operate naturally within its 2D visual domain, simplifying its task and enhancing its effectiveness.

- **Resolving the Dilemma Between Navigation Performance and Inference Efficiency:**
 - **Motivation:** While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning improves planning, existing models often employ dense or rigid CoT schedules (e.g., at every step), leading to severe inference latency. Such approaches fail to balance the need for effective decision-making with computational efficiency.
 - **Objective:** To develop an efficient cognitive architecture that dynamically triggers deep CoT reasoning only at critical junctures, thereby optimizing inference efficiency without compromising navigation performance. This aims to enable adaptive reasoning that is responsive to the complexity of the navigation task.

- **Mitigating the Loss of Key Information and Spatio-Temporal Perception in Long-Horizon Memory:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing memory mechanisms struggle with long-horizon histories. Retaining full visual features causes attention dilution and overflow, while sparse sampling risks losing crucial information. A lack of explicit spatio-temporal coordinates further impairs the agent's comprehension of its trajectory.
 - **Objective:** To design an efficient memory management system that preserves critical historical information and spatio-temporal perception without incurring excessive computational or memory overhead. This involves selectively storing high-fidelity data and compressing redundant information.

By addressing these challenges, the research aims to enable VLMs to be deployed more effectively and efficiently in embodied navigation tasks, achieving a balance between robust reasoning, accurate execution, and practical operational costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TAMP-Nav is a unified framework designed for efficient embodied navigation, operating through a continuous interaction loop that integrates visual observations, memory, reasoning, action formulation, and optimization.

#### 3.1. Pixel-to-3D Action Formulation (Point)

To bridge the geometric gap, TAMP-Nav redefines the VLM's role as a "visual pointer." At each step, the agent receives four egocentric views (360° FoV). The VLM selects an optimal view and outputs a 2D pixel coordinate (u, v) pointing to the desired waypoint. This 2D pixel is then projected into a local 3D point P_t using the depth map D_t,i and camera intrinsic matrix K. This 3D coordinate is subsequently transformed into the world coordinate system and dispatched to a low-level SLAM controller, which handles local movement. This formulation decouples high-level visual-semantic grounding from complex 3D geometric transformations, allowing the VLM to leverage its 2D pre-training priors. The system utilizes ground-truth depth in simulation and a depth camera for real-world deployment, showing robustness to depth inaccuracies.

#### 3.2. Anchor-Trajectory Memory and Selective Reasoning (Think and Memorize)

TAMP-Nav addresses the memory dilemma by integrating dynamic selective reasoning with an Anchor-Trajectory Memory.
- **Dynamic Selective Reasoning:** The model dynamically triggers deep Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning only at critical topological nodes (e.g., intersections, doorways), rather than at every step or fixed intervals. This aims to balance inference efficiency with navigation performance.
- **Anchor-Trajectory Memory:** This mechanism is built upon two components:
 - **Explicit Anchors:** When CoT reasoning is triggered at a critical node t_k, the state is stored as a triplet: an explicit spatio-temporal coordinate (M^(k)_STI), high-fidelity raw visual features (M^(k)_vis), and the CoT reasoning output as a "Planning Beacon" (M^(k)_state). This provides semantic summary and supports pixel-level loop closure.
 - **Space-Time Indicators (STI):** For routine path intervals between anchors, redundant visual features are discarded. Instead, a lightweight spatio-temporal stream is retained, encoded using 2D and 1D Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) for spatial (x, y), temporal (t), and angular (yaw, using sin/cos to avoid discontinuity) information. These embeddings are concatenated and projected via an MLP into a unified STI token. This preserves full-resolution movement trajectories and geometric connectivity without high visual overhead, preventing topological information loss.
- At each decision step, the dynamic input context comprises a "Working Memory" (two uniformly sampled frames between the current step and the most recent anchor) and a "Long-term Topological Memory" (reconstructed sequence of Anchors and STIs).

#### 3.3. MultiNav-CoT Dataset

To train TAMP-Nav with high-level logical reasoning and spatial understanding, the MultiNav-CoT dataset was constructed. This dataset, comprising 90k trajectories derived from VLN-CE, underwent filtering to remove rendering artifacts and low-quality instructions. It features selective CoT annotations to supervise sparse reasoning and aid the RL cold-start problem.
- **Spatiotemporal Key Node Mining:** A three-step strategy determines when to trigger deep reasoning. First, an importance score S(t) = S_sem(t) + S_vis(t) is computed for each frame. S_sem measures CLIP similarity between the current visual observation and instruction, while S_vis quantifies scene transitions via visual feature differences. Both are min-max normalized to a range. Second, a distance-based greedy filtering selects high-scoring nodes, discarding candidates within a minimum spatial distance (D_min) to ensure sparsity. Third, temporal padding inserts the highest-scoring intermediate frame if the spatial gap between consecutive nodes exceeds a maximum distance (D_max), maintaining global topological connectivity. This process yields a sparse set of key nodes (approx. 30% of trajectory length).
- **Structured CoT Generation:** A multi-stage prompt engineering pipeline based on Gemini 2.5 flash generates reasoning annotations. The generation is decomposed into three sub-tasks: Task Phase Localization, Current Observation Analysis, and Future Action Reasoning. Generating these components separately reduces cognitive load and minimizes hallucinations. A final fusion step synthesizes these outputs into a coherent narrative, coupling visual perception with long-horizon planning.

#### 3.4. Two-Level GRPO Paradigm (Align)

To enhance navigation and adaptive reasoning, TAMP-Nav employs a Two-Level Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Cold Start:** Initially, the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model is fine-tuned on the MultiNav-CoT dataset to provide robust priors before the reinforcement learning (RL) phase.
- **Dual Reward System:** This system features two types of rewards:
 - **Local Step Rewards (R^(t)_local):** Guide logical, safe, and timely decisions at each step. Components include Target Approach Reward (encourages movement toward target, signed sigmoid), Collision Avoidance Reward (clamped distance to obstacle), Stop Action Reward (supervises termination, penalizes premature stops), Reasoning Value Reward (evaluates CoT utility by correlating CoT indicator with approach reward margin over baseline), and Format Adherence Reward (ensures valid JSON output and pixel bounds).
 - **Global Trajectory Rewards (R_global):** Evaluate the holistic quality of the completed trajectory. Components include Task Success Reward (relaxes success to oracle boundaries), Trajectory Efficiency Reward (optimizes SPL), and Reasoning Density Reward (non-negative bonus discouraging excessive CoT).
- **Two-Level Policy Optimization:** Unlike step-by-step expert trajectories, GRPO encourages autonomous exploration. For a given instruction, G=8 complete navigation trajectories are generated. At each decision step, M=4 candidate actions are sampled via temperature-based sampling. These candidates are evaluated using local step rewards. An annealed guided sampling strategy is introduced in early RL stages, where the probability of selecting a candidate is weighted by its local reward (controlled by a decaying guidance coefficient β_k), transitioning to a uniform distribution as training progresses. Once G trajectories are generated, a total advantage A^(t)_S is computed by superimposing A_global (Z-score standardized global rewards across G rollouts) and A_local (Z-score standardized local reward against M candidates at a step). The policy is updated via a token-level GRPO objective, where the step-level advantage is broadcast to constituent tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted assess TAMP-Nav across standard benchmarks, emergent cognitive abilities, training efficiency, and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Performance on VLN-CE Benchmarks

TAMP-Nav achieved state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE validation unseen splits. It recorded a 66.2% Success Rate (SR) on R2R-CE Val-Unseen and 65.7% SR on RxR-CE Val-Unseen, outperforming previous methods.
The framework also demonstrated high data and computational efficiency. It achieved these results using 90k training trajectories (700k interactions), significantly fewer than competitors like DualVLN (763k trajectories) and NavFoM (3.37M interactions). The Pixel-to-3D action paradigm reduced average interaction steps to 9 per trajectory, compared to approximately 30 for other state-of-the-art models. This, combined with a sparse reasoning strategy and lightweight memory, resulted in efficient inference, averaging 16.58 seconds per task on a single A800 GPU, notably faster than StreamVLN (37.47s) and DualVLN (41.46s).

#### 4.2. Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks

On a subset of R2R-CE and RxR-CE val-unseen trajectories with expert path lengths exceeding 12.5 meters (5927 trajectories), TAMP-Nav achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 49.8%. This represented a substantial improvement over StreamVLN (30.9%) and DualVLN (41.9%). Performance analysis across varying trajectory lengths indicated that TAMP-Nav maintained its performance margin across all length bins.

#### 4.3. Emergent Reasoning-on-Demand

Analysis of CoT triggers demonstrated that the RL-trained TAMP-Nav autonomously concentrated reasoning at critical topological nodes, such as crossroads, doorways, and near target objects. The proportion of reasoning steps in straight corridors decreased from 38% in the SFT model to 11% after RL alignment, indicating the agent learned to suppress redundant CoT. Quantitatively, TAMP-Nav achieved 66.2% SR with a 26.3% CoT ratio, nearly matching the Dense CoT upper bound (66.8% SR) while significantly outperforming a fixed-interval baseline (60.1% SR with 36.2% CoT ratio).

#### 4.4. Robustness to Depth Uncertainty

To assess sensor inaccuracies, multiplicative Gaussian noise was injected into the depth of the selected pixel. Under a relative noise intensity (σ) of 0.2 (16% relative error), the Success Rate decreased by 2.8 points, indicating robustness to moderate waypoint-depth perturbations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Controlled Component Analysis:**
 - Replacing the Pixel-to-3D action with a NavFoM-style metric-waypoint output (SFT-only) resulted in a substantial SR decrease of 24.8/25.2 points on R2R-CE/RxR-CE, highlighting the efficacy of the pixel-to-3D formulation.
 - In the GRPO framework, incorporating local advantages improved SR by 4.1/4.2 points over trajectory-level advantage alone, and annealed guidance further contributed 2.8/3.1 points.
 - Full Anchor-Trajectory Memory (AT-Mem) improved SR by 4.3/4.6 points over full-history retention, while removing Space-Time Indicators (STI) decreased SR by 2.6/3.3 points.
 - The Auto reasoning strategy (TAMP-Nav) reduced CoT calls by 73.7% relative to Dense CoT with a minimal SR decrease of 0.6 points on R2R-CE and 0.4 points on RxR-CE.
- **Influence of CoT Supervision:** Experiments comparing CoT supervision sources under matched SFT budgets showed that Gemini 2.5 Flash CoT (used in MultiNav-CoT) led to higher navigation performance (55.7% SR on R2R-CE) compared to action-only (50.6% SR) or CoT generated by smaller/other LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 49.4% SR). This indicates sensitivity of downstream performance to the spatial consistency and quality of CoT supervision.
- **Effectiveness of Memory Mechanisms:** On long-horizon tasks, AT-Mem significantly outperformed traditional uniform sampling and full-history retention, which suffered from information loss and attention dilution, respectively. The performance gap between full TAMP-Nav and TAMP-Nav (w/o STI) widened on longer trajectories (beyond 120 actions), demonstrating that STI tokens are crucial for preserving geometric connectivity over extended execution.
- **Effectiveness of the Two-Level GRPO Framework:** Learning curves indicated that the baseline model with only trajectory-level rewards exhibited training instability and slower convergence. Superimposing local step advantages with Two-Level GRPO provided immediate signals that stabilized intermediate state optimization, improving the success reward. The annealed guided sampling further accelerated convergence and achieved higher final performance, demonstrating its role in increasing the density of high-quality trajectories in early samples.

#### 4.6. Real-World Deployment

Without any real-robot fine-tuning, TAMP-Nav achieved a 60.0% Success Rate in real-world environments across various task categories (meeting room, laboratory, hall, cross-area, outdoor navigation). This outperformed StreamVLN (49.0%) and DualVLN (53.0%) over 100 trials, including 80 indoor and 20 outdoor trials. The agent demonstrated effective instruction following and reasoning-on-demand behavior in unseen physical spaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces TAMP-Nav, a framework that addresses several limitations in VLM-based embodied navigation. The proposed **Pixel-to-3D Action Formulation** is significant because it aligns the VLM's 2D pre-training priors with 3D embodied execution by decoupling high-level visual prompting from low-level control. This design choice aims to mitigate spatial hallucinations and improve sample efficiency compared to methods that require VLMs to directly reason about 3D geometry.

The **integrated Selective Reasoning and Anchor-Trajectory Memory mechanism** represents an advancement in cognitive efficiency. By dynamically triggering Chain-of-Thought reasoning only at critical nodes and compressing routine paths into lightweight Space-Time Indicators, the framework seeks to balance inference efficiency with navigation performance. This approach aims to provide robust long-horizon memory management that preserves critical spatio-temporal information without incurring excessive computational overhead.

The **Two-Level Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) paradigm** contributes to efficient model alignment. By superimposing global outcome rewards with fine-grained process rewards and employing an annealed guided sampling strategy, it aims to provide dense supervision that tightly aligns the agent's cognitive planning with physical environmental feedback. This is designed to facilitate adaptive reasoning capabilities and improve learning dynamics.

The **MultiNav-CoT dataset**, curated with selective and structured CoT annotations, provides a valuable resource for training VLMs in sparse reasoning and high-level logical planning.

The main findings indicate that TAMP-Nav achieves competitive performance on VLN-CE benchmarks while demonstrating high data and computational efficiency. Its performance on long-horizon tasks, emergent reasoning-on-demand capabilities, robustness to depth uncertainty, and zero-shot transferability to real-world environments suggest its potential for practical applications.

However, the framework has certain **limitations**. Its dependence on accurate depth information and a reliable SLAM stack for low-level execution means that severe sensor errors or localization failures could degrade performance. The GRPO training relies on privileged simulator signals, and current capabilities do not support online reinforcement learning on a physical robot, limiting real-world deployment to zero-shot transfer. The key-node mining heuristic for CoT supervision may not always select optimal critical states, particularly in visually non-salient decision points or with divergent instruction styles. Furthermore, the Space-Time Indicator (STI) currently lacks height information, which could impact robustness in multi-floor buildings or uneven terrain. The fixed thresholds for the reasoning-density reward may also require re-tuning for different tasks.

Despite these limitations, TAMP-Nav's approach of spatial, cognitive, and optimization alignment offers a direction for developing more efficient, robust, and deployable VLM-based embodied navigation systems. Future work aims to extend this framework to broader embodied tasks and dynamic real-world environments, building upon its demonstrated capabilities.
