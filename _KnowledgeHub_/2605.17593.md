# Motion-Uncertainty-Aware Next-Best-View Planning for Moving Object Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Harvard University and the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia introduces a motion-uncertainty-aware next-best-view (NBV) planning framework for 3D reconstruction of unknown rigid objects undergoing planar motion. The framework accounts for object motion uncertainty during the delay between planning and execution, achieving superior reconstruction completeness in simulations and real-world experiments compared to non-predictive and tracking-only baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional NBV planning assumes static objects, rendering viewpoints suboptimal or uninformative for moving targets by the time of execution.
- Existing active perception for moving targets primarily focuses on tracking or visibility, often overlooking comprehensive surface coverage needed for complete 3D models.
- Addressing combined coverage-driven 3D reconstruction for moving objects with motion uncertainty is challenging due to dynamic camera-object configurations, non-linear coverage scores, and robot kinematic constraints.

## Method

- An online receding-horizon planning loop integrates ==fixed-lag Gaussian Process (GP) smoothing== for current object state estimation and a ==one-step GP predictor== for future object belief.
- Candidate viewpoints are generated on an ==uncertainty-adaptive ellipse== around the predicted object location, dynamically sized based on predicted positional covariance, and filtered for robot kinematic reachability.
- **Next-best-view** is selected by maximizing the expected coverage-driven score (approximated via ==Monte Carlo sampling==) over a predictive distribution of future object states, rather than a single nominal pose.

## Results

- The proposed **Predictive NBV** consistently achieved the **highest final reconstruction completeness** across all tested object process noise levels in simulation, outperforming non-predictive and tracking-only baselines.
- Monte Carlo sampling to approximate expected coverage scores under predictive belief further improved completeness, with **N=40** samples showing gains over **N=10** and single-mean evaluations while maintaining **sub-second scoring times**.
- Real-world experiments confirmed that Predictive NBV achieved **higher final reconstruction completeness** compared to baselines when confronted with injected object-position measurement noise and dynamic object trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly accounting for object motion uncertainty during the planning-to-execution delay is crucial for selecting robust and informative viewpoints for moving object reconstruction.
> - Evaluating the expected coverage gain across a probabilistic distribution of future object states significantly improves reconstruction completeness over methods that use only a single predicted mean object state.
> - Adapting the candidate viewpoint generation geometry based on the directional uncertainty of the object's future position helps in placing viewpoints that remain useful despite motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of two established fields: active 3D reconstruction and active perception for moving targets.

**Active 3D Reconstruction:** Traditional next-best-view (NBV) planning methods in active 3D reconstruction aim to build a comprehensive 3D model of an unknown object by iteratively selecting viewpoints that maximize reconstruction progress, often quantified by surface coverage. A fundamental assumption in most of these methods is that the object being reconstructed is static. This assumption simplifies planning, as a viewpoint evaluated at planning time is expected to maintain its informative quality when executed. Methods in this category often rely on voxel-based representations and evaluate viewpoints by ray-casting to identify frontier or unobserved regions. Examples include methods that maximize visible frontier or estimate expected reconstruction gain.

**Active Perception for Moving Targets:** A separate line of research focuses on active perception for moving targets. These systems typically prioritize objectives such as tracking the target, maintaining visibility, or improving observability. While these methods do account for target motion and often manage state uncertainty, their primary goal is usually not to achieve comprehensive 3D reconstruction of the target's surface. They often focus on ensuring the target remains within the sensor's field of view or optimizing pose estimation accuracy.

**The Research Gap:** The existing literature exhibits a gap in addressing the combined challenge of coverage-driven 3D reconstruction for *moving* objects while explicitly accounting for motion uncertainty during the decision-to-execution delay. When an object moves, the camera-object configuration changes, which can render a previously selected viewpoint suboptimal or uninformative by the time the robot executes the maneuver. This problem requires a planning framework that integrates both coverage-driven objectives from NBV and motion prediction/uncertainty management from active tracking. Prior work that does consider moving object 3D reconstruction often evaluates viewpoint quality at a single nominal object pose, which is susceptible to inaccuracies when motion and measurement noise introduce uncertainty in the object's actual execution-time state.

This work aims to bridge this gap by formulating moving object NBV planning as a belief-space viewpoint selection problem. It integrates predictive belief over the execution-time object state with coverage-driven viewpoint evaluation, thereby addressing the limitations of existing methods that either assume static objects or do not prioritize surface reconstruction completeness for dynamic targets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a motion-uncertainty-aware next-best-view (NBV) planning framework for the active 3D reconstruction of unknown rigid objects undergoing planar motion. This framework specifically aims to account for the uncertainty in object motion that arises during the delay between planning a viewpoint and executing the observation.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and limitations in current active perception and reconstruction paradigms:

1. **Limitations of Static Object Assumption in NBV:** Traditional NBV planners assume static objects. For moving objects, a viewpoint deemed optimal at the planning stage may become irrelevant or misaligned by the time the robot reaches it due to object movement. This leads to suboptimal reconstruction progress and inefficient resource allocation. The core problem is that the "informativeness" of a viewpoint is not static but depends on the evolving relative pose between the camera and the object, which is uncertain for moving targets.
2. **Insufficient Focus on Coverage in Moving Target Perception:** Existing active perception strategies for moving targets predominantly focus on tasks like tracking, visibility maintenance, or improving state observability. While these are critical, they generally do not prioritize achieving comprehensive surface coverage necessary for building complete 3D models. There is a need for methods that actively diversify viewpoints to observe previously unseen surfaces, even as the target moves.
3. **Real-world Applications in Field Robotics:** The problem is particularly relevant in field robotics, such as marine science. For example, robots or drones are increasingly used in whale research to collect visual data during surfacing events. While basic metrics like body length can be obtained from top-down views, more complete individual-level 3D models—valuable for health assessment and long-term monitoring—require broad and diverse surface coverage. This necessitates active viewpoint planning when only coarse target position measurements are available.
4. **Challenges in Belief-Space Planning for Reconstruction:**
 - **Dynamic Camera-Object Configuration:** Viewpoint quality depends heavily on the future camera-object configuration. Object motion can alter self-occlusion patterns and visible surface regions, making a single predicted pose insufficient for evaluation.
 - **Non-linear Coverage Scores:** Coverage-driven viewpoint scores often rely on non-linear visibility calculations and discretized surface representations (e.g., voxels). This non-linearity prevents closed-form expressions for the expected score under object-state uncertainty, requiring approximate evaluation methods.
 - **Robot Kinematic Constraints:** The most informative viewpoint might not be reachable by the robot within a single planning step due to its kinematic limits. The planner must therefore consider reachability as a filtering criterion.

To address these motivations, the key idea is to reformulate coverage-driven NBV planning for moving objects as a belief-space viewpoint selection problem. This entails evaluating candidate viewpoints not at a single predicted object pose, but by their *expected* coverage-driven score across a probabilistic distribution of plausible future object states. This approach aims to make the selected viewpoints robust to motion uncertainty and maximize the likelihood of observing new surfaces upon execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology utilizes an online receding-horizon planning loop that integrates object-state prediction with coverage-driven next-best-view (NBV) selection. This framework operates iteratively, incorporating new measurements, predicting object state, generating and evaluating candidate viewpoints, and executing the best option.

#### 3.1. Object State Estimation and Prediction

At each planning time *t<sub>k</sub>*, the system employs a two-stage process for object state estimation and prediction:

1. **Fixed-lag GP-based Smoother:**
 - The object's planar trajectory is modeled using a white-noise acceleration constant-velocity Gaussian Process (GP) prior. This allows for probabilistic modeling of object motion.
 - The robot receives noisy planar position measurements (˜p<sub>o</sub>) from a separate sensing source. Velocity is not directly measured and is inferred as a latent state.
 - A fixed-lag Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) smoother is applied to the *L* most recent measurements. This provides a Gaussian belief (mean μ<sub>o,k</sub> and covariance Σ<sub>o,k</sub>) over the current object state (position and velocity) at *t<sub>k</sub>*. This smoother captures both the current state and its associated uncertainty.
2. **One-step GP Predictor:**
 - The current Gaussian belief (μ<sub>o,k</sub>, Σ<sub>o,k</sub>) is propagated one step forward in time (∆t) using closed-form transition matrices derived from the GP motion prior.
 - This yields a predictive Gaussian belief (μ<sub>o,k+1|k</sub>, Σ<sub>o,k+1|k</sub>) for the object state at the *next* planning time *t<sub>k+1</sub>*. This predictive belief is crucial as it captures the expected position and velocity, along with their uncertainties, at the moment a selected viewpoint would be executed.
 - **Object Nominal Heading:** A nominal object heading (ˆθ<sub>o,k+1</sub>) is derived from the predicted mean velocity. This heading is used to define the orientation of the predicted object frame for candidate viewpoint generation.

#### 3.2. Prediction-Conditioned Candidate Viewpoint Generation

Candidate viewpoints are generated around the predicted object location at *t<sub>k+1</sub>*, adapting to the shape of the object's predicted positional uncertainty.

1. **Uncertainty-Adaptive Ellipse Discretization:**
 - A predicted object-centric scoring frame (ẽO<sub>k+1</sub>) is established with its origin at the predicted mean position (c<sub>k+1</sub>) and its x-axis aligned with the nominal predicted heading (ˆθ<sub>o,k+1</sub>).
 - Candidate viewpoints are sampled on a planar ellipse centered at c<sub>k+1</sub>. The semi-axes of this ellipse (a<sub>k+1</sub>, b<sub>k+1</sub>) are dynamically adapted based on the predicted positional covariance (Σ<sub>o,p,k+1|k</sub>) in the heading-aligned frame. Specifically, the semi-axes are calculated by adding an uncertainty-inflation factor (κ) multiplied by the forward (σ<sub>∥,k+1</sub>) and lateral (σ<sub>⊥,k+1</sub>) standard deviations, respectively, to a desired stand-off distance (r<sub>off</sub>). This effectively expands the search radius in directions of higher positional uncertainty.
 - The ellipse is discretized into *I* uniformly spaced azimuth angles, defining *I* candidate viewpoints.
2. **Candidate Viewpoint Positions and Yaw:** For each discretized point on the ellipse, a planar candidate position (p<sup>cand</sup><sub>k+1,i</sub>) in the world frame is determined. An inward-facing yaw (ψ<sup>cand</sup><sub>k+1,i</sub>) is assigned, pointing towards the predicted object center. These planar poses are then converted into full SE(3) camera poses (W T<sup>cand</sup><sub>C,k+1,i</sub>).
3. **Reachability Filtering:** From the set of *I* candidates, only those that are kinematically reachable by the robot within one planning step (∆t) are retained. This filtering is based on the robot's current pose and its maximum linear and angular velocity limits. The remaining viewpoints form the feasible set (T<sup>feas</sup><sub>k+1</sub>).

#### 3.3. Coverage-Driven NBV Evaluation

The system employs an efficient coverage-driven NBV proxy to score viewpoints, aiming to maximize the revelation of new surface information.

1. **Object-centric Bounded Voxel Proxy:** As the robot observes the object, incoming depth point clouds are registered into an object-centric bounded voxel grid. Voxels are labeled as Occupied (observed surface), Free (empty space), or Unknown (unobserved). Frontier voxels, at the boundary between observed and unknown regions, indicate potential areas for new information.
2. **Efficient Voxel Map Proxy (PB-NBV):** The deterministic per-view score S(O T C) is instantiated using the Projection-Based NBV (PB-NBV) score. This method efficiently summarizes the voxel map. Frontier and Occupied voxels are clustered, and each cluster is approximated by a minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid. This provides two collections of ellipsoids: O E<sub>F</sub> for Frontier regions and O E<sub>O</sub> for Occupied regions.
3. **Projection-Based Pixel-Area Score:** For a given candidate relative camera-object pose (O T C), these ellipsoids are projected into the camera's image plane. The score S(O T C) is calculated by summing the pixel areas of the projected Frontier ellipsoids, weighted by their depth rank (closer ellipsoids contribute more), and subtracting the weighted pixel areas of the projected Occupied ellipsoids. This rewards viewpoints that are expected to reveal more new surface and penalizes those that observe already-known parts.

#### 3.4. Motion-Uncertainty-Aware NBV Planning

The core contribution is how candidate viewpoints are evaluated under object motion uncertainty.

1. **Camera-Object Configuration under Belief Sampling:** To account for uncertainty, *N* independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples (x<sup>o,(n)</sup><sub>k+1</sub>) are drawn from the predictive Gaussian belief (Eq. 8). Each sample provides a plausible future object position and velocity, which in turn defines a sample-induced world-frame pose for the object. For a fixed candidate camera pose, each object sample then determines a unique relative camera-object transform (ẽO<sup>(n)</sup><sub>k+1</sub> T<sup>cand,(n)</sup><sub>C,k+1</sub>). This means that for a single candidate camera viewpoint, there are *N* possible relative configurations with the object due to uncertainty.
2. **Expected NBV Score:** The uncertainty-aware score (J<sub>k+1</sub>) for a candidate viewpoint is defined as the *expected* value of the deterministic PB-NBV score S(·) (Eq. 16) over the predictive object-state belief. Since S(·) involves non-linear operations (projection, discretization, depth-ordering), this expectation does not have a closed-form solution. Therefore, Monte Carlo sampling is used to approximate the expectation: the deterministic score S(·) is computed for each of the *N* sample-induced relative camera-object configurations, and these *N* scores are averaged.
3. **Motion-Uncertainty-Aware NBV Selection:** The final next-best-view (W T<sup>⋆</sup><sub>C,k+1</sub>) is selected by maximizing this approximated expected coverage-driven score over all feasible candidate viewpoints. This policy prioritizes viewpoints that are most likely to yield high reconstruction gain, even considering the range of plausible future object states.

This methodology thus predicts the object's uncertain future state, generates viewpoints adaptively to this uncertainty, filters for robot reachability, and then evaluates the expected informativeness of each feasible viewpoint using Monte Carlo sampling under the predictive belief, ultimately selecting the most promising view for execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in both simulation and real-world settings, aimed to assess whether the motion-uncertainty-aware NBV framework improves coverage-driven reconstruction of moving objects compared to non-predictive or tracking-only baselines. Reconstruction completeness, defined as the percentage of ground-truth object voxels observed by the reconstruction, served as the primary metric.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

Simulation experiments investigated the framework's performance under varying object process noise, robot reachability limits, and the individual contributions of its components.

- **Baseline Comparison under Process Noise:**
 - The **Predictive NBV (proposed method)** consistently achieved the highest final reconstruction completeness across all tested levels of object process noise (q<sub>c</sub> ∈ {0.005, 0.010, 0.015}). The performance gains were most pronounced at higher process noise levels, where object motion uncertainty was greater.
 - **Non-predictive NBV**, which evaluates viewpoints based on the current object belief without considering future motion, performed less effectively. This demonstrated that ignoring object motion during the planning-to-execution interval leads to suboptimal viewpoint selection as the camera-object configuration changes.
 - **Tracking-only**, which simply followed the predicted object location without coverage-driven scoring, consistently yielded lower completeness. This indicated that proximity to the target, even with prediction, is insufficient for comprehensive reconstruction; active viewpoint diversification for coverage is essential.
 - Visualizations of reconstructions showed that Predictive NBV continued to reveal new surface regions in later iterations, whereas Tracking-only often maintained views of already observed surfaces.
- **Effect of Robot Reachability:** Increasing the robot's maximum per-step travel distance (relative to the object's displacement) improved both the growth rate of coverage and the final completeness. This highlights that while predictive planning can identify informative future viewpoints, the physical ability of the robot to reach these viewpoints within the planning interval is a practical limiting factor.
- **Ablation of Belief-Space Evaluation:**
 - Compared to randomly selecting feasible candidates, scoring viewpoints **only at the predicted mean object state** (without Monte Carlo sampling) improved performance.
 - However, employing **Monte Carlo (MC) sampling** for expected scoring under the predictive belief further improved completeness. Increasing the number of samples from N=10 to N=40 resulted in additional gains, suggesting that a more accurate approximation of the expected score leads to more reliable viewpoint choices. Average scoring times remained subsecond, indicating the approach's computational feasibility.
- **Ablation of Candidate Geometry:**
 - Using an **uncertainty-adaptive ellipse** for candidate viewpoint generation led to improved final completeness compared to an isotropic ring (where the radius was based on the maximum eigenvalue of positional covariance). This suggests that shaping the candidate search space according to the directional uncertainty of the object's future position is beneficial for placing viewpoints that remain useful.
 - However, the gains from adaptive candidate geometry were smaller than those achieved through Monte Carlo belief-space evaluation, implying that candidate generation and expected scoring are complementary, with belief-space evaluation contributing a larger performance improvement.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Real-world experiments utilized a physical mobile robot with a depth camera, an S-curve object trajectory, and injected object-position measurement noise to validate the simulation findings.

- **Baseline Comparison under Measurement Noise:**
 - Similar to simulations, **Predictive NBV** achieved the highest final reconstruction completeness across varying levels of injected object-position measurement noise (σ ∈ {0.10, 0.15, 0.20} m).
 - The trends observed in simulation were confirmed: Predictive NBV demonstrated continuous improvement in coverage over later iterations, while Non-predictive NBV and Tracking-only tended to plateau earlier. This indicates that viewpoints selected without considering future object motion or solely for proximity become less effective over time when faced with real-world noise and object movement.
 - The consistent performance superiority of Predictive NBV over baselines in a physical setting, despite sharing the same smoothing, reachability constraints, and reconstruction update procedures, confirmed the effectiveness of its viewpoint selection strategy.
- **Robot Behavior:** The robot's behavior during real-world trials illustrated that it actively changed its relative viewpoint over successive planning iterations. This demonstrated that the planner selected views intended to expose new object surfaces rather than simply maintaining a fixed perspective or following the object, which is critical for reconstruction completeness.

In summary, the main findings indicate that explicitly accounting for motion uncertainty during NBV planning through a predictive belief-space evaluation significantly improves reconstruction completeness for moving objects. This approach outperforms methods that ignore future motion, as well as those that prioritize tracking over coverage-driven viewpoint selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in active 3D reconstruction by addressing the complex challenge of reconstructing moving objects under uncertainty. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Research Domains:** The work successfully integrates and bridges two previously distinct areas: coverage-driven next-best-view (NBV) planning, traditionally for static objects, and prediction-driven active perception, typically focused on tracking or visibility for moving targets. This combined approach is crucial for real-world scenarios where targets are dynamic and comprehensive 3D models are desired.
2. **Improved Reconstruction Performance:** The primary impact is the demonstrated improvement in reconstruction completeness for moving objects. By explicitly accounting for object motion uncertainty during the decision-to-execution delay, the framework enables a robot to select viewpoints that are more likely to be informative when executed. This leads to more efficient and comprehensive 3D model acquisition in dynamic environments.
3. **Enhanced Robustness to Uncertainty:** The use of a predictive belief over future object states, combined with Monte Carlo evaluation of expected viewpoint quality, makes the planning process robust to the inherent uncertainties in object motion and noisy measurements. This mitigates the risk of selecting suboptimal or uninformative viewpoints, a common failure mode for static-assumption NBV methods when applied to moving targets.
4. **Practical Applicability in Field Robotics:** The motivation explicitly mentions applications in field robotics, such as collecting comprehensive visual data for whale research. This framework directly addresses the need for diverse, informative viewpoints from moving marine wildlife, offering a path toward more detailed health assessments and monitoring efforts than currently possible with limited views. The real-world experiments with a physical robot further validate its practical applicability.
5. **Methodological Contributions:**
 - The integration of fixed-lag Gaussian Process (GP) smoothing and prediction into an NBV pipeline provides a robust mechanism for estimating and forecasting object state and its uncertainty from sparse, noisy measurements.
 - The concept of uncertainty-adaptive candidate generation, where viewpoint search regions are shaped by the predicted uncertainty, represents a novel approach to guide the exploration.
 - The use of Monte Carlo sampling to approximate non-linear expected coverage scores in belief space offers a computationally feasible solution for a problem that lacks a closed-form analytical solution.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper identifies several limitations, such as reliance on accurate calibration and external position measurements, and approximations in voxel-based evaluation. These insights pave the way for future research, including incorporating robot pose uncertainty, integrating onboard target tracking with reconstruction, and exploring alternative 3D representations that may be more robust to thin structures or strong self-occlusion.
7. **Generalizable Framework:** The core idea of evaluating a deterministic coverage-driven score in expectation over a predictive belief is a generalizable concept. It could potentially be adapted to incorporate different deterministic NBV scoring functions or alternative object representation techniques, enhancing its broader utility in active perception research.

In conclusion, this work offers a significant step forward in active 3D reconstruction by providing a principled framework to plan for moving objects under uncertainty. Its impact lies in improving reconstruction quality, enhancing robustness, and opening new possibilities for autonomous data collection in dynamic real-world settings.
