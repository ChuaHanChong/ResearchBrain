# MARS-Sep: Multimodal-Aligned Reinforced Sound Separation

> [!summary] Summary
> MARS-Sep introduces a reinforcement learning framework for universal sound separation, reformulating mask prediction as a stochastic decision-making process guided by multimodal rewards. It aims to align separated audio outputs with the semantic intent of audio, text, or visual queries, achieving a more perceptually consistent separation compared to optimization solely based on signal-level metrics.

## Problem

- Existing sound separation models, primarily optimized using signal-level metrics (e.g., SDR, SIR), often lack semantic fidelity.
- Despite achieving high signal metrics, outputs can still contain perceptually salient interference from non-target sources, failing to semantically correspond to the user's query intent.
- There is a "metric dilemma" where traditional signal quality metrics do not fully capture semantic alignment or perceptual consistency with diverse multimodal queries.

## Method

- Reformulates query-conditioned universal ==sound separation== as a reinforcement learning problem, treating mask prediction as a stochastic decision-making process akin to RLHF.
- Develops a multimodal reward model that assesses semantic alignment between separated audio and various queries (audio, text, image) using a progressively fine-tuned ImageBind encoder and Multi-Modal Low-Rank Bilinear Pooling (MLBP).
- Employs a PPO-style trust-region policy optimization strategy for stable mask generation, using a factorized Beta distribution over masks and a clipped surrogate objective with entropy and KL penalties.

## Results

- MARS-Sep consistently achieved the highest SDR and CLAP scores across various query modalities (Text, Audio, Image, Composed) on both VGGSOUND-clean+ and MUSIC-clean+ datasets.
- A human perceptual study with non-expert listeners demonstrated that MARS-Sep produced higher semantic-matching scores and was perceptually preferred over baselines for query-separated audio consistency.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of both reinforcement learning and progressive fine-tuning for the ImageBind encoder, along with MLBP for robust multimodal reward aggregation, in achieving overall performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sound separation can be effectively reframed as a stochastic decision-making problem, allowing for optimization based on high-level semantic alignment beyond traditional signal-level metrics.
> - Integrating multimodal rewards, derived from large pre-trained encoders like ImageBind that are progressively fine-tuned, provides robust and semantically rich feedback to guide the separation policy.
> - Explicitly modeling multiplicative interactions between modalities via Multi-Modal Low-Rank Bilinear Pooling (MLBP) enhances the robustness of multimodal reward aggregation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Sound separation is a foundational task in audio processing that aims to isolate individual sound sources from complex mixtures. Historically, research in this area progressed from domain-specific applications, such as speech separation and music source separation, towards Universal Sound Separation (USS), which seeks to decompose arbitrary mixtures without class constraints. Key advancements enabling USS include permutation invariant training (PIT) and mixture invariant training (MixIT), alongside large-scale datasets like AudioSet.

A significant development has been Query-Based Sound Extraction (QBSE), which reframes separation as extracting user-specified content guided by various query modalities. These include:
- **Label-queried systems:** Simple but limited to closed-set categories.
- **Text-queried approaches:** Offer open-vocabulary capabilities but face challenges in joint optimization and generalization.
- **Visual queries:** Exploit images for grounding sound sources.
- **Audio queries:** Use exemplar sounds to target abstract or indescribable content.

Recent efforts have aimed to unify these modalities using cross-attention or hybrid encoders, with models like CLIPSEP and AudioSep leveraging multimodal pre-training. Despite these advances, challenges remain in open-vocabulary robustness, multi-polarity operation (extraction and removal), and scalable multimodal composition (e.g., "dog barking in this image"). Query-mixup style training, as seen in OmniSep, addresses some of these issues but leaves room for improved semantic alignment.

Concurrently, a trend towards generative or flow-based paradigms in sound separation has emerged, with models like FlowSep and ZeroSep synthesizing query-consistent sources or performing zero-training separation using diffusion models. DAVIS and DAVIS-Flow apply visually-conditioned rectified-flow trajectories for time-aligned separation. These developments indicate a shift towards richer multimodal conditioning and generative reasoning.

This work draws inspiration from reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), a paradigm that has become central to aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. RLHF involves training a reward model from human preference data and then using this model to optimize a base policy via algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This approach has extended to multimodal models, improving grounding and reasoning by incorporating structured rewards.

The present work, MARS-Sep, integrates the RLHF-style preference alignment concept into query-conditioned universal sound separation. It addresses a recognized "metric dilemma" where models optimized for low-level signal metrics (e.g., SDR, SIR) can still produce outputs with perceptually salient interference, thus lacking semantic fidelity to the user's query. By reformulating separation as a decision-making process guided by multimodal rewards, MARS-Sep aims to align separated audio outputs not only with signal-level quality but also with the semantic intent of audio, text, or visual queries, mirroring the alignment goal of RLHF in LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a "metric dilemma" observed in current sound separation methodologies. Existing models are primarily optimized using signal-level metrics such as Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (SDR), Signal-to-Interference Ratio (SIR), Signal-to-Artifact Ratio (SAR), and Scale-Invariant SDR improvement (SI-SDRi). While these metrics quantify aspects of waveform reconstruction, interference suppression, and artifact reduction, they do not inherently guarantee semantic fidelity or perceptual consistency with the user's intent. Consequently, a model might achieve high scores on these signal metrics yet produce an output that still contains perceptually salient interference from acoustically similar non-target sources, thereby failing to semantically correspond to the query.

To address this misalignment, the key objective is to introduce a "preference alignment" perspective to sound separation. This approach is conceptually analogous to how reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) is used to align large language models (LLMs) with human intent. In the context of sound separation, the user query (which can be audio, text, or an image) is considered the "preference," and the goal is to produce a separated audio output that maximizes semantic alignment with this query.

Specifically, the objectives are:
1. **Reformulate sound separation as a meta-reasoning and stochastic decision-making task:** Instead of directly regressing ground-truth masks, the goal is to learn a policy that makes decisions about mask generation.
2. **Integrate multimodal rewards into the optimization process:** Develop a reward model that can assess semantic consistency between the separated audio and diverse query modalities (audio, text, vision), thus providing a high-level semantic signal to guide the separation policy.
3. **Ensure stable and effective policy optimization:** Adapt a reinforcement learning algorithm, specifically a trust-region method, to optimize the mask generation policy. This involves using a factorized Beta mask policy and a clipped surrogate objective with entropy regularization and a KL penalty to ensure stable learning.
4. **Enhance multimodal encoder discriminability and reward reliability:** Implement a progressive alignment strategy to fine-tune the multimodal encoder (ImageBind) used in the reward model. This aims to improve cross-modal discrimination and stabilize the reward signals, mitigating potential reward hacking.
5. **Achieve consistent improvements across diverse query modalities and benchmarks:** Validate the proposed framework's ability to improve both signal-level metrics and semantic quality in Text-, Audio-, and Image-Queried separation tasks on established datasets like VGGSOUND-clean+ and MUSIC-clean+.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to produce separated sound outputs that are not only acoustically clean but also semantically faithful to the user's explicit or implicit preferences expressed through multimodal queries.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MARS-Sep proposes a reinforcement learning (RL) framework for sound separation, reframing mask prediction as a stochastic decision-making process guided by multimodal rewards. The approach builds upon OmniSep as the base separation architecture.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Universal Sound Separation and OmniSep

Universal Sound Separation (USS) aims to estimate individual source signals {s_i(t)} from an arbitrary audio mixture x(t). In the time-frequency domain, this involves predicting masks {M_i} such that the estimated spectrogram ˆS_i = M_i ⊙ X (where X is the mixture STFT magnitude). The base separation architecture used is OmniSep, which employs a frozen ImageBind encoder to map audio, image, and text queries into a shared feature space. A U-Net based Separate-Net then predicts masks. OmniSep supports query mixing across modalities (Query-Mixup) and negative queries.

#### 3.2. MARS-Sep: Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Source Universal Sound Separation Enhancement

MARS-Sep conceptualizes query-conditioned sound separation as a preference alignment problem, analogous to RLHF. It defines sound separation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with states, actions, transitions, and rewards.

#### 3.2.1. Core Components of the RL Framework

- **Base Policy (π_θ):** The sound separation model that takes the mixture spectrogram (X) and query (Q) as input (state) and outputs a mask (M) as an action.
- **Preference Reward Model (R):** A learned multimodal encoder that scores the semantic alignment between the separated audio and the query, providing a scalar reward.
- **Optimization Process:** An algorithm to update the policy to maximize the reward.

#### 3.2.2. Trust-Region-Style Policy Optimization for Stable Mask Sampling

The mask generation is formulated as an actor-only trust-region optimization problem.
1. **Stochastic Policy:** The separator's deterministic mask proposal P_θ(X, Q) ∈^(H×W×K) is converted into a stochastic policy over masks using a factorized Beta distribution:
 π_θ(M | X, Q) = ∏_(h,w,k)_ Beta(M_h,w,k_; α_h,w,k_, β_h,w,k_)
 where α = 1 + κP_θ and β = 1 + κ(1 - P_θ). The concentration scale κ allows control over exploration, and is annealed during training.
2. **Reparameterized Sampling:** Masks M are sampled from this Beta policy, allowing for exploration while staying close to the proposal.
3. **Reward and Advantage Computation:** At each step, a mask M is sampled from a frozen "old" policy (π_θ_old_), and the corresponding waveform ˆy is reconstructed. A scalar reward R is computed by comparing ˆy against query-conditioned targets. A moving-average baseline 'b' is used to calculate the advantage A = R - b.
4. **Clipped Surrogate Objective:** The policy is updated by minimizing a PPO-style clipped trust-region surrogate objective with entropy regularization and a KL penalty:
 J_clip(θ) = E_M~π_θ_old_ [min(r_θ(M) ˜A, clip(r_θ(M), 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ) ˜A) + λ_H H(π_θ) - λ_KL KL(π_θ || π_θ_old_)]
 where r_θ(M) is the importance ratio, ˜A is the group-relative advantage, ϵ is the clipping range, λ_H is the entropy coefficient, and λ_KL is the KL penalty coefficient. The old policy is updated to the current policy after each optimization step (single-epoch PPO). This design aims to provide stable training without requiring additional value networks.

#### 3.2.3. Multimodal Reward Model

The reward measures the semantic match between the separated audio (ˆy) and the query across modalities in a unified embedding space provided by ImageBind.
1. **Embeddings:** Audio waveforms, text queries, and sampled video frames are projected into a shared space using ImageBind encoders (ϕ_a, ϕ_t, ϕ_v). All embeddings are ℓ2-normalized, and similarity is measured using cosine similarity.
2. **Unimodal Rewards:** Intermediate rewards include acoustic fidelity (r_a->a = sim(ϕ_a(ˆy), ϕ_a(y⋆))), semantic alignment with text (r_t->a = sim(ϕ_a(ˆy), ϕ_t(t⋆))), and consistency with visual context (r_v->a = sim(ϕ_a(ˆy), ϕ_v(v⋆))).
3. **Aggregation Strategy: Query-Pooling (MLBP):** Instead of separate comparisons, the target-side multimodal embeddings (ϕ_a(y⋆), ϕ_t(t⋆), ϕ_v(v⋆)) are fused into a joint representation (z⋆) using Multi-Modal Low-Rank Bilinear Pooling (MLBP). The final scalar reward R is then sim(ϕ_a(ˆy), z⋆). This fusion aims to capture joint multimodal consistency, modeling multiplicative interactions between modalities and providing a robust semantic anchor.

#### 3.3. Multimodal Encoder Fine-Tuning via Progressive Alignment

To enhance the reward model's discrimination between target and non-target sounds and mitigate reward hacking, a progressive alignment strategy is applied to fine-tune the ImageBind encoder. This three-stage curriculum gradually adapts the model from semantic grounding to intra-class discrimination and multimodal alignment, with encoders remaining frozen and only task-specific heads being updated.

1. **Sample Pair Construction:** Multimodal contrastive pairs are built with an audio clip as the anchor. Positives include label text, another audio instance of the same class, or temporally aligned video frames. Negatives use content from other classes or mismatched segments.
2. **Stage 1: Audio-Text Alignment:** The model is trained to align audio signals with their textual labels. Only projection heads and a shared temperature parameter are unfrozen. The objective is a symmetric InfoNCE loss (L_S1), encouraging paired embeddings to converge and repelling mismatched pairs.
3. **Stage 2: Audio-Audio Discrimination:** Focuses on distinguishing same-class audio from different-class audio. The audio projection head and shared temperature are unfrozen. The objective (L_S2) combines an InfoNCE loss, a triplet loss, and a consistency loss. A fraction of audio-text pairs from Stage 1 are mixed in to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
4. **Stage 3: Audio-Video Grounding:** Introduces visual context through audio-video pairs. Audio and vision projection heads are jointly trained. The objective (L_S3) includes InfoNCE and triplet losses for audio-video embeddings, partially incorporating objectives from Stages 1 and 2 to maintain capabilities.

Each stage initializes from the best checkpoint of the previous stage, ensuring a stable and interpretable evolution of the model's discriminative capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of MARS-Sep was evaluated through extensive experiments on VGGSOUND-clean+ and MUSIC-clean+ datasets, comparing it against several baselines across different query modalities. Evaluation metrics included signal-level metrics (SDR, SIR, SAR, SI-SDRi) and a semantic consistency metric (CLAP score).

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results: VGGSOUND-clean+ Dataset (Table 1)

- **Overall Performance:** MARS-Sep demonstrated consistent improvements across query modalities (Text, Audio, Image, and Composed Omni-modal). It achieved the highest SDR and CLAP scores in all settings.
- **Signal-level Metrics:** MARS-Sep recorded the highest SI-SDRi in three settings (tied for best in audio queries). While generally strong, OmniSep showed slightly higher SIR for audio queries and higher SAR for image and composed queries.
- **Semantic Quality:** The CLAP score, which measures semantic consistency, was consistently higher for MARS-Sep, indicating better alignment between separated audio and the query intent.

#### 4.1.2. MUSIC-clean+ Dataset (Table 2)

- **Cross-Domain Generalizability:** On the MUSIC-clean+ dataset, which focuses on structured harmonic signals, MARS-Sep again exhibited gains across all query modalities.
- **Semantic Consistency:** Notably, MARS-Sep achieved higher CLAP scores than all baselines, suggesting its ability to preserve semantic consistency in music-centric scenarios.

#### 4.1.3. Generative Separation Frameworks (Table 3)

- **CLAP Score Comparison:** Generative models like ZeroSep and FlowSep, which do not produce mask-based or waveform-aligned outputs, showed substantially higher variance and often inflated CLAP scores due to their synthesis-based decoding. In contrast, MARS-Sep provided more stable and consistent semantic alignment while allowing evaluation under standard separation metrics. An additional CLAP audio score was introduced, which measures semantic similarity between separated audio and target audio, further highlighting MARS-Sep's fidelity to the target.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Spectrogram Analysis (Figure 3):** Visualizations of log-mel spectrograms from VGGSOUND-clean+ demonstrated that MARS-Sep suppressed non-target components more selectively than baselines, preserving the target's harmonic ridges and temporal continuity.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Appendix E.1)

- **Hyperparameter Settings (Tables 4-7):**
 - **Beta Distribution Concentration (κ):** κ=9 provided the best balance between semantic reward optimization and signal fidelity.
 - **PPO Clip Range (ϵ):** The model showed limited sensitivity to ϵ, indicating stable trust-region updates.
 - **Entropy Coefficient (λ_H) and KL Coefficient (λ_KL):** Moderate entropy regularization and a lightweight KL penalty were found to be effective for maintaining mask diversity and preventing policy drift.
- **Modality Aggregation Module (Tables 8-9):**
 - MLBP (Multi-Modal Low-Rank Bilinear Pooling) generally outperformed Max Pooling, Average Pooling, and Learnable Weighted Sums. MLBP's strength was attributed to its explicit modeling of multiplicative interactions across modalities, which is beneficial when semantic cues are complex or span multiple modalities.
- **Progressive Fine-Tuning Effect on ImageBind (Table 10):**
 - Fine-tuning the ImageBind encoder with the progressive alignment strategy significantly improved its ability to discriminate between target and non-target sounds, yielding a larger average similarity difference between target text embedding and mixture embedding compared to a pretrained ImageBind.
- **Reinforcement Learning vs. Encoder Fine-Tuning (Table 11):**
 - A comparison of training configurations (supervised + frozen encoder baseline, RL-only, FT-only, RL+FT) showed that the FT-only variant improved semantic sensitivity but led to lower SDR, SIR, and SI-SDRi, indicating semantic contamination from interfering sources. The RL-only variant improved all metrics over the baseline. The full model (RL+FT) achieved the best overall performance, demonstrating that RL is crucial for leveraging fine-tuned encoders while maintaining signal fidelity.
- **Resolving Semantic Ambiguity (Appendix E.2, Table 12):**
 - A case study separating "tap dancing" from "typewriting" (acoustically similar sources) demonstrated that MARS-Sep achieved a higher SIR and CLAP score than baselines. This indicated that MARS-Sep effectively mitigated semantic contamination, even when signal-level metrics were comparable, validating its ability to address the "metric dilemma."
- **Efficacy of Progressive Alignment (Appendix E.3, Table 13):**
 - The 3-stage progressive fine-tuning strategy consistently outperformed no fine-tuning and 1-stage fine-tuning, particularly for text and audio queries. This suggested that staged alignment enhances the multimodal encoder’s domain adaptation, especially for modalities with greater semantic granularity differences.
- **Memory Consumption and Inference Latency (Appendix E.4, Table 14):**
 - MARS-Sep had comparable memory consumption to OmniSep (approx. 35.5GB on an A800 GPU). Training time was approximately 8 hours per epoch (50% longer than OmniSep). However, inference latency (Real-Time Factor) remained comparable to OmniSep across all modalities, indicating minimal additional overhead at inference time.
- **User Study (Appendix E.5, Table 15):**
 - A human perceptual study involving 10 non-expert listeners showed that MARS-Sep achieved higher semantic-matching scores and was preferred over OmniSep when evaluating consistency between queries and separated audio. This suggests that the improvements are perceptually aligned and not solely artifacts of CLAP similarities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MARS-Sep offers advancements in query-conditioned universal sound separation by directly addressing the "metric dilemma" – the discrepancy between high signal-level metric scores and perceptually salient semantic contamination. By reformulating separation as a preference alignment problem guided by multimodal rewards, the work proposes a method that aims to produce outputs that are not only acoustically clean but also semantically faithful to user intent across audio, text, and visual queries.

The significance of this approach lies in:
1. **Bridging the Semantic Gap:** It introduces a mechanism to bridge the gap between low-level signal processing and high-level semantic understanding in sound separation, potentially leading to outputs that are more aligned with human perception and expectations.
2. **Enhanced Robustness:** The use of a progressively-aligned multimodal reward model, built upon ImageBind, provides more stable and informative feedback, which can lead to more robust separation in complex, in-the-wild acoustic environments.
3. **Cross-Modal Generalization:** Consistent performance gains across text, audio, image, and composed queries on diverse datasets like VGGSOUND-clean+ and MUSIC-clean+ suggest improved generalization across different input modalities and sound types.
4. **Inspiration from LLM Alignment:** Applying principles from reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), previously successful in aligning LLMs, to audio processing tasks opens new avenues for research in making audio models more controllable and semantically aware.
5. **Practical Implications:** Improved semantically-aware sound separation could have implications for downstream applications such as speech recognition (by providing cleaner speech signals), sound event detection (by isolating target events), and acoustic scene analysis. It could also enhance data augmentation strategies by providing higher-quality isolated sources.

While the approach introduces increased training computational cost compared to traditional supervised methods, its comparable inference-time efficiency suggests its potential for integration into real-world applications where semantically precise sound separation is critical. The human perceptual study further supports that the improvements are perceptible, validating the method's potential for user-centric audio technologies.
