# Look Where It Matters: Adaptive Visual Refinement for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AtVLA enhances Vision-Language-Action models by integrating register tokens to mitigate attention artifacts and an uncertainty-gated, attention-guided mechanism for adaptive visual refinement. This framework achieved an average success rate of 98.4% on the LIBERO benchmark, up from 94.2%, and improved real-world task success from 46.5% to 69.0%, while maintaining limited computational overhead.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit limitations in spatially precise robotic manipulation due to visual bottlenecks.
- Vision encoders develop 'high-norm' tokens in low-information background regions, distorting dense spatial features and corrupting attention maps, hindering precise localization.
- Low-resolution visual observations, especially from third-person cameras, frequently lack the fine-grained geometric details necessary for precise actions.

## Method

- **AtVLA** introduces ==register-enhanced visual encoding== by inserting learnable register embeddings into the visual encoder's input, serving as dedicated carriers for global spatial information.
- Developed an ==uncertainty-gated action prediction== mechanism that estimates action uncertainty from multiple sampled action chunks, triggering visual refinement only when necessary.
- Implemented ==attention-guided local refinement==, which dynamically crops and re-encodes interaction-critical regions using action-conditioned spatial attention maps when high uncertainty is detected.

## Results

- AtVLA improved the average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark from **94.2%** (π0) to **98.4%**, with notable gains on Spatial (**99.3%** vs 96.8%) and Long-10 (**96.5%** vs 85.2%) suites.
- In real-world evaluations, the framework achieved an overall success rate increase from **46.5%** (π0) to **69.0%**, with specific tasks like Kitchen Grab improving from **35%** to **65%**.
- The system maintained a low additional inference cost, estimated at **1.4–1.6×** that of the base π0 model, by efficiently reusing attention computations and KV caches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Attention artifacts in VLA visual encoders can be effectively rectified through embodied post-training by incorporating dedicated register tokens that absorb global spatial information.
> - Register tokens emerge as reliable carriers of task-relevant embodied spatial and geometric knowledge, outperforming CLS tokens and mean-pooled patch features.
> - Adaptive, uncertainty-gated visual refinement, guided by the policy's own attention, provides fine-grained details efficiently without incurring the computational cost of continuous high-resolution processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models represent an emerging paradigm for generalist robot manipulation. These models leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining to acquire semantic knowledge and instruction-following capabilities, which are then transferred to continuous control policies through post-training on robot demonstrations. Recent advancements in VLAs, such as Octo, OpenVLA, and π0, have demonstrated promising generalization across a variety of manipulation tasks.

However, existing VLA models often exhibit limitations in spatially precise robotic manipulation. This work identifies two specific visual bottlenecks that hinder fine-grained control:
1. **Attention Artifacts:** Vision encoders in VLAs, similar to generic Vision Transformers, develop "high-norm" tokens in low-information background regions. These tokens act as repositories for global information, distorting dense spatial features and corrupting attention maps. The paper posits that this phenomenon is exacerbated during embodied post-training, where task-relevant spatial information (e.g., object location, depth, local geometry) spills into patch tokens due to the limited capacity of original global tokens (like the [CLS] token). This misplacement of information undermines precise localization and contact reasoning.
2. **Resolution Limitations:** Low-resolution visual observations, especially from third-person cameras, may lack the fine-grained geometric details necessary for precise actions, even when target objects are correctly localized. While wrist-mounted cameras can provide close-range views, they suffer from viewpoint sensitivity and pose-change variability. Explicit vision experts or 3D representations can offer stronger geometry but introduce additional complexity in sensing, calibration, and modeling.

This research addresses these challenges by integrating two complementary mechanisms: a novel method for rectifying attention distributions within the visual encoder and an adaptive, uncertainty-gated visual refinement strategy. It builds upon previous work on attention artifacts in Vision Transformers (e.g., Darcet et al. 2023) and various visual refinement techniques for Large Multimodal Models (MLLMs), but specifically tailors and integrates these solutions within the VLA framework, leveraging embodied data and the policy's internal attention for adaptive control. The approach aims to restore reliable spatial grounding and recover local geometric details only when necessary, balancing performance gains with computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Uncover and rectify attention artifacts in VLA visual encoders:** The authors aim to demonstrate that the attention artifacts observed in generic Vision Transformers persist and are further amplified in VLA models during embodied post-training. A key objective is to address this by introducing a mechanism that prevents spatial information acquired during manipulation tasks from corrupting patch token representations.
 - **Motivation:** Current VLA models frequently misidentify target objects or struggle with disambiguating task types, despite their strong semantic understanding. This indicates a failure in spatial grounding. The hypothesis is that the limited capacity of existing global tokens forces task-relevant spatial information to be stored in "low-information" background patch tokens, leading to corrupted attention distributions that hinder precise localization and interaction. Rectifying this issue is crucial for improving the fundamental visual perception capabilities of VLA models for robotics.

2. **Develop a method for adaptively recovering fine-grained visual details:** The goal is to provide a mechanism that can enhance visual resolution for specific, task-relevant regions only when required, without incurring the computational cost of high-resolution processing for every observation.
 - **Motivation:** Even with accurate localization, precise manipulation tasks (e.g., fine-grained grasping, contact reasoning) often demand local geometric details that are lost in low-resolution visual observations. Relying solely on base observations can lead to sub-optimal performance. However, continuously processing high-resolution images or integrating complex external vision modules is computationally expensive and introduces additional complexities. An adaptive, selective approach is motivated by the need for a practical and efficient solution that provides high-resolution perception only when the base observation proves insufficient for confident action generation.

3. **Integrate these mechanisms into a unified framework for VLA models:** The research seeks to combine attention rectification and adaptive visual refinement into a cohesive system (AtVLA) that can be trained end-to-end using only embodied data and the original action objective of the VLA model.
 - **Motivation:** To ensure practical applicability and seamless integration, the proposed improvements should leverage the existing VLA architecture and training paradigms as much as possible. Avoiding extensive new pretraining or external supervision sources simplifies deployment and ensures that the enhancements are aligned with the model's embodied learning process. The integration aims to create a more robust and precise manipulation policy while maintaining computational feasibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AtVLA enhances existing VLA models (specifically π0 in this paper) by introducing two core, complementary mechanisms: register-enhanced visual encoding and uncertainty-gated local visual refinement.

#### 3.1. Register-Enhanced Visual Prefix

The approach addresses attention artifacts by modifying the visual encoder (SigLIP-So400m).
- **Register Token Insertion:** A fixed number of learnable register embeddings (N_r = 4) are inserted immediately after the class token in the visual encoder's input sequence. For a 224x224 image with 14x14 patches, this results in N_b = 256 patch tokens. The input sequence becomes `[x_cls; r_1, ..., r_Nr; p_1, ..., p_Nb]`.
- **Output Features:** The register-augmented visual encoder produces `[h_cls, R_t, P_t]`, where `R_t` are the register features and `P_t` are the spatial patch features.
- **Multi-slot Global Context:** Unlike some prior uses where registers are discarded after internal processing, AtVLA retains `R_t` and projects them through the PaliGemma visual projector. These projected register features are then concatenated with the projected patch features `P_t`, and embeddings from the language encoder and proprioceptive state encoder (`E_lang(ℓ); E_state(s_t)`) to form the base multimodal prefix `C_b_t`. This allows registers to serve as dedicated carriers of global, embodied spatial information, preventing this information from corrupting the local spatial representations carried by patch tokens.

#### 3.2. Uncertainty-Gated Action Prediction

This mechanism determines when high-resolution refinement is needed.
- **Prefix Processing:** The LLM backbone processes `C_b_t` once to generate a reusable key-value (KV) cache.
- **Action Chunk Sampling:** Conditioned on this cached prefix, a flow-matching action expert generates K (K=4) independent action chunks from distinct Gaussian initializations.
- **Uncertainty Estimation:** Disagreement among these K sampled action chunks is used to estimate action uncertainty `U_t`. Specifically, `U_t` is calculated as the mean standard deviation across the translational dimensions (`Δx, Δy, Δz`) of the first `h` actions within the sampled chunks. This focuses on end-effector motion most relevant to spatial ambiguity.
- **Gating Decision:** If `U_t` falls below a calibrated threshold `τ`, the mean action chunk is executed directly (confident path). If `U_t` exceeds `τ`, attention-guided local refinement is triggered (uncertain path). The threshold `τ` is calibrated on a held-out set to maximize empirical error reduction at an approximate 30% trigger rate.

#### 3.3. Attention-Guided Local Refinement

When uncertainty is high, the model dynamically crops and re-encodes a task-relevant region.
- **Attention Rollout:** Action-conditioned spatial maps are derived from the attention matrices already computed during the base action sampling. For a given layer, attention matrices are averaged over heads and a fixed subset of denoising steps (e.g., {3, 6, 9}). Residual connections are added, and the matrices are row-normalized. Attention rollout propagates this influence across layers: `M_roll = f_M_L * f_M_L-1 * ... * f_M_1`.
- **Saliency Map Generation:** The rollout entries from the first `h` action tokens to the base image patch columns are averaged and reshaped into a 16x16 saliency map `S_t`. Register columns are excluded as they don't have fixed spatial correspondence. `S_t` is then bilinearly upsampled to the original image resolution.
- **Contrastive Window Search:** A predefined set of square windows is searched. The selected region `b*t` maximizes a contrastive criterion: the average attention within the window minus the average attention in an expanded surrounding ring (`ρ(b) \ b`). This encourages selection of regions with distinctive, concentrated attention.
- **High-Resolution Re-encoding:** The selected region is cropped from the original observation, resized to 224x224, and re-encoded by the same register-augmented visual encoder. The crop-side register outputs are discarded, as global context is already provided by the base registers; only the N_c = 256 crop patch features `P_c_t` are retained.
- **Crop Position Encoding:** To encode the crop's spatial context, a position embedding `e_b` is computed by an MLP from the normalized coordinates of the cropped region and added to every projected crop token.
- **Augmented Prefix:** The projected crop features with position encoding `Π(P_c_t) + 1_Nc * e_b^T` are appended to the cached base prefix `C_b_t` to form `C_t+`. The action expert then uses `C_t+` to generate a refined action chunk. This process reuses the base image, instruction, and state KV cache, only prefretching the new crop segment.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Paradigm

AtVLA is initialized from a pretrained π0 policy and retains its `L_π0` action objective. Training proceeds in three stages:
1. **Stage 0: Register Adaptation (20K steps):** Register embeddings are initially updated using `L_π0` while the backbone is frozen (4K steps). Subsequently, the last four SigLIP transformer blocks are unfrozen and trained alongside the registers (16K steps), allowing the visual encoder to reorganize embodied global information into the registers.
2. **Stage 1: Ground-Truth Crop Alignment (10K steps):** The crop-position encoder and LoRA adapters within the LLM and action components are trained with `L_π0`, using ground-truth annotated crops. The visual encoder and register embeddings remain frozen. This teaches the policy to interpret crop tokens.
3. **Stage 2: Joint Refinement and Inference Alignment (40K steps):** LoRA adapters across the entire policy (SigLIP, Gemma, action expert) are trainable, along with register embeddings and the crop-position encoder. The joint objective is `L = L_π0 + λ_ag * L_ag`, where `L_ag` is a term that encourages action-to-image attention to concentrate on the ground-truth task-relevant object region. A crop curriculum is employed: starting with all ground-truth crops, progressively transitioning to crops generated by the model's own attention rollout (α increases linearly from 0 to 1), and incorporating crop dropout to expose the policy to both base-only and crop-augmented inference paths. This aligns training with deployment inference behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AtVLA was conducted across three benchmarks: LIBERO, SimplerEnv, and a challenging single-view real-world benchmark.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance

- **LIBERO:** AtVLA achieved the highest success rates across all four standard LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long-10). It improved the average success rate from 94.2% (π0) to 98.4%, with notable gains on Spatial (99.3% vs 96.8%) and Long-10 (96.5% vs 85.2%).
- **SimplerEnv (Google Robot):** AtVLA improved performance on Pick Coke Can (91.3% vs 88.0%) and Move Near (81.6% vs 80.3%) compared to π0, and remained competitive on Open/Close Drawer (57.5% vs 56.0%).
- **Real-world Evaluation:** On the custom Kitchen and Building Blocks suites, AtVLA demonstrated the strongest performance across all tasks. For instance, in Kitchen Grab, success increased from 35% (π0) to 65%, and in Building Blocks Spatial, from 55% (π0) to 80%. The overall real-world success rate improved from 46.5% (π0) to 69.0%.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Observations (Attention Rectification and Refinement)

- Figure 4 illustrates that register-enhanced encoding significantly reduces "high-norm artifacts" in the attention maps of the VLA visual encoder, redirecting attention cleanly towards task-relevant objects and interaction regions.
- When uncertainty triggers local refinement, the attention rollout successfully identifies interaction-critical regions for cropping. The subsequent high-resolution re-encoding sharpens perception around these targets and contact areas, demonstrating the effectiveness of the adaptive refinement.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Components and Effects

- **Attention Rectification (Q1):** The inclusion of register tokens effectively corrected attention artifacts observed in the base π0 model, demonstrating that these artifacts can be addressed through embodied post-training alone, without relying on additional vision-only pretraining. The rectified attention maps proved reliable for guiding the cropping mechanism.
- **Embodied Information in Registers (Q2):** Linear probing experiments revealed that register tokens consistently carried the strongest task-relevant spatial information, outperforming both the CLS token and mean-pooled patch features in predicting object localization, depth, and surface normals (all scores exceeding 0.70). This supports the hypothesis that registers serve as dedicated storage for embodied spatial and geometric knowledge. Ablation studies showed that removing registers from the `π0 + Registers` variant led to substantial performance degradation across LIBERO suites, confirming their active role beyond artifact suppression.
- **Benefits of Cropping (Q3):** Cropping particularly benefited object-centric tasks (LIBERO-Object, Kitchen Grab/Pick), spatially demanding tasks (LIBERO-Spatial, Building Blocks Spatial/Grab), and long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long-10, real-world long-horizon categories). This suggests cropping not only improves local precision but also supports temporally extended planning by providing richer visual context for subsequent decisions. Comparisons with an external VLM-based cropping approach (π0 + Cropping) showed that the latter performed slightly worse, highlighting the importance of embodied supervision for learning decision-relevant visual grounding for cropping. Incorrectly localized crops could negatively impact policy performance.
- **System Cost (Q4):** AtVLA maintains limited additional inference cost. With K=4 action samples and an approximate crop-trigger rate `r ≈ 0.3`, the total computation is estimated to be 1.4–1.6× that of the base π0 model for the same number of steps. The attention rollout mechanism reuses attention computed during action sampling, and the base prefix KV cache is preserved during crop token appending, contributing to efficiency.
- **Number of Register Tokens:** Experiments on the capacity of register tokens showed that performance saturated at four register tokens. Using fewer registers could introduce interference and sometimes degrade performance below the π0 baseline, attributed to incomplete absorption of attention artifacts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing fundamental visual perception limitations in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The significance and potential impact of AtVLA include:

1. **Enhanced Spatial Grounding and Precision:** By identifying and rectifying attention artifacts within VLA visual encoders, AtVLA significantly improves the model's ability to spatially ground task-relevant objects and interaction regions. This leads to more reliable object localization and precise contact reasoning, which are critical for fine-grained manipulation tasks. The finding that register tokens emerge as dedicated carriers of embodied spatial information is a novel insight into how VLA models can better organize and leverage visual knowledge for control.

2. **Adaptive and Efficient Visual Refinement:** The uncertainty-gated, attention-guided local refinement mechanism provides a practical solution for incorporating high-resolution visual details only when and where they are needed. This adaptive approach avoids the computational overhead of continuous high-resolution processing or the complexities of integrating external, uncalibrated vision experts. The method's ability to leverage the policy's internal attention for guiding crop selection makes it self-contained and aligned with the learned task objectives.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The substantial performance gains across diverse simulation and real-world benchmarks, including object-centric, spatial reasoning, and long-horizon tasks, indicate that AtVLA enhances the generalization and robustness of VLA policies. The improvements in real-world scenarios, particularly for cluttered scenes and small-object manipulation, demonstrate its practical utility for complex robotic applications.

4. **Advancements in VLA Architecture Design:** The work demonstrates that modifying internal architectural components (like introducing register tokens) and leveraging existing attention mechanisms can effectively improve VLA performance without requiring extensive architectural overhauls or new large-scale pretraining. This provides valuable insights for future VLA design, suggesting pathways to build more capable and efficient generalist robot policies through targeted internal adaptations.

5. **Computational Efficiency:** The modest computational overhead (1.4–1.6× the base model) for significant performance gains makes AtVLA a viable approach for real-world deployment on resource-constrained robot platforms. This balance between performance and efficiency is crucial for the widespread adoption of advanced VLA models in robotics.

Overall, AtVLA offers a robust framework for improving the visual perception and manipulation capabilities of VLA models, paving the way for more precise, reliable, and generalist robots operating in complex environments.
