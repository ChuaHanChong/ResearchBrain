# Learning to Evolve: Multi-modal Interactive Fields for Robust Humanoid Navigation in Dynamic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> The Multi-modal Interactive Field (MIF) framework enables humanoid robots to safely navigate and interact in dynamic environments by addressing locomotion-induced perceptual distortions and map-reality mismatches. It achieved 94% success in interaction pose safety with no observed collisions and robustly adapted to object changes with up to 98% success, significantly outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots' bipedal locomotion causes torso oscillations and viewpoint shifts, leading to noisy egocentric RGB-D observations that degrade semantic grounding and geometric accuracy in scene maps.
- Existing semantic mapping systems assume static environments, failing when objects are relocated, removed, or added, which leads to unsafe navigation and incorrect interaction plans.

## Method

- A Multi-modal Interactive Field (**MIF**) framework integrating Appearance, Spatial, and Geometry fields within a closed-loop perception-adaptation pipeline.
- Confidence-aware Semantic 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (Appearance Field) augmented with a confidence scalar to suppress gait-induced blur and distill open-vocabulary features from stabilized views.
- A discrepancy-triggered local map update mechanism (Spatial Field) for constructing hierarchical scene graphs and adapting topological memory to persistent scene changes without full re-mapping.
- An on-demand high-fidelity object geometry recovery module (Geometry Field) that actively selects optimal views and uses conditional ==Flow Matching== to generate watertight 3D meshes for Interaction Pose Safety (IPS) verification.

## Results

- MIF achieved **92%** semantic localization success with a 0.18m Mean Distance Error and maintained higher image quality (e.g., PSNR 29.8, SSIM 0.89 for fast walking) compared to baseline 3DGS methods.
- The system demonstrated **94%** Interaction Pose Safety (IPS) success with **0%** observed collisions, significantly outperforming point cloud (**62%** IPS, **32%** collision) and static mesh (**78%** IPS, **14%** collision) baselines.
- MIF adapted to object relocations, removals, and additions with up to **98%** task success, substantially surpassing existing scene graph methods (e.g., HOV-SG at 10-**12%** for removal/relocation).
- The discrepancy-triggered adaptation mechanism achieved a **95.1%** True Positive Rate in detecting persistent scene changes and operated efficiently with a 1.6s latency and under 4GB VRAM usage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and suppressing uncertainty in 3D Gaussian Splatting due to humanoid gait effectively maintains semantic and geometric integrity, mitigating locomotion-induced noise.
> - Locally updating scene graphs based on detected inconsistencies between map and reality allows for efficient adaptation in dynamic environments, which is crucial for continuous robot operation.
> - Integrating perception with a comprehensive Interaction Pose Safety (IPS) criterion, supported by on-demand, high-fidelity geometry recovery, is vital for ensuring physically safe and feasible manipulation actions for humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are increasingly being developed for operation in human-centric environments, which necessitates robust navigation capabilities coupled with physical interaction. Existing robotic navigation and mapping systems, particularly those focused on semantic scene understanding and scene graphs, face challenges when deployed on humanoid platforms in dynamic environments.

Traditional semantic mapping approaches, such as those based on Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), provide dense 3D scene representations. Advancements like LangSplat and Feature Splatting integrate vision foundation models (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2) to embed open-vocabulary semantics, enabling language-based querying. However, many of these systems assume stable camera trajectories and static environments, making them susceptible to locomotion-induced perceptual distortions and environmental changes. Methods like Deblur-GS and PUP 3D-GS address motion blur or uncertainty in 3DGS, but typically focus on reconstruction or pruning rather than systemic integration for humanoid navigation.

Similarly, 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs) offer a structured abstraction of environments, organizing information hierarchically (e.g., floors, rooms, objects, relations). More recent developments leverage large language and vision-language models for higher-level reasoning. While dynamic scene-graph methods like Khronos consider memory evolution, and ConceptGraphs can be adapted with periodic rescanning, they often assume periodic revisits or global re-scans, which may not align with continuous, non-revisiting humanoid navigation protocols. Furthermore, the coarse geometric representations (e.g., bounding boxes) common in scene graphs are generally insufficient for detailed manipulation safety checks.

For manipulation, reliable geometric priors are crucial, especially when observations are partial. Generative models, including diffusion-based and Flow Matching-based approaches, have shown progress in shape recovery. However, these are often treated as offline or object-centric modules, lacking integration within online navigation and adaptive memory systems. Active perception explores how agent actions can reduce perceptual ambiguity, but humanoid-specific constraints like viewpoint oscillations are less explored.

This work introduces the Multi-modal Interactive Field (MIF) framework to integrate these distinct areas, addressing the specific challenges of humanoid navigation and interaction. It aims to bridge the gap between robust 3D scene representation, dynamic semantic understanding, and manipulation-level geometric safety, all within a closed-loop system designed for the unique constraints of bipedal robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable safe manipulation-oriented navigation for humanoid robots operating in dynamic, human-centric environments. This overarching goal is motivated by two specific and coupled failure modes identified in existing systems when applied to humanoids:

**P1: Locomotion-Induced Semantic-Geometric Distortion.**
- **Motivation:** Humanoid robots, unlike wheeled platforms, exhibit bipedal dynamics involving torso oscillations and abrupt viewpoint shifts during locomotion. These movements degrade the quality of egocentric RGB-D observations, leading to unstable sensor data. When such noisy observations are fused into a scene map, they can result in blurred semantics and inaccurate geometry. This distortion compromises the robot's ability to accurately ground objects semantically and to derive reliable surface evidence necessary for computing safe terminal interaction poses for manipulation.
- **Objective:** Develop a scene representation that is robust to these locomotion-induced distortions, preserving clear semantic grounding and accurate geometry despite noisy sensory input.

**P2: Map-Reality Mismatch under Scene Changes.**
- **Motivation:** Real-world environments are inherently dynamic, with objects frequently being relocated, removed, or newly introduced. Most existing semantic mapping and scene-graph systems operate on a "map-once-query-forever" paradigm, assuming a static environment. When the stored map no longer reflects the current physical reality, a humanoid robot might attempt to navigate towards obsolete coordinates or plan interactions based on non-existent objects. This can lead to unsafe manipulation poses, necessitate costly global re-planning, or result in the failure to recognize task-relevant changes.
- **Objective:** Design a mechanism that allows the robot's scene memory to adapt dynamically and efficiently to environmental changes, without requiring full re-mapping, thereby ensuring the memory remains consistent with reality for safe navigation and interaction.

To address these coupled problems, the research proposes MIF as a humanoid-oriented system that maintains appearance, spatial, and geometry fields within a closed-loop perception-adaptation pipeline. The system specifically aims to achieve:
- Confidence-aware semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting to suppress gait-induced blur and maintain semantic grounding (for P1).
- A discrepancy-triggered local update mechanism to adapt topological memory to persistent scene changes efficiently (for P2).
- On-demand, high-fidelity object-centric geometry recovery to ensure Interaction Pose Safety (IPS) before manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Multi-modal Interactive Field (MIF) is a hierarchical framework comprising three coupled fields—Appearance, Spatial, and Geometry—integrated within a closed-loop perception-adaptation pipeline. This structure is designed to mitigate locomotion-induced distortion (P1) and map-reality mismatches (P2) for manipulation-oriented navigation.

**A. Appearance Field Construction (F_app)**
The F_app serves as the sensory grounding layer, creating a confidence-aware semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation.
- **Jitter-Aware Confidence Estimation:** Each 3D Gaussian primitive `G_i` is augmented with a confidence scalar `C_i` (0-1). `C_i` is computed based on the Gaussian's weighted gradient magnitude (reflecting optimization instability from position, scale/rotation, color, opacity updates) and its opacity (reflecting stable structural evidence). This differentiable confidence module assigns lower reliability to primitives affected by transient artifacts like motion blur or pose-induced misalignment caused by bipedal locomotion. A smooth gating mechanism penalizes instability while preserving structurally supported primitives.
- **Open-Vocabulary Feature Distillation:** High-dimensional features (e.g., CLIP/DINOv2) are distilled into a compact 32-dimensional latent space (`f_distill_i`). This compression reduces memory footprint for large-scale semantic maps, facilitating online deployment. A co-trained MLP decoder projects these latent features back for open-vocabulary matching. Feature rendering is confidence-weighted, ensuring unstable primitives contribute less to the semantic evidence, thereby reducing semantic smearing (P1).
- **Stabilized View Synthesis:** F_app functions as a confidence-weighted rendering engine. Instead of using raw, gait-affected camera frames, it renders stabilized views from smoothed virtual trajectories. These views have reduced artifacts, providing more stable input for downstream perception, such as object detection and spatial relation extraction.

**B. Spatial Field Construction (F_spat)**
The F_spat acts as the topological memory and reasoning layer, abstracting the scene into a hierarchical graph `M_spat = (V, E)`.
- **Graph Generation via Stabilized Re-perception:** It queries the optimized F_app to render confidence-weighted stabilized views (`I_stable`). Object detection and semantic parsing are performed on these stabilized views to construct the scene graph. Each node `v_i ∈ V` includes a semantic caption, room affiliation, 3D centroid (`c_i`), and a node reliability score (`Ω_i`). `c_i` is computed as a confidence-weighted average of Gaussian positions, and `Ω_i` is the mean `C_j` of associated Gaussians. This reliability filters out detections based on low-confidence primitives, preventing gait-induced artifacts from becoming persistent graph nodes.
- **Detecting Map-Reality Mismatch via Discrepancy D:** To address P2, a closed-loop Interaction and Adaptation Mechanism monitors a multi-modal discrepancy score `D`. `D` compares a live local subgraph (`M_loc`) with the stored global graph (`M_spat`). The node-level discrepancy (`δ_i`) combines weighted positional distance (`∥c_loc_i - c_glob_j∥^2`) and semantic distance (`1 - cos(f̂_i, f̂_j)`) between matched local and global nodes, scaled by the node's reliability (`Ω_i`). The total discrepancy `D` aggregates these node-level shifts and relational changes (`|E_loc Δ E_spat| / |E_loc ∪ E_spat|`). If `D > τ` (a threshold), the system identifies a map-reality mismatch, pausing navigation to trigger a spatially selective update that revises only the inconsistent region using accumulated multi-view evidence.

**C. Geometry Field Construction (F_geom)**
The F_geom provides an interaction-safety layer, functioning as an on-demand module for recovering object-centric meshes for Interaction Pose Safety (IPS) verification.
- **Task-Local View Selection:** Prior to interaction, the agent actively samples candidate viewpoints around the target object on a local hemisphere. These viewpoints are ranked by a task-local utility `Q(k)`, which considers distance to the object, foveal alignment of the camera's optical axis with the object center, and the confidence of Gaussians observed from that view. This strategy prioritizes views that offer stable, high-reliability evidence for the object's interaction surfaces.
- **Conditional Flow Matching for 3D Mesh Recovery:** A pre-trained Flow Matching-based model is used to infer partially observed object surfaces. This model generates a dense, watertight object-centric mesh, conditioned on the image captured from the selected high-utility viewpoint. This ensures the recovered mesh is consistent with the denoised observations from F_app.
- **Asynchronous Grounding and IPS Verification:** Mesh generation is performed asynchronously during the robot's approach to avoid blocking navigation. The generated mesh is then aligned to the Gaussian support from F_app using scale-invariant ICP (Iterative Closest Point). The registered mesh provides dense local geometry for evaluating the final standing pose (`p_se3`).
- **Interaction Pose Safety (IPS):** The `S_IPS(p_se3)` score is an operational safety criterion that verifies if a terminal pose is suitable for manipulation. It evaluates three conditions:
 - `I_col`: Geometric Safety, checking collision margin against the generated mesh (`M_gen`).
 - `I_ik`: Kinematic Reachability, confirming a valid, collision-free inverse-kinematics solution to reach the target point.
 - `I_stab`: Postural Stability, ensuring the robot's projected center of mass lies within its support polygon.
 Navigation is considered complete only when `S_IPS` is satisfied.

**D. Language-Conditioned Navigation and Adaptation**
This stage converts language-conditioned goals into navigation and verification actions, leveraging MIF's hierarchical structure.
- **Hierarchical Semantic Grounding:** Natural language instructions (e.g., "Grasp the blue mug on the table") are parsed by a VLM into ⟨Region, Landmark, Object⟩ triplets. F_spat is then queried to localize the target, providing its confidence-weighted centroid (`c_i`) as the initial navigation goal.
- **Stability-Aware Execution and Safety Verification:** A local planner prioritizes center-of-mass (CoM) stability, adaptively scaling velocity based on angular tracking error to reduce gait-induced oscillations. Navigation completion is dictated by the IPS criterion; if `S_IPS` fails, the local planner adjusts the stance within a feasible region and re-evaluates.
- **Closed-Loop Memory Evolution:** During navigation, the discrepancy score `D` continuously validates memory consistency. `D` differentiates between transient discrepancies (e.g., moving people, triggering avoidance without memory update) and structural mismatches (e.g., relocated furniture, `D > τ`). Structural mismatches trigger active scanning, local F_app and F_spat updates for the inconsistent region, and re-grounding of the query in the revised topology.

The system utilizes a ROS1 backend on a centralized workstation (NVIDIA RTX 4090) communicating with a Unitree G1 humanoid, which handles low-level control (Jetson Orin NX).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation was conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid in a real dynamic office environment, focusing on three key questions related to the identified problems (P1 and P2).

**Q1: Robust Semantic Grounding under Distortion (P1)**
- **Semantic Localization:** MIF demonstrated improved semantic localization for open-vocabulary object queries. It achieved a 92% success rate and a Mean Distance Error (MDE) of 0.18 m, outperforming Feature-Splatting (65% success, 0.42 m MDE) and HOV-SG (74% success, 0.35 m MDE). MIF also exhibited lower latency (1.6 s) compared to Feature-Splatting (3.2 s) and HOV-SG (2.8 s).
- **Rendering Robustness:** Comparisons with other 3DGS methods under humanoid walking speeds (slow and fast) showed that MIF's confidence-gated rendering maintained higher image quality. For fast walking, MIF achieved a PSNR of 29.8 and SSIM of 0.89, significantly higher than the baseline "w/o Confidence" (PSNR 21.6, SSIM 0.72), Deblur-GS (PSNR 24.67, SSIM 0.70), and PUP-3DGS (PSNR 17.89, SSIM 0.53).
- **Scene Graph Quality:** Scene graphs constructed from MIF's stabilized rendered views showed better object localization (higher recall, precision, and mAP) than those built directly from raw walking frames, especially during fast locomotion.

**Q2: High-fidelity Geometry for Interaction Safety (IPS)**
- **Interaction Feasibility:** MIF significantly improved Interaction Pose Safety (IPS) success, achieving 94% success with 0% observed collisions. This was a substantial improvement over the Pt-Cloud Baseline (62% IPS success, 32% collision rate) and the Static Mesh Baseline (78% IPS success, 14% collision rate).
- **Terminal Position Error:** MIF achieved a mean terminal position error of 0.12 m, contributing to accurate reachability checks. The generative Geometry Field, by providing continuous and watertight mesh surfaces, reliably exposed feasible clearances, unlike sparse point clouds that could lead to unreliable collision checks or static meshes prone to integrating locomotion artifacts.

**Q3: Dynamic Adaptation to Map-Reality Mismatches (P2)**
- **Task Success with Scene Changes:** In scenarios involving object relocation (>1.5 m), removal, or addition after initial mapping (without global re-scans), MIF demonstrated high adaptation success rates.
 - **Relocation:** MIF (Post-Update) achieved 94% success, significantly higher than HOV-SG (12%), ConceptGraphs + Rescan (38%), and Khronos (~18%). MIF (Initial), without memory revision, achieved 64% success.
 - **Removal:** MIF (Post-Update) achieved 98% success, compared to HOV-SG (10%), ConceptGraphs + Rescan (35%), and Khronos (~61%). MIF (Initial) achieved 68% success.
 - **Addition:** MIF (Post-Update) achieved 86% success, compared to HOV-SG (0%), ConceptGraphs + Rescan (12%), and Khronos (~12%). MIF (Initial) achieved 42% success.
- **Discrepancy Detection:** The discrepancy-triggered adaptation mechanism, using a threshold `τ = 0.45`, achieved a True Positive Rate of 95.1% and a False Positive Rate of 4.2%, demonstrating its ability to distinguish persistent scene changes from transient sensing artifacts.

**System Efficiency**
- **Memory and Latency:** MIF's 32D semantic feature variant reduced VRAM usage to below 4 GB and exhibited a latency of 1.6 s, demonstrating efficiency suitable for online deployment on an NVIDIA RTX 4090. This compares favorably to LangSplat (18.45 GB VRAM, 2.3 s latency) and LatentBKI-64D (23.5 GB VRAM, 7.1 s latency).
- **Update Rates:** The Appearance Field supported real-time incremental updates at 22 FPS. The asynchronous generation of the Geometry Field took approximately 6.2 s, scheduled during the approach phase or verification to avoid blocking low-level control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Multi-modal Interactive Field (MIF) framework represents an advancement in the capabilities of humanoid robots to operate robustly and safely in dynamic, human-centric environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from its integrated approach to addressing long-standing challenges in robotic perception, mapping, and interaction.

**Addressing Humanoid-Specific Challenges:** A primary contribution of MIF is its explicit mitigation of problems unique to humanoid platforms, particularly locomotion-induced perceptual distortions (P1). By incorporating a confidence-aware mechanism into 3D Gaussian Splatting, the system effectively suppresses gait-induced blur and pose uncertainty, leading to more stable semantic grounding. This capability is crucial for enabling humanoids to maintain reliable perception despite their inherent bipedal dynamics.

**Enabling Safe Manipulation-Oriented Navigation:** The tight coupling of navigation with manipulation safety (Q2) is a key aspect. The Geometry Field, providing on-demand, high-fidelity object meshes, directly supports a comprehensive Interaction Pose Safety (IPS) criterion. This moves beyond simple coordinate-based navigation to ensure that terminal stances are collision-free, kinematically reachable, and stable. Such an approach is essential for humanoids engaging in complex physical interactions within unstructured environments, directly impacting their reliability and safety in tasks like object retrieval or assistance.

**Adaptive and Efficient Scene Understanding in Dynamic Environments:** MIF introduces a robust solution to the map-reality mismatch problem (P2) through its discrepancy-triggered local memory evolution. Instead of costly global re-mapping, the system intelligently detects and revises only inconsistent regions of its spatial memory. This efficient adaptation mechanism allows humanoids to operate continuously in environments where objects are frequently moved, added, or removed, significantly extending their operational autonomy and effectiveness in realistic settings.

**Systemic Integration of State-of-the-Art Techniques:** The work effectively integrates several advanced perception and reasoning techniques—including confidence-aware 3DGS, VLM-based scene graph reasoning, and Flow Matching-based generative geometry—within a cohesive, closed-loop pipeline. This integration demonstrates a practical framework for leveraging these technologies to solve complex embodied AI problems, rather than treating them as isolated components.

**Practical Deployment Potential:** The reported efficiency gains, particularly the reduced memory footprint through feature distillation and the real-time update capabilities, suggest that MIF is viable for online deployment on contemporary robotic hardware. This practical applicability distinguishes it from purely theoretical contributions, paving the way for real-world applications of humanoid robots.

**Foundation for Future Research:** MIF establishes a foundation for future research in active and adaptive perception for embodied AI. The limitations, such as geometry generation latency and reliance on offboard computation, point to clear avenues for further development, including optimizing computational efficiency for onboard processing and exploring more sophisticated multiplex interactive learning strategies.

In summary, MIF's ability to provide robust, adaptive, and safety-aware navigation and interaction capabilities for humanoids in dynamic environments represents a substantial step towards more versatile and reliable autonomous systems.
