# Egocentric Prediction of Action Target in 3D

> [!summary] Summary
> A new task, egocentric 3D action target prediction, is introduced to forecast the 3D location of future hand-object interactions from a first-person view. To support this, the EgoPAT3D dataset is created, and a baseline model leveraging RGB-D and IMU data is proposed, showing that prediction error decreases as more temporal information becomes available.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric vision research primarily focuses on recognizing or anticipating what action will be performed, overlooking where actions will occur in 3D space.
- Accurately predicting the 3D target location of future hand-object interactions from a first-person view is challenging due to limited visual information, motion blur, and varied environments.
- A lack of dedicated datasets and benchmarks specifically for 3D action target prediction from an egocentric perspective hindered research in this area.

## Method

- A new multimodal dataset, EgoPAT3D, is introduced, containing over 1 million frames of egocentric RGB-D and IMU data, along with 3D ground truth annotations for hand-object interaction targets.
- A baseline model is proposed that extracts visual features from point clouds (derived from RGB-D) and motion features from IMU data using distinct sub-networks.
- These features are fused and processed by a recurrent neural network (LSTM or GRU) to model temporal dynamics, predicting 3D target locations. The model is trained using a novel truncated weighted regression loss (TWRLoss) that emphasizes early prediction accuracy.

## Results

- The baseline model achieves satisfactory performance in egocentric 3D action target prediction, with prediction error consistently decreasing as more frames become available over time.
- Multimodal input (RGB-D + IMU) significantly outperforms unimodal approaches, confirming the benefit of combining visual and motion cues for predicting future 3D interaction points.
- The proposed method shows reasonable generalization capabilities to unseen household scenes, indicating its potential applicability beyond specific training environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of both visual (RGB-D) and motion (IMU) data is crucial for robust 3D action target prediction, as these modalities provide complementary information.
> - The proposed truncated weighted regression loss (TWRLoss) is more effective than standard loss functions for this task, particularly in encouraging accurate early predictions.
> - The research establishes that egocentric 3D action target prediction is feasible, with prediction accuracy improving as more temporal information becomes available.
