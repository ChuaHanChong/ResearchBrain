# GeoWorld: Geometric World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoWorld introduces a geometric world model that employs hyperbolic latent spaces and a geometric reinforcement learning framework to enhance multi-step visual planning. It achieves higher stability and success rates on long-horizon tasks, demonstrating up to a 3% increase in 3-step planning success rate and maintaining stronger performance for planning horizons up to T=8 compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing predictive world models use Euclidean latent representations that often fail to preserve the inherent geometric and hierarchical structures among states, hindering geometry-consistent planning.
- The performance of predictive world models degrades rapidly with increasing planning horizons due due to accumulated prediction errors and difficulty in modeling long-term temporal dependencies.
- State-transition trees branch exponentially (B possible actions per step over d steps), and Euclidean space's uniform curvature is ill-suited to represent this hierarchical branching structure faithfully.

## Method

- **GeoWorld** maps Euclidean latent representations onto a hyperbolic manifold (==Hyperbolic JEPA==, H-JEPA) to intrinsically preserve geometric structure and hierarchical relations.
- It employs a two-stage training process: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for initial prediction and Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL) to refine the predictor by aligning hyperbolic energy costs with expected cumulative rewards, utilizing triangle inequality regularization.
- For planning, the model uses an energy-based approach with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to minimize the hyperbolic geodesic distance between predicted and goal latent states.

## Results

- GeoWorld consistently improved multi-step goal-conditioned visual planning across CrossTask and COIN datasets, showing up to a **3%** increase in success rate for 3-step planning compared to V-JEPA 2.
- The model demonstrated superior stability and higher success rates for longer planning horizons, achieving an **18.26%** SR on CrossTask for T=6 and a **13.81%** SR at T=8, significantly outperforming V-JEPA 2's **4.95%** SR at T=8.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual and combined efficacy of hyperbolic geometry and Geometric Reinforcement Learning, with the learnable curvature parameter converging to a value indicating a flatter, yet still hyperbolic, latent space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hyperbolic geometry naturally represents hierarchical and tree-like structures common in sequential decision-making, providing a more effective latent space for long-horizon planning than Euclidean space.
> - Refining a pretrained world model's predictor using geometric reinforcement learning can enhance multi-step planning stability and accuracy without requiring separate policy or reward models.
> - Enforcing geodesic consistency in predicted trajectories through triangle inequality regularization during GRL is crucial for mitigating error accumulation and improving multi-step rollout stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements in planning and reasoning capabilities, particularly with the advent of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). These models often leverage language space to acquire extensive world knowledge. However, they can struggle to fully represent the physical and geometric properties of the real world, where visual information plays a primary role in human and biological cognition.

Visual world models, which learn solely from visual input like videos, constitute an alternative approach. These models generally fall into two categories:
1. **Generative World Models**: These models explicitly generate pixels or latent visual tokens to predict future states, typically one step at a time. This approach can be computationally expensive, introduce unnecessary noise, and may not effectively capture the full trajectory structure or the underlying energy landscape over multiple steps.
2. **Predictive World Models**: In contrast, these models do not generate pixels. Instead, they learn an energy landscape in a latent space that quantifies the compatibility between current and target states. This design enables multi-step hierarchical planning by minimizing energy in latent space, with lower-level modules handling physical details. Examples include models based on Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) such as V-JEPA.

Despite the advantages of predictive world models, existing approaches face two primary limitations:
- **Geometric Neglect**: Their latent representations are typically learned in Euclidean space. This Euclidean embedding often fails to preserve the inherent geometric and hierarchical structures among states, leading to an energy landscape that does not accurately reflect meaningful geodesic distances or hierarchical relationships. Consequently, these models exhibit reduced capability for geometry-consistent planning, especially over long horizons.
- **Multi-step Shortcoming**: While conceptually capable of long-horizon planning by learning an energy landscape over entire trajectories, existing predictive world models often exhibit rapid performance degradation as the planning horizon increases. This indicates a weakness in modeling long-term temporal dependencies and susceptibility to error accumulation across extended rollouts.

GeoWorld positions itself within this landscape by directly addressing these challenges. It proposes to enhance predictive world models by integrating geometric principles into their latent space representations and optimizing their multi-step planning capabilities through a novel reinforcement learning framework. By moving beyond Euclidean latent spaces and incorporating hyperbolic geometry, GeoWorld aims to create more robust and stable models for long-horizon visual planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the performance and stability of energy-based predictive world models for multi-step visual planning, particularly when dealing with long planning horizons. This overarching goal is driven by the identified limitations of existing approaches: the neglect of geometric structure in latent representations and the rapid degradation in long-horizon prediction.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows.

#### 2.1. Addressing Geometric Neglect

- **Motivation**: Real-world state transitions, especially in complex tasks involving sequences of actions, often exhibit an underlying hierarchical or tree-like structure. For example, predicting *d* steps into the future, with *B* possible actions at each step, creates *B^d* possible future trajectories. This exponential branching naturally forms a hierarchical organization, where states at shallower depths represent abstract, high-level states, and deeper states represent finer details. Euclidean space, with its uniform curvature, is not optimally suited to represent such inherently hierarchical data structures, where distances should reflect these relationships. Consequently, current models' Euclidean latent spaces may distort relationships between states, leading to an energy landscape that does not effectively capture meaningful geodesic distances or hierarchical embeddings. This limits the model’s ability to perform geometry-consistent planning and understand the true structure of the physical world.
- **Objective**: To develop a geometry-aware world model that can intrinsically preserve geometric structure and hierarchical relations within its latent space. This involves mapping latent representations from Euclidean space onto a hyperbolic manifold. In hyperbolic space, geodesic distances naturally encode hierarchical relationships, enabling the model to learn a more structured and physically meaningful energy landscape that supports geometry-consistent planning.

#### 2.2. Addressing Multi-step Shortcoming

- **Motivation**: The performance of existing predictive world models degrades significantly as the planning horizon increases. This degradation is attributed to the accumulation of prediction errors and the difficulty in modeling long-term temporal dependencies within standard latent spaces. While the concept of an energy landscape over trajectories supports long-horizon planning, the models' inability to maintain stable rollouts limits practical application. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated effectiveness in adjusting pretrained models to achieve specific objectives by providing a framework for optimizing sequential decision-making.
- **Objective**: To design and integrate a geometry-aware reinforcement learning method that can refine the pretrained world model's predictor. This method, termed Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL), aims to optimize the energy-based value function by encouraging geodesic-consistent rollouts on the latent manifold. By enforcing these geometric constraints, GRL is designed to improve the model's multi-step planning capability, enhance long-horizon stability, and mitigate the rapid performance degradation observed in current models. This refinement is intended to occur without the need for training additional policy or reward models.

In essence, GeoWorld is motivated by the hypothesis that incorporating hyperbolic geometry can provide a more natural and efficient representation for sequential visual data, and that a reinforcement learning approach guided by these geometric principles can effectively train models for stable and accurate long-horizon planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoWorld is designed as a geometric world model that enhances multi-step visual planning by preserving geometric structure and hierarchical awareness in latent space. The methodology comprises three main components: Hyperbolic JEPA (H-JEPA), a two-stage training objective including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL), and energy-based planning using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM).

#### 3.1. Hyperbolic JEPA (H-JEPA)

The core idea behind H-JEPA is to model latent dynamics on a hyperbolic manifold rather than in Euclidean space, allowing geodesic distances to naturally encode hierarchical relations.
- **Euclidean to Hyperbolic Mapping**: An observation `x_t` is first encoded into a Euclidean latent state `s_x_t` using a pretrained encoder `E_θ`. This Euclidean embedding is then interpreted as a tangent vector at the origin of the Poincaré ball model `B_n_c`. The exponential map `exp_0(·)` projects this tangent vector onto the hyperbolic manifold, yielding the hyperbolic latent state `s_x_t,H`. The curvature `K = -c` is a learnable parameter, initialized at `c=1` and clamped within `[0.1, 10.0]` during training.
- **Action-Conditioned Prediction**: The predictor `P_φ(·)` takes a sequence of hyperbolic latent states `(s_x_t,H)` and corresponding actions `(a_t)` over a planning horizon `T`, predicting the sequence of next-state representations `(ˆs_x_t+1,H)`. This predictor learns dynamics along hyperbolic geodesics to preserve latent geometry.

#### 3.2. Training Objective

The training of GeoWorld involves a two-stage process: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL).

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The SFT objective aims to teach the world model to predict future latent states by following geodesic paths of minimum energy cost in hyperbolic space. The primary metric for this is the Poincaré-ball hyperbolic distance `d_H`.
- **Teacher Forcing Loss (`L_TF`)**: This component ensures accurate one-step prediction. It minimizes the hyperbolic geodesic distance between the predicted next-state latent representation `ˆs_x_t+1,H` (output of `P_φ` given `s_x_t,H` and `a_t`) and the ground-truth encoded next-state `s_x_t+1,H`.
- **Rollout Loss (`L_rollout`)**: To enhance multi-step prediction and temporal consistency, a two-step rollout loss is introduced. This loss recursively feeds the model's own predictions as inputs, minimizing the hyperbolic distance between `ˆs_x_t+2,H` (predicted two steps ahead) and the ground-truth `s_x_t+2,H`.
- **Total SFT Loss (`L_SFT`)**: The combined loss is `L_SFT(θ, φ) = λ L_TF(θ, φ) + (1 − λ) L_rollout(θ, φ)`, where `λ` is a weighting hyperparameter (set to 0.5 for balanced emphasis).

#### 3.4. Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL)

GRL refines the predictor by adjusting its energy-based value representation, aligning lower hyperbolic energy with higher expected cumulative reward. This is achieved without training a separate policy or reward model.
- **Energy Cost and Reward**: The energy cost `c_t` of moving from `s_x_t,H` to `s_x_t+1,H` is defined as the hyperbolic distance `d_H(ˆs_x_t+1,H, s_x_t+1,H)`. The reward `r_t` is then defined as the negative of this energy cost: `r_t = -c_t`.
- **Path Value Function Optimization**: The objective is to maximize the expected cumulative reward (path value function) over a planning horizon `T`. This is equivalent to minimizing the total discounted hyperbolic distance between predicted and target states.
- **Triangle Inequality Regularization (`L_Δ`)**: The hyperbolic geodesic distance satisfies the triangle inequality. This property is leveraged by introducing a regularization term that penalizes violations of `d_H(ˆs_x_t,H, ˆs_x_t+2,H) ≤ d_H(ˆs_x_t,H, ˆs_x_t+1,H) + d_H(ˆs_x_t+1,H, ˆs_x_t+2,H)`. This term (`L_Δ`) encourages predicted trajectories to align with geodesic paths, enforcing multi-step rollout consistency.
- **Total GRL Loss (`L_GRL`)**: The final GRL loss is `L_GRL(φ) = E_a_1:T~φ [Σ γ^(t-1) d_H(ˆs_x_t+1,H, s_x_t+1,H)] + β L_Δ`, where `γ` is the discount factor (0.99) and `β` is the regularization factor (0.1).

#### 3.5. Energy-Based Planning

After training, the frozen encoder `E` and predictor `P` are used for planning. The predictor acts as a world model, simulating how latent representations evolve under different action sequences.
- **Encoding**: The current observation `x_1` and target goal `x_1+T` are encoded into their respective hyperbolic latent states, `s_x_1,H` and `s_x_1+T,H`.
- **Energy Cost Function**: The planning energy cost `C` is defined as the Poincaré geodesic distance `d_H` between the predicted final latent state (after applying an action sequence `(ˆa_t)`) and the goal latent state `s_x_1+T,H`.
- **Optimization with CEM**: The optimal action sequence `(a*_t)` is found by minimizing this hyperbolic energy cost using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM). CEM iteratively samples candidate action sequences, evaluates their energy costs, selects the best ones (elites), and updates the sampling distribution to converge towards optimal sequences. The first action of the optimal sequence is executed, and the process is repeated (receding horizon control).

The overall approach combines the representational power of hyperbolic geometry with the optimization capabilities of reinforcement learning and CEM-based planning to enable stable and geometry-aware long-horizon visual planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GeoWorld was evaluated through extensive experiments on standard goal-conditioned visual planning benchmarks, comparing its performance against various baseline models across different planning horizons.

#### 4.1. Benchmarks and Metrics

- **Datasets**: CrossTask (4.7K videos, 83 tasks, 105 actions, 375h duration) and COIN (11,287 videos, 180 tasks, 778 actions, 476h duration) were used. These datasets feature diverse fine-grained action labels and timestamps of human daily activities.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Success Rate (SR)**: Percentage of predicted action sequences that exactly match the ground truth.
 - **Mean Accuracy (mAcc)**: Average accuracy of predicted actions at each time step.
 - **Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU)**: Quantifies the overlap between the predicted and ground truth procedures.

#### 4.2. Experimental Setups and Baselines

Experiments were conducted in two setups:
- **Procedural Planning (PP)**: Both observations and goals are specified as images.
- **Visual Planning with Videos**: Both observations and goals are specified as video clips.

Baselines included LLM-based methods (e.g., LFP, VidAssist, SCHEMA, InternVL3.5, Qwen3-VL-Max, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5), Generative World Models (e.g., DDN, P3IV, PDPP, KEPP, ActionDiffusion, MTID, VideoWorld), and Predictive World Models (e.g., WLTDO, UAAA, UPN, PlaTe, E3P, V-JEPA 2). For a fair comparison, GeoWorld and V-JEPA 2 adopted frozen encoders pretrained on VideoMix22M.

#### 4.3. Main Results (Tables 1 and 2 in the paper)

- **Consistent Improvements**: GeoWorld consistently improved multi-step goal-conditioned visual planning across both CrossTask and COIN datasets in both evaluation setups.
- **Procedural Planning (Image-based)**: GeoWorld demonstrated notable gains over prior predictive world models, including V-JEPA 2 (specifically the ViT-g384 variant), especially in longer-horizon settings. For T=3 planning, GeoWorld ViT-g384 achieved an SR of 47.47% on CrossTask (compared to V-JEPA 2's 45.58%) and 34.85% on COIN (compared to 34.08%). For T=4 planning, the improvements were similar (31.48% vs. 31.36% SR on CrossTask, and 27.79% vs. 23.43% SR on COIN). The paper reports around 3% SR improvement in 3-step planning and 2% SR improvement in 4-step planning.
- **Visual Planning with Videos (Video-based)**: GeoWorld consistently outperformed V-JEPA 2 across all model scales (ViT-L, ViT-H, ViT-g, ViT-g384). The GeoWorld ViT-g384 variant achieved the highest overall results, even surpassing strong LLM-based planners. For T=3 planning, GeoWorld ViT-g384 achieved an SR of 51.71% on CrossTask (vs. V-JEPA 2's 50.16%) and 45.29% on COIN (vs. 42.74%). For T=4 planning, the improvements were 37.04% SR on CrossTask (vs. 35.01%) and 33.29% on COIN (vs. 31.63%). These results indicate the effectiveness of geometry-aware latent dynamics and geometric reinforcement learning in enhancing long-horizon stability and planning accuracy.

#### 4.4. Long-Horizon Planning (Table 3 in the paper, Table 5 in supplementary)

- GeoWorld exhibited superior stability and higher Success Rates as the planning horizon increased from T=3 to T=6 (and up to T=8 in supplementary materials).
- While other predictive and generative world models showed consistent degradation due to accumulated geometric drift in Euclidean latent space, GeoWorld maintained performance more effectively. For instance, on CrossTask with T=6, GeoWorld ViT-g384 achieved an SR of 18.26%, compared to V-JEPA 2's 16.88%. At T=8, GeoWorld (SFT+GRL) reached 13.81% SR, whereas V-JEPA 2 dropped to 4.95% SR.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Supplementary Material)

- **Curvature (c)**: The learnable curvature parameter `c` typically converged from its initial value of 1 to around 0.3, suggesting that a flatter but still hyperbolic latent geometry is learned. This moderate negative curvature effectively captures hierarchical structure while maintaining stable value propagation over long planning horizons.
- **Gromov δ-Hyperbolicity**: GeoWorld's latent space exhibited a more concentrated distribution of near-zero `δ` values compared to Euclidean models, indicating a stronger tree-like hierarchical geometry.
- **Frozen Encoder vs. Fully Fine-Tuned**: Fully fine-tuning the encoder yielded only modest improvements (0.3-0.8% SR), suggesting that the pretrained frozen encoder already effectively captures task-relevant structures, and the primary benefits stem from the geometric and RL components.
- **Effectiveness of GRL**: GRL independently contributed to performance gains over SFT alone. The combination of SFT and GRL achieved the strongest results, indicating that SFT provides a solid initialization, and GRL further refines latent dynamics for energy-minimizing trajectories in long-horizon reasoning.
- **SFT Hyperparameters**: Incorporating the rollout loss (weight `1-λ`) consistently improved visual planning, especially for longer horizons, with `λ=0.5` (equal weighting) providing a good balance.
- **GRL Hyperparameters**: Both the discount factor `γ` and the triangle inequality regularization weight `β` were important. A larger `γ` (0.99) enhanced long-horizon supervision, and `β>0` (0.1 being optimal) enforced hyperbolic geodesic constraints, improving stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of GeoWorld contributes to the field of visual world models by addressing critical limitations in long-horizon visual planning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Latent Representation Learning**: GeoWorld demonstrates that incorporating geometric principles, specifically hyperbolic geometry, into the latent space of predictive world models can yield more meaningful and effective representations. By mapping Euclidean embeddings onto a hyperbolic manifold, the model naturally captures the hierarchical and tree-like structures inherent in sequential decision-making and real-world state transitions. This leads to a more structured, curvature-aware energy landscape where geodesic distances directly encode hierarchical relationships, offering a richer and more interpretable latent space for planning.

- **Enhanced Long-Horizon Planning Stability**: A major challenge for existing world models is the rapid degradation of performance with increasing planning horizons due to error accumulation in Euclidean latent spaces. GeoWorld significantly mitigates this issue. By preserving geometric consistency through hyperbolic dynamics and refining trajectories with Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL), the model achieves substantially higher stability and success rates for multi-step planning tasks (T=3 to T=8). This advancement makes GeoWorld more applicable to complex, multi-stage tasks that require foresight over extended periods.

- **Novel Reinforcement Learning Paradigm**: GeoWorld introduces a novel GRL framework that directly optimizes the predictor of the world model without requiring separate policy or reward models. By reformulating multi-step planning as the minimization of a hyperbolic energy-based value function and incorporating triangle inequality regularization, GRL effectively enforces geodesic-consistent rollouts. This approach offers an efficient and principled method for refining predictive world models, aligning their latent dynamics with optimal planning objectives.

- **Improved Performance over State-of-the-Art**: The consistent improvements demonstrated over strong baselines, including V-JEPA 2 and various LLM-based and generative models, validate the effectiveness of GeoWorld's geometric and RL components. The ability of GeoWorld to outperform even large vision-language models in goal-conditioned visual planning highlights the value of its specialized architectural and training design for visual planning tasks.

- **Broader Implications for Autonomous Systems**: The principles established by GeoWorld have potential implications for a wide range of autonomous systems, including robotics and embodied AI. Robots performing complex manipulation tasks or navigating intricate environments could benefit from world models that can reliably predict and plan over long horizons while accounting for the geometric properties of their surroundings. This work suggests a promising direction for developing more capable and robust intelligent agents.

- **Future Research Avenues**: The authors themselves suggest future work involving explicit sub-task hierarchies (multi-level planning) and extensions to embodied settings. The success of GeoWorld indicates that further exploration into non-Euclidean geometries and geometry-aware learning paradigms could unlock additional capabilities in perception, reasoning, and action for AI systems.
