# GeoWorld: Geometric World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoWorld introduces a geometric world model that employs hyperbolic latent spaces and a geometric reinforcement learning framework to enhance multi-step visual planning. It achieves higher stability and success rates on long-horizon tasks, demonstrating up to a 3% increase in 3-step planning success rate and maintaining stronger performance for planning horizons up to T=8 compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing predictive world models use Euclidean latent representations that often fail to preserve the inherent geometric and hierarchical structures among states, hindering geometry-consistent planning.
- The performance of predictive world models degrades rapidly with increasing planning horizons due due to accumulated prediction errors and difficulty in modeling long-term temporal dependencies.

## Method

- **GeoWorld** maps Euclidean latent representations onto a hyperbolic manifold (==Hyperbolic JEPA==, H-JEPA) to intrinsically preserve geometric structure and hierarchical relations.
- It employs a two-stage training process: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for initial prediction and Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL) to refine the predictor by aligning hyperbolic energy costs with expected cumulative rewards, utilizing triangle inequality regularization.
- For planning, the model uses an energy-based approach with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to minimize the hyperbolic geodesic distance between predicted and goal latent states.

## Results

- GeoWorld consistently improved multi-step goal-conditioned visual planning across CrossTask and COIN datasets, showing up to a **3%** increase in success rate for 3-step planning compared to V-JEPA 2.
- The model demonstrated superior stability and higher success rates for longer planning horizons, achieving an **18.26%** SR on CrossTask for T=6 and a **13.81%** SR at T=8, significantly outperforming V-JEPA 2's **4.95%** SR at T=8.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual and combined efficacy of hyperbolic geometry and Geometric Reinforcement Learning, with the learnable curvature parameter converging to a value indicating a flatter, yet still hyperbolic, latent space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hyperbolic geometry naturally represents hierarchical and tree-like structures common in sequential decision-making, providing a more effective latent space for long-horizon planning than Euclidean space.
> - Refining a pretrained world model's predictor using geometric reinforcement learning can enhance multi-step planning stability and accuracy without requiring separate policy or reward models.
> - Enforcing geodesic consistency in predicted trajectories through triangle inequality regularization during GRL is crucial for mitigating error accumulation and improving multi-step rollout stability.
