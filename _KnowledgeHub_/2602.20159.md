# A Very Big Video Reasoning Suite

> [!summary] Summary
> VIDEO-REASON.COM: A Very Big Video Reasoning Suite introduces an extensive, community-curated dataset with over one million video clips and two million images, alongside a verifiable evaluation framework for video reasoning. Training on this suite improved a Wan2.2 model's reasoning performance by 84.6% relative to its baseline, achieving an overall score of 0.685, while revealing persistent challenges in long-horizon reasoning and cross-task generalization.

## Problem

- Current video generation models lack robust reasoning capabilities, particularly concerning physical world understanding.
- Existing video reasoning benchmarks are small, evaluation-heavy, and do not provide sufficient large-scale training data for investigating scaling behaviors.
- The absence of verifiable and reproducible evaluation frameworks hinders rigorous diagnosis and comparison of video reasoning models.

## Method

- Introduction of ==VBVR-Dataset==, an unprecedentedly large-scale resource (**1M+** video clips, **2M+** images) featuring 200 diverse reasoning tasks across a cognitive architecture-grounded taxonomy.
- Development of ==VBVR-Bench==, a verifiable evaluation framework utilizing rule-based, human-aligned scorers to ensure reproducible and interpretable diagnosis of video reasoning capabilities.
- Conducting a large-scale ==scaling study== to analyze emergent generalization to unseen reasoning tasks, leveraging the extensive VBVR suite.

## Results

- VBVR-Wan2.2, fine-tuned on the dataset, achieved an overall score of **0.685** on VBVR-Bench, representing an **84.6%** relative improvement over its base model (**0.371**) and outperforming other tested state-of-the-art models.
- Despite performance improvements with data scaling, a substantial gap to human performance (**0.974** overall) and a persistent **~15%** gap between in-domain and out-of-domain generalization remain.
- The scaling study demonstrated performance plateaus at **400K-500K** samples, indicating that current video generation architectures face representational and optimization bottlenecks for complex video reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling video reasoning data significantly improves both in-domain and out-of-domain performance, yet gains plateau, suggesting fundamental architectural bottlenecks for complex tasks.
> - Training on VBVR instills a "controllability-first" execution logic in models, enabling precise, verifiable manipulations critical for grounded physical world reasoning.
> - Analysis of capability correlations among cognitive faculties in AI models reveals non-trivial structural dependencies, such as a strong positive coupling between Knowledge and Spatiality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of artificial intelligence has seen groundbreaking progress in large language models (LLMs), with their reasoning abilities generalizing across complex text-based tasks like coding and mathematics. Concurrently, video generation models have advanced rapidly in visual realism, producing increasingly high-fidelity content. However, the paper identifies a significant disparity: while visual quality in video models has soared, their underlying reasoning capabilities, particularly for tasks grounded in the physical world, remain largely "underexplored."

The authors argue that video models hold immense potential for a "new paradigm of reasoning," enabling intuitive understanding of spatiotemporal structures, physical dynamics, continuity, interaction, and causality—concepts that are inherently difficult for text-based models to fully capture. Despite this promise and growing interest in video reasoning, the community has lacked critical infrastructure for systematic progress. Existing video reasoning benchmarks, as highlighted in Table 1, are predominantly "evaluation-heavy," offering limited or no large-scale training data. They are typically small, providing only a few thousand samples, which is insufficient for investigating scaling behaviors or developing generalizable reasoning models. This scarcity of data, coupled with a lack of verifiable and reproducible evaluation frameworks, has made it challenging to conduct systematic studies on how video reasoning capabilities emerge and scale.

"VIDEO-REASON.COM" directly addresses these gaps by introducing the Very Big Video Reasoning (VBVR) suite. By providing an unprecedentedly large-scale, diverse dataset and a rigorous, verifiable evaluation benchmark, this work aims to catalyze research into the reasoning abilities of video models, mirroring the data-driven scaling success observed in LLMs. The project seeks to move beyond models optimized solely for visual realism, pushing the frontier towards models that can truly reason about complex visual dynamics and interactions. This work represents a crucial step towards developing video models capable of "physical world understanding" and "generalizable video reasoning," making it a foundational contribution to the field of multimodal AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the VBVR suite stems from the recognition that while large language models have achieved remarkable reasoning capabilities in text, and video generation models excel in visual realism, there remains a critical void in understanding and fostering the reasoning abilities of AI grounded in the spatiotemporally consistent visual environment of video. The authors posit that video, with its natural encoding of spatial structure, physical dynamics, and long-range causality, is an "ideal substrate for studying reasoning grounded in the physical world."

Despite this inherent potential, three fundamental challenges have hindered systematic progress in video reasoning:
1. **Lack of Large-Scale and Diverse Training Data:** Existing datasets are either too small or too narrowly focused to enable meaningful investigation into the scaling and generalization properties of video reasoning models.
2. **Absence of a Verifiable and Reproducible Evaluation Toolkit:** Current evaluation paradigms, often relying on subjective "VLM-as-a-judge" methods, lack the verifiability and interpretability needed for rigorous diagnosis of reasoning capabilities.
3. **Limited Initial Scaling Studies:** The absence of the above components has prevented large-scale investigations into emergent capabilities in video reasoning models, similar to the insights gained from scaling LLMs.

To address these challenges, the VBVR project sets forth three key objectives, embodied by the Very Big Video Reasoning (VBVR) suite:

1. **Introduce VBVR-Dataset:** To create an "unprecedentedly large-scale resource" comprising over two million images and one million video clips. This dataset is designed to be diverse, spanning 200 curated reasoning tasks structured around a principled taxonomy derived from human cognitive architecture (Spatiality, Transformation, Knowledge, Abstraction, and Perception). Its scale, approximately three orders of magnitude larger than existing alternatives, aims to enable systematic scaling studies.
2. **Present VBVR-Bench:** To establish a "verifiable evaluation framework" that moves beyond subjective model-based judging. VBVR-Bench incorporates rule-based, human-aligned scorers, ensuring reproducible and interpretable diagnosis of video reasoning capabilities.
3. **Conduct a Large-Scale Scaling Study:** To leverage the VBVR suite to perform one of the first comprehensive scaling studies of video reasoning. This objective aims to observe and analyze early signs of emergent generalization to unseen reasoning tasks, providing crucial insights into the architectural and data requirements for advanced video intelligence.

In essence, the project is motivated by the belief that robust video reasoning is essential for AI to understand and interact with the physical world, and its objectives are to build the foundational infrastructure necessary to push this frontier forward.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of the VBVR project is meticulously structured, encompassing a principled approach to dataset creation, benchmark design, and experimental analysis.

#### 3.1. VBVR-Dataset: Creation and Curation

The core of the VBVR suite is the VBVR-Dataset, a synthetic and procedurally generated dataset designed for both scale and systematicity.

- **Cognitive Architecture-Grounded Taxonomy:** The dataset's 200 tasks are organized around a principled taxonomy inspired by well-established theories of human cognitive architecture (Newell & Simon, 1972; Anderson, 2007). Five core visual reasoning capabilities are defined:
 - **Abstraction:** To find rules from observations and use rules to deduce results (e.g., Raven's Matrices).
 - **Knowledge:** Propositional truth statements, either learned or intrinsic (e.g., physics simulations, clock reading).
 - **Perception:** Immediate access to sense data, fundamental visual processing (e.g., edge detection, color/shape discrimination).
 - **Spatiality:** Intuition of basic world properties like 3D-ness and Euclidean geometry (e.g., navigation, pathfinding).
 - **Transformation:** To simulate spatiotemporal continuities with internal models (e.g., mental rotation, object manipulation).
 This taxonomy ensures diverse and comprehensive coverage of reasoning skills.

- **Community-Oriented Task Design:** Over 50 researchers and engineers collaboratively designed tasks, with "full freedom to design the core task semantics and reasoning procedures." A unified task template ensures consistency for automated scaling, separating creativity from standardization. Tasks undergo expert human inspection for quality and correctness.

- **Three-Stage Curation Pipeline:**
 1. **Task Design and Approval:** Task proposals are evaluated against six stringent criteria: information sufficiency, deterministic solvability, video dependency (cannot be solved from a single image), visual clarity, parametric diversity (supporting ≥10,000 non-trivial instances), and technical feasibility. Only approved tasks proceed.
 2. **Task-Specific Generator Implementation:** Approved designs are implemented as parameterized generators. These generators algorithmically compute solutions and produce a four-component output for each instance: `first_frame.png` (initial state), `prompt.txt` (instruction), `final_frame.png` (target state), and `ground_truth.mp4` (complete solution trajectory). This provides verifiable supervision for learning "how" to reason. Parameterization ensures diversity by varying object count, spatial configuration, complexity, etc.
 3. **Large-Scale Distributed Generation with Quality Control:** A cloud-based pipeline (AWS Lambda, SQS, S3) generates 1,000,000 training samples (10,000 per task across 100 training tasks) and 7,500 test samples (50 per task across 150 test tasks). Disjoint random seeds prevent data leakage between training and testing. Automated validation checks for solvability, visual compliance, and boundary constraints. This pipeline is continuously expandable.

#### 3.2. VBVR-Bench: Evaluation Framework

VBVR-Bench provides a systematic, reproducible, and explainable evaluation framework.

- **Dual-Split Evaluation:** 100 diverse test tasks are split into two sets:
 - **In-Domain (ID):** 50 tasks overlapping with training categories but with unseen parameter configurations and instances, testing in-task generalization.
 - **Out-of-Domain (OOD):** 50 entirely novel tasks, measuring cross-task generalization and transferability of reasoning primitives.
- **Rule-Based, Verifiable Scorers:** Crucially, evaluation moves beyond subjective VLM-as-a-judge paradigms by using "verifiable, rule-based scorers." Each test task is paired with a dedicated evaluation rule that decomposes performance into interpretable sub-criteria (e.g., spatial accuracy, trajectory correctness, temporal consistency, logical validity). An example for "Key Door Matching" task illustrates scoring across target identification, path validity, path efficiency, and animation quality.
- **Human Preference Alignment:** To validate the fidelity of automated scores, human preference alignment experiments were conducted. Strong agreement (Spearman’s ρ > 0.9) between automated scores and human judgments was observed, confirming the reliability of VBVR-Bench.
- **Benchmarked Models:** Leading proprietary models (Veo 3.1, Sora 2, Kling 2.6, Runway Gen-4) and representative open-source models (Wan-2.2, CogVideoX-1.5, HuanyuanVideo, LTX-2) were benchmarked.

#### 3.3. Scaling Study Approach

A systematic scaling study was conducted using Wan-2.2 as the base model, without architectural modifications, to isolate the impact of data scale.

- **Progressive Data Increase:** Training data size was varied from 0K (base model) to 500K samples from VBVR-Dataset.
- **Performance Analysis:** In-domain and out-of-domain performance were tracked to observe generalization emergence.
- **Qualitative Evaluation:** Detailed qualitative comparisons between the base model, VBVR-Wan2.2, and strong proprietary baselines (e.g., Sora 2) were performed on held-out OOD tasks to analyze emergent behaviors, controllability, and limitations.
- **General Video Benchmark:** VBVR-Wan2.2 was also evaluated on VBench-I2V to ensure that reasoning-centric training did not degrade general video generation quality.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that the VBVR suite provides not just a large dataset, but a robust framework for understanding, evaluating, and advancing video reasoning capabilities in AI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VBVR project yields several critical findings across dataset utility, model performance, scaling behaviors, and cognitive faculty correlations.

#### 4.1. Dataset and Benchmark Validation

The project successfully established the VBVR-Dataset as an "unprecedentedly large-scale" resource (2.015 million images, 1.0075 million video clips) for video reasoning, approximately 1,000 times larger than existing benchmarks (Table 1). VBVR-Bench was validated as a reliable, reproducible, and human-aligned evaluation framework, with strong Spearman's correlation (ρ > 0.9) between automated scores and human judgments.

#### 4.2. Model Performance on VBVR-Bench

Benchmarking state-of-the-art models revealed a significant gap between current AI capabilities and human performance (Table 3):
- **Human Baseline:** Achieved an overall average score of 0.974.
- **Open-Source Models:** Generally clustered with limited capabilities (0.273 to 0.371 overall), with Wan2.2-I2V-A14B being the strongest at 0.371.
- **Proprietary Models:** Performed better, with Sora 2 (0.546) and Veo 3.1 (0.480) leading, particularly in Abstraction and Transformation.
- **VBVR-Wan2.2 (Fine-tuned):** Fine-tuning Wan2.2-I2V-A14B on VBVR-Dataset dramatically improved its performance to 0.685 overall, a 84.6% relative improvement over its base model. It emerged as the strongest model across all categories, especially in Spatiality and Perception, demonstrating the substantial impact of large-scale, reasoning-oriented data.
Despite these gains, a considerable gap to human performance persists, highlighting the ongoing challenges in long-horizon temporal reasoning and robust symbolic manipulation.

#### 4.3. Scaling Behavior Analysis

The scaling study using Wan2.2 revealed important insights into the relationship between data scale and reasoning capabilities (Table 4):
- **Clear but Saturating Gains:** Both in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) performance improved significantly with increasing training data, rising from 0.412 (ID) and 0.329 (OOD) at 0K samples to around 0.771 (ID) and 0.610 (OOD) at 400K-500K samples. This indicates that scaling data enhances transferable reasoning capabilities beyond mere memorization.
- **Performance Plateau:** Performance gains eventually plateaued, suggesting that current video generation architectures, despite data scaling, face "fundamental representational and optimization bottlenecks" for complex video reasoning tasks requiring simultaneous logical constraints and long-term temporal consistency.
- **Persistent Generalization Gap:** Although OOD performance improved, a consistent 15% gap between ID and OOD performance remained. This implies that increasing data *within fixed task distributions* is insufficient for robust systematic generalization to entirely novel reasoning structures.

#### 4.4. Capability Correlation

Analyzing the residualized correlations among the five cognitive faculties (after regressing out overall model strength) uncovered non-trivial structural dependencies (Figure 5):
- **Knowledge and Spatiality:** Exhibited a strong positive coupling (ρ = 0.461). This finding aligns with neuroscience research suggesting an intricate link between hippocampal place/grid cells and concept learning, supporting the idea of a "cognitive map" for both spatial and abstract knowledge.
- **Knowledge and Perception:** Showed a strong negative correlation (ρ = -0.757). This provokes a debate in cognitive science regarding whether "core knowledge" (e.g., intuitive physics) should be considered perception rather than learned knowledge, possibly mediated by distinct neural circuits.
- **Abstraction:** Correlated negatively with Transformation (ρ = -0.641) and Spatiality (ρ = -0.481), and showed no positive correlations, consistent with the modularity of the abstraction faculty in the prefrontal cortex.
- **Perception:** Traded off with Spatiality (ρ = -0.565) but was nearly uncorrelated with Abstraction and Transformation.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Insights (VBVR-Wan2.2 Analysis)

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 6) of VBVR-Wan2.2 with the base model and Sora 2 highlighted key advancements and limitations:
- **Enhanced Controllable Execution:** VBVR training instilled a "controllability-first" execution logic, enabling VBVR-Wan2.2 to match or surpass Sora 2 on tasks requiring precise, verifiable manipulations under stable scenes (e.g., symbol deletion, 2D geometric rotation, bookshelf organization). It showed an emergent "do exactly what is asked" capability.
- **Emergent Strategies and Multi-Step Behavior:** VBVR-Wan2.2 demonstrated nascent abilities for emergent strategy-level regularities and multi-step behaviors on OOD tasks, such as consistent, self-chosen policies for symmetric pattern completion and "rationalizing" intermediate scene elements to fit an internal narrative.
- **Limitations:** Challenges remain in "process unfaithfulness" (generating correct answers without faithfully reflecting the ground-truth procedural steps) and long-horizon control breakdown (e.g., agent duplication/flickering in complex navigation tasks).

#### 4.6. General Video Generation Performance

On the VBench-I2V suite, VBVR-Wan2.2 maintained the high general generative capabilities of its base model (Wan2.2-I2V-A14B), confirming that reasoning-centric training did not degrade visual quality. Notably, it showed improved Video-Text Camera Motion Consistency and decreased Dynamic Degree, indicating a more precise understanding of motion dynamics and scene stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Very Big Video Reasoning Suite" (VBVR) represents a foundational contribution with significant implications for the future of video AI and general artificial intelligence. Its impact can be categorized as follows:

#### 5.1. Establishing Foundational Infrastructure for Video Reasoning

The most immediate and profound impact is the provision of two critically missing components:
- **Unprecedented Data Scale:** VBVR-Dataset, with over one million video clips, finally offers the large-scale training data necessary to systematically investigate scaling laws and emergent capabilities in video reasoning, similar to how large text corpora propelled LLM development. This resource will be instrumental in moving beyond models focused purely on visual realism.
- **Verifiable and Reproducible Evaluation:** VBVR-Bench, with its rule-based, human-aligned scoring, addresses the pervasive issue of subjective evaluation in generative AI. This framework enables researchers to rigorously diagnose reasoning strengths and weaknesses, fostering more reliable and interpretable progress.

#### 5.2. Driving Architectural Innovation

The scaling studies conducted with VBVR-Wan2.2 highlight that while data scale yields significant improvements, performance eventually plateaus, and a persistent in-domain/out-of-domain gap remains. This finding is crucial as it suggests fundamental limitations in current video generation architectures when applied to complex reasoning. The VBVR suite thus becomes a valuable testbed for developing and evaluating next-generation architectures that incorporate explicit state tracking, structured reasoning modules, causal inference mechanisms, or self-correction abilities. It provides a concrete challenge and a means to measure progress on these architectural advancements.

#### 5.3. Shifting the Paradigm Towards "Controllability Before Reasoning"

The qualitative analysis reveals that VBVR training instills a "controllability-first" execution logic in models. This insight is highly significant: for AI to reason reliably about the physical world, it must first be able to control and maintain consistency within that world. If a model freely alters scene elements or physical laws during generation, any subsequent "reasoning" becomes unverifiable. VBVR demonstrates that explicit training for constrained, verifiable manipulations is key, even generalizing to novel task structures. This paradigm shift will guide the design and evaluation of future video reasoning systems.

#### 5.4. Illuminating Cognitive Faculty Interdependencies in AI

The analysis of capability correlations among the five cognitive faculties (Abstraction, Knowledge, Perception, Spatiality, Transformation) offers unique insights into the internal organization of AI models. The observed strong positive coupling between Knowledge and Spatiality, for instance, resonates with neuroscientific theories of cognitive maps and their role in abstract concept learning. Conversely, the negative correlation between Knowledge and Perception sparks interesting discussions about the nature of "core knowledge" in both biological and artificial intelligence. These findings could inform the development of more biologically plausible and intrinsically structured AI architectures.

#### 5.5. Fostering Community Collaboration and Continuous Advancement

The community-oriented, collaborative nature of the VBVR project, involving researchers from diverse institutions, is a model for large-scale scientific endeavors in AI. The design of an expandable data generation pipeline and an extensible benchmark positions VBVR as a "living benchmark" that can continuously grow with new tasks and evolving understanding of video reasoning. This fosters an ecosystem where collective efforts can accelerate progress beyond what individual labs might achieve.

In conclusion, the VBVR suite is more than just a dataset or a benchmark; it is a comprehensive infrastructure designed to re-orient video AI research towards generalizable reasoning about the physical world. By addressing critical data and evaluation gaps, it lays a robust foundation for future innovations, guiding both architectural design and our understanding of intelligence in multimodal AI systems.
