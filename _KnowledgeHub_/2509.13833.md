# Track Any Motions under Any Disturbances

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage Reinforcement Learning framework for humanoids, called Any2Track, enables robust motion tracking across diverse, highly dynamic, and contact-rich movements, coupled with online adaptation to real-world disturbances. The system demonstrates superior tracking quality and stable operation on both simulation and a physical Unitree G1 robot, with successful zero-shot transfer.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid motion tracking methods struggle to simultaneously track a wide range of diverse, highly dynamic, and contact-rich human motions.
- Humanoid controllers lack robust online adaptability to varied terrains, external forces, and changes in robot physical properties, limiting practical real-world deployment.
- The inherent sim2real dynamics gap often causes performance degradation when transferring policies from simulation to real humanoid hardware.

## Method

- A two-stage ==Reinforcement Learning== framework is proposed: AnyTracker for general motion tracking, and AnyAdapter for online dynamics adaptation.
- AnyTracker utilizes canonicalized action spaces and a specialist-to-generalist distillation strategy to efficiently learn control for highly diverse and complex motion patterns.
- AnyAdapter introduces a history-informed, dynamics-aware world model to learn informative dynamics embeddings, which are then used by a decoupled adapter architecture to inject adaptability without compromising core motion skills.

## Results

- AnyTracker achieved a success rate of **89.23%**, with a Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) of **27.96** mm and Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE) of **6.43** mm/frame in simulation, outperforming baseline general motion trackers.
- Any2Track (with AnyAdapter) consistently demonstrated superior online dynamics adaptation, showing minimal performance degradation under diverse simulated disturbances compared to other leading methods.
- Achieved successful zero-shot sim2real transfer to a Unitree G1 humanoid, maintaining lower MPJPE (**17.38** mm vs. **29.38** mm for PPO) without disturbance and increasing its advantage on complex terrains (**18.34** mm MPJPE vs. **37.21** mm for PPO).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning of core motion tracking skills from dynamics adaptation simplifies optimization and prevents performance degradation of fundamental behaviors.
> - Canonicalizing action spaces across different joints and motion categories allows a unified policy to learn more compact and consistent action distributions for diverse behaviors.
> - Learning dynamics embeddings through a predictive world model from interaction history provides highly informative features for robust online adaptation to various, previously unseen disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid motion tracking is a critical and rapidly evolving area in robotics, aimed at enabling humanoid platforms to reproduce human motion sequences. This capability is vital for endowing robots with expressive, anthropomorphic, and versatile behaviors that go beyond pre-programmed or purely Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based controllers. The ultimate goal is to allow humanoids to operate effectively and safely in diverse, unstructured human environments.

The existing research landscape in humanoid motion tracking faces two primary limitations:
1. **General Motion Tracking:** Many current methods struggle to simultaneously track a wide range of motions that are diverse, highly dynamic, and rich in contact interactions (e.g., jumping, crawling, complex manipulation). Some approaches, like HumanPlus and OmniH2O, primarily focus on quasi-static locomotion and manipulation. Others, such as ASAP, achieve high dynamism but tend to overfit to short, specific motion clips. While methods like GMT aim for generality, they often simplify datasets by removing highly dynamic or contact-rich motions, thus limiting their true versatility.
2. **Dynamics Adaptability:** A major challenge for real-world deployment is the robot's ability to operate stably against various disturbances, including varied terrains, external forces (e.g., pushes, pulls), and changes in physical properties (e.g., payload, friction). Traditional methods often rely on naive domain randomization during training, which can lead to conservative policies that lack adaptive responsiveness to significant or novel dynamics variances. More advanced online adaptation methods for legged robots, like Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), Dual History, and DWL, aim to learn environment dynamics from interaction history. However, integrating these with complex, expressive motion tracking in humanoids, while maintaining performance, remains a significant hurdle. They often struggle to learn sufficiently informative dynamics representations or couple basic action execution with adaptation, leading to performance compromises.

"Any2Track" positions itself directly at the intersection of these two challenges. It aims to develop a *foundational* humanoid motion tracker that not only excels at tracking diverse, highly dynamic, and contact-rich motions (a challenge previous "general" trackers faced) but also simultaneously demonstrates robust, online adaptability to a wide array of real-world disturbances. The paper explicitly claims "unprecedented levels of motion diversity, high dynamism, and contact complexity" coupled with "online dynamics adaptability," marking a significant step toward general-purpose, real-world humanoid deployment. By decoupling the learning of core motion tracking skills from dynamics adaptation, Any2Track seeks to overcome the joint optimization difficulties that have hindered previous approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to create a robust and foundational humanoid motion tracker that can reliably operate in complex, real-world environments. To achieve this, the authors identify and address two critical motivations, framed as key challenges:

1. **To construct a unified general motion tracker capable of efficiently and with high quality learning diverse motion control strategies.** This objective is motivated by the limitations of existing methods that either handle limited motion diversity, lack the dynamism and contact richness required for general human-like behaviors, or struggle with the complexity of high-degree-of-freedom humanoids. The goal is to build a single policy that can track a vast array of human motions without compromising quality or expressiveness.
2. **To endow the controller with online dynamics adaptability to overcome the sim2real dynamics gap and various real-world disturbances.** This motivation stems from the inherent discrepancies between simulation and reality (sim2real gap) and the unpredictable nature of real-world environments. Factors like changing terrains, unexpected external forces, and variations in robot physical properties (e.g., carrying a payload, different joint friction) can severely degrade a robot's performance. The objective is to enable the robot to adapt its behavior in real-time to these dynamic changes, ensuring stable and robust operation for practical use.

The authors propose "Any2Track" as a solution, explicitly reformulating dynamics adaptability as an *additional capability* on top of basic action execution. This design philosophy directly tackles the motivation of avoiding performance degradation in core motion tracking skills while introducing robust adaptability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Any2Track is a novel two-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework built upon the PPO algorithm and simulated in MuJoCo. Its core design philosophy involves decoupling the learning of motion tracking capabilities from dynamics adaptability to simplify optimization and enhance performance.

**Stage 1: AnyTracker – Track Any Motions**

The first stage focuses on building a general motion tracker capable of reproducing diverse, highly dynamic, and contact-rich motions. The policy maps humanoid proprioception states and tracking goals to low-level robot actions, which are then fed into a PD controller. To overcome the challenges posed by high degrees of freedom and motion diversity, AnyTracker employs two key designs:

1. **Canonicalized Action Spaces:** The authors observe that the action distributions vary significantly across different joints and motion categories, making a unified policy prediction difficult. They address this by predicting residual PD offsets relative to the reference motion, rather than absolute PD targets. These residual actions are first mapped to a canonical range `[-1, 1]` using the `tanh` function, and then rescaled for each joint using empirically designed hyperparameters `α`. This canonicalization helps the policy learn a more compact and consistent multi-joint action distribution, simplifying the optimization process.
 `q_d = q_t+1_tilde + α * tanh(π(a_t | s_t))`
2. **Motion Clustering and Specialist-to-Generalist Strategy:** To manage the diversity of motion patterns, the training data (AMASS and LAFAN1 datasets) is clustered. For LAFAN1, existing motion categories are used. For AMASS, motion category labels are embedded using CLIP and then clustered with K-means into six subsets. A "specialist" policy is trained for each cluster, allowing each specialist to focus on a subset of motions with similar action distributions, thus reducing training difficulty. Finally, a "generalist" policy is distilled from all specialists using the DAgger algorithm, enabling a single policy to handle the full spectrum of motions. Importantly, AnyTracker is trained *without* any dynamics randomization in this stage to prevent tracking performance degradation.

**Stage 2: AnyAdapter – Adapt to Any Disturbances**

Building upon the robust AnyTracker, the second stage introduces dynamics variance and develops an online adaptation module called AnyAdapter. This module endows the base tracker with adaptability to terrains, external forces, and physical property changes, while crucially *not* compromising its expressive motion tracking capability. AnyAdapter focuses on two key aspects:

1. **Learning Informative Dynamics Embeddings:** The module employs a "dynamics-aware world model prediction" as a proxy task to extract informative dynamics features from the robot-environment interaction history.
 - **History Encoder (`ϕ`):** This component processes a history buffer of robot states and actions (`h_t`) to generate a compact dynamics feature embedding (`e_t`). The history window size `H` is set to 79.
 - **World Model (`ω`):** This model is tasked with autoregressively predicting the next `N=20` future robot states (`ŝ_t+i+1`) based on the current state, action, and, crucially, the dynamics embedding (`e_t+i`). The loss function `L_wm` (L1 loss on state prediction) simultaneously updates both `ϕ` and `ω`. By forcing the world model to accurately predict future states under varying dynamics, the history encoder is compelled to learn highly informative dynamics features that can identify different environment configurations.
2. **Dynamics Adaptability Injection via Adapter Architecture:** Unlike methods that integrate adaptation directly into the core policy network, AnyAdapter uses an "adapter" architecture to decouple adaptation from basic action execution.
 - The parameters of the pre-trained AnyTracker are *frozen*.
 - An adapter (`ξ`), consisting of `M` layers (the number of layers in AnyTracker) with initially zero-initialized weights, is introduced. This ensures that the adapter does not alter the base tracker's output initially.
 - During fine-tuning, the adapter takes the dynamics embeddings (`e_t`) as input and adaptively adjusts the base policy's actions through layer-wise feature fusion. This strategy allows the policy to learn how to adjust its behavior to different environments without degrading the already acquired complex motion tracking skills.
 - The adapter and the history encoder are alternately updated using the same reward terms as AnyTracker, but within an environment that incorporates dynamics variance (Table III), including floor friction, terrain height, external force magnitudes, DoF friction scaling, armature scaling, torso CoM position changes, and torso mass changes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulation (MuJoCo) and on a real 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, addressing three key questions related to general motion tracking, online dynamics adaptation, and real-world performance. The primary metrics used were Success Rate (SR), Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE, mm), and Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE, mm/frame).

**Q1: Can AnyTracker improve the performance of general motion tracking compared to existing methods?**
- **Results (Simulation, no disturbances):** AnyTracker significantly outperformed baseline general motion trackers (OmniH2O, Exbody2) and its ablated versions (w/o canonicalized action spaces, w/o distillation).
 - AnyTracker achieved an SR of 89.23%, compared to OmniH2O (75.64%) and Exbody2 (79.68%).
 - It demonstrated lower MPJPE (27.96 mm vs. 36.12 mm/34.70 mm) and MPJVE (6.43 mm/frame vs. 12.24 mm/frame / 13.69 mm/frame).
- **Conclusion:** The canonicalized action spaces and specialist-to-generalist distillation effectively alleviated optimization difficulties from complex action spaces, leading to superior motion tracking quality.

**Q2: Can AnyAdapter outperform other baseline methods in online dynamics adaptation capability against disturbances from different sources?**
- **Results (Simulation, with disturbances on LAFAN1 dataset):** Any2Track (incorporating AnyAdapter) consistently surpassed all compared baseline methods (Vanilla PPO, Dual History, RMA, DWL) across environments with various disturbances (terrains, external forces, physical property changes), and even in environments without disturbances.
 - **Without Disturbance:** Any2Track achieved the highest SR (89.8%) and lowest MPJPE (16.46 mm) and MPJVE (6.04 mm/frame).
 - **Under Disturbances:** Any2Track showed the least performance degradation. For example, on complex terrains, it had an SR of 83.2% and MPJPE of 20.68 mm, significantly better than RMA (75.8% SR, 23.99-24.60 mm MPJPE) and PPO (73.0% SR, 31.01 mm MPJPE). Similar superior performance was observed under external forces and physical property changes.
 - Notably, Any2Track outperformed RMA (Stage 1), which had access to *privileged* environment factors, suggesting that Any2Track's dynamics features and adaptation strategy are highly effective.
- **Ablation Studies on AnyAdapter:**
 - "Ours w/o World Model" (without dynamics-aware world model prediction) performed worse, especially under external pushes and physical property changes, highlighting the critical role of the world model in generating informative dynamics embeddings.
 - "Ours w/o Adapter" (without the decoupled adapter architecture) also suffered clear performance drops, validating the effectiveness of separating basic action execution from adaptability.
- **Conclusion:** AnyAdapter's history-informed dynamics embeddings, learned through dynamics-aware world model prediction, and the decoupled adapter architecture enable superior online dynamics adaptation without sacrificing core motion tracking capabilities.

**Q3: How does Any2Track perform in various real-world scenarios?**
- **Results (Real-world deployment on Unitree G1):** Any2Track achieved a successful zero-shot sim2real transfer and demonstrated superior performance compared to vanilla PPO with domain randomization under various real-world disturbances (complex terrains, external rope pulling forces, carrying a 5 kg payload).
 - **Without Disturbance:** Any2Track had significantly lower MPJPE (17.38 mm vs. 29.38 mm for PPO) and MPJVE (11.56 mm/frame vs. 14.26 mm/frame).
 - **Under Disturbances:** Any2Track maintained its advantage, with its performance gap over PPO *increasing* as disturbances were introduced. For instance, on complex terrain, Any2Track's MPJPE was 18.34 mm compared to PPO's 37.21 mm, representing a 18.87 mm improvement.
- **Conclusion:** Any2Track proves its practical utility and robustness by operating effectively on real hardware, showcasing its ability to handle diverse real-world dynamics beyond simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Any2Track" framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, specifically addressing long-standing challenges in achieving versatile and robust motion control for real-world deployment. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap for Complex Humanoids:** By demonstrating successful zero-shot transfer of highly dynamic and adaptable motion tracking from simulation to the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, Any2Track tackles one of the most persistent challenges in robotics. This opens avenues for faster development cycles and more reliable deployment of complex behaviors without extensive real-world training or tuning.
2. **Foundational Robustness for Open-World Environments:** The ability to track diverse, highly dynamic, and contact-rich motions while simultaneously adapting to varying terrains, external forces, and changes in robot physical properties is crucial for humanoids operating in unstructured, unpredictable environments. Any2Track provides a foundational capability that enables such robots to interact more naturally and safely with the physical world. This moves humanoid research closer to practical applications beyond controlled laboratory settings.
3. **Decoupled Learning Paradigm:** The novel two-stage RL framework, particularly the separation of basic motion tracking from dynamics adaptability via the adapter architecture and world model, offers a powerful and efficient learning paradigm. This decoupling reduces the complexity of joint optimization, allowing for specialized learning in each stage and preventing performance degradation in core skills when introducing adaptability. This methodology could inspire similar architectural designs in other complex robotic control tasks.
4. **Enhanced Motion Expressiveness and Anthropomorphism:** By retaining highly dynamic and contact-rich motions from datasets like AMASS and LAFAN1 and developing strategies to track them effectively, Any2Track enables humanoids to exhibit more human-like, expressive, and anthropomorphic behaviors. This is vital for applications requiring natural human-robot interaction or imitation learning.
5. **Enabling Future Research and Applications:** As a "robust base motion tracking model," Any2Track has the potential to serve as a cornerstone for numerous downstream tasks in humanoid robotics. This includes:
 - **Whole-body Tele-operation:** Providing a stable and adaptable base for human operators to control humanoids more intuitively.
 - **Humanoid Skill Learning:** Accelerating the acquisition of new, complex skills by providing a robust underlying motion execution layer.
 - **Humanoid VLA (Visual Language Action) Models:** Offering a high-fidelity control interface for robots to translate high-level commands from vision and language models into physical actions.
 - **General-Purpose Humanoids:** Contributing significantly to the development of versatile humanoids capable of performing a wide range of tasks in diverse environments, from logistics and assistance to exploration and disaster response.

In conclusion, Any2Track represents a critical step towards building truly capable and adaptable humanoid robots, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in motion tracking and robust control.
