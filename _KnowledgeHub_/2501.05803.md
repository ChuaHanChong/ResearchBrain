# Test-time Alignment of Diffusion Models without Reward Over-optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Diffusion Alignment as Sampling (DAS), a training-free, test-time method that aligns pre-trained diffusion models with arbitrary rewards. DAS leverages Sequential Monte Carlo techniques with tempering to achieve high target reward optimization while effectively preserving sample diversity and generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Pre-trained diffusion models generate diverse outputs but often lack alignment with specific user preferences, aesthetics, or task-specific objectives.
- Existing fine-tuning methods (e.g., RLHF) optimize rewards effectively but frequently suffer from reward over-optimization, sacrificing sample diversity and generalizability.
- Training-free guidance methods preserve diversity but often under-optimize target rewards due to inexact score function approximations, especially at high noise levels.

## Method

- The paper proposes **DAS** (Diffusion Alignment as Sampling), which reframes diffusion model alignment as a ==sampling problem== from a reward-aligned target distribution using ==Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)==.
- DAS employs ==intermediate target distributions with tempering==, gradually increasing the influence of the reward function during the iterative denoising process to ensure stable and accurate sampling.
- It utilizes ==locally optimal Gaussian proposals== and ==adaptive resampling== based on ==Effective Sample Size (ESS)== to efficiently sample particles, coupled with theoretical guarantees for asymptotic exactness and improved sample efficiency with tempering.

## Results

- DAS achieves comparable or **superior target rewards** (e.g., LAION aesthetic score, PickScore) compared to state-of-the-art fine-tuning methods while preserving cross-reward generalization and sample diversity (**TCE**, **LPIPS MPD**).
- It demonstrates **Pareto-optimal** solutions for multi-objective tasks (e.g., balancing aesthetic score and CLIPScore) without requiring separate model training for each objective combination.
- In online black-box optimization settings, DAS matches target reward performance of state-of-the-art methods but significantly surpasses them in **unseen reward generalization** and **output diversity**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time alignment can achieve reward optimization comparable to fine-tuning methods while preserving the generalization and diversity of pre-trained diffusion models, overcoming the traditional trade-off.
> - Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) methods, when adapted with tempering and locally optimal proposals, provide a theoretically grounded framework for sampling from reward-aligned distributions at inference time.
> - Tempering (gradually increasing reward influence) significantly improves sample efficiency and mitigates off-manifold guidance, allowing high performance with a practical number of particles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI has seen revolutionary advancements, particularly with the advent of diffusion models. These models, such as Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) and Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in generating high-quality, diverse data across various modalities, from text-to-image (T2I) synthesis to protein structure design. However, a significant challenge remains: these models are typically pre-trained on vast, uncurated datasets, meaning their generative outputs may not inherently align with specific user preferences, artistic styles, or task-specific objectives. For instance, a user might desire an aesthetically pleasing image faithful to a prompt, rather than a generic image from the pre-training distribution. This is known as the "alignment problem."

Existing approaches to addressing this alignment challenge broadly fall into two categories:

1. **Fine-tuning Methods:** These approaches adapt the pre-trained diffusion model directly by training it further using reinforcement learning (RL) from human feedback (RLHF) or direct backpropagation through differentiable reward models. While effective in optimizing target rewards, fine-tuning often suffers from a critical drawback known as "reward over-optimization." This phenomenon leads to a trade-off where the model becomes overly specialized in maximizing the target reward, often at the cost of sacrificing general image quality, reducing sample diversity, and exhibiting "mode-seeking behavior" where it only captures a subset of the target distribution's modes. This issue is analogous to mode collapse in GANs and is a significant hurdle for deploying fine-tuned models in diverse real-world applications. KL regularization is sometimes used to mitigate this, but its effectiveness is limited.

2. **Guidance Methods:** These are training-free alternatives that modify the sampling process of a pre-trained diffusion model at inference time to encourage desired attributes. They typically rely on approximating the score function of a reward-aligned posterior distribution. While guidance methods generally preserve the diversity and generalization capabilities of the pre-trained model by staying closer to its original distribution, they often suffer from "reward under-optimization." This is because their approximations of the target score function can be inexact, especially early in the diffusion process when noise levels are high, leading to sub-optimal performance in truly maximizing the target rewards.

The work by Kim et al. directly addresses the limitations of both fine-tuning and guidance methods. It identifies a critical gap: the absence of a method that can *effectively optimize target rewards* (like fine-tuning) while simultaneously *preserving diversity and generalization* (like guidance methods), all without the computational burden and risk of over-optimization associated with model re-training. Their proposed Diffusion Alignment as Sampling (DAS) method aims to bridge this gap by offering a training-free, test-time solution that achieves superior reward alignment without compromising the model's inherent capabilities, effectively pushing beyond the current Pareto front of reward optimization versus diversity/generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, efficient, and robust method for aligning pre-trained diffusion models with arbitrary, user-defined rewards. The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations of existing alignment strategies: the reward over-optimization and diversity degradation seen in fine-tuning approaches, and the reward under-optimization common in training-free guidance methods.

Specifically, the key objectives driving this work are:

1. **Effective Reward Optimization:** To enable diffusion models to generate outputs that achieve high scores for a given target reward, performing comparably to or even surpassing fine-tuning methods.
2. **Preservation of Generalization and Diversity:** To ensure that the alignment process does not sacrifice the model's ability to generate diverse samples or its generalization to other, unseen reward functions or related concepts. This directly tackles the reward over-optimization problem.
3. **Training-Free and Test-Time Operation:** To eliminate the need for computationally expensive and time-consuming re-training or fine-tuning of the diffusion model for each new alignment objective. This reduces the barriers to rapid deployment and adaptation.
4. **Theoretical Justification:** To provide a rigorous theoretical foundation for the proposed method, including guarantees for asymptotic exactness and an analysis of its sample efficiency, particularly concerning the benefits of tempering techniques.
5. **Versatile Application:** To demonstrate the method's efficacy across a spectrum of alignment scenarios, including:
 - Optimizing a single, specific reward (e.g., aesthetic score, human preference).
 - Handling multi-objective optimization, where multiple, potentially competing, rewards need to be balanced.
 - Operating in online black-box optimization settings, where rewards are unknown or non-differentiable and feedback is iterative and limited.

The overarching motivation is to provide a practical and powerful tool for practitioners and researchers to customize diffusion model outputs without the common pitfalls of existing alignment paradigms, thereby unlocking the full potential of these powerful generative models for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, **Diffusion Alignment as Sampling (DAS)**, reformulates the diffusion model alignment problem as a sampling problem from a reward-aligned target distribution. This target distribution is defined as $p_{tar}(x) \propto p_{pre}(x) \exp(r(x)/\alpha)$, where $p_{pre}(x)$ is the distribution of the pre-trained diffusion model, $r(x)$ is the reward function, and $\alpha$ is a regularization parameter controlling the trade-off between reward maximization and fidelity to the pre-trained distribution. To sample from this complex, unnormalized distribution without training, DAS leverages **Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)** methods, tailored specifically for the iterative nature of diffusion sampling.

The key design choices in DAS for adapting SMC to diffusion models are:

1. **Intermediate Target Distributions with Tempering:**
 - SMC works by defining a sequence of intermediate target distributions that gradually bridge from an easy-to-sample prior distribution (e.g., the initial noisy latent state $x_T$) to the complex final target distribution (the aligned clean image $x_0$).
 - DAS defines these intermediate distributions as $\pi_t(x_t) \propto p_t(x_t) \exp(\frac{\lambda_t}{\alpha} \hat{r}(x_t))$, where $p_t(x_t)$ is the marginal distribution of the noisy latent at time $t$, and $\hat{r}(x_t)$ is an approximate reward function for the noisy latent (derived from the predicted clean image $\hat{x}_0(x_t)$).
 - A crucial innovation is the use of **tempering** (controlled by $\lambda_t$, which ranges from 0 to 1 as time $t$ progresses from $T$ to $0$). Tempering effectively smooths the transition between distributions, especially at early, very noisy stages where $\hat{r}(x_t)$ might be inaccurate. By starting with $\lambda_T = 0$ (effectively sampling from the pre-trained distribution) and gradually increasing to $\lambda_0 = 1$ (sampling from the full reward-aligned target), DAS ensures a smoother and more stable sampling process. This strategy enhances sample efficiency and mitigates off-manifold guidance, a common issue where guidance pushes samples to regions unfamiliar to the diffusion model.

2. **Backward Kernels:**
 - SMC requires defining backward kernels that relate states at different time steps. DAS defines these using Bayes' rule and the stochastic diffusion sampler of the pre-trained model. This allows the SMC process to leverage the learned dynamics of the base diffusion model.

3. **Locally Optimal Proposals:**
 - To maximize sample efficiency (i.e., achieve good results with fewer "particles" or parallel samples), SMC benefits from using "optimal proposals" that minimize the variance of importance weights.
 - DAS derives the locally optimal proposal, which takes the form of a Gaussian distribution. This proposal is essentially the pre-trained diffusion model's reverse step perturbed by the gradient of the *tempered approximate reward*.
 - The use of this Gaussian approximation for the proposal distribution is critical for making DAS practical, as sampling directly from the true optimal proposal is often intractable. The tempering parameter further improves the accuracy of this approximation.

4. **Sequential Monte Carlo with Adaptive Resampling:**
 - The core algorithm iteratively samples "particles" (parallel latent states) from the proposal distribution, updates their importance weights based on how well they align with the intermediate target distribution, and then resamples them based on these weights.
 - Adaptive resampling, based on the Effective Sample Size (ESS), is employed. Instead of resampling at every step (which can lead to particle degeneracy), resampling occurs only when the ESS falls below a threshold, maintaining diversity while improving stability. The Srinivasan Sampling Process (SSP) is used for resampling.

5. **Theoretical Analysis:**
 - The paper provides theoretical guarantees for DAS's performance:
 - **Asymptotic Exactness:** Proves that as the number of particles approaches infinity, the sample estimates produced by DAS converge to the true expectation under the reward-aligned target distribution. This distinguishes SMC from purely approximate guidance methods.
 - **Improved Sample Efficiency with Tempering:** Demonstrates that tempering significantly reduces the asymptotic variance of sample estimates. This is a crucial finding, as it provides a theoretical justification for why tempering allows DAS to achieve high-quality results with a practical number of particles.

In essence, DAS ingeniously combines a theoretically grounded probabilistic sampling framework (SMC) with practical adaptations (tempering, optimal proposals) to align diffusion models at test time, offering a powerful, training-free alternative to existing methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper empirically validates DAS across a variety of scenarios, consistently demonstrating its superiority over existing fine-tuning and guidance methods.

- **Single-Reward Optimization:**
 - **Performance:** DAS consistently achieves comparable or superior target rewards (e.g., LAION aesthetic score, PickScore) compared to state-of-the-art fine-tuning methods (DDPO, AlignProp, TDPO, DiffusionDPO).
 - **Over-optimization Mitigation:** Crucially, unlike fine-tuning methods that tend to suffer from reward over-optimization (sacrificing diversity and generalization), DAS effectively preserves cross-reward generalization (measured by HPSv2 and ImageReward) and maintains high sample diversity (measured by Truncated CLIP Entropy (TCE) and LPIPS Mean Pairwise Distance (MPD)). This is depicted as breaking through the "Pareto front" of previous methods, which typically show a trade-off between reward maximization and diversity/generalization.
 - **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons (e.g., "crocodile" prompt, T2I alignment categories) confirm that DAS generates aesthetically pleasing, diverse, and faithful images, retaining characteristics of the pre-trained model, while fine-tuned models often produce distorted, less diverse, or over-optimized outputs.
 - **SDXL Generalization:** DAS's effectiveness is shown to generalize to more complex diffusion backbones like SDXL, maintaining strong performance in target optimization and cross-reward generalization.
 - **Ablation Study (Tempering):** This experiment provides strong evidence for the theoretical benefits of tempering. Without tempering, the SMC process suffers from over-optimization and off-manifold deviations, even with a large number of particles. With tempering, DAS achieves high performance with significantly fewer particles (e.g., 4 or 8), demonstrating a dramatic improvement in sample efficiency and better adherence to the latent manifold.

- **Multi-Objective Optimization:**
 - **Pareto-Optimality:** DAS demonstrates its ability to achieve Pareto-optimal solutions for multi-objective tasks (e.g., balancing aesthetic score and CLIPScore) without requiring separate fine-tuning or model interpolation for each specific objective combination.
 - **Efficiency:** It outperforms "model souping" approaches that require extensive training for individual objectives and then interpolation.
 - **Qualitative Consistency:** Examples like "classic cars on a city street with people and a dog" show DAS's ability to balance competing objectives effectively (e.g., consistently generating the "dog" while maintaining aesthetic quality at varying reward weights).

- **Online Black-Box Optimization:**
 - **Efficient Exploration:** When adapting to online black-box rewards (e.g., aesthetic score with limited queries), DAS, by combining with UCB/Bootstrap strategies, matches the target reward performance of SEIKO (a state-of-the-art online method that requires fine-tuning), but *significantly surpasses* it in unseen reward generalization and output diversity.
 - **Adaptability:** DAS's training-free nature allows it to quickly adapt to updates in a learned surrogate reward model, enhancing responsiveness in online settings.
 - **Non-Differentiable Rewards:** The framework is extended to handle non-differentiable rewards, demonstrated effectively with JPEG compressibility, where DAS-UCB achieves superior compressibility while preserving image quality and diversity compared to a fine-tuned DDPO model.

These findings collectively establish DAS as a robust, efficient, and versatile solution for aligning diffusion models, effectively overcoming the limitations of prior training-intensive or under-optimizing approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Diffusion Alignment as Sampling (DAS) marks a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for the practical application and customization of diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking the Over-optimization/Diversity Trade-off:** DAS offers a compelling solution to the long-standing problem of reward over-optimization in diffusion models. By effectively maximizing target rewards without sacrificing sample diversity or cross-reward generalization, it eliminates a critical barrier to the broader adoption of aligned generative models in diverse applications. This means developers can achieve highly specific outputs without degrading the model's overall generative capabilities.

2. **Training-Free Efficiency:** The most impactful aspect of DAS is its training-free, test-time nature. This drastically reduces the computational resources, time, and expertise required for model alignment. Instead of re-training a large diffusion model for every new objective or preference, users can simply apply DAS during inference. This democratizes access to powerful alignment capabilities, enabling rapid prototyping, experimentation with diverse rewards, and agile adaptation to changing user needs or market trends.

3. **Versatility Across Scenarios:** DAS's demonstrated effectiveness in single-reward optimization, multi-objective scenarios, and challenging online black-box optimization settings highlights its remarkable versatility. This makes it a suitable tool for complex real-world applications where objectives might be multifaceted, dynamic, or even non-differentiable. For instance, in creative industries, artists could fine-tune generation on a combination of aesthetic and style metrics; in scientific domains, researchers could balance fidelity to biological constraints with novel design exploration.

4. **Enhanced Exploration in Online Settings:** In online black-box optimization, where feedback is costly and limited, DAS's ability to efficiently explore diverse, high-quality solutions is a major advantage. By fostering a more robust surrogate model without over-specialization, it can lead to more effective and safer iterative improvements, which is crucial for applications involving human feedback or expensive simulations.

5. **Robust Theoretical Foundation:** The theoretical analysis confirming asymptotic exactness and the benefits of tempering for sample efficiency provides strong confidence in DAS's reliability and paves the way for future theoretical explorations into optimal particle management and tempering schedules in complex generative processes.

**Potential Future Work (as identified by the authors):**

- **Extended Analysis of Tempering:** A deeper theoretical investigation into how DAS's tempering strategy connects with recent findings on non-uniform behavior and gradient contradictions in diffusion models could provide more fundamental insights into its effectiveness.
- **Extensions to Other Modalities:** The training-free and adaptable nature of DAS makes it highly suitable for generalization beyond image generation. Its application could be explored in:
 - **Protein Structure Design:** Optimizing properties like binding affinity or stability while maintaining structural diversity.
 - **Audio Generation:** Aligning generated speech or music with desired acoustic qualities or emotional tones.
 - **Video Generation:** Improving temporal consistency, visual quality, or adherence to specific motion patterns.

In conclusion, DAS represents a significant leap forward in making diffusion model alignment practical, efficient, and robust. By offering a solution that excels in reward optimization while meticulously preserving generalization and diversity, it substantially lowers the bar for customizability and unlocks new possibilities for these powerful generative AI systems across various domains.
