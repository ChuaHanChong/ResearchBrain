# Efficient Reinforcement Learning by Guiding Generalist World Models with Non-Curated Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Aalto University and collaborators introduced Generalist-to-Specialist Adaptation (GSA), an approach to enhance online reinforcement learning's sample efficiency by effectively leveraging abundant, non-curated offline data. GSA achieved a 102.8% relative improvement in aggregate scores over baselines across 72 visuomotor tasks, matching performance of methods requiring 3.3-6.7 times more online samples.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) is bottlenecked by sample inefficiency, requiring vast amounts of expensive, time-consuming, and often unsafe online interaction data.
- Existing offline-to-online RL methods and representation learning techniques struggle to effectively utilize readily available non-curated, reward-free, mixed-quality, multi-embodiment offline datasets.
- Naive fine-tuning of pre-trained world models often fails to accelerate online RL due to significant distributional shift between offline pre-training data and online fine-tuning data, leading to catastrophic forgetting and degraded model accuracy.

## Method

- The **Generalist-to-Specialist** Adaptation (GSA) framework employs a two-stage approach: ==multi-embodiment world model== pre-training followed by RL-based fine-tuning.
- A scaled recurrent state space model (RSSM) is pre-trained on diverse, reward-free, multi-embodiment offline data by optimizing pixel reconstruction and latent state consistency, without task-specific losses.
- During fine-tuning, GSA incorporates 'Experience Rehearsal' (selectively retrieving and replaying task-relevant offline trajectories) and 'Execution Guidance' (alternating between a behaviorally cloned prior policy and the online RL policy) to mitigate distributional shift and promote efficient exploration.

## Results

- GSA demonstrated a **102.8%** relative improvement in aggregate score compared to learning-from-scratch baselines (DrQ v2, Dreamer v3) across 72 visuomotor tasks (22 DMControl, 50 Meta-World) using only 150k online samples.
- The method matched or exceeded the performance of baselines requiring 3.3 to 6.7 times more online samples (500k-**1M**), indicating substantial gains in sample efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of world model pre-training, experience rehearsal, and execution guidance, with the full GSA consistently yielding the best performance and enabling learning on hard exploration tasks where baselines failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective utilization of non-curated offline data for efficient RL requires not only pre-training a powerful generalist world model but also specific fine-tuning strategies to bridge the distributional gap and maintain learned dynamics.
> - Experience rehearsal, by selectively replaying task-relevant offline data, is crucial for preventing catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained world model and augmenting the initial state distribution for robust policy rollouts.
> - Execution guidance, through a prior actor trained via behavioral cloning on retrieved offline data, accelerates exploration by steering the agent towards regions where the world model is more accurate and confident.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) frequently faces challenges related to sample efficiency, particularly in complex environments requiring extensive interaction. A promising direction to address this involves leveraging offline data. Prior research in offline-to-online RL has primarily concentrated on utilizing *curated* offline datasets, which are typically labeled with rewards and specific to a single task (e.g., methods by Levine et al., 2020; Kumar et al., 2020; Fujimoto & Gu, 2021). Obtaining such curated data is often expensive and labor-intensive, particularly for new tasks or robotic manipulation scenarios that require retrospective reward annotation.

This paper positions itself by expanding the scope of usable offline data to include *non-curated* datasets. These datasets are characterized by being reward-free, of mixed quality, and collected across multiple embodiments. This setting represents a more realistic and abundant data source, contrasting with the assumptions of structured and reward-labeled data made by many existing offline-to-online RL methods (Lee et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2022; Yu & Zhang, 2023).

While pre-training visual encoders from non-curated data (Schwarzer et al., 2021; Nair et al., 2022) is a common strategy, it is noted to not fully exploit rich information such as dynamics models, informative states, and action priors relevant for policy learning. Learning world models from offline data appears to be a more comprehensive approach. However, previous work on world model training has largely focused on scenarios with known rewards (Lu et al., 2023), expert demonstrations (Zhu et al., 2024a), or solely on visual prediction (Zheng et al., 2024). Recent advancements in world model pre-training using "in-the-wild" action-free data (Seo et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2024, 2025) have shown only marginal improvements over learning-from-scratch baselines, and their effectiveness across a broad range of tasks remained underexplored due to computational costs.

This work aims to bridge these gaps by specifically addressing the effective utilization of non-curated, multi-embodiment data for efficient RL, focusing on both the pre-training and, crucially, the fine-tuning stages of world models to overcome limitations observed in prior approaches, particularly the issue of distributional shift.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central research question driving this work is: "How can we effectively leverage non-curated offline data for efficient RL?" The motivation stems from the high cost and labor required to obtain curated, reward-labeled offline data, which limits the scalability and applicability of many existing offline-to-online RL methods. In contrast, non-curated data—characterized as reward-free, of mixed quality, and collected across multiple embodiments—is abundant and thus represents a potentially vast, untapped resource for improving RL sample efficiency.

The authors identified a critical challenge when attempting to use pre-trained world models with non-curated data: naive fine-tuning often fails to accelerate RL training on many tasks. Through investigation, this failure was attributed to a **distributional shift** between the offline data used for pre-training and the online data collected during RL fine-tuning. This mismatch can lead to several issues:
1. **Degradation of world model accuracy:** The model's predictive capabilities can worsen if the online data distribution significantly deviates from the offline pre-training distribution.
2. **Catastrophic forgetting:** For tasks requiring extensive exploration, the agent may struggle to reach high-reward regions, causing the world model to be fine-tuned on a narrow online data distribution, potentially overwriting previously learned representations.
3. **Limited policy updates:** A narrow distribution of initial states sampled from the online replay buffer restricts the world model's ability to generate diverse and promising imagined trajectories for policy learning.

To address these challenges and effectively utilize non-curated offline data across both pre-training and fine-tuning, the paper outlines four key contributions, which also serve as its primary objectives:
- **C1: Propose a realistic setting for leveraging offline data:** To utilize reward-free, mixed-quality multi-embodiment data, moving beyond the assumptions of structured and reward-labeled datasets.
- **C2: Demonstrate the limitations of naive world model fine-tuning:** To highlight that distributional shift between pre-training and fine-tuning data hinders performance.
- **C3: Introduce two techniques to mitigate distributional gaps and encourage exploration:** Experience rehearsal and execution guidance, designed to address the issues identified in C2.
- **C4: Present NCRL:** A comprehensive pipeline that leverages non-curated offline data in both pre-training and fine-tuning stages to outperform existing approaches across a diverse set of tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, Non-curated offline data for efficient RL (NCRL), operates in two main stages: (i) world model pre-training and (ii) RL-based fine-tuning, enhanced by experience rehearsal and execution guidance.

#### 3.1. Multi-Embodiment World Model Pre-Training

The initial stage focuses on learning a task-agnostic world model from the non-curated offline dataset ($D_{off}$). This dataset is characterized by being reward-free, of mixed quality, and containing data from multiple embodiments. Instead of training separate models for each task or embodiment, NCRL trains a single world model per benchmark.

- **World Model Architecture:** The method adopts the recurrent state space model (RSSM) architecture (Hafner et al., 2019), but with specific modifications for the non-curated, multi-embodiment setting:
 - Task-related losses are removed.
 - Actions are zero-padded to unify dimensions across different embodiments.
 - The model scale is increased to 280 million parameters.
- **Components Trained:** The pre-training stage optimizes the following components:
 - **Sequence model** ($f_\theta$): Predicts the next hidden state ($h_t$) given the previous hidden state, latent state, and action.
 - **Encoder** ($q_\theta$): Infers the latent state ($z_t$) from the current hidden state and observation.
 - **Dynamics predictor** ($p_\theta$): Predicts the next latent state ($\hat{z}_t$) from the current hidden state.
 - **Decoder** ($d_\theta$): Reconstructs the observation ($\hat{o}_t$) from the current hidden and latent states.
- **Optimization:** These models are jointly optimized by minimizing a combined loss function $L(\theta)$ (Eq. 1), which includes:
 - $L_{pred}(\theta)$: Reconstruction error, minimizing negative log-likelihood of observations.
 - $L_{dyn}(\theta)$: Encourages the sequence model and dynamics predictor to accurately forecast future latent states by minimizing a KL divergence.
 - $L_{rep}(\theta)$: Regularizes the representation to be more predictable, also via a KL divergence.
 The stop-gradient operator is applied in the KL divergence terms to ensure stable training.

#### 3.2. RL-Based Fine-Tuning with Rehearsal and Guidance

In the fine-tuning stage, the agent interacts with the environment to collect new online data ($D_{on}$). This online data is used to learn a reward function ($r_\xi$) and further fine-tune the pre-trained world model using the same loss function as in pre-training. An actor-critic setup, similar to DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023), is employed, where the actor and critic are trained on imagined trajectories generated by rolling out the policy within the world model.

- **Problem of Naive Fine-Tuning:** The paper identifies that simply pre-training a world model and then fine-tuning it with online RL data is often insufficient, especially for hard exploration tasks. This is primarily due to the aforementioned distributional shift between the broad offline data and the often narrow online data collected in early RL training. This shift can lead to degraded world model accuracy, catastrophic forgetting of pre-training knowledge, and limited diversity in imagined trajectories for policy improvement.
- **Proposed Solutions:** To mitigate these issues, NCRL introduces two key techniques that reuse the non-curated offline data during the fine-tuning stage:
 - **i) Experience Rehearsal:**
 - **Challenge:** Directly replaying the entire large, non-curated offline dataset is impractical due to its size and heterogeneity.
 - **Solution:** The method retrieves a smaller subset of *task-relevant* trajectories ($D_{retrieved}$) from the non-curated offline data. This retrieval is based on the neural feature distance (Eq. 5) between initial observations from the online buffer and those in the offline dataset, using the encoder learned during world model pre-training. Faiss is used for efficient similarity search.
 - **Purpose:** The retrieved data serves three main functions:
 1. **Prevents catastrophic forgetting:** By continually training the world model on relevant pre-training data, it acts as a regularizer, stabilizing fine-tuning and preventing the model from losing previously acquired knowledge.
 2. **Augments initial state distribution:** It enriches the set of initial states ($p_0(s)$) from which the world model generates imagined trajectories, leading to more diverse and potentially more promising rollouts for policy learning.
 3. **Enables policy prior learning:** It provides data for training a prior policy used in execution guidance.
 - **ii) Execution Guidance via Prior Actors:**
 - **Challenge:** Standard RL training often starts with random exploration, which is inefficient, especially for hard exploration tasks. Distributional shift can also make the pre-trained world model less effective early in fine-tuning.
 - **Solution:** A prior policy ($\pi_{bc}$) is trained using behavioral cloning (BC) on the retrieved offline data ($D_{retrieved}$). During online data collection, the agent alternates between this prior policy ($\pi_{bc}$) and the currently learning RL policy ($\pi_\phi$) based on a predefined schedule. This involves probabilistically selecting to use $\pi_{bc}$ at the start of an episode, and if chosen, randomly selecting a starting timestep ($t_{bc}$) and duration ($H$) for $\pi_{bc}$'s activation.
 - **Purpose:** This guidance encourages exploration towards regions where the world model has higher confidence and where valuable behaviors from the offline data exist, thereby mitigating the impact of distributional shift and accelerating learning. This differs from prior methods like JSRL by leveraging non-curated data and allowing mid-episode policy switching.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper evaluates NCRL across a diverse set of 72 visuomotor control tasks, including both locomotion (DMControl) and robotic manipulation (Meta-World) tasks, using pixel-based observations. The offline dataset comprised 60k trajectories (10M state-action pairs) across 6 embodiments, collected by unsupervised RL agents (DMControl) and TDMPC-v2 agents with injected noise (Meta-World), reflecting mixed quality and non-expert behaviors.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Against Offline Data Leveraging Methods:** NCRL was compared against R3M (visual representation pre-training), UDS-RLPD (zero-reward labeling), ExPLORe (UCB reward labeling), and JSRL-BC (behavior cloning prior). Despite these baselines sometimes using pre-processed or better-structured data, NCRL consistently outperformed all of them. For instance, it showed the importance of world model pre-training and reuse during fine-tuning over representation learning alone (R3M). It also demonstrated superior performance compared to reward labeling strategies (UDS-RLPD, ExPLORe) and behavior cloning from non-expert data (JSRL-BC).
- **Against Training-from-Scratch Baselines:** Under a limited sample budget of 150k online samples, NCRL achieved nearly double the aggregate score of learning-from-scratch baselines (DrQ-v2 and DreamerV3). Furthermore, NCRL matched the performance of these baselines when they were given significantly larger sample budgets (3.3-6.7x more samples: 500k for DMControl, 1M for Meta-World). NCRL also demonstrated promising performance on hard exploration tasks where baselines failed.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Other Model-Based Approaches

- The paper compared NCRL with pre-training approaches like iVideoGPT and pre-trained DreamerV3, which typically focus on novel architectures for leveraging in-the-wild data. Despite these baselines' extensive pre-training and use of techniques like reward shaping and expert replay buffer pre-filling, NCRL achieved superior performance without employing such tricks. This indicates the effectiveness of NCRL's strategy for leveraging non-curated data during both pre-training and fine-tuning. An aligned comparison (iVideoGPT-align), removing reward shaping and demonstration pre-filling, also showed NCRL's continued outperformance.

#### 4.3. Fast Task Adaptation

- In a continual task adaptation setting (sequentially learning 6 tasks for a Quadruped robot), NCRL significantly outperformed PackNet, a baseline for preserving previous skills. NCRL enabled adaptation within 100 trials per task, while PackNet achieved only 20-60% of NCRL's episodic returns with limited samples. This highlights NCRL's ability to leverage diverse offline data for efficient skill adaptation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Role of Each Component:** World model pre-training alone showed benefits primarily when the offline data was diverse (e.g., exploratory agents). However, it struggled with narrower offline data distributions. The combination of experience rehearsal and execution guidance was found to stabilize training and substantially improve performance on hard exploration and manipulation tasks. This validates their hypothesis that these techniques mitigate distributional shift and aid exploration.
- **Execution Guidance vs. Uncertainty-Aware Rewards:** NCRL's execution guidance mechanism demonstrated greater effectiveness than an uncertainty-based reward labeling approach (Optimistic Thompson Sampling) on challenging robotic manipulation tasks (Assembly and Stick Pull).
- **Fine-tuning World Model Components:** Fine-tuning the full world model (encoder, decoder, and latent dynamics) yielded the best performance, indicating the importance of updating all components during fine-tuning.
- **Robustness to Retrieved Data Quality:** Experiments showed that NCRL maintained robustness even when a significant percentage (up to 75%) of retrieved task-relevant trajectories were replaced with task-irrelevant data, suggesting its resilience to imperfections in the retrieval process.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The method demonstrated robustness to the annealing schedule used for execution guidance, simplifying hyperparameter tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning by addressing the practical challenge of leveraging abundant, yet unstructured, offline data. The proposed NCRL framework demonstrates a viable path toward improving the sample efficiency of online RL by effectively utilizing non-curated data that is reward-free, of mixed quality, and collected across multiple embodiments.

The **significance** of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Realistic Data Setting:** It moves beyond the often-idealized assumption of curated, reward-labeled data, making RL applicable to real-world scenarios where data is typically messy and unannotated.
- **Addressing Distributional Shift:** The identification of distributional shift as a key failure mode during the fine-tuning of pre-trained world models is a critical insight. The development of experience rehearsal and execution guidance as specific techniques to mitigate this issue is a substantial methodological advancement.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results consistently show NCRL achieving superior sample efficiency, enabling performance comparable to baselines with significantly less online interaction. This translates to faster learning and reduced computational costs.
- **Broad Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across a wide range of 72 visuomotor tasks, including both locomotion and complex manipulation, highlights its generality and robustness.
- **Improved Task Adaptation:** The demonstrated capability for fast task adaptation in continual learning settings suggests potential for more versatile and lifelong learning agents.

The **potential impact** of NCRL is considerable:
- **Reduced Data Acquisition Costs:** By enabling the use of readily available non-curated data, the reliance on expensive and laborious reward labeling or expert demonstrations can be substantially reduced. This lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying RL systems in various domains.
- **Accelerated RL Development and Deployment:** Increased sample efficiency and faster learning can significantly accelerate the research cycle and the transition of RL agents from simulation to real-world applications, particularly in robotics.
- **Foundation for Generalist Agents:** The multi-embodiment world model pre-training, combined with effective fine-tuning mechanisms, contributes to the development of more general and adaptable RL policies that can leverage diverse experiences across different robotic platforms or task variations.
- **Broader Utilization of "In-the-Wild" Data:** The principles established by NCRL could be extended to leverage even larger volumes of truly "in-the-wild" data (e.g., general internet videos), potentially feeding into the creation of more capable world models and general AI systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for further exploration into novel world model architectures that are more scalable and robust to diverse data, advanced retrieval strategies, and real-world deployments to validate the method's practical utility beyond simulated environments.
