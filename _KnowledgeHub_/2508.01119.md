# The Promise of RL for Autoregressive Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mila and affiliated institutions developed EARL, an autoregressive image editing model that applies reinforcement learning (RL) post-training to enhance text-guided image editing. EARL achieved an average VIEScore of 4.80, outperforming prior state-of-the-art diffusion models like Omnigen while using five times less training data and demonstrating 4x faster inference speed.

## Problem

- Existing text-guided image editing models often struggle with complex edits requiring action understanding, spatial reasoning, or physical dynamics, typically relying on diffusion architectures or explicit bounding box conditioning.
- Autoregressive multimodal models, despite their capabilities in joint image and language generation, have been underexplored for complex image editing tasks, with post-training methods like reinforcement learning largely uninvestigated.
- Current supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches for image editing models often face degradation when mixing diverse edit types, limiting their ability to handle a full spectrum of editing complexity effectively.

## Method

- Utilized Emu3-8B, an ==autoregressive multimodal== model, as a consistent base architecture to generate interleaved text and visual tokens for end-to-end image editing.
- Employed Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for reinforcement learning (RL) post-training, using a Qwen2.5-VL-72B model as a reward verifier to evaluate generated edits based on criteria like edit success and natural look.
- Systematically investigated Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), RL, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning paradigms, testing different data integration strategies (simple vs. complex edits) during training.

## Results

- The proposed EARL model, based on SFT(S) → RL(S+C), achieved an average VIEScore of 4.80, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art diffusion model, Omnigen (4.70), using five times less training data.
- EARL demonstrated superior performance on OmniEdit, AURORA, and VisMin benchmarks, along with strong performance on out-of-distribution benchmarks like I2EBench and EmuEdit.
- RL-tuned models showed only a small gap between single-sample and best-of-5 sampling (VIEScore 4.57 vs 4.83), indicating inherently high quality of generated outputs, and exhibited approximately **4x** faster inference speed compared to Omnigen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training consistently improves autoregressive image editing performance, particularly enhancing 'Edit Success' without sacrificing other quality aspects, and is more effective than SFT for incorporating complex editing data.
> - Mixing simple and complex edit data during SFT can degrade overall model performance, whereas introducing complex data during RL post-training is beneficial.
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning supervision did not improve visual editing performance in this autoregressive framework, suggesting that effective CoT for visual tasks might require a higher base model capability or specific pretraining.
