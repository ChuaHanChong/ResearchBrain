# The Promise of RL for Autoregressive Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mila and affiliated institutions developed EARL, an autoregressive image editing model that applies reinforcement learning (RL) post-training to enhance text-guided image editing. EARL achieved an average VIEScore of 4.80, outperforming prior state-of-the-art diffusion models like Omnigen while using five times less training data and demonstrating 4x faster inference speed.

## Problem

- Existing text-guided image editing models often struggle with complex edits requiring action understanding, spatial reasoning, or physical dynamics, typically relying on diffusion architectures or explicit bounding box conditioning.
- Autoregressive multimodal models, despite their capabilities in joint image and language generation, have been underexplored for complex image editing tasks, with post-training methods like reinforcement learning largely uninvestigated.
- Current supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches for image editing models often face degradation when mixing diverse edit types, limiting their ability to handle a full spectrum of editing complexity effectively.

## Method

- Utilized Emu3-8B, an ==autoregressive multimodal== model, as a consistent base architecture to generate interleaved text and visual tokens for end-to-end image editing.
- Employed Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for reinforcement learning (RL) post-training, using a Qwen2.5-VL-72B model as a reward verifier to evaluate generated edits based on criteria like edit success and natural look.
- Systematically investigated Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), RL, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning paradigms, testing different data integration strategies (simple vs. complex edits) during training.

## Results

- The proposed EARL model, based on SFT(S) → RL(S+C), achieved an average VIEScore of 4.80, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art diffusion model, Omnigen (4.70), using five times less training data.
- EARL demonstrated superior performance on OmniEdit, AURORA, and VisMin benchmarks, along with strong performance on out-of-distribution benchmarks like I2EBench and EmuEdit.
- RL-tuned models showed only a small gap between single-sample and best-of-5 sampling (VIEScore 4.57 vs 4.83), indicating inherently high quality of generated outputs, and exhibited approximately **4x** faster inference speed compared to Omnigen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training consistently improves autoregressive image editing performance, particularly enhancing 'Edit Success' without sacrificing other quality aspects, and is more effective than SFT for incorporating complex editing data.
> - Mixing simple and complex edit data during SFT can degrade overall model performance, whereas introducing complex data during RL post-training is beneficial.
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning supervision did not improve visual editing performance in this autoregressive framework, suggesting that effective CoT for visual tasks might require a higher base model capability or specific pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image generation has made substantial progress, with models capable of producing high-quality images from detailed textual prompts. However, the more specific task of text-guided image editing, which requires modifying existing images while preserving contextual details, presents ongoing challenges. Current editing models, predominantly based on diffusion architectures, often focus on simpler object and attribute modifications and frequently rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of pre-trained image generation models. Some approaches also incorporate explicit bounding-box conditioning, which limits their end-to-end applicability. More complex edits, such as those requiring action understanding, spatial reasoning, or physical dynamics, remain difficult for existing systems.

A parallel and emerging line of work involves unified multimodal models that autoregressively process textual and visual tokens. While these autoregressive models have demonstrated capabilities in joint image and language generation, their application to complex image editing tasks has been less explored compared to diffusion models. Existing autoregressive editing models have primarily focused on SFT, leaving post-training methods like reinforcement learning (RL) underexplored for this domain. This research aims to address these gaps by systematically investigating SFT, RL, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning within a consistent autoregressive framework to enhance performance across a wide range of image editing tasks, including both simple and complex types. It positions itself as a development in pushing the capabilities of autoregressive multimodal models for image editing, building on the foundation of models like Emu3.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to:

- **Identify effective approaches for unified end-to-end image editing:** The authors sought to determine the most effective strategies to address both simple and complex image edits within a single, consistent model, without requiring explicit user-provided conditioning like bounding boxes.
- **Investigate key learning paradigms:** A central motivation was to systematically compare the impact of three distinct learning paradigms—supervised fine-tuning (SFT), reinforcement learning (RL), and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning—on the performance of an autoregressive multimodal model for image editing. This exploration aimed to understand which of these paradigms, or combinations thereof, could advance the field.
- **Explore the potential of autoregressive multimodal models for image editing:** Recognizing that autoregressive models are less explored for image editing compared to diffusion models, a specific objective was to assess their competitiveness and potential for this task, particularly when combined with post-training techniques like RL.
- **Determine optimal data training strategies:** The study aimed to analyze how different types of editing data (simple vs. complex) should be incorporated across various training stages (SFT, RL post-training) to maximize model performance and generalization.
- **Develop a strong RL-based image editing model:** A practical objective was to create a novel autoregressive image editing model, named EARL, that demonstrates competitive or superior performance against existing baselines on a diverse set of editing tasks, potentially with reduced data requirements.
- **Analyze the role of Chain-of-Thought reasoning:** The authors aimed to understand whether explicitly teaching the model to reason about intermediate steps, in a CoT style, could improve image editing performance, particularly for complex reasoning-intensive edits.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the observed limitations of current image editing techniques. While image generation has advanced, accurate and diverse text-guided image editing remains challenging. Existing models often struggle with complex edits, rely on fragmented approaches, or require explicit conditioning inputs. The authors hypothesize that a unified autoregressive framework, combined with advanced post-training methods like RL and systematic data integration, could overcome these limitations, enabling more robust, end-to-end image editing capabilities. The goal is to move beyond models that only cover simpler edits towards a model that can handle a full spectrum of editing complexity, offering practical utility in various applications from direct user interaction to robotic simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a unified autoregressive multimodal model, Emu3, as its base architecture to ensure a consistent framework for studying different training paradigms.

**A. Base Model:**
The foundation of the approach is **Emu3-8B**, an autoregressive multimodal model. Emu3 extends the LLaMA-2 architecture by integrating a vision tokenizer (SBER-MoVQGAN) to encode images into discrete tokens and a QwenTokenizer for text. Unlike diffusion models, Emu3 generates both text and visual tokens in a unified autoregressive stream, enabling interleaved image-text generation. The model is pre-trained exclusively on text and image data, formatted with special tokens to distinguish modalities.

**B. Training Paradigms:**
Three learning paradigms were systematically explored:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** The model is fine-tuned using a standard next-token prediction objective, minimizing the cross-entropy loss between generated and ground-truth tokens.
 - **Data:** Training triplets consist of a source image, an edit instruction, and a ground-truth edited image. These are tokenized into interleaved vision and language token sequences.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Post-Training:**
 - **Method:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed to post-train the SFT-initialized model. GRPO trains a policy model ($\pi_\theta$) using a frozen reference model from the SFT checkpoint.
 - **Process:** For a given input, the policy generates a group of responses, and a reward is computed for each. The objective is to maximize a GRPO objective function that incorporates an advantage term based on rewards and a Kullback–Leibler (KL) penalty to maintain stability and prevent policy divergence from the reference model.
 - **RL Verifier:** A large multimodal language model (MLLM), **Qwen2.5-VL-72B**, serves as the reward function (verifier). It evaluates the quality of generated edits based on four criteria from VIEScore:
 - **Edit Success:** Accuracy of the intended modification.
 - **Overedit:** Presence of unintended changes.
 - **Natural Look:** How well the edit blends with the original image.
 - **Artifacts:** Presence of visual distortions.
 - **Reward Aggregation:** Individual scores for these criteria are aggregated into a single reward signal ranging from 0 to 10.

3. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:**
 - **Approach:** This method involves finetuning Emu3 to explicitly generate intermediate reasoning steps before producing the final edited image.
 - **Supervision:** CoT supervision is implemented by prepending the ground-truth edited image token sequence with a tokenized reasoning chain.
 - **CoT Data Synthesis:** Ground-truth reasoning traces are synthesized using the MLLM **Qwen2.5-VL-72B**. This involves providing the MLLM with standard editing data (input image, edited image, instruction, bounding boxes, or keypoints for action edits) and a specific prompting strategy to generate step-by-step reasoning.

**C. Training Details:**
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay 0.1.
- **Learning Rates:** 1e-4 for SFT, 3e-6 for RL.
- **Batch Sizes:** Effective batch size of 128 for SFT (per-device 4, 8 gradient accumulation steps), 128 for RL (8 rollouts per instruction).
- **Hardware:** 4 GPUs for SFT and RL training. VIEScore reward computation used a separate vLLM API server on 4 NVIDIA H100 GPUs.
- **Image Resolution:** All images resized to 256x256.

**D. Training Datasets:**
Datasets were categorized by complexity:
- **Simple Edits (S):** Primarily **OmniEdit** (750K samples), covering object/attribute changes, scene, and style modifications.
- **Complex Edits (C):** Combines **Aurora-AG**, **Aurora-Kubric**, **VisMin**, **Something-Something v2** (total 171K samples, upsampled to 300K for SFT), along with human-curated **Human-Edit** and **MagicBrush**. These datasets contain edits involving counting, spatial relations, and actions.
- **CoT Data Generation:** For complex datasets, few-shot prompting was used with Qwen2.5-VL-72B. For simple edits, existing CoTs were reused.

**E. Evaluation Setup:**
- **Metric:** **VIEScore**, which assesses edit quality from 0-10 across four criteria (Edit Success, Overedit, Natural Look, Artifacts), was used as the primary metric. **GPT4o-mini** served as the MLLM for evaluation to prevent metric hacking from the RL verifier.
- **Benchmarks:** Six diverse benchmarks were used:
 - Simple: **OmniEdit**, **EmuEdit** (OOD)
 - Complex: **MagicBrush**, **Aurora**, **I2EBench** (OOD), and **VisMin** (repurposed from an understanding benchmark).
- **Baselines:** Diffusion-based models (MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix, Aurora, Omnigen) and the autoregressive EditAR.
- **Additional Metrics:** CLIP similarity and FID were also reported for a broader comparison of semantic consistency and image realism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a systematic analysis of SFT, RL, and CoT reasoning for autoregressive image editing.

**A. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Performance:**
- **SFT (S):** When trained solely on simple edits, the SFT model achieved an average VIEScore of 3.88, outperforming MagicBrush and InstructPix2Pix but lagging behind Aurora, EditAR, and Omnigen. Omnigen, benefiting from a stronger base model and significantly more training data (∼4M vs. 750K samples), demonstrated higher overall performance (4.70). All SFT models showed a decline in performance on complex editing benchmarks.
- **SFT (S+C) & SFT (S+C) two-stage:** Joint training on combined simple and complex data (SFT (S+C)) degraded average performance to 3.32, with a notable drop on simple edit benchmarks. A two-stage curriculum (SFT (S+C) two-stage), where the model first fine-tuned on simple edits and then on complex edits, partially recovered performance to 3.69 and showed improvements on some complex benchmarks. This suggested that mixing diverse edit types early in SFT could hinder generalization.

**B. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Post-training Performance:**
- **General Improvement:** RL post-training consistently improved image editing performance compared to SFT baselines. All RL variants starting from SFT (S) outperformed the SFT baseline of 3.88.
- **Impact of Complex Data in RL:** RL (C), trained on complex data, outperformed RL (S) across complex benchmarks without significant degradation on simple benchmarks, indicating that complex data is beneficial during RL post-training, unlike in SFT.
- **Optimal RL Strategy:** The combination of simple and complex data during RL (RL (S+C)) yielded the largest gains. The best-performing setup, **SFT(S) → RL(S+C)**, achieved an average VIEScore of 4.57, surpassing MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix, Aurora, and EditAR, and becoming competitive with Omnigen.
- **Scaling RL (EARL):** Scaling the SFT(S) → RL(S+C) setup by increasing training duration (2000 steps) and data pool size (300K S+C samples) led to **EARL**, which achieved an average VIEScore of **4.80**. This surpassed Omnigen (4.70), demonstrating that EARL can outperform the strongest prior state-of-the-art diffusion models despite using five times less training data and a weaker base model. EARL achieved the best results on OmniEdit, AURORA, and VisMin benchmarks and strong performance on OOD benchmarks like I2EBench and EmuEdit.
- **Stability and Efficiency:** RL training showed stable reward improvements and increasing VIEScore on benchmarks, indicating effective learning dynamics. EARL also exhibited favorable inference speed, running approximately 4x faster than Omnigen while maintaining quality.
- **Best-of-N Evaluation:** While best-of-5 sampling improved SFT results significantly (3.88 -> 4.33), the RL-tuned model still outperformed SFT even with best-of-5 sampling (4.33 -> 4.83), demonstrating that policy optimization provides gains beyond sampling diversity. The small gap between single-sample and best-of-5 for RL (4.57 -> 4.83) suggests high inherent quality of RL outputs.

**C. Studying Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning for Editing:**
- **Limited Efficacy:** Contrary to expectations based on LLM success, CoT reasoning supervision did not improve visual editing performance in the autoregressive framework. SFT think (S) (3.50 avg.) performed worse than SFT (S) (3.88 avg.).
- **Negative Impact of Complex CoT Data:** Adding complex reasoning data (SFT think (S+C) two-stage) further degraded performance (1.52 avg.).
- **RL on CoT Models:** Applying RL on top of SFT think (S) yielded only marginal improvements (to 3.68 avg.), which remained below the non-CoT SFT baseline.
- **Hypotheses for CoT Limitations:** The authors hypothesize that the base model (Emu3) may not have surpassed a sufficient capability threshold for CoT reasoning to be effective, and its lack of pretraining on interleaved image-text-image data may hinder its ability to integrate reasoning effectively for image enhancement, despite generating plausible reasoning chains.

**D. Detailed Metric Breakdown (VIEScore Components, CLIP, FID):**
- **VIEScore Components:** RL significantly improved "Edit Success" (3.7 to 4.6 avg.) while maintaining comparable performance on "OverEdit," "Looks Natural," and "No Artifact." This suggests RL aligns edits more closely with instructions without sacrificing perceptual quality.
- **CLIP Similarity and FID:** EARL achieved competitive CLIP similarity (0.88) and FID (49.64) scores, comparable to strong baselines like Omnigen (CLIP 0.90, FID 45.22) and EditAR (CLIP 0.88, FID 49.47). This indicates that EARL maintains semantic alignment and image realism.

**E. Verifier Analysis:**
- **Limitations for Complex Edits:** The Qwen2.5-VL-72B verifier, while effective for simple edits, exhibited limitations and inconsistencies in verifying complex edits, especially those involving large counting changes or fine-grained spatial relationships.
- **Importance of Strong Verifier:** Replacing the 72B verifier with a smaller 7B MLLM resulted in a significant performance drop (from 3.88 to 2.81 avg.), underscoring that a robust and accurate MLLM is crucial for effective RL in this domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces EARL, a novel autoregressive image editing model that demonstrates competitive performance across a spectrum of editing tasks, including both simple and complex types. The work's significance lies in its systematic exploration of different training paradigms within a unified autoregressive framework, revealing several key insights:

- **Pioneering RL for Autoregressive Image Editing:** EARL represents the first application of RL post-training to autoregressive models for image editing. The findings establish that RL, particularly when combined with a strong MLLM verifier, is a highly effective strategy for enhancing edit success and handling complex reasoning-intensive tasks, outperforming models that rely solely on SFT.
- **Advancing Autoregressive Multimodal Models:** EARL pushes the frontier of autoregressive multimodal models for image editing by demonstrating that they can achieve state-of-the-art performance, even surpassing strong diffusion-based baselines like Omnigen, while using significantly less training data. This suggests that autoregressive models, previously less explored for this task, offer a viable and potentially more efficient alternative.
- **Optimized Data Integration Strategy:** The research provides evidence that the optimal strategy for integrating complex edit data differs across training stages. While incorporating complex edits during SFT can be detrimental to overall performance, their introduction during RL post-training is beneficial, leading to improved capabilities without compromising performance on simpler edits.
- **Insights into Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** The unexpected finding that CoT reasoning did not consistently improve performance, and sometimes even degraded it, provides important feedback for future research. It suggests that merely generating plausible reasoning chains may not be sufficient without a base model that is adequately pre-trained for deep multimodal integration and reasoning, highlighting an area requiring further investigation.
- **Practical Efficiency:** EARL's competitive performance coupled with its inference speed (approximately 4x faster than Omnigen) highlights its potential for practical applications where efficiency is a consideration.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Positive Societal Impacts:** The development of more capable and accessible text-guided image editing systems could enhance human creativity by lowering barriers for users without specialized editing skills. It could also find applications in fields like robotics and planning, where image editing models could act as simulators for "imagining into the future." The RL pipeline offers a method to overcome the reliance on large ground-truth edited image datasets, potentially expanding the scope of solvable editing tasks.
- **Potential Negative Impacts:** As with other high-fidelity image generation and editing technologies, there is a risk of misuse, such as creating convincing misinformation or deepfakes. Furthermore, if the underlying base model or the vision-language verifier used for rewards contains inherent biases from their pre-training data, the RL process could inadvertently amplify these biases. The authors emphasize that EARL is developed for research purposes and is not intended for real-world deployment, advocating for ongoing efforts in safeguards and ethical use.
