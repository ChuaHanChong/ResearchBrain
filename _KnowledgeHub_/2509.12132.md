# Look Again, Think Slowly: Enhancing Visual Reflection in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage training strategy, Reflection-V, enhances visual reflection in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by first generating vision-centered reasoning data and then applying reinforcement learning with a visual attention-based reward. This approach significantly improves reasoning performance across various benchmarks, reduces visual hallucinations, and enables 7B-scale models to achieve performance competitive with much larger proprietary and open-source models.

## Problem

- While large language models (LLMs) excel at iterative 'slow-thinking' reasoning, transferring this capability to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often results in a superficial understanding that relies on image descriptions rather than direct visual interaction.
- Existing Vision Reasoning Models (VRMs) exhibit 'textual hallucinations and visual neglect,' with their attention to visual information rapidly diminishing as reasoning chains lengthen.
- Traditional reinforcement learning techniques, when applied to VRMs, can inadvertently reinforce an over-reliance on previously generated text, further detaching models from actual visual content.

## Method

- A two-stage training strategy, ==Reflection-V==, is proposed to embed and reinforce visual reflection capabilities in VRMs.
- The first stage involves a =='cold-start' initialization== using specially constructed reasoning data with visual reflection, generated via orchestrated LLM-VLM interaction to ensure continuous visual access and re-checking.
- The second stage fine-tunes the VRM with Reinforcement Learning (using GRPO), incorporating a novel ==visual attention-based reward== that specifically encourages sustained attention to visual tokens throughout the generated response, provided the answer is correct.

## Results

- Reflection-V-7B achieved superior performance across mathematical, multi-disciplinary, and general reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVision, MathVista, MMMU), outperforming state-of-the-art open-source vision-language reasoning models.
- The model demonstrated competitive or superior performance compared to significantly larger proprietary models like GPT-4o and GPT-4V, as well as large open-source models such as InternVL2.5-38B and LLaVA-OneVision-72B on various tasks.
- Reflection-V significantly reduced visual hallucinations on the HallBench dataset and maintained consistently higher visual attention and dependency throughout reasoning processes, exhibiting explicit **'Aha moments'** of visual re-engagement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - True 'slow-thinking' in visual contexts requires active visual reflection, where models critically re-evaluate and refine reasoning steps based on the visual input itself, not just textual descriptions.
> - Current VRMs suffer from a quantifiable deficiency in sustained visual attention and dependency, which can even be exacerbated by traditional RL methods that do not specifically incentivize visual grounding.
> - Explicitly designing training data to embed visual reflection patterns and developing a targeted visual attention-based reward function are crucial for mitigating visual neglect and hallucinations, leading to more robust multimodal reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of artificial intelligence is marked by significant advancements in "slow-thinking" reasoning capabilities within large language models (LLMs). Models like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have demonstrated remarkable abilities to solve complex tasks by performing iterative reflection and self-correction during their reasoning processes. This success has naturally spurred efforts to transfer these advanced reasoning paradigms to Vision-Language Models (VLMs), aiming to create more capable Visual Reasoning Models (VRMs).

However, this paper identifies a critical limitation in the existing approaches to training "slow-thinking" VRMs. Many contemporary methods, often distillation-based, primarily transfer superficial reflective behaviors from LLMs that were trained exclusively on text. These VRMs typically leverage LLMs to reason over *image descriptions* rather than directly interacting with the visual input throughout the reasoning chain. This detachment from the visual modality leads to a phenomenon the authors term "textual hallucinations and visual neglect," where models may appear to reason but are, in fact, bypassing the actual visual content.

The paper argues that a true "aha moment" in visual reasoning—the point at which a model critically re-evaluates and refines its understanding—must involve *visual reflection*. This means actively verifying and refining reasoning steps based on the visual input itself, not just textual representations of it. The authors provide quantitative evidence that current VRMs exhibit limited visual reflection, as their attention to visual information rapidly diminishes with longer generated responses. Even reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, while improving performance, have been observed to further reinforce an over-reliance on previously generated text, often at the expense of visual grounding.

This research directly addresses this critical gap. It positions itself as a pioneering effort to explicitly embed *visual reflection* capabilities into VRMs. Unlike concurrent works that attempt to alleviate "visual forgetting" by simple mechanisms such as mutual information decoding (e.g., M3ID, which might reduce linguistic priors) or periodic visual token replay during inference (e.g., TVC, which lacks flexible invocation), this paper proposes a systemic approach that fosters an *active, emergent* visual reflection behavior. By focusing on constructing reasoning data that inherently encourages visual interaction and designing RL rewards that incentivize sustained visual attention, the work aims to create VRMs that genuinely "look again and think slowly" about visual information when needed, thereby advancing the field towards more robust and visually grounded multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: while text-only LLMs have achieved impressive "slow-thinking" reasoning capabilities, their transfer to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is fundamentally flawed without true visual grounding. Current Vision Reasoning Models (VRMs) often simulate reasoning based on textual descriptions of images, leading to a superficial form of reflection that is detached from the visual modality. The authors quantitatively validate this problem, demonstrating that existing VRMs exhibit a rapid decline in attention to and reliance on visual information as their generated responses grow longer. This "visual neglect" not only limits their reasoning accuracy but also makes them prone to "textual hallucinations."

Therefore, the **key objective** of this paper is to significantly enhance the visual reflection capabilities of Vision-Language Models, thereby enabling them to perform more accurate, deliberate, and visually grounded reasoning.

More specifically, the authors set out to achieve the following goals:

- **Identify and quantify the deficiency:** Empirically demonstrate that existing VRMs struggle with sustained visual attention and reflection during long-chain reasoning processes.
- **Develop a novel data construction paradigm:** Create a method for generating vision-centered reasoning data that explicitly incorporates visual reflection patterns, allowing VRMs to learn how to check and refine their reasoning against visual inputs from a "cold-start."
- **Design an innovative reward mechanism:** Introduce a visual attention-based reward function within a reinforcement learning framework to actively encourage VRMs to maintain consistent attention to visual information throughout the reasoning process.
- **Achieve superior reasoning performance:** Demonstrate that the proposed training strategy leads to significant improvements across diverse visual reasoning benchmarks (mathematical, multi-disciplinary, and general reasoning).
- **Reduce visual hallucinations:** Show that by fostering sustained visual attention, the proposed approach can mitigate the common problem of visual hallucinations in VRMs.
- **Provide evidence of true visual reflection:** Validate that the improved performance is indeed a result of the model's enhanced ability to actively engage in visual reflection, evidenced by sustained visual attention, dependency, and explicit re-checking behaviors.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to bridge the critical gap between text-only "slow thinking" and truly multimodal reasoning, laying a foundation for more reliable and robust AI systems capable of deep visual comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel two-stage training strategy for Visual Reasoning Models (VRMs) named **Reflection-V**, designed to embed and reinforce visual reflection capabilities.

**Stage 1: Cold-Start Initialization with Reasoning Data with Visual Reflection Construction**

The first stage focuses on creating high-quality, vision-centered reasoning data to provide a strong "cold-start" initialization. Unlike previous approaches that generate reasoning data based on static image captions, this method ensures continuous interaction with visual information.

1. **LLM-VLM Interaction for Data Generation:**
 - A multi-modal agent orchestrates an interactive reasoning process between a Large Language Model (LLM) (QWQ-32B) and a VLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B).
 - **Visual Requester (LLM):** Based on the current reasoning context and question, the LLM determines what specific visual information is needed. It then formulates a precise visual question for the VLM, adding its own Chain-of-Thought (CoT) as a partial solution to the reasoning context.
 - **Visual Responder (VLM):** The VLM receives the visual question from the requester and provides a concise visual description related to the query. This visual description is then added to the reasoning context.
 - **Summarizer (LLM):** After each interaction round, the LLM summarizes the updated reasoning context and generates a final answer. If the answer is incorrect, a new interaction round is initiated; otherwise, the process for that question is complete, and the reasoning trace is saved. This iterative process ensures that visual information is continuously accessed and repeatedly utilized, naturally embedding a visual reflection pattern.

2. **Post-generation Processing:**
 - **Non-Reflection Filtering:** To ensure the generated data truly reflects sustained visual engagement, samples where the summarizer correctly answers after only the first interaction (indicating a lack of deeper visual reflection) are filtered out.
 - **Cohesion Enhancement:** The generated text, potentially disjointed due to the multi-round interaction, is refined by an LLM to improve coherence. This step introduces connecting phrases like "Let's double-check," "Let's check the image again," and "Wait," which are crucial markers for visual reflection during inference.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Visual Attention Based Reward**

Following the cold-start, the VRM is further fine-tuned using Reinforcement Learning to promote the visual reflection behavior learned from the constructed data.

1. **GRPO Algorithm:** The authors employ Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a rule-based RL algorithm, as their optimization framework.
2. **Visual Attention Based Reward (`r_v`):** A novel reward component is introduced to specifically encourage sustained visual attention.
 - **Principle:** VRMs receive a higher reward if they maintain relatively higher attention to visual tokens in the latter half of their generated response compared to the first half.
 - **Calculation:** `r_v` is calculated as the ratio of average attention weights to visual tokens in the second half of the response (`n > |T_res|/2`) to that in the first half (`n < |T_res|/2`), *only if the accuracy reward (`r_a`) is 1*. If `r_a` is 0, `r_v` is 0. This ensures that sustained visual attention is rewarded only when it leads to a correct answer. The attention weights are averaged over all attention heads in the last transformer layer.
 - **Overall Reward (`r_o`):** The total reward for GRPO is a weighted sum: `r_o = r_a + λ_v * r_v + λ_f * r_f`, where `r_a` is the accuracy reward, `r_f` is a format reward, and `λ_v`, `λ_f` are scaling coefficients (set to 0.5 and 0.1, respectively).

**Evaluation Metrics:**

To quantify visual reflection, the authors utilize two key metrics:
- **Visual Attention Weight:** Tracks the average attention weights from generated response tokens to visual tokens across transformer layers as more tokens are produced.
- **Visual Dependency Measure (VDM):** Quantifies the model's reliance on visual information by measuring the Hellinger distance between next-token prediction distributions *with* and *without* visual tokens after generating a certain number of response tokens. A higher VDM indicates greater dependency.

This two-stage methodology systematically addresses the challenge of visual reflection, first by providing explicit examples in the training data and then by reinforcing the desired behavior through a targeted reward function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating the effectiveness of Reflection-V in enhancing visual reflection and improving reasoning capabilities.

1. **Validation of Visual Reflection Deficiency in Existing VRMs:**
 - Quantitative analysis confirms that current VRMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B, OpenVLThinker-7B) exhibit rapidly diminishing attention to visual tokens and visual dependency as the number of generated tokens increases.
 - Notably, models enhanced with traditional RL (like OpenVLThinker-7B) show an *even lower* reliance on visual prompts than their base VLMs, reinforcing the problem rather than solving it. This underscores the need for a targeted approach like Reflection-V.

2. **Superior Performance Across Diverse Visual Reasoning Benchmarks:**
 - Reflection-V-7B consistently achieves significant performance improvements over its base model (Qwen2.5-VL) and other state-of-the-art open-source vision-language reasoning models (e.g., TVC, R1-VL, MM-Eureka, OpenVLThinker) across math-reasoning (MathVision, MathVista), multi-disciplinary (MMMU, MMMU-Pro), and general reasoning (M3CoT) benchmarks.
 - Remarkably, Reflection-V-7B reaches competitive or even superior performance compared to much larger, widely-used closed-source models like GPT-4o, GPT-4V, and large open-source models such as InternVL2.5-38B and LLaVA-OneVision-72B on several tasks. This indicates a high level of efficiency and effectiveness.

3. **Significant Reduction in Visual Hallucinations:**
 - Reflection-V demonstrates a notable reduction in visual hallucinations, as measured by a significantly higher all accuracy (aAcc) on the HallBench dataset, compared to the Qwen2.5-VL base model and other VRMs.
 - This finding supports the hypothesis that sustained focus on visual information, enabled by visual reflection, effectively suppresses hallucinations that often arise from visual neglect in long-chain reasoning.

4. **Ablation Study Validates Component Contributions:**
 - Both the cold-start initialization with visual reflection data and the visual attention-based reward are crucial for performance gains. The cold-start stage, in particular, contributes a substantial performance improvement, emphasizing the importance of initial learning of visual reflection patterns.
 - The visual attention-based reward further enhances performance, especially when combined with the cold-start data, suggesting a synergistic effect.
 - Replacing the visual reflection-patterned data with traditional image caption-based reasoning data for cold-start results in significantly worse performance, confirming that the *quality and nature* of the cold-start data (i.e., emphasizing visual reflection) are key, not just distilling from larger models.
 - The method shows no bias towards specific model families for data construction, achieving comparable results when using different LLM-VLM pairs (e.g., InternVL3/Qwen3 vs. Qwen2.5-VL/QWQ).

5. **Evidence of Sustained Visual Attention and Dependency:**
 - Analysis using visual attention weights and the visual dependency measure (VDM) demonstrates that Reflection-V maintains significantly higher and more consistent attention to visual tokens compared to baseline models. The decline in visual attention and dependency with increasing token generation is substantially mitigated in Reflection-V.
 - Crucially, case studies visually illustrate that Reflection-V actively engages in visual reflection, with noticeable spikes in visual attention occurring alongside explicit textual "Aha moments" (e.g., "Let's check the image again"). This behavioral evidence strongly supports the claim that the model genuinely re-engages with visual input to verify and refine its reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Look Again, Think Slowly: Enhancing Visual Reflection in Vision-Language Models" holds significant implications for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of multimodal reasoning.

1. **Redefining "Slow Thinking" for VLMs:** The paper fundamentally challenges the prevailing approach to extending "slow-thinking" reasoning from LLMs to VLMs. It convincingly argues that true reflection in visual contexts requires active *visual reflection*, moving beyond mere textual distillation. This paradigm shift encourages researchers to embed visual grounding at every step of complex reasoning, rather than relying on an initial image description.

2. **Improved Robustness and Trustworthiness:** By addressing the critical issue of visual neglect and hallucinations, Reflection-V contributes to building more robust and trustworthy multimodal AI systems. Models that actively verify their reasoning against visual inputs are less likely to generate factually incorrect or unsupported claims, which is crucial for applications in sensitive domains like medical diagnosis, scientific discovery, and autonomous decision-making.

3. **Advancing Open-Source AI Capabilities:** Achieving state-of-the-art performance for open-source 7B-scale models that rival or even surpass much larger closed-source and open-source counterparts (e.g., GPT-4o, InternVL2.5-38B) significantly democratizes access to advanced visual reasoning capabilities. This can accelerate innovation in the broader AI community, allowing more researchers and developers to build upon and integrate sophisticated multimodal reasoning into their applications without requiring immense computational resources or proprietary models.

4. **Actionable Training Strategy:** The proposed two-stage training strategy—combining LLM-VLM interaction for visual reflection-patterned data construction with a visual attention-based RL reward—offers a clear, actionable roadmap for developers. This methodology provides a systematic way to imbue models with a crucial cognitive ability, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable AI agents.

5. **Foundation for Human-like Visual Cognition:** The concept of "visual reflection" and the empirical demonstration of "aha moments" where models re-engage with visual data mark a step towards AI systems exhibiting more human-like cognitive processes. This could open new avenues for research into more sophisticated visual comprehension, iterative problem-solving, and adaptive learning in multimodal environments.

6. **Broader Applications:** Enhanced visual reasoning capabilities have far-reaching implications across various domains. From advanced robotics requiring intricate scene understanding and decision-making, to educational tools that interpret complex diagrams, to scientific research aiding in data analysis and hypothesis generation, the ability of AI to genuinely "look again and think slowly" about visual information will unlock a new generation of intelligent applications. The work establishes a strong foundation for future research aimed at scaling this approach to even larger models and more diverse visual reasoning tasks, further narrowing the gap between human and artificial intelligence in complex visual understanding.
