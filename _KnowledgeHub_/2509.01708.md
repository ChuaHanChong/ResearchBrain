# Articulated Object Estimation in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> The ArtiPoint framework estimates articulated object models from ego-centric, in-the-wild RGB-D videos of human-object interactions by combining deep point tracking with articulation-regularized factor graph optimization. This approach, evaluated on the new Arti4D dataset, achieved angular errors of 14.54 degrees for prismatic joints and 17.14 degrees for revolute joints, demonstrating improved robustness in unconstrained environments.

## Problem

- Existing articulated object estimation methods were primarily developed for controlled settings, failing in real-world scenarios with dynamic camera motion, partial occlusions, and clutter.
- Robots require the ability to understand 3D motion of articulated objects in uncurated environments for advanced mobile manipulation tasks.
- A lack of suitable datasets prevented comprehensive evaluation of articulation estimation algorithms under challenging ego-centric and unconstrained conditions.

## Method

- **ArtiPoint** extracts interaction intervals using ==hand detection==, then employs a ==deep any-point tracking== model (CoTracker3) to generate robust 2D keypoint trajectories on potential articulated objects.
- The framework lifts 2D tracks to 3D, compensates for ==camera motion== using odometry, and filters out static or unreliable points, followed by trajectory smoothing.
- A ==factor graph== formulates articulation estimation as a global optimization problem, jointly optimizing a shared ==1-DoF articulation model== (type and axis) across the entire interaction segment.
- The **Arti4D** dataset was introduced, providing ego-centric, in-the-wild RGB-D videos of human-object interactions with ground-truth articulation and camera poses for robust evaluation.

## Results

- ArtiPoint achieved superior performance on the Arti4D dataset, with an angular error of **14.54 degrees** for prismatic joints and **17.14 degrees** (with **0.07m** positional error) for revolute joints, outperforming all tested baselines.
- The framework demonstrated high joint type classification accuracy, correctly identifying **68%** of prismatic and **98%** of revolute joints.
- Ablation studies confirmed that robust deep point tracking (CoTracker3) and trajectory smoothing are crucial components for ArtiPoint's performance, significantly contributing to reduced errors and improved accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating state-of-the-art deep any-point tracking (CoTracker3) with classical geometric optimization is critical for robustly handling dynamic cameras, occlusions, and clutter in real-world scenes.
> - Articulation-regularized trajectory optimization, which jointly estimates a single articulation model over an entire motion path, is more effective than estimating independent transformations for each frame pair.
> - Leveraging human-object interaction cues (e.g., hand detection) helps focus tracking and estimation on relevant object segments, improving efficiency and accuracy.
> - Careful 3D track processing, including camera motion compensation, static point filtering, and trajectory smoothing, is essential to mitigate noise and ambiguities in real-world RGB-D data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly moving towards deploying autonomous systems in increasingly complex, unstructured, and dynamic real-world environments. A critical capability for such robots, especially for mobile manipulation tasks, is **understanding the 3D motion of articulated objects**. This includes common household items like drawers, doors, and cabinets, as well as industrial equipment. Accurate estimation of an object's articulation model (e.g., whether it's a revolute or prismatic joint, and its axis of motion) is fundamental for tasks such as planning grasp points, predicting object behavior, and executing successful manipulation.

Historically, research in articulated object estimation has been conducted primarily in **controlled settings**. These often involve:
- **Fixed camera viewpoints:** Limiting the camera's motion and simplifying pose estimation.
- **Direct observations of various object states:** Requiring multiple, distinct observations of the object in different configurations.
- **Isolated objects:** Presenting objects without clutter or occlusions.
- **Curated environments:** Laboratory settings designed to minimize unpredictability.

While these controlled environments have allowed for significant theoretical and algorithmic progress (e.g., probabilistic methods like Sturm et al. and early deep learning approaches like ScrewNet and Ditto), they fall short when robots are deployed "in-the-wild." Real-world scenarios inherently feature **dynamic camera motion** (e.g., from a robot's mobile base or an ego-centric human operator), **partial observability** (due to occlusions from hands, other objects, or self-occlusion), and **significant clutter** where objects are not isolated. These conditions cause prior methods to fail or perform poorly because they struggle with establishing robust correspondences, estimating accurate poses under motion, and handling visual ambiguities.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It draws inspiration from how humans effortlessly infer articulation by observing manipulations, aiming to endow robots with a similar capability. By focusing on "ego-centric, in-the-wild" scenarios, the work pushes the boundaries of articulated object estimation from static, idealized setups to dynamic, cluttered, and partially observable real-world conditions. The introduction of the Arti4D dataset is a pivotal contribution in this context, as it provides the necessary benchmark for evaluating methods under these challenging conditions, differentiating itself from existing datasets that maintain more controlled environments. The paper thus positions itself at the forefront of robust robotic perception for complex real-world interaction and manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for **estimating articulated object models from ego-centric, in-the-wild RGB-D videos of human-object interactions**. This involves inferring both the type of articulation (prismatic or revolute) and its parameters (axis of motion) despite common real-world challenges.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Bridging the Gap to Real-World Robotics:** Previous methods' reliance on controlled environments severely limits their applicability to practical robotic deployment. Robots need to understand articulated objects in novel, uncurated environments, where dynamic camera motion, occlusions, and clutter are commonplace. ArtiPoint aims to overcome these limitations.
2. **Enabling General-Purpose Robotic Manipulation:** Understanding articulated objects is essential for advanced robotic tasks like mobile manipulation, motion planning, and human-to-robot imitation learning. Without robust articulation estimation, robots cannot effectively interact with common objects in human environments.
3. **Exploiting Human Interaction Priors:** Humans learn about articulated objects by observing interactions. The authors are motivated to leverage this principle by observing human hands manipulating objects, using these interactions as a crucial cue to identify and analyze object motion.
4. **Addressing Data Scarcity for "In-the-Wild" Settings:** A significant hurdle for developing and evaluating algorithms for unconstrained environments is the lack of suitable datasets. The creation of Arti4D is a direct response to this need, providing a challenging benchmark with relevant annotations (articulation axes, interaction segments, camera poses) that distinguishes itself from existing datasets focused on isolated objects or static cameras.
5. **Robustness under Partial Observability and Dynamic Cameras:** The paper is motivated to create a system that can infer articulation models even when objects are partially occluded or the camera is in constant motion, moving beyond the assumptions of static views and full object visibility inherent in much prior work.

In essence, the paper seeks to enable robotic systems to acquire a more human-like understanding of articulated environments through observation, fostering the development of more capable and adaptable autonomous robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **ArtiPoint**, is a novel estimation pipeline designed to infer articulated object models directly from raw RGB-D videos captured by a dynamic, ego-centric camera during human-object interactions. Its approach combines deep learning for robust point tracking with a classical factor graph optimization framework. The methodology consists of four main stages:

#### 3.1. Extraction of Interaction Intervals

- **Hand Detection:** The process begins by identifying segments in the RGB-D video where human-object interaction is likely occurring. This is achieved by using a pre-existing hand segmentation model to detect human hands in each RGB frame.
- **Segment Filtering:** A binary label indicates hand visibility. To mitigate noise, a moving average of hand detections is computed. Frames where this average exceeds a threshold initiate an "interaction segment." These segments are further filtered by their duration to ensure they represent meaningful interactions (minimum and maximum allowed lengths). This approach exploits the prior that human hands are primary triggers for object articulation in observed scenes.

#### 3.2. Deep Point Tracking

- **Keypoint Identification:** Within the identified interaction segments, points are uniformly sampled around the detected hand masks. These sampled points act as prompts for a class-agnostic instance segmentation model (MobileSAM) to identify masks of potential articulated objects in the hand's vicinity.
- **Feature Extraction:** From these object masks, stable keypoints are extracted using the lightweight Shi-Tomasi method (GFTT). This ensures that robust, trackable features are selected on the potential moving parts.
- **Any-Point Tracking:** The extracted keypoints are then fed into a state-of-the-art deep any-point tracking model, CoTracker3. This model tracks the selected keypoints throughout the entire interaction segment, generating 2D point trajectories and estimating their visibility across all frames.

#### 3.3. 3D Track Estimation and Filtering

- **3D Lifting:** The 2D point trajectories are lifted to 3D using the corresponding depth frames and camera intrinsics. Points with invalid depth values are discarded.
- **Camera Motion Compensation:** To obtain object motion in a global coordinate frame, the 3D point tracks are transformed using accurate camera odometry (which, in this work, relies on ground-truth or high-quality estimated camera poses). This step is crucial for separating object motion from camera motion.
- **Static Point Filtering:** Points belonging to static parts of the scene (e.g., background clutter) are identified and filtered out by computing the positional variance of each 3D track within the segment. Tracks with variance below a threshold are deemed static.
- **Reliable Track Filtering:** Tracks that are frequently occluded (occluded for more than a specified percentage of frames) are also filtered out, as they can be unreliable.
- **Trajectory Smoothing:** To mitigate high-frequency noise introduced during the 2D-to-3D lifting and tracking process, a cost function is minimized to smooth the 3D trajectories. This penalizes spikes in acceleration and high velocities while maintaining fidelity to observed points.

#### 3.4. Exploiting the Articulation Prior (Articulation Model Estimation)

- **se(3) Representation:** ArtiPoint represents 1-DoF articulations (prismatic, revolute, screw joints) using elements from the `se(3)` Lie algebra, which allows for a differentiable and unified representation of rigid-body transformations.
- **Pose Extraction:** Instead of operating directly on individual points, the system extracts relative rigid-body transformations between pairs of points observed at different times within a segment. This forms a set of local transformations.
- **Articulation-Regularized Trajectory Optimization:** The core of the articulation estimation is a factor graph formulation, inspired by Buchanan et al., but extended to be "articulation-regularized." This means that instead of estimating independent transformations for each frame pair, a single shared base twist (`ξ`) and configuration positions (`θ`) are jointly optimized for the entire path, modeling the underlying articulation. This global optimization, performed using GTSAM, finds the articulation model that best fits the refined 3D point trajectories.
- **Global Registration:** The optimized articulation parameters (`ξ`) and configuration states (`θ`) are then registered in the observed scene, providing the estimated articulation model (joint type and axis).

**Arti4D Dataset:**
A significant part of the approach involves the introduction of **Arti4D**, the first ego-centric, in-the-wild dataset for scene-level articulated object manipulation from human demonstrations. It comprises 45 RGB-D sequences across four diverse scenes, capturing 414 human-object interactions under dynamic camera motion, occlusions, and uncurated environments. Crucially, Arti4D provides:
- `se(3)`-labeled ground-truth articulation axes.
- Ground-truth temporal interaction segments.
- Difficulty annotations (EASY/HARD).
- Reconstructed scenes.
- Cm-accurate ground-truth camera poses (obtained via external tracking), which is vital for handling dynamic camera motion.

This dataset serves as both a benchmark for articulated object estimation and a challenging resource for visual odometry and SLAM research in dynamic, interactive environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ArtiPoint was conducted primarily on the newly introduced Arti4D dataset, benchmarking it against a range of classical and deep learning-based baselines.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Arti4D

- **Overall Accuracy:** ArtiPoint consistently demonstrated superior performance in estimating articulation parameters compared to all baselines. For prismatic joints, it achieved an angular error (θ_err) of 14.54 degrees, significantly lower than the nearest competitor (Sturm et al. with ArtiPoint frontend: 26.85 degrees; Buchanan with bbox: 49.67 degrees). For revolute joints, ArtiPoint recorded a θ_err of 17.14 degrees and a positional error (d_L2) of 0.07 meters, again outperforming other methods (e.g., Buchanan with bbox: 58.82 degrees, 0.22m).
- **Joint Type Classification:** ArtiPoint achieved the highest accuracy in classifying joint types, with 68% for prismatic and 98% for revolute joints, indicating its ability to correctly identify the nature of the articulation.
- **Baseline Weaknesses:** Deep learning-based methods like ArtGS and Ditto performed poorly, with high errors (e.g., ArtGS revolute θ_err 56.82 deg, d_L2 0.25m). This is attributed to their susceptibility to partial observability, occlusions, and the non-isolated nature of objects in in-the-wild settings – conditions they were not designed for. Classical factor graph methods like Sturm et al. and Buchanan et al., when combined with bounding box-based frontends, also showed significantly higher errors than ArtiPoint's full pipeline.
- **Benefit of Articulation-Regularized Estimation:** The proposed articulation-regularized estimator (Eq. 4) proved crucial, yielding lower rotational and translational errors compared to an independent-transform estimator operating on a frame-pair basis (Eq. 3), highlighting the importance of jointly optimizing the articulation model over the entire motion.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Difficulty Levels

- ArtiPoint maintained its leading performance across both "EASY" and "HARD" interactions in the Arti4D dataset (as defined by hand visibility, depth measurements, and occlusion levels). While errors naturally increased for "HARD" cases for all methods, ArtiPoint's relative performance remained superior, demonstrating its robustness under challenging conditions.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- Qualitative examples (e.g., drawers, storage cases) depicted ArtiPoint successfully estimating joint axes and pose trajectories even when objects had a small number of keypoints or frequently missing depth measurements, underscoring its robustness in real-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Insights

- **Deep Point Tracking is Key:** Replacing CoTracker3 with traditional ORB keypoints led to a significant degradation in performance across all metrics, emphasizing the critical role of robust deep any-point tracking.
- **Trajectory Smoothing is Important:** Deactivating trajectory smoothing (which mitigates high-frequency jitter) reduced accuracy for both joint types and overall classification, highlighting its contribution to track quality.
- **Filtering Unreliable Tracks:** Including tracks that were highly occluded slightly decreased prediction quality, validating the effectiveness of the proposed filtering mechanism for unreliable tracks.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Adjusting keyframe stride and hand detection parameters showed some impact on performance, indicating the need for careful tuning, though the core robustness remained.
- **Camera Pose Impact (Supplementary):** The supplementary material revealed that while ground truth camera poses yield the best results, using high-quality estimated poses from DROID-SLAM still produced reasonable and only slightly increased errors, suggesting practical deployability.

#### 4.5. Scene-Respective Results (Supplementary)

- Evaluation across different scenes in Arti4D showed varying difficulty, with RH078 identified as the most challenging due to non-separable interactions, textureless objects, and metallic surfaces leading to inconsistent depth observations.

In summary, the main findings confirm that ArtiPoint represents a significant advancement in articulated object estimation for "in-the-wild" settings, largely due to its innovative combination of deep point tracking, robust 3D track processing, and an articulation-regularized factor graph optimization, coupled with the challenging and realistic Arti4D dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Articulated Object Estimation in the Wild" holds significant implications and potential impact across several domains, particularly robotics and computer vision.

#### 5.1. Advancing Robotic Manipulation and Scene Understanding

- **Real-World Deployment:** ArtiPoint directly addresses a critical bottleneck for deploying intelligent robots in unstructured, human-centric environments. By enabling robust articulation estimation under dynamic cameras, partial observability, and clutter, it moves beyond idealized lab settings, bringing robots closer to truly understanding and interacting with everyday objects. This is crucial for domestic robots, industrial collaborators, and autonomous mobile manipulators.
- **Enhanced Manipulation Planning:** Accurate articulation models are fundamental for effective manipulation. Robots can use these models to predict how an object will move, plan optimal grasp points, and anticipate interaction outcomes, leading to more reliable and efficient task execution.
- **Foundation for Imitation Learning:** The ability to infer articulation from human demonstrations, especially in an ego-centric manner, provides a strong basis for human-to-robot imitation learning. Robots can observe humans interacting with objects and automatically learn their kinematic properties, reducing the need for manual programming or extensive pre-training in simulation.

#### 5.2. Contributions to Computer Vision

- **Robust Perception in Dynamic Scenes:** The framework showcases a powerful integration of state-of-the-art deep point tracking (CoTracker3) with classical geometric and optimization techniques (factor graphs). This hybrid approach offers a paradigm for robust perception in highly dynamic and unconstrained visual environments, which is a longstanding challenge in computer vision.
- **High-Quality Dataset for Future Research:** The Arti4D dataset is a major contribution in itself. As the first ego-centric, scene-level, in-the-wild dataset for articulated object manipulation, it provides a unique and challenging benchmark. It will undoubtedly foster further research in:
 - **Articulated Object Estimation:** Providing a standard for comparing future methods in realistic conditions.
 - **Visual Odometry and SLAM:** The dynamic and articulated nature of the scenes, coupled with ego-centric motion, makes Arti4D a demanding testbed for odometry and SLAM algorithms, especially those dealing with dynamic environments where traditional static world assumptions break down.
 - **Interaction Recognition:** The labeled interaction segments can be used to train and evaluate models for detecting human-object interactions.

#### 5.3. Overcoming Limitations of Prior Work

- The paper clearly demonstrates the shortcomings of previous methods (both classical and deep learning-based) when faced with real-world complexities. ArtiPoint's superior performance highlights the necessity of its "in-the-wild" design principles, pushing the community towards more robust and generalizable solutions.
