# DexMan: Learning Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation from Human and Generated Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> DexMan is an automated pipeline that converts monocular RGB videos of humans manipulating rigid objects into bimanual dexterous humanoid robot skills in simulation, using video depth cues for object pose recovery and a contact-based residual RL reward. It needs no camera calibration, depth sensors, 3D scans, or motion-capture labels, and sets state-of-the-art pose estimation on TACO plus a 19% success-rate gain on OakInk-v2.

## Problem

- Prior human-to-robot transfer methods control only simplified floating hands, not full humanoid embodiments with two dexterous arms and fingers.
- Dual-objective RL methods imitating human behavior and target object trajectories require ground-truth object poses, available only from costly motion-capture datasets.
- Existing contact rewards attract hands to the nearest object surface or fixed world-space coordinates, letting policies exploit trivial contacts instead of task-relevant grasps.

## Method

- **DexMan** reconstructs 3D object meshes from a single video frame via ==SAM2== segmentation and ==Trellis== image-to-3D generation, then rescales them using ==VGGT== depth and ==HaMeR== hand-mesh metric scale.
- A sampling-based stabilization step perturbs reconstructed object poses with randomized rotations, simulates each, and selects the configuration with minimal deviation for stable placement.
- A ==contact-prior attraction reward== pulls robot hand keypoints toward human-contacted object vertices with surface-normal alignment, combined with object-following and imitation rewards to train a ==residual RL== policy on a full humanoid.

## Results

- On TACO, DexMan's pose estimation beats FoundationPose and SpatialTracker baselines by absolute gains of **0.08 ADD-S** and **0.12 VSD**, cutting failure rate to **0.01**.
- On OakInk-v2, DexMan's residual RL policy reaches **44.3%** success rate versus MANIPTRANS's **25.3%**, a **19-point** absolute margin, while controlling a full humanoid rather than floating hands.
- Removing the contact reward collapses end-to-end success rate from **27.4%** to **7.2%** on TACO and from **39.0%** to **7.8%** on Veo3-generated synthetic videos, far exceeding the task-reward ablation drop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric, surface-normal-aligned contact rewards generalize better than nearest-point or fixed-world-coordinate contact rewards under noisy, unlabeled video-derived motion references.
> - Depth-and-point-trajectory cues let object pose estimation and rescaling work without ground-truth annotations, opening skill learning to any monocular video, including generated ones.
> - Contact-guided reward shaping matters more than trajectory-imitation reward for recovering physically plausible grasps when reference motion is noisy or physically implausible.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

DexMan addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in robotics: enabling complex, dexterous manipulation, especially bimanual tasks, by learning from human demonstrations. The broader research landscape for robot skill acquisition is characterized by several approaches, each with its own limitations:

- **Traditional Learning Frameworks (Behavior Cloning, Reinforcement Learning):** While effective, these methods face significant hurdles for multi-arm, multi-fingered manipulators. Behavior cloning (BC) requires costly, labor-intensive teleoperated demonstrations, while reinforcement learning (RL) suffers from extreme sample inefficiency in high-dimensional action spaces. DexMan aims to circumvent the need for such costly data collection.
- **Imitation Learning from Human Videos:** This area has gained traction due to the morphological similarity between human and robot hands, and the abundance of human video data. However, direct imitation often struggles with the "embodiment gap" – the kinematic, geometric, and physical property differences between human and robot hands – leading to unstable grasps or unreachable poses. DexMan explicitly acknowledges and works to mitigate this gap.
- **Learning from Human Motion Priors with RL:** Recent work has combined human motion imitation with RL, using dual objectives to guide policy learning. These methods typically rely on accurate ground-truth object poses, often available only in motion-capture (MoCap) datasets. This dependency severely limits scalability for "in-the-wild" monocular RGB videos, which lack such precise labels. DexMan's core contribution is to move beyond MoCap data, working directly with raw, uncalibrated monocular RGB videos.
- **6D Object Pose Estimation:** Accurate 3D scene reconstruction and object pose estimation are foundational for video-to-robot transfer. DexMan builds upon state-of-the-art methods like FoundationPose, SpatialTracker, and Any6D, but identifies their limitations with textureless objects or fast motion, and introduces enhancements to improve robustness and temporal consistency.
- **Robot Embodiment and Complexity:** Many prior works simplify the robotic setup, often considering "floating hands" rather than controlling full humanoid robots with complex kinematics and dynamics. DexMan explicitly tackles the more challenging problem of controlling a complete humanoid robot (Unitree G1 with Shadow Dexterous Hands), making its solutions more realistic and transferable to practical applications.
- **Data Generation:** The ability to learn from synthetic videos (e.g., generated by models like Veo3) is a nascent but promising avenue for creating large-scale, diverse datasets, overcoming the manual data collection bottleneck. DexMan is among the first to demonstrate skill acquisition from such generated content, pushing the boundaries of data sourcing for robot learning.

In summary, DexMan positions itself at the forefront of video-to-robot skill transfer, offering an end-to-end framework that addresses critical limitations of existing methods, particularly the reliance on costly data and simplified robot models. It distinguishes itself by its ability to work with raw monocular RGB inputs (real or synthetic) and control a full humanoid robot, setting a new benchmark for scalability and realism in dexterous manipulation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DexMan is to develop an automated and comprehensive framework that can **translate human visual demonstrations from monocular RGB videos into bimanual dexterous manipulation skills for humanoid robots in simulation.**

The motivations driving this objective are multifaceted:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** Existing methods for training dexterous manipulation skills, such as behavior cloning (BC) or reinforcement learning (RL) from teleoperated demonstrations, are severely limited by the high cost and complexity of collecting human-robot interaction data. Dexterous, multi-fingered, and bimanual tasks exacerbate this issue due to their high dimensionality. DexMan seeks to eliminate this bottleneck by leveraging readily available human videos.
- **Overcoming the "Embodiment Gap":** Directly imitating human motions from videos often leads to unstable or unreachable robot actions due to inherent differences in physical properties, geometries, and kinematics between human and robot hands. DexMan aims to bridge this gap by optimizing neural control policies with RL, allowing the robot to adapt human-like behaviors to its own embodiment.
- **Reducing Reliance on High-Fidelity Sensors and Annotations:** Many state-of-the-art approaches for video-based robot learning require accurate ground-truth object poses, depth information, scanned 3D object assets, or camera calibration, typically only found in controlled motion-capture (MoCap) environments. DexMan's ambition is to operate directly on "in-the-wild" third-person monocular RGB videos, thereby removing these restrictive requirements and significantly expanding the potential data sources.
- **Controlling Full Humanoid Robots:** Prior work often simplifies the robotic control problem by focusing on "floating hands" or single-arm setups. DexMan is motivated to tackle the more realistic and challenging scenario of controlling a complete humanoid robot with two dexterous hands, making the learned skills more directly applicable to advanced robotic systems.
- **Enabling Scalable Skill Acquisition:** By removing the need for manual data collection and MoCap, and by demonstrating the ability to learn from synthetic videos, DexMan aims to facilitate the creation of large-scale, diverse datasets. This, in turn, is a crucial step towards training generalist dexterous manipulation policies that can perform a wide array of tasks.
- **Improving Policy Learning from Noisy Data:** The inherent noise and physical implausibility in 3D hand-object poses estimated from uncalibrated videos can destabilize RL training. DexMan is motivated to introduce robust reward mechanisms, specifically contact-based rewards, to guide policy learning effectively even with noisy reference motions.

In essence, DexMan strives to unlock a scalable and robust pathway for robots to acquire complex dexterous manipulation skills by observing and interpreting human actions in a highly accessible and flexible manner, moving away from restrictive data requirements and simplified robot models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexMan is an automated framework that processes monocular RGB human manipulation videos to train bimanual dexterous robot skills in simulation. The pipeline consists of four main, interconnected stages:

#### 3.1. 3D Object Reconstruction

The first step is to reconstruct the necessary 3D scene from the input video.
- **Object Identification:** Target objects manipulated by the human demonstrator are identified (currently manual, but noted as potentially automatable with vision-language models).
- **Mesh Generation:** From an initial video frame, SAM2 segments the object mask, and Trellis generates a rigid 3D mesh from the cropped object image. These meshes initially lack correct scale and pose.

#### 3.2. Hand and Object Pose Estimation

This stage is critical for rescaling reconstructed objects and recovering accurate 3D motions.
- **Depth Estimation:** VGGT, an image-to-3D foundation model, predicts depth maps from video sequences. To handle long videos and inconsistencies from chunked processing, DexMan uses an object-centric temporal alignment strategy, computing local linear mappings to align depth values within each object region across chunks.
- **Hand Pose Estimation:** HaMeR recovers metrically accurate MANO hand meshes. The global scale factor for VGGT depth maps is computed by aligning the width of the MANO mesh with the VGGT point cloud in the first frame.
- **Object Pose Estimation:** Any6D is used to estimate object scale by sampling candidates and selecting the one with the highest-scoring pose from FoundationPose. To improve robustness against fast motion or occlusion, DexMan enhances FoundationPose by regularizing its estimates with 3D point trajectories from SpatialTracker. This involves calculating rigid transformations between consecutive frames using the Kabsch algorithm and applying them to refine initial poses for FoundationPose, resulting in smoother and more temporally consistent estimates.

#### 3.3. Motion Retargeting from Humans to Humanoids

This stage prepares the simulated environment and translates human actions to the robot's embodiment.
- **Scene Setup:** A Unitree G1 humanoid robot with Shadow Dexterous Hands is placed at the origin in simulation. Unlike prior work, DexMan controls a full humanoid, not floating hands.
- **Spatial Transformation for Scene Alignment:** Since camera extrinsics are unavailable, DexMan approximates scene alignment through three consecutive spatial transformations:
 1. **Gravity Alignment:** The scene is rotated to align the camera's gravity direction (approximated from the table surface normal derived from estimated depth maps) with the simulator's gravity (z-axis).
 2. **Robot Facing Direction Alignment:** The human's hip-to-hip vector (from HUMAN4D keypoints) is rotated to align with the simulator’s x-axis, orienting the human body forward.
 3. **Workspace Translation:** All entities are translated to place them within the robot's workspace, based on the offset between the transformed human pelvis and the robot’s pelvis.
- **Stable Object Configuration Sampling:** Reconstructed 3D objects can be physically unstable. DexMan perturbs each object's initial pose with 20 random rotations, simulates each configuration for 20 steps, and selects the most stable pose closest to the original. This significantly improves simulation stability.
- **Human-to-Robot Motion Retargeting:**
 - **Wrist Retargeting:** An inverse kinematics (IK) solver (Genesis) computes required joint angles for the robot’s wrists, keeping the lower body fixed for tabletop tasks.
 - **Finger Retargeting:** A 5-layer MLP neural IK solver is trained via supervised learning to map 3D fingertip positions to corresponding finger joint angles.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning with Noisy Motion Priors

A residual RL policy refines the retargeted human motions, which may still be noisy or physically inconsistent.
- **Contact-Prior Attraction Reward (Novel Contribution):** This is a key innovation designed to guide the robot towards stable grasps despite noisy reference motions. It has two terms:
 1. **Attraction Term:** Pulls robot hand keypoints (fingertips, MCP joints) towards human-contacted object vertices.
 2. **Directional Alignment Term:** Aligns the keypoint-vertex vector with the robot hand keypoint's surface normal, preventing unrealistic contacts (e.g., back-of-hand).
 - **Offline Contact Prior Extraction:** For each hand keypoint, the closest mesh vertex on manipulated objects is identified. Pairs where the keypoint-vertex distance is below a threshold are designated as contact candidates.
 This object-centric, local relationship focuses on meaningful contacts, making the policy robust to object pose variations and less susceptible to trivial contacts compared to prior methods.
- **Object-following Rewards:** Encourages the robot to reproduce the target object trajectories, defined by positional and rotational differences.
- **Imitation Rewards:** Encourages imitation of human hand motions (wrist position, wrist orientation, per-joint finger positions).
- **Total Reward:** A weighted sum of contact, object-following, and imitation rewards.
- **Policy and Control:** A residual RL policy (Actor-Critic MLP, PPO) predicts small corrections to wrist position, wrist orientation, and hand joint angles. These residuals are accumulated on top of the retargeted motions, converted to executable robot joint angles via IK solvers, and executed by a PD controller in Isaac Gym.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DexMan was rigorously evaluated across three challenging settings, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance and unique capabilities.

#### 4.1. Object Pose Estimation Performance

- **Dataset:** TACO (motion-capture dataset with ground-truth hand-object poses).
- **Comparison:** FoundationPose (direct 6DoF from RGB-D), SpatialTracker (3D point motion tracking).
- **Metrics:** ADD-S (accuracy, AUC), VSD (visible-surface consistency, AUC), Failure Rate (robustness), Temporal Stability.
- **Results:** DexMan consistently outperformed baselines, achieving significant gains:
 - **ADD-S:** 0.57 (DexMan) vs. 0.49 (FoundationPose) – 0.08 absolute gain.
 - **VSD:** 0.82 (DexMan) vs. 0.70 (FoundationPose) – 0.12 absolute gain.
 - **Failure Rate:** 0.01 (DexMan) vs. 0.14 (FoundationPose) – substantial reduction in failures.
 - **Temporal Stability:** 0.76 (DexMan) vs. 0.70 (FoundationPose) – improved consistency.
- **Conclusion:** The integration of 3D point trajectories into pose estimation significantly improves accuracy and temporal consistency, establishing a reliable foundation for downstream skill learning.

#### 4.2. Residual RL Policy Performance

- **Dataset:** OakInk-v2 (challenging bimanual dexterous manipulation benchmark with ground-truth pose annotations).
- **Comparison:** MANIPTRANS (state-of-the-art for bimanual skill transfer using floating robot hands). DexMan controls a full Unitree G1 humanoid with Shadow Dexterous Hands.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate, average object rotation error (Er), average object translation error (Et) for successful rollouts. Evaluation considers entire episodes, not just intermediate frames.
- **Results:** DexMan achieved significantly higher success rates:
 - **Success Rate:** 44.3% (DexMan) vs. 25.3% (MANIPTRANS) – a 19% absolute gain.
 - Er and Et were comparable or slightly higher for DexMan, but this is reported for successful rollouts, which were far more frequent for DexMan.
- **Conclusion:** The novel contact-based reward promotes more stable grasps, leading to a substantial improvement in task success, even when controlling a more complex, full humanoid robot.

#### 4.3. Skill Acquisition from Real and Synthetic Videos

- **Datasets:** 50 sequences from TACO (real videos) and 50 synthetic videos generated by Veo3 (Google DeepMind, 2025).
- **Conditions:** No ground-truth object assets, hand, or object motion annotations were used.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate, Er, Et (for successful rollouts), and Trajectory-to-Mask IoU (proxy for realism of object trajectories).
- **Results:** DexMan demonstrated the first end-to-end video-to-robot skill acquisition pipeline from uncalibrated monocular RGB inputs:
 - **TACO (real videos):** 27.4% success rate, 0.314 rad Er, 0.016m Et, 49.0% Traj→Mask IoU.
 - **Synthetic Videos:** 39.0% success rate, 0.248 rad Er, 0.017m Et, 44.7% Traj→Mask IoU.
- **Ablation Study on Reward Components:**
 - **Without Task Reward:** Success rates dropped by 9.0% (TACO) and 4.4% (Veo3), highlighting its importance.
 - **Without Contact Reward:** Success rates collapsed dramatically from 27.4% to 7.2% on TACO and from 39.0% to 7.8% on Veo3 videos.
- **Conclusion:** The contact reward is the dominant factor for recovering physically plausible robot skills directly from monocular videos, enabling robust learning from noisy data.

#### 4.4. Effect of Object Configuration Sampling

- **Results:** The sampling strategy for stable object configurations achieved 98% stable initializations, significantly higher than 86% without sampling.
- **Conclusion:** This technique is crucial for reducing physical instability in the simulation environment, which would otherwise degrade RL training performance.

#### 4.5. Failure Case Analysis

- **Guitar-lifting task:** Policy failed due to the embodiment gap. The human demonstrator (larger body, longer arms) could easily stabilize the object, but the smaller humanoid robot struggled to reproduce these poses, leading to unstable grasps.
- **Brush-picking task:** The human demonstration contained visual penetration (physically impossible) during in-hand rotation. The robot successfully grasped but failed the rapid reorientation, highlighting the robot's dexterity limitations.
- **Shoe-picking task:** The robot failed to establish a stable grasp because the thumb made contact on the outside surface of the shoe, rather than inside. This revealed a limitation in the contact reward formulation, which encouraged proximity but didn't explicitly differentiate the correct side of contact for concave objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DexMan represents a significant leap forward in robot learning from human demonstrations, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Democratization of Robot Skill Acquisition:** By eliminating the need for camera calibration, depth sensors, scanned 3D object assets, or costly ground-truth hand and object motion annotations, DexMan dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for collecting robot training data. This opens up the vast, readily available pool of "in-the-wild" human videos on the internet as a potent source for robot learning.
- **Scalability for Generalist Robots:** The ability to learn from both real and *synthetic videos* (like those from Veo3) without manual data collection provides an unprecedented pathway to generate large-scale, diverse datasets. This is crucial for training "generalist" dexterous manipulation policies that can handle a wide variety of objects and tasks, moving beyond single-task, highly specialized robots.
- **Enhanced Realism in Simulation:** DexMan directly controls a full humanoid robot (Unitree G1 with Shadow Dexterous Hands), rather than simplified floating hands, making the learned skills more realistic and directly transferable to complex, physical robot systems. The novel object configuration sampling method further ensures the physical plausibility of the simulated environment.
- **Robust Learning from Noisy Data:** The introduction of the contact-prior attraction reward is a key methodological contribution. It allows policies to learn stable grasps and effective manipulation strategies even when the human motion priors extracted from videos are noisy or physically inconsistent. This mechanism addresses a fundamental challenge in video-based imitation learning.
- **Advancing Embodied AI:** DexMan pushes the capabilities of embodied AI systems by enabling them to interpret and translate complex human-object interactions into executable robot behaviors. This brings us closer to robots that can autonomously acquire new skills by simply observing humans, a cornerstone of intelligent agents.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While currently validated in simulation, DexMan lays a strong foundation for future work on sim-to-real transfer for bimanual dexterous manipulation. The insights gained from handling the embodiment gap, noisy priors, and physically unstable scenes in simulation are directly relevant to real-world robot deployment. The identified limitations, such as the need for more sophisticated contact reasoning for concave objects and addressing the full arm posture, also highlight fertile grounds for future research.

In conclusion, DexMan's innovations in video-to-robot skill acquisition, particularly its robustness to uncalibrated monocular inputs and noisy priors, along with its focus on full humanoid control, make it a pivotal development towards creating more autonomous, capable, and generalist robotic systems for complex manipulation tasks.
