# ZeroHSI: Zero-Shot 4D Human-Scene Interaction by Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ZeroHSI synthesizes 4D human-scene interaction motion in unseen 3D scenes without any motion-capture training data, by distilling interactions from a pretrained video generation model and reconstructing them into SMPL-X human motion plus object 6D pose via differentiable Gaussian-splatting rendering. It generalizes to real reconstructed scenes and dynamic objects, beating baselines on realism and alignment.

## Problem

- Scene-aware motion diffusion models require paired 3D scene and motion-capture data, so they generalize poorly even within trained action categories and fail on unseen environments.
- Object-manipulation methods generalize within object categories but break down under significant geometric variation, limiting their use on novel interactable objects.
- Both interaction-synthesis directions rely on paired scene-motion or human-object data unavailable for in-the-wild or reconstructed scenes, blocking zero-shot deployment entirely.

## Method

- **ZeroHSI** pipeline: renders an initial ==Gaussian-composited scene== image, feeds it plus a text prompt to an off-the-shelf ==image-to-video model (KLING)== to generate a 2D human-scene interaction video, then lifts it to 4D.
- ==Differentiable Gaussian-splatting optimization== jointly fits per-frame camera pose, ==SMPL-X human pose== (via an ==Animatable Gaussians== avatar), and object 6D pose against the generated video using photometric, object-center, and depth-regularization losses.
- ==VPoser==-latent refinement stage with physics losses corrects raw per-frame optimization results into temporally smooth, physically plausible motion, plus occlusion-aware masking that aggregates ==SAM2== foreground masks across frames.

## Results

- On the AnyInteraction static-scene benchmark ZeroHSI attains CLIP score **23.52** versus **22.36** (TRUMANS) and **22.61** (LINGO), with lower scene penetration (Pene%scene **0.019** vs **0.046**/**0.058**).
- On dynamic-object interactions ZeroHSI reaches contact ratio **0.835** and object penetration **0.033**, versus CHOIS's **0.687**/**1.581** and LINGO's **0.699**/**0.242**, the best combination of contact and low penetration.
- A **400**-participant 2AFC human study preferred ZeroHSI's realism and text alignment by large margins (up to **96.5%** vs CHOIS, **99.0%** alignment vs TRUMANS); ablation shows full per-frame optimization beats WHAM, WHAC, and a no-body-optimization variant on nearly every metric.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generation models pretrained on broad natural footage can serve as an implicit motion prior, sidestepping the need for paired 3D scene-motion datasets in a new task domain.
> - Treating 2D-to-4D lifting as a model-agnostic differentiable-rendering optimization decouples the method from any specific video generator, letting it ride future video-model improvements for free.
> - Precise root-translation and object-pose recovery, not just plausible body pose, is the key bottleneck separating generic video-to-motion tools from usable scene-interaction reconstruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic human-scene interactions (HSI) is a long-standing challenge at the intersection of computer graphics, computer vision, robotics, and embodied AI. Historically, HSI generation has largely relied on extensive datasets of paired 3D scenes and motion capture (MoCap) data. These datasets, while valuable, are expensive to acquire, limited in diversity, and inherently constrained to specific environments or object types. This fundamental reliance on *seen* data prevents existing methods from generalizing to novel, in-the-wild, or reconstructed 3D scenes, which are ubiquitous in real-world applications.

ZeroHSI positions itself as a **zero-shot** solution, directly addressing this generalization gap. It leverages the recent advancements in **large-scale video generation models**, which have been trained on vast quantities of diverse internet videos. These models implicitly encode a rich understanding of human movements, object interactions, and scene dynamics. By "distilling" this knowledge from 2D video generation into 4D HSI, ZeroHSI proposes a paradigm shift: instead of training explicitly on 3D HSI data, it adapts the knowledge from 2D video priors.

Within the landscape of **human motion generation**, prior works have focused on text-guided motion (e.g., MotionCLIP, HumanML3D) or scene-aware motion (e.g., TRUMANS, LINGO). While diffusion models have significantly improved the quality and diversity of generated motions, these methods often still require 3D scene information during training or are limited to specific interaction categories (e.g., sitting, navigation, simple object manipulation). ZeroHSI extends this by enabling arbitrary interactions in arbitrary *unseen* 3D environments, including those with dynamic objects, without requiring scene-specific training.

In **video generation**, the emergence of models like Lumiere, Stable Video Diffusion, and Kling has revolutionized the synthesis of temporally coherent and contextually rich 2D video. ZeroHSI's key insight is to treat these powerful 2D generative models as a source of implicit HSI knowledge. This work bridges the gap between high-fidelity 2D video synthesis and accurate 3D motion reconstruction by employing **differentiable rendering techniques** (specifically, 3D Gaussian Splatting and Animatable Gaussians). This allows the paper to optimize 3D human and object poses by aligning them with the generated 2D videos, effectively "lifting" 2D information into 4D. This approach is complementary to other zero-shot 3D generation works that focus on static interactions or single time steps (e.g., Genzi), as ZeroHSI aims for dynamic, temporal interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ZeroHSI is to enable **zero-shot 4D human-scene interaction (HSI) synthesis**. This means generating realistic and contextually appropriate human motions interacting with a 3D environment, including potentially dynamic objects, without requiring any prior training on paired 3D scene and captured human motion data for that specific environment or interaction type.

The core motivations behind this objective are:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Generalization Limitations:** Existing HSI generation methods fundamentally rely on paired 3D MoCap data and 3D scene models. Such data is extremely costly, time-consuming, and difficult to acquire at scale, leading to limited diversity and poor generalization to unseen or "in-the-wild" environments (e.g., reconstructed real-world scenes). ZeroHSI aims to eliminate this bottleneck.
- **Unlocking Applications in Novel Environments:** The ability to generate HSI in arbitrary, previously unseen 3D scenes (e.g., reconstructed from NeRFs, scanned environments, or newly designed virtual worlds) is crucial for applications in embodied AI (training agents in diverse virtual worlds), virtual reality (creating immersive and interactive experiences), augmented reality, and robotics (simulating complex human-robot interactions).
- **Leveraging Powerful 2D Video Priors:** The recent explosion of high-quality 2D video generation models (e.g., Kling, Sora) has demonstrated their capacity to implicitly learn vast amounts of common sense knowledge about human behavior and interactions from internet-scale video data. The motivation is to tap into this rich, readily available 2D prior rather than laboriously collecting explicit 3D HSI data.
- **Bridging 2D Generative AI and 3D Embodiment:** The work seeks to bridge the gap between impressive 2D video generation capabilities and the need for 3D understanding and control in embodied systems. By devising a method to "lift" 2D generated videos into precise 4D human and object motions, ZeroHSI enables 3D agents to perform actions inferred from 2D data.
- **Supporting Diverse Interactions:** The paper aims to synthesize both static interactions (e.g., sitting on a sofa) and complex dynamic interactions involving movable objects (e.g., watering plants, lifting a barbell), showcasing versatility across various interaction types.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ZeroHSI's methodology is a novel optimization-based approach that integrates state-of-the-art video generation models with differentiable neural rendering. The pipeline can be broken down into three main stages: HSI Video Generation and Processing, 4D HSI Optimization, and Refinement.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Differentiable 3D Representations

The method heavily relies on:
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** An explicit 3D representation composed of numerous Gaussian particles, each with properties like position, opacity, rotation, scale, and color. It allows for highly efficient and differentiable rendering, enabling gradients to flow from 2D pixel space to 3D scene properties.
- **Animatable Gaussians:** A neural human rendering method that maps SMPL-X (a parameterized human model) parameters to an animatable avatar represented by 3DGS. This allows for differentiable rendering of posed human avatars, crucial for optimizing human pose from 2D image observations.

#### 3.2. HSI Video Generation and Processing

1. **Initial State Rendering:** Given an initial human pose (SMPL-X), initial object 6D pose, 3D scene, and an initial camera view, the system first renders an "initial HSI image" (I₀). The camera view is carefully selected from a pre-defined array to maximize visibility and minimize occlusions of the human and object in the first frame.
2. **Video Generation:** The rendered I₀ and a text prompt (e.g., "watering flowers with a watering can") are fed into an off-the-shelf image-to-video diffusion model (KLING image-to-video model v1.0). This generates a sequence of 2D HSI frames ({Iₜ}ᵀₜ=₀) that are temporally coherent and semantically aligned with the prompt.
3. **Segmentation:** The Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM2) is used to segment the dynamic foreground (human and object) and static background in each generated video frame. These masks are critical for later optimization steps.
4. **Handling Occlusions & Incorrect Content:** A key challenge is that video generation models can produce artifacts or inconsistent content in areas that were initially occluded and later become visible. ZeroHSI addresses this during camera pose estimation by aggregating dynamic foreground masks from *all* previous frames (0 to t) to define the reliable static background region, effectively filtering out incorrect content.

#### 3.3. 4D HSI Optimization (2D-to-4D Lifting)

This is the core of the HSI reconstruction, performed sequentially frame-by-frame:
1. **Camera Pose Estimation:** For each frame *t*, the relative camera transformation from the previous frame *t-1* is optimized. This is done by minimizing the photometric (L2) loss between the rendered static background (using the aggregated static background mask) and the corresponding region in the generated video frame.
2. **Human and Object Pose Optimization:** With the camera pose estimated, the human pose parameters (root translation, global orientation, body pose) and the object's 6D pose for the current frame *t* are directly optimized. This is done by minimizing a composite loss function that drives the rendered human and object to match their counterparts in the generated 2D video frame:
 - **Photometric Loss (L_rgb):** A combination of L1 and D-SSIM loss between the rendered image of the human-object-scene and the generated video frame. This guides the appearance and structure.
 - **Object Center Point Loss (L_center):** An L2 loss between the 2D center of the rendered object's segmentation mask and the 2D center of the generated video's object segmentation mask. This helps localize the object accurately.
 - **Depth Regularization Loss (L_depth):** An L2 loss ensuring the average depth of the rendered object region in the current frame *t* is close to its initial depth in frame 0. This stabilizes object depth, especially important for dynamic interactions.
 - **Handling Appearance Change:** As video generation models may produce inconsistent human appearances, the color network of the Animatable Gaussians (which maps human pose to Gaussian colors) is fine-tuned *during* this optimization process using the photometric loss, adapting to the generated video's appearance variations.

#### 3.4. Refinement

After the per-frame optimization, a global refinement step is applied to the entire motion sequence to enhance physical plausibility and naturalness:
1. **VPoser Integration:** The raw human poses obtained from the optimization are refined in the latent space of VPoser, a variational human pose prior trained on large MoCap datasets (AMASS). This helps ensure human poses are natural and conform to known human body kinematics.
2. **Physics Losses:**
 - **Hand-Object Contact Loss:** Encourages contact between human hands (SMPL-X vertices) and object particles when they are in close proximity, crucial for realistic manipulation.
 - **Smoothness Loss:** Regularizes the motion by penalizing large changes in body pose between adjacent frames, ensuring temporal coherence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ZeroHSI's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on a newly curated benchmark, **AnyInteraction**, and comprehensive comparisons with existing baselines.

#### 4.1. AnyInteraction Dataset

- A diverse dataset comprising 12 distinct 3D environments (7 indoor, 5 outdoor) sourced from existing datasets (TRUMANS), public asset libraries, and real-world reconstructed scenes (Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks and Temples).
- Includes 7 types of rigid dynamic objects.
- Features 22 evaluation instances (13 static, 9 dynamic), each with text prompts and initial states, yielding 100 distinct evaluation instances by synthesizing multiple sequences per input.

#### 4.2. Baselines

- **Static Setting:** TRUMANS (scene-conditioned navigation) and LINGO (text-driven scene-aware motion).
- **Dynamic Setting:** LINGO (grasp/put-down actions) and CHOIS (language-guided human-object interaction with sparse waypoints).

#### 4.3. Evaluation Metrics

- **Semantic Alignment:** CLIP score (text-motion correspondence) and frame-wise CLIP consistency (temporal coherence).
- **Motion Diversity:** Mean per-joint Euclidean distance between generated sequences.
- **Physical Plausibility:** Scene penetration (Pene%, Pene_mean, Pene_max), foot sliding (FS), hand-object contact ratio (Cont.), and object penetration depth (Pene_obj). Penetration metrics are computed using Signed Distance Fields (SDFs).
- **Human Perceptual Study:** Two-alternative forced choice (2AFC) for realism and semantic alignment.

#### 4.4. Key Findings

- **Superior Generalizability and Semantic Alignment:**
 - Qualitatively, ZeroHSI generates more realistic and text-aligned 4D HSIs, particularly in unseen environments and for novel interaction types, where baselines often struggle with collisions or fail to produce meaningful interactions (e.g., LINGO failing to avoid collisions when picking snacks or sitting on sofa backs).
 - Quantitatively, ZeroHSI consistently achieves the highest CLIP scores in both static (23.52 vs. 22.61 for LINGO) and dynamic settings (24.01 vs. 22.99 for LINGO), indicating better semantic alignment with text prompts.

- **Enhanced Physical Plausibility:**
 - ZeroHSI significantly reduces scene penetration in static interactions (e.g., Pene_mean 0.147 vs. LINGO's 0.421), demonstrating better collision avoidance.
 - For dynamic interactions, ZeroHSI excels in maintaining proper object contact (Cont. 0.835 vs. CHOIS's 0.687 and LINGO's 0.699) and minimizes object penetration (Pene_obj 0.033 vs. CHOIS's 1.581 and LINGO's 0.242), highlighting its precise interaction handling.

- **Competitive Motion Diversity:** ZeroHSI maintains good motion diversity, competitive with or exceeding baselines.

- **Human Perceptual Preference:** A human perceptual study involving 400 participants strongly favors ZeroHSI's generated HSIs, rating them as more realistic and better aligned with textual descriptions by significant margins in both static and dynamic scenarios (e.g., 85.0% realism preference over TRUMANS, 96.5% over CHOIS).

- **Long-Term Interaction Synthesis:** ZeroHSI demonstrates the capability to generate cohesive long-term HSI sequences by iteratively using the last frame of a generated segment as the initial state for the next, showcasing its utility for complex narratives.

- **Robustness via Ablation Study:** The ablation study confirms the critical role of ZeroHSI's per-frame optimization approach. Replacing it with existing 2D-to-3D motion reconstruction methods (WHAM, WHAC) or simplifying the optimization ("ZeroHSI w/o OPT_body") leads to significant degradation in motion quality and physical plausibility, particularly regarding root translation accuracy and smoothness.

- **Model Agnosticism:** ZeroHSI proves compatible with various state-of-the-art video generation models (KLING 1.5/1.6, Wan 2.1, Sora), demonstrating its adaptability and ability to leverage future advancements in video generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ZeroHSI represents a significant advancement in human-scene interaction generation, offering a novel paradigm that addresses a fundamental limitation of previous methods: the reliance on expensive and scarce paired 3D MoCap and scene data. Its zero-shot capability makes it highly impactful for several reasons:

- **Democratization of HSI Generation:** By eliminating the need for scene-specific 3D training data, ZeroHSI democratizes HSI generation, making it accessible for novel and reconstructed environments (e.g., from NeRFs or photogrammetry) that lack pre-existing MoCap data. This greatly expands the applicability of HSI synthesis beyond controlled laboratory settings.
- **Enabling Embodied AI in Unstructured Environments:** For embodied AI agents, the ability to generate diverse and realistic interactions in arbitrary virtual or reconstructed real-world scenes is crucial for training, evaluation, and deployment. ZeroHSI can provide a rich source of interaction data and behavioral priors for agents, helping them learn how to navigate and manipulate objects in complex, unseen environments.
- **Revolutionizing VR/AR Content Creation:** In virtual and augmented reality, creating highly interactive and immersive experiences is key. ZeroHSI can dramatically accelerate the generation of realistic character behaviors for virtual avatars, NPCs, or even user-controlled characters interacting with dynamic environments, enabling richer narrative possibilities and more natural user experiences.
- **Advancing Robotics Simulation:** For robotics, ZeroHSI offers a powerful tool for simulating human-robot collaboration or human-like manipulation in diverse scenarios without the need for manual animation or costly data collection. This can aid in developing more robust and adaptable robotic control policies.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models:** The work highlights the immense potential of leveraging large-scale, pre-trained 2D generative models as powerful implicit priors for complex 3D tasks. This "2D-to-3D lifting" approach could inspire similar strategies in other domains where 3D data is scarce but related 2D data is abundant.
- **Robustness to Imperfect Data:** The specific techniques for handling "low-quality" generated video (e.g., incorrect content, appearance changes, body part disappearance) demonstrate the practical robustness of the method, making it deployable even with the current limitations of generative video models.

In conclusion, ZeroHSI offers a novel, data-efficient, and highly generalizable solution for 4D human-scene interaction synthesis. By cleverly bridging the gap between 2D video generation and 3D reconstruction, it pushes the boundaries of embodied AI and computer graphics, with profound implications for the future of virtual worlds, robotics, and interactive media.
