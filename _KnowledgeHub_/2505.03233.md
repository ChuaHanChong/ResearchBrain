# GraspVLA: a Grasping Foundation Model Pre-trained on Billion-scale Synthetic Action Data

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspVLA, a grasping foundation model from Galbot and Peking University, demonstrates robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and open-vocabulary grasping by pre-training exclusively on SynGrasp-1B, a billion-scale synthetic action dataset, achieving around 90% success rates on diverse real-world object categories.

## Problem

- Robot learning, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, faces a severe data bottleneck due to the high cost and labor intensity of collecting real-world robotic action data.
- Existing VLA models primarily rely on expensive real-world data, limiting scalability and generalization capabilities for diverse objects and environments.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex manipulation tasks like grasping, especially with synthetic-only pre-training, has been a significant challenge.

## Method

- Developed SynGrasp-1B, the first billion-frame, photorealistic synthetic robotic grasping dataset, generated with extensive domain randomization and detailed annotations for imitation learning.
- Introduced GraspVLA, a VLA model architecture that integrates a vision-language model with an action expert via a novel Progressive Action Generation (PAG) mechanism.
- Employed a unified training strategy that co-trains GraspVLA on SynGrasp-1B for action supervision and Internet semantics data (GRIT) for perception tasks, enabling open-vocabulary generalization.

## Results

- GraspVLA achieved around 90% success rates in zero-shot real-world grasping across five diverse test sets (lighting, background, distractors, etc.) for both synthetic and unseen web object categories, outperforming existing VLA generalists and imitation learning specialists.
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot performance in the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing fine-tuned baselines, and showed comparable language-conditioned grasping success (93.3%) with a state-of-the-art specialist, AnyGrasp (91.6%).
- Exhibited efficient few-shot adaptability, achieving 90% success on rare industrial components with only bounding box annotations and successfully learning specialized grasping patterns (80% success for mug-handle grasping) with minimal demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, meticulously designed synthetic data can exclusively pre-train a VLA model to achieve direct sim-to-real transfer and robust zero-shot generalization across diverse real-world conditions.
> - The Progressive Action Generation (PAG) mechanism, formulated as a Chain-of-Thought process with intermediate 2D bounding box and 3D grasp pose predictions, effectively combines synthetic action data with Internet semantics to enable open-vocabulary grasping for novel objects.
> - Performance consistently scales with increasing training data (frames and object diversity) in both real-world and simulation, particularly benefiting generalization to unseen object categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on GraspVLA is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of **embodied artificial intelligence**, particularly in the domain of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** and **foundation models for robotics**.

The paper explicitly acknowledges the paradigm shift brought by foundation models in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV), where large models pre-trained on vast internet-scale data demonstrate remarkable zero-shot generalization and few-shot adaptation capabilities. Inspired by this success, the robotics community has sought to extend this concept to the physical world, leading to the development of VLA models like RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, Octo, and GR00T N1. These models aim to enable robots to understand human instructions and generate actions directly from visual observations.

However, a critical challenge for VLA models in robotics is the **data bottleneck**. Unlike vision and language data, which are abundant on the internet, real-world robotic action data is inherently scarce, costly, and labor-intensive to collect. Existing approaches predominantly rely on expensive real-world data collection efforts, such as the Open-X-Embodiment (OXE) and DROID datasets, often involving teleoperation and diverse physical setups.

GraspVLA directly addresses this data bottleneck by advocating for and demonstrating the **underexplored potential of synthetic data** for training VLA models. While synthetic data has been used in prior works for open-loop grasp detection or as augmentation for human demonstrations, GraspVLA makes a significant leap by exploring the feasibility of *training VLA models entirely with large-scale synthetic action data*. This represents a novel pre-training paradigm that aims to drastically reduce the reliance on real-world data acquisition.

The paper focuses on **grasping**, a fundamental robotic manipulation skill, as a crucial first step in this direction. By achieving direct sim-to-real transfer and strong zero-shot generalization with synthetic-only pre-training, GraspVLA sets a new precedent and offers an alternative, more scalable path towards building generalist robot policies. Its co-training strategy with Internet semantics also distinguishes it by enabling open-vocabulary generalization, a key characteristic of advanced foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the GraspVLA project are rooted in overcoming the significant challenges associated with scaling embodied foundation models:

1. **Reduce Reliance on Costly Real-World Data:** The overarching motivation is to find a cost-effective and scalable alternative to labor-intensive and expensive real-world data collection for training VLA models. Synthetic data, generated in simulation, offers this alternative.
2. **Explore the Potential of Large-Scale Synthetic Data:** The paper aims to systematically investigate and demonstrate the viability of using exclusively large-scale synthetic action data for pre-training VLA models, a potential that has largely remained underexplored despite advances in simulation and rendering.
3. **Develop a Grasping Foundation Model with Strong Generalization:** The core objective is to create "GraspVLA," a foundational VLA model specifically for grasping tasks, capable of:
 - **Direct Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The model should generalize effectively from simulated training environments to diverse real-world scenarios without extensive real-world fine-tuning.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalization:** It should perform robustly on unseen objects, environments, lighting conditions, backgrounds, and with distractors without prior exposure.
 - **Open-Vocabulary Grasping:** The model should be able to grasp novel objects whose categories were not explicitly present in the synthetic action data but are covered by Internet semantics.
 - **Few-Shot Adaptability:** It should quickly learn and adapt to specialized grasping preferences or unique task constraints with minimal new demonstrations.
4. **Curate a Billion-Scale Synthetic Grasping Dataset:** A critical objective is the creation of SynGrasp-1B, a massive, photorealistic, and highly diverse synthetic dataset necessary to enable the training of such a foundational model.
5. **Propose an Effective Co-training Mechanism:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap and enable open-vocabulary capabilities, the paper aims to develop a novel mechanism (Progressive Action Generation, PAG) that effectively integrates learning from synthetic action data with knowledge derived from Internet semantics data.

In essence, the authors are motivated to unlock the scalability of foundation models for robotics by shifting the data burden from the physical world to the digital realm, thereby accelerating research and deployment of generalist robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GraspVLA project’s methodology is structured around two core pillars: the creation of a massive synthetic dataset and the design of a novel model architecture for efficient learning.

#### 3.1. SynGrasp-1B Dataset Generation

The foundation of GraspVLA is **SynGrasp-1B**, a billion-frame robotic grasping dataset generated entirely in simulation, marking it as the first dataset of this scale globally. The data generation pipeline is meticulously designed to ensure diversity, realism, and suitability for imitation learning:

- **Object Assets and Layout Generation:** Over 10,680 unique 3D object meshes from 240 categories (from the LVIS subset of Objaverse) are used, with unsuitable categories filtered out. Objects are randomly scaled and dropped onto a table in various poses to create diverse and physically plausible scenes. Manual size constraints and specific orientation definitions for certain objects (e.g., cups) ensure realistic scenarios. Object meshes are simplified for simulation efficiency.
- **Grasp Synthesis and Trajectory Generation:** For each object instance and initial layout, an expert policy generates high-quality trajectories. This involves:
 - Leveraging a grasp synthesis algorithm (e.g.,) to generate stable antipodal grasps.
 - Utilizing motion planning algorithms (CuRobo) to plan collision-free trajectories to reach open-loop grasp poses and lift the object.
 - Validating all candidate trajectories in the MuJoCo physics simulator to ensure successful object lifting.
- **Visual Randomization and Rendering:** High-quality RGB images are rendered using Isaac Sim, which offers efficient photorealistic ray-traced rendering. Extensive domain randomization is applied to:
 - **Lighting:** Various light sources (point, directional, dome lights) with extensive randomization.
 - **Backgrounds:** Randomized textures for ground and walls (1,200 unique textures) and various tablecloths.
 - **Materials:** Randomization of robot and table materials.
 - **Camera Views:** Images rendered from two different viewpoints (front and side cameras), with randomized extrinsics within a 15 cm radius and ±5° rotation around axes.
- **Efficient Data Generation:** To handle the scale, three strategies are employed: a caching mechanism for object meshes, asynchronous data writing (images and labels in parallel), and parallel physics simulation and rendering (using 160 NVIDIA 4090 GPUs for 10 days, costing approximately $5,000).
- **Tailoring for Imitation Learning:**
 - **Single-Step Motion Planning:** Instead of the typical two-step (pre-grasp/grasp) process, a single-step approach prioritizes trajectory smoothness, preventing hesitation in learned policies.
 - **Randomized Initial Robot Poses:** Enhances workspace exploration and observation diversity, improving model robustness.
- **Detailed Annotations:** SynGrasp-1B provides precise and fine-grained annotations for camera calibration, 2D bounding boxes, and 3D poses of the target object and gripper, which are crucial for training the VLA model.

#### 3.2. GraspVLA Model Architecture and Training

GraspVLA integrates a Vision-Language Model (VLM) with an action expert, connected through a novel **Progressive Action Generation (PAG)** mechanism.

- **Overall Architecture:**
 - **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Comprises a trainable large language model (InternLM2 1.8B), a vision encoder (fusing features from frozen DINO-v2 and SigLIP), and a trainable projector connecting vision and language spaces. It processes observation images and text instructions.
 - **Action Expert:** A conditional flow matching model responsible for fine-grained end-effector action generation.
- **Progressive Action Generation (PAG):** This is the core innovation for combining synthetic and Internet data, formulated as a **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)** process:
 - **Intermediate Perception Tasks:** VLM is trained to first generate 2D bounding boxes for target objects, a task shared by both Internet grounding datasets (e.g., GRIT) and SynGrasp-1B.
 - **Grasp Pose Prediction:** For synthetic data, the VLM additionally predicts the target grasp pose in the robot's base frame.
 - **Action Chunk Generation:** Finally, the action expert generates chunked end-effector delta actions, conditioned on the VLM's key-value cache from both input and intermediate reasoning tokens.
 - **Proprioception Integration:** Proprioceptive feedback from the latest two timesteps is tokenized and inserted before grasp pose generation to facilitate accurate 3D sensing.
 - **Unified Training Strategy:** Internet data is used to supervise the VLM's bounding box prediction (partial CoT), while synthetic data supervises the entire CoT pipeline (bounding box, grasp pose, and action prediction). This joint training leverages the rich semantic knowledge from the Internet and detailed geometric interaction information from synthetic data, mitigating sim-to-real gaps and enabling open-vocabulary grasping.
- **Joint Training of VLM and Action Expert:** The model is trained by sampling batches from both GRIT (for VLM bounding box supervision) and SynGrasp-1B (for VLM bounding box/grasp pose and action expert supervision). A combined loss function (sum of autoregressive VLM loss and flow matching action loss) is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GraspVLA demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various generalization benchmarks, validating its foundation model capabilities.

- **Zero-Shot Generalization in Real-World:**
 - **Superior Performance:** GraspVLA achieves consistently high success rates (around 90%) across five diverse real-world test sets (basic, lighting, background, distractors, height) for both synthetic (known) and web (unseen) object categories. This significantly outperforms existing VLA generalists (π0, OpenVLA, Octo) and imitation learning specialists (Diffusion Policy), even when baselines are fine-tuned on SynGrasp-1B.
 - **Open-Vocabulary Success:** Comparable performance on synthetic and web categories highlights the effectiveness of the PAG mechanism in transferring semantic knowledge to novel objects.
 - **Motion Efficiency:** GraspVLA achieves higher Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) scores, indicating more efficient and less hesitant grasping motions compared to baselines.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization in LIBERO Benchmark:**
 - GraspVLA demonstrates strong zero-shot performance (e.g., 82.0% on Long, 91.2% on Goal, 94.1% on Object suites), surpassing the performance of strong baselines (π0, OpenVLA) that were fine-tuned on the LIBERO dataset. This further reinforces its ability to generalize to unseen simulation environments and tasks.
 - The study also reveals that fine-tuned baselines are sensitive to instruction format variations, whereas GraspVLA shows more robustness.
- **Zero-Shot Comparison with AnyGrasp (Grasping Specialist):**
 - **Comparable Language-Conditioned Grasping:** GraspVLA achieves comparable success rates (93.3% vs. 91.6%) in language-conditioned grasping tasks against AnyGrasp, a state-of-the-art grasp detection model. GraspVLA's multi-view observation improves grounding.
 - **Robustness to Transparent Objects:** GraspVLA significantly outperforms AnyGrasp on transparent objects (86.6% vs. 10.0%), attributed to its reliance on RGB input rather than depth-sensing, which struggles with transparent materials.
 - **Inference Speed:** A limitation is GraspVLA's slower inference speed (5 Hz) compared to AnyGrasp (37 Hz), due to its large vision-language backbone.
- **Scaling Law:**
 - Performance consistently improves with an increasing number of training frames in both real-world and simulation, especially for web categories, suggesting that more data leads to better generalization.
 - Generalization to web categories benefits significantly from a broader diversity of training categories, while performance on synthetic categories saturates earlier.
 - Increasing the number of instances per category also leads to consistent improvements across both synthetic and web categories.
- **Efficient Few-Shot Post-Training:**
 - GraspVLA shows superior adaptability to novel tasks with minimal demonstrations. For grasping rare industrial components, it achieves 90% success with only bounding box annotations, demonstrating that new objects do not necessarily require action annotations, reducing data collection effort.
 - It successfully learns specialized grasping patterns (e.g., grasping a mug without touching its interior, 80% success) and sequential grasping behaviors in dense environments (90% success).
 - Pre-training on SynGrasp-1B is crucial, as training from scratch yields significantly lower performance.
- **Effectiveness of Design Choices (Ablation Study):**
 - The Progressive Action Generation (PAG) mechanism is critical. Introducing 2D bounding boxes as an intermediate step (PAG-2D) significantly improves performance, especially for web categories.
 - Further adding grasp pose prediction (PAG-3D) substantially reduces hesitation and improves grasping accuracy, leading to fewer attempts and shorter trajectories (higher SPL scores). PAG contributes to a significant portion of the inference delay (~63%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GraspVLA project represents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning and embodied AI, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Data Collection for Robotics:** GraspVLA pioneers a novel pre-training paradigm that relies *entirely on large-scale synthetic action data*. This fundamentally challenges the prevailing reliance on costly and labor-intensive real-world data collection, offering a scalable and cost-effective alternative. This shift could democratize access to large-scale robotic data and accelerate research in robot learning.
2. **Demonstration of Sim-to-Real Transfer at Scale:** By achieving direct sim-to-real transfer and robust zero-shot generalization across diverse real-world conditions, GraspVLA proves that meticulously designed synthetic data and training methodologies can bridge the simulation-reality gap for complex manipulation tasks like grasping.
3. **Advancement of Grasping Capabilities:** GraspVLA pushes the boundaries of open-vocabulary grasping, enabling robots to grasp novel objects from categories unseen during synthetic pre-training by leveraging Internet semantics. Its robustness to challenging scenarios like transparent objects and cluttered environments is a notable improvement over prior methods.
4. **Enabling Generalist Robot Policies:** The model's strong zero-shot generalization and efficient few-shot adaptability to user preferences and specialized tasks are hallmarks of a "foundation model" for actions. This capability paves the way for more versatile and adaptable robots that can quickly learn new skills and operate in unstructured, dynamic environments.
5. **Introduction of SynGrasp-1B:** The release of SynGrasp-1B, the first billion-frame grasping dataset, provides an invaluable resource for the research community. This massive dataset, with its photorealistic rendering, extensive domain randomization, and detailed annotations, can serve as a benchmark and training ground for future work in synthetic data-driven robot learning.
6. **Progressive Action Generation (PAG) as a Key Innovation:** The PAG mechanism, combining autoregressive perception with flow-matching-based action generation in a Chain-of-Thought process, offers a powerful strategy for integrating complementary data sources (synthetic actions and Internet semantics). This design is critical for achieving open-vocabulary generalization and could inspire similar architectures in other robot learning contexts.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Accelerated Robot Development:** By reducing the data acquisition bottleneck, GraspVLA's approach could significantly speed up the development and deployment of intelligent robotic systems in various industries, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics.
- **More Accessible Robot Learning:** Lowering the barrier of entry for large-scale robot learning could enable smaller research groups and individual researchers to contribute to the field without requiring extensive physical robot setups.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Generalizable grasping and adaptable policies are crucial for robots to operate autonomously in complex, human-centric environments, where they encounter novel objects and require flexible task execution.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors also acknowledge several limitations, which represent important directions for future work:
- **Hardware Generalization:** Current data generation and evaluation focus on a single robot arm (Franka Panda) and camera configuration. Expanding to other robots and camera setups is an engineering effort but crucial for true cross-embodiment generalization.
- **Ambiguous Instructions & Semantic Reasoning:** GraspVLA struggles with vague instructions ("pick up food") or complex spatial reasoning ("pick up the leftmost object"), indicating a need for more advanced vision-language pre-training and architectural innovations for deeper semantic understanding.
- **Deformable Objects:** The reliance on force-closure for grasp labels means it shares a common limitation with most grasping policies regarding deformable objects. Integration of soft-body simulation could address this.
- **Task Scope:** Currently focused on grasping, expanding the data generation pipeline and model design to other manipulation tasks (pick-and-place, pushing) and more complex tasks (non-prehensile manipulation via reinforcement learning) is planned.
- **Inference Latency:** The autoregressive nature of PAG introduces additional latency (around 200ms), which might be too slow for dynamic environments. Research into distillation and quantization techniques is needed to improve real-time performance.

Despite these limitations, GraspVLA stands out as a foundational work, demonstrating a compelling vision for scalable, generalizable, and adaptable robot learning through the strategic exploitation of synthetic data.
