# Asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization: mini-critics boost LLM reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (AsyPPO) enhances Large Language Model reasoning by integrating lightweight mini-critics into the PPO framework, which reduces peak memory usage by 20% and accelerates training time by approximately 20 seconds per step. This approach yields performance gains of over 6% on Qwen3-4b-Base models across various challenging reasoning benchmarks compared to classic PPO.

## Problem

- Traditional PPO's symmetric actor-critic design is computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive for large language models.
- Large critics often struggle to provide accurate value estimates in LLM reasoning tasks characterized by sparse rewards and long reasoning chains.
- Critic-free methods in RL4LLM sacrifice robust state value estimation, which is crucial for mitigating advantage bias and preventing training instability.

## Method

- **AsyPPO** introduces an asymmetric actor-critic architecture, utilizing lightweight ==mini-critics== to guide much larger LLM actors.
- An ensemble of mini-critics is trained using group-level non-overlapping data partitioning to achieve robust and diverse value estimation.
- The PPO objective function is refined using inter-critic uncertainty, incorporating advantage masking and entropy filtering to focus learning on informative states and guide exploration.

## Results

- AsyPPO achieved performance gains exceeding **6%** on Qwen3-4b-Base and around **3%** on Qwen3-8b-Base and Qwen3-14b-Base models across reasoning benchmarks compared to classic PPO.
- The asymmetric architecture reduced peak memory usage by **20%** and accelerated training time by approximately 20 seconds per step, maintaining efficiency comparable to critic-free methods.
- Uncertainty-aware objective refinements, including advantage masking and entropy filtering based on critic disagreement, effectively boosted learning efficiency and stabilized policy training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The rich representational abilities of pre-trained LLMs enable smaller, asymmetric critics to effectively guide significantly larger actors.
> - Training an ensemble of mini-critics on disjoint response subsets within prompts, rather than random splits, ensures functional diversity and reliable value estimation.
> - Inter-critic agreement and divergence patterns provide a precise signal to dynamically adjust the policy's learning, improving sample efficiency and promoting safer exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models (RL4LLM) has seen significant advancements, particularly with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) emerging as a transformative algorithm for post-training LLMs to enhance their reasoning abilities. However, the application of classical PPO to LLMs introduces unprecedented computational challenges due to the sheer scale of these models, which are orders of magnitude larger than traditional RL environments.

Conventionally, PPO employs a symmetric actor-critic design where the critic network is often as large as the actor network. This setup becomes computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive when applied to LLMs. Furthermore, training these large critics effectively is challenging, as value estimation often struggles with sparse rewards and long reasoning horizons, leading to inaccuracies (Yuan et al., 2025b).

In response to these challenges, the RL4LLM community has largely moved away from explicit critics. Algorithms like GRPO (He et al., 2025), GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025), and DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) have gained prominence by replacing conventional value functions with group sampling and average-advantage baselines, providing coarse-grained advantage estimates. While these critic-free approaches have demonstrated success, the authors argue that this paradigmatic shift sacrifices a crucial aspect of classical RL: the ability of robust state value estimation to mitigate training instability caused by advantage bias, especially in off-policy settings (Liu et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2025b).

This paper critically re-examines this "critic bottleneck" not merely as an algorithmic or optimization problem, but as an architectural one. It directly challenges the prevailing trend of sidelining critics in RL4LLM by proposing an alternative approach that restores their role. By doing so, this work aims to bridge the gap between the computational demands of LLMs and the desire for robust, critic-guided policy optimization, thereby seeking to enhance stability and performance in a computationally efficient manner. It positions itself as a fundamental reconsideration of how actor-critic architectures can be adapted for the unique context of LLMs, leveraging their pre-trained capabilities to enable more efficient learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a lightweight yet robust method for value estimation within the PPO framework, specifically tailored for the computational demands of Large Language Models. The authors pose a precise central question that guides their investigation: **"Can we achieve lightweight yet robust value estimation by redesigning PPO to depart from the standard symmetric actor–critic architecture, enabling stable and efficient learning?"**

The motivation behind pursuing this objective stems from several critical observations and challenges in the current RL4LLM landscape:
- **Computational Bottleneck of Symmetric Critics**: Traditional PPO's symmetric actor-critic design, where the critic is as large as the LLM actor, incurs significant computational overhead in terms of memory usage and training time, rendering it impractical for large-scale LLMs.
- **Inaccuracy under Sparse and Long-Horizon Rewards**: Even if computational costs could be managed, large critics often struggle to provide accurate value estimates in LLM reasoning tasks characterized by sparse rewards and lengthy reasoning chains.
- **Loss of Robustness in Critic-Free Methods**: The widespread adoption of critic-free methods (e.g., GRPO) in RL4LLM, while pragmatic, abandons the principle of robust state value estimation. The authors argue that this robust estimation is crucial for mitigating advantage bias and preventing training collapse, particularly in off-policy learning scenarios.
- **Leveraging Pre-trained LLM Capabilities**: A key insight motivating their approach is that LLMs inherit rich representational abilities from their pre-training. The authors hypothesize that this pre-existing knowledge can enable smaller, asymmetric critics to effectively guide much larger actors, a paradigm different from classical deep RL where agents learn from scratch.
- **Improving Learning Stability and Performance**: Ultimately, the goal is to enhance the stability of RL training for LLMs and achieve superior reasoning performance without incurring the prohibitive costs associated with conventional full-sized critics. They seek to reduce critic over-parameterization, memory footprint, and training time while simultaneously boosting the quality of policy optimization.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to reintroduce the benefits of explicit critics into RL4LLM in a scalable and efficient manner, thus improving the overall effectiveness of reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (AsyPPO), departs from the conventional symmetric actor-critic architecture by employing lightweight mini-critics and refining the PPO objective function based on inter-critic uncertainty.

The core approach of AsyPPO is built upon several key components:

1. **Asymmetric Actor-Critic Architecture**:
 - **Initial Hypothesis**: The authors first empirically demonstrate that a small critic (e.g., Qwen3-0.6b-Base) can provide meaningful guidance to a much larger actor (e.g., Qwen3-8b-Base) due to the rich representational ability inherited from pre-trained LLMs. This initial finding validates the potential of an asymmetric setup.
 - **Limitation of Single Small Critic**: However, a single small critic still underperforms a classical symmetric PPO setup, indicating limitations in its value estimation capabilities for complex, long-tail reasoning.

2. **Robust Value Estimation with Ensemble Mini-Critics**:
 - **Naive Ensemble Challenge**: To enhance the small critic's capacity, the authors consider using an ensemble of critics. However, simply ensembling critics initialized from the same pre-trained model and trained on identical data leads to nearly identical behavior, offering no corrective benefit.
 - **Group-level Non-overlapping Data Partitioning**: To address the lack of diversity, AsyPPO introduces a novel data partitioning strategy. Instead of random data splits, critics are trained on *disjoint subsets of responses within each prompt (or group)*. This ensures that each critic sees an equal share of responses per prompt, maintaining "perceptual synchrony" and preventing inconsistent reasoning patterns, while still encouraging functional diversity by exposing them to different reward distributions within the prompt's trajectories.
 - **Optimal Number of Critics**: Empirically, two mini-critics are found to be the "sweet spot" (Figure 9b), providing a qualitative leap in evaluation reliability with minimal redundancy, thereby optimizing for both correction capability and efficiency. The mean of the ensemble's value estimates is used for advantage computation (Eq. 3).

3. **Policy Loss Reconstruction via Uncertainty-Aware Objective Refinement**:
 - AsyPPO leverages the agreement and divergence patterns among its ensemble of mini-critics as a signal to refine the policy optimization objective, enhancing sample efficiency and exploration.
 - **Advantage Masking (based on critic agreement)**:
 - **Insight**: High agreement among critics (low standard deviation of value estimates, $\sigma_t$) suggests that a state is well-modeled, low-informative, and frequently encountered, offering limited learning signal. Overfitting to such states can be detrimental.
 - **Mechanism**: The policy loss (Eq. 4) is modified to mask advantages for states where critics strongly agree (lowest $k\%$ of $\sigma_t$). This suppresses gradient updates from low-informative transitions, focusing policy learning on higher-value, more uncertain data.
 - **Entropy Filtering (based on critic divergence)**:
 - **Insight**: High divergence among critics (high $\sigma_t$) implies that a state might be reasoning-independent, weakly coupled to the final outcome, or noisy (e.g., inference-irrelevant tokens). Persistent exploration in such states is wasteful.
 - **Mechanism**: The entropy regularization term in the policy loss (Eq. 5) is modified to filter out states with high value divergence (highest $h\%$ of $\sigma_t$). This promotes "safer exploration" by avoiding spurious updates on non-decision-making states, preventing entropy collapse, and guiding the policy towards more meaningful exploration.

In essence, AsyPPO orchestrates a team of lightweight critics, each learning from a distinct slice of prompt-level data, to provide robust value estimates. It then ingeniously uses the collective uncertainty of these critics to dynamically adjust the policy's learning signal, focusing on informative states and guiding exploration away from noisy or unproductive regions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various LLM scales and benchmarks consistently demonstrate the efficacy and efficiency of AsyPPO.

1. **Significant Performance Gains**:
 - AsyPPO consistently improves learning stability and performance across multiple challenging reasoning benchmarks, including MATH-500, OlympiadBench, MinervaMath, and AMC 2023.
 - It achieved performance gains of **>6% on Qwen3-4b-Base** and **about 3% on Qwen3-8b-Base and Qwen3-14b-Base** compared to classic PPO, without additional complex tricks.
 - When compared to strong baselines like GRPO, AsyPPO with two 4b critics achieved an average accuracy improvement of approximately **3 percentage points** on the Qwen3-14b-Base actor across evaluated tasks (Figure 8).

2. **Remarkable Computational Efficiency**:
 - AsyPPO's asymmetric architecture significantly reduces computational overhead. It decreases **peak memory usage by 20%** and accelerates **training time by around 20 seconds per step** compared to classic symmetric PPO (Figure 1b).
 - The framework maintains running time and peak GPU memory usage at levels comparable to the critic-free GRPO baseline, demonstrating that it successfully restores the critic's role without incurring the traditional computational burden.

3. **Validation of Asymmetric Architecture and Mini-Critic Design**:
 - Initial experiments confirmed that even a small critic (e.g., Qwen3-0.6b-Base) can provide meaningful guidance to larger actors (e.g., Qwen3-8b-Base), validating the core hypothesis of the asymmetric design (Figure 3, Left).
 - The group-level non-overlapping data partitioning strategy was crucial for enabling diversity among mini-critics, which naive ensembles failed to achieve (Figure 3, Middle & Right). This diversity led to reliable guidance for larger policies.
 - Empirical analysis (Figure 9b) revealed that **two mini-critics represent the sweet spot** for balancing correction capability and efficiency, providing a clear qualitative leap in performance compared to one critic, with diminishing returns for more critics.
 - Increasing the size of the mini-critics (e.g., from 0.6b to 8b) further enhanced AsyPPO's effectiveness, showing a scaling-law–like trend (Figure 9a), suggesting that the largest feasible critic size within memory constraints is beneficial.
 - The "mean" aggregation strategy for critic values outperformed "min" (Figure 9d), indicating that overestimation bias might not be the dominant issue in this context.

4. **Effectiveness of Objective Refinement Mechanisms**:
 - **Advantage Masking**: Masking 20% of states with the highest critic agreement (lowest value standard deviation) substantially boosted AsyPPO’s learning efficiency, particularly in high data reuse settings (UTD=4). Value-standard-deviation (critic-side uncertainty) based masking consistently outperformed entropy (policy-side uncertainty) based masking (Figure 5b,c), suggesting its precision as an uncertainty metric (Figure 6).
 - **Entropy Filtering**: Excluding 20% of states with the highest critic divergence (highest value standard deviation) from entropy regularization proved optimal. This mechanism prevented entropy collapse, stabilized policy learning, avoided spurious exploration, and guided the policy towards better convergence and higher returns (Figure 7b,c). Statistical analysis confirmed that filtered tokens were often reasoning-irrelevant (e.g., adverbs, interjections), supporting the mechanism's rationale (Figure 11, Right).

In summary, AsyPPO not only achieves superior reasoning performance but does so with significantly improved computational efficiency, establishing a highly practical and scalable design for RL4LLM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (AsyPPO) represents a significant contribution to the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models (RL4LLM) by fundamentally reframing and resolving the "critic bottleneck."

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Architectural Innovation in RL4LLM**: This work shifts the focus from purely algorithmic or optimization solutions to an architectural one, demonstrating that the critic's role can be effectively and efficiently restored in LLM-scale training through a clever design. This challenges the prevailing critic-free paradigm in RL4LLM.
- **Enhanced Performance and Stability**: AsyPPO consistently delivers measurable performance gains across diverse reasoning benchmarks, improving learning stability and achieving higher accuracy over strong baselines like GRPO and classic PPO. This makes it a more reliable and powerful tool for post-training LLMs.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability**: By employing lightweight mini-critics and reducing critic over-parameterization, AsyPPO dramatically cuts down peak memory usage and accelerates training time. This is critical for making advanced RL techniques feasible and scalable for increasingly larger LLMs, which often face severe computational constraints.
- **Novel Policy Optimization Mechanisms**: The introduction of uncertainty-aware advantage masking and entropy filtering, derived from inter-critic disagreement, provides a powerful and intuitive way to refine the PPO objective. These mechanisms improve sample efficiency, prevent overfitting to uninformative states, and promote safer, more targeted exploration, which are long-standing challenges in RL.
- **Leveraging Pre-training**: The approach effectively capitalizes on the rich representational capabilities inherent in pre-trained LLMs, showcasing how existing knowledge can be harnessed to design more efficient learning systems.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Broader Adoption of Critic-Based RL4LLM**: AsyPPO's success in mitigating the computational challenges associated with critics might encourage a renewed interest in critic-based RL for LLMs, potentially leading to the development of even more sophisticated value estimation techniques tailored for language models.
- **More Accessible LLM Fine-tuning**: By making RL training more efficient, AsyPPO could lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners to fine-tune and align LLMs, potentially democratizing access to powerful reasoning capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The paper opens up several intriguing avenues for future work, such as exploring heterogeneous ensemble critics (different model families and sizes), investigating confidence-weighted aggregation schemes for critics, and further analyzing the intricate relationship between value uncertainty and policy entropy.
- **Improved AI Safety and Alignment**: More robust and stable RL training, coupled with principled exploration strategies, could indirectly contribute to developing LLMs that are better aligned with human intent and exhibit more reliable reasoning.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations that will be addressed in future work:
- **Model Family Specificity**: Experiments were primarily conducted on the Qwen3 series of models. Generalization to other LLM families (e.g., Llama) needs to be verified.
- **Fixed Generation Length**: All experiments used a fixed maximum generation length of 8k tokens. The algorithm's performance under ultra-long inference budgets requires further assessment.
- **Robustness of Conclusions**: The authors plan to use a more diverse set of random seeds to further strengthen the robustness of their conclusions.

Despite these limitations, AsyPPO marks a significant step forward in making reinforcement learning a more practical, stable, and powerful tool for enhancing the reasoning abilities of large language models. Its architectural and algorithmic innovations provide a blueprint for developing the next generation of efficient RL4LLM systems.
