# Asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization: mini-critics boost LLM reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (AsyPPO) enhances Large Language Model reasoning by integrating lightweight mini-critics into the PPO framework, which reduces peak memory usage by 20% and accelerates training time by approximately 20 seconds per step. This approach yields performance gains of over 6% on Qwen3-4b-Base models across various challenging reasoning benchmarks compared to classic PPO.

## Problem

- Traditional PPO's symmetric actor-critic design is computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive for large language models.
- Large critics often struggle to provide accurate value estimates in LLM reasoning tasks characterized by sparse rewards and long reasoning chains.
- Critic-free methods in RL4LLM sacrifice robust state value estimation, which is crucial for mitigating advantage bias and preventing training instability.

## Method

- **AsyPPO** introduces an asymmetric actor-critic architecture, utilizing lightweight ==mini-critics== to guide much larger LLM actors.
- An ensemble of mini-critics is trained using group-level non-overlapping data partitioning to achieve robust and diverse value estimation.
- The PPO objective function is refined using inter-critic uncertainty, incorporating advantage masking and entropy filtering to focus learning on informative states and guide exploration.

## Results

- AsyPPO achieved performance gains exceeding **6%** on Qwen3-4b-Base and around **3%** on Qwen3-8b-Base and Qwen3-14b-Base models across reasoning benchmarks compared to classic PPO.
- The asymmetric architecture reduced peak memory usage by **20%** and accelerated training time by approximately 20 seconds per step, maintaining efficiency comparable to critic-free methods.
- Uncertainty-aware objective refinements, including advantage masking and entropy filtering based on critic disagreement, effectively boosted learning efficiency and stabilized policy training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The rich representational abilities of pre-trained LLMs enable smaller, asymmetric critics to effectively guide significantly larger actors.
> - Training an ensemble of mini-critics on disjoint response subsets within prompts, rather than random splits, ensures functional diversity and reliable value estimation.
> - Inter-critic agreement and divergence patterns provide a precise signal to dynamically adjust the policy's learning, improving sample efficiency and promoting safer exploration.
