# Curriculum Generation under Structured Parametric Environments for Robust Navigation Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Reparam is a curriculum-generation framework for RL navigation in continuously parameterized environments. It reparameterizes uniform environment sampling so the PPO value network's gradient flows to the curriculum's upper difficulty bounds, updating them only unidirectionally, with an optional distribution-shift penalty between consecutive curricula. On modified Car Racing and Bipedal Walker, it beats vanilla, random, manual, SPRL, ALP-GMM, frontier, and reverse-curriculum baselines over five seeds.

## Problem

- Training on fixed environment distributions limits deployment robustness, while domain randomization samples statically and never adapts difficulty to the learner's current competence.
- Unsupervised environment design and minimax/adversarial curricula rely on black-box or population-based optimization that is costly and unstable in continuous parameterized spaces.
- Competence-based methods like SPRL and ALP-GMM use stochastic task selection rather than direct gradient updates over coupled continuous environment parameters such as curvature and obstacle density.

## Method

- ==Reparameterized curriculum sampling== writes each environment as the lower bound plus uniform noise times the bound gap, making the ==critic== objective differentiable with respect to the upper difficulty bounds.
- A ==unidirectional update rule== advances upper bounds only when the value gradient is negative, with per-parameter annealed learning rates and a normalization factor over the feasible range.
- An ==MSE distribution-shift regularizer== penalizes squared distance between current and previous sampled parameters, damping abrupt curriculum jumps for the multimodal ==Nature CNN== plus ==MLP== policy.

## Results

- On Car Racing over **500** test environments and **five** seeds, Reparam-M reaches **650** reward versus **617** manual, **557** random, **540** vanilla, **528** SPRL, **508** ALP-GMM, and **500** frontier.
- On Bipedal Walker, Reparam attains **127** reward and **128** steps covered against **117** manual, **116** ALP-GMM, **111** random, **90** vanilla, and **88** SPRL, at comparable training cost.
- Ablations show regularization helps only image-based Car Racing (**650** versus **448**), while bidirectional updates hurt (**623** Car Racing, **110** Bipedal Walker), as difficulty reduction slows progress.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reparameterizing the sampling distribution turns curriculum design into a differentiable subproblem, letting an existing value network supply difficulty gradients with no extra teacher model.
> - Curriculum monotonicity matters: permitting difficulty to decrease when value is positive stalls learning, and retaining easy samples inside the growing range mitigates catastrophic forgetting.
> - Distribution-shift regularization pays off mainly with high-dimensional visual observations, where latent representations are shift-sensitive, and adds little for low-dimensional proprioceptive inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader research landscape in reinforcement learning (RL) frequently addresses the challenge of training autonomous agents that can operate robustly across diverse and continuously varying environmental conditions. Model-free RL, while effective in simulated environments, often produces policies with limited generalization capabilities when deployed in scenarios that deviate from their training distributions. Curriculum learning has emerged as a principled approach to enhance generalization by progressively adapting training environments, guiding the agent from simpler to more complex tasks.

Existing curriculum generation methods can be broadly categorized. Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) techniques, for instance, generate adversarial or progressively difficult tasks, often leveraging teacher policies or environment generators. While UED has demonstrated effectiveness across various domains, many implementations rely on computationally intensive black-box or population-based optimization strategies, which may become inefficient in continuous parameterized environments. Other robust and minimax RL approaches frame training as a game against adversarial perturbations, while Bayesian optimization has also been explored for curriculum adaptation, though these too can be computationally demanding and non-differentiable.

Automatic curriculum learning methods, such as Self-Paced Reinforcement Learning (SPRL), Absolute Learning Progress with Gaussian Mixture Models (ALP-GMM), and Prioritized Level Replay (PLR), adapt continuously parameterized environments using competence- or progress-based sampling. However, these methods typically employ stochastic task selection strategies rather than direct, gradient-based updates over the environment parameters themselves. Domain randomization also contributes to robustness by sampling diverse environment parameters but lacks the adaptive nature of curriculum learning, as it does not adjust to the agent's evolving competence.

This research aims to address these limitations by introducing a reparameterized curriculum generation framework that leverages unidirectional gradient-based optimization directly over structured, low-dimensional continuous environment parameter spaces. By focusing on direct gradient-based updates, the proposed method seeks to offer a more sample-efficient and controlled approach to curriculum evolution compared to black-box optimization or stochastic sampling techniques. Furthermore, it incorporates a distribution-shift regularization objective to enhance robustness, particularly in multimodal observation spaces like those involving image-based inputs, aiming to foster the learning of finer-grained latent representations. This positions the work as an advancement in automated, efficient, and stable curriculum design for continuous control problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a sample-efficient and automated curriculum generation framework for training robust navigation policies within structured continuous environment parameter spaces. The framework specifically targets challenges in environments where parameters, such as turn rates, obstacles, friction, pits, and slopes, continuously vary and jointly influence policy performance.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current reinforcement learning approaches and existing curriculum generation methods:

1. **Limited Robustness and Generalization:** Model-free reinforcement learning policies, when trained on fixed environment distributions, often exhibit poor robustness and generalization capabilities when deployed in conditions that differ from the training setup. Autonomous agents, particularly for navigation, require policies that can generalize across a wide spectrum of continuously varying environmental conditions.
2. **Computational Inefficiency and Instability of Existing Curriculum Methods:** Many existing curriculum learning approaches, including black-box optimization, adversarial/minimax formulations, or population-based methods, can be computationally expensive and may suffer from instability when applied to continuous parameterized environments. These methods often require significant computational resources or extensive trial-and-error to design effective curricula.
3. **Lack of Direct Gradient-Based Adaptation:** A significant number of automatic curriculum learning methods rely on stochastic task selection or competence/progress-based sampling strategies rather than direct, differentiable updates to curriculum parameters. This can limit the efficiency and control over how the curriculum evolves, especially in environments defined by coupled continuous parameters.
4. **Challenges in Multimodal Observation Spaces:** Environments that combine different types of observations, such as image-based inputs and scalar features, can pose additional challenges for curriculum learning. Learning robust latent representations in such multimodal spaces, particularly when environmental parameters shift, can be difficult.
5. **Need for Automated and Principled Curriculum Design:** The manual design of effective curricula is often a labor-intensive and expert-driven process. There is a need for automated and principled mechanisms that can adapt training environments in a sample-efficient manner, reducing the reliance on human intervention.

To address these motivations, the proposed framework aims to provide a more controlled and efficient mechanism for curriculum adaptation by using gradient-based optimization directly over the continuous environment parameter bounds. The incorporation of a distribution-shift regularization objective further motivates improving the learning of latent representations in complex, multimodal observation settings, thereby contributing to more stable curriculum evolution and enhanced policy robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed Reparameterization Curriculum (Reparam), utilizes a framework comprising two core modules: a policy network and a curriculum critic. The approach focuses on gradient-based curriculum adaptation over coupled continuous environment parameters and integrates a distribution-shift regularization objective.

#### 3.1. Reparam Curriculum Framework

The Reparam framework operates with a policy network, parameterized by $\theta$, and a critic network, parameterized by $\phi$.
- **Policy Network:** The policy network learns control policies conditioned on both environment observations ($x$) and the current environment parameters ($\psi$).
 - For the Car Racing environment, which involves multimodal observations, a multi-input architecture is used. RGB bird's-eye-view images are processed by a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), while the 2-element environment parameters $\psi = (\kappa, p)$ (road curvature, obstacle probability) are encoded via a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The features from both streams are then fused for policy learning.
 - For the Bipedal Walker environment, 24-element proprioceptive state features are concatenated with the 3-element environment parameters $\psi = (f, \rho, s)$ (friction, pit frequency, slope magnitude) and processed through an MLP.
 - Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed to optimize the policy using clipped policy and value objectives.
- **Curriculum Critic:** The critic is responsible for adapting the curriculum's difficulty by estimating the policy's value across environments sampled within the current curriculum bounds.

#### 3.2. Curriculum Critic Mechanism

The curriculum adaptation process by the critic involves several steps:
1. **Environment Sampling:** Environments are sampled uniformly within the current lower ($\psi_{\min}$) and upper ($\psi_t$) curriculum bounds: $\psi_i \sim U[\psi_{\min}, \psi_t]$.
2. **Reparameterization:** To enable gradient-based updates, the environment sampling is reparameterized. Each parameter $\psi_i[j]$ for the $j$-th dimension is expressed as $\psi_i[j] = \psi_{\min}[j] + z_i[j](\psi_t[j] - \psi_{\min}[j])$, where $z_i[j] \sim U$. This reparameterization allows for the computation of gradients with respect to the upper curriculum bounds $\psi_t[j]$, where $\frac{\partial\psi_i[j]}{\partial\psi_t[j]} = z_i[j]$.
3. **Critic Objective:** The critic objective ($L$) is computed over $n$ sampled environments. It is based on the value function network ($V_\phi$) from the PPO algorithm.
 - **Distribution-Shift Regularization (Optional):** An optional term, $-\beta\delta(\psi_i, \psi_{prev_i})$, is included in the objective. Here, $\delta(\psi_i, \psi_{prev_i}) = ||\psi_i - \psi_{prev_i}||^2$ represents the squared Euclidean distance between the current sampled environment parameters and the parameters from the previous curriculum distribution. This term regularizes the curriculum evolution, encouraging more stable transitions between distributions, particularly relevant for multimodal observation spaces. The parameter $\beta \geq 0$ controls the strength of this regularization. The overall critic objective is $L = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i} (V_\phi(x_i, \psi_i) - \beta\delta(\psi_i, \psi_{prev_i}))$.
4. **Gradient-Based Curriculum Update:** The critic computes gradients of its objective with respect to the upper curriculum bounds, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial\psi_t[j]}$.
 - **Unidirectional Update:** The curriculum upper bounds $\psi_t[j]$ are updated only if the gradient $\frac{\partial L}{\partial\psi_t[j]} < 0$. A negative gradient indicates that increasing the curriculum bound for that parameter would decrease the estimated policy value, implying that the policy is proficient enough to handle more difficult environments. If the gradient is non-negative, the bound remains unchanged, preventing the curriculum from becoming easier.
 - **Learning Rate Annealing and Normalization:** The curriculum update uses an annealed learning rate, $lr_{anneal, \psi[j],t} = \frac{lr_{\psi[j]}}{\alpha^t}$, and a normalization constant, $\eta_{\psi[j],t} = \frac{\psi_t[j] - \psi_{\min}[j]}{\psi_{\max}[j] - \psi_{\min}[j]}$, where $\psi_{\max}$ are the maximum feasible parameter ranges. This ensures controlled and adaptive updates.

#### 3.3. Environments and Metrics

- **Car Racing Environment:** Parameters are road curvature ($\kappa \in [0.31, 0.71]$) and obstacle probability ($p \in [0.05, 0.13]$). Observations include RGB images and $(\kappa, p)$. Rewards incentivize track completion, penalizing delays and obstacle collisions. Metrics include episodic rewards, obstacle collisions, collision ratio, time on grass, and tiles visited.
- **Bipedal Walker Environment:** Parameters are terrain friction ($f \in [2.0, 3.0]$), pit frequency ($\rho \in [0.0, 0.2]$), and terrain slope magnitude ($s \in [0.0, 0.01]$). Observations include proprioceptive state features and $(f, \rho, s)$. Rewards encourage stable forward locomotion, penalizing unstable posture, excessive torque, and falls. Metrics include episodic rewards, pit-fall ratio, slope-failure termination ratio, slip-failure termination ratio, and walking steps completed.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Training:** The curriculum critic adaptively adjusts the running upper bounds ($\psi_t$) from fixed lower bounds ($\psi_{\min}$), exposing the policy to progressively challenging environments.
- **Evaluation and Testing:** During these phases, environments are uniformly sampled from the full feasible parameter ranges ($[\psi_{\min}, \psi_{\max}]$) to assess the policy's generalization capabilities.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against Vanilla Policy, Random Sampling, Manual Curriculum, SPRL, ALP-GMM, Frontier Curriculum, and Reparam-R (reverse curriculum).
- **Ablations:** Three variants are studied: Reparam-A (bidirectional gradient updates), Reparam-M (unidirectional updates with MSE regularization), and Reparam (unidirectional updates without MSE regularization).
- **General Settings:** All methods are trained for 1M timesteps with 5 random seeds using PPO (1000 timestep update frequency, 10 epochs per policy update). An environment set size of $n=200$ is used for stable critic gradient estimates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed Reparameterization Curriculum (Reparam) framework, along with its ablations and various baselines, was conducted across the Car Racing and Bipedal Walker environments. Performance was assessed using a set of quantitative metrics, primarily focusing on mean episodic reward and specific environment-interaction statistics.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Ablation Analysis

- **Car Racing Environment:** In the Car Racing environment, **Reparam-M** (Reparam with MSE-based distribution-shift regularization) achieved the highest average testing reward (650 ± 134), outperforming all other baselines and ablations. It also demonstrated competitive performance in secondary metrics, such as collision ratio and time on grass, compared to other curriculum methods. The standard Reparam method (without MSE regularization) showed lower performance (448 ± 122).
- **Bipedal Walker Environment:** In the Bipedal Walker environment, **Reparam** (unidirectional updates without MSE regularization) yielded the highest average testing reward (127 ± 136). Reparam-M, which includes MSE regularization, showed slightly lower performance (112 ± 131) in this environment compared to Reparam.
- **Impact of MSE Regularization:** The differential performance of Reparam-M across the two environments indicates that the auxiliary distribution-shift regularization objective is particularly beneficial in contexts involving multimodal observation spaces and complex feature extraction, such as the Car Racing environment with its Nature CNN + MLP policy architecture. The regularization appeared to improve stability and performance by encouraging the learning of finer-grained latent representations from image-based inputs. Conversely, for the Bipedal Walker environment, which relies on lower-dimensional proprioceptive states processed by an MLP, the MSE regularization provided limited to no additional benefit, and in some cases, slightly reduced performance.
- **Impact of Unidirectional vs. Bidirectional Updates:** The Reparam framework, which employs unidirectional gradient updates (only increasing curriculum difficulty when estimated value decreases), consistently outperformed **Reparam-A** (bidirectional gradient updates, allowing difficulty to increase or decrease). Reparam-A achieved an average reward of 623 ± 153 in Car Racing and 110 ± 137 in Bipedal Walker, which was lower than the best performing Reparam variants. This finding suggests that allowing the curriculum to decrease difficulty (when value is positive) can hinder overall learning progress and policy robustness by potentially stagnating the exposure to increasingly challenging environments.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

- **Superiority over Non-Curriculum Baselines:** Reparam and Reparam-M significantly outperformed baseline methods that did not employ adaptive curriculum generation. Vanilla Policy and Random Sampling consistently yielded lower average rewards and higher failure rates (e.g., collisions in Car Racing, falls in Bipedal Walker). This highlights the fundamental advantage of adaptive environment selection for training robust policies.
- **Outperformance against Other Curriculum Methods:**
 - **Manual Curriculum:** While Manual Curriculum showed reasonable performance (617 ± 166 in Car Racing, 117 ± 142 in Bipedal Walker), Reparam and Reparam-M generally surpassed it, demonstrating the benefit of automated, data-driven curriculum adaptation over predefined linear schedules.
 - **SPRL and ALP-GMM:** Reparam variants also generally outperformed Self-Paced Reinforcement Learning (SPRL) and Absolute Learning Progress with Gaussian Mixture Models (ALP-GMM). These methods, which rely on stochastic task selection and competence- or progress-based sampling, did not match the performance of the gradient-based Reparam framework, particularly in Car Racing. For instance, ALP-GMM scored 508 ± 148 in Car Racing and 116 ± 139 in Bipedal Walker.
 - **Frontier Curriculum and Reparam-R:** Frontier Curriculum and Reparam-R (reverse curriculum) also generally performed below the optimal Reparam variants. Frontier curriculum (sampling from a local difficult region) scored 500 ± 160 in Car Racing, and Reparam-R (adapting lower bounds from difficult to easy) scored 542 ± 147. This suggests that the strategy of gradually expanding the full range of accessible difficulty while preserving exposure to easier tasks, as implemented by Reparam, is more effective.

#### 4.3. Curriculum Evolution and Training Dynamics

- **Gradual Difficulty Expansion:** The curriculum growth curves (Figure 4) illustrate that the Reparam framework effectively expands the range of environment parameters (κ, p in Car Racing; f, ρ, s in Bipedal Walker) over training time. This gradual expansion exposes the policy to increasing difficulties while retaining exposure to easier environments (since environments are sampled from $[\psi_{\min}, \psi_t]$), which is hypothesized to help mitigate catastrophic forgetting.
- **Evaluation Reward Trends:** Figure 3, showing mean evaluation performance over training timesteps, indicates that Reparam variants typically achieve higher evaluation rewards towards the later stages of training, demonstrating sustained learning progress and better generalization to the full environment parameter space.

In summary, the results indicate that the proposed Reparam framework, particularly with environment-dependent regularization, provides an effective, sample-efficient, and robust method for curriculum generation in continuous parametric environments. Its gradient-based, unidirectional adaptation mechanism demonstrates advantages over both non-curriculum and several existing automatic curriculum learning approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a curriculum generation framework that holds several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of reinforcement learning and autonomous systems:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The primary significance lies in its ability to train reinforcement learning policies that exhibit improved robustness and generalization across continuously varying environmental conditions. By systematically adapting the training environment, the policies learn to handle a wider range of challenges, which is critical for real-world deployment of autonomous agents in dynamic and unpredictable settings.
2. **Sample-Efficient Curriculum Design:** The gradient-based, reparameterized approach offers a more sample-efficient mechanism for curriculum adaptation compared to black-box optimization or population-based methods. This can reduce the computational resources and time required to design effective curricula, making advanced curriculum learning more accessible.
3. **Automated and Principled Approach:** The framework provides an automated and principled method for curriculum generation, reducing the reliance on manual tuning and expert knowledge. This automation can accelerate the development and deployment of robust RL agents.
4. **Effective for Continuous Parametric Environments:** The method is specifically designed for structured continuous environment parameter spaces, which are common in many real-world robotic and control tasks. Its ability to directly optimize these continuous parameters through gradients offers a refined control mechanism over curriculum evolution.
5. **Addressing Multimodal Observation Spaces:** The incorporation of a distribution-shift regularization objective, particularly effective in image-based or multimodal observation spaces, addresses a critical challenge in learning robust latent representations. This enhances the stability of curriculum evolution and the quality of learned features in complex perceptual environments.
6. **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** By progressively expanding environment difficulty while continuing to expose the policy to easier environments, the method inherently helps to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, a common issue in sequential learning.
7. **Foundation for Future Work:** The research explicitly identifies future work in scaling the method to higher-dimensional parameter spaces. This indicates a potential pathway for applying the core principles of this framework to more complex and realistic scenarios, bridging the gap between simulated training and real-world variability.

In summary, this work contributes to the development of more capable and reliable autonomous systems by offering a robust, efficient, and automated solution for curriculum generation, particularly relevant for navigation policies in environments with continuously varying, coupled parameters.
