# Detecting Pose Estimation Failures via Keypoint Self-Consistency

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Meta Pose, identifies 6D object pose estimation failures through geometric self-consistency of predicted 2D keypoints, outperforming established confidence baselines and operating efficiently, even demonstrating enhanced reliability in highly occluded scenes.

## Problem

- Current keypoint-based 6D pose estimation methods often predict 2D keypoints independently, neglecting inherent spatial correlations and fixed geometric relationships of rigid objects.
- Imprecise individual keypoint predictions propagate errors, leading to unreliable 6D pose estimates that hinder the effectiveness of downstream robotic or AR tasks.
- Existing pose failure detection methods are often computationally intensive, require additional sensors, or have not explicitly investigated lightweight, purely geometric approaches for binary failure detection.

## Method

- **Meta Pose** introduces a lightweight framework that constructs a feature vector from hand-crafted ==geometric properties== of predicted 2D keypoints and the resulting 6D pose estimate.
- Features capture ==geometric self-consistency== using relative distances between predicted keypoints, consistency with reprojected 3D model keypoints, and optional consistency with rendered images and segmentation masks.
- A simple ==logistic regression classifier== is trained on these geometric features to predict binary pose estimation failures (correct vs. incorrect rotation angle) without requiring ground truth at test time.

## Results

- Meta Pose (render-free) achieved an average AUROC of **83.04%** and AUPRC of **81.83%** for rotation errors > 5° on LINEMOD Occluded, substantially outperforming conformal keypoint prediction (AUROC **71.18%**, AUPRC **75.08%**).
- The render-free variant exhibited comparable or slightly better performance than the full feature set, with minimal computational overhead (approximately **1.1x** the baseline maximum keypoint confidence method runtime).
- The logistic regression model produced well-calibrated failure probabilities and demonstrated stable performance even with small calibration datasets, enhancing trustworthiness in practical applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Analyzing the spatial relationships and consistency among independently predicted 2D keypoints provides a robust and computationally efficient signal for identifying 6D pose estimation failures.
> - Aggregating diverse geometric features (e.g., relative distances, reprojection consistency, keypoint confidence) is crucial, as pose estimation failures often stem from multiple sources of inconsistency.
> - The method's failure detection performance improves with increasing levels of object occlusion, making it particularly valuable in challenging real-world scenarios where pose estimation itself is most prone to error.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Six-dimensional (6D) object pose estimation, the task of determining an object's 3D orientation and translation relative to a camera from an image, has seen significant advancements. Current approaches often involve a two-stage process: first, localizing predefined 2D object keypoints in the image, and then using Perspective-n-Point (PnP) algorithms to compute the 6D pose. While direct regression methods exist, two-stage approaches are often favored for their accuracy.

A limitation in many keypoint-based pose estimation methods is that 2D keypoints are typically predicted independently. This approach often neglects the inherent spatial correlation and fixed geometric relationships that exist among keypoints of a rigid 3D object. Consequently, imprecise individual keypoint predictions can propagate errors, negatively impacting the accuracy and reliability of the final 6D pose estimate. This lack of reliability can hinder the effectiveness of pose estimation in subsequent downstream tasks such as robotic manipulation or augmented reality.

The broader research landscape also includes efforts in uncertainty quantification and failure detection in computer vision. For general tasks like image classification, baselines often involve thresholding maximum softmax confidence or learning confidence predictors. In 6D pose estimation, existing work explores various methods:
- Integrating uncertainty from image encoding directly into the PnP solver.
- Using ensemble-based uncertainty quantification.
- Modeling orientation uncertainty through learning distributions.
- Selecting poses from hypotheses based on depth or visual feature consistency.
- Render-based uncertainty scores comparing estimated poses with segmentation masks.
- Decomposing the pipeline into failure detection, error source attribution, and mitigation.
- Applying conformal prediction to construct keypoint prediction sets with statistical guarantees and propagate these to obtain worst-case pose error bounds.

This work positions itself by investigating whether pose estimation failures can be identified *purely* from the spatial relationships of predicted 2D keypoint locations, without requiring additional sensors or computationally intensive learning components beyond the standard pose estimation pipeline. It specifically addresses a gap in the application of conformal prediction for keypoints, noting that guarantees on individual keypoints do not directly transfer to the final pose, nor has their practical utility for binary pose failure detection been explicitly investigated. The paper proposes a lightweight approach leveraging hand-crafted geometric features that encode the self-consistency of keypoint predictions, providing a direct method for binary failure detection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized limitation in current keypoint-based 6D object pose estimation. These methods frequently treat 2D keypoint predictions as independent entities, neglecting the intrinsic geometric relationships that govern a rigid object's structure. This oversight can lead to inconsistencies in keypoint localization, which in turn adversely affect the accuracy and reliability of the computed 6D pose. Such unreliable pose estimates limit the utility of pose estimation in practical applications where high precision and dependability are required.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:
1. **Investigate the Utility of Keypoint Self-Consistency**: To explore whether inaccuracies in 6D pose estimates can be identified by analyzing the spatial relationships and consistency among predicted 2D keypoints alone, without external sensor data or complex learning components.
2. **Develop a Lightweight Failure Detection Framework**: To propose a system, named Meta Pose, that can reliably detect pose estimation failures. This framework is designed to be computationally efficient, integrating directly into existing keypoint-based 6D pose estimation pipelines without requiring significant additional processing or specialized hardware.
3. **Introduce Hand-Crafted Geometric Features**: To define and implement a set of geometric features that quantitatively capture the self-consistency of keypoint predictions. These features are derived from multiple complementary sources, including pairwise distances between predicted keypoints, consistency with reprojected 3D model keypoints under the estimated pose, and consistency with keypoint predictions on rendered object images and segmentation masks.
4. **Demonstrate Superior Performance**: To show that a simple logistic regression classifier, when trained on these self-consistency features, can reliably detect pose estimation failures. This objective includes demonstrating that the proposed approach outperforms established confidence-based baselines, such as maximum keypoint confidence thresholding and methods derived from conformal keypoint predictions, in terms of various failure detection metrics.
5. **Evaluate Computational Efficiency and Feature Subset Performance**: To analyze the computational cost of the proposed features and evaluate whether a render-free subset of the hand-crafted features can achieve comparable failure detection performance to the full feature set. This objective aims to identify a practical and efficient configuration for real-world deployment.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to enhance the robustness and trustworthiness of 6D object pose estimation pipelines, thereby improving their applicability in critical downstream tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Meta Pose, focuses on detecting 6D object pose estimation failures by assessing the geometric self-consistency of predicted 2D keypoints. The framework takes the 2D keypoint predictions and the resulting 6D pose estimate from a standard keypoint-based pipeline as input.

**A. Rotation Error Definition**
The work primarily focuses on detecting failures related to rotation accuracy. A pose estimate is classified as "correct" if the geodesic distance (rotation angle $\theta$) between the estimated rotation ($\hat{R}$) and the ground truth rotation ($R$) is below a predefined threshold $\tau$. Otherwise, it is considered "incorrect" or a "failure." The rotation angle is calculated as $\theta = \arccos\left(\frac{\text{tr}(\hat{R}R^\top) - 1}{2}\right) \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}$.

**B. Feature Construction**
For each pose estimate, a feature vector is constructed to capture geometric inconsistencies without requiring ground truth at test time. Deviations from the expected rigid object structure among predicted keypoints serve as indicators of inaccurate predictions. All features are computationally lightweight, relying solely on keypoint locations.

1. **Relative Distances ($d_{rel}$)**: This feature set comprises the pairwise Euclidean distances between all predicted 2D image keypoints ($\hat{x}^{im}_i, \hat{x}^{im}_j$). These distances are scaled by the estimated object distance from the camera ($\hat{t}_z$) to account for perspective changes. Inconsistencies in these distances relative to the known 3D model structure suggest prediction errors.

2. **Reprojection Consistency ($d_{repr}$)**: This feature measures the consistency between the 2D keypoints predicted in the input image ($\hat{x}^{im}_k$) and the 2D projections of the 3D object keypoints ($V$) using the *estimated* 6D pose ($\hat{R}, \hat{t}$). These reprojected keypoints are denoted as $\hat{x}^{repr}_k$. The Euclidean distance between each $\hat{x}^{im}_k$ and its corresponding $\hat{x}^{repr}_k$ is computed and scaled by $\hat{t}_z$. Small distances indicate alignment between predicted keypoints and the estimated pose.

3. **Render Consistency ($d_{render}$)**: Leveraging the availability of the 3D object model, the object is rendered at its estimated 6D pose, and 2D keypoints are then predicted on this rendered image ($\hat{x}^{render}_k$) using the same keypoint detector. The assumption is that the keypoint detector performs more reliably on clean, rendered images. This feature measures the Euclidean distance between the keypoints predicted on the original image ($\hat{x}^{im}_k$) and those predicted on the rendered image ($\hat{x}^{render}_k$), scaled by $\hat{t}_z$. Discrepancies imply an inconsistency between the estimated pose and the keypoint predictions on an idealized image of that pose.

4. **Centroid Distance ($d_{center}$)**: For each keypoint $k$, a centroid $\hat{c}_k$ is calculated as the mean of its three representations: $\hat{x}^{im}_k$, $\hat{x}^{repr}_k$, and $\hat{x}^{render}_k$. The centroid distance feature measures the average spread of these three keypoint types around their respective centroids, scaled by $\hat{t}_z$. A consistent pose estimate is expected to result in these three keypoint locations clustering closely.

5. **Keypoint Confidence ($u$)**: This feature directly utilizes the confidence scores ($s$) provided by the underlying keypoint localization method. The per-keypoint uncertainty ($u$) is defined as $1-s$. Higher uncertainty values are expected to correlate with inaccurate keypoint predictions and thus incorrect pose estimates.

6. **Render Mask Consistency ($d_{cham}, r_{cov,\delta}$)**: This set of features compares the segmentation mask of the rendered object at the estimated pose ($M_{render}$) with a segmentation mask of the object predicted from the original image ($M_{seg}$), obtained using the Segment Anything Model (SAM).
 - **Chamfer Distance ($d_{cham}$)**: A one-directional Chamfer distance between the boundary points of $M_{seg}$ and $M_{render}$, scaled by $\hat{t}_z$. Lower values indicate better alignment of the silhouettes.
 - **Coverage Ratio ($r_{cov,\delta}$)**: The fraction of boundary points from $M_{seg}$ that lie within a pixel tolerance $\delta$ of the $M_{render}$ boundary. High values signify good alignment, while low values indicate inconsistency.

**C. Pose Estimation Failure Detector**
All constructed features are concatenated into a single feature vector **f**. This vector then serves as input to a **logistic regression model**, which is trained to classify whether the corresponding pose estimate is correct ($\theta \le \tau$) or incorrect ($\theta > \tau$).

**D. Evaluation Protocol**
The method is evaluated using a reverse cross-validation protocol on the LINEMOD Occluded dataset. The dataset's 1214 test images are split into 5 folds. In each run, one fold (20%) is used for training the logistic regression classifier, and the remaining four folds (80%) serve as the test set. This process is repeated five times, ensuring each sample is used for training once and evaluated four times. This protocol assesses the detector's sensitivity to the calibration data.

**E. Variants and Baselines**
- **Meta Pose (render-free)**: Uses a subset of features: $d_{rel}, d_{repr}$, and $u$. This variant avoids rendering and additional segmentation, minimizing computational overhead.
- **Meta Pose – Full**: Includes all features: $d_{rel}, d_{repr}, u, d_{render}, d_{center}, d_{cham}$, and $r_{cov,\delta}$.
- **Maximum Keypoint Confidence Thresholding**: A baseline that aggregates per-keypoint uncertainties by taking their maximum value ($u^* = \max u$).
- **Conformal Keypoint Prediction**: A baseline where a logistic regression classifier is trained on scalar statistics (mean, variance, maximum rotation error) derived from a distribution of poses sampled from conformal keypoint sets, as proposed by Yang and Pavone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the Meta Pose framework, comparing its performance against established baselines and analyzing various aspects of its operation.

**A. Per-Object Failure Detection Performance**
On the LINEMOD Occluded dataset, Meta Pose consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to both maximum keypoint confidence thresholding and conformal keypoint prediction across all eight objects and for rotation error thresholds of $\tau = 5^\circ$ and $\tau = 10^\circ$.

- **$\theta > 5^\circ$**: Meta Pose (render-free) achieved an average AUROC of 83.04 and AUPRC of 81.83. This represents a substantial improvement over conformal keypoint prediction (AUROC 71.18, AUPRC 75.08) and maximum keypoint confidence thresholding (AUROC 66.26, AUPRC 70.00). The gains were particularly pronounced for symmetric objects like "eggbox" and "glue," with AUROC values exceeding 91 and 86, and AUPRC values over 95 and 92, respectively.
- **$\theta > 10^\circ$**: Despite increased class imbalance (fewer failures), Meta Pose (render-free) maintained strong performance with an average AUROC of 90.50, outperforming conformal keypoint prediction (AUROC 81.93) and maximum keypoint confidence thresholding (AUROC 79.62). For several objects (ape, can, cat, eggbox, glue), AUROC values exceeded 90 percent points.
- **Robustness**: The consistently small standard deviations across metrics and objects indicated that the failure detector is robust to the choice of calibration fold, suggesting that a small labeled subset of the target domain is sufficient for stable performance.

**B. Comparison of Meta Pose Variants**
Meta Pose (render-free) exhibited performance comparable to Meta Pose – Full (which includes render- and mask-based features). For $\theta > 5^\circ$, the render-free variant often achieved slightly better or equivalent results. For $\theta > 10^\circ$, Meta Pose – Full showed only marginal improvements (at most 0.51 percentage points in F1, AUROC, and AUPRC) over the render-free variant, while Meta Pose (render-free) maintained a slight edge in accuracy. This suggests that the additional computational cost of rendering and segmentation for the 'Full' variant does not yield substantial performance benefits for failure detection.

**C. Failure Probability Distributions and Calibration**
Violin plots demonstrated that incorrect pose estimates generally received higher predicted failure probabilities. Clear separation between correct and incorrect poses was observed for objects like "eggbox" and "glue." The logistic regression model produced well-calibrated failure probabilities, closely following the diagonal line in calibration curves, especially for $\tau = 10^\circ$. This indicates that predicted probabilities can be reliably interpreted as confidence scores for failure.

**D. Sample Efficiency**
The failure detection performance remained stable across a range of calibration set sizes, from 50% down to 10% of the available data, particularly for $\tau = 5^\circ$. For $\tau = 10^\circ$, AUPRC and F1 score showed a more noticeable decline with smaller calibration sets due to increased class imbalance, but the degradation remained moderate, demonstrating meaningful discriminative power even with limited labeled data.

**E. Feature Ablation**
Aggregating different features consistently improved discriminative performance compared to individual features, suggesting that pose estimation failures arise from multiple sources of geometric inconsistency. The combination of non-render features (Meta Pose, render-free) yielded the strongest performance among individual and paired feature combinations, often outperforming render-based and mask-based features, both individually and combined.

**F. Runtime Comparison**
Meta Pose (render-free) exhibited low computational overhead, requiring 374 ms per image, which is only approximately 1.1 times the baseline maximum keypoint confidence method. In contrast, conformal keypoint prediction was significantly slower at 2030 ms per image (6.2x baseline), primarily due to heatmap processing and pose sampling. Meta Pose – Full, including render and mask features, took 1220 ms per image (3.7x baseline). The efficiency of the render-free variant makes it suitable for real-time applications.

**G. Failure Detection vs. Occlusion Level**
Performance metrics, particularly AUPRC and F1 score, improved with increasing levels of object occlusion. For occlusion levels above 80%, the method achieved near-perfect performance. This is a significant finding, as it indicates that Meta Pose is most reliable in highly occluded scenes, which are scenarios where pose estimation itself is most prone to failure. Even with severe class imbalance at low occlusion levels for $\tau = 10^\circ$, the method maintained meaningful discriminative capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Meta Pose, a lightweight and effective framework for detecting 6D object pose estimation failures based on the geometric self-consistency of predicted 2D keypoints. The significance and potential impact of this research are multi-faceted:

**Enhanced Reliability of Pose Estimation Pipelines**: The core contribution is the demonstration that analyzing the spatial relationships and consistency among independently predicted keypoints provides a robust signal for identifying pose estimation failures. This addresses a critical weakness in many keypoint-based methods, where the neglect of rigid object structure can lead to unreliable outcomes. By flagging potentially incorrect poses, Meta Pose improves the overall trustworthiness and utility of 6D pose estimation systems.

**Superior Performance and Practicality**: Meta Pose consistently outperforms established baselines, including maximum keypoint confidence thresholding and conformal keypoint prediction, across various metrics and challenging scenarios. Crucially, the render-free variant of Meta Pose achieves this performance with minimal computational overhead, running at approximately 1.1 times the speed of a basic confidence baseline. This efficiency, combined with its demonstrated robustness to calibration set size, makes it highly practical for integration into real-time or resource-constrained applications, such as robotic manipulation or industrial inspection.

**Well-Calibrated Uncertainty Quantification**: The logistic regression model employed in Meta Pose generates well-calibrated failure probabilities. This allows the predicted probabilities to be interpreted as meaningful confidence scores for failure, which is essential for informed decision-making in downstream tasks. For instance, a high predicted failure probability could automatically trigger a re-estimation process or alert an operator to a potentially erroneous pose.

**Insights into Failure Modes**: The ablation studies highlight that pose estimation failures often stem from multiple sources of geometric inconsistency, rather than a single dominant signal. The effectiveness of aggregating diverse geometric features (relative distances, reprojection consistency, keypoint confidence) underscores the value of a holistic approach to reliability assessment.

**Improved Performance in Challenging Conditions**: The finding that Meta Pose's failure detection performance improves with increasing levels of occlusion is particularly impactful. Pose estimation is inherently more difficult in occluded scenes, where failures are more common. The ability of Meta Pose to be most reliable precisely when the underlying pose estimator is most likely to fail enhances its value in complex, real-world environments.

**Future Directions**: The work also opens several avenues for future research. This includes extending the framework to detect translation failures, integrating the predicted failure probabilities directly into the pose estimation loop for active re-estimation strategies, and validating the approach on a broader range of datasets, object categories, and pose estimation backbones to confirm its generality. Additionally, improving the quality of external segmentation models could indirectly enhance mask consistency features and potentially feed back into improving pose estimation accuracy itself.

In summary, Meta Pose represents a significant step towards building more robust and reliable 6D object pose estimation systems, offering a practical and effective solution for identifying and managing inaccuracies that are often overlooked in current methods.
