# DARO: Difficulty-Aware Reweighting Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DARO introduces an adaptive reweighting algorithm for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) that dynamically adjusts loss contributions based on sample difficulty, achieving state-of-the-art performance and faster convergence in large language model mathematical reasoning tasks. It resolves the "loss scale issue" found in previous GRPO-based methods by employing learnable weights to balance training signals across different problem difficulties.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) algorithms, particularly GRPO-based methods, rely on static or overly simplistic weighting schemes for training samples.
- This leads to a "loss scale issue" where the magnitude of the loss varies dramatically across different problem difficulty levels, causing unbalanced training.
- Such imbalances hinder efficient learning, slow convergence, and lead to suboptimal performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) in complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- ==DARO== provides a unified mathematical framework for GRPO-based RLVR algorithms, revealing their underlying static weighting mechanisms.
- It introduces ==dynamic, learnable weights== for different difficulty groups (defined by empirical pass rates), which are optimized jointly with the LLM's policy parameters.
- The loss function is designed with a ==regularization term== to ensure weights are inversely proportional to their group's loss, thereby balancing training signals across difficulty levels.

## Results

- DARO consistently achieves the highest average accuracy across **three** base LLMs and **six** diverse math reasoning benchmarks, with peak performance of **50.8%** for Qwen2.5-Math-7B.
- The algorithm demonstrates significantly faster convergence rates and reaches higher final performance levels that are often unattainable by other static-weighting methods.
- Ablation studies confirm the crucial role of DARO's dynamic weights, showing substantial performance gains (e.g., **+2.7** over GRPO baseline for Llama3.1-8B) compared to static or simplistic weighting strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prior GRPO-based RLVR methods (GRPO, DAPO, LIPO, Dr. GRPO) can be unified and mathematically shown to employ static or predefined weighting schemes tied to sample difficulty.
> - The "loss scale issue" formally demonstrates that loss magnitude in RLVR is inherently imbalanced across different empirical pass rates (difficulty levels), impeding balanced learning.
> - Dynamically adapting loss weights based on the inverse of their group's loss magnitude effectively mitigates this imbalance, promoting more stable and efficient training.
