# Distributed Pose Graph Optimization via Continuous Riemannian Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> CORD, a distributed Pose Graph Optimization solver from the University of Michigan, utilizes continuous-time Riemannian dynamics to model the problem as a second-order dynamical system on Lie groups. It demonstrates improved convergence speed and robustness against asynchronous communication and packet loss, achieving lower optimality gaps compared to existing distributed PGO methods.

## Problem

- Centralized Pose Graph Optimization (PGO) in multi-robot SLAM limits scalability and introduces single points of failure.
- Existing distributed PGO methods often suffer from slow convergence in large, loosely coupled graphs.
- These methods also lack robustness and stability when dealing with asynchronous communication or stale neighbor information in multi-robot systems.

## Method

- Formulated distributed PGO as a ==second-order continuous-time dynamical system== evolving on ==Lie groups==, integrating physical concepts like inertia and damping.
- Developed **CORD**, a ==geometric integration scheme== that numerically updates pose variables and body velocities.
- Implemented a ==velocity-based neighbor state prediction== mechanism to handle asynchronous communication and stale information in multi-robot settings.

## Results

- CORD achieved **lower optimality gaps** across most 3D benchmark datasets for both chordal and geodesic PGO in synchronous communication, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- The solver demonstrated superior robustness under asynchronous communication, maintaining stability and achieving **lower final costs** even with constant, varying, or randomized delays and **10% packet loss**.
- An ablation study confirmed the critical role of the velocity-based neighbor prediction mechanism for **preventing divergence** and ensuring robust performance in asynchronous settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging geometric mechanics and continuous-time Riemannian dynamics introduces inertia and damping to distributed PGO, enabling faster and more stable convergence.
> - The framework can generalize existing optimization algorithms like Riemannian gradient descent and Gauss-Newton as specific limiting cases.
> - Predicting neighbor states using transmitted velocities is essential for robust operation and stability in the presence of communication delays and asynchronous updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Multi-robot Collaborative SLAM (CSLAM) is a critical component for achieving scalable and globally consistent situational awareness in environments where GPS is unavailable. CSLAM enables multiple robots to collectively estimate their trajectories and construct shared maps using noisy relative measurements, thereby extending the operational scope beyond single-robot systems. Pose Graph Optimization (PGO) is a fundamental backend technique within these systems, responsible for integrating intra- and inter-robot measurements into a coherent global estimate.

Traditional PGO solvers often operate in a centralized manner, which can introduce single points of failure and scalability limitations. Consequently, a significant portion of recent research has focused on distributed PGO approaches to address scalability, communication constraints, and privacy concerns. These methods typically leverage distributed optimization techniques, such as primal or primal-dual iterations (e.g., based on ADMM), or probabilistic message passing frameworks. While these distributed approaches mitigate some of the issues of centralized systems, they frequently encounter limitations in convergence speed and robustness, especially in large, loosely coupled graphs, and under asynchronous updates or stale neighbor information. Some state-of-the-art methods achieve acceleration by exploiting problem-specific algebraic structures, but this often leads to algorithms that are tightly coupled to specific problem formulations and require substantial domain expertise.

This work introduces a novel approach by formulating distributed PGO as a continuous-time dynamical system evolving on Lie groups. This perspective draws inspiration from geometric mechanics, a field largely unexplored in the context of SLAM backend optimization. Previous work has studied dynamical systems for optimization in other contexts, such as Nesterov's accelerated gradient methods (often modeled as second-order ordinary differential equations) and point cloud registration (formulated as damped N-body systems). However, applying this continuous-time Riemannian dynamics perspective to the high-dimensional and ill-conditioned problem of distributed pose graph optimization, especially while explicitly accounting for communication delays, represents a new direction. The proposed framework aims to address the persistent limitations of existing distributed PGO methods by inherently introducing inertia and a mechanism for neighbor state prediction, which are intended to enable accelerated convergence and robustness under asynchronous communication.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel distributed PGO solver, named CORD (Continuous-time Riemannian Dynamics), that overcomes the limitations of existing distributed methods, particularly regarding convergence speed and robustness in asynchronous multi-robot settings.

The motivations for this work are multifaceted:
- **Addressing Convergence Speed:** Existing distributed PGO solvers, particularly generic first-order methods, often exhibit slow convergence in large, loosely coupled pose graphs. While some methods exploit problem-specific algebraic structures for acceleration, they tend to be less generalizable. The authors aim to introduce a mechanism for acceleration that is intrinsic to the optimization process.
- **Enhancing Robustness to Asynchronous Communication:** Multi-robot systems frequently operate under communication constraints such as latency, packet loss, and asynchronous message arrival. Most existing distributed PGO algorithms struggle with stale neighbor information, leading to instability or significant slowdowns. A key motivation is to design an algorithm that maintains performance and stability even under these non-ideal communication conditions.
- **Leveraging Geometric Mechanics:** The research is motivated by the observation that geometric mechanics, a field that describes system dynamics on manifolds, has been underutilized in SLAM backend optimization. By formulating PGO as a second-order continuous-time dynamical system evolving on Lie groups, the authors seek to introduce concepts like inertia and damping, which are fundamental in physical systems, into the optimization process. This approach is intended to provide a principled way to achieve faster and more stable convergence by suppressing oscillatory behavior.
- **Generalizing Existing Optimization Approaches:** The authors aim to develop a unifying framework where existing optimization algorithms, such as Riemannian gradient descent and Gauss-Newton, can be recovered as specific limiting cases of the proposed continuous-time Riemannian dynamics. This objective highlights the foundational nature of their approach.
- **Providing Theoretical Foundations and Practical Guidance:** A further motivation is to provide a theoretical analysis that characterizes the energy dissipation properties of the discretized Riemannian dynamics. This analysis is intended to establish rigorous justification for the algorithm and offer practical guidance for parameter selection, such as the step size, to ensure stability and convergence.
- **Empirical Validation:** Finally, the authors aim to demonstrate the practical efficacy of the proposed solver through extensive experiments on benchmark PGO datasets, showcasing superior performance compared to state-of-the-art distributed baselines in both synchronous and asynchronous regimes.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed CORD framework formulates distributed Pose Graph Optimization (PGO) as a second-order continuous-time dynamical system evolving on the direct product of SE(3) Lie groups. The methodology involves converting the PGO problem into Riemannian dynamics, designing a geometric integration scheme, specializing it for distributed multi-robot settings, and addressing asynchronous communication.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Pose Graph Optimization (PGO)

PGO aims to estimate a set of robot poses {X_u} from noisy relative pose measurements X_uv. The paper considers two widely used formulations:
- **Geodesic metric:** Residuals are defined using the Lie logarithm of the error between the measured and estimated relative poses, `log(X_uv^-1 X_u^-1 X_v)`.
- **Chordal metric:** Residuals are defined as `X_u X_uv - X_v`.
The objective is to minimize a cost function `C(X)` that sums weighted squared norms of these residuals over all edges. In distributed PGO, the graph is partitioned among robots, each managing a subset of poses and exchanging information with neighbors to collectively solve the global objective without a central coordinator.

#### 3.2. From PGO to Riemannian Dynamics

The core idea is to model the pose variables as "massive particles" whose evolution is described by a Lagrangian `L = T - C`, where `T` is kinetic energy and `C` is the PGO cost function (potential energy).
- **Body Velocity:** For each pose `X_i`, a body velocity `ξ_i := (X_i^-1 Ẋ_i)∨ ∈ R^6` is defined, where `(·)∨` maps a Lie algebra element to `R^6`.
- **Kinetic Energy:** The total kinetic energy is defined as `T := 1/2 ξ⊤ M ξ`, where `ξ` stacks all body velocities and `M` is a generalized inertia (mass) matrix.
- **Damping:** To ensure convergence to a First-Order Critical Point (FOCP), a positive definite damping matrix `D` is introduced, leading to energy dissipation.
- **Damped Euler-Poincaré Equation:** Using the Lagrange-d’Alembert principle, the system dynamics are derived as: `d/dt (M ξ) = -∇C - Dξ + ad∗_ξ (M ξ)`. The term `ad∗_ξ (M ξ)` is the co-adjoint operator, accounting for the curvature of the Lie group. When `M` is state-dependent, an additional term `-Ṁ ξ` is included. At steady state (`ξ = 0`), this equation reduces to `∇C = 0`, confirming that equilibrium points are FOCPs of PGO.

#### 3.3. Optimization via Geometric Discretization

Algorithm 1 outlines the numerical integration using a semi-implicit Euler scheme:
1. **Force Computation:** At each iteration `k`, forces are computed based on the current state `X_k` and `ξ_k`:
 - `F_grad = -∇C(X_k)` (potential-induced force)
 - `F_damp = -Dξ_k` (damping force)
 - `F_cor = ad∗_ξk (M_k ξ_k)` (Lie-group curvature term)
 - `F_varM = - (M_k - M_k-1 / ∆t) ξ_k` (for state-dependent mass matrix)
2. **Velocity Update:** The total force `F_total` is used to compute acceleration `a_k = M_k^-1 F_total`, and then `ξ_k+1 = ξ_k + a_k ∆t` (forward Euler step on velocity).
3. **Manifold Update:** A semi-implicit update rule is used for the pose: `X_k+1 = X_k exp((ξ_k+1 ∆t)^∧)`. This utilizes the new velocity `ξ_k+1` for stability.
4. **Mass Matrix Update:** If `M` is state-dependent, `M_k+1` is updated based on `X_k+1`.

The method can generalize existing optimization algorithms: in an overdamped limit (`M = εM` as `ε → 0`), it recovers first-order gradient flow; choosing `D = J⊤ΩJ` (where `J` is the Jacobian) recovers the Gauss-Newton direction.

#### 3.4. Distributed Riemannian Dynamics for Multi-Robot PGO

For distributed PGO (Algorithm 2), `M` and `D` are chosen to be block-diagonal with respect to robot-wise partitions, i.e., `M = blkdiag(M^1, ..., M^R)` and `D = blkdiag(D^1, ..., D^R)`.
- **Decomposed Dynamics:** This choice decomposes the Euler-Poincaré equation into independent systems for each robot `i`: `d/dt (M^i ξ_i) = -∇C_i - D_i ξ_i + ad∗_ξi (M^i ξ_i)`.
- **Gradient Computation:** `∇C_i` involves both intra-robot terms (computed locally) and inter-robot terms (requiring neighbor pose information through communication).
- **Mass and Damping Choice:** `M^i = m H^i` and `D^i = (d/t + ε_d) H^i`, where `H^i` is the block-diagonal of the global Hessian corresponding to robot `i`'s variables. This preconditions the dynamics and ensures `M^i` and `D^i` can be computed distributively.
- **Parallel Updates:** Unlike sequential methods (e.g., Gauss-Seidel), this approach allows all robots to compute updates simultaneously.

#### 3.5. Asynchronous Optimization via Delay Compensation

To handle communication delays, each robot transmits both its pose and body velocity. Upon receiving outdated information `(X_j^τ, ξ_j^τ)` from neighbor `j` at time `τ`, robot `i` predicts `j`'s current pose `ˆX_j^k` at time `t_k` using `ˆX_j^k = X_j^τ · exp((ξ_j^τ (t_k - τ))^∧)`. This predicted pose is then used in computing `F_grad^i`, allowing parallel asynchronous operation.

#### 3.6. Convergence Analysis

For the synchronous case with constant `M`, the authors provide a theoretical analysis of energy dissipation:
- **Lipschitz-type gradient:** Assumption 1 states that the cost function has a Lipschitz-type gradient, implying local well-behavedness.
- **Energy Change Bound (Lemma 1):** An upper bound for the total energy difference `∆E_k = E_k+1 - E_k` is derived.
- **Energy Dissipation Condition (Theorem 1):** A sufficient condition on the time step `∆t` is established to ensure that the total energy `E_k` is non-increasing, thereby guaranteeing stability and convergence to a FOCP.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments evaluated CORD against state-of-the-art distributed PGO baselines under various communication regimes and metrics.

#### 4.1. Evaluation under Synchronous Communication

- **Chordal Distance:** CORD achieved the lowest optimality gap across most 3D benchmark datasets (e.g., Small Grid, Torus, Grid) compared to AMM-PGO and Distributed Jacobi (DJ). For Sphere, AMM-PGO performed better, and for Rim, MESA (geodesic metric) showed better performance. CORD demonstrated superior performance over AMM-PGO on real-world datasets like Cubicle, Rim, and Garage. CORD consistently yielded lower errors than the DJ baseline, indicating an improvement over first-order preconditioned updates due to inertial and damping effects.
- **Geodesic Distance:** CORD significantly outperformed MESA and DJ across most datasets, with the exception of the Rim dataset where MESA showed better performance (Table II).
- **Performance Profiles:** Performance profiles (Figure 3) showed CORD and AMM-PGO had comparable performance for chordal PGO (AUC of 851.36 vs. 855.93 for AMM-PGO), while CORD notably outperformed baselines for geodesic PGO.
- **Computational Overhead:** On the largest Rim dataset, CORD required 101.9 ms per iteration and 5.9 KB communication (for 80 iterations to reach ∆=10^-2), compared to DJ's 43.0 ms per iteration and 3.2 KB (for 924 iterations). This suggests CORD achieves substantially faster convergence with a moderate increase in per-iteration overhead.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Asynchronous Communication

- **Constant Delay:** With a uniform delay of 5 steps, CORD consistently achieved a lower final cost than DJ across all benchmark datasets (Table I). The inherent inertia allowed for acceleration even with a significantly reduced (over 90%) step size to maintain stability.
- **Varying Delays:** Simulations with varying delay steps (Figure 4) demonstrated CORD's robustness. DJ exhibited oscillations proportional to delay length and required aggressive tuning of step size to prevent divergence. CORD, in contrast, maintained smooth convergence trajectories due to its built-in inertia, damping, and neighbor state prediction.
- **Randomized Delays and Packet Loss:** CORD remained stable and effectively reduced the objective function even under challenging conditions of 10% random packet loss and communication delays randomized in the range (Table I, last column), further confirming its robustness.

#### 4.3. Evaluation under Edge-Based Communication

CORD was adapted to an edge-based communication regime and compared against MESA on three datasets. CORD achieved lower costs after 100 iterations using a constant parameter setting (Table III).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study investigated the impact of key algorithmic features:
- **State-Dependent vs. Constant M, D:** While state-dependent mass and damping matrices generally yielded marginally better performance, the constant setting remained competitive (Table IV). This suggests a trade-off between adaptability and computational efficiency.
- **Neighbor Pose Prediction:** Omitting the velocity-based neighbor prediction mechanism led to divergence in asynchronous settings (Table IV). This highlights the critical role of the prediction for maintaining stability and convergence by compensating for inaccurate neighbor information arising from communication latencies.

#### 4.5. Empirical Mass Variation

An empirical analysis of relative mass variation `∥M_k - M_k-1∥_F / ∥M_k∥_F` (Figure 5) showed that after an initial transient, the mass matrix variation remained below 0.1% for most iterations in the state-dependent setup, suggesting that the constant mass assumption for theoretical analysis is locally informative near minimizers.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of multi-robot simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), particularly in the context of distributed pose graph optimization (PGO). The development of CORD, a solver based on continuous-time Riemannian dynamics, offers several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Novel Algorithmic Paradigm:** The work introduces a novel framework for distributed PGO by leveraging principles from geometric mechanics and continuous-time dynamical systems. This approach, which models optimization variables as massive particles subject to damping, is distinct from traditional iterative linearization or message-passing methods. It opens a new avenue for designing optimization algorithms on Lie groups.
- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** CORD demonstrates superior performance in both synchronous and, critically, asynchronous communication settings compared to state-of-the-art distributed baselines. Its ability to achieve faster convergence and maintain stability under communication delays, packet loss, and asynchronous updates is a substantial improvement over existing methods. This directly addresses a major challenge in deploying multi-robot systems in real-world, dynamic environments where communication is often unreliable.
- **Principled Delay Compensation:** The incorporation of velocity-based neighbor prediction provides a principled mechanism for compensating for communication latency. This feature, which was shown to be critical in the ablation study, enables robots to anticipate neighbor states, mitigating the negative effects of stale information and contributing to the algorithm's robustness.
- **Generalization of Optimization Techniques:** The framework generalizes existing optimization algorithms such as Riemannian gradient descent and Gauss-Newton, positioning itself as a more fundamental approach to optimization on manifolds. This theoretical elegance could lead to broader applicability beyond PGO.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** The theoretical analysis of energy dissipation under geometric discretization provides rigorous justification for the algorithm's stability and offers practical guidance for selecting integration step sizes. This blend of theoretical underpinnings and empirical validation strengthens the credibility of the approach.
- **Scalability and Decentralization:** By enabling fully distributed and parallel updates with minimal communication overhead, CORD inherently supports scalable multi-robot SLAM systems. This decentralization reduces reliance on central coordinators, enhances system resilience, and supports flexible deployment scenarios.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work lays a foundation for several promising future research directions, including extending the theoretical analysis to explicitly account for state-dependent mass matrices and communication delays, developing adaptive parameter tuning strategies, and generalizing the framework to arbitrary factor graphs and other matrix Lie groups beyond SE(3).
- **Practical Implications for Robotics:** The enhanced robustness and performance of CORD can significantly impact the development of practical multi-robot systems. It can facilitate more accurate and reliable collaborative mapping and localization in challenging GPS-denied environments (e.g., underground, underwater, urban canyons), which is crucial for applications such as search and rescue, environmental monitoring, and autonomous exploration.
