# Direct Dynamic Retargeting for Humanoid Imitation Learning from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The Direct Dynamic Retargeting (DDR) framework, developed by researchers from LAAS-CNRS and ANITI, converts noisy human video demonstrations into dynamically feasible humanoid robot trajectories using a single-stage Model Predictive Control approach. This significantly improves the physical consistency and tracking accuracy of robot reference motions, leading to faster and more robust learning for reinforcement learning policies, demonstrated by successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree H1-2 robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid imitation learning from videos is hindered by noisy and physically inconsistent pose extraction from monocular demonstrations.
- Traditional motion retargeting methods (Geometric and Indirect Dynamic Retargeting) introduce a 'geometric bias' from kinematic projections, leading to physically infeasible or suboptimal dynamic robot trajectories.
- The morphological mismatch between humans and robots, combined with biased references, results in slow reinforcement learning convergence or suboptimal robot behaviors.

## Method

- Introduced Direct Dynamic Retargeting (DDR), a single-stage ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework that directly computes dynamically feasible robot trajectories from noisy expert video keypoints.
- DDR minimizes an augmented distance function, combining spatial tracking and relative shape-matching via a Laplacian matrix, between robot keypoints and expert keypoints within a physics simulator.
- The optimization problem is solved using the derivative-free Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) within a receding horizon MPC scheme, which is robust to ambiguities in contact sequence identification from video data.

## Results

- DDR-generated trajectories demonstrated superior physical feasibility (e.g., **0%** infeasibility for simpler motions) and higher contact sequence accuracy (e.g., **13.71%** error for one-foot balance vs. GR's **32.12%**), while mitigating foot slippage.
- Reinforcement learning agents trained with DDR references achieved faster convergence and higher final cumulative rewards (e.g., pistol squat policies converged **37%** faster and achieved **12%** higher reward than IDR).
- DDR policies exhibited superior reference tracking fidelity (e.g., pistol squat: **0.729** rad joint RMSE vs. GR's **0.976** rad), enabling robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of agile motions onto a Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Eliminating the intermediate kinematic projection (geometric bias) in motion retargeting is essential for generating truly dynamically optimal and feasible robot trajectories from human demonstrations.
> - Directly incorporating dynamic constraints and physics simulation from the outset yields higher fidelity references, which are critical for efficient and robust reinforcement learning.
> - Derivative-free optimization methods like CEM can effectively handle noisy video data and implicitly identify complex contact sequences without requiring explicit prior knowledge.
