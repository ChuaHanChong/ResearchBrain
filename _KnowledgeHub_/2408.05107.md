# Depth Helps: Improving Pre-trained RGB-based Policy with Depth Information Injection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and Renmin University of China introduce the Depth Information Injection (DI²) framework, which enhances pre-trained RGB-based robotic manipulation policies with 3D perception by integrating predicted depth features. The method achieves an average 63.15% success rate in simulated tasks and 66.67% in real-world scenarios, maintaining robust performance when deployed with RGB-only input.

## Problem

- RGB-only policies from foundation models lack explicit 3D spatial understanding critical for fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks.
- Existing multimodal integration methods (e.g., cross-modal distillation, missing modality learning) often suffer from sequential cumulative errors in complex robotic control.
- A disparity exists between the rich RGB-D data available during training and the practical constraints of RGB-only deployment in real-world robotic setups.

## Method

- The Depth Information Injection (DI²) framework leverages a multi-stage training process combining RGB-D policy training with a Depth Completion Module (DCM) and a Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC).
- The DCM predicts depth features from RGB image features using learnable spatial prior tokens and cross-attention, enabling the policy to infer 3D perception from RGB inputs during inference.
- The DAC quantizes predicted depth features into discrete vectors to reduce noise and mitigate cumulative errors, enhancing robustness in sequential manipulation tasks.

## Results

- The DI² framework achieved an average 63.15% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark with RGB-only inference, outperforming all tested baselines, including a strong RGB-RF baseline (57.95%).
- It demonstrated minimal performance drop (0.8%) from its RGB-D variant (63.95%) to RGB-only inference in simulations, showcasing robustness to modality shift for practical deployment.
- Real-world experiments with a Franka Emika Panda robot showed an average 66.67% success rate for DI² with RGB-only input across four complex manipulation tasks, validating its practical effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly injecting and refining predicted depth information through a specialized module can effectively equip RGB-based policies with 3D spatial awareness for precise manipulation.
> - The Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC) is crucial for robustness, significantly reducing action prediction errors by discretizing depth features and mitigating cumulative errors in sequential tasks.
> - A multi-stage training process, including warm-up with RGB-D, alignment of DCM, and codebook training, allows for effective knowledge transfer and robust RGB-only deployment without explicit depth sensors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in modern robotics: enabling generalizable robotic manipulation using advanced AI models, particularly foundation models.
The broader research landscape for robotic manipulation is characterized by several key trends:

- **Rise of Foundation Models in Robotics:** Large language models (LLMs) and visual-language models (VLMs) have shown immense success in general perception and decision-making tasks across various domains. Researchers are increasingly leveraging the "world knowledge" embedded in these models to develop more generalizable robotic policies. Examples include using VLMs for instruction decomposition, task planning, and even direct action control from extensive robotic datasets.
- **Limitations of RGB-only Perception:** While foundation models often rely on RGB camera inputs, RGB images inherently lack direct 3D spatial information. This limitation becomes pronounced in *fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks* where precise understanding of object positions, depths, and shapes in 3D space is crucial for successful interaction. Relying solely on RGB can lead to spatial misinterpretations and cumulative errors in sequential manipulation.
- **Multimodal Integration Strategies:** To overcome unimodal limitations, multimodal perception (e.g., combining RGB with depth) is a natural solution. Existing strategies for multimodal integration include:
 - **Cross-modal knowledge distillation:** Training a unimodal model (student) to mimic the behavior or features of a multimodal model (teacher). This aims to transfer rich knowledge from one modality to another. However, previous work has found challenges in applying this to fine-grained robotic manipulation due to issues like sequential accumulative errors.
 - **Missing modality learning:** Training models to be robust even when some modalities are absent during inference, often through data augmentation (randomly dropping modalities during training) or by predicting missing modalities. While successful in other perception tasks, direct application to robotics, especially for generating accurate trajectories, can still suffer from prediction errors.

This paper positions itself directly in this landscape by tackling the gap between the power of RGB-based foundation models and the necessity of 3D perception for robust robotic manipulation. It acknowledges the success of foundation models in decision-making but highlights their shortfall in 3D understanding from RGB-only inputs. It also recognizes the limitations of existing cross-modal and missing modality learning approaches in the context of sequential, fine-grained robotic control, specifically addressing the problem of *cumulative errors* that can arise from imperfect depth prediction. By proposing a framework that leverages RGB-D data during training but deploys with RGB-only input (supplemented by *predicted* depth), the authors aim to bridge the practical deployment gap and enhance 3D perception without requiring costly or unreliable depth sensors in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **enhance the 3D perception capabilities of pre-trained RGB-based robotic manipulation policies by injecting depth information, while ensuring robust and efficient deployment that relies solely on RGB images.**

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing the 3D Perception Deficit:** Foundation models, despite their advanced reasoning and generalization capabilities, struggle with fine-grained robotic manipulation when limited to RGB inputs due to their inherent lack of explicit 3D spatial understanding. Tasks like precise grasping, placing, or assembly critically depend on accurate depth information.
- **Bridging Training and Deployment Modality Gaps:** While training data (especially in simulated environments) can readily provide RGB-D information, deploying robots in diverse real-world scenarios often faces challenges with depth sensors (e.g., cost, specific lighting conditions, occlusions, failure modes). The goal is to leverage rich multimodal data during policy development but enable practical RGB-only inference.
- **Mitigating Cumulative Errors in Sequential Tasks:** Existing multimodal integration methods, when applied to robotic manipulation, can suffer from sequential accumulative errors. Imperfect depth predictions at one timestep can cascade and lead to significant deviations in subsequent actions, ultimately causing task failure. The authors are motivated to develop mechanisms that reduce these errors for reliable policy execution.
- **Improving Generalizability and Reliability:** By effectively incorporating 3D spatial priors, the proposed framework aims to make robotic policies more generalizable to novel objects and environments, and more reliable in executing complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- **Utilizing Minimal Aligned RGB-D Data:** The motivation is to achieve this enhancement without requiring massive amounts of *new* RGB-D data for every task, but rather by intelligently leveraging *minimal aligned* RGB-D data for fine-tuning pre-trained policies.

In essence, the research seeks a practical and effective way to give RGB-based robotic policies "depth awareness" for precise manipulation, making them more capable in real-world deployments where only RGB images are readily available.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose the **Depth Information Injection (DI²)** framework, which leverages RGB-Depth modality for policy fine-tuning but relies solely on RGB images for deployment. The framework consists of two core modules: the Depth Completion Module (DCM) and the Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC). The overall approach involves a multi-stage training process.

**A. Overall Framework (Figure 2)**
During training, the model receives both RGB and depth images (o_rgb_t, o_depth_t) and language instructions (L). A pre-trained Visual Language Model (VLM) extracts features (f_text, f_rgb_t, f_depth_t) which are then fed into a policy model (π) to generate actions (a_t).
Crucially, for inference with RGB-only input, the framework predicts the depth feature (e_f_depth_t) from the RGB image feature (f_rgb_t) using the DCM and DAC, allowing the policy to generate actions without actual depth data.

**B. Depth Completion Module (DCM)**
- **Purpose:** To predict depth features (ˆf_depth_t) from RGB image features (f_rgb_t), incorporating spatial prior knowledge. This enables the model to infer 3D perception from RGB inputs when depth data is unavailable.
- **Mechanism:**
 - **Feature Extraction:** A frozen visual encoder (ViT) and a specific projection layer (Proj_rgb) extract RGB features (f_rgb_t). Similarly, a ViT and a separate projection layer (Proj_depth) extract filtered depth features (f_depth_t) from depth images (o_depth_t) during training.
 - **Spatial Prior Integration:** The DCM integrates *k* learnable tokens P (P ∈ R^(k×d)) to explicitly capture spatial prior knowledge.
 - **Cross-Attention:** These learnable tokens P serve as the query (Q). The RGB features (f_rgb_t) are concatenated with P to form the key (K) and value (V) components. Multiple cross-attention layers process these, allowing P to absorb spatial information relevant to depth from the RGB features.
 - **FFN Layer:** The output from the cross-attention layers is passed through a Feed-Forward Network (FFN) to produce the estimated depth feature (ˆf_depth_t). Essentially, P learns to store statistical spatial characteristics from training scenarios to aid in predicting depth features for manipulation tasks.

**C. Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC)**
- **Purpose:** To refine the depth features predicted by the DCM, eliminate noise, and reduce cumulative errors that can arise from continuous, imperfect predictions over time in sequential manipulation tasks.
- **Mechanism:**
 - **Quantization:** The DAC transforms continuous depth features (ˆf_depth_t) into discrete quantized vectors. It defines a codebook Z ∈ R^(N×d) (a set of N codewords/centroids). For a given depth feature, it finds the closest codeword in Z, effectively discretizing the feature.
 - **Robustness:** This quantization process acts like clustering, representing depth features with a set of robust centroids. This makes the model more resilient to noise and variations in predicted depth, which is critical for maintaining trajectory accuracy.

**D. Training Details**
The training process is divided into three distinct phases:
1. **Warm-up:**
 - **Objective:** To train an imitation learning policy using *both* RGB and ground truth depth inputs.
 - **Method:** A joint depth model and a frozen pre-trained RGB-based model are trained together. The loss (L_warmup) is a mean squared error (MSE) between the predicted action (â_t) and the ground truth action (a_t). This step establishes a strong multimodal policy.
2. **Align:**
 - **Objective:** To train the Depth Completion Module (DCM) to predict depth features from RGB features, removing the dependency on actual depth images during inference.
 - **Method:** Using paired RGB-D data from collected trajectories, the DCM is trained to make its predicted depth features (DCM(P, sg(f_rgb_t))) match the ground truth depth features (sg(f_depth_t)). A stop gradient (sg) operation is applied to the input features to ensure only the DCM parameters are updated, transferring knowledge into the DCM without altering the warm-up trained encoders. The loss (L_dcm) is an MSE between the predicted and actual depth features.
3. **Codebook:**
 - **Objective:** To train the Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC) to discretize and refine the depth features, enhancing robustness and reducing cumulative errors.
 - **Method:** The depth branch (from warm-up) is frozen, and only the Codebook is trained using MSE loss. To prevent "codebook collapse" (where only a few codewords are utilized), the authors adopt a strategy inspired by CVQ-VAE. This involves a running average operation for each codeword (c_k), and reinitializing unoptimized points (low usage codewords) in each iteration using probabilistic sampling (¯z_k). This ensures a diverse and effective set of codewords.

**Inference Loop (Figure 1)**
During inference, the policy receives an RGB image and the task instruction. The RGB features are extracted and fed into the DCM to predict depth features. These predicted depth features are then quantized by the DAC. Finally, the instruction features, RGB features, and the quantized *predicted* depth features are combined and fed into the VLM and policy to generate the manipulation action.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents comprehensive experimental results from both simulated LIBERO environments and real-world scenarios to validate the DI² framework.

**A. Preliminary Experiment (RGB-D Input)**
- **Setup:** Models were evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark with full RGB-D inputs (except RGB-RF). Baselines included RGB-RF (RGB-only), RGB-D-RF (direct RGB+D concatenation), Data Aug (random modality removal), MM Prompt (learnable tokens for modality combinations), and *Ours* (DI² framework with ground truth depth, i.e., DCM replaced by actual depth branch).
- **Results (Table I):**
 - *Ours* achieved the highest average success rate of **63.95%**, outperforming the baseline RGB-RF (57.95%) by nearly 6% and RGB-D-RF (61.25%).
 - This indicates that the DI² framework, when provided with actual depth information (even through a trained depth branch and codebook), effectively utilizes 3D perception for superior performance.
 - The model showed a notable advantage in the "Long Horizon" suite (37.4% vs. 30.2% for RGB-D-RF), suggesting improved long-term planning with accurate depth.
 - The superior performance of *Ours* over direct RGB-D-RF highlighted the benefit of the Codebook in reducing noise and enhancing depth feature robustness, even with ground truth depth.

**B. Main Experiment (RGB-only Input at Inference)**
- **Setup:** Models were evaluated on LIBERO using *only* RGB images for inference. Baselines included those from the preliminary experiment, plus two cross-modal knowledge distillation methods: CRD (contrastive learning) and CMKD (MSE loss).
- **Results (Table II):**
 - The proposed *Ours* method achieved an average success rate of **63.15%**, significantly outperforming all baselines.
 - It showed the *smallest performance drop* from its RGB-D counterpart (*Ours* at 63.95%) to RGB-only inference, maintaining nearly equivalent performance (only 0.8% drop). This is a crucial practical advantage.
 - In contrast, RGB-D-RF, which heavily relies on depth, experienced a massive drop from 61.25% (RGB-D) to 15.65% (RGB-only).
 - Data Aug (54.50%) and MM Prompt (48.90%) performed better than RGB-D-RF in RGB-only but still fell short of the RGB-RF baseline (57.95%), indicating they did not successfully enhance RGB-only performance for this task.
 - CRD (47.60%) and CMKD (50.60%) also failed to significantly outperform the RGB-RF baseline, potentially because they focused too broadly on feature transfer rather than manipulation-specific requirements.
 - The *Ours* method demonstrated superior performance across all suites, especially in "Spatial" (71.20%), "Long Horizon" (36.40%), and "Goal" (66.40%), showcasing its enhanced 3D perception and robust decision-making.

**C. Qualitative Results (Figure 3)**
- Visualizations illustrated that models *without* depth features exhibited biased spatial position perception, leading to inaccurate action planning when approaching target objects.
- The *Ours* method, with its depth completion module, accurately perceived 3D positions and shapes, enabling precise planning and execution, especially when the robotic arm was close to the target.

**D. Prediction Error Analysis (Figure 4)**
- This analysis directly compared action sequences generated from predicted depth data (with and without DAC) against those from actual depth images.
- The results showed that using the **Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC) significantly reduced action prediction error** across all suites, highlighting its effectiveness in improving the quality of depth prediction and generating more accurate actions.

**E. Ablation Study (Table III)**
- **Setup:** Evaluated the individual contributions of the Depth Completion Module (DCM) and Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC).
- **Results:**
 - Without either DCM or DAC (using an MLP for depth prediction), average success was only 36.95%.
 - Adding **DCM alone** (`✓ ×`) dramatically increased the average success rate to **60.20%**, with significant improvements in Object (87.00%), Spatial (67.00%), and Long Horizon (34.40%) suites. This confirms the DCM's critical role in extracting spatial information from RGB.
 - Adding **DAC on top of DCM** (`✓ ✓`) further improved the average success rate to **63.15%**. DAC particularly enhanced performance in "Goal" (66.40%), "Spatial" (71.20%), and "Long Horizon" (36.40%) suites.
 - A notable observation was a slight performance drop in the "Object" suite when DAC was added. The authors suggest this might be related to the specific characteristics of this suite, aligning with similar trends observed in Table I and II.

**F. Real-World Experiments (Figure 5, Table IV)**
- **Setup:** A Franka Emika Panda robotic arm with Intel D435 cameras was used for four complex tasks (Stack the block, Close the drawer, Pick up the banana, Cook the pepper). 20 high-quality trajectories were collected for each task, and a multi-task model was trained. 30 repeated tests were conducted for each task.
- **Results:**
 - The *Ours* method with RGB-D input achieved high success rates (73.33% average, up to 80% for "Pick up the banana").
 - Crucially, the *Ours* method with **RGB-only input maintained exceptional performance**, with an average success rate of 66.67% (e.g., 80% for "Pick up the banana," 73.33% for "Stack the block"). The drop from RGB-D to RGB-only was minimal, confirming real-world robustness.
 - In contrast, RGB-D-RF (RGB-only) showed a severe performance degradation (average 7.5%), highlighting its reliance on explicit depth. RGB-RF also performed poorly (average 17.5%).
- These results strongly validate the practical effectiveness and reliability of the DI² framework in complex, real-world robotic manipulation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Depth Information Injection (DI²)" framework presents a significant advancement in generalizable robotic manipulation, with several important implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced 3D Perception for RGB-based Policies:** The most direct impact is the ability to infuse RGB-based robotic policies with crucial 3D spatial perception. This is vital for fine-grained manipulation tasks that demand precise interaction with objects, effectively closing a critical perception gap inherent in RGB-only systems.
- **Practical Real-World Deployment:** By training with RGB-D but deploying with RGB-only input (using predicted depth), the framework offers a highly practical solution. It eliminates the need for depth sensors in deployment, which can be costly, fragile, or ineffective in certain environments (e.g., challenging lighting, reflective surfaces). This significantly broadens the applicability of advanced robotic systems to diverse and unconstrained real-world settings.
- **Robustness against Noise and Cumulative Errors:** The Depth-Aware Codebook specifically addresses the challenge of noise and cumulative errors common in sequential decision-making tasks with predicted modalities. By discretizing and refining depth features, it ensures that even imperfect predictions do not cascade into significant trajectory deviations, leading to more reliable and stable robot operation.
- **Leveraging Existing Foundation Models:** The approach successfully builds upon existing pre-trained RGB-based foundation models (like RoboFlamingo), demonstrating how to effectively enhance their capabilities without requiring a complete architectural overhaul or retraining from scratch on massive multimodal datasets. This promotes efficient knowledge transfer and accelerates robotic AI development.
- **Generalizability and Adaptability:** The framework's success across diverse LIBERO benchmark suites (Object, Spatial, Long Horizon, Goal) and real-world tasks indicates improved generalizability. Robots trained with this method can potentially adapt better to variations in object properties, scene layouts, and task requirements.
- **Reduced Data Requirements for Multimodality:** The method highlights that robust 3D perception can be achieved with *minimal aligned RGB-D trajectory data* for fine-tuning, rather than requiring extensive multimodal datasets for every new task. This makes multimodal robotic learning more accessible.
- **Future Directions in Robotics:** This work opens avenues for future research in several areas:
 - **More sophisticated depth prediction:** Exploring more advanced generative models for depth completion that might capture finer details or handle extreme occlusions.
 - **Adaptive Codebook learning:** Investigating dynamic or adaptive codebooks that can evolve with new task demands or environmental changes.
 - **Integration with other modalities:** Applying similar injection principles to other sensory modalities (e.g., tactile, force) to further enhance robotic intelligence.
 - **Uncertainty quantification:** Incorporating measures of uncertainty in predicted depth to inform the policy's decision-making process, especially in safety-critical applications.

In summary, the DI² framework represents a significant step towards enabling intelligent robots to perform complex manipulation tasks reliably and efficiently in real-world environments, by strategically injecting essential 3D perception capabilities into powerful RGB-based policies.
