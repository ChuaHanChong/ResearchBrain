# See, Point, Fly: A Learning-Free VLM Framework for Universal Unmanned Aerial Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The "See, Point, Fly" (SPF) framework introduces a training-free approach to unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) navigation, utilizing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to ground action prediction as 2D spatial waypoints rather than textual commands. This method enables UAVs to successfully navigate diverse environments using natural language instructions, achieving over 90% success rates in both simulated and real-world scenarios while outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional end-to-end learning methods for UAV navigation struggle with generalization to unseen environments or novel natural language instructions, often requiring extensive retraining.
- Existing VLM-based aerial navigation systems rely on textual action generation, which provides imprecise, discrete control unsuitable for fine-grained 3D UAV movements.
- There is a need for robust, adaptable, and cost-efficient UAV navigation systems that support closed-loop control and do not require extensive task-specific training data.

## Method

- **SPF** leverages frozen Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to predict ==2D waypoints== directly on the input image, reframing action prediction as a ==2D spatial grounding== task.
- An ==adaptive travel distance scaling== module is incorporated to convert the VLM's discrete depth label into an adjusted step size, optimizing movement efficiency and safety.
- A ==policy mapping== stage transforms the 2D waypoints and adjusted depth into 3D displacement vectors, which are then decomposed into standard UAV control primitives (yaw, pitch, throttle) for real-time, closed-loop execution.

## Results

- SPF achieved an average success rate of **93.9%** in simulation and **92.7%** in real-world tasks, consistently outperforming training-free baselines like PIVOT (**28.7%** sim, **5.5%** real).
- The framework demonstrated superior efficiency by completing tasks faster than baselines while maintaining high success rates and proved robust across various VLM backbones.
- Ablation studies confirmed that 2D waypoint labeling is significantly more effective than text generation for action prediction, and the adaptive travel distance scaling improved both navigation efficiency and success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Translating VLM outputs into 2D spatial waypoints on images, instead of text generation, offers a more precise and generalizable method for embodied agent control.
> - Pre-trained VLMs possess inherent common-sense and visual-linguistic understanding sufficient for zero-shot aerial navigation without requiring task-specific training.
> - Combining VLM-generated spatial targets with an adaptive step-size controller and geometric mapping allows for efficient, safe, and continuous 3D UAV control in diverse environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous UAV navigation, particularly in complex and unstructured environments using natural language instructions, remains a significant challenge in robotics. This field, often framed as Aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (AVLN), requires a robust integration of visual perception, language understanding, high-level task planning, and low-level action control.

Traditionally, AVLN tasks have been approached using **end-to-end policy learning frameworks**, which include imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL). These methods train models (consisting of text and vision encoders, followed by a policy head) on extensive datasets of expert demonstrations or through environmental interactions to map observations and instructions directly to UAV actions. While successful in controlled or similar-to-training environments, a major limitation of these approaches is their **poor generalization** to unseen environments, novel task instructions, or dynamic scenarios due to the limited scale and diversity of their training data. Examples include methods that build intermediate map representations (GSMN), utilize mask generators (CIFF), or employ conditional transformers (LLMIR, AVDN).

More recently, the advent of **large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, trained on vast internet datasets, has opened new avenues for training-free, zero-shot generalization in embodied AI. These VLMs possess rich common-sense knowledge, strong visual/language understanding, reasoning, and planning capabilities. However, directly repurposing VLMs, which are primarily designed for text generation, into embodied agents that produce physical actions has been an underexplored challenge in AVLN. Existing VLM-based approaches (e.g., TypeFly, UAV-VLA, Flex, GeoNav) have largely adopted a direct solution: prompting VLMs to generate UAV actions in **textual forms**. This can involve either continuous actions or selections from predefined discrete skill sets. While simple, this paradigm suffers from two critical drawbacks: (1) textual output is inherently **unsuitable for representing high-precision, fine-grained actions** required for embodied agents, and (2) these VLMs are generally **not trained on aerial navigation data to predict 3D actions**, leading to suboptimal control precision and restricted action spaces.

"See, Point, Fly" (SPF) directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel, training-free VLM-based framework that shifts the paradigm from text generation to **2D spatial grounding** for action prediction in AVLN. Instead of asking the VLM to generate text commands, SPF leverages the VLM's inherent ability for visual grounding by prompting it to annotate 2D waypoints directly on the input image. This innovative approach distinguishes SPF from previous VLM-based methods by:
- **Improving precision and action space:** Transforming 2D waypoints into 3D displacement vectors using camera information provides more granular and flexible control compared to discrete text commands or skill libraries.
- **Enhancing generalization:** By relying on general spatial understanding abilities of VLMs, SPF avoids the need for task-specific training or domain knowledge of AVLN.
- **Zero-shot capability:** It operates entirely without training, directly deploying frozen VLMs.

This work builds upon the general idea of using VLMs for 2D waypoint prediction, previously explored in robot manipulation and navigation (e.g., RT-Trajectory, PIVOT). SPF adapts and refines this concept specifically for the AVLN setting, demonstrating superior performance even against other training-free approaches like PIVOT, which requires selecting from pre-sampled candidate actions. Thus, SPF represents a significant advancement in the development of robust, generalizable, and precise autonomous UAV navigation systems by cleverly re-framing the VLM's role from a text generator to a spatial reasoner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "See, Point, Fly" (SPF) framework is to enable **universal unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) navigation** without the need for task-specific training. Specifically, SPF aims to empower UAVs to navigate to **any goal**, based on **any type of free-form natural language instructions**, and in **any kind of environment**.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the limitations of existing approaches to autonomous UAV navigation:

- **Addressing Generalization Gaps in End-to-End Learning:** Conventional end-to-end policy learning methods for AVLN struggle with generalization. They often fail to perform robustly in unseen environments or when faced with novel, free-form instructions that deviate from their training data. This limitation restricts their real-world applicability and requires extensive, diverse, and often costly data collection for new scenarios. SPF aims to bypass this by leveraging the pre-trained, broad knowledge of VLMs, enabling zero-shot generalization.

- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing VLM-based Approaches:** While VLMs offer promising capabilities for zero-shot generalization, prior VLM-based AVLN systems treated action prediction as a text generation task. This approach has two critical shortcomings:
 - **Lack of Precision:** Textual outputs are inherently imprecise for representing the continuous, fine-grained 3D actions (e.g., specific velocities or displacements) required for effective UAV control.
 - **Restricted Action Space:** Generating discrete action commands or selecting from a limited skill library restricts the UAV's movement flexibility, leading to suboptimal trajectories and hindering navigation in complex scenarios.
 SPF is motivated to overcome these by proposing a more granular and spatially aware action representation.

- **Enabling Robust, Adaptable, and Closed-Loop Control:** There is a need for UAV navigation systems that can continuously adapt to dynamic environments and targets. Current methods often struggle with real-time replanning and following moving objects. SPF aims to provide a framework that inherently supports closed-loop control, allowing UAVs to reactively adjust their flight path.

- **Simplifying Deployment and Reducing Training Costs:** The high computational and data requirements for training complex deep learning policies are a significant barrier to widespread adoption. By being "training-free" and utilizing "frozen" pre-trained VLMs, SPF seeks to lower the entry barrier for deploying advanced autonomous UAV capabilities, making them more accessible and efficient.

In essence, SPF is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of VLMs for embodied aerial robotics by reframing the core problem of action prediction in a way that aligns with VLMs' strengths in visual understanding, rather than forcing them into a text-generation paradigm for control. The core insight is to treat action prediction as a **2D spatial grounding task**, which the authors hypothesize will lead to more precise, generalizable, and efficient UAV navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "See, Point, Fly" (SPF) framework operates as a novel, training-free, VLM-based system for universal UAV navigation, leveraging a key insight: framing action prediction as a 2D spatial grounding task rather than text generation. The methodology is structured around an iterative perception-action loop with three main stages: VLM-based obstacle-aware action planning, adaptive travel distance scaling, and policy mapping from image space to 3D actions, all integrated into a reactive control loop.

#### 3.1. VLM-based Obstacle-Aware Action Planning

At the core of SPF is a frozen (pre-trained) Vision-Language Model (VLM) responsible for high-level spatial reasoning.
- **Input:** At each timestep, the VLM receives the current egocentric visual observation ($I_t$, an RGB camera frame) and a natural language instruction ($\ell$) from the user, which specifies the desired UAV task (e.g., "Fly to the person without hitting the obstacle").
- **Output:** Instead of generating text commands, the VLM is prompted to produce a structured spatial understanding, represented as an intermediate spatial plan ($O_t$). This plan consists of:
 - **2D Waypoint (u, v):** Pixel coordinates on the input image indicating the desired navigation target. This is the crucial "2D spatial grounding" element.
 - **Discretized Depth Label ($d_{VLM}$):** A VLM-predicted label (e.g., from 1 to L, where L is a maximum depth) representing the intended travel distance along the UAV's forward direction. This is not a sensor-based depth measurement but an abstract indication of how far to move.
 - **Obstacle Information (Optional):** When obstacle avoidance is activated, the VLM can also output bounding boxes and labels for detected obstacles, allowing it to generate waypoints that inherently guide the UAV around impediments.
- **Mechanism:** This stage effectively transforms complex, vague language instructions into explicit, interpretable spatial waypoints grounded in the visual scene, leveraging the VLM's strong capabilities in affordance annotation and general spatial understanding without specific AVLN training.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Travel Distance Scaling

VLMs, despite their reasoning capabilities, may lack a precise understanding of real-world 3D geometry and the nuances of UAV flight. To prevent overly aggressive or unsafe movements and enhance navigation efficiency, SPF incorporates an adaptive controller module.
- **Problem:** Direct use of $d_{VLM}$ could lead to fixed-step movements, which are inefficient in open spaces and potentially dangerous near obstacles.
- **Solution:** A non-linear scaling curve is applied to convert the VLM's discrete depth label ($d_{VLM}$) into an **adjusted step size ($d_{adj}$)**. The formula is $d_{adj} = \max(d_{min}, s \times (d_{VLM}/L)^p)$, where 's' is a global scaling factor, 'p' controls the nonlinearity, and $d_{min}$ sets a minimum step size for safety.
- **Benefit:** This adaptive scaling allows the UAV to take larger steps in open, unobstructed areas and smaller, more cautious movements when approaching targets or navigating through cluttered environments. This mechanism implicitly adapts the trajectory to scene geometry without requiring external depth sensors or explicit 3D maps.

#### 3.3. Policy Mapping from Image Space to 3D Actions

The structured VLM output, now including the adjusted depth $d_{adj}$, is transformed into executable 3D motion commands.
- **Unprojection:** The 2D waypoint (u, v) and $d_{adj}$ are unprojected through a standard pin-hole camera model to obtain a **3D displacement vector ($S_x, S_y, S_z$)** in the UAV’s body frame.
 - $S_x = u \cdot d_{adj} \cdot \tan(\alpha)$
 - $S_y = d_{adj}$ (forward motion, aligned with UAV's y-axis)
 - $S_z = v \cdot d_{adj} \cdot \tan(\beta)$
 where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the camera's horizontal and vertical half field-of-view angles, respectively.
- **Decomposition into Control Primitives:** This 3D displacement vector is then decomposed into standard UAV control primitives:
 - **Yaw ($\Delta\theta$):** Lateral rotation, calculated as $\tan^{-1}(S_x/S_y)$.
 - **Pitch ($\Delta Pitch$):** Forward/backward tilt, derived from the horizontal magnitude $\sqrt{S_x^2 + S_y^2}$.
 - **Throttle ($\Delta Throttle$):** Vertical movement, corresponding to $S_z$.
- **Command Generation:** Each control primitive is converted into a velocity-duration pair. The duration is calculated based on the magnitude of the required adjustment and a predefined constant speed for each primitive (e.g., $D_{yaw} = \Delta\theta / P_{yaw}$).

#### 3.4. Reactive Control Loop Execution

The system operates in a closed-loop manner, ensuring continuous adaptation and responsiveness.
- **Execution Queue:** The calculated velocity-duration commands (yaw, pitch, throttle) are enqueued and sent to the UAV (e.g., using DJI Tello SDK) with temporal synchronization.
- **Continuous Correction:** The "perception-action-control" cycle repeats, allowing the UAV to continuously update its observations, replan its movements using the VLM, and execute new commands. This enables smooth, responsive, and low-latency control, crucial for following dynamic targets and navigating dynamic environments.
- **Zero-shot & Hardware-Friendly:** The entire framework is training-free, does not require external depth sensors, and utilizes a lightweight geometric controller, making it robust and hardware-friendly for systems like DJI Tello EDU drones.

In summary, SPF's methodology brilliantly repurposes powerful, frozen VLMs for embodied control by translating vague language into precise 2D visual targets, then geometrically transforming these targets into executable 3D UAV actions, all within an adaptive and reactive control framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "See, Point, Fly" (SPF) framework demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot, training-free UAV navigation, significantly outperforming existing baselines in both simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Performance Evaluation

**Quantitative Results (Table 1):**
SPF was extensively evaluated across 6 distinct task categories (Navigation, Obstacle Avoidance, Long Horizon, Reasoning, Search, Follow) with 23 simulated tasks and 11 real-world tasks.
- **Simulation Performance:** SPF achieved an average success rate of **93.9%**, dramatically outperforming its closest training-free baseline, PIVOT (28.7%), and the language-driven TypeFly (0.9%).
 - SPF showed exceptional robustness in complex scenarios: 92% success in Obstacle Avoidance (vs. 16% for PIVOT), 92% in Long Horizon tasks (vs. 28% for PIVOT), and 92% in Search tasks (vs. 36% for PIVOT).
 - It achieved a perfect 100% success rate in Navigation and 93.3% in Reasoning tasks.
- **Real-world Performance:** SPF maintained its strong performance with an average success rate of **92.7%** on a DJI Tello EDU drone. This contrasts sharply with TypeFly (23.6%) and PIVOT (5.5%), both of which struggled significantly in real-world settings due to object recognition issues or reliance on candidate path selection.
 - SPF achieved 100% success rates in Navigation, Reasoning, and Follow tasks in the real world, and high rates in Obstacle Avoidance (70%) and Long Horizon (90%).

**Efficiency (Figure 6):**
Beyond success rate, SPF demonstrated superior efficiency. In 5 representative real-world tasks, SPF not only successfully completed tasks where baselines often failed but also achieved **faster completion times**. This highlights its operational effectiveness and reliability.

**Qualitative Results (Figures 4 & 5):**
Visual comparisons of flight trajectories in both simulation and real-world scenarios indicated that SPF generates smoother navigation trajectories, effectively avoids obstacles, and reliably reaches targets. Baselines often exhibited erratic movements, collisions, or failed to issue any flight commands, resulting in no discernible path.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies validated the effectiveness of SPF's core design choices:

- **Structured Prompting and Grounding (Table 2):**
 - **SPF (2D Waypoint Labeling):** Achieved 100% success rate in navigation tasks with stronger VLMs, and 87% even with a lighter VLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite).
 - **PIVOT (Visual Prompting/Candidate Selection):** Achieved 40% success rate, indicating that directly generating waypoints is more effective than selecting from candidates.
 - **Plain VLM (Text Generation):** Performed poorly with only a 7% success rate, strongly confirming the hypothesis that text generation is unsuitable for precise action prediction in AVLN. This finding directly validates SPF's central insight of 2D spatial grounding.

- **VLM Backbone Generalization (Table 2):**
 SPF demonstrated remarkable robustness and generalization across various VLM backbones. Stronger models like Gemini 2.0 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and GPT-4.1 all achieved 100% success rates. Even less capable models like Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Llama 4 Maverick, and Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite achieved high success rates (93.3% and 87% respectively). This underscores that the SPF framework's effectiveness is largely VLM-agnostic, meaning it can leverage advancements in future VLMs.

- **Adaptive Travel Distance Scaling (Table 3 & 6):**
 The adaptive step-size controller significantly improved efficiency without compromising success.
 - For tasks like "Fly to the cones and the next," the adaptive controller **halved the completion time** (61s to 28s) while maintaining 100% success.
 - In "I’m thirsty. Find something that can help me," it reduced completion time (50.25s to 35.20s) and **improved success rate from 80% to 100%**.
 - Similarly, "It’s raining..." saw a substantial time reduction (47s to 30s) with 100% success. These results confirm the efficacy of dynamic step adjustment for both speed and reliability.

- **VLM-Integrated Obstacle Avoidance (Table 4):**
 SPF's VLM-integrated approach to obstacle avoidance, where bounding boxes are generated directly from the VLM, showed a crucial trade-off. While it had a slightly higher latency (1.077s) than using an external detector (YOLOv8n, 1.726s total due to VLM + detector inference), it offered **higher accuracy (88.8% vs. 72.2%)** and, more importantly, **zero-shot generalization to any object**, unlike specialized detectors limited to known classes. This highlights the VLM's powerful generalization capabilities for dynamic environment understanding.

In conclusion, the results unequivocally establish SPF as a highly effective, robust, and efficient framework for zero-shot UAV navigation, setting new benchmarks in both simulated and real-world AVLN tasks by virtue of its innovative 2D spatial grounding approach and adaptive control mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "See, Point, Fly" (SPF) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of autonomous UAV navigation, with broad implications and potential impact across several domains.

**1. Pushing the Boundaries of Zero-Shot and Training-Free Embodied AI:**
SPF's most significant contribution is demonstrating a highly effective, entirely training-free framework for universal UAV navigation. By cleverly reframing action prediction as a 2D spatial grounding task, it unlocks the immense reasoning and understanding capabilities of large, pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for embodied control without requiring any task-specific data collection or policy optimization. This paradigm shift addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional methods: their inability to generalize to novel environments and instructions without extensive retraining. The achievement of over 90% success rates in diverse, complex tasks in both simulated and real-world settings, purely from zero-shot execution, is a landmark result for AVLN.

**2. Enhanced Precision and Generalization for UAV Control:**
Previous VLM-based approaches struggled with the inherent imprecision of textual action commands and the limited scope of predefined skill sets. SPF's method of converting 2D waypoints into precise 3D displacement vectors provides a more granular and flexible control mechanism. This enables UAVs to navigate with greater accuracy and adapt to a wider range of scenarios, from following dynamic targets to traversing cluttered environments, as evidenced by its superior performance in obstacle avoidance, long-horizon, and follow tasks. The robustness across different VLM backbones further underscores its generalizability and future-proof design.

**3. Practicality and Accessibility for Real-World Deployment:**
The training-free nature of SPF drastically reduces the computational resources, time, and expert knowledge typically required for developing and deploying autonomous UAV systems. This makes advanced navigation capabilities more accessible to a wider range of users and applications. Its demonstration on a low-cost, off-the-shelf DJI Tello EDU drone highlights its hardware-friendliness and potential for deployment on diverse, resource-constrained platforms. The closed-loop control mechanism, while acknowledging VLM latency, allows for responsive adaptation to dynamic real-world conditions.

**4. Foundation for Future Research and Applications:**
SPF lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
- **Improved VLM Integration:** The framework's VLM-agnostic nature means that future advancements in VLMs (e.g., lower latency, higher precision grounding, improved spatial reasoning) can be directly leveraged to enhance SPF's performance.
- **Reduced Latency and Enhanced Reactivity:** Addressing the VLM inference latency (currently 1-3 seconds) is a critical area for improvement, especially for highly dynamic environments or high-speed tasks. This could involve exploring more efficient VLM architectures or edge deployment strategies.
- **Sophisticated Navigation Strategies:** While SPF demonstrates effective point-to-point and obstacle-aware navigation, future work could integrate more sophisticated path planning, optimal search patterns, or long-term memory to handle extremely complex, multi-stage missions.
- **Robustness to VLM Inaccuracies:** Mitigating VLM hallucinations, misinterpretations, and grounding inaccuracies for small or distant targets remains an important challenge, potentially leading to research into uncertainty-aware control or self-correction mechanisms.

**5. Broadening Application Scope:**
The ability of UAVs to understand and execute free-form language instructions in diverse environments has significant implications for various applications, including:
- **Environmental Monitoring:** Automatically surveying specific areas or objects identified by natural language.
- **Search and Rescue:** Locating missing persons or critical points in disaster zones based on descriptive commands.
- **Inspection and Maintenance:** Navigating to and inspecting specific components of large structures.
- **Security and Surveillance:** Following designated individuals or patrolling areas based on contextual instructions.
- **Logistics and Delivery:** Navigating to complex delivery points specified verbally.

In summary, SPF marks a pivotal step in realizing truly autonomous and intelligent UAVs. By demonstrating that high-performance, generalizable navigation can be achieved without task-specific training, it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for advanced aerial robotics and opens up a new era of possibilities for VLM-driven embodied AI systems.
