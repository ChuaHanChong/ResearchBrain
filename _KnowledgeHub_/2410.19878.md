# Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning in Large Models: A Survey of Methodologies

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper from Zhejiang Laboratory systematically classifies over 100 Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methodologies, expanding the taxonomy to six categories beyond traditional NLP to include computer vision, multimodal, and diffusion models. It details how these methods significantly reduce computational costs and parameter counts for adapting large models while maintaining performance.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) and other large models have billions to trillions of parameters, requiring immense computational resources and memory for fine-tuning.
- The high resource intensity of full fine-tuning creates barriers to entry for many researchers and practitioners and limits rapid experimentation.
- Existing PEFT surveys often focus predominantly on NLP applications or use limited classification systems, making it challenging to navigate the rapidly evolving landscape across diverse AI domains.

## Method

- The survey introduces a comprehensive and unified taxonomy for ==PEFT methods==, categorizing them into six types: ==Additive==, ==Reparameterized==, ==Selective==, Hybrid, Quantization, and Multi-task PEFT.
- It provides detailed explanations of core principles and mathematical formulations for over **100** PEFT algorithms, enhancing understanding and accessibility.
- The work broadens the scope of PEFT applications beyond NLP, extensively covering its use in computer vision, diffusion models, and multimodal fusion.

## Results

- The survey presents a unified classification system for PEFT methods, including new categories like Hybrid, Quantization, and Multi-task PEFT, and details representative algorithms for each.
- It provides a comprehensive overview of PEFT applications in vision models, diffusion models, and multimodal large language models, showcasing their practical advantages.
- The work identifies **7** promising future research directions, including PEFT for multi-objective tasks, multimodal learning, automated PEFT design, and integration of differential privacy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - PEFT methods effectively reduce trainable parameters to less than 1-2% of the total, drastically cutting computational and memory costs for adapting large models.
> - The utility of PEFT extends across diverse AI modalities, enabling efficient fine-tuning for tasks in computer vision, diffusion model generation, and multimodal learning.
> - Hybrid PEFT and Quantization PEFT represent emerging trends, combining different efficiency techniques to further enhance performance or reduce resource requirements.
