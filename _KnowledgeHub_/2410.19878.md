# Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning in Large Models: A Survey of Methodologies

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper from Zhejiang Laboratory systematically classifies over 100 Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methodologies, expanding the taxonomy to six categories beyond traditional NLP to include computer vision, multimodal, and diffusion models. It details how these methods significantly reduce computational costs and parameter counts for adapting large models while maintaining performance.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) and other large models have billions to trillions of parameters, requiring immense computational resources and memory for fine-tuning.
- The high resource intensity of full fine-tuning creates barriers to entry for many researchers and practitioners and limits rapid experimentation.
- Existing PEFT surveys often focus predominantly on NLP applications or use limited classification systems, making it challenging to navigate the rapidly evolving landscape across diverse AI domains.

## Method

- The survey introduces a comprehensive and unified taxonomy for ==PEFT methods==, categorizing them into six types: ==Additive==, ==Reparameterized==, ==Selective==, Hybrid, Quantization, and Multi-task PEFT.
- It provides detailed explanations of core principles and mathematical formulations for over **100** PEFT algorithms, enhancing understanding and accessibility.
- The work broadens the scope of PEFT applications beyond NLP, extensively covering its use in computer vision, diffusion models, and multimodal fusion.

## Results

- The survey presents a unified classification system for PEFT methods, including new categories like Hybrid, Quantization, and Multi-task PEFT, and details representative algorithms for each.
- It provides a comprehensive overview of PEFT applications in vision models, diffusion models, and multimodal large language models, showcasing their practical advantages.
- The work identifies **7** promising future research directions, including PEFT for multi-objective tasks, multimodal learning, automated PEFT design, and integration of differential privacy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - PEFT methods effectively reduce trainable parameters to less than 1-2% of the total, drastically cutting computational and memory costs for adapting large models.
> - The utility of PEFT extends across diverse AI modalities, enabling efficient fine-tuning for tasks in computer vision, diffusion model generation, and multimodal learning.
> - Hybrid PEFT and Quantization PEFT represent emerging trends, combining different efficiency techniques to further enhance performance or reduce resource requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The past few years have witnessed a paradigm shift in artificial intelligence, largely driven by the unprecedented success of large language models (LLMs). Models like OpenAI's GPT series, Meta's LLaMA, Anthropic's Claude, and Google's Gemini have demonstrated groundbreaking performance in various natural language processing (NLP) tasks, often approaching or surpassing human capabilities. This progress, however, comes at a significant cost: these models possess billions to trillions of parameters, demanding immense computational resources, memory, and storage for both training and fine-tuning. This resource intensity creates substantial barriers to entry for many researchers and practitioners, limits rapid experimentation, and raises environmental sustainability concerns.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) emerges as a crucial solution to these challenges. Instead of updating all parameters of a pre-trained large model for a specific downstream task, PEFT methods aim to adapt only a small subset of parameters (often less than 1-2% of the total) while keeping the majority of the pre-trained weights frozen. This approach drastically reduces computational cost, memory footprint, and storage requirements, making LLMs more accessible and practical for diverse applications, especially on hardware platforms with limited resources.

This paper fits into the broader research landscape as a comprehensive survey that aims to consolidate and categorize the rapidly proliferating PEFT methodologies. The authors explicitly position their work by comparing it with five other notable PEFT surveys (Table I), highlighting its enhanced scope. Previous surveys often focused predominantly on NLP applications or provided specific taxonomies. For instance, Ding et al. (2022) introduced "Delta Tuning" and focused on NLP, while Lialin et al. (2023) offered a comprehensive classification across five efficiency dimensions. Xu et al. (2023) evaluated PEFT within NLP, and Xin et al. (2024) specifically reviewed visual PEFT. Han et al. (2024) provided a detailed classification across various architectures.

Wang et al.'s survey distinguishes itself by extending the coverage beyond traditional NLP to include **computer vision, multimodal fusion, and diffusion models**. It also aims to offer a more unified and intuitive classification system, incorporating "Hybrid," "Quantization," and "Multi-task" categories beyond the conventional "Additive," "Reparameterized," and "Selective" approaches. By providing an updated and expanded overview, this survey serves as a critical resource for researchers seeking to navigate the complex and rapidly evolving PEFT landscape, accelerate their understanding, and identify fertile ground for future innovation across various AI domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the inherent tension between the groundbreaking capabilities of large language models and the prohibitive computational and storage costs associated with their fine-tuning. While LLMs excel in various generative and understanding tasks, their massive parameter counts make traditional full fine-tuning impractical for many researchers and organizations, especially those with limited hardware. The authors emphasize that PEFT offers a practical solution by enabling efficient adaptation of these models to specific downstream tasks with minimal additional parameters and computational resources.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of this survey are multifaceted:
1. **Introduce Preliminary Knowledge**: To provide foundational understanding of LLMs, including their historical development, emergent abilities (in-context learning, instruction following, step-by-step reasoning), and scaling laws (Kaplan, Chinchilla), as well as related optimization techniques (Instruction Tuning, Alignment Tuning, RLHF), datasets, and evaluation metrics.
2. **Systematically Classify PEFT Methodologies**: To offer a comprehensive, coherent, and updated taxonomy of PEFT algorithms, explaining their core ideas and principles. The survey aims to expand on existing classifications to include more recent and diverse approaches.
3. **Explore Diverse Applications**: To broaden the understanding of PEFT's applicability beyond conventional NLP tasks, explicitly covering its use in computer vision, multimodal fusion, and diffusion models.
4. **Identify Future Research Directions**: To highlight potential avenues for future advancements, addressing current limitations and underexplored aspects within PEFT, thereby guiding researchers towards impactful contributions.
5. **Enhance Readability and Accessibility**: To make the complex landscape of PEFT methodologies accessible to interested parties by employing intuitive schematic diagrams and structured tables, fostering quicker comprehension and accelerating development and innovation in the field.

In essence, the authors aim to create a definitive, systematic, and easy-to-digest resource that can serve as a solid knowledge base for researchers, accelerating PEFT's development and innovation across various AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology employed by Wang et al. is primarily a **literature review and synthesis approach**. Their systematic process can be broken down into several key steps:

1. **Literature Collection and Scope**: The authors collected and reviewed over 100 research articles related to PEFT published from June 2019 to July 2024. This timeframe ensures the inclusion of cutting-edge and recent advancements in the rapidly evolving field of large models.
2. **Foundational Context Establishment**: Before delving into PEFT methods, the survey dedicates a significant section (Section II) to establishing a robust preliminary knowledge base. This includes:
- **Large Language Models**: A summary of their background, key characteristics like emergent abilities (in-context learning, instruction following, step-by-step reasoning), and the influential scaling laws (Kaplan and Chinchilla) that govern their behavior and resource requirements. It also reviews prominent LLM families (GPT, LLaMA, OpenAI o1, DeepSeek, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, PaLM) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), including their architectures.
- **Optimization, Datasets, and Evaluation**: An overview of techniques like Instruction Tuning, Alignment Tuning, and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), along with commonly used datasets for pre-training, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and RLHF, and evaluation benchmarks for LLMs.
3. **Comprehensive PEFT Taxonomy Development**: The core of their methodology involves developing an extended and unified classification framework for PEFT methods. They move beyond the traditional tripartite classification (Additive, Reparameterized, Selective PEFT) to incorporate three additional categories: Hybrid PEFT, Quantization PEFT, and Multi-task PEFT. This structured taxonomy (illustrated in Figure 4) allows for a granular and logical organization of the diverse methods.
4. **Detailed Explanation and Categorization of Methods**: Within each category, the authors delve into representative PEFT algorithms, explaining their core ideas, principles, mathematical formulations where appropriate, and specific implementations (e.g., various Adapter types, LoRA and its derivatives, different masking strategies for Selective PEFT).
5. **Application-Specific Review**: The survey dedicates a section (Section IV) to outlining the applications of PEFT across different modalities and tasks, including:
- **Vision Models**: Focusing on image classification and dense prediction tasks.
- **Diffusion Models**: Covering generation by few-shot fine-tuning and controllable generation.
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLM)**: Emphasizing the role and design of modal connectors.
6. **Comparative Analysis and Visualization**: To enhance readability and academic value, the authors make extensive use of intuitive schematic diagrams (e.g., Figures 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17) to illustrate complex principles. They also utilize structured tables (e.g., Tables V, VI, VII) for comparative analysis of different approaches, summarizing their main ideas, number of trainable parameters, applications, advantages, limitations, and performance results across various tasks and base models.
7. **Future Directions Identification**: Based on their comprehensive review, the authors identify potential issues with existing PEFT techniques and aspects that have not received sufficient attention, proposing a series of promising research directions.

This systematic and detailed approach ensures a thorough and well-organized overview of the PEFT landscape, making the survey a valuable resource for both newcomers and seasoned researchers in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, this paper's "findings" are not experimental results but rather a structured synthesis of the current state of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT). The main contributions and "results" of this survey are:

1. **Comprehensive Categorization of PEFT Methodologies**: The survey provides an expanded and unified taxonomy of PEFT methods, classifying them into six primary categories:
- **Additive PEFT**: Introduces additional trainable components (e.g., Adapter, Soft Prompt, Scale and Shift methods like (IA)³, MoV, SSF, PASTA, and others like IPA, LST, Attention-Fusion). These methods maintain the original model frozen and only update the newly added parameters.
- **Reparameterized PEFT**: Restructures model parameters, typically through low-rank decomposition, to adapt to tasks. Key methods include LoRA and its derivatives (e.g., DyLoRA, AdaLoRA for dynamic rank allocation, and improvements like LoRA+, DoRA). These methods offer the advantage of no additional inference latency as the low-rank matrices can be merged with the original weights.
- **Selective PEFT**: Focuses on optimizing a specific, small subset of the original model's parameters, often using masking strategies. This includes Unstructured Masking (e.g., U-Diff pruning, U-Bitfit, FishMask, LT-SFT, LoRAPrune) and Structural Masking (e.g., S-Diff pruning, BitFit, FAR, Xattn Tuning).
- **Hybrid PEFT**: Combines strengths from multiple PEFT approaches (e.g., UniPELT, S4, MAM Adapter, LLM-Adapters, NOAH, AUTOPEFT). These methods aim for improved performance and flexibility across diverse tasks by leveraging different tuning mechanisms.
- **Quantization PEFT**: Integrates quantization techniques with PEFT to further reduce memory and computational costs, making models deployable on resource-constrained devices. Notable examples include QLoRA, LQ-LoRA, QA-LoRA, QDyLoRA, LoftQ, and BitDelta.
- **Multi-task PEFT**: Designed for scenarios requiring adaptation to multiple downstream tasks simultaneously, often by sharing parameters or dynamically composing adapters. This category is further broken down into Adapter-based (e.g., AdapterFusion, AdaMix, Hyperformer), Soft Prompt-based (e.g., SPoT, ATTEMPT, MPT, IPT), and LoRA-based methods (e.g., LoRAHub, MOELoRA, L-LoRA).

2. **Detailed Explanation of Core Principles**: For each category and its representative methods, the survey elucidates the underlying principles, mathematical formulations, and how they achieve parameter efficiency. Visual aids such as diagrams (e.g., Figures 3, 5-11) and structured tables (Table V) effectively summarize the main ideas, trainable parameters, applications, advantages, and limitations of each PEFT type.

3. **Comprehensive Overview of PEFT Applications Across Modalities**: The survey successfully breaks traditional boundaries by exploring PEFT's utility beyond NLP. It details applications in:
- **Vision Models**: Including image classification (e.g., VP, VPT, AdaptFormer, FacT) and dense prediction tasks (e.g., Polyhistor, ViT-Adapter, SAN, LoRand).
- **Diffusion Models**: Covering generation by few-shot fine-tuning (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, DiffFit, SVDiff, LyCORIS) and controllable generation (e.g., Sketch-guided Diffusion, ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter).
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**: Discussing how PEFT principles are applied to modal connectors (e.g., LLaMA-Adapter, Q-Former) to bridge information between different modalities and LLMs. Table VII quantitatively demonstrates the performance of various PEFT methods in these specific applications, showing significant reductions in trainable parameters while maintaining competitive results compared to full fine-tuning.

4. **Identification of Promising Future Research Directions**: The survey concludes by outlining seven critical areas for future investigation, addressing current limitations and emerging needs:
- PEFT methods for multi-objective tasks (e.g., balancing accuracy with privacy or fairness).
- PEFT methods in multimodal learning, focusing on effective cross-modal information integration.
- Automated design of adapter modules, potentially leveraging NAS techniques more efficiently.
- Heuristic search strategies for optimal hybrid PEFT method combinations.
- Continual learning for PEFT methods to prevent catastrophic forgetting in dynamic environments.
- Improving the calibration of fine-tuned LLMs to address overconfidence, especially in safety-critical applications.
- Integrating differential privacy into PEFT methods to handle sensitive data while maintaining performance and efficiency.

These findings collectively represent a valuable synthesis of the current state of PEFT, providing both a solid educational foundation and a clear roadmap for future research in this vital domain of AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this survey paper are substantial, particularly given the ever-increasing scale and pervasiveness of large models in modern AI.

**1. Accelerating Understanding and Knowledge Dissemination:** The survey provides a highly structured, comprehensive, and intuitive overview of PEFT methodologies. By classifying a vast array of techniques (over 100 articles) into a coherent taxonomy, explaining their core principles with clear diagrams and tables, and detailing their applications across various domains, the paper significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners. This structured knowledge base will allow newcomers to quickly grasp complex concepts and existing researchers to consolidate their understanding, thereby accelerating the overall research and development cycle in PEFT.

**2. Guiding Future Research and Innovation:** By meticulously identifying gaps in current PEFT research and proposing seven concrete future directions, the paper acts as a strategic roadmap for the field. The highlighted areas—such as multi-objective optimization, multimodal learning, automated PEFT design, continual learning, model calibration, and differential privacy—point towards critical challenges and high-impact opportunities. This guidance can prevent redundant efforts, foster interdisciplinary collaborations (e.g., between PEFT and privacy-preserving AI), and channel resources towards solving pressing real-world problems, from ethical AI development to efficient deployment on edge devices.

**3. Broadening Application Scope:** The explicit exploration of PEFT in vision models, diffusion models, and multimodal large language models significantly broadens the perceived applicability and potential impact of these techniques. This encourages researchers in these specific domains to adopt and adapt PEFT, leading to more efficient and scalable solutions for tasks like image generation, object detection, and visual question answering, where full fine-tuning is often prohibitively expensive. The quantitative comparisons in Table VII underscore the practical advantages of PEFT in these diverse applications.

**4. Promoting Sustainable AI Development:** The core motivation for PEFT is to mitigate the exorbitant computational and environmental costs associated with large models. By detailing methods that significantly reduce GPU usage, memory, and storage requirements, the survey implicitly promotes more sustainable AI research and deployment practices. This is crucial for democratizing access to powerful AI technologies and reducing the carbon footprint of AI development.

**5. Facilitating Benchmarking and Comparison:** The comprehensive tables summarizing trainable parameters, performance metrics, and application domains for various PEFT methods (Tables V, VI, VII) offer a valuable resource for comparative analysis. This allows researchers to quickly benchmark their new methods against existing ones, ensuring more rigorous evaluation and fostering healthy competition and advancement in the field.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is an active contribution that structures knowledge, identifies critical pathways for future investigation, and broadens the practical application of parameter-efficient techniques. Its comprehensive nature and forward-looking perspective will undoubtedly serve as a foundational reference for the next generation of PEFT research and its deployment across the rapidly expanding landscape of large AI models.
