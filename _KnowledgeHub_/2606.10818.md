# IMPACT: Learning Internal-Model Predictive Control for Forceful Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> IMPACT introduces a robotic control framework that learns an internal model to predict and compensate for external forces, such as object weight, enabling robust manipulation across varying object dynamics without requiring specialized force sensors. It achieved high success rates in tasks involving out-of-distribution object masses by decoupling high-level task planning from low-level force compensation.

## Problem

- Learning-based approaches for forceful robotic manipulation, which implicitly generate forces through end-effector tracking errors, demonstrate limited generalization to objects with out-of-distribution physical properties.
- Explicit force control methods rely on specialized and costly hardware (e.g., wrist force sensors), increasing system complexity and expense.
- Current methods often require extensive data collection across a wide range of object properties or environmental conditions to achieve robustness.

## Method

- The framework learns an internal model online to estimate slowly varying external wrenches (e.g., payload weight) from robot joint torque residuals.
- A high-level diffusion policy is trained for kinematic trajectory planning, while a low-level ==internal-model-based predictive controller== handles online force compensation.
- The predicted wrench from the internal model is applied as a feedforward command within an impedance control loop, reducing steady-state tracking errors.

## Results

- In simulation, IMPACT maintained high task success rates (near **100%**) across an object mass range of 0-10 kg, significantly outperforming baselines that saw success rates decline sharply beyond their training distributions.
- On a real Franka FR3 robot, IMPACT achieved 21/25 success on a 5 kg dumbbell after being trained only on a **2.5** kg dumbbell, while baselines trained on **2.5** kg failed (0/25) or had limited success even with varied training data (5/25).
- The internal model's 'surprise gate' enabled rapid re-adaptation to new loads (e.g., switching from a **2.5** kg to a 5 kg dumbbell) within a few interactions, maintaining low pose tracking errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level kinematic planning from low-level dynamic force compensation, through an internal model, significantly enhances generalization to unseen object properties.
> - An online-learning internal model, equipped with a 'surprise gate' mechanism, allows for rapid adaptation to unexpected external loads while maintaining stability during known interactions.
> - Robust estimation of external wrenches from readily available joint torque measurements eliminates the need for additional, costly force/torque sensors.
