# FireScope: Wildfire Risk Raster Prediction with a Chain-of-Thought Oracle

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces FireScope, a framework that integrates language-based Chain-of-Thought reasoning with visual generation for high-resolution, continuous wildfire risk prediction. It achieves strong out-of-distribution generalization across continents and provides human-interpretable reasoning traces alongside risk maps, outperforming baselines in OOD settings.

## Problem

- Traditional environmental models for wildfire risk prediction are limited by coarse spatial scales and often omit high-resolution visual and geographic context.
- Existing data-driven computer vision methods are primarily observational, struggling with generalization across diverse biomes and continents (out-of-distribution scenarios).
- There is a lack of causal reasoning, multimodal understanding, and unified benchmarks for reasoning-intensive wildfire risk estimation, along with challenges in model interpretability.

## Method

- The authors introduce **FireScope**-Bench, a large-scale multimodal benchmark comprising Sentinel-2 imagery, climate data, expert-defined risk rasters (USA), and real wildfire events (Europe) for cross-continental evaluation.
- They propose FireScope, a two-stage reasoning-to-generation framework: Stage 1 fine-tunes a Vision-Language Model (VLM) 'Oracle' using Reinforcement Learning to infer overall scalar wildfire risk with explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
- Stage 2 employs a lightweight vision Encoder-Decoder architecture to generate a fine-grained, continuous risk raster, conditioned by the scalar risk estimate from the Oracle via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layers.

## Results

- FireScope, utilizing CoT Oracle conditioning, achieved consistent and substantial performance gains in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, yielding the best Brier score and ROC AUC for distinguishing wildfire events across Europe.
- The CoT Qwen Oracle significantly outperformed all other Oracle baselines (e.g., Climate MLP, FWI, GPT-5, non-CoT Qwen) in OOD settings across Brier, ROC AUC, and ECE metrics, demonstrating the value of explicit reasoning.
- An expert study showed that wildfire experts could partially recover risk scores based solely on the factors considered in the Oracle's CoT summaries (QWK up to **0.33**), and automated metrics confirmed high consistency (**0.91**) and fidelity (**0.33**) of the generated rasters to the CoT reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit language-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, particularly when integrated with Vision-Language Models (VLMs), substantially improves out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization in visual generation tasks for complex spatial problems.
> - Conditioning fine-grained raster generation with an abstract, scalar judgment from a reasoning Oracle leads to more robust and generalizable models than directly leveraging a VLM's raw representational capacity for pixel-level output.
> - The framework provides human-interpretable reasoning traces that encode meaningful cues for domain experts, intrinsically enhancing the transparency and trustworthiness of AI systems in critical applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Wildfire risk assessment constitutes a critical challenge in environmental science and public safety. Accurate prediction necessitates the synthesis of diverse factors, including vegetation, topography, climate interactions, and human activity, to infer continuous risk maps. However, existing methodologies exhibit limitations in comprehensive integration and generalization.

Traditional approaches to wildfire risk prediction, primarily rooted in environmental modeling, often rely on physics-based indices such as the Fire Weather Index (FWI) or hybrid climate models. While these methods capture meteorological variability, they are constrained by coarse spatial scales and typically omit high-resolution visual and geographic context.

Concurrently, data-driven computer vision methods have predominantly focused on observational tasks, such as fire detection, segmentation, or spread estimation. These approaches often learn from imagery alone, which limits their capacity for generalization across diverse biomes and continents, leading to unreliable performance in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

The current research landscape is characterized by several identified gaps:
1. **Lack of Causal Reasoning and Multimodal Understanding:** Many existing models lack the ability to perform causal reasoning, which is essential for understanding the underlying drivers of wildfire risk rather than merely identifying correlations.
2. **Absence of Unified Benchmarks:** There has been no standardized benchmark that integrates multi-source data—specifically imagery, climate, and topography—to facilitate rigorous testing of models for reasoning-intensive wildfire risk estimation.
3. **Limited Cross-Continental Generalization:** Models frequently fail to generalize effectively when applied to regions geographically or climatically distinct from their training data.
4. **Interpretability Challenges:** While some explainable methods exist, they often involve significant computational overhead and are typically post-hoc, not intrinsically integrated into the prediction process.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by introducing a framework that explicitly integrates language-based reasoning with visual generation. By developing a multimodal benchmark and a reasoning-to-generation model, the authors aim to advance the field towards more generalizable, interpretable, and robust spatial prediction systems for wildfire risk, particularly for cross-continental applications. It seeks to bridge the gap between abstract reasoning about causal drivers and precise, pixel-level spatial output.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an advanced framework for predicting wildfire risk through the integration of multimodal data and explicit reasoning, thereby overcoming the limitations of existing methods in generalization and interpretability. The authors outline several specific key objectives:

1. **Develop a Reasoning-Intensive Spatial Prediction Framework:** To create a framework capable of integrating visual, climatic, and geographic factors to infer continuous wildfire risk maps, moving beyond purely observational tasks.
2. **Enhance Generalization Capabilities:** To improve the ability of wildfire risk models to generalize reliably across geographically diverse and out-of-distribution (OOD) regions, specifically demonstrating cross-continental applicability. The motivation here stems from the observed failure of purely visual or correlation-based models to perform robustly in unseen biomes or continents.
3. **Improve Model Interpretability and Transparency:** To provide human-interpretable reasoning traces alongside risk predictions, allowing domain experts to understand the causal factors and logic behind the model’s judgments. This is motivated by the need for greater transparency in socially relevant vision systems.
4. **Establish a Comprehensive Multimodal Benchmark:** To introduce a large-scale dataset and benchmark, FireScope-Bench, that explicitly tests reasoning-intensive wildfire risk estimation by combining Sentinel-2 imagery, climate data, expert-defined risk rasters, and real wildfire event data for cross-continental evaluation. This benchmark is designed to foster systematic studies of robust generalization.
5. **Demonstrate the Efficacy of Language-Based Reasoning in Visual Generation:** To empirically show that explicit language-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, particularly when integrated with Vision-Language Models (VLMs), can significantly improve generalization in visual generation tasks, an area relatively unexplored in existing literature for raster outputs.
6. **Propose a High-Resolution, Cross-Continental Wildfire Risk Model:** To develop a practical model, FireScope, that can produce high-resolution wildfire risk predictions applicable across different continents, addressing the current limitation of fine-scale models being restricted to limited regions.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the critical need for more reliable and understandable wildfire risk assessments, which currently suffer from a lack of causal reasoning, multimodal understanding, and generalization capacity in existing models. The authors are motivated to explore whether combining the reasoning power of VLMs with the spatial precision of vision models can yield superior, more trustworthy predictive tools for this complex environmental problem.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces FireScope-Bench, a large-scale multimodal dataset and benchmark, and FireScope, a reasoning-to-generation framework.

#### 3.1. FireScope-Bench Dataset and Benchmark

FireScope-Bench is designed for reasoning-intensive wildfire risk estimation and cross-continental generalization.
- **Wildfire Risk Rasters:** The dataset utilizes expert-defined long-term wildfire risk rasters from the "Wildfire Risk to Communities" project in the continental United States. These rasters, provided at a 30m per pixel resolution, measure "Risk to Potential Structures" and integrate wildfire likelihood and generalized consequences. They are tiled into 341x341 pixel images (approximately 100km²). The USA partition comprises 50,000 areas for training, validation, and testing (2021 data), with values normalized to a range representing relative risk.
- **Wildfire Events:** For out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation, real wildfire events are curated from the European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS) burnt areas collection across Europe. This includes 3,000 wildfire events and 2,000 inland control locations spanning 2018–2025. Complementary USA wildfire events (MTBS data) are also included for in-distribution (ID) comparison.
- **Predictors:**
 - **Satellite Imagery:** Optical satellite images are sourced from Sentinel-2 Level 2A at 10m resolution (1024x1024 pixels). Cloud-free mosaics from the summer season (June 22 to September 22) are used. For European wildfire events, imagery from the year preceding the event is used to assess predictive capability.
 - **Climatology Variables:** Monthly climatology data from NASA POWER is included at 50km resolution. This comprises a 60-dimensional vector for each tile's centroid, covering near-surface temperature, precipitation, humidity, wind speed, and wind direction.
- **Metrics:**
 - **In-Distribution (ID) Continuous Risk Rasters (USA):** Mean Squared Error (MSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for per-pixel evaluation, and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) for structural consistency.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Wildfire Events (Europe):** Brier score, Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC AUC), Expected Calibration Error (ECE) for event discrimination.
 - **OOD Per-Pixel Burnt Areas (Europe):** ROC AUC and Intersection over Union (IoU) for fine-grained prediction of burnt areas. Non-burnt areas within wildfire tiles are treated as background.
 - **Oracle Evaluation:** Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) for ordinal prediction (0-9 scale) of overall area risk, used to evaluate the classification performance of the VLM Oracle.

#### 3.2. FireScope Framework

FireScope is a two-stage reasoning-to-generation framework:

- **Stage 1: Oracle Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, a Vision-Language Model (VLM), is selected as the "Oracle."
 - **Task:** The Oracle is fine-tuned to infer the overall scalar wildfire risk of a given region by integrating satellite imagery and climate normals. It aims to produce a scalar risk estimate, potentially with explicit reasoning steps.
 - **Method:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) is employed using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enable the Oracle to explore intermediate reasoning steps and iteratively refine its scalar risk estimate. A Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approach is encouraged.
 - **Reward Design:** The reward function (R) is a weighted sum of an accuracy component (R_acc) and a formatting component (R_fmt), with class-dependent weighting to address label imbalance. R_acc rewards ordinal prediction accuracy (1.0 for exact match, decreasing for larger errors), while R_fmt rewards parseable output.
 - **Comparison:** A version of Qwen fine-tuned without CoT (supervised fine-tuning) is also developed for comparison.

- **Stage 2: Vision Encoder-Decoder Training:**
 - **Model:** A lightweight vision Encoder-Decoder architecture is used, with various backbones tested: SegFormer MiT-B5, AlphaEarth (a foundation model pretrained on satellite tasks), and a U-Net trained from scratch.
 - **Conditioning:** The scalar risk estimate produced by the fine-tuned Oracle (from Stage 1) is used to condition the vision Encoder-Decoder. This conditioning is applied through Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layers before each trainable block of the Encoder-Decoder.
 - **Task:** The Encoder-Decoder learns to generate a fine-grained, continuous risk raster (y ∈ [−1, 1]^(341x341)) that is grounded in the satellite imagery and consistent with the Oracle’s scalar judgment.
 - **Loss Function:** The model is trained by minimizing a weighted sum of three terms: Smooth-L1 loss (reconstruction), (1 - SSIM) (structural consistency), and L1 loss of gradients (edge matching).

#### 3.3. Interpretability Experiments

To quantify the interpretability and faithfulness of the CoT reasoning traces:
- **Expert Study:** Two wildfire experts classify risk for 50 test areas based on summaries of CoT traces generated by the Oracle (default) and "golden" CoT traces (generated by GPT-5 with correct risk classification). QWK scores are used to measure agreement.
- **Automated Metrics:**
 - **Fidelity:** Measures how much the predicted raster shifts towards an opposite risk level when the Oracle's CoT is synthetically perturbed to argue for that opposite level.
 - **Consistency:** Assesses how stable the raster prediction is when the Oracle's CoT is paraphrased while preserving its semantic content.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, primarily conducted on a smaller training set for computational feasibility, demonstrate the efficacy of the FireScope framework, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios.

- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Raster Results (Table 1):**
 - FireScope, utilizing CoT Oracle conditioning, consistently achieves substantial performance gains in OOD settings (Europe wildfire events). It yields the best Brier score and ROC AUC for distinguishing wildfire events across all tested vision backbones (AlphaEarth, SegFormer, U-Net).
 - For fine-grained prediction of wildfire pixels, CoT Oracle conditioning also results in the best ROC AUC and IoU scores.
 - Conditioning on Oracles without CoT generally improves OOD performance over unconditioned baselines, but to a lesser extent than CoT-conditioned Oracles.
 - U-Net without conditioning achieved the best Expected Calibration Error (ECE) for OOD wildfire events, though CoT Oracle generally improved ECE as well.

- **Oracle Results (Table 2):**
 - The CoT Qwen Oracle significantly outperforms all other Oracle baselines (Climate MLP, FWI, GPT-5, and non-CoT Qwen) in OOD settings across Brier, ROC AUC, and ECE metrics. This provides evidence that explicit reasoning supports wildfire risk prediction.
 - The Climate MLP Oracle shows strong performance in-distribution (ID QWK of 0.766), comparable to CoT Qwen, but its performance does not transfer to OOD data, suggesting potential overfitting to regional climate signatures.

- **In-Distribution (ID) Raster Results (Table 3):**
 - While not always achieving the absolute best ID performance, FireScope (CoT Oracle conditioning) remains robust in-distribution, exhibiting performance generally on par with or slightly below climate-conditioned baselines. This indicates that the OOD gains do not come at the cost of significant ID performance degradation.
 - Models conditioned on raw climate vectors tend to slightly outperform others in-distribution across MSE, SSIM, and MAE.

- **Ablation Study (Figure 5, Table 6):**
 - Training a U-Net on a 40 times larger dataset (U-Net*) improves ID performance but remains inferior to FireScope’s CoT Oracle-conditioned configuration in OOD settings. This suggests that structured reasoning contributes to generalization beyond what data scaling alone can achieve.
 - A direct VLM-decoder (Qwen+decoder/VLM-enc), where a decoder head is attached to Qwen to generate rasters in one pass, performs worse than FireScope. This indicates that the scalar output from explicit reasoning provides a more structured and semantically grounded form of conditioning compared to directly leveraging the VLM's raw representational capacity for raster generation.

- **Interpretability Results (Table 4):**
 - **Expert Study:** Wildfire experts were able to partially recover risk scores based solely on the factors considered in Oracle’s CoT summaries. One expert achieved a QWK of 0.33 (70% of their score on golden traces), while another achieved 0.11, both statistically significant and indicating that the CoTs contain meaningful cues.
 - **Automated Metrics:** FireScope achieves high consistency (0.91), meaning paraphrasing CoTs has minimal impact on generated rasters. It also shows a fidelity of 0.33, indicating that predicted risk shifts by 33% on average when CoTs are perturbed to argue for the opposite risk scale. These results suggest that the raster generation process is actively and interpretably guided by the Oracle’s CoT.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FireScope research introduces advancements in multimodal vision research and geospatial modeling, with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Generalization for Complex Spatial Problems:** FireScope demonstrates that language-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning can substantially improve out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization in visual generation tasks, particularly for complex spatial problems like wildfire risk prediction. This finding is crucial as many existing models struggle with applying learned patterns to unseen geographical or climatic contexts. The OOD benefits were observed even when compared against models trained on significantly larger datasets, underscoring the efficiency and robustness provided by reasoning-based conditioning.

2. **Increased Interpretability and Trust in AI Systems:** The framework provides human-interpretable reasoning traces alongside continuous risk maps. The expert study confirmed that these CoTs encode meaningful, human-understandable cues, allowing domain experts to partially reconstruct and understand the model's risk judgments. This intrinsic interpretability enhances the transparency and trustworthiness of the AI system, which is vital for adoption in critical applications like public safety and environmental management.

3. **Novel Approach to Wildfire Risk Prediction:** FireScope represents the first framework to propose a high-resolution wildfire risk model applicable across continents, and the first to explicitly demonstrate that language-based reasoning can improve generalization in visual generation within this domain. It bridges the gap between abstract causal reasoning and precise, pixel-level spatial prediction, moving beyond traditional coarse-scale or purely observational methods.

4. **Foundation for Future Research (FireScope-Bench):** The introduction of FireScope-Bench, a large-scale multimodal dataset and benchmark, is a key contribution. It couples Sentinel-2 imagery and climate data with expert-defined risk rasters across the USA and real wildfire events in Europe. This benchmark enables systematic studies of robust cross-continental generalization for multimodal fire risk models, providing a standardized platform for advancing reasoning-driven, interpretable, and generalizable spatial modeling.

5. **Implications for Broader Geospatial AI:** The methodology of coupling a Vision-Language Model Oracle with a fine-grained vision Encoder-Decoder, where reasoning acts as a contextual prior, offers a promising path for other geospatial prediction tasks. This approach could be adapted to enhance generalization and interpretability in diverse areas where integrating multimodal data and complex reasoning is necessary.

6. **Efficiency and Robustness:** The observed gains in OOD performance, which persist even when compared to models trained on orders of magnitude more data, suggest that integrating explicit reasoning is a computationally efficient way to achieve robust models that generalize well, potentially reducing the reliance on massive data scaling alone.

While the current framework’s communication between the Encoder-Decoder and Oracle is bottlenecked by a single scalar signal, the research identifies this as an area for future work, aiming for more spatially resolved or multi-dimensional conditioning to enhance fine-scale pattern inference. Overall, FireScope establishes a significant testbed and methodology for advancing generalizable and interpretable AI in geospatial prediction.
