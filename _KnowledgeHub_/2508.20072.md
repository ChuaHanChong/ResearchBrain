# Discrete Diffusion VLA: Bringing Discrete Diffusion to Action Decoding in Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong, Shanghai AI Laboratory, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Huawei Cloud Computing developed Discrete Diffusion VLA, a method integrating discrete diffusion for action decoding directly into unified Vision-Language-Action models. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on diverse robot manipulation benchmarks while enhancing inference efficiency and retaining strong pre-trained vision-language priors.

## Problem

- Existing autoregressive VLA models suffer from a "left-to-right" bottleneck, causing slow and inflexible action generation for robotics.
- VLA models using separate action decoders (e.g., continuous diffusion) fragment information pathways, risking loss of rich VLM priors and requiring specialized training.
- A fully unified architecture integrating vision, language, and action generation within a single transformer was lacking, limiting scalability and deeper cross-modal understanding.

## Method

- Action decoding is formulated as discrete ==diffusion== through masked-token denoising.
- A unified VLA architecture directly integrates discrete diffusion within the same ==transformer== processing vision and language, utilizing a pre-trained VLM backbone.
- An adaptive inference strategy incorporates iterative refinement, including confidence-guided token commitment ("easy-first") and secondary re-masking for error correction.

## Results

- Achieved a **96.3%** average success rate across all four LIBERO suites, outperforming comparable baselines by **0.9** percentage points.
- Demonstrated superior retention of pre-trained vision-language priors, showing substantially less performance degradation in out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., **1.4%** language degradation on LIBERO-Goal).
- Reduced inference latency by approximately **2x** (68.8 ms per action chunk) and decreased the number of function evaluations by **4.7x** compared to autoregressive methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete diffusion provides an effective mechanism for parallel action token decoding, overcoming the sequential bottlenecks of autoregressive methods.
> - Integrating action generation natively within a unified VLM transformer architecture helps retain and leverage powerful pre-trained vision-language priors.
> - Iterative refinement with adaptive decoding and error correction (secondary re-masking) leads to more robust and consistent action sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models focuses on enabling robots to interpret visual and linguistic inputs to execute corresponding action sequences. A central challenge within this field is effectively integrating large, pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robot action generation mechanisms.

Current VLA frameworks generally employ two primary paradigms for action generation:
1. **Autoregressive (AR) Approaches:** These models, inspired by GPT-style transformers, predict discretized action tokens sequentially, typically in a fixed left-to-right order. Examples include OpenVLA and π0-FAST. While they can integrate action generation within the VLM backbone, they often suffer from compounding errors due to sequential dependencies, inference inefficiency, and an inability to leverage future context within an action chunk.
2. **Separate Action Heads:** This paradigm uses an external module, often an MLP or a continuous diffusion model, to map the VLM's output latent tokens to executable actions. Continuous diffusion models, such as those used in π0 and SmolVLA, have become prevalent due to their capacity to model complex, continuous trajectories. However, this approach typically decouples the action generation from the VLM backbone, introducing distinct training objectives. This can lead to fragmented information pathways, competing gradient signals during training, and a degradation of the VLM's pretrained vision-language capabilities, particularly under out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

Recent advancements in discrete diffusion and discrete flow-matching for language and multi-modal generation have demonstrated their effectiveness in handling tokenized data and facilitating parallel, iterative refinement. This paper introduces Discrete Diffusion VLA as a development within this context, aiming to bridge the gap between maintaining VLM prior integrity and achieving robust, efficient action generation by applying discrete diffusion principles directly within a unified VLM architecture. It attempts to overcome the limitations of both AR and separate continuous diffusion heads by offering a method that is both integrated and capable of parallel, adaptive decoding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Discrete Diffusion VLA, a novel VLA framework that leverages discrete diffusion for action generation. This framework aims to unify vision, language, and action modeling within a single transformer architecture, using a consistent optimization goal.

The specific objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Unify Vision, Language, and Action Modeling:** The core motivation is to overcome the architectural fragmentation observed in VLA models that employ separate continuous diffusion heads. Such decoupled designs often lead to competing gradient signals during training, which can degrade the robust vision-language priors learned by large VLMs. By integrating action generation via discrete diffusion *within* the unified transformer backbone, the authors aim to maintain a consistent training objective, thereby preserving the VLM's emergent multimodal capabilities.
- **Achieve High Action Precision:** The model is designed to generate precise robotic actions. This is crucial for successful task completion in complex manipulation environments where accurate motor commands are required. The use of discrete diffusion, with its iterative refinement process, is hypothesized to contribute to this precision by progressively denoising action tokens.
- **Preserve Pretrained Vision-Language Priors:** A significant motivation is to mitigate the degradation of VLM capabilities under distribution shifts, a known issue with some existing VLA approaches. By operating with discrete action tokens within the same token space and using a cross-entropy objective consistent with the VLM's pretraining, the method seeks to inherently preserve these priors, leading to improved out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Enable Parallel Action Decoding:** In contrast to autoregressive (AR) methods that decode actions sequentially (e.g., left-to-right), Discrete Diffusion VLA aims to support parallel decoding of action chunks. This is motivated by the need to improve inference efficiency and overcome the linearly scaling forward passes inherent in AR approaches. Parallel decoding allows the model to leverage full contextual information from all modalities (vision, language, and partially unmasked action tokens) simultaneously for each prediction.
- **Implement Adaptive Decoding Order:** The work seeks to move beyond fixed decoding orders by introducing an adaptive strategy. This is motivated by the observation that some action elements might be easier or more confident to predict than others. An adaptive order, which prioritizes high-confidence action elements first and then refines harder ones, can lead to more robust and accurate action generation, following a "walk before you run" principle.
- **Develop Robust Error Correction:** Compounding errors are a challenge in sequential generation. The motivation for incorporating a secondary re-masking mechanism is to provide a robust error correction capability. By re-masking uncertain or low-confidence predictions, the model can revisit and correct potential errors introduced in earlier denoising steps, enhancing overall consistency across iterative refinements.

In summary, the research is motivated by the desire to develop a VLA framework that is architecturally unified, robust to distribution shifts, efficient in inference, and capable of generating precise actions through an adaptive and error-correcting decoding process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Discrete Diffusion VLA employs a methodology that discretizes robot actions into tokens and utilizes a discrete diffusion process within a unified Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone for generation.

#### 3.1. Action Tokenization and Chunking

- **Discretization:** Each continuous control dimension (e.g., 3 for translation, 3 for rotation) is converted into a discrete token using a 256-bin quantile-based scheme, focusing on the 1st-99th percentiles to mitigate outlier effects. The gripper state is handled as a separate binary token. This results in a total of D_act = 7 tokens for a single-timestep action.
- **Action Chunking:** To facilitate parallel processing, H consecutive timesteps are grouped into a fixed-length action chunk of L = H × D_act tokens. This chunk-based representation is designed to be compatible with the block-wise parallel generation characteristic of discrete diffusion.

#### 3.2. Unified VLM Backbone

- **Foundation:** The architecture builds upon OpenVLA, which itself is based on a Prismatic-7B VLM with a Llama 2 transformer backbone.
- **Multi-modal Input Encoding:**
 - **Visual Inputs:** Multi-view RGB images (mandatory third-person, optional wrist views) are encoded using SigLIP and DINOv2 vision transformers. Their features are then projected into the Llama2 embedding space.
 - **Language Instructions:** Language instructions are processed via the Llama Tokenizer to generate embeddings.
 - **Proprioceptive States:** When available, proprioceptive states (e.g., end-effector positions) are encoded by a lightweight MLP and concatenated with the visual tokens.
- **Bidirectional Attention:** A key modification from the original autoregressive OpenVLA is replacing causal attention over action tokens with bidirectional attention. This allows each action token position to attend to all other visual, language, and action tokens simultaneously, facilitating comprehensive multimodal fusion.
- **Action Projection Head:** The hidden states corresponding to action token positions within the transformer are projected to a 256-way vocabulary using a shared classification head, producing the model's predictive distribution over action tokens.

#### 3.3. Discrete Diffusion Formulation over Actions

- **Action Space:** An action chunk `a_0 = (a_0,1..., a_0,L)` consists of L tokens, where each `a_0,i ∈ {1..., K}` (K=256 bins). A special mask token [MASK] is added to the vocabulary, resulting in V = K+1 symbols.
- **Forward (Noising) Process:** This is a Markov chain where, at each step `t`, individual tokens `a_t,i` are independently corrupted. A token `a_t,i` is mapped to [MASK] with probability `β_t` or remains unchanged with probability `1 - β_t`.
- **Reverse (Denoising) Process:** The model learns to predict `p_θ(a_t-1 | a_t, c)`, where `c` represents the multimodal (vision and language) context. This process effectively recovers masked positions.
- **Training Objective:** The multi-step diffusion chain is collapsed into a single masked-token prediction objective. During training, a mask ratio `γ_t ∈ (0, 1]` is sampled (e.g., from a cosine schedule). A subset of `γ_t * L` action positions are masked to form `~a_t`. The transformer `f_θ` is trained to predict the original tokens at these masked positions using a cross-entropy loss: `L_CE(θ) = - Σ_{i∈M_γt} log p_θ(a_0,i | ~a_t, c)`. This objective aligns action generation with standard LM training, which helps preserve VLM capabilities. All L positions within an action chunk are optimized jointly in a single forward pass.

#### 3.4. Inference Pipeline

- **Iterative Refinement:** Unlike one-shot parallel decoding, inference involves T refinement steps. The process begins with all L action positions initialized as [MASK].
- **Adaptive Decoding Mechanism:** At each step `t`, the model computes per-position posteriors `p_θ(a_t-1 | a_t, c)`. To decide which tokens to commit, it ranks positions based on confidence metrics:
 - **Max Confidence:** `s_t,i = max_k p_θ(k | a_t, c)`
 - **Confidence Gap:** `g_t,i = p_θ(k^(1) | ·) - p_θ(k^(2) | ·)` (difference between top two probabilities)
 The top `(1 - γ_t+1)L` most confident positions (`K_t`) are selected. The tokens at these positions are updated via tempered Gumbel sampling (with a decaying temperature `τ_t` to encourage initial exploration and later commitment). Positions not in `K_t` remain [MASK]. This instance-wise ranking allows the decoding order to adapt.
- **Secondary Re-masking:** To prevent error propagation and enforce cross-iteration consistency, a lightweight threshold check is applied. If the confidence `s_t,i` of a *previously committed* token `i ∈ K_t` falls below a monotonically increasing, step-dependent threshold `η_abs_t`, that token is re-masked. This ensures that uncertain predictions from earlier steps can be revisited and corrected.

The combination of a unified backbone, discrete diffusion training, adaptive decoding, and secondary re-masking aims to provide a robust, efficient, and context-aware action generation framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Discrete Diffusion VLA was conducted across various simulation benchmarks and a real-robot platform, focusing on task success, out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, inference efficiency, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **LIBERO Benchmark (Franka Panda arm):**
 - **In-Distribution Performance:** Discrete Diffusion VLA achieved an average success rate of 96.4% across four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). This established it as the state-of-the-art among all discrete tokenized methods, surpassing OpenVLA-OFT Discrete by 0.9%. It narrowed the performance gap to the overall continuous SOTA (OpenVLA-OFT L1 at 97.1%) to 0.7%, despite the inherent quantization error of discrete representations.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** This was a notable strength. On LIBERO-Goal-OOD, the method exhibited:
 - Only 0.8% language degradation (vs. 8.0% for parallel decoding, 2.4% for continuous diffusion, 3.2% for L1 regression).
 - 20.4% vision degradation (vs. 22.6% for parallel decoding, 29.0% for continuous diffusion, 23.2% for L1 regression).
 This indicates superior retention of pretrained VLM capabilities, with continuous diffusion methods showing the most severe vision degradation. Similar trends were observed on LIBERO-Spatial.

- **SimplerEnv-Fractal (Google Robot):**
 - Discrete Diffusion VLA achieved state-of-the-art performance across both discrete and continuous methods.
 - It obtained 71.2% average success on Visual Matching, outperforming π0 (58.8%), π0-FAST (61.9%), and OpenVLA-OFT (63.0%).
 - On Variant Aggregation, it achieved 56.9%, competitive with RT-2-X (64.3%).
 - The aggregated overall average was 64.1%, the highest among evaluated methods.

- **SimplerEnv-Bridge (WidowX Robot):**
 - The method achieved SOTA performance with 54.2% overall success, surpassing continuous diffusion policies like π0 (40.1%, a +14.1% improvement) and GR00T-N1 (49.5%, a +4.7% improvement), as well as discrete baselines like π0-FAST (48.3%, a +5.9% improvement).

#### 4.2. Inference Efficiency

- Discrete Diffusion VLA demonstrated efficiency advantages over AR decoding. For a LIBERO action chunk (L=56 tokens), AR requires 56 sequential forward passes. In contrast, Discrete Diffusion VLA denoises the entire chunk in T=12 parallel steps, reducing the number of function evaluations (NFEs) from 56 to 12 (a 4.7x reduction).
- On a single NVIDIA H800 GPU, Discrete Diffusion VLA achieved an end-to-end latency of 68.8 ms per chunk (14.53 Hz), which is approximately 2x faster than AR (136.2 ms, 7.34 Hz). This was comparable to continuous diffusion using the same number of denoising steps (67.1 ms). One-shot parallel decoding was faster (31.1 ms) but at a cost to accuracy. Secondary re-masking added negligible overhead.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Evaluation

- Deployed on an AgileX Cobot Magic dual-arm platform for two tabletop manipulation tasks, Discrete Diffusion VLA demonstrated practical effectiveness.
- On the "click the bell" task, it achieved 66.7% success, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (33.3%) and π0 (53.3%).
- On the "place cup on coaster" task, it matched π0's performance at 40.0%.
- The control frequency was 9.69 Hz on an RTX 4090, which is deemed sufficient for quasi-static tabletop manipulation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Decoding Strategy:** Adaptive decoding (using Max Confidence) yielded 96.8% success on LIBERO-Goal, outperforming one-shot parallel decoding (95.6%), random order (95.8%), and confidence gap selection (96.6%). This supported the benefit of confidence-guided, high-confidence-first resolution.
- **Choice Temperature:** A linear decay temperature (from 1.0 to 0.0) for Gumbel sampling achieved 96.8% success, surpassing hard argmax (96.2%) and fixed temperature (96.4%). This suggests that encouraging mild exploration early and sharper commitment later improves performance.
- **Denoising Steps (T):** Accuracy generally improved with an increasing number of denoising steps, with a trade-off against efficiency. T=12 was identified as a practical operating point.
- **Action Head without Robot Pretraining:** When evaluated on a pure VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) without robot-specific initialization, discrete diffusion achieved the best average performance across all LIBERO suites (95.1%), outperforming AR (81.2%), FAST (91.1%), parallel decoding (94.2%), and continuous diffusion (94.9%). This indicates that the performance advantage is intrinsic to the discrete diffusion paradigm.

#### 4.5. Visualization of Adaptive Decoding Order

- Visualizations indicated that the adaptive decoding process prioritizes high-confidence tokens, often correlating with high-frequency tokens, in early denoising steps. However, low-frequency tokens were also demasked early when contextually predictable, demonstrating the adaptability of the instance-wise confidence ranking.

These findings collectively suggest that Discrete Diffusion VLA provides a robust and efficient approach for VLA tasks, effectively addressing limitations of prior methods by preserving VLM priors and enabling flexible, high-quality action generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Discrete Diffusion VLA presents several significant contributions to the field of robotic learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with potential impacts on the development of more capable and robust robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Unified Architecture and Preservation of VLM Priors

- **Architectural Cohesion:** The work demonstrates a method for unifying perception, language understanding, and action generation within a single transformer backbone. This addresses a common challenge in VLA models, where separate action heads can lead to architectural fragmentation.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By employing a training objective and token vocabulary consistent with the pretrained VLM, Discrete Diffusion VLA effectively preserves the powerful vision-language priors. This is a crucial impact, as it leads to significantly improved out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization and robustness against variations in language instructions or visual appearances, a critical requirement for real-world robotic deployment. The observed minimal degradation under OOD tests, compared to methods that suffer substantial performance drops, highlights this advantage.

#### 5.2. Advanced Action Decoding Capabilities

- **Improved Efficiency:** Discrete diffusion enables parallel action decoding within chunks, offering a substantial efficiency advantage over autoregressive (AR) methods that require sequential forward passes. This reduces inference latency and decouples inference cost from sequence length, making real-time control more feasible.
- **Robustness to Errors:** The introduction of an adaptive decoding order, which prioritizes high-confidence action elements, along with a secondary re-masking mechanism, provides robust error correction capabilities. This mitigates the compounding errors common in AR decoding and allows the model to refine uncertain predictions, leading to more consistent and reliable action sequences.
- **Leveraging Full Context:** Bidirectional attention over the entire action chunk allows the model to utilize comprehensive multimodal context (vision, language, and all action tokens) for each prediction, contrasting with AR models that are restricted to causal, left-to-right processing.

#### 5.3. Competitive Performance and Real-World Applicability

- **State-of-the-Art Results:** The method achieves competitive, and in several cases state-of-the-art, performance on challenging simulation benchmarks across various robot platforms (Franka Panda, Google Robot, WidowX). This indicates its effectiveness in diverse manipulation tasks.
- **Real-Robot Validation:** Successful deployment on a real AgileX Cobot Magic platform for tabletop manipulation tasks confirms the method's practical utility and transferability from simulation to physical hardware. This is essential for demonstrating the readiness of such models for real-world applications.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Broader Implications

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes discrete diffusion as a viable and advantageous paradigm for action generation in VLA models, potentially inspiring further research into more expressive and efficient tokenization strategies for robotics.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The paper acknowledges the reliance on large pretrained vision-language models, which may inherit biases from their pretraining data. This implicit recognition of potential ethical implications encourages responsible development and deployment of VLA technologies, particularly in safety-critical environments where formal safety evaluations are paramount.

In summary, Discrete Diffusion VLA advances the state of VLA models by demonstrating a unified, robust, and efficient approach to action generation. Its ability to preserve VLM priors and deliver high performance across diverse settings suggests a significant step towards generalist robot policies that can operate reliably in complex and variable environments.
