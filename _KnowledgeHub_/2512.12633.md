# DiG: Differential Grounding for Enhancing Fine-Grained Perception in Multimodal Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The DiG framework enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' fine-grained visual perception and precise spatial reasoning through a differential grounding task, leveraging an automated 3D rendering pipeline and curriculum-based reinforcement learning. This approach consistently improves performance across various perception, general multimodal, and visual grounding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with fine-grained visual perception and precise spatial reasoning, often overlooking subtle visual details or changes.
- Existing MLLM training methods and proxy tasks either lead to overspecialized models, rely on costly and limited manual annotations, or are not specifically designed to strengthen fine-grained perceptual sensitivity.
- MLLMs frequently exhibit issues like visual hallucination or inaccurate localization due to insufficient supervision for discerning nuanced visual information.

## Method

- Introduces ==Differential Grounding (DiG)==, a novel proxy task requiring MLLMs to identify and localize all subtle visual discrepancies between two highly similar images.
- Develops a fully automated data generation pipeline using ==Blender 3D rendering== to create scalable, perfectly annotated training data with controllable difficulty by programmatically modifying scene attributes.
- Employs a reinforcement learning framework with a ==multi-component reward function== (format adherence, F1 score for detection completeness, mean IoU for spatial precision), optimized via ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a ==curriculum learning strategy== progressing from single-difference to multi-difference to mixed-difficulty scenarios.

## Results

- DiG consistently improved the performance of Qwen3-VL models on perception-oriented benchmarks (e.g., **+3.4** on HalBench and **+2.1** on V* for the 8B model), demonstrating enhanced sensitivity to visual cues and reduced hallucination.
- Models trained with DiG showed strong transferability, achieving consistent gains on general multimodal benchmarks (e.g., **+2.2** on MMBench and **+17.5** on MME for the 8B model), indicating improved overall multimodal reasoning.
- DiG-trained models significantly outperformed baselines on fine-grained visual grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) with average improvements of **2-3 points**, confirming enhanced spatial localization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training MLLMs to explicitly localize subtle visual differences between similar images directly enhances their fine-grained perceptual and spatial reasoning abilities.
> - Automated 3D rendering is a scalable and cost-effective method for generating high-quality, diverse datasets with precise ground-truth annotations for fine-grained visual tasks, circumventing manual annotation limitations.
> - A curriculum-based reinforcement learning approach effectively mitigates sparse reward challenges in complex visual tasks, allowing MLLMs to acquire sophisticated perceptual skills incrementally and stably.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advancements across a range of vision-language tasks, including image captioning and visual question answering. These models typically exhibit proficiency in holistic scene understanding and the association of global visual semantics with textual reasoning. However, a limitation identified in the current research landscape is their reduced capability in fine-grained visual perception and precise spatial reasoning. Existing state-of-the-art MLLMs frequently do not perceive subtle visual cues, such as minor object changes, color variations, or missing elements, which are critical for detailed visual comprehension. This limitation suggests that current pre-training paradigms for MLLMs, while effective for high-level semantic alignment, may not provide sufficient supervision for developing the fine-grained perceptual sensitivity required for nuanced scene analysis.

Recent developments have seen the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enhance MLLM capabilities, moving beyond conventional supervised training. RL-based post-training has been utilized to improve robustness, alignment, and perceptual fidelity in MLLMs. Research in this area includes efforts to strengthen visual perception through RL, often by fine-tuning models on traditional perceptual tasks like visual grounding or by exploring alternative proxy tasks such as visual jigsaw puzzles or hallucination detection. However, these approaches often present trade-offs. Fine-tuning on specific grounding tasks may lead to overspecialization and reduced generalization to broader multimodal understanding. Furthermore, many traditional grounding datasets necessitate extensive manual annotation, limiting their scalability and diversity for large-scale training. Proxy tasks, while offering auxiliary supervision, are not always explicitly designed to cultivate fine-grained visual perception, potentially leaving MLLMs insufficiently sensitive to subtle visual details. This research aims to address these identified limitations by introducing a scalable and explicitly fine-grained approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the fine-grained visual perception and precise spatial reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The authors aim to achieve this through the introduction of a novel proxy task framework called Differential Grounding (DiG).

The motivation for this work stems from recognized limitations in current MLLMs. While these models perform well in understanding general scene semantics, they often struggle with the granular details essential for accurate perception and reasoning. Specific motivations include:
- **Addressing Deficiencies in Fine-Grained Perception:** Current MLLMs frequently fail to detect subtle visual discrepancies, such as minor attribute changes (e.g., color, shape, size), object additions, or object removals between similar images. This indicates a gap in their ability to perform detailed visual analysis.
- **Improving Spatial Reasoning:** Beyond object identification, MLLMs exhibit limitations in precisely localizing these subtle differences within an image. The research seeks to improve the models' capacity for accurate spatial grounding.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Cost for Fine-Grained Tasks:** Traditional approaches to improving visual grounding often rely on manually annotated datasets, which are expensive to create, limited in scale, and may lack diversity. A key motivation is to develop a method for generating high-quality, large-scale training data automatically and controllably.
- **Developing a Scalable and Generalizable Proxy Task:** Existing proxy tasks for MLLMs may offer auxiliary supervision but are not always tailored to explicitly foster fine-grained visual perception. The authors aim to create a task that directly compels models to compare images at a detailed level, thereby encouraging the development of transferable fine-grained perception skills.
- **Mitigating Optimization Challenges in Reinforcement Learning:** When training MLLMs using reinforcement learning (RL) for tasks requiring fine-grained output, the reward signals can be sparse, particularly at early stages when the model's predictions are far from correct. This sparsity can lead to unstable optimization and poor convergence. The research is motivated to address this challenge through a structured training approach.

By formulating "differential grounding" as a task where models identify and localize all differences between similar image pairs without prior knowledge of the number of differences, the researchers aim to compel MLLMs to develop the necessary fine-grained perceptual and spatial reasoning abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper, named DiG (Differential Grounding), comprises four main components: problem formulation, data construction, reward modeling and policy optimization, and curriculum-based difficulty scheduling.

**Problem Formulation: Differential Grounding (DiG) Task**
The core of the approach is the DiG task, which requires MLLMs to identify and localize all visual differences between a given pair of closely related images. The input consists of a reference image ($I_a$), a visually similar counterpart ($I_b$) containing $M$ subtle modifications, and a textual instruction ($P$) prompting the model to identify and localize discrepancies. The model's output is a textual sequence parsed into a set of predicted bounding boxes ($B_{pred}$), each representing a localized discrepancy on the reference image ($I_a$). The objective is to align $B_{pred}$ with the ground-truth annotations ($B_{gt}$) in terms of completeness and spatial precision.

**DiG Data Construction Pipeline**
To facilitate large-scale training, an automated and scalable data generation pipeline is developed using the Blender 3D rendering engine. This pipeline synthesizes paired images with precise control over visual discrepancies and automatically generates accurate ground-truth annotations.
1. **Base Scene Generation:** A base 3D scene is procedurally generated using a JSON configuration file. This file specifies the number of objects, their geometric and visual attributes (shape, material, color, size, spatial position). The reference image ($I_a$) is rendered from this configuration.
2. **Stochastic Modification:** To create the counterpart image ($I_b$), a stochastic modification process is applied. An integer $K$ (number of differences) is randomly sampled from a predefined range [1, N]. $K$ distinct objects are selected, and for each, one attribute (shape, color, size, material, or object addition/removal) is randomly modified. This modified configuration is then rendered to produce $I_b$. The resulting pair ($I_a, I_b$) ensures visual coherence with exactly $K$ localized differences. The overall task difficulty can be scaled by increasing $N$.
3. **Automatic Ground-Truth Annotation:** A significant advantage of this rendering-based design is the automatic generation of ground-truth bounding box annotations ($B_{gt}$). Since all modifications are programmatically defined in 3D space, their 2D projections can be extracted directly from the renderer, eliminating the need for manual labeling.

**Reward Modeling and Policy Optimization**
The model is optimized using a reinforcement learning paradigm with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The reward function consists of three components:
1. **Format Reward ($r_{format}$):** A binary reward (1 if parsable, 0 otherwise) that encourages the model to generate outputs that conform to a predefined bounding box list format (e.g., `[[x1, y1, x2, y2]...]`). This ensures syntactic correctness.
2. **Accuracy Reward ($r_{acc}$):** Measures the model's fine-grained localization and detection performance.
 - **Correspondence:** A one-to-one correspondence between predicted boxes ($B_{pred}$) and ground-truth boxes ($B_{gt}$) is established using the Hungarian algorithm, maximizing similarity (L1 distance and Generalized Intersection-over-Union, GIoU).
 - **Metrics:** This reward combines an F1 score (derived from precision and recall based on matched pairs, $n_m$) and the average IoU of matched pairs. It is formulated as $r_{acc} = \lambda_1 F1 + \lambda_2 IoU$.
3. **Overall Reward ($r$):** Integrates the format and accuracy rewards hierarchically: $r = (1 - \alpha)r_{acc} + \alpha r_{format}$, where $\alpha$ controls the balance. This design ensures valid output format before focusing on perceptual accuracy.

**Policy Optimization (GRPO):** The policy $\pi_\theta$ is refined by sampling multiple candidate responses, calculating group-level advantages, and updating the model using a token-level clipped surrogate objective with KL-divergence regularization.

**Curriculum-based Difficulty Scheduling**
To address the sparsity of reward signals and ensure stable optimization, a curriculum learning strategy is employed, progressively increasing task difficulty:
1. **Single-Difference Phase (DiG-1):** Initial training focuses on simpler instances with a single visual difference. This provides dense and interpretable feedback, enabling the model to learn basic difference localization.
2. **Double-Difference Phase (DiG-2):** Training progresses to samples containing two differences, fostering compositional reasoning and multi-object perception.
3. **Mixed-Difference Phase (DiG-Mix):** The final stage fine-tunes the model on a dataset with a mixed number of differences (up to four), promoting generalization across diverse configurations. Crucially, in this phase, the model is not informed of the exact number of discrepancies, requiring it to infer this information.

This progressive scheduling aims to stabilize RL by gradually shaping the reward landscape, allowing the policy to acquire fine-grained perceptual skills in a structured manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluates the effectiveness of DiG using Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking and Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking models as backbones, trained on a dataset of approximately 4.8K procedurally generated image pairs. The evaluation covers a range of visual perception, general multimodal, and grounding benchmarks.

**Performance on Perception Benchmarks:**
DiG consistently improved the performance of both 4B and 8B Qwen3-VL models across several perception-oriented benchmarks (HalBench, HRB8K, POPE, V*, VSR, CV-Bench, MMVP).
- The 8B model showed notable gains, including a +3.4 increase on HalBench and a +2.1 increase on V*, with steady improvements on VSR and CV-Bench.
- The 4B model exhibited similar trends, with up to a +3.8 improvement on HalBench and visible gains on HRB8K and VSR.
These results suggest that DiG enhances the model's sensitivity to fine-grained visual cues and its ability to maintain spatial consistency, contributing to reduced hallucination and improved perceptual fidelity.

**Performance on General Multimodal Benchmarks:**
DiG also yielded consistent performance gains for both model variants on comprehensive multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, MMVet, MMStar, ScienceQA, TextVQA, MME, AI2D), indicating improved holistic perception and reasoning.
- The 4B model achieved improvements such as +3.5 on MMStar and +2.3 on ScienceQA.
- The 8B model showed gains including +2.2 on MMBench and +17.5 on MME.
These enhancements suggest that differential grounding strengthens multimodal reasoning and structured visual-text alignment, enabling DiG-equipped models to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding larger proprietary systems, implying more efficient representation learning and perception refinement.

**Performance on Grounding Benchmarks:**
The evaluation on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, which assess fine-grained visual grounding, demonstrated consistent outperformance by DiG-trained models over their base counterparts across all datasets and evaluation splits.
- The 8B variant achieved average improvements of approximately 2-3 points across all benchmarks, with particularly strong gains on RefCOCO (+2.0 to +3.4) and RefCOCO+ (+1.4 to +3.0). The 4B model showed comparable improvements.
This indicates that DiG substantially enhances spatial reasoning and fine-grained localization, leading to more accurate alignment between referring expressions and visual regions. This improvement is attributed to reinforcement of object-level perceptual precision and the grounding-oriented output format during optimization.

**Ablation Study on Curriculum Scheduling:**
An ablation study on the curriculum-based optimization strategy confirmed its effectiveness.
- Training on single-difference instances (DiG-1) already showed gains over the base model.
- Further training on double-difference instances (DiG-2) yielded additional improvements.
- Fine-tuning on mixed-difficulty data (DiG-Mix) resulted in the best overall performance, supporting that progressive exposure to increasing complexity leads to more robust and generalizable visual understanding. The optimization behavior graphs showed rapid reward increase in DiG-1, stabilization in DiG-2, and sustained improvement in DiG-Mix, validating the curriculum's role in mitigating reward sparsity.

**Ablation Study on Reward Components:**
Analysis of reward components showed that combining both the IoU (spatial precision) and F1 (detection completeness) terms resulted in the best performance.
- Relying solely on IoU led to a performance drop, suggesting it was insufficient alone.
- Using only F1 improved detection but lacked stable spatial grounding.
- The integrated IoU and F1 reward achieved superior results on RefCOCO and other benchmarks, indicating that a balanced combination of spatial and categorical signals is crucial for effective grounding.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
Case studies illustrated the model's learning dynamics and improved reasoning.
- During reinforcement optimization, the model progressively refined its localization from coarse to precise bounding box predictions.
- Compared to the baseline Qwen3-VL, the DiG-trained model produced more accurate answers for queries involving subtle spatial relations and object-level attributes, correctly identifying relative size, depth ordering, color, and object categories. This highlights enhanced sensitivity to local visual discrepancies and improved precise perceptual reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of DiG (Differential Grounding) offers several contributions and potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI.

**Addressing Fine-Grained Perception Limitations:** The most direct significance is its targeted approach to a known weakness in current MLLMs: their limited fine-grained visual perception and precise spatial reasoning. By explicitly training models to identify and localize subtle differences, DiG provides a mechanism to improve these capabilities, which are crucial for tasks requiring detailed visual analysis.

**Scalable Data Generation:** The automated 3D rendering-based data generation pipeline is a significant development. It addresses the scalability and cost issues associated with manually annotating large datasets for fine-grained tasks. This pipeline allows for the creation of high-quality, diverse image pairs with precisely controllable discrepancies and perfect ground-truth annotations, facilitating large-scale and reproducible research without human labeling effort.

**Robust Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The incorporation of a curriculum learning strategy to manage reward sparsity in reinforcement learning is a methodological advancement. This approach ensures stable and effective optimization, allowing models to progressively acquire complex perceptual skills, from single to multiple differences, which is particularly relevant for tasks with sparse or delayed rewards.

**Demonstrated Transferability and Generalization:** The extensive experimental results show that the fine-grained perception skills learned through DiG are not confined to the proxy task itself. They transfer effectively to a broad spectrum of downstream tasks, including standard visual grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) and general multimodal perception benchmarks. This indicates that DiG fosters more robust and generalizable visual understanding across diverse scenarios. The fact that DiG-enhanced models, even at smaller scales, can achieve performance comparable to or exceeding larger proprietary systems suggests an improvement in fundamental representational learning and perception refinement.

**Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Perception:** DiG provides a principled framework for bridging the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level visual details in MLLMs. By explicitly focusing on differential comparison and precise grounding, it encourages models to integrate these aspects more cohesively, potentially leading to more perceptually aligned and intelligent multimodal systems.

**Future Research Directions:** This work opens avenues for future research in several areas:
- Exploring more complex types of visual discrepancies and reasoning tasks within the differential grounding framework.
- Applying similar automated data generation and curriculum learning strategies to other fine-grained multimodal challenges.
- Further investigating the mechanisms through which differential grounding improves specific aspects of multimodal reasoning and perception.

Overall, DiG presents a scalable and robust paradigm that could advance the capabilities of MLLMs in fine-grained visual understanding, making them more adept at discerning subtle visual information and performing precise spatial reasoning.
