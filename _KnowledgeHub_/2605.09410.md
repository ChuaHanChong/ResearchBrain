# RePO-VLA: Recovery-Driven Policy Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The RePO-VLA framework enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by enabling autonomous recovery from execution errors through structured learning from successful, failed, and recovered robot trajectories. This approach achieved up to 75% success rates under adversarial perturbations in real-world bimanual tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit brittleness in long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks, often failing due to small execution deviations.
- Current VLA training paradigms discard valuable information from failed robot rollouts and ambiguously utilize recovery demonstrations, which contain both failure and corrective actions.
- Models trained solely on successful demonstrations lack explicit supervision for handling execution drift, making them unable to autonomously recover from adverse states.

## Method

- **RePO-VLA** introduces a two-phase framework: ==Recovery-Aware Initialization (RAI)== and ==Value-Conditioned Refinement (VCR)==, designed to structurally reuse successful, failed, and recovered trajectories.
- ==Trajectory Slicing with History Reset (TSHR)== in RAI disentangles corrective actions from preceding failure histories, mitigating ==causal confusion== during recovery skill learning.
- ==Progress-Aware Semantic Value Learning (PAS-VF)== generates dense progress labels for all types of rollouts, which are then used in VCR to refine the policy to select actions conditioned on desired progress levels.

## Results

- RePO-VLA raised recovery success rates from **15.0%** (baseline π_0.5) to **37.0%** in clean simulation environments and from **15.4%** to **43.0%** in randomized simulation environments under injected failures.
- With scaled recovery data (4x), RePO-VLA achieved average success rates of **80%** in standard conditions and **75%** under adversarial perturbations in real-world bimanual tasks, representing a **+55pp** improvement over baseline π_0.5 in adversarial settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Trajectory Slicing with History Reset (TSHR) was crucial for effective recovery, and value guidance consistently improved performance, demonstrating its role as a stable control signal for recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly leveraging both failed and recovered trajectories, rather than just successful ones, is crucial for learning robust recovery capabilities in VLA models.
> - Mitigating causal confusion by resetting observation history at the start of recovery segments prevents the policy from learning spurious dependencies between errors and corrective actions.
> - Providing dense, progress-aware supervision through a semantic value function enables the policy to differentiate actions based on desired task progress, integrating recovery directly into the VLA architecture.
