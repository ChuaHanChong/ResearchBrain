# RePO-VLA: Recovery-Driven Policy Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The RePO-VLA framework enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by enabling autonomous recovery from execution errors through structured learning from successful, failed, and recovered robot trajectories. This approach achieved up to 75% success rates under adversarial perturbations in real-world bimanual tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit brittleness in long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks, often failing due to small execution deviations.
- Current VLA training paradigms discard valuable information from failed robot rollouts and ambiguously utilize recovery demonstrations, which contain both failure and corrective actions.
- Models trained solely on successful demonstrations lack explicit supervision for handling execution drift, making them unable to autonomously recover from adverse states.

## Method

- **RePO-VLA** introduces a two-phase framework: ==Recovery-Aware Initialization (RAI)== and ==Value-Conditioned Refinement (VCR)==, designed to structurally reuse successful, failed, and recovered trajectories.
- ==Trajectory Slicing with History Reset (TSHR)== in RAI disentangles corrective actions from preceding failure histories, mitigating ==causal confusion== during recovery skill learning.
- ==Progress-Aware Semantic Value Learning (PAS-VF)== generates dense progress labels for all types of rollouts, which are then used in VCR to refine the policy to select actions conditioned on desired progress levels.

## Results

- RePO-VLA raised recovery success rates from **15.0%** (baseline π_0.5) to **37.0%** in clean simulation environments and from **15.4%** to **43.0%** in randomized simulation environments under injected failures.
- With scaled recovery data (4x), RePO-VLA achieved average success rates of **80%** in standard conditions and **75%** under adversarial perturbations in real-world bimanual tasks, representing a **+55pp** improvement over baseline π_0.5 in adversarial settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Trajectory Slicing with History Reset (TSHR) was crucial for effective recovery, and value guidance consistently improved performance, demonstrating its role as a stable control signal for recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly leveraging both failed and recovered trajectories, rather than just successful ones, is crucial for learning robust recovery capabilities in VLA models.
> - Mitigating causal confusion by resetting observation history at the start of recovery segments prevents the policy from learning spurious dependencies between errors and corrective actions.
> - Providing dense, progress-aware supervision through a semantic value function enables the policy to differentiate actions based on desired task progress, integrating recovery directly into the VLA architecture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated capabilities in general-purpose robot control, linking visual-semantic reasoning with robotic actions. However, a significant challenge remains in their reliability for long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks. These tasks often involve scenarios where small deviations can lead to execution drift, requiring targeted corrections to return to a successful state.

Current VLA training paradigms predominantly rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using only successful demonstrations. This approach overlooks information contained in failed robot rollouts, which are typically discarded, and presents ambiguities in recovery demonstrations that contain both failure and corrective actions. The absence of explicit supervision for handling execution drift renders these models brittle when encountering adverse states.

Prior work on robotic failure recovery includes modular replanning, human-in-the-loop interactive correction, and learned refinement techniques. While VLM/LLM-guided systems can assist in high-level replanning and learning-based methods can predict failures or use sparse outcome feedback (e.g., in RL fine-tuning), these often operate independently of the low-level policy or lack fine-grained supervision to distinguish between useful pre-failure behavior, temporary stagnation, active recovery, and terminal breakdown. Learning from mixed-quality data has been explored in offline-to-online reinforcement learning and preference-based refinement, but these typically do not specifically address the temporal structure of failures and recoveries within a single trajectory.

This research addresses the identified gap by proposing a framework that structurally reuses successful, recovery, and failed trajectories. It aims to internalize recovery capabilities directly into the VLA policy, enabling autonomous correction from adverse states, a feature often lacking in existing success-only imitation learning pipelines. The work differentiates itself by providing distinct semantic roles and dense progress signals for various segments of mixed-quality data, rather than treating an entire recovery episode uniformly or discarding failures entirely.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the RePO-VLA framework are detailed as follows:

- **Address Brittleness in Long-Horizon, Contact-Rich Manipulation:** VLA models, despite their advancements, are noted to be brittle when deployed in complex, real-world tasks that involve extended sequences of actions and close physical interaction. Small perturbations often lead to task failures. The motivation is to enhance the robustness of VLA models to such deviations.
- **Leverage Information from Failed and Recovery Trajectories:** A significant limitation of current VLA training is the discarding of failed rollouts. These failures, however, contain valuable information, such as valid initial approach behaviors or clear indicators of where execution deviates from the success manifold. Recovery trajectories, which combine failure histories with corrective actions, are also ambiguously utilized, potentially leading to causal confusion if not properly processed. The objective is to develop a method to effectively utilize this mixed-quality data.
- **Overcome Limitations of Success-Only Imitation:** Purely imitating successful demonstrations provides insufficient supervision for states involving execution drift or stalled progress. The model learns nominal paths but lacks an understanding of how to react when faced with unexpected adverse conditions. The motivation is to move beyond success-only learning to explicitly teach recovery behaviors.
- **Mitigate Causal Confusion in Recovery Learning:** Directly imitating a full recovery trajectory, which often includes a failure phase followed by a corrective phase, can inadvertently teach the policy that the preceding error is a necessary condition for the recovery action. The goal is to disentangle the cause of drift from the necessary corrective actions.
- **Provide Dense, Progress-Aware Supervision:** Traditional sparse binary rewards (success/failure) are insufficient for guiding the policy through nuanced phases of execution, such as useful partial progress, temporary stagnation, active recovery, or terminal failure. A detailed, dense signal reflecting task progress is motivated to enable more precise policy learning.
- **Enable Autonomous Recovery without External Modules:** Many existing recovery solutions rely on external components like online failure detectors, replanning modules, or heuristic retry rules. The objective is to integrate recovery capabilities directly into the VLA policy, allowing it to autonomously steer back to the success manifold during deployment without external intervention.
- **Establish a Standardized Benchmark for Recovery:** Current benchmarks often focus on nominal success rates, not explicitly evaluating a robot's ability to recover from errors. The motivation is to introduce a benchmark (FRBench) that includes standardized error injection and recovery-focused evaluation protocols to facilitate research into robust robotic manipulation.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to improve the reliability and practical applicability of VLA models in complex robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RePO-VLA (Recovery-Driven Policy Optimization for Vision-Language-Action model) is a two-phase framework designed to train VLA policies from a combination of successful, failed, and recovered trajectories.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework Structure

RePO-VLA consists of two main phases:
- **Phase I: Recovery-Aware Initialization (RAI):** Adapts the VLA backbone using expert and recovery data while mitigating causal confusion by disassociating corrective actions from preceding failure histories.
- **Phase II: Value-Conditioned Refinement (VCR):** Learns dense progress labels for all types of rollouts and refines the policy to select actions conditioned on a desired value (e.g., high progress).

#### 3.2. Phase I: Recovery-Aware Initialization (RAI)

The goal of RAI is to initialize the policy with recovery skills that are conditioned on the current adverse state rather than the specific failure history that led to it. This is achieved through **Trajectory Slicing with History Reset (TSHR)**.
- **Trajectory Slicing:** For a raw recovery trajectory that includes an initial failure phase followed by a corrective phase starting at time `t_rec`, the failure prefix before `t_rec` is discarded. The segment from `t_rec` onwards is extracted as an independent correction episode.
- **History Reset:** At the first frame of this extracted recovery segment, the observation history buffer is cleared and then refilled normally over subsequent steps. This prevents the policy from learning a spurious dependency where the preceding error is a necessary trigger for recovery.
- **Training:** The policy is trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a mixture of expert demonstrations (`D_expert`) and these reset recovery datasets (`D_reset_rec`).

#### 3.3. Progress-Encoded Semantic Value Learning (PAS-VF)

PAS-VF is a crucial component for generating dense progress labels across all types of trajectories (success, failure, recovery).
- **Spatiotemporal Alignment:** The system maps robot trajectories and language instructions into a shared semantic latent space `Z`. This is achieved using a frozen V-JEPA spatiotemporal encoder (`E_v`) for trajectories and a frozen text encoder (`E_t`) for language instructions. Lightweight, trainable adapters (`f_θ` and `g_ϕ`) project the outputs of these encoders into `Z`.
- **Monotonic Progress Alignment (Training):** The adapters are trained exclusively on successful trajectories. The objective is to ensure that the cosine similarity between the embedding of a trajectory prefix (`z_v_t`) and the embedding of the language instruction (`z_l`) monotonically tracks the normalized temporal progress (`t/T_τ`).
- **Self-Referential Progress Estimation:** After alignment, successful executions form an internal reference set (`C_ref`). For any unlabeled trajectory (including failures or recovery segments), its progress value `V(τ)` is estimated as the maximum cosine similarity between its semantic embedding (`z_v_τ`) and any embedding in the reference cluster `C_ref`. This provides a dense, task-relative progress score without requiring manual annotation.

#### 3.4. Phase II: Value-Conditioned Refinement (VCR)

VCR uses the dense progress labels from PAS-VF to refine the policy, enabling it to differentiate actions based on desired progress levels. Unlike RAI, VCR trains on raw trajectories with their original rolling histories.
- **Progress-Aware Hindsight Labeling:** A continuous value label `v_t ∈` is assigned to each frame based on its contribution to task progress:
 - **Success and Effective Recovery:** Frames within successful trajectories (`τ_succ`) and effective recovery suffixes (`τ^+_rec`) are assigned `v_t = 1.0`.
 - **Error Segments:** Frames within deviating recovery prefixes (`τ^-_rec`) (the part leading to the adverse state) are assigned `v_t = 0.0`. This ensures that actions causing drift are not positively reinforced.
 - **Pure Failures:** For pure failure rollouts (`τ_fail`), a reliability decay function is applied: `v_t = V(τ_fail) ⋅ (1 - t/T)^α`, where `α = 3.0`. This retains useful early kinematic priors (higher `V(τ_fail)` from PAS-VF) but rapidly diminishes the value as the trajectory approaches an irreversible failure state.
- **Goal-Conditioned Architecture and Training:** The base VLA policy (e.g., `π_0.5`) is augmented with a value token `e_val = MLP_val(v_t)`. This token is attended by the transformer alongside visual, language, and history tokens. The policy is fine-tuned to predict actions conditioned on this value, allowing different actions for the same adverse observation/history depending on the desired progress level (`v_t`).
- **Inference (Autonomous Recovery):** During deployment, RePO-VLA does not estimate `V(τ)` online, clear history, or rely on failure detectors. Instead, the policy uses its standard rolling history buffer and is given a fixed high-value token `v = 1.0` at every step. This effectively biases the policy to execute actions that steer towards the learned success manifold, even when recent history might include low-value observations.

#### 3.5. FRBench: Failure-Recovery Benchmark

To evaluate recovery capabilities, the paper introduces FRBench, a benchmark for bimanual manipulation across simulated (RoboTwin) and real-world tasks.
- **Phase-based Protocol:** Evaluates recovery as a controlled state-transition problem:
 1. **Nominal Phase:** Policy executes under clean conditions (baseline task progress).
 2. **Error Phase:** Structured perturbations (E1-E4) project the scene into an adverse state.
 3. **Recovery Phase:** The policy continues from the adverse state without external logic, measured on its ability to restore task progress.
- **Failure-Recovery Data Engine:** Provides recovery supervision through two sources:
 - **Interception-Based Synthetic Injection:** Expert planner execution is intercepted at critical nodes (e.g., grasp, lift), and targeted errors (e.g., premature gripper close, grasp slip, position/orientation offsets) are injected. A hierarchical recovery planner then synthesizes corrective actions. This generates controlled, diverse failure modes.
 - **Policy-Induced Rollouts:** Closed-loop failures from trained base policies are collected, and an expert planner intervenes to generate recovery data. This captures "in-distribution" failures specific to the learned policy's behaviors.
- **Perturbation Taxonomy (E1-E4):** Defines four types of deterministic overrides: E1 (premature_close), E2 (grasp_slip), E3 (grasp_position_offset), and E4 (grasp_orientation_mismatch), applied consistently in sim and real environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RePO-VLA was conducted on FRBench, encompassing both simulated (FRBench-Sim) and real-world bimanual tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation (FRBench-Sim)

- **Nominal Robustness:** Phase I (Recovery-Aware Initialization) demonstrated improved nominal robustness. Under randomized conditions, its average success rate remained stable (44.6% to 44.0%), contrasting with a decline in baselines like π_0 (33.9% to 12.9%). This suggests that RAI does not compromise base task competence while preparing for recovery.
- **Recovery from Injected Failures:** The full RePO-VLA framework (RAI + VCR) showed substantial improvements in recovery success under injected failures. It raised success rates from 15.0% (π_0.5) to 37.0% in clean environments and from 15.4% to 43.0% in randomized environments. This gain was particularly evident in tasks requiring genuine re-grasping or re-alignment, supporting the efficacy of dense value conditioning in returning to the success manifold.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation & Recovery Data Scaling

- **Baseline Fragility:** Standard imitation baselines (π_0, π_0.5) exhibited significant performance degradation under adversarial perturbations in real-world tasks, confirming their brittleness without explicit recovery mechanisms.
- **Phase I Efficacy:** Phase I alone raised the average adversarial success rate to 37.5%, demonstrating the benefit of explicitly exposing the policy to recovery actions.
- **Data Density Bottleneck:** Initially, the full RePO-VLA (with 1x recovery data) showed improvement over π_0.5 but did not consistently outperform Phase I under adversarial conditions. This indicated that the initial recovery data density might be insufficient for the value-conditioned refinement to robustly model the diverse failure landscape.
- **Recovery Data Scaling:** A scaling study on real-world recovery data (increasing from 1x to 2x and 4x) showed a positive correlation between data density and performance. **Full** (with 4x recovery data) achieved average success rates of 80% in standard conditions and 75% under adversarial perturbations. This represents an improvement of 45 and 55 percentage points over π_0.5 in standard and adversarial settings, respectively, and surpassed Phase I under adversarial conditions. This empirical trend suggests that RePO-VLA benefits from comprehensive coverage of state-action neighborhoods around realistic failures.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **History Reset (RAI):** Ablations demonstrated that training with raw failure-recovery data *without* history reset (TSHR) led to causal confusion, while TSHR was crucial for effective recovery.
- **Value Guidance (VCR):** Comparing full RePO-VLA with a heuristic retry baseline showed that value guidance consistently improved performance. In a 30-trial validation, using a fixed `v = 1.0` token during inference improved success rates across tasks, indicating that value conditioning provides a stable control signal for recovery rather than a fragile prompt.
- **Decay Rate (α) in PAS-VF:** An analysis of the exponential decay rate `α` in the failure trajectory labeling revealed `α = 3` as an optimal balance. A lower `α = 1` under-penalized near-terminal failures, while a higher `α = 10` discarded too much useful early approach behavior, confirming the role of reliability decay in distinguishing valuable early motion from terminal breakdown.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Visualization

Qualitative examples from FRBench-Sim and real-world tasks illustrated RePO-VLA's ability to synthesize valid recovery trajectories in response to various injected errors (E1-E4) and dynamic adversarial perturbations. In contrast, baseline methods frequently failed to react to these deviations, leading to task failure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RePO-VLA framework presents a significant contribution to enhancing the robustness and reliability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robotic Robustness:** By explicitly learning from failures and recovery trajectories, RePO-VLA moves VLA models beyond nominal success rates, equipping them with the ability to autonomously recover from execution drift and adverse states. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic environments where perfect execution cannot be guaranteed.
- **Improved Data Utilization:** The framework offers a structured methodology for reusing mixed-quality interaction data (successful, failed, and recovery trajectories). This approach reduces the reliance on costly, success-only demonstrations and extracts valuable information from data that would traditionally be discarded, potentially making data collection more efficient.
- **Causal Confusion Mitigation:** The Recovery-Aware Initialization (RAI) phase, with its Trajectory Slicing with History Reset (TSHR) mechanism, specifically addresses the problem of causal confusion. This ensures that the policy learns corrective actions conditioned on the current adverse state rather than memorizing a preceding failure history, promoting more generalizable recovery skills.
- **Dense Progress-Aware Supervision:** The Progress-Aware Semantic Value Function (PAS-VF) provides a dense, task-relative progress signal. This fine-grained supervision allows the policy to distinguish between different qualities of behavior (useful approach, drift, active recovery, terminal failure), leading to more nuanced and effective action selection than sparse rewards.
- **Autonomous and Integrated Recovery:** RePO-VLA integrates recovery capabilities directly into the VLA policy through Value-Conditioned Refinement (VCR) and value-conditioned inference. This eliminates the need for external failure detectors, replanning modules, or heuristic retry rules, simplifying the system architecture and enabling seamless autonomous recovery during deployment.
- **Standardized Evaluation Benchmark:** The introduction of FRBench, with its phase-based protocol and standardized error injection, provides a valuable tool for the research community. It enables rigorous, reproducible evaluation of robotic recovery capabilities, encouraging further development and comparison of robust manipulation policies.
- **Applicability to Complex Tasks:** Demonstrated effectiveness in both simulated and real-world bimanual tasks, including contact-rich and deformable object manipulation, suggests its applicability to a wide range of complex robotic applications where precision and adaptability are paramount.

The limitations, such as the reliance on observing representative failure modes, the cost of real-world recovery data, and current simulation gaps, also highlight important avenues for future research. Addressing these could further extend the impact of RePO-VLA by improving its generalization to unseen errors and reducing data collection burdens. Overall, RePO-VLA contributes to making VLA models more resilient and practical for real-world robotic deployments.
