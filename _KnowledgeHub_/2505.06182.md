# Apple: Toward General Active Perception via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The APPLE framework enables autonomous agents to learn general active perception policies by integrating reinforcement learning with supervised learning, optimizing for information-gathering actions and prediction accuracy across various tactile tasks. This approach achieves high performance in classification, regression, and pose estimation, demonstrating improved sample efficiency and robustness over previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing active perception methods are often algorithmically tailored to specific tasks and environments, relying on hand-crafted heuristics that limit their generality.
- Traditional reinforcement learning approaches for active perception can be sample-inefficient, hindering their scalability and practical deployment in real-world robotics.
- A unified framework is lacking that can accommodate diverse sensory inputs and prediction goals without significant task-specific modifications, impeding adaptable robotic capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces APPLE (==Active Perception Policy Learning==), an off-policy reinforcement learning framework designed to learn general active perception policies for various tasks.
- Formulates active perception as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and optimizes a joint objective combining a policy gradient term with a differentiable supervised prediction loss gradient.
- Employs a transformer-based architecture for processing sequences of observations, enabling shared representations across Q-networks, action policies, and prediction policies.

## Results

- APPLE successfully learned exploration strategies and achieved high performance across five diverse tactile benchmarks, including classification (e.g., **89%** accuracy on TactileMNIST) and regression/pose estimation (e.g., **1.9cm**/13° error on Toolbox).
- APPLE methods consistently outperformed a random exploration baseline and the on-policy Haptic Attention Model (HAM), demonstrating significantly better sample efficiency and prediction accuracy.
- APPLE-CrossQ showed enhanced robustness by maintaining high performance when transferred to new environments without specific hyperparameter re-tuning and reduced training time by **53%** compared to APPLE-SAC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly training an information-gathering policy and a prediction module using a unified objective, which combines RL rewards with a differentiable perception loss, is effective for learning task-agnostic active perception.
> - Off-policy actor-critic methods, particularly adaptations like APPLE-SAC and APPLE-CrossQ, offer superior sample efficiency and robustness for active perception compared to on-policy baselines.
> - The use of a transformer architecture for sequential observation processing proves more effective and stable than LSTM-based models for learning complex active exploration behaviors.
