# Unleashing Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models via Textual Representation Guided Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Shanghai AI Lab introduce TRACE, a prompting method enabling multimodal large language models to construct and reason over structured, text-based allocentric representations of 3D environments from egocentric video. This approach consistently improves spatial reasoning accuracy on benchmarks like VSI-Bench, showing gains of up to 7.54% for Gemini 3 Pro without requiring architectural changes.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with 3D spatial reasoning from egocentric video, failing to construct structured, allocentric abstractions of environments.
- Existing MLLMs often over-fixate on 2D visual cues and implicit spatial correlations, leading to inaccuracies in complex spatial question answering.
- Prior solutions, such as large-scale supervised fine-tuning or integrating additional geometric modalities, often face limitations in scalability, generalization, or increased system complexity.

## Method

- ==TRACE (Textual Representation of Allocentric Context from Egocentric Video)== is a prompting strategy that guides MLLMs to generate a detailed, structured, text-based ==allocentric representation== of a 3D environment.
- The generated representation comprises a ==Room Aligned Meta Context==, a ==Camera Trajectory== (observer's path with positions and headings), and an ==Entity Registry== (detailed attributes and spatial relations of observed objects).
- MLLMs perform ==single-pass inference== where they first explicitly generate the schema-compliant TRACE as a 'spatial cache' and then leverage this structured textual context, alongside the video input, to derive the final answer.

## Results

- TRACE consistently improved spatial reasoning accuracy on VSI-Bench, with gains of **+7.54%** for Gemini 3 Pro, **+3.10%** for Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and **+1.63%** for MiMo-VL-7B-SFT over direct baselines.
- The method outperformed other prompting strategies (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Tree-of-Thought, Cognitive Map) on both VSI-Bench and OST-Bench, showing particular superiority for compact MLLM backbones.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the critical role of TRACE's components: removing the entity registry resulted in a **5.24%** performance drop, and removing camera trajectory led to a **1.92%** drop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can be effectively guided to construct and reason over structured allocentric representations of 3D spatial environments from 2D visual observations using only textual prompting.
> - A cognitively inspired, structured textual representation—detailing meta-context, camera trajectory, and entity registry—provides explicit geometric and relational grounding, significantly enhancing MLLM spatial reasoning.
> - Decompositional analysis reveals that MLLM bottlenecks in 3D reasoning can stem from both 3D visual perception (generating the spatial description) and language-based spatial reasoning (parsing the description), with visual perception often posing a greater challenge for smaller models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): their limitations in 3D spatial reasoning, particularly their inability to construct structured abstractions of 3D environments from video inputs. Existing MLLMs frequently depend on 2D visual cues and learn spurious correlations, rather than forming hierarchical representations of 3D scenes.

Human cognitive science studies indicate that 3D world reasoning involves transforming visual input into hierarchical representations of objects and spatial relations, often constructing allocentric (global, map-like) representations from egocentric observations. This human cognitive process, including the ability to describe allocentric representations textually, serves as inspiration for the proposed method.

Previous research on spatially aware MLLMs generally follows two main trajectories:
1. **Data-driven approaches:** Curating large-scale supervised fine-tuning datasets for spatial reasoning Question Answering (QA). This approach can face limitations in scalability and generalization.
2. **Architectural augmentations:** Incorporating additional geometric or stereo modalities into MLLMs. This tends to increase system complexity and restrict applicability to off-the-shelf MLLMs.

Several diagnostic studies have concurrently highlighted that current MLLMs struggle to internally organize spatial information and often rely on shortcut correlations from 2D pretraining, underscoring the need for more explicit scene structure exposure to the model. Notably, "Thinking in Space" (Yang et al., 2025a) demonstrated that externalizing spatial representations, such as cognitive maps, could improve spatial reasoning, while standard Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting had limited benefits. "SpatialEval" (Wang et al., 2024a) further revealed that MLLMs might fail to construct consistent internal 3D representations.

This work distinguishes itself by exploring a different formulation: leveraging text-based spatial descriptions as an intermediate reasoning trace to enhance spatial reasoning in MLLMs. This approach aligns with prior methods that extract textual descriptions from images or videos for LLM-based Visual Question Answering (VQA) (e.g., Wang et al., 2024c; Fan et al., 2025) and Chain-of-Thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022). However, unlike previous efforts that focused on evidence coverage and retrieval over long temporal contexts (e.g., VideoTree, VideoAgent, Video Mind Palace), this research specifically emphasizes designing textual representations that enable MLLMs to explicitly reason over 3D geometric cues. The paper positions its contribution as the first prompting-based method explicitly designed to unleash spatial reasoning capabilities in MLLMs without altering their core architecture or requiring specialized 3D inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the 3D spatial reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This objective stems from the observation that existing MLLMs exhibit limitations in understanding and reasoning about 3D environments, as they often fail to construct structured, abstract representations of scenes from egocentric video inputs. Instead, these models tend to rely on 2D visual signals and learn superficial correlations, which hinders robust spatial comprehension.

The motivation for this work draws inspiration from cognitive theories of human spatial reasoning. Humans are understood to process visual input by transforming it into hierarchical representations of objects and their spatial relationships, constructing an allocentric (world-centered) mental map from egocentric (self-centered) observations. This allocentric representation can be effectively communicated and reasoned about using text alone. This cognitive insight directly informed the central scientific question posed by the authors: Can MLLMs be guided to explicitly construct and reason over structured allocentric representations of 3D spatial environments based on 2D visual observations?

To address this question, the research proposes Textual Representation of Allocentric Context from Egocentric Video (TRACE). TRACE is a prompting method designed to induce MLLMs to generate text-based representations of 3D environments. These textual representations serve as intermediate reasoning traces, intended to facilitate more accurate spatial question answering. The core motivation is to provide MLLMs with a structured, explicit, and globally consistent representation of the 3D scene that can complement the transient and egocentric nature of video inputs. By encouraging MLLMs to perform explicit reasoning over such an intermediate, structured representation, the authors aim to overcome the models' tendency to rely on 2D shortcuts and improve their capacity for holistic 3D spatial understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research formulates spatial reasoning as a generation task, where a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) processes an egocentric video (V) and a natural language query (Q) to produce an answer (A). The proposed approach, TRACE, integrates into this formulation by enforcing a specific reasoning trace, denoted as G, which takes the form of a structured textual representation of allocentric context.

The inference process is conceptualized as a single-turn generation maximizing `P(A|G, V, Q) ⋅ P(G|V, Q)`. Here, a "Spatial Descriptor" component is responsible for producing the intermediate TRACE representation (G) given the video and query. Subsequently, a "Reasoning Parser" component uses both the generated TRACE and the original video input, alongside the query, to derive the final answer.

**Key Components of TRACE (G = ⟨M, T, E⟩):**

1. **Meta Context (M):**
 - **Room Aligned Coordinate System:** To address issues of camera initialization and coordinate system confusion, TRACE requires the model to establish a global coordinate system aligned with the room's geometry. The origin is fixed at the observer's starting position. The Y-axis is defined by detecting the most salient straight line formed by static large objects (rather than the camera's initial heading), and the X-axis is its perpendicular rightward direction in the floor plane. Units are approximate meters.
 - **Room Topology:** A description of the overall spatial structure of the environment (e.g., "Rectangular Office").
 - **Grid Alignment:** Specifies which structural cue defines the allocentric axes.
 - **Initial Camera Heading:** The camera's initial facing direction relative to the room-aligned grid.

2. **Camera Trajectory (T):**
 - This component aims to capture the dynamic aspects of the video by reconstructing the observer's path as a discrete sequence of steps.
 - For each step, TRACE records:
 - A timestamp.
 - Estimated 2D position `[x, y]` within the established coordinate system.
 - The camera's facing direction, approximated using eight discrete cardinal orientations (Y-axis aligned with North), to simplify estimation and parsing.
 - An `action` property describing the camera's motion or viewpoint change.
 - This reconstruction of the surveyor's path allows the model to answer questions involving navigation or route planning by referencing a dynamically built spatial map.

3. **Entity Registry (E):**
 - Instead of coarse grid cells, TRACE maintains a detailed registry of observed entities throughout the video sequence.
 - A structured schema is enforced for each object entity to prevent duplication and ensure precise localization:
 - **Temporal Stamping:** Each entity includes a `first_seen_at` timestamp.
 - **Visual Signature:** A brief, appearance-based description for disambiguating similar instances.
 - **Metric Estimation:** Plausible 2D coordinates `[x, y]` in meters relative to the grid origin. This estimation forces the model to convert qualitative spatial relations (e.g., "near," "between") into quantitative geometric constraints.
 - **Spatial Relations:** Natural language descriptions of an entity's relative spatial relationships to nearby objects or structures.
 - **Strict Serialization:** Entities are listed individually (e.g., "chair_01," "chair_02") to ensure granular counting and positional accuracy.

**Inference Mechanism:**

The standard implementation of TRACE employs a single-pass inference. The generation process is conditioned to explicitly produce the schema-compliant TRACE representation (G) *before* the final answer. This mechanism functions as a structured Chain-of-Thought, effectively loading the MLLM's context window with a "spatial cache" of the environment. The final answer is then derived through TRACE-conditioned inference, which integrates both the egocentric video input and queries the cached TRACE to perform operations such as computing Euclidean distances between object coordinates or traversing trajectory nodes. This grounding in verifiable geometric constraints generated by the model itself is intended to improve final answer accuracy.

The paper provides detailed prompting instructions in an appendix, outlining how MLLMs are guided to generate each component of the TRACE structure in a specific YAML format, including hard rules for completeness, coordinate estimation, and global consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of TRACE was evaluated through extensive experiments on two spatial intelligence benchmarks: VSI-Bench and OST-Bench. VSI-Bench is a video-based benchmark featuring egocentric indoor scene scans with 5,130 question-answer (QA) pairs across 288 videos, covering tasks such as configurational, measurement-estimation, and spatiotemporal reasoning. OST-Bench assesses online spatio-temporal understanding from an embodied agent's perspective, comprising 1,386 scenes and 10,165 QA pairs in a multi-round dialogue format. A reproducible subset of 200 scenes and 1,396 QA pairs was used for OST-Bench evaluations. Metrics included Accuracy (Acc) for multiple-choice questions and Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) for numerical questions.

The evaluation employed several MLLM backbones, including the proprietary Gemini 3 Pro and open-source models such as Qwen2.5-VL-72B and MiMo-VL-7B, with additional tests on o3 and GLM-4.5V.

**Comparison of Different Prompting Methods:**
TRACE was compared against several established prompting strategies: Direct, Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thought (ToT), Least-to-Most (LtM), and Cognitive Map (CM).
- **VSI-Bench:** TRACE consistently yielded notable performance improvements across all tested MLLM backbones.
 - For Gemini 3 Pro, TRACE achieved an average improvement of +7.54% over the Direct baseline and slightly outperformed other advanced prompting methods (+0.43% over CM).
 - For Qwen2.5-VL-72B, TRACE demonstrated a +3.10% average improvement over Direct, significantly outperforming other prompting strategies which often yielded marginal or even negative gains.
 - For MiMo-VL-7B, TRACE showed a +1.63% average improvement over Direct.
- **OST-Bench:** TRACE provided absolute performance gains of +1.2% for Gemini and +2.4% for MiMo, whereas other prompting strategies offered marginal benefits. This suggests TRACE's utility in multi-turn spatial reasoning where explicit spatial grounding is crucial. It was observed that spatial-specific prompting (CM and TRACE) generally performed better than general linguistic reasoning methods (CoT, LtM, ToT) for smaller models like MiMo. A limitation noted was a performance drop in certain agent state predictions in OST-Bench, attributed to TRACE's current static allocentric formulation potentially decoupling from rapid, dynamic egocentric updates.

**Comparison of Different Prediction Settings:**
The research investigated three settings for utilizing TRACE: One-Stage Inference (generate TRACE and answer in a single pass), Two-Stage Inference (generate TRACE, then feed as context for QA), and Text-Only Inference (LLM answers solely based on TRACE).
- One-Stage Inference generally yielded the best performance, indicating that the active reasoning process involved in generating TRACE is beneficial, beyond just the resulting textual representation.
- Text-Only Inference with Gemini achieved performance on par with the direct video-based method, suggesting that TRACE effectively summarizes video content for spatial reasoning.

**Comparison with Other Text-based Spatial Representations:**
When compared with Cognitive Map and Spatial Captioning methods in a text-only inference setting, TRACE outperformed Cognitive Map by 9.7% and Spatial Caption by 3.53% on VSI-Bench (using Qwen2.5-VL-72B). Qualitative analysis further highlighted TRACE's superior 3D granularity.

**Ablation Studies:**
- Removing camera trajectory information resulted in a 1.92% performance drop on average, though it improved performance on some tasks (room size and relative direction). This suggests MLLMs might struggle with reliably estimating camera motion.
- Excluding the entity registry led to a more substantial 5.24% performance drop, particularly affecting object-related tasks, affirming its critical role in spatial QA.

**Additional Analysis:**
- **Decompositional Analysis:** Decomposing 3D spatial reasoning into "3D visual perception" (Spatial Descriptor) and "language-based spatial reasoning" (Reasoning Parser) revealed that both components are bottlenecks. Replacing either with a weaker model (e.g., Qwen vs. Gemini) significantly reduced performance, with the visual perception component often exhibiting a larger impact. This indicates that MLLMs' limitations stem from both their ability to ground 3D information from pixels and their capacity to reason over explicit spatial knowledge.
- **Token Efficiency:** TRACE demonstrated favorable token efficiency for smaller models like MiMo-VL-7B compared to highly branching methods (ToT, LtM), while achieving superior performance. For larger models, TRACE could be more token-intensive due to the exhaustive spatial cache generated, though the accuracy gains often justified this overhead.
- **Cross-Environment Generalization:** Stratified analysis on VSI-Bench datasets (ARKitScenes, ScanNet, ScanNetPP) showed that TRACE consistently delivered robust performance gains across diverse indoor scene layouts and complexities, indicating reliable generalization.
- **Instruction Following for Spatial Reasoning:** Experiments revealed that Qwen-VL models underperformed language-only Qwen models when parsing spatial representations, suggesting that visual instruction tuning might sometimes compromise spatial knowledge parsing ability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces TRACE (Textual Representation of Allocentric Context from Egocentric Video), a prompting method that enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform structured spatial reasoning. This work contributes to the field by demonstrating that MLLMs can leverage text-based, allocentric representations of 3D environments as intermediate reasoning traces, addressing a known limitation where these models often struggle with abstracting 3D scene structure from video inputs.

The significance of TRACE lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning:** It provides a mechanism for MLLMs to move beyond superficial 2D visual cues and develop a more comprehensive understanding of 3D spatial relationships, which is a fundamental requirement for advanced scene comprehension. The consistent performance improvements across diverse MLLM backbones and benchmarks (VSI-Bench and OST-Bench) demonstrate its effectiveness.
- **Cognitive Alignment:** By drawing inspiration from human cognitive theories of allocentric spatial reasoning, the method offers a biologically plausible approach to enhancing artificial intelligence, suggesting that modeling spatial context through structured representations can be beneficial.
- **Broad Applicability and Flexibility:** As a prompting-based method, TRACE is directly applicable to off-the-shelf MLLMs without requiring modifications to their architecture, additional geometric sensor inputs, or extensive new training data. This makes it a widely accessible tool for improving spatial reasoning capabilities in various existing models.
- **Diagnostic Insights:** The decompositional analysis provides valuable insights into the bottlenecks of 3D spatial understanding in MLLMs. By separating 3D visual perception (Spatial Descriptor) from language-based spatial reasoning (Reasoning Parser), the work helps pinpoint areas for future improvement in model development, highlighting the need for both robust visual grounding and strong inferential capacities over spatial knowledge.
- **Value of Intermediate Representations:** The findings underscore the importance of explicitly generating and reasoning over structured intermediate representations for complex tasks. The observation that the *process* of generating TRACE contributes to performance further emphasizes the role of structured thinking in MLLMs.
- **Potential for Embodied AI:** Accurate and robust spatial reasoning is critical for embodied AI applications such as navigation, manipulation, and planning. TRACE offers a promising interface for grounding MLLMs in the physical world, particularly for low-latency deployments where token efficiency, as observed in smaller models, could be advantageous.

Future work outlined by the authors includes developing dynamic streaming TRACE frameworks for real-time updates and persistent world models, internalizing TRACE-like reasoning through MLLM training (e.g., supervised finetuning or reinforcement learning), integrating specialized 3D perception modules for higher fidelity, optimizing token efficiency, and extending TRACE to a broader range of embodied AI tasks beyond question answering. These potential directions suggest that TRACE could serve as a foundational step towards more general and robust spatial intelligence in multimodal systems.
