# Matching Features, Not Tokens: Energy-Based Fine-Tuning of Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Energy-Based Fine-Tuning (EBFT) is introduced as a method for aligning language model rollouts with ground-truth data by optimizing a feature-matching objective, effectively providing dense semantic feedback. The method consistently outperforms standard supervised fine-tuning and matches or surpasses reward-based reinforcement learning on coding and translation tasks, while achieving superior distributional calibration and maintaining language modeling quality.

## Problem

- Cross-entropy (CE) training for large language models suffers from a distribution shift during inference, leading to error accumulation in longer, self-conditioned generations.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning methods require reliable, task-specific scalar reward functions which are often unavailable or misaligned, and do not inherently guarantee distributional calibration.
- There is a lack of principled methods for providing dense, semantic, sequence-level feedback to language models without relying on external verifiers or scalar rewards.

## Method

- Proposes **EBFT** (Energy-Based Fine-Tuning) that optimizes a ==feature-matching loss==, measuring the squared difference between mean feature embeddings of model-generated rollouts and ground-truth completions.
- Utilizes a ==REINFORCE-style gradient estimator== with a novel reward function that balances alignment with ground-truth features and divergence from the model's own expected feature moment, enhanced by a ==leave-one-out baseline==.
- Employs a frozen, pre-trained generator as the feature network and combines the feature-matching loss with a standard ==next-token cross-entropy regularization== to maintain language modeling quality.

## Results

- EBFT consistently outperformed SFT and was competitive with or superior to RLVR across coding (e.g., Q&A coding greedy accuracy **0.548** vs SFT's **0.483**, RLVR's **0.535**) and translation tasks, even without using a correctness signal.
- The approach achieved the lowest feature-matching loss and reduced validation cross-entropy more than SFT (e.g., Q&A coding CE **0.207** vs SFT's **0.289**), indicating improved distributional calibration and better language modeling quality.
- EBFT demonstrated improved generalization on out-of-distribution benchmarks (e.g., MultiPL-E for coding, MTNT for translation) and its improvements were consistent across various model scales (1.5B, 3B, and 7B).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - EBFT provides a principled approach to dense, semantic, sequence-level feedback that directly targets distributional calibration, bridging the gap between token-level CE training and scalar-reward-based RL.
> - The feature-matching objective implicitly defines an energy function over sequences, theoretically connecting EBFT to KL-regularized energy-based models and allowing it to enforce high-dimensional moment constraints in a semantically rich feature space.
> - The method improves downstream task performance while simultaneously enhancing or maintaining language modeling quality (e.g., lower validation cross-entropy), avoiding the common trade-off observed with RL-based fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language model (LLM) training predominantly relies on cross-entropy (CE) training, often implemented with teacher forcing during pre-training and supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This approach, while providing a dense and scalable learning signal, introduces a fundamental distribution shift. During training, models condition on ground-truth prefixes, whereas at inference time, they must condition on their own generated sequences. This discrepancy, termed exposure bias, can lead to compounding errors, causing the model to generate tokens from distributions it was rarely trained on. Previous work has quantified this issue by showing an increase in the expected conditional entropy of generated tokens with sequence length, indicating a divergence from the true data distribution at the sequence level, even for models with low training perplexity.

Existing strategies to address this sequence-level divergence include reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning methods, such as those inspired by RLHF or Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). These methods optimize sequence-level rewards under the model's own rollouts, offering direct behavioral control. However, their efficacy is contingent on the availability of a reliable reward function or verifier, as seen in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). Such reward signals can be unavailable, noisy, or misaligned with desired behaviors in open-ended tasks. Furthermore, RL typically optimizes a scalar reward signal, which may not directly promote distributional calibration of the entire rollout distribution. Observations indicate that while RLVR can enhance downstream performance, it may simultaneously degrade validation cross-entropy and other distributional metrics.

Another line of research incorporates partial rollouts during training, often employing surrogate rewards derived from the model's own log-probabilities or token-overlap similarity between sampled continuations and references. While practical, these surrogate rewards generally lack guarantees for distributional calibration. Self-likelihood-based rewards may reinforce already high-probability samples without necessarily improving coverage, and similarity-based measures might improve a specific metric without comprehensively enhancing the underlying likelihood or calibration of the generated distribution.

This research introduces Energy-Based Fine-Tuning (EBFT) as an alternative. It positions itself by directly addressing the limitations of teacher-forced CE training (distribution shift, token-level focus) and RL-based methods (reliance on external rewards, scalar optimization, potential distributional degradation). EBFT proposes a principled feature-matching objective that functions as a dense, sequence-level training signal, obviating the need for task-specific verifiers or heuristic surrogate rewards. By targeting sequence-level statistics in a semantic feature space, the work aims to achieve distributional calibration, with theoretical connections drawn to KL-regularized feature matching and energy-based modeling. This approach seeks to bridge the gap between token-level likelihood objectives and sequence-level behavioral optimization, offering a method that can improve both downstream task performance and the intrinsic quality of language model generations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and evaluate a novel fine-tuning objective for language models, termed Energy-Based Fine-Tuning (EBFT), that directly targets sequence-level statistics of the completion distribution. This objective aims to provide dense semantic feedback to the model without the necessity of a task-specific verifier or preference model, thereby addressing limitations inherent in current language model training paradigms.

The motivation for developing EBFT stems from several identified shortcomings in existing methods:

1. **Addressing Distribution Shift (Exposure Bias):** Standard cross-entropy (CE) training, which employs teacher forcing, optimizes next-token prediction by conditioning on ground-truth prefixes. However, during deployment, the model generates sequences by conditioning on its own prior predictions. This fundamental mismatch, known as exposure bias or distribution shift, causes errors to propagate and accumulate, leading the model to operate in parts of the input space it was not adequately trained on. This research seeks a fine-tuning method that accounts for this discrepancy by optimizing behavior under the model's own rollouts.

2. **Beyond Token-Level Accuracy to Sequence-Level Calibration:** While CE training can achieve low perplexity (high next-token prediction accuracy), this metric does not guarantee calibrated or coherent behavior over longer generated sequences. Models may locally match the data distribution but diverge significantly at the sequence level. A key motivation is to develop an objective that directly measures and reduces this sequence-level divergence by comparing the statistical properties of model-generated completions against those of ground-truth completions in a rich feature space. The proposed conditional feature-matching loss is designed to formalize this direct comparison.

3. **Limitations of Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-Tuning:** RL-based methods, while capable of optimizing sequence-level behavior, often rely on external reward functions or verifiers. The availability, reliability, and potential misalignment of these reward signals pose significant practical challenges, especially in open-ended generation tasks where clear-cut correctness criteria may not exist. Furthermore, RL optimizes a scalar reward, which may not translate directly to comprehensive distributional calibration of the model's rollouts. Empirical observations show that while RL can improve downstream task performance, it often does so at the cost of degrading the model's fundamental language modeling capabilities, as evidenced by increased validation cross-entropy and feature-matching loss. The research is motivated to find an approach that avoids this tradeoff.

4. **Improving Upon Surrogate Reward Mechanisms:** Some methods incorporate partial rollouts during training using surrogate rewards (e.g., based on log-probabilities or token overlap). However, these surrogates are heuristic and do not provide guarantees of distributional calibration. Self-likelihood rewards might only reinforce already probable samples without enhancing the diversity or coverage of the generated distribution, and similarity measures may improve a narrow metric without improving the model's overall likelihood or calibration. The motivation is to replace these heuristics with a principled objective that, under sufficiently rich features, acts as a proper scoring rule to recover the true conditional distribution.

In summary, the detailed objectives are to introduce a feature-matching objective and an optimization method (EBFT) that provides dense, semantic, sequence-level feedback to language models, improves distributional calibration without requiring external verifiers, and mitigates the exposure bias of traditional CE training, ultimately leading to enhanced performance and intrinsic quality of generated sequences across diverse tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research is centered on a novel feature-matching objective for language model fine-tuning, optimized through a process termed Energy-Based Fine-Tuning (EBFT). This approach aims to align the sequence-level statistics of model-generated text with those of ground-truth data in a semantic feature space.

**Feature-Matching Loss Formulation:**
The research defines a feature-matching loss function, $L_{FM}(\theta)$, that quantifies the squared error between the expected feature embedding of model-generated completions ($E_{\hat{y} \sim p_\theta(\cdot|c)}[\phi(c:\hat{y})]$) and that of ground-truth completions ($E_{y \sim p(\cdot|c)}[\phi(c:y)]$) for a given context $c$. This $L_{FM}(\theta)$ is then shown to be related to a conditional feature-matching loss, $L_{CFM}(\theta)$, which can be estimated directly from ground-truth context-completion pairs. The objective is to achieve $L_{FM}(\theta) = 0$, implying that the model's expected feature embeddings match the data's for all contexts. Under the assumption of a sufficiently rich feature map $\phi$, minimizing this loss can effectively recover the true conditional data distribution $p_\theta = p$.

**Feature Map Construction ($\phi$):**
The feature map $\phi$ is instantiated as a frozen neural network, initialized by copying the generator model $p_\theta$ at its initial state. For a concatenated `context:completion` sequence, $\phi$ produces an embedding by concatenating intermediate activations from specific layers (e.g., 25%, 50%, and 75% depth) of the network. Each block of activations is L2-normalized before concatenation. This design choice is hypothesized to capture diverse semantic and structural information, ranging from lower-level details to higher-level concepts.

**Optimization Strategy: Energy-Based Fine-Tuning (EBFT):**
EBFT optimizes a mixed objective: $L(\theta) = L_{FM}(\theta) + \gamma L_{CE}(\theta)$, where $\gamma \ge 0$ balances the feature-matching objective with a standard next-token cross-entropy ($L_{CE}$) term.
The gradient of $L_{FM}(\theta)$ (and thus $L_{CFM}(\theta)$) with respect to the generator parameters $\theta$ is estimated using a REINFORCE-style policy gradient. This involves sampling multiple completions from the current model and assigning them a reward based on their feature-matching quality.

**Reward Function:**
The reward $r(\hat{y}, c)$ for a sampled completion $\hat{y}$ given context $c$ is defined as:
$r(\hat{y}, c) = 2\phi_c(\hat{y})^\top \phi_c(y) - 2\phi_c(\hat{y})^\top E_{\tilde{y} \sim p_\theta(\cdot|c)}[\phi_c(\tilde{y})]$
This reward comprises an "alignment term" that promotes similarity between the sampled completion's feature and the ground-truth feature, and a "diversity term" that penalizes similarity among multiple sampled completions. To obtain an unbiased estimate of the gradient, $n > 1$ completions $(\hat{y}_j)_{j=1}^n$ are sampled from $p_\theta(\cdot|c)$, and the reward $r_j$ for each $\hat{y}_j$ is computed using a Leave-One-Out (RLOO) approach, where the diversity term excludes $\hat{y}_j$ itself from the expectation over sampled completions. The RLOO baseline is also incorporated to reduce gradient variance.

**Practical Implementation Steps (Algorithm 1):**
1. **Generation:** For each ground-truth `(context, completion)` pair $(c, y)$, $n$ rollouts $(\hat{y}_j)_{j=1}^n$ of length $G$ are sampled from the current generator $p_\theta(\cdot|c)$.
2. **Feature Embedding:** The ground-truth sequence $c:y$ and each sampled sequence $c:\hat{y}_j$ are passed through the frozen feature network $\phi$ to obtain their respective feature vectors, $\phi_c(y)$ and $\phi_c(\hat{y}_j)$.
3. **Reward Calculation:** The feature-matching reward $r_j$ is computed for each sampled completion $\hat{y}_j$ using the embeddings and the RLOO method.
4. **Actor Update:** The generator $p_\theta$ is updated using the policy gradient signal derived from the computed rewards and log-probabilities of the sampled completions.

**Advanced Implementation Techniques:**
- **Alignment Bias:** An optional scaling factor $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ can be applied to the diversity term in the reward function. Values of $\alpha < 1$ bias the objective towards higher fidelity (closer alignment with ground-truth features) potentially at the cost of reduced diversity.
- **Strided Block-Parallel Rollouts:** To efficiently generate a large number of on-policy samples, a strided block-parallel decoding scheme is employed. This method reuses computation by treating a single long training sequence as a source of multiple nested prompts, generating completions for these prompts concurrently within a single batched forward pass using a custom attention mask. This amortizes prefix computation and enables batched feature extraction.

**Feature Whitening:**
To prevent certain feature dimensions from dominating the loss due to correlations or anisotropy, a whitened variant of the feature-matching loss is used. This involves estimating the second-moment matrix $\hat{\Sigma}_c$ of the sampled features and transforming the features using the square root of its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, $\tilde{\phi}_c(z) = (\hat{\Sigma}_c^\dagger)^{1/2}\phi_c(z)$. This approximation connects the loss to a local $\chi^2$ divergence, which is locally equivalent to the KL divergence in the fine-tuning regime. The reward function is adjusted by normalizing the whitened features specifically in the alignment term to maintain signal strength, while the diversity term retains the full whitened geometry.

**Theoretical Connections:**
The research provides a theoretical perspective connecting EBFT to KL-regularized energy-based models. Under KL regularization, the feature-matching objective implicitly defines an energy function over sequences, where the optimal policy takes the form of an exponential tilt of a base model. This connection provides a formal grounding for the "Energy-Based" moniker. Furthermore, the framework can be interpreted as performing a KL projection onto moment constraints, similar to how this principle has been used to correct entropy-rate drift in language models, but extended to high-dimensional, semantically rich feature spaces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Energy-Based Fine-Tuning (EBFT) was conducted across Q&A coding, unstructured coding, and translation tasks, comparing its performance against Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The findings indicate that EBFT consistently delivers performance competitive with or superior to existing methods, particularly in areas of distributional calibration and efficiency.

**1. Downstream Accuracy:**
- **Coding Tasks:** On Q&A coding, EBFT significantly outperformed SFT across all decoding strategies (e.g., HumanEval greedy: 0.548 vs SFT 0.483; pass@16: 0.771 vs SFT 0.728). EBFT also matched or slightly exceeded RLVR (e.g., HumanEval greedy: 0.548 vs RLVR 0.535; pass@16: 0.771 vs RLVR 0.752), despite not using any task-specific correctness signal. For unstructured coding, where RLVR is inapplicable, EBFT consistently surpassed SFT (e.g., pass@1: 0.524 vs SFT 0.467).
- **Translation Task:** On translation, EBFT achieved higher COMET scores across all decoding strategies compared to both SFT and RLVR (e.g., greedy COMET: 0.725 vs SFT 0.717 and RLVR 0.697).

**2. Validation Cross-Entropy (CE):**
- A notable finding is that EBFT consistently reduced validation cross-entropy more effectively than SFT, even though SFT explicitly optimizes this objective. For instance, on Q&A coding, EBFT achieved a CE of 0.207 compared to SFT's 0.289. This trend was also observed in translation (EBFT 1.670 vs SFT 1.782). This outcome is attributed to EBFT with whitening approximately optimizing a relaxation of the $\chi^2$ divergence, which is locally equivalent to KL divergence in the fine-tuning regime.
- In contrast, RLVR consistently degraded validation cross-entropy, often resulting in CE values substantially worse than the base model (e.g., Q&A coding: RLVR 0.774 vs base 0.338). This indicates that RLVR's reward-driven optimization can improve task performance at the expense of the model's fundamental language modeling quality, a trade-off that EBFT avoids.

**3. Feature-Matching Loss (FM):**
- EBFT achieved the lowest feature-matching loss across all tasks. On Q&A coding, EBFT recorded an FM loss of 0.258, compared to SFT's 0.315 and RLVR's 0.442. For translation, EBFT showed the largest improvement with 0.578 compared to SFT's 0.690 and RLVR's 0.641.
- RLVR not only failed to improve this metric but actively worsened it relative to the base model.
- The improvements in feature-matching loss observed with EBFT were sustained across all completion lengths, extending beyond the 8-token rollout horizon used during training, which suggests a genuine improvement in the overall distributional calibration of the model's rollouts.

**4. Impact of CE Loss Coefficient ($\gamma$):**
- Ablations showed that varying the CE regularization weight $\gamma$ in EBFT primarily influenced the rate of cross-entropy reduction. Larger $\gamma$ values led to faster CE decreases, while downstream performance and feature-matching loss remained largely consistent across settings. Even with $\gamma=0$ (pure feature matching), EBFT outperformed SFT in reducing CE, indicating an inherent alignment between the two objectives.

**5. Generalization to Out-of-Distribution Benchmarks:**
- EBFT demonstrated superior generalization capabilities. On out-of-distribution coding languages (MultiPL-E benchmark), SFT showed performance degradation relative to the base model, whereas EBFT yielded improvements. Similarly, on the noisy MTNT translation benchmark, EBFT outperformed both SFT and RLVR.

**6. Feature Network Ablations:**
- The study found that feature whitening and the use of last-token pooling were the most critical components of the feature network design, with their removal leading to the largest performance degradations. Random feature network weights caused only modest drops, suggesting pre-trained representations are beneficial but not strictly essential. Surprisingly, using a larger feature network did not yield additional gains.

**7. Scaling Across Model Sizes:**
- EBFT's benefits were consistent across different model scales (1.5B, 3B, and 7B parameters). All tested model sizes showed substantial and qualitatively similar improvements in downstream accuracy, cross-entropy, and feature-matching losses, indicating that the method scales predictably.

**8. Qualitative Analysis:**
- Qualitative analysis of generated outputs revealed that EBFT consistently produced generations that were more semantically faithful to the prompt and more cleanly formatted. It avoided characteristic failure modes of SFT (e.g., missing subtle requirements, incomplete solutions) and RLVR (e.g., plausible logic but execution failures due to undefined functions, interleaved prose, or multilingual template drift). This suggests that the feature-matching objective encourages outputs that are both semantically accurate and structurally sound.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Energy-Based Fine-Tuning (EBFT) as a significant advancement in fine-tuning language models, addressing several fundamental limitations of current practices. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Mitigation of Distribution Shift and Exposure Bias:** EBFT directly confronts the long-standing problem of distribution shift (exposure bias) in sequence generation. By optimizing sequence-level behavior under the model's own rollouts and matching features to ground truth, it offers a principled way to train models that are more robust to the compounding errors inherent in autoregressive generation.

**2. Enhanced Distributional Calibration:** A core contribution is the demonstrated ability of EBFT to achieve superior distributional calibration of model generations. The consistent reduction in feature-matching loss across various completion lengths, extending beyond the training horizon, indicates that the method genuinely aligns the statistical properties of generated sequences with real data. This is a crucial step towards developing LLMs that not only produce syntactically correct text but also generate semantically appropriate and coherent sequences.

**3. Reduced Reliance on Task-Specific Verifiers and Reward Models:** A key practical advantage of EBFT is its independence from task-specific reward functions or external verifiers. This removes a major bottleneck associated with RL-based fine-tuning (e.g., RLVR), making advanced fine-tuning techniques applicable to a broader range of open-ended and non-verifiable tasks, such as unstructured code generation or creative writing, where explicit correctness signals are scarce or ill-defined.

**4. Synergistic Improvement of Downstream Performance and Language Modeling Quality:** Unlike RLVR, which often exhibits a trade-off between improving downstream task performance and degrading core language modeling quality (e.g., increased cross-entropy), EBFT demonstrates simultaneous improvements in both. Its ability to reduce validation cross-entropy more effectively than SFT, while also boosting downstream accuracy, suggests that feature matching acts as a complementary and reinforcing training signal, rather than one in tension with likelihood-based objectives.

**5. Improved Generalization and Qualitative Output:** EBFT has shown better generalization to out-of-distribution benchmarks and produced qualitatively superior outputs that are more semantically faithful, complete, and correctly formatted. This indicates that promoting distributional calibration in a feature space leads to more robust and reliable model behavior, reducing common failure modes observed in other fine-tuning approaches.

**6. Theoretical Foundation:** The connection of EBFT to KL-regularized energy-based models provides a strong theoretical grounding, offering a deeper understanding of the objective's properties and its relation to established statistical and machine learning frameworks.

**Potential Future Impact:**
- **Broader Applicability of Advanced Fine-Tuning:** By democratizing access to effective sequence-level fine-tuning without heavy reliance on external rewards, EBFT could enable more developers and researchers to fine-tune LLMs for complex, non-verifiable tasks, fostering innovation in areas currently limited by reward engineering challenges.
- **Foundation for More Robust and Reliable LLMs:** Improved distributional calibration and better generalization suggest that models fine-tuned with EBFT may be inherently more reliable and produce fewer "hallucinations" or off-topic responses, particularly in open-ended generative applications.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The success of feature-matching may encourage further exploration into alternative objective functions that leverage rich, high-dimensional representations, moving beyond token-level or scalar-reward-based training.
- **Challenges and Future Work:** The research identifies avenues for future work, including improving the computational efficiency of the rollout-based updates, scaling the method to even larger models and longer rollout horizons, and exploring adaptive or learned feature networks to potentially unlock further performance gains. Addressing these aspects would enhance the practicality and impact of EBFT across a wider range of applications.
