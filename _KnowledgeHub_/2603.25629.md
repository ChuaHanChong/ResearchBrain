# LanteRn: Latent Visual Structured Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> LanteRn introduces a framework that enables Large Multimodal Models to interleave language with compact latent visual representations, facilitating visual reasoning directly within a latent space. This approach, trained via supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, improved performance across perception-heavy benchmarks, including a significant jump in relational reasoning tasks, and demonstrates potential as a compute-efficient alternative to larger models.

## Problem

- Existing Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) primarily reason using text, compressing rich visual data into low-bandwidth symbolic formats, which is insufficient for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial and visual understanding.
- Current 'thinking with images' approaches either rely on external, predefined vision tools with significant computational overhead or generate explicit pixel-space images, which is resource-intensive and often irrelevant for reasoning details.
- Models often struggle with complex relational reasoning and precise object localization when visual information is not actively processed throughout the reasoning chain.

## Method

- LanteRn augments a vision-language transformer (Qwen2.5-VL) with special ==control tokens==, allowing it to dynamically switch between text generation mode and a visual latent mode that produces continuous ==latent 'thought' embeddings==.
- A Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage grounds these latent visual states by aligning them with pooled visual features extracted from the model's own frozen vision encoder via an MSE loss.
- A Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage, utilizing ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, refines latent representations to optimize for task-level utility, driven by accuracy and format rewards, without explicit region-level supervision.

## Results

- SFT variants improved performance over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline on Visual-CoT (e.g., from **0.66** to **0.81** for LantErn-SFT-8), showing initial gains in perception-centric skills.
- The RL stage consistently outperformed SFT and text-only baselines across all evaluated benchmarks (VisCoT, V*, Blink), with LantErn-RL-8 achieving **0.83** on VisCoT and significantly improving relational reasoning on Blink_RP from **0.68** (SFT) to **0.81**.
- LanteRn-RL-8 demonstrated performance parity with 7B models on several benchmarks, highlighting the potential for compute-efficient multimodal reasoning without relying solely on scaling model size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervised fine-tuning effectively provides a perceptual structure for the model's internal latent visual states, particularly enhancing perception-centric skills.
> - Reinforcement learning is crucial for evolving latent representations from mere perceptual fidelity to task-driven utility, enabling more effective use of visual information for complex reasoning.
> - Interleaving language with compact latent visual representations offers a compute-efficient pathway for multimodal reasoning, potentially matching the performance of larger models on perception-heavy tasks without increased parameter counts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have demonstrated proficiency across a range of vision-language tasks, yet their reasoning processes primarily remain text-centric. Current LMMs typically encode visual input once and then conduct all subsequent reasoning using textual representations. This approach, termed 'thinking about images,' often forces high-dimensional perceptual information into a low-bandwidth symbolic medium, which can be insufficient for tasks demanding fine-grained spatial and visual understanding.

To address this limitation, recent research has explored 'thinking with images,' where visual information actively contributes to the reasoning process beyond the initial input stage. This emerging field comprises two main approaches. The first involves tool-based visual reasoning, where models leverage external vision modules—such as object detectors, image croppers, or image generators—during inference. While effective, these methods are constrained by a predefined set of tools and often incur notable computational overhead. The second approach focuses on explicitly generating images as part of the reasoning chain. This forces intermediate visual thoughts into pixel space, consuming substantial computational resources on photorealistic details that may not be directly relevant to the task at hand.

LanteRn positions itself within a more recent development in 'thinking with images': latent visual reasoning. This paradigm aims to internalize perceptual processes by enabling models to maintain and manipulate continuous visual representations in a latent space throughout the reasoning process. By interleaving these latent visual states with textual reasoning, these methods seek to preserve visual structure and operate on abstract visual representations, thereby circumventing the computational inefficiencies associated with explicit image generation in pixel space. LanteRn extends this concept by formulating latent visual reasoning as an interleaved process, allowing for iterative refinement of internal visual representations and a more integrated coupling between perception and language within a transformer architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, LanteRn, that enhances the visual reasoning capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by enabling them to process visual information directly within a compact latent space, interleaved with language. The core motivation stems from identified limitations in existing LMMs and alternative 'thinking with images' approaches.

Current LMMs predominantly rely on verbalizing perceptual content into text. This "thinking about images" paradigm necessitates compressing rich visual data into a lower-bandwidth symbolic format, which often proves inadequate for tasks requiring precise spatial understanding, fine-grained visual details, or complex relational reasoning. This limitation leads to suboptimal performance on perception-heavy benchmarks where purely textual chains of thought fail to capture the nuances of visual and spatial structure.

The paper highlights specific shortcomings in current 'thinking with images' methods:
- **Tool-based visual reasoning:** These approaches involve invoking external vision modules (e.g., object detection, image generation tools). While providing functionality, they are restricted by a predefined set of tools and typically involve significant computational overhead due to the repeated invocation of external processes. This external dependency means the model does not internalize the visual abstraction itself.
- **Explicit image generation:** Methods that generate intermediate images in pixel space during the reasoning chain consume substantial computational resources. This is often inefficient because the generation of photorealistic details may be irrelevant for the reasoning task, leading to unnecessary computation. The reasoning process operates on high-dimensional pixel data rather than more abstract or task-relevant representations.

LanteRn aims to overcome these limitations by introducing a mechanism for LMMs to interleave language with compact latent visual representations. This allows visual reasoning to occur directly in the model's internal latent space, which is hypothesized to be more computationally efficient and capable of preserving necessary visual structure without the overhead of pixel-space operations or external tool calls. The specific objectives include:
1. **Augmenting LMMs for latent visual "thought":** To enable a vision-language transformer to generate and attend to continuous visual "thought" embeddings during inference. This involves extending the model's architecture to support a 'visual latent mode' alongside its standard 'text mode'.
2. **Grounding latent states in visual features:** To establish a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage that explicitly grounds these generated latent visual states in the outputs of the model’s vision encoder. This addresses the challenge of providing ground truth for abstract internal visual thoughts by using the model's own perceptual capabilities as a teacher signal.
3. **Aligning latent reasoning with task-level utility:** To implement a reinforcement learning (RL) stage that optimizes both textual and latent reasoning as a sequential decision-making process. The motivation here is to move beyond mere representational fidelity (achieved in SFT) and encourage the model to generate latent states that directly improve final answer correctness and task-level utility, even in the absence of explicit region-level supervision.

By pursuing these objectives, the research seeks to demonstrate that internal latent representations can provide a more efficient and effective pathway for multimodal reasoning, potentially offering a compute-efficient alternative to continuously scaling model size for perception-centric tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LanteRn is a framework designed to enable LMMs to interleave language with compact latent visual representations, facilitating visual reasoning directly in a latent space. The methodology involves augmenting a vision-language transformer and training it in a two-stage process: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Modeling Latent Visual Reasoning

The reasoning process is modeled as a hybrid trajectory where each state can be either a discrete token from the vocabulary (text mode) or a continuous latent vector (visual latent mode).
The framework builds upon the Qwen2.5-VL architecture, extended with three control tokens: `<|lvr_start|>`, `<|lvr_sep|>`, and `<|lvr_end|>`. These tokens regulate transitions between operating modes:
- **Text Mode:** The model functions as a standard autoregressive language model, predicting a probability distribution over the vocabulary.
- **Visual Latent Mode:** Upon generating `<|lvr_start|>`, for a fixed number of subsequent steps (K, a hyperparameter), the model outputs the unprojected hidden states of its final transformer layer. These K vectors form a block of latent "thought" embeddings. After K steps, an `<|lvr_end|>` token signals a return to text mode. These latent vectors serve as internal reasoning context, allowing the model to attend to its high-dimensional visual thoughts without verbalizing them.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The SFT stage aims to ground the model's internal visual thoughts in concrete visual features, effectively teaching it to "imagine" visual content in its latent space.

- **Visual Feature Extraction as Supervision:** Since human annotations for high-dimensional latent vectors are impractical, the model's pre-trained vision encoder acts as a teacher. For a training sample (image I, question Q, answer A, reasoning trace T), bounding boxes (B) corresponding to relevant visual regions in T are identified. For each region `b ∈ B`, a target latent representation `Z_target` is created by:
 1. Extracting a feature map `F_b = VisionEncoder(I, b)`.
 2. Applying an average pooling operation `Pool(F_b)` to produce a fixed-size sequence of K pooled feature vectors, `Z_target = [z^(1)_target..., z^(K)_target]`. This sequence represents an aggregated visual perception of region `b`.

- **Hybrid Objective Function:** LanteRn is trained with a multi-task objective: `L_LanteRn = L_text + γ L_latent`, where `γ` is a weighting hyperparameter.
 - **Text Generation Loss (`L_text`):** A standard cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction on non-latent tokens, maintaining verbal fluency.
 - **Latent Alignment Loss (`L_latent`):** For each block of K latent tokens generated between `<|lvr_start|>` and `<|lvr_end|>`, a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is applied. This minimizes the discrepancy between the K hidden states produced by the LLM (`H_gen = [h^(1)_gen..., h^(K)_gen]`) and the `Z_target` sequence derived from the vision encoder: `L_latent = (1/K) Σ ||h^(i)_gen - z^(i)_target||^2_2`. This encourages the model to reconstruct the visual features in its latent space.

- **Dataset Construction (SFT):** A synthetic dataset derived from Visual-CoT is used. For image-question pairs and auxiliary images, a large reasoning-oriented multimodal model (Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking) is prompted to generate structured reasoning traces comprising:
 1. Pre-visual thought (textual plan).
 2. Visual grounding (bounding boxes matching ground-truth ROIs).
 3. Post-visual thought (textual deduction from ROIs).
 Bounding boxes are used *only* to extract target feature representations for `L_latent` and are never exposed to the model during training.

- **Training Configuration (SFT):** Models are initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct. Variants `LantErn-SFT-{K}` are trained with `K ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32}` latent tokens. `γ` is set to 0.1, and the vision encoder is frozen. Baselines include the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B and `LantErn-NTP` (a text-only variant that uses control tokens but processes intermediate reasoning as discrete text, without latent loss).

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The RL stage refines latent representations to align with task-level utility, moving beyond mere representational fidelity.

- **Formulation as Sequential Decision-Making:** Latent visual reasoning is framed as a sequential decision-making problem.
- **Policy Optimization:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a PPO-style method, is adopted. The optimization objective is applied to the likelihood of discrete text tokens, with gradients propagating through the continuous latent states via standard backpropagation. This implicitly encourages latent representations that improve downstream text predictions and overall task reward.
- **Latent-Aware GRPO Objective:** The GRPO objective `J(θ)` computes the expectation over sampled rollouts, incorporating an importance ratio `r_i,t(θ)` and a group-normalized advantage `A_i`. The policy `π_θ(y_i,t)` is conditioned on the input `q, I`, the dynamically generated latent states `~h_latent_i`, and previous text tokens `y_i,<t`.
- **Latent State Replay:** To stabilize training, during policy updates, the model is forced to condition on the *exact* latent vectors generated during the rollout phase. This ensures that probability ratios reflect changes in the text policy under fixed internal reasoning traces, while still allowing gradients to flow back to the parameters responsible for latent state generation.
- **Reward Design:** The total reward is a weighted sum:
 1. **Accuracy Reward (`R_acc`):** A binary reward (weight 1.0) for a correct final textual answer, serving as the primary task-oriented signal.
 2. **Format Reward (`R_fmt`):** A structural reward (weight 1.0) that encourages the explicit use of specific tags, including reasoning chain delimiters (`<think>`, `</think>`), latent reasoning delimiters (`<|lvr_start|>`, `<|lvr_end|>`), and answer delimiters (`<|answer|>`, `</answer>`). This discourages collapse to purely textual reasoning and enforces the presence of latent blocks.

- **Dataset (RL):** The VIRL-39k dataset, a diverse collection of visual reasoning problems without explicit region-level supervision, is used. This evaluates RL's ability to guide LanteRn without bounding box annotations.
- **Implementation Details (RL):** The TRL library is used, with extensions for latent state replay. Training uses `k=8` (based on SFT findings), a learning rate of `5 × 10^-6`, and a KL regularization coefficient `β = 0.1` to limit policy drift from SFT.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. SFT Results

The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage produced the following observations, as detailed in Table 1:
- **General Performance Improvement:** All LanteRn variants demonstrated improved performance over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline on the Visual-CoT benchmark (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 0.66 vs. LantErn-SFT-8: 0.81). This indicates that the SFT process effectively generalizes beyond the specific supervision data.
- **Comparison with Text-Only Baseline:** The gains from LanteRn-SFT variants on Visual-CoT were comparable to those achieved by the text-only LantErn-NTP baseline (e.g., LantErn-NTP-4: 0.80, LantErn-SFT-4: 0.80). This suggests that structured reasoning, even if verbalized (NTP) or grounded (SFT), offers benefits, but supervised latent grounding alone does not consistently yield *superior* task-level benefits over a text-based structured approach at this stage.
- **Perception-Centric Skill Enhancement:** SFT primarily improved perception-centric skills. For instance, LantErn-SFT-8 increased Blink_OL (Object Localization) performance from 0.45 (LantErn-NTP-4) to 0.52. This suggests a strengthening of object localization and perceptual grounding through latent representation learning.
- **Relational Reasoning Limitations:** Performance on relational reasoning subsets (V*_RP and Blink_RP) for SFT variants remained similar to or, in some cases, slightly worse than the LantErn-NTP baseline. This indicates that while latent states were grounded perceptually, they were not yet reliably utilized for complex relational reasoning tasks.
- **Effect of Latent Size (K):** Performance did not monotonically increase with the latent block size `K`. Larger latent blocks (e.g., `K=32`) sometimes led to performance degradation on certain benchmarks (e.g., Blink_RP dropped from 0.72 at `K=4` to 0.66 at `K=32`). This suggests a potential trade-off between latent capacity and effective reasoning, indicating that an optimal or adaptive latent capacity might be beneficial.

Overall, the SFT results demonstrated that supervised latent grounding provides a perceptual structure to the model's internal states but is, on its own, insufficient to consistently produce broad task-level improvements, particularly in complex relational reasoning. This motivated the subsequent reinforcement learning stage.

#### 4.2. RL Results

Following SFT, the application of reinforcement learning (RL) on the LantErn-SFT-8 model led to consistent performance improvements, as presented in Table 2:
- **Consistent Performance Gains:** LantErn-RL-8 consistently outperformed both the Qwen2.5-VL-3B base model and the NTP-RL variant across all evaluated benchmarks (VisCoT, V*, Blink). For instance, on VisCoT, LantErn-RL-8 achieved 0.83, compared to 0.66 (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and 0.82 (NTP-RL).
- **Enhanced Out-of-Distribution Performance:** The most significant gains were observed on perception-heavy benchmarks, particularly for out-of-distribution tasks.
- **Improved Relational Reasoning:** Notably, performance on Blink_RP (Relational Position) improved substantially from 0.68 (SFT) to 0.81 (RL). Similar improvements were seen on V*_RP (0.57 for NTP-RL to 0.67 for LantErn-RL-8) and Blink_OL (0.47 for NTP-RL to 0.54 for LantErn-RL-8). These results suggest that RL effectively shifted the latent representations to become more useful for complex spatial and relational reasoning, rather than merely perceptual reconstruction.
- **Task-Driven Latent Representation:** The consistent gains across benchmarks support the hypothesis that RL is instrumental in transitioning latent states from perceptually faithful reconstructions to task-driven internal visual representations. This indicates that outcome-driven optimization encouraged the model to utilize visual information more effectively for problem-solving.
- **Compute-Efficiency Potential:** The paper notes that achieving performance parity with a 7B model on several benchmarks highlights the potential of latent visual reasoning as a compute-efficient alternative to simply scaling model size for perception-centric tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LanteRn represents a contribution to the field of multimodal reasoning by proposing a framework for Latent Visual Structured Reasoning. This work directly addresses a limitation of current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), which often struggle with fine-grained visual understanding due to their reliance on verbalizing perceptual content.

The primary significance of LanteRn lies in its methodology, which enables LMMs to interleave language with compact latent visual representations. This approach offers a potential advantage over existing "thinking with images" strategies:
- **Efficiency:** By reasoning directly in a compact latent space, LanteRn aims to be more computationally efficient than methods that generate intermediate images in pixel space. It avoids the overhead of rendering photorealistic details that may be irrelevant to the task.
- **Internalization:** Unlike tool-based approaches that depend on external modules, LanteRn internalizes the visual reasoning process. This allows for a tighter coupling between perception and language and potentially enables more abstract and integrated visual "thoughts" within the model's own architecture.

The two-stage training paradigm is another key aspect. The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage provides an initial grounding for latent visual states in actual perceptual features derived from the vision encoder. Subsequently, the reinforcement learning (RL) stage refines these latent representations, aligning them with task-level utility and encouraging the generation of internal visual thoughts that directly contribute to correct answers. This progression from representational fidelity to task-driven utility is a notable finding, demonstrating how models can learn to leverage internal visual information for problem-solving in the absence of explicit region-level supervision.

The consistent performance improvements observed on perception-heavy benchmarks (VisCoT, V*, and Blink), particularly after the RL stage, suggest that LanteRn can enhance spatial, relational, and fine-grained visual reasoning capabilities. The reported achievement of parity with larger 7B models on certain benchmarks further indicates that latent visual reasoning could be a compute-efficient alternative to continually scaling model parameters for vision-centric tasks.

The potential impact of LanteRn extends to various applications requiring robust visual understanding, such as:
- **Advanced Visual Question Answering:** Enabling models to answer complex questions that demand deep spatial or relational reasoning.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Providing AI agents with more sophisticated internal visual representations to interpret and interact with their environments.
- **Accessibility Tools:** Developing more precise image description systems for visually impaired individuals.
- **Scientific Image Analysis:** Assisting researchers in analyzing complex visual data by allowing models to reason over subtle visual cues.

However, the authors also acknowledge limitations, including the current dependence on the quality and diversity of multimodal trajectories in training data, the fixed size of latent token blocks, and the need for deeper analysis and visualization of these latent representations. Addressing these areas in future work could further enhance the framework's robustness and interpretability. Additionally, the ethical implications of biases inherited from training data in multimodal models are noted, emphasizing the need for responsible development and deployment, especially in high-stakes contexts.
