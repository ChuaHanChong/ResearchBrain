# Causally-Grounded Dual-Path Attention Intervention for Object Hallucination Mitigation in LVLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China and the University of Auckland developed Owl, a causally-grounded dual-path attention intervention that employs a Structural Causal Model and the Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR) to mitigate object hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models. This method reduces sentence-level hallucination by up to 22.1% and instance-level hallucination by up to 36.7% on CHAIR benchmarks, while preserving general vision-language understanding.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently suffer from "object hallucination," generating content inconsistent with visual input.
- This issue severely compromises LVLM trustworthiness and limits their deployment in safety-critical applications.
- Prior attention-based mitigation methods often address visual or textual attention in isolation, failing to account for their complex, interactive causal relationship in driving hallucinations.

## Method

- Formalizes the object hallucination process using a ==Structural Causal Model (SCM)==, treating visual and textual attention as explicit mediators for intervention.
- Introduces the ==Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR)== to dynamically quantify the cross-modal attention balance and identify hallucination risk.
- Implements an adaptive, token- and layer-wise attention modulation within a ==dual-path contrastive decoding== strategy to enhance visual grounding and suppress textual over-reliance.

## Results

- The Owl framework achieved state-of-the-art hallucination mitigation on CHAIR benchmarks, reducing sentence-level hallucinations by up to **22.1%** and instance-level by up to **36.7%**.
- General vision-language understanding was preserved or enhanced across multiple VQA benchmarks, with LLaVA-1.5 showing a **+3.7** gain on TextVQA and **+7.6%** on VizWiz.
- Owl demonstrated robust generalization against object hallucinations on the POPE benchmark, consistently outperforming other baselines, particularly in adversarial settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object hallucination is causally linked to an imbalance in visual and textual attention, where over-reliance on textual context (linguistic priors) overrides visual evidence.
> - The Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR) provides a dynamic, layer-wise signal to identify insufficient visual grounding during token generation.
> - Intervening on visual and textual attention as explicit mediators within a Structural Causal Model allows for targeted, interpretable control over the hallucination process.
