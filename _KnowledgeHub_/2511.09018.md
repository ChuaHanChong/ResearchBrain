# Causally-Grounded Dual-Path Attention Intervention for Object Hallucination Mitigation in LVLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China and the University of Auckland developed Owl, a causally-grounded dual-path attention intervention that employs a Structural Causal Model and the Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR) to mitigate object hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models. This method reduces sentence-level hallucination by up to 22.1% and instance-level hallucination by up to 36.7% on CHAIR benchmarks, while preserving general vision-language understanding.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently suffer from "object hallucination," generating content inconsistent with visual input.
- This issue severely compromises LVLM trustworthiness and limits their deployment in safety-critical applications.
- Prior attention-based mitigation methods often address visual or textual attention in isolation, failing to account for their complex, interactive causal relationship in driving hallucinations.

## Method

- Formalizes the object hallucination process using a ==Structural Causal Model (SCM)==, treating visual and textual attention as explicit mediators for intervention.
- Introduces the ==Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR)== to dynamically quantify the cross-modal attention balance and identify hallucination risk.
- Implements an adaptive, token- and layer-wise attention modulation within a ==dual-path contrastive decoding== strategy to enhance visual grounding and suppress textual over-reliance.

## Results

- The Owl framework achieved state-of-the-art hallucination mitigation on CHAIR benchmarks, reducing sentence-level hallucinations by up to **22.1%** and instance-level by up to **36.7%**.
- General vision-language understanding was preserved or enhanced across multiple VQA benchmarks, with LLaVA-1.5 showing a **+3.7** gain on TextVQA and **+7.6%** on VizWiz.
- Owl demonstrated robust generalization against object hallucinations on the POPE benchmark, consistently outperforming other baselines, particularly in adversarial settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object hallucination is causally linked to an imbalance in visual and textual attention, where over-reliance on textual context (linguistic priors) overrides visual evidence.
> - The Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR) provides a dynamic, layer-wise signal to identify insufficient visual grounding during token generation.
> - Intervening on visual and textual attention as explicit mediators within a Structural Causal Model allows for targeted, interpretable control over the hallucination process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, enabling impressive capabilities in image-based text generation across applications such as visual question answering and open-ended image description. However, a persistent and critical challenge facing these models is "object hallucination," where LVLMs generate content (e.g., mention objects) that is inconsistent with the visual input. This issue severely undermines the trustworthiness of LVLMs and poses significant risks in safety-critical domains like medical imaging and robotic navigation, making its mitigation a crucial research area.

Existing approaches to combat object hallucination generally fall into three categories:
1. **Human Preference Alignment:** Methods like reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or instruction tuning (e.g., LLaVA-RLHF) aim to align model outputs with human preferences. While these improve consistency and fluency, they often require costly annotations and offer limited interpretability into the root causes of hallucinations.
2. **Post-processing Strategies:** Approaches such as LURE, CGD, and Woodpecker detect or revise hallucinated entities *after* generation using external modules or confidence scoring. These methods offer flexibility but rely on external cues and do not address the fundamental generative process leading to hallucinations.
3. **Decoding Optimizations:** More recent efforts focus on proactively steering generation to reduce hallucinations. This includes perturbing visual inputs (e.g., VCD) or manipulating attention weights within the decoder to either boost visual grounding (e.g., PAI) or suppress over-reliance on previous textual tokens (e.g., OPERA).

This paper, "Causally-Grounded Dual-Path Attention Intervention for Object Hallucination Mitigation in LVLMs," positions itself within the decoding optimization paradigm, specifically focusing on attention manipulation. The authors identify a crucial gap in existing attention-based methods: they often regulate visual *or* textual attention independently, neglecting the intricate *interaction* between these two modalities as key causal factors for hallucination. This work aims to bridge this gap by explicitly modeling and intervening on the causal interplay of visual and textual attention.

Furthermore, the paper leverages the growing interest in **causal inference** within AI systems, particularly in LVLMs, to enhance interpretability and robustness. While prior work (e.g., Huang et al. 2024a, CausalMM) has used causal analysis to probe hallucination triggers by intervening on inputs or latent features, these often rely on coarse-grained manipulations or treat attention as a "black box." This research distinguishes itself by constructing a Structural Causal Model (SCM) where visual and textual attention are treated as *explicit mediators*. This allows for more interpretable, fine-grained mediator-based interventions that directly adjust internal attention weights without altering the input, thereby offering a deeper understanding of how modality interactions drive hallucinations. By focusing on the causal roots of the problem, the paper contributes to developing more robust, explainable, and trustworthy multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent and critical problem of object hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). While LVLMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities, their tendency to generate content inconsistent with visual inputs compromises their reliability and limits their deployment in sensitive applications.

The authors identify a specific lacuna in existing hallucination mitigation techniques, particularly those based on attention manipulation during decoding. They observe that prior methods tend to treat visual and textual attention pathways as independent entities, modulating one or the other in isolation. This overlooks the complex and interactive relationship between these two modalities, which the authors hypothesize is central to the formation of hallucinations.

Through initial empirical analysis, the authors uncover key insights that motivate their approach:
- **Trade-off between Visual Grounding and Output Length:** Solely enhancing visual attention consistently reduces hallucinations but often leads to shorter, potentially less informative outputs. Conversely, increasing textual attention, which encompasses autoregressive history and query text, tends to expand output length but simultaneously aggravates hallucinations. This indicates a delicate balance is required rather than uni-directional interventions.
- **Modality Imbalance in Hallucinated Tokens:** The authors introduce a novel metric, the **Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR)**, to quantify the relative contribution of visual versus textual signals for a token during generation. Their analysis reveals that hallucinated tokens frequently exhibit lower VTACR values, signifying an over-reliance on textual modality and a weakened visual grounding. This imbalance suggests that textual priors often dominate, overriding visual evidence and leading to unfaithful generations.

Based on these motivations and observations, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To develop a causally-grounded framework:** Formalize the hallucination process using a Structural Causal Model (SCM), explicitly treating visual and textual attention as mediators. This aims to provide a more interpretable understanding of how modality interactions contribute to hallucinations.
2. **To introduce a novel metric for cross-modal reliance:** Define and leverage VTACR to quantify the dynamic balance between visual and textual attention during generation, identifying moments of potential hallucination risk.
3. **To design an adaptive, fine-grained attention intervention mechanism:** Implement a token- and layer-wise attention modulation strategy guided by real-time VTACR signals. This allows for dynamic adjustment of attention weights, prioritizing visual or textual cues as needed, rather than applying fixed or uniform scaling.
4. **To propose a dual-path contrastive decoding strategy:** Create two distinct decoding paths—a "vision-favored" path that reinforces visual grounding and a "text-favored" path that amplifies textual reliance (and thus potential hallucination). By contrasting the logits from these two paths, the model can effectively suppress hallucinated content while preserving faithful generations.
5. **To achieve state-of-the-art hallucination mitigation:** Empirically demonstrate that the proposed framework, named "Owl" (Bi-modal attention reWeighting for Layer-wise hallucination mitigation), significantly reduces object hallucinations across various LVLM backbones and benchmarks, while simultaneously preserving or enhancing general vision-language understanding capabilities.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond superficial fixes by targeting the causal roots of hallucination through an intelligent, adaptive, and dual-modality attention intervention strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named **Owl** (Bi-modal attention reWeighting for Layer-wise hallucination mitigation), addresses object hallucination in LVLMs by adopting a causally-grounded, adaptive, and dual-path approach.

#### 3.1. Causal Modeling of Object Hallucination Process

The methodology begins by formalizing the hallucination process within a **Structural Causal Model (SCM)**, as illustrated in Figure 2.
- **Nodes:** The SCM includes image input (X_V), text input (X_T), unobservable visual and language priors (P_V, P_T), visual attention (A_V), textual attention (A_T), and the final language output (Y_T).
- **Causal Paths:** Hallucinations are hypothesized to arise from unbalanced attention influenced by modality-specific inputs and priors (e.g., X_V → A_V → Y_T, P_V → A_V → Y_T, X_T → A_T → Y_T, P_T → A_T → Y_T).
- **Intervention:** Crucially, A_V and A_T are identified as mediators through which priors influence the output. The core idea is to apply **soft interventions** (do(A_V = A*_V), do(A_T = A*_T)) directly on these attention weights, allowing for a fine-grained, interpretable manipulation that isolates the effect of attention from upstream biases.
- **Evaluation:** The **Total Causal Effect (TCE)** is used to quantify the impact of these interventions on hallucination behavior, measured by benchmarks like CHAIR. The authors demonstrate that increasing visual attention reduces hallucinations (positive TCE from A_V), while increasing textual attention exacerbates them (negative TCE from A_T). This validates their role as causal mediators.

#### 3.2. Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR)

To guide the causal interventions, a novel metric, **VTACR**, is introduced. This metric quantifies the relative contribution of visual versus textual signals to the current token being generated.
- **Decomposition:** The language decoder's attention is decomposed into `Visual Token Attention Contribution (ν^(ℓ))` and `Text Token Attention Contribution (τ^(ℓ))` for each layer `ℓ`. These are calculated as the average attention weights from the current token to visual prefix tokens and text prefix tokens (query + history), respectively, across all attention heads.
- **Ratio:** `VTACR^(ℓ) = ν^(ℓ) / τ^(ℓ)`. A low VTACR indicates that the model is over-relying on textual context and potentially neglecting visual grounding, a scenario frequently observed during hallucination.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Attention Modulation

The VTACR metric drives an adaptive, token- and layer-wise attention re-weighting mechanism.
- **Layer-wise Baseline:** A base score `V_b^(ℓ)` is established for each decoder layer `ℓ` by analyzing VTACR distributions from a dataset of hallucinated samples.
- **Dynamic Adjustment:** During generation, for each token and layer, the current VTACR^(ℓ) is computed. If `VTACR^(ℓ) < V_b^(ℓ)`, it signals a risk of hallucination due to insufficient visual grounding. In such cases, specific modulation coefficients `α` and `β` are dynamically adjusted (Equations 9-11), where `α` enhances visual attention and `β` attenuates textual attention. This ensures interventions are applied only when necessary, preventing overcorrection.

#### 3.4. Dual-Path Attention Intervention and Contrastive Decoding

Building on the adaptive modulation, Owl creates two distinct, causally-informed decoding paths:
- **Visual-Favored Path:** This path amplifies visual grounding by increasing attention to visual tokens (A_V) and attenuating attention to textual tokens (A_T) using the adaptively calculated coefficients `α` and `β` (Equations 12-13). This encourages the model to rely more heavily on image evidence.
- **Text-Favored Path:** Conversely, this path downplays visual grounding and enhances textual reliance, effectively simulating conditions that promote hallucinations. It decreases visual attention (A_V) and increases textual attention (A_T) (Equations 14-15).

These two paths generate distinct logit distributions reflecting divergent modality dependencies. A **Dual-path Contrastive Decoding (DCD)** strategy then fuses these logits:
- **Contrastive Fusion:** The final token probability `P_DCD` is computed using a softmax function that combines the log probabilities from the visual-favored path (amplified by `(1 + λ)`) and the text-favored path (suppressed by `-λ`) (Equation 16). The contrastive strength `λ` is a tunable hyperparameter.
- **Mechanism:** By contrasting these two paths, DCD amplifies the distinction between faithful and hallucination-prone tokens. The strong suppression of the text-favored (hallucination-amplifying) path effectively mitigates hallucinations, while the visual-favored path ensures grounded predictions.

#### 3.5. Overall Framework (Figure 3)

The entire process is integrated: given an image, text, and generation history, Owl performs layer-wise attention decomposition. VTACR guides the adaptive modulation of visual and textual attention. This leads to the creation of vision-favored and text-favored decoding paths. Finally, DCD strategy combines the outputs of these paths to suppress hallucinations and produce a faithful, high-quality generation. This comprehensive methodology ensures a causally informed, dynamically adaptive, and robust approach to hallucination mitigation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the Owl framework was rigorously evaluated across several benchmarks and LVLM backbones, demonstrating significant improvements in hallucination mitigation while preserving core vision-language understanding.

#### 4.1. Results on CHAIR Hallucination Evaluation (Table 1)

Owl consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance in reducing hallucinations across three representative LVLM backbones: LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, and Shikra.
- **Significant Reduction:** Compared to PAI, the strongest prior method, Owl showed substantial reductions in both sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S, C_S) and instance-level hallucination (CHAIR_I, C_I). For LLaVA-1.5, C_S reduced by 17.6% and C_I by 21.4%. For MiniGPT-4, C_S reduced by 14.5% and C_I by an impressive 36.7%. For Shikra, C_S reduced by 22.1% and C_I by 24.8%. The abstract highlights a 22.9% improvement on CHAIR overall.
- **Preserved Length:** Importantly, these reductions were achieved while preserving or even improving the average length of generated text (Len↑). This indicates that the intervention effectively suppresses hallucinations without truncating informative content or compromising output richness, a common trade-off in other methods.
- **Effectiveness of Dual-Path Strategy:** The superior performance is attributed to Owl's adaptive, VTACR-guided dual-path decoding strategy, which dynamically adjusts attention based on token-level modality contributions, unlike static or uni-modal interventions.

#### 4.2. Results on POPE Generalization Benchmark (Table 2)

On the POPE benchmark, which evaluates object hallucination via binary QA-style probing in Random, Popular, and Adversarial settings, Owl demonstrated robust generalization.
- **Consistent Outperformance:** Owl consistently outperformed classic decoding strategies (Beam Search, Greedy, Nucleus) and competitive hallucination mitigation baselines (VCD, OPERA, CausalMM) across most settings and LVLM backbones.
- **Robustness in Adversarial Scenarios:** The strongest gains were observed in adversarial scenarios, highlighting Owl's ability to maintain performance even when linguistic priors strongly conflict with visual evidence.
- **Competitive with PAI:** While slightly trailing PAI on MiniGPT-4 and LLaVA-1.5 under the Popular setting (which prioritizes high-frequency objects), Owl delivered the highest accuracy for Shikra across all three settings.

#### 4.3. Results on Vision-Language Understanding Ability (Figure 5)

To assess potential negative impacts on general model capabilities, Owl's performance was evaluated on five VQA benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, VizWiz, ScienceQA-IMG, and TextVQA).
- **Maintained or Enhanced Performance:** Owl achieved consistent performance with the base LVLMs and, in several instances, even outperformed them. For LLaVA-1.5, a notable gain of +3.7 was observed on TextVQA, and a 7.6% increase on VizWiz (from 48.8 to 52.5), suggesting improved utilization of visual cues in challenging conditions.
- **Minimal Trade-offs:** Only marginal decreases were observed in some cases (e.g., VQAv2 for LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4), indicating that the hallucination mitigation strategy does not significantly compromise core vision-language understanding capabilities. These results confirm that Owl strikes an effective balance between reducing hallucinations and preserving overall reasoning.

#### 4.4. Human-like GPT-4V Assisted Hallucination Evaluation (Figure 9)

Using GPT-4V for comprehensive evaluation of "Correctness" (less hallucination) and "Detailedness" (richness of content), Owl showed significant improvements.
- **Improved Correctness:** For LLaVA-1.5, Correctness increased by 20.1% (from 5.58 to 6.70). MiniGPT-4 saw an 11.3% increase (from 5.75 to 6.40), and Shikra a 9.1% increase (from 5.50 to 6.00).
- **Maintained Detailedness:** Detailedness was maintained or slightly enhanced across all backbones, with LLaVA-1.5 showing an 11.3% increase. This reiterates that Owl effectively reduces hallucinations without sacrificing the richness of the generated descriptions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study (Figure 6)

An ablation study on LLaVA-1.5 investigated the impact of the key hyperparameters: `α` (visual attention coefficient), `β` (textual attention coefficient), and `λ` (contrastive decoding strength).
- **`α` (visual attention):** Increasing `α` reduced hallucinations but overly large values decreased F1 scores, indicating a trade-off.
- **`β` (textual attention):** Increasing `β` steadily reduced hallucinations with minimal F1 drop, suggesting effective counteraction of language priors.
- **`λ` (contrastive strength):** Moderate values (0.1–0.4) yielded stable improvements, while excessive `λ` harmed both CHAIR and F1, highlighting the need for careful tuning.
The study confirmed the distinct yet complementary roles of these parameters in achieving a balanced trade-off between hallucination suppression and semantic completeness.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Analysis (Figures 7 & 8)

Qualitative examples provided further insights:
- **Token Logits (Figure 7):** Visualizations showed that Owl consistently suppressed logit probabilities for hallucinated tokens (e.g., "football," "cow," "salad") and elevated those for visually grounded, faithful tokens (e.g., empty string, "Ford," "sheep," "ice cream"), demonstrating its ability to reweight token importance during decoding.
- **POPE Examples (Figure 8):** In diverse scenes, Owl reliably avoided generating false positives for non-existent objects (e.g., "traffic light," "bowl," "bag"), showcasing its robustness against strong language priors that often mislead base models.

In summary, the experimental results unequivocally establish Owl as a highly effective and robust framework for mitigating object hallucination in LVLMs. Its causally-grounded, adaptive, and dual-path approach achieves significant SOTA hallucination reduction without compromising the models' general vision-language understanding or output richness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Causally-Grounded Dual-Path Attention Intervention for Object Hallucination Mitigation in LVLMs" paper makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact across both theoretical research and practical applications of AI.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Causally-Grounded Framework:** The most profound theoretical contribution is the explicit modeling of the hallucination process using a Structural Causal Model (SCM). By treating visual and textual attention as direct mediators, the authors move beyond correlation to infer causation, providing a more interpretable understanding of *why* and *how* hallucinations occur in LVLMs. This SCM-based approach offers a novel lens for analyzing complex multimodal interactions.
- **Novel Metric (VTACR):** The introduction of the Visual-to-Textual Attention Contribution Ratio (VTACR) is a key innovation. This metric provides a fine-grained, dynamic, and interpretable signal to quantify the cross-modal reliance at each token and layer during generation. VTACR serves as a diagnostic tool, revealing the underlying attention imbalance that precedes hallucination, and as a guiding signal for targeted interventions.
- **Mediator-Based Intervention:** Unlike previous causal studies that often intervene on inputs or latent features, this work demonstrates the power of mediator-based interventions directly on attention weights. This approach allows for precise manipulation of the causal paths without altering the original inputs, offering a more controlled and insightful way to study and influence model behavior.

#### 5.2. Practical Significance

- **State-of-the-Art Hallucination Mitigation:** The proposed Owl framework achieves significant reductions in object hallucination rates across multiple benchmark datasets (POPE, CHAIR) and diverse LVLM backbones (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, Shikra). It consistently outperforms existing methods, setting a new state-of-the-art in faithfulness.
- **Preserved General Capabilities:** Crucially, Owl accomplishes this without sacrificing or even enhancing the models' general vision-language understanding abilities, as demonstrated on various VQA benchmarks and GPT-4V assisted evaluations. This addresses a common trade-off in hallucination mitigation, making the solution practical for real-world deployment.
- **Adaptive and Fine-grained Intervention:** The dynamic, token- and layer-wise attention modulation guided by VTACR is a practical advantage. It allows interventions to be applied precisely when and where they are most needed, preventing overcorrection and enabling a more nuanced control over the generation process compared to static or uniform scaling approaches.
- **Training-Free Approach:** The fact that Owl is a training-free decoding optimization method makes it highly adaptable and easy to integrate into existing LVLMs without requiring extensive retraining, which is a significant practical benefit given the computational cost of training large models.

#### 5.3. Potential Broader Impact

- **Increased Trustworthiness and Reliability:** By significantly mitigating object hallucinations, Owl enhances the trustworthiness and reliability of LVLMs. This is paramount for deploying these powerful models in sensitive and safety-critical domains such as medical diagnosis (preventing false medical findings), autonomous navigation (accurate environmental perception), legal applications, and educational tools.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** The causal framework and the VTACR metric provide valuable tools for interpreting the internal workings of LVLMs. Researchers and developers can use these to diagnose why and when a model might hallucinate, leading to more transparent and explainable AI systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for research into causal control in multimodal generation. The SCM-based approach can be extended to analyze and mitigate other forms of biases, factual inaccuracies, or undesirable behaviors in LVLMs, fostering the development of more robust and ethical AI.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** More faithful and grounded LVLM outputs lead to better human-AI interaction, as users can rely more confidently on the generated descriptions and responses, reducing frustration and potential misuse stemming from erroneous information.

In conclusion, Owl represents a substantial leap forward in addressing a fundamental limitation of LVLMs. By rigorously grounding its approach in causal inference and providing an adaptive, fine-grained intervention mechanism, this research not only delivers a highly effective practical solution but also enriches the theoretical understanding of multimodal AI systems, paving the way for more reliable, interpretable, and impactful applications.
