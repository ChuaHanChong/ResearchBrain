# Learning Sim-to-Real Humanoid Locomotion in 15 Minutes

> [!summary] Summary
> A methodology from Amazon FAR, featuring efficient off-policy reinforcement learning and massively parallel simulation, enables training robust sim-to-real humanoid locomotion policies in just 15 minutes. This approach demonstrates successful deployment on real Unitree G1 hardware and yields policies for full-body locomotion and complex motion tracking using minimalist reward functions.

## Problem

- Sim-to-real transfer for complex humanoid robots demands extensive domain randomization and complex reward shaping, often leading to prohibitively slow training times.
- The iterative development cycles for real-world robotic policies become impractical and expensive when each training iteration takes hours or days.
- On-policy RL algorithms like PPO, while commonly used, exhibit sample inefficiency that contributes to lengthy training for high-dimensional humanoid tasks.

## Method

- A "simple and practical recipe" that integrates efficient off-policy ==reinforcement learning== algorithms (FastSAC, FastTD3) with massively parallel simulation.
- Carefully tuned design choices include joint-limit-aware action bounds, observation and layer normalization, and refined critic and exploration hyperparameters.
- A minimalist reward design philosophy uses fewer than 10 essential terms, supported by curriculum learning and symmetry augmentation, to simplify policy optimization.

## Results

- Humanoid locomotion policies for Unitree G1 and Booster T1 robots are trained in 15 minutes on a single RTX 4090 GPU, showing substantial speed improvements over PPO.
- The learned policies demonstrate robust locomotion on rough terrain and under consistent external push perturbations, as well as effective whole-body motion tracking for complex sequences like dance.
- Successfully deployed policies on real Unitree G1 hardware exhibit robust locomotion and complex whole-body motions (e.g., 2-minute dance) for extended durations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy RL algorithms, when properly scaled and tuned, can achieve significantly faster wall-clock training times than on-policy methods for robust, high-dimensional humanoid control.
> - Robust and natural humanoid behaviors can emerge from minimalist reward functions, challenging the need for complex reward shaping and streamlining policy development.
> - Critical design elements like layer normalization, specific critic configurations (e.g., average of Q-values), and refined exploration strategies (e.g., auto-tuning alpha) are essential for stability and performance in complex humanoid tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the intersection of reinforcement learning, robotics, and high-performance computing, specifically addressing the persistent challenge of "sim-to-real" transfer for complex humanoid robots.

The broader research landscape has seen a dramatic acceleration in RL training thanks to **massively parallel simulation frameworks** like NVIDIA's Isaac Gym (Makoviychuk et al., 2021) and MuJoCo Playground (Zakka et al., 2025). These GPU-accelerated simulators have enabled scaling environment throughput to thousands, reducing training wall-clock time for various robotic tasks from days to minutes. This advancement has been crucial for making RL practical for complex control problems.

However, a significant **gap** persists when it comes to high-dimensional and complex systems like **humanoid robots**. Achieving robust sim-to-real transfer for humanoids typically demands:
1. **Extensive Domain Randomization**: To bridge the reality gap, simulations must encompass a wide range of randomized dynamics (mass, friction, PD gains), terrain properties, and external perturbations (pushes). This complexity often hinders exploration and reduces sample efficiency.
2. **Complex Reward Shaping**: Traditional humanoid locomotion and whole-body control often rely on intricate reward functions with dozens of terms to guide learning, making hyperparameter tuning difficult and policies brittle.
3. **Iterative Development**: Sim-to-real deployment is an inherently iterative process. Policies trained in simulation are deployed, reveal real-world mismatches, requiring simulation adjustments and *retraining the entire pipeline*. This iterative cycle becomes prohibitively expensive if training takes hours or days.

Historically, **on-policy RL algorithms**, particularly Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017), have been the de-facto standard for sim-to-real robotics due to their perceived ease of scaling with parallel environments. While PPO has achieved impressive results, its sample inefficiency (discarding interaction data after each policy update) can still lead to lengthy training times for highly complex tasks, especially when strong domain randomization is employed.

This work specifically addresses the **bottleneck of slow iteration cycles for humanoids** by demonstrating that **off-policy RL algorithms** can be scaled effectively to achieve rapid, robust sim-to-real transfer for full-body humanoid control. Recent research (including previous work by Seo et al., 2025) has begun to challenge PPO's dominance by showing that off-policy methods can also scale effectively and be faster in various benchmark tasks by re-using data. This paper extends this line of inquiry, specifically targeting the most challenging aspect: full-body humanoid control, including complex locomotion and whole-body motion tracking. It provides a simple, practical recipe that makes minutes-level training for humanoids a reality, moving beyond simpler humanoid control tasks or subsets of joints that previous off-policy work addressed. This pushes the frontier towards more practical and agile development of advanced humanoid capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly reduce the wall-clock training time for robust sim-to-real humanoid control policies, bringing it down from hours or days to just minutes. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Accelerating Sim-to-Real Iteration:** The development of robot control policies for real-world deployment is inherently iterative. Discrepancies between simulation and reality necessitate repeated cycles of policy training, hardware deployment, and simulation refinement. If each training cycle takes hours, this process becomes prohibitively slow and expensive. The authors aim to make these iterative cycles practical and fast, thus speeding up the overall development of robust robot behaviors.
2. **Overcoming Humanoid Complexity Challenges:** Humanoid robots are high-dimensional systems, making their control particularly challenging. Achieving robust transfer often requires extensive domain randomization (randomizing dynamics, terrain, and applying perturbations) and careful reward shaping. These factors typically complicate exploration and reduce sample efficiency, pushing training times back into the multi-hour regime even with parallel simulators. The work aims to overcome these specific challenges for humanoids.
3. **Leveraging Off-Policy RL at Scale:** While massively parallel simulation has predominantly been paired with on-policy algorithms like PPO, recent work suggests off-policy algorithms can be more sample efficient by reusing data. The motivation is to demonstrate that specifically tuned off-policy RL algorithms (FastSAC and FastTD3) can not only scale effectively but also outperform on-policy methods in wall-clock time for complex, full-body humanoid tasks, an area where they have previously shown limitations (e.g., controlling only a subset of joints).
4. **Simplifying Reward Design:** Traditional humanoid control often relies on complex, hand-engineered reward functions with numerous terms. This complexity makes hyperparameter tuning difficult and often leads to brittle policies. A key motivation is to demonstrate that robust and natural behaviors can emerge from "substantially simpler objectives" (minimalist reward functions), streamlining the development process and improving generalizability.
5. **Achieving Full-Body Humanoid Control:** The research specifically targets full-body control for humanoids, encompassing both general locomotion (velocity tracking with all joints) and complex whole-body motion tracking (e.g., dance sequences), which are more challenging than controlling only a subset of joints.

In essence, the authors are motivated to provide a "simple and practical recipe" that bridges the gap between the theoretical promise of high-throughput simulation and the practical demands of rapid, robust sim-to-real learning for full-body humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is a "simple and practical recipe" that combines efficient off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms with massively parallel simulation and carefully tuned design choices, all underpinned by a minimalist reward function philosophy.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning Algorithms: FastSAC and FastTD3

The recipe is built upon FastSAC and FastTD3 (Seo et al., 2025), which are efficient, scaled variants of Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and TD3, respectively. These off-policy algorithms are chosen over the standard PPO due to their ability to reuse data from the replay buffer, making them more sample-efficient and potentially faster in wall-clock time when simulation speed is a bottleneck. FastSAC, with its maximum entropy learning scheme, is particularly highlighted for its effective exploration capabilities under strong domain randomization.

#### 3.2. Scaling Up Off-Policy RL with Massively Parallel Simulation

The approach leverages massively parallel simulation (similar to frameworks like Isaac Gym) to achieve high environment throughput. Key scaling observations from prior work are reinforced:
- **Large Batch Sizes:** The use of large batch sizes (up to 8K) consistently improves performance.
- **Gradient Steps per Simulation Step:** Taking more gradient steps per simulation step generally leads to faster training, capitalizing on the data efficiency of off-policy methods.
- **Hardware:** Locomotion experiments are conducted on a single RTX 4090 GPU, while more challenging whole-body tracking tasks utilize 4xL40s GPUs and 16,384 parallel environments, demonstrating scalability across different computational budgets.

#### 3.3. Key Design Choices and Hyperparameter Tuning (The "Recipe")

The success of the recipe relies on several crucial algorithmic and architectural refinements:
- **Joint-Limit-Aware Action Bounds:** To stabilize training, especially with Tanh policies, action bounds are dynamically set based on the robot's joint limits, simplifying tuning.
- **Normalization Techniques:**
 - **Observation Normalization:** Found to be generally helpful.
 - **Layer Normalization (Ba et al., 2016):** Crucially stabilizes performance in high-dimensional tasks, aligning with recent findings for SAC.
- **Critic Learning Hyperparameters:**
 - **Average of Q-values:** Instead of Clipped Double Q-learning (CDQ) which uses the minimum Q-value, taking the average is found to improve performance, especially when combined with layer normalization.
 - **Discount Factor (γ):** Tuned based on task complexity – 0.97 for simpler locomotion tasks, 0.99 for challenging whole-body tracking tasks.
 - **Distributional Critic:** C51 (Bellemare et al., 2017) is used for its benefits, but quantile regression is avoided due to its computational expense with large batch sizes.
- **FastSAC Exploration Hyperparameters:**
 - **Standard Deviation (σ) Bounding:** Maximum σ is set to 1.0 (instead of e^2) to prevent excessive exploration instability.
 - **Initial Alpha and Auto-tuning:** Initializing the temperature α with a low value (0.001) and using auto-tuning for maximum entropy learning consistently outperforms fixed alpha values.
 - **Target Entropy:** Set to 0.0 for locomotion and -|A|/2 for whole-body tracking.
- **FastTD3 Exploration Hyperparameters:** Mixed noise schedule with low standard deviation range [0.01, 0.05] for Gaussian noise.
- **Optimization Hyperparameters:**
 - **Adam Optimizer:** Learning rate of 0.0003.
 - **Weight Decay:** Reduced to 0.001 from 0.1, as 0.1 was too strong for high-dimensional control.
 - **Beta2 (β2) for Adam:** Set to 0.95 (instead of 0.99) for improved stability with large batch sizes.

#### 3.4. Simple Reward Design Philosophy

A core tenet of the recipe is a minimalist reward design, diverging from traditional complex reward shaping:
- **Philosophy:** Only essential terms are added (typically fewer than 10), aiming for cross-algorithm and cross-robot applicability. This simplifies hyperparameter tuning and avoids brittle policy optimization.
- **Locomotion Rewards:**
 - Linear and angular velocity tracking.
 - Simple foot-height tracking for swing motion.
 - Default-pose penalty to avoid extreme joint configurations.
 - Feet penalties (parallel orientation, no crossing).
 - Per-step alive reward.
 - Torso upright orientation penalty.
 - Action rate penalty for smooth control.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Penalties are subject to a curriculum, with weights ramping up as episode length increases, aiding exploration.
 - **Symmetry Augmentation:** Used to encourage symmetric walking patterns.
- **Whole-Body Tracking Rewards:** Follows the minimalist structure of BeyondMimic (Liao et al., 2025), focusing on tracking goals with light regularization and DeepMimic-style termination conditions. External velocity pushes are added for robustness.

#### 3.5. Extensive Domain Randomization

To ensure robust sim-to-real transfer, the training incorporates strong domain randomization:
- **Dynamics:** Randomization of PD-gains, mass, friction, and center of mass (for G1).
- **Environment:** Training on mixed flat and rough terrains.
- **Perturbations:** Consistent push perturbations, including "Push-Strong" (pushes every 1-3 seconds).
- **Control Latency:** Action delay is randomized.

This comprehensive yet streamlined approach enables the rapid and robust training of humanoid control policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of their "simple and practical recipe," demonstrating rapid and robust humanoid locomotion and whole-body tracking.

1. **Rapid Training of Humanoid Locomotion Policies:**
 - The most striking finding is the ability to train full-fledged humanoid locomotion policies for Unitree G1 and Booster T1 robots in **just 15 minutes** on a single RTX 4090 GPU. This dramatically reduces wall-clock training time compared to traditional methods.
 - FastSAC and FastTD3 consistently and significantly **outperform PPO** in wall-clock time across various locomotion tasks (Figure 3). This is particularly evident under strong domain randomization, such as rough terrain and "Push-Strong" perturbations (applying pushes every 1-3 seconds), where PPO struggles to converge effectively or as quickly.
 - The learned policies are robust, enabling humanoids to walk and stand in rough terrain, with randomized dynamics, and under consistent external push perturbations.

2. **Effective Whole-Body Tracking:**
 - FastSAC and FastTD3 are shown to be **competitive or superior to PPO** for whole-body motion tracking tasks, including complex dance motions, box lifting, and push recovery (Figure 5).
 - FastSAC often slightly outperforms FastTD3, especially in longer, more challenging motion sequences like the "Dance" task, which the authors attribute to its more efficient exploration mechanism (maximum entropy learning).

3. **Validation of Recipe Components:**
 - Detailed ablation studies (Figure 2) empirically validate the design choices:
 - Using the **average of Q-values** (instead of the minimum in Clipped Double Q-learning) improves performance for FastSAC.
 - Layer normalization significantly **stabilizes performance** in high-dimensional tasks.
 - Optimal **number of update steps** and **discount factor (γ)** are crucial and task-dependent (e.g., lower γ for locomotion, higher γ for WBT).
 - Scaling up the **number of environments** is particularly beneficial for challenging whole-body tracking tasks.
 - Figure 4 explicitly demonstrates the **significant improvement of the new FastSAC recipe** over previous unstable configurations, highlighting the importance of careful tuning of exploration, optimization hyperparameters, and the use of layer normalization.

4. **Sim-to-Real Deployment Success:**
 - Crucially, the policies trained with their recipe (specifically FastSAC) are successfully deployed to **real Unitree G1 humanoid hardware** (Figure 6).
 - These deployed policies demonstrate robust locomotion and the ability to complete complex whole-body motions (dance, box lifting, push), even for extended durations (e.g., a 2-minute dance motion). This confirms that the rapid training in simulation leads to genuinely deployable and robust real-world controllers.

5. **Simplicity and Generalizability:**
 - The results demonstrate that robust and natural behaviors can emerge from a **minimalist reward function** (fewer than 10 terms) without extensive, brittle reward shaping. This simplified design is shown to be effective across multiple robots (G1 and T1) and algorithms (FastSAC, FastTD3, PPO).

Overall, the main findings highlight a breakthrough in the speed and robustness of sim-to-real humanoid control, driven by carefully tuned off-policy RL algorithms and a streamlined training pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work carries significant implications for the field of robotics and reinforcement learning, particularly for humanoid control:

1. **Game-Changing Acceleration of Humanoid Development:** The most immediate and profound impact is the reduction of sim-to-real iteration time from hours or days to **just 15 minutes**. This fundamental shift makes iterative policy development for complex humanoids practical and economically feasible. Researchers and engineers can now rapidly test hypotheses, refine simulation models, and iterate on policy designs in a fraction of the time, dramatically accelerating progress in humanoid capabilities.

2. **Validation and Democratization of Off-Policy RL for Robotics:** For a long time, PPO has been the undisputed standard for sim-to-real robotic control due to its perceived ease of scaling. This paper provides strong empirical evidence that carefully tuned off-policy algorithms (FastSAC and FastTD3), when combined with massively parallel simulation, can not only scale effectively but also **outperform PPO in wall-clock efficiency** for high-dimensional, robust humanoid tasks. This re-validates the potential of off-policy methods and could lead to a broader adoption of these algorithms in real-world robotics, diversifying the algorithmic landscape. By demonstrating rapid training on a single, powerful GPU (RTX 4090 for locomotion), the work also makes advanced humanoid control research more accessible to smaller labs or individual researchers.

3. **Promotion of Minimalist Design Principles:** The success achieved with minimalist reward functions (fewer than 10 terms) challenges the conventional wisdom that complex, heavily shaped rewards are necessary for humanoid control. This finding suggests that simpler objectives, combined with robust learning algorithms and extensive domain randomization, can foster emergent and natural behaviors. This simplicity reduces development overhead, makes policies less brittle, and could lead to more generalizable solutions.

4. **Blueprint for Future Humanoid Research:** By providing a "simple and practical recipe" and an open-source implementation (Holosoma repository), the authors offer a robust baseline and "blueprint" for the research community. This will enable others to build upon this foundation, incorporating newer advances in off-policy RL, exploration, and humanoid learning to push the state-of-the-art even further, potentially leading to humanoids with unprecedented agility and versatility.

5. **Enabling More Capable and Robust Humanoids:** The policies' demonstrated robustness to strong domain randomization (rough terrain, push perturbations, dynamics variations) and successful sim-to-real deployment signify a crucial step towards developing humanoids that can reliably operate in diverse, unstructured real-world environments. This is a prerequisite for broader adoption of humanoids in logistics, manufacturing, hazardous environments, and personal assistance.

In summary, "Learning Sim-to-Real Humanoid Locomotion in 15 Minutes" represents a significant leap forward in making humanoid robot control development faster, more accessible, and more robust. Its impact will likely be felt through accelerated research cycles, a diversification of algorithmic approaches, and ultimately, the deployment of more capable and adaptable humanoid robots in real-world applications.
