# A Survey on Visual Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey from Huawei Noah's Ark Lab and The University of Sydney systematically reviews advancements in Vision Transformers, categorizing models by application and analyzing their performance across various computer vision tasks. The survey highlights their versatility and increasing competitiveness with CNNs, particularly when supported by large datasets, while also addressing their computational challenges and potential for multimodal integration.

## Problem

- The rapid increase in transformer-based vision models made it difficult for researchers to keep pace with new progress and understand the field's advancements.
- A lack of structured overview and critical analysis hindered understanding of diverse architectural adaptations, strengths, and limitations across various applications.
- The computer vision community required a consolidated resource to identify open challenges and promising future research directions in this quickly evolving domain.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic and comprehensive survey of representative Vision Transformer works, detailing their formulations and adaptations for computer vision.
- They categorized models based on distinct application scenarios, including ==backbone networks==, high/mid-level vision, low-level vision, video processing, efficient methods, and ==multi-modal tasks==.
- The survey provides critical analysis, highlighting advantages and disadvantages of different transformer models and discussing broader challenges and future prospects for the field.

## Results

- Transformer-based models are increasingly achieving similar to or better performance than CNNs across diverse visual benchmarks, particularly when trained on massive datasets.
- The survey identified that computational efficiency remains a major challenge for Transformers, spurring ongoing research into methods like pruning, knowledge distillation, and compact architectures for deployment.
- Vision Transformers are proving effective in multi-modal tasks, such as bridging vision and language, suggesting a trajectory towards unified, general-purpose intelligence agents capable of handling various data types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformers exhibit remarkable versatility, successfully adapting from NLP to a vast array of computer vision tasks, demonstrating their potential as a general-purpose architecture.
> - Pure Vision Transformers achieve competitive or superior performance to CNNs on many benchmarks, but heavily rely on large-scale datasets for pre-training to compensate for lacking inductive biases.
> - Hybrid architectures that combine CNNs for local feature extraction with Transformers for global context modeling often yield superior performance and efficiency, leveraging the strengths of both.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research landscape of artificial intelligence has been fundamentally reshaped by deep neural networks (DNNs), with specialized architectures historically dominating different domains. Multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) served as classical networks, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) became the cornerstone for processing shift-invariant data like images, and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) excelled with sequential data. This paper emerges at a pivotal moment, documenting a significant paradigm shift driven by the **Transformer architecture**.

Initially introduced in 2017 (Vaswani et al.) for natural language processing (NLP), the Transformer, based primarily on the self-attention mechanism, rapidly achieved state-of-the-art performance, leading to breakthroughs with models like BERT and GPT-3. The remarkable success in NLP, characterized by strong representation capabilities and efficient parallelization, naturally inspired researchers to explore its applicability to computer vision (CV) tasks.

This survey paper is a direct response to the "rapid increase in the number of transformer-based vision models" following pioneering works like Vision Transformer (ViT) by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020). ViT demonstrated that a pure Transformer architecture, applied directly to sequences of image patches, could achieve competitive or even superior performance to traditional CNNs on image recognition benchmarks, especially when pre-trained on large datasets. This finding challenged the long-held belief that CNN-specific inductive biases (e.g., translation equivariance, locality) were indispensable for visual tasks.

The work fits into the broader landscape by:
- **Consolidating a nascent field:** Vision Transformers (ViTs) are a relatively new but explosively growing area. This survey provides a timely and much-needed comprehensive review, categorizing the diverse applications and architectural adaptations.
- **Highlighting a technological transition:** It underscores the movement away from CNN-exclusive approaches in CV towards a more unified deep learning architecture capable of handling various data modalities.
- **Setting the stage for future research:** By analyzing existing models, their advantages, disadvantages, and open challenges, the survey serves as a critical guide for researchers seeking to contribute to this domain. It frames the debate between inductive bias and data scale, and the potential for "general-purpose intelligence agents" through multimodal transformers.
- **Emphasizing efficiency:** Given the computational intensity of Transformers, the inclusion of "efficient transformer methods" reflects the field's growing concern with deploying these powerful models in real-world, resource-constrained environments.

In essence, this survey captures a significant evolutionary step in deep learning for computer vision, marking the Transformer's ascent from an NLP innovation to a foundational architecture across various AI domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this survey is succinctly articulated by the authors: "Due to the rapid increase in the number of transformer-based vision models, keeping pace with the rate of new progress is becoming increasingly difficult. As such, a survey of the existing works is urgent and would be beneficial for the community." The explosion of research in vision transformers necessitates a structured overview to help researchers navigate the landscape, understand current advancements, and identify promising future directions.

Arising from this motivation, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **Provide a Comprehensive Overview:** To offer a detailed and extensive review of the recent advancements in vision transformers. This involves summarizing the multitude of models that have emerged across various computer vision tasks.
2. **Categorize Vision Transformer Models:** To systematically classify these models based on their application scenarios. The authors establish main categories including backbone networks, high/mid-level vision, low-level vision, and video processing, along with a dedicated section on efficient transformer methods and multi-modal tasks. This categorization aims to facilitate understanding and comparison across different sub-fields.
3. **Analyze Advantages and Disadvantages:** For each category and prominent model, the survey aims to highlight their specific strengths and weaknesses. This critical analysis helps in understanding where transformers excel and where further improvements are needed.
4. **Examine the Self-Attention Mechanism:** As the core component of transformers, the survey includes a brief look at the self-attention mechanism within the context of computer vision, recognizing its foundational role.
5. **Discuss Challenges and Future Research Directions:** A crucial objective is to identify the unresolved issues, limitations, and open problems currently facing vision transformers. Complementary to this, the authors aim to propose several promising avenues for future research, thereby guiding the community's collective efforts.
6. **Facilitate Future Research:** By organizing the current state-of-the-art and pointing out gaps, the survey implicitly aims to serve as a valuable resource that can accelerate further innovation and development in the field.

In summary, the paper is motivated by the rapid expansion of vision transformers and strives to be a definitive guide, offering structure, critical analysis, and forward-looking perspectives to the computer vision community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and comprehensive survey methodology to achieve their stated objectives. Their approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Scope and Selection Criteria:** The survey focuses on "recent advances in vision transformers," acknowledging the vast and rapidly growing nature of the field. To manage this, they primarily include "representative works (early, pioneering, novel, or inspiring works)," recognizing that a truly exhaustive list of all preprinted works (e.g., on arXiv) would be impractical within the confines of a single paper.

2. **Foundational Review:** The paper begins by establishing a strong foundation with "Section 2: Formulation of Transformer." This section meticulously describes the standard Transformer architecture, detailing its core components:
 - **Self-Attention:** Explaining the query, key, and value vectors, score calculation, normalization, and weighted value matrix generation (Eq. 1).
 - **Positional Encoding:** Addressing the sequence-invariant nature of self-attention by explaining the sine and cosine-based positional encoding (Eq. 2, 3) and its variants.
 - **Multi-Head Attention:** Describing how multiple attention heads project inputs into different representation subspaces to capture diverse relationships (Eq. 4).
 - **Other Key Concepts:** Briefly outlining the Feed-Forward Network (FFN), residual connections followed by layer normalization, and the final linear+softmax layer in the decoder. This foundational understanding is crucial before delving into vision-specific adaptations.

3. **Categorization by Application Scenario:** The main methodological approach is to categorize vision transformer models based on their "application scenarios." This structured organization (as listed in Table 1) allows for a clear and logical presentation of advancements:
 - **Backbone for Representation Learning:** Divided into supervised and self-supervised pre-training, further sub-divided into pure transformers (e.g., ViT, Swin) and transformers combined with convolutions (e.g., BoTNet, CMT).
 - **High/Mid-level Vision:** Covering generic object detection (transformer-based set prediction vs. transformer backbones), various forms of segmentation (panoptic, instance, semantic, medical image), pose estimation (hand, human), and other tasks (pedestrian detection, lane detection, scene graph, tracking, re-identification, point cloud learning).
 - **Low-level Vision:** Focusing on image generation (GAN-based, auto-regressive) and image processing (super-resolution, denoising, deraining).
 - **Video Processing:** Distinguishing between high-level (action recognition, retrieval, object detection, multi-task) and low-level (frame/video synthesis, inpainting).
 - **Multi-Modal Tasks:** Discussing models that bridge vision with other modalities, primarily natural language (e.g., CLIP, DALL-E, UniT).
 - **Efficient Transformer:** Addressing the practical challenge of computational cost through methods like pruning, decomposition, knowledge distillation, quantization, and compact architecture design.

4. **Detailed Model Descriptions and Analysis:** Within each category, the authors provide concise yet informative descriptions of representative models. They explain how the core transformer architecture is adapted (e.g., using image patches, specific attention mechanisms, encoder-decoder structures) and highlight their key innovations and performance metrics (e.g., using tables for ImageNet and COCO benchmarks). They also include diagrams to illustrate model architectures (e.g., ViT framework, DETR architecture, IPT architecture, general frameworks for image generation/processing, CLIP, compression methods).

5. **Critical Discussion and Future Outlook:** Each major section concludes with a "Discussions" subsection, offering critical insights into the current state of that specific area, identifying persistent challenges, and suggesting promising directions for further research. The "Conclusion and Discussions" section (Section 4) aggregates these insights into broader challenges (generalization, robustness, interpretability, efficiency) and overarching future prospects (effectiveness vs. efficiency, unified models, CNN vs. Transformer debate, role of massive data).

6. **Supplementary Material:** The paper refers to supplemental material for details on "General Formulation of Self-attention" (providing a connection to non-local filtering operations), "Revisiting Transformers for NLP" (giving historical context and key NLP models like BERT, GPT series), and "Self-attention for Computer Vision" (detailing early applications of self-attention as a building block in CNNs before full transformer adoption). This allows the main paper to focus on Vision Transformers while providing necessary background.

This methodical approach ensures that the survey is not merely a list of papers but a structured, analytical, and forward-looking review of the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey extensively analyzes the burgeoning field of Vision Transformers, revealing several key findings and patterns in their development and application across various computer vision tasks:

1. **Versatility and Adaptability of Transformers:** The most striking finding is the Transformer's remarkable versatility. Originating in NLP, it has been successfully adapted to a vast array of CV tasks—from fundamental representation learning (backbone networks) to complex high-level (object detection, segmentation) and low-level (image generation, enhancement) vision problems, as well as sequential video and multimodal tasks. This demonstrates its potential as a general-purpose architecture.

2. **Performance Parity and Superiority over CNNs (with caveats):** Transformer-based models are increasingly achieving "similar to or better than" CNNs across diverse visual benchmarks. For instance, ViT achieved state-of-the-art results on image recognition when pre-trained on massive datasets. However, a significant caveat is that pure transformers often "lack some inductive biases inherent to CNNs" (e.g., translation equivariance, locality) and thus "do not generalize well when trained on insufficient amounts of data." They heavily rely on "large datasets for large-scale training" to overcome this.

3. **Backbone Network Evolution:**
 - **Pure Transformers:** Models like ViT, TNT, and Swin Transformer have demonstrated the efficacy of pure self-attention for image classification. Subsequent variants have focused on improving local feature extraction (TNT, Swin, RegionViT), refining the self-attention mechanism (DeepViT, KVT), and designing more efficient architectures (pyramid-like structures, NAS).
 - **Hybrid Architectures (CNN + Transformer):** A prominent trend shows that combining CNNs (for local feature extraction) with transformers (for global context modeling) often leads to superior performance and efficiency (e.g., BoTNet, CMT, VOLO). This highlights the complementarity of the two architectures.
 - **Self-Supervised Learning:** Generative approaches (iGPT, MAE) and contrastive learning (MoCo v3) are proving highly effective for pre-training vision transformers, leveraging vast amounts of unlabeled image data. Training stability, however, remains a challenge in this area.

4. **High/Mid-Level Vision Paradigm Shifts:**
 - **Object Detection:** DETR pioneered an "end-to-end set prediction" approach, eliminating traditional components like anchor generation and NMS. Deformable DETR significantly improved convergence and small object detection. Transformers are also integrated as powerful backbones for existing detectors (e.g., Swin Transformer with RetinaNet). Pre-training strategies are crucial for enhancing detection performance.
 - **Segmentation & Pose Estimation:** Transformers have enabled direct prediction of masks (Max-DeepLab for panoptic) and effective modeling of global relationships between body parts for 3D pose estimation (METRO). They show strong results in medical image segmentation and video instance segmentation.

5. **Advancements in Low-Level Vision and Generation:**
 - **Image Generation:** TransGAN and ViTGAN demonstrate the viability of pure transformer-based GANs. Auto-regressive approaches (Image Transformer, Taming Transformer with VQ-GAN) are capable of generating high-resolution images, often by leveraging discrete latent codes. Text-to-image generation (DALL-E, CogView) showcases the multimodal capabilities.
 - **Image Processing:** The Image Processing Transformer (IPT) demonstrates the power of large-scale pre-training on diverse tasks (super-resolution, denoising, deraining), achieving significant performance gains.

6. **Strong Performance in Video Processing and Multimodality:**
 - **Video:** Transformers naturally handle the sequential nature of video data, improving tasks like action recognition, video retrieval, and spatio-temporal modeling for inpainting and synthesis.
 - **Multimodality:** CLIP (image-text for zero-shot classification) and DALL-E (text-to-image generation) exemplify how transformers can bridge different modalities, fostering a move towards unified models for complex, real-world tasks.

7. **Addressing Computational Efficiency:** Despite their power, transformers are computationally expensive. The survey highlights various strategies being employed to mitigate this, including pruning, low-rank decomposition, knowledge distillation (TinyBERT), quantization, and compact architecture design (e.g., sparse or linear attention variants). However, this remains an "open problem," particularly for deployment on resource-limited devices.

In essence, the survey concludes that transformers represent a powerful and flexible architectural family, increasingly outperforming traditional CNNs in many CV tasks, especially when coupled with large datasets or combined with convolutional inductive biases. Their ability to model long-range dependencies and unify different modalities marks a significant leap in AI capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance and has a profound potential impact on the field of computer vision and broader artificial intelligence research for several reasons:

1. **Centralized Knowledge Resource:** The most immediate impact is providing a timely, detailed, and structured compendium of the rapidly proliferating vision transformer literature. For researchers entering or specializing in this area, the paper serves as an invaluable starting point, consolidating a vast and fragmented body of work into a digestible format. This prevents redundant efforts and accelerates the learning curve for the community.

2. **Clarifying the Research Landscape:** By categorizing models based on their application scenarios and architectural nuances, the survey brings much-needed clarity to a fast-evolving domain. It helps researchers understand the different adaptations of the transformer architecture, their specific strengths in various tasks (e.g., backbone, high/mid-level, low-level vision, video, multimodal), and the trade-offs involved.

3. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The "Challenges" and "Future Prospects" sections are perhaps the most impactful part of the paper.
 - **Identified Challenges:** The authors explicitly highlight critical open problems such as the need for CV-specific transformer architectures, improving generalization and robustness (especially with smaller datasets), enhancing interpretability, and addressing the high computational cost. Addressing these challenges is crucial for the widespread adoption of vision transformers.
 - **Proposed Future Prospects:** The suggestions for future study—developing more effective and efficient transformers, working towards "grand unified models" that can handle multiple tasks and modalities, and re-evaluating the fundamental roles of inductive bias versus massive data in neural network design—lay out a compelling roadmap for the field. This can inspire new research questions, foster interdisciplinary collaborations, and drive innovation.

4. **Promoting Practical Deployment:** The dedicated section on "Efficient Transformer" methods underscores the practical significance of this research. By reviewing techniques for model compression and acceleration, the survey directly contributes to making powerful transformer models deployable on resource-limited devices, broadening their real-world impact across industries.

5. **Shaping the "CNN vs. Transformer" Debate:** The paper directly engages with the discussion about whether CNNs or Transformers are superior. It emphasizes that while CNNs excel with smaller datasets due to inherent inductive biases, Transformers can "surpass inductive bias" with large-scale training. The observation that "combining CNN and transformer achieve the better performance" is a particularly significant finding, suggesting that hybrid models might represent the optimal path forward, leveraging the best of both worlds. This nuanced perspective helps to move beyond a simplistic "either/or" mentality.

6. **Catalyst for General AI:** The success of transformers in multi-modal tasks (e.g., CLIP, DALL-E) hints at their potential to serve as foundational architectures for "general-purpose intelligence agents." The idea of "unifying all visual tasks and even other tasks in one transformer" is a bold vision that this survey helps to articulate and encourage, pushing the boundaries towards more holistic and integrated AI systems.

In essence, this survey is not just a summary of existing work; it is a critical analysis that synthesizes current knowledge, points out limitations, and strategically charts a course for the future of computer vision and beyond. Its comprehensive nature and forward-looking discussions are poised to significantly influence research priorities and accelerate the development of next-generation AI technologies.
