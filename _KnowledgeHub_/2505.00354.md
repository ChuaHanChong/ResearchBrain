# Multi-segment Soft Robot Control via Deep Koopman-based Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A Deep Koopman-based Model Predictive Control (DK-MPC) framework enables precise trajectory and moving-target tracking for multi-segment soft robots. This approach linearizes complex robot dynamics using deep learning, achieving an average tracking error of 3.11 mm on a physical soft robot, a substantial improvement over alternative Koopman methods.

## Problem

- Multi-segment soft robots exhibit high-dimensional, nonlinear, and time-varying dynamics, posing significant challenges for precise and dynamic control.
- Traditional model-based control methods often rely on simplified models that fail to accurately capture complex soft robot behavior.
- Data-driven Koopman operator approaches for linearization are limited by the difficulty of selecting appropriate lifting functions for complex real-world systems.

## Method

- A Deep ==Koopman==-based Model Predictive Control (DK-MPC) framework is introduced, transforming soft robot dynamics into a linear representation.
- Deep neural networks, specifically an auto-encoder, automatically learn the lifting functions and linear Koopman operators from robot state-action data.
- This learned linear model is then utilized within an MPC framework to compute optimal control inputs for precise trajectory tracking and constraint satisfaction.

## Results

- The DK-MPC framework achieved an average trajectory tracking error of **3.11** mm on a physical multi-segment soft robot across various complex paths.
- Performance was quantitatively superior to an RBF-based Koopman MPC (K-MPC), which exhibited an average error of **22.49** mm, demonstrating a significant improvement in control accuracy.
- The system successfully tracked and reached multiple sequential moving targets, confirming its robust dynamic control capabilities for tasks requiring adaptation to moving objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep learning, particularly auto-encoders, can effectively learn the complex lifting functions necessary for robust Koopman operator-based linearization of soft robot dynamics.
> - Transforming a soft robot's high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics into a finite-dimensional linear latent space simplifies the design and implementation of advanced control algorithms like MPC.
> - The combination of data-driven Koopman modeling and Model Predictive Control enables precise and dynamic trajectory following for real multi-segment soft robots, surpassing previous limitations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Soft robots, characterized by their inherent flexibility and compliance, offer advantages in terms of safe interaction with humans and adaptable operation in unstructured environments. These properties position them for applications in fields such as rehabilitation, minimally invasive surgery, and underwater operations. However, the development of precise and dynamic control for soft robots presents significant challenges. Their soft materials lead to high-dimensional, nonlinear, and time-varying dynamics with theoretically infinite degrees of freedom, making traditional modeling and control methods difficult to apply effectively.

Current control strategies for soft robots broadly fall into two categories: model-based and learning-based approaches. Model-based control often employs simplified representations, such as pseudo-rigid, constant curvature, or material property-based models. While these provide a structured approach, they frequently exhibit a discrepancy between the simplified model and the actual robot's behavior, which can limit precision. Learning-based control methods, leveraging deep learning, attempt to capture more nuanced dynamics. Inverse models directly map operational space to actuation space, but can struggle with the inherent variability and multiple solutions in soft robot behavior. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers potential for handling stochasticity and discovering control policies, but typically requires substantial data and faces difficulties with sim-to-real transfer.

A promising approach for addressing the complex forward modeling of soft robots involves the Koopman operator. This mathematical framework allows for the transformation of a nonlinear system into a linear representation within a higher-dimensional feature space, simplifying control design. However, a significant practical challenge in applying Koopman operator theory is the selection of appropriate "lifting functions," which map the original state space to this linearizable feature space. Inadequate choices for these functions can lead to substantial modeling errors and compromise controller performance. While Koopman operator-based methods have been explored for control design, previous similar frameworks have primarily focused on rigid robots or were limited to simulation environments, leaving a gap in real-world application to complex multi-segment soft robots. This work positions itself to bridge this gap by developing a data-driven approach to learn effective lifting functions for real soft robot systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve precise and dynamic control, specifically trajectory tracking and position reaching, for multi-segment soft robots. This goal is pursued despite the inherent challenges posed by soft robots' high-dimensional, nonlinear, time-varying nature, and infinite degrees of freedom.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing soft robot control methodologies:
- **Complexity of Soft Robot Dynamics:** Soft robots' unique material properties and segmented structures result in highly complex and coupled dynamics that are difficult to model accurately using analytical or simplified methods. This complexity hinders the design of controllers capable of high-precision tasks.
- **Inadequacy of Traditional Model-Based Control:** Traditional model-based approaches often rely on simplifying assumptions (e.g., pseudo-rigid, constant curvature models) that may not fully capture the real robot's behavior. This can lead to a performance gap and limit the precision achievable in real-world applications.
- **Challenges in Learning-Based Control:** While learning-based methods offer alternatives, they also have drawbacks. Inverse models can face issues with variability and non-unique solutions. Reinforcement learning, though capable of discovering policies, typically demands extensive data and simulation experience, leading to potential sim-to-real transfer problems when deployed on physical robots.
- **Limitation of Koopman Operator Theory:** The Koopman operator offers a promising avenue by linearizing nonlinear dynamics into a higher-dimensional space, simplifying control design. However, its effectiveness heavily relies on the appropriate selection of lifting functions, which is a non-trivial task. Suboptimal lifting functions can introduce significant modeling errors, impacting predictive accuracy and control performance. Furthermore, existing Koopman-based control frameworks have largely been demonstrated on rigid robots or in simulation, with limited application to real-world multi-segment soft robots.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this research include:
1. To develop a Deep Koopman-based Model Predictive Control (DK-MPC) framework that can effectively linearize the high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics of multi-segment soft robots into a finite-dimensional linear representation.
2. To utilize a deep learning approach to automatically learn suitable lifting functions (embeddings) for the Koopman operator from sampled robot data, thereby enhancing the model's predictive quality and overcoming the challenge of manual feature selection.
3. To integrate this learned linear dynamic model into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, allowing for real-time optimization of control inputs to achieve precise position and trajectory tracking while respecting system constraints.
4. To experimentally validate the proposed DK-MPC method on a real multi-segment soft robot, demonstrating its ability to achieve high-precision control in tasks such as path tracking and moving-target tracking, and comparing its performance against alternative Koopman-based control strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a Deep Koopman-based Model Predictive Control (DK-MPC) framework designed for multi-segment soft robots. The approach combines deep learning for system linearization with model predictive control for optimal trajectory following.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework of DK-MPC

The DK-MPC framework operates by first transforming the nonlinear dynamics of the soft robot into a linear, higher-dimensional latent space. This transformation is achieved using a deep learning-based Koopman operator. At each control step, both the robot's current state and the desired reference state are mapped into this latent space by a deep Koopman encoder. Subsequently, a Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithm utilizes this linearized model to compute optimal control inputs. The MPC minimizes a quadratic cost function defined over the latent states and reference states across a finite prediction horizon, aiming to reduce tracking error.

#### 3.2. Koopman Operator for Linearizing Soft Robot Dynamics

The system's nonlinear dynamics are described by x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k), where x_k is the state and u_k is the control input at time step k. To simplify control design, the Koopman operator is employed to transform this nonlinear system into a linear one in a lifted, higher-dimensional feature space. A lifting function, φ, maps the original state and control input to this space: φ(f(x_k, u_k), u_{k+1}) = Kφ(x_k, u_k), where K is the linear Koopman operator.

For practical implementation, the lifting function φ(x, u) is decomposed into state-dependent φ_x(x) and input-dependent φ_u(u) components, such that φ(x, u) = [φ_x(x); φ_u(u)]. Following previous simplifications, the input component is directly represented by the control input itself, i.e., φ_u(u) = u. The lifted state is denoted as z_k = φ_x(x_k). This decomposition allows the dynamics in the latent space to be written in a control-affine linear form: z_{k+1} = Az_k + Bu_k, where A and B are linear operators (matrices K_xx and K_xu, respectively). This formulation provides a globally linearized representation of the original nonlinear system.

#### 3.3. Deep Learning Method for Koopman Operator

A deep learning method is utilized to approximate the Koopman operator and its associated lifting functions. The architecture consists of a deep auto-encoder framework, as depicted in Fig. 2.
- **Autoencoder:** An encoder network, implemented as a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), learns the lifting function φ, mapping the original state space to a higher-dimensional latent space. A decoder network, also an MLP, learns the inverse function φ^{-1}, reconstructing the original states from the latent space.
- **Linear Operators:** Two single-layer MLPs, trained without biases and activation functions, are used to approximate the linear operator A and the control-affine matrix B. This design allows them to represent simple matrix multiplications, consistent with the linear dynamics in the latent space.

The training process optimizes the following loss functions:
- **Reconstruction Loss (L_recon):** An L2 norm, ||x_k - φ^{-1}(φ(x_k))||^2_2, ensures that the auto-encoder accurately reconstructs the original states from their latent representations.
- **Linear Dynamics Loss (L_linear):** An L2 norm, ||φ(x_{k+1}) - (Aφ(x_k) + Bu_k)||^2_2, enforces the linearity of dynamics in the latent space, aligning with the Koopman operator theory.
- **Prediction Loss (L_pred):** An L2 norm, ||φ(x_{k+m}) - z_{k+m}||^2_2, where z_{k+m} is derived from m-step linear prediction, A^m z_k + A^{m-1} B u_k + ... + B u_{k+m-1}. This loss improves the model's ability to predict future states over multiple time steps.
- **Total Loss (L):** The final objective function is a weighted sum of these three losses (λ1 L_recon + λ2 L_pred + λ3 L_linear), augmented with an L2 regularization term (λ4 ||W||^2_2) on the network weights to prevent overfitting.

This comprehensive loss function enables simultaneous end-to-end training of the lifting function φ, its inverse φ^{-1}, and the linear operators A and B.

#### 3.4. Integrating MPC with Deep Koopman Operator

With the learned linear model (z_{k+1} = Az_k + Bu_k) from the deep Koopman operator, a Model Predictive Control (MPC) strategy is implemented. MPC is chosen for its capability to handle system constraints and optimize control actions based on predictions of future behavior.

The MPC solves an optimization problem at each control step over a finite prediction horizon (H). The objective is to minimize a cost function that penalizes deviations of the predicted latent states (z) from desired reference latent states (z_ref), as well as the magnitude of control inputs (u).
The optimization problem is formulated as:
min_{u_{t:t+H}} Σ_{k=0 to H} [ (z_{t+k} - z_{ref_{t+k}})^T Q (z_{t+k} - z_{ref_{t+k}}) + u_{t+k}^T R u_{t+k} ]
subject to:
- z_{t+k} = Az_{t+k-1} + Bu_{t+k-1} for k = 1..., H (linear dynamics constraint)
- z_t = φ(x_t) (current state lifted to latent space)
- z_{ref_{t+k}} = φ(x_{ref_{t+k}}) for k = 1..., H (reference states lifted to latent space)
- u_min ≤ u_{t+k} ≤ u_max for k = 0..., H (control input bounds)

Here, Q and R are positive semi-definite matrices that weight state deviations and control efforts, respectively. H is the prediction horizon, and u_min/u_max are the minimum/maximum allowable control input values.

In real-time operation, at each control iteration, the current real state x_t and the reference trajectory x_{ref_{t:t+H}} are measured and then encoded into their high-dimensional latent representations, z_t and z_{ref_{t:t+H}}. The optimization problem is then solved to yield an optimal sequence of control inputs, u*_{t:t+H}. Only the first element of this sequence, u*_t, is applied to the soft robot. This process is repeated in a receding horizon fashion until the system reaches the desired state or completes the task.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Data Collection

- **Robot Design:** A multi-segment rigid-flexible coupling soft robot named "Chordata" was engineered for experiments. It has a total length of 450mm and comprises three independently actuated segments. Each segment features a dual rigid-flexible coupling platform that uses pneumatic pressure to control three circumferentially arranged bellows, enabling rotational control. Spinal elements integrated along the central axis enhance stability and rigidity, and the robot tapers from base to tip for improved load-bearing at the base and flexibility at the tip.
- **Actuation and Sensing:** The robot is controlled by a pneumatic drive with nine independent air pressure channels (0kPa to 40kPa) via proportional valves. A PC controller manages pressure commands, system identification, and control algorithms. A MicronTracker H3-60 stereo camera system tracks the 3D position of markers attached to the robot's tip, providing real-time positional feedback.
- **Data Collection:** A dataset of 45,607 tuples (x_k, u_k, x_{k+1}) was collected using a randomized actuation strategy. x_k ∈ R^3 represents the 3D tip position, and u_k ∈ R^9 represents the actuation pressures in the nine pneumatic chambers.
- **Model Training:** The collected data was partitioned for training, testing, and validation. All data underwent Min-Max normalization to the range [-1, 1]. Hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate 0.001, batch size 64, latent space dimension 12, Adam optimizer) were chosen to optimize model performance and stability. The encoder and decoder consisted of 2 hidden layers with ReLU activation, while Net A and Net B (for linear operators) were single-layer MLPs without biases or activations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the performance of the proposed DK-MPC method through real-world experiments on the "Chordata" soft robot, comparing it against a closed-loop MPC controller based on an RBF-based Koopman model (K-MPC). Two types of tasks were conducted: trajectory tracking and moving-target tracking.

#### 4.1. Trajectory Tracking Performance

The robot was tasked with following several reference trajectories, including a circular path ("O") and more complex paths shaped like letters ("T", "H", "U").

- **Circular Trajectory ("O"):**
 - **DK-MPC:** The robot's end-effector successfully followed the circular trajectory with an average tracking error of 2.79 mm. The tracking was observed to be consistent and without significant bias.
 - **K-MPC:** In contrast, the K-MPC method exhibited substantial deviations from the reference circular trajectory, resulting in an average error of 22.03 mm.
 - This initial comparison indicated that DK-MPC achieved significantly higher control accuracy.

- **Complex Trajectories ("T", "H", "U"):**
 - Experiments on more complex, dynamically challenging trajectories confirmed the superior performance of DK-MPC.
 - The detailed average errors across all trajectory-tracking tasks were:
 - **"O":** DK-MPC: 2.79 mm; K-MPC: 22.03 mm
 - **"T":** DK-MPC: 3.38 mm; K-MPC: 18.50 mm
 - **"H":** DK-MPC: 3.15 mm; K-MPC: 24.04 mm
 - **"U":** DK-MPC: 3.13 mm; K-MPC: 24.97 mm
 - The overall average error across all tested trajectories for DK-MPC was 3.11 mm, while for K-MPC, it was 22.49 mm.
 - These results demonstrate that DK-MPC consistently achieved precise global dynamic tracking, significantly reducing tracking errors compared to K-MPC. This improvement is attributed to the deeper linearization of the robot model enabled by the deep Koopman operator.

#### 4.2. Moving Target Tracking Performance

To assess the dynamic capabilities and potential for practical applications, the robot was tested on a moving-target tracking task. Five targets were sequentially defined, forming a square path along the edge of the robot's workspace.

- The soft robot successfully tracked and reached each of the five moving targets in sequence.
- Visualizations of the robot's movement demonstrated its ability to dynamically adapt its configuration to move from one target position to the next.
- This experiment confirmed DK-MPC's capacity for dynamic control, indicating its suitability for tasks that require the robot to reach or interact with moving objects, such as grasping and transportation.

In summary, the real-world experiments on the "Chordata" soft robot demonstrated that the proposed DK-MPC method could achieve high-precision control, substantially reducing trajectory tracking errors when compared to an RBF-based Koopman MPC. The system also exhibited robust dynamic control capabilities in a moving target tracking scenario.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, DK-MPC, for the control of multi-segment soft robots. The work addresses the inherent challenges of these robots, specifically their high-dimensional, nonlinear, and time-varying dynamics, which typically impede precise control.

The central contribution lies in the integration of a deep learning-based Koopman operator with Model Predictive Control. By employing deep neural networks to learn the Koopman operator, the framework effectively linearizes the complex nonlinear dynamics of a soft robot into a finite-dimensional linear representation. This approach circumvents the limitation of manually selecting lifting functions, a critical challenge in traditional Koopman operator methods, and enables a more accurate global linearization of the system dynamics.

The experimental results on the "Chordata" soft robot demonstrate that DK-MPC reduces tracking errors significantly compared to an RBF-based Koopman MPC (K-MPC). For instance, across various trajectory tracking tasks, DK-MPC achieved an average error of 3.11 mm, substantially lower than K-MPC's 22.49 mm. This indicates an improvement in control precision for soft robots in real-world environments. Furthermore, the successful demonstration of moving-target tracking validates the method's capability for dynamic control.

The significance of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced Precision and Dynamic Control:** The demonstrated ability to achieve high-precision trajectory tracking and dynamic target reaching directly addresses a core challenge in soft robotics, paving the way for more reliable and accurate operation.
- **Robust Linearization of Nonlinear Systems:** The use of deep learning to discover effective Koopman embeddings provides a data-driven solution to a fundamental problem in control theory, making the Koopman operator framework more accessible and robust for complex nonlinear systems like soft robots.
- **Practical Applicability:** As a data-driven method, DK-MPC exhibits potential for adaptability across different multi-segment soft robot designs by being seamlessly tailored to new systems through retraining with new data. This generalizability could expand the range of applications for soft robots in fields demanding high accuracy and compliance, such as minimally invasive surgery, rehabilitation, and delicate manipulation tasks where human-robot interaction safety is paramount.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for further research into improving the generalizability of deep Koopman models and exploring their application in achieving more complex and dexterous operations for soft robots, including those involving dynamic interaction with their environment.
