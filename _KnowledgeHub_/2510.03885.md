# Seeing the Bigger Picture: 3D Latent Mapping for Mobile Manipulation Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework is presented for mobile manipulation policy learning that utilizes an incrementally constructed 3D latent feature map, enhancing spatial and temporal reasoning by providing persistent global context. This approach consistently improves task success rates across mobile and sequential manipulation benchmarks, demonstrating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing 2D image-based and instantaneous 3D robot learning systems lack consistent 3D environmental understanding and struggle with long-horizon reasoning.
- Policies relying on immediate observations cannot effectively manage tasks requiring memory of out-of-view objects, global context, or extended temporal reasoning.
- Current 3D feature mapping methods often localize objects or predict affordances, but rarely integrate directly into end-to-end policy learning for mobile manipulation.

## Method

- Develop a method to incrementally construct a persistent, multiresolution 3D latent feature map of the workspace, using a modular encoder-decoder architecture.
- Design mobile manipulation policies that are conditioned on this 3D latent map, treating it as a state variable to provide global and long-term context.
- Introduce a 'global map token' that aggregates scene-wide context from the 3D map, which is then fed into the policy network (trainable via Behavior Cloning or Reinforcement Learning).

## Results

- Map-conditioned policies consistently outperformed image-based and transient 3D baselines in mobile manipulation tasks, achieving an SR of 0.31 vs. 0.29 for Image-BC in TidyHouse-Pick (OOD).
- In sequential manipulation tasks, Map-RL (online) policies showed superior temporal memory and achieved significantly higher success rates (1.00 SR OOD vs. 0.75 for Image-RL).
- A simulation-trained map-conditioned policy successfully transferred zero-shot to a physical uFactory xArm6 robot, completing a sequential manipulation task in a real-world setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persistent 3D latent maps serve as effective spatial memory, enabling policies to perform long-horizon reasoning beyond immediate egocentric observations for complex mobile manipulation tasks.
> - A modular encoder-decoder architecture for the latent map, separating scene-specific features from a scene-agnostic decoder, enhances generalization and adaptability to novel environments.
> - Distilling the distributed 3D map features into a compact 'global map token' allows seamless integration of comprehensive spatial context into end-to-end policy learning.
