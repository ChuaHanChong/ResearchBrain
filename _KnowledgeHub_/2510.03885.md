# Seeing the Bigger Picture: 3D Latent Mapping for Mobile Manipulation Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework is presented for mobile manipulation policy learning that utilizes an incrementally constructed 3D latent feature map, enhancing spatial and temporal reasoning by providing persistent global context. This approach consistently improves task success rates across mobile and sequential manipulation benchmarks, demonstrating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing 2D image-based and instantaneous 3D robot learning systems lack consistent 3D environmental understanding and struggle with long-horizon reasoning.
- Policies relying on immediate observations cannot effectively manage tasks requiring memory of out-of-view objects, global context, or extended temporal reasoning.
- Current 3D feature mapping methods often localize objects or predict affordances, but rarely integrate directly into end-to-end policy learning for mobile manipulation.

## Method

- Develop a method to incrementally construct a persistent, multiresolution 3D latent feature map of the workspace, using a modular encoder-decoder architecture.
- Design mobile manipulation policies that are conditioned on this 3D latent map, treating it as a state variable to provide global and long-term context.
- Introduce a 'global map token' that aggregates scene-wide context from the 3D map, which is then fed into the policy network (trainable via Behavior Cloning or Reinforcement Learning).

## Results

- Map-conditioned policies consistently outperformed image-based and transient 3D baselines in mobile manipulation tasks, achieving an SR of 0.31 vs. 0.29 for Image-BC in TidyHouse-Pick (OOD).
- In sequential manipulation tasks, Map-RL (online) policies showed superior temporal memory and achieved significantly higher success rates (1.00 SR OOD vs. 0.75 for Image-RL).
- A simulation-trained map-conditioned policy successfully transferred zero-shot to a physical uFactory xArm6 robot, completing a sequential manipulation task in a real-world setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persistent 3D latent maps serve as effective spatial memory, enabling policies to perform long-horizon reasoning beyond immediate egocentric observations for complex mobile manipulation tasks.
> - A modular encoder-decoder architecture for the latent map, separating scene-specific features from a scene-agnostic decoder, enhances generalization and adaptability to novel environments.
> - Distilling the distributed 3D map features into a compact 'global map token' allows seamless integration of comprehensive spatial context into end-to-end policy learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robot learning have facilitated progress in manipulation within semi-structured environments, particularly through the integration of large vision-language models (VLMs) which translate natural language commands into robot actions. However, a significant portion of current state-of-the-art systems primarily rely on 2D image-based designs operating on raw video streams. While effective for short-term action prediction, these 2D approaches exhibit limitations in consistent 3D environmental understanding and long-horizon reasoning, capabilities that are critical for tasks extending spatially or temporally beyond immediate observations.

The broader research landscape for 3D scene representations in manipulation includes several approaches. Some methods project 2D foundation-model features into 3D on a per-frame basis, offering local scene understanding and geometric preservation. Another category involves encoding raw point cloud observations directly using specialized 3D backbones. While these methods enhance local 3D perception, they often reconstruct the scene from scratch at each time step, which can compromise temporal consistency and hinder long-horizon reasoning. Furthermore, some efforts focus on fusing multiview observations into feature fields offline to improve multiview consistency, but these are typically constrained to tabletop setups where the entire workspace remains continuously visible and lack the ability to adapt to novel views dynamically. Existing 3D feature mapping works, such as LERF, F3RM, and LERF-TOGO, often embed vision-language features into neural scene representations or hierarchical grids for tasks like open-vocabulary querying or 6-DoF grasping. These methods primarily focus on localizing objects or predicting affordances based on language queries rather than integrating these representations directly into end-to-end policy learning.

This paper proposes an alternative by conditioning robot policy learning on an explicit, persistent 3D map representation of the environment. While persistent maps have been integral to robot navigation for decades, their application to enhancing manipulation policies, particularly for mobile manipulation scenarios that require perceiving beyond the current field of view and aggregating observations over long horizons, has been less explored. This work aims to bridge this gap by introducing a 3D latent map that functions as spatial memory and provides long-term context to support robot policy learning, thereby addressing the limitations of instantaneous 3D representations and purely image-based approaches in mobile manipulation tasks requiring extensive spatial and temporal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of existing 2D image-based and instantaneous 3D representation methods in addressing complex mobile manipulation tasks. Current policies that rely solely on raw video streams or reconstruct scene representations at each time step often struggle with:
1. **Consistent 3D Understanding**: Image-based methods can lack a stable and coherent understanding of the 3D environment, which is crucial for precise manipulation and navigation.
2. **Long-Horizon Reasoning**: Tasks that span extended periods or require memory of previously observed, now out-of-view, objects or locations are challenging for policies that only process immediate observations.
3. **Global Context and Spatial Memory**: Robots operating in large environments need to perceive beyond their current field of view and retain memory of object locations and task goals across space and time to mitigate occlusions and perform efficient navigation.

To address these limitations, the paper articulates two main objectives:

1. **Incrementally Construct a 3D Latent Feature Map**: The first objective is to develop a method for building a persistent 3D latent feature map of the robot's workspace. This map is designed to aggregate multiview visual observations over time, creating a comprehensive and compact spatial memory of the environment. The design emphasizes modularity, decoupling scene-specific feature optimization from a pre-trained, scene-agnostic decoder, which is intended to facilitate generalization to diverse and novel environments. This incremental construction allows the map to adapt to environmental changes and provide an up-to-date representation for the policy.

2. **Design Map-Conditioned Manipulation Policies**: The second objective is to integrate this 3D latent map into robot manipulation policies. The map is treated as a state variable, providing global context to the policy. The policy is formulated to leverage this context for spatial and temporal reasoning, enabling the robot to execute tasks specified in natural language. The design includes a mechanism to summarize the spatially distributed map features into a compact "global map token," which then conditions the policy. This approach aims to improve the policy's ability to reason globally and utilize long-horizon memory for complex mobile and sequential manipulation tasks. The policies are designed to be trainable using both behavior cloning (BC) and reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to enable robots to perform mobile manipulation with enhanced spatial and temporal reasoning capabilities, thereby advancing the frontier of robot learning beyond fixed tabletop setups to larger-scale, long-term tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, "Seeing the Bigger Picture (SBP)," is an end-to-end policy learning approach that operates on an incrementally constructed 3D latent feature map. The methodology consists of two primary components: the latent feature mapping process and the map-conditioned policy learning.

#### 3.1. Latent Feature Mapping

The latent feature mapping approach is designed around two principles: incremental updates for persistent spatial memory and modularity for generalization.

**A. Multiresolution Feature Grid**:
The scene is represented as learnable latent vectors anchored at the vertices of a 3D grid. This feature grid, denoted as $G = \{(z_i, f_i)\}$, stores latent vectors $f_i \in F$ at vertex positions $z_i \in X$. To handle information at multiple scales, a hierarchy of $L$ grids, $\{G_l\}_{l=1}^L$, is employed, ranging from coarse to fine resolutions. For any query point $x \in X$, its feature is retrieved through trilinear interpolation of the eight neighboring vertex features within the voxel containing $x$. The final feature representation for a point $x$, $F_\psi(x)$, is formed by concatenating the interpolated features from all grid levels, where $\psi$ represents all scene-specific latent vectors. A hash-based voxel grid is used for efficient implementation.

**B. Encoder-Decoder Architecture for Modularity**:
The latent map is structured as an encoder-decoder architecture, $M = (F_\psi, D_\theta)$.
- The **encoder** $F_\psi: X \to F$ maps 3D workspace points to a latent space $F$. This component consists of the multiresolution feature grid parameters ($\psi$) and is scene-specific.
- The **decoder** $D_\theta: F \to Y$ projects latent features from $F$ to a target embedding space $Y$, such as CLIP language features. This component is implemented as a multilayer perceptron (MLP) and is designed to be scene-agnostic, pre-trained on diverse environments to learn a general mapping.
This modularity disentangles scene-specific parameters from scene-agnostic parameters, enabling fast adaptation to new environments by optimizing only the grid parameters $\psi$ while keeping $\theta$ frozen.

**C. Latent Map Optimization**:
The map is optimized by minimizing a reconstruction loss between the predicted feature $\hat{y}(x) = D_\theta(F_\psi(x))$ and the ground-truth target embedding $y$. Target embeddings are derived from dense visual features extracted from a VLM (specifically, EVA-02-Large) applied to RGB images. Each image patch with valid depth is back-projected into the 3D world frame to create coordinate-feature pairs $(x, y)$. The cosine distance loss is used for optimization, shown to outperform L2 loss in empirical tests. Positional encoding is omitted to prevent overfitting to absolute coordinates and enhance cross-scene generalization.

**D. Online Latent Mapping**:
To account for dynamic environments or task progression, the latent map is updated online. At the beginning of each policy rollout, the feature grid and decoder are initialized from a pre-trained offline map. During task execution, the map is updated periodically (every $T_{update}$ steps) using streaming robot observations. These updates involve a fixed number of optimization steps ($K_{update}$) on the map features, with the decoder parameters typically kept frozen. Dynamic elements, such as the robot arm, are segmented out and excluded from updates to preserve consistency of the static scene.

#### 3.2. Map-Conditioned Policy Learning

The robot manipulation policy is conditioned on the latent feature map to leverage global and long-term context.

**A. Global Map Token**:
A "global map token" ($e_m$) is introduced to distill scene-wide context from the 3D latent map into a compact representation.
1. Features are extracted at the vertices of the finest grid level, and a decoded feature $\hat{y}(z_{L,i})$ is computed for each vertex $z_{L,i}$.
2. A set of coordinate-feature pairs $S_L$ is formed, with a binary mask used to select task-relevant vertices (e.g., excluding unoccupied regions).
3. These pairs are fed into a **3D feature aggregator** ($E_{3D}$), which processes the spatially distributed features.
4. The outputs of $E_{3D}$ are then attention-pooled to produce the global map token $e_m$.
The architecture of $E_{3D}$ is adapted to the environment scale: a Point Transformer is used for room-scale environments, while a lighter PointNet is used for smaller tabletop scenes due to its computational efficiency. The parameters of $E_{3D}$ are optimized jointly with the policy.

**B. Map-Conditioned Policy Network**:
The global map token $e_m$ is integrated as an additional state input to the policy network. Image features $E_I(o_\tau)$, proprioceptive state $s_\tau$, task embedding $e_\ell$, and $e_m$ are concatenated to form a joint embedding $h_\tau$. This $h_\tau$ is then fed into the policy network $\pi_\phi$ to produce an action $a_\tau$.

- **Map-Conditioned Behavior Cloning (BC)**: For BC, the policy architecture is based on ACT. Image features from a Transformer encoder are concatenated with the proprioceptive state, task embedding (a VLM text encoding of a natural language command), and the global map token. This joint embedding is then processed by a Transformer decoder to predict an action sequence. An exponentially decaying loss weight is applied to future action steps. DINOv2-ViT-S serves as the visual backbone.

- **Map-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: For RL, PPO with an actor-critic architecture is used. Both actor and critic heads are two-layer MLPs conditioned on $h_\tau$. The task embedding $e_\ell$ is a learnable target object specification. A two-stage curriculum is adopted: first, a map-agnostic, image-based policy is pre-trained; then, the policy is fine-tuned with the map token enabled via a learnable sigmoid gating mechanism that controls the incorporation of map features. DINOv2-ViT-S is used as the visual backbone, processed by an MLP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SBP focused on its ability to reason globally and leverage long-term context in two settings: mobile manipulation and sequential tabletop manipulation. Performance was assessed using success rate (SR) and episode reward (ER) in both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

#### 4.1. Mobile Manipulation Experiment

**Task**: Object picking subtasks from the ManiSkill-HAB benchmark, trained on a single scene and evaluated on both the training scene (ID) and a novel, unseen scene (OOD). The target object was often outside the robot's initial field of view due to perturbations in the robot's starting pose.

**Comparisons**:
- **Image-BC**: An image-based ACT policy.
- **Uplifted**: An image-based policy with features lifted into transient 3D tokens.
- **Point Cloud**: A policy based on raw point cloud observations.
- **Map-BC (offline)**: The proposed map-conditioned BC policy using a pre-generated offline map.

**Results (Table I, Figure 6)**:
- **Quantitative Performance**: Map-BC (offline) generally outperformed all baselines across different benchmarks (TidyHouse-Pick, PrepareGroceries-Pick, SetTable-Pick). The performance gains were particularly noticeable on complex tasks like TidyHouse, suggesting enhanced scene understanding and efficient target localization. For instance, in TidyHouse-Pick (OOD), Map-BC achieved an SR of 0.31, while Image-BC, Uplifted, and Point Cloud policies achieved 0.29, 0.30, and 0.13 respectively.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figure 6)**: In scenarios where the target object was initially out of view, Image-BC policies exhibited erratic and inefficient trajectories, often failing to reach the target. In contrast, Map-BC policies demonstrated direct and efficient navigation towards the target object, consistently completing the task. This indicates that the latent map enabled the policy to perform global reasoning over the entire scene, irrespective of the robot's immediate egocentric view.

#### 4.2. Sequential Manipulation Experiment

**Task**: A two-stage sequential pick-and-place task on a cluttered tabletop, requiring picking objects in a prescribed order and placing them into a basket. The robot used an egocentric camera, and scenes contained occlusions, necessitating long-horizon memory.

**Comparisons**:
- **Image-RL**: An image-based RL policy.
- **Map-RL (offline)**: The proposed map-conditioned RL policy with a pre-generated offline map.
- **Map-RL (online)**: The proposed map-conditioned RL policy with an online-updated map.

**Results (Table II, Figure 7)**:
- **Quantitative Performance**: Both Map-RL (offline) and Map-RL (online) significantly outperformed Image-RL in SR and ER, particularly in OOD settings. For instance, in OOD scenes, Map-RL (online) achieved an SR of 1.00, compared to 0.75 for Image-RL and 0.95 for Map-RL (offline). This demonstrates the map's role in improving performance on tasks requiring long-term context.
- **Temporal Memory**: Map-RL (online) showed an advantage over Map-RL (offline). While Map-RL (offline) relied on an initial scene configuration, Map-RL (online)'s incremental updates allowed it to track the task state more effectively by incorporating temporal memory from changes in the environment (e.g., objects being moved).
- **Qualitative Observations (Figure 7)**: Image-RL struggled to localize the second target object or the basket once they left the egocentric view. Map-RL, however, successfully leveraged the map as spatial memory to locate these out-of-view elements and complete the sequential task.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Deployment

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The Map-RL (offline) policy, trained exclusively in simulation, was deployed on a uFactory xArm6 robot in a zero-shot manner. An offline latent map of the real scene was constructed from a sequence of egocentric RGB-D images and robot forward kinematics.
- **Success**: The policy successfully completed the sequential manipulation task on the physical robot (Figure 8). The robustness of the frozen DINOv2 visual backbone was cited as a factor in bridging the sim-to-real domain gap without additional transfer techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for integrating 3D latent maps into mobile manipulation policy learning, addressing several limitations of existing approaches and indicating potential for broader impact in robotics.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that mobile manipulation policies can benefit from utilizing a persistent 3D latent map, achieving stronger spatial and temporal reasoning compared to policies relying solely on instantaneous image-based observations. This is critical for tasks that extend beyond the robot's immediate field of view and require memory over long operational horizons.

Key contributions that signify the potential impact include:
- **Enhanced Spatial and Temporal Reasoning**: By providing a global context and functioning as a long-horizon memory, the 3D latent map enables policies to reason about objects and goals that are currently occluded or located outside the robot's immediate visual input. This capability is essential for robust mobile manipulation in cluttered or dynamic environments.
- **Modular and Generalizable Mapping**: The proposed modular design, which separates a scene-specific feature grid from a scene-agnostic decoder, allows for generalization across diverse environments. This approach simplifies adaptation to novel scenes, as only the latent features of the specific scene require optimization, leveraging a pre-trained decoder.
- **Improved Task Success Rates**: The experimental results demonstrate consistent performance improvements of map-conditioned policies over image-based and other 3D representation baselines, particularly in challenging out-of-distribution scenarios and tasks requiring sequential operations. For instance, an improvement of 15% in success rate for the sequential manipulation task highlights the practical benefits.
- **Online Adaptability**: The online update mechanism for the latent map enables policies to track changes in the environment, providing temporal memory that is beneficial for multi-stage tasks where object configurations change over time.
- **Sim-to-Real Transferability**: The successful zero-shot transfer of a simulation-trained map-conditioned policy to a physical robot suggests the potential for developing robust, real-world robotic systems using this methodology, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection and training.

Potential future impacts include the development of more autonomous and capable mobile manipulation robots that can operate effectively in complex, unstructured human environments. This work lays a foundation for future research in several directions, such as:
- **Dynamic Scene Representations**: Reducing reliance on local image features by developing more comprehensive dynamic scene representations that can track the motion of both the robot and other objects.
- **Efficient Policy Learning**: Exploring off-policy or model-based reinforcement learning techniques to mitigate the sample inefficiency observed in the current on-policy RL approach.
- **Unified 3D Aggregation Models**: Developing more efficient and unified 3D feature aggregation models that can support online policy training without requiring a trade-off between expressive power (e.g., Point Transformer) and computational efficiency (e.g., PointNet).
- **Scaling to Larger and More Complex Tasks**: Evaluating the approach on even larger-scale, longer-horizon mobile manipulation tasks and extending it to more intricate dexterous manipulation scenarios to validate its broader applicability.

Overall, this research contributes to advancing robot capabilities by integrating persistent 3D spatial intelligence into policy learning, moving towards robots that can better "see the bigger picture" of their operational environment.
