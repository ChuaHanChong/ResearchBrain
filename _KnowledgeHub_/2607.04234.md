# SoftVTBench: A Safety-Aware Visuo-Tactile Benchmark for Physically Constrained Robotic Manipulation of Deformable Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> SoftVTBench introduces a safety-aware visuo-tactile benchmark for robotic manipulation of deformable objects within a physically constrained simulation environment. This benchmark demonstrates that integrating tactile sensing with vision leads to substantial improvements in manipulation safety for deformable objects, reducing peak deformation by up to 6 percentage points and increasing safety success rates by over 14 percentage points compared to vision-only baselines.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation benchmarks primarily evaluate task completion for deformable objects, often overlooking critical physical safety aspects like preventing damage, slips, or drops.
- Vision-only systems struggle to capture fine-grained contact dynamics (e.g., pressure, shear forces) at the gripper-object interface, which are crucial for precise force regulation in deformable object manipulation.
- There is a lack of integrated benchmarks that combine deformable object manipulation, multimodal visuo-tactile perception, and explicit safety-aware evaluation.
- The inherent approximations of real-world soft-body dynamics in simulation are a limitation.

## Method

- Developed **SoftVTBench**, a simulation environment in Isaac Sim using GPU-accelerated ==PhysX 5== with ==FEM== for realistic soft-body dynamics, featuring a Franka Panda arm with simulated GelSight Mini tactile sensors.
- Introduced a dual evaluation protocol: '==Goal Success==' for task completion and '==Safety Success==' which additionally requires stable grasping (no drop) and limiting peak object deformation below a calibrated, object-specific threshold.
- Provided a multimodal observation-action interface including two RGB camera views, ==tactile RGB images and marker-motion fields==, proprioceptive robot states, and natural language instructions for tasks.

## Results

- Visuo-tactile policies achieved a **14.2%** absolute improvement in Safety Success (from **21.4%** to **35.6%**) on Object-Soft tasks and **12%** on Spatial-Soft tasks (from **32.6%** to **44.6%**) compared to vision-only baselines.
- The visuo-tactile approach reduced the 95th percentile of FEM-RMS deformation for deformable objects by up to **5.89 percentage points** (from **44.70%** to **38.81%** on Object-Soft tasks).
- For rigid object tasks, visuo-tactile policies showed mixed performance for Goal Success, sometimes outperforming (Spatial-Rigid: **63.4%** vs **56.4%**) and sometimes underperforming (Object-Rigid: **32.4%** vs **38.8%**) vision-only methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluating deformable object manipulation solely on task completion is insufficient, as a significant 'Goal-Safety Gap' exists where policies achieve goals but violate physical safety constraints (e.g., excessive deformation, unstable grasps).
> - Tactile sensing provides critical local contact information that vision alone cannot capture, demonstrably enhancing the physical safety of deformable object manipulation by facilitating better contact regulation and reducing damaging interactions.
> - The benchmark's safety-aware metrics expose limitations of current policies and guide the development of more robust, gentle, and stable manipulation strategies for soft and delicate materials.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large-scale robot learning and foundation models have expanded the generality of robotic manipulation across various tasks and environments. However, a significant portion of existing research and benchmarks primarily focuses on rigid-object manipulation, where performance is often measured solely by task completion, and the intricacies of physical interaction are often abstracted.

The manipulation of deformable objects, while receiving increased attention, continues to rely predominantly on success-oriented evaluation protocols. This approach often overlooks critical aspects of physical interaction, such as maintaining stable grasps, preventing slips or drops, and avoiding excessive deformation that could damage or alter the object's integrity. Unlike rigid objects, deformable objects possess contact-rich dynamics and material-dependent constraints, necessitating a more nuanced evaluation beyond mere task completion.

Furthermore, while vision-based systems offer global information about object geometry, they often fail to capture fine-grained contact dynamics at the gripper-object interface, such as pressure distribution, incipient slip, or localized deformation. Tactile sensing has been identified as a complementary modality, providing direct feedback on these local physical interactions crucial for precise force regulation and manipulation. Despite this, existing visuo-tactile and tactile benchmarks primarily address rigid-object or general contact-rich scenarios, without specifically targeting deformation-aware evaluation for deformable objects.

This work addresses the intersection of these identified gaps by introducing a benchmark that provides a standardized evaluation for deformable object manipulation, integrates visuo-tactile perception, and explicitly assesses physically constrained interactions. It seeks to fill the void where deformable object manipulation, multimodal sensing, and safety-aware evaluation converge, offering a more comprehensive assessment of robotic manipulation capabilities for soft and delicate materials.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **SoftVTBench**, a safety-aware visuo-tactile benchmark specifically designed for physically constrained robotic manipulation of deformable objects. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Limitations of Success-Oriented Metrics for Deformable Objects:** Existing manipulation benchmarks, even those for deformable objects, are predominantly "success-oriented." They measure whether a task goal is achieved (e.g., placing an object in a target location) but rarely evaluate the quality or safety of the physical interaction throughout the task execution. For deformable objects, simply completing a task is insufficient; the manipulation must also be gentle enough to avoid damage or excessive deformation, and stable enough to prevent drops or slips. This gap in evaluation means that policies might be deemed "successful" despite causing undesirable physical outcomes.

2. **Importance of Physical Safety in Deformable Object Manipulation:** Real-world applications often involve handling delicate or soft objects (e.g., food, medical supplies, consumer goods). In these scenarios, maintaining physical integrity is as crucial as task completion. An effective manipulation policy must ensure a stable grasp without causing slip or drop, and it must regulate grasping forces to prevent excessive deformation or damage to the object. The absence of explicit safety metrics in current benchmarks hinders the development and assessment of policies capable of such nuanced physical interaction.

3. **Necessity of Visuo-Tactile Sensing for Fine-Grained Interaction Control:** Vision alone provides global information but struggles to perceive the fine-grained contact dynamics at the gripper-object interface, such as contact pressure, shear forces, or local deformation. These signals are critical for adapting manipulation strategies to the object's specific material properties and for maintaining grasp stability. Tactile sensing directly captures these local physical interactions, offering essential feedback that complements visual information. Therefore, a benchmark for physically constrained deformable object manipulation requires support for visuo-tactile observations to enable policies to infer and react to complex contact conditions.

4. **Addressing an Underexplored Research Area:** While progress has been made in robotic manipulation, tactile sensing, and deformable object handling individually, a benchmark that systematically integrates all three—deformable object manipulation, visuo-tactile perception, and physically constrained evaluation—remains largely undeveloped. SoftVTBench aims to fill this void by providing a unified platform to study how policies can achieve manipulation goals while adhering to safety constraints imposed by the deformable nature of objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoftVTBench is implemented within Isaac Sim, utilizing its GPU-accelerated PhysX 5 pipeline for physics simulation, including Finite Element Method (FEM) for simulating soft-body dynamics of deformable objects. The benchmark features a Franka Panda robotic arm equipped with a parallel-jaw gripper, which carries simulated GelSight Mini tactile sensors on both fingers.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design

The core design principle is to evaluate closed-loop robot policies based on two coupled criteria: successful completion of the manipulation goal and maintenance of safe physical interaction throughout the execution. A rollout is considered physically safe only if the object remains stably grasped without slip or drop, and its peak deformation (measured from privileged FEM states) remains below a calibrated, object-specific threshold. The benchmark explicitly reports both "Goal Success" and "Safety Success" to highlight instances where tasks are completed but physical safety constraints are violated.

#### 3.2. Observation and Action Space

The benchmark provides a multimodal observation-action interface:
- **Visual Observations:** Two RGB camera views are provided: a third-person camera for global scene context and a wrist-mounted camera for close-range interaction views. Both are synchronized at 20 Hz.
- **Tactile Observations:** Simulated GelSight Mini sensors on each gripper finger provide RGB tactile images and marker-motion fields, capturing local contact geometry, elastomer deformation, and shear motion.
- **Proprioceptive State:** Includes end-effector pose, arm joint states, and current gripper width.
- **Language Instructions:** Each task is specified by a natural language instruction to identify the target object and goal region.
All observations are synchronized at 20 Hz.
The policy's action space consists of a 7D command: a 3D end-effector position, a 3D axis-angle orientation, and a 1D gripper command (supporting both binary open/close and continuous closure targets).

#### 3.3. Task Suites

SoftVTBench is structured into four matched task suites, categorized by object type (rigid vs. deformable) and variation axis (object-centric vs. spatial variation):
- **Object-Soft:** Policies must grasp a deformable object and place it into a target container without drop or excessive deformation. The scene layout and target region are fixed, but the manipulated deformable object varies across tasks, requiring adaptation to different geometries and contact responses.
- **Spatial-Soft:** Policies perform grasp-and-place with deformable objects under spatial variation. Each scene contains two visually identical deformable objects, and a language instruction specifies which one to manipulate, testing language grounding and safe manipulation under varying placements.
- **Object-Rigid & Spatial-Rigid:** These serve as diagnostic controls, featuring rigid objects in object-centric and spatial variation setups, respectively. They assess basic manipulation competence and robustness to spatial variation in the absence of deformation-related safety constraints.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol and Metrics

Policies are evaluated via closed-loop rollouts. Policies only access designated observations, while privileged simulator states (FEM nodal positions, object poses, contact status, drop events) are recorded purely for evaluation and are hidden from the policy.
- **Goal Success:** Measures if the task objective is completed (e.g., target object placed inside the target region).
- **Safety Success:** A stricter metric requiring Goal Success *and* adherence to physical safety constraints. Specifically, it requires:
 - `NoDrop_episode`: The object must remain stably manipulated throughout the episode (no transient drop, workspace escape, or loss of stable containment).
 - `D_peak ≤ τ_o`: The peak FEM-RMS deformation (`D_peak`), normalized by the object's bounding-box diagonal, must not exceed an object-specific safety threshold (`τ_o`).
The `τ_o` is determined offline through an interaction calibration protocol, where `D_ref_o` (maximum stable FEM RMS deformation) is measured, and `τ_o = κ * D_ref_o` (with `κ=0.5` by default). The "Goal-Safety Gap" quantifies unsafe task completions.

#### 3.5. Baselines

Two `π_0.5`-based baseline policies were implemented:
- **`π_0.5`-Vision:** Inputs include third-person RGB, wrist RGB, robot proprioception, and language instruction. It outputs a binary open-close gripper command.
- **`π_0.5`-Visuo-Tactile:** Uses the same visual and proprioceptive inputs as the vision-only policy, plus tactile observations (RGB tactile images and marker-motion fields from both fingers, 8-frame history). It outputs a continuous gripper-width action.
Both policies output 7D actions (3D position, 3D orientation, 1D gripper command) and were trained using LoRA fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on SoftVTBench compared vision-only (`π_0.5`-Vision) and visuo-tactile (`π_0.5`-Visuo-Tactile) policies across the four task suites, evaluating both Goal Success and Safety Success.

#### 4.1. Performance on Rigid-Object Tasks

- For **Object-Rigid** tasks, the vision-only policy (VO) achieved a Goal Success of 38.8%, which was higher than the visuo-tactile policy (VT) at 32.4%.
- For **Spatial-Rigid** tasks, the visuo-tactile policy (VT) achieved 63.4% Goal Success, outperforming the vision-only policy (VO) at 56.4%.
These results indicate that tactile information does not consistently improve task completion for rigid-object manipulation. In scenarios without deformation-related safety constraints, visual and proprioceptive information often suffice, and additional tactile input might introduce limited marginal benefit or noise.

#### 4.2. Performance on Deformable-Object Tasks

- On **Object-Soft** tasks, both policies achieved comparable Goal Success rates (VO: 70.4%, VT: 71.8%). However, the visuo-tactile policy significantly improved **Safety Success** from 21.4% (VO) to 35.6% (VT).
- For **Spatial-Soft** tasks, the visuo-tactile policy (VT) demonstrated improvements in both Goal Success (from 74.2% to 84.2%) and **Safety Success** (from 32.6% to 44.6%).
These findings suggest that while tactile sensing may not always lead to substantial gains in task completion (Goal Success) for deformable objects, it demonstrably enhances the safety of manipulation by improving contact regulation and reducing unsafe goal-completing behaviors such as slippage, over-compression, and unstable grasping. The substantial gap between Goal Success and Safety Success for deformable tasks highlights that goal-completion alone overstates policy performance without considering physical safety.

#### 4.3. Deformation Analysis

Analysis of FEM-RMS deformation distributions across deformable-object rollouts (Table 4) provided further evidence for the visuo-tactile policy's benefits:
- On **Object-Soft** tasks, VT reduced the mean deformation from 16.10% to 15.12%, the median from 10.65% to 8.67%, and the 95th percentile (P95) from 44.70% to 38.81%.
- Similarly, on **Spatial-Soft** tasks, VT lowered the mean deformation from 13.16% to 11.58%, the median from 10.75% to 9.67%, and the P95 from 28.96% to 26.56%.
These reductions across the deformation distribution, particularly in the P95 tail representing severe over-compression, indicate that tactile sensing facilitates safer interactions by enabling policies to avoid high-deformation scenarios.

#### 4.4. Goal Success vs. Safety Success Gap

The comparison between Goal Success and Safety Success (Figure 4) consistently revealed a significant gap for deformable object tasks. This gap indicates that a substantial proportion of rollouts that successfully achieve the task goal still violate physical safety constraints (e.g., through excessive deformation or unstable contact). This observation underscores the inadequacy of success-only evaluation for deformable object manipulation.

Qualitative observations of tactile RGB images with marker-motion overlays (Figure 3) demonstrated that different deformable objects induce distinct local contact and shear patterns. These cues are challenging to infer from external visual observations but are directly captured by tactile sensors, providing information relevant for regulating interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces **SoftVTBench**, a safety-aware visuo-tactile benchmark that addresses a notable gap in robotic manipulation research. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancing Safety-Aware Evaluation:** By formulating and implementing safety-aware evaluation that distinguishes "Goal Success" from "Safety Success," SoftVTBench provides a more comprehensive and physically meaningful measure of policy performance for deformable object manipulation. This approach exposes unsafe task completions that were previously obscured by traditional success-only metrics, thereby enabling a more rigorous assessment of robotic capabilities in delicate handling tasks.

2. **Facilitating Visuo-Tactile Policy Development:** The benchmark offers a standardized, reproducible simulation environment with realistic FEM-simulated deformable assets, multimodal visuo-tactile observations, and varied task suites. This platform is valuable for researchers developing and testing new learning-based policies that integrate visual and tactile feedback for complex, contact-rich manipulation. It provides the necessary infrastructure to explore how multimodal sensing can contribute to both task achievement and safety adherence.

3. **Highlighting the Value of Tactile Sensing for Deformable Objects:** The experimental results provide quantitative evidence that tactile sensing, while not consistently improving goal success for rigid objects, offers significant benefits for deformable object manipulation by enhancing physical safety and reducing excessive deformation. This insight can guide future research by emphasizing the specific scenarios and objectives where tactile feedback is most impactful, directing efforts toward developing policies that leverage tactile information for precise contact regulation.

4. **Enabling Development of Robust and Safe Robotic Systems:** The benchmark's focus on physical safety is crucial for real-world applications where robots interact with fragile, soft, or irregularly shaped materials (e.g., in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, or domestic assistance). By providing a means to rigorously evaluate safe interaction, SoftVTBench can contribute to the development of more robust, reliable, and trustworthy robotic manipulation systems that minimize damage and ensure stability when handling deformable objects.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, including the current benchmark's limited set of assets and task variations, and the inherent approximation of real-world soft-body dynamics by the simulation. The evaluation primarily focuses on `π_0.5`-based baselines. Future work is proposed to expand the diversity of deformable assets, enhance the realism of deformation and contact modeling, and conduct broader comparisons with other policy architectures, such as world-action models. These extensions would further enrich the benchmark's utility and provide deeper insights into physically constrained deformable object manipulation.
