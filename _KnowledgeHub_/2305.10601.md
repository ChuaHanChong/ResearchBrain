# Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Princeton University and Google DeepMind developed Tree of Thoughts (ToT), a framework enabling large language models to perform deliberate problem-solving by explicitly exploring and evaluating multiple reasoning paths. This approach, which allows LLMs to self-generate and self-evaluate intermediate thoughts, achieved a 74% success rate on the Game of 24 and improved coherence in creative writing tasks compared to traditional Chain-of-Thought methods.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily operate on an autoregressive, token-level, left-to-right generation mechanism, akin to 'System 1' thinking, limiting their ability to perform deliberate planning or strategic lookahead.
- Complex tasks requiring non-trivial planning, lookahead, or where early decisions profoundly impact subsequent steps (e.g., mathematical puzzles, creative composition, constrained combinatorial problems) often cause LLMs to fail with standard prompting techniques.
- Current methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Self-Consistency with CoT (CoT-SC) still produce a single, linear reasoning path or ensemble linear paths, lacking a general paradigm for non-linear exploration.

## Method

- The **Tree of Thoughts (ToT)** framework conceptualizes problem-solving as a ==search over a tree structure==, where each node represents a partial solution and branches are intermediate ==thoughts==.
- It defines a process involving: ==thought decomposition== (defining intermediate steps), ==thought generation== (LLM generates 'k' candidate thoughts), and ==state evaluation== (LLM ==self-evaluates== the promisingness of states through value or voting prompts).
- Various ==search algorithms== (==Breadth-First Search== for shallower problems, ==Depth-First Search== with ==backtracking== for deeper problems) can be integrated to navigate the thought tree.

## Results

- On the Game of 24 mathematical reasoning task, ToT achieved a **74%** success rate with a breadth of 5, significantly outperforming CoT-SC's **9%** success rate.
- For creative writing, ToT-generated passages scored higher in GPT-4 coherence (**7.56** vs. **6.93** for zero-shot CoT) and were preferred by human evaluators in **41%** of cases over CoT.
- In Mini Crosswords, ToT using Depth-First Search (DFS) achieved a **60%** word-level success rate and solved **4 out of 20** games completely, with ablation studies confirming the critical importance of backtracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can be leveraged to generate and evaluate search heuristics, effectively enabling them to perform deliberate, systematic problem-solving beyond linear generation.
> - The Tree of Thoughts (ToT) framework generalizes existing methods like Chain-of-Thought by allowing LLMs to explore multiple, branching reasoning paths through a tree structure.
> - Integrating classical AI search algorithms (like BFS and DFS) with LLMs' language-based reasoning creates a powerful paradigm for tasks requiring planning, lookahead, and backtracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed unprecedented growth, with models like GPT and PaLM demonstrating remarkable capabilities across diverse tasks, from complex reasoning to creative generation. However, a fundamental limitation persists: these models primarily operate on an autoregressive, token-level, left-to-right generation mechanism. This "System 1" mode of operation, analogous to fast, intuitive human cognition, often falls short in tasks requiring deliberate planning, exploration of multiple alternatives, or strategic lookahead—qualities associated with "System 2" thinking in human psychology.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It builds upon the success of **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting**, which enhanced LLMs' reasoning by eliciting intermediate reasoning steps in a sequential manner. CoT, while powerful, still fundamentally produces a single, linear reasoning path. The "Tree of Thoughts" (ToT) framework generalizes CoT by enabling LLMs to explore multiple, branching reasoning paths, akin to a human deliberating over several options before making a decision.

ToT draws significant inspiration from **classical Artificial Intelligence (AI) and cognitive science** research, particularly the work of Newell, Shaw, and Simon from the 1950s. Their characterization of problem-solving as a search through a combinatorial problem space (a tree structure) provides the foundational metaphor for ToT. While classical AI relied on hand-programmed or learned heuristics to guide search, ToT introduces a novel approach: using the LLM itself to generate and evaluate these heuristics through language-based self-reflection. This is a key departure and advancement, as it leverages the LLM's vast internal knowledge and reasoning capabilities not just for content generation, but for meta-reasoning about problem-solving strategies.

The paper positions ToT within the broader context of **planning and decision-making** with LLMs, noting its distinction from methods that rely on external reward models (like in traditional reinforcement learning) or classical planners. It relates ToT to concurrent works such as RAP (Reasoning with Language Model is Planning with World Model), which also explores MCTS-based methods for planning with LLMs, but highlights ToT's greater modularity. Furthermore, ToT intersects with the burgeoning area of **self-reflection and self-correction** in LLMs, where models critique and refine their own outputs. Unlike approaches that merely refine a single solution or work at the token level, ToT integrates this self-evaluation into a systematic search process across higher-level "thoughts."

Finally, ToT can be seen as a modern rendition of **classical search algorithms** like Breadth-First Search (BFS), Depth-First Search (DFS), or even A*. By using the LLM to provide the heuristic evaluations at each node of the search tree, ToT bridges the symbolic reasoning of classical AI with the neural capabilities of modern LLMs, opening a promising avenue for developing more robust and generally intelligent problem-solving AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the problem-solving capabilities of large language models by moving beyond their inherent token-level, left-to-right inference limitations.** The authors aim to introduce a framework that enables LLMs to perform **deliberate decision-making**, similar to System 2 thinking in humans, allowing for exploration, strategic lookahead, and self-evaluation.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations:

- **Inherent Limitations of Autoregressive Decoding:** Despite their impressive scale and emerging capabilities, state-of-the-art LLMs fundamentally generate text one token at a time in a linear fashion. This simple mechanism, while effective for many generative tasks, proves insufficient for complex problems that demand more sophisticated cognitive processes like planning, strategic search, or iterative refinement.
- **Challenges in Complex Tasks:** Tasks requiring non-trivial planning, lookahead, or where early decisions profoundly impact subsequent steps (e.g., mathematical puzzles, creative composition, constrained combinatorial problems) often cause LLMs to "fall short" with standard prompting techniques. They struggle to explore diverse alternatives, evaluate the long-term prospects of intermediate steps, or backtrack when a path proves unfruitful.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** Drawing from dual-process models in human cognition, the authors hypothesize that augmenting LLMs' fast, associative "System 1" (token-level choices) with a slow, deliberate "System 2" planning process could unlock significantly stronger problem-solving abilities. This "System 2" would involve maintaining diverse alternatives and actively evaluating current status with lookahead and backtracking.
- **Generalization of Existing Methods:** The authors observe that popular techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Self-Consistency with CoT (CoT-SC) are still essentially linear or ensemble-based applications of CoT. There's a clear need for a more general paradigm that explicitly supports non-linear exploration of reasoning paths.
- **Need for LM-driven Heuristics:** Classical search methods require heuristics to navigate vast problem spaces. Historically, these heuristics were either hand-programmed or learned through extensive training. The motivation here is to demonstrate that LLMs, given their ability to reason in natural language, can self-generate and self-evaluate these heuristics, making the planning process more flexible and accessible without additional training.
- **Demonstration on Challenging Benchmarks:** To unequivocally prove the framework's value, the authors motivate the creation of novel tasks (Game of 24, Creative Writing, Mini Crosswords) that are specifically designed to expose the shortcomings of existing LLM inference methods and highlight the necessity for deliberate search.

In essence, the research is driven by the ambition to push LLMs beyond being mere "text generators" to becoming more sophisticated and "deliberate problem solvers" by explicitly incorporating structured search and self-reflection into their inference process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Tree of Thoughts" (ToT) framework conceptualizes problem-solving as a **search over a tree structure**, where each node represents a `state` (a partial solution, comprising the input and a sequence of intermediate `thoughts`) and branches correspond to potential `thoughts` that serve as coherent, intermediate steps toward the solution. The methodology is defined by answering four key questions:

1. **Thought Decomposition:**
 - This step defines the granularity of "thoughts." Unlike Chain-of-Thought, where intermediate steps can be ambiguous, ToT explicitly designs thoughts based on the problem structure.
 - Thoughts must be "small" enough for the LLM to generate diverse and promising samples, yet "big" enough for the LLM to meaningfully evaluate their progress toward the problem solution.
 - Examples: In Game of 24, a thought is an intermediate equation. For Creative Writing, it's a short writing plan. In Mini Crosswords, it's a word to fill in for a clue.

2. **Thought Generation `G(pθ, s, k)`:**
 - Given a current state `s`, the LLM generates `k` candidate thoughts for the next step. Two strategies are employed:
 - **Sample i.i.d. thoughts:** Used when the thought space is rich and diverse (e.g., generating multiple creative writing plans). The LM is prompted with a CoT-style prompt to sample `k` independent continuations.
 - **Propose thoughts sequentially:** Used when the thought space is more constrained (e.g., specific arithmetic operations in Game of 24, or words for a crossword clue). A "propose prompt" is used to generate multiple distinct ideas within the same context, avoiding duplication.

3. **State Evaluator `V(pθ, S)`:**
 - This is a crucial innovation: the LLM itself acts as a heuristic function to evaluate the promisingness of different states (partial solutions). This is distinct from programmed or learned heuristics. Two strategies are considered:
 - **Value each state independently:** A "value prompt" asks the LLM to assign a scalar value (e.g., 1-10) or a classification (e.g., "sure," "likely," "impossible") to a state. This evaluation can incorporate short "lookahead" simulations (e.g., quickly checking if numbers can reach 24) or commonsense reasoning (e.g., identifying impossible word combinations). This helps prune "bad" states and promote "good" ones. The LM can be prompted multiple times to aggregate results for more robust evaluation.
 - **Vote across states:** A "vote prompt" asks the LLM to compare several states and select the most promising one. This is suitable when direct numerical valuation is difficult (e.g., judging passage coherency). It's akin to a "step-wise self-consistency" where the LM "votes" on which path to explore.

4. **Search Algorithm:**
 - ToT is modular, allowing various search algorithms to be plugged in. The paper explores two:
 - **Breadth-First Search (BFS):** (Algorithm 1) Maintains a set of the `b` most promising states at each step. This is suitable for problems with a limited tree depth where initial thoughts can be effectively evaluated and pruned (e.g., Game of 24, Creative Writing).
 - **Depth-First Search (DFS):** (Algorithm 2) Explores the most promising path aggressively. It continues exploring until a solution is found or the state evaluator deems the current path unpromising (pruning the subtree). If pruned, it backtracks to the parent state and explores the next alternative. This is suited for deeper search problems (e.g., Mini Crosswords).

**Conceptual Benefits:**
The ToT framework offers several advantages:
- **Generality:** It subsumes existing methods like IO, CoT, and CoT-SC as special cases with limited depth and breadth.
- **Modularity:** The base LM, thought decomposition, generation, evaluation, and search components can all be independently varied and optimized.
- **Adaptability:** It can be customized to different problem properties, LLM capabilities, and computational resource constraints.
- **Convenience:** It requires no additional training, relying solely on a pre-trained LLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the Tree of Thoughts (ToT) framework across three novel and challenging tasks, each designed to test different aspects of deliberate problem-solving with GPT-4:

1. **Game of 24 (Mathematical Reasoning):**
 - **Task:** Use four given numbers and basic arithmetic operations to reach the target of 24. Requires deductive reasoning and combinatorial search.
 - **Baselines:** Standard Input-Output (IO), Chain-of-Thought (CoT), and CoT with Self-Consistency (CoT-SC) performed poorly, achieving success rates of 7.3%, 4.0%, and 9.0% respectively. Even the "best of 100" CoT samples only reached 49% success.
 - **ToT Results:** ToT significantly outperformed baselines. With a breadth limit `b=1` (exploring only the single most promising path at each step), ToT achieved a 45% success rate. Increasing the breadth to `b=5` (exploring 5 paths concurrently) boosted the success rate to an impressive **74%**.
 - **Error Analysis:** CoT failed early, with approximately 60% of samples failing after the very first intermediate step, indicating its struggle with initial decision-making in a linear fashion. ToT's ability to explore multiple initial thoughts and prune unpromising ones was critical.

2. **Creative Writing (Open-ended Generation & Planning):**
 - **Task:** Generate a coherent four-paragraph passage, with each paragraph ending in a specific, given random sentence. This task requires high-level planning, structural coherence, and creative thinking.
 - **Evaluation:** Assessed using an automatic GPT-4 coherence score (1-10) and human judgments via blind pair-wise comparisons.
 - **Baselines:** IO and zero-shot CoT (which generated a plan first) achieved average GPT-4 coherence scores of 6.19 and 6.93, respectively.
 - **ToT Results:** ToT (which generated 5 plans, voted for the best, then generated 5 passages from that plan, and voted for the best) achieved the highest average GPT-4 coherence score of **7.56**.
 - **Human Judgment:** Human evaluators preferred ToT-generated passages over CoT in 41% of cases, while only preferring CoT in 21% (38% were judged as similarly coherent).
 - **Refinement:** Iterative refinement further improved scores, suggesting that refinement could be another form of "thought generation" within the ToT framework.

3. **Mini Crosswords (Constrained Lexical Search):**
 - **Task:** Solve 5x5 crossword puzzles given clues. This involves iterative, constrained search and the ability to backtrack.
 - **Baselines:** IO and CoT performed poorly, with word-level success rates below 16%. They lacked mechanisms for dynamic adjustments, backtracking, or trying different clues systematically.
 - **ToT Results:** Using a Depth-First Search (DFS) strategy, ToT achieved a word-level success rate of **60%** and solved 4 out of 20 games completely.
 - **Oracle Analysis:** If the best possible DFS state (regardless of the evaluation heuristic) was chosen, the word-level success rose to 67.5% and 7/20 games were solved, indicating room for improvement in the LM's self-evaluation heuristics.
 - **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing pruning (`-prune`) generally worsened performance, but it *did* find solutions for some games that the pruned ToT couldn't, highlighting the imperfection of the LM's self-evaluation.
 - Removing backtracking (`-backtrack`), essentially a greedy BFS with b=1, drastically reduced word-level success to 20%, unequivocally demonstrating the critical importance of systematic search and backtracking for this task.

**Overall Finding:** The consistent and substantial performance improvements across all three diverse and challenging tasks clearly demonstrate that the Tree of Thoughts framework enables LLMs to perform deliberate problem-solving beyond the capabilities of traditional autoregressive decoding and even advanced Chain-of-Thought prompting. The modularity of ToT, allowing different decomposition, generation, evaluation, and search strategies, proves highly adaptable and effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Tree of Thoughts" (ToT) framework represents a significant advancement in the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in their ability to tackle complex, multi-step reasoning and problem-solving tasks.

**Significance of the Work:**

- **Bridging Modern LLMs and Classical AI:** ToT effectively marries the powerful generative and reasoning capabilities of contemporary LLMs (like GPT-4) with foundational principles of classical AI, specifically the concept of problem-solving as a search through a combinatorial tree space (as pioneered by Newell, Shaw, and Simon). This integration allows LLMs to move beyond mere pattern recognition and associative generation towards more deliberate and structured reasoning.
- **Enabling "System 2" Cognition in LLMs:** By introducing structured search, explicit intermediate "thoughts," and language-based self-evaluation, ToT endows LLMs with an analogue to human "System 2" thinking—slow, deliberate, and conscious problem-solving. This is a crucial step towards building more robust and human-like AI.
- **Generalized Inference Framework:** ToT is presented as a generalization of existing popular methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT). It provides a flexible and modular paradigm where various components (thought definition, generation, evaluation, and search algorithms) can be adapted to different problem types and constraints, making it a versatile tool for diverse applications.
- **LLM-as-Heuristic Paradigm:** A key innovation is leveraging the LLM itself to generate and evaluate search heuristics. Unlike traditional search algorithms that rely on programmed or learned heuristic functions, ToT uses the LLM's natural language reasoning capabilities for self-assessment, which is both flexible and powerful, especially for problems that are not easily formalized.
- **Demonstrated Superiority on Challenging Tasks:** The paper effectively demonstrates ToT's superiority over state-of-the-art baselines (including GPT-4 with CoT) on tasks specifically designed to require deliberate planning and search, proving its practical utility and highlighting the limitations of current LLM inference mechanisms.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Problem-Solving Capabilities:** ToT promises to unlock LLMs' potential for a wider array of complex applications requiring multi-step planning, strategic decision-making, and robust reasoning. This includes domains like advanced code generation, scientific discovery, complex data analysis, and even autonomous agent control (e.g., in robotics or virtual environments).
- **Improved Reliability and Interpretability:** By explicitly exploring multiple reasoning paths and self-evaluating choices, ToT's process becomes more transparent and interpretable than single-shot generation. The "thoughts" are coherent language sequences, allowing human users to trace the model's decision-making process, which is critical for trust and debugging.
- **New Avenues for AI Research:** The success of ToT encourages further research into integrating symbolic AI, classical search, and planning algorithms with large neural models. Future work could explore more sophisticated search algorithms (e.g., A*, Monte Carlo Tree Search) with LLM-driven heuristics, or investigate how to fine-tune LLMs specifically for "Tree of Thoughts"-style high-level counterfactual decision-making rather than just next-token prediction.
- **Practical Deployment:** While computationally more expensive than simpler prompting (a acknowledged limitation), the authors argue that the cost will decrease with more efficient and cheaper LLMs (especially open-source models). The performance gains justify the increased computational demands for critical applications.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The "Broader Impact" statement briefly acknowledges that empowering LLMs with more autonomous decision-making capabilities could potentially facilitate harmful uses. However, it also points to the increased interpretability offered by ToT's explicit reasoning paths as a potential benefit for human alignment and control.

In conclusion, "Tree of Thoughts" represents a significant conceptual and empirical leap forward, pushing LLMs from being associative generators towards becoming truly deliberate and strategic problem solvers, opening up vast possibilities for their future applications.
