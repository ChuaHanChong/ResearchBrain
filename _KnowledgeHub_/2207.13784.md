# AvatarPoser: Articulated Full-Body Pose Tracking from Sparse Motion Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> AvatarPoser enables real-time, full-body pose tracking for virtual avatars by using only a Mixed Reality headset and hand controller input. It demonstrates state-of-the-art accuracy in predicting joint rotations, positions, and motion smoothness on large motion capture datasets while operating at high inference speeds.

## Problem

- Mixed Reality (MR) environments typically track only head and hands, resulting in "floating avatar" representations that limit user embodiment and immersion.
- Reconstructing a full-body pose from sparse input (headset and hand controllers) is an underdetermined problem, leading to challenges in accuracy and realism.
- Existing solutions either demand additional body-worn sensors, produce unnatural motions with Inverse Kinematics (IK), or implicitly rely on unavailable pelvis tracking.

## Method

- A Transformer-based deep learning model processes a time series of headset and hand motion data to predict local rotations for 22 body joints.
- A Stabilizer module estimates a robust global orientation for the pelvis, decoupling it from independent head rotations for more natural full-body motion.
- ==An Inverse Kinematics (IK)== post-processing module refines arm joint rotations to precisely match the predicted hand positions with the original sparse tracking input.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art accuracy on the AMASS dataset for full-body pose tracking using only three inputs (headset, two controllers), with a Mean Per Joint Rotation Error (MPJRE) of **3.21**° and Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) of **4.18** cm.
- Demonstrated robust generalization across various motion capture datasets (CMU, BMLrub, HDM05) during cross-dataset evaluations, consistently outperforming baselines.
- The Inverse Kinematics (IK) module refined hand positions, reducing their specific error by approximately **41%** (from **3.17** cm to **1.86** cm), while the system maintained real-time inference speeds (up to 662 fps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling global body orientation estimation from potentially independent head movements via a dedicated Stabilizer module leads to more robust full-body pose predictions.
> - Combining a data-driven Transformer network with a post-processing Inverse Kinematics (IK) step effectively balances realistic full-body motion with precise end-effector control for hands.
> - Utilizing a Transformer encoder is effective for capturing long-range temporal dependencies in sparse time-series motion data, crucial for generating fluid human-like movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Interaction in contemporary Mixed Reality (MR) environments predominantly relies on tracking the user's head pose and hand movements. While this input is sufficient for many first-person interactions, it results in virtual representations limited to the upper body, commonly manifesting as "floating avatars." This limitation impacts immersion and the fidelity of collaborative virtual experiences.

The estimation of a full-body pose from such sparse input sources presents an underdetermined problem in computer graphics and vision. Prior research has attempted to address this challenge through various approaches:
- **Augmenting Input with Additional Trackers:** Some methods incorporate extra sensors, such as 6D pelvis trackers or multiple body-worn inertial measurement units (IMUs). While these approaches improve pose estimation accuracy, they increase setup complexity and restrict applicability in mobile or less controlled environments, which is contrary to the trend of future MR systems optimizing for portability.
- **Learning-Based Methods from Sparse Input:**
 - CoolMoves employed a K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)-based method using only headset and hand controller input. However, its reliance on interpolating poses from smaller, specific motion datasets raises questions about its scalability to larger datasets with diverse activities and subjects.
 - LoBSTr utilized a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network to predict lower-body poses from head, hand, and pelvis tracking signals, while the upper-body pose was solved using an Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver. This work acknowledged the inherent difficulty of accurate full-body estimation from only three input sources due to significant ambiguity.
 - Dittadi et al. (VAE-HMD) proposed a Variational Autoencoder (VAE)-based method to generate plausible body poses from sparse input. A notable limitation, however, is that this method implicitly assumed knowledge of the pelvis pose by encoding all joint positions relative to the pelvis, thus not fully solving the ill-posed problem with only three direct inputs.
- **General Inverse Kinematics (IK):** Traditional IK solutions, often used for full-body pose estimation, can produce static and unnatural human motion, particularly for joints distant from the known end-effector locations in the kinematic chain.
- **Transformer Models:** Transformer architectures have demonstrated significant advancements across various domains, including natural language processing and a range of computer vision tasks such as image classification, object detection, and 3D human pose estimation. These models are notable for their ability to capture long-range dependencies in data. However, previous Transformer-based pose estimation methods often operate in offline settings or with richer input data.
- **Hybrid Learning and IK Approaches:** Recent work has explored combining deep learning with IK optimization to leverage the strengths of both, aiming for more robust and flexible solutions.

AvatarPoser positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing the challenge of robust, real-time full-body pose tracking using *only the sparse motion input from an MR headset and hands/controllers*. It seeks to overcome the limitations of prior learning-based methods that implicitly rely on additional inputs (like pelvis pose) and traditional IK approaches that produce unnatural motion, by integrating a Transformer-based deep learning model with an IK refinement step.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current Mixed Reality (MR) systems in providing holistic user representation.
- **Limited Avatar Representation:** Current MR head-mounted displays (HMDs) typically track only the user's head and hands (either through articulated hand tracking or hand-held controllers). While sufficient for basic interaction, this sparse input restricts virtual avatars to an upper-body representation, often perceived as "floating" figures. This diminishes the sense of embodiment and presence in virtual environments, which is particularly evident when users view their own virtual bodies or engage in collaborative tasks with other participants represented as upper-body avatars.
- **Need for Holistic Avatars:** Applications such as telepresence and virtual meetings could benefit from more complete and realistic avatar representations to enhance user immersion and social interaction fidelity.
- **Hardware Constraints:** It is anticipated that future MR systems will continue to prioritize mobility, implying that the available tracking information will likely remain sparse (e.g., primarily head and hand tracking). Relying on comprehensive body tracking, such as that provided by dedicated motion capture setups, would be impractical for widespread mobile use outside controlled environments.
- **Limitations of Existing Solutions:**
 1. **Unnatural Motion from IK:** General-purpose Inverse Kinematics (IK) methods, when applied to sparse inputs, frequently generate human motions that appear static or unnatural, especially for joints far from the known input locations in the kinematic chain.
 2. **Implicit Pelvis Assumption:** Many existing deep learning-based methods for full-body estimation from sparse inputs implicitly assume knowledge of the pelvis pose by expressing joint rotations relative to it. However, pelvis tracking is often unavailable in portable MR systems, making these methods less practical for the target application scenario.
 3. **Jitter and Sliding Artifacts:** Even when a pelvis tracker is used, its placement on the abdomen can lead to movement discrepancies compared to the actual pelvis joint, resulting in jitter or sliding artifacts in the estimated lower-body animations.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the AvatarPoser research are:
- To develop a novel learning-based method capable of predicting full-body poses in world coordinates using **only motion input from the user's head and hands** (or hand-held controllers).
- To design a system that effectively **decouples global motion from learned local joint orientations** to ensure robust pose estimation, particularly in the absence of additional inputs like pelvis tracking.
- To incorporate an **optimization routine with inverse kinematics** to refine the arm joints' positions, matching the original tracking input for the hands and thereby achieving accurate end-effector control while maintaining realistic full-body motion.
- To achieve **state-of-the-art accuracy** in full-body avatar estimation on large motion capture datasets.
- To ensure the method operates at an **inference speed suitable for real-time applications**, providing a practical interface for holistic avatar control in MR environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AvatarPoser addresses the problem of reconstructing the position of an articulated full-body skeleton (specifically, the first 22 joints of the SMPL model, excluding fingers) within world coordinates, given the global positions and orientations of the headset and hands/controllers over a sequence of past frames.

**Input and Output Representation:**
- **Input:** The system processes the 6D position and orientation data from the sparse trackers. Orientations (initially axis-angle) are converted to a 6D rotation representation (by discarding the last row of a 3x3 rotation matrix) for continuity during neural network training. Additionally, linear and angular velocities are calculated via backward finite differences at each time step. The final input for each time step is a concatenated vector comprising the position, linear velocity, 6D rotation, and 6D angular velocity for each of the three sparse inputs (headset, left hand, right hand), resulting in a 54-dimensional feature vector per time step.
- **Output:** The network's output consists of the local rotation (in 6D representation) for each of the 22 body joints relative to its parent joint in the kinematic chain. The rotation at the pelvis (the root joint of the SMPL model) represents the global orientation. The total output dimension per time step is 132 (22 joints * 6 dimensions).

**Overall Framework:** AvatarPoser is structured as a time series network, processing a sequence of N frames (N-1 past frames and the current N-th frame). It comprises four main components:

1. **Transformer Encoder:**
 - **Function:** Extracts deep pose features from the time-series input signals from the headset and hands.
 - **Mechanism:** Input signals are linearly embedded into a 256-dimensional feature space. A Transformer Encoder, consisting of 3 self-attention layers with 8 heads, is then applied to these features. The self-attention mechanism is utilized to capture long-range temporal dependencies in the motion data.
 - **Output:** The extracted deep pose features are subsequently fed into both the Stabilizer and a 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for local pose estimation.

2. **Stabilizer:**
 - **Function:** Estimates the global orientation of the human body, specifically the rotation of the pelvis (the root of the SMPL model). It is responsible for global motion navigation.
 - **Mechanism:** A 2-layer MLP (with 256 nodes in the hidden layer) takes the 256-dimensional pose features from the Transformer Encoder as input.
 - **Rationale:** Directly deriving global orientation from the head's pose can be problematic because head rotation is often independent of overall body motion. This can lead to significant errors in global orientation, resulting in an unnatural "floating avatar." The Stabilizer learns a more robust global orientation by leveraging the richer, context-aware features extracted by the Transformer, decoupling it from potentially sensitive head movements. Global translation of the pelvis is determined through the body's kinematic chain from the headset's position.

3. **Forward-Kinematics (FK) Module:**
 - **Function:** Computes the 3D positions of all body joints based on a human skeleton model (SMPL) and the predicted local joint rotations (and global pelvis orientation).
 - **Role:** While the primary network output is rotation-based, the FK module is crucial during training. It allows the loss function to consider the actual resulting joint positions, thus minimizing positional errors that accumulate along the kinematic chain and preventing unrealistic bone stretching or invalid configurations.

4. **Inverse-Kinematics (IK) Module:**
 - **Function:** Refines the estimated arm joint rotations (shoulder and elbow) to ensure that the predicted hand positions precisely match the known, tracked hand positions from the input.
 - **Mechanism:** After the neural network produces its initial pose prediction, an iterative optimization algorithm (Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-3 for 5 iterations) is applied. This module fixes the shoulder position and adjusts the rotation angles of the shoulder and elbow joints. The objective is to minimize the squared L2 positional error between the ground-truth tracked hand positions and the hand positions calculated via FK using the estimated joint angles.
 - **Rationale:** Even with accurate rotation predictions, errors can accumulate along the kinematic chain, causing discrepancies between predicted and actual end-effector (hand) positions. Accurate hand positioning is critical for interaction with virtual objects in MR. This hybrid approach combines the realism of data-driven methods for full-body style with the precision of model-based optimization for end-effector control. The IK module is not included in the training stage but applied as a post-processing refinement during inference.

**Loss Function:**
The total loss function comprises three components:
- L1 local rotational loss: Measures error in local joint rotations.
- L1 global orientation loss: Measures error in global pelvis orientation.
- L1 positional loss: Measures error in joint positions after forward kinematics.
The loss is formulated as: L_total = λ_ori * L_ori + λ_rot * L_rot + λ_fk * L_fk. The weights are set to λ_ori = 0.05, λ_rot = 1, and λ_fk = 1.

**Data Preparation and Training:**
- **Dataset:** The AMASS dataset, specifically its CMU, BMLrub, and HDM05 subsets, is utilized. These datasets contain motion capture data converted to SMPL model parameters.
- **Split:** Data is split into 90% for training and 10% for testing.
- **Sampling:** The frame rate is unified to 60 Hz, and input sequences (chunks) of 40 frames are used.
- **Optimization:** The Adam optimizer is used with a batch size of 256. The learning rate is initialized at 1e-4 and decays by a factor of 0.5 every 20,000 iterations.
- **Hardware:** Training is performed on a single NVIDIA GeForce GTX 3090 GPU, with a training duration of approximately two hours.

**Evaluation:**
Performance is assessed using standard metrics: Mean Per Joint Rotation Error (MPJRE in degrees), Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE in cm), and Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE in cm/s). The proposed AvatarPoser is compared against state-of-the-art methods including Final IK, CoolMoves, LoBSTr, and VAE-HMD. For fair comparison, these methods are either reproduced or adapted and trained/tested on the same datasets, considering both three (headset, two controllers) and four (headset, two controllers, pelvis tracker) input scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AvatarPoser against state-of-the-art methods on the AMASS dataset demonstrated its performance capabilities and robustness.

**Quantitative Performance on AMASS Dataset (Table 1):**
AvatarPoser achieved new state-of-the-art results across all evaluated metrics for both three and four input scenarios.
- **Three Inputs (Headset, Two Controllers):**
 - MPJRE: 3.21° (compared to VAE-HMD 4.11°, CoolMoves 5.20°, LoBSTr 10.69°, Final IK 16.77°).
 - MPJPE: 4.18 cm (compared to VAE-HMD 6.83 cm, CoolMoves 7.83 cm, LoBSTr 9.02 cm, Final IK 18.09 cm).
 - MPJVE: 29.40 cm/s (compared to VAE-HMD 37.99 cm/s, LoBSTr 44.97 cm/s, CoolMoves 100.54 cm/s, Final IK 59.24 cm/s).
 These results indicate AvatarPoser's superior accuracy in predicting both joint rotations and positions, as well as the smoothness of the generated motion. VAE-HMD generally performed as the second-best method for MPJPE. Methods relying heavily on traditional IK, such as Final IK and LoBSTr (for upper body), showed comparatively higher errors, which suggests the benefits of data-driven learning for realistic full-body motion generation.

- **Four Inputs (Headset, Two Controllers, Pelvis Tracker):**
 - AvatarPoser also demonstrated superior performance in the four-input scenario, maintaining its lead with MPJRE of 2.59°, MPJPE of 2.61 cm, and MPJVE of 22.16 cm/s, further indicating its robust architecture.

**Ablation Studies (Table 3):**
Ablation studies were conducted on the HDM05 test set to evaluate the contribution of each component:
- **No Stabilizer:** Removing the Stabilizer module resulted in an increase in MPJPE (from 8.05 cm to 9.29 cm). This supports the Stabilizer's role in providing a more robust global orientation by learning it from the Transformer features rather than directly deriving it from the head's possibly independent rotation.
- **Predict Pelvis Position (direct regression):** Directly regressing the global translation of the pelvis within the network, instead of computing it from the head position via the kinematic chain, led to worse performance (MPJPE increased from 8.05 cm to 8.82 cm). This indicates that the kinematic chain approach for global translation was more effective.
- **No FK Module:** When the FK Module was removed, MPJPE increased (from 8.05 cm to 8.41 cm), while MPJRE slightly decreased (from 6.39° to 6.24°). This suggests that optimizing rotations without considering the resultant joint positions via FK can lead to less accurate overall body configurations despite potentially smaller rotational errors.
- **No IK Module:** Removing the IK Module had a negligible impact on the overall full-body MPJPE. However, the mean position error specifically for the hands increased significantly by approximately 41% (from 1.86 cm to 3.17 cm). This confirms the IK module's effectiveness in accurately refining end-effector positions.

**Running Time Analysis (Fig. 6):**
- AvatarPoser demonstrated a favorable trade-off between inference speed and pose accuracy. While specific network inference time for AvatarPoser alone is not detailed in the text, the abstract states "up to 662 fps" (~1.5 ms per frame). The iterative IK algorithm adds approximately 6 ms per iteration. With 5 iterations, this amounts to an additional ~30 ms. The overall inference performance supports real-time operation.

**Commercial VR System Test (Fig. 7):**
- Qualitative tests performed on live recordings from an HTC VIVE HMD and two controllers demonstrated AvatarPoser's ability to generalize to real-world, unseen user input from a commercial VR system, producing reasonable full-body poses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AvatarPoser represents a significant advancement in the field of human pose estimation for Mixed Reality environments, particularly for its ability to generate full-body avatar poses from highly sparse input.

- **Addressing a Core MR Limitation:** The work directly tackles the problem of "floating avatars" in current MR systems by enabling holistic avatar representation using only the widely available head and hand tracking data. This is crucial for enhancing the sense of embodiment and presence, which are foundational elements for immersive experiences in Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR).
- **Reduced Hardware Requirements:** A key strength of AvatarPoser is its independence from additional body-worn sensors (such as pelvis trackers or IMUs). This eliminates the need for extra hardware, simplifies setup, and removes constraints on user movement, making full-body tracking more accessible and practical for mobile and consumer-grade MR systems.
- **Enhanced Realism and Accuracy:** By combining a Transformer-based deep learning approach with a targeted Inverse Kinematics (IK) refinement, AvatarPoser achieves a balance between stylistic realism for the entire body and precise control for critical end-effectors like hands. This hybrid methodology improves upon traditional IK methods that often produce unnatural motions and addresses limitations of previous learning-based techniques that implicitly relied on additional inputs.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The method's ability to decouple global motion and its consistent performance across diverse motion capture datasets, coupled with successful qualitative tests on real-world VR system data, demonstrate its robustness and generalization capabilities to various user movements and environments.
- **Real-time Applicability:** With an inference speed capable of supporting real-time operation, AvatarPoser is suitable for immediate deployment in interactive MR applications. This practical aspect is vital for dynamic user experiences, such as telepresence, collaborative virtual spaces, and productivity meetings in the envisioned Metaverse.
- **Methodological Contribution:** The introduction of a Transformer-based architecture specifically designed for streaming sparse input, coupled with a Stabilizer module for robust global orientation estimation and an IK module for end-effector refinement, offers a novel methodological framework for addressing underdetermined pose estimation problems.

In summary, AvatarPoser offers a practical, high-performance solution for a long-standing challenge in MR, with the potential to significantly improve user experience, foster deeper immersion, and facilitate more natural interactions in current and future AR/VR applications and the broader Metaverse.
