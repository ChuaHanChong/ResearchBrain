# AvatarPoser: Articulated Full-Body Pose Tracking from Sparse Motion Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> AvatarPoser enables real-time, full-body pose tracking for virtual avatars by using only a Mixed Reality headset and hand controller input. It demonstrates state-of-the-art accuracy in predicting joint rotations, positions, and motion smoothness on large motion capture datasets while operating at high inference speeds.

## Problem

- Mixed Reality (MR) environments typically track only head and hands, resulting in "floating avatar" representations that limit user embodiment and immersion.
- Reconstructing a full-body pose from sparse input (headset and hand controllers) is an underdetermined problem, leading to challenges in accuracy and realism.
- Existing solutions either demand additional body-worn sensors, produce unnatural motions with Inverse Kinematics (IK), or implicitly rely on unavailable pelvis tracking.

## Method

- A Transformer-based deep learning model processes a time series of headset and hand motion data to predict local rotations for 22 body joints.
- A Stabilizer module estimates a robust global orientation for the pelvis, decoupling it from independent head rotations for more natural full-body motion.
- ==An Inverse Kinematics (IK)== post-processing module refines arm joint rotations to precisely match the predicted hand positions with the original sparse tracking input.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art accuracy on the AMASS dataset for full-body pose tracking using only three inputs (headset, two controllers), with a Mean Per Joint Rotation Error (MPJRE) of **3.21**° and Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) of **4.18** cm.
- Demonstrated robust generalization across various motion capture datasets (CMU, BMLrub, HDM05) during cross-dataset evaluations, consistently outperforming baselines.
- The Inverse Kinematics (IK) module refined hand positions, reducing their specific error by approximately **41%** (from **3.17** cm to **1.86** cm), while the system maintained real-time inference speeds (up to 662 fps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling global body orientation estimation from potentially independent head movements via a dedicated Stabilizer module leads to more robust full-body pose predictions.
> - Combining a data-driven Transformer network with a post-processing Inverse Kinematics (IK) step effectively balances realistic full-body motion with precise end-effector control for hands.
> - Utilizing a Transformer encoder is effective for capturing long-range temporal dependencies in sparse time-series motion data, crucial for generating fluid human-like movements.
