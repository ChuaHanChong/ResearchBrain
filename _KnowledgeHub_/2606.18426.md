# VEGA: Learning Navigation VLAs from In-the-Wild Egocentric Video with Geometric Trajectory Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> VEGA is a method for training navigation Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using unlabeled egocentric videos with geometric trajectory supervision. It produces policies capable of robust obstacle avoidance and multimodal goal reaching by leveraging reconstructed scene geometry for rich training data.

## Problem

- General mobile robot navigation lacks large-scale, diverse datasets providing explicit goal-conditioned, obstacle-aware trajectories in cluttered, real-world environments.
- Abundant internet-scale egocentric videos are not directly usable for robot policy learning due to the absence of robot actions, explicit navigation goals, and obstacle-aware trajectories.
- Current navigation VLAs excel at semantic understanding but often lack the precise geometric reasoning needed for safe close-range obstacle avoidance, while classical planners lack open-vocabulary semantic capabilities.

## Method

- **VEGA** reconstructs dense 3D geometry, extracts obstacles and free space, and generates diverse multimodal goals (text, image-region, waypoint) from unlabeled egocentric video frames.
- A ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) planner generates obstacle-aware, goal-conditioned waypoint trajectories for each scene using a Euclidean Signed Distance Field (ESDF) for safety guidance.
- A multimodal goal-conditioned VLA (based on {pi}₀.₅) is trained using a flow-matching action expert, leveraging a frozen vision-language backbone and learning continuous, multimodal action distributions from the generated trajectories.

## Results

- On the VEGA-Bench evaluation, VEGA reduced collisions by **33.0%** (**4.52%**) and improved obstacle clearance by **17.9%** (**2.77**) compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- In real-world robot deployment, VEGA achieved at least a **150.0%** improvement in success rate, a **66.7%** reduction in collisions, and a **60.0%** improvement in obstacle clearance over VLA baselines.
- VEGA demonstrated strong multimodal goal reaching capabilities, achieving **1.0** success for text and image goals, and **0.8** for waypoint goals on VEGA-Bench, producing distinct trajectories for different goal specifications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric reasoning and planning (e.g., ESDF, MPPI) can be used offline during training to effectively distill obstacle-aware behavior into a purely vision-based policy for real-time deployment.
> - Abundant, unlabeled egocentric videos, despite their inherent lack of robot actions or explicit goals, can be transformed into a rich source of geometric and semantic supervision for VLA navigation.
> - Employing multimodal goal anchoring and a flow-matching objective helps policies learn robust goal grounding and diverse action distributions, mitigating goal-conditioned mode collapse.
