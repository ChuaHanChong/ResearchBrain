# Integrating Model-based Control and RL for Sim2Real Transfer of Tight Insertion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Rutgers University developed a hybrid control framework for robotic tight insertion tasks, integrating a potential field-based controller with a residual reinforcement learning policy. This approach enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving success rates exceeding 90% in real-world experiments on diverse objects without requiring real-world fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Robotic assembly tasks requiring sub-millimeter precision are challenging due to the difficulty of transferring policies trained in simulation to the real world.
- Tight insertion tasks suffer from complex contact dynamics, high precision requirements, and pervasive sensor noise that are difficult to model accurately.
- Traditional approaches struggle with either relying too heavily on precise models (model-based) or requiring extensive real-world data and fine-tuning (data-driven).

## Method

- Rutgers University researchers developed a hybrid control framework that integrates a potential field-based controller with a residual reinforcement learning (RL) policy.
- The potential field controller provides a foundational, deterministic insertion behavior, while the RL policy learns residual corrections to compensate for real-world uncertainties and model inaccuracies.
- A curriculum learning strategy progressively increases the difficulty during training by escalating observation noise and the magnitude of the learned RL corrections, preparing the policy for real-world conditions.

## Results

- Achieved over 90% success rates for tight insertion tasks across various object configurations in real-world zero-shot deployments, outperforming state-of-the-art RL-based methods like IndustReal.
- Demonstrated significantly improved training efficiency compared to pure reinforcement learning methods, benefiting from the strong model-based foundation.
- Validated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of policies trained entirely in simulation to real robots using vision-based perception, effectively handling realistic perception challenges and object variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a model-based potential field controller with residual reinforcement learning enables robust sim-to-real transfer for precise robotic manipulation tasks by learning only the necessary corrections.
> - A multi-dimensional curriculum learning strategy, varying observation noise and action magnitude, effectively trains policies to handle real-world uncertainties without explicit real-world data.
> - The model-based component provides a stable foundation, allowing the reinforcement learning policy to focus on fine adjustments, which significantly improves training efficiency and generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Robotic assembly tasks requiring sub-millimeter precision represent one of the most challenging problems in modern robotics. This research, conducted at Rutgers University, addresses the critical gap between simulation-trained policies and real-world deployment for tight insertion tasks. The work presents a method that integrates model-based control with residual reinforcement learning to achieve reliable sim-to-real transfer without requiring real-world fine-tuning.

The significance of this problem extends beyond academic interest. Industrial automation increasingly demands robots capable of performing precise assembly operations that traditionally required human dexterity. Tasks such as inserting electronic components, assembling mechanical parts, or fitting connectors all require the ability to handle tight tolerances while adapting to real-world uncertainties like sensor noise and contact dynamics.

### 2. The Challenge of Tight Insertion Tasks

Tight insertion tasks present unique challenges that distinguish them from other robotic manipulation problems. The primary difficulties include:

**Precision Requirements**: Successful insertion often requires positional accuracy within fractions of a millimeter. This level of precision exceeds the typical accuracy of robot positioning systems and requires sophisticated control strategies.

**Contact Dynamics**: During insertion, complex contact forces arise between the objects. These forces can provide valuable feedback but are difficult to model accurately, especially when transitioning from simulation to reality.

**Perception Uncertainty**: Real-world sensors introduce noise and uncertainty that is difficult to model precisely in simulation. Vision-based pose estimation, in particular, suffers from lighting variations, occlusions, and calibration errors.

**Generalization**: Assembly systems must handle variations in object geometry, pose, and environmental conditions without extensive retraining or manual adjustment.

Traditional approaches have typically fallen into two categories: model-based methods that rely on precise geometric models and careful engineering, and data-driven approaches that learn from experience but require extensive training data and careful reward design.

### 3. Hybrid Approach Combining Model-based Control and Reinforcement Learning

The core innovation of this work lies in combining the strengths of both model-based and learning-based approaches through a residual learning framework. The system consists of two complementary components:

**Model-based Foundation**: A potential field-based controller provides the basic insertion behavior. This controller uses the geometric understanding of the task to generate smooth trajectories that guide the plug toward the socket. The potential field method is chosen for its stability properties and ability to handle the approach and alignment phases of insertion reliably.

**Residual Learning Component**: A reinforcement learning policy learns to correct the model-based controller's actions. Rather than learning the entire insertion behavior from scratch, the RL component focuses on learning the residual corrections needed to handle uncertainties and model inaccuracies.

The mathematical formulation combines these components as:

```
a_t^total = a_t^PF + α × a_t^RL
```

where `a_t^PF` represents the potential field action, `a_t^RL` is the residual RL correction, and `α` is a learned scaling factor that determines the magnitude of the residual correction.

### 4. Methodology and Technical Implementation

The potential field controller forms the deterministic backbone of the system. It generates smooth trajectories by treating the insertion task as a navigation problem in SE(3) space. The field consists of:

- **Attractive Component**: Draws the plug toward the socket center
- **Repulsive Component**: Prevents collisions with the socket walls
- **Alignment Component**: Ensures proper orientation for insertion

The RL component uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a carefully designed observation space that includes the SE(3) poses of both plug and socket. The action space consists of SE(3) corrections to the potential field output, allowing the learned policy to make fine adjustments for successful insertion.

**Observation Space**: The system observes the relative poses between plug and socket, providing rotation and translation information in a format that facilitates learning of geometric relationships.

**Action Space**: Actions represent corrections in SE(3) space, maintaining the geometric nature of the task while allowing the RL component to learn appropriate adjustments.

**Reward Structure**: The system uses sparse, goal-reaching rewards that provide clear success/failure signals without requiring extensive reward engineering. This approach reduces the manual effort typically required for RL-based manipulation tasks.

### 5. Curriculum Learning Strategy

A key innovation is the curriculum learning approach that gradually increases task difficulty during training. The curriculum operates along two dimensions:

**Observation Noise Curriculum**: The level of noise added to pose observations increases progressively during training. This prepares the RL policy for the higher noise levels encountered in real-world deployment.

**Action Magnitude Curriculum**: The scaling factor α that determines the magnitude of RL corrections is gradually increased. This allows the policy to first learn small, safe corrections before taking on more significant adjustments.

The curriculum ensures that the RL component learns to handle the types of uncertainties it will encounter in real deployment while maintaining training stability. Early training phases focus on learning basic correction patterns under idealized conditions, while later phases prepare the system for realistic noise levels.

### 6. Experimental Results and Performance

The experimental evaluation demonstrates significant improvements over existing methods. Key results include:

**Success Rate Improvements**: The integrated approach achieves substantially higher success rates compared to IndustReal, a state-of-the-art RL-based method for insertion tasks. Success rates exceed 90% across various object configurations.

**Training Efficiency**: The hybrid approach requires significantly less training time than pure RL methods. The model-based foundation provides a strong starting point, allowing the RL component to focus on learning corrections rather than the entire insertion behavior.

**Robustness to Variations**: The method demonstrates robustness to variations in object geometry, initial poses, and environmental conditions. This generalization capability is crucial for practical deployment.

**Ablation Studies**: Systematic ablation studies confirm the importance of each component. Removing either the potential field foundation or the RL corrections results in degraded performance, validating the hybrid design.

### 7. Real-world Validation

The real-world validation represents the ultimate test of the approach's effectiveness. Key aspects include:

**Zero-shot Transfer**: Policies trained entirely in simulation transfer directly to real robots without fine-tuning. This eliminates the need for real-world data collection and significantly reduces deployment time.

**Vision-based Implementation**: The real-world system uses RGB-D cameras for pose estimation, introducing realistic perception challenges that the trained policies must handle.

**Diverse Object Set**: Validation includes various plug-socket combinations with different geometries, tolerances, and materials, demonstrating the approach's generality.

**Robust Performance**: Real-world experiments achieve success rates comparable to simulation results, confirming effective sim-to-real transfer.

### 8. Significance and Implications

This research makes several important contributions to robotic manipulation and assembly:

**Methodological Innovation**: The integration of model-based control with residual RL provides a template for combining the precision of engineered solutions with the adaptability of learning-based approaches.

**Practical Impact**: The zero-shot transfer capability significantly reduces the engineering effort required to deploy robotic assembly systems, making automation more accessible for various applications.

**Curriculum Learning Insights**: The systematic curriculum approach provides guidelines for training robust policies that can handle real-world uncertainties.

**Performance Benchmarks**: The work establishes new performance standards for tight insertion tasks and provides fair comparisons with existing methods.

The implications extend beyond assembly tasks to other precision manipulation problems where the combination of geometric understanding and adaptive learning could prove beneficial. The approach suggests a pathway toward more practical and deployable robotic systems that combine the best aspects of traditional control methods with modern learning capabilities.

Future directions include extending the approach to more complex assembly sequences, handling partial observability, and developing online adaptation mechanisms that could further improve real-world performance. The work establishes a foundation for practical robotic assembly systems that could significantly impact manufacturing and automation applications.
