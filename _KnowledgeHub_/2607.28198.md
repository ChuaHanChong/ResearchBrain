# UniCross: Unified Cross-Skill Dexterous Manipulation Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> UniCross presents a unified framework for dexterous manipulation that models grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, and in-hand translation from a shared hand-object relational perspective. This framework enables seamless skill chaining for complex long-horizon tasks, achieving success rates of up to 96.3% while demonstrating generalization to novel objects and hand morphologies and robustness to external disturbances.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous manipulation methods address individual skills (e.g., grasping, in-hand rotation) in isolation, leading to task-specific designs and limited reusability.
- This skill-specific fragmentation breaks compatibility and continuity, hindering the seamless composition of skills into long-horizon manipulation sequences.
- Prior approaches often lack generalization to novel object geometries or diverse hand morphologies, and may suffer from physical implausibility without explicit physics modeling.

## Method

- A unified framework, **UniCross**, is proposed to model four fundamental dexterous manipulation skills (grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, translation) under common state and action spaces.
- The approach involves training individual skill policies via ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== in a physics simulator, followed by distilling them into a single cross-skill policy using the ==DAgger (Dataset Aggregation)== algorithm.
- An ==interaction-aware object representation==, relational-based objective features, and a multi-component reward design enable learning diverse contact regimes and movements across skills.

## Results

- The unified policy demonstrated strong per-skill performance, often matching or surpassing specialized baselines in their respective general settings, and significantly outperforming them in more generalized scenarios.
- It exhibited robust generalization, maintaining high performance on unseen object geometries and successfully transferring skills to different hand morphologies (Allegro, MANO, Sharpa Wave).
- The framework enabled seamless execution of long-horizon tasks, achieving high success rates (**87.4%** for Grasp+Relocate+Rotate and **96.3%** for Grasp+Relocate+Translate), which was previously infeasible with isolated skill approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse dexterous manipulation skills can be effectively unified under a single framework, suggesting an intrinsic consistency and shared underlying principles across these behaviors.
> - A single distilled policy can effectively learn and combine multiple manipulation skills, demonstrating near-lossless performance compared to individual expert policies.
> - The use of an interaction-aware object representation and relational objectives allows for robust and generalizable manipulation across various object geometries and hand morphologies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation is a fundamental capability for numerous human activities and is a critical area of research in robotics, computer graphics, and virtual reality. The broader research landscape has explored various aspects of hand-object interaction (HOI) synthesis, traditionally approaching different manipulation skills in isolation. These skills include grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, and in-hand translation, which are canonical behaviors that humans combine flexibly to accomplish complex tasks.

Prior work in HOI synthesis often frames the problem as an optimization challenge, fitting existing poses or motions, or explicitly incorporating contact physics. While effective for specific manipulation problems, these methods are often computationally intensive and rely on task-specific objectives or simplified physical constraints, leading to limitations in realism or generalization. More recent data-driven learning approaches use generative models trained on captured motion datasets. However, their generalization capability is often constrained by dataset coverage, particularly for novel object geometries or hand morphologies, and they may suffer from physical implausibility without explicit physics modeling.

Reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation has emerged as a promising paradigm, offering stronger physical grounding and mitigating real-world data limitations. Nevertheless, existing RL methods in dexterous manipulation have predominantly focused on individual skills. This skill-specific focus often necessitates task-specific heuristics, reward shaping, network architectures, or exploration constraints, such as restricted action spaces, specific hand-object configurations (e.g., fixed palm-up poses for in-hand manipulation), or specialized hand morphologies. These isolated approaches lead to breaks in the compatibility and continuity of hand-object states across skills, making seamless composition into long-horizon tasks challenging or infeasible.

The present work addresses this gap by proposing a unified framework. It seeks to move beyond skill-specific designs by formulating diverse dexterous manipulation behaviors from a shared hand-object relational perspective. This approach aims to identify and leverage the intrinsic consistency and shared structures across different manipulation skills, thereby enabling a single policy to perform multiple skills and compose them into complex, long-horizon sequences. This effort aligns with the emerging field of generalist policy learning, which has seen success in character animation for diverse whole-body behaviors but remains nascent in dexterous manipulation due to the intricate dependencies on object geometry, contact configurations, and hand morphology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework for dexterous manipulation that can model four canonical skills—grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, and in-hand translation—under a single, coherent formulation. The overarching motivation stems from the observation that human hands exhibit remarkable dexterity by flexibly composing these skills to perform complex tasks, whereas existing computational and robotic approaches typically treat them in isolation.

The authors identify several specific challenges and motivations for pursuing a unified framework:
1. **Lack of Cross-Skill Compatibility and Continuity**: Current methods model skills separately with distinct action constraints, objectives, or hand morphologies. This fragmentation breaks the compatibility and continuity necessary for seamlessly chaining skills into long-horizon manipulation sequences. The motivation is to overcome these discontinuities by finding a shared underlying structure.
2. **Diverse Contact Regimes**: The skills require fundamentally different contact patterns. Grasping and relocation demand stable, persistent contacts, while in-hand rotation and translation often require frequent contact reconfiguration to balance stability with mobility. Unifying these diverse contact requirements within a shared state space, action space, and reward structure is a significant challenge that the work aims to address.
3. **Unified Object Shape Representation**: Different skills require different aspects of object shape. Grasping needs to identify stable contact regions, while in-hand manipulation requires dynamic capture of shape features for finger adjustments. The objective is to develop a unified object representation capable of serving all skills without relying on skill-specific heuristics or object-dependent tuning.
4. **Embodiment-Agnostic Framework**: Many prior works are restricted to specific hand morphologies or configurations (e.g., symmetric-finger hands, palm-up wrist poses). The motivation is to develop a framework that is embodiment-agnostic, allowing for effective transfer across different hand morphologies and operation in diverse hand-object configurations without imposing restrictive assumptions.
5. **Efficiency and Generalization**: By formulating skills under a common relational perspective, the goal is to enable the distillation of a single cross-skill policy. This policy is expected to exhibit strong per-skill performance, generalize to unseen objects, maintain robustness against disturbances, and facilitate continuous, long-horizon manipulation through seamless skill chaining.
6. **Revealing Intrinsic Consistency**: More broadly, the research aims to investigate whether different dexterous manipulation skills can be viewed as instantiations of a shared solution space, thereby revealing intrinsic consistencies across diverse behaviors. This understanding could provide a practical foundation for general-purpose hand-object motion synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a unified framework that models four dexterous manipulation skills (grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, and in-hand translation) from a shared hand-object relational perspective. This unification allows for common state and action spaces, a shared network architecture, and a consistent reward structure.

The approach involves two main phases:
1. **Per-Skill Policy Training**: Ten individual policies (one for grasping, one for relocation, six for rotation around different axes, and two for translation along different axes) are initially trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in the Isaac-Gym physics simulation environment. The simulation runs at 120 Hz, with a control frequency of 20 Hz.
2. **Unified Policy Distillation**: These independently trained per-skill policies are then distilled into a single, unified cross-skill policy using the DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) algorithm. During this process, the distilled policy is rolled out to collect states, and the corresponding expert actions from the per-skill policies are queried. The unified policy is then trained on these aggregated state-action pairs using an MSE imitation loss, with environments uniformly distributed across all valid (object, skill) combinations.

**Task Formulation:**
- **Hand and Object State Representation**: A root frame is defined at the initial wrist pose, and wrist motion is parameterized by six zero-initialized virtual joints representing movement relative to this initial pose. The hand frame is based on the current hand pose. Object poses are tracked in both the hand frame (`x_h_o`, `q_h_o`) and the root frame (`x_r_o`, `q_r_o`).
- **Skill Definitions**: Each skill is defined by specific relational movements:
 - **Grasp**: The object is fixed in the root frame, and the hand moves towards it.
 - **Relocate**: The object's pose is fixed relative to the hand, and the wrist moves in the root frame to achieve a target object pose.
 - **Rotate**: The wrist is fixed in the root frame, the object's position is fixed in the hand frame, and its orientation rotates about a designated axis in the hand frame.
 - **Translate**: The wrist is fixed in the root frame, the object's orientation is fixed in the hand frame, and its position translates along a designated axis in the hand frame.

**Observation Space (`o_t = (s_h_t, s_o_t, g_t)`)**:
- `s_h_t` (Hand State): Comprises current and previous target positions of all hand joints (finger and virtual wrist joints).
- `s_o_t` (Interaction-Aware Object Representation): An interaction-centric representation, inspired by GraspXL, including per-link contact states, contact force magnitudes, and distance vectors from each finger link to the object surface. This aims to capture hand-object spatial relationships and dynamic object geometry features relevant across skills.
- `g_t` (Relation-Based Objective Features): A 55-dimensional vector containing:
 - A 10-dimensional one-hot vector indicating the current task mode.
 - A 3-dimensional unit direction vector (`d_h_t`) in the hand frame, defining the task axis.
 - Current multi-frame relational poses (`g_current_t`).
 - Target multi-frame relational poses (`g_target_t`).

**Action Space**:
- The policy outputs incremental motion commands (`a_t`) for all finger joints and virtual wrist joints. These commands are transformed into target joint positions, clamped within anatomical limits.
- A PD controller then converts these target positions into torques applied in the simulation. This incremental parameterization is designed to facilitate smooth finger motions and accelerate wrist motion exploration.

**Reward Design (`r_t = r_goal_t + r_track_t + r_reg_t`)**:
- `r_goal_t` (Interaction Objective): Encourages stable contact (`r_contact_t` penalizing distance, rewarding contact and force magnitude) and desired object motion along the task axis (`r_motion_t`).
- `r_track_t` (Target Tracking): Penalizes deviations of hand and object from target poses in both root and hand frames (position and orientation errors).
- `r_reg_t` (Regularization): Includes penalty terms for excessive finger movements (`r_pose_t`), high wrist velocities (`r_vel_t`), high torque magnitudes (`r_energy_t`), and object dropping (`r_drop_t`).

**Relational-Based Objective Instantiation**:
- The `d_h` vector and `g_target_t` (target poses) are dynamically instantiated for each skill. Targets can be "Tracked" (updated towards objective), "Fixed" (held at initial value), or "Free" (set to current value). For instance, in grasping, the object's root-frame pose is fixed, and the target object position in the hand frame is the origin. In relocation, the object's hand-frame pose is fixed, and root-frame object and hand targets interpolate towards a final pose.

**Network Architecture and Domain Randomization**:
- A shared MLP encoder processes `g_t`, and a main MLP takes `s_h_t`, `s_o_t`, and the encoder output as input to predict actions.
- To enhance robustness, training incorporates domain randomization, including variations in object mass, friction coefficients, joint-position noise, and external forces applied to the object.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the framework's effectiveness, generalization, robustness, and capability for long-horizon manipulation.

**Per-skill Comparison**:
- The unified policy was compared against per-skill baselines (GraspXL for grasping, RotateIt for rotation, for translation, and a naive baseline for relocation) under both the baselines' original settings and a more general setting.
- Under the baselines' original, often simplified settings (e.g., no table collision for grasping, fixed wrist-up poses for in-hand manipulation), the unified policy, despite not being specifically trained for these conditions, either matched or slightly outperformed the specialized baselines.
- In the more general setting (which includes diverse hand-object configurations and table collision), the baselines for rotation and translation experienced sharp performance degradation even after retraining. This was attributed to their reliance on specific support conditions (e.g., palm support), which became less effective when the hand moved away from upward orientations.
- In contrast, the unified policy maintained high performance in the general setting across all skills, demonstrating its ability to stably hold objects and perform manipulations in varied configurations.

**Generalization**:
- **Object Property Generalization**: The policy was tested on unseen geometries (spheres, hexagonal, and octagonal prisms) after being trained solely on boxes and cylinders. It achieved comparable performance, indicating strong generalization capabilities. Qualitative observations suggested that the policy adapted its finger motion patterns to object geometry (e.g., smooth wrapping for round objects, edge-aware motions for prisms) and adjusted contact spacing and manipulation gaits to different object scales and aspect ratios.
- **Hand Morphology Generalization**: The framework was tested with two additional hand models, MANO (a human hand model) and Sharpa Wave (a robot hand). After training a unified policy for each hand (with object scaling for smaller hands), the framework achieved good overall performance across all three hand types (Allegro, MANO, Sharpa Wave). A slight performance drop for MANO was noted, attributed to its more limited joint ranges. This demonstrated the framework's morphology-agnostic generalization.

**Robustness**:
- The policy was evaluated under external disturbances, where random forces (up to 10 times object mass * gravity) were applied to the object.
- The policy exhibited robustness, maintaining stable object holding even under persistent perturbations. Rotation and translation skills showed slightly larger performance drops compared to grasping and relocation, suggesting that maintaining stable object constraint is more challenging during active relative hand-object motions.

**Long-horizon Manipulation**:
- The unified policy's cross-skill compatibility and continuity were assessed on sequential tasks: Grasp + Relocate + Rotate, and Grasp + Relocate + Translate. These tasks involved continuous execution of three phases in a single episode.
- The policy achieved consistently good overall success rates (87.4% for rotation sequence, 96.3% for translation sequence).
- The success rate for rotation in long-horizon tasks was lower than in per-skill evaluations, as relocation could lead to challenging initial orientations for subsequent rotation. Relocation performance also experienced a slight drop due to residual motion from the grasping phase.
- Despite these challenges, the results demonstrated that the unified policy could seamlessly chain skills for long-horizon manipulation, a capability noted as infeasible for prior per-skill methods.

**Ablation Study**:
- **Removal of Target Pose Observation (`W/o Tar. Obs.`)**: Led to performance degradation, though less severe than other ablations, indicating that the task axis vector (`d_h`) preserved some relational objective information.
- **Removal of Residual Wrist Action (`W/o Tar. Act.`)**: Caused significant performance drops, particularly for relocation, highlighting its importance for efficient exploration of large-amplitude wrist motions.
- **Removal of Tracking Reward (`W/o Tar. Rew.`)**: Also resulted in notable degradation for relocation, emphasizing the necessity of dense pose-tracking supervision. Other skills were less affected due to the continued presence of `r_goal_t`.
- **Comparison with Per-Skill Experts**: The unified policy's performance closely matched that of the ten individual per-skill expert policies it was distilled from. This indicated a near-lossless distillation process, suggesting that the learned motions were compatible and non-conflicting across skills, allowing a single network to capture them.
- **Removal of Distance Vectors (`W/o Distance`) and Contact States (`W/o Contact`)**: Removing these components from the object representation generally led to performance drops. Rotation and translation were particularly affected by the absence of contact states, confirming the critical role of the interaction-aware representation for dynamic manipulation requiring continuous fine-grained finger-object interactions. Relocation was less affected as it required less finger reconfiguration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a meaningful step toward advancing dexterous manipulation capabilities by proposing a unified framework that models diverse manipulation skills from a shared hand-object relational perspective.

The significance and potential impact of these findings are multi-faceted:

1. **Advancement in General-Purpose Dexterous Manipulation**: By demonstrating that grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, and in-hand translation can be unified under a single formulation, the research offers a practical basis for developing general-purpose manipulation policies. This contrasts with the prevalent paradigm of developing skill-specific solutions, simplifying the complexity of designing and deploying manipulation systems.
2. **Enabling Long-Horizon and Complex Tasks**: The framework's ability to facilitate seamless skill chaining into long-horizon manipulation sequences addresses a critical limitation of previous approaches. This capability is fundamental for tackling more complex, multi-stage tasks common in real-world scenarios, such as assembling objects, using tools, or performing intricate object reorientation maneuvers.
3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The demonstrated generalization to unseen object geometries and different hand morphologies, coupled with robustness to external disturbances, suggests a higher potential for real-world applicability. This reduces the need for extensive retraining for new objects or robot hands, making the approach more adaptable and practical for diverse robotic platforms and dynamic environments.
4. **Insights into Manipulation Principles**: The success of the unified formulation suggests an intrinsic consistency and shared underlying principles across seemingly distinct dexterous manipulation behaviors. This provides a theoretical contribution by proposing that these behaviors can be viewed as structured variations of a common task formulation, potentially guiding future research into more abstract and fundamental representations of manipulation.
5. **Impact on Robotics, Animation, and AR/VR**: The capabilities offered by UniCross have implications across several domains:
 - **Robotics**: Enabling more autonomous, adaptable, and versatile robotic systems for tasks requiring fine motor skills and complex object interaction.
 - **Computer Animation**: Facilitating the generation of more realistic and diverse hand-object interaction animations for virtual characters.
 - **AR/VR**: Enhancing immersive experiences by synthesizing plausible and responsive virtual hand-object interactions.

In conclusion, UniCross contributes a cohesive framework that unifies multiple fundamental dexterous manipulation skills, offering a robust, generalizable, and adaptable solution that pushes the boundaries of autonomous and intelligent interaction with objects.
