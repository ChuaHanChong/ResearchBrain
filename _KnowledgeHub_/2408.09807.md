# Reset-free Reinforcement Learning with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MoReFree adapts model-based RL (built on PEG/DreamerV2) to reset-free training via Back-and-Forth Go-Explore, cycling the agent between initial and evaluation goals, plus biased imagination training toward task-relevant states. Without environmental reward or demonstrations, it outperforms privileged reset-free baselines on 7/8 tasks and beats reset-free PEG on the 3 hardest.

## Problem

- Real-robot RL setups rely on human-engineered resets (funnels, extra arms, scripted behaviors), creating major manual-effort barriers to autonomous training.
- Prior reset-free methods are almost all model-free, suffering poor sample efficiency and weak exploration, and most still need environmental reward or demonstrations for supervision.
- Directly porting strong episodic model-based exploration (PEG) to reset-free settings causes over-exploration, wasting time on task-irrelevant states instead of the task-relevant region.

## Method

- **Back-and-Forth Go-Explore**: a data-collection curriculum sampling paired initial/evaluation goals with probability alpha, alternating Go-Explore toward each to emulate resets, interspersed with exploratory Go-Explore phases.
- ==Task-relevant imagination training==: biases the goal-conditioned policy's in-world-model rollouts to sample evaluation goals, initial-state goals, and replay-buffer goals with fixed proportions (alpha/2, alpha/2, 1-alpha).
- ==Self-supervised dynamical distance reward== (inherited from PEG) trains the goal-conditioned policy inside a learned RSSM world model without any environmental reward signal.

## Results

- **MoReFree** and reset-free PEG outperform privileged baselines (IBC, MEDAL, R3L, Oracle) in final performance and sample efficiency on **7 of 8** reset-free tasks despite using no reward or demonstrations.
- MoReFree beats reset-free PEG by large margins on the 3 hardest tasks: Push (hard) **+45%**, Pick&Place (hard) **+13%**, Ant **+36%**, collecting **1.3-5x** more task-relevant data.
- Ablations show removing Back-and-Forth Go-Explore or task-relevant imagination sampling each degrades performance sharply, confirming the two mechanisms are synergistic and necessary.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based agents with strong learned-world-model exploration can outperform model-free reset-free methods even when stripped of reward and demonstration supervision.
> - Unconstrained strong exploration is a liability without resets; exploration must be periodically anchored back to task-relevant regions or it wastes budget uniformly over state space.
> - Biasing both data collection and imagined policy training toward the same task-relevant goal set (initial and evaluation states) forms a reinforcing cycle that neither piece alone can sustain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in training intelligent agents to acquire policies through autonomous experience generation. However, a practical challenge in real-world RL applications, particularly in robotics, is the dependency on environmental resets. These resets often require substantial human intervention, involving manual repositioning of agents or objects, or the implementation of complex, scripted reset mechanisms. Examples cited include setting up funnels for dropped items or deploying auxiliary robot arms for resetting, indicating the significant effort required even for seemingly simple tasks.

The research landscape for addressing this "reset-free" training paradigm is evolving. Prior work has largely explored two main directions:
- **Learning Reset Policies**: Agents are trained to autonomously return to suitable states for further learning. This includes methods like LNT (Eysenbach et al., 2017) and Kim et al. (2022), which learn to return to initial state distributions, often supervised by dense rewards or demonstrations. Other approaches, such as MEDAL (Sharma et al., 2022; 2023) and IBC (Kim et al., 2023), employ goal-conditioned reset policies or bidirectional curricula without demonstrations. VaPRL (Sharma et al., 2021a) trains a single policy to reach high-value states.
- **Robust Policy Learning**: Methods like R3L (Zhu et al., 2020) and Xu et al. (2020) focus on training policies that are robust to diverse starting states, thereby reducing the need for resets. However, these methods are often limited to tasks where exploration is not the primary challenge.

A common characteristic of most existing reset-free RL approaches is their model-free nature. Model-free RL, while powerful, can suffer from poor sample efficiency and difficulties in exploration, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks where getting stuck in irrelevant state regions is a significant concern. Unsupervised model-based RL (MBRL) approaches, such as LEXA (Mendonca et al., 2021) and PEG (Hu et al., 2023), have demonstrated robust exploration and high data efficiency in episodic, long-horizon tasks. This paper positions itself by investigating the applicability and potential benefits of MBRL in the reset-free setting, addressing the limitations of existing model-free methods and direct applications of episodic MBRL. The work specifically aims to leverage the exploration capabilities of MBRL while mitigating its tendency towards over-exploration in task-irrelevant regions during continuous, reset-free operation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the significant human effort required to implement environmental resets in conventional RL training. The abstract and introduction highlight this as a major barrier to the real-world deployment of RL agents, particularly in robotics. The objective is to enable "reset-free" reinforcement learning, where agents can learn autonomously without manual or scripted environmental resets.

The research pursued specific objectives:

1. **Evaluate Model-Based RL in a Reset-Free Setting**: An initial objective was to determine if model-based RL (MBRL) methods, known for their strong exploration and data efficiency in episodic settings, could inherently outperform existing model-free state-of-the-art methods when adapted for reset-free tasks. This involved a straightforward adaptation of an unsupervised MBRL agent (PEG) to a continuous, reset-free task.
2. **Identify Limitations of Direct MBRL Application**: Following the initial evaluation, the researchers aimed to identify specific limitations that arise when directly extending episodic MBRL to the reset-free paradigm. The preliminary findings suggested that while MBRL could outperform baselines, it suffered from "over-exploration" in task-irrelevant regions without periodic resets. This presented a core problem to address.
3. **Propose a Solution for Task-Relevant Exploration and Policy Learning**: The central objective was to develop a novel MBRL agent, named MoReFree (Model-based, Reset-Free), that could address the over-exploration problem and enhance performance in reset-free settings. This involved adapting key mechanisms within the MBRL framework:
 - **Focused Exploration**: To develop an exploration strategy that balances discovering novel states with revisiting task-relevant areas (initial states, evaluation goals).
 - **Biased Policy Learning**: To ensure that the agent's goal-conditioned policy is effectively trained to achieve these task-relevant states within its learned world model.
4. **Demonstrate Superiority and Data Efficiency**: The ultimate objective was to empirically demonstrate that MoReFree significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art reset-free methods and adapted MBRL baselines across a range of challenging tasks, especially in terms of data efficiency and final performance, while requiring less supervision (e.g., no environmental reward or demonstrations).
5. **Provide Behavioral Analysis and Ablations**: To conduct in-depth analysis of MoReFree's exploration patterns and learned behaviors, and perform ablation studies to justify the design choices and confirm the contributions of each proposed mechanism.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need to reduce human effort in RL training by moving towards reset-free paradigms, with a specific focus on leveraging and improving model-based RL's strengths to achieve this.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves building upon an existing unsupervised model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) framework, PEG (Hu et al., 2023), and adapting it to the reset-free setting. The approach can be broadly divided into defining the reset-free RL problem, selecting an MBRL backbone, and then proposing specific modifications within the MoReFree framework.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

- **Reset-free RL Definition**: The work adopts the definition from EARL (Sharma et al., 2021b), extending it to goal-conditioned RL. The environment is modeled as a goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP) M = (S, G, A, p, r, ρ₀, ρ∗g, γ). The agent's learning algorithm is tasked with outputting actions for non-episodic data collection and an optimal policy πt for episodic evaluation. A key aspect is that environmental resets to the initial state (s₀ ~ ρ₀) occur rarely (e.g., once at the start or a few times across training).
- **MBRL Backbone (PEG)**: PEG is chosen as the foundational MBRL agent due to its strong exploration abilities and sample efficiency in episodic, long-horizon tasks. PEG is a model-based Go-Explore framework that uses a learned world model (a recurrent state-space model, RSSM) to generate synthetic data for training policies and value functions. The components are:
 - **World Model ($\hat{T}_\theta$)**: Predicts future states (s_{t+1}|s_t, a_t).
 - **Goal-Conditioned Policy ($\pi_G_\theta$)**: Trained to reach random states sampled from the replay buffer.
 - **Exploration Policy ($\pi_E_\theta$)**: Trained on an intrinsic motivation reward derived from world model error (ensemble variance, Plan2Explore objective).
 - **Self-supervised Goal-reaching Reward Function**: Instead of an environmental reward, PEG learns a dynamical distance function (Hartikainen et al., 2019) that predicts the number of actions between two states. This function is used as a reward for training $\pi_G_\theta$ in imagination to minimize the distance to commanded goals.
 - **Phased Exploration (Go-Explore)**: For data collection in the real environment, PEG uses a two-phase strategy. In the "Go"-phase, $\pi_G_\theta$ is run for H_G steps towards a sampled goal. In the "Explore"-phase, starting from the last state of the Go-phase, $\pi_E_\theta$ is run for H_E steps. Goals for exploration (ρE) are generated by planning through the world model to maximize exploration value.

#### 3.2. MoReFree's Adaptations

MoReFree introduces two key mechanisms to address the over-exploration of task-irrelevant states in the reset-free setting:

1. **Back-and-Forth Go-Explore (for real environment data collection)**:
 - This strategy modulates between various Go-Explore objectives:
 - **Task Solving (Forward)**: With probability $\alpha/2$, a goal is sampled from the evaluation goal distribution (ρ*g), and $\pi_G_\theta$ attempts to reach it.
 - **Emulating Resets (Back)**: With probability $\alpha/2$, a goal is sampled from the initial state distribution (ρ₀), and $\pi_G_\theta$ attempts to reach it. This is designed to bring the agent back to task-relevant starting regions.
 - **Exploration (General)**: With probability 1 - $\alpha$, an exploratory goal is sampled from PEG's exploratory goal distribution (ρE), and $\pi_G_\theta$ aims for it, followed by $\pi_E_\theta$.
 - These goal-conditioned behaviors are interspersed with the standard "Explore"-phase using $\pi_E_\theta$. The combination of "Back" and "Forward" trajectories with general exploration ensures the agent balances discovering novel states with staying within or returning to task-relevant areas.

2. **Learning to Achieve Relevant Goals in Imagination (for policy training)**:
 - During the imagination phase, where $\pi_G_\theta$ is trained using synthetic trajectories generated by the world model, MoReFree biases the goal sampling to focus on task-relevant goals.
 - Goals for imagination training are sampled from three distributions:
 - **Evaluation Goals ($\rho^*_g$)**: With probability $\alpha/2$.
 - **Initial States ($\rho_0$)**: With probability $\alpha/2$.
 - **Random Replay Buffer States**: With probability 1 - $\alpha$.
 - This ensures that $\pi_G_\theta$ is explicitly optimized to achieve the types of goals it will encounter during both evaluation and the "Back-and-Forth Go-Explore" process, creating a synergistic learning cycle.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- MoReFree builds on the PEG codebase, using default hyperparameters for the world model, policies, value functions, and temporal reward function.
- The length of Go-Explore phases (H_G, H_E) is set to half the evaluation episode length for each task.
- The parameter $\alpha$ (which controls the emphasis on task-relevant goals) is fixed at 0.2 across all tasks without tuning.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines**: Seven baselines were evaluated:
 - PEG (episodic, original version)
 - reset-free PEG (direct adaptation of PEG to reset-free)
 - DreamerV2 (general MBRL, without PEG's exploration)
 - MEDAL (model-free, requires demonstrations and environmental reward)
 - IBC (model-free, bidirectional curriculum, requires environmental reward)
 - R3L (model-free, perturbs agent to novel states, no additional supervision assumed)
 - Oracle (episodic SAC, with full resets and environmental reward)
- **Environments**: Eight challenging reset-free tasks were used, ranging from navigation to manipulation:
 - PointUMaze, Tabletop, Sawyer Door (from existing benchmarks).
 - Fetch Push, Fetch Pick&Place (modified from IBC's suite, with extended workspaces, physical walls instead of artificial constraints, and harder goal states).
 - Ant (a locomotion task adapted from PEG's codebase).
- **Evaluation**: Performance was measured by success rate, averaged over 10 episodes for tasks with random goals. Most methods were run with 5 seeds, reporting mean performance and standard error.
- **Supervision Comparison**: MoReFree and reset-free PEG operate without environmental reward or demonstrations, providing a comparison against baselines that often require these forms of supervision (e.g., MEDAL, IBC, Oracle).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation and analysis addressed the research questions regarding MBRL performance in reset-free settings, MoReFree's effectiveness, and the underlying behavioral characteristics.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Data Efficiency

- **MBRL Superiority**: MoReFree and its predecessor, reset-free PEG, demonstrated superior performance and data efficiency compared to all other baselines in 7 out of 8 tasks. This was achieved without requiring environmental reward or demonstrations, which were often utilized by competitive baselines like MEDAL, IBC, and Oracle.
- **MoReFree vs. reset-free PEG**: MoReFree consistently performed competitively or better than reset-free PEG. Significant performance gains were observed in the three most challenging tasks: Push (hard) (45% improvement), Pick&Place (hard) (13% improvement), and Ant (hard) (36% improvement).
- **Limitations of Direct MBRL**: The original PEG (episodic) and DreamerV2 (general MBRL) performed poorly in the reset-free setting, indicating that direct application of episodic MBRL methods without adaptations is suboptimal.
- **Baseline Failures**: Many model-free baselines failed to learn meaningful behavior within the given sample budget, even with increased training steps (e.g., IBC with 3x budget in Appendix G). This underscores the difficulty of the tasks and the sample efficiency advantages of the MBRL approaches.
- **Sawyer Door Anomaly**: MBRL agents (MoReFree and reset-free PEG) struggled with the Sawyer Door task. Analysis suggested that the underlying MBRL backbones (DreamerV2/V3) also struggle with this task even in the episodic setting with environmental reward, possibly due to difficulties in modeling the task's dynamics or sparse reward.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Exploration Behaviors

- **Task-Relevant Focus**: State visitation heatmaps (Figure 5, 9-12) revealed that MoReFree and reset-free PEG explored the state space more thoroughly than other baselines, which exhibited more myopic exploration patterns.
- **MoReFree's Targeted Exploration**: In harder environments with larger state spaces and complex resetting dynamics (Ant, Push (hard), Pick&Place (hard)), MoReFree collected significantly more "task-relevant" data (states near initial and goal regions) compared to reset-free PEG (1.3 to 5 times more). This focused data collection enabled MoReFree policies to be better suited for the episodic evaluation criteria.
- **Effective Reset Behaviors**: Analysis of "Back" trajectories (Figure 6) demonstrated that MoReFree effectively learned reset behaviors. It could guide the agent/object back from "sink states" (e.g., corners or walls) to more central, task-relevant regions. For instance, in Fetch manipulation tasks, MoReFree learned to pick up and push blocks out of corners, functionally resetting the environment.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on MoReFree's two core mechanisms (back-and-forth exploration and task-relevant goal-conditioned policy training) confirmed their synergistic importance (Figure 7, 13, 14).
- **Impact of Removing Mechanisms**: Removing both mechanisms (reducing to reset-free PEG) resulted in a significant performance drop.
- **Extreme Bias (α=1)**: Setting $\alpha=1$ (MF with Only Task Goals), which heavily biases both exploration and imagination towards task-relevant states, also degraded performance. This indicated that some degree of general exploration (lower $\alpha$) is necessary, particularly in the early training phases.
- **Individual Component Importance**: Disabling Back-and-Forth Go-Explore (MF w/o BF-GE) or removing task-relevant goal sampling from imagination (MF w/o Imag.) individually led to poor performance. This supported the hypothesis that these components form a critical, mutually reinforcing cycle:
 1. Focused policy optimization on initial/goal states enables the policy to effectively execute "Back-and-Forth" exploration.
 2. "Back-and-Forth" exploration collects relevant data, which in turn improves the world model and policy optimization for those specific goal types.
 Breaking this cycle by removing one component compromised the effectiveness of the other.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by demonstrating the viability and advantages of model-based methods in reset-free training settings. The findings suggest that:

1. **Reduced Human Effort**: By enabling agents to learn without requiring frequent or complex environmental resets, this work moves towards increasing the autonomy of RL systems. This has direct implications for real-world robotic applications, where manual resets are time-consuming, costly, and often impractical.
2. **Superior Performance and Data Efficiency**: MoReFree and its underlying MBRL backbone (reset-free PEG) significantly outperform prior state-of-the-art model-free reset-free methods. This suggests that the sample efficiency and robust exploration capabilities inherent to model-based approaches are particularly beneficial in the continuous, long-horizon nature of reset-free tasks.
3. **Autonomous Learning with Less Supervision**: MoReFree achieves its performance without relying on environmental reward functions or human demonstrations, making it more autonomous and applicable in scenarios where such supervision is unavailable or difficult to provide. The use of self-supervised reward functions for goal-reaching is a key enabler.
4. **Targeted Exploration for Reset-Free Tasks**: The introduction of "Back-and-Forth Go-Explore" and biased imagination training addresses a critical challenge of MBRL in reset-free settings: preventing over-exploration in task-irrelevant regions. By strategically directing exploration and policy learning towards task-relevant states (initial and evaluation goals), MoReFree maintains broad exploration while ensuring focus on learning for successful evaluation.

**Potential Impact**: The success of MoReFree in solving complex manipulation and locomotion tasks without resets, using less supervision than existing methods, indicates a pathway for more practical and scalable RL deployments. This could accelerate the development of autonomous robots capable of continuous learning and adaptation in unstructured environments.

**Limitations and Future Work**: The paper acknowledges several limitations. As a model-based approach, MoReFree inherits challenges in tasks where dynamics modeling is inherently difficult (e.g., Sawyer Door). The use of a fixed hyperparameter ($\alpha$) for balancing exploration and task-relevant goal-seeking suggests future work could explore adaptive curricula for this balance. Scaling MoReFree to high-dimensional visual observations and directly applying it to real-world robotic systems are natural extensions that could further validate its real-world utility. These insights provide directions for future research aimed at further enhancing the autonomy and robustness of RL agents.
