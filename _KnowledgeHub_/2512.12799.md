# DrivePI: Spatial-aware 4D MLLM for Unified Autonomous Driving Understanding, Perception, Prediction and Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> DrivePI presents a spatial-aware 4D Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) that integrates understanding, perception, prediction, and planning for autonomous driving. The system achieves new state-of-the-art results in 3D occupancy and occupancy flow, while also improving planning collision rates and text understanding accuracy using a compact 0.5B MLLM backbone.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Action (VA) models for autonomous driving excel in spatial perception and planning but lack natural language interaction and interpretability.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks, while enabling language interaction, struggle to generate fine-grained intermediate 3D perception and prediction outputs crucial for reliability and safety.
- There is a need to bridge the gap between precise spatial capabilities and human-like interpretability/interaction in autonomous driving systems.

## Method

- A unified spatial-aware ==4D MLLM== architecture that processes multi-modal inputs (LiDAR, multi-view cameras, language instructions) via an advanced vision encoder and spatial projector.
- The MLLM backbone utilizes four specialized ==decoding heads== for parallel execution of coarse-grained text responses, fine-grained 3D occupancy, occupancy flow prediction, and action diffusion for planning.
- A multi-stage data engine generates over 1.0M comprehensive instruction-tuning QA pairs, including text-occupancy and text-flow queries, to train the MLLM in 4D spatial understanding.

## Results

- Achieved an OccScore of **49.3%**, RayIoU of **49.3%**, and mean Average Velocity Error (mAVE) of **0.509** on OpenOcc, surpassing the previous best ALOcc-Flow-3D by **7.4%** RayIoU and reducing mAVE by **0.047**.
- Reduced planning collision rates to **0.11%** on nuScenes, outperforming the VA method VAD and the VLA method ORION by **70%**, while achieving a **0.40m** L2 error with ego status.
- Demonstrated strong text understanding with **60.7%** accuracy on nuScenes-QA, outperforming OpenDriveVLA-7B despite using a significantly smaller MLLM backbone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The synergistic integration of both text and fine-grained visual heads within a unified MLLM significantly boosts performance across perception, prediction, and planning tasks, exceeding vision-only approaches.
> - A compact MLLM backbone (0.5B parameters) can achieve state-of-the-art or competitive results across diverse, unified autonomous driving tasks, demonstrating efficiency in complex VLA systems.
> - Explicitly training MLLMs with rich text-occupancy and text-flow QA pairs is crucial for endowing them with fine-grained 4D spatial reasoning capabilities and enhancing interpretability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving research has seen a significant evolution, broadly categorized into modular pipelines and end-to-end systems. Early end-to-end systems, termed Vision-Action (VA) models, directly map sensor inputs (e.g., LiDAR, cameras) to driving actions. While these models, like UniAD and VAD, have achieved notable progress in accurate spatial perception and planning, they inherently lack natural language interaction, limiting user-friendliness and interpretability.

More recently, the advent of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has inspired the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks for autonomous driving. Models such as OpenDriveVLA and ORION aim to leverage the powerful reasoning and human-like decision-making capabilities of MLLMs, allowing for language-based interaction and high-level scene understanding. However, a significant drawback of these VLA approaches has been their struggle to generate fine-grained intermediate 3D perception and prediction outputs, which are critical for guaranteeing reliability, interpretability, and safety in autonomous driving. This limitation stems from their typical reliance on coarse-grained linguistic representations and image-only inputs.

DrivePI emerges precisely at this intersection, aiming to bridge the gap between the spatial precision of VA models and the interactive capabilities of VLA frameworks. It proposes a **unified spatial-aware 4D MLLM** that combines the strengths of both paradigms. This work positions itself as a significant advancement by demonstrating that fine-grained 3D perception (occupancy), prediction (occupancy flow), and planning can be seamlessly integrated with coarse-grained linguistic understanding within a single, end-to-end optimized MLLM architecture. The "4D" aspect refers to its ability to capture fine-grained spatial-temporal dynamics through 3D occupancy and occupancy flow outputs. By incorporating LiDAR inputs alongside cameras and developing specialized data engines for 4D spatial understanding, DrivePI addresses the core limitations of existing VLA models, pushing the frontier of MLLMs in achieving robust, interpretable, and safe autonomous driving. This work contributes to the growing trend of generalist AI models applied to specialized domains, adapting powerful LLMs for complex real-world control tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DrivePI is to overcome the inherent trade-offs between existing autonomous driving paradigms: the precision of Vision-Action (VA) models versus the interpretability and interactive capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors identify a critical gap: while MLLMs show strong capabilities across diverse domains, their application in generating fine-grained 3D perception and prediction outputs in autonomous driving remains underexplored.

The key objectives of DrivePI are:

1. **Develop a unified framework:** To create a single, end-to-end optimized system that can handle spatial understanding, 3D perception, prediction, and planning tasks concurrently. This unification aims to combine the strengths of VA and VLA models.
2. **Achieve spatial-aware 4D MLLM capabilities:** To enable the model to generate both 3D occupancy (static scene understanding) and occupancy flow (dynamic motion analysis), thereby capturing fine-grained spatial-temporal dynamics critical for autonomous driving.
3. **Integrate multi-modal inputs:** To leverage both precise geometric information from LiDAR point clouds and rich visual appearance from multi-view images, along with language instructions, within a unified MLLM architecture. This is crucial for eliciting robust spatial understanding.
4. **Enhance interpretability and safety:** By producing intermediate fine-grained 3D perception and prediction outputs, the model aims to provide reliable and interpretable features that improve the safety assurances of autonomous driving systems, a common limitation of purely VLA-based methods.
5. **Develop enriched data engines:** To generate comprehensive text-occupancy and text-flow Question-Answer (QA) pairs for 4D spatial understanding, enabling the MLLM to reason about complex spatial-temporal dynamics through textual understanding.
6. **Demonstrate superior performance with efficiency:** To show that even with a compact MLLM backbone (e.g., 0.5B parameters), the unified DrivePI can match or exceed the performance of existing specialized VA models in perception/prediction and VLA models in interactive capabilities.

In essence, DrivePI seeks to answer the question: "Can we develop a unified framework that combines the precise spatial perception of VA models with the natural language interaction of VLA-based approaches?" The authors are motivated by the need for autonomous driving systems that are not only capable and safe but also user-friendly and interpretable through natural language.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DrivePI proposes a novel spatial-aware 4D MLLM architecture that integrates vision, language, and action into a unified, end-to-end optimized framework.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline (Figure 2)

The DrivePI pipeline consists of three main stages:
1. **Multi-modal Input Processing:** The system takes LiDAR point clouds, multi-view camera images, and language instructions as inputs. LiDAR is crucial for precise 3D geometric information, complementing camera imagery. An advanced multi-modal vision encoder (specifically referenced as) processes these visual and LiDAR inputs to extract compact latent Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) features.
2. **Spatial Projection to MLLM:** The high-resolution latent BEV features are then passed through a spatial projector. To manage computational costs while preserving fine-grained spatial information, the BEV features are patchified, and a cross-attention mechanism (similar to HiRes-LLaVA) aggregates these features into vision tokens. These vision tokens are then linearly projected to match the MLLM's language embedding space.
3. **MLLM with Specialized Heads:** The vision tokens, along with text tokens from language instructions, are fed into a large language model backbone (Qwen2.5-0.5B in the main experiments). The MLLM processes these combined tokens and outputs responses through four specialized decoding heads, enabling parallel execution of tasks:
 - **Text Head:** Generates responses for coarse-grained scene understanding in an auto-regressive manner.
 - **3D Occupancy Head:** Provides accurate fine-grained 3D perception, predicting the volumetric occupancy of the scene.
 - **Occupancy Flow Head:** Performs fine-grained motion prediction at a pixel level, forecasting the velocity of occupied voxels.
 - **Action Diffusion Head:** Generates trajectory planning outputs for the ego-vehicle.

All tasks are jointly optimized during training, and the MLLM backbone itself is trainable (with the vision encoder typically frozen in the initial stage).

#### 3.2. Coarse-grained Spatial Understanding with Data Engine (Figure 3)

A key component is the development of a multi-stage data engine to create comprehensive instruction-tuning data for coarse-grained spatial understanding:
1. **Scene Description:** InternVL3-78B is used to generate detailed scene descriptions from front and back camera views, which are then merged and refined to create high-quality, comprehensive textual descriptions of the entire scene.
2. **4D Spatial Understanding Annotation:** This stage focuses on equipping the MLLM with fine-grained spatial reasoning capabilities in a textual format. Ground-truth 3D occupancy and flow data are leveraged to generate diverse text-occupancy and text-flow QA pairs. These questions query specific 3D positions for occupancy status, object category, and velocity information, enabling the MLLM to understand 4D spatial dynamics textually.
3. **Planning Reasoning Annotation:** Text-planning QA pairs are generated based on the ego-vehicle's future trajectory annotations. These prompts require the MLLM to analyze the environment and provide high-level driving commands (e.g., "Go straight," "Turn left") along with suggested trajectories.

This data engine generates over 1.0M QA pairs, training the MLLM to reason about complex spatial-temporal dynamics through language.

#### 3.3. Fine-grained Spatial Learning with Visual Heads

For explicit, precise spatial capabilities, DrivePI employs three fine-grained visual heads:
- **3D Occupancy Head:** Follows methods like FlashOcc. It reshapes MLLM-derived vision tokens into a 4D feature map (H x W x Z x C') and uses an MLP to predict 3D occupancy categories. It uses a combination of focal, geometric, semantic, and Lovász losses.
- **Occupancy Flow Head:** An additional MLP predicts velocity for 3D occupancy based on the occupancy head. It employs L1 loss with weighted balancing to account for the imbalance between static and dynamic objects.
- **Action Diffusion Head:** Implemented based on previous action diffusion models, it predicts trajectories via a denoising procedure, distinct from methods requiring external plan query initialization like SparseDrive. L1 loss is used for this head.

#### 3.4. Loss Function

The total loss is a weighted sum of four components: cross-entropy loss for the text head ($L_{llm}$), and task-specific losses for 3D occupancy ($L_{occ}$), occupancy flow ($L_{flow}$), and action diffusion ($L_{action}$). All tasks are optimized jointly and end-to-end. Default balancing weights ($\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = \lambda_3 = \lambda_4 = 1$) are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DrivePI demonstrates remarkable performance across unified autonomous driving tasks, often outperforming both specialized VA models and existing VLA frameworks, particularly given its compact MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-0.5B).

#### 4.1. 3D Occupancy and Occupancy Flow on OpenOcc (Table 1)

- DrivePI achieved an OccScore of 49.3%, RayIoU of 49.3%, and a mean Average Velocity Error (mAVE) of 0.509.
- It significantly surpassed the representative FB-OCC by 10.3 RayIoU for 3D occupancy and reduced mAVE from 0.591 to 0.509 for flow.
- DrivePI established new state-of-the-art results, outperforming the previous best method, ALOcc-Flow-3D, by 6.3% in OccScore, 7.4% in RayIoU, and reducing mAVE by 0.047, all while using only a 0.5B MLLM backbone. This highlights its effectiveness in fine-grained 4D spatial perception within a VLA framework.

#### 4.2. Planning on nuScenes (Table 2)

- With ego status information, DrivePI achieved an L2 error of 0.40m and a collision rate of 0.11%. This outperformed the VA method VAD and the VLA method OpenDriveVLA-7B in collision rate.
- Notably, DrivePI reduced the collision rate by 70% compared to ORION (from 0.37% to 0.11%).
- Even without ego status, DrivePI showed strong planning capabilities, achieving 32% lower L2 error than VAD (0.49m vs. 0.72m). These results confirm DrivePI's efficacy as a VLA model for planning tasks.

#### 4.3. Text Understanding on nuScenes-QA (Table 3)

- Despite its smaller 0.5B MLLM size, DrivePI achieved 60.7% accuracy on the nuScenes-QA benchmark, outperforming OpenDriveVLA-7B by 2.5%. This demonstrates its promising text understanding capabilities crucial for VLA models.

#### 4.4. 3D Occupancy on Occ3D (Table 4)

- When trained exclusively on the 3D occupancy task of Occ3D, DrivePI achieved a state-of-the-art RayIoU of 46.0%, surpassing the previous best method OPUS by a significant margin of 4.8%. This further confirms its strong capabilities in fine-grained 3D perception.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Table 5 & 6)

- **Text Head vs. Vision Head:** The ablation studies showed that combining both text and vision heads (3D occupancy, flow, planning) significantly improved performance across tasks. The unified model (III) increased RayIoU by 1.8%, reduced mAVE by 0.18, and decreased L2 error by 0.52 compared to the vision-only setting (II), while maintaining comparable text understanding. This highlights the synergistic benefits of unifying linguistic and spatial reasoning.
- **Scaling Text Data:** Experiments with varying amounts of instruction-tuning data revealed that larger datasets, especially for occupancy QA pairs, led to substantial performance improvements in 4D spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., 14% accuracy gain for occupancy status and 44.9% for category when scaling occupancy QA).
- **Learned Weights of Hidden States (Table 8 & 9):** Analysis showed that deeper transformer layers within the MLLM received larger importance weights, indicating that higher-level features are more crucial for DrivePI's effectiveness.

#### 4.6. Visualization (Figure 4, Table 11-13)

Qualitative results showcased DrivePI's multi-granularity understanding. It generated detailed scene descriptions (coarse-grained) and accurate 3D occupancy, flow, and trajectory predictions (fine-grained). A strong alignment between coarse-grained linguistic descriptions and fine-grained spatial predictions was observed, validating the unified framework's interpretability. The model also demonstrated robustness in challenging conditions, such as nighttime turning scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DrivePI represents a significant stride in the development of autonomous driving systems by proposing the first unified spatial-aware 4D MLLM framework that seamlessly integrates coarse-grained linguistic spatial understanding with fine-grained 3D perception, prediction, and planning capabilities. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the VA-VLA Divide:** DrivePI effectively resolves the long-standing trade-off between the precise spatial capabilities of Vision-Action (VA) models and the interactive, interpretable nature of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. By inheriting the complementary strengths of both, it paves the way for a new generation of autonomous systems that are both highly capable and user-friendly.

2. **Enhanced Interpretability and Safety:** The ability to generate fine-grained 3D occupancy and occupancy flow, alongside linguistic scene descriptions and planning rationale, significantly enhances the interpretability and explainability of autonomous driving decisions. This is crucial for building trust, debugging systems, and ultimately ensuring safety, as the model can explicitly articulate *why* it perceives a situation in a certain way and *how* it intends to act.

3. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** Remarkably, DrivePI achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across multiple complex tasks using only a compact 0.5B parameter MLLM backbone. This demonstrates impressive efficiency, making advanced unified autonomous driving systems more accessible for deployment and research, reducing the computational resources typically associated with large models.

4. **New Benchmarking and Data Generation Paradigms:** The development of enriched data engines for generating text-occupancy and text-flow QA pairs introduces novel benchmarks for 4D spatial understanding. This data generation approach will inspire future research in creating more comprehensive datasets that push MLLMs towards deeper spatial and temporal reasoning in real-world environments.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** DrivePI lays a robust foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Loss Balancing and Optimization:** The identified limitation regarding loss balancing suggests avenues for developing more sophisticated multi-task learning strategies.
 - **Integration of Reinforcement Learning:** Incorporating RL techniques, as suggested by the authors, could further enhance reasoning capabilities, particularly for complex planning scenarios involving trial-and-error learning.
 - **Generalization to Diverse Driving Scenarios:** Further research can explore the model's robustness and generalization capabilities across a wider range of environmental conditions, vehicle types, and driving cultures.
 - **Embodied AI:** This work advances the broader field of embodied AI, demonstrating how language models can be endowed with precise spatial and temporal awareness for physical interaction in complex dynamic environments.

In conclusion, DrivePI represents a substantial step towards truly intelligent, unified autonomous driving systems that can perceive, predict, plan, and communicate in a human-like manner, marking a promising direction for future advancements in the field.
