# Compositional Human-Scene Interaction Synthesis with Semantic Control

> [!summary] Summary
> COINS synthesizes 3D humans interacting with scenes under semantic control given action-object pairs. Transformer conditional VAEs encode body vertices and object point clouds as tokens, with action embeddings added as positional encoding; a two-stage PelvisVAE then BodyVAE pipeline plus contact-guided optimization produces bodies. Trained only on atomic interactions, it composes multi-object interactions, reaching 0.93 semantic contact and 63.8-79.7% realism preference over baselines.

## Problem

- Prior scene-population methods model only geometric body-scene relations and proximity, so they cannot condition generation on action categories or on specific interacting object instances.
- Existing semantic approaches encode objects by category label only, ignoring instance geometry, leaving ambiguity when a scene holds several objects of the same category.
- Motion methods handle one object or the whole scene, and collecting composite interaction data is exponentially costlier than atomic data, so multi-object interactions stay unsupported.

## Method

- Compositional interaction representation: a body plus a variable-size set of atomic action-object pairs, with objects as 8192-point oriented clouds reduced to 256 keypoints by ==PointNet++==.
- ==Transformer conditional VAEs==, **PelvisVAE** for global pelvis location/orientation then **BodyVAE** for mesh vertices and per-vertex contact features, with action embeddings added to paired object tokens as positional encoding.
- Composition at inference: latent optimization intersecting per-atomic pelvis distributions, plus contact-probability-derived attention masks composing upper limbs, lower limbs, and torso; contact-guided post-optimization refines penetration.

## Results

- On PROX-S test scenes, the method reaches **0.93** semantic contact versus **0.84** for PiGraph-X and **0.67** for POSA-I, with **0.97** non-collision and highest entropy **3.77**.
- Perceptual studies favor it as more realistic in **63.8%** of comparisons against POSA-I and **79.7%** against PiGraph-X, and **45.3%** against pseudo ground truth.
- Ablations: one-stage generation collapses semantic contact to **0.20** (**0.31** re-centered) versus **0.72** two-stage; vertex-location bodies beat joint representations at **0.01m** consistency error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding intent as positional encoding on paired object tokens localizes each action's influence to its own object, a cheap way to keep multi-condition attention from bleeding across entities, enabling compositional generalization at inference through latent intersection and attention masking without combinatorially expensive composite training data.
> - Factorizing generation into coarse global placement then local articulation matters enormously; joint prediction of placement and pose is much harder and degrades contact quality.
> - Composition fails when component action distributions never co-occur in training or when the specified pairing is physically impossible.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of synthesizing realistic interactions between virtual humans and 3D environments is an active area within computer vision and graphics, driven by applications in areas such as computer games, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and the generation of synthetic data for machine learning. Existing research has primarily concentrated on modeling the geometric relationships between 3D environments and human bodies. For instance, methods like PSI, PLACE, and POSA have focused on inferring physically plausible human poses and contacts within scenes, often leveraging parametric body models like SMPL-X to achieve high realism. Earlier approaches often relied on simplified human body representations, which limited the realism of their synthesized interactions.

A significant gap in these existing methods is the frequent neglect of high-level semantic control over human-scene interactions. While some works, such as PiGraph, NSM, and SAMP, have attempted to incorporate action categories or coarse semantic labels, they often overlook the specificity of interacting objects (e.g., distinguishing between different chairs in a scene) or the complexities of compositional interactions involving multiple objects simultaneously. Conditional action synthesis methods generate human motion based on action categories or text but often do not explicitly model the interaction with specific 3D scene objects. Furthermore, many previous approaches operate at the object *category* level, which can lead to ambiguity when multiple instances of the same object category are present in a scene.

This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a framework that enables semantic control over human-scene interaction synthesis at the object *instance* level. It specifically tackles the challenge of generating realistic interactions based on explicit semantic specifications, such as "sit on *this specific* chair" or "lie on *that particular* sofa." A key innovation lies in its capacity to model the compositional nature of human interactions, allowing for the synthesis of complex scenarios where a human interacts with multiple objects simultaneously (e.g., "sit on the sofa and touch the table") without requiring dedicated training data for such composite actions. The work therefore aims to bridge the gap between geometric plausibility and semantic interpretability and control in human-scene interaction synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize virtual humans that interact naturally with a given 3D scene, under the explicit control of high-level semantic specifications. These specifications are defined as pairs of action categories and specific object instances, for example, "sit on the chair" or "touch the table."

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges and practical needs:
1. **Enhancing Realism and Controllability in Virtual Environments:** For applications like computer games, AR/VR experiences, and virtual prototyping, the ability to create virtual characters that respond precisely to semantic commands for interaction with their environment is crucial for achieving high levels of realism and user immersion. Current methods often lack this fine-grained semantic control, limiting their utility in such dynamic, interactive settings.
2. **Addressing Heterogeneous Information Integration:** Synthesizing semantically-aware human-scene interactions requires jointly considering diverse data types: detailed human body articulation (e.g., joint angles, mesh deformations), complex 3D object geometry (e.g., shape, size, location), and the abstract "intent" or semantics of the interaction. A significant challenge is to learn a unified and generalizable representation that effectively captures this heterogeneous information to produce plausible outcomes.
3. **Modeling Compositional Interactions:** Human interactions in the real world are often compositional; individuals frequently interact with multiple objects simultaneously (e.g., "sit on a chair and type on a keyboard"). Existing models often struggle to represent or generate such composite behaviors, largely due to the combinatorial explosion of possible action-object combinations and the associated difficulty in collecting sufficient training data for every composite scenario. This research is motivated to develop a framework that can naturally incorporate varying numbers of atomic interaction units (action-object pairs) without requiring explicit training on composite interaction data.
4. **Enabling Generalization and Data Efficiency:** The creation of synthetic data for training machine learning perception algorithms benefits immensely from diverse and controllable human-scene interactions. A system capable of synthesizing novel interactions, including retargeting interactions to unseen objects or composing new interactions from basic units, can significantly reduce the burden of data collection and expand the scope of synthetic data generation.

By addressing these challenges, the work aims to advance the state of the art in virtual human generation, making them more semantically controllable, realistic, and adaptable to complex interactive scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a multi-stage method, named COINS (COmpositional INteraction Synthesis with Semantic Control), for generating human-scene interactions conditioned on semantic specifications.

#### 3.1. Representations

- **Body Representation:** The 3D human body is represented using a simplified SMPL-X model. This includes 655 vertices and 1296 faces, along with auxiliary per-vertex binary contact features (0 or 1, indicating contact with scene objects based on a 5cm distance threshold).
- **3D Scene Representation:** A 3D scene is represented as a set of object instances. Each instance is an oriented point cloud of 8192 points, including location, color, and normal attributes. This point cloud representation allows for the incorporation of detailed object geometry and aims to facilitate generalization to open-set objects, moving beyond reliance on predefined object categories.
- **Compositional Interaction Representation:** An interaction `I` is defined as `(B, {(a_i, o_i)}_M)`, where `B` is the human body and `{(a_i, o_i)}_M` is a set of `M` atomic interactions. An atomic interaction is a pair of a one-hot encoded action and a specific object instance (e.g., "sit on a chair"). Composite interactions consist of more than one such atomic unit.

#### 3.2. Multi-Stage Interaction Synthesis

The synthesis process is decomposed into three stages:
1. **Pelvis Generation:** Given action-object pairs, the method first infers a plausible global 3D location and orientation for the human pelvis. This is handled by a transformer-based conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) called `PelvisVAE`.
2. **Body Generation:** After transforming objects to the generated pelvis coordinate frame, a second transformer-based cVAE, `BodyVAE`, generates the detailed body articulation and per-vertex contact map in this local space.
3. **Interaction-Based Optimization:** A post-optimization step refines the generated interactions. This optimization minimizes an objective function `E(t, R, θ)` consisting of three terms:
 - `L_interaction`: Encourages body vertices with positive contact features to have zero distance to objects.
 - `L_coll`: A scene Signed Distance Function (SDF) based collision term, penalizing body-scene penetration.
 - `L_reg`: A regularization term that penalizes deviations of the optimized body translation (`t`), global orientation (`R`), and pose (`θ`) from their initial generated values.

#### 3.3. Transformer-Based Conditional VAE (cVAE) Architecture

Both `PelvisVAE` and `BodyVAE` employ a transformer-based cVAE architecture designed to handle heterogeneous inputs:
- **Input Encoding:** Object point clouds are processed by a PointNet++ module to extract 256 local keypoints, which are then converted into tokens. Human body vertices (with location and contact features) are mapped to tokens, with vertex index-based positional encoding to capture body topology.
- **Semantic Embedding:** Action categories are mapped to learnable embeddings. These action embeddings are added to the tokens of their paired object as positional encoding. This mechanism ensures that each action primarily influences its associated object, rather than having a global effect.
- **Encoder:** Maps input interactions (body vertices, object tokens, semantic embeddings) into a unified latent space, predicting the parameters of a normal distribution (mean `μ` and variance `Σ`).
- **Decoder:** Samples a latent code from this distribution. For `BodyVAE`, it uses a personalized template body (with shape parameters) as initial body tokens. The latent code is used in encoder-decoder attention layers to generate the final body mesh and contact features. `PelvisVAE` uses a zero vector as the body token, focusing solely on pelvis frame generation.
- **SMPL-X Regressor:** An MLP is jointly trained with `BodyVAE` to regress SMPL-X body parameters from the sampled body meshes, ensuring the generated meshes correspond to valid human body shapes.

#### 3.4. Compositional Interaction Generation

A two-step approach is proposed to generate composite interactions using models trained exclusively on atomic (single action-object pair) interactions, avoiding the need for composite training data:
1. **Compositional Pelvis Generation:** For a composite interaction (e.g., "sit on sofa and touch table"), each atomic interaction contributes a distribution of plausible pelvis frames. The method samples latent codes for each atomic interaction, then optimizes these latent codes to minimize the spatial difference between their decoded pelvis frames while maintaining high probability within each component's distribution. The average of these optimized pelvis frames forms the composite pelvis frame.
2. **Compositional Body Generation:** This step leverages the attention mechanism within the `BodyVAE` at a body part level. Based on pre-calculated per-vertex contact probabilities for each action (e.g., "sitting" strongly correlates with hip contact), attention masks are created. If an action has a significantly higher contact probability in a specific body part (e.g., "touching" on upper limbs), the attention between that body part's tokens and objects *not* paired with that action (e.g., the "sofa" when the action is "touch table") is masked to zero. This allows the `BodyVAE` to naturally compose the atomic interactions by selectively focusing body parts on their relevant objects.

#### 3.5. Dataset (PROX-S)

To train and evaluate the method, the PROX dataset was extended to PROX-S. This new dataset includes:
- 3D instance segmentation for all 12 PROX scenes.
- 3D human body reconstructions (SMPL-X fits).
- Per-frame interaction semantic labels, specified as action-object pairs. This dense annotation (including multiple labels per frame for composite interactions) is crucial for semantic control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of COINS was evaluated through quantitative metrics, perceptual studies, and qualitative comparisons against modified state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Dataset Contribution

The creation of the PROX-S dataset, incorporating 3D instance segmentation and per-frame semantic interaction labels, represents a significant contribution, providing a resource for research into semantic-aware human-scene interaction. It comprises approximately 32,000 frames from 43 sequences across 12 indoor scenes, with annotations covering 17 distinct actions and 42 categories of action-object pairs.

#### 4.2. Baselines

Since no existing method directly addressed the problem of 3D human body generation with semantic control, two state-of-the-art methods were adapted:
- **PiGraph-X:** A modified version of PiGraph, which typically generates human skeletons, was adapted to use the SMPL-X body model.
- **POSA-I:** A modified version of POSA, which relies on contact features for human-scene interaction, was extended to incorporate global semantic control for generating these features.
For compositional interaction generation, a `PiGraph-X-C` extension was also implemented.

#### 4.3. Evaluation Metrics

The evaluation employed:
- **Semantic Contact:** A newly proposed metric, calculated as a weighted sum of binary contact features, with weights derived from action-specific body vertex contact probabilities observed in training data. This quantifies how well body contact aligns with specified semantics.
- **Physical Plausibility:** Measured by the non-collision metric, which is the ratio of body vertices not penetrating the scene, based on SDF values.
- **Diversity:** Assessed using K-Means clustering (50 clusters) of generated SMPL-X parameters, quantified by the entropy of the cluster ID histogram and the mean distance to cluster centers.
- **Perceptual Study:** Conducted on Amazon Mechanical Turk (AMT), including:
 - Binary-choice realism: Participants chose which of two generated samples (from different methods) was more natural.
 - Unary semantic accuracy: Participants rated the consistency between generated interactions and semantic labels on a 1-5 scale.

#### 4.4. Interaction Synthesis with Semantic Control

- **Perceptual Realism (Table 1a):** COINS significantly outperformed both baselines. In head-to-head comparisons, COINS was rated as "more realistic" by 79.7% of users against PiGraph-X and 63.8% against POSA-I. Its perceptual realism was close to pseudo ground truth, with 45.3% choosing COINS over pseudo ground truth.
- **Perceptual Semantic Accuracy (Table 2):** COINS achieved the highest average semantic accuracy rating of 4.17 (out of 5), surpassing POSA-I (4.03) and PiGraph-X (3.90).
- **Semantic Contact (Table 2):** COINS demonstrated a semantic contact score of 0.93, notably higher than PiGraph-X (0.84) and POSA-I (0.67), indicating better alignment with semantic specifications.
- **Physical Plausibility (Table 2):** COINS achieved a non-collision score of 0.97, comparable to POSA-I (0.98) and significantly better than PiGraph-X (0.85).
- **Diversity (Table 2):** COINS yielded a higher cluster entropy (3.77) and a smaller mean cluster size (0.83), suggesting a greater diversity and more realistic distribution of generated poses compared to the baselines.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 6):** Visual comparisons illustrated that COINS generated physically plausible and semantically accurate interactions, avoiding the unnatural poses, poor contact, and penetrations observed in the baseline methods.

#### 4.5. Compositional Interaction Generation

- **Perceptual Realism (Table 1b):** The compositional variant, COINS-C, significantly outperformed PiGraph-X-C, with 81.2% of users rating COINS-C as more realistic.
- **Semantic Accuracy (Table 3):** COINS-C achieved a semantic accuracy rating of 3.95, slightly higher than PiGraph-X-C (3.88).
- **Physical Plausibility (Table 3):** COINS-C demonstrated superior physical plausibility with a non-collision score of 0.98, compared to PiGraph-X-C (0.84).
- **Semantic Contact (Table 3):** COINS-C had a slightly lower semantic contact score (0.76) than PiGraph-X-C (0.80), which the authors attribute to challenging combinations of actions and objects in test scenes that may not always afford natural contact.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 7, S11):** The method successfully generated novel composite interactions, such as "sit on sofa and touch table," despite not being trained on such specific composite data.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Body Representation (Table C.1):** Using mesh vertex locations (VL) for body representation yielded the best semantic contact (0.72) and body consistency error (0.01m) compared to joint locations (JL) or joint locations and orientations (JLO), suggesting that regression of SMPL-X parameters from full mesh vertices is more effective.
- **Two-Stage Generation Design:** The two-stage approach (pelvis generation then body generation in a local frame) was found to be crucial. One-stage models, whether using original or re-centered scene coordinates, resulted in significantly lower semantic contact scores (0.20 and 0.31 respectively) compared to the two-stage method (0.72), highlighting the difficulty of jointly predicting global interaction parameters and detailed body pose in a single step.

#### 4.7. Generalization Capabilities

The method demonstrated the ability to retarget learned interactions to unseen objects with similar geometry and affordance (Fig. S7). It also featured explicit body shape control, generating plausible interactions for extremely thin or heavy bodies not encountered during training (Fig. S8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for synthesizing human-scene interactions that offers a combination of semantic control, compositional capability, and realism previously underexplored. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Semantic-Aware Interaction Synthesis:** The primary contribution is the ability to generate human-scene interactions explicitly controlled by high-level semantic specifications, down to specific object instances. This moves beyond purely geometric or coarse-category-based methods, enabling more intuitive and precise control over virtual human behavior in 3D environments. This level of control is critical for creating interactive virtual characters that can follow specific commands.

2. **Compositional Interaction Generation without Dedicated Data:** The framework's ability to synthesize novel composite interactions (e.g., "sit on the chair and touch the table") by composing atomic interaction units, without requiring explicit training data for every composite scenario, is a notable advancement. This addresses a significant data scarcity problem, as collecting data for all possible composite interactions is combinatorially expensive. This approach improves data efficiency and promotes generalization.

3. **Unified Representation of Heterogeneous Information:** The proposed transformer-based generative model effectively learns a joint representation that integrates diverse and heterogeneous information: articulated human body geometry, 3D object geometry, and the semantic intent of the interaction. This unified approach simplifies the modeling of complex human-environment dynamics.

4. **Creation of the PROX-S Dataset:** The extension of the PROX dataset to PROX-S, incorporating 3D instance segmentation and detailed per-frame interaction semantic labels, provides a valuable new resource for the research community. This dataset can facilitate future research in semantic-aware human-scene interaction, offering a benchmark for methods aiming to achieve similar capabilities.

5. **Enhanced Realism and Plausibility:** Through robust generative models and an interaction-based optimization, the method produces highly realistic, physically plausible, and semantically accurate interactions, as validated by quantitative metrics and perceptual studies that show significant outperformance against existing baselines.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Computer Graphics and Animation:** The work has direct applications in creating more realistic and controllable virtual characters for computer games, films, and virtual productions, allowing animators and designers to specify interactions through high-level semantic commands rather than manual keyframing.
- **AR/VR Experiences:** For immersive AR/VR applications, the ability to generate virtual humans that naturally interact with virtual environments based on semantic input can significantly enhance user experience and believability.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** This method can be leveraged to generate diverse and semantically controlled synthetic data. This is particularly valuable for training machine learning perception algorithms for tasks such as human activity recognition, human-object interaction detection, and scene understanding, especially in scenarios where real-world data collection is challenging or expensive.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** Insights from this work could inform models for robots to better understand and execute human-like interactions with objects in complex environments, contributing to more natural and intuitive human-robot collaboration.

While the work presents advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, including potential penetration with unspecified objects (requiring post-processing), challenges with fine-grained hand-object contact due to dataset limitations, and current coarse-grained action semantics. Future work could address these by integrating more advanced collision handling, leveraging datasets with higher-quality hand estimation, and modeling finer-grained semantic distinctions and corresponding body part segmentation. Despite these, the paper represents a substantial step forward in creating semantically controllable and realistic virtual human-scene interactions.
