# Generalist Robot Manipulation beyond Action Labeled Data

> [!summary] Summary
> MotoVLA introduces a generalist robot manipulation policy that learns embodiment-agnostic motion priors from extensive unlabeled human and robot videos using dynamic 3D point clouds. This policy then aligns these priors with executable robot actions, demonstrating up to 68.2% success in simulator tasks and facilitating direct skill transfer from human demonstrations to real-world robot operations.

## Problem

- Generalist robot manipulation policies (VLAs) are limited by the high cost and difficulty of collecting large volumes of action-labeled robot demonstration data.
- Leveraging readily available unlabeled video data (human or robot) is challenging due to the domain gap between different embodiments and the absence of explicit action labels.
- Prior attempts to use unlabeled data for robot learning have been restricted to specialist policies, failing to scale to open-world generalist robot capabilities.

## Method

- **MotoVLA** employs a ==two-stage training== process, leveraging large-scale unlabeled human and robot videos to learn general motion priors before fine-tuning with a smaller labeled dataset.
- ==Dynamic 3D point clouds== serve as an embodiment-agnostic representation for hand/gripper actions, extracted from videos to bridge the domain gap between human and robot demonstrations.
- The system uses a ==Mixture of Transformers== architecture, with a pre-trained VLM backbone and a Predictor Transformer, to learn motion dynamics via ==flow matching== in both prediction tasks.

## Results

- MotoVLA achieved an average success rate of **68.2%** in the SIMPLER simulator, outperforming strong baselines like LAPA (**54.1%**) and π0 (**56.8%**).
- The model demonstrated best overall generalization on eight real-world out-of-domain tasks, showing direct skill transfer for unseen objects and tasks from unlabeled human videos to robot actions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of dynamic 3D point clouds, with 2D pixel tracks leading to a **4.0%** performance drop in simulation and **12.5%** on real robot tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic 3D point clouds provide an effective embodiment-agnostic representation for transferring manipulation skills from diverse, unlabeled videos across human and robot domains.
> - Pre-training with unlabeled human demonstrations directly enables zero-shot skill transfer to robots for tasks present in the human video dataset, demonstrating effective cross-embodiment learning.
> - The two-stage training framework, separating self-supervised motion learning from supervised action alignment, is crucial for leveraging unlabeled data efficiently and achieving out-of-action domain generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself squarely within the rapidly evolving field of generalist robot manipulation, a critical area for achieving truly autonomous and versatile robotic systems. The overarching goal in this domain is to enable robots to perform diverse tasks in novel environments without explicit, task-specific programming, often referred to as "zero-shot manipulation."

Historically, robot manipulation policies have been task-specific and required extensive hand-engineering or large amounts of meticulously labeled robot data. Recent advancements, particularly inspired by the success of large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in open-world reasoning, have led to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These end-to-end models extend VLMs by integrating embodied understanding, allowing them to process visual and linguistic inputs and directly predict robot commands. Existing VLA models like OpenVLA and π0 have demonstrated impressive zero-shot capabilities within their training distribution.

However, a fundamental bottleneck for scaling these generalist VLAs remains the prohibitive cost and difficulty of acquiring large volumes of *high-quality, action-labeled* robot demonstration data. This limitation severely hampers their generalization capabilities when tasks move outside the narrow confines of their training distribution.

This paper directly addresses this critical data-scaling challenge by proposing a method to leverage readily available, *unlabeled* video data, including human demonstrations and passive robot videos. Previous attempts to utilize unlabeled data for robot learning have faced several obstacles:
- **Domain Gap**: Human demonstrations often differ significantly from robot kinematics and embodiment.
- **Lack of Direct Action Labels**: Videos, by nature, lack explicit robot action commands.
- **Specificity**: Most prior work using unlabeled data has been confined to specialist, small-scale policies, failing to scale to generalist VLA capabilities. Some methods focused on visual representations, missing unseen motions, while others predicted visual plans requiring bespoke inverse-dynamics models for execution, or relied on retargeting human hands to robot grippers, which often suffered from large domain gaps.

MotoVLA distinguishes itself by presenting the *first end-to-end VLA model* that can learn fine-grained motion priors from large-scale unlabeled demonstrations. It introduces dynamic 3D point clouds as a novel *embodiment-agnostic representation*, effectively bridging the human-to-robot domain gap and enabling the transfer of knowledge from unlabeled videos to generalist robot policies. This approach is a significant step towards enabling robots to learn new tasks and improve their generalization without the sole reliance on expensive, action-labeled data, thus greatly expanding the potential for scalable robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the recognized bottleneck in scaling generalist robot manipulation: the overwhelming reliance on vast quantities of high-quality, action-labeled robot demonstration data. While current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models show promise for diverse tasks, their generalization capabilities quickly degrade when confronted with scenarios outside their specific training data distribution. Collecting such bespoke, action-labeled datasets for every conceivable task is economically and logistically infeasible for truly open-ended, autonomous robotics.

Therefore, the authors are motivated to explore alternative, more scalable data sources. They identify unlabeled videos—featuring both humans and robots—as a rich, untapped resource containing valuable spatiotemporal information relevant to robotic control. However, these videos present significant challenges, including the absence of direct action labels, the substantial domain gap between human and robot embodiments, and the presence of irrelevant features.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the MotoVLA project are:

1. **Enhance Open-Vocabulary Performance through Unlabeled Data**: To develop a method that effectively utilizes videos without explicit action labels (from humans and/or robots) to improve the robot's ability to understand and execute novel tasks described in natural language.
2. **Enable Data-Efficient Learning of New Tasks**: To design a training paradigm that reduces the dependency on large action-labeled datasets, allowing robots to learn new manipulation skills more efficiently by leveraging the broader context and motion information from unlabeled demonstrations.
3. **Achieve Out-of-Action Domain Generalization**: To empower robots to learn and perform tasks whose specific actions were not explicitly labeled in any training dataset, demonstrating true generalization beyond the specific actions seen during supervised training.
4. **Develop an Embodiment-Agnostic Representation**: To propose and validate a robust representation (dynamic 3D point clouds) that can capture the essence of manipulation motions independently of the specific actor (human hand or robot gripper), thereby facilitating knowledge transfer across different embodiments.
5. **Create an End-to-End Generalist VLA Model**: To build a VLA architecture and a two-stage training approach that integrates these objectives, allowing for the direct prediction of robot actions from visual and linguistic inputs, even when motion priors are learned from unlabeled cross-embodiment data.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to push the boundaries of generalist robot manipulation, making it more scalable, adaptable, and closer to real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **MotoVLA**, a generalist robot manipulation policy that leverages a novel two-stage training approach to benefit from videos without explicit action labels. The core idea is to learn embodiment-agnostic motion priors from large-scale unlabeled data, which are then aligned with executable robot actions using a smaller, action-labeled dataset.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Point Cloud Representation

A central innovation is the use of **dense, dynamic 3D point clouds** to represent the actions of a hand or gripper. These point clouds are designed to be embodiment-agnostic, capturing the spatial and temporal relationships of manipulation motions regardless of whether they are performed by a human or a robot.
- **Extraction from Unlabeled Videos**: To obtain these dynamic point clouds from raw videos (human or robot), the method employs a sophisticated pipeline:
 1. A frame is sampled from the video.
 2. The hand or gripper is detected using **Grounding DINO** for bounding box detection.
 3. **Segment Anything V2** generates a precise segmentation mask from the bounding box.
 4. Pixel positions are uniformly sampled from this mask and tracked across time (forward and backward) using **BootsTAPIR**.
 5. The resulting 2D tracks are lifted into 3D using the monocular depth estimator **MoGE**, which provides depth and camera intrinsics to produce a sequence of 3D hand/gripper point clouds.

#### 3.2. VLA Architecture

MotoVLA employs a **Mixture of Transformers architecture**, designed for efficient vision-language reasoning and action prediction.
- **VLM Backbone**: The foundation is a pre-trained Vision-Language Model, **Paligemma (3 billion parameters)**, which uses a SigLIP vision encoder and Gemma language model. Paligemma processes current image and text inputs to generate a key-value cache, representing high-level semantic and visual features. During the first training stage, the vision encoder of Paligemma is frozen to preserve its open-vocabulary capabilities.
- **Predictor Transformer**: A smaller Transformer (300 million parameters, Gemma-like architecture) serves as the "Predictor." It attends to the VLM backbone's key-value cache and is responsible for predicting either dynamic point clouds (Stage 1) or robot actions (Stage 2). **Flow matching** is utilized for continuous distribution sampling in both prediction tasks, enabling robust generation of future states/actions.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Approach

This is the core of MotoVLA, enabling the integration of unlabeled and labeled data:

- **Stage 1: Dynamic Point Cloud Training (Self-Supervised Pre-training)**
 - **Data**: A large-scale mixture of unlabeled human (RH20T) and robot (BridgeData V2, Rt-1) demonstration videos. Crucially, any action labels in the robot datasets are *discarded* for this stage.
 - **Objective**: Train the Predictor (now called **3D Dynamics Predictor**) to forecast future dynamic point clouds, conditioned on the current image, language description, and a sequence of past point clouds. This self-supervised objective encourages the model to learn shared, embodiment-agnostic motion dynamics across human and robot demonstrations. The VLM backbone provides conditioning, but its vision encoder is frozen.
 - **Loss Function**: A flow-matching objective ($L_{points}$) is used to predict the denoising vector field for the continuous distribution of future point clouds.

- **Stage 2: Action Alignment (Tuning)**
 - **Data**: A considerably smaller dataset of action-labeled robot demonstrations (BridgeData V2, specific to the WidowX robot used for evaluation).
 - **Objective**: Align the rich motion representations learned in Stage 1 with executable robot commands. The Predictor, initialized with the weights from the 3D Dynamics Predictor, is now fine-tuned as an **Action Predictor**. It learns to directly predict future robot proprioceptive observations (actions) given current image, language, and past proprioceptions.
 - **Mechanism**: This stage effectively performs an *implicit hand-eye calibration* and adaptation of the learned motion priors to the specific robot embodiment and action space. The linear encoders/decoders for action are randomly initialized. Unlike Stage 1, the VLM's vision encoder is now trainable (though for a shorter duration), allowing for minor adjustments that can improve action accuracy without significantly compromising general vision capabilities.
 - **Loss Function**: A flow-matching objective ($L_{action}$) similar to Stage 1, but applied to robot actions.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, the VLM processes the current image and text. The Action Predictor, conditioned on the VLM's features and current robot proprioception, predicts the next chunk of robot actions using Euler integration of the learned denoising vector. The entire action chunk is then executed on the robot.

This two-stage approach allows MotoVLA to leverage the vastness of unlabeled video data for learning robust motion priors and then efficiently translate these priors into precise robot control using a comparatively small amount of labeled data, thereby addressing the core data-scaling challenge in generalist robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate MotoVLA's effectiveness across various settings, validating its ability to learn from unlabeled data and achieve improved generalization in robot manipulation.

#### 4.1. In-domain Experiments (SIMPLER Simulator)

- **Superior Performance**: MotoVLA (R + H), trained on both robot and human unlabeled data, achieved the highest average success rate of 68.2% in the SIMPLER simulator across four BridgeData V2 tasks.
- **Outperforming Baselines**: It significantly outperformed strong baselines:
 - LAPA (OXE) (54.1%), despite LAPA being pre-trained on the full Open X-Embodiment dataset and specifically finetuned on 100 episodes from the target simulation tasks.
 - π0 (B) (56.8%), a state-of-the-art VLA baseline trained from scratch on BridgeData V2.
- **Value of Unlabeled Data**: These results indicate that self-supervised pre-training with dynamic point clouds from cross-domain and cross-embodiment videos translates into improved downstream performance, even for tasks where action supervision is available in the second stage.
- **Impact of Human Data**: MotoVLA (R + H) showed a marginal improvement over MotoVLA (R) (trained only on robot unlabeled data), particularly in tasks like cube stacking, which were well-represented in the human demonstration data.

#### 4.2. Out-of-domain Experiments (Real WidowX Robot)

- **Best Overall Generalization**: MotoVLA (R + H) achieved the best average success rate across eight real-world out-of-domain tasks, showcasing its ability to generalize to unseen scenarios.
- **Direct Skill Transfer from Unlabeled Human Demonstrations**: The most significant performance gains were observed in tasks present in the *human demonstration dataset* during Stage 1 pre-training (e.g., "Push Button," "Cube on Scale," "Cable in Basket," "Clamp in Cup"). This is a critical finding, directly demonstrating that MotoVLA can *transfer knowledge* on unseen objects and tasks from *unlabeled human videos across embodiments* to robot actions.
- **Improved Trajectory Quality**: In these tasks, MotoVLA (R + H) exhibited faster and more precise trajectories compared to baselines (π0-B, MotoVLA (R)) that had not encountered these tasks during pre-training, which often suffered from stuck states or erratic movements.
- **Comparison to OpenVLA**: OpenVLA's 9.8% inferior performance, despite being trained on more robot data, further highlights the value of task-specific human demonstrations, which are more scalable to collect.
- **Completely Out-of-Domain Tasks**: For tasks completely unseen in both training stages, MotoVLA (R) (robot unlabeled data only) performed best, followed closely by π0-B, indicating that fully novel tasks remain challenging even with this approach.
- **Conclusion on Out-of-Domain**: The results strongly support that including tasks as unlabeled human or robot demonstrations in the first training stage significantly improves downstream VLA performance on similar tasks, even without explicit action labels.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of 3D Point Clouds**: Replacing 3D dynamic point clouds with 2D pixel tracks for Stage 1 pre-training resulted in a notable performance drop (4.0% in SIMPLER, 12.5% on the real robot). This confirms the superior benefit of 3D representation in reducing the domain gap between observed motion and robot actions.
- **Track Points Selection**: Using fewer points (32 vs. 200) or non-gripper-specific sampling led to significant performance degradation, highlighting the importance of dense, targeted point cloud extraction.
- **Predictor Co-Training vs. Separation**:
 - Jointly training the predictor for both point cloud and action prediction in Stage 2 (**Co-Training**) degraded action prediction performance, despite helping to preserve Stage 1 features.
 - Training completely separate, randomly initialized predictors for point clouds and actions (**Predictor Separation**) without weight transfer was ineffective in leveraging unlabeled data.
- **Justification for Two-Stage Approach**: These ablations validate the proposed two-stage training approach where the same predictor is initialized from Stage 1 and then tuned for actions in Stage 2, confirming its effectiveness in transferring learned motion priors.

In summary, MotoVLA successfully demonstrates that leveraging large-scale unlabeled human and robot video data through dynamic 3D point cloud pre-training significantly enhances the generalization capabilities and performance of generalist robot manipulation policies, particularly for tasks similar to those in the unlabeled datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MotoVLA represents a significant stride forward in generalist robot manipulation, offering a pathway to overcome one of the most critical bottlenecks in the field: the immense difficulty and cost of acquiring large-scale, high-quality, action-labeled robot demonstration data. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Democratizing Robot Learning**: By demonstrating the effective use of unlabeled human and robot videos, MotoVLA opens up vast, readily available, and scalable data sources (e.g., internet videos, passive recordings) for training robotic policies. This fundamentally changes the data acquisition paradigm, making advanced robot learning more accessible and less dependent on specialized, expensive teleoperation setups. This could democratize access to and accelerate innovation in robotics research and development.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability**: The ability to transfer motion priors from diverse, unlabeled demonstrations (especially human ones) to robot actions enables robots to perform tasks that were not explicitly included in their action-labeled training data. This "out-of-action generalization" is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments where encountering novel objects, settings, and task variations is the norm. It moves robots closer to human-like adaptability.

3. **Novel Embodiment-Agnostic Representation**: The introduction and validation of dynamic 3D point clouds as an interpretable and embodiment-agnostic representation is a key methodological contribution. This representation effectively bridges the domain gap between human and robot actions, allowing for seamless knowledge transfer. This concept could inspire new approaches in cross-embodiment learning and more robust perceptual representations for robotic control.

4. **Data-Efficient Learning for New Tasks**: The two-stage training approach allows for data-efficient learning of new tasks. Robots can gain a broad understanding of manipulation dynamics from a large, unlabeled corpus and then quickly specialize or adapt to new tasks with a comparatively small amount of action-labeled data. This reduces the time and resources needed to teach robots new skills, making them more versatile and cost-effective.

5. **Accelerating Robot Deployment**: By addressing the data bottleneck and improving generalization, MotoVLA contributes directly to accelerating the deployment of generalist robots in various applications, from manufacturing and logistics to service robotics and domestic assistance. Robots trained with this methodology could more readily handle variations in objects, environments, and tasks, increasing their utility and reliability.

6. **Inspiring Future Research**: The paper also highlights limitations, such as handling highly diverse egocentric human videos, robustness to rapid hand movements, and improving monocular depth estimation. These identified areas provide clear directions for future research, indicating that MotoVLA is not an endpoint but a strong foundation for continued advancements in scalable and robust robot manipulation.

In conclusion, MotoVLA offers a compelling solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics, significantly advancing the quest for truly generalist and autonomous robot manipulation. Its impact is likely to be felt across the research community and in practical applications, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and deployable robots.
