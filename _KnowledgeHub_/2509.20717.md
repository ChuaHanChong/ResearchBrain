# RobotDancing: Residual-Action Reinforcement Learning Enables Robust Long-Horizon Humanoid Motion Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> RobotDancing, a framework developed by researchers from TUM, BAAI, Tsinghua, and Nanjing Universities, enables humanoid robots to track robust, long-horizon, and high-dynamic motions like dance. It achieves this by using selective residual-action reinforcement learning and a sophisticated motion sampling strategy, demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to Unitree humanoids and reducing global mean per-body position error by 15.7% compared to absolute-command baselines.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based controllers struggle with error accumulation in long-horizon, high-dynamic humanoid motions due to predicting absolute joint commands, leading to instability.
- Traditional methods do not explicitly compensate for dynamics discrepancies between idealized simulation physics and the robot's true dynamics, limiting robustness.
- Inefficient motion sampling in reinforcement learning often over-revisits easy segments and undersamples critical, challenging poses, hindering learning for diverse and complex behaviors.

## Method

- A residual-action reinforcement learning policy is introduced, where the policy outputs corrections to reference joint positions, focusing on compensating for dynamics discrepancies.
- A selective residualization approach applies these corrections only to error-sensitive and dynamic-demanding DOFs (e.g., hip/knee pitch), improving learning efficiency and stability.
- A two-stage motion sampling strategy combines offline distribution-aware balancing and online failure-aware priority adaptation to efficiently cover diverse poses and prioritize difficult segments during training.

## Results

- The selective residual-action approach reduced global mean per-body position error by **15.7%** compared to absolute-command baselines and by **11.7%** compared to applying residuals to all DOFs.
- RobotDancing successfully achieved robust tracking of multi-minute, high-dynamic dance sequences on real Unitree G1, H1, and H1-2 humanoid robots with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- The framework demonstrated cross-platform and cross-motion generalization, with policies transferring effectively to different robot morphologies and adapting to simpler, unseen motion references.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Selectively learning residual actions is more effective than learning absolute commands or applying residuals indiscriminately, as it efficiently directs policy capacity to dynamics compensation.
> - Combining offline analysis of motion distribution with online adaptation to learning difficulty significantly enhances sample efficiency and stability for long-horizon dynamic tasks.
> - A unified, single-stage RL framework, supported by extensive domain randomization, can achieve robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across multiple humanoid robot platforms and diverse, complex motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot control has seen significant advancements, particularly with the advent of learning-based methods, but robustly controlling these complex machines for long-horizon, high-dynamic behaviors remains a formidable challenge. RobotDancing directly addresses this critical gap, positioning itself at the forefront of physics-based humanoid control using reinforcement learning.

**Context of Humanoid Control and Motion Tracking:**
Traditionally, motion tracking in humanoids aims to reproduce human demonstrations or pre-defined trajectories. Early foundational work like DeepMimic established a canonical simulation-based formulation, demonstrating the potential of reference-conditioned policies. Subsequent research, such as ASAP and KungfuBot, extended this paradigm to achieve agile and highly dynamic skills on real-world humanoids, often relying on careful physics alignment, curriculum learning, and domain randomization to bridge the sim-to-real gap. More recent efforts like ExBody2, OmniH2O, and PHC have pushed towards broader motion coverage, expressive whole-body control, and long-lived avatar tracking, some incorporating teleoperation.

**Identified Challenges and the Gap:**
A persistent challenge in these methods is the "model-plant mismatch" – the discrepancy between idealized simulation physics and the robot's true dynamics (e.g., actuation limits, friction, inertia, latency). When controllers predict *absolute joint commands*, small tracking errors caused by this mismatch tend to accumulate rapidly, leading to instability and failure on long, high-energy sequences. While some efforts, like BeyondMimic, address long-sequence execution by optimizing sampling and trajectory stitching, they still rely on absolute action commands. This leaves untapped the potential of explicitly modeling and compensating for dynamic discrepancies.

**The Role of Residual Learning:**
Residual learning, where a learned correction is added on top of a nominal policy or controller, has shown promise in various robotic domains, from manipulation to locomotion. For bipedal systems, residual RL has been used to refine model-based controllers, and I-CTRL specifically employed residual actions for humanoid motion tracking in simulation, suggesting its ability to stabilize imitation. RobotDancing builds upon this idea but significantly expands its scope and impact.

**RobotDancing's Unique Contribution to the Landscape:**
RobotDancing differentiates itself by:
1. **Explicitly Learning Dynamics Discrepancy:** Unlike most prior works that predict absolute commands, RobotDancing's policy learns *residual actions*. These residuals explicitly correct the reference joint targets, forcing the policy to focus its capacity on compensating for the robot's physical dynamics discrepancies rather than re-synthesizing the entire motion. This directly tackles the error accumulation issue in long-horizon tasks.
2. **Selective Residualization:** The paper introduces the concept of applying residuals only to "error-sensitive and dynamic-demanding DOFs" (e.g., hip/knee pitch), rather than indiscriminately to all joints. This sparsifies the action space, reduces variance, and improves learning efficiency, making the approach more effective and stable.
3. **Advanced Sampling Strategy:** It proposes a novel two-part sampling scheme (offline distribution-aware balancing + online failure-aware prioritization). This addresses the non-uniform difficulty and "long-tail" distribution of dynamic motions, improving sample efficiency and ensuring coverage of challenging but informative poses.
4. **Simplicity and Scalability:** RobotDancing offers an end-to-end, single-stage RL framework, avoiding complex multi-stage distillation or teacher-student schemes often found in other methods, thus enhancing its scalability and practical deployability.
5. **Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real and Generalization:** The work showcases successful zero-shot transfer to multiple real-world humanoid platforms (Unitree G1, H1, H1-2) with a unified training configuration. This includes high-dynamic, multi-minute dance sequences, which is a notable demonstration of generalization across different robot morphologies and complex motion types.

In essence, RobotDancing advances the state-of-the-art by providing a robust, data-efficient, and generalizable solution to long-horizon, high-dynamic motion tracking in humanoids, specifically targeting the limitations of absolute-action policies through a well-designed residual learning framework and intelligent sampling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the RobotDancing framework is to enable humanoid robots to execute **robust, high-fidelity, long-horizon, and highly dynamic motions**, such as complex dance sequences, in the real world with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the inherent challenges in current humanoid control paradigms:

1. **Overcoming Error Accumulation in Long-Horizon Motions:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing learning-based controllers often predict *absolute joint commands*. While effective for short, quasi-cyclic skills, this approach becomes brittle for long, highly dynamic sequences (e.g., multi-minute dance routines). Small discrepancies between the robot's true physics and the idealized reference trajectories (model-plant mismatch, actuation limits, friction, latency) compound over time, leading to rapid error accumulation and eventual control instability or failure.
 - **Objective:** Develop a control strategy that explicitly mitigates this error accumulation to achieve stable tracking over extended durations.

2. **Explicitly Compensating for Dynamics Discrepancies:**
 - **Motivation:** Reference motions, often retargeted from human demonstrations, are kinematically consistent but rarely dynamically consistent with the robot. Traditional absolute-action policies struggle to account for these dynamic mismatches directly.
 - **Objective:** Introduce a mechanism that allows the control policy to specifically learn and compensate for these dynamics discrepancies between the robot's physics and the reference motion. By doing so, the policy can allocate its learning capacity more efficiently.

3. **Improving Sample Efficiency and Coverage for Diverse Motions:**
 - **Motivation:** Long-horizon, high-dynamic motions like dance exhibit significant inter- and intra-motion variability, with a "long-tail" distribution of poses (some common, some rare but critical). Uniform sampling in reinforcement learning often over-revisits easy segments and undersamples rare, challenging, or "unstable" configurations, slowing down learning and hindering generalization.
 - **Objective:** Design an effective motion sampling strategy that improves sample efficiency, ensures broad coverage of informative poses, and prioritizes learning on persistently difficult segments.

4. **Achieving Generalization Across Motions and Robot Platforms:**
 - **Motivation:** Many advanced controllers require task-specific tuning, multi-stage pipelines, or specialized interfaces, limiting their broad applicability. A key goal in robotics is to develop generalist policies that can perform diverse tasks and transfer seamlessly to different hardware.
 - **Objective:** Create a unified, single-stage RL framework with a consistent observation, reward, and hyperparameter configuration that can train diverse high-dynamic motions without per-task tuning and transfer zero-shot to multiple humanoid platforms (including full-size robots) with varying morphologies.

In summary, RobotDancing aims to fundamentally improve the robustness and generalizability of humanoid motion tracking by shifting from absolute-command control to a residual-action paradigm, supported by intelligent sampling, all within a simple and scalable learning framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RobotDancing introduces a simple, scalable, and end-to-end framework based on residual-action reinforcement learning, designed for robust long-horizon, high-dynamic motion tracking. The core of its methodology lies in learning to predict small corrections (residuals) to reference joint positions, coupled with a sophisticated motion sampling strategy and extensive domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Residual Actuation

The task is formulated as a discrete-time partially observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **Observation (O_t):** Concatenation of robot proprioception (P_t, e.g., joint positions/velocities, base linear/angular velocity, projected gravity, last action) and time-indexed reference motion data (G_t+1, primarily joint-space targets).
- **Action (a_t):** Critically, the policy outputs a *residual joint position* vector. This residual action (a_t) is *added* to the reference joint position (q_ref_t+1) to produce the commanded joint target (q_tar_t = q_ref_t+1 + a_t). A low-level joint-space PD controller then computes motor torques based on this target. This design offloads kinematic consistency to the reference, allowing the policy to focus purely on compensating for dynamics discrepancies between the robot and the reference.
- **Selective Residualization:** Not all Degrees of Freedom (DOFs) require residuals. The approach selectively applies residuals only to dynamic-demanding and error-sensitive DOFs (e.g., hip and knee pitch joints), while other DOFs directly use the reference. This sparsifies the action space, reduces variance, and enhances learning efficiency.

#### 3.2. RL Training Framework: Asymmetric Actor-Critic with PPO

- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used, following an asymmetric actor-critic architecture.
- **Actor:** Receives partial observation (O_t-k:t), consisting of stacked proprioception and reference information over a short look-back window (k timesteps). It predicts the residual actions.
- **Critic:** Benefits from an augmented, "privileged" observation (O_priv_t-k:t), which includes information unavailable to the actor at test time, such as base linear velocity, calculated base robot link position references, and randomized physical parameters (e.g., friction, masses, PD gains, delays). This allows the critic to provide a more accurate value estimate for faster and more stable policy optimization.
- **Goal Conditioning:** The policy is implicitly conditioned on the reference by directly feeding reference joint positions into the observation, avoiding an explicit time-phase variable.

#### 3.3. Reward Function Design

The reward function is vectorized and modular, separating imitation from physical regularization to stabilize PPO updates.
- **Task (Motion Tracking) Reward (r_track_t):** A DeepMimic-style shaping reward based on a compact set of task-space and joint-space errors (root/EE poses, twists, joint positions/velocities). Gaussian kernels are used to smooth these errors, encouraging close imitation.
- **Regularization Reward (r_reg_t):** Penalizes undesirable physical behaviors:
 - Excessive torques (∥τ_t∥^2_2)
 - Non-smooth actions (∥a_t - a_t-1∥^2_2)
 - Approaching joint/velocity/torque limits (soft-limits penalty)
 - Undesirable contact behavior (e.g., slip, stumble, excessive forces)
 - Large terminal penalty for collisions or safety-critical terminations (e.g., falls).
- **Curriculum Learning:** A curriculum-controlled penalty scale (s_pen(t)) is applied to the regularization term, gradually tightening error thresholds and increasing regularization weights as training progresses. Reward weights are tuned once and reused across tasks.

#### 3.4. Motion Sampling Strategy

To handle the variability and long-tail nature of dynamic motions, a two-stage resampling strategy is employed:
- **Offline Distribution-Aware Balancing:** Addresses the intrinsic long-tail structure of dance motions. Motion clips are partitioned into segments, and key lower-body DOFs (hip/knee pitch) are binned to form occupancy histograms. Sampling weights are then computed to match a uniform target distribution, ensuring rare but informative poses are adequately covered in early training.
- **Online Failure-Aware Priority Adaptation:** Addresses non-uniform difficulty during training. Each motion is divided into one-second segments. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) stability score is maintained for each segment, based on whether the robot failed in that segment. A tempered difficulty prior is then used to increase the sampling probability of segments where the robot persistently fails, allocating more training budget to bottlenecks.
- **Reference State Initialization (RSI):** At the start of each episode, a random time index from the reference trajectory is sampled. The robot's state (positions, velocities) is initialized to the corresponding reference state with small Gaussian perturbations to enhance robustness and coverage.

#### 3.5. Domain Randomization

Extensive domain randomization is applied to key physical and actuation factors to improve robustness and enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. This includes varying:
- Link masses and inertias
- Terrain friction
- Motor and PD gains
- Small sensing and actuation delays and noise.

The learned policy is stress-tested via sim-to-sim evaluation across randomized parameters before direct deployment to real hardware.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

- **Dataset:** LAFAN1 dance subset, retargeted for Unitree robots.
- **Platforms:** Unitree G1 (primary), H1, and H1-2. All use a unified framework and low-level joint-space PD controller running at 50 Hz.
- **Policy Architecture:** Actor and critic share an MLP trunk (512-256-128, ReLU). The actor outputs a platform-dependent residual action vector (23 for G1, 21 for H1-2, 19 for H1). The critic uses multi-head value outputs.
- **Termination Criteria:** Episodes terminate upon global joint tracking failure (mean joint-position error exceeds a time-varying tolerance), excessive base attitude deviation, or self-collision.
- **Metrics:** Global mean per-body position error (E_g-mpbpe), mean per-body position error (E_mpbpe), mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe), and velocity variants (E_mpbve, E_mpjve).

This comprehensive methodology, combining residual learning with targeted sampling and robust sim-to-real techniques, forms the foundation of RobotDancing's high performance and generalizability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Unitree G1, H1, and H1-2 humanoid robots demonstrate that RobotDancing achieves superior performance in robust long-horizon, high-dynamic motion tracking, with strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

#### 4.1. Ablations on Residual Actions

The study compared three variants:
- **NONE (baseline):** Absolute joint commands, akin to DeepMimic.
- **ALL:** Residual actions on all DOFs.
- **SELECTIVE (ours):** Residual actions only on hip/knee pitch DOFs.

- **Learning Dynamics:** SELECTIVE exhibited the fastest improvement and reached the highest asymptotic reward, indicating superior sample efficiency and long-horizon credit assignment. ALL performed better than NONE but plateaued lower than SELECTIVE, suggesting that indiscriminate residual application can be less effective than targeted intervention. NONE showed a pronounced early overshoot and quickly stagnated, highlighting the difficulty of long-horizon tasks with absolute commands.
- **Final Accuracy (Quantitative Metrics):** SELECTIVE consistently outperformed both NONE and ALL across most task and joint space error metrics:
 - Compared to NONE (baseline), SELECTIVE reduced global mean per-body position error (E_g-mpbpe) by **15.7%**, mean per-body position error (E_mpbpe) by **18.2%**, mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe) by **20.5%**, and mean per-body velocity error (E_mpbve) by **4.3%**.
 - Compared to ALL, SELECTIVE further improved E_g-mpbpe by **11.7%**, E_mpbpe by **11.6%**, E_mpjpe by **9.6%**, and E_mpbve by **1.0%**.
 - These results strongly support the hypothesis that selective residualization focuses learning on dynamics-sensitive joints, leading to better sample efficiency, reduced error propagation, and improved long-horizon stability.

#### 4.2. Ablations on Sampling Strategy

Two variants were compared:
- **Failure:** Only online Failure-Aware Priority Adaptation.
- **Failure+Balanced (ours):** Blends Failure-Aware Priority Adaptation with offline Distribution-Aware Balancing.

- **Learning Curves:** Failure+Balanced demonstrated both faster convergence and a higher asymptotic reward compared to Failure-only. The steeper slope in the early training regime and consistently higher plateau level indicate improved sample efficiency and reduced gradient noise. This highlights the synergistic benefits of addressing both the intrinsic long-tail distribution of poses (offline balancing) and the dynamic difficulty of segments (online prioritization).

#### 4.3. Performance on Dance Sequences and Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer

- **Long-Horizon, High-Dynamic Tracking:** RobotDancing successfully tracked multi-minute (approximately 3 minutes each) and highly dynamic dance sequences from the LAFAN1 dataset, including jumps, 360-degree spins, sprints, abrupt stops, and rapid transitions.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment:** Policies were trained entirely in simulation with a single, unified configuration (observations, rewards, hyperparameters) and deployed directly to real Unitree G1, H1, and H1-2 robots without any fine-tuning or test-time modifications (e.g., Kalman filtering, PD gain retuning, action rescaling). The real robots exhibited high motion tracking quality, confirming robust transfer.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations (e.g., Figure 2) demonstrated the robots performing complex maneuvers like cartwheels, leaps, and fast heading changes with stable tracking, matching the simulation behavior.

#### 4.4. Cross-Platform Validation

- The same training recipe and pipeline successfully transferred to Unitree H1 and H1-2, enabling these full-size humanoids to reproduce high-dynamic behaviors. This indicates the generalizability of the proposed framework across different robot morphologies.

#### 4.5. Cross-Motion Generalization

- Policies trained on complex dance motions exhibited zero-shot transfer to simpler, unseen references (e.g., walking or standing). While accuracy was lower than for in-distribution dance sequences, this demonstrated that the policy had internalized general platform dynamics rather than merely memorizing motion-specific commands, a crucial step toward broader applicability.

#### 4.6. Hardware Capabilities and Limits

- The method enabled high-dynamic tracking on full-size humanoids. However, the authors noted that torque-to-weight constraints remain a practical bottleneck, especially for heavier robots like H1 (∼50 kg) and H1-2 (∼80 kg). While residualization reduced unnecessary actuation, peak torque margins still limited the amplitude and frequency of extreme maneuvers.

Overall, the findings conclusively demonstrate that RobotDancing's residual-action framework, combined with its innovative sampling strategy, represents a significant step forward in robust and generalizable humanoid motion tracking, particularly for challenging long-horizon, high-dynamic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RobotDancing makes several significant contributions to the field of humanoid robotics and reinforcement learning, with a substantial potential impact on the development and deployment of agile and versatile humanoid robots.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Robust Long-Horizon, High-Dynamic Control

The primary significance lies in addressing one of the most persistent and challenging problems in humanoid control: achieving robust tracking of complex, multi-minute, high-dynamic motions. By effectively mitigating error accumulation—a critical failure mode for traditional absolute-command policies—RobotDancing pushes the boundaries of what humanoids can perform stably over extended periods. This is crucial for applications requiring sustained, intricate movements, such as entertainment, sports robotics, and sophisticated human-robot interaction.

#### 5.2. Novelty and Efficacy of Residual-Action Reinforcement Learning

The introduction of a selective residual-action policy is a key methodological breakthrough. Instead of forcing the RL agent to learn the entire complex dynamics and kinematics from scratch, it allows the policy to specialize in compensating for the *discrepancies* between a kinematically consistent reference and the robot's actual physics. This targeted learning:
- **Improves Sample Efficiency:** The policy focuses its capacity on the most challenging aspects of control.
- **Enhances Stability:** Reduces error propagation and accumulation.
- **Simplifies the Learning Problem:** Making it more tractable for RL algorithms.
This approach sets a new standard for how dynamics discrepancies can be explicitly handled in physics-based character control.

#### 5.3. Practicality and Scalability of the Framework

RobotDancing offers a "simple, open, and scalable" end-to-end pipeline. The use of a single-stage RL setup with unified observation, reward, and hyperparameter configurations avoids the complexity of multi-stage distillation or teacher-student schemes often found in state-of-the-art methods. This simplicity makes the framework more accessible, easier to implement, and more scalable for integrating new motions or adapting to new platforms, thus accelerating research and development in the field.

#### 5.4. Demonstrated Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer and Generalization

The successful zero-shot transfer of policies from simulation to multiple real-world humanoid platforms (Unitree G1, H1, H1-2) without any task-specific retuning or test-time modifications is a monumental achievement. This demonstrates an exceptional level of robustness achieved through systematic domain randomization and the inherent stability of the residual-action approach. Furthermore, the cross-platform and cross-motion generalization capabilities (e.g., tracking walking with a dance-trained policy) suggest that the learned policies are internalizing fundamental platform dynamics rather than memorizing specific motions, paving the way for more truly generalist humanoid controllers.

#### 5.5. Impact on Future Humanoid Applications

The capabilities demonstrated by RobotDancing open up numerous avenues for future humanoid robot applications:
- **Entertainment and Arts:** Enables robots to perform complex dance routines, potentially revolutionizing performances and interactive experiences.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Allows for more nuanced and dynamic interactions, where robots can closely mimic or adapt to human movements.
- **Education and Training:** Provides a robust platform for studying complex motor control and learning in dynamic environments.
- **General Mobile Manipulation:** While currently focused on whole-body motion tracking, the underlying principles of robust, dynamic control could be extended to more general mobile manipulation tasks requiring agile body movements.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions and Unlocking New Capabilities

The paper also clearly outlines its limitations, which in turn highlight exciting future research directions:
- **Automated Selective Residualization:** Replacing hand-crafted selection with model-based gating mechanisms for optimal residual application.
- **General Motion Tracking and Teleoperation:** Extending to arbitrary references and truly whole-body teleoperation, leading to highly adaptable and responsive humanoids.
- **Integration of Visual Perception:** Moving beyond proprioception-only systems to environment-aware tracking, enabling obstacle avoidance, terrain adaptation, and more complex interactive tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Energy/Safety-Aware Objectives:** Integrating more sophisticated constraints for practical, long-term deployment.
- **Online System Identification:** For rapid re-calibration and adaptation to changing robot conditions.
- **Richer Motion Libraries:** Leveraging advanced data and automated retargeting to broaden the robots' behavioral repertoire.

In conclusion, RobotDancing significantly advances the state-of-the-art in humanoid control by offering a robust, scalable, and generalizable framework for high-dynamic, long-horizon motion tracking. Its innovative residual-action approach, combined with intelligent sampling and strong sim-to-real transfer, lays a crucial foundation for the development of truly versatile and performant humanoid robots capable of navigating and interacting with the complex real world.
