# Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Large Models: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This comprehensive survey by Han et al. (2024) systematically reviews Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods for large models, classifying algorithms into four types and detailing their application across various deep neural networks including LLMs, ViTs, and Diffusion models. It also addresses crucial system-level challenges and solutions for deploying and training PEFT-enabled models.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning large models for specific tasks is computationally intensive, memory-demanding, and time-consuming due to their vast number of parameters.
- The high resource requirements limit the accessibility and practical deployment of state-of-the-art large models for many users and organizations.
- The rapid growth of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods created a need for a consolidated, systematic overview of algorithmic, application, and system-level challenges.

## Method

- The survey provides a comprehensive review and systematic classification of existing ==Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)== algorithms.
- It analyzes techniques used to enhance computational efficiency during PEFT, such as ==KV-cache management, pruning, and quantization==.
- The work extends its scope to cover the application of PEFT across diverse deep learning architectures, including ==Vision Transformers and Diffusion models==, and addresses critical ==system-level design challenges== for practical PEFT deployment.

## Results

- A systematic taxonomy of PEFT methods is established, categorizing them into **additive, selective, reparameterized, and hybrid** approaches, and demonstrates their broad applicability across Vision Transformers, Vision-Language Alignment models, Diffusion models, and LLMs.
- Critical efficiency techniques are highlighted, including KV-cache management, pruning, and quantization, which further reduce computational and memory overheads of PEFT itself.
- Key system-level challenges for PEFT deployment are identified, encompassing centralized query serving, distributed training, and multi-PEFT training, along with existing solutions and evaluation metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A comprehensive taxonomy categorizes PEFT methods into additive, selective, reparameterized, and hybrid types, detailing their mechanisms and efficiency.
> - Beyond parameter reduction, computational efficiency in PEFT relies on critical techniques like KV-cache management, pruning, and quantization.
> - PEFT is broadly applicable across various deep neural networks, including Vision Transformers, Vision-Language Alignment models, and Diffusion models, beyond just Large Language Models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement and widespread adoption of Large Models (LMs), particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs), represent a groundbreaking shift in artificial intelligence. These models, often comprising billions of parameters, have achieved unprecedented performance across a multitude of tasks in natural language processing (NLP), computer vision (CV), and multimodal domains. However, their sheer scale comes with significant computational and resource overheads. Training and, more importantly, fine-tuning these models from scratch for every new downstream task is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and often unsustainable due to the vast GPU memory and compute power required. This challenge creates a bottleneck for wider accessibility and practical deployment of LMs.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a crucial solution to this problem. PEFT methods aim to adapt pre-trained large models to specific tasks or domains by adjusting only a small fraction of their parameters, thereby drastically reducing computational costs, memory footprint, and storage requirements. This paper positions itself as a timely and comprehensive survey within this dynamic and rapidly evolving landscape. While previous works might have surveyed specific PEFT techniques or their application to a single domain (e.g., NLP), this survey distinguishes itself by offering a holistic view. It not only categorizes and analyzes a wide array of PEFT algorithms but also extends its scope to diverse applications beyond traditional LLM tasks, including Vision Transformers, Vision-Language Alignment models, and Diffusion models. Crucially, the survey addresses the often-overlooked yet critical aspect of **system design and implementation costs** associated with PEFT, a vital contribution for enabling practical, scalable deployment of fine-tuned large models. By consolidating knowledge across algorithms, applications, and systems, this work aims to serve as a foundational reference for the entire PEFT research community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the inherent challenges posed by the massive scale of modern large models. While these models offer remarkable capabilities, their customization for specific downstream tasks demands immense computational resources, making full fine-tuning impractical for many users and organizations, especially those operating on hardware platforms with constrained computational capabilities. PEFT offers a practical and efficient pathway to adapt these models, but the field is rapidly expanding with a myriad of proposed techniques and applications.

Against this backdrop, the authors articulate several key objectives for their comprehensive survey:

- **Comprehensive Algorithmic Review:** To present detailed studies of various PEFT algorithms, analyzing their performance characteristics and computational overheads. This includes classifying them into coherent categories (additive, selective, reparameterized, and hybrid) and explaining their underlying mechanisms.
- **Application Landscape Overview:** To provide an extensive overview of real-world applications where different PEFT algorithms have been successfully employed, demonstrating their versatility across various domains and tasks beyond typical NLP scenarios.
- **Computational Cost Mitigation:** To discuss common techniques and strategies that are used to further reduce the computational demands of PEFT itself, such as KV-cache management, pruning, quantization, and memory optimization.
- **System-Level Analysis:** To move beyond the algorithmic perspective and delve into the practical challenges and solutions associated with real-world system designs for PEFT deployment. This involves investigating the implementation costs and architectural considerations for serving and training PEFT-adapted models.
- **Resource for Researchers:** Ultimately, the survey aims to serve as a valuable and centralized resource for researchers, providing detailed insights into the latest advancements and practical applications of PEFT from both an algorithmic and system implementation standpoint. This objective emphasizes guiding future research and development in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology employed by Han et al. is centered on a structured and systematic review, categorization, and synthesis of existing literature on Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning. The authors did not conduct new experiments but rather analyzed and organized the vast body of research in the PEFT domain.

Their approach can be broken down into the following key methodological steps:

- **Foundational Background:** The survey begins by establishing a foundational understanding of Large Language Models (LLMs), using LLaMA-7B as a case study to explain their computational flow, architecture (embedding, decoder blocks with Multi-head Self-Attention and Feedforward Networks), and computational complexities (e.g., quadratic scaling of attention with input length). It also introduces the fundamental concepts of PEFT, common downstream tasks for evaluation (e.g., GLUE, common sense reasoning for LLMs; image recognition, video action recognition for vision models), and standard evaluation benchmarks.
- **Taxonomic Classification of PEFT Algorithms:** A core part of the methodology is the development and presentation of a novel, systematic taxonomy for PEFT algorithms. The authors categorize PEFT methods into four main types based on their operational approach:
 - **Additive PEFT:** Methods that introduce new, small trainable modules or parameters into the pre-trained model (e.g., Adapters, Soft Prompts, (IA)³).
 - **Selective PEFT:** Methods that identify and fine-tune only a subset of the existing parameters within the pre-trained model, either unstructured (randomly masked) or structured (patterned masking).
 - **Reparameterized PEFT:** Techniques that construct a low-rank reparameterization of the original model parameters for efficient training, which can then be integrated back into the original model for inference without speed overhead (e.g., LoRA and its derivatives, Compacter, DoRA).
 - **Hybrid PEFT:** Approaches that combine the advantages of multiple PEFT methods or leverage techniques like Neural Architecture Search (NAS) to find optimal PEFT configurations.
 - For each category, the authors provide detailed explanations, illustrative diagrams (where applicable), and examples of prominent algorithms, describing their mechanisms and relative merits.
- **Analysis of Computational Efficiency Enhancements:** The survey then dedicates a section to techniques designed to further improve the computational efficiency of PEFT methods themselves. This includes discussions on KV-cache management (critical for LLM inference), pruning strategies (to reduce parameter count), quantization (to reduce precision and memory), and other memory-efficient fine-tuning approaches (e.g., circumventing full backpropagation).
- **Expansion to Diverse Applications:** Moving beyond LLMs, the authors extend their review to demonstrate the applicability and adaptations of PEFT techniques across different Deep Neural Network (DNN) architectures. This includes a focused discussion on PEFT for Vision Transformers (ViTs), Vision-Language Alignment (VLA) models, and Diffusion models, highlighting how PEFT is tailored for specific tasks like visual instruction following, continual learning, context window extension, image classification, video recognition, open-vocabulary image classification, and customized content generation.
- **System-Level Perspective:** A distinctive aspect of this survey is its dedicated focus on the system design challenges and solutions for deploying PEFT-enabled large models. The authors outline three main scenarios: centralized PEFT query serving, distributed PEFT training (addressing privacy concerns), and multi-PEFT training. They propose a set of evaluation metrics for system performance (throughput, memory footprint, accuracy, quality of service) and discuss state-of-the-art frameworks (e.g., PetS, DLoRA, Punica) that tackle these challenges through optimized batching, scheduling, and kernel design.
- **Future Directions:** Finally, the authors conclude by summarizing key insights and identifying promising avenues for future research from both algorithmic and system perspectives, thereby offering a roadmap for the PEFT community.

By adopting this multi-faceted and structured approach, the survey provides a comprehensive, insightful, and practical overview of the PEFT landscape, catering to both theoretical understanding and real-world implementation challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, this work does not present new experimental results but rather synthesizes and categorizes the extensive body of research on PEFT. Its main "findings" are the comprehensive insights derived from this synthesis, which are organized into clear categories:

- **Comprehensive PEFT Taxonomy:** The survey establishes a robust taxonomy for PEFT methods, classifying them into four primary categories:
 - **Additive PEFT:** These methods strategically insert a small number of new, trainable parameters (e.g., Adapter layers, Soft Prompts, (IA)³, SSF) into the pre-trained model while keeping the backbone fixed. This approach reduces memory and computational requirements during fine-tuning.
 - **Selective PEFT:** These techniques fine-tune only a small, pre-selected subset of the *existing* parameters of the large model. This can involve unstructured masking (e.g., Diff Pruning, FishMask) or structural masking (e.g., BitFit, Xattn Tuning) for better hardware efficiency.
 - **Reparameterized PEFT:** These methods introduce low-rank trainable parameters during training, which are then integrated with the original model weights for inference, ensuring no added latency. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) and its derivatives (DyLoRA, AdaLoRA, DoRA) are highlighted as the most prominent examples due to their effectiveness and seamless integration. Other methods like Compacter and VeRA also fall into this category.
 - **Hybrid PEFT:** These approaches combine multiple PEFT techniques or leverage automated methods like Neural Architecture Search (NAS) to discover optimal configurations for different tasks (e.g., UniPELT, S4, NOAH, AUTOPEFT).
- **Importance of Efficiency Techniques Beyond Parameter Count:** The survey emphasizes that parameter efficiency does not automatically translate to computational or memory efficiency during training. It details critical techniques to further optimize PEFT:
 - **KV-cache Management:** Essential for accelerating LLM inference, optimized by methods like S-LoRA through unified paging.
 - **Pruning Strategies:** Applying pruning to PEFT modules (e.g., AdapterDrop, SPLoRA) or integrating with LoRA weights (LoRAPruning) to improve efficiency.
 - **Quantization:** Reducing the precision of parameters (e.g., BI-Adapter, QLoRA, LoftQ, QA-LoRA) significantly lowers memory footprint and accelerates computation, especially for low-bit quantization.
 - **Memory-efficient PEFT Methods:** Techniques like Side-Tuning, LST, Res-Tuning, MEFT, and MeZO (zeroth-order optimization) are crucial for reducing activation memory overhead during backpropagation, making fine-tuning feasible on limited hardware.
- **Broad Applicability Across DNN Architectures:** PEFT is not confined to LLMs but is widely and effectively adapted for:
 - **Vision Transformers (ViTs):** Used for tasks like image classification (AdaptFormer, VPT) and video recognition (ST-Adapter, AIM) to efficiently adapt pre-trained vision models.
 - **Vision-Language Alignment Models (VLAs):** Applied to tasks like open-vocabulary image classification (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, CLIP-Adapter) to adapt large multimodal models.
 - **Diffusion Models:** Employed for integrating additional input modalities (GLIGEN, ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) and customized content generation (Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion, IP-Adapter), enabling fine-grained control and personalization of generative models.
- **System-Level Challenges and Solutions:** The survey identifies critical system design challenges for deploying PEFT and reviews existing solutions:
 - **Centralized PEFT Query Serving:** Managing high-throughput, low-latency inference for thousands of PEFT-adapted models (e.g., PetS, DLoRA), requiring efficient batching and scheduling strategies.
 - **Distributed PEFT Training:** Addressing data privacy and computational inefficiency issues when training PEFT modules on user devices (e.g., DLoRA, Offsite-Tuning).
 - **Multi-PEFT Training:** Optimizing memory and computation for concurrently training multiple PEFTs, potentially with different LLM backbones (e.g., Punica with its SGMV kernel for batched LoRA operations).
- **Proposed Evaluation Metrics:** The paper introduces a set of standard metrics for evaluating both PEFT serving systems (throughput, memory footprint, accuracy, quality of service) and training systems (accuracy, compute cost, communication cost), providing a framework for consistent assessment.

In summary, the survey's main "findings" are its comprehensive mapping of the PEFT landscape, highlighting the diverse algorithmic approaches, the critical role of efficiency-boosting techniques, the broad applicability of PEFT beyond NLP, and the significant system-level considerations necessary for practical deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey holds significant value for the entire research community working with large models, offering both immediate utility and long-term impact:

- **Consolidating Dispersed Knowledge:** The field of PEFT is vast and rapidly evolving, with numerous papers introducing new methods and applications. This survey serves as a critical knowledge aggregator, bringing together a scattered body of work into a coherent and easily digestible format. By providing a clear taxonomy and detailed explanations of diverse PEFT algorithms, efficiency techniques, and application scenarios, it acts as a much-needed central reference point for researchers and practitioners.
- **Bridging Algorithm and System Perspectives:** A unique and highly impactful contribution of this survey is its dedicated focus on the *system-level challenges* and solutions for PEFT. Historically, algorithmic advancements and system implementations have often been studied in silos. This paper explicitly bridges this gap by discussing practical issues like KV-cache management, memory optimization, and the design of serving and training frameworks. This holistic view is essential for developing PEFT solutions that are not only theoretically sound but also practically deployable and scalable.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By systematically reviewing existing work, the authors effectively identify current limitations and propose concrete, promising directions for future research. These include the critical needs for simplifying hyperparameter tuning, establishing unified benchmarks for fair comparison, further enhancing training efficiency (beyond just parameter reduction), exploring scaling laws for PEFT with increasingly larger models, expanding PEFT to new model architectures (e.g., Sora, Mamba) and tasks, enhancing data privacy in distributed settings, and integrating PEFT with advanced model compression techniques. This roadmap can significantly influence and accelerate future breakthroughs in the field.
- **Promoting Accessibility and Sustainability:** The core premise of PEFT is to make large models more accessible by reducing the formidable computational and memory barriers associated with their adaptation. By synthesizing the most effective PEFT strategies and discussing efficient system designs, this survey directly contributes to democratizing the use of powerful AI models, enabling researchers and developers with more constrained resources to leverage state-of-the-art LMs for their specific needs. This, in turn, fosters innovation and wider application of AI.
- **Educational Resource:** The clear organization, detailed explanations, and extensive referencing make this survey an invaluable educational tool. It can serve as an excellent starting point for new researchers entering the field of large models and fine-tuning, providing them with a structured understanding of the landscape and key concepts without having to sift through hundreds of individual papers.

In conclusion, "Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Large Models: A Comprehensive Survey" is a significant contribution that not only synthesizes the current state of PEFT but also provides a forward-looking perspective, making it an indispensable resource for advancing research and practical applications in the era of large-scale AI models.
