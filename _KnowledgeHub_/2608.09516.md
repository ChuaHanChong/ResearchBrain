# HarnessWAM: Bridging Prediction and Deliberation in World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HarnessWAM is a model-external agentic runtime around a World Action Model. A vision-language Task Manager keeps an evidence-grounded scene belief and task graph, a capability-conditioned projection compiles open-ended plans into WAM-executable primitives, and a dual-timescale loop pairs a fast progress estimator with milestone deliberation and embodiment-state recovery, reaching 59.6% full-task and 69.9% subtask success on RoboMemArena.

## Problem

- World Action Models predict only finite-horizon dynamics and local skills, never maintaining the global task state needed to verify outcomes or propagate evidence across stages.
- Existing agent harnesses target digital environments with discrete interfaces and repeatable feedback, unlike physical control with partial observability and irreversible state changes.
- Prior planners ground language in affordances or scene geometry but treat memory, verification, and recovery as separate additions rather than one closed decision process.

## Method

- Evidence-grounded task state: scene facts tagged observed/inferred/unknown with confidence and image evidence, plus a ==task graph== of motor and cognitive nodes holding unresolved entities as symbolic variables.
- Capability-conditioned executable-space projection: a deterministic ==plan compiler== checks argument types, dependencies, precondition-effect consistency, and holding state, mapping semantic subgoals onto validated WAM primitives.
- Event-driven dual-timescale control: a ==SigLIP2==-plus-causal-Transformer ==progress estimator== gives high-frequency cues, while the ==Task Manager== decides continue/advance/observe/replan/recover/terminate and restores saved joint and gripper state on failure.

## Results

- On RoboMemArena, **59.6%** full-task and **69.9%** subtask success, beating PrediMem by **21.1** and **14.7** points and static-plan decomposition on the same checkpoint by **11.7** and **7.9**.
- Ablating executable-space projection is most damaging, cutting full-task success from **59.6%** to **18.5%**; removing progress-conditioned events costs **21.3** points, task state **11.9**, recovery **5.4**.
- Plan diagnostics: raw plans reach **13.8%** executability, lexical normalization **42.3%**, full projection **72.9%**, with dependency satisfaction rising to **92.9%**; RoboCerebra Ideal SR is **23.70%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A foundation model's usable capability depends heavily on its harness: structured external state and closed-loop orchestration extend a frozen policy without touching its architecture.
> - Canonical vocabulary alone does not fix plan-model interface mismatch; enforcing capability, dependency, binding, and embodiment constraints is what makes plans actually executable.
> - High local progress scores can mask task failure, so semantic outcome verification, not a progress signal, should gate subtask transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of World Action Models (WAMs), embodied artificial intelligence, and language-model-driven agents. World Action Models represent a significant advancement in robotic control, as they jointly learn environmental dynamics and robot actions, allowing control policies to incorporate physical evolution priors. These models, exemplified by systems like LingBot-VA and DreamZero, provide predictive representations that facilitate outcome-aware control, closed-loop correction, and the development of generalizable embodied agents capable of reliably predicting near-term physical evolution and executing local manipulation skills.

However, the paper identifies a key limitation of conventional WAMs, termed the "prediction-deliberation gap." This gap arises because WAMs typically focus on finite-horizon prediction and action generation, which are insufficient for complex embodied tasks that demand global planning, persistent maintenance of task state across stages, verification of outcomes, propagation of evidence, and robust failure recovery. They lack explicit mechanisms to maintain a global task state, verify subtask outcomes, transfer information across different phases of a task, or intelligently recover from execution failures.

The concept of "harnesses" for foundation models has emerged in the field of language-model agents, demonstrating that a model's effective capabilities are significantly amplified by external loops that manage state, invoke actions, and integrate feedback. Existing harnesses primarily target digital environments with discrete interfaces and explicit feedback mechanisms. Applying this concept to embodied agents presents unique challenges due to the continuous control, partial observability, uncertain outcomes, and potentially irreversible state changes inherent in physical interaction. An embodied harness must ground semantic reasoning in physically executable skills while persistently tracking task state and physical effects.

HarnessWAM aims to bridge this prediction-deliberation gap by integrating elements from several related areas of robot research:
- **Robot Planning:** Drawing inspiration from methods that ground language plans in skill affordances (e.g., SayCan), incorporate environment feedback into planning (Inner Monologue), or ground high-level reasoning in scene geometry (SayPlan, VoxPoser). It also leverages task-and-motion planning principles to connect symbolic structure to continuous feasibility.
- **Persistent Embodied Memory:** Addresses the need for robots to remember past observations and task states, even when objects are no longer in view, building on work in persistent spatial semantic representations and experience retrieval.
- **Progress and Outcome Modeling:** Incorporates stage-aware progress estimation and outcome verification to determine subtask completion and transition between stages.
- **Failure Detection and Recovery:** Implements mechanisms for detecting execution deviations and initiating corrective actions or recovery procedures.

By unifying these ingredients around a WAM executor, HarnessWAM proposes a novel framework that orchestrates local WAM predictions and actions into persistent, verifiable, and recoverable task-level behavior. It positions planning, memory, verification, and recovery not as independent modules, but as integral components of a single event-driven decision process external to the WAM itself.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for HarnessWAM stems from the identified **"prediction-deliberation gap"** in existing World Action Models (WAMs). While WAMs are capable of learning environmental dynamics and generating robot actions for local, finite-horizon control problems, they inherently lack the mechanisms required for complex, persistent, and adaptive execution of long-horizon embodied tasks.

The specific problems and limitations of conventional WAMs that motivate this work are:
1. **Insufficient Global Planning:** WAMs do not explicitly maintain a global task state, making it difficult to plan beyond immediate, short-term actions or coordinate multiple subtasks towards a larger goal.
2. **Lack of Cross-Stage State Maintenance:** Information acquired in one stage of a task (e.g., identifying an object in a drawer) cannot be reliably carried over or recalled when that information is no longer directly observable (e.g., after the drawer is closed). This leads to reliance on immediate observations, hindering tasks in partially observable environments.
3. **Absence of Execution Verification:** WAMs do not have inherent mechanisms to verify if a subtask has been successfully completed or if its expected physical effects have materialized. This makes robust progression through a task sequence challenging.
4. **Limited Failure Recovery:** Without a global task state or verification mechanisms, WAMs struggle to diagnose local failures, recover to a stable state, or adapt their plans to overcome unexpected deviations, often leading to task termination upon encountering a minor issue.
5. **Mismatch between Semantic and Executable Spaces:** High-level instructions often involve open-ended semantic plans, but WAMs operate on concrete, low-level skills. A systematic way to bridge this gap, ensuring that semantic plans are executable by the underlying WAM, is often missing.

To address these limitations, the paper introduces HarnessWAM with the following key objectives:
- **To develop an agentic framework external to the WAM:** This framework is designed to manage the overall task execution by providing persistent decision-making capabilities without modifying the internal architecture of the underlying WAM.
- **To maintain an evidence-grounded scene belief:** The system aims to build and update a comprehensive understanding of the environment, including objects, their attributes, relations, and epistemic status, grounded in visual evidence and retained across task stages, even when objects are occluded.
- **To represent global instructions as a structured task graph:** This graph should encapsulate both physical actions and cognitive operations, explicitly detailing dependencies, preconditions, effects, and recovery strategies for each subtask. It should also support symbolic variables for entities not yet resolved by observation.
- **To implement a capability-conditioned executable-space projection:** The framework seeks to translate open-ended semantic plans generated by a vision-language model (VLM) into sequences of atomic skills that are actually supported and validated by the underlying WAM, while respecting task dependencies and current robot/environment constraints.
- **To establish an event-driven, dual-timescale feedback loop:** This loop should integrate a fast-timescale, lightweight progress estimator for continuous execution monitoring with a slow-timescale Task Manager for deliberation at salient milestones, enabling adaptive decision-making (e.g., advance, acquire evidence, replan, recover).
- **To enable robust local failure recovery:** The system should allow the robot to recover its embodiment state after a subtask failure, preserving acquired scene knowledge and task memory, and subsequently resume or revise the task plan.
- **To demonstrate improved reliability in complex embodied tasks:** Ultimately, the objective is to show that this structured, agentic orchestration significantly enhances the reliability of WAMs in partially observable, memory-dependent, and multi-stage manipulation tasks, leading to higher full-task and subtask success rates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HarnessWAM is formulated as a model-external decision process (Harness `H`) that operates over discrete task events, orchestrating the local, finite-horizon actions of a World Action Model (WAM) to achieve global task success. This approach decouples continuous WAM control from task-level deliberation, enabling the WAM to focus on realizing local skills while the harness determines task flow, outcome verification, and failure recovery.

The core components and their interactions are detailed as follows:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The harness takes a global language instruction `x` and current observation `o_t` to update an embodied runtime state `z_k = (B_k, G_k, M_k, r_k)`, which includes scene belief, task graph, task memory, and execution state. It then selects the next local skill instruction `g_k+1` for the WAM. The WAM `W_θ` generates action chunks `A_t` based on `o_≤t`, `q_t`, and `g_k`.

#### 3.2. Evidence-Grounded Task State

To address partial observability and cross-stage information retention, HarnessWAM maintains several structured state representations:
- **Scene Belief (`B_k`):** A collection of facts `f = (s, p, o, v, η, c, E)`, where `s, p, o, v` are subject, predicate, object, and value; `η` indicates epistemic status (observed, inferred, unknown); `c` is confidence; and `E` references supporting RGB evidence. This distinguishes between facts that are 'not observed' and those 'observed to be false.'
- **Task Memory (`M_k`):** Comprises `M_task` (completed/failed nodes, retry counts, variable bindings, plan revision history) and `M_evidence` (visual evidence for salient events).
- **Task Graph (`G_k`):** A directed graph `(V_k, E_k, X_k, β_k)` where `V_k` are nodes, `E_k` are dependency edges, `X_k` are unresolved symbolic variables, and `β_k` is a partial binding from variables to scene entities. Each node `v_i = (op_i, arg_i, pre_i, eff_i, term_i, rec_i)` specifies operations, arguments, preconditions, expected effects, termination conditions, and a recovery strategy. The graph includes both motor (physical actions) and cognitive (observation, verification, binding) nodes. The **VLM Task Manager** is responsible for updating `B_k` and `M_k` at event times.

#### 3.3. Capability-Conditioned Executable-Space Projection

This component bridges the gap between open-ended VLM plans and the WAM's specific executable skills:
- **Extensible Ontology of Operations (`P*`):** A predefined set of parameterized primitives covering common robot interactions (motion, grasp, contact, articulation, assembly, tool use). This ontology is motivated by observations of low-dimensional, repetitive structure in motor behaviors.
- **WAM-Executable Primitive Set (`P_W`):** A subset of `P*` that represents skills empirically validated as reliably realizable by the specific underlying WAM (`W_θ`).
- **Deterministic Plan Compiler:** This module projects a VLM-generated graph (`G_vlm`) into an executable graph (`G_exec`). It performs rigorous checks for argument types, node dependencies, precondition-effect consistency, single-arm holding state, and graph acyclicity. Valid nodes are canonicalized into WAM instructions. If no feasible projection exists, it reports violated constraints to the Task Manager for replanning. A history-invariance constraint (`G_k+1|V_executed_k = G_k|V_executed_k`) ensures that only the unexecuted plan suffix can be revised, maintaining consistency with past physical actions.

#### 3.4. Progress-Conditioned Event Control (Dual-Timescale)

HarnessWAM employs a dual-timescale control loop to manage task execution efficiently:
- **Fast Loop (WAM + Progress Estimator):** The WAM continuously generates action chunks for the active skill. A lightweight, prompt-conditioned **progress estimator (`F_ϕ`)** monitors execution by predicting continuous progress `p_t`, completion likelihood `c_t`, and discrete progress intervals `π_bin_t` from recent multi-view RGB observations and the skill instruction. This estimator is trained with a multi-component loss (`L_prog`) to ensure accurate progress tracking.
- **Slow Loop (Task Manager Deliberation):** The Task Manager is invoked only at salient semantic events (e.g., candidate milestones from the progress estimator, budget exhaustion, changes in conditions, verified failures). At these events, the **VLM Task Manager (`T_VLM`)** reasons over current observations, progress history, scene belief, task graph, memory, and execution state. It determines the outcome (`ν_k` ∈ {success, failure, uncertain}) and makes a high-level execution decision (`d_k` ∈ {continue, advance, observe, replan, recover, terminate}). It also updates the scene belief (`∆B_k`) and variable bindings (`∆β_k`), and decides if the unexecuted plan suffix needs revision (`ρ_k`). Task graph advancement occurs only when `d_k = advance` after semantic verification.

#### 3.5. Embodiment-State Recovery and Task Termination

- **Local Recovery:** At the start of each motor node, HarnessWAM records the robot's initial arm (`q_0_k`) and gripper (`u_0_k`) states. Upon a verified failure or budget exhaustion, if the Task Manager decides to `recover`, multi-step joint control drives the robot's arm and gripper back to this saved initial state. Critically, this recovery mechanism only resets the robot's embodiment state; the environment, scene belief, and acquired task memory are preserved. After recovery, the WAM state is cleared, and the Task Manager may retry the active node or revise the remaining plan.
- **Dynamic Task Termination:** Task success is declared when all required nodes in the task graph are completed and final visual verification confirms the global goal. Failure occurs if the graph terminates without goal satisfaction, no feasible plan supported by WAM skills remains, or the overall task-level execution and recovery budget is exhausted. A finite task graph and bounded budget ensure eventual termination.

#### 3.6. Overall Inference Procedure

The entire process is summarized as an event-driven recursive state-estimation and decision system. The VLM Task Manager initializes the scene belief and task graph from the global instruction and initial observation. It then iteratively selects ready nodes, executes cognitive nodes directly, or invokes the WAM for motor nodes. The progress estimator monitors WAM execution, triggering the Task Manager at critical events. The Task Manager makes high-level decisions, updates the task state, and manages recovery, until the task is successfully completed or fails.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HarnessWAM was evaluated on two challenging benchmarks for long-horizon embodied tasks, RoboMemArena and RoboCerebra Ideal, using LingBot-VA as the underlying WAM. The evaluations included comparisons with published baselines and controlled ablation studies to isolate the contributions of specific components.

#### 4.1. RoboMemArena Performance

**Overall Performance:** HarnessWAM achieved a full-task success rate (SR) of 59.6% and a subtask SR of 69.9% on RoboMemArena.
- **Comparison to Published Baselines:** HarnessWAM surpassed all published baselines. For instance, it exceeded PrediMem, the highest-performing baseline, by 21.1 percentage points in full-task SR and 14.7 percentage points in subtask SR.
- **Comparison to WAM-based Diagnostic Baselines:**
 - **WAM + Whole Task:** This baseline, using the same LingBot-VA WAM but directly conditioned on the global instruction without explicit decomposition or persistent state, achieved 44.4% full-task SR and 52.3% subtask SR. HarnessWAM demonstrated improvements of 15.2 and 17.6 percentage points, respectively, highlighting the necessity of structured orchestration.
 - **WAM + Static Plan:** This baseline used the same WAM and an initial, fixed subtask decomposition but lacked memory updates or replanning. It achieved 47.9% full-task SR and 62.0% subtask SR. HarnessWAM further improved these scores by 11.7 and 7.9 percentage points, indicating that initial decomposition alone is insufficient, and persistent task state and closed-loop management are crucial.
- **Task-Family Breakdown:**
 - HarnessWAM showed notable improvements in full-task success over WAM + Static Plan, with a 16.8-point increase on occlusion tasks and a 12.5-point increase on sequential execution tasks. Its largest subtask gain was 14.8 points on transfer tasks.
 - On counting tasks, while subtask success slightly decreased by 1.9 points, full-task success increased by 6.4 points, suggesting that HarnessWAM more effectively integrates locally completed stages into a correct end-to-end execution.

#### 4.2. RoboCerebra Ideal Performance

**Overall Performance:** On the RoboCerebra Ideal subset, HarnessWAM achieved an SR of 23.70%.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** This performance surpassed GPT-4o Planner + OpenVLA by 1.78 percentage points and the HPE Framework by 2.60 percentage points. Given that RoboCerebra Ideal features static and fully observable environments, this result indicates that the benefits of HarnessWAM extend beyond explicit memory recovery, demonstrating the value of dependency-aware planning, outcome-conditioned transitions, and failure-aware adaptation even in such settings.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on RoboMemArena

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of individual components of HarnessWAM:
- **Without executable projection:** This ablation resulted in the most significant performance degradation, reducing average full-task SR from 59.6% to 18.5% (a 41.1-point drop) and subtask SR from 69.9% to 38.3% (a 31.6-point drop). This indicates that the executable-space projection is critical for ensuring that VLM-generated plans are actually executable by the WAM.
- **Without progress events:** Removing progress-conditioned event triggering and replacing it with fixed-interval Task Manager invocation led to a substantial drop, lowering full-task SR by 21.3 points and subtask SR by 19.2 points. This demonstrates the importance of execution-aware event selection over arbitrary deliberation frequency.
- **Without task state:** Eliminating structured scene facts, interaction history, and variable bindings resulted in an 11.9-point decrease in full-task SR and an 8.8-point decrease in subtask SR. The impact was particularly noticeable on occlusion tasks, affirming the necessity of persistent memory for partially observable environments.
- **Progress-only switching:** Allowing the progress estimator alone to determine subtask completion without semantic outcome verification maintained a similar average subtask SR (68.4% vs. 69.9%) but led to a lower full-task SR (55.4%). This suggests that while local progress might be high, semantic verification is essential for ensuring correct task transitions and end-to-end success.
- **Without recovery:** Disabling the embodiment-state recovery mechanism resulted in a 5.4-point drop in average full-task SR and a 2.2-point drop in subtask SR, with the largest impact seen in sequential execution tasks. This highlights the value of being able to recover from local failures and adapt the remaining plan.

#### 4.4. Plan-Level Diagnosis of Executable-Space Projection

A detailed analysis of plan quality demonstrated the effectiveness of the executable-space projection:
- **Raw VLM plans:** Showed low quality, with only 60.8% syntactic validity and 13.8% executability.
- **With normalization and aliasing:** Initial lexical processing significantly improved syntactic validity to 84.6%, object binding accuracy to 63.8%, and executability to 42.3%.
- **With full executable-space projection:** Further, substantial improvements were observed, with syntactic validity rising to 95.2%, dependency satisfaction to 92.9%, object binding accuracy to 88.3%, and executability to 72.9%. These results confirm that comprehensive constraint enforcement, beyond mere lexical alignment, is vital for generating plans reliably executable by the WAM, aligning with the large performance drop observed in the corresponding ablation.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

A representative rollout on RoboMemArena Task 4 demonstrated HarnessWAM's ability to sequentially explore drawers, accumulate evidence about their contents, and then use this retained (but no longer observable) knowledge to bind the target object to the correct drawer. The system then successfully reopens the target drawer and performs the necessary manipulation, illustrating the integration of information-gathering, cross-stage evidence, delayed target binding, and local WAM skills for coherent long-horizon execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HarnessWAM represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly in extending the capabilities of World Action Models (WAMs) for complex robotic tasks.

The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Prediction-Deliberation Gap:** The core contribution is the successful addressing of the identified "prediction-deliberation gap." By introducing an external, agentic framework, HarnessWAM enables WAMs to transition from being primarily local, finite-horizon control systems to persistent, deliberative agents capable of handling long-horizon tasks, partial observability, and dynamic environments. This significantly broadens the applicability of WAMs to real-world scenarios.

- **Enhanced Task Reliability and Robustness:** The comprehensive framework, which integrates evidence-grounded task state, capability-conditioned planning, dual-timescale control, and embodiment-state recovery, leads to substantially improved task success rates. This enhanced reliability and robustness are critical for deploying robots in practical applications where failures can be costly or require human intervention. The ability to recover from local errors without discarding accumulated knowledge is a key aspect of this robustness.

- **Modular and Extensible Architecture:** HarnessWAM operates as an external harness, meaning it does not require modifications to the underlying WAM architecture. This modularity is a significant advantage, allowing the framework to be potentially applied to different WAMs or other low-level control policies. This separation of concerns simplifies development and allows for independent advancements in both the WAM and the deliberative harness.

- **Validation of Key Components:** The detailed ablation studies empirically validate the necessity of each component—structured task state, executable-space projection, event-driven control, and recovery mechanisms. The particularly strong impact of the executable-space projection highlights the critical importance of systematically translating high-level semantic plans into physically realizable actions for robots. This provides a clear roadmap for future research in robust robot planning.

- **Advancement in Embodied AI with Foundation Models:** This work demonstrates a successful strategy for integrating powerful but context-limited foundation models (like VLMs for high-level planning and WAMs for low-level control) into a cohesive and effective embodied agent. It reinforces the idea that an external "agentic runtime" is essential to unlock the full potential of such models in complex physical environments, mirroring trends seen with language models in digital domains.

- **Implications for Future Research:** The paper identifies several important future directions, including extending the framework to real-world manipulation, expanding the repertoire of WAM skills, and incorporating uncertainty-aware deliberation. These directions suggest a path towards more general-purpose and intelligent robots that can operate autonomously in complex, unconstrained environments. The explicit representation of scene belief and task graph also opens avenues for more explainable and auditable robot behavior.

In summary, HarnessWAM represents a significant step towards enabling robots to execute complex, multi-stage tasks reliably by effectively integrating predictive control with deliberative planning and adaptive execution management, thereby advancing the state of the art in WAM-based embodied intelligence.
