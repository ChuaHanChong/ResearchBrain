# FrameThinker: Learning to Think with Long Videos via Multi-Turn Frame Spotlighting

> [!summary] Summary
> FrameThinker introduces a framework for Large Vision-Language Models to perform active, iterative reasoning over long videos by dynamically selecting relevant frames based on their evolving cognitive state. This approach significantly enhances reasoning performance while drastically reducing the number of frames processed.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) process long videos inefficiently by uniformly sampling numerous irrelevant frames.
- This uniform sampling degrades reasoning performance due to cluttered context and static textual processing after initial visual input.
- Existing video agents often rely on predefined workflows, limiting their autonomy and end-to-end learnable policies for dynamic video interaction.

## Method

- A ==multi-turn reasoning== loop enables LVLMs to iteratively generate thoughts and select actions for dynamic frame exploration.
- Specific actions like choose frames between START FRAME and END FRAME and get frame number at time MM:SS facilitate targeted visual information retrieval.
- A two-phase training approach combining Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning cultivates strategic action policies, complemented by a Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module for logical consistency.

## Results

- FrameThinker achieved an average accuracy improvement of **10.4%** over the Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** baseline across six video understanding benchmarks.
- It established new state-of-the-art results on LongVideo-Reason with **76.1%** accuracy and Video-Holmes with **46.8%** accuracy.
- The framework drastically improved efficiency, processing over 20 times fewer frames (20.6 frames on average) on LongVideo-Reason compared to LongVILA-R1, while maintaining higher accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Moving from passive, uniform frame sampling to active, iterative frame spotlighting dramatically improves both efficiency and reasoning accuracy in long video understanding.
> - Reinforcement Learning reward design, particularly conditional action rewards, is essential to prevent mode collapse and guide the model towards purposeful exploration.
> - A Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module effectively enforces logical consistency between thoughts and actions, enhancing interpretability and driving performance gains.
