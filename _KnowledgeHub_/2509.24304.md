# FrameThinker: Learning to Think with Long Videos via Multi-Turn Frame Spotlighting

> [!summary] Summary
> FrameThinker introduces a framework for Large Vision-Language Models to perform active, iterative reasoning over long videos by dynamically selecting relevant frames based on their evolving cognitive state. This approach significantly enhances reasoning performance while drastically reducing the number of frames processed.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) process long videos inefficiently by uniformly sampling numerous irrelevant frames.
- This uniform sampling degrades reasoning performance due to cluttered context and static textual processing after initial visual input.
- Existing video agents often rely on predefined workflows, limiting their autonomy and end-to-end learnable policies for dynamic video interaction.

## Method

- A ==multi-turn reasoning== loop enables LVLMs to iteratively generate thoughts and select actions for dynamic frame exploration.
- Specific actions like choose frames between START FRAME and END FRAME and get frame number at time MM:SS facilitate targeted visual information retrieval.
- A two-phase training approach combining Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning cultivates strategic action policies, complemented by a Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module for logical consistency.

## Results

- FrameThinker achieved an average accuracy improvement of **10.4%** over the Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** baseline across six video understanding benchmarks.
- It established new state-of-the-art results on LongVideo-Reason with **76.1%** accuracy and Video-Holmes with **46.8%** accuracy.
- The framework drastically improved efficiency, processing over 20 times fewer frames (20.6 frames on average) on LongVideo-Reason compared to LongVILA-R1, while maintaining higher accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Moving from passive, uniform frame sampling to active, iterative frame spotlighting dramatically improves both efficiency and reasoning accuracy in long video understanding.
> - Reinforcement Learning reward design, particularly conditional action rewards, is essential to prevent mode collapse and guide the model towards purposeful exploration.
> - A Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module effectively enforces logical consistency between thoughts and actions, enhancing interpretability and driving performance gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research paper "FRAMETHINKER: LEARNING TO THINK WITH LONG VIDEOS VIA MULTI-TURN FRAME SPOTLIGHTING" introduces a novel framework, FrameThinker, designed to overcome the limitations of current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) in processing and reasoning with long video content. This work proposes a paradigm shift from passive, uniform video frame sampling to an active, iterative reasoning process, where LVLMs dynamically select and analyze relevant frames based on their evolving cognitive state. The paper highlights a two-phase training strategy involving Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), alongside a crucial Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module to ensure logical consistency and interpretability.

FrameThinker addresses a critical bottleneck in the application of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to long video understanding: the inefficiency and performance degradation caused by uniform frame sampling and static textual reasoning.

#### 1.1. Limitations of Current LVLMs in Video Understanding

Current state-of-the-art LVLMs, including powerful closed-source models like Gemini-2.5 and GPT-4o, and open-source counterparts such as Qwen2.5-VL, have made significant strides in video understanding. However, the paper argues that their operational paradigm remains fundamentally flawed for long videos. These models typically rely on processing a large, pre-selected set of frames obtained through uniform sampling. This approach suffers from several issues:
1. **Inefficiency:** It processes a vast number of irrelevant frames, leading to high computational costs.
2. **Performance Degradation:** The sheer volume of noisy, irrelevant frames clutters the context, which can confuse the model and degrade its reasoning performance.
3. **Static Reasoning:** Reasoning is conducted exclusively through text tokens *after* the initial visual input, restricting multimodal perceptual abilities and preventing dynamic interaction with the video content based on evolving reasoning needs.

#### 1.2. Distinction from Video Agents

The paper also distinguishes FrameThinker from existing Video Agents (e.g., VideoAgent, VideoTree). While video agents can interact with videos using tools, they often depend on predefined workflows or external models. This limits their autonomy and flexibility in dynamically exploring video content based on reasoning needs. Furthermore, many video agent methods are not end-to-end learnable from data, restricting their potential for continuous improvement and generalization. FrameThinker, in contrast, learns an autonomous, end-to-end policy directly from data, enabling the model to decide when and how to interact with the video.

#### 1.3. Evolution from "Thinking with Images"

Inspired by the emerging paradigm of "Thinking with Images" (e.g., Pixel Reasoner, OpenThinkImg), which challenges the static processing of visual information by enabling models to iteratively consult and leverage visual data as an integral part of their thought process, FrameThinker extends this concept to the temporal domain. Instead of static images, FrameThinker allows the model to dynamically query a *video* for relevant frames based on its evolving cognitive state, thus enabling it to "think with long videos." This represents a significant advancement by integrating active visual information seeking into the core reasoning loop for dynamic, long-duration content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to perform efficient and effective reasoning over long video content by moving beyond the limitations of passive, uniform frame sampling. The core motivations are:

1. **Address Inefficiency in Long Video Processing:** Current LVLMs process many irrelevant frames in long videos, leading to high computational costs and slow inference times. The researchers aim to drastically reduce the number of processed frames while maintaining or improving performance.
2. **Improve Reasoning Performance:** The presence of a large number of irrelevant frames clutters the contextual understanding and can degrade the reasoning capabilities of LVLMs. The goal is to develop a mechanism that allows the model to focus on only the most pertinent visual information, thereby enhancing its ability to extract and connect crucial clues.
3. **Enable Dynamic, Iterative Multimodal Reasoning:** Existing methods conduct reasoning primarily in the textual domain after an initial visual input. The motivation is to allow LVLMs to actively and iteratively interrogate video content, interweaving textual thought with dynamic visual exploration, much like a human would selectively review video segments.
4. **Develop Autonomous and Learnable Policies:** Unlike video agents that often rely on fixed workflows or external tools, the objective is to create an end-to-end learnable framework where the LVLM autonomously learns a strategic policy for interacting with the video, making decisions on when and how to gather more visual evidence.
5. **Ensure Interpretability and Consistency:** In an iterative reasoning process, it is crucial to ensure that the model’s actions are logically consistent with its stated thoughts. A key motivation is to build in mechanisms that enforce this consistency, making the model's decision-making verifiable and interpretable.

In essence, FrameThinker seeks to empower LVLMs to "think with long videos" by proactively seeking out and spotlighting critical visual information, thereby making video understanding more efficient, accurate, and transparent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FrameThinker adopts a novel multi-turn reasoning loop, enabling LVLMs to actively and strategically gather information from videos. The methodology is structured around a clear framework, defined actions, a two-phase training strategy, and a carefully designed reinforcement learning reward system.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview: Multi-Turn Reasoning

Instead of a single, passive pass, FrameThinker engages in an iterative reasoning process. At each step, the model generates a textual thought (`<think>...</think>`) and then selects a specific action (`<action>...</action>`) from a predefined space. This explicit separation creates a trajectory of thought-action-observation triplets, where observations are returned by the environment after action execution (e.g., new frames, frame numbers, or a terminal signal). This allows the model to perform a sparse initial scan for an overview, then dynamically "zoom-in" on promising temporal segments for fine-grained analysis, repeating this refinement as needed.

#### 3.2. Defined Actions

FrameThinker utilizes three primary actions for long video reasoning:
- **`choose frames between START FRAME and END FRAME`**: The main action for visual exploration, allowing the retrieval of a sequence of frames (e.g., 8-12 frames) from a specified video segment.
- **`get frame number at time MM:SS`**: An auxiliary action that converts a human-readable timestamp into a precise frame index, enhancing temporal awareness and supporting subsequent `choose frames` actions.
- **`output answer`**: A terminal action, executed when sufficient evidence has been gathered to provide the final answer.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy: SFT and RL

The model is trained using a two-phase strategy:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This initial phase uses a small, curated dataset (2,392 examples) to teach the model the correct syntax and basic mechanics of executing the defined actions. The model is fine-tuned using a standard autoregressive language modeling objective, calculating cross-entropy loss only on the tokens within the `<think>` and `<action>` tags. The SFT data is synthesized using Gemini-2.5-Pro, covering five reasoning templates (Direct Answering, Single-Pass Frame Selection, Timestamp-Grounded Selection, Hierarchical Frame Selection, Multi-Interval Exploration), and is filtered for correctness and logical paths.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Following SFT, this phase transitions the model from memorization to generalization. It utilizes a much larger (28k examples) and more diverse dataset to cultivate an optimal strategic policy for action selection. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed, optimizing an objective function that normalizes outcome rewards to construct advantages, with the KL divergence term omitted for efficiency.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning Reward Design

A key contribution of FrameThinker lies in its in-depth exploration and design of the reward function for multi-turn video analysis:

- **Omission of Format Reward:** Unlike some RLVR frameworks, FrameThinker deliberately omits a direct format reward. Experiments showed that a large format reward can create a perverse incentive, causing the model to suppress action usage and avoid exploration, as outputting a direct, albeit random, answer can stably earn the format reward with less risk than attempting complex actions. Malformed actions are inherently penalized by execution errors that terminate the trajectory and forfeit the accuracy reward.

- **Conditional Action Rewards and Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV):**
 - **Conditional Rewards:** Unconditional action bonuses were found to lead to mode collapse (e.g., repetitive, nonsensical actions). Therefore, action rewards are made *conditional* on the final outcome: `R_total = R_acc + R_action`, where `R_acc` is 1 for a correct answer and 0 otherwise, and `R_action` is a weighted sum of taken actions (choose frames, get frame number) *only if* `R_acc` is 1. This resolves mode collapse from simple action repetition.
 - **Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV):** Even with conditional rewards, the model might learn to execute spurious or illogical actions that happen to be part of a successful trajectory. To prevent the reinforcement of such flawed reasoning, a rule-based CCV module acts as a filter during training (after rollout) and inference (runtime safeguard). It validates trajectories for logical consistency, checking for:
 - **Redundancy:** No identical actions are repeated within a trajectory.
 - **Logical Flow:** Causally linked actions (e.g., `choose frames` after `get frame number`) must utilize information correctly.
 - **Fidelity:** The executed action must be faithful to the preceding thought process (e.g., frame interval in action matches thought).
 Trajectories failing these checks are terminated, and their reward is set to zero (`R_final = R_total * V_CCV(τ)`). This ensures rationality, interpretability, and further improves performance.

- **Action Reward Prioritization:** The weights for action bonuses (`λ_cf` for `choose frames`, `λ_gfn` for `get frame number`) are set such that `λ_gfn >> λ_cf`. This prioritizes the `get frame number` action because it provides objectively correct and verifiable temporal information. While `choose frames` is harder to supervise directly, successful use of `get frame number` indirectly guides and validates the subsequent `choose frames` actions, especially with CCV enforcement.

- **Discouraging Excessive Turns:** Experiments with directly rewarding a higher number of turns (e.g., `R_action = k * (T - 1)`) led to training instability and mode collapse, as the model prioritized increasing turn count over maintaining coherent reasoning. Therefore, this configuration was not adopted, emphasizing that quality and purpose of interactions are more important than sheer quantity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FrameThinker demonstrates superior performance and remarkable efficiency across a comprehensive suite of six benchmarks, including complex reasoning tasks and long-video comprehension.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Overall Average Improvement:** FrameThinker achieves a significant average improvement of +10.4% in accuracy over baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B across all tested benchmarks.
- **State-of-the-Art on Reasoning Benchmarks:**
 - **LongVideo-Reason:** FrameThinker establishes a new state-of-the-art with 76.1% accuracy, outperforming LongVILA-R1 (72.0%).
 - **Video-Holmes:** FrameThinker achieves 46.8% accuracy, also setting a new state-of-the-art on this reasoning-intensive benchmark, surpassing leading closed-source models and other open-source methods.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Gains

- **Drastic Reduction in Processed Frames:** A hallmark of FrameThinker's performance is its efficiency. On LongVideo-Reason, it achieves its superior accuracy using an average of only 20.6 frames, which is over 20 times fewer frames than LongVILA-R1 (which uses 512 frames).
- **Consistent Frame Reduction:** Across long-video comprehension benchmarks (LongVideoBench, MLVU, VideoMME-Long, LVBench), FrameThinker consistently uses significantly fewer frames (e.g., -34% on LongVideoBench, -28% on MLVU) compared to baselines while achieving higher accuracy. This demonstrates unparalleled efficiency alongside effectiveness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on the Video-Holmes benchmark validated the critical components of FrameThinker's methodology:
- **Effect of Training Stages:** A direct comparison showed that the full FrameThinker approach, combining SFT and RL, yields a clear performance advantage over simply fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B with SFT or RL alone, confirming the efficacy of the architecture and training pipeline.
- **Impact of Format Reward:** Including a format reward caused a rapid decline in action usage during early training, confirming the hypothesis that such rewards disincentivize exploration and action utilization.
- **Effectiveness of CCV Module:** The Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module was shown to significantly improve performance when applied during both training and inference. This underscores its role not just in enhancing interpretability but also in driving tangible accuracy gains by preventing the reinforcement of illogical thought-action pairs.
- **Reward Configuration:** Assigning a larger reward bonus to `get frame number` (λ_gfn) than to `choose frames` (λ_cf) yielded the most significant performance benefit. This supports the rationale that precise temporal awareness from `get frame number` provides a more reliable foundation for subsequent visual exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FrameThinker represents a significant advancement in the field of video understanding, particularly for long-duration content. Its contributions offer a new paradigm and substantial impact:

1. **Paradigm Shift to Active Reasoning:** The most profound impact is the shift from passive, uniform video processing to an active, iterative, and dynamic reasoning process. This approach allows LVLMs to intelligently "think with long videos" by actively querying for information, mirroring human cognitive processes when confronted with complex, lengthy visual narratives. This could unlock more sophisticated understanding capabilities for AI systems.

2. **Unprecedented Efficiency:** FrameThinker drastically reduces the computational resources required for long video analysis by processing only a minimal, relevant subset of frames. This efficiency is critical for deploying LVLMs in real-world applications where computational cost and inference latency are major concerns (e.g., video surveillance, content moderation, educational platforms, autonomous driving). The >20x reduction in frames on challenging benchmarks is a testament to this breakthrough.

3. **Enhanced Reasoning and Interpretability:** By enabling multi-turn, multimodal reasoning, FrameThinker improves the model's ability to tackle complex reasoning tasks that require connecting disparate clues across extended temporal contexts. The Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module is a particularly impactful innovation, ensuring that the model's actions are logically consistent with its stated thoughts. This makes the reasoning process more transparent, verifiable, and therefore more trustworthy, which is crucial for high-stakes applications.

4. **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal AI:** The comprehensive exploration of reward design for multi-turn multimodal interaction, including the insights on format rewards, conditional rewards, and the CCV module, provides valuable lessons for the broader RL community working on agentic AI. It addresses common challenges like mode collapse and designing incentives for complex, sequential decision-making in multimodal environments.

5. **Foundation for Future Autonomous Agents:** FrameThinker's end-to-end learnable policy for dynamic video interaction lays a strong foundation for developing more autonomous and intelligent video agents. Such agents could perform complex tasks like "finding an anomaly," "summarizing an event," or "answering a query about a specific moment" without rigid, predefined scripts, allowing for greater adaptability and generalization.

In conclusion, FrameThinker addresses a fundamental limitation of current LVLMs, enabling them to comprehend and reason with long videos with unprecedented efficiency and effectiveness. This work paves the way for more intelligent, resource-efficient, and interpretable AI systems capable of interacting with the dynamic and rich information present in video content.
