# Towards Human-Like Manipulation through RL-Augmented Teleoperation and Mixture-of-Dexterous-Experts VLA

> [!summary] Summary
> An integrated framework for bimanual dexterous manipulation on a 63-DoF robot with 22-DoF hands. IMCopilot supplies RL-trained in-hand rotation and grasp skills that both assist teleoperators via foot pedals and act as callable primitives for the policy. MoDE-VLA fuses arm torque and fingertip tactile tokens into a pretrained flow-matching backbone through sparse expert routing and residual injection, doubling average success rate.

## Problem

- Teleoperating 63-DoF bimanual systems exceeds expert operator skill, so high-quality in-hand manipulation demonstrations for contact-rich dexterity cannot be reliably collected.
- Long-horizon tasks mix vision-guided gross motion, force-guided fine motion, and tactile-guided in-hand routines; one policy struggles to master all phases in high-dimensional action spaces.
- Naively concatenating force and tactile readings into pretrained VLA backbones ignores their distinct physical semantics and temporal scales, degrading rather than improving performance.

## Method

- **IMCopilot**: ==PPO==-trained atomic skills (stable grasp, axis-specified in-hand rotation) with ==asymmetric actor-critic== ==teacher-student distillation== and ==domain randomization==, triggered by foot pedal during collection and by a scalar action-vector signal at inference.
- **MoDE-VLA**: ==force tokens== from 14 arm joint torques and ==tactile tokens== from 60 fingertip wrench dimensions are linearly projected, replicated across the 50-step horizon with ==sinusoidal encodings==, then ==self-attended== with backbone prefix and suffix tokens.
- **Mixture-of-Dexterous-Experts**: 8 ==expert MLPs== with ==top-1 routing== refine force and tactile tokens, injected as modality-specific ==residual corrections== through separate linear heads for arm versus hand actions atop the ==flow-matching== prediction.

## Results

- IMCopilot achieves **89%** in-hand rotation success (**80/90**) versus **34%** (**31/90**) for plain teleoperation, with the biggest gap on ping-pong balls: **83%** versus **10%**.
- MoDE-VLA reaches **34%** average success across four tasks versus **15%** for the baseline, gaining **20 points** on gear assembling and **10** on charger plugging; apple peeling hits **30%** success and **73%** peel completion.
- Ablations: removing force costs **11 points** average success, removing tactile costs **8 points**, and removing IMCopilot drops peel completion from **73%** to **25%** due to dropped or unrotated apples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When a manipulation phase defeats human teleoperators, it will likely also defeat a policy imitating them; inserting a dedicated skill primitive fixes both data and execution.
> - Heterogeneous sensing should get its own pathway with residual injection, so corrections vanish in free space and pretrained backbone knowledge survives fusion.
> - Modality routing should follow physical semantics: arm torques inform arm actions, fingertip wrenches inform finger actions, and mixing them invites cross-contamination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper operates within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing the challenges of extending advanced learning models to complex, human-like dexterous tasks. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as π₀, OpenVLA, and RT-2, have enabled robots to perform general-purpose manipulation by leveraging large-scale vision and language pretraining. These models often map visual and linguistic inputs to robotic actions, showing capabilities in tasks like pick-and-place.

However, existing VLA models typically operate with low-degree-of-freedom (DoF) end-effectors, such as parallel-jaw grippers, and excel in simpler tasks that primarily rely on visual guidance. The transition from basic grasping to dexterous, in-hand manipulation, especially in contact-rich scenarios involving multi-finger coordination and bimanual operations, remains a significant challenge. Human dexterity, exemplified by tasks like peeling an apple, involves a seamless integration of hierarchical decision-making, rich tactile feedback, and a repertoire of practiced manipulation skills. Replicating these capabilities in robots requires both high-DoF hardware and sophisticated learning frameworks that can acquire hierarchical skills grounded in multimodal sensory feedback.

This work contributes to addressing three key bottlenecks in this domain:
1. **Data Acquisition:** Teleoperating high-DoF bimanual systems for complex in-hand manipulation is difficult, leading to a bottleneck in collecting high-quality demonstrations for imitation learning. Previous teleoperation systems, including vision-based, glove-based, and exoskeleton-based approaches, face limitations such as occlusion, lack of explicit force/contact awareness, or high cognitive load for operators.
2. **Multi-Skill Learning:** Complex tasks often involve distinct phases requiring different types of skills (e.g., vision-guided gross motions, force-guided fine motions, tactile-guided in-hand routines). Learning these diverse skills within a single, high-dimensional action space with a single policy is challenging.
3. **Modality Heterogeneity:** Integrating heterogeneous sensory data, particularly force and tactile feedback, into existing pretrained VLA backbones is non-trivial. Naïve concatenation of these modalities can degrade performance, as they have different physical semantics and temporal dynamics compared to visual and linguistic inputs. Prior work has explored integrating force/tactile feedback into imitation learning, often for two-finger grippers, but large-scale integration for high-DoF dexterous hands remains less explored due to hardware complexity and dataset scarcity.

By introducing an RL-augmented teleoperation system and a novel VLA architecture, the paper aims to push the boundaries of robotic dexterity, allowing VLAs to extend beyond simple pick-and-place to more intricate, human-like contact-rich manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to extend the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models beyond low-degree-of-freedom, vision-guided pick-and-place tasks to enable human-like, bimanual dexterous manipulation, specifically in contact-rich in-hand operations. This overarching goal is motivated by three identified challenges:

1. **Addressing the Data Acquisition Bottleneck for High-DoF Systems:**
 - **Motivation:** Directly teleoperating bimanual robotic systems with a large number of degrees of freedom (e.g., 63 DoFs in their setup) for precise, contact-rich in-hand manipulation is cognitively demanding and often exceeds the capabilities of human operators, even experts. This difficulty leads to inconsistent or low-quality demonstration data, which is crucial for training imitation learning policies. For instance, tasks requiring continuous object reorientation within the hand, like peeling an apple, are exceptionally difficult to perform reliably through direct teleoperation.
 - **Objective:** To develop a system that simplifies data collection for complex dexterous tasks, enabling the acquisition of high-fidelity demonstrations that would otherwise be unobtainable. This involves assisting human operators during challenging in-hand manipulation phases.

2. **Facilitating Multi-Skill Learning for Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:**
 - **Motivation:** Tasks like apple peeling involve a sequence of qualitatively distinct manipulation phases, such as vision-guided approaching, force-guided cutting, and tactile-guided in-hand object rotation. A single, monolithic policy attempting to master all these distinct skills within a high-dimensional action space often struggles to achieve robust performance across the entire task horizon. This suggests a need for a hierarchical approach where specialized skills can be leveraged.
 - **Objective:** To enable a learning framework that can seamlessly integrate different manipulation skills, allowing a high-level policy (like a VLA) to handle planning and gross motions while delegating specific, complex sub-skills (like in-hand rotation) to dedicated, robust low-level primitives.

3. **Effectively Integrating Heterogeneous Force and Tactile Modalities into Pretrained VLAs:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing VLA backbones are typically pretrained on datasets where force and tactile feedback are absent or not explicitly utilized. Simply concatenating force and tactile observations to the state vector fails to account for their distinct physical semantics (e.g., arm-level torques vs. fingertip contact patterns) and different temporal dynamics compared to visual and linguistic inputs. Prior research indicates that such naive integration can degrade rather than enhance performance.
 - **Objective:** To develop a robust fusion mechanism that can integrate heterogeneous force and tactile information into a pretrained VLA backbone in a contact-aware manner, allowing for refined action generation without undermining the model's existing knowledge and generalization capabilities. This requires a method that respects the unique characteristics of each sensory modality.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes an integrated framework built upon two synergistic components: **IMCopilot (In-hand Manipulation Copilot)** and **MoDE-VLA (Mixture-of-Dexterous-Experts VLA)**.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates two main components: IMCopilot for skill-level shared autonomy and hierarchical control, and MoDE-VLA for multimodal sensory fusion.

### 4. A. Robot Platform and Data Acquisition System

The research utilizes a sophisticated robotic platform and a corresponding data acquisition system.

- **Robot Platform (SharpaNorth):**
 - Features dual 7-DoF robotic arms, each equipped with 22-DoF SharpaWave dexterous hands.
 - Each fingertip includes a vision-based tactile sensor, providing 6-DoF force and wrench readings.
 - Additional DoFs include a 2-DoF neck and a 3-DoF upper body, resulting in 63 active DoFs when the lower body is fixed.
 - Observations are captured by two head-mounted RGB cameras (stereo vision) and two wrist-mounted fish-eye cameras (close-range views).
 - Crucially, the system records joint torque readings from both arms and 6-DoF force/wrench signals from all ten fingertip tactile sensors, which serve as the force and tactile modalities for MoDE-VLA.

- **Teleoperation System:**
 - Comprises an upper-body exoskeleton for arm kinematics, a pair of exoskeleton gloves for hand articulation, and a VR headset for visual feedback.
 - Exoskeletons directly map human arm and finger joint positions to the robot's action space.
 - The VR headset streams stereoscopic vision from the robot's head cameras.
 - Force and tactile feedback from the robot's joint torque sensors and fingertip tactile arrays are rendered as visual overlays in VR and provided as vibrotactile feedback on the gloves to enhance operator awareness.

- **From Teleoperation to Shared Autonomy:**
 - While effective for standard tasks, complex maneuvers like stable, continuous in-hand rotation (e.g., for apple peeling) proved infeasible for even expert operators via direct teleoperation.
 - An RL-augmented shared-autonomy mechanism is introduced: operators use foot pedals to trigger pre-trained in-hand manipulation skills (IMCopilot) while retaining control of gross arm motions via the exoskeleton. This hybrid approach aims to reduce cognitive load and yield high-quality demonstrations for difficult phases.

### 5. B. Learning In-hand Manipulation Copilots (IMCopilot)

IMCopilot is a suite of Reinforcement Learning (RL)-trained atomic in-hand manipulation primitives designed for dual use: assisting teleoperation and acting as callable low-level skills for the VLA.

- **Skill Repertoire:**
 - Includes primitives such as stable grasp maintenance (to hold objects under perturbation) and in-hand object rotation around a specified axis. These are designed to be composable.
- **RL Training:**
 - Each skill is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within the IsaacLab simulation environment.
 - An asymmetric actor-critic architecture with teacher-student distillation is employed, where the teacher receives privileged information (object pose, velocities, mass, friction) to learn an optimal policy, and the student learns to regress this information from standard observations.
 - Observations ($o_t$) for the student comprise a 3-step history of proprioception, fingertip contact forces, and the target rotation axis.
 - The policy outputs relative joint position offsets ($\Delta\theta_t$), which are integrated and tracked by low-level PD controllers.
 - Domain randomization (object scale, mass, friction, CoM offset, gravity, PD gains) is applied to facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
 - The reward function is a weighted sum of components: encouraging angular velocity ($r_{rot}$), penalizing linear velocity ($r_{vel}$), excessive joint work ($r_{work}$), torque ($r_{torq}$), and joint deviation ($r_{diff}$).
- **Dual Role:**
 - **Data Collection:** Human operators trigger IMCopilot via foot pedals for in-hand manipulation, simplifying demonstration collection.
 - **Autonomous Execution:** The VLA issues a scalar trigger signal ($c \in$). If $c > 0.5$, IMCopilot takes over hand actions for in-hand rotation, while the VLA retains control of arm actions.

### 6. C. Fusing Force and Tactile into VLA with MoDE (Mixture-of-Dexterous-Experts VLA)

MoDE-VLA addresses the challenge of integrating heterogeneous force and tactile modalities into a VLA backbone pretrained without such feedback. It follows three design principles: a dedicated pathway, modality-aware routing, and residual injection.

- **VLA Backbone:**
 - Extends a pretrained VLA backbone, π₀, which consists of a SigLIP vision tokenizer, a PaliGemma vision-language model, and a lightweight action expert.
 - The model predicts a velocity field using a flow-matching objective, minimizing the difference between the predicted velocity and the true velocity required to denoise a noisy action sequence.
 - Actions are generated by integrating this velocity field from pure noise to a clean action using Euler's method.
 - The full action vector includes arm actions ($a_{arm}$), hand actions ($a_{hand}$), and other actions ($a_{other}$, including waist actions and the IMCopilot trigger $c$).

- **Force and Tactile Token Construction:**
 - **Force Signal ($f$):** 14 joint torque readings (7 joints × 2 arms), reflecting arm-level contact forces.
 - **Tactile Signal ($g$):** 60 6-DoF force and wrench measurements (5 fingers × 6 dimensions × 2 hands), capturing fingertip-level contact patterns.
 - Each modality is projected into the PaliGemma embedding space via learned linear layers ($z_f, z_g$).
 - Each embedding is replicated $H$ (action horizon) times, and sinusoidal step-positional encodings are added to create temporally-indexed token sequences ($\tilde{Z}_f, \tilde{Z}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times d_{pali}}$). This allows for per-timestep expert specialization by the MoE.

- **Mixture-of-Dexterous-Experts Module:**
 - The MoDE block takes the backbone's contextual output, the current denoising state, and the force/tactile tokens.
 - It concatenates four token sequences: backbone's prefix output, action expert's suffix output, and the force/tactile token sequences.
 - A self-attention layer processes this concatenated sequence, allowing force and tactile tokens to interact with visual-linguistic context and denoising state.
 - The attended force and tactile tokens are then passed through a token-level Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer with $E=8$ expert MLPs and top-k=1 sparse scatter routing. This allows different experts to specialize in different contact regimes or joint groups (arms vs. fingers).

- **Residual Injection and Action Generation:**
 - The MoE layer outputs refined force tokens ($\hat{Z}_f$) and tactile tokens ($\hat{Z}_g$).
 - These are injected as residual corrections to the backbone's action prediction through modality-specific projection heads:
 $v_\theta(x_t, t) = [W_1(\hat{Z}_f + Z_{suffix}) \parallel W_2(\hat{Z}_g + Z_{suffix})]$
 where $W_1$ and $W_2$ are linear layers for arm and hand actions, respectively.
 - This residual structure ensures MoDE refines, rather than overwrites, the base VLA prediction, maintaining pretrained knowledge.
 - Modality-specific routing (force for arm actions, tactile for hand actions) avoids cross-contamination.
 - When IMCopilot is triggered ($c > 0.5$), it overrides the VLA's hand actions ($a_{hand}$).

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MoDE-VLA involved four contact-rich manipulation tasks: Gear Assembling, Charger Plugging, Tube Rearranging, and Apple Peeling. Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR) and, for Apple Peeling, Peel Completion Ratio (PCR). Experiments compared MoDE-VLA against a baseline (π₀) and included ablation studies to assess individual component contributions.

### 8. A. Performance of Data Acquisition System (Q1)

- **Force and Tactile Feedback in Teleoperation:** The integration of force and tactile visual feedback significantly improved teleoperation efficiency and reliability. For example, in Gear Assembling, operators completed 100 trials in 65 minutes with 93 successes with feedback, compared to 75 minutes and 85 successes without feedback. This indicates reduced operator cognitive load and higher quality demonstrations.
- **IMCopilot for In-hand Manipulation:** IMCopilot enabled reliable in-hand rotation, a task difficult to achieve via plain teleoperation. Across various objects (ping-pong ball, tennis ball, apple), IMCopilot achieved an average success rate of 89% (80/90 trials) for in-hand manipulation, in contrast to 34% (31/90 trials) for direct teleoperation. The most notable improvement was for small objects like a ping-pong ball (83% vs. 10%) and for the apple (90% vs. 27%), where precise multi-finger coordination is critical. These results support the necessity of IMCopilot for collecting high-fidelity demonstrations for complex in-hand tasks.

### 9. B. MoDE-VLA Policy Performance (Q2)

- **Overall Performance:** MoDE-VLA achieved an average success rate of 34% across the four tasks, outperforming the baseline π₀ by 19% (π₀ average SR was 15%).
- **Single-Arm Insertion Tasks (Gear Assembling, Charger Plugging):** MoDE-VLA showed substantial improvements. For Gear Assembling, its SR was 60% compared to π₀'s 40%. For Charger Plugging, it achieved 15% SR against π₀'s 5%. These improvements are attributed to the MoDE module's ability to activate force-specialized experts, enabling dynamic compliance and precise force regulation during contact onset and insertion.
- **Bimanual Tasks (Tube Rearranging, Apple Peeling):**
 - **Tube Rearranging:** MoDE-VLA achieved a 30% SR, doubling π₀'s 15% SR. This suggests improved inter-limb synchronization due to the joint processing of force and tactile streams with visual context.
 - **Apple Peeling:** MoDE-VLA achieved a 30% SR and a 73% Peel Completion Ratio (PCR). The PCR is notable, indicating that the policy could often complete a full ring of peel, whereas baseline methods frequently failed due to slippage or insufficient rotation. This is attributed to the IMCopilot mechanism, which dispatches the RL-trained rotation expert at appropriate moments, enabling closed-loop completion of the peel-and-rotate cycle.

### 10. C. Ablation Studies (Q3)

Ablation studies quantified the contribution of each MoDE-VLA component:
- **Effect of Force Sensing (w/o Force):** Removing force input led to the largest degradation in performance, reducing the average SR by 11% (from 34% to 23%). Specifically, success rates in insertion tasks dropped significantly (e.g., Gear Assembling from 60% to 45%, Charger Plugging from 15% to 5%). The force-ablated model also struggled with establishing initial contact during apple peeling. This confirms that force signals are crucial for contact detection and compliant control.
- **Effect of Tactile Sensing (w/o Tactile):** Ablating the tactile stream resulted in an 8% reduction in average SR (from 34% to 26%). This primarily manifested as increased slip events during grasp-intensive phases across tasks. The tactile modality provides local fingertip deformation cues not captured by other sensors. However, its removal did not significantly impact apple peeling SR or PCR, as IMCopilot (which utilizes tactile information internally) handles apple manipulation.
- **Effect of IMCopilot (w/o IMCopilot):** Directly training the VLA to learn hand actions from IMCopilot demonstrations, without explicitly invoking IMCopilot as a primitive, led to a substantial degradation in the Apple Peeling task. The PCR dropped from 73% to 25%. This failure was primarily due to accidental apple drops or inability to rotate the apple after the first peel. This highlights IMCopilot's critical role in providing robust, low-level in-hand manipulation skills, particularly for complex cyclic tasks.

### 11. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a framework that addresses several challenges in extending Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to complex, human-like dexterous manipulation. The contributions suggest pathways for advancing robotic capabilities in contact-rich and multi-fingered tasks.

- **Enabling High-Fidelity Data Acquisition:** The introduction of IMCopilot in a shared-autonomy teleoperation system directly tackles the data acquisition bottleneck for high-DoF dexterous systems. By offloading challenging in-hand manipulation phases to an autonomous copilot, operators can generate higher quality and more consistent demonstration data. This approach has the potential to accelerate the development of complex manipulation skills by making data collection more efficient and less burdensome.
- **Promoting Hierarchical Skill Learning:** IMCopilot's dual role as a teleoperation assistant and a callable low-level primitive for the VLA establishes a hierarchical control framework. This approach allows the VLA to focus on high-level vision-language-guided planning and gross motions, while specialized, robust RL-trained skills handle reactive, fine-grained in-hand dexterity. This hierarchical paradigm could be significant for developing policies for long-horizon, multi-phase tasks that are difficult for monolithic end-to-end learning.
- **Effective Multimodal Sensory Integration:** The MoDE-VLA architecture demonstrates a method for integrating heterogeneous force and tactile feedback into pretrained VLA backbones. By using dedicated pathways, sparse Mixture-of-Experts routing, and residual injection, the framework enables contact-aware action refinement without degrading the backbone's existing knowledge. This indicates a method for incorporating rich physical interaction data, which is essential for tasks requiring precise contact detection, force regulation, and slip prevention.
- **Advancing Dexterous Manipulation Capabilities:** The experimental validation, particularly the successful demonstration of autonomous dual-dexterous-hand apple peeling, showcases a significant step towards more human-like, contact-rich manipulation. This task requires complex bimanual coordination, precise force control, and adaptive in-hand manipulation, demonstrating the synergy of the proposed components. The framework's generalization to other tasks like gear assembling, charger plugging, and tube rearranging further suggests its applicability to a range of fine motor control challenges.

The implications of this research include moving robotic systems towards greater autonomy and adaptability in unstructured environments where delicate object interaction and fine motor skills are required. The proposed methodology could inform the design of future VLA models, teleoperation systems, and training paradigms for complex robotic tasks, ultimately contributing to the development of more capable and dexterous robots.
