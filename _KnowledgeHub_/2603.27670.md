# ProgressVLA: Progress-Guided Diffusion Policy for Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ProgressVLA introduces a framework for robotic manipulation that integrates an explicit, differentiable task progress estimator to guide diffusion policies. This approach enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to generate more goal-directed and efficient trajectories, resulting in improved success rates and reduced execution steps across complex, long-horizon tasks in benchmarks and real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack an explicit, dense understanding of task progress, leading to reliance on implicit cues or brittle, hand-crafted termination heuristics.
- Generative policies, such as diffusion models, may produce visually plausible actions but often fail to efficiently advance towards the specified task goal, particularly in complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Learning robust task progress directly from raw visual observations is challenging due to nuisance variations, which limits generalization to new environments or object instances.

## Method

- A robust ==progress estimator== is developed, utilizing frozen **DINOv2** and **CLIP** features, to output a normalized scalar progress score (0-1) conditioned on language instructions and current/initial visual observations.
- A two-stage ==diffusion policy==, operating in a ==latent action space==, is guided at inference time by gradients derived from the ==differentiable progress estimator== via an ==action-conditioned world model==, steering action sampling towards maximizing progress.
- The framework incorporates online ==Reinforcement Learning (RL) finetuning==, employing a ==margin-based monotonicity loss== for the progress estimator and a ==KL-regularized objective== to refine the diffusion policy based on real-world experience.

## Results

- **ProgressVLA** achieved a **95.2%** 1-in-a-row success rate and an average completed length of **3.73** on the CALVIN benchmark, demonstrating improved performance over unguided policies and baselines.
- The full **ProgressVLA** model attained an average success rate of **84.5%** on the LIBERO benchmark, showing a substantial improvement on the LIBERO-LONG split (**66.2%**) compared to baselines like OpenVLA (**53.7%**).
- In real-world robot experiments, **ProgressVLA** with guidance achieved an average **76%** success rate, reducing end-effector travel distance by **35%** and execution steps by **72%** compared to Octo, indicating increased efficiency and reliability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating a differentiable task progress signal directly into the action generation process significantly enhances the goal-directedness and efficiency of diffusion policies for robotic manipulation.
> - Pretraining the progress estimator and world model on large-scale datasets with robust visual features is crucial for achieving strong generalization to unseen appearance shifts and novel objects.
> - Online RL finetuning, incorporating progress-specific regularization like a monotonicity loss, further improves the alignment of the learned progress estimator and the robustness of the guided policy to real-world execution variability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to enable robots to understand and execute tasks based on natural language instructions and visual observations. These models often leverage large-scale datasets and multimodal representations. Current VLA research approaches typically fall into three paradigms: autoregressive tokenization of continuous control signals, direct supervised regression to joint action spaces, and generative trajectory modeling, such as diffusion policies.

While diffusion policies and autoregressive models can generate plausible action trajectories, many existing VLA models primarily rely on implicit goal-satisfaction cues or dense action supervision, which can limit scalability and robustness in complex, long-horizon tasks. A significant limitation is the lack of explicit "progress awareness" or a dense notion of task completion. This often leads to reliance on brittle, hand-crafted heuristics for task termination, and generative policies may produce actions that appear visually reasonable but are not effectively advancing towards the specified goal. Previous work on world models has focused on learning latent dynamics for prediction and planning, or estimating sparse objectives like success classifiers.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing an explicit mechanism for estimating and integrating task progress into the action generation process of diffusion policies. It builds upon the strengths of large-scale pretraining, latent action spaces, and world models, but differentiates itself by making progress estimation a core, differentiable component used to guide action sampling. By embedding progress awareness directly into policy generation, this research aims to move beyond passive vision-language conditioning towards more goal-directed and robust robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving cascaded sub-goals and long horizons.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by explicitly integrating task progress awareness. This involves two main technical contributions: developing a robust progress estimation mechanism and implementing a differentiable method to use this progress signal to guide action generation.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing VLA models, especially when applied to long-horizon manipulation tasks:

- **Lack of Progress Awareness:** Many current VLA models do not possess an explicit, dense understanding of how much progress has been made towards a task goal. Instead, they often rely on hand-crafted heuristics for task termination, which can be brittle and unreliable, particularly in tasks involving multiple, sequential sub-goals.
- **Inefficient Generative Policies:** Generative models, such as tokenized autoregressive models and diffusion policies, can produce diverse and plausible trajectories. However, their sampling processes are largely driven by conditioning and typically lack an explicit mechanism to steer generation directly towards goal completion. Without a dense notion of progress, these policies might generate actions that are visually reasonable but do not efficiently advance the task, leading to suboptimal or failed executions.
- **Difficulty with Long-Horizon Tasks:** Tasks requiring a sequence of sub-goals or extended execution often suffer from compounding errors when there is no clear internal measure of advancement. Policies may get stuck in local optima or diverge from the optimal path without a progress signal to reorient them.
- **Noisy Goal-Satisfaction Cues:** Some approaches depend on implicit and often noisy cues for goal satisfaction, making it challenging to provide consistent and reliable feedback for policy learning and execution.
- **Challenges in Learning Progress:** Learning progress directly from raw pixels can be difficult due to nuisance variations (e.g., camera jitter, background shifts, distractor objects) that can be entangled with task dynamics, leading to noisy and poorly aligned progress signals.

To address these issues, the authors propose ProgressVLA, which aims to:
1. **Develop a Robust Progress Estimator:** This estimator is designed to output a normalized completion score (0-1) conditioned on language instructions and visual observations. It is pre-trained on large-scale datasets and specifically designed to be robust to visual variations and generalize to unseen scenarios.
2. **Integrate Progress Guidance into Policy Generation:** Rather than using progress as a post-hoc evaluator, the goal is to embed it directly into the action generation process. This is achieved through a differentiable pipeline where the progress estimator provides guidance to a diffusion policy, steering it towards actions that are predicted to maximize progress.
3. **Improve Task Success Rates and Efficiency:** By making policies "progress-aware," the research seeks to improve task success rates, reduce the number of steps required for task completion, and enable more principled and robust termination criteria.
4. **Enhance Generalization:** Leveraging pre-trained visual feature spaces and large-scale datasets is intended to improve the generalization of both the progress estimator and the policy to new environments and variations.

A motivating empirical observation (Table I in the paper) demonstrates that explicit progress guidance significantly improves progress alignment, reduces completion steps, and increases success rates compared to a standard diffusion policy, providing a strong basis for these objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ProgressVLA framework integrates a progress estimator with a diffusion policy, leveraging a latent action space and a world model. It consists of three main components: a progress estimator, an action-conditioned world model, and a progress-guided diffusion policy, which can be further enhanced by RL finetuning.

**A. Progress Estimator**
The progress estimator, denoted as P, functions as a vision-language evaluator. It takes as input a language instruction `l`, an initial observation `o₀` (serving as a task anchor), and the current observation `o_t`. It outputs a normalized scalar progress score `p` between 0 and 1, indicating the estimated task completion.
- **Training:** P is trained as a regressor using an L₁ loss, `L_prog = |p - p*|`, where `p*` is the ground-truth progress label. This `p*` is approximated by the normalized timestep `t/T` (current timestep / total trajectory length), assuming monotonic advancement in expert demonstrations.
- **Architecture:** It uses frozen pretrained features from DINOv2 (visual patch tokens) and CLIP (text tokens). These are projected into a shared embedding space, augmented with learnable role embeddings (start, current, text), and processed by a lightweight cross-attention stack. A fusion MLP with a sigmoid head outputs the scalar progress score.

**B. World Model**
A compact latent world model is incorporated to capture visual states and dynamics over short horizons. This model comprises:
- **Vision Encoder (E) / Inverse Dynamics Model:** Maps the transition between two observations (`o_t` and `o_{t+N}`) into a compressed latent action space, `a_z = E(o_t, o_{t+N})`.
- **Decoder (D) / Forward Dynamics Model:** Predicts a future image (`o_{t+N}`) given a current observation `o_t` and a latent action `a_z`, `o_{t+N} = D(o_t, a_z)`. This operates in a visual feature space (DINOv2 features) rather than pixel space for robustness.
- **Training Objective:** The world model is trained with `L_world = Σ ||o_{t+N} - o*_{t+N}||² + KL(a_z, N(0, I))`. This combines a latent-dynamics reconstruction loss with a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term to regularize the latent action distribution towards a normal distribution, improving stability. A VQ bottleneck is used for latent-action discretization.

**C. Joint Finetuning of World Model and Progress Estimator**
After separate pre-training, the world model and progress estimator are jointly fine-tuned to align latent dynamics with task progression. The joint loss, `L_joint = ||p_{t+N} - p*_{t+N}||`, supervises the predicted progress `p_{t+N} = P(l, o₀, D(o_t, a_z_{t:t+N}))` against the ground truth. The overall fine-tuning objective is `L_ft = L_world + L_prog + L_joint`.

**D. Progress-Guided Diffusion Policy**
The policy employs a two-stage generation pipeline:
1. **Latent Action Expert:** A diffusion model (based on a causal Transformer like DiTA) generates an action chunk `a_z_{t:t+N}` in an embodiment-agnostic latent space, focusing on high-level task strategy. It is trained to optimize a denoising objective `L_diff = E ||ϵ - ϵ_θ(x_τ, τ, l, o_t)||²`.
2. **Action Decoder:** Another diffusion model maps the generated `a_z_{t:t+N}` into a low-level executable action sequence `a_{t:t+N-1}`.

- **Classifier Guidance (CG):** At inference time, the diffusion process is guided by the progress estimator through the world model. Given a noisy latent action sample `x_τ`, the world model predicts the resulting future image `ô_{t+N} = D(o_t, x_τ)`. The progress estimator then assigns a predicted progress score `p̂_{t+N} = P(l, z₀, ô_{t+N})`. Since `p̂_{t+N}` is differentiable with respect to `x_τ`, its gradients (`∇_xτ p̂_{t+N}`) are used as classifier guidance to modify the diffusion update rule, steering the sampling towards actions that maximize progress: `x_{τ-1} = μ_θ(x_τ, τ, c) + s ∇_xτ p̂_{t+N} + σ_τ ϵ`, where `s` controls guidance strength. A noise-conditioned evaluator `D(o_t, x_τ, τ)` is used during guidance to stabilize gradients.
- **Two-stage inference with latent warm-start:** Inference involves two coupled diffusion processes. The latent-action diffusion runs first for `T_z = 1000` steps until a compatible noise scale (`T_a = 100`) is reached. Then, a joint denoising loop updates both the latent variable and the action chunk for `T_a = 100` steps, with the action decoder conditioned on the updated latent.

**E. RL Finetuning with Progress**
To further improve robustness, ProgressVLA allows for finetuning both the progress estimator and the diffusion policy with online experience:
1. **Online Trajectory Collection:** The current policy periodically collects trajectories in the environment, including observations, language instructions, actions, predicted progress, and episode success labels.
2. **Progress Estimator Finetuning:** For successful episodes, progress should be monotonic. A margin-based monotonicity loss `L_mono` is applied to correct instances where the predicted progress violates this expectation (`I_anom = {t | p̂_t > p̂_t' + ϵ}`). The estimator is finetuned by minimizing `L_prog + L_mono` on the online buffer.
3. **Diffusion Policy Finetuning:** Policy improvement is formulated as a KL-regularized optimization problem that maximizes a task-aware score `Q(s, a) = P(l, o₀, D(o_t, a_z))` (predicted progress after applying action `a`) while staying close to the current policy. This leads to an update rule `π*(a|s) ∝ π_θ(a|s) exp(1/α Q(s, a))`. In diffusion policies, this translates to adjusting the noise target `ϵ̃ = ϵ - (σ_τ/α) ∇_xτ Q(s, x_τ)` and training the policy with the standard denoising objective `L_policy = E ||ϵ̃ - ϵ_θ(x_τ, τ, l, o_t)||²`.

**Pretraining Data:** All components are pre-trained on the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the CALVIN and LIBERO benchmarks, as well as on real-world robot deployment, to evaluate the performance of ProgressVLA.

**A. CALVIN Benchmark (ABC→D protocol)**
CALVIN evaluates long-horizon, language-conditioned manipulation tasks. Metrics include success sequence length (1 to 5 subtasks completed in a row) and average tasks completed per episode.
- **Pretraining Contribution:** Pretraining the diffusion policy alone (Pretrained ProgressVLA (w/o CG)) yielded substantial improvements over a from-scratch trained policy (ProgressVLA (w/o CG)). For example, the 5-in-a-row success rate increased from 43.1% to 51.6%, and the average length improved from 3.24 to 3.57. This suggests pretraining establishes a robust latent-action prior.
- **Impact of Classifier Guidance (CG):** Adding CG to a pretrained policy (Pretrained ProgressVLA (w/ CG)) showed moderate improvement. The 5-in-a-row rate slightly increased to 52.8%.
- **Reliability of Evaluator:** The benefit of CG became more pronounced when the evaluator (world model + progress predictor) was also pretrained. Pretrained ProgressVLA (w/ CG (pretrained)) achieved a 5-in-a-row rate of 56.4% (+3.6% over w/ CG) and an average length of 3.68, suggesting that a robust, pretrained evaluator provides higher-quality guidance gradients.
- **RL Finetuning:** The full model (Pretrained ProgressVLA (Full)), which incorporates RL finetuning, demonstrated the best overall performance, achieving a 1-in-a-row success rate of 95.2% and an average completed length of 3.73. This indicates that online experience improves progress-completion alignment and policy robustness.

**B. LIBERO Benchmark**
LIBERO assesses knowledge transfer in multitask and lifelong robot learning across spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon tasks.
- **Consistent Gains:** ProgressVLA with classifier guidance (ProgressVLA w/ cg) consistently outperformed its unguided counterpart (ProgressVLA w/o cg) across all subsets, leading to an average success rate improvement from 81.5% to 83.3%.
- **Strongest Performance:** The full ProgressVLA model achieved the best overall average success rate of 84.5%, with notable improvements on the long-horizon LIBERO-LONG split (66.2%) compared to baselines like OpenVLA (53.7%). This supports the effectiveness of progress-guided diffusion for complex, long-horizon tasks.

**C. Real-World Evaluation (ARX AC-One robotic dual-arm)**
Experiments on five real-world manipulation tasks validated the model's performance in a physical setting. Metrics included success rate, end-effector travel distance, and executed action chunks (steps).
- **Superiority over Baseline:** ProgressVLA (w/o CG) substantially outperformed Octo, a strong pretrained VLA policy. Averaged across tasks, Octo achieved 23% success with 1.30m distance and 187.9 steps, while ProgressVLA (w/o CG) increased success to 66% and reduced distance/steps to 0.96m/100.8.
- **Impact of Guidance:** Progress-guided classifier guidance (ProgressVLA (w/ CG)) further improved both reliability and efficiency. It achieved an average 76% success rate, 0.81m distance, and 53.3 steps. The gains were particularly noticeable in tasks prone to redundant motion, indicating more decisive and goal-directed execution.

**D. Progress Estimator Generalization on Real-Robot Expert Trajectories**
The progress estimator's robustness was evaluated independently using real-robot expert demonstrations under various conditions: original setting, lighting shift, and novel objects. Metrics included Pearson correlation (progress alignment), stop reliability (fraction of trajectories with high progress near termination), and MAE (progress error).
- **Importance of Pretraining:** Pretraining the progress predictor on large-scale data proved crucial for generalization. In the original setting, a pretrained+finetuned estimator significantly outperformed a from-scratch trained one (Pearson: 0.977 vs. 0.912; Stop: 82.1% vs. 53.8%; MAE: 0.10 vs. 0.14).
- **Robustness to Appearance Shifts:** Under lighting shift, the from-scratch model degraded significantly (Pearson: 0.809, Stop: 3.6%), whereas the pretrained+finetuned model maintained strong performance (Pearson: 0.953, Stop: 80.8%, MAE: 0.12). A similar trend was observed for novel objects (Pearson: 0.972 vs. 0.810; Stop: 81.2% vs. 37.5%; MAE: 0.11 vs. 0.15). These results indicate that a pretrained progress predictor, even with limited finetuning on real data, is robust to common scene variations and provides a reliable guidance signal.

**E. Visualization**
Visualizations confirmed that ProgressVLA generated more plausible and goal-directed trajectories compared to the baseline diffusion policy. Progress score curves across operations were generally smoother and more monotonic with guidance, indicating consistent task advancement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces ProgressVLA, a framework that integrates an explicit progress signal into diffusion-based action generation for robotic manipulation. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Efficiency in Long-Horizon Tasks:** By enabling VLA models to be "progress-aware," ProgressVLA addresses a core limitation of existing generative policies that often lack explicit goal-directedness. The demonstrated improvements in success rates, reduced execution steps, and lower end-effector travel distance on complex, long-horizon tasks (CALVIN, LIBERO-LONG, real-world scenarios) indicate that robots can complete tasks more reliably and efficiently. This could lead to more practical and robust robot deployments in environments requiring sequential or extended operations.

- **Principled Task Termination:** The learned progress estimator provides a dense, normalized completion score that can serve as a principled and explicit termination criterion. This moves beyond brittle, hand-crafted heuristics, potentially simplifying policy design and improving the autonomy of robotic systems. A clear progress signal also offers a mechanism for monitoring task execution and identifying potential failures or deviations early.

- **Improved Generalization under Distribution Shifts:** The use of pre-trained visual features (DINOv2) and large-scale robotic datasets (OXE) for the progress estimator, combined with finetuning, contributes to its robustness. The ability of the progress estimator to generalize well under appearance shifts such as lighting changes and novel objects suggests that the framework is less susceptible to domain gaps, which is crucial for real-world applications where environments are dynamic and varied.

- **Foundation for Differentiable Planning and Control:** The introduction of a differentiable progress guidance mechanism, where gradients from the progress estimator are backpropagated through a world model to refine latent actions, establishes a paradigm for differentiable planning within generative policies. This allows for iterative optimization of action chunks during generation, steering the policy toward optimal paths without requiring costly explicit planning or extensive re-sampling.

- **Robustness through RL Finetuning:** The integration of online RL finetuning with specific mechanisms like the monotonicity loss for the progress estimator and KL-regularized policy improvement for the diffusion policy further enhances the system's robustness. This allows the model to adapt to online experience, refine its understanding of progress, and become more resilient to execution noise and edge cases not fully captured in offline datasets.

- **Contribution to Generalist Robot Policies:** By improving performance and robustness in a general VLA framework, ProgressVLA contributes to the development of more capable and generalist robot policies that can perform a wider array of tasks in diverse conditions with minimal re-training or adaptation. The latent action space also promotes cross-embodiment flexibility.

In summary, ProgressVLA represents a step towards more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems by endowing them with an internal sense of task accomplishment, facilitating more goal-directed, efficient, and robust manipulation in complex, long-horizon scenarios.
