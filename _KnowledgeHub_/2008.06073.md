# Visuomotor Mechanical Search: Learning to Retrieve Target Objects in Clutter

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford, Caltech, University of Toronto, and Nvidia developed a Deep Reinforcement Learning procedure for robotic mechanical search, enabling continuous visuomotor policies to uncover specific target objects from clutter. The approach, which integrates teacher guidance, asymmetric learning, and mid-level visual representations, achieved increased target visibility and improved graspability scores, converging within approximately 8,000 environmental interactions.

## Problem

- Retrieving specific known objects from unknown, unstructured clutter is challenging for robots due to complex physical interactions and dynamic environments.
- Traditional approaches often rely on simplified action spaces or heuristics, leading to slow execution, unintended clutter movement, or a focus on general object singulation rather than target uncovering.
- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for visuomotor control in this context suffers from sample inefficiency, brittleness, and difficulty processing high-dimensional visual inputs.

## Method

- The authors formulate the task as a continuous-action ==Reinforcement Learning== problem, with rewards designed to promote target uncovering and penalize non-target movement.
- A novel Deep RL procedure combines teacher-aided exploration, providing guidance during training, with asymmetric learning where the critic uses privileged state information.
- The actor policy utilizes mid-level visual representations (3D offsets to target pixels) alongside depth images to facilitate learning from high-dimensional sensor data.

## Results

- The method demonstrated consistent convergence to improved performance within 8,000 action executions, achieving higher discounted rewards and increased target visibility.
- Substantial improvements in target object graspability were observed, with grasp score changes comparable to teacher policies, alongside reduced disturbance to non-target objects.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of asymmetric learning and teacher guidance for effective learning, and highlighted the mid-level representation's advantage in complex, multi-heap environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective Deep RL for visuomotor mechanical search requires a combination of teacher-guided exploration and asymmetric learning with privileged information for the critic to ensure sample efficiency.
> - Mid-level representations of target object position, derived from visual input, are critical for the actor to learn robust policies, particularly in highly cluttered or ambiguous environments.
> - Continuous visuomotor control enables the robot to learn nuanced, gentle interactions with clutter, minimizing disturbance to non-target objects and improving overall task efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a problem within robotic manipulation in unstructured and cluttered environments, specifically an instance of the "Mechanical Search" problem. Mechanical Search, as defined by Danielczuk et al., involves searching for a known object amidst unknown clutter using robotic interactions.

Previous approaches to object retrieval in clutter have explored several avenues. Early methods often employed carefully coded heuristics or learned discrete sequences of actions. These approaches frequently simplified the action space, for instance, to linear pushes parameterized by a point and direction, followed by a retraction motion. While these methods demonstrated capabilities in object singulation or searching, they presented limitations such as longer execution times, potential for undesired clutter motion due to retraction, and a focus that was sometimes more on singulation rather than uncovering.

The broader field of interactive perception has investigated using manipulation actions to facilitate perception tasks, such as segmenting objects in a pile. Similarly, the grasping community has focused on planning and executing grasps to clear entire piles of objects, using either model-based or model-free strategies. However, these works typically aim to clear an environment or perform general grasping, rather than specifically uncovering a pre-defined target object to improve its graspability.

This paper positions itself as a development beyond these prior works, particularly in contrast to Danielczuk et al. and Yang et al.. While Danielczuk et al. defined Mechanical Search and used discrete action policies, this work utilizes Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) to optimize *continuous visuomotor policies* for more reactive and optimal pushing. The authors suggest their learned pushing policies could complement existing frameworks by providing more robust action primitives. Compared to Yang et al., which also used pushing actions and Q-learning in the image domain, this work focuses on learning *continuous control actions* without retracting the robot arm, aiming for more adaptive and responsive manipulation.

The use of Deep RL for visuomotor control in complex environments is a central theme, building on successes in image-based sequential decision-making. However, the paper acknowledges that Deep RL can be sample-inefficient and brittle when applied directly to complex tasks like uncovering objects from images. To address these limitations, the authors draw inspiration from several algorithmic strategies:
- **Asymmetric learning and privileged information:** Utilizing simulation-provided state information during training (e.g.,), where a critic can access low-dimensional ground truth data while the actor learns from high-dimensional observations.
- **Teacher guidance:** Employing expert or suboptimal policies to guide exploration during training, which has been shown effective for manipulation tasks.
- **Mid-level representations:** Supplying agents with processed inputs rather than raw pixels to improve learning efficiency and facilitate sim-to-real transfer.

By combining these strategies, the work aims to offer a novel and more efficient Deep RL procedure specifically tailored for the challenges of continuous visuomotor control in mechanical search, particularly for object uncovering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a robot to effectively uncover a known target object from a pile of unknown, occluding objects to improve its graspability. This task is motivated by its frequent occurrence in practical robotic applications, such as home robotics or logistics centers, where objects often need to be retrieved from cluttered environments.

The motivation stems from several specific challenges:

1. **Complexity of Physical Interaction:** Interacting with unknown, deformable, or unstable clutter involves complex physics. Accurate forward models for planning precise predefined interactions with predictable outcomes are often unavailable or extremely difficult to construct, rendering traditional planning methods inefficient or unfeasible.
2. **Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Previous methods, including carefully coded heuristics or learned discrete actions, have exhibited limitations. These include longer execution times, undesirable displacement of clutter due to discrete push-and-retract cycles, and a primary focus on singulation rather than specifically uncovering a target object to make it graspable. The need for a more natural, closed-loop, and continuous pushing strategy that can adapt to the dynamic reactions of clutter based on visual feedback is identified.
3. **Challenges of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL):** While DRL has shown promise in learning visuomotor policies for tasks with complex or unknown dynamics, its direct application to the problem of uncovering objects from images is hampered by significant drawbacks:
 - **Sample Inefficiency:** DRL algorithms typically require a large number of environmental interactions to learn effective policies, which is costly and time-consuming in real-world robotics.
 - **Brittleness and Exploration:** The algorithms can be sensitive to hyperparameters and often struggle with exploration in large, high-dimensional state spaces characteristic of continuous control problems with image inputs.
 - **High-Dimensional Inputs:** Learning directly from raw RGB-D pixel data adds complexity, requiring the agent to learn object detection and state estimation simultaneously with control.

To address these motivations and challenges, the paper sets out the following key objectives:

- **Develop a novel Deep RL procedure:** This procedure is intended to overcome the sample inefficiency and brittleness of standard DRL by integrating three specific algorithmic strategies:
 1. **Teacher-aided exploration:** To guide the agent towards relevant areas of the state space.
 2. **A critic with privileged information:** To leverage simulator-provided ground truth data during training, improving learning efficiency without requiring the actor to access such information during deployment.
 3. **Mid-level representations:** To provide the agent with more informative inputs than raw pixels, thereby facilitating learning from high-dimensional visual data and potentially aiding sim-to-real transfer.
- **Apply and instantiate this learning procedure:** The goal is to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in solving the specific problem of uncovering a partially visible target object from clutter.
- **Improve target object graspability:** The ultimate measure of success for the uncovering task is to significantly enhance the ability of a downstream gripper to grasp the target object, thus facilitating full retrieval.
- **Achieve sample efficiency and effective learning:** The new procedure aims to train faster and converge to more efficient uncovering solutions compared to baseline methods and ablations.

In essence, the research aims to provide a robust, efficient, and intelligent robotic solution for a common, challenging manipulation problem by enhancing Deep RL with specific training methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors frame the problem of uncovering a known object through continuous pushing actions, based on visual inputs, as a reinforcement learning problem within a Markov Decision Process (MDP).

**A. Problem Statement and Reward Function:**
The MDP is defined by a continuous state space (S), a continuous action space (A), and observations (Y) comprising RGB-D images and the robot’s end-effector position. Actions are defined as small end-effector position changes (offsets).
The reward function is a sum of five components, designed to encourage uncovering while penalizing undesirable actions:
1. **Target Uncovering Reward:** Positive reward (2.5 \* c) for increased target object visibility, where `c` is the fractional change in visibility.
2. **Heap Movement Penalty:** A penalty (75.0/N \* m\_o) for unnecessarily moving non-target objects, where `m_o` is the distance moved by an object. This discourages general pile spreading.
3. **Target Movement Penalty:** A penalty (75.0 \* m\_t) if the target object itself is moved, where `m_t` is the distance the target moved.
4. **Workspace Limits Penalty:** A fixed negative reward (-0.5) to keep the agent within the operational workspace.
5. **Idleness Penalty:** A fixed negative reward (-0.5) to encourage continuous action and goal-seeking.
An episode concludes when the target object becomes fully visible or a maximum action limit is reached.

**B. Closed-Loop Visuomotor Control Policy (Actor Architecture):**
To address the challenge of learning from high-dimensional visual inputs and adapting to different target objects, the policy employs a mid-level representation for the target object.
- **Mid-Level Representation:** Instead of raw RGB images, the agent receives a 3-channel image representing the 3D offsets (X, Y, Z) from the end-effector to the visible pixels of the target object in meters. This is created by:
 1. Masking the depth image to include only pixels belonging to the target object (using ground truth segmentation from the simulator).
 2. Using known camera intrinsics and extrinsics to back-project these masked pixels into 3D positions relative to the robot's end-effector.
 This representation is designed to explicitly provide the agent with target object positional information, as CNNs can struggle with regressing coordinates.
- **Actor Network Structure:** The actor (deterministic policy π_θ(s)) takes three inputs:
 1. The mid-level representation image.
 2. A depth image of the tabletop and object heap.
 3. The 3D position of the end-effector.
 Each image input is processed by a separate 3-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). The end-effector position passes through a fully connected (FC) layer. The outputs from these parallel processing streams are flattened, concatenated, and then fed into two FC layers (size 256). A final FC layer with a tanh activation produces the continuous action (end-effector position offset).

**C. Teacher-Guided Actor-Critic Policy Learning:**
The training procedure combines three strategies: teacher guidance, asymmetric learning, and the aforementioned mid-level representation.
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** The core algorithm is a probabilistic variant of Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG).
- **Teacher Guidance:** To simplify exploration in continuous control with many actions, a set of "black box" suboptimal teacher policies are utilized during training. These teachers suggest actions, guiding the agent's exploration. The teachers implemented are:
 - **Straight Line Push:** A random straight push above the target.
 - **Zig Zag Push:** A zig-zag pattern push along a straight line.
 - **Spiral Push:** A spiral motion outward from near the target.
 These teachers require knowledge of the target object's location, which is available in the PyBullet simulator environment (using Gibson v2 renderer).
- **Asymmetric Learning with Privileged Information:** The critic network, unlike the actor, has access to "privileged information" during training. This information consists of low-dimensional ground truth data: the precise 3D positions of the target object and all other non-target objects, all relative to the end-effector. This strategy, similar to, allows the critic to learn a more accurate value function efficiently, while the actor learns to achieve similar performance using only visual and end-effector inputs.
 - **Critic Network Structure:** The privileged information (target position, concatenated non-target object positions, and end-effector position) are each processed by separate FC layers. These outputs are concatenated, passed through two FC layers, and finally an output FC layer produces the Q-value estimate. No CNNs are used for the critic.
- **Training Process:**
 1. On each training step, the actor outputs its action, and all teachers also output their respective actions.
 2. The critic (using privileged information) evaluates all these potential actions.
 3. The action with the highest Q-value (either from the actor or a teacher) is selected for execution in the environment.
 4. The resulting transition (s, a, s', r) is stored in a replay buffer.
 5. Periodically, batches of transitions from the replay buffer are used to train the actor and critic networks. The critic is trained using the Bellman residual loss, and the actor is trained with a deterministic policy gradient update to maximize the critic's output.

**D. Simulation Environment and Evaluation:**
- **Heap Generation:** Experiments are conducted in two conditions: single-heap (5, 10, or 15 objects) and dual-heap (5-10 objects per heap). Heaps are generated programmatically using objects from the YCB object set by simulating dropping. A target object is selected if its occlusion is between 10% and 90%.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance is evaluated based on: discounted sum of rewards, percentage increase in target object visibility, number of steps to complete an episode, and change in grasp quality score (ratio of mean grasp quality of the best 10 grasps, before and after interaction, using a fully convolutional grasp quality network). Experiments use 5 random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation in simulation aimed to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed Deep RL approach for uncovering target objects and improving their graspability.

**A. Overall Performance and Efficiency:**
- **Convergence and Task Success:** The proposed method demonstrated consistent convergence to improved performance within approximately 8,000 action executions in the environment. This was observed in both the single-heap and the more challenging dual-heap conditions (Figure 3).
- **Reward Maximization:** The agent learned to achieve higher discounted sums of rewards over training, indicating successful policy learning within the defined reward structure.
- **Visibility Improvement:** The policies consistently led to significant increases in the target object's visibility, directly addressing the core objective of the task.
- **Episode Length:** The agent learned to uncover the target object in fewer steps, indicating increased efficiency in achieving the goal.
- **Graspability Enhancement:** The method achieved substantial improvements in the target object's graspability, with grasp score changes (ratio) reaching levels comparable to the teacher policies, despite the actor not having access to privileged information at test time (Figure 3d).
- **Reduced Clutter Disturbance:** A notable finding was that the learned policies achieved the task with less disturbance to non-target objects in the heap compared to the teacher policies. This suggests that the agent learned a more nuanced interaction strategy, possibly influenced by the heap movement penalty in the reward function.

**B. Comparison to Baselines and Ablation Studies:**
- **Baseline Comparison:** A direct comparison with a standard DDPG algorithm (without teacher guidance or asymmetric learning, but with the same inputs as the proposed agent) revealed that the baseline failed to learn any improvements across the evaluated metrics within the same training timeframe. This indicates the importance of the proposed algorithmic enhancements.
- **Ablation Studies (Figure 4):**
 - **Asymmetry and Teacher Guidance:** The ablation results clearly showed that both asymmetric learning (privileged information for the critic) and teacher guidance were essential components for effective and efficient learning. Removing either led to substantially degraded performance, highlighting their contribution to sample efficiency and overall task success.
 - **Mid-Level Representation (Pose Input):** The mid-level representation, which explicitly encodes the approximate target object position, proved particularly beneficial in the dual-heap setting. While an RGB input performed comparably in the simpler single-heap condition, the mid-level representation was critical when there was greater uncertainty or ambiguity regarding the target's location amidst multiple heaps. This confirms its value in facilitating policy learning from visual inputs in more complex scenarios.

**C. Qualitative Results:**
- **Learned Behaviors:** Qualitatively, the learned policies exhibited complex continuous control actions. Unlike the teachers' more aggressive continuous pushes, the agent learned to approach and gently "nudge" occluding clutter. This behavior is hypothesized to be a consequence of the negative reward for moving non-target objects and the actor's lack of privileged information available to the teachers, leading to a more conservative and targeted interaction.
- **Failure Modes:** The paper also identified common failure modes:
 1. **Repetitive Actions:** In some instances, the agent would repeat the same actions without causing any significant change in the environment.
 2. **Lack of Interaction:** The agent might move near the target object but fail to interact with the occluding objects directly on top of it.
 These failure modes indicate areas for potential future work, such as refining the agent's architecture or improving teacher suggestions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the challenges of operating in cluttered environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Visuomotor Control with Deep RL:** The paper introduces a novel combination of three algorithmic strategies—teacher-aided exploration, asymmetric learning with privileged information, and mid-level representations—to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of Deep Reinforcement Learning for visuomotor continuous control tasks. This combined approach directly tackles the known limitations of DRL, such as sample inefficiency and difficulty with high-dimensional visual inputs and complex dynamics, providing a more robust framework for learning manipulation skills.
- **Effective Solution for Mechanical Search:** By applying this enhanced DRL procedure, the authors present a demonstrated solution to the "Mechanical Search" problem, specifically focusing on the critical sub-task of uncovering a target object to improve its graspability. This addresses a practical need in domains like home robotics and logistics, where robots often encounter obscured objects that require pre-grasp manipulation.
- **Improved Performance and Efficiency:** The empirical results confirm that the proposed method achieves superior sample efficiency and task performance (higher rewards, greater target visibility, and improved graspability) compared to baseline DRL approaches. The ability to converge to effective policies within 8,000 action executions is notable for complex continuous control problems.
- **Development of Nuanced Interaction Strategies:** The qualitative findings highlight that the learned policies develop subtle and efficient interaction behaviors, such as gently "nudging" occluding objects. This contrasts with the potentially more aggressive or generalized actions of hand-coded teachers. Learning such refined, task-specific behaviors that minimize unnecessary disturbance to the environment is beneficial for real-world deployment, reducing potential damage or further clutter.
- **Complementary to Existing Frameworks:** The continuous pushing policies learned in this work can be seen as a valuable enhancement to existing mechanical search or grasping frameworks. They offer a more reactive and optimized method for manipulating clutter, which could be integrated as robust action primitives within broader multi-step retrieval strategies.
- **Foundation for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While the experiments are conducted in simulation, the use of mid-level representations, which process raw visual data into more abstract and actionable information, is a common strategy aimed at facilitating the transfer of learned policies from simulation to real-world robots. Although not demonstrated in this paper, the methodological choices lay groundwork for future sim-to-real applications.
- **Identification of Future Research Directions:** By detailing specific failure modes (e.g., repetitive actions, failure to interact), the research also clearly delineates avenues for future improvements. These include refining agent architectures or developing more sophisticated teacher-guided exploration strategies, which could further enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of visuomotor mechanical search.

In conclusion, this work offers a valuable contribution by advancing Deep RL methodologies for complex robotic manipulation, specifically providing an effective and efficient solution for target object uncovering in cluttered environments with significant implications for practical robotic applications.
