# Thruster-Enhanced Locomotion: A Decoupled Model Predictive Control with Learned Contact Residuals

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northeastern University developed a decoupled control architecture for hybrid legged-aerial robots, integrating learned Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for thrusters. This approach enhances stability and robustness for thruster-assisted locomotion, particularly improving roll dynamics and reducing thruster effort in both simulation and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Low torque-control bandwidth in lightweight actuators used for hybrid legged-aerial robots hinders the direct application of unified Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- Impulsive dynamics from leg-ground impacts are typically unmodeled, introducing disturbances that current nominal thruster dynamic models cannot adequately handle.
- A need exists for robust and efficient control strategies to enable multimodal robots to maintain stability and agility in complex, dynamic environments.

## Method

- A decoupled control architecture separates legged locomotion (managed by a position-based Raibert-type controller) from thruster regulation (handled by an ==MPC==).
- Learned Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) are introduced, where a neural network, trained with a physics-informed loss function, estimates ground reaction forces and contact probabilities to account for unmodeled impact effects.
- The thruster MPC is augmented with the learned CRD to proactively compensate for anticipated disturbances, thereby enhancing the predictive accuracy and robustness of angular control.

## Results

- The CRD-augmented dynamic model achieved lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) in roll, pitch, and yaw angular dynamics compared to the nominal model in simulation, with significant improvement in roll.
- The CRD-augmented controller successfully recovered from a 15N external push force in simulation, demonstrating enhanced robustness against perturbations where the nominal controller failed to re-stabilize.
- Hardware experiments on the Husky-β robot showed the CRD-augmented controller maintained more stable roll dynamics with lower RMSE during challenging narrow-path gaits and required less thruster force output for stability during normal trotting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling control for different robot modalities effectively bypasses hardware limitations like low torque-control bandwidth, enabling more practical and robust control implementations on hybrid platforms.
> - Integrating learned contact residual dynamics into a traditional MPC framework significantly improves the system's ability to anticipate and compensate for unmodeled impulsive disturbances during dynamic interactions.
> - A physics-informed loss function, combining angular acceleration error with contact prediction error, proves effective in training neural networks to accurately capture complex, unmodeled physical dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of multimodal robotics, specifically focusing on hybrid legged-aerial platforms. This domain seeks to combine the terrain agility and adaptability of legged robots with the speed and obstacle-circumventing capabilities of aerial platforms. The integration of these modalities aims to create robots with enhanced versatility for navigating complex and dynamic environments.

Existing design strategies for multimodal locomotion generally fall into two categories: additive integration, where dedicated legged and aerial subsystems are combined, and appendage repurposing, where a robot's limbs serve dual functions, such as legs transforming into thrusters. The Husky Carbon robot, developed at Northeastern University, exemplifies the latter approach, utilizing propellers mounted at its knee joints to provide both ground locomotion and aerial thrust.

This research specifically addresses a control challenge inherent in the appendage repurposing paradigm, focusing on the use of sagittal thrusters for assisted walking to enhance frontal and roll stability. While a unified Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework could theoretically optimize both ground reaction forces and thruster forces, its practical implementation is often hindered by the low torque-control bandwidth of the lightweight actuators commonly used in such hybrid systems. These actuators are critical for translating desired ground reaction forces into precise joint torques. This paper thus contributes to the ongoing effort to develop effective and robust control strategies for hybrid robots, particularly by addressing the practical limitations imposed by hardware characteristics. It aims to bridge the gap between theoretical unified control and its feasible application on platforms with specific actuator constraints, while leveraging learning-based methods to account for unmodeled dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the practical limitations encountered when attempting to implement a unified Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for hybrid legged-aerial robots like the Husky Carbon. Although a unified MPC could theoretically optimize both ground reaction forces and thruster forces to achieve desired robot states, the lightweight actuators chosen for such platforms often exhibit low torque-control bandwidth. This characteristic presents a significant challenge because accurate and responsive torque control is essential for translating MPC-derived ground reaction force solutions into appropriate joint torques, particularly during dynamic interactions with the ground. The authors identify this low control rate as a critical bottleneck preventing the direct application of a unified MPC approach for thruster-assisted walking.

To overcome this constraint, the work proposes a decoupled control architecture. The key objectives of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **To develop a decoupled control strategy:** The core objective is to bypass the torque-control rate limitation by separating the control of legged locomotion from thruster regulation. This involves using a position-based controller for the legs and an MPC for the thrusters.
2. **To incorporate learned dynamics for robustness:** A specific objective is to account for the impulsive dynamics arising from leg-ground impacts, which are typically unmodeled in simplified thruster dynamics. This is achieved by learning these "Contact Residual Dynamics" (CRD) and integrating them into the thruster MPC.
3. **To design a physics-informed learning mechanism:** The authors aim to introduce a novel loss function that leverages physical principles to enable a neural network to effectively capture residual dynamics from offline-collected leg-ground impact data. This loss function combines an angular acceleration error with a contact prediction error.
4. **To create an online execution pipeline:** A further objective is to design a control pipeline that can seamlessly integrate the trained CRD model with the nominal MPC framework for real-time operation on the robot.
5. **To validate the proposed approach comprehensively:** The research aims to validate the effectiveness and stability of the decoupled control architecture, particularly with CRD augmentation, through rigorous experiments in both high-fidelity simulation and on the physical Husky Carbon hardware. This validation focuses on demonstrating improved stability during tasks such as push recovery and various walking gaits, including challenging narrow-path movements.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for a practical and robust control solution for thruster-enhanced legged robots, addressing inherent hardware limitations while leveraging machine learning to enhance dynamic modeling and control performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on a decoupled control architecture designed to circumvent the torque-control bandwidth limitations of lightweight actuators in the Husky Carbon robot. This architecture separates the control of legged locomotion from the regulation of thruster forces, integrating learned contact dynamics to enhance overall stability.

**A. Decoupled Control Architecture**
The proposed system consists of two primary controllers operating in parallel:
1. **Legged Controller:** A Raibert-type controller manages the swing and stance phases of the robot's legs. This controller operates primarily with position-based commands.
2. **Thruster Controller:** A Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework regulates the thrusters. Crucially, this MPC is augmented with learned Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) to account for the impact forces between the legs and the ground.

**B. Legged Control: Raibert Strategy**
The legged controller implements a Raibert-type strategy, a common approach for dynamic legged locomotion.
- **Swing Phase:** The desired foot placement for the swing leg (`p_d,i`) is calculated based on a reference position (`p_ref,i`), the robot's current body velocity (`v`), the gait period (`T`), and a desired velocity (`v_d`), incorporating a feedback term `k(v - v_d)`.
- **Stance Phase:** During the stance phase, the foot position (`p_i`) is used to compute a vertical adjustment (`∆z_i`). This adjustment is determined by the dot product of the foot position with a gain matrix (`K_ori`) and the robot's pitch and roll errors (`e`), aiming to stabilize the body's orientation.
- **Inverse Kinematics:** Joint positions are derived from desired end-effector positions using a Jacobian-based inverse kinematics method implemented with the Pinocchio library.

**C. Thruster Control: Model Predictive Control (MPC)**
The thruster controller utilizes MPC to regulate the robot's angular dynamics.
- **State and Control Input:** The state vector (`x`) comprises Euler angles (roll, pitch, yaw, `θ`) and angular velocities (`ω`). The control input (`u`) consists of the individual propeller speeds (`v_i`), which are mapped to thruster forces (`f_i = c_f v_i^2`).
- **Nominal Angular Dynamics:** The MPC initially considers a nominal model of the robot's angular dynamics, which only accounts for the moments generated by the thruster forces. This is expressed as `I ω_nominal_dot = Σ (r_i × c_f v_i^2 ê_i)`, where `r_i` is the vector from the center of mass to the i-th propeller, and `ê_i` is the thrust direction unit vector.

**D. Residual Dynamics Learning (CRD)**
To address unmodeled impulsive effects from leg-ground impacts, the concept of Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) is introduced.
- **Residual Term:** The true angular acceleration (`ω_true_dot`) is modeled as the sum of the nominal angular acceleration and a residual term (`ω_residual_dot`). This residual term is approximated as `I^-1 Σ (d_i × F_i)`, where `d_i` is the vector from the center of mass to the i-th leg, and `F_i` is the ground reaction force.
- **CRD Network:** A neural network, replicated for each of the four legs, is employed to estimate the ground reaction forces (`F_i,NN`) and a scalar contact probability (`C_i,NN`). The estimated CRD is then calculated by `ω_residual_dot = I^-1 Σ (C_i,NN (d_i × F_i,NN))`.
- **Physics-informed Loss Function:** The network is trained with a composite loss function:
 - **Force Loss (`L_GRF`):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted residual angular acceleration (derived from `F_i,NN`) and the ground truth residual angular acceleration (calculated from `ω_true_dot - ω_nominal_dot`). Only active for legs predicted to be in contact.
 - **Contact Loss (`L_contact`):** Binary cross-entropy between the predicted contact probability (`C_i,NN`) and the ground truth contact flag (`C_i,GT`).
 - **Overall Loss (`L`):** A weighted sum of `L_GRF` and `L_contact`, with a weighting parameter `α` to balance the importance of force estimation and contact prediction.
- **Network Architecture:** Each subnetwork takes a 21-dimensional feature vector as input (including joint angles/velocities, foot/propeller positions, base orientation/angular rates/linear velocity, and thruster actions). It features an input layer, four hidden layers with ReLU activations (64, 128, 512, 64 units), and two outputs: a 3-unit force vector and a 1-unit contact flag (sigmoid activated). The force and contact outputs share a common final embedding layer, and the effective force is computed as `F_i,NN · C_i,NN`.

**E. CRD Augmented MPC Formulation**
The learned CRD is integrated into the MPC formulation.
- **Continuous-Time Model:** The combined dynamic model for MPC incorporates both nominal thrust dynamics and the learned residual dynamics: `ω_dot = I^-1 Σ [r_i × (c_f v_i^2 ê_i) + C_i,NN (d_i × F_i,NN)]`.
- **Discretization:** The nominal dynamics are linearized and discretized to create a discrete-time state-space model (`x_k+1 = A_d x_k + B_d u_k`) over an MPC horizon.
- **CRD Integration:** For each step in the MPC horizon, the reference state (`x_r,k`) is augmented with the predicted `ω_residual_dot` obtained from the CRD network. This allows the MPC to anticipate and compensate for the impact effects.
- **MPC Problem:** The augmented MPC problem is formulated as a quadratic program (QP) that minimizes a cost function comprising tracking error (weighted by `Q`) and control effort (weighted by `R`), subject to thruster force constraints.

**F. Experiment Setup**
- **Simulation:** A PyBullet simulator with a high-fidelity model was used for both offline data collection and online execution. Randomized noise perturbations (positional offsets, external forces with varied start times, durations, and magnitudes) were introduced during data collection to enhance model robustness.
- **Hardware:** Experiments were conducted on the Husky-β robot in a tethered configuration for safety. Two gait patterns were evaluated: a standard leg width gait and a narrow, cat-like gait, chosen to emphasize roll dynamics stabilization. Due to the absence of contact sensors on the hardware, a transfer learning approach was used for training: a pre-trained simulation-based CRD model was adapted, with the weights of the contact probability output layer frozen.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated the proposed decoupled control architecture with Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) augmentation through both simulation and hardware experiments.

**A. Simulation Results**
Two primary simulation experiments were conducted to evaluate the CRD network and the augmented controller's performance:

1. **CRD Network Performance Evaluation:**
 - The performance of the proposed CRD network was evaluated using a trajectory distinct from the training data. The angular dynamics predicted by both the nominal MPC and the CRD-augmented MPC were compared against ground truth data from the PyBullet simulator.
 - The **CRD-augmented dynamic model achieved a lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE)** across roll, pitch, and yaw angular dynamics compared to the nominal dynamic model.
 - A **significant improvement was observed in the roll dynamics**, which the authors attribute to the sagittal-facing thruster's primary role in roll stabilization. This indicates that the learned residuals effectively capture and compensate for unmodeled impact effects influencing the robot's rotational motion.

2. **Push Recovery Experiment:**
 - Both the nominal-only MPC and the CRD-augmented MPC were subjected to an external 15N push force along the robot's y-axis for 0.5 seconds, applied after two seconds of trotting gait.
 - Both controllers initially exhibited roll instability due to the perturbation. However, the **CRD-augmented controller demonstrated recovery, returning to a normal trotting gait at approximately 3.5 seconds**.
 - In contrast, the **nominal controller failed to re-stabilize**, indicating its inability to effectively counter the disturbance without accounting for contact residuals. This highlights the practical benefit of CRD in enhancing robustness against external perturbations.

**B. Hardware Results**
Hardware experiments on the Husky-β robot were conducted using two distinct gait types to validate the effectiveness of the CRD-augmented dynamics in a physical setting:

1. **Narrow-Path Cat Gait:**
 - A narrow-path cat gait, executed at 1.5 Hz, was specifically chosen to present a challenging condition for roll dynamics stabilization.
 - Time-series data showed that with the CRD-augmented dynamics, when the robot began rolling leftward at approximately 0.5 seconds, the left propeller activated to counteract this instability, demonstrating active stabilization.
 - A direct comparison between the CRD-augmented and nominal dynamic-based cat walking gaits revealed that the **CRD-augmented dynamic achieved a lower RMSE across all three rotational axes (roll, pitch, and yaw)**.
 - Consistent with simulation results, the **most significant improvement was observed in the roll dynamics**, further supporting the efficacy of the side-mounted thruster in combination with learned residuals for roll stabilization.

2. **Normal-Width Gait Trotting:**
 - Experiments with a standard normal-width gait trotting were also performed.
 - The **CRD-augmented dynamic model exhibited more stable roll dynamics** compared to the nominal dynamic model.
 - Furthermore, the **CRD-augmented model required less thruster force output** to achieve this enhanced stability. This result suggests that by more closely approximating the robot's true dynamics, the CRD-augmented controller can maintain stability more efficiently, potentially leading to reduced energy consumption for thruster-assisted locomotion.

Collectively, the results from both simulation and hardware indicate that integrating learned contact residual dynamics into a decoupled MPC framework significantly enhances the stability and robustness of thruster-assisted legged locomotion, particularly in dynamic and challenging scenarios, while also potentially improving control efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a control methodology that addresses a specific technical challenge in the development of hybrid legged-aerial robots, offering implications for their design and operational capabilities.

The primary significance lies in its **practical solution to the low torque-control bandwidth problem** inherent in lightweight actuators often used in multi-modal robots. By proposing a decoupled control architecture that separates legged locomotion (position-based Raibert control) from thruster control (MPC), the work bypasses this bottleneck. This approach enables the effective integration of thruster assistance without requiring high-bandwidth torque control from the leg actuators for dynamic tasks.

The introduction of **learned Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) is a key contribution**. By training a neural network with a physics-informed loss function to predict unmodeled impulsive effects from leg-ground impacts, the control system can proactively account for these disturbances. This enhances the predictive accuracy of the thruster MPC, leading to more precise and robust control, particularly for angular stability. The physics-informed loss function itself represents an advancement in integrating domain knowledge into learning for robotics, potentially making the learning process more efficient and the resulting models more robust.

The validation across both **high-fidelity simulation and physical hardware experiments** underscores the practical applicability and reliability of the proposed method. Demonstrations of improved stability in critical scenarios such as push recovery and during challenging narrow-path (cat-type) walking gaits indicate that this approach can enable robots to operate more robustly in dynamic and complex terrains. The finding that the CRD-augmented system achieved better stability with **reduced thruster force output** in some cases suggests a potential for increased energy efficiency, which is a crucial factor for extending the operational endurance of battery-powered robots.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** Improved stability and robustness in hybrid locomotion can enable robots to navigate more challenging and diverse environments autonomously, moving seamlessly between different modes of locomotion.
- **Wider Adoption of Hybrid Platforms:** By offering a viable control strategy that mitigates hardware limitations, this research may contribute to the broader adoption and development of more agile and versatile hybrid legged-aerial robots for applications in inspection, search and rescue, and exploration.
- **Advancements in Learning-Based Control:** The methodology contributes to the growing body of knowledge on how to effectively integrate learning models for complex, unmodeled dynamics into traditional control frameworks, paving the way for more adaptive and intelligent robotic systems.
- **Development of More Capable Actuators:** While addressing current limitations, this work also highlights the continuous need for high-performance, lightweight actuators that could further simplify control architectures in the future.

In conclusion, this research provides a significant step towards practical and robust control of thruster-enhanced legged robots, addressing key control challenges and demonstrating improved stability and efficiency through a hybrid control and learning approach.
