# Thruster-Enhanced Locomotion: A Decoupled Model Predictive Control with Learned Contact Residuals

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northeastern University developed a decoupled control architecture for hybrid legged-aerial robots, integrating learned Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for thrusters. This approach enhances stability and robustness for thruster-assisted locomotion, particularly improving roll dynamics and reducing thruster effort in both simulation and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Low torque-control bandwidth in lightweight actuators used for hybrid legged-aerial robots hinders the direct application of unified Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- Impulsive dynamics from leg-ground impacts are typically unmodeled, introducing disturbances that current nominal thruster dynamic models cannot adequately handle.
- A need exists for robust and efficient control strategies to enable multimodal robots to maintain stability and agility in complex, dynamic environments.

## Method

- A decoupled control architecture separates legged locomotion (managed by a position-based Raibert-type controller) from thruster regulation (handled by an ==MPC==).
- Learned Contact Residual Dynamics (CRD) are introduced, where a neural network, trained with a physics-informed loss function, estimates ground reaction forces and contact probabilities to account for unmodeled impact effects.
- The thruster MPC is augmented with the learned CRD to proactively compensate for anticipated disturbances, thereby enhancing the predictive accuracy and robustness of angular control.

## Results

- The CRD-augmented dynamic model achieved lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) in roll, pitch, and yaw angular dynamics compared to the nominal model in simulation, with significant improvement in roll.
- The CRD-augmented controller successfully recovered from a 15N external push force in simulation, demonstrating enhanced robustness against perturbations where the nominal controller failed to re-stabilize.
- Hardware experiments on the Husky-β robot showed the CRD-augmented controller maintained more stable roll dynamics with lower RMSE during challenging narrow-path gaits and required less thruster force output for stability during normal trotting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling control for different robot modalities effectively bypasses hardware limitations like low torque-control bandwidth, enabling more practical and robust control implementations on hybrid platforms.
> - Integrating learned contact residual dynamics into a traditional MPC framework significantly improves the system's ability to anticipate and compensate for unmodeled impulsive disturbances during dynamic interactions.
> - A physics-informed loss function, combining angular acceleration error with contact prediction error, proves effective in training neural networks to accurately capture complex, unmodeled physical dynamics.
