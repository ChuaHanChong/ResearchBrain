# MOTIF: Learning Action Motifs for Few-shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named MOTIF was developed to enable efficient few-shot cross-embodiment transfer in robotic manipulation by explicitly decoupling embodiment-agnostic spatiotemporal action motifs from robot-specific execution details. This approach achieved a 6.5% improvement in simulation few-shot transfer and a 43.7% improvement in real-world 5-shot transfer over baselines.

## Problem

- Transferring learned robotic policies to new platforms is difficult due to significant kinematic heterogeneity across different robot embodiments.
- Adapting policies to new robots requires extensive real-world demonstrations, which are costly and time-consuming to collect, especially in few-shot scenarios.
- Existing methods struggle with rapid, data-efficient adaptation to novel kinematics, often relying on massive co-training or implicit alignment with limited capacity.

## Method

- A three-stage hierarchical framework learns unified, embodiment-agnostic "action motifs" from canonicalized end-effector pose trajectories using a VQ-VAE with progress-aware and adversarial losses.
- A lightweight multimodal predictor infers appropriate action motifs from real-time visual observations and language instructions using frozen foundation models (DINOv2, T5).
- A motif-conditioned flow-matching Diffusion Transformer policy generates precise, executable actions tailored to the target robot's kinematics, guided by the learned motifs.

## Results

- In simulation, MOTIF achieved a 36.00% transfer success rate in 1-shot scenarios, outperforming the best baseline (π0) by 2.67%, and showed a 6.5% overall improvement in few-shot transfer.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of kinematic trajectory canonicalization (10.33% drop), progress-aware alignment (4.66% drop), and embodiment adversarial loss (2.66% drop) for transfer performance.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated a 67.50% transfer success rate in 5-shot tasks, yielding a substantial 43.7% improvement over the best baseline (Diffusion Policy) on physical ARX5 and Piper arms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling spatiotemporal "action motifs" from robot-specific kinematics provides a robust structural prior for few-shot transfer.
> - Canonicalizing end-effector trajectories and enforcing embodiment invariance through adversarial learning and progress-aware alignment are crucial for learning generalizable motifs.
> - Conditioning a robot-specific generative policy on these abstract motifs allows for efficient adaptation to new hardware with minimal demonstration data.
