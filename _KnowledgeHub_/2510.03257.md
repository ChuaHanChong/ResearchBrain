# Triple-BERT: Do We Really Need MARL for Order Dispatch on Ride-Sharing Platforms?

> [!summary] Summary
> Triple-BERT, developed by researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, introduces a centralized Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning framework for large-scale ride-sharing order dispatching that addresses the scalability and cooperation limitations of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning. It achieves an average 11.95% higher reward and a 22.25% reduction in pickup times compared to prior state-of-the-art methods on real-world datasets.

## Problem

- Managing real-time order dispatch on large-scale ride-sharing platforms, which involves dynamic demand, supply, system uncertainties, and extensive observation/action spaces.
- Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods face limitations: independent MARL struggles with global information and cooperation, while centralized training decentralized execution (CTDE) MARL suffers from the Curse of Dimensionality with many agents.
- The combinatorial nature of assigning orders to drivers results in extremely large action spaces and extensive observation spaces, complicating efficient exploration and stable training for standard RL methods.

## Method

- A centralized ==Single-Agent== Reinforcement Learning (SARL) framework, named **Triple-BERT**, is proposed, built upon a TD3 variant, designed to inherently leverage global information for coordinated dispatching decisions.
- A novel network architecture incorporates BERT-based self-attention mechanisms for processing vast observation spaces and a QK-Attention module with positive normalization to efficiently manage the combinatorial action space.
- A two-stage training process is implemented: initial decentralized IDDQN pre-training to acquire general feature extraction capabilities and address sample scarcity, followed by centralized TD3 fine-tuning for global policy optimization.

## Results

- Triple-BERT demonstrated superior performance on real-world ride-hailing datasets, achieving an average **11.95%** higher reward, **4.26%** increase in served orders, and **22.25%** reduction in pickup times compared to state-of-the-art MARL baselines.
- The framework showed strong generalization across different geographical datasets and time periods, maintaining leading performance even in varying demand conditions, and exhibited robust scalability for up to 2,000 drivers without retraining.
- Computational efficiency was confirmed with decision times consistently under **0.2** seconds for large scenarios, making it suitable for real-time deployment, and ablation studies validated the necessity of the two-stage training and positive normalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Centralized Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning can effectively address large-scale, multi-entity problems like ride-sharing dispatch by leveraging global information and mitigating the Curse of Dimensionality, often outperforming MARL.
> - Combining BERT-based architectures with an approximate action decomposition strategy (QK-Attention) and an Integer Linear Programming (ILP) solver allows for efficient processing of extensive observation spaces and handling of combinatorial action spaces.
> - A staged training approach, moving from decentralized pre-training to centralized fine-tuning, is crucial for stable learning and sample efficiency in centralized SARL applied to complex, multi-agent-like environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

On-demand ride-sharing platforms, such as Uber and Lyft, manage the complex task of dynamically matching passengers to available vehicles while contending with system uncertainties and large, dynamic observation and action spaces. The established paradigm for addressing the order dispatching problem in these large-scale environments has predominantly been Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL).

Within MARL, two main categories are commonly employed:
- **Independent MARL (DTDE - Decentralized Training Decentralized Execution):** Methods like Independent Double DQN (IDDQN) and Independent SAC (ISAC) treat each driver as an independent agent. While computationally efficient, these approaches are noted to inadequately capture global information, leading to suboptimal cooperation among agents. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have been integrated into some of these methods to incorporate neighborhood information, partially addressing this limitation.
- **Centralized Training Decentralized Execution (CTDE):** Approaches such as QMIX and Coordinated Policy Optimization (CoPO) aim to improve cooperation by training agents in a centralized manner while allowing for decentralized execution. However, these methods often encounter the Curse of Dimensionality (CoD) in scenarios with a large number of agents (e.g., thousands of drivers), which can result in slow convergence and degraded performance.

Model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) has gained traction in this domain due to its capacity to learn optimal dispatching policies without requiring explicit system modeling. However, the scale of real-world ride-sharing operations presents significant challenges for RL, particularly concerning extensive observation and action spaces, which complicate efficient exploration and stable training.

This paper positions itself by questioning the necessity of MARL for large-scale order dispatching. It proposes an alternative paradigm: a centralized Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning (SARL) framework. The work argues that a centralized SARL approach can inherently leverage global information more effectively than MARL, thereby overcoming the limitations of poor cooperation in independent MARL and the scalability issues of CTDE MARL, while directly addressing the combinatorial complexity of order dispatching. This research thus represents a shift in methodology within the field of ride-sharing optimization by advocating for a centralized SARL solution where MARL has typically been favored.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and scalable centralized framework for real-time order dispatching on large-scale ride-sharing platforms. This objective is motivated by several challenges inherent in the current state of practice and existing research:

1. **Complexity of Ride-Sharing Dispatch:** On-demand ride-sharing platforms face the real-time challenge of matching passengers (with distinct origins and destinations) to available vehicles, necessitating efficient resource allocation. This process must account for significant system uncertainties, including fluctuating demand, varying traffic conditions, and dynamic driver availability. The goal is to minimize detours, reduce waiting times, and optimize platform objectives such as revenue and customer satisfaction.

2. **Limitations of Existing MARL Approaches:**
 - **Poor Cooperation in Independent MARL:** Current independent MARL methods, despite their computational efficiency, are limited in their ability to capture global information across all drivers and orders. This often results in agents making decisions based solely on local perspectives, leading to suboptimal overall system performance and inadequate cooperation among drivers.
 - **Curse of Dimensionality (CoD) in CTDE MARL:** While CTDE methods aim to enhance cooperation through centralized training, they face severe scalability issues. When applied to large-scale scenarios involving hundreds or thousands of drivers and orders, the size of the joint observation and action spaces can become prohibitively large, leading to the CoD. This makes efficient learning and convergence challenging, ultimately hindering performance in real-world large-scale deployments.

3. **Vast Action and Observation Spaces:** The order dispatching problem intrinsically involves extremely large and dynamically changing action and observation spaces. For instance, with 1,000 drivers and 10 orders, the action space can encompass approximately 10^30 possible assignments. The observation space, comprising the states of all drivers and all available orders, is also extensive. These dimensions make sufficient exploration and stable network convergence difficult for standard Reinforcement Learning methods.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives for the proposed Triple-BERT framework are:
- **Develop a Centralized SARL Framework:** Introduce a centralized Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning framework to inherently leverage global information for improved coordination and cooperation among drivers, thereby overcoming the limitations of MARL in this context.
- **Manage the Extensive Action Space:** Propose a method to handle the combinatorial explosion of possible assignments (actions), which is critical for real-time decision-making in large-scale scenarios.
- **Handle the Extensive Observation Space:** Design a neural network architecture capable of efficiently processing the large volume of driver and order state information without experiencing parameter growth issues.
- **Address Sample Scarcity in Centralized SARL:** Implement a training strategy to mitigate the issue of sample scarcity, which can arise when consolidating multiple agents' experiences into a single training stream for a centralized SARL model.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** Validate the proposed method against state-of-the-art MARL approaches using real-world data, aiming for significant improvements in key operational metrics such as served orders, pickup times, and overall platform reward.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Triple-BERT, is a centralized Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning (SARL) framework designed for large-scale order dispatching, built upon a variant of the TD3 algorithm. Its methodology involves a novel network architecture and a two-stage training process.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture

The Triple-BERT network consists of three primary components: feature extractors, an actor sub-network, and a critic sub-network.

- **Feature Extractors:**
 - **Worker Encoder:** Processes the state of each worker ($w_{i,t}$). It uses a bi-directional LSTM for sequences of onboard orders and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for other non-sequence information. These are combined into a primary feature vector ($\tilde{w}_{i,t}$).
 - **Order Encoder:** Processes the state of each order to be assigned ($o_{j,t}$) using an MLP, yielding a feature vector ($\tilde{o}_{j,t}$).
 - The dimensions of $\tilde{w}_{i,t}$ and $\tilde{o}_{j,t}$ are consistent. All primary features are concatenated into a single sequence ($\tilde{S}_t = [\tilde{w}_{1,t}, \dots, \tilde{w}_{n,t}, \tilde{o}_{1,t}, \dots, \tilde{o}_{m_t,t}]$).
 - A normalization layer and an Adaptive Re-weighting Layer (ARL) are incorporated to address varying input magnitudes and learn input importance, respectively, facilitating network convergence and enhancing feature extraction.

- **Actor Sub-network:**
 - **Actor-BERT:** Takes the concatenated feature sequence ($\tilde{S}_t$) as input. It utilizes a BERT-based architecture with self-attention mechanisms to capture complex relationships among workers and orders. Positional embeddings are omitted due to the permutation invariance of the input, ensuring that the order of workers and orders in the input does not influence the encoding. This design allows for the aggregation of global worker information.
 - **QK-Attention Module with Positive Normalization:** This module addresses the vast and dynamic action space. Instead of evaluating each worker-order pair separately (which has high computational complexity), it approximates the utility of each pair by decomposing it into two smaller networks, $f(w_{i,t})$ and $g(o_{j,t})$, and calculating their dot product. This reduces computational complexity from a multiplicative relationship to an additive one. To improve training stability and mitigate parameter redundancy, a positive normalization method is introduced, where $g(o_{j,t})$ is passed through a Softplus activation and then L2-normalized. The output is a matrix $M_t$ representing the utility of each worker selecting each order.

- **Critic Sub-networks:**
 - The critic's role is to evaluate the quality of actions. A new sequence ($\dot{S}_t$) is constructed from the selected worker-order assignments.
 - **Critic-BERT:** This network processes $\dot{S}_t$ to further extract features.
 - **Critic-MLP:** A self-attention mechanism and a linear layer are then used to estimate the Q-value from the Critic-BERT output.
 - As TD3 requires two critics for stable learning, two distinct Critic-BERT and Critic-MLP networks are employed, sharing initial features from Actor-BERT but processing them independently.

#### 3.2. Training Process

The training of Triple-BERT is a two-stage process:

- **Stage 1: Decentralized IDDQN Training:**
 - **Objective:** To pre-train the feature extractors (Worker Encoder, Order Encoder, and QK-Attention) to acquire general feature extraction capabilities using a large volume of samples.
 - **Approach:** The dispatching problem is framed as a multi-agent scenario. Each worker is treated as an independent agent. All agents share the same policy and contribute to a common experience replay buffer, increasing the available sample count.
 - **Q-value Calculation:** The QK-Attention-Norm module computes a Q-value ($y_{i,j,t}$) for each worker-order pair.
 - **Action Selection:** To maximize the global Q-value, a bipartite graph is constructed with workers and orders as nodes, and edges weighted by $y_{i,j,t}$. Integer Linear Programming (ILP), specifically the Hungarian algorithm, is used to solve the maximizing bipartite matching problem (i.e., assign orders to available workers).
 - **Training:** Standard IDDQN algorithm is used, involving $\epsilon$-greedy exploration and minimizing the Temporal Difference (TD) error to update network parameters. Target networks are updated softly for stability.
 - **Rationale:** This stage leverages the simplicity and sample efficiency of independent MARL as a warm-up phase, despite its known limitations in capturing global cooperation.

- **Stage 2: Centralized TD3 Training:**
 - **Objective:** To fine-tune the entire Triple-BERT network as a centralized SARL model, enabling it to leverage global information for better worker cooperation.
 - **Actor Optimization (Policy Gradient):**
 - **Action Decomposition:** The probability ($P_{i,j,t}$) of worker $i$ choosing order $j$ is derived from the utility matrix $M_t$ (extended to include a "no order" option) using a logit model ($P_t = \text{Softmax}(M_t)$). The aggregate policy $\pi_{T_\Theta}(A_t|S_t)$ is assumed to be proportional to the product of these individual probabilities, mediated by an increasing function $z(\cdot)$.
 - **Action Selection:** During inference, the action that maximizes the sum of $\log P_{i,j,t}$ is greedily selected via ILP on a bipartite graph. During training, random noise is added to $P_t$ for exploration.
 - **Update:** An approximate policy gradient optimization method is used to update the actor network, maximizing the estimated Q-value. The elasticity of the function $z(\cdot)$ is simplified to a positive constant for tractability.
 - **Critic Optimization:** The critic networks are updated following the standard TD3 methodology, minimizing the TD-error using two critics and a slowly updating target network to ensure stable learning. Target actions are computed with smoothing noise for regularization.

#### 3.3. Reward Function

The global reward is the sum of individual worker rewards. An individual worker's reward ($r_{i,t+1}$) is calculated based on:
- Income from customers ($p_{in_{i,t}}$).
- Payout to workers ($p_{out_{i,t}}$).
- Penalties for en-route orders exceeding scheduled time ($\chi_{i,t}$).
- Penalties for additional travel time for en-route orders when a new order is added ($\rho_{i,t}$).
Non-negative weights ($\beta_1$ to $\beta_5$) balance these terms. If no order is assigned, the reward is zero. The Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) is utilized for route optimization and travel time calculations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Triple-BERT was conducted using real-world ride-hailing datasets from Manhattan and Queens, New York City, and compared against several existing MARL methods categorized as Independent, CTDE, and Centralized.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison with Baselines

- **Superior Performance:** Triple-BERT consistently demonstrated superior performance across various metrics and scenarios compared to state-of-the-art MARL methods, including DeepPool, BMG-Q, HIVES, Enders et al., and CEVD.
- **Quantitative Improvements (Average across test periods, Table 1, 4):**
 - Approximately 11.95% improvement in average reward over the best alternative method (CEVD).
 - A 4.26% increase in served orders (0.98 for Triple-BERT vs. 0.94 for CEVD).
 - A 22.25% reduction in pickup times (5.73 minutes for Triple-BERT vs. 7.37 minutes for CEVD).
- **Metrics Breakdown (Figure 6, Table 4):**
 - Triple-BERT achieved the highest cumulative reward and service rate.
 - It significantly reduced average pickup times, which directly contributes to customer satisfaction.
 - In high-order volume scenarios, Triple-BERT showed improved delivery and detour times compared to other methods. However, in low-order volume scenarios, the differences were less pronounced. Increased order bundling by Triple-BERT to serve more orders can sometimes result in slightly longer delivery and detour times for individual trips, but this is offset by the overall system benefits.
 - Triple-BERT exhibited higher confirmation times, similar to methods that include an explicit action for rejecting orders. This allows the model to decline unprofitable orders and reserve capacity for future, potentially more valuable assignments.

#### 4.2. Generalization and Scalability

- **Generalization Capacity (Table 2, Table 5, Table 8):** Triple-BERT maintained its leading performance when evaluated on different datasets (Manhattan FHV data, Queens FHV data) and across various time periods on different days. Its advantage was particularly evident in scenarios with higher order volumes. Even in the Queens dataset, characterized by lower trip volumes and dispersed orders, Triple-BERT remained the best performer, though average pickup times were longer across all methods due to geographical factors.
- **Extensibility and Scalability (Table 6):** The method demonstrated strong extensibility and scalability as the number of drivers increased to 1,500 and 2,000 in high-concurrency periods (6,775 orders), achieving the highest rewards without requiring retraining.
- **Computational Efficiency (Table 7):** Decision time for Triple-BERT remained consistently below 0.2 seconds for up to 2,000 drivers and 500 orders at a single time step. This suggests that the computational overhead for decision-making is minimal, making it suitable for real-time deployment.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Stage 1 Pre-training:** Training without Stage 1 pre-training led to significant performance degradation, including failure to converge and substantial fluctuations in rewards. This validates the importance of the two-stage training strategy in addressing sample scarcity for centralized SARL.
- **Efficacy of Positive Normalization in QK-Attention:** Removing the positive normalization module from QK-Attention resulted in poor performance, underscoring its role in mitigating parameter redundancy and enhancing training stability.
- **Robustness to Noise in Policy Gradient:** Experiments with different types of random noise (Gaussian, uniform, Binary Symmetric Channel) in the policy gradient optimization all demonstrated superior performance over conventional MARL methods, indicating the robustness of the proposed policy gradient approach.
- **Effect of Positional Embedding in BERT (Table 9):** Omitting positional embeddings in BERT led to improved performance, especially in generalization tasks. The presence of positional embeddings introduced interference and restricted the model's effectiveness when order volumes exceeded the maximum length used during training.
- **Effectiveness of ARL-based Encoder (Table 10):** The ARL-based encoder significantly enhanced the performance of Triple-BERT and independent MARL methods (DeepPool, BMG-Q). However, it did not provide substantial benefits to CTDE or centralized MARL methods (HIVES, Enders et al., CEVD), suggesting that their primary limitations lie in the Curse of Dimensionality within their critic networks, which a more powerful feature extractor alone cannot resolve. Triple-BERT's performance remained superior even with a simpler MLP-based encoder, indicating the robustness of the SARL framework itself.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Triple-BERT holds several significant implications for the field of ride-sharing optimization and Reinforcement Learning:

- **Paradigm Shift in Ride-Sharing Dispatch:** This work represents a notable shift from the prevalent Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) approaches to a centralized Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning (SARL) framework for large-scale order dispatching. By demonstrating that SARL can effectively address the complexities of this domain, the paper challenges the assumption that MARL is inherently necessary or superior for problems with numerous interacting entities.

- **Addressing Key Scalability and Coordination Challenges:** Triple-BERT directly tackles fundamental limitations faced by existing methods:
 - **Curse of Dimensionality (CoD):** The centralized SARL design, coupled with a BERT-based architecture and parameter reuse, mitigates the CoD issues that plague many MARL methods when scaling to thousands of agents.
 - **Global Information Leverage and Cooperation:** By operating as a single, centralized agent, Triple-BERT inherently captures and utilizes global information, leading to more effective cooperation among drivers and optimizing overall system objectives, a common weakness in independent MARL.
 - **Vast Action and Observation Spaces:** The proposed action decomposition strategy, combined with ILP for action selection, efficiently handles the combinatorial explosion of the action space. The BERT-based network architecture with self-attention effectively processes extensive observation spaces without prohibitive parameter growth.
 - **Sample Scarcity:** The novel two-stage training strategy (MARL pre-training followed by SARL fine-tuning) provides a practical solution to the sample scarcity issue often encountered in centralized SARL settings.

- **Demonstrated Performance Gains:** The significant quantitative improvements demonstrated (e.g., 11.95% higher average reward, 4.26% increase in served orders, 22.25% reduction in pickup times) over established state-of-the-art MARL methods validate the efficacy of the Triple-BERT approach. These results are particularly important as they are achieved on real-world ride-hailing datasets and show strong generalization capabilities across varying demand patterns and geographical areas.

- **Practical Applicability:** The low inference time (consistently under 0.2 seconds for large numbers of drivers and orders) indicates the practical viability of Triple-BERT for real-time deployment in operational ride-sharing platforms. This could lead to more efficient and responsive services for users.

- **Potential Societal and Economic Impacts:**
 - **Enhanced Platform Efficiency and Profitability:** By optimizing dispatching decisions, platforms can achieve higher service rates and better resource utilization, potentially increasing revenue.
 - **Improved Customer Experience:** Significant reductions in pickup times and potentially better delivery services contribute to higher customer satisfaction.
 - **Environmental Sustainability:** More efficient ride-sharing operations can lead to reduced total vehicle miles traveled, lower fuel consumption, and decreased per-capita carbon emissions, supporting urban sustainability goals.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens new avenues for research within the SARL paradigm for complex, multi-entity systems. It identifies future work, including the exploration of more advanced SARL frameworks, enhancements to the current method (e.g., importance sampling for policy gradient, offline training), and the critical consideration of ethical implications such as algorithmic fairness and discrimination against workers within closed-box dispatching systems.

- **Reproducibility and Transparency:** The public availability of the source code, trained model parameters, and processed data enhances the transparency and reproducibility of the research, allowing other researchers to build upon this work and further validate its findings.
