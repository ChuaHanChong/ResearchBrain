# DexForce: Extracting Force-informed Actions from Kinesthetic Demonstrations for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DexForce offers a novel approach to generate high-quality demonstrations for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, translating human kinesthetic guidance into force-informed actions. The method enables robots to learn policies that achieve an average 76% success rate on diverse tasks, showing that incorporating 6-axis force-torque data in both actions and observations is crucial for precision and robustness.

## Problem

- Current robot learning methods, especially imitation learning, struggle with contact-rich dexterous manipulation due to inadequate demonstration data.
- Teleoperation, a common demonstration method, suffers from human-to-robot kinematic mismatches and a lack of direct haptic feedback, making precise force application difficult.
- Kinesthetic teaching (manual guidance) provides haptic feedback and avoids retargeting issues but typically only records robot states, not executable force-informed actions.

## Method

- A ==two-stage demonstration collection pipeline== uses kinesthetic teaching with 6-axis force-torque sensors on a dexterous hand.
- **DexForce** ==force-informed position targets== are computed by augmenting observed positions with measured contact forces, allowing a ==Cartesian impedance controller== to reproduce desired forces.
- A robot-only replay stage collects consistent visual observations (without the human demonstrator) alongside the force-informed actions for ==Diffusion Policy== training.

## Results

- Policies trained with DexForce's force-informed actions achieved an average **76%** success rate across six challenging dexterous manipulation tasks, compared to near-zero success for position-only trained policies.
- Including 6-axis force-torque observations boosted success rates, with notable improvements such as a **1600%** increase for "open AirPods" and **136%** for "unscrew nut" compared to RGB-only policies.
- The method demonstrated robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios and confirmed that a single, tuned stiffness parameter `kf` (**0.0045**) can effectively apply across various dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct imitation of robot joint positions from kinesthetic demonstrations is insufficient for contact-rich tasks; policies require actions that explicitly encode desired contact forces.
> - High-fidelity 6-axis force-torque sensor data, when included in policy observations, significantly enhances policy performance and robustness, outperforming simpler binary contact signals.
> - The method systematically transforms kinesthetic guidance into executable force-informed actions, overcoming the human-to-robot correspondence and haptic feedback limitations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"DexForce" addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of robot learning, specifically for **dexterous manipulation:** the collection of high-quality, actionable demonstration data. Imitation learning, a dominant paradigm for teaching robots complex tasks, heavily relies on such demonstrations, typically comprising state-action pairs. However, obtaining these for contact-rich, fine-grained dexterous tasks presents significant challenges.

Current widely-used demonstration collection methods, primarily **teleoperation**, often involve retargeting human hand motions to a robot hand. While effective for many tasks, these methods suffer from two inherent limitations for dexterous manipulation:
1. **Human-to-robot correspondence problem:** Human hands have different kinematics and degrees of freedom compared to robot hands, making direct motion retargeting unintuitive and often inaccurate for precise tasks.
2. **Lack of direct haptic feedback:** Demonstrators cannot physically feel the forces and contacts experienced by the robot, making it exceedingly difficult to perform tasks that demand nuanced force application.

Prior work has attempted to mitigate these issues. Some methods improve retargeting (e.g., ResPilot) or use twin robots (e.g., Tilde) but still lack haptic feedback. Other paradigms like "hand-over-hand" or exoskeletons aim to make human movements more robot-like but often focus on simpler grasping motions and may not equip the robot with comprehensive force sensing.

"DexForce" carves its niche by leveraging **kinesthetic teaching** (manually guiding the robot) combined with **direct force sensing** on the robot hand. Kinesthetic teaching naturally resolves the human-to-robot correspondence problem and provides immediate haptic feedback to the demonstrator. However, kinesthetic demonstrations traditionally only provide observed robot states (positions, measured forces), not the *actions* that would reproduce those forces.

This paper builds upon a smaller body of work that uses forces to inform actions for imitation learning. Previous efforts in this direction for robot hands often assume fixed grasps or require hand-designed heuristics for contact switching, limiting their general applicability. For robot arms, similar principles have been applied using Dynamic Movement Primitives or impedance control, but these are typically restricted to arm-level tasks and may not generalize to dexterous hand manipulation or incorporate vision effectively.

DexForce differentiates itself by:
- **Focusing on general dexterous, contact-rich manipulation tasks with a multi-fingered hand**, moving beyond fixed grasps or single-finger interactions.
- **Systematically extracting "force-informed actions" from kinesthetic demonstrations** that explicitly account for measured contact forces, enabling the robot to reproduce desired forces via an impedance controller.
- **Integrating vision-based learning (Diffusion Policies)** with these force-informed actions, allowing for more robust and generalizable policies.
- **Providing a nuanced study on the role of force observations** in policy learning, showing that 6-axis force-torque data is crucial for precision tasks and outperforms binary contact signals.

In essence, DexForce positions itself as a critical advancement for enabling imitation learning in scenarios where force control and tactile feedback are paramount, bridging the gap between human intuition and robotic execution for dexterous, contact-rich tasks that have been challenging for conventional data collection methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "DexForce" is to develop a method for collecting **high-quality demonstrations for contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks** that effectively capture the necessary force interactions. The motivation stems from the significant limitations of existing demonstration collection methods, particularly teleoperation, when applied to fine-grained tasks requiring precise force application and contact coordination.

Specifically, the authors identify the following key motivations:
- **Address the inadequacy of current demonstration methods:** Traditional teleoperation, relying on human-to-robot motion retargeting, fails to provide intuitive control or essential haptic feedback for contact-rich tasks. This makes it difficult for a human demonstrator to apply the "right forces, at the right times, in the right contact locations."
- **Enable successful imitation learning for dexterity:** For policies to learn how to perform contact-rich tasks (e.g., opening an AirPods case, unscrewing a nut), the demonstrations must include actions that inherently encode the required force profiles. Current methods struggle to provide these "force-informed actions."
- **Leverage the benefits of kinesthetic teaching:** Kinesthetic teaching naturally overcomes the human-to-robot correspondence problem and provides direct haptic feedback, making it ideal for demonstrating contact-rich tasks. However, it typically only records states, not the executable actions needed for policy learning.
- **Compute force-informed actions from raw kinesthetic data:** The core motivation is to devise a systematic way to translate observed robot positions and measured contact forces during kinesthetic demonstrations into effective, executable actions that can reproduce the demonstrated forces.
- **Study the role of force data in robot learning:** Beyond just collecting demonstrations, the paper aims to investigate how using these force-informed actions, and including force data in policy observations, impacts the learning and performance of dexterous manipulation policies. This includes understanding the specific benefits of detailed 6-axis force-torque data compared to simpler binary contact signals.

In summary, DexForce seeks to create a robust and intuitive pipeline for demonstrating complex, contact-rich dexterous tasks, thereby enabling robots to learn policies that can reliably execute these tasks by applying appropriate forces and motions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexForce proposes a two-stage demonstration collection procedure that leverages kinesthetic teaching and integrated force-torque sensing to extract "force-informed actions" for policy learning. The overall approach is designed to overcome the limitations of traditional teleoperation for contact-rich tasks.

**Hardware Setup:**
The system utilizes an **Allegro Hand** (a common dexterous robot hand) instrumented with **6-axis force-torque sensors** (specifically CoinFT sensors) mounted at the bases of the thumb and index fingers. A wrist-mounted RealSense D435 camera with a fisheye lens provides RGB image observations of the workspace.

**Cartesian Impedance Control:**
The robot fingers are controlled using a Cartesian impedance controller. This controller models the relationship between force and motion as a second-order mechanical system, allowing the robot to apply desired forces by tracking desired position targets. The control force F for a finger is defined as:
`F = kp(xd - xc) - kv(ẋc)`
where `xd` is the desired fingertip position, `xc` is the current position, `ẋc` is the current velocity, `kp` is the stiffness gain, and `kv` is the damping gain. Joint torques are then computed using the finger Jacobian and gravity compensation. This method is preferred over explicit force control due to its greater stability, especially during discontinuous contact.

**Computing Force-informed Position Targets:**
The core innovation of DexForce lies in how it computes actions. During kinesthetic demonstrations, the human operator directly moves the robot hand. While this provides observed fingertip positions (`xo`) and measured contact forces (`f`), directly using `xo` as an action would not reproduce the applied forces (as `xo` remains the same regardless of the force applied, as illustrated in Fig. 2).
To address this, DexForce computes a "force-informed target" (`xf`) for each fingertip. This target position, when tracked by the impedance controller, is designed to reproduce the measured contact force `f`. Assuming quasi-static motion, `xf` is calculated as an augmentation of the observed position `xo` based on the measured contact force `f` (exerted by the finger onto the object):
`xf = xo + kf * f`
where `kf` is a hand-tuned scalar stiffness parameter. Intuitively, if an operator presses harder (larger `f`), the `xf` target moves "deeper" into the object or surface, causing the impedance controller to exert more force to reach that target.

**DexForce Two-Stage Demonstration Collection Procedure (Fig. 3):**

1. **Stage 1: Extract Force-informed Targets from Kinesthetic Demonstration:**
 - A human operator physically guides the robot hand to perform the desired task.
 - During this kinesthetic demonstration, the system records two key data streams for each finger over `T` timesteps:
 - Observed fingertip positions: `xo,1 ... xo,T`
 - Corresponding measured contact forces (and moments, `f1 ... fT`) from the 6-axis force-torque sensors.
 - Using the recorded `xo` and `f` data, the system computes a trajectory of force-informed position targets `xf,1 ... xf,T` using the equation `xf = xo + kf * f`.

2. **Stage 2: Obtain Final Demonstration for Policy Learning (Robot Replay):**
 - The trajectories `xf,1 ... xf,T` computed in Stage 1 are replayed by the robot. The robot's impedance controller tracks these force-informed targets.
 - This replay stage is crucial because images collected during a kinesthetic demonstration would include the operator's hand, which is not present during test-time robot execution.
 - During this robot-only replay, the system records:
 - New observed fingertip positions: `x*o,1 ... x*o,T`
 - New contact forces: `f*1 ... f*T`
 - New contact moments: `m*1 ... m*T`
 - Wrist camera images: `I1 ... IT`
 - The `kf` parameter is tuned such that these replays successfully reproduce the intended kinesthetic demonstration. For all tasks, `kf = 0.0045` was used.

**Policy Learning:**
- **Policy Architecture:** Diffusion Policies (a CNN-based model) are used for training.
- **Observations:** At each timestep `t`, the policy's observation consists of image features extracted from `It` (using ResNet18) concatenated with the Stage 2 contact forces `f*t` and moments `m*t` for each finger.
- **Actions:** The policy outputs force-informed targets (`xf`), which are then executed by the Cartesian impedance controller.
- **Supervision:** Policy training is supervised directly on the `xf` targets computed during the Stage 1 kinesthetic demonstration.

This methodical approach ensures that the learned policies are trained on actions that are intrinsically linked to the forces required for successful contact-rich manipulation, while also providing consistent, robot-only visual observations for robust learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in "DexForce" focused on answering three key questions, yielding significant results that underscore the importance of force-informed actions and observations for dexterous manipulation. The evaluation was performed on six contact-rich tasks: slide cube, reorient smiley, open AirPods, grasp battery, unscrew nut, and flip box, covering both one-finger and two-finger interactions.

**1. Importance of Force-informed Actions for Imitation Learning:**
- **Key Result:** Policies trained on DexForce’s **force-informed targets achieved an average success rate of 76%** across all six tasks (ranging from 57% for "open AirPods" to 90% for "slide cube"). In stark contrast, policies trained directly on observed fingertip positions (which do not account for contact forces) had **near-zero success rates**.
- **Interpretation:** This dramatic difference highlights that simply mimicking robot motion is insufficient for contact-rich tasks. The ability to reproduce the *intended forces*—enabled by force-informed actions—is absolutely critical. Policies trained without force information often fail to apply any effective force, or apply incorrect forces, leading to task failure even if they reach the object. For example, in "unscrew nut," the non-force-informed policy could partially unscrew by pushing on corners but consistently failed to grasp the nut for full task completion, indicating the specific need for force-informed actions in grasping. The "open AirPods" task, while having a lower overall success rate, still benefited significantly from force-informed actions, achieving success or partial success in 25 out of 30 trials, demonstrating its ability to apply meaningful force.

**2. Impact of Including Contact Forces in Policy Observations:**
- **Key Result:** Including 6-axis force (and moment) data in policy observations (RGB, F/T) generally **improved success rates for all tasks**. This improvement was statistically significant and most pronounced for tasks requiring advanced precision and coordination: **open AirPods (1600% increase), unscrew nut (136% increase), and slide cube (58.8% increase)**, compared to RGB-only policies.
- **Comparison to Binary Contact:** Policies using only binary contact signals (RGB, 0/1) generally performed worse than or equivalently to RGB-only policies. This suggests that simply knowing "contact" or "no contact" is insufficient; the nuanced, continuous 6-axis force and moment information is crucial for informed decision-making in complex contact scenarios.
- **Task-Specific Benefits:** For tasks like "open AirPods," "unscrew nut," and "slide cube," RGB-only policies often failed due to incorrect force application (e.g., pushing too hard, failing to apply enough torque, or mismanaging friction). Force observations provided the necessary sensory feedback for policies to achieve the required precision and coordination.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Performance:** On OOD variations of "flip box" (heavier box) and "unscrew nut" (different initial screw rotation), policies trained with force-informed observations (RGB, F/T) continued to outperform RGB-only policies, completing tasks successfully about 50% of the time despite the increased challenge. This suggests that force observations contribute to greater robustness.

**3. Sensitivity to the Stiffness Parameter `kf`:**
- **Key Result:** A sensitivity analysis of `kf` (the parameter that scales force into position displacement for force-informed targets) showed that **DexForce is not overly sensitive to its choice**. Each task exhibited a permissible *range* of `kf` values that resulted in successful demonstration replays. Crucially, these ranges for all six tasks overlapped, indicating that a single `kf` value (`kf = 0.0045`, used for all experiments) can work effectively across diverse tasks.
- **Implication for Force Magnitude:** The existence of a `kf` range implies that tasks can tolerate a range of applied force magnitudes, as long as they fall within the permissible window. Applying too little (too small `kf`) or too much force (too large `kf`) led to task failure.
- **Correlation with Force-Sensitivity:** The tasks that benefited most from force observations (open AirPods, unscrew nut, slide cube) also exhibited the smallest `kf` ranges. This suggests a direct correlation: tasks that require higher precision and coordination are inherently more sensitive to the exact magnitude of applied forces, and thus benefit more from explicit force sensing and force-informed actions. The minimum `kf` value always being greater than 0 further reinforced the necessity of using measured forces to compute actions.

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate that integrating contact force measurements—both in action generation and policy observations—is fundamental for achieving capable and precise dexterous manipulation in contact-rich environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"DexForce" presents a significant advancement in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring intricate contact interactions. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling High-Quality Data Collection for Complex Tasks:** The most direct impact of DexForce is its ability to generate high-quality, actionable demonstrations for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. These tasks have historically been challenging for imitation learning due to the limitations of existing teleoperation methods (human-to-robot mismatch, lack of haptic feedback). By leveraging kinesthetic teaching and systematically computing force-informed actions, DexForce provides a crucial bridge between human intuition and robot execution for these demanding scenarios.

2. **Highlighting the Critical Role of Force-informed Actions:** The stark contrast in success rates between policies trained on force-informed actions (76% average success) and those trained on purely position-based actions (near-zero success) definitively establishes that accounting for contact forces in robot actions is paramount for dexterous, contact-rich manipulation. This finding challenges approaches that might oversimplify actions to just position trajectories, emphasizing the need for more sophisticated action representations.

3. **Providing Nuanced Insights into Force Observations:** The detailed ablation study on force observations contributes valuable insights into how robots perceive and utilize force information. The finding that 6-axis force-torque data significantly outperforms binary contact signals for precision tasks, and generally improves performance, underscores the value of rich tactile sensing. This can guide future sensor development and observation space design for robot learning.

4. **Advancing Robot Learning for Dexterity:** DexForce demonstrates a concrete pathway for training robust policies for tasks involving contact switching, articulated objects, and frictional interactions—areas where previous force-based learning methods for dexterous hands often struggled (e.g., due to fixed grasp assumptions). The use of Diffusion Policies in conjunction with DexForce's data further shows how modern learning architectures can be effectively applied to these complex problems when provided with appropriate data.

5. **Valuable Research Tool and Foundation for Future Work:** While the authors acknowledge that DexForce might not be ideal for "large-scale data collection" due to the manual kinesthetic teaching component, its primary value lies as a "research tool." It enables researchers to:
 - Systematically study the impact of force sensing on imitation learning.
 - Generate benchmark datasets for contact-rich tasks that are difficult to create otherwise.
 - Inform the design of future, potentially more scalable, data collection methods (e.g., enhancing existing teleoperation systems with haptic feedback or more intuitive human-to-robot correspondence, or integrating these insights into wearable robotic hands like those in).

6. **Potential for Broader Impact:** The principles established by DexForce – emphasizing the generation of force-informed actions and the critical role of detailed force observations – are generalizable. They could influence the development of robotic systems in various applications requiring fine manipulation, such as:
 - **Assembly and manufacturing:** Where precise force control is necessary for fitting parts or handling delicate components.
 - **Service robotics:** For tasks like opening containers, manipulating household objects, or operating complex interfaces.
 - **Medical robotics:** In surgical procedures or rehabilitation, where delicate interaction and haptic feedback are paramount.

In conclusion, "DexForce" provides a compelling methodology and strong empirical evidence for the indispensable role of force information in learning capable dexterous manipulation policies. It sets a new standard for demonstration quality in contact-rich scenarios and offers a clear direction for advancing both robot hardware and learning algorithms toward truly dexterous and robust robotic interaction.
