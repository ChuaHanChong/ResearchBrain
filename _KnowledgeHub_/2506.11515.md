# Manager: Aggregating Insights from Unimodal Experts in Two-Tower VLMs and MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology, Central South University, and National University of Singapore developed "Manager," a plugin that adaptively aggregates multi-level insights from unimodal encoders to enhance vision-language understanding. This approach boosted Two-Tower VLM performance on tasks like VQAv2 to 79.15% accuracy and improved MLLM zero-shot capabilities across 20 diverse datasets by over 1% on average, demonstrating powerful synergy with multi-grid algorithms.

## Problem

- Existing Two-Tower Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like BridgeTower ineffectively utilize multi-level semantic knowledge from unimodal encoders due to fixed layer-by-layer connections.
- Prior multimodal fusion architectures suffered from rigid coupling between cross-modal and unimodal layers, limiting scalability and increasing computational costs.
- The evolving Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) architecture and its challenges with high-resolution images via multi-grid algorithms were not adequately addressed by previous VLM-focused research.

## Method

- Introduced =="Manager"==, a lightweight plugin for adaptively aggregating insights from diverse levels of pre-trained unimodal experts.
- Developed the ==Adaptive Aggregation Unimodal Manager (AAUM)== which dynamically generates token-level weights from a cross-modal fused query for flexible knowledge integration.
- Integrated managers into both Two-Tower VLMs (==ManagerTower==) and Multimodal Large Language Models (LLaVA-OV-Manager) to enhance vision-language understanding across different architectural paradigms.

## Results

- ManagerTower achieved state-of-the-art performance in Two-Tower VLMs, surpassing strong baselines like METER and BridgeTower on VQAv2 (**79.15%** accuracy) and Flickr30K (**95.64%** TR@1).
- Managers consistently improved LLaVA-OV's zero-shot performance across 20 diverse MLLM datasets, yielding an average **+1.06%** score with minimal overhead.
- Combining Manager with multi-grid algorithms resulted in the highest overall performance, with a **+4.60%** increase over the baseline, effectively mitigating semantic ambiguity in high-resolution image processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive aggregation, particularly with token-level dynamic weighting guided by a cross-modal query, is crucial for effectively leveraging multi-level unimodal semantic knowledge.
> - Managers increase the diversity of internal model representations and attention patterns, facilitating richer information flow and preventing over-focusing on redundant features.
> - The "Manager" plugin offers an efficient solution, providing substantial performance gains with negligible additional parameters (0.08M) or computational overhead (1.03-1.04x).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the forefront of Vision-Language (VL) representation learning, a rapidly evolving field driven by advancements in Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) techniques. The core objective of VLP is to acquire transferable multimodal knowledge from vast image-text pairs, subsequently improving VL representation and performance on various downstream tasks like visual question answering, visual entailment, and image-text retrieval.

Historically, VLMs have evolved from architectures that process modalities separately before fusion. Two-Tower VLMs, such as METER and BridgeTower, represent a significant paradigm where visual and textual inputs are encoded by unimodal encoders before being fused in a cross-modal encoder. METER, a foundational model, primarily utilizes only the last-layer representations from unimodal encoders, thereby overlooking the rich, multi-level semantic knowledge embedded within earlier layers. BridgeTower attempted to address this by building layer-by-layer connections between top unimodal layers and each cross-modal layer.

However, the authors identify critical limitations in BridgeTower and similar prior works:
1. **Ineffective Layer-by-Layer Utilization:** BridgeTower's fixed layer-by-layer connections constrain the flexible exploitation of different levels of unimodal semantic knowledge.
2. **Rigid Architecture Coupling:** The number of cross-modal layers is strictly bound to the number of unimodal layer representations, leading to scalability issues, increased parameters, and computational cost.
3. **Limited Architectural Scope:** Prior explorations, including BridgeTower, have predominantly focused on the Two-Tower VLM architecture, neglecting the burgeoning Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) architectures.
4. **Narrow Evaluation Scope:** Evaluations were often confined to traditional low-resolution datasets, limiting the generalizability of findings to more challenging, high-resolution, or complex real-world scenarios.

This work directly tackles these limitations by introducing "Manager," a plugin designed for adaptive aggregation of unimodal insights. By doing so, it advances the state-of-the-art in multimodal fusion beyond static or fixed integration schemes. Furthermore, the paper makes a crucial contribution by extending its exploration to MLLMs, which are increasingly dominant due to their superior zero-shot performance. Within the MLLM context, it also addresses the challenges posed by multi-grid algorithms (used for handling high-resolution images), specifically their potential to introduce semantic ambiguity. The "Manager" plugin, in synergy with multi-grid approaches, is posited as a solution to enhance visual representation from both "depth" (multi-level semantic knowledge) and "width" (higher resolution) perspectives, effectively mitigating ambiguity. This positions the paper as a significant step towards more robust, scalable, and versatile multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations underpinning this research are multifaceted, aiming to resolve extant challenges in multimodal representation learning and pave the way for more advanced Vision-Language Models:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Existing Two-Tower VLMs:** The authors explicitly aim to address the shortcomings of models like BridgeTower. This includes tackling the ineffective layer-by-layer utilization of unimodal representations, which restricts the flexible exploitation of diverse semantic knowledge across different unimodal layers. They also seek to decouple the rigid relationship between the number of cross-modal layers and the number of unimodal layer representations, enhancing scalability and reducing computational overhead.

2. **Propose a Lightweight, Efficient, and Effective Aggregation Mechanism:** The core motivation is to introduce "Manager," a novel plugin that can adaptively aggregate insights from different levels of pre-trained unimodal experts. This aggregation is intended to facilitate more comprehensive vision-language alignment and fusion than previous methods. The emphasis is on a solution that is minimal in computational footprint ("lightweight and efficient") while yielding substantial performance gains ("effective").

3. **Introduce ManagerTower as an Enhanced Two-Tower VLM:** The research motivates the creation of ManagerTower, a new VLM that integrates the proposed "Manager" within each cross-modal layer. A key objective here is to demonstrate that this adaptive aggregation strategy significantly outperforms strong baselines like METER and BridgeTower on conventional VLM tasks, both with and without VLP.

4. **Extend Manager's Applicability to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** Recognizing the growing dominance and capabilities of MLLMs, a crucial objective is to assess the generality and effectiveness of the "Manager" plugin within this architecture. This involves integrating managers into a prominent MLLM baseline, LLaVA-OV, to create LLaVA-OV-Manager.

5. **Enable Broader and More Challenging Zero-Shot Evaluation:** By extending to MLLMs and leveraging their inherent capabilities (e.g., handling high-resolution images via multi-grid algorithms), the authors aim to conduct more comprehensive zero-shot evaluations. This includes assessing performance across a wider range of downstream datasets, especially those involving high-resolution document understanding and diverse real-world scenarios, which are often beyond the scope of traditional VLMs.

6. **Investigate Synergy with Multi-Grid Algorithms and Mitigate Semantic Ambiguity:** A key motivation is to explore how the "Manager" plugin interacts with multi-grid algorithms in MLLMs. The authors hypothesize that both Manager (providing "deeper" visual details) and multi-grid (providing "wider" visual details) improve visual representation orthogonally. They aim to demonstrate that their synergy can not only enhance performance but also specifically mitigate semantic ambiguity that might arise from multi-grid approaches by capturing more diverse visual details.

7. **Provide In-Depth Analysis and Insights:** Beyond empirical performance, the study seeks to offer theoretical and mechanistic insights into *why* managers are effective. This includes visualizing aggregation weights and analyzing how managers influence consecutive layer representations and attention weight distributions within the model.

In essence, the motivation is to develop a more intelligent and adaptive mechanism for multimodal fusion that leverages the hierarchical nature of unimodal representations, thereby improving the performance, scalability, and interpretability of both established VLMs and cutting-edge MLLMs across a broader spectrum of tasks and data complexities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is structured in two main parts: an exploration of the "Manager" plugin within the Two-Tower VLM architecture (resulting in ManagerTower) and its extension to the Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) architecture (creating LLaVA-OV-Manager).

#### 3.1. Manager Design Principles

The core innovation is the "Manager," a plugin designed to aggregate insights from different levels of pre-trained unimodal experts. The design adheres to principles of being lightweight, flexible, effective, efficient, and maintaining spatial consistency.

- **Static Aggregation Manager (SAM):** As a preliminary exploration, SAM linearly combines representations from *all* previous unimodal and cross-modal layers using learned, static weights. Initial tests showed this approach was less effective, potentially due to numerical scale differences between unimodal and cross-modal representations and redundancy.
- **Static Aggregation Unimodal Manager (SAUM):** To address SAM's limitations, SAUM was introduced to focus solely on aggregating insights from the *top N unimodal layers* (pre-trained unimodal experts) and retaining only the representation from the *immediate previous cross-modal layer*. Learned static weights are applied to unimodal representations. This significantly improved performance over SAM and BridgeTower.
- **Adaptive Aggregation Unimodal Manager (AAUM):** Recognizing that static weights might not be optimal for all tokens and samples, AAUM adaptively generates token-level aggregation weights. These weights are dynamically produced using a linear projection of a "cross-modal fused query" (derived from the previous visual and textual cross-modal layer representations) via a softmax function. This allows for more flexible and context-aware aggregation of unimodal semantic knowledge.

#### 3.2. Exploration on Two-Tower VLM (ManagerTower)

- **Architecture:** ManagerTower uses a pre-trained CLIP-ViT B-224/16 as the visual encoder and RoBERTa-BASE as the textual encoder, similar to METER and BridgeTower. A randomly initialized 6-layer transformer-based cross-modal encoder incorporates the managers.
- **Manager Integration:** SAUM is used in the 1st cross-modal layer, as there is no "previous cross-modal layer representation" to form a query. AAUMs are employed in the subsequent (2nd to 6th) cross-modal layers to leverage adaptive aggregation.
- **Pre-training (VLP):** ManagerTower is pre-trained using standard VLP objectives: Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and Image-Text Matching (ITM). The training dataset comprises 4M public image-caption pairs (Conceptual Captions, SBU Captions, MSCOCO Captions, Visual Genome). Pre-training settings (e.g., 100k steps, batch size 4096, learning rate 1e-5) are consistent with previous strong baselines for fair comparison.
- **Fine-tuning and Evaluation:** Performance is evaluated on four established downstream VL tasks: VQAv2 (visual question answering), SNLI-VE (visual entailment), NLVR2 (visual reasoning), and Flickr30K (image-text retrieval). Ablation studies are conducted on:
 - **Manager Type and Query:** Comparing SAM, SAUM, AAUM, and different query mechanisms for AAUM.
 - **Number of Cross-Modal Layers:** Evaluating ManagerTower against BridgeTower with varying layer counts to assess scalability and efficiency.
 - **Number of Unimodal Experts (N):** Investigating the impact of varying the number of top unimodal layers used for aggregation.
- **Visualization:** Aggregation weights of managers are visualized to understand how semantic knowledge is utilized across layers.

#### 3.3. Exploration on MLLM (LLaVA-OV-Manager)

- **Baseline:** LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B-SI (LLaVA-OV), a prominent open-source multi-grid MLLM, is used. It features a 27-layer SigLIP visual encoder, a 24-layer Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct LLM, and a 2-layer MLP projector.
- **Manager Adaptation:** Visual managers (SAUM, chosen for better efficiency in MLLMs based on preliminary experiments) are introduced into LLaVA-OV. They aggregate insights from the top half of the visual encoder and inject them into the LLM at regular intervals (e.g., 6 managers at an interval of 4 layers). An optimized SAUM version removes Layer Normalization, softmax, and W_C, initializing W to zero with multiplicative jitter noise, to better adapt to the pre-trained nature of the LLM.
- **Training:** Follows LLaVA-OV's multi-stage training strategy using approximately 8M data samples with an autoregressive objective.
- **Evaluation:** Zero-shot performance is assessed on 20 diverse downstream datasets, categorized by:
 - **Capability:** General, Text-rich, Knowledge, Real-world.
 - **Image Type:** Natural, Abstract, Hybrid.
 - **Resolution:** Low, High.
 - This broad evaluation validates the manager's effectiveness in more challenging, real-world scenarios.
- **Synergy Analysis:** Investigates the interaction between the "Manager" (capturing deeper visual details) and the multi-grid algorithm (capturing wider visual details). Ablation studies compare:
 - Baseline vs. Baseline+Manager vs. Baseline+Grid vs. Baseline+Grid+Manager.
 - Visual representation selection (which layers of SigLIP to aggregate).
 - Manager injection times within the LLM.
 - Manager's scope (base image only, grids only, or both).
- **In-Depth Analysis:**
 - **Case Studies:** Qualitative analysis on specific datasets (DocVQA, SEED-Bench, OCRBench) to illustrate how managers improve understanding and mitigate semantic ambiguity.
 - **Visualization Analysis:** Quantitative analysis using cosine similarity of consecutive layer representations, average attention entropy, and Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between attention heads to explain the underlying mechanisms of performance improvement.

This comprehensive methodology, combining architectural innovation, rigorous empirical testing across diverse settings, and in-depth analytical tools, provides strong evidence for the effectiveness and interpretability of the proposed "Manager" plugin.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across both Two-Tower VLM and MLLM architectures, demonstrating the efficacy and versatility of the proposed "Manager" plugin.

#### 4.1. Two-Tower VLM (ManagerTower) Results

- **Superior Manager Design:** The Adaptive Aggregation Unimodal Manager (AAUM), particularly when guided by a cross-modal fused query (derived from previous visual and textual cross-modal layer representations), consistently outperformed other manager types (SAM, SAUM, and attention-based variants). This validates the importance of adaptive, token-level aggregation for effectively leveraging multi-layer unimodal semantic knowledge.
- **Enhanced Performance over BridgeTower:** ManagerTower consistently and significantly outperformed BridgeTower across various numbers of cross-modal layers. Notably, ManagerTower with only three cross-modal layers achieved better performance than BridgeTower with six layers, highlighting its efficiency and ability to leverage unimodal insights more effectively.
- **State-of-the-Art on Downstream Tasks:** After pre-training on commonly used 4M VLP data, ManagerTower achieved superior results on all four downstream tasks (VQAv2, SNLI-VE, NLVR2, Flickr30K). It not only surpassed strong baselines like METER and BridgeTower but also outperformed several base-size models trained on more data and/or larger models, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness within a constrained data setting. For example, it achieved 79.15% accuracy on VQAv2 Test-Std and 95.64% TR@1 on Flickr30K.
- **Adaptive Aggregation Confirmed:** Visualization of aggregation weights revealed that ManagerTower learns diverse distributions across different cross-modal layers. This contrasts with the fixed, layer-by-layer distributions of BridgeTower and strongly supports the claim that managers adaptively aggregate required unimodal semantic knowledge for comprehensive vision-language representation learning.
- **Flexible Unimodal Expert Utilization:** The study showed that ManagerTower's performance peaks when using unimodal representations from the top half of the unimodal encoders (N=6 in this case), and then decreases with more lower-layer representations. This suggests that while multi-layer information is valuable, judicious selection of "unimodal experts" is crucial.

#### 4.2. MLLM (LLaVA-OV-Manager) Results

- **Significant Zero-Shot Performance Boost:** Managers consistently and significantly improved the zero-shot performance of LLaVA-OV across all 20 diverse downstream datasets. This improvement was observed across different categories of capabilities (General, Text-rich, Knowledge, Real-world), image types (Natural, Abstract, Hybrid), and resolutions (Low, High).
- **Lightweight and Efficient Plugin:** The "Manager" plugin adds negligible parameter overhead (0.08M), FLOPs (1.02x), and computational cost (1.03-1.04x for training/inference) to MLLMs, while yielding substantial performance gains (e.g., +1.06% overall average score for Baseline+Manager over Baseline).
- **Powerful Synergy with Multi-Grid Algorithm:** The combination of Manager and the multi-grid algorithm (Baseline+Grid+Manager) achieved the highest overall performance (+4.60% over Baseline). This demonstrates that Manager and multi-grid are complementary, improving visual representation from "depth" (multi-level semantic knowledge) and "width" (higher resolution) directions, respectively. Their synergy yielded greater improvements than either method alone, effectively addressing semantic ambiguity.
- **Optimal Visual Representation Selection:** Similar to Two-Tower VLMs, managers showed the best performance when aggregating visual representations from the top half of the visual encoder, aligning with observations that higher layers capture more global visual features.
- **Impact on Internal Dynamics:**
 - **Representation Diversity:** Managers reduced the cosine similarity between consecutive layer representations within the LLM, especially in bottom layers. This indicates that managers inject more diverse insights and visual details, facilitating richer vision-language representation learning in subsequent layers.
 - **Attention Diversity:** Managers increased both the average attention entropy and the average KL divergence between attention heads in most layers. This suggests that managers lead to broader attention weights and more diverse attention heads, enabling the MLLM to capture a wider array of features and prevent over-focusing on redundant information.
- **Detailed Case Studies:**
 - **DocVQA:** Managers, especially in collaboration with multi-grid, significantly improved table understanding and heading recognition, demonstrating enhanced fine-grained visual detail capture.
 - **SEED-Bench:** Managers helped mitigate performance degradation and semantic ambiguity caused by the multi-grid algorithm (which sometimes cuts off objects), particularly for tasks involving instance counting and spatial relations.
 - **OCRBench:** Synergy with multi-grid brought significant improvements in artistic and handwriting text recognition. However, managers sometimes caused slight degradation in "Non-Semantic Text Recognition," suggesting that semantic knowledge can interfere when the task requires literal, non-semantic processing.

In summary, the main findings strongly support that "Manager" is an effective and efficient mechanism for adaptive aggregation of unimodal insights, significantly improving the performance of both Two-Tower VLMs and state-of-the-art MLLMs, while also providing valuable insights into their internal working mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of "Manager" as a lightweight, efficient, and effective plugin carries significant implications for the future of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with broad potential impact across research and application domains.

1. **Advancement in Multimodal Fusion Mechanisms:** The primary significance lies in proposing a novel and superior mechanism for multimodal fusion. By enabling adaptive aggregation of insights from different levels of pre-trained unimodal experts, "Manager" moves beyond previous static or rigidly structured approaches (like BridgeTower's layer-by-layer connections). This more intelligent and context-aware integration of unimodal semantic knowledge can lead to richer, more comprehensive multimodal representations, enhancing VL understanding across the board.

2. **Generalizable Architectural Plugin:** The "Manager" is designed as a modular, lightweight plugin, demonstrating its effectiveness across two distinct VLM paradigms: Two-Tower VLMs and MLLMs. This plug-and-play nature signifies its potential for widespread adoption, allowing researchers and practitioners to easily integrate it into various existing or future multimodal architectures without significant architectural overhauls or prohibitive computational costs. This enhances model design flexibility and accelerates innovation.

3. **Boost for MLLM Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated significant zero-shot performance improvements in LLaVA-OV-Manager across a diverse range of 20 datasets, capabilities, image types, and resolutions highlight its immediate impact on MLLMs. This is particularly crucial for real-world applications where MLLMs need to handle complex and varied visual inputs (e.g., high-resolution documents, detailed images). The negligible increase in computational overhead for substantial gains makes it a highly attractive enhancement for practical MLLM deployment.

4. **Mitigation of Multi-Grid Semantic Ambiguity:** A critical contribution is the discovery that "Manager" can effectively synergize with and even mitigate the semantic ambiguity issues inherent in multi-grid algorithms, which are vital for MLLMs to process high-resolution images. By complementing "wider" visual details from multi-grid with "deeper" multi-level semantic insights, the paper offers a robust solution to improve performance on tasks that require fine-grained spatial, categorical, and textual understanding from potentially fragmented visual inputs. This enhances the reliability of MLLMs in interpreting complex visual information.

5. **Deeper Mechanistic Understanding:** The in-depth analysis, including visualization of aggregation weights, layer representation similarity, and attention distribution entropy/KL divergence, provides valuable mechanistic insights. These analyses explain *why* managers work by showing how they increase the diversity of learned representations and attention patterns. This understanding is invaluable for guiding future architectural designs and optimizing multimodal learning, moving beyond purely empirical improvements towards theoretically informed advancements.

6. **Paving the Way for Future Research:** This work opens several promising avenues for future research:
 - **Advanced Manager Designs:** Exploring more sophisticated, potentially sparse, or dynamically activated manager mechanisms beyond the current SAUM and AAUM.
 - **Adaptive Injection Strategies:** Investigating more flexible and adaptive ways to select which unimodal layers to aggregate and how to inject them into different parts of the cross-modal encoder or LLM.
 - **Integration with Other Paradigms:** Extending managers to other VLM architectures, multimodal in-context learning, and chain-of-thought reasoning, as well as multi-grained multimodal data.
 - **Handling Non-Semantic Tasks:** Further research is needed to understand and mitigate potential performance degradation when managers, with their inherent semantic aggregation, are applied to tasks requiring purely non-semantic pattern recognition (as observed in OCRBench).

In conclusion, "Manager" presents a powerful and generalizable approach to improving multimodal understanding by intelligently leveraging the hierarchical information within unimodal encoders. Its demonstrable success across diverse models and tasks, coupled with the insights into its underlying mechanisms, positions this work as a significant step forward in the development of more capable, efficient, and robust Vision-Language Models and Multimodal Large Language Models.
