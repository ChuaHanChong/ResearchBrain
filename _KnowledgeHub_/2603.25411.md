# HiSpatial: Taming Hierarchical 3D Spatial Understanding in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HiSpatial introduces a framework and RGB-D VLM designed to foster comprehensive hierarchical 3D spatial understanding in vision-language models from 2D inputs. The model leverages a vast, automatically generated dataset of over 2 billion spatial QA pairs, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple spatial reasoning benchmarks, including outperforming GPT-4o and GPT-5 in quantitative spatial VQA.

## Problem

- Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with inferring intricate 3D structures, recognizing object properties and relationships, and performing high-level spatial reasoning from standard 2D visual inputs.
- A unified and systematic task design that holistically captures the diverse cognitive levels of 3D spatial intelligence is absent, and the interdependencies between different spatial reasoning skills are not well-defined.
- The acquisition of large-scale, diverse, and accurately 3D-grounded data remains a significant hurdle, as existing 3D datasets are often limited in scale or scope, and web data lacks explicit 3D supervision.

## Method

- A principled ==hierarchical framework== was formulated, decomposing 3D spatial understanding into four progressively complex levels: ==Basic Geometric Perception==, Object-level, Inter-object Relational, and Abstract Spatial Reasoning.
- An automated pipeline was developed to construct a large-scale and diverse ==3D spatial VQA dataset==, synthesizing over 2 billion QA pairs from approximately 5 million images, covering all hierarchical tasks.
- An ==RGB-D VLM== architecture was developed, built on ==PaliGemma-2==, which effectively integrates ==metric-scale 3D point maps== as auxiliary inputs to enhance spatial grounding.

## Results

- The HiSpatial-3B model, utilizing RGB-XYZ input, achieved state-of-the-art performance on various spatial reasoning benchmarks, surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4o and GPT-5 in quantitative spatial VQA.
- On qualitative spatial VQA benchmarks, HiSpatial-3B achieved **80.71%** on EmbSpatial and **86.18%** on RoboSpatial, demonstrating comprehensive spatial understanding across multiple levels.
- Evaluation on general VQA benchmarks (e.g., MMBench, POPE) showed that HiSpatial-3B not only retained but generally surpassed its base model's performance, indicating that spatial fine-tuning did not negatively impact general visual understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lower-level spatial tasks (geometric perception and object-level understanding) implicitly contribute to the capture of richer spatial information, benefiting the performance of higher-level relational and abstract reasoning tasks.
> - Integrating explicit metric-scale 3D point maps as auxiliary input is more effective for enhancing VLM spatial understanding, especially for quantitative tasks, compared to using relative depth maps.
> - The proposed hierarchical training approach enables VLMs to acquire comprehensive 3D spatial intelligence without compromising their general visual understanding capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated progress across various 2D vision-language tasks, including visual question answering (VQA), image captioning, visual grounding, and action recognition. However, extending these capabilities to comprehensive 3D spatial understanding presents challenges. Traditional VLMs often struggle with inferring intricate 3D structures, recognizing object properties and relationships in 3D space, and performing high-level spatial reasoning from standard 2D visual inputs.

Existing efforts to imbue VLMs with 3D reasoning typically involve supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) on spatially oriented VQA tasks. Despite these advancements, two primary limitations persist in the current research landscape. First, a unified and systematic task design that holistically captures the diverse cognitive levels of 3D spatial intelligence, from basic perception to abstract reasoning, is largely absent. The relationships and dependencies between different spatial reasoning skills are not well-defined or systematically explored. Second, the acquisition of large-scale, diverse, and accurately 3D-grounded data remains a significant hurdle. Datasets with explicit 3D annotations are often restricted to specific environments, such as indoor scenes, while large-scale web-scraped data typically lacks explicit 3D supervision, thereby limiting its utility for robust 3D spatial training. Although some prior studies have explored creating spatial reasoning datasets from in-the-wild images, their task-level coverage has been limited.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a structured hierarchical framework for 3D spatial understanding and developing an automated data generation pipeline to construct a large-scale, diverse dataset spanning these hierarchical levels. Furthermore, it introduces an RGB-D VLM that integrates metric-scale point maps to enhance spatial grounding, thereby contributing a comprehensive approach to developing and analyzing 3D spatial intelligence in VLMs. The work aims to provide both a conceptual framework and practical tools for advancing VLMs' capabilities beyond 2D perception into robust 3D spatial reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to equip vision-language models (VLMs) with comprehensive human-like 3D spatial intelligence, enabling them to interpret 3D structures and relationships from 2D visual observations and perform high-level spatial reasoning. The authors are motivated by several challenges inherent in extending VLMs from 2D to 3D understanding.

One key motivation stems from the perceived lack of a unified and systematic approach to defining and cultivating 3D spatial intelligence across multiple cognitive levels. While many studies address individual aspects of spatial reasoning, there has been an absence of a principled hierarchical framework that systematically decomposes spatial understanding into progressively complex tasks. The authors aim to conceptualize spatial intelligence as a continuum, ranging from fundamental geometric perception to intricate abstract reasoning, and to investigate the interdependencies between these cognitive levels. This structured approach is intended to clarify how different spatial skills build upon one another and how their collective development contributes to holistic spatial intelligence.

A second significant motivation is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and 3D-grounded training data. Existing datasets with 3D annotations are often limited in scale or restricted to specific environments (e.g., indoor scenes), while vast quantities of web data lack explicit 3D supervision, making them insufficient for robust VLM training. This data bottleneck hinders the ability of VLMs to learn generalizable 3D spatial reasoning capabilities from diverse real-world scenes. To overcome this, the authors aim to develop an automated data generation pipeline capable of synthesizing a massive corpus of diverse spatial VQA pairs, covering a broad spectrum of tasks and scenes from both in-the-wild and 3D-annotated images.

Furthermore, the research is motivated by the challenge of extracting precise 3D information solely from monocular 2D images, which can be ambiguous for spatial inference. To enhance spatial grounding, the authors seek to explore the integration of explicit 3D cues, specifically metric-scale depth information, as an auxiliary input to VLMs. This is proposed as an improvement over approaches that rely solely on relative depth or pure RGB inputs, with the goal of providing richer and more accurate 3D context for spatial reasoning.

In pursuit of these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **To formulate a principled hierarchical framework** that decomposes 3D spatial understanding into four progressively complex levels: Basic Geometric Perception (Level 0), Object-level Spatial Understanding (Level 1), Inter-object Relational Understanding (Level 2), and Abstract Spatial Reasoning (Level 3).
2. **To construct a large-scale and diverse 3D spatial VQA dataset** by developing an automated pipeline that can process millions of images and objects to generate billions of question-answering pairs across the defined hierarchical tasks.
3. **To develop an RGB-D VLM architecture** that effectively incorporates metric-scale 3D point maps as auxiliary inputs, thereby enhancing its spatial reasoning capabilities.
4. **To achieve state-of-the-art performance** on a wide array of existing and custom spatial understanding and reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating the comprehensive and generalizable spatial intelligence acquired by the proposed model.
5. **To analyze and uncover the correlations and dependencies** among the different hierarchical task levels, providing empirical insights that can inform the design of future training strategies for 3D spatially intelligent VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is structured around a hierarchical framework for 3D spatial understanding, an automated pipeline for generating large-scale spatial VQA data, and a specifically designed RGB-D VLM architecture for fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical 3D Spatial Understanding

The authors define a four-level hierarchy for spatial understanding, designed to progressively increase in complexity:

- **Level 0: Basic Geometric Perception**: Focuses on inferring fundamental 3D geometry from 2D visual inputs. Tasks include:
 - (i) **Pixel-wise 3D point querying**: Predicting metric-scale 3D coordinates (x, y, z) for a given 2D pixel in the camera coordinate system.
 - (ii) **Pairwise depth ordering**: Determining the relative depth order between two specified 2D image coordinates.

- **Level 1: Object-level Spatial Understanding**: Integrates semantic understanding with 3D localization of individual objects. Tasks involve:
 - (i) **Object localization**: Predicting the 3D bounding box (AABB) of an object in camera coordinates, given a linguistic or visual reference.
 - (ii) **Object orientation estimation**: Describing an object's yaw direction (e.g., "front," "back," "left," "right").
 - (iii) **Object size estimation**: Estimating an object's physical dimensions (width, height, depth).

- **Level 2: Inter-object Relational Understanding**: Focuses on reasoning about the relationships between multiple objects in a scene. Tasks include:
 - (i) **Relative direction estimation**: Predicting the qualitative (e.g., "left/right," "above/below") or precise 3D direction vector between two objects.
 - (ii) **Relative distance estimation**: Quantitatively predicting various distance metrics between objects (Euclidean, horizontal, vertical, depth-wise).
 - (iii) **Relational comparison**: Comparing two or more objects based on shared attributes like position, size, or orientation (e.g., identifying the nearest/farthest, smallest/largest, or ordering objects).

- **Level 3: Abstract Spatial Reasoning**: Targets high-order inference requiring generalization, mental simulation, and multi-step problem-solving. Tasks include:
 - (i) **Perspective taking**: Inferring relative object directions and distances from an imagined observer's or object's viewpoint.
 - (ii) **Spatial object counting**: Identifying and enumerating objects that satisfy specific spatial relationship constraints relative to a reference object.
 - (iii) **Spatial problem solving**: Formulating and solving complex spatial problems that require translating high-level objectives into quantifiable spatial properties and performing multi-step reasoning and computation.

#### 3.2. Spatial VQA Data Construction

An automated pipeline was developed to generate hierarchical spatial VQA data, comprising three main stages:

- **1. Spatial Information Estimation**:
 - **Metric-scale 3D point maps** are generated for 2D images using the MoGe-2 monocular geometry estimator.
 - **Object localization** for unannotated images involves sequentially applying RAM for categorization, GroundingDINO for 2D bounding boxes, and SAM for segmentation masks. For images with existing 2D bounding boxes, SAM is directly prompted with these boxes.
 - **Object-level point clouds** are derived by combining the masks with the 3D point map, which are then used to compute 3D bounding boxes and sizes. DBSCAN clustering is applied to refine object-level point clouds.
 - **Object orientations** are estimated using OrientAnythingv2.
 - A **gravity-aligned world coordinate system** (y-axis parallel to gravity) is established using Perspective Fields to compute relative spatial relationships.
 - When ground-truth 3D annotations are available (e.g., from CA-1M), this entire estimation pipeline is bypassed.

- **2. Textual Reference Generation**:
 - Comprehensive object descriptions are generated using a combination of Describe Anything, Qwen2.5-VL, and Qwen3-VL.
 - A **verification process** is implemented to enhance accuracy: a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) is prompted to ground each generated description, and the correspondence between the predicted and original bounding box is evaluated using IoU. References with an IoU below a threshold or that are ambiguous (matching multiple objects) are discarded. If no generated reference passes, a fallback mechanism uses the object's class label with a visual cue.
 - For CA-1M data, category labels are assigned to 3D bounding boxes using RAM, GroundingDINO, and Qwen2.5-VL, followed by Hungarian matching. Spatial references (e.g., "second farthest chair," "largest pillow") are also generated to disambiguate objects within the same category.

- **3. Task-Oriented QA Synthesis**:
 - QA instances are generated following the four-level hierarchical taxonomy.
 - To promote diversity, most tasks are generated in three formats: free-form question answering, multiple-choice questions (MCQs), and true/false statements.
 - For Level-3 spatial problem-solving tasks, GPT (specifically GPT-4.1) is prompted with image, spatial information, and textual references to formulate complex, multi-step reasoning questions. This involves a two-stage prompting process: a "teacher model" generates detailed reasoning traces and structured spatial facts using privileged 3D information, followed by a "student model" generating a simplified trace from these facts.

- **Data Sources**: The training dataset is constructed from three primary sources:
 - **KosMos-2**: A subset of COYO-700M, contributing 3.8 million filtered in-the-wild images.
 - **Objects365**: Provides 1 million in-the-wild images with ground-truth object bounding boxes.
 - **CA-1M**: Samples 200,000 images from 2 million indoor video frames with dense point clouds and 3D boxes.
 - The total curated dataset comprises approximately 5 million images, 45 million objects, and over 2 billion QA pairs. General VQA data from LLaVA-Next is also incorporated during supervised fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. Spatial VLM Fine-tuning

- **Model Architecture**: The VLM is built upon PaliGemma-2, which combines a SigLIP visual encoder with a Gemma-2 language model.
 - It is augmented with a metric-scale 3D point map (X ∈ R^(H×W×4)), where the first three channels store 3D coordinates (x, y, z) and the fourth channel is a binary validity mask.
 - The point map undergoes sinusoidal positional encoding and a learnable Conv2D patchify layer to produce a feature map of compatible spatial dimensions with the RGB features from the visual encoder.
 - These two feature maps (RGB and point map) are concatenated along the feature dimension and projected linearly to generate fused tokens, which replace the original visual input tokens for the language model.
 - Textual information is then concatenated, and the language model autoregressively generates the answer.
 - By default, point maps are estimated by MoGe-2; however, ground-truth point maps can be used when available, with sparse GT depth maps densified using Prior Depth Anything.

- **Training Objective**: Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is employed, minimizing the cross-entropy loss for token generation. All QA pairs are converted into text descriptions for unified training. The visual encoder is frozen, while the point map patchify layer, fused-token projector, and the LLM are fine-tuned end-to-end. Training combines spatial VQA data with general VQA data from LLaVA-Next, using a sampling ratio of 1:7 (general:spatial) to retain general-purpose abilities. The model is trained for 70,000 iterations with a batch size of 256, utilizing the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 2 × 10^(-5).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations across multiple spatial and general VQA benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed hierarchical framework, data generation, and model architecture.

#### 4.1. Spatial Understanding Evaluation

The model was evaluated on public spatial reasoning benchmarks (SpatialRGPT, QSpatial, CV-Bench, EmbSpatial, 3DSRBench, RoboSpatial) and a custom benchmark designed to cover all four hierarchical levels.

- **Quantitative VQA (Levels 1 and 2):** On benchmarks requiring numerical predictions (Table 1), the proposed HiSpatial-3B model, utilizing RGB-XYZ input (metric-scale point map), demonstrated performance improvements over proprietary large models such as GPT-4o, GPT-5, and Gemini-2.5-Pro, as well as existing open-source and specialized spatial models. For instance, HiSpatial-3B achieved an average accuracy of 79.28% on SpatialRGPT-Quantitative and 85.16% on QSpatial-Bench. A variant, HiSpatial-3B-RGB (without explicit point map input), also surpassed several general VLMs and achieved performance comparable to some specialized models that use depth inputs. When ground-truth point maps were provided (HiSpatial-3B\*), performance saw further improvements, suggesting benefits for applications with depth sensors.

- **Qualitative VQA (Levels 1-3):** On benchmarks evaluating qualitative spatial understanding and reasoning (Table 2), HiSpatial-3B (RGB-XYZ) achieved state-of-the-art results across EmbSpatial (80.71%), RoboSpatial (86.18%), CV-Bench-3D (97.58%), and 3DSRBench (63.81%). These results indicate that the model developed comprehensive spatial understanding, outperforming both proprietary and specialist models. Substantial gains were observed compared to the base model, PaliGemma2-3B, underscoring the efficacy of the spatial data and training approach.

- **Custom Spatial VQA Benchmark (Levels 1-3):** The custom benchmark (Table 3) further confirmed the broad capabilities of HiSpatial-3B. It achieved 92.18% for Object-to-Camera Distance (Level 1), 67.21% for Object Direction (Level 2), and 47.44% for Spatial Problem Solving (Level 3), significantly surpassing GPT-5 and RoboRefer-8B-SFT on these tasks. The Level 3 problem-solving task, which requires multi-step reasoning, highlights the model's ability to tackle complex spatial challenges.

#### 4.2. General VQA Evaluation

To assess whether spatial fine-tuning compromised general VQA capabilities, the model was evaluated on MMBench, POPE, SEED, and RealWorldQA (Table 4). HiSpatial-3B not only retained but generally surpassed the performance of its base model, PaliGemma2-3B, across these general benchmarks. This outcome suggests that the spatial supervised fine-tuning, despite constituting 88% of the training data mix, did not negatively impact general visual understanding, aligning with prior research findings.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Inter-level Task Dependencies (Table 5):** Ablation studies explored the impact of removing lower-level tasks on higher-level performance.
 - Removing tasks from Levels 0 and 1 resulted in a performance decrease on Level 2 tasks (e.g., -1.52% average) and Level 3 tasks (e.g., -8.14% average). This suggests that lower-level tasks, even when not explicitly part of a chain-of-thought, contribute to an implicit capture of richer spatial information that benefits higher-level reasoning.
 - Removing tasks from Levels 1 and 2 led to a more substantial performance degradation on Level 3 tasks (e.g., -14.51% average). This indicates that Level 3 tasks, being more complex, rely more directly on the specific skills developed in Levels 1 and 2. The transfer of knowledge from these intermediate levels is crucial, especially given the comparatively smaller training data for Level 3. The analysis provides empirical evidence for a clear hierarchical dependency, where lower-level tasks enhance the emergence of higher-level spatial intelligence.

- **Influence of Auxiliary 3D Input (Table 6):** The study compared the use of metric-scale point maps against relative depth maps.
 - Incorporating metric-scale point maps (RGB+XYZ) demonstrated greater effectiveness in enhancing spatial understanding, particularly for quantitative tasks, compared to using relative depth maps (RGB+Relative Depth).
 - When ground-truth point maps were utilized, further performance improvements were observed, especially in quantitative evaluations. This highlights the value of precise metric-scale 3D information and indicates potential for applications involving depth sensors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of vision-language models by addressing several challenges in achieving robust 3D spatial understanding.

The proposed **hierarchical framework** for 3D spatial intelligence, decomposing it into four progressively complex levels, offers a structured approach to defining and evaluating spatial reasoning capabilities. This taxonomy can serve as a conceptual foundation for guiding future research in designing VLM architectures and training curricula focused on comprehensive spatial understanding.

The development of an **automated data generation pipeline** is significant as it overcomes a major bottleneck in acquiring large-scale, diverse, and 3D-grounded datasets. By synthesizing over two billion hierarchical spatial VQA pairs from approximately five million images, the work provides a substantial resource that can enable more effective supervised fine-tuning of VLMs for complex 3D tasks. This pipeline also offers a generalizable method for expanding spatial datasets, potentially reducing reliance on manual annotation.

The **RGB-D VLM architecture**, integrating metric-scale 3D point maps as auxiliary inputs, demonstrates an effective method for enhancing spatial grounding. The empirical results indicate that metric-scale 3D information is more beneficial than relative depth, offering a practical pathway for improving VLM performance in scenarios where depth data is available, such as in embodied AI and robotics. The model's ability to achieve state-of-the-art results across diverse spatial benchmarks, often outperforming larger proprietary models, suggests an efficient and effective approach to imparting spatial intelligence.

The **analysis of inter-level task dependencies** provides valuable insights into how different cognitive levels of spatial understanding interact during learning. The finding that lower-level geometric and object-centric tasks facilitate the development of higher-level relational and abstract reasoning skills offers guidance for designing more principled and progressive training strategies for VLMs. This understanding can lead to more efficient training protocols that optimize the learning process for complex spatial abilities.

The combined contributions of this work have potential impact across several application domains. Enhanced 3D spatial understanding in VLMs is critical for advancements in **embodied AI and robotics**, enabling agents to navigate, manipulate objects, and interact with the physical world more effectively. It could also benefit **augmented reality (AR)** applications by providing more accurate spatial reasoning for virtual object placement and interaction. Furthermore, the demonstrated ability to maintain and even improve general VQA performance while specializing in spatial tasks indicates that these advancements can be integrated without compromising the broad utility of VLMs.
