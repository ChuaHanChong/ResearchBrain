# Low-Rank Dynamics-Effective Latent Carriers for Counterfactual Rollout in Learned World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper defines and tests "dynamics-effective" hidden-state interventions in a recurrent world model with a 192-dimensional state: a compact, addressable, low-rank patch to the hidden state at one anchor step that lets the model autonomously continue a physically valid counterfactual rollout for 12 steps, with no further correction. A rank-4 carrier, built from SVD of factual-counterfactual hidden differences plus a ridge-regression coefficient map, meets this bar and replicates across independently trained checkpoints, while an analogous position-edit intervention fails specificity controls.

## Problem

- Prior probing and activation-steering work shows hidden directions correlate with physical variables or shift immediate outputs, but never tests whether the model can autonomously sustain a counterfactual future from that edit.
- Causal probing studies often lack the specificity controls (wrong-object, wrong-time, random equal-norm) needed to separate genuine dynamics-relevant edits from generic hidden-state sensitivity.
- Existing physical-representation studies in video encoders and RL world models establish decodability and one-step steerability but not whether an edit can be addressed from a request alone, without first observing the target counterfactual state.

## Method

- An operational ==dynamics-effective test== requiring a one-shot hidden patch at the anchor to be followed by a 12-step autonomous rollout satisfying direct-target, derived-quantity, downstream, preservation, and physical-validity criteria, with no teacher forcing.
- A **low-rank carrier** basis built via ==uncentered SVD== over 1024 factual-to-native-counterfactual hidden differences, paired with an addressable ==ridge-regression map== that predicts carrier coefficients purely from the factual state and requested edit, never the target hidden state.
- A preregistered ==rank-selection protocol== on a 3-checkpoint development panel across two trajectory strata, followed by frozen fresh-checkpoint replication, temporal transport tests (B1/B2), and a position-edit stress test used as a deliberate negative control.

## Results

- Rank **4** is the smallest tested rank passing both strata for at least 2 of 3 development checkpoints; rank 1 fails entirely and rank 2 passes only 1 of 3, with rank-4 cumulative variance capture at **0.802-0.866** across checkpoints.
- Fresh-checkpoint replication yields joint coverage of **0.867/0.914**, **1.000/0.922**, and **0.711/0.973** across the two strata for three independent models, while random equal-norm perturbations score **0 of 3** target passes.
- The position-edit stress test passes the raw rollout criterion in **3 of 3** models at all three tested amplitudes (0.05, 0.10, 0.20), but fails wrong-object specificity in **0 of 3** models at every amplitude, unlike the velocity carrier.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successful hidden-state editing or high decoding accuracy is not sufficient evidence of a functional latent state; the stronger test is whether the model autonomously sustains the edited future on its own.
> - A passing intervention rank reflects the smallest tested addressable interface for a bounded edit family, not the model's intrinsic state dimension or a closed low-dimensional reduced state.
> - Specificity controls like random, wrong-object, wrong-time, and no-patch conditions are necessary rather than optional, since raw outcome improvement can appear even under non-specific perturbations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of deep learning, specifically focusing on the internal mechanisms and latent representations of "world models." World models are a class of artificial intelligence systems designed to anticipate how an environment will evolve, allowing agents to learn policies by simulating future trajectories. This area has seen significant advancements, from early formulations by Ha and Schmidhuber (2018) to systems like PlaNet (Hafner et al. 2019), Dreamer (Hafner et al. 2023), and more recent, larger-scale models like Genie (Bruce et al. 2024) and V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al. 2025). A recurring theme in these developments is the evolving role of the latent state, shifting from a mere engineering convenience to a central scientific object for understanding internal information retention and its guidance of future predictions.

The paper connects with several specific lines of research:

- **Latent State Sufficiency:** Previous work, such as DeepMDP (Gelada et al. 2019) and action-sufficient state representations (Huang et al. 2022), established that latent states should be functionally judged by their support for tasks like prediction, control, or planning. More recent arguments (K.W. Kim 2026; Thorpe et al. 2026) emphasize that accurate prediction alone is insufficient for embodied reasoning, necessitating representations that preserve distinctions crucial for intervention and counterfactual reasoning. This paper directly addresses counterfactual reasoning as a functional requirement for latent states.
- **Interpretability and Causal Intervention:** The work builds on techniques for probing and causally editing neural representations. Early methods like TCAV (B. Kim et al. 2018) focused on readout, while later approaches like INLP (Ravfogel et al. 2020), Amnesic Probing (Elazar et al. 2021), and LEACE (Belrose et al. 2023) introduced subtractive interventions. Constructive edits, as seen in Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al. 2020) and Activation Engineering (Turner et al. 2023), aim to steer model outputs. Concepts like "completeness and selectivity" for causal interventions (Canby et al. 2025) are particularly relevant, as this paper applies stricter downstream criteria for evaluating interventions in world models.
- **Physical Reasoning and Object-Centered Dynamics:** The research draws from the extensive literature on how models represent and predict physical phenomena. Interaction Networks (Battaglia et al. 2016) and subsequent object-centric models (Wu et al. 2022; Villar-Corrales et al. 2023; Song et al. 2025) have explored structured representations for physical prediction. Benchmarks such as IntPhys (Riochet et al. 2018), CLEVRER (Yi et al. 2019), and Physion (Bear et al. 2021) assess behavioral physical competence. Recent studies (Joseph et al. 2026; Zhang 2026; Alam 2026) have begun to directly inspect physical representations within world and video models, showing that physical information can be readable, geometrically organized, distributed, and causally steerable. This paper extends this by asking whether an edit can lead to a *sustained, autonomous* counterfactual rollout, going beyond immediate output changes.

The key distinction of this work from prior efforts is its focus on a "dynamics-effective" intervention, where a single, limited hidden-state change must initiate an *autonomous* and *physically valid* counterfactual rollout over multiple steps, without subsequent correction or observation, a stronger criterion than mere instantaneous changes or plausibility scores.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether a small, directly addressable change to a world model's hidden state can induce a specific counterfactual trajectory that the model then autonomously sustains. This goes beyond simply predicting the future or identifying decodable information within the hidden state.

The motivation stems from the limitations of existing world model evaluation and interpretability paradigms:
- **Beyond Prediction Accuracy:** While modern world models excel at anticipating future states, it remains unclear which specific parts of their hidden state genuinely drive these predictions. The ability to predict a future does not inherently reveal how the model's internal state functions in guiding that future.
- **Functional Utility of Latent States:** For an agent, an internal state is useful if it can support the model's subsequent computations and predictions. This paper argues that merely being able to read information from a representation, or even editing it to change an immediate output, is insufficient. A stronger test is required: can an intervention place the model in a new state from which it can generate a coherent, correct future on its own?
- **Counterfactual Reasoning in Physical Systems:** Physical dynamics present a demanding context for this question. Two physical situations might appear similar under passive observation but respond differently to intervention. A functionally useful world model should capture these distinctions. The paper aims to test if a model's internal state encodes physical quantities in a way that allows them to be causally manipulated to produce intended counterfactual physics.
- **Operationalizing "Dynamics-Effective" Intervention:** The research seeks to establish an operational definition for a "dynamics-effective" hidden-state intervention. This implies that a one-shot hidden patch must result in an autonomous, physically valid counterfactual rollout that matches a simulator-grounded reference, while satisfying multiple, stringent evaluation criteria and controls.
- **Identifying Low-Rank, Addressable Interfaces:** A specific motivation is to determine if the necessary hidden-state change for a local physical counterfactual lies within a compact, low-dimensional subspace, termed a "carrier." Furthermore, this carrier must be "addressable"—meaning the coefficients for the hidden perturbation can be predicted directly from the factual physical state and the requested edit, without requiring knowledge of the actual counterfactual hidden state. This would provide a compact and actionable interface for steering the model's dynamics.
- **Defining the Evidence Standard:** Through a "position-edit stress test," the paper aims to provide a negative contrast, demonstrating that not all physically meaningful and editable quantities automatically yield evidence for a dynamics-effective latent carrier. This helps define the rigorous evidence standard for such claims.

In summary, the key objectives are to establish a robust framework for testing dynamics-effective hidden-state interventions, identify a compact and addressable latent carrier for specific physical edits (velocity changes), evaluate its temporal stability, and clarify the necessary conditions for claiming such an interface exists, distinguishing it from simpler forms of interpretability or steering.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a structured, multi-stage methodology to construct, test, and validate dynamics-effective latent carriers in a learned world model.

**Environment and World Model:**
- **Environment:** A controlled 2D collision environment featuring two circular objects (A and B) moving in a bounded arena. The simulator is deterministic.
- **Observations:** The world model receives numeric object-state observations (position and velocity for each object) with small observation noise.
- **World Model Architecture:** A recurrent world model with a 192-dimensional hidden state (composed of object-specific and global recurrent components). It is a Deterministic Belief-like Model with 496,200 trainable parameters. The intervention point is post-recurrent update and post-observation assimilation at the anchor time.
- **Training:** The model is trained on 5,120 units (episodes) with a training objective combining rollout mean-squared error with auxiliary anchor-state, filtering-state, relative-state, and delta-velocity losses. It does not explicitly supervise low-rank carriers or use contact labels.

**Counterfactual Construction:**
- **Factual-Counterfactual Pairs:** Each pair shares an identical history before an "anchor" time. At the anchor, a specific local physical edit is applied to one object.
- **Local Velocity Edit Family:** The primary intervention involves changing exactly one velocity component (x or y) of exactly one object (A or B) by a specified signed magnitude. All positions and other primitive velocity components remain unchanged at that instant.
- **Simulator Ground Truth:** After the edit, the deterministic simulator generates the "simulator-grounded counterfactual trajectory" as the reference.

**Evaluation of Model Adequacy (G0-G2):**
Prior to low-rank intervention, the study verifies the model's native capacity and the intervention interface:
- **G0 (Factual Route):** Model predicts autonomously from the factual anchor hidden state.
- **G1 (Native Counterfactual Route):** The model is explicitly given the fully edited anchor observation, producing a "native counterfactual hidden state," and then rolls out autonomously. This tests if the model *can* represent and propagate the counterfactual.
- **G2 (Full-Hidden Equivalence):** The *complete* native counterfactual hidden difference (`h^CF - h^F`) is added to the factual hidden state (`h^F`). This patched state should exactly reproduce the native counterfactual hidden state (`h^CF`) and its rollout, verifying the hidden-state patching mechanism itself.

**Low-Rank Carrier Construction:**
- **Hidden Difference Matrix (D):** From a dedicated `carrier-fit` data split (1,024 units), the difference between native counterfactual and factual hidden states (`Δh_i`) is computed for each unit. These 192-dimensional vectors are stacked as rows to form the matrix `D` (1024x192).
- **Uncentered SVD:** Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is performed on `D` (`D = LΣV^T`). The first `r` columns of `V` form the orthonormal candidate carrier basis `U_r` (192 x `r`). This basis is checkpoint-specific.
- **Oracle Coefficients:** For diagnostic purposes, `c_i^oracle = U_r^T Δh_i` represents the true hidden difference projected onto the rank-`r` carrier. This is a privileged, capacity-testing metric.

**Addressable Coefficient Operator:**
- **Input Encoding:** A 12-dimensional input vector `x_i` is constructed by concatenating the 4-component requested edit `e_i` (one non-zero slot) and the 8-component factual primitive state `s_t,i^F`.
- **Standardization:** Input features are standardized using means and standard deviations computed *only* on the `carrier-fit` split.
- **Ridge Regression:** An affine ridge regression map (`λ = 10^-4`) is learned to predict the `r`-dimensional oracle coefficients (`c_i^oracle`) from the standardized input `x_i`. This map (`g`) is the *addressable* component, as it does not require the true counterfactual hidden state at evaluation.

**One-Shot Autonomous Rollout:**
- **Hidden State Patch:** At evaluation time, the predicted coefficients `ĉ` are used to compute a hidden perturbation `Δĥ = U_r * ĉ`. This is added once to the factual anchor hidden state: `h_t^* = h_t^F + Δĥ`.
- **Autonomous Rollout:** From `h_t^*`, the model is released for 12 future transitions without any further observations, teacher forcing, or corrections.

**Evaluation Criteria and Metrics (Appendices F):**
- **Scientific Metrics (M1-M5):** Evaluate the decoded anchor state (M1: direct velocity fidelity, M2: derived state fidelity, M4: unaffected primitive preservation) and the 12-step autonomous future (M3: overall RMSE, M5: physical law and geometry consistency like simulator replay, overlap, arena bounds, displacement). Metrics are normalized by frozen scales.
- **Eligibility Gates (M6E, M7):** Ensure numerical stability (finite values, explosion bounds) and confirm autonomous rollout structure (e.g., zero future observations, no teacher forcing).
- **Strata:** Evaluations are separated into S1 (no contact in factual or counterfactual rollout) and S2 (factual contact, edit causes significant difference in future trajectory).
- **Unit and Cell Pass Criteria:** A unit passes if all M1-M5 are below thresholds and M6E/M7 pass. A cell (all units in a stratum for a checkpoint) passes if at least 80% of units pass (`Coverage >= 0.8`), all units meet eligibility, median metrics are within thresholds, and controls pass.

**Controls and Rank Selection:**
- **Development Panel:** Three independently trained models (`development models 1-3`) are used for method development and rank selection.
- **Rank Grid:** R = {1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 32} is the primary grid.
- **Selection Rule:** The *smallest tested rank* that passes both S1 and S2 for at least two of the three development checkpoints is selected.
- **Specificity Controls (Appendix G):** Include sham edits (zero change), rank-zero (no patch), random equal-norm perturbations, wrong-object edits, wrong-time edits, leakage checks, and identity/order controls. These verify that the effect is specific, targeted, and not due to generic perturbations or artifacts.

**Fresh Replication:**
- After rank selection and freezing all procedures, the entire process is repeated on three *new, independently trained* checkpoints (`fresh replication models 1-3`). For each, carrier basis and ridge map are refitted using *its own* `carrier-fit` split, ensuring independent validation.

**Temporal Tests:**
- **B1 (Per-Anchor Existence):** Assesses if a rank-4 carrier can be established independently at different anchor times (t=5, 6, 7, 8, 9) by refitting for each.
- **B2 (t=7 Carrier Transport):** Tests if the *exact* rank-4 carrier and addressable operator learned at t=7 can be reused without refitting at nearby anchors (t=5, 6, 8, 9).

**Position-Edit Stress Test:**
- A development-only diagnostic that changes one position coordinate (x or y) of one object while keeping velocities fixed. It uses the same evaluation logic to contrast with the velocity edits, checking if position edits achieve the same "dynamics-effective" standard.

**Simultaneous Velocity Edits:**
- A development-only extension explores changing both velocity components of the same object simultaneously. This probes the compositional addressability of the carrier.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the dynamics-effective latent carriers for velocity edits and identified boundaries for other intervention types.

**Model Native Counterfactual Adequacy:**
- All three development checkpoints and all three fresh replication checkpoints successfully passed the G0 (factual), G1 (native counterfactual), and G2 (full-hidden equivalence) tests across both S1 (non-contact) and S2 (future-contact) strata. This established that the world model inherently possessed the capacity to represent and propagate the requested counterfactual futures when fully informed, and that the hidden-state patching interface functioned correctly to reproduce these native counterfactual states.

**Rank Selection for Velocity Edits:**
- On the development panel, a clear transition in performance was observed across tested ranks. Rank 1 failed to meet the passing criteria for both strata in any of the three development checkpoints, and Rank 2 passed for only one checkpoint.
- Rank 4 was the smallest tested rank where all three development checkpoints passed both the S1 and S2 strata, including mandatory controls. Higher ranks (8, 12, etc.) also passed for all three checkpoints.
- According to the preregistered rule (smallest tested rank passing both strata for at least two of three checkpoints), rank 4 was selected.
- Diagnostic "oracle-capacity" analysis, which uses privileged knowledge of the true hidden difference, also indicated rank 4 as the smallest passing rank, suggesting no practical gap between the subspace's capacity and the addressable operator's ability to utilize it for rank selection.
- Geometrically, rank 4 captured between 80.2% and 86.6% of the cumulative uncentered second-moment energy of the hidden-difference matrix across the development checkpoints. However, rank selection was based on autonomous rollout success rather than this spectral property alone.

**Fresh-Checkpoint Replication (Frozen Rank-4 Procedure):**
- The frozen rank-4 procedure, with its specific carrier basis and addressable operator refitted for each new checkpoint, successfully replicated at the panel level. Two of the three independently trained fresh models (Fresh Model 1 and 2) passed both S1 and S2 strata, meeting the preregistered "2-of-3" rule. Fresh Model 3 passed S2 but failed S1 due to insufficient joint unit-level coverage (71.1%, below the 0.80 requirement), despite meeting median thresholds and eligibility checks.
- All mandated specificity controls (sham, rank-zero, random equal-norm perturbation, wrong-object, wrong-time, leakage, identity/order checks) behaved as expected across the fresh panel. The random equal-norm perturbation consistently failed to reproduce the target counterfactual, demonstrating that the observed effect was not attributable to generic hidden-state perturbations.

**Temporal Robustness of the Rank-4 Carrier:**
- **B1 (Per-Anchor Existence):** A rank-4 addressable carrier could be independently established (fitted separately for each anchor) at every tested anchor time (t=5, 6, 7, 8, and 9). Each anchor met the "2-of-3" fresh-model passing criterion, indicating the consistent presence of low-rank dynamics-effective structure across this time window.
- **B2 (t=7 Carrier Transport):** The exact rank-4 carrier and addressable operator learned at t=7 could be directly transported and effectively reused at nearby anchors (t=5, 6, 8, and 9) without refitting. All four transport targets again met the "2-of-3" fresh-model criterion. This suggests that the intervention coordinates are temporally stable within the tested window.

**Position-Edit Stress Test (Negative Contrast):**
- When attempting to edit an object's position directly, raw performance metrics showed apparent success: the intended position patch, a no-patch condition, and a random equal-norm perturbation all passed the raw rollout criteria for all three development models at every tested amplitude.
- However, the specificity controls revealed a failure. The intended position patch failed the "wrong-object" test in every development model and did not satisfy the full specificity criterion. This indicated that the intervention did not reliably act on the intended object, and the raw improvement was not specific to the learned patch. The study concluded that successful editing and raw outcome improvement alone are insufficient; an intervention must demonstrate target specificity and not be reproducible by control conditions to be deemed dynamics-effective.

**Simultaneous Velocity Edits (Development-Only Extension):**
- For simultaneous changes to both velocity components of the same object, the world model natively supported these joint counterfactuals, and the frozen rank-4 carrier possessed sufficient capacity to represent them. The original affine addressable operator also achieved satisfactory absolute joint-rollout performance.
- Nevertheless, these joint interventions did not achieve the same level of specificity as the single-component velocity edits, particularly in interaction-sensitive S2 strata. Controls (no-patch, random, wrong-object, matched wrong-vector) were not adequately rejected. This indicates a boundary to the current result, suggesting that while the carrier has the capacity for joint edits, simple addressable maps may not achieve equally specific compositional control for interaction-sensitive multi-component changes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of learned world models by establishing a rigorous operational framework for evaluating "dynamics-effective" latent interventions. Its findings have several implications for the fields of representation learning, interpretability, and the development of intelligent agents.

**Functional, Not Semantic, Notion of Effective State:**
The work emphasizes a functional definition of an effective latent state. It differentiates between information that is merely present or decodable from a hidden representation, and information that is actively used by the model's dynamics to sustain a future trajectory. The term "dynamics-effective" is introduced to describe interventions that result in a sustained, target-specific change in the model's autonomous future computation, rather than just an immediate readout change. This aligns with broader movements in latent-state design that prioritize functional sufficiency (e.g., for counterfactual reasoning) over semantic alignment with human-interpretable variables.

**Compact Intervention Interface, Not a Reduced World Model:**
The identification of a rank-4 addressable latent carrier for local velocity edits demonstrates that a small set of latent directions can reliably redirect the model's complex future dynamics. This provides a compact "intervention interface" but is explicitly noted as *not* a "closed low-dimensional state." The model continues to evolve its full 192-dimensional hidden state after the one-shot patch. This distinction is significant: it suggests that while a low-dimensional subspace can be sufficient to *initiate* a specific counterfactual, it does not necessarily imply a full reduction of the model's state space to that dimension, or that these dimensions correspond to named physical variables. The finding offers a practical handle for steering specific aspects of a world model's behavior without requiring a complete understanding or decomposition of its entire internal state.

**Importance of Rigorous Controls and Specificity:**
The "position-edit stress test" serves as a crucial negative contrast, demonstrating that apparent improvements in rollout metrics are insufficient to claim a dynamics-effective intervention if those improvements can also be reproduced by no-patch, random, or non-specific control conditions. This highlights the necessity of comprehensive specificity controls in evaluating latent-space interventions. The research establishes that a dynamics-effective edit must genuinely move the model towards the intended counterfactual in a target-specific manner, distinguishing it from generic hidden-state sensitivity. This rigorous evidentiary standard is an important contribution to interpretability research.

**Local, Bounded, and Temporally Stable Structure:**
The findings underscore that the identified dynamics-effective structure is local and bounded, specific to the controlled environment, the recurrent model family, and the tested family of local velocity edits within a particular time window. However, the temporal tests show that this structure is not ephemeral; the ability to independently establish rank-4 carriers at various anchors (B1) and, more strongly, to reuse the exact t=7 carrier and operator at nearby times (B2) suggests a degree of temporal stability for these intervention coordinates within a given checkpoint. This indicates that such functional interfaces can persist over relevant operational horizons.

**Diagnostic for World Model Development:**
The methodology proposed, focusing on dynamics-effective interventions, can serve as a valuable diagnostic tool for evaluating and comparing world models. Models exhibiting similar external performance (e.g., predictive accuracy) might differ significantly in their internal organization and the ease with which their latent states can be controllably manipulated to generate counterfactuals. A model that requires large, non-specific changes for simple counterfactuals might be considered more brittle or entangled. This type of analysis could inform the design of future training objectives that specifically encourage properties like counterfactual consistency, target specificity, temporal stability, or compact intervention interfaces, potentially leading to more robust and steerable world models.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations: the controlled 2D, two-object environment, deterministic simulator, numeric observations, and zero-action setting restrict the generalizability to raw-video world models, action-conditioned agents, or real-world systems. The claim itself is narrow, focused on local velocity edits. Future work is suggested to extend this analysis to different architectures, larger hidden dimensions, partially observed settings, and action-conditioned models, and to investigate whether encouraging these internal properties through training objectives ultimately improves predictive, planning, or control performance in more complex scenarios. The concept of "dynamical closure"—where a compact representation could update itself without relying on the full recurrent state—is also proposed as a more advanced next step.
