# Developing Vision-Language-Action Model from Egocentric Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Kyoto University, NII, and Sony Interactive Entertainment developed a method to pre-train Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using raw egocentric videos. Their approach automatically extracts explicit 6DoF object manipulation trajectories without requiring auxiliary labels, creating a scalable dataset that enables VLA models to achieve performance comparable to those trained on costly real-robot data.

## Problem

- Training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics demands massive datasets, but human teleoperation is prohibitively costly and labor-intensive, limiting data scalability.
- Prior attempts to use egocentric videos for robot learning either require dense auxiliary annotations (e.g., hand poses, depth) or learn implicit action representations that lack the fine-grained detail for complex manipulation.
- Robot policies trained on real-world egocentric data can face a visual domain gap when applied in simplified simulated environments, leading to performance degradation.

## Method

- Utilized and extended the EgoScaler framework to automatically extract explicit 6DoF object manipulation trajectories (position and rotation) directly from raw egocentric videos.
- Constructed a large-scale pre-training dataset by applying EgoScaler to diverse egocentric video resources, including Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, HD-EPIC, and Nymeria.
- Implemented a rigorous rule-based data curation process (e.g., travel distance threshold, background track similarity, smoothing) to filter out noisy or incomplete trajectories and ensure data quality.

## Results

- VLA models pre-trained on the egocentric video-derived dataset achieved an average of **55%** success on real-robot tasks, substantially outperforming models trained from scratch (**0%** success) and prior implicit pre-training methods like LAPA (38-**45%** success).
- The performance of models pre-trained on the egocentric dataset (21 successful rollouts out of 40) was competitive with or superior to models pre-trained on existing real-robot datasets such as BC-Z (**19/40**) and BridgeData V2 (**17/40**) in real-robot evaluations.
- Combining the egocentric-derived dataset with existing real-robot data (BridgeData V2) led to synergistic improvements, achieving **27/40** successful rollouts, demonstrating its complementary value for building multi-embodiment VLA models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 6DoF object manipulation trajectories extracted from raw egocentric videos provide more robust and informative supervision for VLA models than implicit latent action representations, especially for complex manipulation tasks.
> - Large-scale egocentric video datasets, when effectively processed and curated, offer a scalable and cost-effective alternative to human teleoperation for VLA pre-training data acquisition.
> - Rigorous data curation, including automatic filtering based on movement characteristics, is crucial for mitigating inaccuracies from automated trajectory extraction and enhancing the utility of the derived datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical bottleneck in the rapidly evolving field of robotics and artificial intelligence: the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale datasets for training general-purpose Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLAs are designed to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and execute diverse tasks across various environments, a cornerstone for achieving truly generalist robots.

Traditionally, VLA models are pre-trained on massive datasets collected through human teleoperation, where experts manually control robots to perform tasks. While effective, this process is prohibitively costly, labor-intensive, and inherently limits data scalability. This reliance on teleoperation has become a major impediment to progress toward more capable and general-purpose robotic foundation models, such as RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment (OXE), Octo, OpenVLA, π₀, and others.

Recognizing this data scarcity problem, researchers have explored alternative data sources. One promising direction is leveraging first-person perspective (egocentric) videos of humans performing everyday tasks. The proliferation of AR/VR devices and smart glasses, means these videos are becoming increasingly abundant and diverse, inherently capturing close-range human-object interactions with rich motion cues.

However, prior attempts to use egocentric videos for robot learning have faced their own challenges:
- **Reliance on auxiliary annotations:** Studies like EgoMimic and EgoVLA successfully use egocentric recordings but require dense auxiliary data such as hand poses, depth information, or action start/end timestamps. Obtaining such data necessitates specialized hardware (multi-camera systems, depth sensors) or extensive manual annotation, limiting scalability.
- **Lack of fine-grained motion cues:** Approaches like LAPA avoid auxiliary labels by learning latent action representations from raw egocentric videos. While scalable, these latent representations often struggle to capture fine-grained manipulation details, performing well on simple actions (e.g., pushing) but falling short on complex tasks (e.g., pick-and-place).
- **Sim-to-real gap:** Robot policies trained on visually rich real-world egocentric data can underperform in controlled environments, especially simulators, due to simplified visual systems.

This paper critically intervenes in this landscape by proposing a method to extract *explicit 6DoF object manipulation trajectories* directly from raw egocentric videos *without* auxiliary labels. By addressing the limitations of prior work, it aims to unlock the full potential of large-scale egocentric video datasets as a scalable, cost-effective, and rich source of supervision for VLA pre-training. It positions itself as a significant step towards enabling robotic foundation models to learn general manipulation skills from the vast and growing reservoir of human everyday activity videos. The work directly contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in robot learning by addressing the foundational data challenge, making VLAs more accessible and powerful.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the inherent limitations and costs associated with current methodologies for pre-training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics.

**Motivation:**
1. **Data Scarcity and Cost of Teleoperation:** The state-of-the-art in VLA models heavily relies on massive datasets collected through human teleoperation, where human experts directly control robots to perform tasks. This process is exceedingly expensive, labor-intensive, and time-consuming, creating a significant bottleneck in scaling up VLA research and the development of general-purpose robotic foundation models.
2. **Scalability Potential of Egocentric Videos:** Egocentric (first-person) videos, capturing humans performing daily tasks, are a rapidly growing data source, fueled by advancements in AR/VR devices and smart glasses. These videos intrinsically contain diverse human-object interactions and motion cues highly relevant for object manipulation learning. They offer a promising, scalable alternative to teleoperation.
3. **Limitations of Existing Egocentric Video Utilization:**
 - **Dependency on Auxiliary Data:** Prior successful attempts to leverage egocentric videos for robot learning (e.g., EgoMimic, EgoVLA) mandate dense auxiliary recordings, such as hand poses or depth information. Acquiring this auxiliary data requires specialized hardware or extensive manual annotation, thus undermining the scalability advantage of egocentric videos.
 - **Lack of Fine-grained Motion:** Approaches that eschew auxiliary labels (e.g., LAPA) typically learn latent action representations. While scalable, these implicit representations often fail to capture the fine-grained, precise motion trajectories necessary for complex robotic manipulation tasks like pick-and-place.
 - **Visual Domain Gap:** Policies trained on real-world egocentric recordings can sometimes struggle when evaluated in controlled environments, particularly simulators, due to differences in visual fidelity and dynamics.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

To overcome these challenges, the paper sets out to achieve the following primary objectives:
1. **Direct VLA Training from Raw Egocentric Videos:** The overarching goal is to demonstrate that VLA models can be effectively pre-trained directly from *raw egocentric videos*, eliminating the need for costly auxiliary labels or specialized hardware.
2. **Extraction of Explicit Object Manipulation Trajectories:** To provide the necessary fine-grained supervision for robot learning, the work aims to extract explicit 6DoF (6 Degrees of Freedom) object manipulation trajectories from egocentric videos. This addresses the limitation of implicit action representations.
3. **Construction of a New Large-Scale Pre-training Dataset:** By applying a robust trajectory extraction framework (EgoScaler) to diverse, large-scale egocentric video datasets (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, HD-EPIC, Nymeria), the objective is to create a novel, extensive dataset suitable for VLA pre-training.
4. **Automatic Data Curation:** To ensure the quality and utility of the extracted trajectories, the work aims to develop and apply automatic, rule-based filtering methods to remove noisy or incomplete instances from the dataset.
5. **Empirical Validation:** Rigorously evaluate the performance of VLA models pre-trained on this new egocentric video-derived dataset in both simulated and real-robot environments, comparing its effectiveness against models trained from scratch, those using latent action representations, and those pre-trained on traditional real-robot datasets.
6. **Investigate Data Synergies:** Explore whether combining the egocentric video-derived dataset with existing real-robot datasets can lead to further performance enhancements, thereby showcasing its complementary value.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to democratize and scale up data collection for robot learning, enabling more generalist robotic AI by leveraging the abundance of human demonstration videos, while ensuring the extracted information is precise enough for complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around leveraging the EgoScaler framework to extract explicit 6DoF object manipulation trajectories from raw egocentric videos, followed by rigorous data curation and VLA model training.

#### 3.1. Pre-training Dataset Construction

The central tenet is to transform raw egocentric videos, which lack direct action labels, into a structured dataset of (image sequence, text instruction, 6DoF object pose trajectory) triplets suitable for VLA pre-training.

**A. Re-visiting EgoScaler Framework:**
EgoScaler is the foundational tool for trajectory extraction. It operates in four sequential stages:
1. **Action and Object Identification:** Given an egocentric video clip, GPT-4o is used to identify the precise start and end timestamps of a manipulation action and the specific object being manipulated within the scene.
2. **2D Position Sequence Extraction:** An open-vocabulary segmentation model, identifies the manipulated object in each frame. A dense 3D point tracker then tracks the 2D image-plane position sequence of the object.
3. **3D Projection and Camera Motion Removal:** The 2D object position sequence is projected into the camera coordinate system of the action's start frame. This projection uses point cloud registration to stabilize the object's trajectory relative to the environment, effectively eliminating motion caused by the camera-wearer's head movements. For Ego4D, which lacks camera intrinsics, COLMAP, is used for prior estimation.
4. **Rotation Sequence Estimation:** The rotational component is derived by computing the transformation between consecutive object point clouds using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).
The output of EgoScaler is a sequence of 6DoF poses, $\tau_t = (x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw)$, representing the centroid and orientation of the manipulated object over time. These poses are approximated as the end-effector states of a robot, excluding gripper states.

**B. Egocentric Video Resources:**
The EgoScaler framework, originally designed for Exo-Ego4D, is extended to four large and diverse egocentric video datasets:
- **Ego4D:** A large-scale dataset covering human daily activities across hundreds of scenarios (cooking, crafting, repair).
- **Ego-Exo4D:** Similar to Ego4D, focusing on first- and third-person perspectives of skilled human activity.
- **HD-EPIC:** Primarily focuses on human activities within the cooking domain.
- **Nymeria:** Emphasizes full-body motion understanding, containing fewer hand-object interactions relevant for manipulation.
Initially, 124,559 episodes are extracted across these datasets.

**C. Data Curation Methods:**
To mitigate inaccuracies arising from object detection and point cloud registration errors, two rule-based filters and a smoothing operation are applied:
1. **Travel Distance Threshold ($\delta_{DT}$):** Trajectories with abnormally large cumulative displacement ($D$)—calculated as the sum of Euclidean distances between consecutive positional components—are discarded. These typically indicate corrupted trajectories due to severe registration errors.
2. **Background Track Similarity Threshold ($\delta_{BGTS}$):** To identify detection errors where the 'object' track behaves like background clutter, the Background Track Similarity (BGTS) is computed. BGTS is the average cosine similarity between the velocity vectors of the object track and a corresponding background track. Trajectories with BGTS above a threshold (empirically set to 0.7) are filtered out, as they suggest the supposed object is actually static background.
3. **Smoothing Filter:** A five-frame window averaging filter is applied to the translational components of the trajectories to suppress jitter noise caused by depth inconsistencies.
After this rigorous curation process, the dataset is refined to 45,157 episodes, containing diverse manipulation tasks with associated language instructions and 6DoF object pose trajectories.

#### 3.2. Policy Training

**A. VLA Architecture:**
The research utilizes $\pi_0$, a state-of-the-art VLA architecture. $\pi_0$ is built upon the pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) PaliGemma and incorporates a flow-matching-based, action head that processes robot state to generate continuous, high-frequency actions. The VLM parameters are frozen during both pre-training and post-training for efficiency.

**B. Action Representation:**
During pre-training, an action $a_t$ is represented as the displacement of the 6DoF object pose over time: $(\Delta x_t, \Delta y_t, \Delta z_t, \Delta rot6D_t)$, where $rot6D_t$ is a 6-dimensional flattened vector of the first two columns of the object's rotation matrix. This results in a 9-dimensional action vector. Gripper states are excluded as they cannot be derived from egocentric videos. Proprioceptive states $\tau_t$ are the raw 6DoF object trajectories, also converted to the $rot6D$ representation. All actions and proprioceptive states are normalized to mitigate distribution shifts between human and robot data.

**C. Dataset Merging and Training Objective:**
For comprehensive pre-training, the newly constructed egocentric dataset can be merged with existing real-robot datasets (e.g., BridgeData V2). Action and proprioceptive vectors are padded and normalized to ensure dimensionality consistency across different datasets.
The model is trained using a standard imitation learning objective: minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted action chunk ($\hat{a}_t$) and the ground truth action chunk ($a_t$) over $H$ future steps. This loss is used for both pre-training on the egocentric dataset and subsequent post-training on embodiment-specific robot datasets.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:** Evaluation is conducted in both simulated (SIMPLER BridgeData V2 environment) and real-robot (ALOHA hardware) settings.
- **Tasks:** Four language-aware pick-and-place tasks are designed for both environments, requiring the robot to grasp specific objects (carrot/onion) and place them into target locations (pot/bowl).
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against:
 - Training from scratch (only on post-training data).
 - LAPA (an implicit pre-training method) on Something-Something V2 and the constructed dataset.
 - Pre-training on existing real-robot datasets: BC-Z, BridgeData V2, and Fractal.
- **Implementation:** Trained on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs using AdamW optimizer with bfloat16 precision, a constant learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$. Pre-training runs for 20,000 steps (batch size 1,024).

This meticulously designed methodology allows the researchers to systematically investigate the efficacy of using raw egocentric videos as a scalable, high-quality data source for training VLA models, addressing critical gaps in current robot learning paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness and scalability of using egocentric videos for VLA pre-training. The key findings are threefold:

#### 4.1. Superiority Over Training From Scratch and Latent Action Pre-training (LAPA)

- **Significant Improvement over Training from Scratch:** The model pre-trained on the egocentric video dataset (referred to as "Ours") significantly outperforms training a $\pi_0$ model from scratch (i.e., only on the small post-training dataset). In real-robot tasks, the scratch baseline achieves 0% success, indicating a complete failure to ground instructions or generate meaningful trajectories. Our method, in contrast, achieves an average success rate of 55% on real-robot tasks. While the gain is smaller in simulation, it is consistent. This highlights the crucial role of pre-training on diverse, large-scale data for robust robot behavior.
- **Outperformance of LAPA:** Our approach consistently outperforms LAPA, a prior method for implicit pre-training from egocentric videos without auxiliary labels.
 - In real-robot tasks, our method achieves 55% success, compared to LAPA pre-trained on Something-Something V2 (SthV2) at 45% and LAPA on our dataset at 38%.
 - In simulated environments, our method achieves 26% success, while LAPA (SthV2) yields 21% and LAPA (Ours) yields 23%.
 This suggests that extracting explicit 6DoF object trajectories provides more robust and informative supervision for fine-grained manipulation compared to learning latent action representations, especially when handling complex skills like pick-and-place. The performance degradation of LAPA in simulation, particularly when using a visually diverse dataset like ours, further indicates that explicit action cues are more stable across visual domain shifts.

#### 4.2. Competitive Performance with Real-Robot Datasets

- **On-Par Performance:** Pre-training on our egocentric video-derived dataset alone yields performance competitive with leading real-robot datasets in real-robot tasks.
 - Our dataset achieves a total of 21 successful rollouts out of 40 across the four real-robot tasks.
 - This is higher than BC-Z (19/40) and BridgeData V2 (17/40).
 - It is slightly lower than Fractal (22/40), which is a much larger dataset.
This finding is particularly significant as it demonstrates that data automatically extracted from readily available egocentric videos can provide comparable pre-training benefits to data collected through costly human teleoperation of robots.

#### 4.3. Synergistic Improvements with Combined Datasets

- **Further Gains from Merging:** Combining our egocentric video-derived dataset with BridgeData V2 (a real-robot dataset) yields superior performance compared to using either dataset alone.
 - When combined, the "Ours + BridgeData V2" model achieves 27 successful rollouts out of 40, surpassing our dataset alone (21/40) and BridgeData V2 alone (17/40).
This result highlights the complementary nature of our dataset; it can effectively enrich and enhance existing robot datasets, contributing to larger, more diverse multi-embodiment collections for VLA pre-training.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Dataset Scalability:** Scaling the size of our dataset significantly improves performance in real-robot settings (e.g., 5K episodes: 40% success; 45K episodes: 55% success). However, in simulation, the full dataset slightly underperforms smaller subsets (45K episodes: 26% success; 5K episodes: 27% success). This suggests a visual domain gap between the rich, diverse visual cues in real-world egocentric videos and the simplified visual systems of simulators, indicating an area for future work.
- **Hyperparameter of Background Trajectory Similarity ($\delta_{BGTS}$):** The ablation study on the background track similarity threshold underscores the importance of the data curation process. A threshold of $\delta_{BGTS} = 0.7$ (resulting in 45,157 episodes) provides the best trade-off between dataset size and task performance, outperforming both stricter filtering (0.5, 28,719 episodes) and looser filtering (1.0, 86,427 episodes) in both real-robot and simulated environments. This confirms the necessity of automatic noise reduction for data quality.

In summary, the results firmly establish that raw egocentric videos, when processed to extract explicit object trajectories, form a powerful and scalable resource for advancing VLA research, offering a viable path to circumvent the data scarcity imposed by traditional teleoperation methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of robotic learning, particularly for the development of general-purpose Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Addressing the Data Scarcity Bottleneck:** The most immediate and profound impact is demonstrating a viable and scalable solution to the data scarcity problem that plagues robotic foundation models. By effectively utilizing raw egocentric videos—an increasingly abundant data source—without requiring specialized hardware or laborious manual annotation, this work unlocks a vast, previously underutilized reservoir of human manipulation data. This fundamentally shifts the paradigm of data collection from costly expert teleoperation to leveraging passively collected human demonstrations.

2. **Enabling Scalable VLA Pre-training:** The ability to automatically construct large-scale datasets of explicit 6DoF object manipulation trajectories from readily available egocentric videos paves the way for truly massive VLA pre-training. This scalability is crucial for developing more generalist and capable robots that can perform a wider array of tasks in diverse real-world environments. It moves the field closer to realizing "foundation models" for robotics, much like those seen in natural language processing and computer vision.

3. **Validation of Explicit Trajectory Supervision:** The superior performance of this method over approaches relying on latent action representations (like LAPA) highlights the critical importance of explicit, fine-grained 6DoF action trajectories. For complex manipulation tasks, knowing "how to move and rotate objects" precisely is more robust and informative than inferring abstract action tokens. This finding provides valuable guidance for future research in designing effective supervision signals from human demonstrations.

4. **Complementary Data Source for Multi-Embodiment Learning:** The finding that combining the egocentric-derived dataset with existing real-robot datasets yields even greater performance underscores its complementary value. This suggests that data derived from human observation is not merely an alternative, but an enhancement for existing robot learning datasets. It encourages a hybrid approach, integrating diverse data modalities to build richer and more robust multi-embodiment VLA models, aligning with the vision of projects like Open X-Embodiment.

5. **Lowering the Barrier to Entry for Robot Learning:** By reducing the reliance on expensive hardware and expert teleoperation, this research makes advanced robot learning techniques more accessible to a broader community. Researchers and developers can potentially leverage widely available egocentric video datasets to jumpstart their VLA models, accelerating innovation and application development.

6. **Advancing Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While the paper notes a visual domain gap between real-world egocentric videos and simulators, the ability to generate structured trajectories from real human data provides a concrete target for improving simulator fidelity and bridging the sim-to-real gap. Future work can focus on adapting policies trained on such rich real-world human data to perform robustly in diverse simulated environments, and vice-versa.

7. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - Developing more sophisticated techniques for robust trajectory extraction in even more challenging and diverse egocentric video scenarios.
 - Exploring methods to further reduce the visual domain gap between human egocentric videos and robot environments.
 - Investigating how to incorporate gripper states or finer dexterous manipulation cues from egocentric videos, potentially using complementary AI models.
 - Applying this methodology to even larger, continuous streams of egocentric data as smart glasses and AR devices become ubiquitous.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a significant step forward in robot learning by tapping into an abundant, scalable, and previously underutilized data source. Its impact will likely be felt in the accelerated development of more capable, general-purpose VLA models, ultimately bringing us closer to a future where robots can seamlessly assist humans in a wide array of everyday tasks.
