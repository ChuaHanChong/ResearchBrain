# GelNeuro: A Sensing-Computing Integrated Neuromorphic Tactile System for Texture Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> GelNeuro presents a fully integrated neuromorphic tactile system, directly connecting an optical tactile sensor to a neuromorphic System-on-Chip to eliminate host-computer reliance. This approach achieves 96.3% accuracy in 15-class texture recognition within 80 ms while consuming over 3800x less power than conventional CPU baselines, also demonstrating generalization to unseen contact depths.

## Problem

- Traditional visuo-tactile systems using frame-based cameras incur high latency and power consumption from fixed frame rates and substantial data transfer to external processors.
- Prior neuromorphic tactile systems maintain a "sensor-host-chip" architecture, causing data movement bottlenecks and diminishing the latency and power benefits of event-driven perception.
- Deploying floating-point Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) to low-bit fixed-point neuromorphic hardware frequently leads to accuracy degradation due to quantization and physical device effects.

## Method

- Integrated a GelSight-style optical tactile front end directly with the Speck2f neuromorphic SoC, enabling autonomous, host-computer-free tactile perception in the **GelNeuro** system.
- Utilized the Speck2f's on-chip ==Dynamic Vision Sensor (DVS)== and directly routed asynchronous event streams to ==Spiking Convolutional Neural Network (SCNN)== processing cores, eliminating off-chip data movement bottlenecks.
- Implemented a ==hardware-aware training== strategy incorporating ==synaptic weight clamping== and ==random input masking== to reduce accuracy degradation during 32-bit to 8-bit ==quantization== for deployment.

## Results

- The GelNeuro system achieved **96.3%** accuracy for **15**-class texture recognition within an **80 ms** inference window, with minimal accuracy degradation when deployed on the Speck2f chip.
- Active power consumption was measured at **19.6 mW**, representing an energy reduction exceeding **3800x** compared to a high-performance CPU baseline for the same task.
- The system demonstrated robust generalization to unseen contact depths, achieving up to **85.2%** accuracy on out-of-distribution indentation depths not encountered during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A relatively short 80 ms inference window is sufficient for high-accuracy texture recognition (96.3%), demonstrating the efficiency of event accumulation for tactile perception.
> - Hardware-aware synaptic weight clamping during training is critical for successful deployment, significantly reducing accuracy loss from floating-point to fixed-point quantization on neuromorphic hardware.
> - Integrating event-based sensing with on-chip neuromorphic computation yields over 3800x energy reduction compared to conventional CPU/GPU systems, highlighting its potential for edge applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile sensing is a critical component for dexterous robotic manipulation, providing local contact information such as surface geometry and incipient slip that is often difficult to infer solely from visual data. Texture recognition serves as a benchmark for tactile systems, requiring both high spatial resolution for intricate surface profiles and high temporal resolution for transient contact dynamics. For practical robotic applications, particularly in closed-loop control or on resource-constrained edge platforms, low latency and minimal power consumption are essential.

Conventional visuo-tactile sensors, such as the GelSight and TacTip series, typically employ frame-based cameras. While these offer high spatial resolution by imaging the deformation of an elastomeric medium, they are limited by fixed frame rates (e.g., 30-60 fps), which restrict their ability to resolve fast contact events. Furthermore, the transfer and processing of large image frames from the sensor to an external processor introduce significant latency and energy costs, impeding their use on mobile and agile robotic systems.

Dynamic Vision Sensors (DVS), or event cameras, represent an alternative paradigm by asynchronously reporting pixel-level brightness changes with sub-millisecond temporal resolution and reduced data redundancy. Integrating DVS into a visuo-tactile front end allows for the capture of sparse, high-frequency spatiotemporal information from contact-induced marker motions. This event-based data is naturally compatible with spiking neural networks (SNNs) and neuromorphic processors, which are designed for ultra-low-power, event-driven computation. This compatibility avoids the power-intensive, dense-frame operations of conventional processors.

Prior work has explored neuromorphic visuo-tactile sensing, with systems like NeuroTac and studies evaluating texture recognition on platforms such as Loihi2 and Speck. However, a common limitation in many existing pipelines is their reliance on a host computer for tasks such as reading out raw sensor data, cropping or accumulating events, and relaying the preprocessed data to the neuromorphic chip. This "sensor-host-chip" architecture retains an off-chip data-movement bottleneck, thereby diminishing the theoretical latency and power benefits of event-driven perception. Additionally, deploying high-precision, floating-point SNN models onto low-bit fixed-point physical hardware frequently leads to accuracy degradation due to quantization noise and device-level effects.

GelNeuro addresses these specific limitations by proposing a fully integrated system that combines an event-based optical tactile sensor with a neuromorphic System-on-Chip (SoC) for direct, host-computer-free, in-sensor computation. This approach aims to reduce data movement bottlenecks and overcome quantization challenges, advancing the state of the art in autonomous, low-power, and low-latency tactile perception for robotic platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate GelNeuro, a fully integrated sensing-computing neuromorphic visuo-tactile system that directly interfaces an optical tactile front end with a neuromorphic System-on-Chip (SoC). This integration is designed to enable autonomous, low-latency, and low-power tactile perception without reliance on a host computer for data preprocessing or relay.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations observed in existing visuo-tactile perception systems:

- **Host-Computer Dependency:** Conventional visuo-tactile pipelines, even those utilizing event cameras and neuromorphic processors, often depend on a host computer to read out sensor data, perform preprocessing (e.g., event cropping or accumulation), or relay the data to the neuromorphic chip. This "sensor-host-chip" architecture creates an off-chip data-movement bottleneck, which increases latency and power consumption, thus counteracting the benefits offered by event-driven sensing and neuromorphic processing. The objective is to eliminate this dependency, thereby achieving a truly autonomous, fully integrated edge perception loop.
- **Latency and Power Bottlenecks:** Traditional frame-based cameras in tactile sensors are inherently limited by fixed sampling rates, dense data readouts, and substantial host-side processing overheads. These factors result in severe latency penalties and high energy costs, which restrict the deployment of such systems on mobile or agile robotic platforms with limited computational and power resources. GelNeuro aims to mitigate these bottlenecks by leveraging event-driven processing from the sensor to the chip, scaling computation with tactile activity rather than fixed frame rates.
- **Accuracy Degradation in Hardware Deployment:** The migration of SNN models, typically trained with floating-point precision, to low-bit fixed-point neuromorphic hardware often leads to a decrease in inference accuracy. This degradation is attributed to quantization noise and non-ideal device-level effects inherent in physical silicon. A key objective is to address this by developing hardware-aware training and deployment strategies to ensure robust performance on the physical chip.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** For real-world robotic applications, tactile systems need to exhibit robust performance across variable contact conditions, such as different indentation depths or contact patterns, without extensive re-training. Evaluating generalization to unseen contact depths is therefore an objective to demonstrate the system's practical applicability.

To achieve these objectives, the research proposes specific contributions:
1. The development of the first sensing-computing neuromorphic visuo-tactile sensor that directly connects an elastomeric optical gel front end with the Speck2f neuromorphic SoC, thereby eliminating host-side data preprocessing and bus-relaying overheads.
2. The introduction of a hardware-aware SCNN mapping workflow that incorporates synaptic weight clamping and random input masking. This co-design strategy is intended to suppress quantization noise and device-level hardware effects, facilitating migration from software simulation to physical hardware.
3. Comprehensive hardware-in-the-loop evaluations on a 15-class natural texture recognition task to demonstrate high on-chip accuracy, low power consumption, and robust generalization across unseen contact depths.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GelNeuro system integrates an optical tactile front end with the Speck2f neuromorphic System-on-Chip (SoC) to create a sensing-computing platform. The methodology encompasses the hardware design, data collection, SNN training, and hardware-aware deployment strategies, followed by comprehensive evaluation protocols.

**A. GelNeuro Design and Operation**
The GelNeuro hardware module combines a GelSight Mini marker-gel module as the tactile front end. This elastomeric gel is dotted with markers whose motion upon contact provides deformation information. Illumination is provided by a monochrome COB LED strip (4000 K, 12 V). The imaging core is the built-in 128 × 128-pixel Dynamic Vision Sensor (DVS) of the Speck2f SoC. A custom 3D-printed housing is used to fix the camera, gel, and light source, ensuring proper alignment and focal settings so that the DVS field of view covers the gel surface and markers.

During physical interaction, contact with a surface causes the markers on the gel to move, generating local brightness changes. The DVS captures these changes as asynchronous events, each containing spatial location (x, y), polarity (brightness increase or decrease), and a timestamp (t). A key aspect of GelNeuro's operation is that this raw asynchronous event stream from the DVS is routed directly to the Speck2f neuromorphic processor. An on-chip preprocessing module performs spatial Region of Interest (ROI) cropping (100x100 pixels) to focus on the active contact area. The cropped, sparse event stream is then passed through the on-chip network-on-chip (NoC) to the spiking convolutional processing cores without any frame buffering or host-computer relay. This establishes a direct sensor-to-SCNN tactile pipeline for inference.

**B. Texture Dataset Construction**
A custom dataset was created using 15 natural texture materials, including some with similar surface appearances to evaluate fine texture discrimination. The GelNeuro sensor was mounted on the end-effector flange of a Franka Emika robotic arm for controlled data collection. For each sample, the robot performed a normal press onto the texture surface to a prescribed depth, followed by a 1000 ms sliding motion at 20 mm/s. Indentation depths were limited to 0.5 mm to prevent gel damage.

The training set was collected at three indentation depths (0.1 mm, 0.3 mm, and 0.5 mm) and three sliding angles (0°, 45°, and 90° relative to the texture axis). For each texture, 15 samples were recorded per depth-angle combination, resulting in 135 training samples per class and a total of 2025 training samples.
The test set was collected independently and divided into two groups:
- **In-distribution (ID) test set:** Used the same three indentation depths as the training set, with 27 samples per class (405 samples total). This evaluated baseline recognition performance.
- **Out-of-distribution (OOD) test set:** Used two intermediate indentation depths (0.2 mm and 0.4 mm) that were not included in training. This set, comprising 18 samples per class (270 samples total), was used to evaluate generalization capabilities to unseen contact depths.

**C. Offline Training and Deployment**

1. **SCNN Topology and Offline Training:**
 For offline SCNN training, the recorded tactile events were preprocessed. Events were cropped to a 100 × 100 pixel ROI. For the purpose of training with surrogate gradients and backpropagation through time (BPTT), the cropped events were accumulated into 1 ms bins, forming a spatiotemporal input tensor of size (2, T, 100, 100), where '2' represents event polarity channels and 'T' is the temporal window. On-chip inference, however, processes events asynchronously without explicit binning.
 The SCNN utilized non-leaky integrate-and-fire (IAF) neurons, which are suitable for short inference windows and sparse event streams. The network topology was compact to fit the nine physical processing cores on Speck2f, consisting of 5 convolutional layers followed by 1 fully connected (FC) readout layer. Bias terms were removed to comply with chip constraints. The convolutional layers used 4×4 kernels, stride 2, and padding 1, with channel dimensions increasing from 16 to 64 and spatial resolution downsampling from 100×100 to 3×3. The final 64×3×3 feature tensor was flattened and mapped to 15 output neurons by the FC layer.
 Training was performed using the Sinabs framework with surrogate-gradient BPTT. An "over-driven" initialization strategy multiplied Kaiming-initialized weights by 2 to enhance initial membrane drive and firing activity. To improve robustness to noise, a 10% random input mask was applied to the binned event tensor during training. A dropout layer (rate = 0.3) was placed before the FC readout. Classification was determined by rate coding, selecting the output neuron with the highest accumulated spike count.

2. **Quantized Deployment and On-Chip Inference:**
 Deployment on Speck2f necessitated the conversion of 32-bit floating-point weights (learned offline) to the chip's signed 8-bit integer range ([-128, 127]). To mitigate accuracy degradation from direct post-training quantization, a hardware-aware synaptic weight clamping strategy was introduced during training. After each BPTT update, trainable weights were symmetrically clipped to a range of [−c, c]. Prior to deployment, these clipped weights were discretized using the Sinabs/DynapCNN toolchain according to the formula: `w_q = round(clamp(w, −c, c) × 127/c)`. The clamping threshold 'c' was experimentally optimized to balance the retention of discriminative weights with the resolution of the 8-bit mapping.
 For hardware-in-the-loop inference, the quantized network was loaded onto Speck2f. Asynchronous tactile event streams from the sensor front end were delivered directly to the chip. The on-chip inference window (T_infer) could be independently configured. Within this window, the readout layer continuously integrated spikes from the 15 output neurons, with the neuron accumulating the most spikes determining the predicted texture class.

**D. Evaluation Metrics and Protocols**

1. **Temporal Performance Metrics:**
 - **Matched-window setting:** Evaluated models trained and tested at 8 different temporal window lengths (20 ms to 150 ms) to assess the effect of event duration on classification accuracy.
 - **Early-inference setting:** Used a model trained at a baseline of 80 ms, but systematically shortened the on-board inference window (T_infer) from 80 ms down to 20 ms. This measured the ability to make early decisions. The "silent rate," defined as the proportion of samples with no output spike at the readout layer, was reported alongside accuracy.

2. **Cross-Depth Generalization:**
 Accuracy was assessed separately for the in-distribution (ID) test set (0.1 mm, 0.3 mm, 0.5 mm depths) and the out-of-distribution (OOD) test set (0.2 mm, 0.4 mm depths). This evaluated the SCNN's ability to maintain texture discrimination under varying contact conditions not encountered during training.

3. **Power Consumption Metrics:**
 Board-level active power consumption was measured for the 80 ms texture-classification task on the GelNeuro system. This was compared against an Intel i9-14900KF CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, measuring both idle and continuous inference power to determine dynamic inference components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GelNeuro system was evaluated comprehensively across temporal performance, hardware deployment strategies, generalization capabilities, and power efficiency.

**A. Effect of Training and Inference Windows**
The impact of temporal windows on texture classification accuracy was assessed in both PC software simulation and physical Speck hardware deployment. In the matched-window setting, where training and testing windows ranged from 20 ms to 150 ms, on-board accuracy was low at short durations (20-40 ms). As the window increased, Speck accuracy improved, reaching 96.3% at an 80 ms window. Extending the window further to 120 ms or 150 ms yielded only marginal improvement, with accuracy hovering around 97%. This indicated that 80 ms captured most discriminative events while maintaining a short response time, hence it was selected as the baseline inference window.
In the early-inference setting, utilizing the model trained at 80 ms, the system's ability to make decisions with shorter inference windows (T_infer) was examined. With only 20 ms of input, the system exhibited a 19.8% silent rate (no output decision spike) and an accuracy of 27.9%. As T_infer increased to 60 ms, accuracy rose to 85.4%, and the silent rate decreased to 4.4%. At the full 80 ms inference window, the system achieved 96.3% accuracy with a silent rate of 0.2%. This demonstrates the network's ability to accumulate tactile evidence progressively, allowing for early, coarser decisions and stable recognition with a modest increase in integration time.

**B. Weight Clamping for Quantized Deployment**
The study investigated the sensitivity of deployment accuracy to the synaptic weight clamping threshold 'c' during the 32-bit floating-point to 8-bit integer (INT8) conversion on Speck2f. With fixed training and testing durations of 80 ms, the PC simulation model maintained near 100% accuracy for c ≥ 0.6. On the physical chip, however, accuracy increased as 'c' was raised from small values, peaking at c = 0.9 with 96.3% accuracy. Beyond this point, accuracy either decreased or fluctuated. This indicated that while a smaller 'c' offers finer resolution for weights within the clamped range, too small a 'c' leads to significant clipping of high-magnitude weights, degrading performance. An optimal 'c' of 0.9 was identified as it retained discriminative weights effectively while keeping the mapped range compact, resulting in the smallest accuracy gap between the PC model (99.0%) and the deployed Speck model (96.3%).

**C. Cross-Depth Generalization**
To evaluate the system's robustness to varying contact conditions, cross-depth generalization was tested on out-of-distribution (OOD) depths of 0.2 mm and 0.4 mm, which were not used during training. The system maintained high classification accuracies for in-distribution (ID) depths (0.1 mm, 0.3 mm, 0.5 mm) across the temporal window spectrum, reaching 96.3% for 0.5 mm at 80 ms. For the OOD depths, accuracy scaled positively with extended integration times, stabilizing at 74.8% for 0.2 mm and 85.2% for 0.4 mm with a 120 ms temporal window. This performance, significantly above chance, suggests that GelNeuro can decouple texture features from depth-dependent event intensities, demonstrating robust generalization to unseen contact depths without zero-shot fine-tuning.

**D. Edge Power Consumption**
Board-level active power consumption was measured for the 80 ms inference task and compared against conventional CPU and GPU platforms. The Speck2f platform consumed 1.4 mW in idle state and 19.6 mW during continuous tactile spike-stream inference, indicating a dynamic power increment of 18.2 mW. In contrast, an Intel i9-14900KF CPU drew 7.7 W idle and 74.7 W active, while an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU consumed 25.9 W idle and 87.4 W active. These results show that the GelNeuro system's operating power is over three orders of magnitude lower than conventional digital computing architectures, achieving an approximate 3800x energy reduction compared to the high-performance CPU baseline. This substantial power reduction is attributed to the event-driven operation of both the tactile front end and the neuromorphic processor, where computation scales with actual tactile activity.

**E. Discussion**
GelNeuro distinguishes itself by uniquely integrating on-chip SCNN inference with direct, autonomous sensor-to-chip tactile event routing, eliminating host-side processing overheads. The system achieves competitive texture classification accuracy (96.3% for 15 classes) within a short inference window (80 ms) while demonstrating power consumption significantly lower than conventional CPU/GPU platforms. Challenges encountered include the memory overhead of BPTT training with fine temporal discretizations (leading to a 1 ms binning trade-off) and the residual accuracy gap between software models and 8-bit hardware, despite the hardware-aware weight clamping. Circuit-level noise and thermal fluctuations in physical silicon were partially addressed by random input masking during training. These observations highlight areas for future optimization, such as quantization-aware training and advanced compilation strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GelNeuro system represents a notable advancement in neuromorphic tactile perception by demonstrating a fully integrated sensing-computing paradigm. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Enabling Autonomous Edge Perception:** The most direct impact of GelNeuro is its successful implementation of a direct sensor-to-chip perception pathway, completely bypassing the need for a host computer in the active inference loop. This eliminates the "sensor-host-chip" data-movement bottleneck prevalent in prior neuromorphic tactile systems. Consequently, GelNeuro enables truly autonomous, integrated edge perception, which is critical for deploying intelligent robots in environments where computational resources and power budgets are constrained.
- **Ultra-Low Latency and Power Efficiency:** By leveraging event-driven sensing and neuromorphic processing, GelNeuro achieves an inference latency of 80 ms and an active board-level power consumption of only 19.6 mW. This power efficiency is more than three orders of magnitude lower than conventional CPU/GPU baselines. Such characteristics are fundamental for time-critical robotic tasks, enabling faster reaction times and longer operational durations for mobile and agile robotic platforms. This energy efficiency can reduce the need for large, heavy batteries, contributing to lighter and more agile robot designs.
- **Robustness and Generalization for Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated ability of GelNeuro to generalize robustly across unseen contact depths is important for its practical utility. Real-world robotic interactions involve variability in contact force and depth. The system's capacity to maintain useful performance under these domain shifts, without requiring specific re-calibration or fine-tuning, enhances its applicability in unstructured and dynamic environments. This indicates that the extracted tactile features are sufficiently abstract and discriminative to be invariant to certain physical perturbations.
- **Advancing Neuromorphic Hardware Deployment:** The research contributes to the methodology of deploying SNNs on fixed-point neuromorphic hardware. The introduction and experimental validation of a hardware-aware weight clamping strategy address the challenge of accuracy degradation due to quantization. While not fully eliminating the precision gap, this technique provides a practical approach to facilitate the transition of SNN models from software simulation to physical silicon, offering insights for future hardware-software co-design efforts.
- **Foundation for Future Robotic Capabilities:** Beyond texture recognition, the core architecture of GelNeuro is inherently suited for extension to other time-critical tactile applications. These include transient slip detection and real-time, closed-loop slip-preventive control, which are vital for dexterous manipulation. In such scenarios, the ultra-low latency provided by the direct sensor-to-chip pathway is expected to yield more pronounced and definitive operational advantages, potentially leading to more robust and responsive robotic manipulation capabilities.
- **Catalyst for Further Research:** The study also highlights persistent challenges, such as optimizing temporal discretization in BPTT training to match asynchronous chip operation and further reducing quantization loss. These identified areas provide clear directions for future research, including exploring advanced quantization-aware training (QAT) techniques and developing lightweight surrogate-gradient algorithms, which could further bridge the performance gap between software-trained SNNs and physical neuromorphic hardware.
