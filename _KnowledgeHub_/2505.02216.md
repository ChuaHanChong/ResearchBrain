# LLM-Guided Probabilistic Program Induction for POMDP Model Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MIT CSAIL and Cornell University developed POMDP Coder, a framework that uses large language models to guide the induction of interpretable probabilistic programs for Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) models. The system demonstrates enhanced sample efficiency and generalization by outperforming traditional model-learning, policy-learning, and direct LLM-based approaches across various simulated and real-world robotic environments.

## Problem

- Specifying accurate Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) models for complex, real-world environments is challenging and often requires extensive manual engineering.
- Many existing POMDP model-learning or policy-learning methods are data-intensive and lack interpretability, making them unsuitable for data-scarce domains like robotics.
- Leveraging large language models (LLMs) for world modeling has largely been restricted to fully observable settings, leaving partial observability unaddressed.

## Method

- An iterative learning framework, **POMDP Coder**, combines initial human demonstrations with online experience to refine POMDP models.
- Large language models (LLMs) guide the induction of ==probabilistic programs== for the initial state, transition, observation, and reward functions through a ==Thompson sampling==-based refinement process.
- Learned probabilistic programs are integrated with a ==determinized belief-space planner==, which uses ==particle filtering== for belief updates and a composite cost function for online decision-making.

## Results

- POMDP Coder consistently matched or outperformed all traditional and LLM-based baselines across various simulated POMDP and MiniGrid environments in terms of normalized discounted reward.
- In real-world robot navigation tasks with a Boston Dynamics Spot, the method achieved superior mean discounted reward and higher success rates by learning generalizable initial state knowledge.
- The hybrid learning approach, combining initial demonstrations with online refinement, demonstrated superior performance compared to learning solely offline or online, highlighting its sample efficiency and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating LLMs with probabilistic program induction enables the automated discovery of interpretable, low-complexity POMDP models from limited data.
> - Combining initial human demonstrations with online self-generated experience is crucial for balancing efficient exploration with comprehensive model learning.
> - Symbolic probabilistic programs derived from LLM guidance facilitate superior generalization to novel states and tasks compared to data-driven or direct LLM approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: model-based reinforcement learning, probabilistic programming, and large language models (LLMs) for decision-making. The core problem it addresses is decision-making under uncertainty, specifically within the framework of Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs).

**Challenges in POMDPs and Model Learning:** POMDPs offer a principled framework for sequential decision-making where the agent does not have direct access to the true state of the environment. While powerful, their practical application is bottlenecked by the difficulty of accurately specifying environment models (transition, observation, reward functions) and the computational complexity of solving them. Traditionally, learning POMDP models from experience has been a significant challenge. Prior methods, such as Bayesian approaches (Bayes-Adaptive POMDPs), spectral techniques (Predictive State Representations), and optimism-based exploration, often suffer from computational intractability, require strong structural assumptions, or demand extensive exploratory data. Apprenticeship learning can alleviate data needs but is sensitive to expert quality.

**The Rise of Probabilistic Programming:** Probabilistic programming (PP) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for modeling complex systems with concise, symbolic representations. It allows for the specification of generative models with inherent stochastic behavior as executable code. Research in probabilistic program induction (PPI) has shown that these representations can lead to marked improvements in data efficiency and enable few-shot learning, offering an alternative to data-intensive deep learning methods. However, the vast search space of possible programs remains a computational bottleneck for PPI.

**LLMs for World Modeling and Decision Making:** More recently, large language models (LLMs) have shown remarkable capabilities in code generation, reasoning, and understanding natural language. This has led to an emerging area of research exploring their use in world modeling for decision-making. Prior work has primarily focused on fully observable settings, where LLMs are used to generate PDDL domains or code-based models to represent transition dynamics and reward structures. These approaches leverage LLMs to infuse human priors and domain-specific constraints into the model discovery process.

**Bridging the Gap: This Paper's Contribution:** This paper uniquely bridges these fields by extending LLM-guided model learning to the *partially observable* setting of POMDPs. It tackles the challenge of POMDP model estimation by treating the components of a POMDP (initial state, transition, observation, and reward functions) as "low-complexity probabilistic graphical models" that can be represented as "short probabilistic programs." By leveraging LLMs as powerful priors, the authors aim to overcome the computational bottleneck of searching for these programs. This approach represents a novel synthesis:
- **From POMDP learning:** It addresses the problem of learning explicit, interpretable POMDP models, rather than just policies, from limited data.
- **From Probabilistic Programming:** It harnesses the data efficiency and interpretability benefits of PP.
- **From LLM-based AI:** It uses LLMs to guide the search and refinement of probabilistic programs, incorporating rich semantic understanding.

To the authors' knowledge, this work is the first to extend LLM-based model learning techniques to the POMDP setting, directly addressing the additional complexities introduced by partial observability. This positions the paper as a significant step towards building more robust, generalizable, and transparent AI agents for complex, real-world tasks, particularly in robotics where partial observability is ubiquitous.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method for learning **interpretable, low-complexity Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) models directly from data**. The authors specifically target a subclass of POMDPs where the constituent components—the initial state distribution, transition dynamics, observation function, and reward function—can be succinctly represented as probabilistic graphical models encoded by short probabilistic programs.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several critical challenges and opportunities in decision-making under uncertainty:

1. **Difficulty in POMDP Model Specification:** While POMDPs provide a principled and powerful framework for modeling decision-making under uncertainty, their practical application is severely bottlenecked by the difficulty in specifying accurate models of environments. This often requires painstaking manual engineering and a deep theoretical understanding, making them challenging to deploy in complex real-world scenarios, especially in robotics and autonomous systems.
2. **Need for Interpretable Models:** The increasing complexity of AI systems necessitates models that are not just performant but also interpretable. Traditional model-free reinforcement learning approaches often yield opaque policies. Explicit, low-complexity probabilistic programs offer a transparent representation of the world dynamics, which can be inspected, debugged, and understood by humans.
3. **Enhancing Sample Efficiency:** Many existing model-learning or policy-learning methods for POMDPs are notoriously data-intensive. In domains like robotics, collecting vast amounts of interaction data is expensive, time-consuming, and potentially dangerous. The authors are motivated to achieve high sample efficiency, enabling effective learning from limited demonstrations and online interaction.
4. **Leveraging LLMs as Informative Priors:** Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated their strong capabilities in code generation and symbolic reasoning. The authors are motivated to leverage LLMs as powerful, informed priors to guide the search for probabilistic programs. This is expected to mitigate the computational burden traditionally associated with searching the vast space of possible programs, making the induction process more efficient.
5. **Addressing Partial Observability in LLM-driven World Models:** While LLMs have been explored for world-modeling in fully observable settings, their application to decision-making under *partial observability* is an unexplored frontier. This work aims to extend the success of LLM-guided program synthesis to handle the inherent uncertainty of POMDPs, a crucial step for real-world robotic applications.
6. **Outperforming Traditional Baselines:** The authors aim to demonstrate that their LLM-guided approach can be more effective in terms of sample efficiency, accuracy, and generalization than traditional model-learning techniques (like tabular methods), policy-learning methods (like behavior cloning), or direct LLM-based planning in partially observable environments.

In essence, the research seeks to overcome the challenges of POMDP model specification and data inefficiency by integrating the structured reasoning of probabilistic programming with the generative power of LLMs, thereby enabling robust, interpretable, and sample-efficient learning of world models for autonomous agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel approach called **POMDP Coder**, which decomposes the problem into two main components: (1) learning probabilistic models that define the POMDP and (2) using these learned models for online planning. The method is designed to learn interpretable, low-complexity POMDP models directly from data by leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to guide probabilistic program induction.

#### 3.1. Inputs and Setup

- **Initial Demonstrations (D):** The agent is provided with an initial small set (ten) of human-generated demonstrations. Each demonstration consists of (state, action, observation, next-state) transitions. Crucially, no assumptions are made about the quality of these demonstrations regarding optimality.
- **Post-hoc Full Observability:** A key assumption is that the agent gains access to intermediate states *after* an episode terminates. This simplifies the data collection aspect, particularly in real-world settings where full state information during an episode is hard to acquire.
- **Code-based API:** The agent is provided with a code-based API defining the structure of the state, action, and observation spaces, allowing the LLM to understand the domain's symbolic representation.
- **Probabilistic Programming Framework:** Pyro, a flexible probabilistic programming framework built on Python, is used to specify the generative models.

#### 3.2. Overall POMDP Coder Loop (Algorithm 1)

POMDP Coder operates in an iterative loop:
1. **Model Learning (`LearnModels`):** The LLM-guided learning procedure (detailed below) is invoked to generate and refine probabilistic programs for the four core POMDP components: initial state distribution, transition model, observation model, and reward model. This step uses the accumulated demonstration dataset `D`.
2. **Belief Initialization:** Based on the learned initial state model, a belief (probability distribution over states) is initialized using particle filtering.
3. **Online Planning and Execution:**
 - An online POMDP solver (detailed below) uses the current belief and the learned POMDP models to find the optimal action.
 - The action is executed in the environment, and a new observation is received.
 - The belief is updated using particle filtering based on the action and observation.
4. **Data Accumulation:** The trajectory from the current episode is added to the demonstration dataset `D`, enriching the data for subsequent model refinements.
5. The loop continues for a specified number of episodes, continually refining the POMDP models with new experience.

#### 3.3. Learning Models (Algorithm 2 - `LearnModels`)

This is the core of the probabilistic program induction. Its objective is to maximize a "dataset coverage" metric, defined as the proportion of data in `D` that has support under the current probabilistic model.
1. **Data Splitting:** The dataset `D` is split into `D_train` and `D_test` to mitigate overfitting.
2. **Initial Program Proposal:** If no models exist, an LLM is prompted (Appendix E.2) to propose initial code snippets for each POMDP component (initial state, transition, observation, reward) based on `D_train` and the code API.
3. **Iterative Refinement with Thompson Sampling:**
 - The proposed models are evaluated against the empirical conditional probability distributions in `D_test` and `D_train`. Since directly checking model density is hard, Monte Carlo approximation is used. Any empirical sample not produced by the model is considered a "failure" and recorded in `D_errors`.
 - The coverage score for the model is calculated, and a Beta distribution is initialized for this candidate model, representing uncertainty over its true coverage.
 - The process then enters an iterative loop using a **Thompson sampling exploration strategy**. At each step, a node (representing a candidate model with its Beta distribution and errors) is selected from a tree of candidates by sampling from each Beta distribution and picking the highest. This balances exploration (trying uncertain models) and exploitation (refining promising models).
 - **Program Repair:** The selected candidate model's code is refined by prompting the LLM (Appendix E.3) with the `D_errors` (examples it failed to cover). This encourages the LLM to generate a corrected or improved version.
 - The refined model's coverage is re-evaluated, its Beta distribution updated, and it is added as a child node.
4. The iterative refinement continues until coverage is sufficiently high or an iteration budget is exhausted. The model with the highest overall empirical coverage is returned.

#### 3.4. POMDP Solvers (Algorithm 3 - `BeliefPlanner`)

The learned probabilistic programs are then used within an online POMDP solver.
1. **Determinized Belief-Space Planning:** The solver performs forward search directly in belief space, simplifying the stochastic nature of the problem by determinizing dynamics. This allows for an A*-style expansion strategy.
2. **Cost Function:** The planner balances exploitation (maximizing reward) and exploration (information gain) through a composite cost function: `g' = g - r̂ - λ log p̂ + α ĥ + cost(a)`.
 - `r̂`: Expected reward.
 - `λ log p̂`: Term for observation likelihood, trading off risk sensitivity (λ).
 - `α ĥ`: Term for belief entropy, encouraging information gathering (α).
 - `cost(a)`: Action cost penalty.
3. **Particle Filtering:** Beliefs are updated using particle filtering after each action and observation.
4. The planner returns the first action in the path to the node with the minimum accumulated cost. This planner is not optimal but computationally efficient and suitable for the tested problems.

This comprehensive methodology allows POMDP Coder to iteratively learn interpretable models, adapt to new data, and make informed decisions in partially observable environments by strategically combining the strengths of LLMs for program synthesis with principled probabilistic inference and planning techniques.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings from both simulated and real-world experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness of POMDP Coder in learning robust and interpretable POMDP models.

#### 4.1. Simulated Experiments

Experiments were conducted on two categories of problems:
- **Classical POMDPs:** The Tiger problem and the Rock Sample problem, serving as benchmarks for decision-making under uncertainty.
- **MiniGrid Tasks:** Five modified MiniGrid environments (Empty, Corners, Lava, Rooms, Unlock) designed for navigation and planning under partial observability. These modifications highlight the method's ability to generalize to unseen environments.

**5.1.1. Baselines:** The performance of POMDP Coder was compared against several baselines:
- **Oracle:** Hardcoded, true environment models, representing an upper bound.
- **Random:** Arbitrary actions, representing a lower bound.
- **Direct LLM:** LLM directly queried for the next action at each step, given the same information as POMDP Coder during model learning.
- **Behavior Cloning (BC):** A policy learned by mapping states to actions from demonstrations.
- **Tabular:** POMDP models learned as conditional probability tables from demonstration counts.
- **Ablations of POMDP Coder:**
 - **Offline Only:** Uses only human demonstrations, no online updates.
 - **Online Only:** Learns solely from its own experiences, no initial expert demonstrations.

##### 4.1.1. Simulation Results (Figure 3)

- **Superior Performance:** POMDP Coder consistently matched or outperformed all baseline methods across all simulated domains in terms of normalized discounted reward. This indicates its ability to effectively learn and utilize POMDP models for optimal decision-making.
- **Generalization Advantage:** Behavior Cloning and Tabular baselines were fundamentally limited in generalization, especially when initial state spaces were vast or new situations were encountered. POMDP Coder, by contrast, leveraged symbolic abstraction in its probabilistic programs, allowing it to generalize effectively to new, unseen situations.
- **Robustness over Direct LLM:** While the Direct LLM baseline showed some effectiveness (e.g., in Rock Sample), it frequently encountered issues like infinite loops and failed to understand critical environmental constraints (e.g., obstacle obstruction), even with relevant examples. POMDP Coder's explicit model and planning approach proved more robust.
- **Benefits of Hybrid Learning:** POMDP Coder outperformed both the *Offline-only* and *Online-only* ablations. The *Offline-only* ablation struggled with missing transitions outside the expert demonstrations (e.g., not knowing lava causes death). The *Online-only* ablation suffered from inefficient random exploration, failing to discover crucial behaviors (e.g., using a key in the Unlock environment). This highlights the importance of combining initial demonstrations with iterative online refinement.
- **Runtime and Coverage (Tables 2, 3):** Supplemental data showed the average number of LLM-generated candidate programs sampled during learning and the training/testing coverage scores after offline learning. These metrics provide insight into the efficiency and accuracy of the model learning process.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Experiments

Experiments were conducted using a Boston Dynamics Spot robot searching for an apple in two distinct real-world environments: a small, closed-off room ("Small-Cabinets") and a large, open lobby area ("Large-Tables").
- **Action Space:** Discretized rotations and cardinal movements.
- **Object Detection:** Grounding SAM was used for online, open-vocabulary object detection.
- **Scene Representation:** A pre-scanned map of the empty room provided an object-centric scene graph and an occupancy grid.
- **Task Distributions:** Apple location varied on specific furniture types (e.g., cabinets or tables).

**5.2.1. Baselines:** A subset of simulated baselines were used: Behavior Cloning, Direct LLM, and a hardcoded Uniform baseline (assumes uniform object distribution).

##### 4.2.1. Real Robot Results (Table 1)

- **Enhanced Exploration Efficiency:** POMDP Coder achieved superior mean discounted reward and higher success rates compared to all baselines.
- **Generalizable Initial State Knowledge:** The method successfully learned that objects (apples) were consistently placed on specific classes of furniture (e.g., "on top of one of the three drawer cabinets"). This allowed it to construct a much narrower and more informed initial state distribution, leading to significantly more efficient exploration compared to the naive uniform baseline or other methods that overfit to specific initial states.
- **Practical Applicability:** These results demonstrate the method's practical applicability in complex, partially observable real-world robotics tasks, where efficient and accurate modeling of uncertainty is paramount.

In summary, the main findings robustly establish POMDP Coder as a highly effective approach for learning interpretable POMDP models. It leverages the complementary strengths of LLMs for symbolic reasoning and probabilistic programming for structured representation, leading to superior sample efficiency, generalization, and robustness compared to both traditional and recent LLM-centric baselines in both simulated and real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence and robotics, offering a novel paradigm for learning decision-making models under uncertainty. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Disparate AI Paradigms:** The most profound impact of POMDP Coder lies in its successful integration of symbolic AI (probabilistic programming, POMDPs), modern large language models (LLMs), and model-based reinforcement learning. It effectively bridges the gap between these powerful but often isolated approaches, demonstrating how LLMs can be harnessed to inform and accelerate the principled model induction processes of probabilistic programming. This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of each, leading to more capable and robust AI systems.

2. **Enabling Interpretable and Robust AI Systems:** By generating low-complexity probabilistic programs, POMDP Coder offers inherently interpretable world models. Unlike black-box neural networks, these code-based representations explicitly define the transition dynamics, observation functions, and reward structures. This transparency is crucial for debugging, verification, and building trust in autonomous systems, especially in safety-critical applications like robotics. The increased robustness over direct LLM planning, which often struggles with implicit constraints, is a testament to the value of explicit model-based reasoning.

3. **Enhancing Sample Efficiency for Real-world Robotics:** The demonstrated superior sample efficiency is a critical advantage for robotics. Collecting interaction data in physical environments is often costly, time-consuming, and potentially dangerous. By learning effective POMDP models from a small number of demonstrations and refining them online, POMDP Coder significantly reduces the data burden, making it more feasible to deploy intelligent robots in novel and complex settings. This could accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous agents capable of learning and adapting in the real world.

4. **Improved Generalization through Symbolic Abstraction:** The ability of POMDP Coder to generalize to new situations and initial states, outperforming tabular and behavior cloning methods, is a key impact. Probabilistic programs, through their symbolic abstraction, capture underlying causal and logical structures rather than just memorizing observed transitions. This allows agents to reason about and act effectively in conditions beyond their immediate training experience, which is essential for intelligence in dynamic, partially observable environments.

5. **Advancing Decision-Making under Uncertainty:** This research pushes the boundaries of how AI agents can handle partial observability. By providing a method to automatically learn POMDP components—which are notoriously difficult to specify—it opens up new avenues for tackling complex sequential decision-making problems in domains where sensory information is incomplete or noisy. This is highly relevant for fields such as autonomous navigation, human-robot interaction, medical diagnosis, and financial modeling.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work also lays a strong foundation for future research. The identified limitations, such as the reliance on human-designed representations, the post-hoc observability assumption, and scalability issues with particle filters, point to exciting avenues for further development. Addressing these could lead to more autonomous model discovery, improved inference techniques for high-dimensional spaces, and extensions to continuous state/action spaces, broadening the applicability of the framework to even more complex robotic manipulation and navigation tasks.

In conclusion, POMDP Coder represents a significant step towards creating more intelligent, adaptable, and transparent AI systems. By effectively blending the representational power of probabilistic programming with the generative capabilities of large language models, it offers a robust solution for learning interpretable world models in partially observable environments, with particular relevance and impact for the future of robotic autonomy.
