# Training Efficient Controllers via Analytic Policy Gradient

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a method for training control policies using Analytic Policy Gradients (APG) that achieves trajectory tracking performance similar to Model Predictive Control (MPC) with significantly faster inference times. The approach stabilizes APG training through a concurrent receding-horizon strategy and curriculum learning, outperforming Reinforcement Learning (RL) in accuracy, efficiency, and sample complexity across various robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides high accuracy but is computationally intensive, limiting its real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods offer fast runtime but often lack the precision of MPC for trajectory tracking and demand extensive training data.
- Analytic Policy Gradient (APG) approaches suffer from training instabilities, such as vanishing/exploding gradients and local minima, which hinder their practical application in complex robotic systems.

## Method

- A framework for offline control policy training is developed using Analytic Policy Gradients (APG) within a differentiable simulator, minimizing a fixed-horizon trajectory tracking cost function.
- A concurrent receding-horizon training strategy is introduced, where the policy network outputs all actions for the entire prediction horizon simultaneously, reducing gradient chain length and enhancing training stability.
- A curriculum learning scheme is implemented to stabilize the training process, gradually increasing the allowable divergence from reference trajectories and target speeds to promote robust learning.

## Results

- APG achieved tracking errors comparable to MPC (e.g., 0.05m for quadrotor vs. MPC's 0.03m) while reducing runtime by more than an order of magnitude (e.g., 0.17ms for quadrotor vs. MPC's 5.2ms).
- APG consistently outperformed both model-free (PPO) and model-based (PETS) RL algorithms in trajectory tracking accuracy and demonstrated significantly lower inference times than model-based RL.
- For vision-based control, APG achieved competitive performance (0.15 m/s cart velocity for CartPole) using only 1K samples, demonstrating superior sample efficiency compared to PPO (230K samples).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The concurrent receding-horizon training strategy provides superior stability and performance compared to traditional autoregressive APG approaches, especially for longer prediction horizons.
> - Curriculum learning is crucial for stabilizing APG training, preventing convergence to local minima and enabling the controller to learn robustly across varying initial conditions and task complexities.
> - APG exhibits orders of magnitude higher sample efficiency than both model-free and model-based RL, making it highly effective for learning directly from high-dimensional inputs and for rapid adaptation to new system dynamics.
