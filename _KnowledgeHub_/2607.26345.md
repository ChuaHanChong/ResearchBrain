# MetaKoopman: Bayesian Meta-Learning of Koopman Operators for Modeling Structured Dynamics under Distribution Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KTH Royal Institute of Technology and TRATON introduced MetaKoopman, a framework for modeling nonlinear dynamics using Bayesian meta-learned Koopman operators. It enables real-time adaptation to distribution shifts with quantifiable uncertainty, improving predictive accuracy and safety in autonomous systems like heavy-duty trucks.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems operate in complex, dynamic environments where system dynamics are non-constant due to factors like changing terrain or payloads (distributional shifts).
- Learning a single, universally accurate predictive model for such variability is challenging, and unquantified prediction errors can lead to unsafe system behavior in safety-critical applications.
- Existing meta-learning and adaptive Koopman approaches often incur high computational overhead or lack principled uncertainty estimation, limiting their real-time deployment and reliability.

## Method

- **MetaKoopman** frames ==Koopman operator== learning as a ==Bayesian inference== problem, using a ==Matrix Normal-Inverse Wishart== prior distribution for the operator and process noise, which is meta-learned across tasks.
- It employs a sequence-based embedding with a ==Transformer encoder== for past states/actions and an ==action encoder== for future control inputs to capture temporal dependencies.
- The framework enables ==closed-form Bayesian updates== for online adaptation and recursive propagation of mean and covariance for multi-step predictions, alongside a ==variational action encoder== for efficient trajectory generation in planning.

## Results

- MetaKoopman consistently achieved lower Mean Squared Error in multi-step prediction across diverse simulated environments and a real-world heavy-duty truck dataset compared to baselines.
- The system demonstrated effective online adaptation, enabling a **37.5-ton** autonomous truck to perform precise braking on polished ice and safe evasive lane changes on snow, which non-adaptive models failed to execute.
- It exhibited high Pearson correlation between predicted uncertainty and actual error, confirming well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, and reduced per-step inference runtime to **0.087 ms** using a variational action encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Bayesian meta-learning with Koopman operators allows for computationally efficient, closed-form online adaptation of dynamics models under distribution shifts.
> - Principled uncertainty quantification (both epistemic and aleatoric) through the Bayesian framework provides more trustworthy predictions essential for safety-critical applications.
> - A meta-learned tempering factor for the prior precision allows dynamic adjustment of prior influence, enhancing adaptability to diverse operational conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern autonomous systems are required to operate in complex and dynamic environments where system dynamics are not constant. Factors such as changing terrain, variable payloads, or mechanical degradation introduce distributional shifts, causing a mismatch between training and deployment conditions. Learning a single, universally accurate predictive model for such variability is challenging. In safety-critical applications, unquantified prediction errors can lead to unsafe system behavior. This challenge highlights a limitation of standard dynamics modeling approaches, which often assume a globally valid model.

The research landscape offers several approaches to address this:
- **Meta-learning** frameworks aim to enable models to adapt quickly to new conditions with limited data. However, many meta-learning methods, particularly gradient-based ones like MAML, can incur latency and computational overhead, limiting their suitability for real-time deployment in high-stakes scenarios.
- **Koopman operator theory** offers an alternative by representing nonlinear dynamics through linear latent-space operators. This approach facilitates efficient multi-step prediction and integration with classical control methods. However, traditional Koopman-based models are typically trained offline and remain static during deployment, which restricts their ability to respond to evolving dynamics.
- **Adaptive and time-varying Koopman operators** have been explored to address changing dynamics. Some methods introduce online neural modules to update operators or use global and local operators for non-stationary systems. However, these often involve online optimization or lack principled uncertainty estimation.
- **Bayesian approaches** in meta-learning and neural networks (e.g., Bayesian MAML, Bayesian Neural Networks) provide uncertainty quantification but can also be computationally intensive or rely on approximate inference methods.

MetaKoopman positions itself by integrating the structured efficiency and linearity of Koopman operators with the adaptivity and data efficiency of Bayesian meta-learning. This aims to overcome the limitations of static Koopman models and computationally expensive meta-learning frameworks, while simultaneously providing principled uncertainty quantification. The work specifically targets real-time online updates for robust decision-making in environments characterized by significant distribution shifts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop learning-based systems that can maintain accuracy and reliability even when the underlying system dynamics evolve due to distribution shifts. This is particularly crucial for safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving and robotic control, where small prediction errors can lead to compounded issues and unsafe operations, especially when models cannot assess or convey their predictive uncertainty.

The specific motivations and objectives that drive the MetaKoopman framework include:
- **Addressing Distributional Shifts:** Real-world autonomous systems encounter nonstationary environments where dynamics are continuously changing (e.g., terrain properties, payload distribution, mechanical degradation). The development of a model capable of adapting to these shifts is a core motivation.
- **Enabling Online Adaptation with Limited Data:** The goal is to facilitate rapid adaptation to new operational conditions using only short segments of recent trajectory data, bypassing the need for extensive retraining or globally valid models.
- **Achieving Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Deployment:** Many adaptive or meta-learning methods involve iterative, gradient-based optimization during deployment, which can introduce unacceptable latency. A key objective is to provide a framework that allows for online updates with minimal computational overhead, suitable for real-time control and planning.
- **Quantifying Uncertainty:** The framework aims to provide a closed-form posterior predictive distribution over future state trajectories. This is intended to capture both epistemic uncertainty (due to limited data or model knowledge) and aleatoric uncertainty (inherent system noise), offering a more complete and trustworthy assessment of predictions.
- **Leveraging Koopman Operator Structure:** By representing nonlinear dynamics linearly in a lifted latent space, the framework aims to capitalize on the analytical tractability and efficiency offered by Koopman theory for multi-step prediction and integration with classical control.
- **Demonstrating Effectiveness in Safety-Critical Applications:** The ultimate motivation is to validate the proposed approach on real-world systems, specifically a heavy-duty autonomous truck, to show that it can enable safer and more reliable closed-loop control, especially during challenging maneuvers and under adverse conditions.
- **Accelerating Trajectory Generation for Planning:** For model-based planning, efficiently generating a large number of dynamically feasible trajectories is essential. A specific objective is to mitigate the computational bottleneck associated with encoding actions, allowing for faster planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MetaKoopman is a Bayesian meta-learning framework designed to model nonlinear dynamics through linear latent representations, specifically Koopman operators, while accounting for distribution shifts and providing uncertainty quantification.

#### 3.1. Latent Koopman Dynamics Model

The framework approximates the infinite-dimensional Koopman operator by learning a finite-dimensional embedding function `g: R^n × R^m → R^d` that maps state-action pairs `(x_t, u_t)` into a latent space `˜z_t ∈ R^d`. In this latent space, the dynamics are modeled as approximately linear: `˜x_t+1 = K˜z_t`, where `K ∈ R^η×d` is the Koopman operator, and `˜x_t` is the state-related portion of the embedding.

Key aspects of the model structure include:
- **Sequence-based Embedding:** Instead of embedding single state-action pairs, MetaKoopman learns embeddings that capture temporal dependencies by using a `context encoder` (`G_θ`) to process a window of past states and actions (`[x_t-q, u_t-q, ..., x_t, u_t]`). This provides a more informative representation of local dynamics. The context encoder is implemented as a Transformer encoder.
- **Action Encoder:** Future control inputs (`u_t+1, ..., u_t+h`) are encoded using a dedicated `action encoder` (a Transformer decoder) to ensure consistency with the inferred latent dynamics.
- **Observation Matrix:** Predicted latent states (`˜x_t`) are mapped back to the original state space (`ˆx_t`) using a learned linear observation matrix `C ∈ R^n×η`.

#### 3.2. Closed-Form Bayesian Inference for Koopman Dynamics

To enable data-efficient and uncertainty-aware adaptation, Koopman operator learning is framed as a Bayesian inference problem.
- **Probabilistic Formulation:** Latent dynamics are modeled as stochastic: `˜x_t+1 = K˜z_t + ϵ`, where `ϵ ~ N(0, Σ)` represents process noise.
- **Conjugate Prior:** A Matrix Normal–Inverse Wishart (MNIW) distribution is placed as a conjugate prior over the Koopman operator `K` and process noise covariance `Σ`.
- **Closed-Form Posterior Update (Lemma 4.1):** Given observed latent transitions `(˜Z, ˜X)`, the posterior distribution `p(K, Σ | ˜X, ˜Z)` remains in the MNIW family, and its parameters can be updated analytically. This provides computational efficiency, avoiding iterative optimization.
- **Posterior Predictive Distribution (Lemma 4.2):** The one-step posterior predictive distribution `p(˜x_t+1 | ˜z_t, ˜X, ˜Z)` is a multivariate Student-t distribution. For practical purposes, and given a sufficient number of degrees of freedom (which is typically met), it is well-approximated by a multivariate Gaussian distribution. This approximation allows for closed-form recursive propagation of mean and covariance, enabling multi-step forecasting while capturing both aleatoric (via `Σ`) and epistemic (via `K`) uncertainty.

#### 3.3. Meta-Learning the Koopman Prior

Instead of manually specifying the MNIW prior parameters `{˘M, ˘V, ˘ν, ˘Ψ}`, MetaKoopman meta-learns them across a distribution of tasks (`T ~ ρ(T)`).
- **Meta-Objective:** For each task, data is split into an adaptation set (`D^tr_T`) and an evaluation set (`D^test_T`). The prior is updated using `D^tr_T` to compute posterior parameters, and the posterior predictive is evaluated on `D^test_T`. The meta-learning objective minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the ground truth in `D^test_T` under this posterior predictive distribution.
- **Online Adaptation with Tempering:** A learnable scalar `β ∈ (0, 1]` is introduced to temper the prior precision (`˘V` and `˘Ψ`) by scaling them with `β^-1`. This allows the model to dynamically adjust the influence of the meta-learned prior, enabling more aggressive adaptation to new data when `β` is smaller, or relying more on the prior when `β` is larger. `β` is optimized jointly with the other MNIW prior parameters.

#### 3.4. Uncertainty-Aware Planning with Variational Action Encoding (VAE)

To integrate MetaKoopman into a sampling-based motion planner for real-time operation, a variational action encoder is employed.
- **Cost-Guided Latent Action Sampling (CLAS):** The action encoder `q_ϕ(˜u_i | u_i)` models a distribution over latent actions, regularized to match a standard Gaussian prior `N(0, I)` via a KL divergence term in the loss function. At test time, this enables direct sampling of future action embeddings `˜u_i ∼ N(0, I)` without needing to pass raw actions through the encoder. This significantly reduces the computational cost of generating numerous candidate trajectories, as rollouts only require linear Koopman dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MetaKoopman was conducted across both simulated environments and a real-world autonomous truck system, comparing its performance against a range of baselines including Deep Koopman Operator (DKO), Gradient-Based Adaptive Learner (GrBAL), Bayesian MAML, Bayesian Neural Networks (BNN), Ensemble MLP (EMLP), and Neural ODEs.

#### 4.1. Predictive Dynamics Under Distribution Shift

- **Superior Accuracy:** MetaKoopman consistently achieved lower Mean Squared Error (MSE) in multi-step prediction across all evaluated environments. This included simulated tasks like HalfCheetah-Slope, Hopper-Gravity, Walker-Friction, Ant-DisabledJoints, Panda-Damping, HalfCheetah-Stationary, and a real-world truck dataset.
- **Faster Convergence:** The model demonstrated faster convergence to lower prediction errors compared to baselines in plots of validation MSE over time.
- **Robustness:** The consistent performance across diverse distribution shifts (e.g., varying slopes, friction, gravity, disabled joints) indicates MetaKoopman's robustness.

#### 4.2. Evidence of Meaningful Adaptation

- On the real-world truck dataset, the distribution of normalized MSE significantly shifted towards lower error values after a Bayesian meta-update of the Koopman operator using recent trajectory history. This indicates that the online adaptation mechanism effectively refines the dynamics model to align with evolving operating conditions (e.g., changes in surface friction), leading to improved predictive accuracy.

#### 4.3. Uncertainty Quantification

- MetaKoopman exhibited the highest Pearson correlation coefficient between its predicted uncertainty and the actual mean squared error across various datasets (Truck Dataset, Ant-DisabledJoints, HalfCheetah-Slope). This suggests that the model's uncertainty estimates are well-calibrated, with higher uncertainty correlating with larger prediction errors, which is a desirable property for safety-critical applications.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Tempering:** The inclusion of the meta-learned tempering factor (β) consistently improved predictive accuracy, especially in environments with pronounced distribution shifts such as truck dynamics and Ant-DisabledJoints. This demonstrates the effectiveness of modulating prior influence for better adaptation.
- **Effect of History Length:** Increasing the length of the trajectory history provided to the context encoder generally improved predictive accuracy. While longer histories yielded the best performance, diminishing returns were observed, indicating a trade-off between model expressiveness and computational efficiency.

#### 4.5. Closed-Loop Impact: Safer Planning Through Adaptive Uncertainty-Aware Dynamics

- **Real-World Deployment:** MetaKoopman was deployed on a 37.5-ton autonomous truck-trailer system during winter tests in harsh conditions (snow, ice, mixed-friction).
- **Precise Stops on Polished Ice:** In a scenario involving a transition to a low-friction ice patch, non-adaptive models mispredicted braking feasibility, leading to overshooting the target. MetaKoopman, by rapidly inferring the changing surface dynamics through online adaptation, enabled the planner to execute accurate braking, demonstrating adaptive braking behavior.
- **Evasive Lane Change on Snow:** When a virtual obstacle appeared on a low-traction, snow-covered road, non-adaptive models failed due to physically infeasible braking. MetaKoopman detected the distribution shift (indicated by increased prediction error) and adapted its dynamics model. This enabled the planner to accurately assess braking infeasibility and execute a safe evasive lane change to avoid collision, showcasing enhanced safety and robustness in critical maneuvers.

#### 4.6. Runtime Analysis

- The integration of the variational action encoder with Cost-Guided Latent Action Sampling (CLAS) significantly reduced the per-step inference runtime. MetaKoopman with the variational action encoder achieved the lowest runtime (0.087 ms) compared to all baselines on an NVIDIA A10 GPU, confirming its suitability for real-time planning by enabling efficient generation of numerous trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MetaKoopman represents an advancement in modeling complex nonlinear dynamical systems, particularly under real-world conditions characterized by significant and evolving distribution shifts. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Bridging Methodological Gaps:** The framework successfully integrates the computational efficiency and analytical structure of Koopman operator theory with the adaptive capabilities of meta-learning and the principled uncertainty quantification of Bayesian inference. This addresses a recognized limitation in current dynamics modeling where methods often excel in one area but are deficient in others (e.g., adaptive but slow, efficient but static).
- **Enabling Real-Time, Adaptive Control:** The use of closed-form Bayesian updates for the Koopman operator allows for fast, analytical adaptation to new dynamics, which is critical for real-time planning and control in latency-sensitive applications. The Variational Action Encoder further enhances efficiency by accelerating trajectory generation for planning.
- **Enhanced Safety and Reliability in Autonomous Systems:** By providing well-calibrated uncertainty estimates (both epistemic and aleatoric) over future predictions, MetaKoopman allows autonomous systems to make more informed and robust decisions, especially in safety-critical scenarios and at the limits of traction. The real-world deployment on a heavy-duty autonomous truck, demonstrating adaptive braking on ice and evasive maneuvers on snow, provides empirical evidence of this enhanced safety and reliability.
- **Robustness to Distributional Shifts:** The consistent outperformance of MetaKoopman across a diverse set of simulated and real-world environments with varying distribution shifts highlights its ability to generalize and adapt beyond training conditions, a crucial requirement for practical deployment.
- **Justified Modeling Choices:** The deliberate design choice to place uncertainty explicitly over the Koopman operator while keeping the encoder deterministic is argued to be effective in structured environments where dynamic changes are the dominant source of variability. This pragmatic approach balances comprehensive uncertainty modeling with computational tractability.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** The success of tractable Bayesian meta-learning in safety-critical systems opens avenues for future extensions. This includes integration with reinforcement learning for uncertainty-aware exploration, application to multi-agent systems, and deployment in partially observable environments, potentially leading to more intelligent and resilient autonomous agents.

In summary, MetaKoopman offers a practical and principled path toward deploying learning-based dynamics models in demanding real-world applications where systems must be both performant and trustworthy amidst dynamic environmental conditions.
