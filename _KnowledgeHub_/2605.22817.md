# Vector Policy Optimization: Training for Diversity Improves Test-Time Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Vector Policy Optimization (VPO) trains large language models to generate diverse candidate solutions by leveraging vector-valued reward structures and stochastic scalarization. This method consistently achieves higher best@k scores and maintains greater reward-space diversity compared to scalar reinforcement learning baselines across multiple domains, significantly enhancing performance in test-time search applications.

## Problem

- Standard reinforcement learning (RL) post-training methods often lead to LLMs producing low-entropy, mode-collapsed response distributions, hindering performance in search-augmented AI systems.
- The 'diversity collapse' in LLM outputs reduces the effectiveness of test-time search procedures (e.g., best-of-N sampling, evolutionary algorithms) which require a rich and varied pool of competent solutions.
- Traditional RL typically optimizes a pre-specified scalar reward, failing to leverage the naturally vector-valued reward structures present in many complex tasks.

## Method

- **VPO** utilizes multi-answer chains, where the LLM generates a set of 'm' candidate completions sequentially within a single autoregressive pass, allowing for in-context exploration and diversification.
- It employs set-level optimization through stochastic reward scalarization, sampling weighting vectors from a Dirichlet distribution for each rollout to incentivize policies that cover the Pareto frontier of underlying reward components.
- The method integrates as a drop-in replacement for the advantage estimator in ==policy gradient== methods (e.g., ==GRPO==), applying a set-level, scalarized reward based on the best candidate in the generated set.

## Results

- VPO consistently yielded higher best@k scores across all four evaluated domains (Maze, MuSiQue, EUREQA, ToolRL), with performance gaps relative to scalar RL baselines increasing for larger sample budgets 'k'.
- Analysis showed VPO maintained substantially higher reward-space diversity throughout training, while scalar baselines like Multi-RLVR often experienced early diversity collapse, confirming the necessity of stochastic scalarization.
- In a LiveCodeBench case study, VPO-trained models, when integrated into an evolutionary search loop (OpenEvolve), were able to discover solutions for problems that GRPO models could not solve, particularly under increased problem difficulty and search iterations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating the responsibilities of exploration (during training) and exploitation (via test-time search) by training LLMs for diversity dramatically improves their overall performance in search-augmented systems.
> - While multi-answer generation provides the capacity for diversity, a diversity-preserving reward signal via stochastic scalarization is crucial to genuinely incentivize the model to produce specialized and diverse candidates, rather than collapsing to redundant solutions.
> - Leveraging the intrinsic vector-valued nature of rewards, instead of collapsing them into a single scalar, allows models to implicitly learn solutions that excel along different objective dimensions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work by Bahlous-Boldi et al. addresses a challenge in the post-training of large language models (LLMs), particularly when these models are integrated into larger AI systems that employ inference-time search procedures. Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) post-training often optimizes a pre-specified scalar reward, which can lead to LLMs producing low-entropy, mode-collapsed response distributions. This characteristic hinders their performance in search-augmented settings, such as AlphaEvolve or best-of-N sampling, where a diverse pool of competent solutions is crucial for effective exploitation and problem-solving.

The existing landscape of RL research has extensively studied the exploration-exploitation trade-off, both in classical RL and in the context of modern foundation models. While exploration typically aims to discover new strategies during training, many recent AI systems augment LLMs with test-time search, shifting the burden of exploitation from the policy to the search mechanism. However, current RL post-training methods, such as GRPO, tend to concentrate probability mass onto a narrow set of high-probability responses, leading to candidate pools where additional samples offer diminishing returns due to redundancy. This "diversity collapse" is a recognized issue in RL post-training.

This research distinguishes itself from classical multi-objective RL, which often aims to learn policies conditioned on user-specified preferences or to explore unknown deployment objectives. Instead, VPO's objective is to enhance performance under a *fixed* deployment weighting by ensuring the policy generates diverse solutions during training that span the Pareto frontier of underlying reward components. It builds upon ideas from multi-objective RL and reward randomization, adapting them specifically for the capabilities and challenges of LLMs operating within search pipelines. It also contrasts with methods that train directly for best@k performance by emphasizing *how* candidate sets are generated and *what kind* of diversity is preserved during training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing Vector Policy Optimization (VPO) are:

1. **Addressing Diversity Collapse in LLM Post-Training**: Current RL post-training methods, which typically optimize a single scalar reward, tend to cause the policy to converge on a narrow set of responses. This results in low-entropy output distributions, where subsequent samples are often near-duplicates. This "diversity collapse" is suboptimal for scenarios requiring varied solutions.
2. **Enhancing Performance in Search-Augmented AI Systems**: Many complex AI applications do not rely on a single LLM output but rather embed LLMs within sophisticated search procedures. These procedures—ranging from simple rejection sampling with a verifier to complex evolutionary algorithms like AlphaEvolve—benefit significantly from a rich and diverse pool of candidate solutions from which to select or evolve. The low-diversity outputs from standard LLM training directly impede the effectiveness of such search mechanisms.
3. **Reframing the Role of RL Post-Training**: The authors propose a conceptual shift: rather than asking a single training algorithm to balance both exploration and exploitation, they advocate for separating these responsibilities. If test-time search handles exploitation, the role of RL post-training should be to exclusively focus on producing a *diverse set of competent solutions* for the search process.
4. **Leveraging Vector-Valued Rewards**: The core motivation for VPO stems from the observation that rewards in many practical tasks are naturally decomposable into multiple components (e.g., per-test-case correctness in code generation, multi-criteria ratings in RLHF, or per-sub-question success in multi-hop reasoning). Instead of collapsing these components into a single scalar reward, the authors propose exploiting this vector structure to encourage the model to produce solutions that excel along different reward dimensions, thereby covering a broader Pareto frontier of possible trade-offs. This approach aims to maintain a richer candidate distribution, allowing test-time search to extract increasingly better solutions as its sample budget grows.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vector Policy Optimization (VPO) is an RL algorithm designed to train LLM policies to produce diverse candidate solutions by leveraging vector-valued reward structures. It aims to prepare models for improved performance in downstream search procedures. VPO integrates two primary components: multi-answer generation within a single autoregressive rollout and set-level optimization via stochastic reward scalarization.

**Core Components of VPO:**

1. **Multi-Answer Chains as In-Context Exploration:**
 - VPO adopts a strategy where the language model generates a set of *m* candidate completions, denoted as $S = \{y_1, \ldots, y_m\}$, within a single autoregressive pass.
 - These completions are produced sequentially, with a delimiter separating each candidate. This sequential generation allows later candidates ($y_i$) to attend to and implicitly learn from the preceding candidates ($y_1, \ldots, y_{i-1}$) in the same rollout. This mechanism grants the model the capacity to identify covered regions of the solution space and deliberately steer subsequent candidates towards alternative, uncovered regions.
 - Unlike independent sampling, where diversity arises purely from stochastic decoding, multi-answer chains facilitate an explicit, in-context form of exploration.

2. **Set-Level Optimization via Stochastic Scalarization:**
 - To incentivize the generation of diverse solutions, VPO replaces the conventional fixed scalar reward weighting with a dynamic distribution over scalarizations.
 - For each rollout, a weighting vector $w$ is sampled from a Dirichlet distribution $Dir(\alpha)$ (specifically $Dir(1)$ for a uniform distribution over the simplex in experiments).
 - The entire set of generated solutions $S$ is then evaluated by selecting the best-performing element under the sampled scalarization: $R(S) = E_{w \sim Dir(\alpha)} [\max_{y \in S} w^\top r(x, y)]$.
 - This objective directly rewards sets whose elements demonstrate specialization across different reward trade-offs. A set that collapses to identical responses will perform well only under a narrow region of the reward simplex, whereas a set that spans multiple trade-offs will yield higher expected rewards across many sampled scalarizations. This objective encourages the policy to cover the Pareto frontier of potential solutions.

**Integration with Policy Gradients:**
VPO is designed as a drop-in replacement for the advantage estimator in policy gradient methods. In the reported experiments, it is combined with GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization).
- For a given prompt $x$, a group of $G$ rollouts are sampled, each yielding a set $S^{(g)}$ of $m$ completions.
- Concurrently, $K$ scalarization weights $w^{(1)}, \ldots, w^{(K)}$ are sampled independently and identically from $Dir(1)$ and are shared across all $G$ rollouts in that group.
- The per-rollout Monte-Carlo reward $\hat{R}(S^{(g)})$ is calculated by averaging the maximum scalarized reward over the $K$ sampled weights: $\hat{R}(S^{(g)}) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \max_{s \in S^{(g)}} w^{(k)\top} r(x, s)$.
- The GRPO advantage is then computed based on this set-level reward and applied uniformly to every token within the rollout $g$.

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation of VPO was conducted across four distinct domains to assess its performance under varying multi-objective structures:
- **Maze**: A synthetic 9x9 navigation task with a 4-component reward (completion, gold, diamond, lava-avoidance), engineered to have conflicting objectives.
- **MuSiQue**: A multi-hop question-answering benchmark with a 5-component reward (four binary citation indicators and a continuous answer-F1).
- **EUREQA**: A 5-hop chain-reasoning task with a 5-component binary reward (per-entity exact match).
- **ToolRL**: A function-calling benchmark with a 4-component reward (binary structural format and three continuous F1 dimensions).
Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B models were used. All experiments used $m=3$ candidates per multi-answer chain.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
To isolate the sources of VPO's performance, it was compared against several baselines:
- **GRPO**: Standard scalar RL baseline, single-answer generation.
- **Multi-RLVR**: Uses multi-answer generation but with a fixed scalar reward, testing the impact of multi-answer chains alone.
- **Random-Weighting GRPO**: Uses single-answer generation with stochastic scalarization, testing the impact of random reward weightings alone.
- **Max-at-K Training**: Directly optimizes a best@k/max@k objective, representing a direct approach to inference-aware training.
- **MaxRL**: Another search-aware RL objective that employs additional sampling compute during training.
- **Goal-Conditioned GRPO**: Trains a policy explicitly conditioned on a target scalarization vector $w$.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Best@k**: The maximum scalarized reward (using the domain's GRPO training scalar $w^*$) obtained from a pool of $k$ sampled candidates. This metric directly assesses the effectiveness of test-time search.
- **Reward-space diversity**: The average pairwise L1 distance between the reward vectors of completions in the candidate pool. This quantifies the spread of solutions in the reward space, indicating how well candidates realize different reward trade-offs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Vector Policy Optimization (VPO) demonstrated its effectiveness in improving test-time search performance by fostering diversity in generated solutions, particularly when compared against scalar RL baselines.

1. **Improved Test-Time Search Performance (Best@k)**: Across all four primary domains (Maze, MuSiQue, EUREQA, and ToolRL), VPO consistently led to higher best@k scores compared to the strongest scalar RL baselines. A notable observation was that scalar GRPO performance tended to plateau quickly as the number of sampled solutions ($k$) increased, indicating a diminishing return from additional samples due to a lack of diversity. In contrast, VPO models continued to extract value from larger sample budgets, achieving substantially higher performance levels with increasing $k$.

2. **Necessity of Diversity-Preserving Reward Signal**:
 - The Multi-RLVR baseline, which incorporates multi-answer generation but retains a fixed scalar reward, was outperformed by VPO on best@k across all domains. This performance gap widened with increasing $k$.
 - Analysis of reward-space diversity revealed that Multi-RLVR's diversity often collapsed early during training on most domains, whereas VPO maintained substantially higher reward-space diversity. This finding suggests that while multi-answer prompting provides the *capacity* for diversity, the stochastic scalarization in VPO is critical for providing the *incentive* to generate genuinely specialized and diverse candidates.

3. **Insufficiency of Increased Compute or Normalization for Scalar Methods**:
 - Experiments on the MuSiQue domain showed that giving scalar GRPO and GDPO (Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization) three times the computational budget (number of rollouts) did not enable them to match VPO's performance. Even with more evaluator signal and better gradient conditioning, these scalar methods remained below VPO's performance, indicating that VPO's gains are not solely attributable to computational advantages or improved normalization.

4. **Ineffectiveness of Goal-Conditioned Policies for Diversity Generation**:
 - A goal-conditioned GRPO policy, designed to explicitly condition its output on a target scalarization vector $w$, was unable to match VPO's performance on the Maze domain. This policy often exhibited mode collapse and struggled to reliably translate text-encoded preferences into diverse, effective behavior, suggesting that explicit conditioning might not be as effective as VPO's implicit diversity incentive.

5. **Performance on Harder Problems and Sophisticated Search (LiveCodeBench Case Study)**:
 - A case study on LiveCodeBench, a challenging competitive programming benchmark, further highlighted VPO's advantages. While GRPO performed better on single-shot pass@1 (where diversity is not leveraged), VPO surpassed GRPO in best@k evaluations, with the gap increasing for larger $k$.
 - Crucially, when integrated into OpenEvolve, an evolutionary test-time search loop, VPO-trained models demonstrated the ability to discover solutions for problems that GRPO models could not solve, even after hundreds of search iterations. This indicates that VPO's benefits become more pronounced as problem difficulty increases and when combined with more sophisticated search mechanisms.

6. **Contexts Where VPO's Benefit May Diminish**:
 - The research identified a scenario where VPO's advantages are reduced: when the underlying reward components are highly collinear, effectively collapsing the multi-dimensional reward space into a near-scalar one. On UltraFeedback, where the reward model's components were found to be near-collinear, VPO performed comparably to scalar baselines in terms of absolute best@k, although it still maintained a larger headroom for improvement with increasing $k$. This supports the hypothesis that VPO is most beneficial when reward components offer genuinely distinct trade-offs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Vector Policy Optimization (VPO) represents a notable advancement in the training of large language models for complex problem-solving, particularly in systems augmented with test-time search.

1. **Reframing LLM Post-Training**: VPO advocates for a paradigm shift in RL post-training objectives. Instead of optimizing for a single, fixed best response, it proposes training models to produce a diverse pool of competent candidates. This separation of responsibilities—training for exploration and leveraging search for exploitation—aligns with the operational realities of many advanced AI systems.
2. **Enhanced Test-Time Search Effectiveness**: The primary impact of VPO is its ability to significantly improve the performance of downstream test-time search procedures. By providing richer and more diverse candidate sets, VPO allows methods like best@k and evolutionary search to explore the solution space more effectively, leading to higher-quality final solutions, especially as the search budget or problem complexity increases. This is critical for applications in code generation, multi-hop reasoning, and complex tool use, where iterative refinement and diverse approaches are often necessary.
3. **Leveraging Intrinsic Reward Structure**: VPO demonstrates a practical method for exploiting the naturally vector-valued structure of rewards that exists in many real-world tasks. Moving beyond a simplistic scalar aggregation of rewards, VPO's stochastic scalarization encourages the policy to implicitly map out the Pareto frontier of different objective trade-offs. This capability is significant for tasks where multiple criteria define "goodness," and different solutions might excel along different dimensions.
4. **Counteracting Diversity Collapse**: The research directly addresses the problem of diversity collapse, a known limitation of standard RL post-training methods that tend to concentrate probability mass onto a narrow set of outputs. VPO's mechanism helps maintain a broader set of reasoning strategies and solution types throughout training, ensuring that the model retains its exploratory capacity even after extensive optimization.
5. **Broader Applicability and Future Directions**: As test-time search becomes a more standardized component in AI pipelines, VPO's approach of optimizing for diversity may become a default post-training objective for foundation models. This could lead to more robust, adaptable, and generally capable LLM agents. Future research could explore more sophisticated methods for integrating multi-answer generation with other multi-objective RL techniques, investigate the optimal design of reward vector components, and analyze VPO's benefits in even more diverse and challenging domains. The insights gained from VPO also contribute to a deeper understanding of exploration dynamics and reward-shaping in complex RL environments.
