# Seeing is Not Reasoning: MVPBench for Graph-based Evaluation of Multi-path Visual Physical CoT

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, Los Angeles, introduce MVPBench, a benchmark designed to assess whether Multimodal Large Language Models truly understand the physical world or merely describe what they see. Evaluation on MVPBench reveals that even state-of-the-art models exhibit significant limitations in visual physical reasoning, and current reinforcement learning-based post-training methods can inadvertently reduce performance in these tasks.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs demonstrate impressive visual processing but their ability to perform genuine visual physical reasoning, beyond mere image description, remains unclear.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks for MLLMs often rely on synthetic data, allow textual shortcuts, lack sufficient visual grounding, or use training methods that prioritize conversational fluency over physical accuracy.
- Despite high performance on various benchmarks, MLLMs frequently fail at basic physical reasoning tasks, leading to an overestimation of their understanding of the physical world.

## Method

- Introduced MVPBench, a comprehensive benchmark with 1,211 examples across four domains (Physics Experiments, Physics Problems, Spatial Relations, Dynamic Prediction) to evaluate visual physical reasoning.
- MVPBench utilizes real-world visual scenarios, multi-image inputs for temporal context, and visually highlights salient objects to force reliance on visual inference.
- Developed a novel graph-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) evaluation framework that assesses reasoning quality, diversity (via multiple valid CoT paths), and efficiency, moving beyond final answer correctness.

## Results

- On MVPBench, no evaluated MLLM reached human-level performance in visual physical reasoning, with OpenAI o3 generally achieving the highest scores among models.
- Multi-image inputs led to substantial improvements for most models; for example, GPT-4o's CoT Quality scores increased by 44% and Diversity scores by 21% when given image sequences.
- The 'Spatial Relations' subset was the most challenging for all models, with open-source models averaging only 37.10 in quality scores, highlighting fine-grained spatial reasoning as a key bottleneck.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) consistently struggle with genuine visual physical reasoning, indicating a gap between descriptive abilities and true understanding.
> - Providing multi-image inputs that offer temporal context significantly improves MLLM performance in physical reasoning tasks, suggesting the importance of sequential visual information.
> - Reinforcement learning-based post-training, commonly used for MLLM alignment, can surprisingly degrade visual-physics reasoning capabilities, pointing to a potential misalignment in current reward functions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like GPT-4o and OpenAI o3 demonstrate impressive capabilities in processing visual information and generating human-like text. However, a critical question remains: do these models truly understand the physical world, or are they simply sophisticated pattern matchers that can describe what they see without genuine reasoning?

This paper by Dong et al. tackles this fundamental question by introducing MVPBench, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate whether MLLMs can perform genuine visual physical reasoning rather than mere image description. The research reveals a concerning gap between what models can see and what they can reason about, challenging prevailing assumptions about current AI capabilities in understanding the physical world.

### 2. Research Context and Motivation

The motivation for this work stems from a simple yet revealing observation: despite their impressive performance on various benchmarks, state-of-the-art MLLMs often fail at basic physical reasoning tasks. The authors demonstrate this with a "one-minute sanity check" where GPT-4o incorrectly determined the direction of a car's movement from visual cues, generating plausible-sounding but physically incorrect explanations.

This failure highlights a broader issue in the field. Current evaluation benchmarks suffer from several limitations:

- **Synthetic data reliance**: Many benchmarks use game-engine videos or CAD renderings that don't capture real-world complexity
- **Textual shortcuts**: Questions often allow models to rely on language-based common sense rather than visual understanding
- **Insufficient visual grounding**: Existing benchmarks don't require models to link reasoning steps to specific visual evidence
- **Misaligned training**: Reinforcement learning-based post-training may prioritize conversational fluency over physical accuracy

The authors argue that these limitations create a "weak compass" for evaluating true visual physical reasoning capabilities, leading to overestimation of model performance and misdirected research efforts.

### 3. The MVPBench Framework

MVPBench addresses these limitations through a carefully designed benchmark comprising 1,211 examples across four distinct domains:

#### 3.1. Physics Experiments (440 questions)

This subset features real-world physics experiments extracted from educational videos. Key frames showing initial setup, intermediate steps, and final results form multi-image inputs. The experiments span five categories:
- Mechanics
- Thermodynamics
- Electromagnetism
- Optics
- Kinematics

Importantly, textual cues are removed and salient objects are visually highlighted to force reliance on visual inference rather than textual shortcuts.

#### 3.2. Physics Problems (311 questions)

These are challenging, visually grounded physics problems sourced from academic examinations including Chinese college entrance exams and International Physics Olympiad questions. The problems require advanced symbolic reasoning tightly coupled with visual diagram interpretation.

#### 3.3. Spatial Relations (400 questions)

This subset assesses spatial perception and 3D understanding through four types of tasks:
- Direction judgment
- Distance estimation
- First-person view transformation
- Topological relation judgment

#### 3.4. Dynamic Prediction (100 questions)

These questions evaluate models' ability to predict time-varying physical outcomes using temporally spaced key frames. Categories include:
- Multi-object collision
- Liquid diversion
- Physical state prediction
- Shadow transformation prediction

A crucial innovation in MVPBench is the annotation of multiple valid Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paths for each instance, reflecting the reality that complex physical reasoning often has multiple correct approaches.

### 4. Graph-based Chain-of-Thought Evaluation

Traditional evaluation methods focus primarily on final answer correctness, missing crucial information about the quality of reasoning processes. The authors introduce a comprehensive evaluation framework with three dimensions:

#### 4.1. CoT Quality Evaluation

- **Step Accuracy Score (SAS)**: Measures the proportion of correct atomic reasoning steps
- **CoT Reasoning Score (CRS)**: Combines step accuracy with final answer correctness
- **Key Step Coverage (KSC)**: Assesses how well models cover essential reasoning steps

#### 4.2. CoT Diversity Evaluation

This novel graph-based approach evaluates models' ability to generate multiple valid reasoning paths:

1. **Reference Graph Construction**: Ground truth CoT paths are converted into directed graphs where reasoning steps are nodes and logical flows are edges
2. **Model Path Embedding**: Generated reasoning steps are mapped onto the reference graph
3. **Path Matching**: Three metrics assess the quality of path matching:
 - **Path Validity Rate (PVR)**: Proportion of model edges matching reference graph edges
 - **Path Coverage Score (PCS)**: Length of longest matched sub-path
 - **CoT Match Score (CMS)**: Harmonic mean of PVR and PCS

#### 4.3. CoT Efficiency Evaluation

- **Step Relevance Score (SRS)**: Measures proportion of steps directly contributing to problem solving
- **Reflection Validity Rate (RVR)**: Assesses quality of self-correction attempts

This multi-faceted evaluation provides unprecedented insight into how models reason, not just whether they reach correct conclusions.

### 5. Key Findings

The evaluation of various MLLMs on MVPBench revealed several significant insights:

#### 5.1. Models Struggle with Visual Physical Reasoning

Even state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o and OpenAI o3 show notable limitations in visual physical reasoning. While OpenAI o3 generally achieved the highest scores, no model reached human-level performance, confirming that "seeing is not reasoning."

#### 5.2. Multi-image Inputs Are Crucial

Providing temporal context through image sequences significantly improved performance across most models. GPT-4o showed particularly striking improvements with CoT Quality scores increasing by 44% and Diversity scores by 21% when given multi-image inputs.

#### 5.3. RL-based Post-training Can Harm Performance

A surprising finding revealed that reinforcement learning-based post-training, commonly believed to improve reasoning, actually reduced visual-physics performance. Models like InternVL2.5-78B-MPO underperformed their base versions, suggesting current reward designs may sacrifice grounded reasoning for conversational fluency.

#### 5.4. Quality vs Diversity Trade-off

High diversity in CoT paths doesn't guarantee reasoning accuracy. Models with fewer but higher-quality reasoning paths often outperformed those generating many diverse but less accurate paths.

#### 5.5. Category-specific Challenges

The "Spatial Relations" subset proved most challenging across all models, with open-source models averaging only 37.10 quality scores. This indicates that fine-grained spatial reasoning remains a persistent bottleneck in current MLLMs.

### 6. Implications for Future Research

The findings have several important implications for the field:

#### 6.1. Rethinking Model Architecture and Training

Future MLLMs need enhanced visual grounding capabilities and better integration of causal reasoning structures. The detrimental effects of current RL alignment suggest a need for new reward function designs that prioritize physical accuracy alongside conversational quality.

#### 6.2. Benchmark Design Standards

MVPBench establishes new standards for evaluation complexity, emphasizing the importance of:
- Real-world visual scenarios
- Multi-modal reasoning requirements
- Comprehensive process evaluation beyond final answers
- Multiple valid solution paths

#### 6.3. Applications in Embodied AI

The benchmark provides crucial evaluation tools for embodied AI systems that must interact safely with physical environments. Understanding these reasoning limitations is essential for developing reliable robotic systems.

#### 6.4. Educational and Scientific Applications

Improved visual physical reasoning capabilities could revolutionize AI applications in education and scientific research, enabling more accurate explanations of physical phenomena and better hypothesis generation tools.

### 7. Conclusion

"Seeing is Not Reasoning" presents a sobering assessment of current MLLM capabilities in visual physical reasoning. By introducing MVPBench and its comprehensive evaluation framework, the authors provide both a diagnostic tool for current limitations and a roadmap for future improvements.

The work challenges the field to move beyond surface-level visual processing toward genuine physical understanding. The surprising finding that current alignment techniques may actually harm reasoning capabilities calls for fundamental reconsiderations of training methodologies.

Most importantly, this research establishes that the journey from seeing to reasoning requires more than simply scaling up existing approaches. It demands new architectural innovations, training objectives, and evaluation standards that prioritize grounded understanding over fluent description. As the field progresses toward more capable AI systems, MVPBench provides an essential compass for measuring genuine progress in visual physical reasoning.
