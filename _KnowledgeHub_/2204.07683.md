# Safe Self-Refinement for Transformer-based Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Safe Self-Refinement for Transformer-based Domain Adaptation (SSRT), integrates Vision Transformers with a robust self-refinement and adaptive 'safe training' mechanism to improve performance in Unsupervised Domain Adaptation. This approach achieved 45.2% average accuracy on the challenging DomainNet dataset, demonstrating enhanced stability and effectiveness across large domain gaps.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks require extensive labeled data, which is costly, leading to the challenge of adapting models trained on a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain (Unsupervised Domain Adaptation).
- Existing UDA methods, primarily based on CNNs, struggle to bridge large domain gaps, showing relatively low performance on complex, large-scale datasets like DomainNet.
- Despite their powerful feature learning capabilities, Vision Transformers have been largely underexplored in UDA, and their high capacity can make them susceptible to model collapse when faced with noisy target domain predictions.

## Method

- The ==Safe Self-Refinement for Transformer-based Domain Adaptation (SSRT)== framework utilizes pre-trained Vision Transformers (ViT) as its backbone for robust feature extraction.
- SSRT employs a multi-faceted ==self-refinement strategy==, applying ==random offsets to latent token sequences== within transformer blocks and using a ==bi-directional self-refinement loss== for consistency regularization, filtered by prediction confidence.
- A =='Safe Training' mechanism== adaptively adjusts perturbation strength and self-refinement loss weight by monitoring prediction diversity, restoring model states and resetting parameters if diversity drops to prevent model collapse.

## Results

- SSRT-B achieved state-of-the-art performance on Office-Home (**85.43%**), VisDA-2017 (**88.76%**), and a substantial **45.2%** average accuracy on DomainNet, demonstrating significant improvements over prior methods.
- A simple adversarial adaptation with a ViT-B backbone (**38.5%** on DomainNet) already surpassed the best CNN-based methods (**33.3%**), confirming the strong transferable representations of Vision Transformers.
- Ablation studies showed that multi-layer perturbation, bi-directional self-refinement, and especially the 'Safe Training' mechanism, were critical for SSRT's performance and preventing model collapse, particularly on tasks with large domain gaps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Transformers learn highly transferable features that provide a strong foundation for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, often outperforming CNNs even with basic adaptation techniques.
> - Applying perturbations to latent feature representations within transformer blocks is more effective for consistency regularization than perturbing raw input images, enhancing robustness for high-capacity models.
> - Adaptive monitoring of prediction diversity and dynamic adjustment of training parameters are crucial for stable training, effectively preventing model collapse in challenging UDA tasks with large domain shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)

Deep neural networks typically require vast amounts of labeled data, which is often expensive and time-consuming to acquire. Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) addresses this challenge by enabling a model trained on a label-rich "source" domain to perform tasks on a related, but unlabeled, "target" domain. This is particularly crucial when there is a "domain gap" – a shift in data distribution between the source and target. Common UDA paradigms include discrepancy-based methods, which minimize the statistical divergence between source and target features, and adversarial adaptation, which uses a domain discriminator to learn domain-invariant feature representations. While UDA has achieved impressive results on medium-sized benchmarks using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) like ResNet, performance on large-scale, challenging datasets such as DomainNet has remained relatively low, indicating a persistent difficulty in bridging significant domain gaps.

#### 1.2. Vision Transformers (ViT) in Vision Tasks

The emergence of Vision Transformers (ViT) has marked a significant shift in computer vision. Initially designed for natural language processing, the transformer architecture, with its powerful self-attention mechanisms, has demonstrated remarkable success when adapted for image-related tasks. By processing images as sequences of patches (tokens) and leveraging global self-attention, ViTs can capture long-range dependencies and learn highly discriminative and generalizable feature representations. Pre-trained on massive datasets like ImageNet-21k, ViTs have shown superior performance in tasks such as image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation compared to traditional CNNs, particularly in scenarios benefiting from extensive pre-training and global context understanding.

#### 1.3. Gap in Existing UDA Research

Despite the widespread success of Vision Transformers in various vision tasks, their application in Unsupervised Domain Adaptation has been largely underexplored. Most state-of-the-art UDA methods continue to rely on CNN backbones. The authors identify two critical gaps:
1. **Under-utilization of powerful backbones:** The potential of ViTs to learn more transferable features for UDA, especially across large domain gaps, has not been fully leveraged.
2. **Lack of robust adaptation strategies for large domain gaps:** Strong backbones like ViTs, with their high model capacity, are susceptible to overfitting to the source domain. Existing self-training or consistency regularization techniques, while helpful, can be sensitive to noisy target domain predictions, leading to model collapse when domain gaps are substantial. This necessitates a "safe" adaptation strategy that can prevent such failures.

This paper directly addresses these gaps by integrating ViTs into UDA and proposing a novel "safe" self-refinement mechanism designed to handle challenging adaptation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly improve the performance of Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, particularly in challenging scenarios characterized by large domain gaps. The authors aim to achieve this through a novel solution named SSRT (Safe Self-Refinement for Transformer-based domain adaptation).

The core motivations behind SSRT are:

1. **Leveraging Vision Transformers for Enhanced Feature Representation:** Recognizing the superior feature learning capabilities and transferability of Vision Transformers (ViT) over traditional CNNs, the authors hypothesize that integrating a ViT backbone into UDA will yield more robust and discriminative domain-invariant representations. This is motivated by the observation that even a simple combination of ViT with adversarial adaptation already surpasses CNN-based state-of-the-art on complex benchmarks like DomainNet.
2. **Ensuring "Safe" Knowledge Transfer:** Large domain gaps and the high capacity of ViT models increase the risk of "model collapse" – where the model's predictions on the target domain become overconfident but incorrect, leading to a breakdown in learning. The authors are motivated to develop a reliable strategy that safeguards the learning process, making it resilient to noisy target domain supervisions. This involves designing mechanisms to adaptively adjust training parameters and prevent performance degradation.
3. **Refining Models with Perturbed Target Data:** Drawing inspiration from consistency regularization in semi-supervised learning, the authors seek to refine the model by ensuring consistent predictions for perturbed versions of target domain data. However, unlike traditional methods that perturb raw images, they aim to apply perturbations in the latent feature space of the transformer, which is hypothesized to be more effective and robust for the ViT architecture.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of UDA by introducing a powerful backbone and, crucially, a mechanism to ensure stable and effective adaptation even in the most difficult cross-domain tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (SSRT)

The proposed solution, SSRT (Safe Self-Refinement for Transformer-based Domain Adaptation), introduces a novel framework that combines a Vision Transformer backbone with a sophisticated safe self-refinement strategy. The overall objective function for UDA, building on adversarial adaptation, is formulated as $min_{f,g} max_{d} L = L_{CE} - L_d + \beta L_{tgt}$, where $L_{CE}$ is source cross-entropy loss, $L_d$ is domain adversarial loss, and $L_{tgt}$ is a target domain loss, which SSRT instantiates as a self-refinement loss ($L_{SR}$).

#### 3.1. Transformer Backbone Integration

SSRT employs pre-trained Vision Transformers (ViT-B/16 or ViT-S/16, pre-trained on ImageNet-21k and fine-tuned on ImageNet-1k) as its feature extractor backbone. The ViT processes input images by converting them into sequences of tokens via a patch embedding layer, followed by multiple transformer blocks that refine these representations using global self-attention. A classifier head then takes the special class token to output label predictions. The powerful representation capabilities of ViT are central to SSRT's performance gains.

#### 3.2. Safe Self-Refinement Strategy

To leverage unlabeled target domain data effectively and safely, SSRT introduces a multi-faceted self-refinement strategy:

#### 3.2.1. Multi-layer Perturbation

Instead of perturbing raw input images, SSRT applies random offsets to the latent token sequences within the transformer blocks. The intuition is that perturbing at hidden layers can be more effective for complex architectures like ViT. Since the optimal layer for perturbation can vary, SSRT randomly selects one from a set of predefined layers (e.g., {0, 4, 8} for ViT) to inject the offset. The perturbed token sequence $\tilde{b}^l_x$ for the $l$-th transformer block's input $b^l_x$ is derived by adding an offset derived from another randomly chosen target image's token sequence: $\tilde{b}^l_x = b^l_x + \alpha[b^l_{x_r} - b^l_x]_\times$, where $\alpha$ is a scalar and $[\cdot]_\times$ indicates no gradient back-propagation through the offset. This ensures regularization across multiple layers and implicitly leverages structural information from other target samples.

#### 3.2.2. Bi-directional Self-Refinement

The core of the self-refinement is to minimize the discrepancy between predictions of an original target sample ($p_x$) and its perturbed version ($\tilde{p}_x$). Unlike conventional consistency regularization that often uses asymmetric KL divergence (teacher-student), SSRT proposes a bi-directional self-refinement loss:
$L_{SR} = \mathbb{E}_{B_t \sim \mathcal{D}_t} \left[ \omega \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{F}[B_t; p]} D_{KL}(p_x \| \tilde{p}_x) + (1-\omega) \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{F}[B_t; \tilde{p}]} D_{KL}(\tilde{p}_x \| p_x) \right]$
where $\omega$ is a random variable (e.g., from Bernoulli(0.5)). This bi-directional approach is found to be more robust, especially when predictions are noisy. Crucially, gradients are back-propagated through *both* teacher and student probabilities, as well as through $b^l_x$ in the perturbation equation, which prevents excessively large gradients and contributes to stability.

#### 3.2.3. Confidence Filter

To mitigate the impact of noisy predictions on the unlabeled target domain, SSRT employs a confidence filter. Only target samples whose maximum predicted probability (i.e., confidence) exceeds a predefined threshold $\epsilon$ are used for calculating the self-refinement loss. This ensures that only "reliable" pseudo-labels contribute to the model's refinement, preventing the propagation of erroneous self-supervision.

#### 3.3. Safe Training Mechanism

A critical innovation of SSRT is its "Safe Training" mechanism, designed to prevent model collapse due to overly aggressive perturbation or loss weighting, especially with large domain gaps. This mechanism adaptively adjusts the perturbation scalar ($\alpha$) and the self-refinement loss weight ($\beta$) during training.

The core idea is to monitor the "diversity" of model predictions on target domain data. When the model begins to collapse, its predictions tend to become overly confident and converge to a few classes, leading to a sharp drop in prediction diversity. The diversity measure used is the number of unique predicted labels in a target mini-batch.

The safe training mechanism operates as follows:
- An adaptive scalar $r \in$ modulates $\alpha$ and $\beta$ ($ \alpha_r = r\alpha, \beta_r = r\beta$). Initially, $r$ ramps up to 1.0 over a fixed period $T$.
- At regular intervals, the diversity of model predictions within that interval is checked.
- If a significant drop in diversity is detected (checked at multi-scales), indicating potential collapse:
 - The model is restored to its state from the beginning of the last stable interval (the "last snapshot").
 - The ramp-up period $T_r$ is doubled if restoration occurred recently, preventing oscillation.
 - The start time for $r$'s ramp-up ($t_r$) is reset to the current step, effectively restarting the adaptive modulation.

This adaptive adjustment and restoration process ensures that the model can recover from unstable states and continue learning safely, making the adaptation process more robust for challenging tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SSRT's performance was rigorously evaluated on four widely-used UDA benchmarks: Office-31, Office-Home, VisDA-2017, and DomainNet. The results consistently demonstrate SSRT's superiority, especially on large-scale and challenging datasets.

#### 4.1. Performance on UDA Benchmarks

- **Overall Superiority:** SSRT-B (using ViT-base backbone) consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance across Office-Home, VisDA-2017, and DomainNet.
 - **Office-Home:** SSRT-B achieved an average accuracy of 85.43%, outperforming previous CNN-based methods (e.g., SHOT at 71.8%) and even other transformer-based UDA methods like TVT (83.56%) and CDTrans (80.5%).
 - **VisDA-2017:** SSRT-B reached 88.76%, surpassing CNN-based SHOT (82.9%) and transformer-based TVT (83.92%) and CDTrans (88.4%).
 - **DomainNet (most challenging):** This dataset represents the most significant leap. SSRT-B achieved an impressive 45.2% average accuracy, a substantial improvement over the best previously reported CNN-based MDD+SCDA (33.3%) and transformer-based baselines (e.g., simple ViT-B with adversarial adaptation at 38.1%). The authors highlight that SSRT's gain of 6.7% over the strong ViT-B baseline on DomainNet is particularly noteworthy, indicating its effectiveness in bridging very large domain gaps (e.g., 29.3% average accuracy on tasks with 'qdr' as target domain, where other methods struggle).
- **Impact of Transformer Backbone:** Even a simple adversarial adaptation with a ViT-B backbone ("Baseline-B") significantly outperformed CNN-based methods. For instance, on DomainNet, Baseline-B achieved 38.5% compared to 33.3% for MDD+SCDA (ResNet-101). This confirms the strong transferable feature representation learned by Vision Transformers.
- **ViT-Small Performance:** Even with the smaller ViT-small backbone, SSRT-S achieved 38.4% on DomainNet, surpassing the larger ResNet-101-based MDD+SCDA (33.3%), demonstrating efficiency gains.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Component Analysis

The authors conducted extensive ablation studies to validate the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Multi-layer Perturbation:** Perturbing latent token sequences performed better than perturbing raw input images (e.g., 45.2% vs. 44.2% on DomainNet). Randomly choosing from multiple layers ({0,4,8}) was more robust and effective than perturbing any single fixed layer, which could lead to performance degradation on specific tasks.
- **Bi-directional Self-Refinement:** Comparing with uni-directional self-refinement (fixing $\omega=0$ or $\omega=1$), the bi-directional approach (random $\omega \sim B(0.5)$) demonstrated greater robustness and superior performance, particularly on challenging DomainNet tasks, especially when combined with Safe Training. Allowing gradients to back-propagate through both teacher and student probabilities, and through the latent token sequence ($b^l_x$), was crucial for optimal performance, preventing degradation.
- **Safe Training Mechanism:** This mechanism was vital for preventing model collapse on tasks with large domain gaps, particularly evident on DomainNet's clp→qdr task. Without safe training, the model's accuracy on the target domain dropped sharply after a certain number of iterations due to decreased prediction diversity. Safe Training successfully detected these drops, restored the model, and adaptively adjusted learning configurations, enabling stable and superior convergence. The adaptive scalar $r$ and diversity monitoring proved effective in navigating unstable learning phases.
- **Confidence Filter:** Removing the confidence filter (setting $\epsilon=0$) could lead to significant performance drops in certain tasks, emphasizing the importance of filtering out noisy pseudo-labels for self-refinement. Safe Training, however, could mitigate some of this degradation, making the overall method safer even without strict confidence filtering.
- **Comparison with SSL methods (Mixup, VAT):** While standard semi-supervised learning techniques like Mixup and Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT) improved performance over baselines when applied to UDA with ViT backbones, SSRT consistently achieved superior results. Mixup, in particular, struggled on some DomainNet tasks where SSRT thrived.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

#### 5.1. Significance and Impact

SSRT represents a significant advancement in Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, primarily due to its pioneering and effective integration of Vision Transformers and a robust "safe training" mechanism.

- **Pioneering ViT in UDA:** SSRT is among the first works to systematically explore and demonstrate the powerful benefits of Vision Transformers for domain adaptation. Its results establish ViT as a superior backbone for UDA tasks compared to traditional CNNs, especially on large-scale, complex datasets. This opens new avenues for future research in transformer-based domain adaptation.
- **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** The "Safe Training" mechanism is a crucial innovation. By adaptively managing training parameters and preventing model collapse, SSRT makes UDA more reliable and applicable to real-world scenarios where domain gaps are often unpredictable and severe. This addresses a major vulnerability in existing self-training and consistency regularization approaches that can fail in the presence of noisy target data.
- **Practical Implications:** The impressive performance on DomainNet, a dataset reflecting real-world complexities, suggests significant practical impact. For applications like autonomous driving, where data annotation is costly and environments are diverse, SSRT could enable more robust perception systems that generalize well across various conditions with minimal labeled data. The collaboration with XPeng Motors underscores this industrial relevance.
- **Generalizable Strategy:** The principles of multi-layer perturbation and adaptive safe training could potentially be generalized to other deep learning tasks involving self-supervision or consistency regularization, especially where model capacity is high and data quality varies.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its significant contributions, the paper acknowledges certain limitations and outlines directions for future research:

- **Unsaturated Performance on DomainNet:** While SSRT significantly advanced the average accuracy on DomainNet to 45.2%, this is still far from the performance achievable with fully supervised training, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- **Future Directions:** The authors propose two main avenues for future work:
 1. **Multi-Source Domain Adaptation:** Extending SSRT to leverage multiple source domains could provide richer, more diverse knowledge for adaptation, potentially further bridging large domain gaps.
 2. **Incorporating Meta Knowledge:** Integrating meta-knowledge about the target domains, perhaps through meta-learning or more advanced self-supervision techniques, could lead to more intelligent and effective adaptation strategies.

Overall, SSRT demonstrates a powerful and robust approach to UDA, marking a critical step towards deploying high-performance models in diverse, unlabeled target environments.
