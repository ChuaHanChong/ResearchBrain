# SketchVLM: Vision language models can annotate images to explain thoughts and guide users

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Auburn University and Adobe Research introduced SketchVLM, a training-free, model-agnostic framework allowing vision-language models to generate non-destructive SVG annotations directly on images to explain their reasoning. This method improved explainability and task performance, with `sketchVLM` achieving an RMSE of 5.92 pixels on Connect-the-Dots and a 95.5% annotation-text alignment score for Gemini-3-Pro-Preview.

## Problem

- Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) primarily provide text-only responses, making it challenging for users to understand or verify the model's spatial reasoning or sequential instructions.
- Existing visual explanation methods are often limited to specific domains, rely on destructive image modifications, or require external tools and extensive fine-tuning.
- The absence of human-like visual cues (e.g., pointing, drawing) in VLM outputs leads to ambiguity and reduced user trust in complex visual tasks.

## Method

- **SketchVLM** is a ==training-free==, ==model-agnostic== framework that enables VLMs to output structured stroke sequences in an ==XML-style format==, converted into non-destructive ==SVG overlays== on the input image.
- Utilizes ==visual prompting== (optional coordinate grids) and a detailed system prompt to guide VLMs in generating precise graphical annotations (rectangles, arrows, text labels, Bézier curves).
- Supports both ==single-turn== and ==multi-turn== interaction modes, allowing iterative visual explanations where previous annotations are fed back to the VLM.

## Results

- **SketchVLM** (Gemini-3-Pro-Preview) achieved an **RMSE of 5.92 pixels** on Connect-the-Dots, outperforming fine-tuned models and accurately localizing up to **35 points**.
- Significantly higher annotation-text alignment (**95.5%** for Gemini-3-Pro-Preview, **94.2%** for GPT-5) compared to baselines.
- On unseen physics tasks (VPCT, Ball Drop), achieved **96.0%** and **79.7%** accuracy, where fine-tuned models performed near random chance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-destructive SVG overlays enhance VLM explainability and user trust by making visual explanations verifiable without altering the original image.
> - The ability to generate explicit visual annotations can intrinsically improve VLM performance on visual reasoning tasks that benefit from spatial grounding and sequential visualization.
> - A training-free and model-agnostic approach allows the framework to be widely applied to various state-of-the-art VLMs, leveraging their existing capabilities without significant retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as Gemini-3-Pro and GPT-5, typically provide responses to image-based queries solely in text format. This textual output can present challenges for users seeking to understand or verify the model's reasoning, particularly for complex visual tasks where spatial understanding or sequential instructions are critical. Humans, in contrast, frequently employ visual cues like pointing, labeling, or drawing directly on an image to clarify their thought processes.

The existing research landscape addresses this limitation through several avenues:
- **Point Coordinate Emission**: Some VLMs (e.g., MoonDream, Molmo) can output point coordinates to reference objects, but they do not support free-form visual annotation for detailed explanations.
- **Trained Visual Reasoning Traces**: Other models are specifically trained to generate visual reasoning traces (e.g., MVoT, LatentSketchpad, DeepSketcher), but these often demonstrate limited generalizability beyond their specific training domains.
- **Image-Editing Models**: Models capable of image editing can visualize intermediate reasoning steps (e.g., Nano Banana Pro). However, they carry the risk of destructively altering the original source image, which can undermine user trust and lead to unintended modifications.
- **Tool-Calling and Code Generation**: Agentic systems (e.g., V*, Visual Sketchpad, PyVision) leverage LLMs to generate code that interacts with external vision tools (like segmentation or object detection) or manipulates images. While effective for fine-grained understanding, these methods typically involve external tools or code execution rather than direct VLM-generated user-facing annotations.
- **Visual Prompting/Sketching**: Approaches like SketchAgent append coordinate grids to images to aid spatial referencing, or Whiteboard-of-Thought uses LLMs to generate drawing code on a blank canvas. D2R interleaves text and visual drafts.
- **Fine-tuned Sketching Models**: Models like ViLaSR and ThinkMorph are explicitly fine-tuned from existing VLMs to produce visual sketches or annotations, either as SVG overlays or by directly editing the image.

SketchVLM differentiates itself by proposing a *training-free* and *model-agnostic* framework. It allows frontier VLMs to produce non-destructive, editable SVG overlays directly on the input image, grounding visual explanations without modifying the original pixels. This approach aims to combine the generalizability of state-of-the-art VLMs with the intuitive clarity of human-like visual annotation, without requiring extensive task-specific fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SketchVLM stems from the observation that current VLMs predominantly communicate through text, creating a gap between machine explanations and human intuition. When humans reason about an image, especially for tasks requiring spatial understanding, sequential steps, or detailed object identification, they naturally augment textual explanations with visual aids. These might include pointing, drawing circles, underlining, or labeling directly on the image. The absence of such capabilities in contemporary VLMs leads to responses that can be ambiguous, difficult for users to quickly understand, and challenging to verify.

The key objectives of this research were:

1. **Enhance Explainability and Verifiability**: To enable VLMs to visually explain their reasoning by generating graphical annotations directly on images. This aims to make VLM outputs more transparent and verifiable for users, fostering greater trust and comprehension.
2. **Non-Destructive Annotation**: To develop a system that produces visual explanations as a separate, editable layer (SVG overlay) on top of the original image, rather than destructively modifying the image pixels. This preserves the integrity of the original input.
3. **Model Agnosticism and Training-Free Operation**: To create a framework that can be applied to various state-of-the-art VLMs without requiring task-specific training or fine-tuning. This enhances the framework's adaptability and broad applicability.
4. **Improve Task Performance**: To investigate whether the ability to generate visual annotations can inherently improve VLM performance on visual reasoning tasks that benefit from explicit spatial grounding and sequential visualization.
5. **Support Human-AI Collaboration**: To facilitate more intuitive and effective human-AI interaction by allowing VLMs to guide users step-by-step through complex visual tasks using direct image annotations, mimicking natural human instruction.
6. **Evaluate Annotation Quality and Alignment**: To establish metrics and conduct rigorous evaluations of the quality of generated annotations and their faithfulness to the model's textual answer, addressing both visual plausibility and logical consistency.

By achieving these objectives, SketchVLM aims to bridge the communication gap between advanced VLMs and human users, making AI assistants more helpful and trustworthy in practical, image-intensive workflows.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SketchVLM is designed as a training-free, model-agnostic framework that integrates three core components to enable VLMs to produce SVG annotations: visual prompting, a structured system prompt, and XML-to-SVG conversion.

#### 3.1. Framework Components

1. **Visual Prompting**:
 - To improve precision for tasks requiring exact spatial referencing (e.g., Connect-the-Dots), a coordinate grid is optionally appended to the left and bottom edges of the input image. This grid is scaled to the image resolution, providing a visual reference for the VLM to specify x-y coordinates.
 - Ablation studies were conducted to assess the impact of this grid, revealing that it benefits some models (e.g., `sketchVLM`) but can degrade performance for others (e.g., `sketchVLM`).

2. **Input Prompt (System Prompt)**:
 - A detailed system prompt is provided to the VLM, instructing it to output structured stroke sequences in an XML-style format (e.g., `<s1>`, `<s2>`... `<sN>`).
 - This prompt defines various drawing primitives, including rectangles, arrows, text labels, straight lines, and Bézier curves. It specifies the required format for defining points (e.g., `xAyB`) and associated `t_values` for smooth curve fitting.
 - Rules are included to guide the model on producing coherent strokes, incrementing stroke IDs, and embedding final answers in a `<final_answer>` tag.

3. **SVG Conversion**:
 - The XML output generated by the VLM is parsed and converted into a standardized SVG format.
 - For strokes with exactly two points, a straight line is rendered. For strokes with multiple ordered samples, a smooth Bézier curve is fitted using a least-squares solution for control points and rendered as SVG.
 - This SVG output is then overlaid on the original input image as a separate, non-destructive layer.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Setup

1. **7 Benchmarks**: A comprehensive evaluation was conducted across seven diverse tasks:
 - **Connect-the-Dots**: Requires locating and connecting dots in sequence (RMSE, ordering error).
 - **Counting Objects**: Counting target objects and placing numbered markers (accuracy, marker location accuracy).
 - **Drawing Shapes around Objects**: Localizing objects by drawing rectangles or ovals (Average Precision at IoU 0.5 – AP50).
 - **Part Labeling**: Placing text labels at corresponding object part locations (localization accuracy with boundary dilation).
 - **Maze Navigation**: Tracing a proposed path and determining its validity (accuracy).
 - **VPCT (Visual Physics Comprehension Test)**: Predicting landing container of a dropped ball (accuracy).
 - **Ball Drop**: Synthetically generated physics task, predicting landing container and tracing ball trajectory (accuracy).

2. **Single-turn vs. Multi-turn**:
 - **Single-turn**: The VLM generates all annotations and the final answer in a single response.
 - **Multi-turn**: The VLM generates one stroke per turn, with the rendered image and text representations of previous annotations fed back as input for subsequent turns. The final text answer is given on the last turn.

3. **Visual Prompting Ablation**: Investigated the effect of providing a coordinate grid on drawing precision and task accuracy for different VLM backbones.

**4.3 Baselines**: SketchVLM was compared against three categories of state-of-the-art approaches:
1. **Default VLMs**: Text-only responses from Gemini-3-Pro (denoted as ) and GPT-5 (denoted as ).
2. **Image Editing Model + VLM**: Nano Banana Pro (denoted as ) for image editing, combined with Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (denoted as `++`) to provide a text answer based on the edited image.
3. **Fine-tuned Sketching Models**: ViLaSR (denoted as ) (fine-tuned from Qwen-2.5-VL-7B) and ThinkMorph (denoted as ) (fine-tuned from BAGEL-7B-MoT), both designed to produce image annotations.

**4.4 Metrics**: Three distinct aspects of model performance were measured:
1. **Accuracy**: The primary measure of correct task completion (e.g., correct answer to a question, correct count).
2. **Annotation-Text Alignment**: Assessed by a VLM-as-a-Judge (Gemini-3-Flash-Preview) which inferred the answer solely from the annotations. This metric measured how consistently the visual explanation supported the final text answer.
3. **Annotation Quality**: Evaluated using a VLM-as-a-Judge (Gemini-3-Flash-Preview) and, for a subset, human annotators. A 1–5 rubric assessed plausibility, visual clarity, and logical coherence of the annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SketchVLM across seven benchmarks yielded several findings regarding its performance, annotation quality, and efficiency compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Grid Prompting Impact

- For `sketchVLM` (GPT-5), the addition of a coordinate grid consistently improved spatial precision, particularly on tasks like Connect-the-Dots (RMSE decreased from 134.40 to 46.69 with grid and sketching prompt).
- For `sketchVLM` (Gemini-3-Pro-Preview), the grid was found to degrade performance, especially on Connect-the-Dots (RMSE increased from 5.92 to 99.34). Consequently, `sketchVLM` experiments utilized the grid, while `sketchVLM` experiments did not.

#### 4.2. Connect-the-Dots Performance

- `sketchVLM` demonstrated high accuracy in localizing and connecting up to 35 points, achieving an RMSE of 5.92 pixels. It correctly ordered points 99% of the time.
- `sketchVLM` had a higher RMSE (46.69) but still significantly outperformed `++` (198.74). `sketchVLM` correctly ordered points 74% of the time.
- Fine-tuned models (` ` and ` `) frequently exhibited ordering errors and sometimes altered the input image.

#### 4.3. Counting Accuracy

- `sketchVLM` showed strong consistency between counting accuracy (94.5%) and numeric-marker location accuracy (95.9%), indicating well-grounded explanations.
- `sketchVLM` achieved high counting accuracy (75.4%) but lower numeric-marker location accuracy (51.0%), suggesting correct counts with less precise marker placement.
- SketchVLM generally improved counting accuracy by +1.5 percentage points for ` ` and +3.4 percentage points for ` ` compared to their text-only counterparts.

#### 4.4. Object Localization with Shape Primitives

- Using pre-defined shape primitives (rectangles, ovals) for object localization generally yielded better results than free-form strokes.
- `sketchVLM` with stroke-based rectangles showed slight improvements for medium (+0.6 AP50) and large objects (+1.4 AP50) but significantly degraded performance for small objects (-10.1 AP50), reducing overall AP50 from 63.1 to 58.8.
- Analysis indicated that stroke-based outputs suffered from lower recall rather than precision issues.

#### 4.5. Part Labeling Accuracy

- SketchVLM improved part labeling accuracy for ` ` (+1.3 percentage points) but slightly underperformed ` ` at strict boundary matching (-3.8 percentage points).
- With increasing boundary dilation tolerance, the performance gap between `sketchVLM` and ` ` narrowed, indicating that most errors were minor spatial shifts rather than semantic labeling failures.

#### 4.6. Maze Navigation Performance

- `sketchVLM` and `sketchVLM` achieved high accuracies of 98.0% and 92.8%, respectively, on maze navigation tasks.
- Fine-tuned models (` ` and ` `) performed near random chance (50.8% and 62.5% respectively), despite being trained on similar tasks. `++` performed better at 93.3% but sometimes undesirably altered images.

#### 4.7. Physics Understanding Generalization

- Fine-tuned sketching models (` ` and ` `) demonstrated poor generalization on unseen physics understanding tasks like VPCT and Ball Drop, performing near random chance.
- In contrast, `sketchVLM` achieved 96.0% accuracy on VPCT and 79.7% on Ball Drop, showcasing strong reasoning and trajectory drawing capabilities.

#### 4.8. Annotation-Text Alignment

- SketchVLMs achieved substantially higher annotation-text alignment scores than all baselines. `sketchVLM` and `sketchVLM` had mean alignment scores of 95.5% and 94.2% respectively, compared to 71.6% for `++`, 46.8% for ` `, and 28.6% for ` `.
- This indicates that SketchVLMs' visual explanations are more faithful to their textual answers, improving reliability for user verification.

#### 4.9. Annotation Quality

- `sketchVLM` achieved the highest mean VLM-as-a-Judge annotation quality score of 3.70 (on a 1-5 scale), followed by `++` at 2.60, and `sketchVLM` at 2.26.
- Human ratings corroborated these findings, with `sketchVLM` scoring 4.14 and `sketchVLM` scoring 3.70, outperforming other baselines.
- Fine-tuned models (` ` and ` `) scored lowest, producing incoherent or difficult-to-interpret annotations, which correlated with their low task accuracy.

#### 4.10. VLM Judge Correlation with Human Judgments

- Human-human agreement on annotation quality was high (quadratic Kappa of 0.84 ± 0.04, Pearson r of 0.85 ± 0.04).
- Human-VLM judge agreement was moderate (quadratic Kappa of 0.51 ± 0.02, Pearson r of 0.52 ± 0.01). While the VLM judge was not as consistent as human evaluators, its ratings positively correlated with human judgments across all tasks and models, establishing it as a useful proxy metric.

#### 4.11. Single-turn vs. Multi-turn Efficiency

- Single-turn generation achieved comparable or higher accuracy than multi-turn across all tasks, while requiring approximately 5.92 times fewer turns on average.
- Providing the text representation of prior strokes in multi-turn mode was crucial for maintaining annotation quality; removing it led to noticeable degradation.

In summary, SketchVLM significantly enhanced the explainability and verifiability of frontier VLMs through non-destructive SVG annotations. It outperformed specialized fine-tuned sketching models in both accuracy and annotation quality across a range of tasks, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities and consistent alignment between visual explanations and textual answers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SketchVLM represents an advancement in the field of explainable AI for multimodal models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced VLM Explainability and Trust**: By enabling VLMs to visually explain their reasoning through direct image annotations, SketchVLM addresses a critical limitation of text-only responses. This capability allows users to quickly understand and verify the model's logic, which can increase user trust in AI systems, especially in applications where decisions are image-based or spatially complex.
2. **Improved User Guidance and Human-AI Collaboration**: The framework facilitates intuitive step-by-step visual guidance, mirroring human communication patterns (e.g., showing how to check car oil, setting up a cloud instance). This can significantly enhance human-AI collaboration in various domains, from technical support and instructional design to creative workflows and educational tools.
3. **Non-Destructive and Editable Annotations**: The use of SVG overlays is a key innovation. Unlike image-editing models that alter source images, SketchVLM's non-destructive approach preserves the original data and allows for post-generation editing of annotations, providing greater flexibility and control to the user.
4. **Model Agnosticism and Training-Free Nature**: The framework's ability to operate without task-specific training or fine-tuning makes it highly adaptable. It can be readily integrated with various frontier VLMs, leveraging their existing capabilities without the significant computational and data costs associated with fine-tuning, thereby promoting wider adoption and impact.
5. **Performance Improvements on Reasoning Tasks**: The research demonstrates that the explicit visual grounding provided by annotations can lead to accuracy improvements in visual reasoning tasks, suggesting that the act of "sketching thoughts" aids the VLM's internal reasoning process.
6. **Benchmarking for Visual Explanation**: The comprehensive evaluation across seven diverse benchmarks, including novel tasks and metrics for annotation quality and alignment, provides a robust framework for future research in visual explanation and multimodal reasoning. The validation of a VLM-as-a-judge for annotation quality offers a scalable evaluation method.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Performance on Smaller VLMs**: SketchVLM performs optimally with large, frontier models like Gemini-3-Pro and GPT-5, but its effectiveness diminishes with smaller VLMs that may struggle with complex instruction following.
- **Lack of Stroke Editing/Undoing**: The current framework does not allow models to dynamically undo or edit previously drawn strokes in multi-turn interactions, which could further improve collaborative performance and correctness.

Future work could explore:
- Integrating explicit stroke editing and undo functionalities to enhance multi-turn interaction and allow for more refined iterative annotations.
- Investigating methods to improve the performance of SketchVLM with smaller, open-source VLMs, potentially through more specialized prompting strategies or lightweight adaptation techniques.
- Applying SketchVLM to a wider array of real-world applications to further validate its utility and impact across different industries and user needs.

Overall, SketchVLM offers a practical and impactful approach to making advanced VLMs more interpretable and useful, moving towards more intuitive and trustworthy human-AI interaction.
