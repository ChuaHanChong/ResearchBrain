# What to Ignore, What to React: Visually Robust RL Fine-Tuning of VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PAIR-VLA introduces an RL fine-tuning framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, augmenting PPO with auxiliary objectives that provide explicit behavior-level guidance. It significantly improves out-of-distribution visual generalization, boosting average OOD success rates by 9.10 percentage points for OpenVLA and 16.62 points for π0.5 on robotic manipulation tasks, while enhancing fine-tuning efficiency approximately threefold.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit fragility to visual shifts encountered in real-world environments, such as variations in lighting, textures, distractors, or target object poses.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning and data augmentation methods often fail to provide explicit guidance on which visual changes to ignore and which to adapt to.
- Standard task rewards in RL fine-tuning are insufficient for policies to effectively discriminate between task-irrelevant and task-relevant visual variations, leading to suboptimal or incorrect manipulation behaviors.

## Method

- PAIR-VLA augments the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm with two complementary auxiliary objectives that provide explicit behavior-level guidance during fine-tuning.
- For each observed state, the method constructs two types of 'paired views': task-preserving views (e.g., altered backgrounds or removed distractors) and task-altering views (e.g., perturbed target object poses).
- It introduces an invariance objective to encourage consistent action distributions under task-preserving changes and a sensitivity objective to promote distinct action distributions under task-altering changes, integrated as KL divergence terms in the PPO loss.

## Results

- PAIR-VLA consistently improved average out-of-distribution (OOD) task success rates, with gains of 9.10 percentage points for OpenVLA (achieving 87.00%) and 16.62 percentage points for π0.5 (achieving 62.87%) across various visual shifts.
- The framework enhanced the efficiency of RL fine-tuning, allowing OpenVLA to reach a 90% success rate in approximately 80 training steps, compared to 240 steps required by standard PPO.
- An ablation study confirmed that both the invariance and sensitivity objectives contribute to performance, with the invariance objective alone yielding substantial improvements (+12.61 percentage points on average for π0.5), and their combination achieving the highest overall robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing explicit behavior-level guidance through auxiliary objectives during RL fine-tuning is crucial for enhancing visual generalization in VLA models, outperforming approaches that rely solely on observation diversity.
> - Separating visual changes into 'task-preserving' and 'task-altering' categories and then training for invariance and sensitivity, respectively, offers an effective strategy for improving robustness to out-of-distribution conditions.
> - The learned behavioral robustness demonstrates transferability to unseen visual changes, such as variations in lighting conditions or novel camera viewpoints, suggesting the policy acquires more abstract principles of visual relevance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm for general-purpose robotic manipulation, leveraging pretraining on large-scale vision-language datasets and diverse robot demonstrations. Despite their capabilities, a notable challenge for VLA models is maintaining robustness under visual shifts encountered during real-world deployment. These shifts can include variations in lighting, scene textures, the presence of distractor objects, or changes in target object pose. A key difficulty arises from the dual nature of visual changes: some are task-irrelevant, requiring the robot's behavior to remain consistent, while others are task-altering, necessitating an adjustment in manipulation strategy. Standard task rewards, often used in fine-tuning, typically do not provide explicit guidance for distinguishing between these types of visual changes.

Reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning has been explored as a post-training method to enhance VLA policies beyond supervised fine-tuning (SFT) by optimizing for expected task rewards through environmental interaction. However, existing RL fine-tuning approaches still exhibit limitations in generalization when faced with out-of-distribution (OOD) visual shifts at deployment time.

Current strategies to improve visual robustness in robot learning often focus on increasing observation diversity during training through techniques such as image-level augmentations (e.g., random cropping, color perturbation) or domain randomization (varying textures, lighting, camera poses, object placements). While these methods expose policies to a broader range of visual inputs, they generally do not explicitly inform the policy *how* its actions should respond to different types of scene changes – specifically, which changes to disregard and which to actively adapt to. Other approaches involve modifying observations at inference time, such as learning masks to suppress distractors or using image editing modules. These methods, however, introduce additional modules and computational overhead during deployment. Another line of research regularizes visual representations for invariance across perturbed views using methods like contrastive learning or information bottlenecks, but invariance in latent space does not inherently guarantee desired behavior at the action-distribution level.

This research addresses the gap by proposing a method that provides explicit *behavior-level guidance* during RL fine-tuning. This guidance distinguishes between visual changes that should elicit invariant actions and those that should prompt sensitive, adaptive actions, thereby aiming to improve visual generalization beyond the training distribution without altering the deployed policy architecture or inference costs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed fragility of VLA models to visual shifts in real-world environments, even after extensive pretraining and initial RL fine-tuning. The core problem identified is that standard RL fine-tuning, which typically relies on task success rewards, does not provide sufficient behavioral signal for the policy to effectively discriminate between task-irrelevant visual variations (e.g., changing background textures or distractor objects) and task-relevant variations (e.g., a change in the target object's pose that alters the required grasp or trajectory). Without this distinction, policies may either overreact to nuisance changes or fail to adapt appropriately when the underlying manipulation task truly requires a different response.

The key objectives of this research were:
1. **Formulate Visual Generalization as Behavior-Level Guidance**: To develop a conceptual framework where visual robustness in VLA RL fine-tuning is directly tied to guiding the policy's action responses to visual shifts, rather than solely relying on observation diversity or representation-level regularization.
2. **Develop a PPO-Compatible Framework**: To propose PAIR-VLA (Paired Action Invariance & Sensitivity for Visually Robust VLA), an RL fine-tuning framework that augments standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with auxiliary objectives.
3. **Encourage Dual Policy Behaviors**:
 - **Invariance**: To promote consistent action distributions when faced with task-preserving visual changes (e.g., different distractors, background changes).
 - **Sensitivity**: To encourage discernible changes in action distributions when encountering task-altering visual changes (e.g., a different pose of the target object).
4. **Improve OOD Generalization and Efficiency**: To demonstrate that the proposed framework can consistently enhance visual robustness and generalization to out-of-distribution visual shifts, and potentially improve the efficiency of the RL fine-tuning process.
5. **Maintain Inference-Time Efficiency**: To ensure that the auxiliary objectives are utilized only during the fine-tuning phase, such that the deployed policy architecture remains unchanged, incurring no additional inference-time computational cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, PAIR-VLA (Paired Action Invariance & Sensitivity for Visually Robust VLA), is an RL fine-tuning framework designed to enhance the visual robustness of VLA models. It operates by augmenting the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm with two complementary auxiliary objectives that provide behavior-level guidance during training.

**VLA Model Integration:**
The framework supports two representative VLA architectures:
- **Autoregressive Models**: Exemplified by OpenVLA, which inherently provide action log-likelihoods suitable for PPO.
- **Flow-Matching Models**: Exemplified by $\pi^{0.5}$, which typically generate actions through iterative ODE denoising. To apply PPO, the method follows prior work by converting the ODE denoising process into an SDE, formulating a two-layer Markov Decision Process (MDP), and thereby enabling tractable log-likelihood computations for PPO updates.

**Paired View Construction:**
A central component of PAIR-VLA is the generation of two types of perturbed visual variants for each observed state $o_t$:
1. **Task-Preserving View ($\tilde{o}^{prev}_t$)**: This view aims to alter task-irrelevant visual factors while maintaining the underlying task state and the required manipulation action.
 - **Main Construction**: Foreground-background compositing is employed. During training, a binary segmentation mask $m_t$ is obtained (identifying pixels belonging to the robot, target object, or receptacle). A background snapshot $o^k_{bg}$ is uniformly sampled from a pre-rendered set of backgrounds (created by rendering scenes with all objects invisible). The task-preserving view is then formed by combining the foreground from $o_t$ with the sampled background: $\tilde{o}^{prev}_t = m_t \odot o_t + (1 - m_t) \odot o^k_{bg}$. This process effectively removes distractors and changes background appearance without altering the core task. In a separate evaluation, an alternative task-preserving view for viewpoint variation was constructed by re-rendering the same scene state from a different camera pose.
2. **Task-Altering View ($\tilde{o}^{alt}_t$)**: This view modifies task-relevant properties of the target object, thereby potentially changing the required manipulation behavior.
 - **Construction**: The pose of the target object is perturbed. Specifically, small translation perturbations (sampled from a Gaussian distribution) and rotation perturbations (from a categorical distribution) are applied to the target object's pose. The scene is then re-rendered to obtain $\tilde{o}^{alt}_t$. This simulates scenarios where a different grasp or motion might be required.

**Auxiliary Objectives:**
These objectives are calculated as KL divergences between action distributions and integrated into the PPO loss:
1. **Invariance Objective ($L_{inv}(\theta)$)**: This objective encourages the policy to produce consistent action distributions under task-preserving visual changes. It minimizes the KL divergence between the action distribution of the original observation and that of the task-preserving view:
 $L_{inv}(\theta) = E_{(o_t,l)} [ D_{KL} ( \pi_\theta (\cdot | o_t, l) \parallel sg[\pi_\theta (\cdot | \tilde{o}^{prev}_t, l)] ) ]$
 The stop-gradient operator (sg[·]) is applied to the target distribution from the task-preserving view to stabilize training, treating it as a fixed target.
2. **Sensitivity Objective ($L_{sens}(\theta)$)**: This objective encourages the policy to produce meaningfully different action distributions when task-relevant changes occur. It maximizes the KL divergence between the action distribution of the original observation and that of the task-altering view:
 $L_{sens}(\theta) = -E_{(o_t,l)} [ min( c, D_{KL} ( \pi_\theta (\cdot | o_t, l) \parallel sg[\pi_\theta (\cdot | \tilde{o}^{alt}_t, l)] ) ) ]$
 A clipping threshold $c$ is introduced to prevent the divergence from growing unboundedly, which could destabilize PPO training. The stop-gradient is also applied to the target distribution here.

**Augmented PPO Objective:**
The overall training objective combines the standard PPO objective ($L_{PPO}(\theta)$) with these auxiliary terms:
$L(\theta) = L_{PPO}(\theta) + \alpha L_{inv}(\theta) + \beta L_{sens}(\theta)$
Here, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are coefficients that control the relative weighting of the invariance and sensitivity objectives.

A critical aspect of the methodology is that both the paired-view construction and the auxiliary objectives are utilized exclusively during the RL fine-tuning phase. This design ensures that the policy deployed at inference time uses its original architecture, without requiring any additional modules or incurring extra computational costs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PAIR-VLA was conducted on the ManiSkill3 simulator, focusing on a pick-and-place task involving visual distractors. The study utilized two VLA backbones, OpenVLA (autoregressive) and $\pi^{0.5}$ (flow-matching), both initialized from supervised fine-tuning (SFT) checkpoints. The experiments systematically assessed visual robustness under various out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Training Conditions**: Target objects, distractors, and table textures were sampled from predefined training sets. Initial positions of objects and receptacles were randomized on a $6 \times 6$ grid. Scene lighting was fixed.
- **OOD Scenarios**: Four distinct OOD conditions were tested:
 1. **Unseen Table Texture**: Table textures sampled from a held-out set.
 2. **Unseen Lighting**: Scene lighting varied using unseen configurations.
 3. **Unseen Target Object Pose**: Target object poses sampled from translation and rotation distributions different from training.
 4. **Unseen Clutter**: Increased number of distractors, with half sampled from a held-out object set.
- **Metrics**: Task success rate, calculated as the fraction of successful episodes over 128 evaluations, averaged across three independent runs.

**Main Results (OOD Generalization):**
PAIR-VLA consistently outperformed the standard PPO fine-tuning baseline across all OOD settings and for both VLA architectures.
- **OpenVLA**: The average OOD success rate improved from 77.90% (PPO) to 87.00% (PAIR-VLA), representing an absolute gain of 9.10 percentage points.
- **$\pi^{0.5}$**: The average OOD success rate improved from 46.25% (PPO) to 62.87% (PAIR-VLA), an absolute gain of 16.62 percentage points.
These results indicate that the auxiliary objectives effectively enhance visual generalization across diverse OOD conditions.

**RL Fine-Tuning Efficiency:**
Evaluation of OpenVLA checkpoints during training revealed that PAIR-VLA also improved fine-tuning efficiency.
- In an in-distribution (ID) scenario with one distractor, PAIR-VLA achieved a 90% success rate within approximately 80 training steps, whereas PPO required roughly 240 steps to reach the same performance level, indicating an approximate 3-fold improvement in efficiency.
- A similar efficiency advantage was observed in an OOD clutter scenario with four distractors. PAIR-VLA converged faster and achieved a higher plateau in both settings.

**Ablation Study:**
An ablation study using the $\pi^{0.5}$ backbone investigated the individual contributions of the invariance ($L_{inv}$) and sensitivity ($L_{sens}$) objectives.
- **Invariance Objective Only ($PPO\ w/\ L_{inv}$)**: This configuration yielded a substantial average gain of +12.61 percentage points over the PPO baseline. Notable improvements were observed under unseen lighting (+16.04) and unseen clutter (+13.54).
- **Sensitivity Objective Only ($PPO\ w/\ L_{sens}$)**: This configuration resulted in a smaller but consistent average gain of +1.73 percentage points.
- **Combined Objectives (Ours)**: The combination of both objectives achieved the highest performance across all OOD settings, with an average improvement of +16.62 points over PPO. This suggests that while the invariance objective provides the primary robustness signal, the sensitivity objective offers a complementary contribution to overall robustness.

**Analysis of OOD Generalization under Increasing Clutter:**
Further analysis showed that as the number of unseen distractors increased from 2 to 8, both methods experienced a decrease in success rate. However, PAIR-VLA consistently maintained higher success rates across all clutter levels, exhibiting a more robust degradation profile. For example, with 8 distractors, PAIR-VLA achieved 72% success on OpenVLA (vs. 56% for PPO) and 33% on $\pi^{0.5}$ (vs. 21% for PPO).

**Effect of the Invariance Coefficient $\alpha$:**
Investigating the weight of the invariance objective (while disabling sensitivity) showed that any non-zero value of $\alpha$ (tested at 1, 2, 4) consistently improved OOD generalization compared to PPO ($\alpha=0$). The best performance was observed at $\alpha=1$, yielding gains exceeding 10 points in each OOD setting and over 15 points under unseen lighting. This generalization to lighting changes, which were not directly used in the task-preserving view construction, suggests a broader transferability of the learned behavior-level guidance.

**Generalization to Unseen Camera Viewpoints:**
When PAIR-VLA was instantiated with a task-preserving view based on viewpoint variation (re-rendering the scene from different camera poses), it demonstrated improved generalization to OOD camera poses. While matching PPO performance within the training viewpoint range ($[0^\circ, 20^\circ]$), PAIR-VLA substantially outperformed PPO at unseen angles ($24^\circ, 28^\circ$), achieving 32.92% versus 20.63% at $28^\circ$. This indicates that the method can extrapolate effectively to novel viewpoints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces PAIR-VLA, a framework that provides explicit behavior-level guidance during RL fine-tuning of VLA models. This approach distinguishes between visual changes that should elicit invariant actions and those that require adaptive responses, addressing a core challenge in visual generalization for robotic manipulation.

**Significance:**
- **Behavior-Level Generalization**: The work highlights the importance of behavior-level guidance in RL fine-tuning, moving beyond reliance on observation diversity or representation-level regularization alone. This formulation allows policies to learn *how* to react to visual shifts rather than merely *what* to see.
- **Enhanced Visual Robustness**: PAIR-VLA consistently improves out-of-distribution success rates across diverse visual shifts, including unseen textures, lighting conditions, object poses, and varying clutter levels, for both autoregressive (OpenVLA) and flow-matching ($\pi^{0.5}$) VLA architectures.
- **Improved Efficiency**: The framework demonstrates an acceleration in RL fine-tuning, achieving higher performance with fewer training steps compared to standard PPO.
- **Zero Inference-Time Overhead**: A key practical advantage is that the auxiliary objectives and paired-view construction are confined to the fine-tuning phase. The deployed policy retains its original architecture, incurring no additional computational cost during inference.
- **Transferability of Guidance**: The ablations and cross-domain generalization results (e.g., to unseen lighting conditions without explicit lighting variation in paired views, or to novel viewpoints) suggest that the learned invariance and sensitivity behaviors are broadly transferable, indicating that the policy learns more abstract principles of robustness.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Increased Reliability of Robotic Systems**: By enhancing the robustness of robot manipulation policies to real-world visual variations, this work could contribute to the development of more reliable and deployable embodied AI systems, such as assistive robots, industrial manipulators, and autonomous agents operating in dynamic environments. Reduced brittleness under visual shifts could mitigate failures and improve task completion rates.
- **Advancement in General-Purpose Robotics**: The ability of VLA models to generalize to unseen visual conditions could further enable their application in open-world settings, reducing the need for extensive task-specific data collection or domain engineering.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The current study was conducted within a simulated environment, and the task-preserving view construction relies on ground-truth object masks, which are readily available in simulators. Future research could investigate:
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The transferability of the learned robustness from simulation to real-world deployment.
- **Real-World Paired-View Construction**: How segmentation masks estimated by off-the-shelf real-world segmentation models (e.g., SAM) would affect the construction of paired views and the resulting robustness gains in physical robots.
