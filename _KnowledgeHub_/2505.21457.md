# Active-O3: Empowering Multimodal Large Language Models with Active Perception via GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> Zhejiang University and Ant Group researchers develop ACTIVE-O3, a reinforcement learning framework using Group Relative Policy Optimization that enables multimodal large language models to perform active perception by strategically selecting regions for closer examination, achieving improvements of +2.7/+3.5 APs/ARs on LVIS small object detection and +8.5 APs on SODA-A autonomous driving tasks compared to Qwen2.5-VL baselines, while demonstrating zero-shot generalization on V* fine-grained understanding benchmarks through a dual-form reward system that combines task-aware performance metrics with heuristic constraints for region proposal quality.

## Problem

- Current multimodal large language models operate as passive consumers of visual information, lacking the ability to actively control their perception to gather more informative sensory data
- Existing active perception approaches like GPT-o3's zoom-in strategy suffer from inefficient region proposals and low target localization accuracy
- No systematic frameworks or evaluation protocols exist for studying and developing active perception capabilities in MLLMs
- Performance degrades significantly on visually complex tasks involving small objects, dense scenes, and fine-grained details

## Method

- Formalize MLLM-based ==active perception== as coordination between a ==Task Model== (deciding actions) and ==Sensing Model== (controlling perception parameters)
- Implement a unified two-stage policy separating region proposal (sensing) from task execution using MLLM instruction-following capabilities
- Apply ==Group Relative Policy Optimization== to enable MLLMs to improve sensing policies based on task-level reward signals
- Design ==dual-form rewards== combining task-aware metrics (average precision/recall, mIoU) with heuristic constraints (format validity, non-overlapping proposals, coverage measures)

## Results

- Consistent improvements across all evaluated tasks under fixed computational budgets, with particularly strong gains on small object detection (**+2.7 APs** on LVIS) and autonomous driving scenarios (**+8.5 APs** on SODA-A)
- Progressive performance improvement with increasing zoom-in budget on ThinObjects segmentation (mIoU reaching **0.863**), while baseline methods degrade
- Demonstrated zero-shot capabilities on V* benchmark for fine-grained understanding tasks without domain-specific training
- Comprehensive benchmark suite established covering open-world grounding, remote sensing, autonomous driving, and interactive segmentation with released code and evaluation protocols

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active perception can be effectively implemented in MLLMs through reinforcement learning without requiring architectural changes to existing models
> - Combining task-aware and heuristic rewards enables more robust learning of region proposal strategies compared to single reward types
> - MLLMs can leverage their inherent reasoning capabilities to implement sensing policies through strategic prompting rather than requiring separate specialized modules
> - Zero-shot generalization emerges naturally from the active perception framework, enabling transfer to unseen domains and tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a gap in the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) within the context of active perception. Active perception, defined as the process of actively selecting where and how to look to gather task-relevant information, is a fundamental aspect of efficient perception and decision-making in biological systems and advanced embodied agents. Historically, active vision research has demonstrated that intelligently controlling sensors can transform ill-posed perception problems into well-posed ones, leading to more efficient information gathering for tasks such as object recognition and scene understanding.

With the increasing integration of MLLMs into robotic and embodied AI systems, these models are becoming central planners for reasoning, planning, and decision-making. However, current MLLMs typically operate as passive consumers of visual inputs, relying on static, pre-defined views of an environment. This contrasts with the dynamic and goal-directed information-seeking behavior required for effective active perception. Previous attempts, such as the zoom-in search strategy in GPT-o3, represented an initial step towards active perception in MLLMs but demonstrated limitations in efficiency and accuracy of region selection, particularly in fine-grained or dense visual scenarios.

This paper positions itself as introducing a systematic framework and evaluation protocol to enable MLLMs to perform active perception. It leverages reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to teach MLLMs to autonomously learn efficient and stable region selection strategies. By doing so, the work aims to bridge the gap between MLLMs' strong generalization and reasoning capabilities and the necessity for dynamic, goal-directed sensing in complex visual environments, thereby contributing to the development of more autonomous and intelligent AI systems, particularly in embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to empower Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with active perception capabilities. This overarching goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations:

- **Systematic Definition of MLLM-based Active Perception:** A key motivation is the observed lack of a formal and systematic framework for MLLM-based active perception tasks. The authors aim to provide a clear definition and conceptual foundation, including a modular decoupling of sensing and action, to guide future research and development in this area. This formalization positions existing MLLM behaviors, such as GPT-o3's zoom-in, as specific instances within a broader active perception paradigm, while also highlighting their limitations.

- **Develop Efficient and Stable Region Selection Strategies:** Current MLLMs, even those with rudimentary active perception like GPT-o3, suffer from inefficiencies and inaccuracies in proposing regions for detailed inspection. The paper aims to develop a method that allows MLLMs to autonomously learn to select informative subregions in parallel, thereby improving search efficiency and accuracy under fixed computational budgets. This is motivated by the need for MLLMs to effectively handle scenarios with small, dense, or fine-grained objects that are challenging to discern from a low-resolution global view.

- **Leverage Reinforcement Learning for Policy Optimization:** Traditional supervised learning methods are often unsuitable for training active perception policies due to the sparsity of task-oriented rewards and the absence of explicit region-selection supervision. The research is motivated by the potential of reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize the sensing policy based on downstream task performance. Specifically, the adoption of GRPO is motivated by its suitability for large language models, as it avoids the need for a separate critic model and enhances reasoning capabilities.

- **Design a Robust Dual-Form Reward Mechanism:** Relying solely on sparse, task-oriented rewards can hinder stable and diverse learning of sensing strategies. The motivation behind the dual-form reward (combining task-aware and heuristic components) is to provide dense, interpretable feedback during RL training. This design aims to balance the need for stabilizing the learning process with the objective of aligning the sensing policy with ultimate task performance.

- **Establish a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** The absence of standardized benchmarks for MLLM-based active perception is a limiting factor. The authors aim to establish a diverse benchmark suite that covers both open-world grounding tasks (e.g., small and dense object detection on LVIS) and domain-specific applications (e.g., remote sensing, autonomous driving on SODA, interactive segmentation on ThinObjects). This is motivated by the need for rigorous and reproducible evaluation of active perception capabilities across various challenging visual scenarios.

- **Demonstrate Generalization and Proxy Task Potential:** Beyond direct task performance, the research is motivated by the hypothesis that enhancing active perception might also improve the MLLM's general visual understanding and reasoning abilities. By showing that active perception can serve as a proxy task for leveraging perception annotations, the authors aim to demonstrate a broader impact of their framework on MLLM development.

In summary, the key motivation is to transform MLLMs from passive visual information consumers into active information seekers, making them more adaptable, efficient, and intelligent for complex, real-world visual tasks, particularly in embodied AI contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ACTIVE-o3 framework is a reinforcement learning-based approach designed to empower Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with active perception capabilities. It is built upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

#### 3.1. Modular View of Active Perception

The framework conceptualizes active perception as a coordination between two decoupled modules, both driven by a single MLLM backbone:
- **Sensing Model (M_O):** Decides *how to look* by controlling perception parameters. It takes an initial observation (e.g., low-resolution global view) and a task instruction, then outputs perception actions, which are bounding box proposals for regions to zoom into.
- **Task Model (M_A):** Decides *how to act* on the environment to accomplish external tasks. It takes a detailed observation (cropped region) and task instruction, then outputs task-level actions (e.g., object detections, segmentation masks).

#### 3.2. 2D Active Perception with Parallel Region Selection

To enable reproducible training and controlled evaluation, the framework instantiates active perception in a 2D setting over static images. This simplifies the problem from a sequential decision-making process in a 3D environment to a single-step decision problem (T=1) where the M_O generates *parallel* region selections. This parallel design encourages the policy to propose a diverse yet complementary set of regions in one forward pass, aiming to improve coverage and efficiency under a fixed sensing budget.
Given an initial image `I` and instruction `I_task`, M_O generates `K` candidate bounding boxes `{a_cam_k}`. For each `a_cam_k`, a high-resolution crop `o_k = ResizeCrop(I, a_cam_k)` is generated. The M_A then operates on each `o_k` to produce task-level outputs `a_env_k`.

#### 3.3. Initial Sensing Policy via MLLM (Zero-Shot Initialization)

To bootstrap active perception without extensive supervised fine-tuning, an initial sensing policy for M_O is constructed using the instruction-following and reasoning abilities of the MLLM itself. This involves designing task-specific instruction prompts (`I_O`, shown in Figure 2 and 5 of the appendix). These prompts serve to:
- **Regularize Format:** Enforce structured output (e.g., JSON with bounding boxes, `<think>` and `<answer>` tags).
- **Provide Task Guidance:** Incorporate domain-specific priors, such as encouraging inference of likely object locations, promoting spatially diverse and minimally overlapping regions, and preferring regions with sufficient context.
This prompt-based policy generates both reasoning traces and candidate regions, serving as a transparent and effective starting point for subsequent RL optimization.

#### 3.4. Policy Improvement with GRPO

The initial prompt-based policy, while functional, cannot adapt to task-specific feedback. Therefore, the M_O is optimized using Reinforcement Learning. The paper adopts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a lightweight policy optimization method that is suitable for large language models because it estimates the advantage function using the mean and variance of rewards across a group of responses, thus avoiding the need for a separate critic model. The GRPO objective aims to maximize expected rewards while regularizing policy updates with a frozen reference policy to maintain stability.

#### 3.5. Dual-Form Reward Design

A central component of the methodology is the dual-form reward, which combines task-independent heuristics with task-specific feedback to address the challenges of sparse and unstable rewards in active perception.
- **Heuristic Reward (R_heuristic):** Evaluates a single MLLM response based on intrinsic qualities of the proposed regions, independent of the downstream task model's performance. It comprises:
 - **Format Validity:** Rewards adherence to expected JSON and reasoning structure.
 - **Non-overlapping Proposals:** Penalizes significant overlap between proposed regions to encourage spatial diversity.
 - **Area Range Constraint:** Rewards proposals within a reasonable size range relative to the image (e.g., 1% to 50% of image area).
 - **Coverage-Based Reward:** Assesses how well proposals align with task-relevant ground-truth areas (e.g., mask purity, GT box coverage).
- **Task-Aware Reward (R_task):** Evaluates the utility of the selected regions based on the performance of the downstream task model (M_A).
 - **For Object Detection:** Uses standard metrics like Average Precision (AP) and Average Recall (AR) against ground-truth bounding boxes.
 - **For Interactive Segmentation:** Uses mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) between predicted and ground-truth masks, where predictions are made by a local instance of Segment Anything (SAM) using M_A's inferred interaction points.

#### 3.6. Benchmarking and Evaluation

The framework is evaluated across a comprehensive benchmark suite:
- **Open-World Small/Dense Object Grounding:** Utilizes the LVIS dataset, focusing on instances under 100 pixels for small objects and images with over 15 instances for dense objects. Metrics are COCO-style AP/AR.
- **Domain-Specific Small Object Detection:** Employs the SODA benchmark, including SODA-D (autonomous driving) and SODA-A (aerial imagery), which present diverse small-object detection scenarios. Metrics are AP/AR (with a lower IoU threshold of 0.1 for domain shift).
- **Fine-Grained Interactive Segmentation:** Uses the ThinObjects dataset, where M_A is simulated by an oracle, to isolate and evaluate the sensing policy M_O. Performance is measured by mIoU after multiple zoom-in steps.
The base MLLM used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Baselines include Grounding DINO, Qwen2.5-VL-CoT, and V* + GDINO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results demonstrating the effectiveness of the ACTIVE-o3 framework:

- **Enhanced Active Perception Capabilities:** Across all evaluated tasks, ACTIVE-o3 consistently improved active perception capabilities compared to baseline models. This indicated that the reinforcement learning framework, combined with the modular design and dual-form reward, successfully teaches MLLMs to proactively identify and focus on informative visual regions.

- **Superior Performance in Open-World Small/Dense Object Grounding:**
 - On the LVIS dataset, which features small and densely packed objects, ACTIVE-o3 significantly outperformed the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline and its Chain-of-Thought (CoT) variant. For instance, on LVIS_small, ACTIVE-o3 showed an improvement of +1.0 AP_s and +2.8 AR_s. On LVIS_dense, improvements were +2.7 AP_s and +3.5 AR_s.
 - When the sensing policy (M_O) from ACTIVE-o3 was paired with a strong, specialized task model like Grounding DINO (ACTIVE-o3+GDINO), it achieved even higher performance, surpassing Grounding DINO alone by +1.3 AP_s and +1.6 AR_s on LVIS_dense, demonstrating the generalizability and utility of the learned sensing policy.

- **Robust Generalization to Domain-Specific Small Object Detection:**
 - On the SODA benchmark, covering autonomous driving (SODA-D) and aerial imagery (SODA-A), ACTIVE-o3 achieved strong performance (9.2/10.4 AP_s/AR_s on SODA-A and 15.1/22.0 on SODA-D).
 - The improvements over Qwen2.5-VL were substantial, such as an 8.5 AP_s gain on SODA-A, indicating the policy's robustness and transferability across distinct visual domains, even with significant domain gaps.

- **Effective Fine-Grained Interactive Segmentation:**
 - In interactive segmentation on the ThinObjects dataset, ACTIVE-o3 demonstrated progressive performance improvement with an increased zoom-in budget. While Qwen2.5-VL-CoT showed performance degradation with more zoom steps (from 0.796 to 0.561 mIoU at budget 3), ACTIVE-o3 consistently improved (from 0.796 to 0.863 mIoU). This indicated that the reinforcement learning policy effectively learned to identify errors and refine segmentation by selectively zooming into challenging regions.

- **Preservation and Enhancement of General Visual Understanding:**
 - Despite not being explicitly trained on reasoning or QA data, ACTIVE-o3 preserved or even improved general understanding and reasoning abilities on benchmarks such as MMBench, MME, and RealWorldQA. No degradation was observed across any benchmark.
 - Notably, on RealWorldQA and MME, ACTIVE-o3 surpassed its initialization model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), suggesting that active perception can function as an effective proxy objective, leveraging perception annotations to enhance broader visual understanding and reasoning capacities.

- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **RL Algorithm Effectiveness:** The framework was shown to be robust across various RL optimizers, with GRPO providing a strong balance between stability and precision.
 - **Necessity of Active Perception:** Direct Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (Direct RFT) of the task model without the sensing module struggled significantly with small and dense objects, confirming that the active sensing module is crucial for addressing resolution limitations and fine-grained details.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** The introduction of the task reward for ACTIVE-o3 added a marginal 3.5 seconds per training step (approximately 17% increase in total training time), demonstrating an efficient trade-off between computational cost and performance gains.
 - **Deployment Latency:** ACTIVE-o3 exhibited lower deployment-side latency compared to the CoT baseline, which was attributed to the learned sensing policy producing more concise outputs and informative grounding regions.
 - **Dual-Form Reward Validation:** The combined reward (heuristic + task-aware) consistently outperformed either component in isolation across all object sizes, validating the effectiveness of balancing exploration with task-driven optimization. This design also helped preserve general reasoning capabilities.
 - **Multi-Candidate Selection:** Increasing the number of parallel selected regions from 1 to 3 consistently improved performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of the joint selection mechanism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ACTIVE-o3 framework represents a notable contribution to the field of multimodal AI, particularly at the intersection of large language models and computer vision. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Reinforcement Learning for MLLM Active Perception:** This work introduces the first reinforcement learning framework specifically designed to equip MLLMs with active perception capabilities. It addresses a fundamental limitation of current MLLMs, which are typically passive consumers of visual information, by enabling them to actively interrogate their visual inputs to achieve specific goals. This shifts MLLMs towards more dynamic and intelligent visual processing.

- **Enhanced Efficiency and Accuracy in Visual Tasks:** By empowering MLLMs with the ability to reason about "where to look" and "how to act," ACTIVE-o3 demonstrably improves accuracy and efficiency in challenging visual tasks, such as small/dense object grounding and fine-grained interactive segmentation. This capability is critical for applications where objects are difficult to discern from a global view or require detailed inspection, leading to more robust performance under limited computational budgets.

- **Bridging the Gap for Embodied AI Systems:** MLLMs are increasingly being deployed as central planners in robotic and embodied AI systems. By providing these "brains" with active perception, ACTIVE-o3 offers a foundational step towards enabling more autonomous and intelligent embodied agents that can strategically gather visual information to better understand and interact with complex physical environments.

- **Generalization Across Diverse Visual Domains:** The framework demonstrates strong generalization across various visual domains, including open-world scenarios (LVIS) and specialized applications like autonomous driving and aerial imagery (SODA). This suggests that the learned sensing policy is robust and adaptable, making it applicable to a wide array of real-world visual perception challenges.

- **Active Perception as a Proxy for General Visual Understanding:** A significant finding is that training MLLMs for active perception not only enhances their specific sensing abilities but also preserves and can even improve their general visual understanding and reasoning capabilities on benchmarks like RealWorldQA and MME. This indicates that active perception can serve as a valuable proxy objective, allowing MLLMs to leverage perception annotations more effectively to boost broader cognitive functions. This opens new avenues for leveraging diverse visual datasets in a self-supervised or weakly supervised manner.

- **Modular and Interpretable Design:** The modular decoupling of sensing and action, combined with structured instruction prompts, contributes to a more interpretable framework. The generated reasoning traces ("<think>") provide insights into the model's decision-making process for region selection, which can be crucial for debugging and building trust in AI systems.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a systematic definition and benchmark for MLLM-based active perception, providing a solid foundation for future research. It encourages further exploration into aspects such as more flexible action spaces (e.g., rotation, more regions), the incorporation of memory mechanisms for long-term planning, and advanced RL strategies tailored for MLLM-specific challenges.

The research also includes an impact statement acknowledging potential risks associated with enhanced perception systems, such as privacy, bias, and overreliance. By encouraging responsible deployment guidelines, anonymization, and robust evaluation, the authors demonstrate a commitment to addressing the societal implications of their work.
