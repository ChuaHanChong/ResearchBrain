# TransMamba: A Sequence-Level Hybrid Transformer-Mamba Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A sequence-level hybrid Transformer-Mamba language model, TransMamba, unifies these architectures through shared parameters and dynamic switching, achieving improved performance on various NLP and long-text tasks while reducing training time by up to 25%.

## Problem

- Transformers face quadratic computational complexity (O(T^2)) with sequence length, leading to high costs for very long sequences in both training and inference.
- Initial Mamba models, while efficient with linear complexity, exhibit unstable contextual learning and suboptimal multitask generalization compared to Transformers.
- Existing layer-level hybrid models suffer from rigid structural restrictions and often fail to dynamically adapt to varying sequence lengths and contextual demands.

## Method

- Unifies Transformer and Mamba architectures through ==shared parameter matrices==, allowing a single set of core parameters to serve as both QKV for Attention and CBx for SSM.
- Introduces a sequence-level dynamic switching mechanism via a ==TransPoint== within each layer, allowing the model to process different parts of the input sequence with either Attention or SSM.
- Employs a lossless ==Memory Converter== to seamlessly and without additional parameters transfer contextual information from the Attention mechanism to the initial hidden state of the ==Mamba SSM==.

## Results

- TransMamba consistently outperforms pure Transformer, pure Mamba2, and naive Hybrid models across general English tasks and long-context benchmarks like LongBench-v2 (e.g., **38.76** for TransMamba-1.5B vs. **31.61** for Transformer).
- Achieves up to a **2**5%** reduction** in average training time compared to Transformer models, demonstrating significant efficiency improvements consistent with theoretical FLOPs analysis.
- Effective TransPoint scheduling, particularly layer-specific, broad-range, and fine-grained approaches, yields the best validation loss (**2.898**) and perplexity (**1.813**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A deeper consistency exists between Transformer's attention and Mamba's state space model, enabling them to share core parameter matrices within a unified architecture.
> - Dynamic, sequence-level adaptation between different mechanisms (Attention and SSM) using principled scheduling can effectively balance computational efficiency and robust contextual learning.
> - Lossless information transfer between distinct architectural components, facilitated by a Memory Converter, is critical for effective integration in hybrid models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of sequence modeling, particularly in natural language processing, has been profoundly transformed by the **Transformer architecture** since "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017). Transformers excel at capturing long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms and have become the cornerstone of modern large language models (LLMs) like GPT and Llama. However, their primary limitation is the **quadratic computational complexity** (O(T^2)) with respect to sequence length (T), which leads to significant computational costs and memory requirements for very long sequences, especially during training and inference due to the growing KV cache.

In response to these limitations, **State Space Models (SSMs)**, particularly the **Mamba architecture** (Gu & Dao, 2023), have emerged as a promising alternative. Mamba offers **linear computational complexity** (O(T)) for sequence processing, providing substantial efficiency gains for long sequences. However, initial Mamba models have shown certain drawbacks, including **unstable contextual learning** and **suboptimal multitask generalization** compared to Transformers (Waleffe et al., 2024).

This dichotomy has naturally led to the exploration of **hybrid architectures** that combine Transformer and Mamba components. Previous works (e.g., Lieber et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2025) often adopt **layer-level hybrid structures**, where entire layers are either Transformer blocks or Mamba blocks, typically arranged in a static sequence or fixed ratio. While these hybrids aim to balance advantages, they often suffer from structural restrictions and may not fully exploit the dynamic nature of sequence processing.

"TransMamba" directly addresses these limitations by proposing a more fundamental and flexible unification. It builds upon recent theoretical insights that highlight a **deeper consistency between the underlying mechanisms of Transformer and Mamba**. Notably, Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024) revealed a "dual form" symmetry between Transformer attention and SSM, and subsequent work (Wang et al., 2024) demonstrated successful distillation and transfer of QKV parameters from Transformer to CBx parameters in Mamba. These findings strongly motivate the bold idea of **shared parameter matrices** between the two mechanisms.

TransMamba distinguishes itself by introducing a **sequence-level hybrid framework**, a departure from most layer-level approaches. Instead of alternating entire layers, TransMamba can dynamically switch between Attention and SSM mechanisms *within a single layer for different parts of the input sequence*. This dynamic, fine-grained control allows for an adaptive approach to sequence modeling that is more responsive to varying token lengths and contextual demands. This work represents a significant step towards truly unified and adaptive sequence models, moving beyond simple concatenation of existing building blocks to a more integrated paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of TransMamba are multifaceted, aiming to resolve the inherent trade-offs between Transformer and Mamba architectures:

1. **Combine Strengths and Mitigate Weaknesses:** The overarching goal is to create a language model that harnesses the **efficiency of Mamba** for processing long sequences (due to its linear complexity) while retaining the **robust contextual learning and strong multitask generalization capabilities of Transformer** (especially for shorter contexts). This addresses the limitations of each architecture when used in isolation.
2. **Overcome Limitations of Naive Hybrid Models:** Existing layer-level hybrid models often face issues such as rigid structural restrictions, suboptimal performance if specific layer ratios or orders are not met, and an inability to dynamically adapt to varying sequence lengths. TransMamba aims to provide a more **flexible and dynamic framework** that transcends these static limitations.
3. **Validate Deeper Consistency between Transformer and Mamba:** Motivated by recent research showing mathematical symmetries and parameter transferability between Transformer's QKV and Mamba's CBx parameters, a key objective is to empirically demonstrate that these mechanisms can share a single set of core parameters within a unified architecture. This validates a profound consistency that could lead to more elegant and efficient model designs.
4. **Achieve Superior Efficiency and Performance:** The ultimate objective is to develop a model that not only performs on par with or better than existing baselines (pure Transformer, pure Mamba, or naive hybrids) across diverse NLP tasks, but also offers **significant improvements in training and inference efficiency**, particularly for long sequences. The goal is a scalable solution for next-generation language modeling.
5. **Enable Dynamic Adaptability:** TransMamba seeks to enable the model to **dynamically switch** between Attention and SSM mechanisms at different token lengths and layers within a sequence. This adaptive switching, governed by a "TransPoint scheduling" mechanism, allows the model to optimize its computational approach based on the specific characteristics of the input sequence.
6. **Ensure Lossless Information Flow:** A crucial technical challenge is ensuring that information learned by one mechanism (e.g., Transformer attention) is seamlessly and losslessly transferred to the other (Mamba SSM) when switching occurs at "TransPoints." This necessitates a dedicated mechanism, which motivates the design of the "Memory Converter."

In essence, TransMamba is driven by the ambition to create a "best of both worlds" model that is dynamically adaptive, parameter-efficient through sharing, and built upon a deeper theoretical understanding of sequence modeling paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TransMamba proposes a novel **sequence-level hybrid Transformer-Mamba framework** built upon three core methodological innovations: shared parameter matrices, a Memory Converter for lossless information transfer, and a flexible TransPoint scheduling mechanism.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture: Shared Parameters and Sequence-Level Hybridization

The central tenet of TransMamba is the unification of Transformer and Mamba through **shared parameter matrices**. Specifically, the model uses a single set of parameters that can serve as both the QKV (Query, Key, Value) matrices for the Attention mechanism and the CBx (C, B, x) parameters for the State Space Model (SSM). This bold design is justified by prior research demonstrating the mathematical consistency and parameter transferability between these core components.
Within each layer, TransMamba dynamically switches between Attention and SSM modes **at the sequence level**. This means that for a given input sequence, a portion of the tokens might be processed by the Attention mechanism, while the remaining tokens are processed by the SSM mechanism. This is governed by a **TransPoint**, which defines the token position where the switch occurs. For efficiency, only a single TransPoint is set per layer, transforming from Attention mode to Mamba mode for the rest of the sequence.

**Formalized Calculation Process:**
For an input sequence `h`:
- **Before TransPoint (h_s = h[:TransPoint]):** The model uses the shared parameters to calculate Q, K, V (Equation 1) and then computes the output `y_s` via the standard attention mechanism (Equation 2).
- **After TransPoint (h_l = h[TransPoint:]):** The model utilizes the same shared parameters to calculate C, B, x, as well as ∆ and A specific to SSM (Equation 3). The output `y_l` is then generated using the SSM mechanism. Crucially, the initial state `h0` for the SSM part is derived from the preceding Attention mechanism via the Memory Converter (Equation 4 & 5).
The final output is the concatenation `y = [y_s, y_l]` (Equation 6).

#### 3.2. Lossless Memory Converter

A critical challenge in switching between Attention and SSM is ensuring that the latter seamlessly picks up the contextual information learned by the former. TransMamba addresses this with a **Memory Converter**, which is designed to losslessly translate the intermediate representations of the Attention mechanism into the initial hidden state required by the Mamba SSM.
The converter leverages the structural consistency between Attention and SSM. Specifically, it converts the K (Key) and V (Value) outputs from the Attention mechanism into an estimated hidden state `h_s = (A^x ◦ K^T)V` (Equation 9), where `A^x` is a lower triangular matrix derived from A. The final hidden state of the Attention part, `h_s[-1]`, then becomes the initial state `h0` for the subsequent SSM computation (Equation 4). Importantly, this converter requires **no additional parameters**; it is a theoretical solution derived from existing results, ensuring efficiency and avoiding additional model complexity.

#### 3.3. Flexible TransPoint Scheduling

The position of the TransPoint (P) within a sequence determines the relative usage of Attention and SSM in a given layer. Optimizing this schedule is crucial for balancing effectiveness and efficiency.

**Principles of TransPoint Scheduling:**
1. **Efficiency Impact:** TransPoint significantly affects training time. The schedule should align with the optimal computational balance between quadratic (Attention) and linear (SSM) complexities, as summarized in Table 2 (FLOPS_TransMamba = O(P^2 N + (T-P)N^2)).
2. **Effectiveness and Smoothness:** TransPoints should not be overly concentrated at a single position across all layers. A gradual, dispersed transition from Transformer-dominant to Mamba-dominant processing across layers prevents "sudden mutations" that could degrade performance.
3. **Leveraging Hybrid Strengths:** The varying TransPoint schedules at different layers and token lengths allow TransMamba to be viewed as a dynamic combination of hybrid structures, harnessing insights from superior fixed hybrid designs.

**Detailed TransPoint Schedule Designing:**
- **Token Length Aspect:** Experiments show that training efficiency follows a quadratic curve with respect to TransPoint P, with an optimal point around P=2,048 for their specific setup (N=1,536, T=8,192).
- **Layer Aspect:** To improve performance and smoothness, TransPoints are set to be **layer-specific**, rather than global across all layers. They are also designed to cover a **broad-range** of sequence lengths and to be **fine-grained** (e.g., cycling every 8 layers through values like 0, 128, 256..., 8192). This gradual transition helps guide the model from more Transformer-like behavior to more Mamba-like behavior across the depth of the network.

#### 3.4. Diverse Inference Strategy

The flexible nature of TransMamba also allows for different TransPoint schedules during inference than during training. This opens up possibilities for task-specific optimization, where the model could be trained for maximum efficiency and then inferred with a structure best suited for a particular task's demands.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TransMamba demonstrates superior performance and efficiency across a range of benchmarks, validating its design principles and marking it as a promising advancement in sequence modeling.

#### 4.1. General Task Evaluations (Table 3)

- **Overall Superiority:** TransMamba consistently achieves the best performance across various general English tasks, including question answering (ARC-E, ARC-C, CoQA, OBQA, BoolQ), physical commonsense reasoning (PIQA), and precise search (PhoneBook).
- **Outperforming Baselines:** It generally outperforms pure Transformer, pure Mamba2, and even naive Hybrid models (Transformer-Mamba layer-level concatenation). For instance, TransMamba-400M shows improvements over Hybrid-400M on ARC-E (62.50 vs 62.33), ARC-C (59.33 vs 55.78), CoQA (6.23 vs 5.52), and PhoneBook (39.69 vs 17.60). Similar trends are observed for the 1.5B models.
- **Addressing Mamba's Weakness (PhoneBook):** TransMamba significantly closes the gap on tasks like PhoneBook, where Mamba-based models traditionally struggle with precise search (PhoneBook score for Mamba2-400M is 13.07, Hybrid-400M is 17.60, while Transformer-400M achieves 38.70, and TransMamba-400M achieves 39.69). This highlights the effectiveness of dynamically switching to Attention for segments requiring precise contextual recall.

#### 4.2. Long-Text Benchmark Evaluation (Table 4)

- **Long-Context Proficiency:** On the challenging LongBench-v2 benchmark, which evaluates deep understanding and reasoning in ultra-long contexts, TransMamba (1.5B) again outperforms all baselines (Transformer: 31.61, Mamba: 30.62, Hybrid: 35.79, TransMamba: 38.76). This demonstrates the effectiveness of the Memory Converter in preserving information across TransPoints and Mamba's efficiency for longer sequences.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Analysis (Table 5 & Figure 4)

- **Training Time Reduction:** TransMamba achieves a significant reduction in average training time. Compared to Transformer, TransMamba shows a maximum efficiency improvement of **25%** (relative time 0.75 vs 1.00). Even when comparing to Mamba, TransMamba is slightly more efficient (0.75 vs 0.77).
- **Consistency with Theoretical FLOPs:** The observed efficiency gains are consistent with the theoretical FLOPs analysis (Table 2), which predicts a quadratic relationship between training time and the TransPoint.
- **Optimal TransPoint:** Experiments on layer-shared TransPoint schedules (Figure 4a) clearly demonstrate a quadratic curve trend for relative training time, with the **optimal TransPoint for efficiency around 2,048** tokens for the given model and sequence length (T=8192, N=1536).

#### 4.4. In-depth Analyses on TransPoint Schedule (Table 6 & Figure 4)

- **Layer-Shared Limitations:** Simple layer-shared TransPoint schedules (V1-V3) result in unsatisfactory validation loss and perplexity (PPL) due to the abrupt structural change for all layers simultaneously.
- **Benefits of Layer-Specific Schedules:** Layer-specific TransPoints (V4-V6) show improved results compared to layer-shared, highlighting the importance of distributed transformation.
- **Importance of Broad-Range and Fine-Grained Schedules:** TransPoint schedules that cover a **broad-range** of sequence lengths (V7, V8) and are **fine-grained** (V9) significantly improve performance. The final proposed schedule (V9: cycling through 0, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192 every 8 layers) yields the best validation loss (2.898) and PPL (1.813), affirming the principles of dispersed and smooth transitions.

#### 4.5. Explorations on Inconsistent Training/Inference (Table 9)

- **Adaptive Inference Potential:** Surprisingly, TransMamba can function normally even with completely different TransPoint schedules during inference than during training. In some cases (e.g., OBQA evaluated with a Hybrid inference structure), it even surpassed the original TransMamba and all baselines. This suggests a promising future direction for decoupling training and inference strategies for task-specific optimization.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies (Table 10)

- **Memory Converter's Efficacy:** The theoretical solution-based Memory Converter significantly outperforms a simple MLP-based fitting, improving both running speed and training effect.
- **Attention vs. SSM Components:** The ablation studies also reveal that mapping attention's `z` component to SSM's `z` is not optimal, and other component choices contribute to better training loss.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TransMamba represents a significant advancement in the field of sequence modeling, with profound implications and potential impact on the development of next-generation language models:

1. **Validation of Deeper Architectural Consistency:** The most significant contribution is the empirical validation of a "deeper consistency" between Transformer and Mamba architectures. By successfully unifying them at the sequence level through shared parameters (QKV for Attention, CBx for SSM), TransMamba moves beyond superficial layer-stacking to a more integrated paradigm. This finding could inspire entirely new model architectures that leverage this fundamental commonality.
2. **Scalable and Efficient Language Modeling:** TransMamba offers a truly scalable solution for processing long sequences, addressing one of the most critical limitations of current Transformer-based LLMs. Its linear complexity for longer sequences, combined with the efficiency advantages shown in experiments (up to 25% faster training), makes it highly attractive for developing and deploying LLMs that handle vast contexts, such as document analysis, long-form content generation, or scientific texts.
3. **Flexible and Adaptive Sequence Processing:** The dynamic, sequence-level switching between Attention and SSM, governed by principled TransPoint scheduling, introduces a new level of adaptability to sequence models. This flexibility allows the model to optimize its computational strategy based on the specific demands of different parts of an input sequence, ensuring that the most suitable mechanism is employed where it is most effective. This contrasts sharply with static, fixed-architecture models.
4. **Novel Architectural Design Principles:** The work introduces key architectural components like the **Memory Converter**, which losslessly bridges the information flow between disparate mechanisms, and **principled TransPoint scheduling**, which guides the dynamic configuration for optimal performance and efficiency. These innovations provide blueprints for future hybrid and adaptive model designs.
5. **Broadened Research Horizons:**
 - **Inconsistent Training/Inference:** The discovery that TransMamba can perform effectively even with different TransPoint schedules during training and inference opens up exciting possibilities. This "structural decoupling" could lead to a paradigm where models are trained for maximum efficiency and then dynamically reconfigured for optimal performance on specific inference tasks, potentially enabling a new level of task-specific optimization.
 - **Exploring Scaling Laws:** Future work can investigate the scaling laws of TransMamba with larger model sizes, providing insights into how this hybrid approach behaves at extreme scales.
 - **Variant Combinations:** The framework allows for exploration of combining different variants of Transformer (e.g., Longformer, Performer) and Mamba (e.g., different SSM parameterizations), potentially leading to further performance gains and specialized models.
6. **Enhanced Performance on Challenging Tasks:** TransMamba's superior performance on long-context benchmarks like LongBench-v2 and its ability to mitigate Mamba's weakness in precise recall tasks like PhoneBook demonstrate its practical utility and robustness across a diverse range of NLP challenges.

In conclusion, TransMamba is not merely an incremental improvement; it represents a conceptual shift in how we might design and combine sequence modeling architectures. By proving the viability and benefits of deep unification and dynamic adaptation, it provides a strong foundation and inspiration for the next generation of highly efficient and powerful language models.
