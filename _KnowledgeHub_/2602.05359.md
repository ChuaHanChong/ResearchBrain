# Multimodal Latent Reasoning via Hierarchical Visual Cues Injection

> [!summary] Summary
> The HIVE framework introduces a multimodal latent reasoning approach that uses a recurrent transformer architecture and hierarchical visual cue injection to achieve deliberate, multi-step inference. This method enhances complex visual-linguistic reasoning capabilities, achieves an F1 score of 91.6% on ScienceQA-Img, and reduces inference computations by up to 43% by enabling faster convergence of latent states.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often employ a 'fast thinking' approach, struggling with complex, compositional questions requiring deeper logical inference.
- Current 'slow thinking' MLLMs primarily rely on explicit textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which can be inefficient, verbose, and susceptible to language biases or hallucinations.
- Most MLLMs use 'flat' visual feature projections from a single vision encoder layer, proving insufficient for nuanced understanding of complex scenes that need both global context and fine-grained details.

## Method

- HIVE implements a ==recurrent visual-language backbone==, built on Huginn, that iteratively refines latent representations within a fixed parameter count, allowing for multi-step reasoning.
- It integrates ==hierarchical visual cues== by extracting features from multiple layers of a pre-trained vision encoder and adaptively injecting them into recurrent blocks based on an iteration schedule.
- The framework incorporates an ==adaptive computation mechanism== that dynamically adjusts recurrence steps during inference using a 'norm diff' metric for early exit, optimizing computational efficiency.

## Results

- HIVE achieved **91.6%** on ScienceQA-Img, outperforming larger MLLMs, and demonstrated the highest score (**87.6%**) on the POPE benchmark for hallucination mitigation among evaluated models.
- Recurrent iterations significantly improved performance, with the hierarchical model climbing from **32.82%** at **1** step to **91.57%** at **32** steps on ScienceQA-Img, confirming iterative refinement benefits.
- Hierarchical visual cues consistently enhanced reasoning, improving metrics across various benchmarks and reducing average inference steps by up to **43%** on RealWorldQA (from **25.5** to **14.5** steps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deliberate 'slow thinking' in MLLMs can be effectively achieved through latent-space iterative refinement within a recurrent architecture, moving beyond reliance on explicit linguistic chains.
> - Injecting hierarchical visual cues directly into the latent reasoning process, rather than solely relying on text-derived supervision, is crucial for grounded and robust multimodal understanding.
> - Combining recurrent architectures with adaptive computation and rich hierarchical visual features leads to substantial inference efficiency gains, reducing computational overhead without sacrificing accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has experienced significant advancements with the development of large-scale pre-trained models, initially in natural language processing (NLP) with models like GPT-3, and subsequently expanding into multimodal domains through systems such as GPT-4 and Qwen-VL. These Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in tasks like visual question answering and image captioning, primarily operating on a "System 1" or "fast thinking" paradigm characterized by rapid, intuitive, and end-to-end processing.

However, this "fast thinking" approach often proves insufficient for complex, compositional questions that necessitate deeper logical inference or multi-step analysis. This limitation has motivated research into equipping MLLMs with "System 2" or "slow thinking" capabilities. Previous efforts in this direction have largely focused on generating explicit textual rationales, often referred to as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), to guide the reasoning process. Examples include LLaVA-CoT and Vision-r1. While these methods introduce deliberation, they rely on language-centric explicit rationales, which can be inefficient, verbose, and susceptible to language biases or hallucination, thereby not truly integrating visual information into the core reasoning mechanism.

A parallel line of research in Large Language Models (LLMs) has explored "thinking in the latent space," where computation occurs within the continuous hidden states of the model, rather than through explicit text. Coconut and SoftCoT are examples that abstract CoT into hidden states or soft prompts. The extension of this latent-space reasoning to multimodal settings has been initiated by works like Heima, which compresses textual CoT into "thinking tokens." However, Heima's reasoning is still driven by textual CoT supervision, suggesting that visual information is not intrinsically integrated into the latent reasoning process.

This paper addresses the gap by proposing a framework that enables multimodal latent reasoning driven by visual representations, without direct reliance on explicit textual CoT. It integrates architectural recurrence, inspired by loop transformers like Huginn, with a novel approach to inject hierarchical visual cues directly into the model's latent representations. This positions HIVE as an effort to move beyond surface-level linguistic reasoning in MLLMs toward a more deeply integrated, computationally efficient, and robust multimodal reasoning paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel multimodal latent reasoning framework, named HIVE (HIerarchical Visual cuEs injection), that instills deliberate, "slow thinking" capabilities in MLLMs without relying on explicit, language-centric textual rationales.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing MLLMs and multimodal reasoning paradigms:

- **Limitations of "Fast Thinking" MLLMs:** Current MLLMs primarily operate on an end-to-end, "System 1" or "fast thinking" paradigm. While effective for simple pattern recognition and descriptive tasks, this approach struggles with complex, compositional questions that require multi-step inference, sequential deliberation, or nuanced analysis. Such tasks often lead to superficial responses or failures in logical grounding.
- **Inefficiencies and Risks of Textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Recent attempts to introduce "slow thinking" often depend on generating explicit textual rationales (CoT). While these methods can improve reasoning, they introduce several drawbacks:
 - **Inefficiency and Verbosity:** Generating lengthy textual rationales increases computational overhead and output verbosity.
 - **Susceptibility to Hallucination:** Relying on generated text can make models prone to hallucinating facts or reasoning steps that are not grounded in the input, particularly visual information.
 - **Language Bias:** Textual CoT can reinforce language biases and may not fully leverage or integrate multimodal signals in a balanced manner, limiting true multimodal understanding.
- **Lack of True Visual Integration in Latent Reasoning:** While some prior work has explored latent-space reasoning in LLMs (e.g., Coconut, SoftCoT) and even extended it to multimodal settings (e.g., Heima), these multimodal extensions still often rely on compressing or being supervised by textual CoT. This means the visual information is not genuinely inducing or driving the latent reasoning process, but rather is processed alongside a text-driven "thought" process.
- **Need for Grounded, Multi-step Inference:** A robust multimodal reasoning system should be able to perform inference that is inherently grounded in visual information and can refine its understanding through iterative steps. This requires an architecture that can seamlessly integrate multimodal signals and perform computations entirely within an aligned latent space, rather than relying on an external, text-based scaffolding.

To address these motivations, HIVE aims to:
1. Leverage a loop-based transformer architecture to enable iterative refinement of latent representations, thus facilitating "slow thinking" through recurrent computation.
2. Introduce a hierarchical injection strategy for visual cues, ranging from global scene context to fine-grained regional details, directly into the model's latent space, ensuring visual information actively guides the reasoning process.
3. Demonstrate that this strategy allows MLLMs to perform grounded, multi-step inference effectively and efficiently within an aligned latent space, overcoming the limitations of explicit textual rationales.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HIVE operates as a multimodal latent reasoning framework built upon a looped transformer architecture, extending the principles of Huginn. The framework's core design involves recursively extending transformer blocks to create an internal loop for iterative reasoning refinement, coupled with a hierarchical injection of visual cues.

#### 3.1. Recurrent Visual-Language Backbone

The model processes input text (x) and flattened image patches (Xv) separately before fusion.
- **Visual Feature Extraction:** A pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) extracts visual features, which are then mapped into the LLM embedding space by a projector, Proj(·), yielding visual embeddings (ev). The [CLS] token is removed during this process.
- **Textual Embedding:** Text input is processed by Embedding Blocks (E) to produce textual embeddings (et).
- **Multimodal Fusion:** The visual and textual embeddings are concatenated to form the combined input embedding (e = [ev; et]).
- **Recurrent Block (R-Block):** HIVE employs a tripartite loop transformer architecture comprising Embedding Blocks (E), a Recurrent block (R), and a Language Head (H). All these modules are constructed from fundamental decoder blocks.
 - **Initialization:** For the initial iteration, a random vector (s0) sampled from a normal distribution (N(0, σ²I)) is concatenated with the input embeddings. This combined vector is then dimensionally mapped by an adapter within the R-Block.
 - **Iterative Refinement:** In subsequent iterations, the hidden states (sr) from the preceding block are concatenated with the input embeddings (e) and fused visual cues (ˆev). The R-Block then computes the next hidden state: sr+1 = R − Block (e, ˆev; sr).
- **Output:** After `r` recurrent iterations, the final hidden state (sr) is passed to the Language Head (H) to produce output probabilities (p = H(sr)).

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Visual Injection

To integrate both structural details and high-level semantics, HIVE extracts hidden states from multiple representative layers of the vision encoder (specifically, layers L = {6, 12, 18, 24}).
- **Layer Selection Rationale:**
 - Lower layers (e.g., Layer 6) are used to capture high-resolution spatial information, textures, and edges, useful for grounding.
 - Intermediate and higher layers (e.g., Layers 12-24) provide increasingly complex semantic concepts and global scene context.
- **Feature Projection:** A set of patch mergers (M = {ml}l∈L), inspired by Qwen3-VL, projects the visual features (hlv) from each selected layer into the language embedding space (vl ∈ Rⁿˣʰ).
- **Adaptive Injection Schedule:** The projected hierarchical visual cues (ˆev) are injected into the recurrent blocks during the early stages of reasoning according to an adaptive schedule:
 - `ˆev = vi` if `t < K`, where `i = L[t]`. Otherwise, `ˆev = 0`.
 - **Case I (Sufficient Iterations, R ≥ 4):** Visual cues are injected in a "top-down" hierarchical order during the initial 4 recurrent steps. For subsequent steps (t > 4), the model performs pure language modeling, refining the reasoning internally.
 - **Case II (Constrained Iterations, R < 4):** If the sampled recurrent depth is shallower than the visual hierarchy, a subset of visual cues is selected with an interval of ⌊4/R⌋ to ensure a representative spectrum of visual information is still provided.
This progressive injection strategy from fine-grained to coarse-grained information aims to stabilize hidden state transitions during early reasoning.

#### 3.3. Training Objective and Configuration

- **Loss Function:** The training objective is a cross-entropy loss L(θ), which minimizes the difference between the model's output at the `r`-th recurrence step and the target text (x'). The recurrence depth `r` is sampled from a log-normal Poisson distribution (Λ) with a targeted mean, forcing the model to maintain semantic consistency across varied computational depths.
- **Truncated Backpropagation:** Huginn employs truncated backpropagation through depth, propagating gradients only through the final `k` iterations of the recurrent unit to reduce memory overhead.
- **Three-Stage Training Pipeline:**
 - **Stage 1 (Pre-alignment):** Only the projector and patch mergers are trained using the LCS-558K dataset to align vision-language modalities.
 - **Stage 2 (General Pre-training):** Enhances image-text alignment using EMOVA, ShareGPT4V, ALLaVA, ShareGPT-4o, and OCR datasets (SynthDog, MMC-Alignment, K12 Printing, UReader Text Reading). Text-only corpus from Magpie Pro is integrated to maintain linguistic proficiency.
 - **Stage 3 (Instruction Tuning):** Fine-tuning on 3.4M samples from EMOVA-SFT and various high-quality open-source visual instruction datasets (InternVL, Meteor, Idefics-2, Cambrian, LLaVA-OneVision).
- **Vision Backbone:** InternViT-300M-448px-V2.5 is used as the vision backbone, leveraging a dynamic high-resolution strategy.
- **Tokenization:** Dedicated tokens `<|image start|>`, `<|image|>`, and `<|image end|>` are used, with `<|image|>` being replaced by projected visual features.
- **Hyperparameters:** AdamW optimizer with specific β parameters, cosine decay learning rate, and a weight decay of 10⁻³.

#### 3.4. Adaptive Compute (Inference)

HIVE incorporates an adaptive computation mechanism for inference, dynamically adjusting the number of recurrence iterations based on the convergence of hidden states.
- **Convergence Metric:** `norm_diff = ||ht − ht-1||₂ / ||ht||₂` is used to determine when hidden states have stabilized sufficiently.
- **KV-Cache Management:** A specialized KV-cache management scheme, `latest-m4`, employs a periodic retrieval strategy to maintain temporal consistency and reduce redundant computations, facilitating dynamic inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HIVE involved comparisons with various MLLMs across several benchmarks, focusing on general VQA, OCR & Chart tasks, mathematical reasoning, and hallucination detection. Key findings include:

- **Competitive Performance with Parameter Efficiency:**
 - HIVE, with its 4B parameter architecture, demonstrated competitive performance against larger models. For instance, it achieved 91.6% on ScienceQA-Img, outperforming the 8B Emu3 (89.2%) and 7B MobileVLM V2 (74.8%). This suggests effectiveness in complex, knowledge-based visual tasks.
 - On the POPE benchmark, HIVE achieved the highest reliability score (87.6%), indicating a robust capability to mitigate object hallucination compared to its peers.
- **Data Efficiency:**
 - Despite being trained on 6.5M samples, HIVE either matched or surpassed models like Gemma3-4B-PT, which were pre-trained on a significantly larger 4T tokens, across benchmarks such as RealWorldQA (57.9% vs 45.5%) and DocVQA (73.2% vs 72.8%). This highlights HIVE's efficiency in leveraging training data.
- **Impact of Recurrence on Performance:**
 - Comparing three model variants (non-recurrent baseline `r=1`, recurrent `r=32` without hierarchical cues, and recurrent `r=32` with hierarchical cues), both recurrent variants exhibited substantial performance improvements with increasing recurrence steps.
 - On ScienceQA-Img, the non-recurrent baseline achieved 60.09%, while the recurrent variant without hierarchical cues reached 89.39% at `r=32`, and HIVE (with hierarchical cues) achieved 91.57% at `r=32`. This validates the effectiveness of iterative refinement through recurrent computation.
 - Performance gains showed diminishing returns beyond `r=32`, suggesting a saturation of the model's representational capacity at that depth, where additional steps yielded negligible improvements.
- **Contribution of Hierarchical Visual Cues:**
 - The integration of hierarchical visual cues (HIVE) was associated with moderate performance gains across several categories of the MMBench benchmark compared to the recurrent baseline without hierarchical cues.
 - Notable improvements were observed in Logic Reasoning (LR, +2.54%), Attribute Reasoning (AR, +1.99%), Relation Reasoning (RR, +1.74%), and Coarse Perception (CP, +3.04%). This indicates that hierarchical visual priors can be effectively incorporated into the recurrent framework, providing additional support for complex visual understanding.
 - While differences were limited in instance-level perception (FI), the overall trend suggests compatibility and benefit of hierarchical cues for loop-based latent reasoning.
- **Adaptive Compute Efficiency:**
 - Under an adaptive early-exit setting (maximum `r=32`), the incorporation of hierarchical cues led to faster convergence of hidden states, resulting in a reduction of average recurrence steps required for the first token generation across multiple-choice benchmarks.
 - For example, mean reasoning steps decreased from 25.4 to 18.1 on MMBench, 24.9 to 17.7 on MMStar, and 24.8 to 17.0 on ScienceQA-Img. On RealWorldQA, the average computation depth decreased from 25.5 to 14.5.
 - This "leftward shift" in the step distribution indicates that hierarchical visual cues can provide multi-scale information that helps the model meet the exit criterion earlier, thereby enhancing inference efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HIVE framework represents an advancement in multimodal large language models by introducing a mechanism for latent-space reasoning that is intrinsically guided by hierarchical visual cues and employs an iterative, recurrent architecture.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Research

- **Shift from Explicit to Latent Reasoning:** HIVE moves beyond the reliance on explicit textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting for complex reasoning tasks. By performing inference within an aligned latent space and recursively refining representations, it offers a more integrated and potentially less verbose approach to multimodal "slow thinking." This represents a conceptual shift from language-centric explicit rationales to modality-synchronized internal computation.
- **Architectural Innovation:** The integration of hierarchical visual cue injection into a loop-based Transformer architecture (derived from Huginn) is a novel structural contribution. It demonstrates that test-time scaling remains effective with visual knowledge and that multi-scale visual information can be leveraged within recurrent reasoning frameworks.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Grounding:** By injecting visual cues from global scene context to fine-grained regional details, HIVE allows the model's reasoning to be more directly grounded in the visual input, potentially mitigating issues like hallucination that can arise from purely language-driven reasoning.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The looped transformer architecture, combined with adaptive computation mechanisms and specialized KV-cache management, provides a pathway for achieving high-level cognitive depth without a proportional increase in physical parameter count or static computational budget. The demonstrated reduction in average inference steps due to hierarchical cues further contributes to computational efficiency.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Development of More Robust MLLMs:** HIVE’s approach to grounded, multi-step inference could lead to the development of MLLMs that are more capable of understanding and responding to complex, compositional visual-linguistic queries with increased accuracy and reduced instances of ungrounded responses.
- **Computational Sustainability:** By enabling reasoning within a fixed-parameter recurrent framework and reducing the reliance on extremely long text sequences or massive parameter scaling, HIVE contributes to the development of more computationally efficient AI systems. This could result in lower energy consumption and a reduced carbon footprint associated with deploying high-performance reasoning models.
- **Advanced Decision Support Systems:** Improved, grounded reasoning from complex visual inputs could benefit applications requiring nuanced visual-logical analysis. This includes fields such as medical imaging interpretation (e.g., assisting diagnoses), assistive technologies for individuals with visual impairments, and enhancing the safety and reliability of autonomous systems through better scene understanding.
- **Broader AI Research:** This work provides a practical architectural framework for balancing cognitive depth with manageable computational costs. It could motivate further research into implicit reasoning mechanisms, alternative recurrent architectures for multimodal inputs, and dynamic computation strategies across different modalities.

**Ethical Considerations:**
As with any advanced large-scale multimodal model, HIVE carries inherent ethical considerations. There is a risk that the model might perpetuate or amplify biases present in its extensive training datasets (e.g., InternViT or various text corpora). The enhanced reasoning capabilities could also potentially be misused, for instance, in generating sophisticated misinformation or manipulating visual content. The authors acknowledge these risks and recommend applying standard rigorous bias-detection and safety-filtering protocols during the deployment of such recursive latent reasoning frameworks.
