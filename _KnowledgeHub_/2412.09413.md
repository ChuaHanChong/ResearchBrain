# Imitate, Explore, and Self-Improve: A Reproduction Report on Slow-thinking Reasoning Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Renmin University of China introduce STILL-2, an open-source framework that reproduces o1-like "slow-thinking" reasoning in LLMs through an "imitate, explore, and self-improve" approach. The system achieves performance closely approaching leading proprietary systems on complex reasoning tasks, with 90.2% accuracy on MATH-OAI and 46.7% on AIME, suggesting that fine-tuning with high-quality, long-form thought data can elicit strong reasoning without explicit tree search.

## Problem

- Proprietary "slow-thinking" LLMs (like OpenAI's o1) exhibit advanced, multi-step reasoning capabilities but operate as "black boxes," hindering academic understanding, reproduction, and open research.
- Previous academic attempts to replicate such systems were often limited to specific domains, used weaker base models, or failed to achieve performance comparable to industrial counterparts.
- The authors' prior framework (STILL-1) faced challenges with domain-specific reward models, high inference costs due to tree search, and a lack of train-time scaling.

## Method

- Introduces the ==STILL-2 framework==, employing an =="imitate, explore, and self-improve" (IES)== three-phase training approach for developing LLM-based slow-thinking reasoning.
- Shifts away from complex reward models and explicit tree search during inference, opting for a ==single-pass text generation== process that produces both internal reasoning (thought) and the final solution in a unified output.
- Constructs high-quality, long-form thought datasets by distilling trajectories from open o1-like systems (DeepSeek-R1-Lite-Preview, QwQ-32B-preview), with meticulous data cleaning, format unification, and mixing across diverse, challenging domains.

## Results

- The STILL-2 distillation variant, trained on **3.9k** instances, achieved **90.2%** accuracy on MATH-OAI and an impressive **46.7%** on AIME, demonstrating a **+251.1%** gain over the Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct backbone model on AIME.
- Exploration and self-improvement strategies (SFT or DPO) further improved performance over the baseline fine-tuned model (e.g., AIME scores improved from **33.3%** to **40.0%** or **46.7%**), though constrained by current limitations in the number of rollouts.
- Training on hard mathematical problems was found to be crucial for developing long-form thinking capabilities, which then generalize effectively across different reasoning domains (e.g., math and science).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-tuning with a limited amount of carefully curated, high-quality long-form thought data, especially from challenging problems, is highly effective for eliciting advanced LLM reasoning, with performance approaching industry-leading systems.
> - The ability to perform long-form reasoning, once instilled through focused training, demonstrates strong cross-domain transferability, generalizing across mathematical, coding, and scientific reasoning tasks.
> - Complex reward models or explicit tree search during inference may not be strictly necessary; a finely tuned, single-pass generation of interwoven thought and solution can achieve robust "slow-thinking" capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and highly active area in the field of large language models: the development of advanced reasoning capabilities, particularly those that emulate human-like "slow thinking" or System 2 cognition. The landscape is currently dominated by proprietary, industry-developed systems like OpenAI's o1, DeepSeek-R1-Lite-Preview, and QwQ-32B-preview, which have demonstrated exceptional performance on complex tasks by engaging in extended, multi-step thought processes before generating a final response. However, the core techniques and architectural details of these industry systems are largely undisclosed, presenting a "black box" challenge for the academic research community.

The paper frames its work as a "reproduction report," aiming to demystify these powerful systems by implementing an open-source, o1-like reasoning system with publicly disclosed details. This effort is crucial for fostering open science, enabling further academic research, and democratizing access to advanced LLM capabilities. Previous academic attempts to reproduce such systems have often been limited to specific domains (e.g., mathematics) or have utilized relatively weaker base models, leading to systems that are significantly inferior to their industrial counterparts. This paper seeks to overcome these limitations by developing a system that generalizes across domains and achieves performance comparable to industry standards.

The authors' approach is specifically inspired by two recent developments:
1. **Availability of o1-like APIs/checkpoints:** The release of DeepSeek-R1-Lite-Preview and QwQ-32B-preview has allowed researchers to closely examine the *actual* thought processes generated by these systems, providing valuable data for imitation learning, unlike OpenAI's o1 which often summarizes its internal thoughts.
2. **Effectiveness of small-scale CoT fine-tuning:** Empirical evidence, including prior studies and their own findings, suggests that fine-tuning LLMs with even a small amount of high-quality long-form chain-of-thought (CoT) data can significantly boost performance on complex reasoning tasks.

This work specifically challenges their own previous paradigm that relied on complex reward models and explicit tree search during inference. The authors hypothesize that o1 might employ a simpler, one-pass decoding process that integrates both internal thought and the final solution. This shift in perspective, moving away from explicit search mechanisms and towards a more integrated, fine-tuned generation process, represents a significant contribution to the ongoing dialogue about the underlying mechanisms of advanced LLM reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **implement and detail a robust, open-source reproduction of o1-like slow-thinking reasoning systems**, capable of generalizing across various domains and achieving performance comparable to, or even approaching, leading industry systems.

The motivations driving this objective are multi-faceted:
- **Addressing the "Black Box" Problem:** The advanced reasoning capabilities of proprietary systems like OpenAI's o1 are impressive but lack transparency. This research aims to open up the internal mechanisms, allowing the broader academic community to understand, analyze, and build upon these techniques.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Prior Academic Efforts:** Existing academic reproductions have often been constrained by domain specificity, weaker base models, or limitations in achieving competitive performance. The authors are motivated to create a more generalized and high-performing open system.
- **Rectifying Shortcomings of Previous Internal Work:** The authors explicitly acknowledge the limitations of their earlier framework (STILL-1), which struggled with domain generalization, high inference costs due to tree search, and a lack of train-time scaling. This paper is a direct response to those challenges, seeking a more efficient and scalable approach.
- **Exploring a Novel Hypothesis for o1's Mechanism:** Based on recent observations from open o1-like systems (DeepSeek, Qwen) and empirical findings, the authors hypothesize that a complex reward model or explicit tree search might not be strictly necessary. Instead, a finely tuned, single-pass generation of interwoven thought and solution could be the core mechanism. This hypothesis guides their new "imitate, explore, and self-improve" framework.
- **Contributing to Open Science:** By releasing their resources (code, models), the authors aim to foster collaboration and accelerate research in advanced LLM reasoning, fulfilling their commitment to keeping their technical approach open.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a desire to push the boundaries of open LLM reasoning systems, make advanced techniques more accessible, and explore a potentially simpler yet highly effective pathway to achieving slow-thinking capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the **STILL-2 framework**, built upon a three-phase training approach: "imitate, explore, and self-improve" (IES). This framework aims for a single-pass text generation process during inference, where the LLM produces both its internal reasoning (thought) and the final solution in a unified output.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Three-Phase Framework

- **Imitate:** The initial phase focuses on teaching the LLM to adopt the slow-thinking mode and output format.
- **Explore:** The model is then encouraged to generate progressively better training data by tackling challenging problems and producing multiple "rollouts" or candidate solutions.
- **Self-Improve:** Finally, the high-quality trajectories discovered during exploration are used to further enhance the model's reasoning abilities through iterative refinement.

#### 3.2. Imitation Learning for Slow-Thinking Reasoning

This phase is crucial for enabling the LLM to generate long-form thoughts before producing a solution.
- **Long-form Thought Dataset Construction:**
 - **Data Collection:** To simplify and manage budget, data is distilled from open o1-like reasoning systems: DeepSeek-R1-Lite-Preview (R1) and QwQ-32B-preview (QwQ). The authors explicitly avoid OpenAI's o1 due to its summarized thought process. Multiple rollouts are performed, and solutions are automatically labeled for correctness.
 - **Format Unification:** R1's clear separation of thought and solution is adopted. The unified format uses specific tokens: `<|begin_of_thought|>...<|end_of_thought|>` and `<|begin_of_solution|>...<|end_of_solution|>`. For QwQ, which lacks an explicit solution component, the fine-tuned reasoning model is prompted to complete the solution based on the generated thought.
 - **Data Mixing:** To ensure domain generalization, demonstration instances are collected from diverse domains: mathematics (NuminaMATH, AIME), coding (LeetCode "Hard"), science (college entrance exams, camel-ai), and puzzles (RiddleSense). The focus is on *challenging problems*, as these are hypothesized to be more beneficial for eliciting long-form reasoning.
 - **Pre-processing:** Rule-based methods (regex, n-gram matching) are used to remove low-quality instances (repetitions, gibberish, language mixtures) and short examples, prioritizing longer, higher-quality thought processes.
- **Long-form Thought Instruction Tuning:**
 - **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is selected for its strong foundational capabilities.
 - **Method:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is applied, with a carefully determined data ratio for each domain. A specific prompt template guides the model to structure its response into "Thought" and "Solution" sections, detailing the reasoning steps. The instruction set, though carefully constructed, is limited to "several thousand demonstration instances," highlighting the efficiency of this approach.

#### 3.3. Exploration and Self-Improvement

This phase aims to move beyond static distillation by allowing the model to generate its own high-quality training data.
- **Exploration on Hard Problems:**
 - **Strategy:** For challenging problems with ground-truth answers, the fine-tuned model performs multiple rollouts. The process continues until a correct trajectory (thought + solution) is generated. This increases the likelihood of finding correct solutions to problems the model initially struggles with.
 - **Focus:** Emphasizes hard problems, particularly from mathematics, as they typically require longer thought processes and are more effective for improving reasoning.
- **Iteratively Refined Training Data:**
 - **Process:** An initial dataset D0 (distilled trajectories) is used to train model M0. M0 then explores and generates new trajectories, which are added to D0 to form D1. This iterative process alternates between training stronger models and generating refined training data.
 - **Filtering:** Strict pre-processing is applied to filter out low-quality trajectories (short, noisy). Perplexity is used as a metric to select and retain more challenging trajectories, further refining the dataset.
 - **Limitation:** Acknowledged scarcity of challenging problems with ground-truth answers, leading to potential exhaustion of the problem pool after a few iterations.
- **Optimization for Self-improvement:**
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The iteratively refined datasets are used for SFT. This is viewed as rejection sampling, as only high-quality rollouts (filtered by length and perplexity) are used. The base model M0 (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) is trained on D_{t-1} to produce M_t.
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** For DPO, paired positive (correct responses with higher perplexity) and negative instances (incorrect responses with lower perplexity) are selected to enhance discriminative learning. M1 (the model after initial imitation learning) serves as the base. An SFT loss is incorporated for stability. The authors explore aligning either the entire response (thought + solution) or just the thought part.
 - **Future Work:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) is identified as a promising but computationally intensive alternative, left for future work.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on three challenging benchmarks: MATH-OAI, AIME2024 (American Invitational Mathematics Examination), and GPQA (Graduate-level Google-Proof Q&A). The backbone model for their variants was Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Effectiveness of Slow Thinking in Industry Systems:** Industry-level slow-thinking systems (o1-preview, DeepSeek-R1-Lite-P, QwQ-32B-preview) consistently achieve excellent performance across all benchmarks, with significant improvements on the highly challenging AIME dataset. This confirms the value of the slow-thinking paradigm for complex reasoning.

2. **Competitive Performance from Distillation-based Imitation (STILL-2):**
 - The distillation-based variant of STILL-2, trained on 3.9k carefully pre-processed instances, achieves highly competitive results, approaching industry-level performance.
 - It reaches 90.2% accuracy on MATH-OAI and an impressive 46.7% on AIME, showing a substantial gain of +251.1% over the backbone model on AIME.
 - This highlights the critical role of careful data cleaning, selection, and mixing of high-quality demonstration instances. Increased quantity of high-quality data also correlates with improved performance.

3. **Promising Results from Exploration and Self-Improvement:**
 - Variants incorporating exploration and self-improvement strategies (SFT or DPO) show significant improvements over the baseline fine-tuned model (e.g., AIME scores improved from 33.3% to 40.0% or 46.7%).
 - Utilizing more explored instances generally leads to better performance.
 - However, the improvements from iterative training are often limited to initial iterations, potentially due to the constrained number of rollouts (max 20) which limits the model's capacity to sufficiently explore challenging problems and find correct solutions. This suggests a bottleneck in the exploration phase itself.

4. **Crucial Role of Hard Problems and Domain Generalization:**
 - **Hard Problems are Essential:** Removing hard mathematical problems from the training data significantly degrades performance, especially on AIME. Problems requiring longer thought processes (often found in hard math) are particularly effective in developing the LLM's long-form thinking capabilities.
 - **Cross-Domain Transferability:** Training primarily on mathematical data (even without explicit data from other domains) yields strong performance across all three benchmarks (math, science). This indicates that the ability to perform long-form reasoning is an **inherent LLM capability** that, once properly elicited or taught, can generalize across different domains.

5. **Insights into DPO Training:**
 - **Thought Alignment:** Aligning only the thought process during DPO training can yield promising results, suggesting that the thought process is the primary learning target, and a well-formed thought often leads predictably to the correct solution.
 - **SFT Loss Integration:** The SFT loss incorporated for DPO stability showed little positive influence when both thought and solution were already included in DPO alignment.
 - The authors note performance fluctuations across DPO runs, suggesting further research is needed for optimal DPO alignment strategies.

In summary, the best-performing variant of STILL-2, relying on distillation from open o1-like systems, achieves near industry-level performance on challenging reasoning tasks. The "explore and self-improve" phases also demonstrate substantial gains over base models, although their full potential is currently limited by exploration constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work holds significant implications for the research and development of advanced LLM reasoning systems, contributing to both open science and the fundamental understanding of LLM capabilities.

1. **Demystification of Proprietary Systems:** By providing a detailed, open-source reproduction report for an o1-like system, the authors significantly contribute to demystifying the "black box" nature of industry-leading LLMs. This transparency allows the broader academic community to understand, replicate, and build upon these advanced reasoning mechanisms, fostering innovation and reducing the reliance on proprietary models.

2. **Validation of a Simplified Paradigm:** The paper provides strong evidence for the hypothesis that complex reward models and explicit, time-consuming tree search algorithms during inference might not be strictly necessary for achieving o1-like reasoning. The success of their "imitate, explore, and self-improve" framework, which emphasizes fine-tuning with high-quality long-form thought data for a single-pass generation, suggests a potentially more efficient and scalable pathway to powerful reasoning. This paradigm shift could redirect research efforts towards data quality, format adherence, and iterative self-improvement rather than solely focusing on complex search strategies or external reward models.

3. **Emphasis on High-Quality Data and Training Strategies:** The findings underscore the critical importance of carefully curated, high-quality long-form thought data, especially from challenging problems, for effectively eliciting and enhancing LLM reasoning. The success of data mixing and pre-processing techniques, alongside the iterative refinement process, provides a practical blueprint for researchers seeking to improve LLM capabilities.

4. **Demonstrated Domain Generalizability of Slow Thinking:** The observation that reasoning ability, once instilled through focused training (e.g., on math problems), generalizes across different domains (math, science) is profound. This suggests that slow thinking is not a domain-specific trick but an inherent, transferable capability of LLMs that can be unlocked and enhanced, paving the way for more generalized and robust AI reasoning agents.

5. **Roadmap for Self-Improvement and Autonomous Learning:** The "explore and self-improve" phases, despite current limitations, offer a concrete framework for LLMs to generate and leverage their own progressively better training data. This moves towards more autonomous and continuous learning, an essential step for future AI systems that can independently refine their skills.

6. **Open Science and Community Enablement:** The commitment to releasing resources (code and models) is a valuable contribution to the open-source community. It lowers the barrier to entry for other researchers to experiment with, extend, and benchmark against this o1-like system, accelerating collective progress in the field.

Looking forward, the authors acknowledge a "substantial capacity gap" still exists compared to leading industry systems, primarily due to current limitations in the exploration phase (e.g., limited rollouts for challenging problems). Future work will focus on scaling their training approach, extending to even more complex tasks, and exploring more advanced methods like reinforcement learning, which could further bridge this gap and unlock the full potential of self-improving slow-thinking LLMs.
