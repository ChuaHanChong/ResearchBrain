# MARBLE: Multi-Aspect Reward Balance for Diffusion RL

> [!summary] Summary
> MARBLE, a gradient-space optimization framework developed by researchers at Zhejiang University and HiThink, enables a single diffusion model to be simultaneously optimized across multiple reward objectives, efficiently balancing competing signals. This approach improves overall quality across five training rewards and four held-out metrics while maintaining training speed comparable to single-reward baselines.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods primarily optimize a single scalar reward, limiting the ability to align models with multi-faceted human preferences.
- Current multi-reward strategies are inefficient (training separate models), labor-intensive and prone to catastrophic forgetting (sequential fine-tuning), or suboptimal (naive weighted-sum aggregation due to signal dilution).
- Gradient interference, a known challenge in multi-task learning, had not been effectively addressed for multi-reward fine-tuning of diffusion models.

## Method

- **MARBLE** decomposes reward signals by maintaining ==independent advantage estimators== and computing policy gradients for each reward, preserving reward-specific supervision.
- Harmonizes per-reward gradients using a ==Quadratic Programming (QP) solver== to find a balanced update direction, adaptively allocating optimization emphasis without manual reward weighting.
- An ==amortized gradient harmonization== technique, combined with ==EMA smoothing== of balancing coefficients, reduces computational overhead and stabilizes the optimization process.

## Results

- MARBLE achieved simultaneous improvements across **five training rewards** (PickScore, HPSv2, CLIPScore, OCR accuracy, GenEval) and demonstrated strong performance on **four held-out metrics** (Aesthetic Score, ImageReward, UniReward, Composite Score), outperforming previous multi-reward baselines.
- The amortized MARBLE variant trained with **0.97x** the speed of a single-reward baseline with a modest **1.14x** GPU memory increase, demonstrating practical efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that **gradient normalization** and **adaptive coefficients** are critical for balanced convergence, and that amortization with EMA smoothing significantly enhances stability and performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive weighted-sum reward aggregation leads to a 'specialist sample phenomenon' and 'dilution of supervision,' where strong signals for one reward are diluted by uninformative signals from others, often resulting in gradient anti-alignment.
> - Gradient-space harmonization, by operating on normalized per-reward gradients, effectively mitigates inter-reward interference and facilitates balanced progress across diverse objectives.
> - Amortization and EMA smoothing of balancing coefficients are crucial for making gradient-space multi-reward optimization practical and stable for diffusion RL, maintaining efficiency while preventing transient batch-level fluctuations from disrupting learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have become a dominant paradigm for generating high-fidelity images, with advancements in latent diffusion, rectified flow, and transformer-based architectures extending their capabilities to diverse tasks such as image customization, editing, and video generation. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a key approach for fine-tuning these diffusion models to align their outputs with human preferences, improving aspects like aesthetic quality, text-image alignment, and compositional accuracy. Early work in diffusion RL primarily utilized policy-gradient-based methods, while more recent advancements, inspired by the success of techniques like GRPO in large language models (LLMs), have adapted similar ideas to diffusion models, leading to improved empirical performance and training efficiency, as seen in methods like DiffusionNFT.

Despite these advancements, existing diffusion RL methods primarily focus on optimizing a single scalar reward. When multiple reward signals are available, practitioners typically resort to several strategies:
1. **Training separate models:** A distinct model is trained for each individual reward. This approach does not yield a unified model and may hinder generalization across different reward dimensions.
2. **Sequential fine-tuning:** A single model is fine-tuned iteratively on different reward datasets following a hand-crafted schedule. This necessitates substantial manual effort for designing the training sequence and hyperparameter tuning, and can lead to catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired rewards.
3. **Weighted-sum aggregation:** Multiple reward signals are combined into a single scalar objective using a weighted sum, R(x) = Σ w_k R_k(x). In practice, directly optimizing with this aggregated reward often results in performance degradation rather than improvement.

This challenge of effectively combining multiple objectives in diffusion RL parallels issues in multi-task learning (MTL), where inter-task gradient interference can cause updates beneficial for one objective to be detrimental to another. Supervised MTL has addressed this through gradient-level optimization strategies, such as finding minimum-norm points in the convex hull of task gradients or projecting out conflicting components. Similar efforts have also gained attention in multi-reward alignment for LLMs. However, the application of gradient harmonization techniques to the multi-reward setting in diffusion RL has been limited. This paper, MARBLE, aims to bridge this gap by adapting gradient harmonization principles to the specific context of diffusion model fine-tuning with RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a principled and effective approach for optimizing a single diffusion model across multiple reward objectives simultaneously, while minimizing heuristic manual tuning. The authors identify specific shortcomings in current multi-reward diffusion RL practices that motivate their work:

1. **Inefficiency and Lack of Unity in Separate Models:** Training a dedicated model for each reward is inefficient, increases model maintenance overhead, and prevents a single generative model from exhibiting diverse desired qualities simultaneously.
2. **High Manual Effort and Forgetting in Sequential Training:** Approaches like DiffusionNFT's manually scheduled multi-stage training (e.g., specific iterations on different rewards) require extensive hyperparameter tuning and curriculum design. This process is time-consuming, prone to trial-and-error, and susceptible to the model forgetting previously learned skills when shifting focus to new rewards. The effort scales unfavorably with the number of rewards.
3. **Failure of Naive Weighted-Sum Aggregation:** The common strategy of combining rewards into a simple weighted sum, R(x) = Σ w_k R_k(x), frequently leads to suboptimal performance. The authors attribute this failure to what they term the "specialist sample phenomenon" and subsequent "dilution of supervision":
 - **Specialist Samples:** Many generated samples are highly informative for only a subset of reward dimensions and largely irrelevant or inapplicable to others. For instance, an image of an animal provides little to no signal for an OCR-related reward, and an aesthetically pleasing image might be average in terms of text rendering accuracy.
 - **Dilution of Supervision:** When such specialist samples are processed with a weighted-sum reward, the genuine signal from the relevant dimension is diluted by the presence of uninformative dimensions. This results in an aggregated advantage value that does not accurately reflect the specific utility of the sample for the dimensions on which it is truly strong.
 - **Empirical Gradient Anti-Alignment:** The authors empirically demonstrate this dilution at the gradient level. They observe that the update direction derived from a weighted-sum reward is anti-aligned with at least one single-reward gradient in 80% of mini-batches, meaning the model's overall update actively pushes against improvements for certain reward dimensions.

To address these issues, MARBLE aims to:
- Preserve reward-specific supervision throughout the optimization process.
- Compute policy gradients independently for each reward.
- Harmonize these per-reward gradients into a single, balanced update direction without requiring manual reward weighting.
- Develop an efficient formulation to minimize computational overhead during multi-reward training.
- Stabilize the balancing mechanism against transient batch-level fluctuations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MARBLE (Multi-Aspect Reward BaLancE) is a gradient-space optimization framework designed to jointly optimize a single diffusion model across multiple reward objectives. It builds upon the DiffusionNFT framework for its noise-free training (NFT) loss.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: DiffusionNFT Loss

The DiffusionNFT loss for a single reward R(x) is given by ℓ(θ; x, t) = r · L+(θ) + (1 − r) · L−(θ), where `r = clamp(1/2 + A(x)/(2A_max), 0, 1)` maps the advantage `A(x)` to an interpolation coefficient. A crucial property is that the velocity prediction losses, L+ and L-, are independent of the advantage value; the advantage only influences the loss through the affine mapping to `r`.

#### 3.2. Multi-Reward Gradient Harmonization

- **Per-Reward Advantage Decomposition:** Instead of aggregating rewards into a single scalar, MARBLE maintains an independent advantage estimator for each reward R_k. For each reward, `A_k(x)` is calculated by normalizing `R_k(x)` within its prompt group using running mean `μ_k` and standard deviation `σ_k`. Each `A_k` then yields a reward-specific interpolation coefficient `r_k`, which defines a separate NFT loss `ℓ_k`. A backward pass through `ℓ_k` computes the corresponding policy gradient `g_k = ∇_θ (1/(NT) Σ ℓ_k(θ; x_i, t))`. This decomposition ensures that each sample contributes its specific, undiluted signal to the relevant reward dimensions.

- **Gradient Normalization and Harmonization:** Different reward models can produce gradients of vastly different magnitudes. To resolve this scale disparity, each gradient `g_k` is first normalized to `ˆg_k = g_k / ||g_k||`. MARBLE then computes a unified update direction by solving a convex Quadratic Programming (QP) problem, a technique adapted from multi-task learning:
 `α* = arg min_α∈Δ_K ||Σ α_k ˆg_k||^2`
 where `Δ_K` is the probability simplex. The solution `α*` represents a set of balancing coefficients. The resulting direction `d* = Σ α*_k ˆg_k` is the minimum-norm point in the convex hull of the normalized gradients, offering a balanced compromise across all reward dimensions.

- **Rescaling and KL-Decoupled Update:** Since `d*` is derived from unit-normalized gradients, its magnitude needs to be restored to align with the optimizer's expected step size and the KL regularization schedule. This is achieved by multiplying `d*` by the mean norm of the original gradients: `d_final = d* · ¯n`, where `¯n = (1/K) Σ ||g_k||`. The final parameter update combines `d_final` with the KL regularization term as a separate component: `θ ← θ − η (d_final + β_KL · ∇_θ D_KL(π_θ || π_ref))`. This separation is crucial because reward gradients optimize for specific aspects, while the KL term controls the divergence from the reference model.

#### 3.3. Amortized Gradient Harmonization

To mitigate the computational cost of performing K+1 backward passes per iteration and reduce variance from per-step QP solutions, MARBLE introduces an amortized variant:

- **Scalarization Equivalence:** The authors leverage a proposition demonstrating that, due to the affine structure of the NFT loss, a convex combination of per-reward NFT gradients (`Σ α_k ∇_θ ℓ_k(θ; A_k)`) is exactly equivalent to `∇_θ ℓ(θ; Σ α_k A_k)`, which is a single backward pass using a combined advantage `¯A = Σ α_k A_k`. This equivalence holds when the clamping mechanism in the NFT loss is inactive, which is empirically observed to be the case during training.

- **Amortized Procedure:** The full harmonization procedure (computing `α*` from normalized gradients) is performed only every `N` steps. During the `N-1` intervening steps, the *cached* coefficients `α*` are used to compute the combined advantage `¯A = Σ α*_k A_k`, and only a single reward backward pass is performed. This approach retains the scale-invariant balancing information from the full harmonization while significantly reducing the average per-step computational cost from `(K+1)×` to `(K+N)/N ×` that of a single-reward baseline.

#### 3.4. Coefficient Smoothing for Stable Amortization

Amortization can make the optimization sensitive to short-term fluctuations in `α*` (e.g., a mini-batch with weak signal for a specific reward might cause its `α_k` to drop to near zero, effectively silencing it for the entire amortization window). To address this, MARBLE applies Exponential Moving Average (EMA) smoothing to the balancing coefficients: `¯α_t = ρ¯α_(t-1) + (1 − ρ)α*_t`, where `ρ` is the EMA decay factor (set to 0.7 by default). The smoothed `¯α_t` is then used to construct the combined advantage `¯A`. This mechanism prevents transient batch failures from completely removing a reward signal, improving stability while allowing coefficients to adapt over time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates MARBLE on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium, fine-tuning LoRA adapters to optimize five distinct reward functions: PickScore, HPSv2, CLIPScore (general-purpose), and OCR accuracy, along with GenEval (specialist rewards). Performance is also assessed on three held-out metrics: Aesthetic Score, ImageReward, and UniReward. Comparisons are made against single-reward FlowGRPO specialists and two multi-reward DiffusionNFT variants (sequential fine-tuning and simultaneous weighted-sum aggregation).

- **Overall Performance (Table 1):**
 - MARBLE demonstrates simultaneous improvement across all five training rewards within a single model.
 - Single-reward FlowGRPO specialists perform well on their specific objective but show limited transfer to other rewards.
 - DiffusionNFT† (sequential) achieves comparable quality but at the cost of extensive manual tuning for stage scheduling and hyperparameters.
 - DiffusionNFT‡ (simultaneous, weighted-sum) performs substantially worse, particularly on specialist objectives, supporting the paper's "specialist sample" hypothesis.
 - MARBLE matches or exceeds both DiffusionNFT baselines, particularly excelling on four held-out quality metrics. While DiffusionNFT† shows slightly higher PickScore and CLIPScore, MARBLE achieves the highest "Composite" score (mean of column-wise z-scores across all eight metrics), indicating a superior overall balance.

- **Training Efficiency (Table 2):**
 - The full harmonization procedure (without amortization) has a noticeable overhead, reducing training speed to 0.56× relative to the weighted-sum baseline due to multiple backward passes.
 - The amortized MARBLE variant, however, significantly reduces this overhead, achieving 0.97× relative speed, which is close to the weighted-sum baseline.
 - Both MARBLE variants require a modest increase in GPU memory (1.14× relative memory). These results indicate that amortization makes gradient-space reward balancing practical without substantial training speed degradation.

- **Ablation Studies and Analysis (Table 3, Appendix C.3-C.6):**
 - **Gradient Normalization:** It is critical for the harmony mechanism. Without normalizing gradients before solving the QP, optimization fails due to degenerate or numerically unstable coefficients, as the optimization becomes sensitive to raw gradient magnitudes rather than directional relationships.
 - **Adaptive vs. Fixed Coefficients:** Using fixed uniform coefficients (`α_k = 0.2`) leads to imbalanced convergence. General-purpose rewards improve quickly, while harder specialist objectives remain under-optimized. MARBLE's adaptive coefficients dynamically allocate more optimization emphasis to tasks that are harder to improve, leading to more balanced progress. The best performance is achieved by dynamic coefficients during most of training, followed by a short uniform-coefficient stage.
 - **Coefficient Amortization:** Solving for `α` at every step without amortization increases training cost and introduces high-frequency variation into the update direction due to mini-batch fluctuations, negatively affecting stability.
 - **Amortization Interval (N):** An interval of N=10 for refreshing coefficients provides a balance between responsiveness and efficiency, yielding similar performance to N=5 but being faster, while N=20 shows performance degradation.
 - **EMA Decay (ρ):** An EMA decay of `ρ = 0.7` for coefficient smoothing is found to provide the optimal balance between adaptivity and stability, preventing transient batch failures from suppressing reward signals for extended periods.

- **Update-Direction Harmony (Appendix C.2):** The weighted-sum method exhibits negative worst-reward alignment in 80% of mini-batches. In contrast, MARBLE consistently maintains a positive worst-reward alignment, indicating that its updates consistently move in a direction that benefits all reward objectives.

- **Human Preference Evaluation (Appendix C.7):** A blind user study revealed that MARBLE-generated images received the highest average scores for both text-image alignment and image quality, despite not having the highest scores on every individual automatic metric (e.g., PickScore/CLIPScore). This suggests that MARBLE's balanced multi-dimensional improvement leads to outputs that are more consistently preferred by human evaluators.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MARBLE represents a significant contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in the alignment of diffusion models with complex, multi-faceted human preferences.

- **Novelty in Multi-Reward Diffusion RL:** MARBLE is presented as the first method to address multi-reward balancing specifically for diffusion model RL fine-tuning. It fills a critical gap by providing a principled framework for joint optimization across diverse objectives.
- **Addressing Key Limitations of Prior Work:** The method effectively overcomes the limitations of previous approaches:
 - It eliminates the need for labor-intensive, heuristic manual tuning required by sequential fine-tuning schedules.
 - It offers a unified model capable of simultaneously satisfying multiple quality dimensions, unlike methods that train separate specialist models.
 - Crucially, it resolves the "specialist sample dilution" problem inherent in naive weighted-sum reward aggregation by preserving reward-specific supervision through gradient-space harmonization.
- **Practical Efficiency and Stability:** The amortized gradient harmonization, combined with EMA smoothing for balancing coefficients, makes MARBLE computationally efficient (training speed comparable to scalarized baselines) and stable. This ensures its practicality for large-scale, real-world diffusion model training.
- **Foundation for Scalable Alignment:** By demonstrating the effectiveness of gradient-space optimization for balancing competing rewards in diffusion RL, MARBLE provides a useful foundation for future work on scalable multi-objective alignment for a broader range of generative models.
- **Broader Applicability:** The principles underlying MARBLE could potentially extend beyond image generation. The authors propose future work in video diffusion and generative world models, where the complexity and heterogeneity of quality dimensions (e.g., temporal consistency, motion realism, physical plausibility) make robust multi-objective optimization even more critical. Scaling MARBLE to larger and more diverse reward sets is also identified as an important future direction.

In summary, MARBLE offers a robust, efficient, and principled solution for a prevalent challenge in aligning advanced generative models, potentially paving the way for more controllable and comprehensively high-quality outputs across various generative tasks.
