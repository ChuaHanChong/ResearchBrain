# DEAS: DEtached value learning with Action Sequence for Scalable Offline RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST, UC Berkeley, UT Austin, and NVIDIA developed DEAS, an offline reinforcement learning framework leveraging detached value learning with action sequences. This method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on complex, long-horizon tasks and enhances large-scale Vision-Language-Action models in simulated and real-world robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods struggle with complex, long-horizon sequential decision-making due to issues like distribution shift and compounding errors.
- Directly incorporating action sequences into offline RL actor-critic frameworks often leads to severe value overestimation and instability, hindering their practical use.
- Enhancing large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for generalist robots through offline RL is challenging, typically requiring extensive and costly expert demonstrations.

## Method

- Formalizes consecutive H-step action sequences as 'options' within a ==Semi-Markov Decision Process== (SMDP) to achieve principled horizon reduction for long-term planning.
- Introduces detached value learning, inspired by Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), to prevent value overestimation by decoupling critic and actor training and biasing value estimates towards in-distribution, high-return actions.
- Integrates distributional RL with categorical value and critic networks, along with dual discount factors, to enhance stability and model the full return distribution with temporal abstractions.

## Results

- DEAS consistently outperformed baselines across 30 long-horizon tasks in OGBench, achieving higher success rates in the majority of complex scenarios.
- It significantly improved the fine-tuning of GR00T N1.5, a large-scale VLA model, achieving higher success rates in 3 out of 4 RoboCasa Kitchen simulation tasks and demonstrating superior partial success in real-world robotic manipulation.
- The framework scaled robustly across varying dataset sizes (**1M** to **100M** transitions), consistently outperforming baselines and reducing the reliance on costly expert demonstrations for VLA model deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporally extended action sequences, when treated as options within an SMDP, effectively reduce the planning horizon for long-horizon tasks without requiring explicit goal conditioning.
> - Decoupling value network training from the actor, combined with distributional value estimation using expectile regression, stabilizes learning and mitigates value overestimation when using action sequences in offline RL.
> - The use of dual discount factors and moderate action sequence lengths is crucial for balancing immediate and future returns, optimizing performance and stability in offline learning with temporal abstractions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "DEAS" directly addresses critical limitations within the rapidly evolving field of **Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL)**. Offline RL aims to learn effective policies from static, pre-collected datasets without further interaction with the environment. This paradigm offers significant advantages by eliminating the risks and costs associated with online data collection, especially in real-world robotics.

Historically, offline RL methods have primarily focused on **short-horizon tasks** with dense reward signals. Scaling these methods to **complex, long-horizon sequential decision-making problems** has been a major challenge. The difficulty arises from several factors:
1. **Distribution Shift:** Policies learned offline can easily drift out of the data distribution, leading to querying Q-values for out-of-distribution actions, which can result in severe value overestimation and suboptimal performance.
2. **Effective Planning Horizon:** In long-horizon tasks, agents must plan over many steps, making accurate value estimation and policy learning incredibly difficult due to the compounding of errors. While n-step TD updates can reduce the effective planning horizon, they often introduce increased bias without explicit goal information.
3. **Scalability to Real-World Systems:** Applying offline RL to large-scale robotic systems, particularly those powered by **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**, is a crucial next step for generalist robots. However, current RL methods often struggle to effectively leverage the multi-step action predictions inherent in these VLAs.

**DEAS positions itself in the landscape by:**
- **Tackling Long-Horizon Tasks without Goal Conditioning:** Unlike prior work that relies on explicit goal-conditioned RL or expert-provided goals for horizon reduction, DEAS employs action sequences, inherently providing richer temporal information and reducing the effective planning horizon in a goal-free manner. This is a significant advancement for broader applicability.
- **Mitigating Value Overestimation with Action Sequences:** The use of action sequences in standard actor-critic frameworks exacerbates value overestimation due to the expanded action space and limited data coverage in offline settings. DEAS proposes "detached value learning," building on Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), to address this by biasing value estimates towards in-distribution, high-return actions from the offline dataset. This is a novel approach to combining the benefits of action sequences with stability guarantees.
- **Enhancing VLA Models:** The work explicitly demonstrates DEAS's ability to improve the performance of large-scale VLA models (e.g., GR00T N1.5) on complex manipulation tasks. This bridges a critical gap between powerful generative models that predict action sequences and robust RL fine-tuning, offering a pathway to reduce the reliance on expensive expert demonstrations for VLA deployment.
- **Leveraging Hierarchical RL Principles:** By treating action sequences as "options" within a Semi-Markov Decision Process (SMDP), DEAS implicitly taps into principles from hierarchical reinforcement learning. This allows for temporal abstraction without requiring explicit option discovery or human-defined hierarchies, making it more flexible.

Compared to related work like Q-Chunking (which struggles with overestimation) or CQN-AS (which introduces discretization errors and limits policy expressiveness), DEAS offers a robust and scalable solution that combines the benefits of temporal abstraction with stable offline learning mechanisms, making it a significant contribution to the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop a scalable offline RL framework for complex, long-horizon tasks:** Current offline RL methods struggle when the decision-making horizon becomes long, leading to poor performance. The authors aim to overcome this by introducing a mechanism that effectively reduces the planning horizon.
2. **Leverage action sequences for value learning:** The core idea is that temporally extended actions, or "action sequences," provide richer information and can simplify long-horizon planning compared to single-step actions. This is inspired by the options framework and n-step TD updates.
3. **Address value overestimation and instability:** Directly incorporating action sequences into standard actor-critic algorithms can lead to severe value overestimation, especially in offline settings with distribution shift. A key objective is to develop a robust method to prevent this instability.
4. **Enhance the performance of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:** The authors aim to demonstrate the practical applicability of their method by improving the fine-tuning of existing VLA models, which are increasingly used in robotics but often require extensive expert demonstrations. This objective seeks to reduce the need for costly expert data by effectively utilizing suboptimal datasets.

**Motivation:**
The motivation for DEAS stems from the limitations of existing offline RL approaches:
- **Inability to Scale:** Current methods predominantly succeed on short-horizon tasks, leaving a significant gap for real-world applications that require complex, sequential decision-making.
- **Dependency on Expert Data/Goals:** Some advanced techniques for horizon reduction rely on explicit goal conditioning or expert-provided goals, which are often unavailable or expensive to obtain. The authors are motivated to find an alternative that works in goal-free settings.
- **Challenges with Action Sequences:** While action sequences hold promise for capturing temporal dependencies and improving exploration (as shown in behavior cloning), their direct integration into RL has been problematic due to issues like value overestimation and discretization errors. The authors are motivated to harness the power of action sequences while addressing these inherent difficulties.
- **Practical Relevance for Robotics:** The rise of powerful VLA models offers unprecedented capabilities for robotics, but their deployment often involves expensive fine-tuning. A robust offline RL method that can leverage suboptimal data to improve these models would significantly accelerate real-world robotic applications by reducing data collection costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DEAS (DEtached value learning with Action Sequence) is a novel offline RL framework designed to learn scalable policies for complex, long-horizon tasks by explicitly leveraging action sequences. The methodology integrates several key components:

#### 3.1. Options Framework for Action Sequence RL

- **Action Sequences as Options:** DEAS formalizes consecutive H-step action sequences, denoted as $o_t := a_{t:t+H-1}$, as single decision units, or "options." This is inspired by the **options framework** in hierarchical RL, which allows for temporally extended actions.
- **Semi-Markov Decision Process (SMDP) Q-learning:** By treating action sequences as options, the underlying decision process becomes an SMDP. This formulation naturally leads to a Q-learning update rule that aggregates rewards over the H steps of an option ($ \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} \gamma_1^k R(s_t, a_{t+k}) $) and propagates value estimates across these extended transitions. This effectively achieves **horizon reduction**, similar to n-step TD learning, but with a more principled approach through temporally extended actions that carry richer information without requiring explicit goal conditioning.

#### 3.2. DEAS: Detached Value Learning with Action Sequence

The core innovation of DEAS lies in its approach to stable value learning with action sequences, which addresses the inherent instability and value overestimation issues:

- **Detached Value Learning (Inspired by IQL):** To prevent value overestimation, which is exacerbated by expanded action spaces of sequences, DEAS adopts a detached value learning approach. This method, building on **Implicit Q-Learning (IQL)**, decouples the training of the critic from the actor.
 - It maintains a critic network $Q(s_t, o_t; \theta)$ and a value network $V(s_t; \psi)$.
 - The value network $V$ is trained using an **expectile regression loss** ($L_V$) against the target Q-values from the offline dataset. By using an expectile parameter $\tau > 0.5$, $V$ is encouraged to approximate the value of *high-return actions* within the offline data distribution, effectively penalizing overestimation for out-of-distribution actions.
 - The critic network $Q$ is then trained to minimize the squared error ($L_Q$) between its prediction and a Bellman target, which incorporates the aggregated rewards over the action sequence ($ \hat{R}_{t:t+H-1} $) and the value estimate of the next state after the option completes ($ \gamma_2^H V(s_{t+H}; \psi) $).
 - This decoupling ensures that the critic's estimates are steered toward values achievable by in-distribution actions, preventing the actor from exploiting erroneous critic estimates for out-of-distribution actions.

- **Distributional RL for Enhanced Stability:** To mitigate variance from cumulative rewards, especially with longer action sequences, DEAS integrates **distributional RL**. Instead of predicting a single Q-value, both the critic and value networks model **categorical distributions** over a fixed support range ($[v_{min}, v_{max}]$) discretized into *m* bins.
 - The learning objectives ($L_V$, $L_Q$) are modified to use **cross-entropy loss** between predicted categorical distributions and target distributions.
 - The target probabilities are derived from a truncated normal distribution centered around the Bellman target (which includes the sum of intra-option rewards and the discounted next state value). This helps stabilize training by modeling the full return distribution rather than just its expectation.

- **Dual Discount Factors:** DEAS employs two distinct discount factors for improved stability and expressiveness:
 - **$ \gamma_1 $ (Intra-option discount factor):** Applied to rewards *within* an action sequence ($ \hat{R}_{t:t+H-1} $).
 - **$ \gamma_2 $ (Inter-option discount factor):** Applied to the value of the next state *after* an option completes ($ \gamma_2^H V(s_{t+H}; \psi) $).
 This dual-discounting scheme allows for more nuanced control over immediate vs. future returns, which is crucial for stable learning with variable-length temporal abstractions.

- **Compatible Policy Methods:** A significant advantage of DEAS is its flexibility in policy extraction. Since the value function training does not directly rely on the actor's outputs (due to detachment), DEAS is compatible with various policy extraction strategies, such as weighted behavior cloning, deterministic policy gradient (DPG), best-of-N sampling, or flow-matching approaches. This modularity allows it to be combined with advanced policy architectures, including large-scale VLAs.

The overall approach of DEAS combines the benefits of temporal abstraction from the options framework with robust offline RL techniques (detached value learning, distributional RL, dual discount factors) to achieve stable and scalable learning on complex, long-horizon tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors validate DEAS through extensive experiments across challenging benchmarks and real-world scenarios, demonstrating its superior performance and practical applicability.

#### 4.1. OGBench Experiments (Complex Long-Horizon Tasks)

- **Superior Performance:** DEAS consistently outperformed all baselines (FQL, N-step FQL, QC-FQL, CQN-AS) across 6 task categories, encompassing 30 diverse scenarios, from OGBench. It achieved the highest success rates in the majority of tasks, especially those requiring long-horizon reasoning like "puzzle" and "cube-quadruple" tasks.
- **Addressing N-step TD Limitations:** While N-step FQL (extending FQL with n-step TD updates) often showed performance degradation due to increased bias, DEAS maintained stability and significantly improved results, validating the benefits of its detached value learning with action sequences.
- **Outperforming Q-Chunking:** DEAS matched or outperformed QC-FQL across all tasks, highlighting the effectiveness of detached value learning in mitigating instability that arises when action sequences are directly used in actor-critic frameworks.
- **Limitations of CQN-AS:** CQN-AS, a value-only method with action sequences, showed significantly lower performance, attributed to strong BC regularization on suboptimal data and cumulative discretization errors limiting action precision.
- **Scalability Analysis:** DEAS demonstrated robust scaling across varying dataset sizes (1M to 100M transitions), consistently outperforming baselines and maintaining performance gains even with larger datasets, confirming its capability to leverage suboptimal data effectively.

#### 4.2. VLA Experiments (Robotics Applications)

- **RoboCasa Kitchen Simulation:** DEAS was applied to fine-tune GR00T N1.5, a large-scale VLA model, on 4 challenging RoboCasa Kitchen tasks.
 - It achieved the highest success rates in 3 out of 4 tasks and showed improved performance in the remaining one, outperforming Filtered BC, IQL, and QC.
 - This demonstrates DEAS's ability to effectively utilize suboptimal data alongside limited expert demonstrations, reducing the reliance on costly expert data for VLA fine-tuning.
- **Real-World Manipulation Tasks:** DEAS was further validated on real-world pick-and-place tasks using a Franka Emika Research 3 Robot Arm with three different objects (peach, milka, hichew).
 - DEAS achieved the highest partial success rates across all three tasks, showing consistent improvements over baselines.
 - It demonstrated particular strength on challenging objects like 'milka' (a deformable object), where other approaches struggled.
 - QC showed degraded performance, indicating its instability with action sequences on relatively small datasets, whereas DEAS maintained stable improvement even with limited real-world data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analyses

- **Effect of Action Sequence Length (H):** Optimal performance was observed at moderate sequence lengths (H=4 or H=8). Very short sequences (H=1, 2) yielded poor results, confirming the necessity of temporal abstraction. Excessively long sequences (H=16) required larger actor networks, suggesting a trade-off.
- **Effect of Network Size:** Increasing critic network capacity initially improved performance, while the value network required sufficient but not excessive capacity, as overly large value networks without proper regularization could lead to instability.
- **Effect of Training Objective:** Combining detached value learning (IQL) with distributional estimation (HLG) significantly improved results compared to using either component alone, indicating their synergistic importance for stable learning with action sequences.
- **Effect of Dual Discount Factors ($\gamma_1, \gamma_2$):** Proper tuning of $\gamma_1$ (intra-option discount) was crucial for performance, especially maintaining it at a slightly lower value (e.g., 0.9) than $\gamma_2$ (e.g., 0.99 or 0.999), demonstrating its role in balancing immediate vs. future returns for stability.

In summary, DEAS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art offline RL methods on complex long-horizon tasks, scales robustly with varying dataset sizes, and significantly enhances the performance of large-scale VLA models in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation. The ablation studies provide strong evidence for the necessity and effectiveness of each proposed component.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DEAS framework makes several significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning and robotics, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Advancing Offline RL for Long-Horizon Tasks:** DEAS directly addresses one of the most persistent challenges in offline RL: effectively learning policies for complex, long-horizon tasks. By principled horizon reduction via action sequences and SMDP Q-learning, it pushes the boundaries of what offline RL can achieve beyond short-horizon, dense-reward scenarios. This opens up new possibilities for applying offline RL to more realistic and intricate problems.

2. **Robustness to Value Overestimation with Action Sequences:** The introduction of detached value learning, combined with distributional RL, provides a stable and effective mechanism to use temporally extended action sequences in offline RL. This overcomes a critical limitation that has hindered prior attempts to leverage action sequences in actor-critic frameworks, making temporal abstraction a more viable strategy for offline learning.

3. **Scalability and Reduced Data Requirements for Robotics:** DEAS's ability to enhance large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by effectively utilizing suboptimal datasets is a game-changer for robot learning.
 - **Reduced Expert Data Dependence:** It significantly reduces the reliance on expensive and time-consuming expert demonstrations, making the fine-tuning of generalist robot models more practical and accessible. This is crucial for democratizing advanced robotics.
 - **Improved Performance with Existing Models:** By showing performance gains on models like GR00T N1.5 in both simulation (RoboCasa Kitchen) and real-world robotic tasks, DEAS offers a concrete method to immediately improve the capabilities of current state-of-the-art robotic systems.

4. **Modular and Compatible Framework:** The compatibility of DEAS with various policy extraction methods and expressive policy architectures (like transformer-based VLAs) highlights its modularity and broad applicability. This allows researchers and practitioners to leverage DEAS with their preferred policy models, maximizing flexibility.

5. **Path Towards Generalist Agents:** By enabling robust learning from diverse, suboptimal data and enhancing VLA models, DEAS contributes to the ongoing effort to develop more generalist and adaptable robotic agents that can perform a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments. Its goal-free approach to temporal abstraction further supports this vision.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact of DEAS is in making offline RL more practical and scalable for real-world robotic deployments, potentially accelerating the development of autonomous systems in logistics, manufacturing, and even household assistance. By lowering the data barrier for VLA model fine-tuning, it could lead to faster iteration cycles and more capable robots. Looking ahead, the principles of detached value learning with action sequences could inspire new architectures and algorithms for hierarchical and temporal abstraction in various RL domains, fostering more efficient and robust learning across complex decision-making problems.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as fixed action sequence lengths and challenges in scaling to unified value functions for thousands of tasks, the foundational contributions of DEAS lay a strong groundwork for future research in these areas.
