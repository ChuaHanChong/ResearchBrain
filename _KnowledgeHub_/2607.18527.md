# DASH Robot: Minimalistic Design and Optimal Aerial-Terrestrial Locomotion via Contact-Implicit Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The DASH robot, developed at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, integrates a minimalistic ducted fan and spring-loaded leg design with Contact-Implicit Control to achieve versatile aerial-terrestrial locomotion. This system autonomously transitions between hopping and flying based on environmental constraints, demonstrating that hopping reduces active control effort to 26.78% of continuous hovering.

## Problem

- Current mobile robots are limited by single-modality locomotion (e.g., legged robots struggle with large obstacles, aerial robots are energy-intensive).
- Hybrid robots often suffer from mechanical complexity and lack unified control frameworks for seamless, optimal mode transitions.
- Traditional control strategies for hybrid systems rely on mode-specific controllers or predefined schedules, resulting in suboptimal performance and limited adaptability.

## Method

- Designed **DASH**, a hybrid robot featuring a ducted fan for aerial thrust and a pogo-stick inspired spring leg for energy-efficient terrestrial hopping.
- Implemented ==Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC)== to autonomously determine locomotion modes and contact sequences through a unified optimization framework.
- Utilized ==Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP)== with relaxed analytic gradients to solve the MPC problem, enabling smooth handling of contact dynamics and mode transitions.

## Results

- Achieved a reduction in active control effort for hopping to **26.78%** of the energy required for continuous hovering.
- Successfully demonstrated autonomous transitions between hopping and flying in response to physical obstacles, without predefined mode schedules.
- DASH performed stable hovering, tracked linear velocities up to **0.5 m/s**, and executed continuous in-place hopping in both simulation and hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A minimalistic co-design of ducted fan propulsion and a spring-loaded leg enables a versatile aerial-terrestrial hybrid robot.
> - Contact-Implicit Control can autonomously orchestrate distinct locomotion behaviors, like hopping and flying, without requiring pre-programmed mode switches.
> - Leveraging passive energy exchange through a spring leg during ground interaction significantly improves energy efficiency compared to continuous aerial flight.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of mobile robots capable of operating across diverse and challenging environments has motivated research into hybrid locomotion systems. Traditional legged robots excel at traversing irregular ground but encounter limitations with large discontinuities or obstacles beyond their reach. Conversely, aerial robots can bypass such obstacles but often incur high energetic costs and have limited endurance, restricting their operational scope. The integration of these complementary modalities into hybrid aerial-terrestrial robots seeks to combine the ground efficiency of legs with the agility of flight, aiming to expand mobility for applications such as search-and-rescue, inspection, and exploration.

Within this field, several platforms have explored combinations of wheeled or legged locomotion with aerial capabilities. Prior work includes wheeled aerial robots and legged systems like PogoX and Hopcopter, which demonstrate hopping-flying capabilities. These systems typically utilize open-propeller designs. Ducted fans, while recognized in aerial vehicle design for their thrust efficiency, compactness, safety, and potential for physical interaction, have been less frequently applied to hybrid mobile robots. Existing ducted fan applications in robotics have largely focused on purely aerial vehicles or specific interactions like flying-crawling or bipedal assistance.

A significant challenge in hybrid robot design lies in locomotion control, particularly in managing the inherent coupling of continuous dynamics with discrete contact events (stance, liftoff, landing). Conventional control approaches often rely on designing separate controllers for individual modes (e.g., terrestrial vs. aerial) or predefining mode schedules. This can restrict optimality and requires anticipating all possible contact sequences, limiting the robot's adaptability in unstructured environments. Layered designs, which combine high-level planning with low-level control, often use simplified models that may not capture complex dynamic constraints.

To address these control limitations, the Contact-Implicit Control (CIC) paradigm has emerged in legged robotics and manipulation. CIC aims to eliminate the need for manually specifying contact sequences or preselecting locomotion modes. By formulating contact dynamics using complementarity conditions, CIC allows the robot to autonomously determine contact events and optimize locomotion behaviors directly from the control problem, rather than through handcrafted logic. While CI-MPC has been applied to legged locomotion and manipulation, its potential for planning across diverse locomotion modes in hybrid mobile robots has remained largely unexplored. This research contributes to integrating ducted fan design with a CIC framework to enhance the versatility and mobility of hybrid aerial-terrestrial robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to design and control a novel hybrid aerial-terrestrial robot, named DASH (Ducted Aerial Spring Hopper), that exhibits minimalistic mechanical design, locomotion versatility, and energy efficiency. The authors aim to enable both aerial flight and ground hopping capabilities on a single platform.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several limitations in existing hybrid robotic systems:
- **Mechanical Complexity:** Many hybrid robots tend to be mechanically complex, which can impact robustness, manufacturing cost, and energy consumption. The authors aim for a minimalistic design that organically integrates propulsion and terrestrial interaction mechanisms.
- **Locomotion Versatility:** Single-modality robots are inherently limited by their environment. Legged robots struggle with large gaps and obstacles, while aerial robots are energy-intensive. A versatile hybrid robot can overcome these limitations, traversing varied terrains and obstacles more effectively.
- **Energy Efficiency:** Sustained aerial flight is energetically costly. Integrating ground locomotion, particularly hopping with energy circulation via elastic elements, can significantly improve overall energy efficiency and endurance, especially for tasks requiring extensive ground-based travel with intermittent aerial maneuvers.
- **Control Challenges for Hybrid Systems:** Traditional control methods for hybrid robots often involve:
 - **Mode-Specific Controllers:** Designing separate controllers for each locomotion mode (e.g., flying, hopping) and then implementing explicit switching logic between them. This approach is often suboptimal and inflexible, requiring manual anticipation of all possible contact sequences.
 - **Layered Architectures:** Using simplified models for high-level planning, which may not capture full dynamic constraints, leading to suboptimal or infeasible trajectories during dynamic maneuvers.
 - **Lack of Unified Planning:** Current approaches often lack a unified framework that can autonomously select and transition between locomotion modes based on environmental context and task optimality.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives for DASH include:
1. **Novel Design:** Develop a minimalistic mechanical design that organically integrates a ducted fan for aerial thrust and thrust vectoring with a spring-loaded leg for efficient terrestrial hopping. The use of a ducted fan is motivated by its advantages over open propellers in terms of thrust density, compactness, safety, and durability.
2. **Optimal Locomotion:** Implement a control strategy that can autonomously choose between aerial and terrestrial locomotion modes and actuation, without requiring predefined mode schedules.
3. **Energy-Efficient Performance:** Achieve efficient ground hopping through energy circulation via the spring leg, reducing the active energy injection compared to continuous flight.
4. **Seamless Mode Transitions:** Enable autonomous and seamless transitions between hopping and flying, particularly during obstacle traversal, based on dynamic constraints and optimality criteria.
5. **Validation:** Validate the design and control of DASH through experimental tasks, including periodic hopping, aerial flight, and mode-free locomotion with autonomous mode transitions in response to environmental changes.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to demonstrate an advanced hybrid robot capable of agile and efficient movement in complex environments, driven by a unified, contact-implicit control framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved a co-design approach, integrating a minimalistic robot hardware design with an advanced contact-implicit control framework.

#### 3.1. A. Robot Design (DASH: Ducted Aerial Spring Hopper)

The mechanical design of DASH focused on combining thrust-assisted aerial locomotion with spring-loaded terrestrial hopping through an integrated architecture.

1. **Mechanical Design:**
 - **Central Body:** A 3D-printed duct (using PLA-Aero) encloses a coaxial fan assembly, providing safety, aerodynamic efficiency, and structural support. The duct has a 10-inch radius, contributing to moment authority for thrust vectoring.
 - **Propulsion:** Two motorized coaxial propellers mounted within the duct via carbon-fiber tubes and 3D-printed PLA-CF materials generate vertical thrust.
 - **Thrust Vectoring:** Four 3D-printed vanes attached to the bottom of the duct provide directional control by vectoring the airflow, essential for stabilization and maneuverability.
 - **Leg Mechanism:** A pogo-stick inspired spring leg is attached to the duct. This elastic tubing spring stores and releases elastic energy for efficient hopping and reduces thrust requirements for takeoff. The leg's vertical motion is constrained by spaced pulleys to ensure smooth translation and minimize slack.
 - **Weight:** The total system mass is approximately 3.27 kg, with a height of 4 feet, balancing robustness and manufacturability.

2. **Electronics and Actuation:**
 - **Propulsion Motors:** Two coaxial brushless DC motors drive the ducted fan.
 - **Servos:** Four servo actuators control the vanes for thrust vectoring.
 - **Flight Controller (PX4):** Manages low-level motor regulation and actuator communication. A quaternion-based geometric attitude controller tracks planned orientations.
 - **Single-Board Computer (SBC):** An Intel i7-equipped UP-Squared-i12 executes higher-level planning and control in real-time.
 - **Sensors:** A top-mounted IMU provides orientation estimation. Translational states are obtained from an external motion capture system, with a future goal of onboard visual-inertial estimation.
 - **Power:** A single 6S LiPo battery powers all electronics, with a distribution system designed to isolate high-current actuation from sensitive components.

#### 3.2. B. Robot Dynamics

The robot's dynamics are modeled to capture both aerial and terrestrial behaviors.

1. **Actuation Model:**
 - **Thrust Force:** Modeled as `T_p = [0, 0, c_1 Ω_1^2 + c_2 Ω_2^2]^T`, where Ω are propeller rotational velocities and `c_1, c_2` are experimental coefficients.
 - **Moment from Co-Axial Motors:** Modeled as `τ_p = [0, 0, c_3 (Ω_1^2 - Ω_2^2)]^T`.
 - **Vane Force:** Modeled as `F_i = (1/2) ρ A C V_o^2 α_i`, simplified to `F_i = (1/2) ρ A c_4 T_p α_i` (proportional to total thrust `T_p` and servo angle `α_i`).
 - **Total Force & Moment:** Sum of propeller thrust/moment and vane forces/moments. Parameters `c_1` to `c_4` are identified experimentally.

2. **Hybrid Locomotion Dynamics:**
 - **Aerial Dynamics:** The robot is modeled as a single rigid body with 7 degrees of freedom (DOF). Equations of motion: `I ω̇ + ω × (Iω) = τ` (angular) and `m v̇ = R T + mg` (linear), where `I` is inertia, `m` is mass, `R` is orientation, `v` is linear velocity, and `ω` is angular velocity. The leg dynamics are neglected due to small weight and natural stability.
 - **Terrestrial Dynamics:** Modeled as a regular legged robot with a floating base and a prismatic springy joint (7 DOF). Equations: `M(q) v̇ + h(q, v) = u_r + J(q)^T f` (Lagrangian dynamics) and `J(q) v̇ + J̇(q, v)v = 0` (holonomic contact constraint), where `M` is mass matrix, `h` is Coriolis/gravity, `u_r` is robot actuation (`[T^T, τ^T, 0]^T`), `J` is Jacobian, and `f` is ground reaction force.
 - **Discrete Transitions:** Landing and liftoff events are handled by an impact map that updates velocity instantaneously: `M(q)(v^+ - v^-) = J(q)^T λ` and `J(q)v^+ = 0`.

#### 3.3. C. Contact-Implicit Control (CIC)

The core control approach is Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC), which unifies planning and control without predefined contact sequences or mode switching.

1. **Complementarity-Conditioned Dynamics:**
 - **Time-Stepping Dynamics:** Continuous dynamics are approximated by a discrete-time system using semi-implicit Euler integration.
 - **Non-Penetration:** Enforced by a complementarity condition: `ϕ(q^+) ≥ 0 ⊥ λ_n ≥ 0`, where `ϕ` is signed distance and `λ_n` is normal contact impulse. A linear approximation is used.
 - **Maximum Dissipation (Coulomb Friction):** Approximated by a polyhedral cone, yielding KKT conditions as complementarity constraints for tangential friction impulses.
 - **LCP Formulation:** These conditions, combined with discrete-time robot dynamics, form a Linear Complementarity Problem (LCP), whose solution determines the next state `x^+ = LCP(x, u)`.

2. **Model Predictive Control (MPC):**
 - **Optimization Problem:** At each control step, a nonlinear program is solved over a prediction horizon `N` to find an optimal control sequence. Only the first control input is executed.
 - **Cost Function (`J_MPC`):** Includes trajectory tracking, control regularization, and mechanical energy tracking. The energy term `∥E(x_k) - E_ref∥^2_Q_E` promotes energy circulation and influences the emergence of locomotion behaviors.
 - **Constraints:** System dynamics (LCP), initial state, control input bounds, and obstacle avoidance (minimum distance constraint).

3. **Solution Method via Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP):**
 - **DDP Application:** DDP is used to solve the finite-horizon MPC subproblem. It implicitly enforces dynamics by rolling out state trajectories and optimizes the control sequence through local quadratic approximations.
 - **Relaxed Analytic Gradient:** Since LCP dynamics are piecewise differentiable, relaxed analytic gradients are computed. This involves classifying contact modes (no contact, clamping, sliding) and transforming the LCP into velocity-level constraints. To enable smooth contact breaking and provide informative gradients, the normal direction complementarity constraint (`v_n ≥ 0 ⊥ λ_n ≥ 0`) is smoothed using a relaxation variable `ρ > 0` during differentiation. This allows for smooth transitions between contact modes.

#### 3.4. D. System Identification, Simulation, and Software

- **System Identification:** Aerodynamic coefficients (`c_1`–`c_4`) for the thrust and moment models were estimated experimentally using a single-DOF testing stand (for thrust) and a 3-DOF rotational stand (for moments). The model was simplified by enforcing `Ω_1 = Ω_2` to yield `τ_p = 0`.
- **Simulation:** The robot design from SolidWorks (with assigned mass and geometric properties) was simulated using a time-stepping LCP-based contact dynamics model within a ROS2 environment.
- **Software:** The controller and communication stack were implemented in C++, utilizing Crocoddyl for optimal control computation. The CI-MPC was configured with a 0.6 s prediction horizon and 0.03 s time discretization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated the design and control methodology of DASH through a series of experiments in both simulation and hardware, demonstrating its capabilities in individual locomotion modes and autonomous mode transitions.

#### 4.1. A. System Identification

The actuation model's parameters (`c_1`–`c_4`) were experimentally identified using dedicated test setups. This empirical approach was used to determine the relationships between propeller speeds, vane angles, and the resulting thrust forces and moments, rather than relying on first-principles derivations. The simplification of setting coaxial propeller speeds equal (`Ω_1 = Ω_2`) resulted in `τ_p = 0`, streamlining the model without compromising the achievable behaviors.

#### 4.2. B. Flying and Hopping Performance

1. **Aerial Flight:** In controlled laboratory environments, the robot successfully performed hovering maneuvers with global position tracking. It also demonstrated linear velocity tracking up to 0.5 m/s, limited by the workspace dimensions. These experiments validated the ducted fan's ability to provide stable lift and controlled directional movement.
2. **Terrestrial Hopping:** DASH executed continuous in-place hopping while maintaining global position targets (zero translational velocity). The CI-MPC successfully generated this behavior, which was observed to facilitate energy circulation and reduce the control effort.
 - **Energy Efficiency Comparison:** A key finding was the significant reduction in injected control effort during hopping compared to hovering. The optimized hopping solution decreased the control effort (defined as the integral of `|T(t) - T_min|` over time) to 26.78% of that required for continuous hovering. This indicated that hopping leverages intermittent energy storage and release through contact dynamics, requiring less sustained active thrust injection than continuous aerial support against gravity. The majority of residual energy expenditure during hopping was attributed to maintaining minimal motor activity rather than active thrust generation for lift.

#### 4.3. C. Contact-Implicit Control for Mode-Free Locomotion

The CI-MPC framework demonstrated its ability to autonomously generate locomotion behaviors and transitions without explicit mode scheduling.

1. **Simulation Results (Obstacle Traversal):** In simulation, DASH successfully tracked a 0.3 m/s velocity command while traversing obstacles. The CI-MPC autonomously selected between hopping and flying based on feasibility and optimality trade-offs. When ground contact was feasible and energetically favorable, hopping emerged. When obstacles impeded ground interaction, the controller transitioned the robot to flight.
2. **Hardware Demonstration (Obstacle Avoidance and Transition):** On the physical robot, the CI-MPC successfully executed mode-free locomotion.
 - **Obstacle-Triggered Flight:** When an obstacle was introduced beneath the robot, activating collision avoidance constraints, feasible ground contact configurations were eliminated. In response, the optimizer autonomously transitioned the robot to aerial flight to maintain feasibility and continue progress. This transition was an emergent property of the constrained optimization, not a predefined switch.
 - **Return to Hopping:** Once the obstacle was removed and ground contact became feasible again, the CI-MPC naturally reverted to the hopping regime. The controller replanned the contact sequence to recover the energy-efficient hopping behavior, demonstrating the framework's adaptability and ability to balance feasibility with energy-optimality.
 - **Energy and Feasibility:** The results indicated that the CI-MPC exploits passive energy exchange through terrain interaction during feasible ground contact, resulting in reduced active thrust. When contact becomes infeasible due to environmental constraints, the controller prioritizes maintaining feasibility by shifting to flight, showcasing the dynamic interplay between cost function and constraints in determining locomotion mode.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on the DASH robot presents several significant contributions and potential impacts in the field of robotics:

1. **Novel Integration of Ducted Fan and Spring Leg:** The minimalistic design of DASH, integrating a ducted fan with a spring-loaded leg, demonstrates an effective synergy for hybrid aerial-terrestrial locomotion. The ducted fan offers advantages such as high thrust-density, compactness, enhanced safety, and protection against debris compared to open-propeller systems common in similar platforms. The spring leg facilitates efficient ground interaction and energy circulation, addressing the energetic limitations of purely aerial systems. This mechanical design approach could inform future hybrid robot development, prioritizing integrated functionality and robust operation.

2. **Validation of Contact-Implicit Control for Hybrid Locomotion:** The work provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of Contact-Implicit Control (CIC) in managing complex, multi-modal locomotion for hybrid aerial-terrestrial robots. By formulating contact dynamics using complementarity conditions within an MPC framework, the controller eliminated the need for manual mode scheduling or predefined contact sequences. This represents a substantial advancement over traditional hybrid control methods, which often lead to suboptimal and less adaptable behaviors.

3. **Autonomous Mode Transition:** The demonstration of autonomous and seamless transitions between hopping and flying based on environmental feasibility and optimality trade-offs is a core contribution. The robot's ability to "discover" the most appropriate locomotion mode, such as transitioning to flight when obstacles make ground contact infeasible and returning to hopping when ground contact is restored, enhances its autonomy and adaptability in unstructured environments. This capability is critical for robust operation in real-world scenarios where environmental conditions are dynamic and unpredictable.

4. **Enhanced Energy Efficiency:** The quantitative demonstration that hopping reduces injected control effort to 26.78% of that required for hovering highlights the energy benefits of the hybrid design and control strategy. By leveraging passive energy exchange through the spring leg during ground interaction, DASH can achieve more efficient terrestrial movement, extending its operational range and endurance compared to purely aerial platforms or less optimized hybrid designs. This is crucial for practical applications where battery life is a significant constraint.

5. **Foundation for Advanced Hybrid Robotics:** This research provides a foundational framework for developing more versatile and intelligent hybrid robots. The successful integration of a robust mechanical design with a sophisticated control algorithm that handles inherent hybrid dynamics and contact events opens avenues for future work.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Search and Rescue:** Robots like DASH could navigate disaster zones more effectively, hopping over rubble and flying over large gaps to reach inaccessible areas and locate survivors.
- **Inspection and Exploration:** The ability to seamlessly transition between modes would enable efficient inspection of complex industrial facilities (e.g., pipelines, elevated structures) or exploration of natural environments with varied terrain, such as caves or dense forests.
- **Logistics and Delivery:** For tasks requiring package delivery or material transport in complex urban or outdoor settings, DASH could adapt its locomotion to overcome obstacles, reducing transit times and improving efficiency.
- **Advancements in Robotics Control:** The application of CI-MPC to a hybrid aerial-terrestrial system broadens the scope of this control paradigm, potentially inspiring its use in other complex robotic systems with multi-domain dynamics and contact interactions, such as climbing robots or manipulation tasks involving intermittent contact.

Future work aims to enable DASH to operate in natural environments without external motion capture and to expand its repertoire to include additional hybrid modes like swimming, further extending its versatility and real-world applicability.
