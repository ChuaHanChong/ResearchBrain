# Crossing the Reward Bridge: Expanding RL with Verifiable Rewards Across Diverse Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent AI Lab and Soochow University developed an approach to extend Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to diverse, unstructured domains by employing a generative, model-based soft reward mechanism. Their method successfully trains a compact 7B reward model that achieves performance competitive with a much larger 72B model, enabling RL to enhance LLM reasoning on complex, free-form tasks where traditional verification is challenging.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has been limited to highly structured tasks (e.g., math, code) due to its reliance on rule-based or programmatic binary feedback.
- Real-world domains (e.g., medicine, economics) often involve free-form, unstructured answers that lack easily verifiable objective ground truth, making traditional RLVR difficult to apply.
- Obtaining reliable reward signals for complex, nuanced, and open-ended LLM responses traditionally requires extensive domain-specific human annotation or computationally expensive large LLMs as judges.

## Method

- The authors propose using a generative Large Language Model as a verifier to produce both binary and novel ==soft rewards==, where soft rewards are derived from the probability of the verifier's judgment tokens (e.g., '1' for correct, '0' for incorrect).
- A compact (7B parameter) cross-domain ==reward model (RM-7B)== is trained by ==knowledge distillation== from a larger, fixed generative teacher model (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) on responses generated during RL exploration.
- The approach is validated on large-scale datasets featuring free-form, unstructured reference answers from diverse domains, including mathematics (773k QA pairs) and multi-subject college-level exams (638k QA pairs spanning 48 subjects).

## Results

- Reinforcement Learning consistently outperformed Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) across mathematics and multi-subject tasks, with model-based RL achieving **63.0%** accuracy on math tasks compared to SFT's **45.7%**.
- A distilled 7B reward model (RM-7B) achieved competitive or superior performance compared to a 72B teacher model, demonstrating the efficacy of compact cross-domain generative reward verifiers.
- Soft rewards demonstrated a clear advantage over binary rewards in multi-subject tasks (e.g., **31.2%** vs. **27.3%** accuracy for REINFORCE with RM-7B), and learned reward models exhibited superior scalability and out-of-distribution generalization compared to rule-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Binary correctness judgments on broad, nuanced tasks show high agreement across powerful LLMs when high-quality expert-written reference answers are provided, simplifying ground-truth signal acquisition.
> - It is feasible and effective to distill multi-domain generative verifier models from relatively compact LLMs (as small as 7B) without extensive, costly domain-specific human annotation.
> - Soft reward functions, leveraging the probability of a generative verifier's judgment tokens, can better accommodate the inherent uncertainty and ambiguity of open-domain evaluation compared to binary rewards.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) and reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing the emerging paradigm of **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. RLVR has gained traction as an effective method for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, particularly in domains where objective ground-truth answers are available for verification. Prior successes have predominantly been observed in highly structured tasks, such as mathematical reasoning and code generation. In these settings, rule-based or programmatic verifiers can reliably provide binary feedback (correct/incorrect) on model responses.

However, the broader research landscape of LLMs is rapidly expanding into more complex, real-world domains like medicine, chemistry, and economics, where answers are often free-form, unstructured, and lack easily verifiable objective ground truth. This poses a significant challenge for traditional RLVR methods, as their reliance on structured verification signals becomes a limiting factor.

This work directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to "cross the reward bridge," as the title metaphorically suggests, by extending RLVR's applicability beyond its current confines. It challenges the prevailing assumption that RLVR requires precisely structured solutions and easy binary verification. By proposing and validating the use of generative, model-based soft rewards, the paper contributes to the evolving field of reward modeling, moving beyond simple rule-based binary judgments towards more nuanced, confidence-aware signals. It also contributes to the efficiency of RL applications by demonstrating the feasibility of training compact, cross-domain reward models, thereby reducing the dependency on large-scale, domain-specific human annotation or computationally expensive closed-source LLMs as judges. This aligns with the broader trend in LLM research of making advanced techniques more accessible and scalable for diverse applications.

Furthermore, the paper implicitly engages with the ongoing debate in LLM alignment research regarding the nature of reward signals (e.g., human preference data vs. verifiable ground truth), the utility of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in reward assignment, and the trade-offs between model size and performance in reward modeling. By releasing a large-scale dataset with free-form multi-domain data, the authors also contribute to the community's resources for developing and evaluating LLMs in less-explored, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **expand the applicability and effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to diverse, less structured real-world domains**, moving beyond its traditional success in structured tasks like mathematical reasoning and coding.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Limitation of Structured Domains:** Previous RLVR applications have primarily focused on domains where structured reference answers allow for straightforward binary verification. This significantly limits RLVR's utility in real-world scenarios where answers are often free-form and complex (e.g., medical diagnoses, economic analyses). The authors explicitly aim to explore RLVR's effectiveness in domains such as medicine, chemistry, psychology, economics, and education, where such structured answers are typically unavailable.

2. **Leveraging LLM Consistency in Broad-Domain Judgments:** A crucial motivating observation is that binary correctness judgments, even on broad and nuanced tasks, exhibit "remarkable agreement" across various powerful LLMs (both closed-source like GPT-4o and open-source like Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) *when high-quality expert-written reference answers are provided*. This finding is significant because it suggests that reference-based evaluation is inherently easier than reference-free evaluation, which simplifies the task of obtaining reliable ground-truth signals for RL. This insight challenges the presumed necessity for extensive domain-specific annotation.

3. **Overcoming the Limitations of Binary Rewards for Unstructured Answers:** The authors highlight that a substantial portion of real-world exam questions (e.g., only 45.4% of complex multi-domain queries) do not have single-term numerical answers that are easily verifiable by rule-based binary methods. This inherent limitation of binary reward schemes necessitates a richer and more granular verification mechanism to handle free-form, unstructured responses effectively. This motivates the exploration of "soft scores" from generative, model-based verifiers.

4. **Demonstrating Feasibility of Training Compact, Cross-Domain Reward Models:** The traditional approach might involve using large, expensive LLMs as reward models or requiring vast amounts of domain-specific human annotation. The authors are motivated to demonstrate that it is feasible and effective to distill multi-domain generative verifier models from relatively compact LLMs (as small as 7B) without the need for extensive, costly domain-specific annotation. This would significantly improve the efficiency and scalability of RLVR.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to make RLVR a more practical, flexible, and scalable paradigm for aligning LLMs in complex, ambiguous, and noisy-label environments, thereby unlocking its potential for a much wider range of real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology revolves around extending Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to diverse domains by introducing a novel generative, model-based reward mechanism and a robust training strategy for compact reward models.

1. **Verifiable Reward Signal Generation:**
- **Generative Verifier LLM:** The approach utilizes a generative Large Language Model ($\pi_{\phi}$) to act as a verifier. This LLM is instructed to output a judgment (e.g., '0' or '1') on whether a model's response ($y_i$) matches a given reference answer ($a$) for a prompt ($x$).
- **Binary Reward:** For a binary reward, the verifier LLM explicitly outputs '1' if the response matches the reference, and '0' otherwise. The reward $r_{\phi}(x, a, y_i)$ is then 1 if the sampled judgment $c_i$ is '1', and 0 otherwise.
- **Soft Reward (Novelty):** To address the limitations of binary rewards in unstructured domains, the paper proposes a soft reward function. This function leverages the *probability* of the generative verifier's judgment tokens. If the verifier predicts '1' (correct) with probability $\pi_{\phi}(1|x, a, y_i)$, this probability itself becomes the reward. If it predicts '0' (incorrect) with probability $\pi_{\phi}(0|x, a, y_i)$, the reward is calculated as $1 - \pi_{\phi}(0|x, a, y_i)$. This yields a score in the range, reflecting the verifier's confidence in the correctness or incorrectness of the answer, which is particularly beneficial for ambiguous cases.
- **Reward Normalization:** To ensure stable training and encourage learning from above-average samples, z-score normalization is applied to the rewards within each batch.

2. **Reward Model Training (RM-7B):**
- A key innovation is the training of a moderately sized (7B) reward model, rather than solely relying on a much larger, potentially closed-source, off-the-shelf LLM.
- **Data Collection:** Training data for the reward model is collected during the RL exploration phase. As the actor policy improves and generates diverse responses, these responses, along with their corresponding prompts and reference answers, are fed to a larger, fixed, cross-domain generative teacher model (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct in this case) to obtain binary judgments (hard labels). This process yields "distilled training samples" (160k in total).
- **Fine-tuning:** The compact (7B) reward model is then fine-tuned via supervised learning on this collected (x, a, y, c) dataset, where 'c' is the binary judgment from the teacher model. This strategy aims to transfer the judgment capabilities of the larger model to a smaller, more efficient one, while also exposing the reward model to varied and potentially "noisy" outputs from the improving actor policy, enhancing its robustness.

3. **Reinforcement Learning for LLM Alignment:**
- The paper employs policy gradient algorithms, including REINFORCE, RLOO (Reinforcement Learning with Optimal Ordering), and REINFORCE++.
- A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty is integrated into the reward function to prevent the RL model from drifting too far from the initial base model distribution, mitigating potential biases from the reward model.

4. **Datasets for Training and Evaluation:**
- **Mathematics Data:** A large-scale dataset of 773k Chinese QA pairs, covering elementary to high school levels. Crucially, the reference answers are **free-form** and often interwoven with rationales, making them challenging for traditional rule-based verification. A 9,000 QA test set (3,000 from each level) is used.
- **Multi-Subject Data:** The ExamQA dataset (638k college-level multiple-choice QA, converted to free-form QA pairs). This dataset spans 48 subjects (e.g., medicine, law, psychology) with expert-written objective answers. A 6,000 question test set is randomly sampled.
- Both datasets are translated into English using GPT-4o-mini, making the research accessible to a wider audience. The use of free-form, unstructured reference answers in these datasets is a deliberate choice to test the extended RLVR framework.

5. **Evaluation Metrics and Protocols:**
- **Agreement:** Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa$) is used to measure the agreement between the Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct evaluator (via majority voting) and GPT-4o, validating the reliability of LLM-based judgments on free-form answers. High agreement ($\kappa \ge 0.81$) is observed.
- **Performance:** Accuracy is the primary metric for evaluating the fine-tuned LLMs on reasoning tasks.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against a base model (Qwen2.5-7B), Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), rule-based reward RL, and RL using Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct as the reward model.
- **Scaling Experiments:** Investigate how model performance scales with increasing training data volume (up to 100k samples) for both rule-based and model-based rewards.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Evaluation:** Test the generalizability of the trained reward model on unseen datasets (NaturalReasoning, WebInstruct) from different domains.

This comprehensive methodology allows the authors to rigorously test their hypotheses regarding the effectiveness of generative model-based soft rewards and the feasibility of training compact, general-purpose reward models for RLVR across diverse, unstructured domains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted reveal several significant findings regarding the effectiveness and scalability of the proposed RLVR framework:

1. **Difficulty of Benchmark Datasets:** The newly introduced math and multi-subject datasets are challenging. Even strong state-of-the-art open-source aligned models like Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (22.6% accuracy on multi-subject tasks) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (21.7%) perform unsatisfactorily. This underscores the complexity of free-form, multi-domain reasoning and highlights the need for advanced alignment techniques.

2. **RL Outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The results consistently demonstrate that Reinforcement Learning (RL) significantly outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) across both mathematics and multi-subject tasks. For example, on math tasks, SFT only marginally improved performance from 43.4% to 45.7%, whereas rule-based RL achieved 58.8% and model-based RL (RM-7B) reached 63.0%. This finding reinforces RL's superior ability to enhance reasoning capabilities, especially in scenarios lacking high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training data.

3. **Superiority of Model-Based Rewards:** Model-based rewards (using either Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct or their distilled RM-7B) consistently and significantly outperform traditional rule-based rewards in scenarios involving free-form reference answers. For instance, with the RLOO algorithm, RM-7B binary reward achieved 63.0% accuracy on math tasks, compared to 58.5% for rule-based binary reward. This demonstrates the model-based approach's better ability to handle the nuances and diversity of unstructured answers.

4. **Effectiveness of Compact Distilled Reward Models (RM-7B):** A crucial finding is that the authors' distilled 7B reward model (RM-7B) achieves competitive, and in some cases even superior, performance compared to the much larger Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct model (which served as the teacher model for distillation). For instance, in multi-subject evaluations using REINFORCE, the model trained with RM-7B achieved 31.2% accuracy, slightly outperforming the 72B model's 30.3%. This validates the feasibility and efficacy of training compact, cross-domain generative reward verifiers without extensive domain-specific annotation, offering significant efficiency gains.

5. **Soft Rewards Excel in Complex Scenarios:**
- For **rule-based rewards**, soft rewards generally underperform binary rewards, likely due to simplistic similarity metrics struggling with superficial token overlaps.
- For **model-based rewards**, binary and soft rewards yield comparable results on math tasks, suggesting high confidence judgments where distinctions are less critical.
- However, in **multi-subject tasks**, where reference answers are highly diverse and judgment complexity is higher, soft rewards demonstrate a clear advantage over binary rewards (e.g., 31.2% vs. 27.3% for REINFORCE, RM-7B). This indicates that soft scoring better accommodates the inherent uncertainty and ambiguity of open-domain evaluation, leading to more robust policies.

6. **Scalability Advantages of Learned Reward Models:** Scaling experiments demonstrate that the learned reward model (RM-7B) exhibits a "consistent improvement trend throughout the training process" as data volume increases. In stark contrast, rule-based rewards show "unstable scalability," with performance fluctuations and eventual degradation at larger data scales. This highlights the inherent scalability and robustness of model-based rewards over rigid rule-based systems.

7. **Strong Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** The RM-7B model maintains significantly superior performance over rule-based rewards when evaluated on external, unseen datasets (NaturalReasoning and WebInstruct). This confirms that the general-purpose reward model developed in this work can effectively generalize to other domains, further underscoring its versatility and robustness.

In summary, the results conclusively show that extending RLVR with model-based soft rewards, trained via distillation into a compact cross-domain verifier, not only significantly outperforms traditional approaches but also demonstrates superior scalability and generalization, particularly in complex, unstructured, and diverse reasoning domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a substantial advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and its application to Large Language Models (LLMs), with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Broadened Applicability of RLVR:** The most direct significance is the successful expansion of RLVR beyond its traditional confines of structured tasks (like math and coding) into diverse, real-world, and often unstructured domains such as medicine, chemistry, psychology, and economics. This "crossing of the reward bridge" unlocks the potential of RLVR for a much wider array of practical applications where precise, rule-based verification is impossible due to the free-form nature of answers.

2. **Introduction of Robust Generative Soft Rewards:** The proposed generative model-based soft reward mechanism is a key innovation. By moving beyond rigid binary judgments and leveraging the confidence scores of a generative verifier, the system can handle the inherent ambiguity and complexity of unstructured answers more effectively. This leads to more robust and higher-performing LLM policies, especially in challenging, nuanced scenarios. This method effectively transforms the complex task of semantic evaluation into a more tractable generative modeling problem.

3. **Cost-Effective and Scalable Reward Modeling:** Demonstrating the feasibility of training compact (7B-scale) cross-domain generative reward models without extensive domain-specific annotation is a major breakthrough for practical deployment. This significantly reduces the computational cost and human effort traditionally associated with reward model development and maintenance, making advanced LLM alignment techniques more accessible to a broader range of organizations and researchers. It mitigates the reliance on large, expensive closed-source models or prohibitively expensive human labeling campaigns.

4. **Enhanced Robustness and Flexibility:** The empirical evidence of consistent performance gains and superior scalability of the learned reward model over rule-based methods highlights a substantial enhancement in the robustness and flexibility of RLVR. This is critical for deploying LLMs in "noisy-label scenarios" where perfect ground truth is rare, allowing models to learn effectively even from imperfect or ambiguous signals.

5. **Acceleration of Research and Development:** The public release of a large-scale (570k examples) multi-domain free-form dataset and the corresponding trained reward model is a valuable contribution to the research community. This resource will facilitate further research into LLM reasoning, reward modeling, and alignment in complex domains, accelerating progress and fostering new innovations.

6. **Rethinking Reward Signal Generation:** The work challenges conventional assumptions about the nature and scale of reward signal generation in RL for LLMs. By proving that robust rewards can be learned and distilled from larger models into smaller ones, and that soft scores can outperform binary ones in complex settings, the paper opens new avenues for future research into more sophisticated and efficient reward modeling paradigms, potentially leading to more generalized and human-aligned LLMs.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust framework for expanding the reach of RLVR to diverse and unstructured problem domains. By innovating in generative reward modeling and demonstrating efficient reward model training, it significantly advances the practical applicability and scalability of reinforcement learning for aligning LLMs with complex, real-world tasks, setting a new benchmark for open-source LLM performance in these areas.
