# Physics-Guided Biomechanical Gait Adaptation for Humanoid Locomotion on Extreme Sloped Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> The HumoSlope framework introduces a two-stage physics-guided approach to enhance humanoid robot locomotion on extreme sloped terrains by integrating a slope-adaptive balance prior and biomechanically inspired gait modulation. It enabled the Unitree G1 robot to achieve a 77.1% success rate on 300 slopes in simulation and blind traversal on real grass slopes up to 32.10, outperforming baselines that failed to complete these challenges.

## Problem

- Existing model-free reinforcement learning policies for humanoid locomotion on slopes often adopt a conservative, crouched "Groucho gait," leading to inefficient movement, increased joint loading, and limitations on traversable slope grades.
- Traditional ZMP-based balance criteria typically assume a flat, world-horizontal support plane, causing misalignment and geometric bias when applied to inclined surfaces.
- Generic reward formulations in RL often fail to incorporate sophisticated, slope-conditioned posture control and biomechanically informed asymmetric gait adaptations (e.g., hip-dominant for uphill, knee-oriented for downhill).

## Method

- **HumoSlope** introduces a two-stage training framework: Stage I focuses on learning a fundamental, terrain-consistent balance prior using a novel ==slope-adaptive Zero Moment Point (ZMP) regularizer==.
- Stage II builds on the warm-started policy, integrating a **Biomechanical Slope Gait Adapter (BSGA)** which uses a ==Principal Component Analysis (PCA) terrain descriptor== to gate biomechanically motivated reward priors for slope-conditioned posture and asymmetric gait.
- The framework employs an ==asymmetric actor-critic== observation setup, where the deployed actor is purely proprioceptive, but the training-time critic has access to privileged state and terrain information to guide learning.

## Results

- In simulation, HumoSlope achieved **100%** success rates on 200 compound slopes and a **77.1%** success rate at 300, reaching a maximum traversable grade of **360**, while all baselines failed to complete the 300 track.
- The trained policy enabled the Unitree G1 robot to perform robust blind, continuous locomotion on **six** real-world outdoor terrains, including challenging grass slopes with mean angles up to **32.10** and traversal on wet asphalt/wavy slopes.
- Biomechanical diagnostics confirmed HumoSlope's adaptive posture and gait, showing a more upright CoM height, increased hip-pitch torque for uphill propulsion, and increased knee flexion during downhill traversal, consistent with human locomotion principles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Redefining ZMP balance criteria to align with the local inclined support plane (via a slope-adaptive ZMP regularizer) is crucial for robust stability on non-flat terrains.
> - Encoding biomechanically inspired, asymmetric reward priors (e.g., hip-dominant propulsion uphill, knee-oriented braking downhill) and dynamic CoM height regulation effectively mitigates undesirable 'Groucho gait' and enables more efficient locomotion.
> - A purely proprioceptive policy, when trained with privileged macroscopic terrain cues (PCA descriptor) for the critic and extensive domain randomization, can achieve robust Sim-to-Real transfer of complex slope-adaptive behaviors on humanoid robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model-free reinforcement learning has advanced humanoid locomotion capabilities significantly in recent years, enabling robots to traverse complex terrains such as stairs, stepping stones, and parkour-style obstacles. These advancements are largely attributed to massively parallel simulation and Sim-to-Real transfer techniques, allowing policies to learn robust skills leveraging proprioceptive feedback, observation histories, and domain randomization. Existing literature categorizes terrain adaptation strategies into perceptive approaches, which utilize exteroceptive sensors, and blind strategies, which rely solely on internal joint states and contact histories.

Despite these developments, continuous steep slopes remain an area that is comparatively underexplored as a dedicated locomotion challenge. Previous studies often include inclined surfaces as one test case within broader multi-terrain benchmarks, offering limited in-depth analysis of how slope grade impacts traversal limits, posture, joint loading, and specific gait adaptations. The distinction is critical because sloped terrains introduce a persistent gravitational bias that necessitates continuous dynamic stability and posture control throughout the robot's body. This differs from discrete terrains, which primarily require short-horizon foothold selection or recovery. As the incline intensifies, the robot must manage dynamic stability under shifted Center of Mass (CoM) projection, altered support geometry, and sustained lower-limb loading. These challenges are exacerbated on outdoor, irregular slopes where local surface variations interact with the global inclination.

Under generic reward formulations prevalent in many reinforcement learning approaches, humanoid robots tend to adopt a conservative, low-CoM crouched posture, often described as a "Groucho gait," when attempting to traverse slopes. While this strategy may offer some immediate stability by lowering the CoM, it leads to several drawbacks, including slower traversal speeds, degraded posture quality, overuse of CoM stability mechanisms, increased hip and knee loading, and ultimately, limitations on the maximum slope grade that can be reliably traversed. This indicates that effective slope locomotion requires not only robust dynamic stability but also sophisticated, slope-conditioned posture control to mitigate such undesirable gait degenerations. The current work aims to address this gap by developing a framework specifically tailored for robust and efficient humanoid locomotion on challenging sloped terrains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary research question addressed by this work is: how can a humanoid robot simultaneously maintain stability and control its posture under the persistent gravitational bias imposed by extreme sloped terrains?

The motivation for developing the HumoSlope framework stems from observed limitations in existing model-free reinforcement learning approaches for humanoid locomotion on slopes. Specifically, policies trained with generic reward functions tend to converge to a "Groucho gait" characterized by a persistently crouched, low-CoM posture. While this strategy might offer a form of stability on steep inclines by lowering the center of gravity, it presents several drawbacks:
1. **Posture Degeneration:** The crouched gait is inefficient and unnatural, leading to poor posture quality on both flat and mild slopes.
2. **Overuse of CoM Stability:** It relies excessively on a low CoM for stability, rather than active and adaptive gait modulation.
3. **Increased Joint Loading:** The sustained crouched posture increases mechanical load on hip and knee joints, potentially leading to increased energy consumption and wear.
4. **Traversal Limitations:** This conservative strategy ultimately caps the robot's ability to traverse steeper slopes effectively.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose HumoSlope with the key idea of achieving slope-adaptive locomotion balance through asymmetric biomechanical gait modulation. The core objectives are:

1. **Establish Terrain-Consistent Balance:** To address the physical misalignment introduced when applying traditional ZMP-based balance criteria (which typically assume a flat, world-horizontal support plane) to inclined surfaces. The goal is to develop a balance metric that is consistent with the local inclined support plane.
2. **Prevent Posture Degeneration:** To mitigate the undesirable crouched gait by incorporating mechanisms that encourage more upright and efficient postures dynamically adapted to the slope geometry.
3. **Integrate Biomechanical Insights:** To translate known principles of human uphill and downhill locomotion into robot control. Human studies indicate that uphill movement demands increased positive work and hip involvement for propulsion, while downhill movement requires braking and increased knee involvement for controlled descent. The objective is to encode these asymmetric requirements into the robot's gait.
4. **Maintain Proprioceptive Deployment:** A crucial objective is that the final deployed actor policy should rely solely on proprioceptive feedback (internal joint states, IMU data) without requiring online exteroceptive sensing, making it suitable for deployment in dynamic and unstructured outdoor environments where external sensors might be unreliable or costly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed solution, HumoSlope, is a novel two-stage physics-guided framework designed for robust humanoid locomotion on diverse sloped terrains. It employs an asymmetric actor-critic observation setup, where the deployed actor is purely proprioceptive, but the training-time critic has access to privileged state and terrain information.

**Framework Overview:**
The framework consists of two sequential training stages:
- **Stage I:** Focuses on establishing a fundamental, terrain-consistent balance prior.
- **Stage II:** Builds upon the warm-started policy from Stage I, introducing biomechanically inspired adaptations for slope-conditioned posture and gait.

#### 3.1. Stage I: Slope-Adaptive ZMP Regularization

The objective of Stage I is to learn a stable, warm-start locomotion policy that can traverse moderate slopes while addressing the limitations of traditional balance criteria.
- **Problem with Traditional ZMP:** Existing Zero Moment Point (ZMP) or Zero Moment Line (ZML) formulations in reinforcement learning often evaluate balance deviation relative to a world-horizontal reference plane. On inclined surfaces, this reference plane is misaligned with the robot's actual support surface, introducing a geometric bias in the balance deviation measurement.
- **Slope-Adaptive ZMP Regularizer:** To counter this, the framework introduces a slope-adaptive ZMP regularizer.
 - **Support Anchor (p_sa):** Instead of relying on noisy contact wrench estimations or brittle contact patch boundaries, a contact-force-weighted support anchor is defined. This anchor (p_sa = ((F_L + ε)p_L + (F_R + ε)p_R) / (F_L + F_R + 2ε)) interpolates between the left (p_L) and right (p_R) foot positions based on their respective contact forces (F_L, F_R), serving as a conservative interior support target.
 - **Local Support Plane Estimation:** The local inclined support plane's normal (n_c) is estimated from the orientation of the dominant stance foot (n_c = R(q_foot)ᵀ).
 - **Terrain-Aligned ZMP Calculation:** The terrain-aligned ZMP position (p_ta_zmp) is calculated by intersecting a ray originating from the CoM (p_com) in the direction of the point-mass apparent specific force (F_app = g - a_com) with the estimated local support plane.
 - **Reward Formulation:** The ZMP deviation from the support anchor is shaped into a dense reward: r_ta_zmp = exp(−d_ta_zmp/σ_zmp), where d_ta_zmp = ∥p_ta_zmp − p_sa∥₂. This regularizer is used exclusively during Stage I to instill a terrain-consistent balance prior in the policy.

#### 3.2. Stage II: Biomechanical Slope Gait Adapter (BSGA)

Stage II addresses the issue of crouched gait degeneration and enables slope-conditioned posture and gait adaptation, building on the stable policy from Stage I.
- **PCA Terrain Descriptor (ϕ_PCA_5):** A critical component is the use of a five-dimensional macroscopic PCA terrain descriptor during training (as a privileged signal for the critic and reward gates).
 - **Generation:** Local height-scan points obtained via ray-casting are processed using Principal Component Analysis (PCA). The resulting plane normal is projected into the robot's heading frame to yield the longitudinal slope angle (θ_slope) and cross-slope tilt angle (θ_bank).
 - **Descriptor Components:** ϕ_PCA_5 = (θ_slope, θ_bank, |θ_slope|, 1_up, 1_down), where 1_up and 1_down are indicator functions for ascending and descending regimes, respectively. Absolute world height is intentionally excluded to focus on terrain orientation.
 - **Role:** This descriptor augments the privileged critic with macroscopic terrain cues and gates the BSGA reward priors. A compressed height scan (H_49) is still provided to the critic to capture local surface details.
- **BSGA Core Reward (r_core_BSGA):** The BSGA gates a compact set of descriptor-conditioned soft reward priors, encoding biomechanically motivated ascent/descent asymmetries without direct imitation of human trajectories. The core reward is defined as:
 r_core_BSGA = w_com r_com + w_bio r_bio + w_swing r_swing
 - **CoM Height Regulation (r_com):** This branch replaces a fixed CoM height reference with a slope-conditioned target height (h_tgt).
 - h_tgt = h_nom cos(|θ_slope|) + ρ_slope (b_up 1_up + b_down 1_down), where h_nom is the nominal flat-terrain height, ρ_slope is normalized slope intensity, and b_up/b_down are asymmetric offsets for ascent/descent.
 - The reward softly penalizes deviations from h_tgt, discouraging persistent low-CoM crouching.
 - **Biomechanical Gait Asymmetry (r_bio):** This branch encodes ascent/descent asymmetry based on human slope locomotion studies.
 - r_bio = (w_up r_hip + w_down r_down) / (w_up + w_down).
 - **Uphill (r_hip):** Biases ascent towards hip-dominant propulsion.
 - **Downhill (r_down):** r_down = λ_brake r_brake + λ_stride r_stride. This combines controlled knee-oriented braking and reduced over-stepping.
 - These terms are gated by the 1_up and 1_down indicators.
 - **Swing-Leg Guidance (r_swing):** This branch provides a calibrated, slope-dependent hip-pitch reference during training.
 - q*_hip,swing (θ_slope) = β_0 + β_1 clip(θ_slope, 0, θ_clip).
 - Coefficients (β_0, β_1) are fitted from Stage-I rollouts, representing an upper-percentile hip-pitch trend across slope angles.
 - The reward softly tracks this target during swing without enforcing a rigid trajectory.

**Two-Stage Training Summary:**
- Stage I trains an actor-critic policy on mixed terrains using the slope-adaptive ZMP regularizer.
- The Stage-I actor is used to warm-start Stage II to preserve general locomotion capabilities.
- The critic is reset in Stage II to accommodate the new terrain distribution (slope-track terrains), augmented privileged observations (including PCA descriptor), and the BSGA-integrated reward objective.
- The entire framework is optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), leveraging thousands of parallel simulation environments in Isaac Lab on a single GPU. Domain randomization is applied to enhance Sim-to-Real robustness. The action space is joint-position residual targets tracked by a low-level PD controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HumoSlope framework was extensively evaluated through Sim-to-Real experiments on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating its effectiveness in robustly traversing diverse and challenging sloped terrains.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Held-Out Compound Slope-Track Benchmark:** HumoSlope was compared against proprioceptive baselines (Unitree RL Lab (URL), FastTD3) and an exteroceptive baseline (Gallant) on a 35m held-out compound slope track. This track featured irregular sequences of flat, uphill, downhill, and plateau segments, with varied surface types (smooth, wavy, banked, rough, striped) and friction tiers.
 - **High Success Rates and Traversal Limits:** HumoSlope achieved near-perfect success rates (100% SR) and the shortest successful-trial traversal times across nominal grades up to 36.4% (20°). At the most challenging 57.7% (30°) grade, HumoSlope maintained a 77.1% SR and an average maximum forward distance (MXD) of 27.54 m, whereas all baselines failed to complete the track (0% SR). In an additional slope-limit sweep, HumoSlope reached the highest maximum grade of 73% (36°).
 - **Posture Control:** Qualitatively, HumoSlope's policy maintained a more upright posture on flat terrain and exhibited adaptive, slope-varying torso and leg behavior (forward lean uphill, slight backward lean downhill). In contrast, the URL baseline consistently adopted a conservative, low-CoM "Groucho gait" across different slope conditions.
- **Biomechanical Diagnostics:** Quantitative analysis confirmed the intended biomechanical adaptations:
 - **CoM Height:** HumoSlope maintained a higher CoM height with subtle, slope-dependent modulation, diverging from the consistently low-CoM crouched posture observed in URL.
 - **Hip-Pitch Torque:** On a 20° track, HumoSlope demonstrated higher hip-pitch torque during uphill segments, consistent with a hip-dominant propulsion strategy.
 - **Knee Flexion:** During downhill traversal, HumoSlope showed increased knee flexion, aligning with a knee-oriented braking mechanism.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Blind Traversal:** The trained HumoSlope policy was deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid using only proprioceptive observations (no online exteroceptive sensing).
- **Diverse Outdoor Terrains:** The robot successfully performed blind, continuous traversal on six real-world outdoor terrains, including asphalt, wavy, roadside, and two grass slopes, as well as a flat walkway.
- **Extreme Slopes and Conditions:** This included challenging grass slopes with measured mean angles up to 32.1° (with local measurements reaching 36.4°), and traversal on asphalt and wavy slopes even under wet conditions after rainfall.
- **Adaptive Real-World Posture:** Consistent with simulation, the robot exhibited different postures on real grass terrain—a forward lean during ascent, a near-upright stance on flat ground, and a slight backward lean during descent. This confirmed the effective transfer of learned, descriptor-gated adaptation from simulation to real-world deployment.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on a 36.4% (20°) compound slope track highlighted the contribution of each component:
- **Stage I only:** The policy trained in Stage I alone demonstrated strong initial performance (100% SR) but adopted a conservative, low-CoM crouched strategy (lower mean CoM height and longer traversal time), confirming its role as a stable warm-start prior.
- **Excluding Slope-Adaptive ZMP:** Removing the slope-adaptive ZMP regularizer from Stage I significantly degraded task performance (55.6% SR, reduced MXD), indicating its importance for acquiring a robust balance prior.
- **Excluding BSGA Critic Cue:** Disabling the PCA terrain descriptor for the Stage II critic led to degraded robustness, slower traversal speed, and reduced directional stability (93.2% SR, increased traversal time and yaw-rate variance), emphasizing the role of privileged terrain information for the critic.
- **Excluding BSGA Reward Priors:** Removing the biomechanically motivated reward priors from BSGA resulted in a substantial performance drop (26.9% SR, reduced MXD), underscoring the necessity of these priors for effective slope-conditioned adaptation.
- **Excluding Full BSGA:** Completely removing the BSGA module (both critic cue and reward priors) led to complete failure (0% SR, minimal MXD). The diagnostics showed a low CoM height and the highest peak knee torque, confirming that without BSGA, the policy defaults to posture degeneration and uncontrolled lower-limb loading, leading to task failure.

These results collectively indicate that the full HumoSlope framework effectively transforms the stable but conservative gait from Stage I into a faster, more upright, and biomechanically adapted locomotion strategy for sloped terrains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid locomotion control, particularly for challenging environments.

- **Addresses a Critical Gap:** The work directly addresses the under-explored challenge of robust humanoid locomotion on continuous steep slopes, a common yet difficult terrain type for robots. It highlights and effectively mitigates the "Groucho gait" phenomenon, a known issue in generic RL policies that hinders performance on inclines.
- **Physics-Guided Principles:** By integrating a slope-adaptive ZMP regularizer, the framework redefines balance criteria to be consistent with inclined support planes, representing a physics-guided approach to stability that is crucial for non-flat terrains. This moves beyond traditional assumptions of flat ground.
- **Biologically Inspired Adaptation:** The introduction of the Biomechanical Slope Gait Adapter (BSGA) leverages insights from human biomechanics regarding asymmetric uphill propulsion (hip-dominant) and downhill braking (knee-oriented). Translating these principles into robot reward functions enables more efficient, natural, and robust gait adaptation.
- **Robustness and Performance:** The achieved traversal limits (up to 32.1°/36.4° on real grass and 36° in simulation) and superior performance over existing baselines demonstrate the framework's effectiveness in enhancing robot robustness and speed on complex slopes.
- **Proprioceptive Deployment and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ability to achieve robust real-world locomotion on diverse outdoor slopes without online exteroceptive sensing is a major strength. This purely proprioceptive deployment simplifies the robot's sensor suite and improves robustness in unstructured environments where external sensors might be unreliable or introduce latency. The successful Sim-to-Real transfer validates the realism of the simulation environment and the robustness of the learned policies.
- **Potential Impact:** The advancements presented in HumoSlope could significantly impact the practical deployment of humanoid robots in various real-world scenarios, including:
 - **Outdoor Exploration:** Navigating natural hillsides, uneven paths, and mountainous regions.
 - **Disaster Response:** Traversing rubble and inclines in collapsed structures.
 - **Logistics and Construction:** Operating on ramps and varied terrain in industrial or construction sites.
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Providing mobility assistance in environments with varied elevation changes.

The work also opens avenues for future research, particularly in integrating look-ahead visual perception to anticipate abrupt terrain changes while preserving the core proprioceptive adaptation capabilities, further enhancing robot autonomy in highly irregular environments.
