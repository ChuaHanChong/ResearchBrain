# Affordance-Based Manipulation Planning with Text Goals and Sim-to-Real Generalisation via Real-to-Sim Image Conversion

> [!summary] Summary
> An affordance-based robotic manipulation planning system is introduced, interpreting text-based goals and predicting visual state changes to generate multi-step plans. The system achieves real-world transfer for varied objects and occlusions by integrating a real-to-sim image conversion module, demonstrating robust planning in physical environments.

## Problem

- Traditional robot action planning methods struggle with real-world complexity, fine-grained physical effects, and often rely on limited symbolic or abstract representations.
- Existing affordance-based manipulation planning systems often lacked practical text-based goal specification, robust generalization to varied object appearances, and seamless sim-to-real transfer.
- Robotic manipulation tasks in dynamic environments require planning systems to effectively handle object occlusion and diverse spatial arrangements.

## Method

- Developed an integrated system of five neural network modules, including a **Goal Interpretation Module** for text goals and an **Effect Prediction Module** for visual state prediction.
- Introduced an **Image Conversion Module (ICM)** to bridge the reality gap by converting real-world ==RGBD== images into a simulation-consistent style, enabling ==sim-to-real generalization==.
- Incorporated ==object mask channels== into state representations and leveraged ==orthogonal projection== and ==virtual camera motion== in the **Effect Prediction Module** to enhance generalization over object layouts and track occluded objects.

## Results

- Individual modules achieved high performance, with the Affordance Recognition Module showing **92% accuracy** for GRASP affordances and the Goal-Matching Module reaching **98% accuracy** on test data.
- In simulation experiments, the integrated system successfully generated sensible multi-step plans for **9 out of 10** complex tasks, demonstrating foresight and the ability to track fully occluded objects.
- The system achieved consistent success (**5/5 trials**) on **6 out of 10** real-world manipulation tasks, validating the effectiveness of the Image Conversion Module for sim-to-real generalization despite challenges with specific object configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining learned fine-grained visual effect prediction with multimodal (text and vision) goal assessment enables robust multi-step planning for complex manipulation tasks with high-level instructions.
> - Real-to-sim image conversion, facilitated by generative augmentation using depth-conditioned diffusion models, provides an effective strategy for bridging the reality gap and generalizing to varied real-world objects without extensive real-world training data.
> - Enriching state representations with explicit object mask channels and exploiting translational and rotational invariance in prediction significantly improves robustness to object occlusion and diverse object arrangements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Action planning in robotics has traditionally relied on symbolic methods or, more recently, large language models (LLMs). While these approaches can be effective in environments that can be fully described symbolically or linguistically, they often face limitations in real-world manipulation scenarios where the environment's complexity and unforeseen interactions challenge manually defined effect rules or abstract representations. The granularity of symbolic predictions can also restrict planning fidelity, as designers may overlook or struggle to define all relevant physical effects and side-effects.

A complementary approach involves grounding robot behavior in a more granular understanding of the physical world, similar to how biological agents learn from experience. The concept of "affordances"—which actions are available in a given situation based on the robot's physical characteristics and the environment's properties—has proven useful for structuring robot behavior. Previous work established affordances as a way to connect perception and action spaces. However, modeling the effects of executing these affordances, especially in varied and unknown environments, remains a challenge.

This paper builds upon a manipulation planning method introduced in, which combined neural network-based recognition of prespecified affordances with learned prediction of their effects. The current work addresses several limitations of and other related research:
- **Goal Specification:** used image-based goals, which are impractical. This paper introduces text-based goal definitions, aligning with research integrating natural language instruction, but crucially focusing on desired outcomes rather than direct action instruction.
- **Object Generalization:** assumed known objects observed during training. This work targets varied instances from object classes, enabling greater generalization.
- **Real-World Transfer:** It incorporates a style transfer approach, via generative augmentation to facilitate generalization over object shape, size, and visual appearance, enabling real-world experiments.
- **Robustness to Occlusion:** It enhances state representations with object mask channels, allowing planning for tasks involving full object occlusion and tracking objects even when their initial descriptors become invalid, which relates to works on semantic object tracking.
- **Generalization over Layouts:** It introduces a dynamic camera viewpoint into the prediction process to improve generalization over object positions and orientations, building on ideas of translational and rotational invariance in object manipulation.
- **Prediction Granularity:** Unlike earlier symbolic learning approaches, or some graph neural network methods with high-level descriptors, this work emphasizes fine-grained visual state prediction, similar to. It extends multi-step prediction, by operating on visual states rather than latent spaces and tracking specific objects.
- **Multimodal Goal Assessment:** While multimodal NNs have been used to assess action quality or success probabilities, this work uses a multimodal network to assess goal agreement over predicted future states, allowing quality assessment across various affordance types and focusing on outcome instead of action.

By integrating these advancements, this research aims to push towards more robust, generalizable, and user-friendly manipulation planning systems capable of operating in diverse real-world environments with high-level textual goals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a manipulation planning system that can reason through sequences of actions to achieve user-specified goals, exhibiting robustness to real-world complexities such as varied object appearances and occlusions. The core motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

1. **Enhancing Goal Specification Practicality:**
 - **Motivation:** Previous systems, such as, relied on image-based goals, which are cumbersome and inflexible for users. Real-world users require more intuitive and expressive ways to define desired outcomes.
 - **Objective:** To propose a manipulation planning system that allows for intuitive, text-based goal specification. This involves developing a mechanism to translate natural language goals into a format usable by the planning system and to assess the agreement between predicted visual states and these text goals.

2. **Improving Generalization to Varied Object Appearances and Real-World Scenarios:**
 - **Motivation:** Robotic systems trained solely in simulation often struggle when deployed in the physical world due to the "reality gap" – discrepancies in visual appearance, physics, and sensor noise. Limiting training to a fixed set of known objects also severely restricts applicability.
 - **Objective:** To integrate a real-to-sim style-transfer approach (Image Conversion Module, ICM) to enable seamless operation in physical robot setups. This module is designed to translate real-world state images, featuring objects of varied shapes, sizes, colors, and textures, into a consistent visual style that matches the simulation environment where the planning system's core modules are trained. This relaxes the assumption that objects are observed during training and enhances generalization across object instances within defined classes.

3. **Enabling Planning with Occluded or Dynamically Changing Object Identifiers:**
 - **Motivation:** In multi-step manipulation tasks, objects can become temporarily or fully occluded, or their initial visual descriptors might change (e.g., an object picked up is no longer on the table). A robust planning system must track objects crucial to the goal even under these conditions.
 - **Objective:** To expand the prediction-based planning logic by enriching state representations with dedicated object-tracking mask channels. These channels explicitly keep track of objects named in the goal text, allowing the system to generate action plans even when key objects become fully occluded or cease to be identifiable by their initial visual characteristics in future states.

4. **Improving Generalization Over Object Layouts and Positions:**
 - **Motivation:** Robotic manipulation often involves diverse spatial arrangements of objects. A system that cannot generalize well to different object positions and orientations will have limited utility.
 - **Objective:** To integrate orthogonal projection and virtual camera motion (translation and rotation) into the system's Effect Prediction Module (EPM). This design aims to exploit the translational and rotational invariance of affordance effects, allowing the EPM to predict local effects relative to the affordance's pose, thereby improving generalization over arbitrary object layouts.

In summary, the motivation is to create a more capable, practical, and robust robotic manipulation planner by leveraging learned visual dynamics and multimodal goal understanding, while explicitly tackling the challenges of real-world deployment and diverse task conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The system presented is a manipulation planning architecture comprising several interconnected neural network-based modules designed for vision-based, text-goal-driven robotic manipulation.

#### 3.1. A. Task Environment

The system is trained and evaluated in a tabletop manipulation environment.
- **Simulation:** Built in Unity, it features a virtual UR5 robot arm controlled via Unity-ROS infrastructure. Objects are drawn from categories including balls, cubes, cups, and plates. Object arrangements are randomized for data generation and manually designed for evaluation.
- **Real-world:** A physical setup closely replicates the simulation environment, using a DOBOT CRA5 arm and Robotiq 2F-140 gripper, with an Azure Kinect RGBD camera.
- **Affordances:** The defined affordance repertoire (𝒜) includes {GRASP, PLACE, PUSH, INSERT}. Each affordance is parameterized by a 3D pose and a gripper orientation angle. Affordance existence is determined programmatically during data generation (e.g., checking for gripper collisions for GRASP, stability for INSERT). Object shapes influence gripper orientation for GRASP/PUSH (e.g., 0, 90, 180, 270 degrees for round objects; aligned with object axes for trapezoidal objects).

#### 3.2. B. Data Generation

Training data for the system's modules is generated procedurally in the Unity simulation:
- **Randomized Objects and Layouts:** Object instances within categories (balls, cubes, cups, plates) have randomized size and shape features (e.g., top/bottom radius, height for cups/plates), promoting visual variability. Object arrangements are random.
- **Affordance Detection:** Programmatic checks (collision detection with simplified grippers, stability checks for insertion) determine the existence of affordances at candidate poses.
- **Action Sequences:** Sequences of up to six affordances are executed, with affordances randomly selected from the detected set.
- **State Recording:** At each step, the object configuration is stored as a top-down RGBD image, separate depth images for each object, and object center coordinates.
- **Visual Representation:** Objects are rendered with a custom shader mapping surface normals to RGB values, encoding rich shape information. All state images are captured in **orthogonal projection** to ensure that image translations directly correspond to camera translations, which is crucial for generalizing prediction across object positions.

#### 3.3. C. System Architecture

The integrated system consists of five main modules:
1. **Goal Interpretation Module (GIM):**
 - **Function:** Takes the initial RGBD state image and user-provided goal text.
 - **Output:** An enriched state representation (RGBDMMM) with up to three object mask channels, identifying objects named in the goal, and the goal text converted to a standardized format (e.g., "#1 is on #2.").
 - **Mask Generation:** Uses a semi-automated process where a user marks named objects, and SAM generates pixel-level binary masks. Mask intensity (1.0 for visible, 0.5 for occluded, 0.0 for absent) captures occlusion relations.
2. **Affordance Recognition Module (ARM):**
 - **Function:** Given an RGBD image, it outputs a list of existing affordances (pose and gripper angle).
 - **Implementation:** A modified ScaledYOLOv4 network, adapted for 4-channel RGBD input and outputting affordance poses instead of bounding boxes.
 - **Training:** Uses tuples of world states and ground truth affordance lists from the dataset. Incorporates artifact augmentation to robustify against visual leftovers from moved objects.
3. **Effect Prediction Module (EPM):**
 - **Function:** Predicts the future state (RGBDMMM) resulting from a sequence of affordances, starting from a given RGBDMMM state.
 - **Generalization:** Addresses shape and placement variation by exploiting translational and rotational invariance of affordance effects.
 - **Dual Viewpoints:** Processes states using a "robot view" (fixed) and a "table view" (shifted and rotated to center the affordance, thanks to orthogonal projection).
 - **Architecture:** An encoder-decoder network with affordance-type-specific predictors. It computes a latent state differential (δ) and a memory trace (m), then decodes δ to an RGBDMMM image with an "update mask" for combining with the previous state.
 - **Transformations:** Uses PIX library in JAX to allow gradients to pass through image rotations, enabling training over sequential affordance executions despite viewpoint transformations. The memory trace is carried across steps for delayed effects.
4. **Goal-Matching Module (GMM):**
 - **Function:** Quantifies the agreement between a predicted state (via its mask channels) and the standardized goal text as a real value in.
 - **Implementation:** A multimodal neural network based on CLIP, with scaled-down image encoder/transformer and fine-tuned text encoder.
 - **Input:** Mask channels of the state image and a list of goal sentences. Object masks are mapped to latent embeddings and combined with text embeddings via a Transformer and MLP.
 - **Training:** Uses sentences generated from object configurations (Table 1), oversampling rarer patterns. Negative examples are created by pairing unrelated states/sentences or by making small, untrue modifications to sentences. Blur and noise augmentations are applied to state images.
5. **Image Conversion Module (ICM) (for real-world only):**
 - **Function:** Bridges the reality gap by converting real-world RGBD images into the sim-style RGBD format expected by the planning system.
 - **Implementation:** A Swin-Unet modified for 4-channel RGBD.
 - **Real-to-Sim Conversion:** Real-world images are first converted to orthogonal projection using depth data, then style-converted by the Swin-Unet.
 - **Training Data Generation:** Uses a novel pipeline combining Stable Diffusion with ControlNet conditioned on depth images and text prompts (e.g., object class, color, material) to synthesize visually varied real-style RGB images that precisely match the shape and pose of objects in sim-style images. Gaussian noise and smoothing are applied to depth data. A One-Class SVM filters out low-quality synthetic examples.

#### 3.4. D. Planning Logic

The planning process operates as follows:
1. **Initial State Acquisition:** An RGBD image of the initial world state is obtained (sim-style for simulation, real-style for hardware, then converted via ICM and perspective projection).
2. **Goal Interpretation:** The GIM processes the state and goal text to produce RGBDMMM and standardized goal text.
3. **Iterative Search:**
 - The ARM identifies available affordances in the current state.
 - For each affordance, the EPM predicts the resulting future state.
 - The GMM scores each predicted state for agreement with the goal. Mask channel pixel values are rounded to 0.0, 0.5, or 1.0 before GMM input to remove blur and artifacts.
 - This recognition, prediction, and scoring process repeats up to a user-defined search depth, forming a search tree.
4. **Plan Selection:** The sequence of affordances leading to the predicted state with the highest goal agreement is returned as the manipulation plan.
5. **Pruning:** To manage computational cost, a simple pruning strategy discards predicted states (child nodes) that are visually (RGB channels) nearly identical to other expanded states but were generated by lower-confidence affordance recognitions.
6. **Execution:** For physical execution, MoveIt generates joint trajectories for the specified gripper coordinates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the individual modules and the integrated planning system in both simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. A. Module Evaluations

- **Affordance Recognition Module (ARM):** Achieved high recognition rates: 92% on test data and 93% on training data for GRASP affordances, with similar high performance (86-94%) across other affordance types (PLACE, PUSH, INSERT). The mean number of spurious recognitions was low (2.8/state on test data). Recognition failures typically occurred on edge cases, where minor positional or angular deviations could render an affordance non-executable.
- **Effect Prediction Module (EPM):** Demonstrated low pixel-level error between predicted and ground truth state images. The mean pixel value error was 0.0069 overall and 0.057 specifically for pixels that changed between states. Error tended to increase with the number of prediction steps. The authors noted that predictions for stochastic effects (e.g., pushing an object off another) could be "fuzzy."
- **Goal-Matching Module (GMM):** Achieved an overall accuracy of 98% (with 98% recall and precision) on a test dataset of 25,764 examples. Most misclassifications occurred on borderline cases, such as very small overlaps between objects intended to be "stacked."
- **Image Conversion Module (ICM):** On synthetic data, the ICM showed good conversion quality with high SSIM (0.9795) and low LPIPS (0.0153) and L1 errors (1.0784 overall, 10.208 for objects), indicating close perceptual and pixel-level similarity between converted and target sim-style images. Challenges were noted with thin object edges.

#### 4.2. B. Planning Experiments

- **Simulation Experiments (10 tasks):**
 - The system generated sensible plans for 9 out of 10 challenging tasks, demonstrating capabilities such as multi-step foresight, "detouring" (temporarily undoing satisfied goal conditions, e.g., Task 1, 7), understanding secondary affordance effects (e.g., object on a plate moves with the plate), and tracking fully occluded objects (e.g., Task 4, 10).
 - Tasks included scenarios requiring specific object alignments, sequential push actions (Task 3), placing large objects on small ones leading to occlusion, and indirect solutions (e.g., moving the obstacle instead of the target object, Task 5).
 - In one instance (Task 6), the system devised an unintended but potentially valid solution that ultimately led to execution failure due to an initial affordance being poorly parameterized.
- **Real-World Experiments (10 tasks, 5 trials each):**
 - The system achieved consistent success (5/5 trials) on 6 out of 10 tasks.
 - **Failure Patterns:**
 - The ICM struggled with real-world states where objects were stacked (leading to 0/5 success on Tasks 1 and 6).
 - Prediction failures occurred for cup grasping actions when cups were positioned out-of-center on plates in the initial state, likely due to unfamiliarity in the EPM's training data.
 - The ARM made erroneous recognitions, such as classifying an obstructed ball as graspable (leading to 0/5 success on Task 8).
 - Despite these challenges, for successful trials, the ICM effectively converted real-world images with varied object appearances to the consistent sim-style format required by the planning system.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a manipulation planning system capable of interpreting natural language goals and generating multi-step plans in complex visual environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key advancements:

1. **Bridging Language and Visual Dynamics:** The system effectively translates abstract text-based goals into concrete manipulation plans by predicting visual futures and evaluating them against a multimodal goal-matching module. This represents a step towards more intuitive human-robot interaction where users can specify *what* to achieve rather than *how*.
2. **Robustness to Real-World Variability:**
 - **Object Generalization:** By learning over parametrically varied object classes rather than fixed instances, the system exhibits improved generalization to diverse object shapes and sizes.
 - **Occlusion Handling:** The explicit tracking of goal-relevant objects via mask channels, even under full occlusion or changing identifiers, enhances the system's ability to tackle realistic manipulation scenarios where objects are frequently hidden.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The Image Conversion Module (ICM) offers a viable strategy for transferring simulation-trained capabilities to the physical world, accommodating varied real-world object appearances without extensive real-world data collection for the core planning modules. This reduces the cost and complexity of deploying robotic systems.
3. **Advanced Planning Capabilities:** The system demonstrates multi-step foresight, enabling it to navigate tasks requiring "detouring" (temporarily undoing a goal condition to achieve the overall goal) and exploiting secondary affordance effects (e.g., objects moving together). These capabilities go beyond simple reactive policies and suggest a deeper learned understanding of physical interactions.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Industrial Automation:** More adaptable robots in manufacturing and logistics, capable of handling diverse products and dynamically changing environments.
- **Service Robotics:** Development of robots that can perform complex household or care tasks with high-level instructions, adapting to variations in everyday objects.
- **Research in Embodied AI:** Provides a framework for investigating how fine-grained visual prediction and multimodal understanding can underpin robust robotic intelligence, potentially serving as a substrate for grounding and discovering symbolic rules in future work.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, the system exhibits limitations. A notable drop in success rates on real-world experiments highlights challenges in transferring performance from simulation, attributed partly to the propagation of imperfections between sequentially connected modules (e.g., ICM output quality affecting EPM). Future improvements could involve integrated training of modules to better account for characteristic imperfections, or a single-framework implementation to reduce computational overhead from data conversions. The significant planning time (tens of seconds to minutes) also suggests a need for efficiency improvements, possibly through hybridizing sub-symbolic reasoning with symbolic planning methods to guide search more effectively, drawing parallels to human cognitive processes.
