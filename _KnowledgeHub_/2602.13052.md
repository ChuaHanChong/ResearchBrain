# Quantization-Aware Collaborative Inference for Large Embodied AI Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research establishes a quantization-aware collaborative inference framework for Large Artificial Intelligence Models (LAIMs) in embodied AI, characterizing the trade-off between quantization bit-width and inference distortion. It jointly optimizes quantization and computation frequencies under delay and energy constraints, demonstrating superior inference quality (CIDEr score) compared to DRL-based methods in simulations and real-world testbeds.

## Problem

- Deploying Large AI Models (LAIMs) in embodied AI is challenging due to limited computational and energy resources on agents, and network/privacy issues with cloud-only inference.
- Current collaborative inference techniques are insufficient for LAIMs, as they overlook the unique resource demands and the coupling between quantization-induced distortion and computational load.
- A tractable method to characterize how quantization bit-width affects LAIM inference distortion was missing, along with a way to jointly optimize this with computation frequencies.

## Method

- Developed a tractable ==parameter distortion metric (L1-norm)== as a surrogate for output distortion, supported by ==LAIM parameter distribution modeling==.
- Derived ==information-theoretic lower and upper bounds== for the ==quantization rate-distortion function==, effectively characterizing the trade-off between bit-width and distortion.
- Formulated a ==joint optimization problem== (quantization bit-width and computation frequencies) under delay and energy constraints, solved efficiently using ==Successive Convex Approximation (SCA)==.

## Results

- The derived parameter distortion metric reliably upper bounds actual output distortion for LAIMs, tightening at moderate bit-widths, and the rate-distortion bounds accurately characterize distortion scaling.
- The proposed joint quantization and computation design consistently achieved superior inference quality (CIDEr score) in simulations compared to DRL-based and fixed-frequency baselines.
- Real-world testbed experiments validated the design's effectiveness, showing improved LAIM co-inference performance by adaptively controlling frequency profiles under varying delay and energy constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameter distortion, based on the L1-norm of weight differences, reliably and tractably approximates the output distortion caused by quantization in LAIMs.
> - Information-theoretic rate-distortion bounds define a computable interval for quantization, revealing an approximate exponential decay of distortion with increasing bit-width.
> - Jointly optimizing quantization bit-width and computation frequencies under delay and energy constraints is critical to balance inference quality, latency, and energy efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in deploying Large Artificial Intelligence Models (LAIMs) for embodied AI applications within evolving mobile networks, specifically Artificial Intelligence Radio Access Networks (AI-RANs). Embodied AI systems, characterized by a closed-loop among perception, communication, understanding, and action, are increasingly reliant on LAIMs for advanced capabilities in areas such as navigation, dialogue, and collaboration.

Traditional LAIM deployment paradigms face limitations:
- **On-device (agent) deployment:** Restricted by the limited computational capabilities, memory, and energy budgets of embodied agents, which struggle with LAIMs' massive parameter scales and computational intensity.
- **On-cloud deployment:** Offers abundant resources but is sensitive to network conditions, prone to server-side congestion with massive concurrent requests, and raises data privacy/security concerns, particularly for safety-critical applications.

**Collaborative inference (co-inference)** has emerged as an alternative, distributing LAIM processing across device, edge, and cloud resources. This approach allows agents to perform lightweight front-end processing and offload intermediate features to edge or cloud servers for the remainder of the inference. This mitigates on-device bottlenecks, improves privacy, and reduces communication overhead for multimodal data by transmitting structured representations.

Existing co-inference research generally falls into two categories:
- **Task-oriented feature encoding (semantic/goal-oriented communications):** Focuses on transforming raw data into semantic representations to reduce communication overhead.
- **Joint task offloading and resource allocation:** Aims to partition and dynamically assign inference workloads across distributed entities based on heterogeneous resources.

While these studies have advanced co-inference for regular-scale AI models, they have predominantly overlooked the unique demands of LAIMs. Recent works have begun to explore LAIM co-inference, introducing frameworks for cloud-edge collaboration, multimodal task offloading using deep reinforcement learning (DRL), hybrid large-small language model architectures, and multi-device multi-server settings with joint device selection and model partitioning.

Despite these advancements, the on-agent component in LAIM co-inference remains resource-constrained, making model compression crucial. Techniques like pruning, knowledge distillation, and **quantization** are explored. Quantization, which reduces numerical precision by representing parameters and activations with low bit-width formats, is particularly attractive due to its substantial reduction in storage and computation complexity, low engineering overhead, and compatibility with standard edge hardware.

This paper specifically focuses on **quantization-aware collaborative inference for LAIMs**, addressing a gap in understanding how quantization-induced distortion scales with bit-width in a tractable manner and how to jointly optimize quantization bit-width and computation frequencies under real-world Quality-of-Service (QoS) constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from several identified gaps in the existing literature regarding LAIM deployment in embodied AI systems:

1. **Lack of Tractable Quantization Distortion Characterization:** While model quantization is widely studied, a fundamental understanding of how quantization-induced inference distortion scales with the quantization bit-width in a tractable manner is largely missing. Existing works often lack a unified theoretical characterization of the quantization-distortion trade-off that could inform system-level optimization.
2. **Coupling of Quantization Bit-width and Computation Frequency:** From a system perspective, quantization not only affects inference accuracy (distortion) but also the computational workload. Concurrently, computation frequency control at both the device and server processors is a critical design degree of freedom that determines inference delay and energy consumption. These factors (quantization bit-width, computation frequency, inference accuracy, delay, and energy) are tightly coupled. For instance, reducing bit-width decreases computational load but increases distortion, while increasing frequency shortens latency but amplifies energy consumption.
3. **Untackled Quality-Energy-Delay Trade-offs:** The intricate coupling between quantization and computation creates non-trivial trade-offs among inference quality, energy consumption, and inference delay. These trade-offs are particularly significant in heterogeneous and resource-constrained embodied AI systems. Existing research has largely not explored these integrated aspects, indicating a need for a joint design framework.

Motivated by these challenges, the paper adopts a unified approach combining theoretical analysis and system-level design. The key objectives are:

1. **Develop an efficient approximation for quantization-induced inference distortion in LAIMs:** This involves establishing a theoretical link between parameter-level perturbations and output-level distortion, aiming for a tractable metric.
2. **Characterize the fundamental rate-distortion relationship for quantization:** Using information theory, the objective is to derive lower and upper bounds on the quantization rate-inference distortion function, revealing its dependence on LAIM statistics like the quantization bit-width.
3. **Formulate and solve a joint quantization bit-width and computation frequency design problem:** Under practical delay and energy constraints, the goal is to minimize the derived distortion upper bound while simultaneously ensuring the tightness of this approximation by maximizing the corresponding lower bound. This aims to provide practical design insights for model compression and computation resource management in embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper integrates theoretical modeling, rate-distortion analysis, and an optimization framework to address the joint quantization and computation design problem.

**A. System Model (Section II)**
The paper considers a two-stage collaborative inference system involving one embodied agent and one edge server.
- **On-agent Inference:** The agent receives input data (`x`), performs partial inference using a quantized model (`ˆw` with `ˆb` bits), and extracts intermediate embeddings (`o`). The original model parameters (`w`) are assumed to be `b` bits.
- **Embedding Transmission:** The intermediate embedding (`o`) is transmitted to the server.
- **On-server Inference:** The server completes the remaining inference using its model (`v`) and the received embedding, producing the final output (`˜o`). The result is then fed back to the agent.

**LAIM Parameter Distribution Modeling:** To enable tractable analysis, the magnitudes of LAIM parameters (`θ = |w|`) are modeled as following an exponential distribution (`P_Θ(θ) = λe^(-λθ)`). This assumption is empirically supported by analyzing parameters from various LAIMs (ResNet-152, VideoMAE, BERT, BLIP-2, GIT, GPT-3), which exhibit a sharp peak of weight values around zero.

**Inference Delay and Energy Consumption Modeling:**
- **Delay:** On-agent inference delay (`t(ˆb, f)`) is modeled as proportional to the quantized bit-width (`ˆb`) and inversely proportional to the agent's clock frequency (`f`). On-server inference delay (`˜t(˜f)`) is inversely proportional to the server's clock frequency (`˜f`). Total delay `T` is the sum of these two.
- **Energy Consumption:** On-agent energy (`e(ˆb, f)`) is modeled as proportional to `ˆb` and `f^2`. On-server energy (`˜e(˜f)`) is proportional to `˜f^2`. Total energy `E` is the sum of these two.

**B. Model Output Distortion Approximation (Section III)**
Direct analysis of LAIM output distortion due to quantization is intractable. The paper approximates this by first analyzing a simplified Fully Connected Deep Neural Network (FC DNN) with `L` layers, considering specific assumptions:
- **Normalized input:** `||x||_1 <= 1`.
- **Activation smoothness:** 1-Lipschitz activation function `σ(·)` with `σ(0)=0`.
- **Bounded quantization error:** `||W^(l) - ˆW^(l)||_1 <= τ^(l)`.
Under these assumptions, an upper bound on the output distortion (`||f(x,W) - f(x,ˆW)||_1`) is derived, showing it is a weighted sum of layer-wise parameter distortions. This motivates using a `L1`-norm based **parameter distortion (`d(W, ˆW) = ||W - ˆW||_1`) as a tractable surrogate metric** for inference output distortion. For general AI models, including Transformers, a first-order Taylor expansion approximation is used to justify that output distortion is approximately bounded by a constant multiple of the parameter perturbation norm.

**C. Rate-Distortion Analysis for Quantization (Section IV)**
The paper provides an information-theoretic characterization of the trade-off between model quantization bit-width and inference distortion, focusing on the surrogate parameter distortion `d(θ, ˆθ) = |θ - ˆθ|`.
- **Lower Bound (R_L(D) and D_L(R)):** A Shannon-type lower bound is derived. This involves using the differential entropy of the exponential distribution for LAIM parameters and maximizing the entropy of the quantization noise (`Z`) under an `L1` distortion constraint, which is achieved by a zero-mean Laplacian distribution. This bound quantifies the minimum bits per parameter for a given average distortion.
- **Upper Bound (R_U(D) and D_U(R)):** An upper bound is derived by constructing a specific test channel where quantization noise `Z` is an independent Laplacian random variable. This provides a computable upper limit on distortion for a given bit-width.
Together, these bounds `D_L(R) <= D(R) <= D_U(R)` define a computable interval for the distortion-rate function, which is utilized for system design.

**D. Joint Quantization and Computation Design (Section V)**
The core problem (P1) is formulated as an optimization problem:
- **Objective:** Minimize the gap between the distortion upper bound and lower bound (`D_U(ˆb-1) - D_L(ˆb-1)`), aiming for a tight approximation and better inference quality.
- **Decision Variables:** On-agent quantization bit-width (`ˆb`), agent computation frequency (`f`), and server computation frequency (`˜f`).
- **Constraints:** Maximum allowable total delay (`T(ˆb, f, ˜f) <= T_0`), maximum allowable total energy consumption (`E(ˆb, f, ˜f) <= E_0`), feasible bit-width range (`ˆb ∈ B`), and feasible frequency ranges (`0 <= f <= f_max`, `0 <= ˜f <= ˜f_max`).

**Proposed Solution:**
Problem (P1) is a mixed-integer non-linear problem (MINLP) and non-convex. The paper proposes an efficient solution using **Successive Convex Approximation (SCA)**:
1. **Relaxation:** The integer bit-width `ˆb` is relaxed to a continuous variable `˜b`.
2. **Reformulation:** An auxiliary variable `˜b'` is introduced to linearize the coupled terms in constraints.
3. **Iterative Approximation:** The non-convex objective function terms and non-convex constraint (`˜b <= 1/˜b'`) are approximated using first-order Taylor expansions at each iteration (`k`). This transforms the original problem into a series of convex subproblems (P4.k) that can be solved efficiently using standard convex optimization tools (e.g., CVX).
4. **Convergence:** The SCA approach guarantees a monotonically non-increasing objective value, ensuring convergence.
5. **Rounding:** After obtaining the relaxed continuous solution `˜b*`, it is rounded to the nearest feasible integer bit-width `ˆb*` in `B`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation section validates both the theoretical analyses and the system-level design using simulations and real-world testbed experiments.

**A. Verification of Model Output Distortion Approximation (Section VI-A)**
- **Models and Quantization Schemes:** The approximation was tested on FCDNN-16 (an autoencoder on MNIST), BLIP-2 (image understanding LAIM, 3.75B params), and GIT (video understanding LAIM, 176M params). Both uniform and non-uniform (power-of-two logarithmic) quantization schemes were considered.
- **Results (Fig. 3):**
 - Across all models and quantization schemes, the proposed parameter distortion consistently **upper bounds the actual output distortion**.
 - The gap between the theoretical bound and measured distortions narrows as the quantization bit-width increases.
 - For moderate bit-widths (e.g., >3 bits for non-uniform, >4 bits for uniform), the theoretical bound becomes notably tight.
- **Conclusion:** The model output distortion approximation provides a reliable and computationally tractable characterization of quantization-induced performance distortion, justifying its use as a surrogate metric.

**B. Verification of the Distortion-Rate Bounds (Section VI-B)**
- **Method:** The analytical lower bound `D_L(R)` and upper bound `D_U(R)` were compared against a numerically estimated distortion-rate function `D(R)` obtained using a Blahut-Arimoto type procedure.
- **Results (Fig. 4):**
 - The simulated `D(R)` exhibits an approximate exponential decay with increasing rate, consistent with the analytical bounds.
 - `D_U(R)` is looser in the very low-rate regime but tightens considerably as the rate increases (e.g., >2 bits).
 - `D_L(R)` accurately captures the scaling law of distortion reduction with bit-width.
 - As the rate further increases, both bounds converge towards a small distortion floor.
- **Conclusion:** The interval `[D_L(R), D_U(R)]` provides an efficient and effective characterization of the distortion-rate behavior for LAIM quantization, suitable for guiding system design.

**C. Performance of the Proposed Joint Quantization and Computation Design (Section VI-C)**
- **Simulation Setup:** Evaluations were conducted using MS-COCO for image captioning (BLIP-2) and VaTeX for video understanding (GIT). Inference quality was measured by the CIDEr score. The simulation environment included a device (Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU) and a server (Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU) with specified max frequencies, FLOPs/cycle, PUEs, and power coefficients.
- **Benchmark Schemes:** The proposed design was compared against:
 1. **PPO-based design:** A state-of-the-art DRL approach for similar problems.
 2. **Fixed-frequency design:** Device and server frequencies are fixed to maximums, only bit-width is optimized.
 3. **Feasible random design:** Random bit-widths are sampled, and feasible computation frequencies are found.
- **Simulation Results (Figs. 5-8):**
 - The **proposed design consistently achieved the highest CIDEr score** across varying delay and energy constraints, for both BLIP-2 and GIT, and under both uniform and non-uniform quantization.
 - This performance advantage is attributed to the proposed design's explicit joint optimization, which fully exploits the available degrees of freedom in quantization and computation.
 - The proposed design generally outperformed the PPO-based baseline, suggesting the challenges of DRL in achieving optimal solutions without proper initialization and constraint handling.
 - Relaxing delay or energy constraints generally led to increased CIDEr scores, as more budget allows for higher quantization precision and/or more favorable computation frequencies.
 - The design demonstrated robustness across different quantization schemes.

- **Real-world Testbed Experiments (Fig. 9 & Table I):**
 - **Hardware:** An NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin 64GB as the edge device and a Dell PowerEdge R740 server (Intel Xeon Gold 6246R CPUs, NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs) as the edge server, connected via Wi-Fi.
 - **Frequency Control:** Due to practical limitations, coarse-grained frequency profiles (low, medium, high) were used instead of precise control on the Jetson AGX Orin.
 - **Testbed Results (Table I):**
 - In **delay-limited but energy-sufficient scenarios**, adopting a higher-frequency profile yielded better task performance (higher CIDEr). This is because higher frequencies reduce latency, allowing for larger quantization bit-widths and thus less distortion.
 - In **energy-limited but delay-sufficient scenarios**, a lower-frequency profile achieved better task performance. Under tight energy budgets, higher frequencies consume more energy, necessitating more aggressive quantization to stay within limits, which degrades inference quality.
- **Conclusion:** The testbed experiments confirm the practical necessity and effectiveness of the proposed joint quantization and computation design in improving LAIM co-inference performance, even with coarse-grained frequency control, in real-world edge embodied AI systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI and wireless edge networks by providing a theoretically grounded and practically validated framework for efficient LAIM co-inference.

The **theoretical contributions** include:
- A tractable approximation of quantization-induced inference distortion for LAIMs, bridging the gap between parameter-level perturbations and output-level performance. This approximation, derived initially for FC DNNs and extended to general LAIMs, offers a simplified yet effective metric for optimization.
- The derivation of tight lower and upper bounds on the quantization rate-distortion function for exponentially distributed LAIM parameters. These bounds provide fundamental insights into the trade-off between quantization bit-width and distortion, and are crucial for guiding system design by quantifying the achievable performance limits and variations.

The **system-level design contributions** involve:
- The formulation of a joint optimization problem for quantization bit-width and computation frequencies under combined delay and energy constraints. This holistic approach addresses the complex interplay of inference quality, latency, and energy consumption, which are critical for resource-limited embodied agents.
- The development of an efficient solution method based on successive convex approximation (SCA), which tackles the non-convex and mixed-integer nature of the problem, making it computationally tractable.

The **practical impact** of this work is significant for the deployment of LAIMs in real-world embodied AI systems:
- **Enhanced LAIM Deployment:** The proposed design enables the practical integration of massive LAIMs into resource-constrained embodied agents operating within edge environments, which was previously challenging due to computational and energy limitations.
- **Optimized Resource Management:** By jointly considering quantization and computation, the framework offers a methodology for dynamically managing system resources to achieve a desired balance between inference quality, latency, and energy efficiency, adapting to varying QoS requirements.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness:** Evaluations demonstrate consistent performance gains over existing methods, suggesting that the proposed joint design can yield higher inference quality under given resource budgets, and maintain robustness across different quantization schemes and constraint settings.
- **Guidance for Future Systems:** The theoretical insights into distortion-rate trade-offs and the practical validation on a testbed can inform the design of future AI-RANs and embodied AI architectures, particularly as they move towards more sophisticated agentic AI scenarios and multi-agent systems where efficient resource utilization is paramount.

In summary, this research advances the fundamental understanding of quantization effects in LAIM co-inference and provides a robust, efficient framework for optimizing inference performance under practical resource constraints, paving the way for more capable and deployed embodied AI applications.
