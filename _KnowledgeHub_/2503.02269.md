# Experience Replay with Random Reshuffling

> [!summary] Summary
> New methods, Random Reshuffling with Circular Buffer (RR-C) and Random Reshuffling by Masking (RR-M), adapt random reshuffling from supervised learning to address the dynamic nature of reinforcement learning's experience replay. These techniques reduce variance in how transitions are sampled, leading to improved performance on Atari-10 benchmarks for various deep RL algorithms.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning from sequential data suffers from temporal correlations, leading to unstable learning and reduced sample efficiency.
- Standard experience replay samples transitions with replacement, which incurs higher gradient variance compared to random reshuffling techniques used in supervised learning.
- Adapting random reshuffling to dynamic RL replay buffers, especially with non-uniform, dynamically updated priorities (as in prioritized experience replay), poses significant challenges.

## Method

- For uniform experience replay, **RR-C** (Random Reshuffling with a Circular Buffer) shuffles buffer indices at each "epoch" start and samples them sequentially, skipping invalid entries.
- For prioritized experience replay, **RR-M** (Random Reshuffling by Masking) temporarily masks the priorities of oversampled transitions to guide sampling towards under-sampled ones.
- Both methods guarantee ==without-replacement sampling== over a set period or against an ideal distribution, aiming to reduce variance in transition utilization.

## Results

- Numerical simulations confirmed both RR-C and RR-M substantially reduce variance in the number of times each transition is sampled compared to standard methods.
- RR-C demonstrated modest performance gains for DQN and C51 on Atari-10, significantly outperforming with-replacement sampling in **6 out of 10 games** for C51.
- RR-M improved DDQN+LAP performance on Atari-10 in **8 out of 10 games**, with significant improvement in one game, and showed additional benefits when integrated with stratified sampling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Random reshuffling, a technique known for variance reduction and faster convergence in supervised learning, can be effectively adapted to reinforcement learning's dynamic experience replay buffers.
> - The performance improvements observed come from applying without-replacement sampling at the buffer level across an "epoch," rather than merely preventing duplicates within a single mini-batch.
> - Systematically balancing the sampling frequency of transitions throughout the entire replay buffer leads to more robust and effective reinforcement learning agent training.
