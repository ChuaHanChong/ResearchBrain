# Opening the Sim-to-Real Door for Humanoid Pixel-to-Action Policy Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> The DoorMan framework, developed by researchers from NVIDIA, UC Berkeley, CMU, and CUHK, enables a humanoid robot to autonomously open diverse real-world doors using only egocentric RGB vision. This achievement results from policies trained entirely in a massively randomized, photorealistic simulation, demonstrating superior performance to human teleoperation.

## Problem

- Developing generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation policies that rely solely on RGB vision for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Bridging the visual sim-to-real gap for complex, contact-rich tasks without relying on depth sensors, object-centric features, or hard-coded motion primitives.
- Addressing algorithmic scalability and robustness to partial observability for autonomous policies coordinating vision and whole-body control.

## Method

- A teacher-student-bootstrap learning pipeline trained in NVIDIA's IsaacLab, a massively randomized and photorealistic simulation environment.
- A privileged teacher policy trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using a staged-reset exploration strategy to learn long-horizon door opening.
- A student policy distilled from the teacher using DAgger, relying on egocentric RGB images and proprioception, and further refined with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for partial observability.

## Results

- Achieved an **83%** success rate across diverse real-world door types, demonstrating robust zero-shot generalization from simulation.
- Outperformed expert human teleoperators in task fluency, completing door opening **23.8%** faster, and matched their success rate (**83%** vs. **80%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that extensive visual randomization, GRPO fine-tuning, and the staged-reset exploration mechanism are all crucial for the system's high performance and generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Massive photorealistic visual and physical randomization in simulation is critical for achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex humanoid tasks.
> - A staged-reset exploration strategy is essential for stabilizing and accelerating reinforcement learning for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks by effectively sampling intermediate task states.
> - Reinforcement learning fine-tuning (GRPO) after imitation learning can effectively mitigate the performance gap caused by partial observability in vision-only policies, enabling compensatory behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses one of the most challenging frontiers in robotics: **humanoid loco-manipulation using pure RGB vision for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.** The field of robot learning has seen tremendous progress, particularly in leveraging simulation for data generation and policy training. Advances in GPU-accelerated, photorealistic simulation (e.g., NVIDIA's IsaacLab/Isaac Sim) have enabled scalable data generation and domain randomization, which are critical for bridging the "sim-to-real gap"—the challenge of transferring policies trained in simulation to the real world.

Prior work has demonstrated significant success in sim-to-real for **locomotion** (e.g., agile quadrupedal robots, humanoid parkour) and **dexterous manipulation** (e.g., Rubik's Cube solving, in-hand manipulation). These achievements often involve either isolated tasks (locomotion without manipulation, or manipulation without needing complex balancing) or rely on privileged information like depth sensors, precise object localization, or hard-coded motion primitives. For example, many door-opening systems in the past have used wheeled platforms, depth sensing, object-centric features, or extensive scripting. The DARPA Robotics Challenge era systems, while impressive, required heavy operator intervention and task-specific programming. More recent teleoperation-centered pipelines still suffer from brittleness and lack the scalability for diverse, autonomous skills.

The unique challenge this paper tackles is **loco-manipulation**, where perception, balance, contact-rich control, and navigation must be tightly coordinated across the entire robot body. Humanoid robots, with their complex kinematics and dynamics, present an even higher bar for such tasks. Previous efforts in humanoid loco-manipulation often decouple locomotion from manipulation or rely on hierarchical control structures that may limit end-to-end learning. Critically, very few prior works have demonstrated a **vision-only, end-to-end policy** for complex humanoid loco-manipulation that generalizes **zero-shot** from simulation to diverse real-world scenarios using only RGB perception.

This work positions itself as a significant step towards general-purpose humanoid autonomy by demonstrating that a solely RGB-perceptive, simulation-trained policy can robustly perform a complex, contact-rich task like door opening. It pushes the boundaries of sim-to-real transfer by extending it to whole-body humanoid control without reliance on external sensors beyond an egocentric camera, nor on hard-coded estimators or task-specific priors. By surpassing human teleoperation baselines, it provides strong evidence for the potential of large-scale synthetic data and advanced reinforcement learning techniques in unlocking new capabilities for humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **develop a generalizable learning pipeline for vision-based humanoid loco-manipulation.** The authors select **door opening** as a representative, high-difficulty benchmark task because it encapsulates a wide array of challenges inherent in everyday humanoid interactions:
- **Precise Perception-Action Coupling:** The robot must accurately perceive the door handle, its orientation, and the door's articulation state from its egocentric camera feed, and translate this into precise joint movements.
- **Contact-Rich Control:** Interacting with the handle and the door panel involves managing continuous physical contact, including the spring-loaded resistance of the handle and the forces exerted by the hinges.
- **Whole-Body Coordination and Balance:** The humanoid must maintain balance while exerting forces, navigating, and manipulating, adapting its entire body to the dynamic interaction with the door.
- **Long-Horizon Task:** Door opening is a multi-stage task, from approaching and grasping to rotating the handle, pushing/pulling the panel, and traversing through the doorway.

The authors identify two fundamental challenges that prevent existing systems from achieving truly generalizable learning for such tasks:
1. **Algorithmic Scalability and Robustness to Partial Observability:** The learning algorithm needs to be simple, scalable, and robust enough to handle the inherent partial observability of vision-only control. It must produce autonomous policies that seamlessly coordinate vision and whole-body control across diverse tasks, a requirement often unmet by prior specialized approaches.
2. **Bridging the Visual Sim-to-Real Gap with Broad Data:** The visual sim-to-real gap is vast, encompassing a wide range of appearance and physics variations. Achieving robust real-world generalization requires training on broad, heterogeneous data rather than a few curated scenes, which is challenging to achieve in the real world.

Motivated by these challenges, the research aims to demonstrate a solution that:
- Eliminates reliance on depth sensing, object-centric features, or hard-coded motion primitives.
- Achieves zero-shot transfer from simulation to diverse real-world environments.
- Produces policies that coordinate whole-body control and vision for complex, contact-rich tasks.
- Outperforms human teleoperators in terms of efficiency and success rate, highlighting the benefits of learned policies over human-controlled inverse kinematics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DoorMan framework employs a novel **teacher-student-bootstrap learning pipeline** trained entirely in a massively randomized, photorealistic simulation environment. This three-phase approach is designed to overcome the challenges of long-horizon tasks, partial observability, and the sim-to-real gap.

**Phase 1: Teacher Policy Training (Privileged Reinforcement Learning)**
- **Objective:** To learn a highly capable policy with access to comprehensive, "privileged" information unavailable in the real world. This teacher serves as an optimal demonstrator.
- **Algorithm:** Standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used.
- **Observations:** The teacher policy receives ground-truth data such as robot-root-to-door transform, hand-to-door-handle transforms, net contact wrenches on hands, and root linear velocity.
- **Reward System:** A multi-stage reward system decomposes the complex door-opening task into six atomic stages (Walk to door, Pre-grasp, Grasp, Open, Swing, Pass through door), each with specific reward formulations to guide learning.
- **Staged-Reset Exploration Strategy:** This is a crucial innovation for stabilizing long-horizon task training. To address the difficulty of exploring narrow transitional regions between task stages (e.g., grasping the handle without incurring penalties), the simulator is leveraged to cache snapshots of successful progress. When an environment resets, it can randomly re-initialize from a prior stage's snapshot, effectively "rewriting" the initial state distribution. This increases the frequency of reaching later stages, improving credit assignment and overall training efficiency.

**Phase 2: Student Policy Distillation (Imitation Learning)**
- **Objective:** To distill the expert knowledge of the privileged teacher policy into a student policy that relies only on non-privileged, real-world relevant observations.
- **Algorithm:** DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) is employed. DAgger is an interactive imitation learning algorithm that iteratively collects data by having the student query the teacher for actions on states it encounters, thereby correcting for distribution shift that often plagues standard behavioral cloning.
- **Observations:** The student policy uses egocentric RGB images (processed by a vision encoder) concatenated with proprioceptive information (joint angles, joint velocities, root angular velocities).
- **Architecture:** The vision encoder and proprioceptive features are fed into an LSTM (for temporal context), followed by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that outputs target joint positions. The vision encoder is jointly fine-tuned with the policy.
- **Domain Randomization:** Aggressive visual randomization (materials, lighting, camera parameters) is applied during this phase to prepare the student for diverse real-world appearances.

**Phase 3: Student Policy Bootstrapping/Refinement (Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning)**
- **Objective:** To mitigate the performance gap caused by the partial observability inherent in vision-only control and enable the student to learn compensatory behaviors.
- **Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used. GRPO is an actor-only variant of PPO that estimates baselines from grouped trajectory scores, making it a lightweight and stable reinforcement refinement method.
- **Reward Signal:** Primarily a binary task success signal, supplemented by simple shaping reward terms (joint velocity/acceleration penalties, action rate penalty) for regularization.
- **Rationale:** This phase allows the student to go beyond simply imitating the teacher. By optimizing directly under its own partial observations, the student can discover emergent strategies (e.g., keeping the manipulated object centered in view, adjusting end-effector poses for better visibility) that the teacher (with privileged observations) might not have explicitly demonstrated.

**Massive-Scale Simulation Randomization (in IsaacLab):**
The efficacy of the sim-to-real transfer is heavily reliant on the diversity of the training data generated within NVIDIA's IsaacLab.
- **Physical Variations:** The pipeline procedurally generates 5 different door types (pushing/pulling with rotational handles, push-bar), randomizes dimensions, handle locations, hinge damping, latch dynamics, and resistive torques. A realistic latching mechanism is included to capture abrupt dynamic changes.
- **Visual Variations:** Random textures are drawn from IsaacLab’s Physically Based Rendering (PBR) materials. Over 5233 dome-light textures are used to simulate varied lighting conditions and environments. The RTX Real-Time renderer operates in performance mode with post-processing effects enabled to accurately mimic real-world camera conditions on a moving robot. This extensive randomization ensures the policy is exposed to a broad "variability envelope" rather than specific scenes, a prerequisite for robust zero-shot generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the robustness and efficacy of the DoorMan pipeline:

1. **Robust Zero-Shot Real-World Generalization:**
 - DoorMan achieves robust zero-shot performance across diverse real-world door types, articulation mechanisms, appearances, and spatial layouts, all of which were unseen during training.
 - The robot successfully handles different handle types (rotational, push-bar), opening directions (push vs. pull), and a wide array of visual appearances.

2. **Superior Performance to Human Teleoperation:**
 - **Success Rate:** DoorMan achieved an 83% success rate across various real-world door opening tasks. This is on par with expert human teleoperators (80% success) and significantly outperforms non-expert teleoperators (60% success) who use the same whole-body control stack.
 - **Task Fluency/Completion Time:** DoorMan demonstrated superior efficiency, completing tasks 23.8% faster than expert teleoperators (15.40 seconds vs. 20.02 seconds) and 31.7% faster than non-experts (15.40 seconds vs. 22.55 seconds).
 - **Qualitative Observations:** The authors noted that human teleoperators often struggle with accurately gauging spring-loaded forces, tracking the compliant circular motion of the door, and maintaining whole-body balance, issues that the learned DoorMan policy adeptly manages.

3. **Effectiveness of Photorealistic Visual Randomization:**
 - An ablation study on visual diversity highlighted the critical role of extensive randomization.
 - Using all available textures and dome-light randomization during training resulted in the best generalization (81-86% success rates).
 - Removing dome-light randomization led to a substantial 15-30% performance drop, emphasizing the importance of varied lighting conditions.
 - Training with only solid-color randomization (similar to older sim-to-real approaches) yielded 65.8-70% success, demonstrating the clear advantage of modern high-fidelity rendering with rich material and lighting variations.
 - Absence of any visual randomization reduced success rates to a mere 5-20%, definitively confirming its necessity.

4. **Performance Boost from GRPO Fine-Tuning:**
 - After DAgger distillation, the initial student policy suffered from a "non-recoverable observability gap," achieving only 50-70% success rates, despite the teacher performing at 80-90%.
 - The GRPO bootstrapping phase effectively closed this gap, elevating student success rates to 80.8-85.8%, aligning closely with the teacher's upper bound. This demonstrates GRPO's efficacy in mitigating partial observability and learning compensatory behaviors for vision-based control.

5. **Impact of Staged-Reset Exploration:**
 - An ablation study showed that the staged-reset exploration mechanism significantly improves the stability and efficiency of teacher training for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks.
 - With a large buffer size (100 snapshots), the teacher policy rapidly reached most task stages within 500 iterations and all stages by approximately 1700 iterations.
 - Reducing the buffer size (e.g., 10 snapshots) extended training completion significantly to over 4000 iterations.
 - Without any staged-reset buffer, the exploration failed, with the policy struggling to even enter challenging intermediate stages like grasping the door handle due to early penalties and "unlearning" behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "DoorMan" research represents a substantial advancement in the field of humanoid robotics and sim-to-real transfer, with profound implications for the future of general-purpose robot autonomy.

**Pioneering Pure RGB Loco-Manipulation:** This work presents one of the first successful demonstrations of a humanoid robot policy capable of diverse articulated loco-manipulation solely from pure RGB perception, without relying on depth sensors, object-specific priors, or hand-coded estimators. This eliminates a significant dependency on specialized hardware and domain expertise, paving the way for more flexible and scalable robot deployments in unstructured environments.

**Validation of Large-Scale Simulation and RL:** By outperforming human teleoperators in both success rate and efficiency, DoorMan emphatically validates the potential of GPU-accelerated, photorealistic simulation and scalable reinforcement learning pipelines for training complex whole-body humanoid skills. It highlights that learned policies can surpass human-controlled interfaces, especially in tasks requiring nuanced interaction with physics and continuous balance. This finding could accelerate the shift towards fully autonomous robot systems.

**Robust and Generalizable Methodological Contributions:**
- The **teacher-student-bootstrap pipeline** offers a robust framework for transferring skills from privileged, high-fidelity control to realistic, partial-observability settings.
- The **staged-reset exploration mechanism** is a critical innovation for stabilizing RL training in long-horizon, contact-rich tasks, overcoming common exploration challenges. This technique can be broadly applied to other complex robotic tasks.
- The **GRPO-based fine-tuning** effectively bridges the gap caused by partial observability, allowing policies to self-improve and learn adaptive behaviors that are crucial for real-world robustness.

**Implications for Humanoid Autonomy:** Demonstrating robust door-opening capabilities—a quintessential everyday task—is a significant step toward developing humanoids capable of operating autonomously in human environments. The ability to interact with common articulated objects without prior knowledge or specific task programming opens doors (pun intended) for applications in logistics, elder care, home assistance, and hazardous environment exploration.

**Future Research Directions:** The authors acknowledge opportunities for further improvement, such as reducing reliance on task-specific reward engineering (e.g., via high-capacity behavioral-cloning teachers) and extending the framework to an even broader array of everyday whole-body interactions. This research lays a strong foundation for tackling more complex, open-ended tasks that require comprehensive perception and dexterous whole-body control.

In conclusion, "DoorMan" underscores NVIDIA's leadership in leveraging simulation and AI for robotics, offering a compelling vision for how advanced deep learning and extensive synthetic data can enable autonomous humanoids to perform complex, real-world tasks with unprecedented efficiency and robustness.
