# Opening the Sim-to-Real Door for Humanoid Pixel-to-Action Policy Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> The DoorMan framework, developed by researchers from NVIDIA, UC Berkeley, CMU, and CUHK, enables a humanoid robot to autonomously open diverse real-world doors using only egocentric RGB vision. This achievement results from policies trained entirely in a massively randomized, photorealistic simulation, demonstrating superior performance to human teleoperation.

## Problem

- Developing generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation policies that rely solely on RGB vision for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Bridging the visual sim-to-real gap for complex, contact-rich tasks without relying on depth sensors, object-centric features, or hard-coded motion primitives.
- Addressing algorithmic scalability and robustness to partial observability for autonomous policies coordinating vision and whole-body control.

## Method

- A teacher-student-bootstrap learning pipeline trained in NVIDIA's IsaacLab, a massively randomized and photorealistic simulation environment.
- A privileged teacher policy trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using a staged-reset exploration strategy to learn long-horizon door opening.
- A student policy distilled from the teacher using DAgger, relying on egocentric RGB images and proprioception, and further refined with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for partial observability.

## Results

- Achieved an **83%** success rate across diverse real-world door types, demonstrating robust zero-shot generalization from simulation.
- Outperformed expert human teleoperators in task fluency, completing door opening **23.8%** faster, and matched their success rate (**83%** vs. **80%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that extensive visual randomization, GRPO fine-tuning, and the staged-reset exploration mechanism are all crucial for the system's high performance and generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Massive photorealistic visual and physical randomization in simulation is critical for achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex humanoid tasks.
> - A staged-reset exploration strategy is essential for stabilizing and accelerating reinforcement learning for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks by effectively sampling intermediate task states.
> - Reinforcement learning fine-tuning (GRPO) after imitation learning can effectively mitigate the performance gap caused by partial observability in vision-only policies, enabling compensatory behaviors.
