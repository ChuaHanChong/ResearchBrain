# rMuscle: Robotic Muscle Memory for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Model Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> rMuscle is a real-time VLA inference framework that exploits similarity across repeated robot executions of the same task. A dual-phase muscle-memory cache reuses retrieved reference FFN outputs for low-drift visual tokens during compute-bound VLM prefill, and reuses reference neuron-activation masks to load only important weights during memory-bound action denoising. It delivers 1.20-1.50x speedup over FlashRT while matching original success rates in simulation and on real robots.

## Problem

- Existing VLA acceleration (visual-token reuse, step skipping) exploits only the immediately preceding frame or one denoising pass, ignoring similarity across a robot's repeated executions of recurring tasks.
- Prior optimizations target one inference stage, yet VLM prefill is compute-bound while action denoising is memory-bound, and both contribute substantially to end-to-end latency, capping single-stage speedups.
- Whole-step diffusion caching from text-to-image serving assumes 20-50 near-redundant steps; VLAs use as few as four denoising steps, so skipping any step discards unrecoverable refinement.

## Method

- **Context Cache**: ranks visual tokens by ==RMS FFN-input drift== against a retrieved reference, recomputes only the top 40% through the MLP, and reuses cached reference outputs for the rest.
- **Action Cache**: group-shared ==important-neuron masks== from a same-task reference select half the denoising FFN neurons; non-anchor steps update only those neurons' delta atop a dense anchor step's output.
- **Cache management**: asynchronously reconstructs reference FFN states during robot action execution, retrieves references by ==ViT-feature similarity== then joint-history distance within a ==progress-aligned sliding GPU window==, falling back to dense below 0.8 similarity.

## Results

- Over FlashRT, end-to-end speedups reach **1.29x**/**1.20x**/**1.50x** on RTX 4090 and **1.42x**/**1.23x**/**1.43x** on Jetson Thor for π0.5, GR00T N1.6, and X-VLA; gains grow to **1.6-1.9x** at 7B+7B scale.
- Success rates match dense execution: **35.2%** RoboTwin average (DP-Cache **33.9**, NIRVANA **26.9**), **76%** ALOHA pick-and-place and **84%** conveyor packing, versus NIRVANA's **70%** and **66%**.
- Replacing the cross-execution repository with the previous in-episode call drops RoboTwin success from **35.2%** to **25.9%** at similar latency; Context Cache alone gives **1.25x** on RTX 4090, Action Cache speeds denoising **1.49x** on Thor.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repetitive factory workloads make a VLA's internal states, not just its observations, predictable across episodes; retrieving a same-task past execution is a stronger reuse reference than the previous frame.
> - Match the acceleration trick to each stage's roofline: skip computation where compute-bound (VLM prefill), skip weight loads where memory-bound (few-token denoising); single-stage optimizations cap end-to-end gains.
> - Few-step flow VLAs break whole-step diffusion caching; reuse must be sub-step (per token, per neuron) and anchored on the current call's dense activations, with a similarity-gated dense fallback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses inference efficiency for embodied artificial intelligence, particularly robots operating in structured and repetitive environments such as factory workstations. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have become a common policy architecture for these systems because they combine visual perception, language instructions, robot state, and action generation within a single model. Recent VLAs, including π0.5, GR00T, and X-VLA, generally use a two-stage inference process: a vision-language model first encodes the multimodal context, and a diffusion- or flow-based action expert then generates a sequence of robot actions.

This architecture creates a latency problem. The robot repeatedly follows an observe–infer–execute loop, so delays in policy inference reduce responsiveness, make action transitions less smooth, and limit how frequently the system can react to changes. Existing inference engines such as FlashRT and vla.cpp reduce software and kernel overhead through operator fusion, optimized kernels, graph execution, and hardware-specific implementation. Other methods apply caching or denoising-step skipping techniques developed for video-language or image-generation models.

The paper argues that these approaches do not fully exploit the workload structure of industrial manipulation. A robot at a fixed workstation often performs the same task many times, with only moderate variation in object positions, lighting, camera observations, and motion trajectories. The authors further argue that similarity is not limited to external observations or actions; it also appears in internal VLA states, including feed-forward network inputs, outputs, and neuron activation patterns. rMuscle is introduced to exploit this repetition through two different caches matched to the distinct computational bottlenecks of VLA inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective is to reduce end-to-end VLA inference latency without lowering task success rates. The paper focuses on three related questions.

First, it investigates whether repeated executions of the same embodied task exhibit sufficient similarity to support computation reuse. This is important because physical environments are not perfectly deterministic. Object poses, lighting, tabletop textures, and robot joint configurations can vary across trials. A useful acceleration method must therefore tolerate moderate changes rather than relying on exact frame duplication.

Second, the authors seek to identify which parts of VLA inference should be optimized. Their profiling shows that multimodal context encoding and action denoising have different hardware characteristics. The VLM prefill stage is largely compute-bound, particularly in its multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), whereas the action-denoising stage is relatively small and memory-bound because each denoising step repeatedly loads model weights. A method that accelerates only one phase may therefore have limited effect on total latency.

Third, the paper aims to design a cache system that does not introduce prohibitive memory or retrieval overhead. Storing complete intermediate states for every previous policy call would require substantial memory. The system must therefore reconstruct cache entries selectively and maintain only a small set of likely references in GPU memory.

The biological analogy in the paper is human muscle memory: familiar contexts can trigger previously learned motor patterns, reducing the amount of computation required for routine actions. In rMuscle, the analogy is implemented as reuse of internal model states rather than as a change to the learned policy itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Workload Characterization

The authors first profile π0.5 using PyTorch, torch.compile, and FlashRT. FlashRT reduces a representative inference from 194.3 milliseconds with standard PyTorch to 46.0 milliseconds. Within FlashRT execution, the VLM prefill and action-denoising stages remain substantial contributors to latency.

The VLM MLP accounts for approximately **83.5% of VLM-prefill latency**, while MLP operations account for **51.1% of action-denoising latency**. Roofline analysis indicates that the VLM MLP has high arithmetic intensity and is primarily compute-bound on an RTX 4090. The denoising MLP has much lower arithmetic intensity and is memory-bound. On Jetson Thor, the VLM stage lies near the compute–memory transition, while action denoising remains strongly memory-sensitive.

The paper then measures similarity across repeated executions. In a simulated dual-arm bottle-moving task, changes in lighting and initial bottle locations still produce action trajectories with cosine similarities of **0.995** and **0.994** for the two arms. Internal VLM states also remain similar: MLP inputs reach cosine similarities of up to **98.5%**, while outputs reach up to **81.0%**.

For visual-token reuse, the authors rank tokens according to the relative change in their normalized MLP inputs. Recomputing the top **40%** of visual tokens captures approximately **80%** of the total squared output change. The important tokens tend to correspond to bottles, grippers, arms, and relevant background regions, and their positions change as the manipulation progresses.

For the action expert, the authors measure the contribution of each hidden neuron using the magnitude of its post-GeGLU activation multiplied by the norm of its down-projection weight. The top **50%** of neurons capture approximately **84.4%** of the total contribution score. Important-neuron sets overlap by an average of **86%** between executions of the same task, compared with approximately **71%** for different tasks. This difference motivates task-specific reference masks.

#### 3.2. Dual-Phase Cache Design

rMuscle contains a **Context Cache** and an **Action Cache**.

The Context Cache targets the compute-intensive VLM prefill stage. For each layer, it stores reference FFN inputs and outputs for visual tokens. At runtime, the system compares the current normalized FFN input for each visual token with the corresponding reference input. Tokens with the largest input drift are recomputed through the full FFN. The remaining visual-token outputs are copied from the reference cache. Instruction and robot-state tokens are always recomputed. Attention remains dense, so the method selectively reduces FFN computation rather than approximating the entire VLM.

The Action Cache targets memory-bound action denoising. It stores important-neuron masks derived from related reference executions. Instead of loading all FFN weights at every denoising step, rMuscle loads and evaluates only the neurons selected by the reference mask. The method uses dense “anchor” denoising steps to establish current activations. For subsequent steps, it starts from the anchor output and updates the contribution of the selected neurons. This preserves the current policy call’s anchor contribution for unselected neurons while reducing weight traffic.

To lower overhead, consecutive denoising steps share a neuron mask within a group. In π0.5’s ten-step configuration, step zero anchors steps one through four, and step five anchors steps six through nine. Group-shared masks reduce repeated weight-gathering operations while retaining substantial important-neuron coverage.

#### 3.3. Cache Management

Complete FFN states are too large to retain for every reference. In a three-view π0.5 configuration, one policy call requires approximately **107 MB** for selected VLM inputs and outputs. Forty calls could therefore require about **4.28 GB**. rMuscle stores compact inputs and reconstructs expanded cache states online during robot action execution, overlapping reconstruction with physical movement.

Reference retrieval first filters candidates using the language instruction. It then compares intermediate visual features and reranks candidates using recent robot joint-state histories. This combination distinguishes visually similar situations that may require different motions.

The system maintains a sliding GPU working set. The reported default configuration retains four candidate episodes and the current position plus four future positions from each episode, for a total of 20 expanded entries. The authors report that **96.6%** of retrieved references remain within roughly ten percentage points of the current episode’s progress, supporting this temporal-locality strategy. Reconstruction is limited by available GPU memory, transfer time, and the time before the next inference request.

#### 3.4. Evaluation

The evaluation covers π0.5, GR00T N1.6, and X-VLA across **92 embodied tasks**: 40 LIBERO tasks, 50 RoboTwin tasks, and two physical tasks. The physical evaluations involve dual-arm bottle placement with ALOHA and conveyor-based furniture-component packing using DOBOT and Franka robots.

Experiments use an RTX 4090 and a Jetson Thor. The comparison methods include FlashRT, torch.compile, vla.cpp, DP-Cache, NIRVANA, and a variant using only the previous policy call as a reference. Latency includes retrieval and weight gathering after warm-up. Success rate is measured across seeded simulation rollouts and physical trials.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

rMuscle reduces inference latency across all evaluated models and both hardware platforms. Relative to the corresponding state-of-the-art exact inference baseline, the paper reports speedups of approximately **1.20–1.29× on RTX 4090** and **1.23–1.42× on Jetson Thor** for the main model comparisons. Figure 15 also reports model-specific speedups of **1.29×, 1.20×, and 1.50×** for π0.5, GR00T N1.6, and X-VLA on RTX 4090, and **1.42×, 1.23×, and 1.43×** on Jetson Thor.

The system reaches inference rates of **28.1, 51.0, and 35.7 Hz** for π0.5, GR00T N1.6, and X-VLA on RTX 4090. On Jetson Thor, the corresponding rates are **13.4, 17.5, and 19.7 Hz**. When model size is increased, the relative benefit grows: reported speedup rises from approximately **1.3× to 1.6×** on RTX 4090 and from **1.4× to 1.9×** on Thor across the tested π0.5-scale configurations.

Ablation results show that the Context Cache provides the larger benefit for π0.5, producing a **1.25×** speedup on RTX 4090 and **1.22×** on Thor relative to FlashRT. The Action Cache produces a smaller **1.11×** speedup on RTX 4090 but a larger **1.49×** speedup on Thor, consistent with Thor’s more memory-constrained profile. Together, the caches reduce total latency beyond either mechanism alone.

Policy quality is generally preserved. On LIBERO, rMuscle matches the average success rates of vanilla execution and DP-Cache for all three evaluated models. On RoboTwin, it matches vanilla’s average success rate of **35.2%**, while outperforming DP-Cache and NIRVANA by **1.3** and **8.3 percentage points**, respectively. Some individual RoboTwin tasks show small reductions near the visual-similarity fallback threshold, but follow-up tests indicate that raising the threshold or adding reference episodes can address these cases.

On physical tasks, rMuscle matches vanilla execution: **76%** success on ALOHA bottle placement and **84%** on conveyor furniture packing. It performs better than both DP-Cache and NIRVANA in these tests.

The memory overhead is moderate. On RTX 4090, GPU usage rises from **8.4 GB** for vanilla execution to approximately **9.4–11.4 GB**, depending on cache capacity and working-set size. The system supports up to 800 cached entries with less than 3 GB of additional GPU memory. On Jetson Thor, the maximum reported increase is 6.0 percentage points of unified-memory occupancy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper’s main contribution is an inference-optimization strategy designed around the structure of embodied workloads rather than around generic sequence or diffusion-model redundancy. Its dual-cache design recognizes that VLM prefill and action denoising have different bottlenecks and require different forms of reuse. The internal-state analysis also provides evidence that repeated physical tasks can produce stable computational patterns even when observations and actions are not identical.

If the assumptions hold, rMuscle could improve responsiveness and motion continuity for robots operating at fixed workstations, particularly on edge devices where memory bandwidth and compute resources are limited. Lower latency may also reduce idle gaps between action chunks and increase production throughput without changing the trained policy.

However, the approach depends on repeated tasks, adequate reference coverage, and reliable similarity retrieval. Cache construction requires offline profiling and produces hundreds of inference references per task. Performance and accuracy may degrade for unfamiliar tasks, substantial scene changes, or executions that fall just above the similarity threshold. The reported RoboTwin success rates are also relatively low in absolute terms, indicating that inference acceleration does not resolve broader policy limitations. Future work would need to examine online cache updates, more diverse environments, long-term cache maintenance, and safety behavior when retrieval is incorrect. Nevertheless, the study establishes a framework for exploiting persistent task structure in real-time VLA deployment.
