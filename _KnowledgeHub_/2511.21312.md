# Neural NMPC through Signed Distance Field Encoding for Collision Avoidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NTNU developed a neural Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework for mapless aerial robot navigation that encodes single range observations into a Signed Distance Function (SDF) using deep neural networks. This system demonstrates collision-free navigation and resilience to odometry drift in both simulated and real-world forest environments, achieving real-time control frequencies around 40 Hz and outperforming state-of-the-art baselines in collision avoidance and path optimality in simulations.

## Problem

- Autonomous navigation for Aerial Robots (ARs) in unknown and unstructured environments using only onboard sensing faces challenges, especially in perceptually-degraded conditions.
- Traditional map-based navigation systems suffer from error propagation (e.g., localization drift) and latency due to sequential map updates, limiting responsiveness to dynamic or unforeseen events.
- Existing mapless navigation approaches often lack formal safety guarantees or struggle with rich, differentiable 3D environmental representations suitable for optimization-based controllers.

## Method

- A novel neural architecture, combining a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), is developed to encode a single range observation (depth camera or LiDAR) into a compact, continuous, and differentiable 3D Signed Distance Function (SDF).
- The learned neural SDF is explicitly integrated as a position constraint within a Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (**NMPC**) framework to ensure collision-free navigation for multirotors.
- The NMPC framework is theoretically analyzed for recursive feasibility and stability, and solved in real-time using a receding horizon Nonlinear Programming (NLP) approach with a Real-Time Iteration (RTI) solver.

## Results

- The SDF-NMPC method achieved a **100%** success rate (zero collisions) in Flightmare simulations, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like EGO-Planner, ORACLE, and KDtree-NMPC in collision avoidance and maintaining competitive path optimality and speed.
- Real-world experiments in forest environments demonstrated collision-free navigation with a quadrotor, successfully avoiding obstacles even with adversarial reference inputs and significant odometry drift (up to ~**3%** Relative Position Error).
- The neural VAE and SDF components, coupled with the NMPC solver, operated in real-time, achieving a control frequency of approximately 40 Hz on a custom quadrotor, suitable for dynamic aerial robot control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding instantaneous local range observations into a continuous, differentiable neural SDF enables robust, mapless navigation that is resilient to global localization errors and avoids the latency of traditional mapping.
> - Integrating the differentiable neural SDF as an explicit constraint within an NMPC framework provides a principled method for formal collision avoidance with theoretical guarantees for recursive feasibility and stability.
> - A distance-weighted loss function for the VAE prioritizes reconstruction accuracy for closer obstacles, which is critical for safety-sensitive control, while the two-step neural architecture efficiently learns this representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the problem of autonomous navigation for Aerial Robots (ARs) in unknown and unstructured environments using only onboard sensing. This area is recognized as a challenging aspect of robotics, particularly in perceptually-degraded conditions where traditional mapping systems may experience failures.

Conventional navigation systems typically involve constructing a volumetric map of the environment, followed by a motion planning stage. While effective in many scenarios, this approach is susceptible to error propagation from the mapping stage (e.g., localization drift) and introduces latency due to sequential map updates, which limits responsiveness to dynamic or unforeseen events.

In response to these limitations, the robotics community has explored "mapless navigation" strategies. This landscape includes:
- **Early heuristic approaches** for reactive collision avoidance.
- **Model-based methods** that generate local volumetric representations from immediate observations, facilitating fast collision checking (e.g., k-d trees, ray-casting, motion primitives).
- **Deep learning-based techniques**, which have gained prominence for their ability to handle high-dimensional exteroceptive inputs like depth images. These include Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for end-to-end control.

This research aligns with a hybrid category of approaches that utilize deep learning for implicit environment representations, which are then integrated with principled control strategies. Specifically, it distinguishes itself from prior work on neural implicit Signed Distance Function (SDF) encoding, which often targets scene-scale mapping and planning applications, by focusing on encoding a *single range observation* into an SDF without aggregating multiple observations. This emphasis on instantaneous local information is intended to enhance reactivity and resilience to localization issues.

The work builds upon previous contributions in neural Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) for collision avoidance but extends it by generalizing to the nonlinear dynamics of multirotors, utilizing a richer 3D SDF representation (as opposed to 2D occupancy maps), and conducting a more comprehensive evaluation, including an assessment of resilience to drifting odometry. The use of SDF is highlighted as advantageous due to its continuous and differentiable nature, encoding both distance and direction to obstacles, which is beneficial for gradient-based optimal controllers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and reactive framework for mapless, collision-free navigation of Aerial Robots (ARs) in unknown environments, relying solely on onboard range sensing and odometry.

The work aims to achieve this through three principal contributions:
1. **Develop a novel neural architecture for environment encoding:** This architecture is designed to transform a single range observation (e.g., from a depth camera or LiDAR) into a Signed Distance Function (SDF). The motivation here is to obtain a compact, continuous, and differentiable 3D representation of the local environment, which is suitable for integration into an optimization-based controller. Unlike approaches that build global maps, this method prioritizes instantaneous local awareness to avoid latency and mitigate the effects of errors in global state estimation.
2. **Integrate the neural SDF into a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework:** The explicit SDF representation is intended to serve as a position constraint within the NMPC optimization problem. This integration seeks to enable collision-free navigation by formally incorporating environmental boundaries into the control law. A further objective is to theoretically analyze the NMPC’s properties, specifically verifying recursive feasibility and stability under fixed observations. This provides a formal basis for the controller's safe operation.
3. **Conduct a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed framework:** This includes a component-wise assessment of both the learning-based encoding modules and the closed-loop performance of the NMPC. Key evaluation aspects involve quantitative analysis of SDF reconstruction accuracy, ablation studies to understand sensitivity to various parameters (e.g., sensor frequency, noise, obstacle density), comparisons with state-of-the-art local navigation methods, and real-world experiments under challenging conditions, such as forest environments with adversarial reference inputs and drifting odometry. The motivation for this detailed evaluation is to demonstrate the method's robustness, computational efficiency, and practical applicability.

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the limitations of traditional map-based navigation systems, particularly their vulnerability to localization errors and their latency in dynamic or complex environments. By adopting a mapless approach that leverages the SDF as a differentiable, implicit representation, the authors aim to overcome these challenges, providing a more resilient and responsive navigation solution for ARs in real-world deployment scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed SDF-NMPC, integrates deep neural networks for environmental encoding with a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) for collision avoidance.

#### 3.1. Learning the Environment Representation

The core of the approach is to learn an implicit SDF from a single range observation.
- **SDF Definition:** The SDF is defined as the spatial distance to the surface of visible obstacles, taking negative values for non-visible space and positive for visible space. It is truncated at a distance of 1 meter (`T_SDF`). The definition is heuristically extended beyond the sensor's Field of View (FoV) for training purposes to ensure proper boundary conditioning.
- **SDF Ground Truth Approximation:** For training data generation, an approximation algorithm is used to compute target SDF values and spatial gradients for sampled 3D points. This algorithm fits a 3D grid of adaptive resolution, denser near the center, to locate the closest cell with a change in occupancy. This process is tensorized for efficient online training data generation on a GPU.
- **Neural Network Architecture:** A two-step process is employed:
 1. **VAE (Variational Autoencoder):** A β-VAE, comprising a ResNet-10 convolutional encoder and a deconvolutional decoder, processes the input range image (depth or LiDAR) into a low-dimensional latent vector `z`. The encoder extracts mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) for a Gaussian latent space. The training loss for the VAE includes a pixel-wise MSE reconstruction loss (distance-weighted to prioritize closer obstacles) and a β-scaled Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD) term. The decoder is used only during VAE training.
 2. **MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron):** The latent vector `z` from the trained VAE encoder is concatenated with the positional embedding `γ(p)` of a queried 3D point `p`. This combined input is fed into an MLP with four hidden layers, sine activations, and dropout regularization, which then outputs the estimated SDF value `SDF_θ,z(p)`. Positional embedding uses an off-axis sinusoidal mapping to help the MLP represent higher-frequency content. The MLP is trained separately after the VAE encoder weights are frozen.
- **Training Procedure:**
 - **VAE Training:** Conducted using a dataset of real and simulated images (Gazebo, Aerial Gym). The distance-weighted MSE loss ensures closer obstacles are reconstructed with higher accuracy.
 - **SDF Training:** Performed using simulated data (to ensure valid pixel ground truth) by regressing the target SDF values and their spatial gradients at sampled 3D points. The loss function is a weighted sum of MSE losses for both the SDF value and its unit norm gradient, encouraging satisfaction of the eikonal equation.

#### 3.2. Neural NMPC

The NMPC framework utilizes the learned SDF for collision avoidance.
- **System Modeling:** A standard co-planar multirotor is modeled as a rigid body. The state vector includes position, a 2-component yaw quaternion, and linear velocity, all expressed in a switching inertial vehicle frame (`F_V0`). The input vector comprises collective thrust, roll, pitch angles, and body yaw rate.
- **Constraints:**
 - **Obstacle Avoidance:** The primary collision avoidance constraint is `SDF_θ,z(S0_pB) ≥ r + ϵ`, where `S0_pB` is the robot's predicted position in the sensor frame (`F_S0`), `r` is the robot's radius, and `ϵ` is a safety margin.
 - **Field of View (FoV):** Additional constraints ensure that the robot's predicted trajectory remains within the sensor frustum (`F(t0)`), expressed as bounds on the azimuth and elevation angles of the sensor's origin in `F_S0`.
- **Objective Function:** A stage cost `ℓ(x, u)` is defined to minimize the deviation from a reference velocity and heading (`V0_v_ref`, `V0_ψ_ref`) while penalizing excessive control inputs (roll, pitch, yaw rate, and deviation of vertical thrust from gravity `mg`). A terminal cost `V(x_N)` is also included.
- **Theoretical Analysis:**
 - **Recursive Feasibility:** A terminal constraint is formulated to ensure that from any terminal state `x_N`, a "maximum braking to standstill" policy is recursively feasible. This involves checking that the robot can come to a full stop without colliding, based on a computed braking distance `d_b`, and remains within the FoV. `d_b` is approximated using a polynomial.
 - **Stability:** Conditions are derived for a quadratic terminal cost `V(x) = p * v^T * v` to ensure that the optimal cost function `J*` remains non-increasing. This analysis considers the impact of the braking policy and conflicting objectives with a non-zero reference velocity, assuming the use of a reference governor to adjust `v_ref` to zero when the system approaches a stop.
- **Nonlinear Programming (NLP):** The NMPC is formulated as a discrete-time NLP solved over a receding horizon. It includes the dynamic model, input and state bounds, collision avoidance, FoV constraints, and terminal constraints derived from the feasibility and stability analysis.
- **Implementation:** The NLP is implemented using Acados and Casadi in Python, with PyTorch for the neural networks, interfaced via L4Casadi. An SQP solver with Real-Time Iteration (RTI) is used, incorporating Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) regularization for stability. Constraints are slackened to maintain feasibility with noisy feedback and switching observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the SDF-NMPC framework comprised quantitative analyses of the neural components, ablation studies in simulation, comparisons with other navigation methods, and real-world experiments.

#### 4.1. VAE Evaluation

- A "biased" VAE, which prioritizes reconstruction accuracy for closer obstacles, demonstrated improved performance in the foreground compared to a standard VAE and FFT-based compression.
- The depth camera VAE achieved lower reconstruction RMSE (0.182 m) than the LiDAR VAE (0.375 m) for the full image, attributed to the higher spatial frequency in LiDAR images due to broader FoV.
- Generalization was assessed on the TartanAir dataset, yielding a pixel-wise RMSE of 0.225 m, consistent with in-domain test results.
- The VAE encoder achieved an average inference time of 1.27 ms on a GPU, supporting real-time operation.

#### 4.2. SDF Reconstruction

- **Neural Network Size:** The accuracy of the SDF approximation was found to increase with network size. The `SDF_256-64` architecture (decreasing layer sizes from 256 to 64 neurons) was selected as a balance between computational efficiency (0.69 ms query time for SDF value and gradient on CPU) and accuracy (RMSE of 8.4 cm for depth, 9.6 cm for LiDAR; gradient direction errors of 28° for depth, 31° for LiDAR). Smaller networks (`SDF_128`) showed significant obstacle penetration, rendering them unsuitable for navigation.
- **Comparison:** The neural SDF showed comparable reconstruction errors to other SDF encoding approaches (e.g., GP-based, other neural SDFs), with query times generally faster than GP-based methods but slower than k-d trees (which lack analytical differentiability).
- **Generalizability:** On the TartanAir dataset, `SDF_256-64` exhibited an SDF estimation RMSE of 9.1 cm and a gradient error of 31°, suggesting robust generalization to unseen environments.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Simulations in Aerial Gym)

- The framework demonstrated resilience to reduced sensor frequency (down to 2 Hz) and moderate state noise (standard deviation σ=0.05) with failure rates below 4%.
- Increased reference velocity (`v_ref` = 3 m/s) led to higher failure rates, which were mitigated by using a larger `SDF_512` network, indicating improved reconstruction accuracy aids in faster reaction scenarios.
- FoV constraints had a marginal impact in 2D pillar environments but significantly improved success rates in cluttered 3D environments, especially with a 360° FoV.
- High obstacle density and thin obstacles presented challenges, leading to increased failure rates. The `SDF_512` network generally improved performance in these scenarios, suggesting better encoding of complex geometries.
- The SDF network was found to be generally conservative in its distance estimates, leading to occasional constraint violations attributed to discontinuities in observations or approximation errors.
- Average NMPC solving times were 8.51 ms (`SDF_256-64`) and 15.18 ms (`SDF_512`) on a CPU, supporting real-time control frequencies.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Existing Methods (Simulations in Flightmare)

- The proposed SDF-NMPC method achieved a 100% success rate (zero collisions), outperforming EGO-Planner, ORACLE, and KDtree-NMPC.
- It maintained a control frequency above 100 Hz, demonstrating competitive computational efficiency. Its computation time consistency was noted as an advantage over baselines which showed increased load in more challenging environments.
- SDF-NMPC outperformed EGO and ORACLE in path optimality and average speed, while being comparable to KDtree-NMPC.
- Energy efficiency was generally comparable across methods, with ORACLE being an outlier due to less dynamically feasible outputs.

#### 4.5. Resilience to Drifting Odometry

- Simulations in Gazebo evaluated the method's resilience to increasing odometry drift (Relative Position Error, RPE) and decreasing sensor frequency.
- The NMPC, operating in a local observation frame, demonstrated collision avoidance even with significant global position drift, highlighting its mapless and local nature.
- Success rates decreased with higher RPE and lower sensor frequencies, but the framework exhibited robustness within certain operational thresholds (e.g., 8 out of 10 successes at ~5% RPE with 10Hz sensor frequency).

#### 4.6. Real-World Experiments (Forest Environments)

- Experiments were conducted with a custom quadrotor equipped with a depth camera or LiDAR. VAE encoding and NMPC solving times (12.4 ms on GPU and 15.4 ms on CPU, respectively) allowed for a control frequency of approximately 40 Hz.
- **Depth Camera Experiment:** The AR successfully navigated a tree-filled section, achieving collision avoidance despite a naive, obstacle-agnostic reference velocity input. The encoded SDF effectively guided the robot away from obstacles.
- **LiDAR with Adversarial Reference Velocity:** The NMPC effectively prevented collisions against adversarial inputs from a human operator, either by deflecting the trajectory or bringing the robot to a full stop. The VAE successfully reconstructed range images even with significant invalid pixels.
- **Drifting Odometry Experiment:** Using radar-inertial odometry (with a ~3% RPE relative to LiDAR odometry), the AR maintained collision avoidance in a cluttered forest environment against adversarial inputs, even with substantial global position drift. This validated the simulation findings regarding resilience to odometry inaccuracies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces a neural NMPC framework that integrates a learned Signed Distance Function (SDF) for mapless collision avoidance, presenting several points of significance and potential impact.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Integration of Learning and Control:** The work demonstrates a tightly integrated approach that combines deep neural networks for rich environmental representation (SDF from single range images) with a principled, optimization-based controller (NMPC). This hybrid strategy aims to leverage the representational power of deep learning while retaining the formal guarantees and control over system dynamics offered by NMPC.
- **Mapless Navigation for Robustness:** By relying solely on instantaneous local observations and avoiding explicit map construction, the framework inherently addresses key limitations of traditional map-based systems, such as vulnerability to localization drift and latency in dynamic environments. The demonstrated resilience to drifting odometry is a notable aspect of this.
- **Formal Safety Guarantees:** The embedding of the neural SDF as an explicit, differentiable constraint within the NMPC allows for formal consideration of collision avoidance. The theoretical analysis verifying recursive feasibility and stability (under fixed observations) contributes to understanding the safety properties of the control law.
- **Real-time Performance:** Both simulation and real-world experiments indicate that the proposed framework operates at frequencies suitable for real-time control of aerial robots, even with computationally intensive neural networks and NMPC solvers, through optimized implementation and GPU acceleration.
- **Generalizability and Scalability:** The evaluation across diverse environments, sensor modalities (depth camera, LiDAR), and varying levels of clutter and obstacle types suggests a degree of generalizability. The component-wise assessment provides insights into the scalability of the neural encoding.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Autonomous Navigation Capabilities:** This framework could improve the robustness and adaptability of autonomous aerial robots in challenging, unstructured, and unknown environments, such as search and rescue operations in disaster zones, exploration of subterranean or forest areas, and industrial inspections where mapping is difficult or unreliable.
- **Advancements in Robotics Control:** The approach offers a paradigm for combining advanced perception (neural implicit representations) with sophisticated control (NMPC), which could be adapted to other types of robotic platforms facing similar real-time collision avoidance challenges.
- **Improved Safety in Human-Robot Interaction:** Explicit and differentiable safety constraints, derived from real-time sensor data, contribute to building safer autonomous systems, potentially allowing robots to operate more reliably in environments shared with humans or other critical infrastructure.
- **Reduced System Complexity and Cost:** By reducing the reliance on highly accurate global localization systems and dense environmental maps, the method could simplify the overall navigation stack and potentially lower the hardware requirements or computational burden associated with global mapping.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work identifies several avenues for future research, including:
 - Optimizing sensing-to-action delay through architectural and memory transfer improvements.
 - Investigating energy-based control approaches for dissipative behavior when collision constraints are active.
 - Extending the neural representation to account for robot orientation, moving beyond spherical assumptions.
 - Incorporating multimodal sensing (e.g., visual data alongside range data) to enhance perception in degraded conditions.
 - Relaxing the "0-memory" assumption by integrating temporal observation windows or neural world models.
 - Addressing dynamic obstacles by predicting their motion over time.
 - Developing uncertainty-aware control formulations to account for noise in state estimates and SDF approximations.
