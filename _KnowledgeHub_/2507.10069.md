# ElasticMM: Efficient Multimodal LLMs Serving with Elastic Multimodal Parallelism

> [!summary] Summary
> ElasticMM, a new serving system, enhances Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) inference efficiency by implementing Elastic Multimodal Parallelism, which dynamically manages resources across decoupled request types and inference stages. It achieves up to 4.2x lower Time-to-First-Token and 4.5x higher throughput compared to existing systems.

## Problem

- MLLM inference suffers from increased latency and resource contention due to additional vision components and complex pipelines in existing tightly coupled serving systems.
- Current LLM serving architectures do not differentiate between text-only and multimodal requests, nor do they decouple inference stages, leading to inefficient resource utilization under heterogeneous workloads.
- Static resource allocation and fixed parallelism strategies fail to adapt to the dynamic, bursty nature of MLLM workloads and the varied computational demands of different inference stages.

## Method

- **ElasticMM** introduces ==Elastic Multimodal Parallelism== (EMP), a two-level hierarchical scheduling framework that decouples requests by modality (text-only vs. multimodal) and inference stages (encoding, prefill, decode).
- It employs modality-aware load balancing with proactive and reactive mechanisms to dynamically allocate resources across modality groups and uses elastic partition scheduling for intra-group parallelism adjustments.
- The system integrates MLLM-specific optimizations, including a Unified Multimodal Prefix Cache for efficient data reuse and Non-blocking Encoding to asynchronously process image data, reducing latency.

## Results

- ElasticMM reduced Time-to-First-Token by up to **4.2x** (Qwen2.5-VL) and increased throughput by up to **4.5x** (Qwen2.5-VL) compared to baselines on the ShareGPT-4o dataset.
- The system consistently delivered lower output latency, demonstrating effective isolation of the decoding stage and superior performance under high request rates.
- Ablation studies validated that both the Elastic Multimodal Parallelism framework and specific MLLM inference optimizations (Unified Multimodal Prefix Cache and Non-blocking Encoding) independently contributed to these performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical decoupling of MLLM serving by request modality and inference stage effectively mitigates resource contention and allows for tailored resource management.
> - Dynamic and elastic resource allocation, adapted to both long-term workload patterns and sudden bursts, is critical for maintaining high efficiency and meeting Service-Level Objectives (SLOs) under heterogeneous MLLM traffic.
> - Targeted multimodal optimizations, like a unified prefix cache and non-blocking encoding, are necessary to overcome unique MLLM overheads and improve latency beyond general LLM serving techniques.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated performance in various applications. Their evolution into Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) enables processing diverse inputs such as images, videos, and audio by integrating feature extractors and projection modules. MLLMs are employed in tasks like visual question answering and image captioning, increasingly deployed in online services where performance is subject to service-level objectives (SLOs).

However, MLLMs present distinct inference challenges compared to text-only LLMs. The MLLM inference pipeline typically involves additional stages, including image preprocessing, image encoding, and language model inference (prefill and decode stages). These components increase computational complexity and extend the average context length. Existing LLM serving systems, such such as vLLM, primarily adopt tightly coupled architectures. These systems do not differentiate between text-only and multimodal requests, nor do they decouple the various inference components. All stages are often executed on the same hardware instances with uniform batching and model parallelism strategies. This architectural approach can lead to increased time-to-first-token (TTFT) and reduced resource utilization, particularly under heavy or heterogeneous multimodal workloads.

Previous research has explored decoupled architectures for LLMs, separating prefill and decode stages to improve efficiency. For MLLMs, some recent efforts have focused on separating inference stages, but often still batch text-only and multimodal requests together during the language model backend inference, which can lead to continued efficiency issues, especially with encoder-decoder architectures. Furthermore, MLLM workloads exhibit bursty patterns with sudden spikes in image inputs, and different inference stages benefit from varying degrees of parallelism (e.g., image encoding and LLM prefill stages are compute-intensive, while the decode stage has limited scalability). Static resource allocation and fixed parallelism strategies in existing systems struggle to adapt to these dynamic workload characteristics and heterogeneous computational demands. This leads to inefficient resource usage and difficulty in meeting SLOs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to address the inefficiencies encountered when serving Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in real-world deployment scenarios. The paper identifies significant challenges arising from the architectural and workload complexities inherent to MLLMs, particularly within existing tightly coupled LLM serving systems.

The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current MLLM serving approaches:
- **Increased Inference Overhead**: MLLMs introduce additional model components, such as vision encoders and projection modules, and a more complex inference pipeline (image preprocessing, encoding, language model prefill, and decode). These elements contribute to higher computational costs and significantly extend context lengths, leading to increased latency, especially Time-to-First-Token (TTFT), and higher memory consumption.
- **Tightly Coupled Architectures**: Existing serving systems for LLMs are typically designed with a tightly coupled architecture. This design often treats unimodal (text-only) and multimodal requests identically, routing them through the same inference pipeline. All components—preprocessors, vision encoders, and the LLM backend—are frequently co-located on the same hardware server. This co-location forces components to share compute and memory resources under a uniform batching and model parallelism strategy, leading to resource contention and inefficiency when processing heterogeneous multimodal workloads.
- **Inability to Adapt to Workload Heterogeneity**: MLLM workloads are inherently diverse, consisting of both text-only and multimodal requests with different resource demands. Multimodal workloads can also be bursty, characterized by sudden spikes in image inputs. Existing static resource allocation and fixed parallelism strategies cannot dynamically adapt to these fluctuating and varied demands. This results in underutilization of resources or performance degradation during peak loads.
- **Inefficient Parallelism Management**: Different stages of MLLM inference exhibit varying computational characteristics and scalability. For instance, image encoding and LLM prefill are compute-intensive and can benefit from high degrees of parallelism, whereas the decode stage is often memory-bound and scales poorly with additional resources. Current systems’ inability to adjust parallelism at the granularity of individual inference stages leads to suboptimal performance and resource allocation.

To address these challenges, the authors propose to develop an MLLM serving system that is both decoupled and elastic. The key objectives are:
1. **Decouple request types**: Separate text-only and multimodal requests into independent modality groups to tailor resource allocation and processing strategies to their distinct requirements.
2. **Decouple inference stages**: Disaggregate the MLLM inference pipeline into distinct stages (encoding, prefill, decode) to enable stage-specific parallelism and resource management.
3. **Enable elastic resource adaptation**: Implement mechanisms for dynamic resource allocation across modality groups and elastic parallelism adjustment within inference stages to handle fluctuating and bursty workloads efficiently.
4. **Introduce MLLM-specific optimizations**: Develop targeted optimizations like unified multimodal prefix caching and non-blocking encoding to mitigate unique overheads associated with multimodal inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To address the identified challenges, the authors propose **Elastic Multimodal Parallelism (EMP)**, a new serving paradigm implemented in a system named **ElasticMM**. ElasticMM employs a hierarchical scheduling framework structured at two levels: the modality level and the stage level. This framework is designed to provide both decoupling and elasticity, aiming to eliminate resource contention and maximize utilization.

#### 3.1. Elastic Multimodal Parallelism (EMP) Framework

The EMP framework organizes instances at two levels:
- **Modality Level**: Instances are grouped based on the type of requests they serve (text-only or multimodal).
- **Stage Level**: Within each modality group, the inference pipeline is further disaggregated into distinct stages: image encoding, LLM prefill, and LLM decode.

#### 3.2. Modality-Aware Load Balancing

To manage load imbalance between different request modalities, ElasticMM combines proactive and reactive mechanisms:
- **Proactive Mechanism**: Based on the predictability of long-term workload patterns (e.g., daily cycles), idle elastic instances are proactively assigned to modality groups. The objective is to maximize the minimum burst tolerance across modality groups, defined as the ratio between peak-available and average-required instances. A greedy strategy is employed, incrementally assigning instances to the group with the lowest burst tolerance until resources are fully allocated.
- **Reactive Scaling**: This mechanism addresses sudden, unpredictable traffic bursts. When resource shortages are detected, the system evaluates the trade-off between adjusting parallelism within the current group and triggering inter-group reactive scaling. If inter-group scaling is more beneficial, the modality-level manager preempts instances from other groups with minimal impact, and their existing workload is merged into other instances at the same inference stage.

#### 3.3. Elastic Partition Scheduling

After inter-group GPU allocation, this stage-level manager handles intra-group request scheduling and parallelism adjustment, adapting to the characteristics of each inference stage. The process is decomposed into three subproblems:
- **Request Dispatching**: A subset of pending requests is selected from the queue (First-Come-First-Served policy, with a priority for text-only dialogues redirected to a multimodal group). This process considers both GPU memory availability (KV slots) and compute throughput to optimize batch formation.
- **Stage Allocation**: For the prefill stage, the system prioritizes allocating idle instances to maximize GPU efficiency. If idle instances are insufficient, prefill requests are permitted to preempt instances from the decoding stage. A gain-cost model is used to quantify the benefits of preemption (acceleration for prefill) against the costs (KV cache migration overhead and performance impact on the preempted decode stage). The system primarily uses Data Parallelism (DP) for within-stage scaling, reserving Tensor Parallelism only for cases where model weights exceed single-GPU capacity. This choice simplifies KV cache migration during elastic scaling as it avoids expensive weight transfers.
- **Elastic Auto-Scaling**: The decode phase is monitored to trigger elastic adjustments. Recognizing the poor scalability of the decode stage, it is typically scaled down to minimum parallelism to free resources. If GPU resources become insufficient during decoding, reactive scaling (up) is triggered. It first attempts to allocate idle instances from the same group. If that is not possible, it evaluates preempting instances from either intra-group prefill stages or inter-group instances using a gain-cost model that considers potential speedup versus migration overhead. The instance offering the highest net gain is selected.

#### 3.4. Multimodal Inference Optimizations

ElasticMM incorporates two specific optimizations to mitigate MLLM-specific overheads:
- **Unified Multimodal Prefix Cache**: This strategy addresses redundant computation and data transfer. It creates a unified caching scheme by integrating text prefix caching with multimodal input caching. Cached objects are categorized into two pools: one for tokens encoded from multimodal inputs (using hashing to skip re-encoding) and another for prefix tokens from unified sequences (multimodal and text tokens), managed via a prefix tree for KV cache reuse, thereby bypassing the prefill step. Both pools employ a Least Recently Used (LRU) dynamic eviction strategy.
- **Non-blocking Encoding**: To minimize the blocking effect of vision encoder latency (which can be substantial), image preprocessing and encoding are decoupled from the language model. This process is isolated into a separate, asynchronously executed process or instance, allowing subsequent prefill and decode stages to proceed without waiting for encoding completion, thereby reducing TTFT.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ElasticMM was conducted using two representative MLLMs and two real-world datasets, compared against state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Models**: LLaMA3.2-Vision-11B (encoder-decoder) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (decoder-only).
- **Datasets**: VisualWebInstruct (characterized by longer average text inputs) and ShareGPT-4o (containing higher-resolution images). Workloads were simulated using a Poisson distribution for request arrival rates and real-world production service traces.
- **Testbed**: A workstation equipped with eight NVIDIA A800 80GB GPUs, two 64-core Intel Xeon 8358P CPUs, and 2 TB DDR4 memory, with NVLink providing 400 GB/s bandwidth.
- **Baselines**:
 - **vLLM (v0.6.6)**: A state-of-the-art LLM serving system, extended to support MLLM serving with a coupled architecture where all inference stages run on the same hardware.
 - **DistServe**: A decoupled architecture that separates encode/prefill from decode stages but relies on static resource allocation, extended for multimodal inference.
- **Metrics**: Normalized input latency (average prefill time divided by input length, representing TTFT), normalized output latency (average decoding time divided by output length), and maximum throughput achieved under varying Service-Level Objectives (SLOs). SLOs were set to 10 times the latency under light load, scaled by factors of one to five.

#### 4.2. End-to-End Performance

- **Input Latency (Time-to-First-Token, TTFT)**: ElasticMM consistently demonstrated the lowest input latency across all tested request rates and workloads. On the ShareGPT-4o dataset, ElasticMM reduced TTFT by up to 4.2x for Qwen2.5-VL (decoder-only) and 3.5x for LLaMA3.2-Vision (encoder-decoder) compared to baselines. On the VisualWebInstruct dataset, TTFT was reduced by up to 3.7x and 2.9x, respectively. The more significant improvements observed in decoder-only models and on the visually intensive ShareGPT-4o dataset indicate the effectiveness of the decoupled design and multimodal optimizations in scenarios with heavier prefill computation and more visual data.
- **Output Latency**: ElasticMM achieved consistently lower output latency. This is attributed to the decoupling of computation stages and elastic resource allocation, which effectively isolates the decoding phase from interference by the encoding or prefill stages. In contrast, vLLM experienced severe resource contention and increased output latency at higher request rates. DistServe, while showing some improvement over vLLM by separating prefill and decode, still suffered from memory contention on decode nodes under high loads due to its static resource allocation, leading to increased output latency.
- **Throughput**: ElasticMM achieved the highest throughput across all defined SLO settings. Specifically, on the ShareGPT-4o dataset, ElasticMM delivered up to 4.5x higher throughput than vLLM for Qwen2.5-VL and 3.2x higher for LLaMA3.2-Vision. Furthermore, ElasticMM achieved up to 2.3x higher throughput than DistServe, indicating the benefits of its hierarchical elastic scheduling strategy and non-blocking encoding design.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Effectiveness of Elastic Multimodal Parallelism (EMP)**: An ablation study comparing ElasticMM with EMP enabled against variants using static resource allocation policies (text-dominant, equal, multimodal-dominant) demonstrated that static schemes led to suboptimal performance. ElasticMM, by dynamically adjusting inter-group resource allocation via load balancing and applying elastic partitioned scheduling, achieved substantial throughput improvements of 1.8x for Qwen-2.5-VL and 2.3x for Llama-3.2-Vision, particularly under heavy loads from the ShareGPT-4o dataset.
- **Effectiveness of MLLM Inference Optimizations**: Evaluating the individual contributions of the Unified Multimodal Prefix Cache and Non-blocking Encoding, the study found that EMP alone provided limited improvements in TTFT. The Unified Multimodal Prefix Cache significantly reduced redundant computation and data transfer in both vision and language models, thereby lowering latency. Non-blocking Encoding further reduced latency by eliminating the blocking effect between the image encoding and subsequent inference stages. Both optimizations contributed to consistent performance gains in normalized token input latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper introduces a serving paradigm and system for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that addresses several limitations of existing approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved MLLM Serving Efficiency**: ElasticMM significantly reduces Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) and increases overall throughput for MLLM inference. This improvement can lead to faster response times for end-users interacting with MLLMs, enhancing the perceived quality and responsiveness of AI services.
- **Optimized Resource Utilization**: By dynamically adapting resource allocation across different modality groups and elastically adjusting parallelism for distinct inference stages, ElasticMM can utilize GPU resources more efficiently. This optimization can translate into lower operational costs for MLLM providers and better scalability under varying workloads.
- **Robustness to Heterogeneous Workloads**: The system’s ability to distinguish and manage text-only and multimodal requests independently, coupled with its adaptive scaling mechanisms, makes it more robust to the heterogeneous and bursty traffic patterns typical of real-world MLLM deployments. This adaptability helps maintain service-level objectives (SLOs) even under unpredictable load spikes.
- **New Serving Paradigm**: The introduction of Elastic Multimodal Parallelism (EMP) provides a systematic framework for rethinking MLLM serving. By emphasizing explicit decoupling at both the modality and stage levels, and integrating elasticity, it offers a principled approach to managing the complexities of multimodal inference.
- **Compatibility and Generality**: Unlike some model-level optimizations that might trade off accuracy for speed or be constrained by specific model architectures, ElasticMM's approach is designed to be compatible with various MLLM architectures (decoder-only and encoder-decoder) without compromising model accuracy, as demonstrated by the theoretical proofs of inference equivalence.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The principles and techniques demonstrated in ElasticMM, particularly hierarchical elastic scheduling and multimodal-specific optimizations, lay a foundation for further advancements in large-scale, distributed MLLM serving systems. The acknowledged future work on multi-node clusters indicates the potential for extending these concepts to more complex and larger deployments.

In summary, ElasticMM contributes to the development of more scalable, efficient, and cost-effective infrastructure for deploying MLLMs, thereby supporting the broader adoption of advanced multimodal AI capabilities in diverse applications.
