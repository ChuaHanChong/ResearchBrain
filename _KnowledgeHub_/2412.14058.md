# What Matters in Building Vision-Language-Action Models for Generalist Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A detailed empirical study by Tsinghua University, ByteDance Research, and collaborators systematically investigated design principles for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in generalist robotics. The research identified optimal VLM backbones, architectural structures, and data strategies, achieving state-of-the-art robot manipulation performance on benchmarks and in real-world settings with emergent self-correction.

## Problem

- Developing generalizable robot policies capable of perceiving, reasoning, and interacting with diverse physical environments remains a grand challenge.
- The effective transfer of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into high-performing robot policies for VLA models is not straightforward, lacking systematic understanding of optimal design choices.
- Fundamental questions persist regarding suitable VLM backbones, architectural formulations for action components, and optimal strategies for incorporating diverse robotic datasets.

## Method

- The researchers developed **RoboVLMs**, an open-source, flexible framework allowing easy integration of various ==VLM backbones== and configurations of ==VLA structures==.
- An extensive empirical investigation was conducted, comprising over **600 experiments** across **8 VLM backbones**, diverse VLA architectures (e.g., ==one-step==, ==history modeling==, ==continuous/discrete actions==), and data utilization strategies.
- The proposed methods were trained and evaluated on simulation benchmarks (CALVIN, SimplerEnv) and a custom real-world dataset from a Kinova robot to assess performance and generalization.

## Results

- The best **RoboVLM** achieved new state-of-the-art performance on CALVIN, showing absolute gains of **12.6%** for single tasks and **30.3%** for 5 consecutive tasks on the unseen split D.
- RoboVLMs demonstrated robust real-world effectiveness, generalizing across unseen distractors, backgrounds, target objects, and novel skill descriptions, while exhibiting emergent self-correction abilities.
- Specific VLM backbones (KosMos, Paligemma) with extensive vision-language pre-training proved most suitable, and continuous action spaces consistently outperformed discrete actions for robot manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-language pre-training is essential for achieving strong generalization and high data efficiency in VLA models for robot manipulation tasks.
> - Continuous action spaces and the incorporation of historical observations via a 'Policy Head' structure significantly enhance VLA performance, particularly with robustly pre-trained VLM backbones like KosMos and Paligemma.
> - Optimal utilization of large-scale cross-embodiment data is achieved through post-training or by enhancing few-shot learning capabilities, rather than solely through direct co-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalizable robot policies capable of perceiving, reasoning, and interacting with diverse physical environments remains a grand challenge in robotics. Historically, approaches have fallen into categories such as Model-Free Learning, Model-Based Learning, and World Model Based Learning, each with its own strengths and limitations. Model-Free methods, for instance, excel in generalization across embodiments but often require extensive data, while Model-Based methods are precise but task-specific.

In recent years, the explosive growth of large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has paved the way for a new paradigm: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLAs are a specialized branch of model-free learning that directly build upon and leverage pre-trained VLMs as state encoders. The core hypothesis is that VLAs can inherit the powerful generalized and robust representations learned by VLMs from web-scale multimodal data, thereby facilitating adaptation to diverse, open-world robotic tasks with limited domain-specific data. This promise has led to a surge of interest and several promising early-stage VLA models, such as RT-1, RT-2, Octo, and OpenVLA.

However, despite early successes, the effective transfer of pre-trained VLMs into high-performing robot policies is far from straightforward. The field currently lacks a comprehensive understanding of the optimal design choices for VLAs. Critical questions surrounding the selection of appropriate VLM backbones, the most effective architectural formulations for integrating action components, and the optimal strategies for incorporating diverse robotic datasets remain largely underexplored through systematic studies.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap by performing an extensive empirical investigation into these fundamental design choices. It positions itself not just as another VLA model contribution, but as a foundational "guidebook" for the community, aiming to systematically dissect and provide answers to *what truly matters* in building these generalist robot policies. By conducting over 600 distinct experiments across various VLM backbones, policy architectures, and data utilization strategies, the authors seek to establish best practices and illuminate the underlying mechanisms that drive VLA performance. In doing so, this work aims to solidify the theoretical and practical foundations of VLA research, accelerating the development of truly generalist and robust robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to rigorously investigate and disclose the key factors that significantly influence the performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on robot manipulation problems. The authors are motivated by the non-trivial challenge of effectively transferring the immense power of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into robust and generalizable robot policies.

To achieve this overarching goal, the paper focuses on answering three essential, yet underexplored, design choices for VLAs:

1. **Which kind of VLM backbones are suitable for VLAs?** The motivation here stems from the wide variety of modern VLMs, differing in architecture, model capacity, visual encoder structure, fusion mechanisms, and data scale. While these VLMs are powerful for general vision-language tasks, their effectiveness in robot policy learning depends on their ability to encode physical properties, spatial relationships, and task-relevant semantics crucial for manipulation. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive study of how backbone choices impact downstream robot manipulation performance.

2. **How should VLAs be formulated to best leverage VLMs?** Beyond backbone selection, the structural formulation of VLAs is complex and varied. This question explores how observations, states, and actions should be modeled. The motivation is to understand the impact of different approaches to incorporating historical information (one-step vs. history modeling, interleaved vs. policy-head aggregation) and the choice of action space (continuous vs. discrete) on robustness, generalization ability, and data efficiency.

3. **When should we leverage the public diverse robot data for optimal training?** The increasing availability of large-scale cross-embodiment robot datasets (like Open X-Embodiment) is widely believed to improve VLA performance. However, the optimal strategy for integrating such data—whether through pre-training, co-training, or post-training—remains ambiguous. This objective aims to provide empirical clarity on when and how to effectively utilize these diverse data sources to achieve robust and generalizable VLAs.

Ultimately, the motivation for addressing these questions is to develop a "detailed guidebook for the future design of VLAs." To facilitate this systematic study and ensure fair comparisons, the authors propose and open-source a highly flexible framework called RoboVLMs. This framework supports easy integration of new VLMs and free combinations of various design choices, serving as both an experimental testbed and a valuable community resource. The project's dedication to open-sourcing all details, including code, models, datasets, and toolkits, further underscores its commitment to advancing the entire field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors undertake an extensive and systematic empirical study to address their core research questions, employing a rigorous methodology built around their proposed **RoboVLMs** framework. RoboVLMs is designed as a general and flexible architecture that allows for easy integration of various Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as backbones and flexible configuration of different Vision-Language-Action (VLA) structures.

**1. VLA Formulation within RoboVLMs:**
The framework models robot control as predicting action sequences based on language instructions ($l$) and historical observations ($o_{t-H+1:t}$). Observations typically include proprioceptive states (e.g., joint angles) and visual inputs (RGB images from multiple cameras). Actions are represented as a 7-dimensional vector (6-DoF gripper pose + open/close status).

- **Action Pre-processing:** Continuous actions are normalized to $[-1, 1]$ using 1st and 99th quantiles from training data. For discrete actions, each dimension is discretized into 256 bins, offset to avoid special tokenizer tokens.
- **Action Prediction:**
 - **Continuous Actions:** Optimized using a combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) for pose and Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) for gripper status.
 - **Discrete Actions:** Optimized using Cross-Entropy (CE) for predicting action tokens.
- **VLA Structures (categorized by action space and history aggregation):**
 - **One-step Models:** Predict actions based only on the current observation.
 - *One-step Continuous-Action:* VLM predicts a learnable token ([LRN]), which an MLP then maps to action vectors.
 - *One-step Discrete-Action:* VLM directly predicts discrete action tokens, similar to next-word prediction.
 - **History Models:** Incorporate a sliding window of historical observations.
 - *Interleaved-Continuous-Action:* Historical observation-action sequences are interleaved as input tokens to a decoder-only VLM. An MLP then predicts actions from the latest learnable action token.
 - *Policy-Head-Continuous-Action:* VLM provides single-step multimodal representations. An *additional policy head* (e.g., RNN, Transformer, Diffusion model) then processes a sequence of these [LRN] tokens to predict the action sequence. This is the most flexible approach, supporting both encoder-decoder and decoder-only VLMs.

**2. VLM Backbones Investigated:**
To assess the impact of VLM backbones, the study evaluates 8 diverse, off-the-shelf, pre-trained VLMs:
- **Encoder-Decoder:** Flamingo family (3B, 4B, 9B parameters).
- **Decoder-Only:** LLaVA, Qwen-VL, MoonDream, UForm, Paligemma, KosMos.
These models vary in architecture, training data scale, model size, and latent embeddings.

**3. Experimental Settings and Data:**
- **Simulation Benchmarks:**
 - **CALVIN:** A multitask table-top manipulation benchmark (24K human teleoperated demonstrations, 34 basic tasks). Evaluated on 1-5 consecutive tasks, success rates, and average tasks completed (Avg. Len.). Generalization tested by training on splits A, B, C and evaluating on unseen split D.
 - **SimplerEnv:** A real-to-sim benchmark suite (WidowX+Bridge and Google Robot environments) for evaluating success rates on various manipulation tasks.
- **Real-World Benchmark:** A custom dataset of 100 manipulation tasks (74K trajectories) collected on a 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 robot arm with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, equipped with side and wrist cameras. Evaluated across 20 tasks, 5 settings per task (Basic, Novel Skill Description, Unseen Distractor, Unseen Background, Unseen Target Object), and 3 rollouts per setting.
- **Data Scale and Cross-Embodiment Data:** Experiments are conducted with varying data scales (0.1x, 1x, 5x CALVIN data) and strategies for incorporating Open X-Embodiment (OXE) large-scale cross-embodiment data:
 - **Co-train:** Training with in-domain and cross-embodiment data simultaneously.
 - **Post-train:** Co-training first, then fine-tuning exclusively on in-domain data.
 - **Finetune:** Training only with in-domain data (no cross-embodiment data).

**4. Training Objectives and Inference Strategies:**
- **Training Objectives:**
 - MSE+BCE: Standard for continuous actions.
 - Flow Matching: A diffusion-based objective for generative modeling of action trajectories.
- **Inference-Time Action Aggregation:**
 - Chunk: Execute the entire predicted action chunk before next inference.
 - First: Execute only the first action in the chunk.
 - Ensemble: Aggregate predictions at the same time step from different action chunks.

**5. Architectural Augmentations:**
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Structure:** Evaluated for its impact on generalization, where vision-language tokens and action tokens use separate feed-forward networks within VLM layers, interacting only via self-attention.

**6. Implementation Details:**
Hyperparameters are fine-tuned from VLM training setups, with grid search for optimal batch size, learning rate, and weight decay. Models are trained for fixed epochs/iterations (e.g., 5 epochs for CALVIN, 100K iterations for SimplerEnv) for fair comparison, mitigating the challenge of offline metrics not fully correlating with long-horizon rollout performance.

This comprehensive and structured approach allows the authors to dissect the complex interplay of VLM characteristics, VLA architectural choices, and data utilization strategies, providing robust empirical evidence for their findings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental study yields several crucial findings that provide a "guidebook" for building high-performance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

**1. Why VLAs are preferred for Generalist Robot Policies:**
- **Superior Performance:** The best-performing VLA (RoboVLM based on KosMos with a Policy Head) consistently sets new state-of-the-art performance across both simulation benchmarks (CALVIN and SimplerEnv) and real-world experiments. On CALVIN, it achieves significant improvements (e.g., 12.6% absolute gain for single task, 30.3% for 5 consecutive tasks in generalization to unseen split D) over existing generalist policies like GR-1.
- **Generalization and Data Efficiency:** Vision-language pre-training is found to be essential for both strong generalization to unseen scenarios and higher data efficiency, making VLAs highly desirable for real-world applications with limited robotic data.
- **Real-World Effectiveness:** The best RoboVLM demonstrates strong effectiveness and robustness in real-world scenarios, generalizing across unseen distractors, backgrounds, target objects, and novel skill descriptions. A notable qualitative finding is the emergence of self-correction abilities, even without such data in training.

**2. Which VLM Backbones are Most Suitable for VLAs:**
- **Impact of Pre-training:** A comparative study of 8 diverse VLM backbones reveals that KosMos and Paligemma significantly outperform others. This superior performance is directly attributed to their *sufficient and comprehensive vision-language pre-training on large vision-language datasets*. This outcome is intuitive, as extensive pretraining fosters stronger alignment between visual and linguistic features, which is critical for language-conditioned manipulation tasks.

**3. How VLAs Should Be Formulated to Best Leverage VLMs:**
- **Continuous Actions are Superior:** Continuous action spaces consistently outperform auto-regressive discrete actions, especially as task horizons increase. This is due to continuous actions' ability to represent high-precision values, mitigating compounding errors.
- **History Observation is Crucial:** Incorporating historical context (multi-step historical observations) as input is crucial for enhancing VLA performance and addressing partial observability, regardless of the fusion strategy.
- **Policy Head Improves History Fusion:** Among history-incorporating formulations, the "Policy Head" structure (where the VLM provides single-step representations, and a separate policy head processes history) demonstrates superior performance compared to "Interleaved" modeling. This is hypothesized to be because the policy head preserves the VLM's original vision-language fusion capabilities while effectively integrating historical information efficiently.
- **Larger VLMs Enhance Efficiency:** Larger VLM backbones (e.g., 9B Flamingo vs. 3B) generally lead to higher data efficiency, requiring less training data to achieve better performance.
- **Training Objectives:** Diffusion-based objectives (Flow Matching) do not offer significant performance improvements over simpler MSE+BCE losses for short-horizon tasks, suggesting that their added inference-time complexity might yield limited benefits in such contexts.
- **Inference Strategies:** For one-step-continuous policies, executing *full action chunks* consistently yields the best performance, particularly in novel scenarios, as it preserves temporal coherence. Relying solely on the first action in a chunk ("First" paradigm) significantly underperforms.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):** While MoE structures (e.g., as in π0) consistently improve generalization in *visually zero-shot settings* by preserving pre-trained VLM capabilities, they do not necessarily boost performance in *seen scenarios*, where direct fine-tuning allows the model to specialize more effectively.

**4. When to Leverage Public Diverse Robot Data for Optimal Training:**
- **In-domain Data is Critical:** Even task-agnostic in-domain data (from the same robot/embodiment) proves more effective than large-scale cross-embodiment data for improving model performance on specific tasks.
- **Co-training Alone is Insufficient:** Directly co-training with large-scale cross-embodiment data (like OXE) without subsequent fine-tuning does not consistently lead to significant performance improvements.
- **Post-training is Beneficial:** Post-training a cross-embodiment pre-trained model on the target dataset can lead to notable performance gains, particularly for "high-frequency" tasks (e.g., pick-and-place) that are abundant in large datasets. This suggests that cross-embodiment pre-training helps VLAs learn transferable skills for common manipulation patterns.
- **Few-shot Learning:** Cross-embodiment pre-training significantly improves few-shot learning performance on out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., CALVIN tasks not part of OXE), by learning more effective, generalizable representations.

In summary, the research establishes VLAs as a strong paradigm for generalist robotics and provides concrete empirical guidance on how to optimize their design across backbone selection, architectural formulation, and data utilization strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant importance and potential impact across several dimensions within the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence.

**1. A Definitive "Guidebook" for VLA Design:**
The most immediate and impactful contribution is the provision of a comprehensive, empirically-backed "guidebook" for designing high-performance VLA models. By systematically investigating and answering fundamental questions about VLM backbones, VLA architectures, and data strategies, the authors resolve ambiguities that have hampered progress in the nascent VLA field. This clarity will significantly accelerate future research and development, allowing researchers and practitioners to make informed design choices rather than relying on intuition or trial-and-error.

**2. Advancement of Generalist Robot Capabilities:**
The work contributes directly to the long-standing challenge of building generalizable robot policies. By demonstrating that carefully constructed VLAs achieve state-of-the-art performance in both simulated and real-world complex manipulation tasks, the paper validates the VLA paradigm as a promising path towards truly generalist robots. The observed ability of RoboVLMs to generalize to unseen objects, backgrounds, distractors, and novel skill descriptions, coupled with emergent self-correction capabilities, indicates a tangible step towards robots that can operate robustly in diverse, unstructured environments.

**3. Open-Source Ecosystem and Reproducibility:**
The commitment to open-sourcing the RoboVLMs framework, including code, models, datasets, and detailed training/evaluation recipes, is a critical enabler for the wider research community. This fosters reproducibility, allows other researchers to build upon the established findings, and facilitates fair comparisons with new methods. This open science approach will undoubtedly spur innovation and collaboration, lowering the barrier to entry for researchers interested in robot foundation models.

**4. Optimized Resource Utilization:**
By identifying optimal choices for VLM backbones (e.g., KosMos, Paligemma) and architectural formulations (continuous actions, policy-head for history), the research helps optimize computational and data resources. Knowing which models and structures are most effective can prevent wasted effort on suboptimal designs, allowing researchers to focus resources on more promising avenues. The findings on data efficiency and the nuanced benefits of cross-embodiment data further guide data collection and training strategies, which are often the most expensive components of robot learning.

**5. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:**
The successful deployment and evaluation of RoboVLMs in real-world scenarios, complementing extensive simulation studies, addresses the notorious sim-to-real gap. The strong real-world performance under various unseen conditions strengthens confidence in the practical applicability of VLA models and the robustness of the identified design principles.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
Beyond its immediate findings, the paper also clearly articulates current limitations (e.g., action tokenization techniques, breadth of VLM backbones, real-time deployment challenges) and outlines crucial future research directions (e.g., long-horizon tasks, complex reasoning, symbolic planning integration). This provides a clear roadmap for subsequent investigations, ensuring sustained progress in the field.

In essence, "What Matters in Building Vision-Language-Action Models for Generalist Robots" is a seminal work that not only pushes the state-of-the-art in robot learning but also provides a foundational empirical framework and critical insights that will guide and accelerate the development of more capable, general-purpose robots for years to come.
