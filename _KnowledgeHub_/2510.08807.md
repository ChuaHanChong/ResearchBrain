# Humanoid Everyday: A Comprehensive Robotic Dataset for Open-World Humanoid Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Humanoid Everyday introduces a large-scale, multimodal dataset of over 10.3k trajectories across 260 diverse tasks for real-world humanoid manipulation, collected using a high-efficiency teleoperation pipeline. It also presents a cloud-based platform for standardized policy evaluation, revealing that even leading imitation learning models like GR00T N1.5 struggle significantly with high-dimensional humanoid control and complex tasks such as loco-manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing robot learning datasets primarily focus on stationary robotic arms, lacking the diversity, full-body capabilities, and multimodal data required for humanoid robots in open-world scenarios.
- Current humanoid datasets have limited task variety, often neglecting full-body locomotion and human-humanoid interaction, which are crucial for real-world humanoid operation.
- There is a lack of standardized evaluation platforms for humanoid manipulation, making it difficult to rigorously compare and assess learning-based policies due to high-dimensional action spaces and complex balance requirements.

## Method

- Developed "Humanoid Everyday," a large-scale, multimodal dataset comprising 10.3k trajectories across 260 diverse tasks, featuring full-body locomotion, dexterous hands, and rich sensory data collected in varied indoor and outdoor environments.
- Designed an efficient, multi-processing teleoperation pipeline using an Apple Vision Pro headset for intuitive human control, significantly reducing data collection time and control latency from 500ms to 2ms.
- Introduced a novel cloud-based evaluation platform that enables remote deployment and testing of humanoid manipulation policies on physical robots, providing standardized and reproducible benchmarking.

## Results

- The Humanoid Everyday dataset comprises 10.3k trajectories and over 3 million frames across 260 unique tasks, collected with a re-engineered pipeline that reduced control delay from 500ms to 2ms and halved data collection time.
- Evaluation of representative imitation learning policies, including the best-performing GR00T N1.5, revealed that all models struggled significantly, achieving 0% success rates on challenging tasks like "Insert rose into vase" due to insufficient fine-grained visuospatial perception and control.
- An ablation study demonstrated that pretraining VLA models on the Humanoid Everyday dataset consistently improved their performance on downstream tasks, validating its utility as a valuable prior for learning complex humanoid behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The high-dimensional action space of humanoid robots poses a fundamental challenge for current end-to-end imitation learning policies, leading to considerable struggle on complex manipulation and loco-manipulation tasks.
> - Pretraining on a diverse, large-scale humanoid dataset like "Humanoid Everyday" significantly improves the robustness and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for downstream manipulation tasks.
> - While 3D point cloud observations often offer better robustness to environmental variations than RGB, policies still struggle with dynamic changes during locomotion, indicating limitations in current visual processing for moving humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has witnessed notable advancements, particularly in the capabilities of humanoid robots to perform dynamic activities such as running, dancing, and complex full-body movements. Concurrently, data-driven approaches, especially through large-scale datasets, have become fundamental for training robot policies.

However, the existing research landscape exhibits several limitations concerning humanoid manipulation:
- **Dataset Scope:** The majority of current robot learning datasets and benchmarks primarily focus on stationary robot arms or mobile platforms equipped with simpler grippers and wheeled bases. While some humanoid-specific datasets have emerged, they are often confined to fixed environments or offer limited task diversity. Many emphasize repetitive tasks with restricted locomotion or primarily focus on upper-body manipulation, overlooking full-body actions, human-humanoid interaction, and lower-body locomotion capabilities. This scarcity of diverse, interactive humanoid manipulation data hinders the development of general-purpose humanoid agents capable of operating in varied real-world scenarios.
- **Evaluation Standards:** Beyond data scarcity, a lack of standardized evaluation practices for humanoid manipulation exists. Although deep learning methods have shown progress in robotic manipulation, a unified evaluation framework for systematically comparing these approaches in the context of complex humanoid tasks has been absent. Current cloud-based evaluation systems tend to focus on robotic arms or simulated agents, not extending to the unique challenges of real-world humanoid robots. This absence makes fair and rigorous comparisons difficult, impeding a clear understanding of what contributes to effective humanoid manipulation.
- **Learning Approaches:** Recent works have explored reinforcement learning and sim-to-real transfer for whole-body coordination, balance, and locomotion in humanoids,–. Imitation learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often relying on teleoperation data from VR applications, are used to train robots to replicate demonstrated behaviors,–. However, these approaches are inherently constrained by the diversity of available demonstrations, which typically concentrate on a narrow set of tasks or environments.

This work positions itself as an effort to address these identified gaps by providing a large-scale, diverse, and multimodal dataset for humanoid manipulation, along with a standardized evaluation platform. It seeks to bridge the limitations in data availability, embodiment representation, and systematic policy evaluation for the advancement of humanoid robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were threefold, aiming to address critical challenges in advancing humanoid robotics:

1. **To develop and release a comprehensive, large-scale, and diverse humanoid manipulation dataset.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing datasets for robot learning predominantly focus on stationary robotic arms or mobile platforms with limited manipulation capabilities. The few available humanoid datasets are often restricted to fixed environments or possess limited task diversity, frequently lacking full-body locomotion, complex dexterous manipulation, and human-humanoid interaction. This limitation prevents the training of general-purpose humanoid agents that can operate effectively in varied, open-world environments. The motivation was to create a resource that captures the full spectrum of human-like capabilities, including full-body movements and interactions, across diverse indoor and outdoor settings. This dataset was designed to incorporate multimodal sensory data and natural language annotations to support the development of more adaptable and context-aware humanoid policies.

2. **To conduct an analysis of representative policy learning methods on the newly developed dataset.**
 - **Motivation:** With the introduction of a more challenging and diverse dataset, a key motivation was to evaluate the current capabilities and limitations of existing learning-based policies, particularly imitation learning approaches, when applied to embodied humanoid manipulation. This analysis aimed to provide initial insights into which policy architectures perform effectively across different task categories and where significant challenges remain, thereby guiding future research directions for model design and training strategies.

3. **To introduce a standardized, cloud-based evaluation platform for humanoid manipulation policies.**
 - **Motivation:** The absence of a unified and reproducible evaluation framework for humanoid manipulation policies makes it challenging for researchers to compare different approaches fairly and rigorously. This lack of standardization hinders cumulative progress and effective benchmarking within the community. The motivation was to create a platform that would lower the barrier to entry for researchers without direct access to humanoid robot hardware, enable remote deployment and testing of policies in a controlled real-world environment, and foster collaborative and reproducible research in humanoid robotics.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the overarching goal of advancing research in general-purpose humanoid manipulation, laying the groundwork for the development of more robust, capable, and embodied robotic agents that can perform complex tasks in real-world, open-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research involved the creation of the Humanoid Everyday dataset, development of an efficient data collection pipeline, implementation of a cloud-based evaluation platform, and an experimental analysis of various imitation learning policies.

**A. Humanoid Everyday Dataset Creation**

1. **Hardware Setup:**
 - **Robots:** Data was collected using two Unitree humanoid robots: the G1 (29-DoF with 7-DoF three-fingered dexterous Dex3-1 hands, featuring tactile sensors) and the H1 (27-DoF with 6-DoF INSPIRE hands).
 - **Sensors:** Both robots were equipped with Intel RealSense RGB-D cameras and a Livox LiDAR system, providing multimodal perception capabilities.
2. **Teleoperation Interface:**
 - Operators used an Apple Vision Pro headset to capture wrist and finger keypoints.
 - Finger motions were mapped to the robot's dexterous hands using a dex-retargeting system.
 - Wrist poses were converted into arm joint commands via a Pinocchio-based inverse kinematics (IK) algorithm, enabling full upper-body teleoperation.
3. **Efficient and Scalable Data Collection Pipeline:**
 - The authors re-engineered the official Unitree teleoperation script to optimize for large-scale, high-quality data acquisition.
 - The pipeline employs multi-processing and asynchronous I/O operations to decouple I/O data, IK computation, and robot joint control into separate processes. Shared memory buffers facilitated low-latency inter-process communication.
 - Data recording and sensor processing were handled asynchronously in parallel threads within the main pipeline, ensuring non-blocking and temporally aligned data collection at 30Hz.
 - The system provided an intuitive interface, streamed robot binocular IR feeds to the VR headset for improved situational awareness, and supported multiple recording sessions without program restarts.
 - Performance metrics indicated a reduction in control delay from 500 ms to 2 ms and a halving of data collection time compared to the official Unitree system.
4. **Dataset Composition:**
 - The dataset comprises 10.3k trajectories and over 3 million frames of data, covering 260 unique tasks across 7 main categories: Basic Manipulation, Deformable Manipulation, Articulated Manipulation, Tool Use, High-Precision Manipulation, Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), and Loco-Manipulation.
 - Tasks were performed in diverse indoor and outdoor environments, involving complex interactions with objects. A portion of tasks incorporated lower-body locomotion.
 - Each episode recorded comprehensive multimodal data including RGB video, depth maps, LiDAR scans, tactile feedback (for G1), IMU information, joint poses, joint actions, and natural language task descriptions.

**B. Cloud-Based Evaluation Platform**

- **Architecture:** The platform enables remote policy deployment on physical Unitree G1 or H1 humanoid robots hosted locally.
- **Interaction Flow:** Researchers specify their policy server IP and port. The platform streams real-time visual inputs (RGB, depth) and robot state information to the client. Users then run local inference and send action commands back to the server for real-time execution on the physical robot.
- **Monitoring:** Both egocentric RGB and depth images from the robot, and third-person camera views, are streamed to a web interface for remote monitoring of task execution.
- **Standardization:** A subset of task environments from Humanoid Everyday was reconstructed to support realistic and standardized evaluations.
- **Efficiency:** The platform demonstrated continuous operation for over 100 minutes before battery depletion, requiring only three human interventions due to motor overheating, indicating high evaluation efficiency.

**C. Policy Learning Analysis**

- **Policies Evaluated:** Seven representative imitation learning policies were evaluated: Diffusion Policy (DP), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), OpenVLA, π₀-FAST, π₀.₅, and GR00T N1.5.
- **Training:** Policies were trained on the 30Hz humanoid data from Humanoid Everyday until convergence. For VLA-based policies, a two-stage finetuning strategy was employed: initial finetuning on the full Humanoid Everyday dataset, followed by adaptation to task-specific data from individual categories.
- **Experimental Design:** Experiments were conducted across all seven task categories of Humanoid Everyday, with 10 trials performed for each task under each of the seven policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the Humanoid Everyday dataset, the performance of imitation learning policies, and the functionality of the cloud-based evaluation platform.

**A. Characteristics of Humanoid Everyday Dataset**

- The Humanoid Everyday dataset provides a large-scale, diverse, and multimodal collection of humanoid manipulation tasks. It encompasses 10.3k trajectories and over 3 million frames across 260 tasks in 7 categories, including dexterous object manipulation, human-humanoid interaction, and locomotion-integrated actions, performed in varied indoor and outdoor environments.
- The data collection pipeline achieved low-latency teleoperation (2 ms control delay) and high-frequency data capture (30 Hz) across multimodal sensors, including RGB, depth, LiDAR, tactile, IMU, joint poses, joint actions, and natural language annotations. This efficiency was attributed to a multi-processing and asynchronous I/O design.

**B. Performance of Imitation Learning Policies**

- **General Performance:** All evaluated end-to-end imitation policies demonstrated struggles in humanoid manipulation tasks, which was primarily attributed to the high-dimensional action space of the humanoid robots (28 DoFs).
- **Diffusion-based Policies:**
 - **DP3 (3D Diffusion Policy)** generally outperformed **DP (Diffusion Policy)** in most scenarios. This was hypothesized to be due to DP3's use of 3D point cloud observations, which provided increased robustness to environmental variations.
 - However, in Loco-Manipulation tasks, where the robot's movement caused significant frame-to-frame changes in the point cloud, 3D-based inputs became less reliable, leading to lower success rates for DP3.
- **Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT):** ACT performed poorly overall. The analysis suggested that ACT tended to overfit to demonstrated trajectories and did not effectively integrate visual feedback, resulting in mechanical replay of actions without adequate adaptation to scene variations.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** VLA models demonstrated more consistent and stable performance, benefiting from pretraining priors that enhanced generalization, particularly in Deformable Manipulation and Loco-Manipulation tasks requiring both precision and robustness.
 - **OpenVLA** showed difficulties when trained on high-frequency (30 Hz) data without action space compression, often failing to produce meaningful motions. Downsampling actions to 2 Hz alleviated this issue but resulted in less smooth behaviors.
 - **π₀-FAST** employed a DCT-based tokenizer for action compression. However, for the high-dimensional humanoid actions, this approach led to an excessive number of tokens, incorrect output tokens, and decoding errors, occasionally causing the robot to halt during inference.
 - **π₀.₅** produced smoother humanoid actions compared to OpenVLA and π₀-FAST, but exhibited a tendency to overfit to specific trajectories, sometimes disregarding visual feedback.
 - **GR00T N1.5** achieved the strongest overall performance (an average success rate of 51%). This was largely attributed to its extensive pretraining across multiple large-scale humanoid datasets, which provided a powerful prior well-suited for the diverse manipulation tasks in Humanoid Everyday.
- **Challenging Task Categories:** All imitation learning policies struggled significantly with the most challenging categories, specifically Loco-Manipulation and High-Precision Manipulation tasks. For instance, in the "Insert rose into vase" task, nearly all policies achieved a 0% success rate. While many could lift the rose, they consistently failed to accurately insert its thin stem into the vase, indicating a current lack of fine-grained visuospatial perception in these models.

**C. Humanoid Everyday as a Pretraining Prior**

- An ablation study was conducted to assess the value of Humanoid Everyday as a pretraining prior for VLA models. Comparing a two-stage finetuning pipeline (pretraining on Humanoid Everyday then task-specific finetuning) against direct task-specific finetuning, the results indicated that pretraining on Humanoid Everyday consistently improved the performance of VLA models.
- This finding suggests that exposure to the diverse humanoid behaviors within Humanoid Everyday provides a valuable prior that facilitates learning downstream manipulation tasks, thereby enhancing robustness and stability in humanoid manipulation policies.

**D. Cloud-based Evaluation Platform**

- The implemented cloud-based evaluation platform for humanoid robots was demonstrated to be highly efficient and stable. It operated continuously for over 100 minutes on a real humanoid robot with only three human interventions due to motor overheating. This demonstrated its capacity for reliable, hardware-agnostic, and reproducible policy evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes contributions to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing several critical infrastructure and methodological gaps.

1. **Advancing Dataset Diversity and Scale:** The Humanoid Everyday dataset significantly expands the available resources for humanoid robot learning. By providing a large-scale, multimodal dataset covering a broad spectrum of full-body activities, diverse environments, and human-humanoid interactions, it offers a more comprehensive foundation than previous efforts. This diversity is expected to be instrumental in training more generalizable and robust manipulation policies that can adapt to complex, real-world scenarios, moving beyond specialized or confined tasks.

2. **Standardizing Evaluation and Lowering Barriers:** The introduction of a cloud-based evaluation platform specifically designed for humanoid robots is a notable development. This platform addresses the challenge of disparate evaluation methodologies and the high barrier to entry for researchers lacking direct access to expensive humanoid hardware. By enabling remote policy deployment and standardized testing on real robots, it promotes fair benchmarking, reproducibility, and collaborative progress within the global humanoid robotics community. This infrastructure is likely to accelerate research by allowing broader participation and systematic comparison of different learning approaches.

3. **Guiding Future Research in Policy Learning:** The detailed analysis of representative imitation learning policies provides valuable insights into the current strengths and limitations of these methods when applied to high-dimensional humanoid control. The identification of specific challenges, such as difficulties with high-precision tasks and the impact of 3D vision in dynamic locomotion, highlights areas requiring further research and specialized model designs. The finding that Humanoid Everyday serves as an effective pretraining prior for VLA models also underscores the importance of diverse, large-scale data for improving policy generalization and robustness.

4. **Laying Groundwork for Embodied AI:** By addressing the fundamental needs for diverse data and standardized evaluation, Humanoid Everyday contributes to laying a stronger foundation for the development of truly general-purpose and embodied robotic agents. The work moves towards equipping humanoids with the versatility and intelligence required to perform complex tasks in unstructured, open-world environments, bringing them closer to real-world deployment.

In summary, the Humanoid Everyday dataset, coupled with the policy analysis and evaluation platform, constitutes a valuable resource and framework that is expected to advance the research landscape for versatile and intelligent humanoid robotic agents.
