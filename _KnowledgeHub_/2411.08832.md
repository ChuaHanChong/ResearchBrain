# Offline Adaptation of Quadruped Locomotion using Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Oxford Robotics Institute developed a framework for quadruped locomotion using a stochastic differential equation (SDE)-based diffusion model combined with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG). This framework enables offline adaptation of behaviors from unlabelled datasets and multi-skill learning, demonstrating real-time deployment on robot onboard CPUs while achieving velocity tracking comparable to expert models.

## Problem

- Current learning-based quadruped locomotion methods struggle to effectively manage and smoothly interpolate between multiple distinct skills within a single policy, often relying on complex hierarchical architectures.
- Adapting trained locomotion policies to new or unforeseen behaviors, especially those not explicitly optimized for during initial training, typically requires extensive and costly retraining.
- Deploying advanced locomotion controllers on resource-constrained robot hardware often necessitates high computational resources, such as GPUs, limiting real-time performance on onboard CPUs.

## Method

- A ==score-based SDE diffusion model== is trained to represent a multi-modal distribution of diverse quadruped locomotion trajectories, allowing for faster and deterministic sampling using a ==probability flow ODE== with minimal denoising steps (3 steps).
- ==Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)== is integrated into the diffusion model, enabling ==post-training adaptation== of generated trajectories to optimize for desired, user-defined reward functions, even when the original training data lacks explicit labels for these objectives.
- An ==Offline Reinforcement Learning== paradigm retrospectively labels an unconditioned dataset with a ==velocity tracking reward== function, which is then transformed and normalized to serve as the conditioning variable for CFG, guiding the model towards higher-reward trajectories.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved velocity tracking rewards comparable to expert models (e.g., **0.84 ± 0.11** for 0.8 m/s target vx vs. expert's **0.87 ± 0.09**) using post-hoc reward guidance on unlabelled data.
- Stable interpolation and transitions between distinct "walking" and "crawling" skills were demonstrated on the ANYmal quadruped hardware, proving the model's ability to learn and adapt multiple behaviors.
- The policy was successfully deployed for real-time inference on the robot's onboard CPU using multi-threading, requiring only **3** denoising steps and eliminating the need for a dedicated GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SDE-based diffusion models effectively capture and allow for stable interpolation between multiple distinct locomotion skills within a single model, simplifying policy design compared to hierarchical approaches.
> - Classifier-Free Guidance can effectively adapt robot behavior to new, retrospectively defined reward functions by dynamically reshaping the generation process, with an optimal guidance strength (λ) identified to balance reward maximization and dynamical stability.
> - Efficient deployment on onboard robot CPUs is feasible through the SDE framework's deterministic sampling, which requires only a few (e.g., 3) denoising steps for real-time inference, and proper scaling of the return distribution is crucial for stable reward-guided generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of quadrupedal robot locomotion is progressing rapidly, with robots demonstrating increasing capabilities in traversing complex and varied terrains. The state-of-the-art largely leverages two primary paradigms: learning-based methods, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), and gradient-based optimal control techniques.

Reinforcement learning has been instrumental in producing highly dynamic locomotion policies for quadruped robots. However, existing RL approaches often encounter limitations when tasked with managing multiple distinct locomotion skills (e.g., walking, crawling, trotting) within a single policy. Many prior works have addressed this by employing hierarchical frameworks, where individual policies are trained for specific skills, and a higher-level policy coordinates transitions between them. This hierarchical structure can lead to complex multi-step training pipelines and restricts the robot's ability to smoothly interpolate between different skills or generate novel behaviors outside of the explicitly trained repertoire. Furthermore, policies trained online, which is typical for RL, generally lack the capacity for adaptation to new, unforeseen locomotion requirements without extensive retraining.

To mitigate these issues, imitation learning offers an alternative by training agents to replicate reference motions. Early successful methods in imitation learning often utilized adversarial training loops, such as Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP). While effective, these adversarial methods can suffer from training instability and scalability challenges.

More recently, diffusion models have emerged as powerful generative models capable of approximating highly multi-modal distributions with high fidelity. Originating from image and video generation, diffusion models have demonstrated superior performance over previous generative approaches by learning the score function of a distribution and iteratively applying Langevin dynamics to generate samples. Their capacity for multi-modality makes them well-suited for learning multiple skills within a single model, potentially circumventing the need for hierarchical planning. Additionally, diffusion models offer flexible conditioning mechanisms, allowing for generation guided by various inputs, including desired goals or reward functions. Their ability to generate variable-length trajectories in a single step also helps in producing long-horizon predictions that are less susceptible to compounding errors typical in sequential decision-making.

Within the context of control and robotics, diffusion models have been applied to tasks ranging from simple maze navigation to robot manipulation. However, their application to complex, real-world quadruped locomotion, particularly for offline adaptation and efficient deployment on constrained hardware, has been less explored. Prior work on diffusion for quadruped locomotion has primarily focused on multi-skill capabilities using the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) framework.

This research distinguishes itself by employing a stochastic differential equation (SDE)-based diffusion framework, which allows for faster inference through deterministic sampling (requiring fewer denoising steps), a crucial aspect for real-time control on onboard robot CPUs. Furthermore, it introduces the novel application of classifier-free guidance (CFG) in conjunction with an Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigm to enable post-training adaptation of locomotion behaviors based on user-defined reward functions, even from originally unlabelled datasets. This addresses the significant challenge of extracting specific goal-conditioned behaviors when the training data lacks explicit labels for these objectives. By proposing a solution that simultaneously tackles multi-skill learning and offline adaptation while remaining computationally efficient for onboard deployment, this work contributes to advancing the robustness, versatility, and practicality of quadruped locomotion controllers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to develop a quadrupedal locomotion framework that can overcome two specific limitations prevalent in current learning-based approaches:
1. **Learning and Interpolating Between Multiple Locomotion Skills (Modes):** Current methods, particularly those based on reinforcement learning, often require hierarchical architectures to manage discrete skills. This approach limits the robot's ability to smoothly transition or interpolate between different gaits or behaviors and often involves complex multi-step training procedures for each skill and their coordination. The objective is to enable a single policy to acquire and fluidly switch between diverse locomotion skills.
2. **Offline Adaptation to New Locomotion Behaviors After Initial Training:** Policies are typically trained for a specific set of behaviors or objectives. Adapting these policies to generate new or desired behaviors, especially those not explicitly optimized for during initial training, usually necessitates retraining from scratch. The goal is to develop a mechanism that allows for post-training modification of the policy's output to achieve new goal-conditioned behaviors without altering the underlying model or re-running extensive training.

The motivation behind addressing these limitations is rooted in enhancing the flexibility, efficiency, and real-world applicability of quadruped robots:

- **Improved Behavioral Flexibility:** By enabling a single model to learn and interpolate between multiple skills, the robot gains a more expansive and continuous repertoire of movements. This allows for more natural transitions between gaits, better adaptation to varying terrain conditions, and the potential to discover novel, hybrid behaviors that are not strictly discrete. This contrasts with systems that are limited to switching between a fixed set of pre-defined behaviors.
- **Reduced Training Overhead and Increased Robustness:** The ability to adapt a policy offline, without retraining, significantly reduces the computational burden and development time associated with deploying robots in new scenarios or refining their behavior. This is particularly relevant when faced with large, unlabelled datasets, where manually annotating for specific desired behaviors would be impractical. The proposed method aims to "stitch together" existing trajectories to satisfy new reward functions, offering a more efficient way to customize behavior.
- **Practical Deployment on Resource-Constrained Hardware:** A key practical motivation is to develop a system capable of running efficiently on a robot's onboard CPU, eliminating the need for dedicated Graphics Processing Units (GPUs) or external computational resources. This is crucial for expanding the range of platforms that can deploy such advanced locomotion controllers and for enabling real-time operation in diverse environments.
- **Leveraging Data Efficiently:** Large datasets of robot locomotion are increasingly common. However, these datasets are often unlabelled or lack specific annotations relevant to desired test-time behaviors. The motivation is to devise a method that can extract goal-conditioned behavior from such unlabelled datasets by inferring and optimizing for reward functions *after* data collection, thereby maximizing the utility of available data.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose a framework that leverages the multi-modal approximation capabilities of diffusion models for multi-skill learning and integrates classifier-free guidance (CFG) with an Offline RL paradigm for post-training behavioral adaptation. This combination seeks to provide a comprehensive solution that is both versatile and computationally viable for real-world quadrupedal systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology focuses on training a diffusion model to represent a diverse range of quadruped locomotion behaviors and then adapting its outputs using classifier-free guidance (CFG) to optimize for specific, test-time reward functions.

**A. Diffusion Formulation**
The core of the method utilizes a score-based diffusion model, drawing from the continuous generalization provided by Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs).
- **Score Function Learning:** The model learns the score function, defined as the gradient of the log-density function of the data distribution. This is achieved by minimizing a denoising score matching loss, where a neural network `s_θ(x̃)` predicts the score for noise-perturbed samples `x̃`.
- **SDE Generalization:** The discrete process of iterative denoising is cast into a continuous Ito SDE framework. This generalization offers several advantages: enhanced performance, decoupling of inference steps from training, and significantly faster sampling through the derivation of a probability flow ODE. The probability flow ODE allows for deterministic sampling without additive noise, requiring fewer inference steps to generate high-quality motions.
- **k-diffusion Framework:** The method employs the k-diffusion framework for its implementation, defining the ODE through a specific equation (Eq. 3) where `σ(t)` governs the variance schedule. Preconditioning (Eq. 4) is applied to stabilize training and improve prediction accuracy across different noise levels, allowing the network to predict either the score function directly or a residual term.
- **Training and Sampling:** The denoiser is trained by predicting the original, uncorrupted samples from noise-corrupted inputs, with noise levels drawn from a log-logistic distribution. For inference, the authors utilize 3 denoising steps, having determined that additional steps yielded marginal performance gains compared to increased inference time. Various samplers (DDPM, DDIM, DPM++(2M)) are evaluated at inference.

**B. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**
CFG is integrated to adapt the diffusion model's output to optimize for desired reward functions after training.
- **Bayes Rule Application:** CFG leverages Bayes' rule to combine an unconditional score (representing the general data distribution) with a conditional score (representing the distribution given a specific condition `y`). This is expressed by the equation `∇_x log p(x | y) = (1 - λ)∇_x log p(x) + λ∇_x log p(x | y)` (Eq. 6).
- **Guidance Strength (λ):** The parameter `λ` acts as a guidance strength. While `λ=1` corresponds to exact conditional sampling, values `λ > 1` can amplify the effect of the conditioning, potentially leading to higher-quality results by trading off dynamics consistency for stronger adherence to the conditioning variable.
- **Training for CFG:** During training, the conditioning variable (in this case, the expected return) is randomly masked with a fixed probability. This allows the single diffusion model to learn both the conditional and unconditional distributions simultaneously.
- **Test-Time Guidance:** At inference, the conditioning variable is set to a value corresponding to maximum desired return (e.g., 1) to guide the generation of trajectories that are predicted to maximize the reward function.

**C. Offline Adaptation After Dataset Collection**
The methodology addresses the challenge of adapting to specific goal-conditioned behaviors from datasets that lack explicit labels for these goals.
- **Offline RL Paradigm:** This problem is framed as an Offline Reinforcement Learning task. The dataset, originally collected without specific velocity commands, is retrospectively labelled with a velocity tracking reward function.
- **Reward Function Definition:** A specific reward function `r = e^(-3(v - v_target)^2) - 1` (Eq. 7) is defined to penalize deviations from a target velocity `v_target`.
- **Return Calculation and Transformation:** The discounted sum of these rewards over a future horizon (50 timesteps) constitutes the "return." This raw return `R_0` is then transformed using `R = e^(R_0/A)` (Eq. 8), where `A` is a temperature parameter. This transformation ensures the return is positive and allows for scaling, which is then linearly normalized to the range based on batch statistics. The scaled return acts as the conditioning variable `y` for CFG, guiding the model towards trajectories that yield higher returns.

**D. Diffusion for Locomotion Architecture**
The model architecture is designed for effective control of quadruped robots.
- **Inputs:** The model takes a history of `T_cond` observations, including the robot's orientation, twist (linear and angular velocities), joint positions, and joint velocities. Additionally, the diffusion timestep, a one-hot skill vector (e.g., for walk or crawl), and the transformed return value are provided as conditioning inputs.
- **Conditioning Embedding:** Each input component is embedded using separate linear layers and then concatenated to form a comprehensive conditioning input.
- **Transformer Decoder:** The main processing block is a multi-headed transformer decoder. It receives an embedded noise trajectory as input, applies causally-masked self-attention, and then cross-attention with the conditioning input.
- **Action Trajectory Prediction:** This process is repeated `N` times to progressively denoise and refine the trajectory. The final output is a partially denoised trajectory of future actions of length `T`. From this, the fully denoised action trajectory is computed.
- **Receding Horizon Control:** Only the first action from the predicted trajectory is applied to the robot, and the planning process is repeated in a receding horizon fashion. Predicting multiple actions helps enforce temporal consistency, and using state history is critical for accurate system identification, especially on hardware.

**E. Dataset Collection**
The training dataset is generated using a pre-trained reinforcement learning policy.
- **RL Policy Training:** An RL policy is trained with a reward function derived from prior work, configured with random velocity commands and randomized physical parameters to enhance robustness.
- **Multi-Skill Data:** Two distinct 25Hz policies are trained for "walking" and "crawling" skills, with differing desired base heights and nominal joint positions. An action delay of one timestep is incorporated to account for real-time inference latency on hardware.
- **Data Volume:** A total of 1 million episode steps are collected for each skill, providing a diverse set of locomotion behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy of diffusion models for quadruped locomotion, particularly in multi-skill learning and offline adaptation, supported by both simulation and hardware experiments.

**A. Offline Adaptation**
The study demonstrates that Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) enables offline adaptation to novel locomotion behaviors.
- **Velocity Tracking Performance:** Table I compares the velocity tracking reward of the proposed model against an "expert" model. The expert model had access to ground truth velocity commands during training, while the proposed model used CFG to maximize a *post-hoc* velocity tracking reward function (Eq. 7) from an unlabelled dataset.
 - The results indicate that the proposed method, using reward guidance, achieves velocity tracking performance comparable to the expert model across various target velocities (0.8, -0.8, 0.5, 1.0 m/s for vx, vy, ωz respectively). For instance, with a target vx of 0.8, the expert model achieved 0.87 ± 0.09, while the DDPM and DDIM samplers of the proposed method achieved 0.84 ± 0.11 and 0.84 ± 0.11, respectively.
 - The tight error bounds suggest stability in the generated behaviors.
- **Sampler Performance:** Among the SDE diffusion samplers tested, DDPM and DDIM performed comparably well, with slight variations across tasks. The DPM++(2M) sampler consistently yielded significantly lower performance. This indicates that while the SDE framework allows for flexible sampler choice, not all samplers are equally effective for this specific control task.
- **Conclusion:** These experiments validate that CFG can effectively guide quadruped behavior using reward functions specified *after* data collection, thereby addressing the challenge of extracting goal-conditioned behavior from unlabelled datasets.

**B. Reward Tracking with Skill Switching**
The research confirms the model's capability to learn and interpolate between discrete locomotion skills and demonstrates its compatibility with reward guidance.
- **Multi-Skill Interpolation:** The model successfully learned interpolations between distinct "walking" and "crawling" skills, despite these skills being collected from separate datasets without explicit transition data. The interpolations were stable across the full range of velocity commands.
- **Hardware Validation:** Snapshots from hardware deployment on the ANYmal quadruped (Figure 3, bottom row) visually illustrate stable transitions between skills, confirming the robustness of the approach in a physical environment.
- **Combined Capabilities:** A key finding is the seamless integration of skill switching with reward guidance. Figure 3 (top two rows) shows that a model trained with a forward velocity reward function maintains consistent velocity tracking performance (as indicated by the reward) even during transitions between walking and crawling skills. This suggests the approach generalizes to unseen transitions while simultaneously being guided by a reward function, indicating robustness.
- **Onboard CPU Deployment:** The model was successfully deployed for real-time inference on the robot's onboard CPU by utilizing multi-threading, negating the need for a dedicated GPU. This highlights the computational efficiency of the SDE-based diffusion framework with its 3-step inference.

**C. Effect of Lambda (Guidance Strength)**
An ablation study on the `λ` parameter in CFG (Figure 4) revealed its impact on performance and stability.
- **Reward vs. Stability Trade-off:** Increasing `λ` from 0 (unconditional sample) to 1 (regular conditioning) and beyond initially led to higher velocity tracking rewards. Optimal performance was observed at `λ` values between 1.5 and 2, indicating that a modest amount of reward guidance (beyond strict conditioning) can improve results.
- **Instability with High λ:** Beyond `λ=2`, the number of terminations increased rapidly, and tracking performance degraded. This supports the prediction that excessive guidance strength sacrifices dynamics consistency, leading to unstable motions.
- **Dynamic Optimal λ:** The optimal `λ` value during training started higher (around 10) and gradually decreased to 1.5-2 as the model improved, suggesting that less guidance is required as the model learns to generate high-quality trajectories.

**D. Return Distribution**
The study investigated the effect of the return distribution's shape on the model's performance (Table II).
- **Generalization of CFG:** When conditioned on a target return outside its training distribution (by omitting the scaling of returns to), the CFG mechanism still generalized and produced an increase in reward compared to unconditional samples. This demonstrates the model's ability to respond to desired rewards even beyond its immediate training range.
- **Impact of Scaling:** However, omitting the normalization resulted in a higher number of terminations, implying that larger deltas between desired and actual returns lead to more aggressive and potentially unstable guidance steps. Proper scaling of return distributions is therefore necessary to prevent instability.
- **Temperature (A) Effect:** Varying the temperature parameter `A` (from Eq. 8) for the exponential transformation of returns showed that a wider return distribution (empirically produced by `A=10`) yielded the best performance. Models with `A=1` or `A=100` performed significantly worse. This suggests that a broad return distribution is crucial for the diffusion model to effectively distinguish between and learn from trajectories with different levels of return.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for quadrupedal locomotion that advances the state of the art in several key areas, demonstrating both scientific novelty and practical applicability.

**A. Scientific Novelty and Contributions:**
- **First SDE-Based Diffusion with CFG for Quadruped Locomotion:** This work introduces the first application of a stochastic differential equation (SDE)-based diffusion framework to quadruped locomotion. This specific formulation is significant because it allows for faster inference through deterministic sampling, making it viable for real-time control on hardware with limited computational resources.
- **Offline Adaptation through Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** The paper is the first to apply classifier-free guidance to quadruped locomotion for offline adaptation. This allows for extracting goal-conditioned behaviors from an originally unlabelled dataset by optimizing for reward functions *after* the diffusion model has been trained. This addresses a critical limitation of traditional imitation learning where specific labels or explicit conditioning are often required.
- **Unified Multi-Skill and Adaptive Policy:** The framework successfully integrates the capabilities of learning multiple distinct locomotion skills (e.g., walk, crawl) within a single policy and adapting these skills via reward guidance. This avoids the complexities of hierarchical policy designs and offers a more flexible and continuous control space.

**B. Addressing Key Limitations in Robotics:**
- **Overcoming Multi-Skill Learning Challenges:** By leveraging the multi-modal nature of diffusion models, the research demonstrates a method for learning and interpolating between discrete skills without resorting to complex hierarchical structures, simplifying the training pipeline.
- **Enabling Post-Training Adaptation:** The ability to adapt policy behavior to new reward functions post-training, without retraining the entire model, significantly enhances the flexibility and utility of learned controllers. This is particularly impactful for real-world deployments where new requirements or environmental conditions may emerge.
- **Computational Efficiency for Hardware Deployment:** The demonstrated capability of running the policy efficiently on the robot's onboard CPU using multi-threading, with only 3 denoising steps, is a substantial practical advantage. This removes the dependency on high-power GPUs, making the approach accessible for a wider range of robotic platforms and applications with constrained compute.

**C. Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Versatility:** Quadruped robots employing this framework could exhibit greater autonomy by adapting their locomotion on the fly to diverse, unpredicted environmental conditions or mission objectives. The ability to generate novel behaviors from existing data can increase robustness and adaptability in complex scenarios like search and rescue, inspection, or logistics.
- **More Efficient Robot Development Cycles:** The offline adaptation capability can streamline the development process for new robot behaviors. Instead of extensive retraining, engineers could fine-tune robot movements by simply defining new reward functions and guiding the pre-trained diffusion model, leading to faster iteration and deployment.
- **Leveraging Unlabelled Data:** The method's effectiveness in extracting goal-conditioned behavior from unlabelled datasets has implications for utilizing vast amounts of unstructured demonstration data that might otherwise be underutilized, furthering the impact of large-scale data collection efforts in robotics.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a foundation for future research in several directions, including mechanisms to guarantee that adapted behaviors remain within the training distribution (to prevent unsafe or unstable actions) and expanding capabilities to more dynamic and complex skills using richer datasets like motion capture data.

While the method depends on high-quality, diverse training data and has limitations in extrapolating to entirely out-of-distribution rewards, its ability to dynamically adapt to a broad range of feasible behaviors without requiring labelled data marks a substantial step towards more versatile and intelligent quadruped locomotion systems.
