# Policy Agnostic RL: Offline RL and Online RL Fine-Tuning of Any Class and Backbone

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Policy Agnostic Reinforcement Learning (PA-RL), a novel framework that decouples the RL algorithm from the policy class, enabling effective training of diverse policy types including Gaussian, diffusion, and transformer policies. PA-RL achieved substantial performance gains in simulated D4RL benchmarks and demonstrated successful fine-tuning of expressive policies like Diffusion Policies and OpenVLA on real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning algorithms are tightly coupled with specific policy classes, like Gaussian policies, limiting their flexibility.
- Existing RL methods struggle to leverage more expressive policy representations such as diffusion models or transformers without significant algorithm modifications.
- There is a need for RL algorithms that can work with any policy class and backbone architecture to fully utilize diverse policy representations.

## Method

- **PA-RL** introduces a framework that decouples the reinforcement learning algorithm from the policy class.
- It optimizes actions directly by sampling, filtering by ==Q-values==, and applying ==gradient ascent==, then distills these optimized actions into the policy via ==supervised learning==.
- The approach integrates with existing ==actor-critic== RL methods (e.g., Cal-QL, IQL) by replacing their policy improvement steps with this action optimization and distillation procedure.

## Results

- PA-RL with diffusion policies achieved a normalized average score improvement of **79% to 100%** in D4RL Kitchen tasks, compared to **43%** for Gaussian Cal-QL.
- It achieved near-perfect performance in the challenging D4RL AntMaze-large-diverse-v2 environment, substantially outperforming traditional methods.
- PA-RL successfully fine-tuned diffusion policies on real robots, improving cup retrieval success rates from **50% to 95%**, and increased OpenVLA policy success rates from **40% to 70%** in vegetable manipulation tasks with minimal online fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy Agnostic Reinforcement Learning (PA-RL) decouples policy improvement from policy training, optimizing actions directly before distilling them into the policy.
> - A two-stage action optimization process combines global selection of high-Q-value actions with local gradient ascent to refine them.
> - The method uses supervised learning for policy updates, allowing it to support any policy class that can be trained with a supervised objective, including expressive models like diffusion policies and autoregressive transformers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms have traditionally been tightly coupled with specific policy classes, limiting their flexibility and applicability across different domains. Most RL methods are designed to work with specific policy parameterizations, particularly Gaussian policies, which restricts their ability to leverage more expressive policy representations like diffusion models or transformers.

This paper introduces Policy Agnostic Reinforcement Learning (PA-RL), a novel approach that decouples the reinforcement learning algorithm from the policy class. This decoupling allows PA-RL to effectively train a wide range of policy types—including Gaussian, diffusion, and autoregressive transformer policies—without requiring algorithm-specific modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

PA-RL addresses a fundamental challenge in the field: how to design RL algorithms that can work with any policy class and backbone architecture, enabling researchers and practitioners to leverage the strengths of various policy representations without being constrained by the limitations of specific RL algorithms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The Policy Agnostic RL Approach

The core idea behind PA-RL is to separate policy improvement from the training of the parametric policy. Instead of directly optimizing policy parameters using policy gradients, PA-RL optimizes actions directly and then trains the policy to mimic these optimized actions.

The PA-RL approach consists of three main components:

1. **Q-Function Training**: A standard critic (Q-function) is trained to evaluate state-action pairs.

2. **Action Optimization**:
 - **Global Optimization**: Multiple actions are sampled from a base policy, and the top actions with the highest Q-values are selected.
 - **Local Optimization**: Gradient ascent is performed on these selected actions to further optimize them with respect to the Q-function.

3. **Policy Distillation**: The policy is trained to mimic the optimized actions through supervised learning, using a loss function appropriate for the specific policy class (e.g., negative log-likelihood for autoregressive policies, diffusion loss for diffusion policies).

Mathematically, the action optimization process can be described as:

1. Sample actions $a_1, a_2..., a_k \sim \pi_\theta(a|s)$
2. Select top-m actions with highest $Q(s, a_i)$
3. Perform gradient ascent on these actions: $a_i^t = a_i^{t-1} + \alpha \nabla_a Q(s, a)|_{a=a_i^{t-1}}$

The policy is then updated to maximize the likelihood of these optimized actions:

For a Gaussian policy:
$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s, a^T}[- \log \pi_\theta(a^T|s)]$

For a diffusion policy:
$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s, a^T, t, \epsilon}[||\epsilon_\theta(s, a^T_t, t) - \epsilon||^2]$

For an autoregressive policy:
$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s, a^T}[-\sum_i \log \pi_\theta(a^T_i|s, a^T_{<i})]$

#### 3.2. Key Innovations and Method Details

PA-RL introduces several key innovations that enable its policy-agnostic nature:

1. **Decoupling Policy Improvement from Policy Training**: By optimizing actions directly and then training the policy to imitate these optimized actions, PA-RL avoids the need for specific policy gradients that are tied to particular policy parameterizations.

2. **Combination of Global and Local Optimization**: The two-stage action optimization process (global selection followed by local refinement) enables effective exploration of the action space while also fine-tuning the selected actions.

3. **Flexible Policy Training via Supervised Learning**: By using supervised learning to update the policy, PA-RL can accommodate any policy class that can be trained with a supervised learning objective, including more expressive models like diffusion policies and autoregressive transformer policies.

4. **Integration with Actor-Critic RL**: PA-RL can be integrated into existing actor-critic RL algorithms like Cal-QL and IQL by replacing the policy improvement step with the action optimization and supervised learning procedure.

The method can be effectively applied in both offline RL (learning from static datasets) and online fine-tuning (adapting a pre-trained policy through online interaction), making it versatile across different learning scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

PA-RL was evaluated across a diverse set of simulated environments and real-world robotic tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness with various policy classes.

In simulated environments from the D4RL benchmark:

1. **Kitchen Tasks**: PA-RL with diffusion policies significantly outperformed Gaussian policy baselines in kitchen manipulation tasks, showing a normalized average score improvement of 79% to 100% compared to 43% for Gaussian Cal-QL.

2. **AntMaze Navigation**: In the challenging antmaze-large-diverse-v2 environment, PA-RL achieved near-perfect performance, far surpassing traditional methods like SIL (Supervised Imitation Learning).

3. **Transformer Policies**: PA-RL enabled effective training of autoregressive transformer policies, demonstrating its flexibility across different policy architectures.

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of both components of action optimization.

#### 4.2. Applications to Real-World Robotics

One of the most compelling aspects of PA-RL is its successful application to real-world robotic manipulation tasks:

1. **Diffusion Policy Fine-Tuning**: PA-RL successfully fine-tuned diffusion policies on real robots for tasks like cup retrieval, improving from an initial 50% success rate to 95% after online fine-tuning.

2. **OpenVLA Fine-Tuning**: PA-RL significantly improved the performance of a 7B parameter language-conditioned policy (OpenVLA) on a vegetable manipulation task, increasing success rates from 40% to 70% with only 40 minutes of fine-tuning.

The real-world results demonstrate that PA-RL can effectively bridge the reality gap and adapt pre-trained policies to new environments and tasks with minimal real-world data, which is crucial for practical robotics applications.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Existing Approaches

PA-RL was compared with several state-of-the-art methods across both offline RL and online fine-tuning scenarios:

1. **Comparison with Offline RL Methods**: PA-RL outperformed established offline RL algorithms like DQL, IDQL, and Cal-QL in most environments, particularly when using diffusion policies.

2. **Comparison with Online Fine-Tuning Methods**: In online fine-tuning experiments, PA-RL demonstrated faster learning and higher asymptotic performance compared to methods like CEM (Cross-Entropy Method) combined with Cal-QL.

3. **Policy Class Comparisons**: The experiments also compared different policy classes (Gaussian, diffusion, and transformer) within the PA-RL framework, showing that diffusion policies generally achieved the best performance in complex tasks with multimodal action distributions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Several ablation studies were conducted to understand the contribution of different components of PA-RL:

1. **Local Optimization**: Varying the number of gradient steps for local optimization showed that more gradient steps generally led to better performance, confirming the value of the local optimization component.

2. **Global Optimization**: Experiments varying the number of samples for global optimization demonstrated that sufficient sampling is crucial for effective action selection, particularly in complex environments.

3. **Pre-trained Policy Initialization**: Using a pre-trained policy for action sampling initialization was shown to be beneficial compared to random initialization, especially in challenging tasks.

4. **Q-function Analysis**: Studies on the stability of Q-function training with different policy classes revealed that PA-RL maintains stable Q-values throughout training, contributing to its robust performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PA-RL has several important implications for the field of reinforcement learning:

1. **Enabling Expressive Policy Classes**: By decoupling policy improvement from policy parameterization, PA-RL enables the effective use of more expressive policy classes like diffusion models and transformers, which can capture complex action distributions and lead to improved performance in challenging tasks.

2. **Bridging Offline RL and Online Fine-Tuning**: PA-RL provides a unified framework for both offline RL and online fine-tuning, facilitating the transition from pre-trained policies to deployed systems that can adapt to new environments.

3. **Practical Robotics Applications**: The successful demonstration of PA-RL on real robots highlights its potential for practical applications in robotics, where adaptability and sample efficiency are crucial.

4. **Foundation Model Fine-Tuning**: PA-RL's success in fine-tuning large language-conditioned policies like OpenVLA suggests a promising avenue for adapting foundation models to specific robotic tasks without requiring extensive retraining.

Future research directions include extending PA-RL to handle partially observable environments, incorporating safety constraints into the action optimization process, and exploring hierarchical policy structures for long-horizon tasks.

The policy-agnostic nature of PA-RL represents a significant step toward more flexible and generalizable reinforcement learning algorithms that can leverage the strengths of various policy representations while maintaining strong performance across diverse tasks and environments.
