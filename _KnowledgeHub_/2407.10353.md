# UMI on Legs: Making Manipulation Policies Mobile with Manipulation-Centric Whole-body Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> UMI on Legs presents a framework for enabling dynamic mobile manipulation on quadrupedal robots by integrating robot-agnostic human demonstration data with a simulation-trained, manipulation-centric whole-body controller. The system successfully deploys pre-trained policies onto a dynamic quadruped in a zero-shot manner, achieving 70% success in tossing and 90% in pushing tasks while leveraging an accessible iPhone-based odometry system.

## Problem

- Scaling the acquisition of complex robot skills is bottlenecked by the high cost and time of real-world robot data collection, and the difficulty of accurately modeling diverse tasks in simulation.
- Existing mobile manipulation systems for quadrupedal robots often rely on quasi-static assumptions, require extensive teleoperation, or are limited by their dependence on robot-specific commands and on-robot data collection.
- Traditional whole-body controllers (WBCs) for legged robots often track body-frame targets or simplified velocity commands, which makes achieving precise task-space control during dynamic manipulation challenging and limits generalizability.

## Method

- A bi-level control architecture is employed: a high-level diffusion policy infers task-relevant end-effector trajectories from visual observations, trained on real-world, robot-agnostic human demonstrations collected via the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI).
- A low-level whole-body controller, trained entirely in massively parallelized simulation using reinforcement learning, robustly tracks the predicted end-effector trajectories in the task-frame by coordinating the robot's legs and arm.
- An accessible iPhone (via Apple's ARKit) provides real-time, in-the-wild odometry for the robot, circumventing the need for external motion capture systems and enabling practical deployment.

## Results

- Demonstrated successful dynamic whole-body tossing of balls into a bin with 70% success and robust pushing of a 10lbs kettlebell with 90% success on a quadruped, both using accessible iPhone-based odometry.
- Achieved zero-shot cross-embodiment deployment of a pre-trained manipulation policy (originally for a fixed-base arm) for cup rearrangement on a dynamic quadruped, yielding an 80% success rate.
- The whole-body controller autonomously learned complex dynamic behaviors, such as balancing on one or two feet during tossing and adapting leg placement to maintain balance while pushing heavy objects, without explicit task-specific training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling task-centric data collection (via robot-agnostic UMI demonstrations) from robot-specific control learning (via simulation-based reinforcement learning) provides a scalable and efficient paradigm for acquiring complex robot skills.
> - Training a whole-body controller to explicitly track end-effector trajectories in the task-frame enables robust and precise manipulation on dynamic platforms, inherently compensating for base movements and external perturbations.
> - A simple yet expressive interface, end-effector trajectories, facilitates the zero-shot deployment of embodiment-agnostic manipulation policies onto highly dynamic and distinct robotic systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is actively pursuing methods to scale up the acquisition of complex robotic skills. Two major paradigms exist: real-world robot data collection and simulation-based training. Both have inherent limitations: real-world data is costly, time-consuming, robot-specific, and poses safety concerns, while simulation often struggles with accurately modeling diverse tasks, objects, and their dynamics. "UMI on Legs" directly addresses this fundamental challenge by proposing an innovative way to combine the strengths of both paradigms for mobile manipulation on quadrupedal robots.

Existing research in mobile manipulation, particularly for quadrupedal systems, has shown promising results in pick-and-place tasks. However, many of these systems either assume quasi-static manipulation, rely on heavy, stable bases, or require carefully engineered manipulation primitives and extensive teleoperation. The dependence on robot-specific commands and the physical presence of the robot during data collection for these systems significantly bottlenecks scalability.

Simultaneously, advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) combined with massively parallel simulators (e.g., Isaac Gym) have revolutionized the learning of dynamic locomotion skills for legged robots. More recently, this approach has extended to manipulation skills and whole-body control (WBC) for quadrupeds. However, a common limitation in these WBC systems is their reliance on human teleoperation or simplified scenarios (e.g., using AprilTags for tracking) and their tendency to produce body-frame commands. This means they often defer the complex task of precise task-space tracking to a high-level policy or human, making fully autonomous, dynamic manipulation challenging to achieve at scale.

This paper positions itself as a critical bridge. It leverages the authors' prior work on "Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI)", which enables the collection of large, diverse, and robot-agnostic manipulation data using a hand-held gripper. By decoupling the task-centric data collection from the robot hardware, UMI dramatically reduces the cost and complexity of acquiring demonstration data. "UMI on Legs" takes this a step further by demonstrating how such embodiment-agnostic manipulation policies (trained on UMI data) can be seamlessly integrated with a simulation-trained, robot-specific whole-body controller for a highly dynamic quadrupedal platform.

The key innovation lies in the *interface* between these two components: end-effector trajectories in the task-frame. Unlike previous WBCs that track body-frame targets or rely on simplified body velocity commands, UMI on Legs' WBC is explicitly trained to maintain precise task-space control regardless of base movements. This manipulation-centric approach, combined with an accessible iPhone-based odometry solution, distinguishes it from concurrent works that might use robot-specific demonstrations or tethered setups. The ability to perform "zero-shot cross-embodiment deployment" of pre-trained policies onto a completely different robot platform (from a fixed-base industrial arm to a mobile quadruped) represents a significant leap towards truly generalizable and mobile robot manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "UMI on Legs" is to introduce a novel framework that combines real-world and simulation data streams to enable fully autonomous, expressive, and robust manipulation skills on dynamic quadrupedal robots. The authors aim to overcome the traditional bottlenecks in robot learning by:

1. **Scaling Task-Centric Data Collection in the Real World without a Robot:** The motivation here is to circumvent the expense, time, and safety concerns associated with collecting diverse manipulation data directly from robots. By leveraging the UMI concept, humans can demonstrate a wide range of complex manipulation tasks using a simple, hand-held gripper, which is then used to train an embodiment-agnostic manipulation policy. This makes data collection significantly cheaper and more versatile.
2. **Scaling Robot-Centric Control Learning in Simulation without Task Setups:** Training low-level robot controllers, especially whole-body controllers for complex platforms like quadrupeds, is often done in simulation. However, setting up diverse, realistic manipulation tasks, object dynamics, and reward functions in simulation remains a major challenge. The objective is to train a robust whole-body controller in simulation that focuses solely on tracking end-effector trajectories, thereby bypassing the need for complex task-specific simulation environments.
3. **Developing a Seamless and Expressive Interface:** A crucial motivation is to define an interface between the high-level manipulation policy and the low-level whole-body controller that is both simple enough for non-expert users to demonstrate (via UMI) and expressive enough to convey complex, dynamic manipulation trajectories. The choice of "end-effector trajectories in the task-frame" serves this purpose, allowing the manipulation policy to focus purely on task progress while providing anticipatory information to the controller.
4. **Enabling Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The ultimate goal is to allow manipulation policies trained on data from one embodiment (e.g., fixed-base robot arms, or even human demonstrations) to be directly deployed onto drastically different, dynamic robot platforms like quadrupeds, without requiring additional robot-specific fine-tuning. This speaks to the broader vision of "universal" robot policies.
5. **Achieving Robust Real-World Deployment:** Practical deployment of mobile manipulation systems often hinges on reliable real-time localization and state estimation. A key objective is to address this by integrating an accessible and robust odometry solution for "in-the-wild" task execution.

In essence, the motivation is to create a scalable, practical, and highly capable mobile manipulation system that breaks down traditional barriers between data collection, policy learning, and robotic deployment across diverse hardware.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "UMI-on-Legs" framework is built upon a bi-level control architecture, separating high-level task planning from low-level robot execution:

**a) High-level Manipulation Policy (2Hz):**
- **Purpose:** To infer task-relevant end-effector trajectories from visual observations.
- **Architecture:** It employs a **diffusion policy**, specifically a U-Net architecture with a DDIM scheduler, leveraging a pre-trained CLIP vision encoder.
- **Input:** Primarily wrist-mounted camera views (from a GoPro attached to the robot's arm).
- **Output:** Sequences of end-effector pose targets (position and 6D rotation) extending into the future (with an action horizon of 64 steps) in the *camera frame*.
- **Training Data:** Crucially, this policy is trained using data collected in the real world *without any robot hardware*. Instead, human operators use a hand-held gripper device, the **UMI**, to demonstrate diverse manipulation tasks (prehensile, non-prehensile, dynamic). For tasks like cup rearrangement, a pre-trained UMI checkpoint is directly used. For pushing and tossing, new diffusion policies are trained from scratch using UMI demonstrations. This approach significantly reduces the cost and complexity of data acquisition.

**b) Low-level Whole-Body Controller (WBC) (50Hz):**
- **Purpose:** To robustly track the end-effector trajectories predicted by the manipulation policy by coordinating the robot's legs and arm.
- **Architecture:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) controller trained using **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**.
- **Input:** Comprehensive robot proprioception (18 joint positions and velocities, base orientation and angular velocity, and the previous action) combined with the target end-effector trajectory inferred by the high-level policy. The trajectory is provided with preview information, including densely sampled targets from -60ms to 60ms and a future target 1000ms ahead, allowing for anticipation.
- **Output:** Joint position targets for both the 12-DOF legs and the 6-DOF arm. These targets are then tracked by independent PD controllers.
- **Training Environment:** The WBC is trained entirely in **massively parallelized simulation** (using Isaac Gym). A key design choice here is that the simulation *does not* require setting up complex manipulation tasks or modeling object dynamics; it only needs to simulate the robot's ability to track specified end-effector trajectories. This simplifies the simulation setup and accelerates training.
- **Task-Frame Trajectory Tracking:** Unlike many prior legged manipulation systems that train WBCs to track body-frame end-effector targets, this work trains the controller to track poses in the *task-frame*. This means the WBC learns to actively compensate for and cancel out base movements and perturbations, ensuring the end-effector maintains its precision relative to the task environment (Fig 4). This is critical for dynamic and precise manipulation.
- **Reward Function:** The primary reward incentivizes minimizing position and orientation errors to the target pose, with a curriculum of scaling terms (σ) to facilitate exploration early in training and demand high precision later. Additional regularization and shaping terms, common in RL for legged robots (e.g., joint limits, acceleration, torque, root height, collision avoidance, action rate, body-EE alignment, even mass distribution, feet under hips), are also included to ensure stable and energy-efficient behavior.
- **Sim2Real Transfer:** Robustness in the real world is achieved through domain randomization during training, including random push forces, joint friction/damping, contact frictions, and randomization of body/arm masses and CoMs. Crucially, a 20ms control latency is modeled, and the robot's initial position is randomized every 20 seconds to account for odometry noise.

**c) Interface and System Integration:**
- **Interface:** The end-effector trajectory in the task-frame serves as the critical communication channel. This simple yet expressive interface allows for:
 - **Intuitive Demonstration:** Humans can easily provide demonstrations using UMI without needing to understand robot-specific commands.
 - **High-Level Intention:** The preview horizon allows the WBC to anticipate future movements and adjust whole-body coordination (e.g., bracing for a toss).
 - **Precise and Stable Manipulation:** Task-frame tracking ensures precision regardless of base perturbations.
 - **Asynchronous Multi-frequency Execution:** The low-frequency manipulation policy (2Hz) can provide high-level goals to the high-frequency WBC (50Hz).
 - **Compatibility:** The interface is generic, allowing any trajectory-based manipulation policy to be "plug-and-played."
- **Hardware Setup:** The system uses a 12-DOF Unitree Go2 quadruped robot integrated with a 6-DOF ARX5 robot arm, customized with Finray grippers and a GoPro camera.
- **Accessible Real-time Odometry:** To enable "in-the-wild" deployment without external motion capture systems, an iPhone (mounted on the robot's base) is used for real-time pose estimation via Apple's ARKit. The rear mounting site minimizes weight on the arm, avoids collisions, and reduces motion blur/occlusion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and the real world validate the core design decisions and overall capabilities of the UMI-on-Legs framework across three challenging task categories: dynamic manipulation, robust interaction, and cross-embodiment generalization.

**a) Capability: Whole-Body Dynamic Tossing**
- **Task:** Tossing pre-grasped balls into a bin 90cm away (success within 40cm of center).
- **Performance:** Achieved **75% success rate** using an OptiTrack motion capture system (oracle odometry) and **70% success rate** with the iPhone-based odometry.
- **Key Observation:** The WBC, without explicit training on dynamic tossing primitives, learned a sophisticated whole-body strategy. This involved using power from all joints, momentarily balancing on one or two feet during the toss, and leveraging body mass inertia to regain balance by tucking legs and curling the arm to induce a backward torque (Fig 5). This demonstrates the framework's ability to capture complex, dynamic skills.
- **Interface Impact (Ablations in Sim):**
 - Training on **task-frame manipulation trajectories** was shown to be crucial; removing it (e.g., " Task-space" and " UMI Traj" ablations) severely degraded position and orientation precision, often leading to 0% survival (e.g., 15.49cm position error for Task-space).
 - Removing **trajectory preview information** (e.g., " Preview" ablation) led to more jittery actions, dangerous reaching behaviors, and reduced survival (93% vs 98.4%), as the controller couldn't anticipate future motions.
 - A baseline similar to DeepWBC performed extremely poorly (22.26cm position error, 0% survival), highlighting the superiority of their approach.
- **Failure Analysis:** Some tossing misses were attributed to the manipulation policy's imprecision or noisy end-effector trajectories rather than the WBC itself. An "oracle" manipulation policy (calibrated trajectory replay) improved success by 10%, suggesting further data scaling could enhance performance.

**b) Robustness: End-effector Reaching Leads to Robust Whole-body Pushing**
- **Task:** Pushing a 10lbs kettlebell into a goal region (varied distances from 80cm-120cm).
- **Performance:** Achieved **95% success rate** with motion capture and **90% success rate** with iPhone odometry. Demonstrated robustness even with a 20lbs kettlebell (4 out of 5 successful episodes).
- **Key Observation:** The controller exhibited surprising zero-shot robustness to unseen external dynamics (kettlebell friction and mass). It learned to adapt by leaning forward to exert more force when encountering resistance, and then dynamically adjusting its leg placement to regain balance ("push-and-catch" strategy) as the kettlebell suddenly slid forward (Fig 6).
- **Failure Analysis:** Common failures involved the kettlebell toppling sideways, which the arm couldn't reorient due to weight. The robot also experienced overheating, suggesting the need for more energy-efficient pushing strategies.

**c) Scalability: Plug-and-play Cross-Embodiment Manipulation Policies**
- **Task:** In-the-wild Cup Rearrangement (placing an espresso cup on a saucer with a specific handle orientation), using a pre-trained policy from Chi et al. intended for fixed-base robot arms.
- **Performance:** Achieved **85% success rate** with motion capture and **80% success rate** with iPhone odometry, despite the significant cross-embodiment gap.
- **Key Observation:** The framework successfully deployed a manipulation policy, originally designed for a static, industrial 6-DoF arm with a larger reach, *zero-shot* onto an 18-DoF dynamic quadruped. The WBC dynamically tilted and leaned the robot's base to increase reach and counterbalance the arm, adapting to the different kinematics and dynamics of the quadruped. This validates the "plug-and-play" nature of the interface.

**d) Accessible Real-time Odometry:**
- The iPhone-based ARKit odometry system proved remarkably effective, achieving comparable success rates to the high-precision OptiTrack motion capture system across all tasks. This demonstrates its practical viability for in-the-wild deployment, addressing a common bottleneck in mobile manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"UMI on Legs" represents a significant advance in the field of mobile robot manipulation, particularly for dynamic and agile platforms like quadrupeds. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Scalable Robot Learning Paradigm:** The framework offers a practical and highly scalable pathway for acquiring complex robot skills. By decoupling task-centric data collection (via cheap, robot-agnostic UMI demonstrations) from robot-specific control learning (via efficient simulation-based RL), it mitigates the major bottlenecks of traditional robot learning. This approach could significantly accelerate the development of new robot skills.

2. **Enabling Generalizable Mobile Manipulation:** The ability to "zero-shot" deploy pre-trained manipulation policies, originally designed for fixed-base robot arms, onto a highly dynamic quadrupedal platform is a transformative achievement. This opens up possibilities for universal manipulation policies that are independent of the specific robot embodiment, dramatically expanding the reach and applicability of learned skills to a wider range of mobile and dynamic robots.

3. **Advancement in Whole-Body Control for Dynamic Systems:** The "manipulation-centric whole-body controller" that tracks end-effector trajectories in the *task-frame* is a crucial methodological innovation. This design allows the WBC to inherently compensate for base movements and perturbations, ensuring precise and stable manipulation even during dynamic actions like tossing or pushing heavy objects. This approach improves robustness and precision compared to controllers relying on body-frame targets.

4. **Practical Real-World Deployment:** The successful integration of an accessible, real-time iPhone-based odometry solution addresses a major practical limitation in deploying mobile robots in unstructured environments. This makes advanced mobile manipulation capabilities more attainable for researchers and practitioners without requiring expensive, specialized infrastructure like motion capture systems. It lowers the barrier to entry for mobile robotics research and application development.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** UMI on Legs lays robust groundwork for several exciting future research directions:
 - **Beyond Grippers:** Extending the framework to support whole-body manipulation beyond just end-effector control, leveraging the full dexterity of the quadruped's legs as manipulators.
 - **Embodiment-Aware High-Level Policies:** Incorporating embodiment-specific constraints and affordances back into the high-level manipulation policy to optimize performance and safety.
 - **Advanced Control Capabilities:** Integrating collision avoidance, force feedback, and force-based control for safer and more dexterous interactions in complex environments.
 - **Foundation Models for Robotics:** The modular design of UMI on Legs aligns well with the trend of developing generalist robot policies. It provides a robust control layer for future, even more general, visuo-motor policies, accelerating their deployment across diverse robot platforms.

In conclusion, "UMI on Legs" not only demonstrates impressive robotic capabilities but also proposes a paradigm shift in how manipulation skills can be learned and transferred, offering a clear and scalable path towards ubiquitous, intelligent mobile robots.
