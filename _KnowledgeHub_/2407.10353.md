# UMI on Legs: Making Manipulation Policies Mobile with Manipulation-Centric Whole-body Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> UMI on Legs presents a framework for enabling dynamic mobile manipulation on quadrupedal robots by integrating robot-agnostic human demonstration data with a simulation-trained, manipulation-centric whole-body controller. The system successfully deploys pre-trained policies onto a dynamic quadruped in a zero-shot manner, achieving 70% success in tossing and 90% in pushing tasks while leveraging an accessible iPhone-based odometry system.

## Problem

- Scaling the acquisition of complex robot skills is bottlenecked by the high cost and time of real-world robot data collection, and the difficulty of accurately modeling diverse tasks in simulation.
- Existing mobile manipulation systems for quadrupedal robots often rely on quasi-static assumptions, require extensive teleoperation, or are limited by their dependence on robot-specific commands and on-robot data collection.
- Traditional whole-body controllers (WBCs) for legged robots often track body-frame targets or simplified velocity commands, which makes achieving precise task-space control during dynamic manipulation challenging and limits generalizability.

## Method

- A bi-level control architecture is employed: a high-level diffusion policy infers task-relevant end-effector trajectories from visual observations, trained on real-world, robot-agnostic human demonstrations collected via the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI).
- A low-level whole-body controller, trained entirely in massively parallelized simulation using reinforcement learning, robustly tracks the predicted end-effector trajectories in the task-frame by coordinating the robot's legs and arm.
- An accessible iPhone (via Apple's ARKit) provides real-time, in-the-wild odometry for the robot, circumventing the need for external motion capture systems and enabling practical deployment.

## Results

- Demonstrated successful dynamic whole-body tossing of balls into a bin with 70% success and robust pushing of a 10lbs kettlebell with 90% success on a quadruped, both using accessible iPhone-based odometry.
- Achieved zero-shot cross-embodiment deployment of a pre-trained manipulation policy (originally for a fixed-base arm) for cup rearrangement on a dynamic quadruped, yielding an 80% success rate.
- The whole-body controller autonomously learned complex dynamic behaviors, such as balancing on one or two feet during tossing and adapting leg placement to maintain balance while pushing heavy objects, without explicit task-specific training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling task-centric data collection (via robot-agnostic UMI demonstrations) from robot-specific control learning (via simulation-based reinforcement learning) provides a scalable and efficient paradigm for acquiring complex robot skills.
> - Training a whole-body controller to explicitly track end-effector trajectories in the task-frame enables robust and precise manipulation on dynamic platforms, inherently compensating for base movements and external perturbations.
> - A simple yet expressive interface, end-effector trajectories, facilitates the zero-shot deployment of embodiment-agnostic manipulation policies onto highly dynamic and distinct robotic systems.
