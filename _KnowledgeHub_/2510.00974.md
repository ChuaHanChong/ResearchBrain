# JEPA-T: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture with Text Fusion for Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> JEPA-T introduces a multimodal framework for text-to-image generation, leveraging a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture with a novel multi-stage text-visual fusion strategy. The model achieves an FID of 1.42 on ImageNet-1K, demonstrating competitive performance against leading diffusion and autoregressive models.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-image (T2I) models, like diffusion and autoregressive architectures, suffer from slow sampling speeds, high computational costs, and scalability issues.
- A key challenge in token-centric T2I models is effectively fusing text and visual tokens to achieve strong semantic alignment without compromising the generality of the visual backbone.
- The need to extend the benefits of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) from self-supervised vision tasks to the more complex multimodal domain of text-to-image generation.

## Method

- **JEPA-T** employs a Transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture that processes discrete visual tokens derived from a VAE and conditioning vectors from a frozen CLIP text encoder.
- A multi-stage text-visual fusion strategy integrates text via input-level injection at the predictor, post-predictor cross-attention for refinement, and objective-level text embedding concatenation.
- Training utilizes a hybrid objective combining a text-conditioned masked prediction loss (L_JEPA) for robust feature learning and a conditional flow matching loss (L_FM) for a smooth generative process.

## Results

- JEPA-T achieved an FID score of 1.42 on ImageNet-**1K**, outperforming many state-of-the-art models including Stable Diffusion (4.21), Imagen (3.98), and previous JEPA-style architectures like D-JEPA-L (1.58).
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing the post-predictor cross-attention significantly degraded performance (FID increased to 1.75), validating its critical role in refining semantic details.
- Disabling the objective-level text injection or the conditional flow matching loss also led to noticeable performance drops, emphasizing their importance for deep cross-modal consistency and generative quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The multi-stage text fusion strategy, which includes late architectural cross-attention and objective-level text injection, effectively balances the preservation of a general visual backbone with strong semantic conditioning.
> - Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures can be successfully adapted for complex multimodal generative tasks, demonstrating their versatility beyond pure self-supervised vision.
> - Combining masked prediction with conditional flow matching provides a robust and scalable training methodology for high-quality, semantically consistent text-to-image generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Text-to-Image (T2I) generation has witnessed rapid advancements, becoming a prominent area within artificial intelligence. This paper positions itself at the forefront of this landscape, specifically addressing current limitations and exploring new architectural paradigms.

Historically, T2I generation has been dominated by two main architectural approaches:

- **Diffusion Models:** Examples include Stable Diffusion and Imagen. These models have achieved remarkable photorealism and semantic fidelity by iteratively denoising visual tokens guided by text. However, their primary drawbacks are slow sampling speeds and high computational requirements, particularly during inference.
- **Autoregressive Models:** Models like Parti and MaskGIT approach image generation as a sequence of discrete tokens. While offering improved compositionality, they often struggle with long-range dependencies and face scalability challenges when dealing with high-resolution or complex images.

In parallel to these dominant paradigms, several emerging trends have begun to shape the T2I landscape:

- **Token-Centric Multimodal Frameworks:** A push towards unifying image and text representations into discrete tokens, processed by shared Transformer backbones. This approach aims for efficiency and seamless conditional generation, moving away from direct pixel-level manipulation.
- **Semantic Augmentation via Language Supervision:** Techniques like CLIP-aligned or text-conditioned objectives (e.g., LSeg, CLIPSeg) have enabled powerful open-vocabulary capabilities, allowing models to generate images from diverse, unconstrained textual prompts. A key design challenge here is how to effectively weave cross-modal interactions throughout the network without adding excessive complexity.
- **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA):** Pioneered by works like the original JEPA and D-JEPA, these architectures are rooted in self-supervised learning. They aim to learn robust, generalizable representations by predicting masked parts of an input from unmasked contexts, often achieving strong generative scaling and data efficiency in vision tasks.

JEPA-T directly addresses a critical open question arising from these trends: **"where and how should text be fused with visual tokens to preserve a task-agnostic backbone while retaining strong conditioning?"** Existing solutions often involve injecting semantic guidance at early stages (e.g., TokLIP during tokenization) or throughout the network (traditional cross-modal interactions), potentially complicating the backbone or sacrificing its generality. JEPA-T seeks an optimal balance by integrating a JEPA-style self-supervised backbone with carefully designed late-stage cross-attention and objective-level text injection.

The paper builds upon the success of cross-attention mechanisms, which have proven effective in injecting text into vision features across various architectures (retrieval, GANs, diffusion models like AttnGAN, Stable Diffusion, TACA). By applying and refining this proven mechanism within a JEPA-style framework, JEPA-T contributes to the ongoing effort to create more efficient, scalable, and semantically aligned T2I models that overcome the limitations of their predecessors while leveraging the strengths of self-supervised representation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the JEPA-T research is to develop a unified, efficient, and scalable multimodal framework for high-quality text-to-image (T2I) generation. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aimed at resolving key challenges in the current T2I landscape:

1. **Addressing Inefficiencies of Existing T2I Paradigms:**
 - **Diffusion Models:** While highly effective, diffusion-based T2I models are notoriously slow in sampling and demand substantial computational resources during both training and inference. JEPA-T aims to avoid these inefficiencies by adopting a different generative mechanism.
 - **Autoregressive Models:** These models, while offering better compositionality, struggle with scalability and capturing long-range dependencies in complex images. JEPA-T seeks a path that overcomes these limitations.

2. **Optimizing Text-Visual Fusion in Token-Centric Architectures:** Modern T2I models increasingly rely on token-centric approaches where both images and text are converted into discrete tokens. A significant challenge lies in effectively and efficiently fusing these textual and visual tokens to ensure strong semantic alignment without compromising the generality of the underlying visual backbone. The authors explicitly state the "open question: where and how should text be fused with visual tokens...?" as a core motivation.

3. **Leveraging Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) for T2I:** JEPA-style models have demonstrated remarkable success in self-supervised learning for vision, offering strong generative scaling and robust representation learning. The motivation is to extend the benefits of this architecture—known for its data efficiency and task-agnostic backbone—to the complex multimodal task of T2I generation.

4. **Enhancing Semantic Alignment and Generative Quality:** The ultimate goal is to generate images that are not only visually high-quality but also deeply consistent with the semantic intent of the input text. This requires a fusion mechanism that can guide the generative process with precise textual cues.

5. **Achieving Scalable Training and Open-Vocabulary Generalization:** The research aims for a self-supervised training recipe that is computationally efficient and allows the model to generalize effectively across various datasets and for both class-conditional and free-text generation, without relying on computationally expensive pixel-level reconstruction.

In summary, JEPA-T is motivated by the desire to push beyond the current trade-offs in T2I models—specifically, the balance between generative quality, computational efficiency, and architectural generality—by proposing a novel fusion strategy within a powerful, self-supervised JEPA framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JEPA-T introduces a unified multimodal framework for text-to-image generation, building upon a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and incorporating novel strategies for text-visual fusion.

#### 3.1. Core Architecture and Tokenization

The foundation of JEPA-T is a Transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture designed to process discrete tokens.

- **Visual Tokenization:** Input images are first processed by a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The VAE encodes images into non-overlapping patches, converting them into a sequence of continuous latent vectors, which are then linearly projected to form discrete visual tokens (denoted as `z`). This leverages the efficiency of working in a compressed latent space.
- **Text/Condition Tokenization:** Text captions or class labels are tokenized using a frozen CLIP text encoder. The resulting text embeddings are then projected via a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to produce a conditioning vector (`c`), which serves as the semantic guide.
- **JEPA-T Backbone:** The core consists of:
 - **Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) that processes unmasked visual tokens and a set of learnable knowledge buffer tokens. The conditioning vector `c` is prepended to this buffer to guide the encoding process.
 - **Predictor (Decoder):** This component integrates `c` through element-wise addition and cross-attention. Its primary role is to reconstruct the masked visual tokens from the encoder's output.

#### 3.2. Cross-Attention for Multimodal Fusion (Key Innovation)

JEPA-T's most significant contribution lies in its multi-stage text-visual fusion strategy, designed to balance backbone generality with strong conditioning:

1. **Input-level Text Injection at Predictor Stage:** Text embeddings are injected *before* the prediction process. This early bias ensures that the initial, coarse reconstruction of visual tokens already reflects the semantic intent of the text, making the denoising dynamics more semantically aware from the outset, while the core vision backbone remains task-agnostic.
2. **Post-Predictor Cross-Attention:** After the predictor generates an initial prediction of the visual token embeddings (`z_pred`), a dedicated cross-attention module refines these predictions. Conditioned on the text embeddings (`t_emb`), this module allows for the injection of high-resolution semantic cues, enabling text to correct or sharpen details that might be missed by the coarse predictor. The output of this cross-attention (`z_attended`) is then combined with `z_pred` via a residual connection: `z_fuse1 = z_pred + z_attended`.
3. **Objective-level Alignment (Text Embedding Injection):** To further reinforce text-visual alignment, the fused features (`z_fuse1`) are concatenated with the original text embeddings (`t_emb`, expanded to match sequence length). This concatenated vector (`z_concat`) is then projected back to the decoder's embedding dimension (`D_dec`) using a linear layer (`W_P`), forming `z_final`. This direct incorporation of text information into the feature representation *before* the final flow matching loss ensures deep semantic consistency during generation.

#### 3.3. Training Objectives

The model is trained with a hybrid objective, combining self-supervised masked prediction with a conditional generative loss:

1. **Masked Prediction Loss (L_JEPA):**
 - Inspired by Masked Autoencoders (MAE) and MaskGIT, a high proportion of visual tokens (e.g., 70-100%) are randomly masked.
 - Unlike traditional MAE, JEPA-T's masked prediction is **text-conditioned**. The context encoder processes unmasked tokens, learnable buffer tokens, and the projected CLIP text embedding of the class label. This ensures that reconstruction is guided by both visual context and semantic cues.
 - The loss is an EMA-based consistency objective (Equation 4), where the decoder's latent representations (`z`) are trained to predict those from a momentum-updated EMA encoder (`z_EMA`), promoting stable and consistent learning.

2. **Conditional Flow Matching Loss (L_FM):**
 - This loss extends the concept of flow matching to the discrete visual token space. Instead of operating in pixel space with unconditional dynamics, this loss directly conditions the latent transport process on raw text embeddings (`t_proj`).
 - The decoder's output (`z`) interacts with `t_proj` via cross-attention *before* predicting the clean target (`v_0`), effectively guiding the transport trajectory with language semantics (Equation 5).
 - During training, this loss is exclusively computed over masked positions, focusing the generative process on accurate inpainting.

3. **Total Loss:** The final training objective is a weighted sum of the two components: `L_total = L_FM + λ_jepa · L_JEPA` (Equation 6), where `λ_jepa` is a weighting coefficient. This hybrid approach enables the model to learn robust, context-aware visual features (via masked reconstruction) and a smooth, conditional generative process (via flow matching).

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, the same unified network is capable of performing both class-conditional and free-text image generation. This is achieved by iteratively denoising visual tokens, which are conditioned on the provided textual input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

JEPA-T was evaluated on the ImageNet-1K dataset, a standard benchmark for image generation, using images of 256x256 resolution. The evaluations covered both class-conditional generation and text-conditioned generation using captions generated by Qwen-VL.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

The performance was assessed using established metrics for generative models:
- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)↓:** A lower FID indicates higher quality and diversity of generated images, reflecting closer resemblance to real images.
- **Inception Score (IS)↑:** A higher IS suggests better image quality (distinguishable objects) and diversity.
- **Precision↑ and Recall↑:** These metrics provide a more comprehensive analysis of sample quality and coverage, respectively.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results (Table 1)

JEPA-T demonstrated highly competitive performance against several state-of-the-art text-to-image generation models:

- **Overall Performance:** JEPA-T achieved an FID score of **1.42**, an IS of **298.3**, Precision of **0.79**, and Recall of **0.63**.
- **Comparison to Leading Models:**
 - JEPA-T significantly outperformed many established models such as Stable Diffusion (FID 4.21), Imagen (FID 3.98), Parti (FID 3.75), MaskGIT (FID 3.50), and TokLIP (FID 3.30).
 - It also surpassed recent Diffusion-based models like ADM (FID 4.59), LDM-4 (FID 3.60), U-ViT-H/2-G (FID 2.29), and DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27).
 - Crucially, JEPA-T achieved a lower FID score (1.42) compared to D-JEPA-L (FID 1.58) and MAR-L (FID 1.78), demonstrating its specific improvements in JEPA-style architectures for T2I.
 - While MAGVIT-v2 (FID 1.78, IS 319.4) and MDTv2-XL/2 (FID 1.58, IS 314.7) showed higher IS, JEPA-T's superior FID score of 1.42 indicates that it generates images that are overall closer in distribution to real images, often considered a stronger indicator of quality.
- **Effectiveness of Fusion:** The results highlight that JEPA-T consistently outperforms non-fusion and late-fusion baselines, underscoring the efficacy of its proposed cross-attention fusion and objective-level text injection strategies.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 2)

Ablation studies were conducted to understand the contribution of each key component to JEPA-T's performance:

- **"w/o Cross-Attn" (without Post-Predictor Cross-Attention):** Removing this architectural fusion component led to a significant performance degradation, with FID increasing from 1.42 to 1.75 and IS dropping from 298.3 to 198.2. This confirms the critical role of late-stage cross-attention in refining visual tokens with semantic details.
- **"w/o Text-Inj" (without Objective-Level Text Injection in Flow Matching):** Disabling the direct incorporation of raw text embeddings before the flow matching loss also resulted in a noticeable performance drop (FID increased to 1.48, IS dropped to 200.1). This emphasizes the importance of objective-level alignment for deep cross-modal consistency.
- **"w/o Flow Matching" (without Conditional Flow Matching Loss):** Removing the flow matching objective, relying solely on masked prediction, led to a substantial decline in generative quality (FID 1.42 to 1.60, IS 298.3 to 190.5). This demonstrates that flow matching is essential for enabling high-quality, conditional generative modeling within the JEPA-T framework.

#### 4.4. Summary of Findings

The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that JEPA-T achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance in text-to-image generation on ImageNet-1K. The ablation studies provide strong evidence that both the architectural innovation (post-predictor cross-attention) and the objective-level text injection in flow matching are indispensable for JEPA-T's success, highlighting the effective balance achieved between conditioning strength and backbone generality. The combination of masked prediction and conditional flow matching proves to be a robust strategy for scalable, high-quality generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

JEPA-T represents a significant advancement in the field of text-to-image (T2I) generation, offering a novel architectural and training paradigm with several impactful implications:

1. **Advancement in Efficient T2I Generation:** By moving away from the iterative denoising processes of diffusion models and the scalability challenges of autoregressive models, JEPA-T proposes a more efficient route for T2I. Its token-centric and predictive architecture, combined with a self-supervised training recipe, promises faster inference and reduced computational overhead without sacrificing quality. This efficiency is crucial for deploying T2I models in real-world applications requiring rapid generation or operating on resource-constrained devices.

2. **Novel and Effective Fusion Strategy:** The core innovation of "late architectural fusion combined with objective-level alignment" offers a powerful solution to the long-standing challenge of effectively integrating textual conditioning into visual generative models. By refining predictions with cross-attention *after* an initial coarse reconstruction and directly injecting text embeddings into the generative objective, JEPA-T strikes an effective balance between preserving a general, vision-centric backbone and achieving strong, semantically precise conditioning. This approach could inspire new designs for multimodal interaction across various tasks.

3. **Enhanced Semantic Alignment and Generative Quality:** The superior performance on ImageNet-1K, particularly the low FID score, demonstrates JEPA-T's capability to generate images that are not only visually compelling but also highly consistent with the provided text prompts. This strong semantic alignment is critical for user satisfaction and practical utility in applications like creative content generation, design, and personalized media.

4. **Expansion of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA):** This work successfully extends the promise of JEPA-style self-supervised learning from pure vision tasks (like image understanding and representation learning) to complex multimodal generative tasks like T2I. This highlights the versatility and scalability of JEPA as a foundational architecture for learning robust representations that can bridge modalities and enable advanced generative capabilities. It paves the way for further exploration of JEPA in other multimodal domains.

5. **Unified and Versatile Framework:** The ability of the same network to support both class-conditional and open-vocabulary text-to-image generation enhances its versatility. This means a single trained model can be used for a wider range of applications, from generating images based on broad categories to fulfilling highly specific and nuanced textual prompts.

6. **Data Efficiency and Scalability:** The self-supervised training recipe, which avoids computationally expensive pixel-level reconstruction and leverages masked prediction, suggests strong data efficiency and scalability. This is vital for training models on ever-growing datasets and for future scaling to higher resolutions or more complex generative tasks.

In conclusion, JEPA-T offers a compelling direction for the future of T2I generation. Its innovative fusion mechanism within a JEPA framework addresses critical performance bottlenecks and architectural challenges, leading to models that are more efficient, scalable, and capable of generating high-quality, semantically consistent images. The open-sourcing of the code further enables reproducibility and fosters rapid community adoption and future research.
