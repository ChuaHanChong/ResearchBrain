# From Prediction Uncertainty to Conformalized Distance Fields for Safe Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Functional Conformal Prediction-Model Predictive Control (FCP-MPC), a framework for safe motion planning that quantifies prediction uncertainty on distance fields. It achieves real-time robust safety guarantees, showing improved scalability and goal reachability in dense, dynamic environments compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous robots operating in dynamic environments face prediction errors in future environmental occupancy, which motion planners must account for to ensure safety.
- Tightly coupling prediction uncertainty with real-time planning in dense scenes, while providing quantitative safety guarantees, presents significant computational challenges.
- Existing conformal prediction (CP) methods in robotics struggle with scalability, as online computation costs increase with the number of obstacles and planning horizon, and lack spatial coherence for continuous region safety.

## Method

- The proposed **Functional Conformal Prediction-Model Predictive Control (FCP-MPC)** framework ==conformalizes== the *entire predicted distance field* at once, treating prediction error as a continuous function over the workspace.
- It leverages an offline-online computational decomposition: expensive conformalization is done offline using ==Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA)== and a ==Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)==, while online planning requires only lightweight, low-dimensional updates.
- The framework generates a continuous, queryable safety envelope, which is then integrated as a tightened safety constraint (either hard or soft) within a sampling-based ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== formulation.

## Results

- FCP-MPC (soft variant) consistently achieved the lowest steps-to-goal and highest goal reachability (**100%** in 3D dense scenarios) across 2D pedestrian navigation and 3D quadrotor benchmarks, with competitive collision rates.
- The per-step control time of FCP-MPC was largely insensitive to the number of dynamic obstacles, maintaining the lowest computational cost among all methods (e.g., **74.8 ms** vs. **~100 ms** for baselines at **280** obstacles).
- The online adaptive FCP (AFCP) update successfully restored the target **1-alpha=0.9** full-field coverage under deployment-time density shifts, demonstrating robustness to changes in environment density.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prediction errors in distance fields are not arbitrary; they are spatially structured, approximately time-invariant, and low-rank, allowing for a compact functional representation.
> - Conformalizing the entire spatial field of prediction errors, rather than scalar scores, yields a continuous, distribution-free safety certificate applicable uniformly over the workspace, resolving spatial incoherence issues.
> - The low-rank and approximately time-invariant properties of residual fields enable an offline-online decomposition, making online computation largely insensitive to the number of obstacles and suitable for real-time deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robots operating in dynamic environments, shared with agents such as pedestrians or vehicles, necessitate safe behavior rooted in anticipating future world states. The inherent unobservability of future environmental occupancy means forecasts carry inherent errors, which motion planners must account for. The central challenge lies in tightly coupling prediction uncertainty with real-time planning, especially in dense scenes, while delivering quantitative safety guarantees.

Existing approaches to incorporating prediction uncertainty into planning fall into several categories:
- **Chance-constrained formulations**, **risk-sensitive planners**, and **distributionally robust approaches**: These methods provide strong theoretical guarantees by bounding collision probabilities or hedging against worst-case distributions. However, they typically require committing to specific ambiguity sets or parametric error models, and the associated worst-case optimizations are computationally intensive when solved online, especially as scene density increases.
- **Conformal Prediction (CP)**: Offers a complementary, distribution-free method that converts raw predictor errors into prediction sets with finite-sample coverage guarantees. CP has been applied in robotics to tasks like planning among dynamic agents, perception-based navigation, and decision-theoretic control.

Despite the appeal of CP, most current applications in motion planning face a structural limitation: they conformalize a *scalar score* (e.g., per-obstacle radius or per-state margin) and recompute it point-wise online. This approach scales poorly with the number of obstacles and the planning horizon, precisely when real-time safety is critical. Furthermore, ensuring safety over a continuous region, rather than just at discrete points, often requires a worst-case score that is computationally intractable to enforce exactly, leading to heavy approximations. Consequently, these scalar CP methods are either computationally inexpensive but lack spatial coherence, or theoretically faithful but too expensive for real-time deployment.

This paper addresses these limitations by shifting the representational paradigm. It proposes to conformalize the *entire predicted distance field* at once, treating the prediction error as a function over the workspace. This approach, termed Functional Conformal Prediction (FCP), leverages the empirical observation that residual distance fields are spatially structured, approximately time-invariant, and low-rank. By operating at the field level, FCP aims to yield a continuous, queryable safety envelope, decoupling the cost from obstacle count and providing a more robust safety certificate compared to point-wise or per-obstacle methods. This work positions itself as a cheaper-online, finite-sample alternative to distributionally robust planners, while offering a novel, functional extension to existing conformal prediction schemes in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a safe motion planning framework for autonomous robots operating in dynamic and potentially dense environments. This framework aims to quantitatively guarantee safety by effectively reasoning about uncertainty in predicted obstacle motion, without sacrificing real-time performance.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent difficulties in coupling prediction with planning:
- **Uncertainty Management:** Future environmental occupancy is unobservable and must be forecast from past observations, leading to prediction errors. A motion planner consuming these forecasts must tightly account for this uncertainty to ensure safety.
- **Real-time Performance:** The uncertainty reasoning must be computationally efficient enough to close the control loop in real time, even in scenes with high densities of dynamic obstacles. Existing methods often struggle with this dual requirement.
- **Limitations of Current Conformal Prediction (CP) in Robotics:** As discussed in Section 2, most CP applications in robotics rely on conformalizing scalar scores (e.g., individual obstacle radii or state margins) online. This leads to:
 - **Scaling Issues:** Online computation costs increase with the number of obstacles and the planning horizon.
 - **Spatial Incoherence:** Scalar scores provide point-wise safety, but certifying safety over an entire region, like a robot's trajectory, becomes intractable without heavy approximations. This results in either loose safety or prohibitive computational costs.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a novel approach motivated by empirical observations about the nature of prediction errors in distance fields:
- **Spatially Structured and Low-Rank Error:** The error in a predicted distance field is not arbitrary. It exhibits spatial structure, is approximately time-invariant, and can be accurately represented in a low-dimensional space using functional principal components (low-rank property). This suggests that the error can be treated as a continuous function over the workspace rather than a collection of independent scalar errors.
- **Field-Level Safety Certification:** By conformalizing the entire spatial field of prediction errors, the framework can yield a continuous, queryable safety envelope. This certificate applies uniformly over the entire field, meaning any trajectory satisfying the resulting constraint is certified safe, irrespective of how the control space is sampled. This resolves the spatial incoherence issue of scalar CP.
- **Offline-Online Decomposition:** The low-rank and approximately time-invariant properties of the residual field enable an offline-online computational decomposition. Expensive conformalization procedures can be performed once offline using a large calibration dataset, while online planning only requires lightweight, low-dimensional updates. This significantly reduces the per-step computational cost, making it largely insensitive to the number of obstacles and suitable for real-time deployment in dense environments.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Formulate prediction-uncertainty quantification for safe planning as a functional conformal prediction problem on the residual distance field, generating a continuous, queryable, distribution-free, and field-level lower bound.
2. Establish asymptotic closed-loop safety guarantees for hard-constrained MPC, adapting to non-exchangeable data streams via an online adaptive fieldwise update, and also certify the behavior of soft-penalty deployments.
3. Develop a computationally efficient method by decomposing the field-level bound into an offline GMM-based inductive conformal envelope and a lightweight online adaptive update, making per-step computation low and largely independent of obstacle count.
4. Demonstrate the framework's ability to achieve a favorable balance of safety, feasibility, and efficiency in challenging dynamic environments, surpassing existing baselines in scalability to high obstacle densities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Functional Conformal Prediction-Model Predictive Control (FCP-MPC), integrates a novel functional conformal prediction (FCP) framework with a sampling-based model predictive controller (MPC) for safe motion planning in dynamic environments.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Safety Representation

The robot's configuration space is denoted by Q, with its position x(q) in the world X. The robot's footprint A(q) is assumed to be a ball of radius r_robot. The environment contains dynamic obstacles O_t. The objective is to minimize a cost function while satisfying a probabilistic safety guarantee: P[A_t ∩ O_t = ∅] ≥ 1 − α, or equivalently, P[D(x_t, O_t) ≥ δ + r_robot] ≥ 1 − α, where D(x, O_t) is the closest distance from x to O_t, and δ is a user-specified margin. This is achieved using an MPC framework that plans a trajectory over a horizon N, incorporating predicted occupancies O_t+i|t.

The core idea is to account for the imperfections in obstacle predictions. The prediction error is defined by the score function S_t+i|t(x) := D_t+i|t(x) − D_t+i(x), where D_t+i|t(x) is the predicted distance field and D_t+i(x) is the true distance field. Positive values of S indicate that the obstacle is closer than predicted, implying an overestimation of safety. The goal is to construct a high-probability upper bound U_t+i|t(x) for S_t+i|t(x) such that P[S_t+i|t(x) ≤ U_t+i|t(x) ∀x ∈ X] ≥ 1 − α. This directly yields a probabilistic lower bound L_t+i|t(x) := D_t+i|t(x) − U_t+i|t(x) for the true distance field D_t+i(x). This lower bound L is then embedded as a tightened safety constraint in the MPC problem: L_t+i|t(x_t+i|t) ≥ r_robot + δ.

#### 3.2. Functional Conformal Prediction (FCP) Framework

The error signal S_t+i|t is functional, an element of the Hilbert space L^2(X). FCP, specifically tailored for functional objects, is used to generate the field-level bound. The practicality of FCP relies on two empirical properties of the residual distance field:
1. **Approximately Time-Invariant:** Residual statistics are stable across different time instances.
2. **Low-Rank:** A few functional principal components (FPCA modes) capture most of the variance in the residual field, allowing for a compact, low-dimensional representation.

The FCP framework decomposes S_t+i|t into a finite-dimensional projection ˘S_t+i|t and a residual R_t+i|t.
- **Data-Driven Basis Functions:** A p_i-dimensional orthonormal basis {ψ_i,j} is learned from a calibration dataset of score functions at each horizon step `i` using Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA). This basis captures the leading principal components in the L^2 sense.
- **Inductive Conformal Prediction in Coefficient Space:** For each score function S, a coefficient vector ξ_i(S) = [⟨S, ψ_i,1⟩..., ⟨S, ψ_i,p_i⟩]^T is computed.
 - A K-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is fitted to these coefficient vectors from a training subset.
 - A max-component pseudo-density conformity score g(ξ) is defined based on the fitted GMM. This score assigns higher conformity to high-density regions, producing ellipsoidal regions in the coefficient space.
 - Using a calibration subset, a conformal quantile λ_i is determined, yielding an ICP prediction set T_t+i|t = {ξ ∈ R^p_i : g(ξ) ≥ λ_i}.
 - A high-confidence upper bound ˘U_t+i|t(x) on the projected field ˘S_t+i|t(x) is derived as sup_{ξ∈T_t+i|t} ξ^Tψ_i(x), which admits a closed-form expression (Eq. 19).
- **Bounding Projection Residuals:** The L-infinity norm of the projection residual R_t+i|t is bounded by a conformal quantile ε_i, ensuring that |R_t+i|t(x)| ≤ ε_i for all x ∈ X with high probability.
- **Combined Upper Bound:** The final upper bound U_t+i|t(x) is given by ˘U_t+i|t(x) + ε_i, providing a distribution-free coverage guarantee at level 1-α via a union bound.

#### 3.3. FCP-Informed Safe Motion Planning (FCP-MPC)

The conformalized lower bound L_t+i|t(x) is integrated into an MPC framework. The workspace X is discretized on a finite grid X_bar, with a resolution δ_d.
- **Hard Constraints:** The MPC directly enforces L_t+i|t(x_bar_t+i|t) ≥ r_robot + δ + δ_d. If no sampled trajectory satisfies this, a fallback action (e.g., brake-to-hover) is issued.
- **Soft Penalization:** Alternatively, a penalty term w * Σ[g_t+i|t(x_bar_t+i|t)]_+^2 is added to the objective function, where g_t+i|t(x) := r_robot + δ + δ_d − L_t+i|t(x) is the constraint violation. This makes the MPC problem always feasible, degrading gracefully when hard constraints cannot be met.

#### 3.4. Offline Initialization and Online Adaptation

- **Offline Stage (Stage 1):** A large calibration dataset of residual fields is collected. FPCA is applied to learn the basis functions {ψ_i,m}. A GMM is fitted to the projected coefficients, and the conformal parameters (mixture parameters, radii {r_i,k}, and projection residual ε_i) are computed for each horizon step `i` and cached as Π_i. This expensive computation is performed only once.
- **Online Stage (Stage 2 - Adaptive FCP, AFCP):** To handle potential distribution shifts, the cached envelope is refined online. An adjustable scalar multiplier c_t+i|t is introduced for the radii r_i,k. The upper envelope becomes U_t+i|t(x; c) = ε_i + max_k[μ^T_i,k ψ_i(x) + c * r_i,k sqrt(ψ_i(x)^T Σ_i,k ψ_i(x))]. A violation indicator err^(t)_i is computed when a residual field S_t+i|t becomes available, and the multiplier is updated via a simple adaptive rule: c_t+1+i|t+1 = c_t+i|t + γ(err^(t)_i - α). A similar adaptive update can be applied to ε_i (Eq. 32). This ensures long-run empirical coverage even under non-exchangeable data streams, with a minimal online computational cost (only re-scaling cached grids).

#### 3.5. Safety Analysis

The framework provides formal safety guarantees:
- **Theorem 2 (Asymptotic Safety Guarantee):** If the FCP-MPC (hard constraint) is always feasible and the online envelope U_online asymptotically covers the true score field, the closed-loop system is asymptotically (1-α)-safe (i.e., the time-averaged frequency of collision-free steps is at least 1-α). The functional coverage is key here, as it implies point-wise safety for any path satisfying the constraint.
- **Theorem 3 (Asymptotic Safety with AFCP):** By using the AFCP update, the asymptotic (1-α)-safety is robustly maintained even in dynamic, non-exchangeable environments, as AFCP guarantees long-run field coverage.
- **Theorem 4 (Safety of the Soft Deployment):** The soft-penalty MPC variant is also asymptotically safe up to a controllable slack, which diminishes as the penalty weight increases. For sufficiently large weights, it can achieve collision-free performance with probability 1-α. The soft deployment also gracefully degrades to the least-unsafe plan when hard feasibility is impossible.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluates FCP-MPC against several baselines (ACP-MPC, CC-MPC, ECP-MPC) across 2D pedestrian navigation benchmarks (ETH-UCY datasets) and a dense 3D quadrotor task. All methods use the same underlying sampling-based planner and are calibrated to a target miscoverage level α = 0.1.

#### 4.1. Properties of the Residual Field

- **Time-Invariance:** Per-cell residual statistics from temporally disjoint halves of ETH-UCY scenes exhibit close agreement (Pearson r = 0.71–0.91 across eth, univ, hotel), indicating the residual field is a stable characteristic of the scene.
- **Low-Rank Nature:** Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA) shows that 5–7 leading components explain approximately 90% of the variance in residual fields on most scenes, demonstrating that the uncertainty is spatially structured and compressible.

#### 4.2. 2D Pedestrian Navigation (ETH-UCY Datasets)

- **FCP-MPC Performance:**
 - **FCP-MPC (soft):** Consistently achieved the lowest steps-to-goal (shortest paths) across all five scenes (e.g., 41.0 ± 4.4 steps for eth), incurring no infeasible rate by design. Collision rates ranged from 0.045 to 0.191. Per-step control time was low (1.80–6.93 ms).
 - **FCP-MPC (hard):** Showed competitive collision rates in more open scenes (e.g., 0.057 ± 0.024 for eth) but higher infeasible rates (0.357–0.700) and increased collision rates in denser scenes. Per-step control time remained very low (0.78–2.52 ms).
 - **Source of Hard Variant Collisions:** When restricted to steps where the hard-constrained MPC was actually feasible, collision rates were within the target α=0.1 on all scenes (e.g., 0.026 for univ), significantly lower than the full-episode rates. The higher full-episode collisions were largely attributed to post-infeasibility fallback actions.
- **Baseline Comparisons:**
 - **CC-MPC:** Exhibited low collision rates but was conservative (high steps-to-goal) and often timed out in crowded scenes.
 - **ECP-MPC:** Incurred substantially higher online costs (40–83 ms per step) compared to FCP-MPC, occasionally timing out.
 - **ACP-MPC:** Provided low collision rates in some scenes but struggled in dense, dynamic environments with high infeasible rates and frequent timeouts.
- **Effect of Online Adaptation:** Ablation studies showed that enabling the online Adaptive FCP (AFCP) update resulted in only marginal and scene-dependent shifts in collision and infeasible rates for in-distribution benchmarks. This indicates that the offline-conformalized envelope is robust when deployment conditions match calibration, with AFCP adding only a few milliseconds per step.

#### 4.3. 3D Quadrotor Navigation (Dense Dynamic Obstacles)

- **Scalability and Efficiency:** FCP-MPC’s per-step control time was largely insensitive to the number of dynamic obstacles (N_obs) ranging from 10 to 280. Its cost remained the lowest among all methods across this range (e.g., 74.8 ms for FCP-MPC (soft) vs. ~100 ms for CC/ACP-MPC and ~2400 ms for ECP-MPC at N_obs=280). This is attributed to querying a cached conformal field rather than re-evaluating individual obstacle distances.
- **Performance at N_obs = 280 (Very Dense):**
 - **FCP-MPC (soft):** Achieved 100% goal reachability, lowest steps-to-goal (100.4 ± 33.2), and the lowest collision rate (0.028 ± 0.032) among all goal-reaching methods.
 - **FCP-MPC (hard):** Timed out on all seeds due to high infeasibility (0.833 ± 0.118), indicating excessive conservativeness in extremely dense scenarios.
 - **Baselines:** ACP-MPC and ECP-MPC timed out on all seeds, being too conservative to navigate the dense obstacle field. CC-MPC reached the goal on 41% of seeds, but with longer paths and higher computational cost.
- **Performance at N_obs = 50 (Lower Density):**
 - **FCP-MPC (hard):** Successfully reached the goal on 88% of seeds, demonstrating that its hard constraint is viable when sufficient free space is available.
 - **FCP-MPC (soft):** Again reached the goal on 100% of seeds with the lowest collision rate.
 - **Baselines:** ACP-MPC and ECP-MPC still timed out on all seeds, suggesting their per-obstacle/egocentric inflation methods remain overly conservative even in less cluttered environments.

#### 4.4. Field Coverage under Deployment-Time Density Shift

- **Static vs. Adaptive Slacks:** When the functional envelope was trained offline at N_obs=50 and deployed at higher densities (N_obs=80, 120), the static projection slack ε_i (without online adaptation) led to a degradation in full-field coverage (from 0.080 to 0.034).
- **AFCP Restoration:** The online adaptive update for the projection slack (via Eq. 32, Corollary 1) successfully restored the target 1-α=0.9 coverage across all tested densities, demonstrating its robustness to distribution shifts.
- **Collisions vs. Coverage:** Despite the degradation in static coverage, collision rates remained small (≤ 0.05). This suggests that closed-loop replanning in FCP-MPC can mitigate many transient envelope violations before they lead to actual collisions, highlighting that while coverage is sufficient for safety, real-time feedback can absorb some coverage loss.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of autonomous robotics by presenting a novel and effective solution for safe motion planning under prediction uncertainty in dynamic and dense environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Advancement in Uncertainty Quantification:** The introduction of Functional Conformal Prediction (FCP) to reason about prediction uncertainty in distance fields represents a significant methodological advance. By treating prediction errors as functional objects and conformalizing the entire spatial field, the method moves beyond the limitations of scalar-based conformal prediction, which often struggle with spatial coherence and computational scalability in complex scenarios.

2. **Enhanced Safety Guarantees:** FCP-MPC provides quantitative, distribution-free safety guarantees. The field-level certificate ensures that any trajectory satisfying the conformal constraint is safe, regardless of the control sampling strategy. This is a robust form of safety assurance, further enhanced by adaptive online updates (AFCP) that maintain long-run coverage even under deployment-time distribution shifts, crucial for real-world robustness.

3. **Superior Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The key innovation of decomposing the conformalization process into an offline, expensive phase and a lightweight, low-dimensional online adaptation phase dramatically improves computational efficiency. FCP-MPC's per-step cost remains largely insensitive to the number of dynamic obstacles, enabling it to scale effectively to highly dense environments (e.g., 280 dynamic obstacles in 3D). This contrasts sharply with existing online uncertainty-reasoning baselines whose computational cost escalates with obstacle count, making them impractical for real-time applications in crowded scenes.

4. **Improved Feasibility and Goal-Reaching:** The research demonstrates that FCP-MPC achieves a favorable balance of safety, feasibility, and efficiency. Particularly, the soft-penalty deployment of the conformal field enables the robot to reach goals in extremely dense environments where hard-constrained baselines (and even the hard FCP-MPC variant) become overly conservative and fail to find feasible paths. This pragmatic approach allows for graceful degradation in highly constrained situations rather than outright failure.

5. **Applicability to Complex Robotic Systems:** The demonstrated performance on both 2D pedestrian navigation and 3D quadrotor tasks indicates the versatility and potential applicability of FCP-MPC across different robotic platforms and environments. The framework's ability to handle high-dimensional workspaces and numerous dynamic agents makes it relevant for challenging tasks in urban navigation, logistics, and aerial robotics.

6. **Broader Impact on Autonomous Systems:** By providing a principled and computationally efficient method for quantifying and leveraging prediction uncertainty, this work contributes to building more reliable, safer, and ultimately more autonomous robotic systems. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in shared human-robot spaces, where guaranteeing safety under dynamic and uncertain conditions is paramount. The distinction between proactive safety certification and reactive closed-loop correction also offers valuable insights for designing robust control architectures.

In conclusion, this research presents a significant step forward in robust and efficient safe motion planning. By leveraging the structured nature of prediction errors through functional conformal prediction, it offers a scalable, real-time solution with strong theoretical guarantees, addressing a fundamental challenge in autonomous robotics.
