# LAOF: Robust Latent Action Learning with Optical Flow Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> A pseudo-supervised framework called LAOF is introduced, which leverages optical flow as a constraint to learn robust latent action representations from videos. This approach enhances the quality of learned actions by emphasizing agent-driven motion, leading to improved performance in both imitation learning and reinforcement learning tasks, particularly when ground-truth action labels are scarce.

## Problem

- Existing unsupervised latent action learning methods often entangle learned actions with environmental distractors, assuming all observed changes are solely agent-driven.
- Explicit action supervision is limited by the scarcity of ground-truth action labels in large-scale video datasets, leading to unstable learning and overfitting with sparse data.
- The high cost of manual action annotation hinders the scalability of supervised approaches for pre-training embodied foundation models.

## Method

- **LAOF** proposes a pseudo-supervised framework that integrates ==optical flow== as an auxiliary, action-driven signal to constrain latent action learning during pre-training.
- The framework employs a three-stage process: pre-training with optical flow and optionally sparse action labels, distillation to learn a policy, and fine-tuning an action decoder.
- For dynamic environments, object-centric optical flow, generated via text-guided segmentation, filters out motion irrelevant to the agent, providing a more focused physical motion signal.

## Results

- LAOF (unsupervised) improved average task success rates on LIBERO by **4.2%** and increased average normalized episodic rewards on PROCGEN by **16%** compared to the LAPO baseline.
- LAOF-Action, with only **1%** action supervision, showed significantly improved training stability and faster convergence compared to LAPO and LAOM-Action, which struggled with sparsity.
- A dedicated flow decoder, coupled with a forward dynamics model, was identified as the most effective architectural integration for optical flow constraints, outperforming other variants in an ablation study.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous latent action representations consistently outperform discrete ones across various downstream imitation and reinforcement learning tasks.
> - Optical flow constraints significantly enhance the quality of learned latent actions by providing physically grounded supervision, improving robustness to distractors, and stabilizing training.
> - LAOF demonstrates that optical flow can effectively substitute or supplement scarce action labels, achieving performance comparable to or surpassing action-supervised methods with very limited ground-truth data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of latent action learning, a domain critical for the pre-training of scalable embodied foundation models. These models aim to enable intelligent agents to learn complex behaviors from large-scale, often action-free, video datasets. A foundational paradigm in this area is Latent Action Policies (LAPO), which facilitates unsupervised training of latent action models (LAMs) by jointly optimizing an inverse dynamics model (IDM) and a forward dynamics model (FDM) using a reconstruction objective. This framework allows latent actions to encode changes between consecutive observations.

However, existing approaches like LAPO face limitations. The primary challenge is that they implicitly assume all observed changes between consecutive frames are solely caused by the agent's actions. This assumption is often violated in real-world large-scale videos, where environmental dynamics can arise from stochastic factors, such as moving background objects or irrelevant distractors. Consequently, latent action representations can become entangled with visual appearance rather than accurately capturing the agent's physical motion.

To mitigate these issues, prior research has explored incorporating action supervision by decoding latent actions into ground-truth actions during training (e.g., LAOM, villa-X). While this approach can reduce the impact of distractors and enhance physical consistency, its effectiveness is restricted by the scarcity of available action labels in real-world datasets. Alternating training strategies under extremely sparse supervision can lead to instability and overfitting, causing models to drift toward spurious correlations in visual features instead of physically meaningful action spaces.

LAOF (Latent Action learning with Optical Flow constraints) addresses these limitations by proposing a pseudo-supervised framework. It leverages the agent's optical flow as an auxiliary, action-driven signal to constrain latent action learning. This approach aims to provide robust representations that are less susceptible to distractors and more stable during training, particularly in conditions with limited or no ground-truth action labels. The work extends the LAPO paradigm by integrating a novel form of supervision derived from optical flow, thereby aiming to improve the quality of learned latent actions for downstream tasks like imitation learning and reinforcement learning.

The paper also contextualizes its approach within discussions on latent action representation forms (continuous vs. discrete), visual encoder choices (e.g., DINOv2), and the broader advancements in LAPO's capabilities, including visual-language-action models and world models. It distinguishes itself from contemporaneous work like FlowVLA by using continuous optical flow as a constraint for robust latent action learning without requiring language supervision or discretization of flow tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust latent action learning framework, termed LAOF, capable of acquiring high-quality latent action representations from videos, even in the presence of action-irrelevant distractors and under conditions of extreme action-label scarcity.

The motivation for LAOF stems from several observations regarding the limitations of existing latent action learning methods:

1. **Distractor Sensitivity in Unsupervised Methods:** Current unsupervised latent action learning paradigms, such as LAPO, rely on reconstructing future observations or features. They implicitly assume that all observed changes between consecutive frames are solely attributable to the agent's actions. This assumption is often violated in real-world, large-scale video datasets, where environmental dynamics (e.g., moving background objects, visual clutter) are not exclusively agent-driven. As a result, the learned latent actions can become entangled with these distractors, failing to isolate and represent the agent's true physical motion.

2. **Limitations of Action Supervision:** To counter distractor sensitivity, some methods incorporate explicit action supervision by decoding latent actions into ground-truth physical actions. While this can enhance physical consistency and reduce distractor influence, ground-truth action labels are typically very scarce in large-scale datasets. Training with sparse action labels often leads to unstable learning and overfitting, where the model may form spurious correlations with visual features rather than anchoring latent actions in a physically meaningful space. The cost and effort associated with manual action annotation further limit the scalability of such supervised approaches.

3. **Potential of Optical Flow as a Pseudo-Supervision Signal:** The authors observe that optical flow, which represents pixel-level motion between consecutive frames, naturally emphasizes moving objects and suppresses static background elements. This property makes optical flow inherently correlated with an agent's physical actions. Furthermore, recent advancements in optical flow estimation have yielded robust models (e.g., RAFT) that generalize well across diverse scenes, allowing for the generation of high-quality optical flow pseudo-labels without manual annotation. This presents an opportunity to leverage optical flow as a readily available and physically grounded auxiliary signal to constrain latent action learning.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the research are to:
- Introduce optical flow constraints to provide a pseudo-supervision signal for physical motion, aiming to achieve more stable and robust latent action learning.
- Validate the effectiveness of these constraints on downstream imitation learning tasks (LIBERO benchmark) and reinforcement learning tasks (PROCGEN benchmark).
- Evaluate the extent to which optical flow constraints improve performance on datasets with varying proportions of action labels, specifically up to 10% and beyond.
- Determine if LAOF, even without explicit action supervision, can match or surpass the performance of action-supervised methods trained with limited action labels (e.g., 1%).
- Conduct an ablation study to identify the optimal architectural integration of optical flow constraints within the latent action learning framework, exploring different constraint structures and their coupling with the forward dynamics model.
- Investigate the comparative effectiveness of continuous versus discrete latent action representations in the context of optical flow-constrained learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LAOF framework is a pseudo-supervised approach designed to learn robust latent action representations by integrating optical flow constraints. Its training process is structured into three distinct stages: pre-training, distillation, and fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Pre-training

The pre-training stage focuses on learning high-quality latent action representations from an unlabeled dataset, `D_unlabel = {(o_t, f_rgb,t)}`, consisting of observations `o_t` and corresponding RGB-formatted optical flow `f_rgb,t`.

- **Model Components:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** A frozen DINOv2 (ViT-B/14) encoder extracts image features `s_t` from normalized input frames.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):** Infers a latent action `z_t` from a pair of consecutive states `(s_t, s_t+1)`. Implemented as a spatial-temporal transformer for LIBERO and a lightweight CNN encoder for PROCGEN.
 - **Forward Dynamics Model (FDM):** Predicts the next state `s_t+1_hat` conditioned on the current state `s_t` and the inferred latent action `z_t`. Implemented as a spatial transformer for LIBERO and a U-Net for PROCGEN.
 - **Flow Decoder (`d_flow`):** Decodes the latent action `z_t` into agent's optical flow features `f_t_hat`. Implemented as a spatial transformer for LIBERO and a U-Net for PROCGEN.

- **Pre-training Objective (LAOF):**
 The framework is optimized using a combined objective:
 - **Next-state Reconstruction Loss (`L_reconstruction`):** `||s_t+1_hat - s_t+1||^2`, encouraging the FDM to accurately predict future states.
 - **Optical Flow Constraint Loss (`L_flow`):** `||f_t_hat - f_t||^2`, where `f_t` is the RGB-formatted optical flow `f_rgb,t` encoded into the state space via the visual encoder. This loss provides pseudo-supervision for physical motion.
 - **Total Pre-training Loss:** `L_pretrain = L_reconstruction + L_flow`.

- **LAOF-Action (for Sparse Action Supervision):**
 For scenarios with a small set of action labels, LAOF-Action extends the base LAOF by incorporating an additional lightweight multilayer perceptron (MLP) action decoder (`d_action`).
 - **Action Supervision Loss (`L_action`):** `||d_action(a_t_hat | z_t) - a_t||^2`, applied to labeled samples.
 - **Modified Pre-training Loss:** `L_pretrain = L_reconstruction + (1 - lambda) * L_flow + lambda * L_action`. The coefficient `lambda` balances the contributions, set as the proportion of action labels in the overall training dataset (`M / (N + M)`).
 - Training involves an alternating update strategy, where unlabeled data update the flow decoder and action-labeled data update the action decoder.

#### 3.2. Distillation

This stage enables a latent action policy `pi` to infer latent actions directly from the current state and a language instruction `l_t`, without requiring future frames.
- A latent-labeled dataset `D_latent = {(o_t, z_t, l_t)}` is constructed by using the frozen pre-trained IDM to annotate consecutive observation pairs with their corresponding latent actions `z_t`.
- A policy `pi` is initialized and trained via behavior cloning using the distillation loss: `L_distillation = ||pi(z_t_hat | s_t, l_t) - z_t||^2`.

#### 3.3. Fine-tuning

To deploy the latent action policy on a real agent, a small action-labeled dataset `D_action = {(o_t, a_t, l_t)}` is used to train an action decoder `d_action: Z -> A`.
- The action decoder (MLP) maps latent actions `z_t` (from the frozen `pi`) to physical actions `a_t`.
- The decoder is optimized using `L_action = ||d_action(a_t_hat | z_t) - a_t||^2`.
- The final deployable policy is `d_action ◦ pi`, mapping states and language instructions to physical actions.

#### 3.4. Optical Flow Generation and Processing

- **Raw Optical Flow Estimation:** RAFT, a pre-trained optical flow model, estimates `f_uv,t` (2-channel u,v vectors) between consecutive observations.
- **RGB Formatting:** `f_uv,t` is converted to `f_rgb,t` to be compatible with DINOv2's input. This involves mapping vector direction to Hue and magnitude to Saturation and Value channels, then converting from HSV to RGB. Magnitude is normalized using a sensitivity factor `sigma`.
- **Object-Centric Optical Flow:** For environments with dynamic distractors (e.g., PROCGEN), LangSAM (a text-guided segmentation model) generates an object-centric semantic mask for `o_t` based on object-related textual prompts. This mask is applied to the global optical flow `f_all_rgb,t` to filter out motion not belonging to the agent, yielding `f_sam_rgb,t`. The final supervision signal `f_rgb,t` is adaptively chosen: global optical flow for static environments (LIBERO) and object-centric for dynamic environments (PROCGEN).

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Benchmarks:** LIBERO (robot manipulation, continuous control, static distractors) and PROCGEN (procedurally generated games, discrete control, static and dynamic distractors).
- **Latent Action Dimensionality:** Set to 128.
- **Baselines:** LAPO, CoMo, and LAOM-Action.
- **Representation Forms:** Both continuous (standard VAE with KL-divergence weight 1e-6) and discrete (VQ-VAE) latent action representations are evaluated.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Latent Action Quality:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) for continuous actions and classification accuracy (top-1) for discrete actions, measuring the decoder's ability to recover ground-truth actions from latent actions.
 - **Downstream Performance:** Average task success rate (LIBERO, imitation learning) and average normalized episodic return (PROCGEN, reinforcement learning).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to address five specific research questions, providing a comprehensive evaluation of LAOF.

1. **Effectiveness of Continuous vs. Discrete Latent Action Representations (Q1):**
 - The study found that continuous latent action representations consistently outperformed discrete ones across all downstream tasks on both LIBERO (imitation learning) and PROCGEN (reinforcement learning). This was indicated by larger areas under the learning curves for continuous representations in Figure 3a and 3b, and corroborated by performance metrics in Tables 1 and 2.

2. **Impact of Optical Flow Constraints on Representation Quality and Downstream Performance (Q2):**
 - Optical flow constraints were demonstrated to substantially enhance the quality of learned latent action representations.
 - On the LIBERO benchmark, LAOF (unsupervised) improved average task success rates by 4.2% compared to the LAPO baseline. LAOF-Action (action-supervised with 1% labels) showed an even greater improvement of 11.5% in success rates over LAPO (Table 1).
 - On the PROCGEN benchmark, LAOF (unsupervised) increased average normalized episodic rewards by 16% over LAPO. LAOF-Action achieved a 22% increase in rewards (Table 2).
 - Methods like CoMo, which use inter-frame differences, did not show consistent performance improvements and sometimes degraded performance, suggesting that explicit physical motion information from optical flow is more effective than abstract temporal "changes." The evaluation metrics for latent action quality (MSE for continuous, accuracy for discrete) were found to be highly correlated with downstream task performance (Pearson correlations of 0.8288 for PROCGEN returns and -0.7311 for LIBERO success rates), validating their use for assessing representation quality (Figure 3c, 3d).

3. **Performance Gains Across Varying Action Label Proportions (Q3):**
 - The optical flow constraints consistently improved the quality of latent action representations for baselines as the proportion of action labels in the training dataset increased, up to 10% (Figure 4).
 - Beyond a 10% action ratio, the positive effects of optical flow diminished. At a 100% action ratio, performance degradation was observed in some PROCGEN tasks (BIGFISH and CHASER), which the authors attribute to potential noise in the optical flow pseudo-labels.
 - The authors suggest applying optical flow constraints when the dataset contains less than 10% action-labeled data. For higher proportions, a case-by-case evaluation is recommended, potentially favoring action supervision alone if performance is comparable, due to simpler architecture and reduced noise risk.

4. **Optical Flow as a Substitute for Action Labels Under Limited Supervision (Q4):**
 - Under extremely sparse action supervision (e.g., 1% action ratio), LAPO exhibited unstable performance and large standard deviations, particularly in PROCGEN tasks (CHASER and HEIST), indicating susceptibility to distractors. LAOM-Action, despite using action supervision, also showed training instability and overfitting in certain tasks (LEAPER and CHASER) under a 1% action ratio (Figure 5).
 - LAOF-Action, by integrating optical flow constraints, demonstrated significantly improved training stability across all tasks, characterized by faster convergence, smaller variance, and higher final action accuracy.
 - Notably, LAOF (without any action supervision) achieved performance comparable to or even superior to LAOM-Action trained with a 1% action ratio. This finding indicates that optical flow constraints can effectively capture meaningful physical dynamics and mitigate overfitting, serving as a powerful alternative to scarce action labels (Figure 4, Figure 5).

5. **Optimal Architecture for Introducing Optical Flow Constraints (Q5):**
 - An ablation study on various LAOF variants revealed a clear performance hierarchy: LAOF > LAOF-AE > LAOF-Only(z_t) > LAOF-FlowFDM > LAOF-Only(z_t, s_t) (Table 3).
 - Removing the FDM (LAOF-Only(z_t)) relative to LAOF reduced performance, suggesting that the FDM provides beneficial structural context for latent action learning.
 - Naively integrating the flow decoder into the FDM (LAOF-FlowFDM) or applying optical flow constraints to both latent actions and the current state (LAOF-Only(z_t, s_t)) proved suboptimal. This indicates that applying constraints directly to latent actions via a dedicated decoder (as in LAOF) provides the most effective physical supervision.
 - The LAOF-AE variant, which performs optical flow autoencoding, showed competitive performance, suggesting that optical flow supervision alone provides strong prior information about physical motions for learning latent actions.

Additional results in the Appendix (Table 4, 5) indicated that LAOF consistently improved robustness over LAPO, regardless of the specific optical flow model employed (RAFT, SEA-RAFT) or even using simple differencing of DINOv2 features. A clear relationship was observed between motion–action alignment (measured by Canonical Correlation Analysis) and downstream performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in LAOF introduces a framework that addresses key challenges in latent action learning, particularly the issues of distractor sensitivity and reliance on scarce action labels. The findings have several implications for the development of embodied AI and robotics:

1. **Reduced Dependency on Action Labels:** By leveraging optical flow as a pseudo-supervision signal, LAOF significantly reduces the need for expensive and labor-intensive ground-truth action annotations. This is crucial for scaling latent action learning to large, unlabeled video datasets, which are far more abundant than explicitly labeled action trajectories. This capability accelerates the pre-training of embodied foundation models.

2. **Enhanced Robustness to Distractors:** The integration of optical flow constraints enables latent action representations to better isolate agent-driven motion from action-irrelevant distractors, such as moving background objects or environmental changes. This results in more robust and physically meaningful latent actions, which is essential for deploying agents in complex and dynamic real-world environments.

3. **Improved Training Stability:** The pseudo-supervision provided by optical flow contributes to more stable training, especially under conditions of sparse action supervision. It helps mitigate overfitting to spurious visual features, ensuring that the learned latent actions are more anchored in physical dynamics rather than superficial correlations.

4. **Advancement in Embodied Foundation Models:** LAOF offers a viable approach for pre-training scalable embodied foundation models from readily available video data. By providing a robust and efficient method for learning latent actions, it contributes to building more generalist robots capable of performing a wider range of tasks in diverse environments. The competitive performance of the LAOF-AE variant suggests that unsupervised optical flow encoding could serve as a powerful pre-training strategy for such models.

5. **Architectural and Design Insights:** The detailed ablation study provides valuable insights into the optimal design for incorporating motion constraints. The finding that a dedicated decoder for optical flow, coupled with an FDM, yields superior performance offers guidance for future architectural choices in latent action learning systems.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the precision of text-driven agent segmentation and the current applicability mainly to "eye-to-hand" scenarios, suggest clear avenues for future research. Extending LAOF to "eye-in-hand" settings, incorporating egocentric human videos, and addressing challenges like camera shake and viewpoint variations would broaden its applicability to more diverse and complex real-world datasets, paving the way for a truly general LAOF framework.

In summary, LAOF represents a step towards developing more autonomous and capable embodied agents by offering a robust, data-efficient, and scalable method for learning physically grounded latent action representations.
