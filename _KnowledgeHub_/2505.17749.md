# Mind the GAP! The Challenges of Scale in Pixel-based Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind researchers identified that the bottleneck between convolutional encoders and dense layers limits scaling in pixel-based deep reinforcement learning. They demonstrated Global Average Pooling (GAP) as a simpler, more computationally efficient, and effective architectural modification to enable performance improvements as network size increases, outperforming prior complex methods.

## Problem

- Naive scaling of deep reinforcement learning (RL) networks, particularly in pixel-based environments, often leads to performance degradation rather than improvement.
- Existing methods to enable scaling in pixel-based deep RL networks are typically complex, computationally intensive (e.g., Mixtures-of-Experts, sparse training), and lack a unified underlying explanation.
- A fundamental understanding was needed of the specific architectural limitations causing performance issues when scaling the dense layers following a convolutional encoder in pixel-based RL.

## Method

- Diagnosed the primary cause of scaling degradation in pixel-based deep RL as the "==bottleneck==" connection between the convolutional encoder and subsequent dense layers.
- Proposed Global Average Pooling (GAP) as a simple, hyperparameter-free architectural modification to structure and reduce the density of this identified bottleneck.
- Implemented and evaluated GAP across various network scales, architectures (Impala, CNN), agents (Rainbow, DER, SAC), and environments (Atari, Procgen, DMC) to demonstrate its efficacy.

## Results

- Global Average Pooling (GAP) successfully addresses the scaling challenge, enabling performance improvements as deep RL network size (width and depth) increases, across different base architectures (Impala, CNN).
- GAP demonstrates superior computational efficiency and faster GPU runtime compared to more complex scaling techniques like SoftMoE and sparse methods, while yielding comparable or better overall performance.
- The efficacy of GAP generalizes broadly across various reinforcement learning benchmarks and agents, including diverse Atari games, Procgen environments, Atari100K, and continuous control tasks in the DeepMind Control suite.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The connection between the convolutional encoder and the subsequent dense layers (termed the "bottleneck") is the critical point where scaling issues arise in pixel-based deep RL, leading to high neuron dormancy and poor feature utilization in dense layers.
> - Complex methods designed to enable scaling in deep RL, such as sparse training or Mixtures-of-Experts, implicitly derive their benefits by reducing the effective parameter density or structuring the information flow at this bottleneck.
> - Providing more abstract, higher-level features from the encoder significantly mitigates performance degradation in scaled dense layers, indicating the importance of structured feature input to the value estimator.
