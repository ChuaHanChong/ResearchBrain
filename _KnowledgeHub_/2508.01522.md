# Decentralized Aerial Manipulation of a Cable-Suspended Load using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A research team from Delft University of Technology developed a decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning method for 6-DoF manipulation of cable-suspended loads using MAVs, achieving the first real-world demonstration without inter-MAV communication and enabling onboard deployment with a constant 6 ms inference time. The system exhibits comparable setpoint tracking performance to centralized NMPC, with 0.04m positional and 5.78° attitude steady-state errors, and demonstrates strong robustness to load uncertainties and in-flight MAV failures.

## Problem

- Centralized control methods for cooperative multi-MAV cable-suspended load manipulation suffer from exponential computational scaling, limiting scalability, and heavy reliance on global state information and high-bandwidth communication.
- Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) approaches in robotics often require explicit inter-agent communication or onboard perception, and face significant sim-to-real transfer challenges in dynamic aerial manipulation tasks.
- A practical, truly decentralized method capable of real-world 6-DoF manipulation of cable-suspended loads by MAVs, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and onboard deployment, was lacking.

## Method

- A ==Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)== framework within a ==Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE)== paradigm is employed, where policies are trained in simulation and deployed independently on each MAV.
- The method utilizes a novel '==accelerations and body rates (ACCBR)==' action space for the outer-loop RL policy, which is then fed into a robust ==Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (INDI)== low-level controller.
- Each MAV's policy operates with only partial local observations (load pose, ego-MAV state, unique ID) and without any explicit inter-agent communication, coordinating implicitly through shared observations of the load's pose.

## Results

- Achieved the first real-world, decentralized 6-DoF manipulation of a cable-suspended load, demonstrating setpoint tracking performance comparable to state-of-the-art centralized NMPC (final errors of **0.04m** positional, **5.78°** attitude).
- Exhibited high computational efficiency, with a constant **6 ms** inference time per agent deployed onboard a Raspberry Pi 5, significantly outperforming NMPC whose computation time scaled exponentially with agent count.
- Demonstrated strong robustness and fault tolerance in real-world experiments against load mass/distribution mismatches, operation with heterogeneous agents, and the complete in-flight failure of one MAV, allowing remaining agents to maintain control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit coordination through partial observations of the shared load pose is sufficient for decentralized 6-DoF multi-MAV manipulation, effectively eliminating the need for explicit inter-agent communication.
> - The ACCBR action space, when combined with a robust INDI low-level controller, provides the optimal balance of control authority and robustness, enabling successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for highly dynamic cable-suspended load systems.
> - The decentralized architecture, coupled with the robust low-level control, inherently provides strong resilience against external disturbances, load model uncertainties, heterogeneous agents, and even complete in-flight MAV failures, promoting fault tolerance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the control of cooperative Micro-Aerial Vehicle (MAV) teams for manipulating cable-suspended loads, a field with applications in aerial construction, inspection, and logistics in hazardous or remote environments. Traditional approaches to this problem have predominantly utilized centralized control frameworks. These methods, often relying on model simplifications in early research (e.g., quasi-static assumptions), evolved to incorporate system flatness and dynamic equations, leading to cascaded control schemes, inverse dynamics control, linear quadratic regulators, and nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC). Recent state-of-the-art centralized methods, such as those employing whole-body dynamics and NMPC, have demonstrated high agility and accuracy in controlling the full pose of a cable-suspended load.

However, a significant limitation of these centralized strategies is their computational complexity, which tends to scale exponentially with the number of MAVs. This makes real-time control infeasible for larger teams and creates a heavy dependence on global state information and reliable, high-bandwidth communication, which are often impractical in real-world scenarios due to sensor and communication constraints.

Decentralization has been proposed as a solution to these challenges. Distributed model predictive control (MPC) methods have been explored in simulation but often still require inter-agent data transfer, which does not fully resolve communication bandwidth issues. Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for complex multi-agent systems, including cooperative robotics, demonstrating success in tasks like robot football and multi-robot object manipulation. However, many existing MARL approaches in robotics either rely on explicit communication between neighboring agents or onboard perception for coordination. While some MARL applications in object manipulation have shown potential without explicit inter-agent communication, they often depend on handcrafted reward functions and specific object properties. In MAV applications, MARL has been applied to tasks like swarming, but the inherent agility, instability, and requirement for high-frequency, low-latency control in multi-MAV lifting systems present unique sim-to-real and partial observability challenges, particularly concerning dynamic uncertainties from cable tensions and aerodynamic disturbances.

This paper addresses a gap in the existing literature by presenting a decentralized method that achieves real-world 6-DoF manipulation of a cable-suspended load using MAVs, specifically without requiring global state observations or inter-agent communication. It focuses on enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and onboard deployment, thereby advancing the practical applicability and scalability of cooperative aerial manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a decentralized control method for achieving 6-DoF manipulation of a cable-suspended load using a team of Micro-Aerial Vehicles (MAVs) in real-world environments. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific aims, motivated by the limitations of current approaches and the demands of practical applications:

1. **Achieve Decentralized Control without Explicit Communication:** A core objective is to enable each MAV to independently determine its actions without relying on global state information, direct inter-MAV communication, or knowledge of neighboring MAV states. The motivation stems from the exponential computational scaling and communication overhead inherent in centralized control, which limit the scalability and practicality of multi-MAV systems. By allowing agents to communicate implicitly through shared load pose observations, the method aims to enhance scalability and flexibility.

2. **Enable Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The research aims for zero-shot transfer of policies trained exclusively in simulation to real MAVs. This is motivated by the significant sim-to-real gap encountered in highly dynamic cooperative tasks, particularly those involving cable-suspended loads where uncertainties from cable tension and aerodynamic disturbances are substantial. A key part of this objective is to design an action space and integrate a low-level controller that can bridge this gap reliably.

3. **Ensure Computational Efficiency for Onboard Deployment:** The method is designed to be computationally efficient enough for real-time execution directly onboard each MAV. This addresses the practical constraint of limited computational resources on individual MAVs and eliminates the need for offboard processing, which can introduce latency and communication bottlenecks. The aim is to significantly reduce inference costs compared to centralized schemes.

4. **Demonstrate Robustness to Uncertainties and Failures:** A critical objective is to validate the system's robustness under various challenging conditions encountered in real-world deployment. This includes maintaining performance despite:
 - **Load model uncertainties:** Variations in load mass distribution and center of mass that are not explicitly modeled during training.
 - **Heterogeneous agents:** Scenarios where MAVs might operate with different controllers or unexpected behaviors.
 - **In-flight MAV failure:** The ability of the remaining agents to continue the task or safely manage the load after the complete loss of one MAV.
 This motivation arises from the need for reliable operation in unpredictable environments where perfect conditions cannot be guaranteed.

5. **Achieve Comparable Performance to Centralized Baselines:** Despite the constraints of decentralization, the method aims to achieve setpoint tracking performance that is comparable to state-of-the-art centralized controllers. This objective establishes the viability of the decentralized approach for high-precision manipulation tasks.

In summary, the motivation for this work is to overcome the scalability, communication, computational, and robustness limitations of current centralized cooperative aerial manipulation methods by developing a novel decentralized MARL framework that is effective, efficient, and robust for real-world MAV deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method leverages Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) within a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm to enable real-world decentralized 6-DoF manipulation of a cable-suspended load.

**Overall Architecture:**
The approach involves training an outer-loop control policy for each MAV using MARL. This policy generates reference linear accelerations and body rates. These references are then tracked by a robust, model-based low-level controller based on Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (INDI). During training, a centralized critic observes a privileged global state, while individual actors (policies) make decisions based solely on local observations. At execution time, only the decentralized actors are used, running onboard each MAV independently.

**MARL Framework:**
The cooperative aerial manipulation task is formalized as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP). This is defined by a set of agents `I`, the environment state `S`, joint action space `A`, partial observation space `O` for each agent, a transition model `P`, a shared reward function `R`, and a discount factor `γ`. The system employs parameter sharing, meaning all MAVs use a homogeneous policy (π) with shared network parameters, facilitating learning and deployment.

**Observation Space (`o_i`):**
Each MAV's local observation `o_i` is designed to provide sufficient information for coordination without explicit inter-agent communication. It comprises:
- The absolute position (`p_L`) and rotation matrix (`R_L`) of the suspended load.
- The goal position (`d_G`) and relative rotation matrix (`R_G`) of the load, relative to its current state.
- The ego-MAV's complete state (`x_M,i`), including its position, rotation matrix, linear velocity, and angular velocity.
- A one-hot vector (`e_i`) indicating the MAV's unique identity, which allows for role differentiation among agents sharing the same policy.
To address partial observability, the policy inputs a history of observations, specifically stacking the current and the two previous observations (history length H=3).

**Action Space Design (ACCBR):**
A critical component of the methodology is the proposed action space, termed "accelerations and body rates" (ACCBR). The outer-loop RL policy outputs desired linear accelerations (`a_i,ref`) and desired body rates (`ω_i,ref`) for each MAV. This mid-level action space is chosen to balance control authority with robustness, offering a compromise between high-level outputs (e.g., position/velocity, which limit performance but are robust) and low-level outputs (e.g., snap/thrust, which offer precision but suffer from larger sim-to-real discrepancies).

**Low-Level Controller:**
The low-level controller is a robust, model-based system built upon Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (INDI). It translates the ACCBR commands from the outer-loop policy into rotor speed commands.
1. **Thrust Direction Command:** The desired linear acceleration `a_i,ref` is used to compute the desired thrust direction `z_i,des`. This calculation involves compensating for gravity `g` and estimated external forces `f_i,ext` acting on the MAV. The external forces, predominantly from cable tensions, are estimated dynamically using the MAV's mass, filtered accelerometer measurements, and filtered collective thrust computed from rotor speed feedback.
2. **Attitude Control:** The calculated `z_i,des` (which implies a desired attitude) and the `ω_i,ref` (body rate command) from the RL policy are then fed into the INDI attitude controller. This controller generates precise rotor speed commands, enabling agile and smooth control maneuvers while compensating for disturbances.

**Reward Function Formulation (`r_t`):**
A shared reward function guides the MARL training. It consists of multiple components:
- `r_pos`: Rewards for minimizing the position error between the load and the goal.
- `r_ori`: Rewards for minimizing the orientation error between the load and the goal.
- `r_down`: Penalizes MAVs for directing their (proxy) downwash towards the load, promoting stability against aerodynamic disturbances. This is estimated by finding the closest intersection point of the MAV's thrust vector with the load's plane.
- `r_act`: Penalizes large changes in actions between time steps, encouraging smoother control.
- `r_br`: Penalizes large body rates, also contributing to smoother flight.
- `r_thrust`: Penalizes high collective thrust outputs, promoting energy efficiency.
These components are weighted and normalized by the simulation frequency, with specific hyperparameters tuned iteratively.

**Training Setup:**
The training is conducted entirely in simulation using NVIDIA's Isaac Lab environment, with MARL algorithms adapted from the SKRL library. The policy and critic networks are 4-layer MLPs. The MAVs and load are initialized with random positions and headings within specified ranges. Goal poses are also sampled with position, pitch, and roll variations. A form of advantage filtering is employed, dropping 50% of samples with the lowest advantage magnitude to focus learning on more informative state transitions. Cables are modeled as rigid rods for 3-MAV setups and as three rigid segments with ball joints for overconstrained (>3 MAVs) systems to account for cable slack. Episodes last 20 seconds or terminate early if safety conditions (e.g., MAV/payload too close to ground, cable angles, collisions) are violated. Zero-shot transfer to real hardware is achieved.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results from both real-world MAV deployments and simulation studies, demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed decentralized method.

**Real-World Experiments:**

1. **Setpoint Tracking Performance:**
 - The method achieved agile 6-DoF pose control of a cable-suspended load using three MAVs, successfully tracking a 2 m displacement with significant attitude changes (30°, -20°, -90°).
 - Performance was compared to a state-of-the-art centralized Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) method. The decentralized method exhibited comparable tracking, with positional RMSEs of 0.52 m (ours) versus 0.45 m (NMPC), and attitude RMSEs of 22.93° (ours) versus 16.24° (NMPC). While NMPC shows slightly lower RMSEs, it tracks a reference trajectory, whereas the proposed method tracks only target poses, which may contribute to larger transient errors in RMSE comparisons for the latter. In steady-state, the final displacement RMSEs were 0.04 m / 5.78° (ours) compared to 0.05 m / 4.02° (NMPC), indicating similar final accuracy.
 - The time-to-target (error < 0.10 m/10°) was 8.36 s for the proposed method and 6.84 s for NMPC.
 - The method also demonstrated successful pose control with four MAVs, albeit with increased RMSEs (0.92 m positional, 42.67° attitude), which may be attributed to the system becoming overconstrained and introducing more complex dynamics.

2. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - Deployed onboard a Raspberry Pi 5, the decentralized policy performed inference in 6 ms at 100 Hz. Critically, its computation time remained constant irrespective of the number of agents.
 - In contrast, the NMPC method required 78 ms at 10 Hz for three agents, with computation time scaling exponentially (e.g., 174 ms for five agents and 267 ms for six agents). This highlights the scalability advantage of the decentralized approach.

3. **Robustness Against Load Model Mismatch:**
 - The system maintained robust tracking performance even when additional objects (0.216 kg, 15.4% of original load mass) were added, dynamically perturbing the load's mass distribution and center of mass.
 - Positional RMSE was 0.63 m (with mismatch) vs 0.60 m (without), and attitude RMSE was 26.93° vs 26.49°. This resilience is attributed to the low-level feedback controller compensating for disturbances.

4. **Heterogeneous Agent Operation:**
 - The policy demonstrated effectiveness in a heterogeneous setting where one MAV used a different, manually controlled, model-based controller to perturb the load (e.g., pulling it away or pushing it closer).
 - The two remaining MAVs, controlled by the RL policy, effectively compensated for these external interferences, maintaining load pose stability. The partially observable policy showed lower RMSEs (0.28 m position, 8.88° attitude) compared to a fully observable policy (0.42 m position, 30.08° attitude) in simulated equivalent conditions. This robustness is linked to the policy's independence from other agents' states.

5. **Robustness to In-Flight MAV Failure:**
 - The system exhibited strong fault tolerance to the complete in-flight failure of one MAV. The remaining two MAVs were able to control the load's remaining 5 degrees of freedom (with orientation around the line joining the remaining attachment points becoming unactuated).
 - Even with a failed MAV hanging and causing additional disturbances, the system successfully performed complex maneuvers, including a -180° yaw and positional control (0.5 m down, 1 m along the y-axis).
 - The partially observable policy achieved RMSEs of 0.67 m position and 50.31° attitude in this scenario, outperforming a fully observable policy (1.50 m position, 73.37° attitude) which exhibited unstable, oscillatory behavior. This resilience is attributed to the decentralized policy's independence from neighboring agent states, preventing cascading failures.

6. **Trajectory Tracking (Appendix A.4):**
 - While not trained for trajectory tracking, the method was evaluated on a figure-eight trajectory. It successfully tracked the trajectory, albeit with higher errors (0.82 m positional RMSE, 18.22° attitude RMSE) compared to NMPC (0.10 m positional RMSE, 4.80° attitude RMSE), which utilizes velocity information and future reference points.

**Simulation Comparisons (Gazebo environment):**

1. **Action Space Comparison:**
 - **ACCBR (Proposed):** Achieved 0.64 m positional RMSE and 33.87° attitude RMSE, offering stable hovering.
 - **VEL (Velocity commands):** Achieved the lowest RMSEs (0.56 m positional, 25.74° attitude) but resulted in hazardous oscillations, making it less suitable for stability-critical tasks.
 - **ACC (Linear acceleration only):** Performed poorly in orientation tracking (87.89° attitude RMSE).
 - **CTBR (Collective thrust and body rates):** Failed to learn effectively and could not take off. This failure is hypothesized to be due to the unpredictable cable forces not being compensated by a low-level controller.
 - Conclusion: ACCBR provided the most effective balance of performance and stability for robust sim-to-real transfer.

2. **Observation Space Comparison:**
 - Testing with fully observable (global state), augmented partial (load twist + neighbors' positions), and the proposed partially observable (load pose, ego-MAV state, ID) observation spaces showed comparable convergence.
 - This suggests that load pose information alone provides a sufficient statistic for implicit MAV coordination, indicating the efficiency of the chosen observation space.
 - **Impact of Centralized Critic:** Training with a centralized critic (access to global state) significantly outperformed a local critic (access only to local observations), which struggled to converge. This underscores the importance of the CTDE paradigm for effective credit assignment.
 - **History Length:** Varying the history length (H=1 to H=10) in the partially observable policy showed minimal impact on performance, implying that the instantaneous load pose contains enough information for coordination in the evaluated scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of cooperative aerial manipulation, particularly concerning the practical deployment of multi-MAV systems. The primary significance lies in the **first real-world demonstration of fully decentralized, 6-DoF manipulation of a cable-suspended load using Micro-Aerial Vehicles (MAVs) without any inter-MAV communication**, with policies deployed entirely onboard.

The key contributions that underpin this significance include:

1. **Scalability and Efficiency:** By eliminating the need for global state information and inter-MAV communication, the method bypasses the exponential computational scaling and communication bottlenecks that plague centralized approaches. The constant, low inference time (6 ms at 100 Hz on a Raspberry Pi 5) regardless of team size makes the system highly scalable and suitable for real-time onboard deployment, addressing a critical barrier to wider adoption of multi-MAV systems.

2. **Robustness and Fault Tolerance:** The demonstrated resilience to significant load model uncertainties, operation with heterogeneous agents, and the ability to continue operations after the complete in-flight failure of an MAV are major practical achievements. This robustness is attributed to a synergistic combination of:
 - **The robust low-level controller:** Based on Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (INDI), which inherently compensates for external disturbances and model mismatches.
 - **The decentralized policy architecture:** Where individual agents operate independently without reliance on neighboring states, preventing cascading failures and allowing remaining agents to compensate for unexpected events.
 This level of robustness is crucial for real-world applications in unpredictable and dynamic environments.

3. **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The introduction of a novel action space (accelerations and body rates, ACCBR) in conjunction with the robust INDI low-level controller enabled successful zero-shot transfer from simulation to real hardware. This addresses a pervasive challenge in robotics and indicates a promising direction for developing generalizable control policies. The comparative analysis confirmed ACCBR's superior balance of stability and performance for this specific task compared to alternative action spaces.

4. **Implicit Coordination through Partial Observability:** The finding that load pose observations alone are sufficient for implicit MAV coordination, without explicit inter-agent communication, simplifies sensing requirements and communication infrastructure. This reduces the complexity and cost of deploying such systems.

**Potential Impact:**

The potential impact of this work is substantial across several domains:

- **Expanded Applications of Multi-MAV Systems:** By making cooperative aerial manipulation more scalable, robust, and computationally efficient, this research can unlock new possibilities for applications such as:
 - **Aerial Construction:** Assembling large structures, lifting and placing components in dangerous or inaccessible areas.
 - **Large-Scale Inspection and Maintenance:** Inspecting infrastructure or complex machinery without human intervention.
 - **Disaster Response and Logistics:** Delivering supplies or clearing debris in hazardous zones where human access is limited.
 - **Environmental Monitoring:** Deploying and retrieving sensors over large areas.
- **Advancement in Multi-Agent Robotics:** The methodologies, particularly the decentralized MARL approach with a carefully designed action space and robust low-level control, provide a blueprint for addressing complex coordination problems in other multi-robot systems.
- **Further Research Directions:** The work lays a foundation for future research by highlighting the effectiveness of implicit communication and robust low-level control. It also explicitly identifies limitations, such as the reliance on external motion capture for load pose in the current setup and the absence of obstacle avoidance, prompting future work on integrating robust onboard perception (e.g., vision-based load pose estimation, SLAM) and developing navigation capabilities for unstructured environments.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes to bridging the gap between theoretical multi-agent control and practical, real-world deployment of cooperative aerial manipulation systems, offering a robust, scalable, and efficient solution for complex tasks.
