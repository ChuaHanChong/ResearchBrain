# SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SEED (SElf-Evolving On-Policy Distillation) enables large language model (LLM) agents to learn more effectively from their own experiences by generating and internalizing self-evolving, on-policy hindsight skills. This framework achieves substantial performance gains, such as a 14.9 to 45.9 percentage point improvement in macro-average success rate over outcome-only reinforcement learning on ALFWorld, through dense, token-level supervision and enhanced sample efficiency.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) acting as interactive agents for long-horizon tasks suffer from a 'supervision gap' due to sparse, delayed, and trajectory-level rewards from outcome-based reinforcement learning (RL).
- Scalar rewards provide insufficient guidance for fine-grained, token-level policy learning, especially for intermediate decisions in complex multi-turn interactions.
- Existing on-policy self-distillation methods often rely on static or externally generated privileged supervision, which can become outdated or mismatched with the current policy's evolving behavior.

## Method

- **SEED** employs a two-stage training process: ==Hindsight Skill Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== to enable the LLM policy model to generate natural-language hindsight skills from completed trajectories, initially annotated by an external LLM.
- In the ==Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation (OPD)== stage, the same policy acts as both an actor and a self-evolving analyzer, generating on-policy hindsight skills from newly collected trajectories.
- A ==confidence-gated distillation loss== integrates this dense, token-level hindsight guidance into the policy's parameters, alongside a standard ==reinforcement learning== objective, ensuring continuous adaptation of supervision.

## Results

- SEED consistently improved macro-average success rates, achieving a **14.9 to 45.9 percentage point** gain over outcome-only RL (GRPO) on ALFWorld and generally outperforming other distillation baselines across diverse benchmarks.
- Policies trained with SEED demonstrated enhanced sample efficiency and faster learning, achieving higher performance with **60%** of the training data compared to GRPO with the full dataset, and reducing mean episode length.
- The framework exhibited robust cross-domain generalization, improving the macro-average success rate by **15.3 percentage points** on ALFWorld's unseen split, and successfully extended to vision-based tasks with a **14.0 percentage point** average success rate improvement over GRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internalizing self-generated, dense, and on-policy hindsight skills directly into the policy parameters is more effective than providing them as external prompts during inference.
> - Synchronizing the policy's acting and trajectory-analysis capabilities (self-evolving hindsight) ensures the supervision remains relevant and adapts to the agent's current behaviors and evolving strategies.
> - Converting implicit hindsight from both successful and failed trajectories into explicit, token-level guidance effectively bridges the 'supervision gap' inherent in long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the application of large language models (LLMs) as interactive agents, particularly for long-horizon tasks involving multi-turn interaction, tool use, and environment feedback. Such agentic LLMs rely on reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize their policies against task-level feedback.

The broader landscape of agentic RL for LLMs faces a fundamental limitation: outcome-based RL typically provides sparse, delayed, and trajectory-level rewards. This creates a "supervision gap" where episode-level outcomes offer limited guidance for fine-grained, token-level policy learning, especially for intermediate decisions. While successful trajectories may contain reusable strategies and failed ones may reveal specific mistakes, scalar rewards do not explicitly identify these nuances.

Previous research has explored several avenues to address sparse rewards:
- **Hindsight Learning**: Techniques like hindsight relabeling, verbal reflection, and episodic memory leverage information from completed trajectories to improve learning. However, many of these methods treat hindsight as static experience, inference-time context, or retrieved memory, which may not adapt to an agent's evolving capabilities.
- **On-Policy Distillation (OPD)**: This paradigm involves transferring "teacher" behavior to a "student" policy using token- or sequence-level supervision, often by comparing the same model under different contexts (e.g., privileged reasoning traces). Existing on-policy self-distillation methods often use static or externally generated privileged supervision, which can become stale or mismatched with the current policy's behavior and distribution.

This paper positions itself by proposing a framework that seeks to overcome these limitations by generating *self-evolving*, *on-policy*, and *dense* hindsight supervision. Instead of relying on static external teachers or inference-time skill prompting, the proposed method integrates the learning of decision-making with the learning of hindsight analysis within the same model, ensuring that the supervision dynamically adapts to the agent's progress. It aims to internalize behavioral guidance directly into the policy parameters, removing the need for external memory or augmented prompts during deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the "supervision gap" prevalent in long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs). This gap arises because outcome-based RL often provides sparse and delayed rewards at the trajectory level, offering insufficient guidance for the fine-grained, token-level decisions that LLM agents must make over extended interactions.

The motivation behind developing SEED stems from several observations and requirements:

1. **Leveraging Hindsight from Completed Trajectories**: Regardless of success or failure, a completed trajectory contains rich, albeit often implicit, hindsight information. This includes reusable workflows, decisive observations, and failure-avoidance rules that are not apparent during online decision-making. The goal is to explicitly extract and utilize this information.
2. **Addressing the Limitations of Outcome-Only RL**: Scalar trajectory-level rewards cannot differentiate between locally useful partial behaviors within a failed trajectory or identify reusable strategies within a successful one. This leads to inefficient credit assignment. Therefore, the work aims to supplement outcome-based RL with a more granular, decision-level signal.
3. **Ensuring On-Policy Supervision**: Effective corrections and guidance are highly dependent on the specific states, actions, and failure modes encountered by the *current* policy. Static or off-policy supervision can lead to distribution mismatch and reduced relevance. The objective is to ensure that the auxiliary supervision is derived directly from the current policy's own experiences.
4. **Providing Dense, Token-Level Guidance**: To effectively guide individual decision tokens, hindsight information needs to be converted into a dense signal that informs choices at each step, rather than just reinforcing or penalizing entire trajectories.
5. **Achieving Self-Evolving Adaptation**: As the agent's policy improves, it explores new states, develops new strategies, and encounters different failure modes. The hindsight supervision should adapt synchronously with these changes. This necessitates a self-evolving mechanism where the policy's decision-making and its trajectory-analysis capabilities advance together, preventing the supervision from becoming stale or misaligned.
6. **Internalizing Behavioral Guidance**: A practical motivation is to internalize this rich behavioral guidance directly into the policy model's parameters. This design choice eliminates the need for external memory, separate skill banks, retrieval modules, or additional prompts during inference time, simplifying deployment and reducing computational overhead in real-world applications.

By integrating these requirements, the research aims to develop a robust and efficient learning framework that allows LLM agents to learn more effectively from their own experiences in complex, long-horizon environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, SEED (SElf-Evolving On-Policy Distillation), consists of two main training stages designed to imbue a policy model with the ability to both act and analyze its own trajectories, internalizing valuable hindsight guidance.

**Stage 1: Hindsight Skill Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**

This initial stage prepares the LLM policy model to generate natural-language "hindsight skills" from completed interaction histories.
1. **Offline Trajectory Collection**: A base policy (π_θ_base) is used to collect an offline dataset of trajectories. For a set of training tasks, multiple rollouts (e.g., 8 per task) are executed in the environment, generating diverse interaction histories, including observations, actions, rewards, and final outcomes (success/failure). These trajectories are collected without any pre-existing skill augmentation.
2. **Hindsight Skill Annotation**: An *external, powerful LLM analyzer* (e.g., GLM-5.2) processes each collected trajectory. For successful trajectories, it generates a skill describing reusable strategies or workflows. For failed trajectories, it extracts corrective or avoidance guidance. Only skills adhering to a specific format are retained, forming a supervised fine-tuning dataset consisting of trajectory-skill pairs.
3. **Supervised Fine-Tuning**: The base policy model is fine-tuned on this dataset using a standard negative log-likelihood objective. This process trains the model to predict the hindsight skill given a serialized completed trajectory. The resulting SFT-tuned model (π_θ_sft) then serves as the initial policy for the subsequent RL stage and, crucially, as the *initial* synchronized trajectory analyzer. This means the same model is now capable of both acting in the environment and generating analytical hindsight.

**Stage 2: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation**

This stage integrates reinforcement learning with a continuous, self-evolving distillation process.
1. **Policy Snapshot and Experience Collection**: At the beginning of each training iteration, the current policy's parameters are frozen, creating a snapshot (π_θ_old). This frozen policy serves as the *actor* to collect a group of `N` on-policy trajectories for each task in the current batch.
2. **Synchronized Hindsight Skill Generation**: For each of these newly collected trajectories, the *same frozen snapshot* π_θ_old, acting as the *analyzer*, generates a corresponding natural-language hindsight skill. This shared parameterization is central to the "self-evolving" aspect: as the policy improves through training, its ability to both execute actions and analyze its own experiences improves concurrently, ensuring the generated hindsight supervision remains relevant and high-quality.
3. **On-Policy Distillation (OPD) Objective**:
 - For every action token generated by the actor during trajectory collection, SEED re-scores it under two contexts using the *trainable policy* (π_θ, initialized from π_θ_old):
 - **Student Branch**: The log-probability of the token given the ordinary interaction history.
 - **Teacher Branch**: The log-probability of the token given the interaction history *augmented with the hindsight skill* generated for that specific trajectory.
 - The "teacher" (skill-augmented) log-probability is detached, meaning gradients do not flow back through it. A **skill-induced log-probability shift** is calculated by comparing the detached teacher and student log-probabilities.
 - This shift is then converted into a **confidence gate** using a sigmoid function. A positive shift (meaning the skill supports the sampled action) results in a larger gate value, amplifying the distillation signal, while a negative shift attenuates it.
 - The **OPD loss** is a confidence-gated negative log-likelihood, encouraging the ordinary policy to increase the probability of actions strongly supported by the generated hindsight skills.
4. **Joint Training Objective**:
 - **Reinforcement Learning Loss (L_RL)**: A group-relative RL objective (similar to GRPO) is employed. It computes normalized advantages for each trajectory within a rollout group based on its terminal reward, and these advantages are used with a clipped importance-ratio objective to update the policy. A KL regularization term is also included.
 - **Combined Loss (L_SEED)**: The final objective is a weighted sum: L_SEED(θ) = L_RL(θ) + λ_opd * L_OPD(θ). This ensures that the policy simultaneously optimizes for task-level outcomes and internalizes the dense behavioral guidance from self-generated hindsight skills.
5. **Self-Evolving Loop Closure**: After the policy update, the newly optimized π_θ becomes the π_θ_old for the next iteration, completing the self-evolving loop.
6. **Inference Time**: During deployment, the agent acts solely based on the ordinary interaction history. All hindsight skills are used exclusively during training, as their behavioral effects are internalized into the policy parameters.

**Experimental Setup**:
- **Benchmarks**: Evaluated on ALFWorld (text-based embodied interaction), WebShop (web navigation), and Search-based QA (tool-augmented information seeking). A multimodal extension was also tested on Sokoban and EZPoints.
- **Backbone Models**: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-1.7B-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct for multimodal tasks.
- **Baselines**: Included prompting-only methods (Vanilla, Skill-Prompt*), outcome-based RL (GRPO, Skill-GRPO, Skill-GRPO*), and various self-distillation or skill-distillation methods (OPSD, GRPO+OPSD, Skill-SD, RLSD, SDAR).
- **Metrics**: Success rate (ALFWorld), answer accuracy (Search-based QA), and task-completion score/success rate (WebShop).
- **Training Details**: 150 policy updates, rollout group size of 8, with varying batch sizes depending on the benchmark. The external analyzer for SFT used GLM-5.2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SEED consistently improves agent performance, sample efficiency, and generalization across diverse long-horizon agentic benchmarks.

1. **Superior Task Performance**:
 - SEED consistently outperformed outcome-only RL methods (GRPO) across all three primary benchmarks and model sizes. For instance, on ALFWorld, SEED improved the macro-average success rate by 14.9 to 45.9 percentage points over GRPO.
 - Compared to other skill-distillation methods (OPSD, Skill-SD, RLSD, SDAR), SEED achieved the best or tied-best results in 10 out of 12 aggregate comparisons, with particularly strong advantages on ALFWorld (e.g., 7.4 to 38.1 points over the strongest static baseline).
 - SEED also generally outperformed baselines that incorporate skills during training or evaluation (Skill-GRPO, Skill-GRPO*), indicating the effectiveness of its dense, token-level hindsight supervision and internalization approach.

2. **Internalization of Skills vs. External Prompting**:
 - Methods that relied on providing natural-language skills as inference-time prompts (Skill-Prompt\*, Skill-GRPO\*) consistently underperformed SEED. This suggests that distilling hindsight skills directly into the policy parameters, as SEED does, is more effective than supplying them as external context at test time.

3. **Benefit of Self-Evolving Hindsight**:
 - The consistent performance advantages of SEED over static self-distillation methods underscored the value of its self-evolving mechanism. By synchronizing the analyzer with the latest policy, SEED ensures that the generated hindsight supervision adapts to the current behavior, strategies, and failure modes encountered during training, preventing it from becoming stale.

4. **Improved Training Dynamics and Efficiency**:
 - On ALFWorld, SEED achieved higher success rates earlier in training compared to GRPO and significantly reduced the mean episode length. This indicates that SEED leads to more efficient task execution and the learning of more direct solution strategies.
 - The framework demonstrated enhanced sample efficiency. For example, SEED achieved higher performance with 60% of the training data than GRPO did with the full dataset, suggesting it extracts more informative supervision from each collected trajectory.

5. **Robust Cross-Domain Generalization**:
 - On the ALFWorld unseen split, SEED improved the macro-average success rate by 15.3 percentage points over GRPO, showing better performance in five out of six task families. This indicates that SEED-trained policies acquire reusable behavioral strategies that generalize effectively to novel environments rather than merely memorizing training trajectories.

6. **Multimodal Extension (Supplementary Results)**:
 - SEED was successfully applied to vision-based agentic tasks (Sokoban and EZPoints) using a multimodal backbone. It achieved success rates of 82.0% on Sokoban and 100.0% on EZPoints, improving average success rate by 14.0 percentage points over GRPO, demonstrating its applicability beyond text-only interactions.

7. **Ablation Studies**:
 - Removing Hindsight Skill SFT (Stage 1) reduced ALFWorld average performance by 5.8 points, highlighting the importance of initializing the model's trajectory-analysis capability.
 - Removing Self-Evolving OPD (Stage 2) led to a 4.8-point drop, indicating that continuous distillation from newly collected trajectories is crucial beyond one-time SFT.
 - Replacing on-policy skills with skills from a static offline library resulted in the largest performance degradation (7.4 points), emphasizing the importance of deriving guidance from the *current* policy's own evolving trajectories.

8. **Qualitative Analysis**:
 - Qualitative examples from ALFWorld illustrate that SEED-trained agents exhibit more coherent and goal-directed trajectories, effectively decomposing tasks, managing preconditions, and systematically searching for objects, in contrast to GRPO agents that might engage in unproductive exploration or off-task loops.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SEED framework presents a notable advancement in the field of agentic reinforcement learning for large language models, with several significant implications:

1. **Addressing the Sparse Reward Problem**: SEED provides a practical and effective solution to the fundamental challenge of sparse and delayed rewards in long-horizon RL. By converting implicit hindsight into dense, token-level supervision, it enables more granular credit assignment, which is crucial for complex sequential decision-making tasks.
2. **Enhancing Agent Learning Efficiency and Performance**: The empirical results demonstrate consistent and substantial improvements in task performance, sample efficiency, and training dynamics across diverse benchmarks and model scales. This suggests that SEED can lead to more capable and faster-learning LLM agents.
3. **Advancing Self-Supervised and Adaptive Learning**: The self-evolving nature of SEED, where the policy acts as both the decision-maker and the analyzer of its own experience, represents a significant step towards more autonomous and adaptive AI agents. This mechanism ensures that the internal supervision remains aligned with the agent's current capabilities and evolving understanding of the environment, reducing reliance on static external knowledge or separate teacher models.
4. **Practicality for Deployment**: A key strength of SEED is that the hindsight skills are internalized during training and are not required at inference time. This design choice eliminates the need for additional computational overhead, such as skill retrieval or context augmentation, during deployment, making SEED-trained agents more efficient for real-world applications.
5. **Broad Applicability**: The demonstrated effectiveness across text-based embodied interaction, web navigation, search-based question answering, and even vision-based tasks suggests that SEED's principles are broadly applicable to various forms of agentic interaction. This could unlock improvements in a wide array of LLM-powered agent systems.
6. **Foundation for Future Research**: SEED opens several avenues for further research:
 - **Robustness to Self-Generated Errors**: Investigating methods to enhance the reliability of self-generated hindsight skills and mitigate the risk of reinforcing model errors (e.g., through confidence-aware skill generation, cross-policy validation, or anchoring skills to verifiable state changes).
 - **Scalability to Ultra-Long-Horizon Tasks**: Exploring SEED's performance in even more complex and extended task environments, which often present greater challenges in maintaining decisive event tracking and rare terminal successes.
 - **Training Efficiency Optimizations**: Developing techniques (e.g., speculative decoding for skill generation, selective trajectory analysis based on novelty or uncertainty) to reduce the computational cost of the two-stage training process.
 - **Hierarchical Skill Learning**: Extending the framework to learn hierarchical skills or abstract reasoning patterns from hindsight, further improving complex planning and multi-step tasks.

In summary, SEED provides a robust and generalizable framework that significantly advances the state of agentic RL for LLMs by effectively leveraging and internalizing self-generated, evolving hindsight, offering a promising direction for developing more capable and autonomous AI agents.
