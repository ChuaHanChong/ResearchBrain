# Dual-Robust Cross-Domain Offline Reinforcement Learning Against Dynamics Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at CityUHK and collaborators introduced Dual-RObust Cross-domain Offline RL (DROCO), a framework addressing both training-time dynamics mismatches and deployment-time dynamics perturbations in reinforcement learning settings with limited target domain data. DROCO, featuring a Robust Cross-domain Bellman (RCB) operator, demonstrated superior performance and consistently higher robustness against various dynamics shifts compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents require extensive datasets, often leading to data scarcity which is addressed by leveraging data from a related source domain with differing dynamics.
- Existing Cross-Domain Offline RL methods primarily focus on 'train-time robustness' to handle dynamics mismatches between source and target environments during policy learning.
- Policies trained with current cross-domain offline RL methods exhibit high fragility to unforeseen dynamics perturbations encountered during *deployment* (test-time robustness), especially when target domain data is limited.

## Method

- A ==Dual-RObust== Cross-domain Offline RL (**DROCO**) framework is proposed, introducing a novel Robust Cross-domain Bellman (RCB) operator designed for dual robustness against dynamics shifts.
- The RCB operator applies a standard Bellman update for target domain data and a robust Bellman update (incorporating an infimum over a Wasserstein dynamics uncertainty set) for source domain data to account for worst-case dynamics.
- Practical implementation of the RCB operator uses a dual reformulation for tractable state uncertainty, ensemble dynamics modeling for uncertainty estimation, a dynamic value penalty to mitigate value over/underestimation, and Huber loss for robust Bellman updates.

## Results

- Motivational experiments revealed that policies trained with **10%** of target domain data experienced significantly greater performance degradation (up to **77.3%**) under test-time kinematic shifts compared to policies trained with full data (**72.4%**).
- DROCO achieved superior train-time robustness across both kinematic and morphology shifts, yielding total normalized scores of 1105.2 and 1166.4 respectively, outperforming the second-best baseline (OTDF) by **14.0%** and **13.8%**.
- The framework consistently demonstrated enhanced test-time robustness against various dynamics perturbations (kinematic, morphology, min Q), suffering substantially less performance degradation (e.g., **19.3%** for easy kinematic shifts vs. over **50%** for baselines) across different target dataset sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-domain offline RL policies, particularly when trained with limited target data, can overfit to the observed dynamics, making them highly susceptible to performance degradation under test-time dynamics perturbations.
> - The proposed Robust Cross-domain Bellman (RCB) operator is theoretically proven to be a gamma-contraction, ensuring convergence, and provides formal guarantees for both train-time conservatism and test-time resilience.
> - A controllable trade-off exists between train-time robustness (preventing overestimation from out-of-distribution source dynamics) and test-time robustness (ensuring performance under bounded target dynamics perturbations), managed by the uncertainty set's epsilon parameter.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often necessitate extensive online interaction with an environment to learn effective policies. Offline RL addresses the cost and risk associated with online interaction by training policies exclusively from pre-logged datasets. However, a limitation of offline RL is its reliance on a large and well-covering offline dataset. To mitigate this data scarcity, Cross-Domain Offline RL has emerged, a paradigm where limited data from a target domain is augmented with more abundant data from a related but distinct source domain, which typically exhibits differing transition dynamics.

Existing research in Cross-Domain Offline RL has predominantly focused on *train-time robustness*. This involves developing methods to handle the dynamics mismatch that exists between the source and target environments during the training phase. Approaches include learning domain classifiers to quantify dynamics gaps, filtering source data based on information-theoretic measures, or utilizing optimal transport techniques. These methods aim to prevent policy divergence and ensure robust performance in the target environment given the disparate training data.

However, a gap in the current literature is the consideration of *test-time robustness* within the Cross-Domain Offline RL setting. This refers to the ability of a learned policy to maintain performance when deployed in a real-world environment where its dynamics may perturb or shift from the observed target domain dynamics. Such shifts can occur due to factors like component degradation in robotics or unforeseen environmental variations. While Robust RL is a recognized field, much of its work focuses on single-domain settings or addresses train-time data corruption rather than the specific interplay of cross-domain data and test-time dynamics shifts.

This paper addresses this identified gap by initiating the investigation into *dual robustness* in cross-domain offline RL, encompassing both train-time and test-time resilience to dynamics shifts. It extends beyond single-domain robust RL by combining the challenges of limited target data, a disparate source domain, and deployment-time dynamics perturbations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The work is motivated by an empirical observation regarding the sensitivity of policies trained with existing cross-domain offline RL methods. The authors demonstrate that such policies can be highly fragile to dynamics perturbations encountered during evaluation or deployment, particularly when the amount of target domain data used for training is limited.

Specifically, the motivational experiments showed that a policy trained on a `hopper-expert-v2` task (target domain) with a `hopper-kinematic-v2` source domain (simulating kinematic shifts by constraining joint rotation range) exhibited significant performance degradation under test-time kinematic perturbations. This degradation was substantially more pronounced when the target domain dataset was reduced (e.g., 10% of the original D4RL dataset) compared to using a full dataset. For instance, with 10% target data, performance degradation under "hard" kinematic shifts intensified to 77.3%, versus 72.4% with full data, and similarly for "medium" shifts (56.2% vs. 40.9%). This phenomenon is attributed to the policy overfitting to the limited observed dynamics in the target domain, which reduces its ability to generalize to unseen dynamics perturbations at deployment.

Based on this empirical evidence, the key objectives of this research are:

1. To empirically demonstrate and quantify the fragility of cross-domain offline RL policies to test-time dynamics shifts, especially under conditions of limited target domain data.
2. To formally investigate and propose a framework for achieving *dual robustness* in cross-domain offline RL, addressing both train-time dynamics shifts (mismatch between source and target training data) and test-time dynamics perturbations (mismatch between target and deployment environments).
3. To introduce a novel robust cross-domain Bellman (RCB) operator designed to enhance test-time robustness against dynamics perturbations while maintaining conservatism with respect to out-of-distribution (OOD) dynamics transitions from the source domain, thereby ensuring train-time robustness.
4. To develop practical techniques that mitigate potential value overestimation or underestimation issues that may arise from the application of the proposed RCB operator.
5. To integrate these components into a practical algorithm, Dual-RObust Cross-domain Offline RL (DROCO), and empirically validate its performance and robustness against a range of dynamics shift scenarios and strong baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Dual-RObust Cross-domain Offline RL (DROCO), aims to achieve dual robustness against dynamics shifts by introducing a novel Bellman operator and complementary practical techniques.

**Robust Cross-domain Bellman (RCB) Operator:**
The core of DROCO is the RCB operator. It applies a differential approach to Bellman updates based on the data source:
- For transitions originating from the **target domain dataset** (D_tar), the standard in-sample Bellman operator is used. This maximizes performance in the nominal target environment.
- For transitions originating from the **source domain dataset** (D_src), a robust Bellman operator is applied. This operator incorporates an infimum over a dynamics uncertainty set (M_epsilon), specifically a Wasserstein uncertainty set, to consider worst-case dynamics. The formal definition involves `inf_{M_c in M_epsilon} E_{s'~P_Mc(.|s,a)} [max_{a'~hat_mu(.|s')} Q(s',a')]`. The authors theoretically demonstrate that this RCB operator is a $\gamma$-contraction.

**Practical RCB Operator via Dual Reformulation:**
A direct application of the robust Bellman operator is often intractable because the dynamics uncertainty set M_epsilon is unknown and difficult to work with. To address this, the authors utilize a dual reformulation for the Wasserstein uncertainty set. This reformulation transforms the optimization over unknown dynamics into an optimization over state perturbations. The intractable `inf_{M_c in M_epsilon} E_{s'~P_Mc(.|s,a)} [...]` term becomes `E_{s'~P_M(.|s,a)} [inf_{s_bar in U_epsilon(s')} max_{a'~hat_mu(.|s_bar)} Q(s_bar, a')]`, where `U_epsilon(s')` is a tractable state uncertainty set (states within an $\epsilon$-radius of $s'$). This "practical RCB operator" also retains the $\gamma$-contraction property.

**Dual Robustness Guarantee (Theoretical Analysis):**
Under the assumption of a Lipschitz Q-function, the authors provide theoretical guarantees for dual robustness:
- **Train-time Robustness:** Proposition 4.4 states that if $\epsilon$ is chosen such that the support of the target dynamics `P_tar` is covered by the uncertainty set `U_epsilon(s'_src)` for source transitions, then the learned Q-function (`Q_RCB`) is conservatively bounded, preventing overestimation due to OOD dynamics from the source.
- **Test-time Robustness:** Proposition 4.5 demonstrates that for any perturbed deployment dynamics `P_per` whose Wasserstein distance from `P_tar` is below a certain threshold `c` (which is related to $\epsilon$), the value of the policy learned by DROCO in `P_per` is guaranteed to be no less than the value learned under the worst-case scenario. This implies improved performance under test-time dynamics perturbations. The theoretical analysis also highlights a trade-off between the two robustness notions, controllable by $\epsilon$.

**Practical Algorithm Components of DROCO:**
To implement the practical RCB operator and mitigate value estimation challenges, DROCO incorporates several techniques:
1. **Ensemble Dynamics Modeling for Uncertainty Set:** Instead of a fixed $\epsilon$ and random sampling, DROCO trains an *ensemble of neural network dynamics models* (`P_psi`) using Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) on the *target domain dataset* (D_tar). The predictions from this ensemble provide samples (`s'_1..., s'_N`) that approximate the state uncertainty set `U_epsilon(s')` for source domain transitions. This approach focuses on covering the support of `P_tar` rather than `s'_src`, aiming for targeted conservatism.
2. **Dynamic Value Penalty:** To address potential value overestimation or underestimation (due to the infimum operator or dynamics model errors), a dynamic value penalty `u(s,a,s')` is introduced. This penalty is applied specifically to source domain data and is calculated as the difference between the current value function `V(s')` and the minimum `V` among the ensemble predictions `inf[{V(s'_i)}]`. A coefficient `beta` allows flexible control over the penalty strength.
3. **Huber Loss for Bellman Update:** The standard L2 loss in the Bellman update for source domain data is replaced with Huber loss. Huber loss combines the properties of L2 loss (for small errors) and L1 loss (for large errors), making the value function update more robust to outliers and large estimation errors, thus enhancing stability.
4. **Policy Optimization:** The overall framework leverages existing offline RL algorithms, such as Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), to optimize the policy using the modified Bellman targets that incorporate the RCB operator, dynamic value penalty, and Huber loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to answer two primary questions: whether DROCO outperforms baselines across train-time dynamics shifts and dataset qualities, and whether it exhibits enhanced robustness against test-time dynamics perturbations.

#### 4.1. Sensitivity to Test-Time Perturbations (Motivation Study)

The initial empirical study confirmed that cross-domain offline RL policies are sensitive to test-time dynamics perturbations, particularly when target domain data is limited. Policies trained with 10% of the D4RL `hopper-expert-v2` dataset showed significantly greater performance degradation under kinematic, morphology, and min Q perturbations compared to policies trained with 100% of the data. This supports the paper's motivation for dual robustness.

#### 4.2. Train-Time Robustness against Dynamics Shifts

- **Kinematic Shifts (Table 1):** DROCO achieved superior performance in 9 out of 16 tasks involving train-time kinematic shifts. In terms of total normalized score across all tasks, DROCO scored 1105.2, surpassing the second-best method (OTDF) by 14.0% (969.8).
- **Morphology Shifts (Table 2):** Similarly, under train-time morphology shifts, DROCO again outperformed baselines in 9 out of 16 tasks and achieved the highest total normalized score of 1166.4, exceeding OTDF (1025.1).
These results indicate that DROCO demonstrates strong train-time robustness in handling dynamics mismatches between source and target domains.

#### 4.3. Test-Time Robustness against Dynamics Perturbations

- **Kinematic, Morphology, and Min Q Perturbations (Figure 2, Figure 6):** DROCO consistently exhibited superior robustness across various types (kinematic, morphology, min Q) and levels (easy, medium, hard) of test-time dynamics perturbations, compared to IGDF and OTDF. For instance, under easy kinematic shifts on `halfcheetah-kinematic-expert`, DROCO experienced a 19.3% performance degradation, while IGDF and OTDF suffered over 50% degradation.
- **Robustness Across Data Sizes (Figure 7):** DROCO maintained its superior test-time robustness even with varying target domain dataset sizes (100%, 50%, 10% D4RL) under min Q perturbations, consistently outperforming baselines.
The findings suggest that DROCO enhances the deployment reliability of learned policies in environments prone to dynamics variations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Dynamic Value Penalty (Figure 8, Figure 9):** The dynamic value penalty generally led to better performance than a fixed penalty (beta=1.0). While a fixed penalty sometimes yielded marginally better robustness, it often came at the cost of overall performance, indicating a trade-off.
- **Huber Loss vs. L2 Loss (Figure 10, Figure 11):** Employing Huber loss for Bellman updates generally resulted in superior performance and consistently stronger test-time robustness compared to using the standard L2 loss, highlighting its effectiveness in mitigating value estimation errors.

#### 4.5. Parameter Sensitivity

- **Penalty Coefficient ($\beta$) (Figure 3a, Figure 12):** Optimal $\beta$ values varied across tasks, but typically $\beta \leq 1.0$ was preferred, suggesting that the infimum operator could lead to value underestimation if the penalty is too strong.
- **Transition Threshold ($\delta$) (Figure 3b, Figure 13):** A larger $\delta$ (e.g., 30 or 50) was often preferred, implying that L2 loss (used when error is below $\delta$) contributes to training stability, though task-specific tuning was beneficial.
- **Ensemble Size (N) (Table 3):** The ensemble size for the dynamics model (N) was not a sensitive hyperparameter, with similar performance observed across N={3, 5, 7, 9}.

#### 4.6. Generality (Appendix E.6, E.7)

- **Observation and Reward Shifts (Table 4, 5, 6):** DROCO demonstrated superior performance even under observation and reward shifts, attributing this to its dynamic value penalty and Huber loss components which mitigate value estimation errors caused by such shifts.
- **Distinct Behavior Policies (Table 7):** DROCO maintained its superior performance when source and target domain datasets had different quality levels (e.g., medium source with expert target), showing robustness to differing behavior policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of reinforcement learning:

1. **Novel Problem Formulation:** It introduces and formally investigates the concept of dual (train-time and test-time) robustness against dynamics shifts in the context of cross-domain offline RL. This expands the scope of robustness considerations beyond the previously dominant focus on train-time dynamics mismatches.
2. **Theoretical Foundation:** The paper proposes the Robust Cross-domain Bellman (RCB) operator and provides theoretical analyses demonstrating its $\gamma$-contraction property and its ability to achieve both train-time conservatism (bounding overestimation) and test-time resilience (guaranteeing performance under bounded perturbations). The dual reformulation of the Wasserstein uncertainty set into a tractable state uncertainty set provides an important practical bridge from theory to implementation.
3. **Algorithmic Development:** DROCO offers a practical algorithm incorporating these theoretical insights. The use of ensemble dynamics modeling for approximating uncertainty sets, dynamic value penalties, and Huber loss for robust Q-value estimation are specific techniques designed to address the challenges inherent in this dual-robust setting.
4. **Comprehensive Empirical Validation:** Extensive experiments across diverse dynamics shift types (kinematic, morphology), dataset qualities, and perturbation levels demonstrate that DROCO consistently outperforms established baselines in terms of both policy performance and robustness. This empirical evidence supports the effectiveness and practical utility of the proposed method.

The significance and potential impact of this work are particularly relevant for real-world applications of RL. In domains such as robotics, autonomous systems, and manufacturing, learned policies must operate robustly not only when deployed in a new, albeit related, environment (cross-domain), but also when that environment itself undergoes unforeseen changes or degradation over time (test-time perturbations). By explicitly addressing both aspects of robustness, DROCO contributes to the development of more reliable and trustworthy RL agents that can function effectively in non-stationary and uncertain real-world conditions, potentially reducing deployment risks and costs associated with extensive online fine-tuning or re-training.
