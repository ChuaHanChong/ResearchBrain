# Ag2Manip: Learning Novel Manipulation Skills with Agent-Agnostic Visual and Action Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Ag2Manip learns manipulation skills without expert demonstrations by combining an agent-agnostic visual representation, trained on human videos with humans/robots inpainted out, with an agent-agnostic action representation abstracting robot motion to a universal proxy agent; achieves 78.7% sim success versus 18.5% baseline and lifts real-world imitation success from 50% to 77.5%.

## Problem

- Visual representations from human-centric demonstrations suffer domain gap from appearance and kinematic mismatch between humans and robots, biasing decoded task execution.
- Human hands dominate training footage, biasing reward models toward hand appearance and movement rather than the true task objective like object state change.
- Rewards derived from visual or language models are too coarse for precision manipulation, causing large performance loss on tasks needing exact contact interactions.

## Method

- **Ag2Manip**'s agent-agnostic visual representation: segments and inpaints humans/robots out of demonstration frames (ODISE plus E2FGVI), then trains a ResNet50 encoder via ==time-contrastive loss== on the agent-obscured data.
- Agent-agnostic action representation: abstracts robot kinematics into a free-floating proxy sphere with exploration and interaction phases, controlled by a ==PD controller== and GraspNet-identified interactable regions.
- ==Importance-weighted reward shaping== combined with ==goal-conditioned PPO== trains the proxy policy, then an ==inverse-kinematics retargeting== policy maps proxy trajectories onto real robot end-effector actions.

## Results

- Across 24 tasks in FrankaKitchen, ManiSkill, and PartManip, Ag2Manip reaches **78.7%** overall success versus **11.1%** for R3M, **12.0%** for VIP, and **18.5%** for Eureka.
- Ablations show removing action representation drops success by **35.2** points and removing reward shaping drops it by **11.1** points, while the proxy-only variant hits **85.7%** before retargeting.
- Real-world few-shot imitation learning success rises from **50%** (best baseline VIP) to **77.5%** average across four tasks, and task-progress consistency (Spearman correlation) scores **0.740** versus **0.401-0.490** for baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stripping embodiment-specific visual cues (hands, robot morphology) from pretraining data is a general recipe for closing the human-to-robot domain gap in visual reward learning.
> - Decomposing manipulation into agent-agnostic exploration and interaction phases simplifies reward design and generalizes better than learning directly in native robot action space.
> - Reward shaping that reweights progress relative to the start state, rather than raw embedding similarity, materially improves policy learning under sparse successful executions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous robotic systems capable of learning and executing novel manipulation tasks is a significant area of research aimed at transforming various industries. Traditional approaches often rely on expert demonstrations or task-specific reward functions, which can be costly and challenging to scale. Recent advancements have explored leveraging extensive pre-training and large datasets to enhance manipulation learning.

One line of research, exemplified by methods like R3M and VIP, focuses on developing comprehensive visual representations derived from human-centric video datasets. These representations are used to infer task objectives and temporal dynamics, subsequently generating rewards to guide robot actions. Another approach, such as Eureka, utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) to directly generate reward functions from task descriptions.

Despite these developments, existing methodologies encounter several limitations, particularly when confronted with complex manipulation tasks. These limitations include:
1. **Domain Gap in Visual Representations**: Visual representations trained on human demonstrations often struggle to bridge the appearance and kinematic discrepancies between humans and robots. This can lead to biases, causing models to misinterpret optimal robot trajectories or task completion criteria.
2. **Bias Towards Human Hands**: Human-centric training data can lead models to prioritize the appearance and movement of human hands over the actual object manipulation or task objective. For instance, a model might focus on the upward motion of hands rather than the successful grasping of an object.
3. **Lack of Precision in Rewards**: Rewards derived from high-level visual or linguistic models are frequently too broad, lacking the precision required for delicate robotic manipulation tasks. Minor deviations in trajectory or interaction can result in significant performance degradation, highlighting the need for more accurate guidance in tasks such as opening a door or grasping a handle.

Ag2Manip positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified challenges. It builds upon the concept of learning from human demonstrations but introduces agent-agnostic visual and action representations. By abstracting away embodiment-specific details and focusing on generalized interactions, Ag2Manip aims to improve the adaptability, generalizability, and precision of robotic skill acquisition, thereby mitigating the limitations of previous methods and enabling more robust learning of novel manipulation tasks without direct expert intervention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the Ag2Manip framework is to enable robotic systems to autonomously acquire and execute novel manipulation skills without the need for expert demonstrations or task-specific reward functions. This aim is motivated by the recognized challenges in the current state of robotic manipulation learning, which hinder the widespread deployment of adaptable autonomous robots.

The specific objectives and underlying motivations are:

- **To overcome the domain gap between human and robot embodiments**: Current methods often rely on visual data from human demonstrations. However, the visual appearance, kinematic structures, and operational strategies of humans differ significantly from those of robots. This discrepancy introduces biases into learned representations, making it difficult for robots to accurately interpret tasks and generalize skills from human examples. Ag2Manip seeks to mitigate this by developing an agent-agnostic visual representation that obscures embodiment-specific details, thereby focusing on task progression rather than the specific agent performing the task.

- **To reduce visual representation bias towards manipulators (e.g., human hands)**: In human-centric training data, human hands are frequently present and prominent. This can lead visual models to prioritize the position and movement of hands, potentially overlooking the actual interaction with the object or the true task objective. For example, a model might interpret an upward hand movement as successful even if the object was not correctly grasped. The motivation is to create a visual representation that emphasizes the interaction between the end-effector and the object, or the object's state changes, rather than the manipulator itself.

- **To enhance the precision and effectiveness of learned actions**: Robotic manipulation often requires high precision, where even minor trajectory deviations can lead to task failure. Existing visual or linguistic reward functions, being high-level, may not provide the granular guidance necessary for such precision. This motivates the development of an agent-agnostic action representation that abstracts complex robot kinematics into a more universal and simplified proxy agent. This abstraction, coupled with a structured learning process, aims to simplify the acquisition of precise interaction skills, reducing the impact of sparse guidance signals.

- **To facilitate generalizable skill acquisition across diverse tasks and environments**: By addressing the embodiment gap and precision issues, the framework aims to enable robots to learn a broader array of manipulation skills that are transferable across different objects, environments, and robot platforms. The motivation here is to move beyond task-specific solutions towards a more general capability for autonomous skill learning.

In essence, Ag2Manip is motivated by the need for robotic systems to learn more robustly and autonomously from diverse data, reducing the manual effort and domain specificity often associated with current robot learning paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Ag2Manip is a framework designed for learning novel manipulation skills without expert demonstrations, founded on two primary innovations: an agent-agnostic visual representation and an agent-agnostic action representation. The methodology integrates these representations within a reinforcement learning (RL) framework, followed by a retargeting mechanism for real-world robot execution.

**A. Agent-Agnostic Visual Representation**

The aim of this component is to generate a visual representation that is independent of specific agents (humans or robots), thereby bridging the domain gap between human demonstration videos and robot execution.
1. **Data Pre-processing**: The process begins with human demonstration videos, specifically the Epic-Kitchen dataset. For each frame in a video, the human body is segmented using the ODISE algorithm. Subsequently, a video inpainting model, E2FGVI, is employed to fill in the areas previously occupied by the segmented human, ensuring temporal coherence. This yields an agent-agnostic manipulation dataset (D_a) where the focus shifts from the human operator to the objects and environment.
2. **Time-Contrastive Pre-training**: An encoder, F_ϕ (a ResNet50 architecture), is trained on the agent-agnostic dataset D_a to map visual observations into a latent embedding space. The training objective minimizes a combined loss function:
 - **Time-Contrastive Loss (L_tcn)**: This loss encourages temporally proximate frames within the same video clip to have similar embeddings, while frames that are temporally distant or from different video clips are mapped further apart in the embedding space.
 - **Regularization Penalty (L_reg)**: This term promotes a more compact embedding space, preventing embedding explosion.
 The visual encoder is optimized using an Adam optimizer over 24 hours on an NVIDIA A100 GPU.

**B. Agent-Agnostic Action Representation**

This component abstracts a robot's kinematics and interactions into a universal proxy agent, simplifying the learning process. The learning process is bifurcated into two phases:
1. **Exploration Phase**:
 - The robot's end-effector is abstracted as a free-floating, agent-agnostic sphere with a defined collision radius (r_e = 2 cm).
 - The learning objective in this phase is to determine the optimal sequence of positions for this proxy sphere, mimicking the end-effector's movements towards an object.
 - Control over the proxy's movement is managed by a proportional-derivative (PD) controller.
 - This phase continues until the proxy sphere enters a pre-calculated "interactable region" around an object. For robots with two-finger grippers, these regions are identified using point cloud scans and GraspNet, which detects feasible parallel grip points.
2. **Interaction Phase**:
 - Once the proxy enters an interactable region, indicating a potential grasp or attachment, the focus shifts.
 - In this phase, the proxy's actions are abstracted to the forces it exerts on the environment, effectively simulating the robot's manipulation of the object.

**C. Reinforcement Learning and Reward Shaping**

A model-free, Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) framework is used to learn a manipulation policy π = {π_exp, π_int} for both exploration and interaction phases.
1. **Policy Input and Output**: The policy takes robot states (r_t) and environment states (s_t) as observations. It outputs an action a_t = (a_t^p, a_t^f), where a_t^p is the desired position for the proxy in exploration and a_t^f is the intended force in interaction. A PD controller then guides the proxy to execute these actions.
2. **Reward Function**: To guide the policy towards a goal depicted by an image (g), the framework maximizes the similarity S(z_t, z_g) between the embeddings of the current observation (o_t) and the goal image (g). A specific importance-weighted reward function is introduced:
 R(o_t, g; ϕ) = exp( (`(1 + α ⋅ 1_{S(z_t,z_g)−β>0})` * (`S(z_t, z_g) − β`)) / β ) - 1
 Here, β = S(z_0, z_g) is the similarity between the initial state (z_0) and the goal state (z_g), and α (set to 3.0) is a hyperparameter. This function incentivizes states that improve upon the initial state, promoting exploration while not overly penalizing minor deviations.
3. **Policy Optimization**: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is utilized to maximize the expected cumulative reward, known for its training stability and convergence efficiency.

**D. Robot-Specific Action Retargeting**

The learned proxy agent's trajectory needs to be translated into executable actions for a real robot:
1. **Exploration Retargeting**: During the exploration phase, the proxy agent's positions are directly mapped to the robot's end-effector positions.
2. **Transition to Interaction**: As the proxy moves into an interactable region, the robot's end-effector 6D pose is adjusted to align with the nearest viable grasp pose identified by GraspNet.
3. **Interaction Retargeting**: In the interaction phase, the trajectory of the manipulated object dictates the robot end-effector's 6D trajectory. Inverse Kinematics (IK) is used to calculate the specific joint movements for the robot arm to achieve these desired end-effector poses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Ag2Manip was conducted across diverse simulated environments and in real-world imitation learning scenarios, demonstrating performance improvements over baseline methods.

**A. Simulation Results**

- **Setup**: 24 distinct tasks from FrankaKitchen, ManiSkill, and PartManip environments were used, involving actions like opening, pulling, and moving with a 9-DOF Franka Emika robotic arm. Experiments were run in NVIDIA IsaacGym, with task goals defined by images from different camera perspectives. Each task was evaluated across 9 runs (3 camera angles × 3 initialization seeds).
- **Baselines**: Ag2Manip was compared against R3M, VIP (both using agent-aware visual representations and time-contrastive learning), and Eureka (an LLM-based reward generation method with human feedback deactivated for fair comparison). All methods, except Eureka, used a ResNet50 architecture.
- **Overall Performance (Table I)**: Ag2Manip achieved an overall task success rate of 78.7%, which represents a significant increase compared to the baseline methods. R3M achieved 11.1%, VIP 12.0%, and Eureka 18.5%. This indicates that Ag2Manip provides a 325% increase in performance relative to the best baseline.
- **Task-Specific Performance**: Baseline methods and Eureka showed limitations in tasks requiring precise robot-object interactions, such as opening doors or lifting kettles, often failing to perform initial attachment actions. Ag2Manip demonstrated a higher capability in acquiring these challenging skills, attributed to its agent-agnostic representations.
- **Limitations**: Ag2Manip encountered consistent challenges in specific tasks like cube stacking (due to robot arm collisions), peg insertion (due to complex object interactions beyond the training scope), and button pressing (due to minimal visual cues from appearance changes).

**B. Ablation Studies (Table I)**

- **Ours w/o Rew.Shp.** (without importance-weighted reward function): Replacing the tailored reward function with a basic similarity metric resulted in an 11.1% reduction in overall task success (from 78.7% to 67.6%). This highlights the contribution of the specific reward shaping to handling intricate tasks requiring precise movements.
- **Ours w/o Act.Repr.** (without agent-agnostic action representation, learning in robot's native action space): This led to a 35.2% drop in success (from 78.7% to 43.5%). Despite this reduction, this configuration still outperformed the R3M baseline by 32.4%, suggesting that the agent-agnostic visual representation alone provides substantial benefit. This ablation emphasizes the critical role of the agent-agnostic action representation for tasks demanding accurate control.
- **Ours (Proxy)** (evaluating proxy agent performance before robot retargeting): This revealed that the proxy agent achieved an 85.7% success rate, 7.0% higher than the full Ag2Manip framework (78.7%). This suggests that the robot-specific action retargeting step introduces some performance degradation and represents an area for further optimization.

**C. Visual Representation: Task Progress Consistency (Table II)**

- **Method**: Spearman Rank Correlation was used to assess how consistently the visual representation mirrored the temporal progression of expert task trajectories. This involved comparing the temporal order of frames with their similarity to the goal state.
- **Baselines**: ResNet50 (ImageNet), CLIP, R3M, and VIP.
- **Results**: Ag2Manip achieved an overall correlation of 0.740, surpassing ResNet50 (0.418), CLIP (0.490), R3M (0.474), and VIP (0.401). This indicates that Ag2Manip's agent-agnostic visual representation provides more accurate and dependable cues regarding task progression.

**D. Visual Representation: Real-World Imitation Experiments (Table III)**

- **Setup**: A Franka Emika FR3 robot equipped with a Kinect Azure camera was used for four manipulation tasks (PushDrawer, CloseDoor, PickBag, MoveBasket). Each task used 20 human demonstrations for few-shot imitation learning via advantage-weighted regression.
- **Results**: Ag2Manip consistently achieved higher success rates (e.g., 7/10 for PushDrawer, 8/10 for CloseDoor, PickBag, and MoveBasket) compared to all baselines (ResNet50, CLIP, R3M, VIP). For example, R3M achieved 4/10 for PushDrawer and 3/10 for MoveBasket, while VIP achieved 6/10 for PushDrawer and 2/10 for PickBag. This demonstrates the superior ability of Ag2Manip's agent-agnostic visual representation to bridge the domain gap between training data and real-world robot observations, effectively capturing critical action trajectories for successful task execution in a few-shot learning context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Ag2Manip framework contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a method for acquiring a range of manipulation skills without relying on expert demonstrations or task-specific rewards. This work addresses several fundamental challenges that have limited the adaptability and generalizability of autonomous robots.

**Significance:**
- **Bridging the Embodiment Gap:** The introduction of agent-agnostic visual representations directly confronts the issue of domain discrepancy between human demonstrators and robotic systems. By obscuring embodiment-specific details, the framework enables visual representations to focus on task-relevant information, enhancing their transferability across different agents.
- **Enhanced Precision and Generalizability:** The agent-agnostic action representation, which abstracts robot kinematics into a universal proxy, simplifies the learning of complex interactions. This, combined with a tailored reward function, allows for the acquisition of more precise manipulation skills that are less susceptible to the inaccuracies of high-level visual guidance.
- **Improved Autonomous Skill Acquisition:** The empirical results demonstrate that Ag2Manip significantly outperforms existing baselines in learning novel manipulation tasks across diverse simulated and real-world environments. This indicates progress towards more autonomous robot learning, reducing the need for extensive human intervention or specialized data collection.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Increased Robotic Autonomy:** Ag2Manip's ability to learn novel skills without expert demonstrations could enable robots to adapt more quickly to new tasks and environments, decreasing operational downtime and increasing flexibility in industrial and service applications.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By leveraging readily available human manipulation videos and minimizing the requirement for domain-specific robotic demonstrations or hand-crafted reward functions, the framework could reduce the cost and complexity associated with developing and deploying robotic systems for new tasks.
- **Broader Application Scope:** The generalizable nature of the agent-agnostic representations may allow for the development of robots that are versatile across various manipulation scenarios, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics in unstructured environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The agent-agnostic paradigm could serve as a basis for further research into learning transferable skills, developing more robust reward functions, and exploring more sophisticated planning methods to address current limitations, such as complex collision avoidance or interactions with highly articulated objects. Continued refinement of the retargeting phase is also identified as a promising area for future work.

In conclusion, Ag2Manip represents an advancement in enabling robots to learn a broader array of manipulation skills more autonomously, addressing key obstacles in the path toward more adaptable and versatile embodied agents.
