# CapRL: Stimulating Dense Image Caption Capabilities via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory, USTC, CUHK, and Alibaba Cloud developed CAPRL, a framework that applies Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to image captioning by defining caption quality based on its utility for a vision-free LLM in answering VQA questions. This approach produces dense, accurate, and structured captions, leading to substantial performance gains across diverse benchmarks when used for Large Vision-Language Model pre-training.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for image captioning is limited by the high cost and lack of scalability of human or proprietary-model annotations, often leading to models that memorize descriptions and lack diversity.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to subjective tasks like image captioning is challenging due to the difficulty of defining objective reward functions, often resulting in reward hacking with previous approaches.
- There is a need for a scalable, robust method to generate high-quality, dense, and comprehensive image captions crucial for effective pre-training of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs).

## Method

- CAPRL introduces a ==decoupled two-stage== pipeline where an LVLM generates captions (policy model), and a separate, vision-free LLM calculates verifiable rewards by answering MCQs about the image using only the generated caption.
- A utility-based reward is designed: the vision-free LLM's accuracy in answering questions related to the original image (without seeing it) using only the caption determines the reward, making it objective and verifiable.
- A three-stage QA curation pipeline generates and rigorously filters visual QA pairs, ensuring questions are strictly visually-grounded and answerable exclusively from the image content, preventing information leakage and enabling reliable reward signals.

## Results

- CapRL-trained models produce qualitatively superior captions that are better structured, more comprehensive, and factually accurate, particularly for complex images like charts and documents, while significantly reducing hallucinations.
- LVLMs pre-trained on CapRL-**5M** data achieve substantial performance gains (e.g., **6.8%** on InfoVQA, **3.6%** on ChartVQA) across 12 diverse benchmarks compared to baselines, demonstrating improved understanding and generalization.
- CapRL-**3B** achieves caption quality comparable to much larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-**72B** within the Prism Framework, showing an average performance increase of **8.4%** over its baseline and significant domain-specific gains (e.g., **24.6%** on InfoVQA).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Subjective tasks like image captioning can be optimized with RLVR by reframing caption quality as its objective 'utility' for downstream tasks, specifically, its ability to enable a non-visual LLM to answer visual questions.
> - Decoupling the caption generation from reward calculation using a vision-free LLM allows for efficient and robust reward signals, overcoming issues of reward hacking prevalent in 'LLM-as-a-judge' methods.
> - High-quality, automatically generated captions (like those in CapRL-5M) are critical for effective LVLM pre-training, significantly improving modality alignment and performance across various visual understanding benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image captioning is a foundational task that bridges computer vision and natural language processing, crucial for developing advanced vision-language understanding and generation capabilities. Its importance has grown with the rise of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), where high-quality, dense captions are essential for pre-training models to align visual and linguistic information effectively.

Historically, state-of-the-art captioning models have relied heavily on **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**. This paradigm involves training models on massive datasets of images paired with human-annotated or proprietary-model-generated captions. While SFT has been effective, it faces inherent limitations:
- **Cost and Scalability:** Human annotation is expensive and does not scale well to the ever-increasing volume of visual data. Relying on proprietary models for annotation introduces dependency and can be costly.
- **Generality and Diversity:** SFT models tend to memorize specific ground-truth descriptions, limiting their ability to generate diverse, creative, and generalizable captions for open-ended visual content where multiple valid descriptions exist. This often leads to "memorization" rather than genuine understanding.

In parallel, **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** has emerged as a promising paradigm for training Large Language Models (LLMs) on tasks with objective, easily verifiable reward signals, such as mathematical problem-solving or code generation. RLVR encourages models to explore a wider range of solutions and meet verifiable criteria, fostering robustness and diversity.

The challenge lies in extending RLVR to *open-ended and subjective tasks* like image captioning, where defining an objective "good" caption and, consequently, an objective reward function, is difficult. Previous attempts to use reward models or "LLM-as-a-judge" approaches for subjective tasks have been prone to "reward hacking," where models learn to exploit biases in the reward function (e.g., favoring brevity or verbosity) rather than producing genuinely high-quality outputs.

CapRL directly addresses this critical gap. It is presented as the **first study to successfully apply RLVR to the subjective image captioning task** by redefining caption quality based on its *utility*. This novel approach offers a way to generate more general, diverse, and accurate image descriptions at scale, moving beyond the inherent limitations of traditional SFT-based methods and unreliable subjective reward models. It represents a significant step towards leveraging the strengths of RLVR for a broader range of complex, open-ended multimodal generation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the CapRL framework are rooted in overcoming the limitations of current image captioning methodologies, particularly in the context of pre-training Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs):

1. **Overcome Limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The authors aim to move beyond the SFT paradigm, which is constrained by expensive, non-scalable human- or proprietary-model-annotated data. SFT often leads to models that memorize specific answers, limiting their generality and ability to generate diverse and creative descriptions for the open-ended nature of image captioning.
2. **Develop a Scalable and Robust Training Paradigm:** There is a strong need for a captioning model training approach that is both powerful and scalable, capable of generating high-quality, dense, and accurate descriptions without the prohibitive costs of human annotation.
3. **Design an Objective Reward Function for Subjective Tasks:** The central challenge for applying RLVR to image captioning is the subjective nature of what constitutes a "good" caption. The core motivation is to design an *objective* reward function for this inherently subjective task, avoiding the pitfalls of "reward hacking" observed in previous reward models or "LLM-as-a-judge" approaches.
4. **Enhance Caption Quality for LVLM Pre-training:** The ultimate goal is to produce captions that are not only accurate but also *dense and comprehensive*, providing sufficient detail and semantic information. Such high-quality captions are crucial for the pre-training stage of LVLMs, enabling better modality alignment and a deeper understanding of visual features, which in turn boosts performance across various downstream tasks.
5. **Promote Generality and Diversity in Generated Captions:** By moving away from ground-truth memorization, CapRL seeks to encourage models to explore and discover optimal solutions, leading to more diverse and robust outputs that meet verifiable criteria, rather than mimicking a single reference response.

In essence, CapRL seeks to redefine caption quality not by direct human judgment, but by its *utility*: a high-quality caption should be informative enough for a separate non-visual language model to accurately answer detailed questions about the corresponding image. This utility-driven approach is the key to unlocking objective reward signals for RL in a subjective domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CapRL proposes a novel, decoupled two-stage pipeline for applying Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to image captioning. The core idea is to measure a caption's quality by its utility: how well it enables a vision-free Large Language Model (LLM) to answer questions about the image.

#### 3.1. The CapRL Framework (Decoupled Two-Stage Pipeline)

1. **Stage 1: Caption Generation by LVLM (Policy Model):**
 - An input image and an instruction (e.g., "Describe this image in detail.") are fed into an LVLM, which acts as the *policy model*.
 - The LVLM generates a set of candidate captions.

2. **Stage 2: Verifiable Reward Calculation via Decoupled VQA:**
 - For each generated caption, a separate, *vision-free LLM* (denoted as $M_L$) is tasked with answering Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs) related to the original image, using *only the caption* as its source of information. The LLM does not have direct access to the image.
 - **Reward Design:**
 - The LLM's answer ($a_m$) to a question ($q_m$) is compared to a ground-truth answer ($GT_m$).
 - A binary reward $r(a_m)$ is assigned: 1 if $a_m = GT_m$, and 0 otherwise.
 - To eliminate bias in the LLM's preference for specific answer choices, options are randomly shuffled for each question.
 - To ensure robustness, the final reward for a caption ($R_{c_i}$) is computed as the average accuracy over N sampled questions related to the image. This process ensures the reward is *objective* and *verifiable*.
 - The GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization) training process then uses these objective rewards to update the LVLM (policy model) via policy gradient optimization, incorporating a KL-divergence penalty for stability. The Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model is used as the vision-free LLM ($M_L$) for reward calculation, ensuring efficiency.

#### 3.2. QA Curation Pipeline

A high-quality Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset of multiple-choice questions is crucial for providing reliable reward signals. The authors developed a structured three-stage curation pipeline:

1. **Image Collection:** Diverse images are sourced from the web and existing open-source datasets (natural scenes, charts, documents) to maximize variety.
2. **QA Generation:** For each image, a powerful LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is used to automatically generate multiple question-answer pairs in a multiple-choice format.
3. **QA Filtering (Crucial Step):** This stringent filtering process ensures that questions are strictly *visually-grounded* and *answerable exclusively through analysis of the image content*.
 - It prevents "information leakage" by verifying that an LVLM ($M_V^f$, specifically Qwen2.5-VL-3B for efficiency) can answer the question correctly *only when conditioned on both the question and the image*, but *not when conditioned on the question alone*.
 - This ensures that the LLM in the reward stage *must* rely on the caption's visual information, forcing the caption to be truly informative.
 - Approximately 75k images with their corresponding filtered QA pairs are retained for CapRL training.

#### 3.3. CapRL-5M Dataset Construction

After training an LVLM using the CapRL framework, the resulting model (dubbed CapRL-3B, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B) is then used to annotate a large-scale image-caption dataset:

1. **Image Collection and Processing:** 5 million images are collected from ShareGPT4V-1M, DenseFusion-1M, and the web. Rigorous filtering is applied for diversity, quality, and safety (removing low-resolution, simple, or unsafe content; avoiding benchmark leakage).
2. **Caption Model Selection:** CapRL-3B, a lightweight yet powerful captioner trained with CapRL, is chosen to annotate these 5 million images, creating the **CapRL-5M** dataset. This step demonstrates the scalability of the CapRL approach to generate high-quality captions at a low annotation cost.

This methodology ensures that captions are optimized not just for stylistic fluency, but for their ability to convey factual, detailed, and visually-grounded information, a key requirement for robust multimodal understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate CapRL's significant effectiveness across various settings, validating its ability to produce dense and accurate captions and improve LVLM performance.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Improvements (Figure 2, Appendix A)

- **Enhanced Structure, Coverage, and Accuracy:** CapRL-trained models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B after CapRL) produce captions that are better organized, more comprehensive, and factually accurate compared to baseline models.
- **Fewer Hallucinations and More Details:** CapRL significantly reduces hallucinations and provides richer details, especially in complex images like charts, infographics, and technical diagrams. Examples illustrate how CapRL-3B accurately extracts and structures numerical data and specific entities, whereas baselines might misinterpret or invent information.
- **Contrast with Reward Hacking:** Qualitative examples (Figures 9, 10, 11) vividly show that traditional "LVLM-as-a-judge" reward methods lead to reward hacking, resulting in either overly brief and incomplete captions (UnifiedReward-as-Judge) or verbose, irrelevant content designed to satisfy the judge's bias (Qwen2.5VL-as-Judge). CapRL, by contrast, produces genuinely informative captions.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation: LVLM Pretraining Setting (Table 1 & 2, Figure 4)

- **Superior Performance across Benchmarks:** Pretraining LVLMs on the CapRL-5M (or CapRL-1M subset) dataset yields substantial improvements across 12 diverse benchmarks (InfoVQA, DocVQA, ChartQA, MathVista, MMStar, SEED2Plus, etc.).
 - In the Qwen2.5-3B + Qwen2.5-ViT setting, CapRL-1M outperforms DenseFusion-1M by significant margins, e.g., 6.8% on InfoVQA, 2.7% on DocVQA, and 3.6% on ChartVQA.
 - **CapRL-5M consistently achieves the best performance**, showing further gains as data scales from 1M to 5M.
- **Effectiveness for Specialized Domains:** CapRL-annotated data shows particular strength in domains like documents, charts, and infographics, which demand fine-grained perception and structured descriptions. This indicates that CapRL-generated captions effectively enable LVLMs to achieve better modality alignment and deeper understanding of these complex visual features.
- **Benefits for Natural Images:** CapRL also improves performance on natural image benchmarks (e.g., MMStar, MMBench), suggesting it helps multimodal models acquire richer world knowledge.
- **Caption Quality, Not Just Image Diversity:** Ablation studies (Table 2) confirm that CapRL's advantage primarily stems from the *superior quality of its captions*, rather than differences in image diversity, as performance improves even when using the same set of images as other datasets.
- **Strong Scaling Properties:** CapRL datasets consistently outperform baselines across various scales of pretraining data, and this performance gap tends to widen as data size increases (Figure 4), highlighting the practical value and scalability of CapRL for constructing high-quality datasets at low cost.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Evaluation: Prism Framework (Table 3)

- **Comparable to Larger Models:** CapRL-3B, when evaluated within the Prism Framework for caption quality, achieves performance comparable to the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B model. It significantly surpasses its baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) by an average margin of 8.4%.
- **Domain-Specific Gains:** CapRL-3B shows remarkable gains in chart and infographic understanding (e.g., 14.9% on ChartQA, 24.6% on InfoVQA over Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and strong improvements in natural image understanding. This demonstrates that GRPO training with CapRL unlocks the model's potential to organize detailed visual information into comprehensive captions.
- **Failure of LVLM-as-a-Judge Rewards:** As discussed qualitatively, models trained with UnifiedReward-as-Judge or Qwen2.5VL-as-Judge show *deteriorated* or significantly inferior captioning ability due to inherent biases and reward hacking, validating CapRL's objective reward design.

#### 4.4. Comprehensive Discussion and Ablations (Table 4, 5, Figure 5)

- **Generalization Capability:** CapRL-trained models exhibit strong generalization, even when trained exclusively on specific domains (e.g., document/chart images vs. natural images). Improvements extend to out-of-domain benchmarks.
- **Scaling of QA Training Data:** Performance consistently improves as the amount of QA data used for GRPO training increases, indicating promising scaling potential for CapRL itself.
- **Sparse QA Supervision Sufficiency:** Even a single QA pair per image is sufficient to achieve substantial improvements in captioning performance (7.4% average gain over baseline with 1 QA per image), highlighting CapRL's remarkable data efficiency.
- **Impact of Sampling Rounds (N):** Performance improves with increased sampling rounds (N) for reward calculation, reaching saturation around N=4 or N=8. This ensures robustness by mitigating potential option biases in the LLM.
- **Importance of QA Filtering:** Training with unfiltered QA data (containing leakage) leads to performance degradation (1.1% average drop), confirming the necessity of rigorous QA curation to ensure reliable reward signals.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that CapRL is a robust, scalable, and highly effective framework for improving image captioning capabilities. By converting subjective caption quality into objective, verifiable rewards, it successfully enhances both the inherent captioning ability of LVLMs and their subsequent performance in multimodal pretraining, outperforming existing SFT-based and flawed RL approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CAPRL framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with profound implications for the development and application of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering RLVR for Subjective Tasks:** CAPRL is the first successful study to apply Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to an open-ended and inherently subjective task like image captioning. This breaks new ground by demonstrating how objective reward signals can be designed for tasks traditionally deemed too qualitative for direct RL optimization. This methodology could pave the way for applying RLVR to other challenging generative tasks in NLP and vision.

2. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Caption Generation:** By leveraging a utility-based, objective reward function, CAPRL enables the creation of high-quality, dense image captions at an unprecedented scale and significantly lower cost than human annotation or reliance on expensive proprietary models. The CapRL-5M dataset is a testament to this, showing how a lightweight yet powerful captioner (CapRL-3B) can generate valuable data for pre-training. This addresses a major bottleneck in scaling up multimodal datasets.

3. **Enhanced LVLM Pre-training and Performance:** High-quality, dense captions are critical for effective modality alignment during LVLM pre-training. CAPRL-generated captions have been shown to substantially promote this alignment, leading to significant performance gains across a wide array of visual question answering (VQA) and general VLM benchmarks. This directly translates to more capable and robust LVLMs for various downstream applications.

4. **Improved Generality and Reduced Memorization:** Unlike SFT, which can lead to models memorizing specific ground-truth answers, CAPRL encourages models to learn underlying concepts and generate diverse, creative, and generalizable descriptions. This fosters a deeper understanding of visual content, enabling LVLMs to handle a broader range of real-world scenarios more effectively.

5. **Robust Evaluation of Caption Quality:** The paper introduces a reliable and objective method for evaluating caption quality through the "utility" lens (enabling a vision-free LLM to answer VQA questions). This framework offers a more stable alternative to subjective human judgments or easily exploitable "LVLM-as-a-judge" reward models, which suffer from intrinsic biases and reward hacking. This objective evaluation method could become a standard for assessing captioning models.

6. **Broader Impact on Multimodal AI Research:** The success of CAPRL highlights the potential of perception-reasoning decoupled architectures and objective reward design for advancing multimodal understanding. It encourages further exploration into how tasks can be reframed to yield verifiable rewards, thereby unlocking the power of RL for complex AI problems. The robust scaling properties demonstrated by CAPRL-annotated data further underscore its practical value for industrial-scale multimodal model development.

In conclusion, CAPRL not only provides a solution to the long-standing challenges in image captioning but also offers a paradigm shift in how reinforcement learning can be effectively applied to open-ended, subjective generative tasks. Its contributions are poised to significantly impact the development of future multimodal AI systems, making them more capable, scalable, and less reliant on costly human supervision.
