# CapRL: Stimulating Dense Image Caption Capabilities via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory, USTC, CUHK, and Alibaba Cloud developed CAPRL, a framework that applies Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to image captioning by defining caption quality based on its utility for a vision-free LLM in answering VQA questions. This approach produces dense, accurate, and structured captions, leading to substantial performance gains across diverse benchmarks when used for Large Vision-Language Model pre-training.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for image captioning is limited by the high cost and lack of scalability of human or proprietary-model annotations, often leading to models that memorize descriptions and lack diversity.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to subjective tasks like image captioning is challenging due to the difficulty of defining objective reward functions, often resulting in reward hacking with previous approaches.
- There is a need for a scalable, robust method to generate high-quality, dense, and comprehensive image captions crucial for effective pre-training of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs).

## Method

- CAPRL introduces a ==decoupled two-stage== pipeline where an LVLM generates captions (policy model), and a separate, vision-free LLM calculates verifiable rewards by answering MCQs about the image using only the generated caption.
- A utility-based reward is designed: the vision-free LLM's accuracy in answering questions related to the original image (without seeing it) using only the caption determines the reward, making it objective and verifiable.
- A three-stage QA curation pipeline generates and rigorously filters visual QA pairs, ensuring questions are strictly visually-grounded and answerable exclusively from the image content, preventing information leakage and enabling reliable reward signals.

## Results

- CapRL-trained models produce qualitatively superior captions that are better structured, more comprehensive, and factually accurate, particularly for complex images like charts and documents, while significantly reducing hallucinations.
- LVLMs pre-trained on CapRL-**5M** data achieve substantial performance gains (e.g., **6.8%** on InfoVQA, **3.6%** on ChartVQA) across 12 diverse benchmarks compared to baselines, demonstrating improved understanding and generalization.
- CapRL-**3B** achieves caption quality comparable to much larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-**72B** within the Prism Framework, showing an average performance increase of **8.4%** over its baseline and significant domain-specific gains (e.g., **24.6%** on InfoVQA).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Subjective tasks like image captioning can be optimized with RLVR by reframing caption quality as its objective 'utility' for downstream tasks, specifically, its ability to enable a non-visual LLM to answer visual questions.
> - Decoupling the caption generation from reward calculation using a vision-free LLM allows for efficient and robust reward signals, overcoming issues of reward hacking prevalent in 'LLM-as-a-judge' methods.
> - High-quality, automatically generated captions (like those in CapRL-5M) are critical for effective LVLM pre-training, significantly improving modality alignment and performance across various visual understanding benchmarks.
