# PointACT: Vision-Language-Action Models with Multi-Scale Point-Action Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> PointACT enhances Vision-Language-Action models for robotic manipulation by incorporating hierarchical 3D point clouds and a multi-scale point-action interaction mechanism. The framework achieved an 82.3% success rate on RLBench and demonstrated improved precision and robustness on real-world robots compared to 2D-centric baselines.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models rely on 2D image representations, which limits their spatial understanding and precision in 3D physical manipulation tasks.
- Existing 3D-aware VLA approaches often suffer from ambiguous 3D reconstruction, weak coupling between geometric cues and action generation, or the use of only coarse-grained 3D features, hindering fine-grained control.
- Developing general-purpose robots capable of precise and robust spatial grounding for complex manipulation tasks from natural language instructions remains a central challenge.

## Method

- **PointACT** proposes a dual-system VLA architecture that combines a frozen pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone for high-level semantic understanding with a dedicated 3D-aware action expert.
- A novel multi-scale point-action interaction mechanism is introduced within the action expert, using bottleneck windowed self-attention to integrate hierarchical ==point cloud== features directly into action decoding.
- The framework is trained using behavior cloning on expert demonstrations, employing either a regression head for continuous delta actions or a classification head for discretized keypoint actions.

## Results

- PointACT achieved an average success rate of **96.0%** on the LIBERO benchmark and a mean success rate of **82.3%** on the RLBench benchmark, outperforming existing 2D-based and some 3D-aware VLA baselines.
- Real-world experiments on SO-100 and UR5 robot platforms demonstrated superior performance, smoother trajectories, and higher success rates in contact-rich tasks compared to current state-of-the-art VLAs.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the multi-scale point-action interaction mechanism significantly contributed to performance gains, proving more effective than coarse-grained 3D feature injection or simple feature concatenation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained, multi-scale interaction between action tokens and hierarchical 3D point cloud features is crucial for achieving precise spatial grounding in robotic manipulation.
> - A dual-system architecture effectively leverages the general semantic representations of a frozen VLM while enabling specialized, geometry-aware action generation without interfering with VLM pre-training.
> - Pre-trained 3D encoders, even with domain gaps between pre-training data and manipulation scenes, provide useful geometric priors that enhance VLA performance, particularly with larger model capacities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robots capable of interpreting natural language instructions for diverse physical actions represents a central challenge in contemporary robotics. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising paradigm by integrating large pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) into robotic control policies. These VLAs benefit from the semantic understanding capabilities of VLMs, which theoretically enables broad generalization across objects, scenes, and instructions, surpassing the capabilities of traditional task-specific policies.

However, a fundamental limitation of most state-of-the-art VLAs is their reliance on 2D image representations. The physical world is inherently three-dimensional, and the absence of explicit geometric input in 2D-centric VLAs often compromises spatial understanding, leading to reduced performance in tasks requiring precise and robust manipulation. Such policies may struggle with accurate spatial reasoning and often necessitate extensive training data to achieve viewpoint invariance.

Prior research has attempted to address this 2D limitation through various strategies:
- **3D Reconstruction from 2D**: Some approaches attempt to infer 3D structure from single-view or sparse-view RGB images using external depth models or jointly trained predictors. However, this reconstruction is often inherently ambiguous and may lack the reliability required for high-accuracy control.
- **Auxiliary 3D Signals**: Other methods integrate depth or 3D positional cues as auxiliary inputs layered onto existing 2D feature representations. In these cases, action generation often remains primarily driven by image tokens, resulting in a weak coupling between geometric structure and the final control output.
- **Coarse-grained 3D Inputs**: Some 3D-aware VLAs introduce explicit 3D inputs, such as point clouds, directly into action experts. However, these methods typically extract only coarse-grained global features from the 3D data, thereby limiting fine-grained geometric interaction essential for precision. Furthermore, previous studies have reported limited benefits from transferring pre-trained 3D representations to policy learning in this context, partly due to the scarcity of in-domain pre-trained point cloud encoders.

PointACT addresses these limitations by proposing a method for integrating hierarchical 3D point cloud representations directly and tightly into the action decoding process of a VLA. This work aims to leverage the semantic understanding of pre-trained VLMs while explicitly enhancing spatial grounding through multi-scale geometric interaction, thereby enabling more precise and robust robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to overcome the limitations of 2D-centric Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation, particularly their inability to perform fine-grained geometric reasoning and robust spatial grounding in 3D environments. While existing VLAs benefit from the strong semantic understanding of large pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs), their reliance on 2D visual representations creates a mismatch with the inherently 3D nature of the physical world. This mismatch results in struggles with precise manipulation tasks and necessitates large datasets to achieve basic spatial invariance.

The paper identifies specific gaps in existing 3D-aware VLA approaches:
- **Ambiguity of 3D Reconstruction**: Methods that infer 3D information from 2D images are inherently ambiguous and often unreliable for high-accuracy control.
- **Weak 3D Coupling**: Approaches that use depth or 3D positional cues as auxiliary signals often layer this information onto 2D features, leading to a weak coupling between geometry and action generation.
- **Coarse 3D Feature Use**: When 3D inputs are incorporated, they typically rely on coarse-grained point cloud features, which restrict fine-grained geometric interaction and have shown limited gains from pre-trained 3D representations, partly due to domain gaps.

Against this background, the key objectives of the PointACT research are:
1. **Systematic Study of 3D Integration Strategies**: To investigate and compare different methods for incorporating 3D information (specifically point clouds) into VLAs, focusing on two main paradigms: monolithic VLA backbone injection and integration within a dedicated action expert.
2. **Development of PointACT Framework**: To propose a 3D-enhanced VLA framework, PointACT, that combines a pre-trained VLM backbone with a dedicated 3D action expert. This framework aims to preserve the VLM's general-purpose representations while enabling direct geometric influence on action generation.
3. **Introduction of Multi-Scale Point-Action Interaction**: To design a novel point-action expert within PointACT that facilitates fine-grained, multi-scale interaction between point cloud features and evolving action tokens. This mechanism aims to capture both local geometric detail and global scene structure efficiently through bottleneck windowed self-attention.
4. **Demonstration of Performance Improvements**: To evaluate PointACT on established robotic manipulation benchmarks (LIBERO and RLBench) and real-world robot platforms, demonstrating consistent and substantial improvements in success rates across diverse tasks and action types (e.g., keypoint pose prediction, delta action movements), particularly in scenarios requiring precise spatial reasoning.
5. **Assessment of Pre-trained 3D Representations**: To explore the utility of initializing the point-cloud expert with a pre-trained point encoder (e.g., Point Transformer v3), even when facing domain gaps between the pre-training data and manipulation scenes, to determine if useful geometric priors can be leveraged.

Ultimately, this work is motivated by the need for more robust, precise, and spatially grounded robot control, which is seen as essential for advancing general-purpose robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PointACT is formulated as a vision-and-language guided policy learning problem for robotic manipulation. At each timestep `t`, the agent receives a multi-modal observation `O_t = {I_t, P_t, s_t, L}`, comprising multi-view RGB images (`I_t`), a colored 3D point cloud (`P_t`), the robot's proprioceptive state (`s_t`), and a natural language instruction (`L`). The objective is to learn a policy `π_θ` that predicts a sequence of future actions `A_t` to accomplish the task.

**Point Cloud Encoder**: The system employs a Point Transformer v3 (PTv3) to encode 3D geometric observations `P_t`. PTv3 operates directly on unordered point sets, utilizing a serialization-based grouping strategy for local neighborhoods and self-attention within groups. Its hierarchical design progressively downsamples point features across stages, generating multi-scale geometric representations that capture both local detail and global layout. The encoder is initialized from a PTv3 model pre-trained via large-scale self-supervised learning on building-level 3D scenes, providing geometric priors despite a domain gap.

**Baseline 3D-aware VLA Strategies (for comparison and context)**:
The paper first outlines two baseline strategies for incorporating 3D information into VLAs:
1. **Monolithic 3D-aware VLA**: This approach integrates geometric data as an additional input modality directly into a unified VLM backbone (e.g., EO1 built on Qwen2.5-VL). Point embeddings from the final PTv3 layer are projected into the VLM's latent space and concatenated with image, text, and proprioceptive tokens, feeding into a single transformer. Training uses a flow-matching objective to predict a velocity field for iterative denoising of action tokens.
2. **Dual-system 3D-aware VLA**: This strategy decouples semantic reasoning from motor control, similar to GR00T architecture (using Qwen2.5-VL as VLM backbone). The VLM backbone is kept frozen to preserve its general-purpose representations. A separate action expert (e.g., a diffusion transformer) is trained from scratch to predict actions `A_t`, conditioned on VLM features, proprioceptive state, and coarse-grained point embeddings from the last layer of the PTv3 encoder.

**PointACT: Multi-scale Point-Action Interaction**:
PointACT builds upon the dual-system VLA paradigm, enhancing the action expert with geometry-aware action decoding. Its core components include:
- **Dual-Model Architecture**: A frozen pre-trained VLM backbone handles high-level visual and linguistic instruction processing. A dedicated point-action expert integrates structured point cloud information into action decoding.
- **Multi-scale Point-Action Expert**: This expert utilizes hierarchical point embeddings (`Z_p^l`) from different layers of the PTv3 encoder.
- **Bottleneck Window Self-Attention**: To manage the potentially large number of point tokens (`N_p^l`), a bottleneck window-based self-attention mechanism is introduced.
 - The point cloud is partitioned into `K` disjoint spatial windows.
 - Action tokens (`Z_a^l`) are broadcast to point tokens within each window, forming a joint representation `X_k^l`.
 - A shared self-attention block processes `X_k^l` within each window.
 - Action tokens serve as a latent bottleneck, aggregating local geometric context efficiently, with attention complexity linear in the number of windows.
 - Updated action features (`Z_a^l_hat`) are extracted from each window and averaged across all `K` groups to form a global action representation.
 - Distinct feed-forward networks (FFN) are applied to action and point tokens after self-attention.
- **Cross-Attention with VLM Embeddings**: After geometric fusion, the action tokens (`Z_a^l_bar`) undergo cross-attention with the VLM's output embeddings (`Z_vlm`) to integrate high-level visual and linguistic instructions.
- **Hierarchical Conditioning**: This entire process is repeated across the hierarchical stages of the PTv3 encoder, enabling action tokens to be continuously conditioned on a spectrum of geometric information, from fine-grained local details to coarse-grained global scene structures.

**Action Prediction and Training**:
PointACT is trained using behavior cloning on expert demonstration trajectories.
- **Regression Head**: For continuous control and short-horizon action chunking (e.g., delta end-effector poses), an MLP maps output action token embeddings to a sequence of control parameters. It is optimized using an L2 loss over the predicted action chunk.
- **Classification Head**: For keypoint actions (e.g., long-term end-effector poses), a point-anchored classification strategy is used. The target workspace is discretized into bins relative to the nearest point in the observed point cloud. Rotations are also discretized. Final point embeddings are concatenated with action tokens before the prediction layer. The model is trained using a cross-entropy loss.

**Implementation Details**:
- The VLM backbone is Qwen2.5-VL. The PTv3 encoder uses the PTv3-Large configuration.
- The PointACT module (action expert) has approximately 300M trainable parameters. The VLM backbone is typically frozen.
- Input point clouds are cropped using a workspace bounding box, voxelized to 1cm, and downsampled to a maximum of 4096 points.
- Training is performed with a batch size of 128 on 2 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for 20K to 50K gradient steps, using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 5 × 10−5 and a cosine decay schedule.
- Standard point cloud augmentations (e.g., random rotation) and image-level augmentations are applied.
- Experiments use a single front-view RGB image (256 × 256 resolution) and a point cloud derived from depth camera intrinsics and extrinsics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PointACT was evaluated on two simulation benchmarks, LIBERO and RLBench, and on two real-world robot platforms, SO-100 and UR5, with comprehensive ablation studies.

**Simulation Benchmarks:**

- **LIBERO Benchmark**: PointACT achieved an average success rate of 96.0% across four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), outperforming the reproduced EO1 baseline (93.1%) and most other 2D VLA methods. The gains were particularly notable in the LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Long task suites, which require precise spatial reasoning and extended temporal actions, demonstrating the benefit of explicit 3D geometric information. The model performs comparably on Object and Goal suites.
- **RLBench Benchmark (10 tasks)**: PointACT achieved a mean success rate of 82.3%, which represents a substantial improvement over several state-of-the-art 2D-based VLAs like HybridVLA (74%) and EO1 (73.2%), and also outperformed the 3D-aware ACT3D (64.5%). This indicates improved performance across diverse manipulation skills, including object placement, articulated object interaction, and tool use, under conditions with distinct object placements between training and testing.

**Observed Failure Modes on RLBench:**
- **Perceptual Occlusion**: Tasks like "CloseFridge" demonstrated failures due to partial views, suggesting a need for multi-view integration or active perception.
- **Limited Failure Recovery**: PointACT, while precise initially, showed limitations in reactive recovery from deviations or physical perturbations, such as object shifts during "Frame off Hanger." This indicates a need for enhanced closed-loop temporal reasoning.
- **Tool-Mediated Interaction**: In tasks like "Sweep to Dustpan" and "Water Plants," the model exhibited inaccuracies in indirect object manipulation, particularly in estimating tool-to-target spatial relationships (e.g., broom-dustpan contact, watering bottle-plant collisions).

**Ablation Studies:**

- **3D Injection Architectures**:
 - **Monolithic VLA (EO1 + Point)**: Injecting point cloud features directly into the VLM backbone led to inconsistent improvements and a significant performance decrease on RLBench (18.6% SR vs. 73.2% for 2D EO1). This suggests that direct augmentation can interfere with pre-trained VLM representations without sufficient 3D-integrated pre-training.
 - **Dual-system VLA (GR00T(arch) + Point, coarse-grained)**: Integrating coarse-grained point features into a separate action expert yielded more stable improvements (69.7% SR on RLBench).
 - **PointACT (dual-system with multi-scale interaction)**: Achieved the highest performance (82.3% SR on RLBench) with fewer trainable parameters (~300M) compared to the coarse-grained GR00T(arch)+Point baseline (~1B), demonstrating the effectiveness of fine-grained, multi-scale point-action interaction.
- **Multi-scale Point Features**: Naive concatenation of features from different PTv3 scales into a single token sequence did not improve performance over using only the final layer's coarse features (65.2-65.6% vs. 69.7% on RLBench). This result underscores the necessity of the proposed multi-scale point-action interaction mechanism over simple feature aggregation.
- **Contribution of 2D Image Modality**: Removing image features from PointACT reduced performance (e.g., 79.8% on RLBench vs. 82.3% with images). This indicates that 2D visual features provide critical semantic cues that complement 3D geometric representations, highlighting the importance of joint 2D-3D reasoning.
- **Model Size of PointACT Expert**: Increasing the capacity of the PointACT action expert (from Small to Large, 59M to 314M parameters) led to slight performance improvements (e.g., 94.2% to 97.4% on LIBERO-Spatial). Notably, even the smallest PointACT expert outperformed 2D baselines without a 3D action expert.
- **PTv3 Pretraining**: Initializing the PTv3 encoder with pre-trained weights, despite the domain gap between architectural scenes and manipulation scenes, provided benefits, especially for larger model capacities (e.g., 97.4% vs. 93.2% for the Large model on LIBERO-Spatial). This suggests that pre-trained 3D encoders offer useful geometric priors.
- **Classification vs. Regression Head**: On RLBench, the classification head (for keypoint actions) converged faster and achieved better early-stage performance compared to a regression head, with comparable final success rates. This is attributed to the more stable learning signals from discretized classification.
- **Robustness to Point Cloud Parameters**: PointACT demonstrated robustness to variations in point cloud density and generally improved performance with larger spatial window sizes.

**Real-World Experiments:**

- **SO-100 Robot Platform (Microstep Actions)**: PointACT achieved comparable or superior performance to existing VLAs (π0 and GR00T-N1.5) on tasks like "Put Banana In Plate," "Put Sock In Drawer," and "Open Microwave." For example, on the complex "Put Sock In Drawer," PointACT achieved 9/10 successes compared to π0's 2/10 and GR00T's 5/10. PointACT produced smoother trajectories and more accurate actions, attributed to explicit 3D perception.
- **UR5 Robot Platform (Keypoint Actions)**: PointACT outperformed 2D VLAs (π0, GR00T-N1.5) on most tasks. Crucially, it demonstrated stronger results than the purely 3D-based 3DLotus on tasks like "Close Drawer" where transparent objects caused noisy point clouds, highlighting the benefit of fusing RGB and point cloud cues. On tasks like "Stack Yellow Cup Onto Pink Cup," requiring precise localization, PointACT achieved performance comparable to 3DLotus and significantly better than 2D VLAs.

**Efficiency Analysis:**
PointACT showed an inference speed of 6.7 Hz on RLBench, which is higher than EO1 (3.8 Hz), partly because PointACT does not rely on a flow-matching objective for action prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PointACT, a Vision-Language-Action framework that enhances robotic manipulation capabilities through the integrated use of 3D point clouds. The work systematically investigates strategies for incorporating 3D information into VLAs, identifying that fine-grained, multi-scale interaction within a dedicated action expert is more effective than direct injection into a monolithic VLM backbone or reliance on coarse 3D features.

The **significance** of PointACT lies in several key contributions:
1. **Improved Spatial Grounding**: By directly integrating hierarchical 3D point cloud representations into the action decoding process, PointACT addresses a fundamental limitation of 2D-centric VLAs, enabling more precise and robust spatial reasoning. This is demonstrated by consistent performance gains on tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation and spatial understanding across diverse benchmarks and real-world scenarios.
2. **Effective Dual-System Architecture**: The proposed dual-system VLA design effectively leverages the semantic understanding of a frozen pre-trained VLM while introducing a dedicated 3D-aware action expert. This modularity allows for targeted geometric reasoning without disrupting the VLM's general-purpose representations, leading to efficient and parameter-economical solutions compared to monolithic approaches.
3. **Novel Multi-Scale Interaction Mechanism**: The bottleneck window self-attention mechanism is a methodological innovation that enables dense interaction between action tokens and multi-scale point cloud features. This mechanism efficiently captures both local geometric detail and global scene structure, which is crucial for high-accuracy control, while managing computational complexity.
4. **Validation on Real Robots**: The successful deployment and performance gains on SO-100 and UR5 robot platforms under realistic conditions, including noisy sensor data and actuation uncertainty, underscore the practical applicability and robustness of PointACT. The results demonstrate the critical role of joint 2D-3D reasoning, particularly when 3D data quality is imperfect.
5. **Relevance of Pre-trained 3D Representations**: The study highlights the promise of pre-trained 3D encoders (like PTv3) in providing useful geometric priors for robotics, even when domain gaps exist between pre-training data and manipulation scenes.

The **potential impact** of PointACT is substantial for the field of robotics and embodied AI:
- **Advancement in General-Purpose Robotics**: By enabling robots to better understand and interact with the 3D physical world, PointACT contributes to the development of more capable and general-purpose robotic manipulation systems that can perform complex tasks with higher precision and reliability.
- **Reduced Data Dependency**: Enhanced spatial understanding through explicit 3D inputs may reduce the reliance on vast amounts of 2D image data to achieve viewpoint invariance and robust spatial generalization.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The systematic study of 3D integration strategies provides valuable insights for future VLA model designs. It encourages further exploration into optimal architectures for combining multimodal perception and action generation.
- **Inspiration for 3D Pre-training**: The observed benefits of pre-trained 3D encoders, even with domain gaps, emphasize the need for and potential impact of large-scale, manipulation-centric 3D pre-training datasets and models.

**Future work** directions, as identified by the authors, include scaling point-based pre-training on large-scale robot datasets tailored for manipulation, improving robustness under noisy real-world point cloud observations (e.g., from transparent objects), and enhancing failure recovery capabilities with 3D-aware VLAs through more sophisticated temporal reasoning. These advancements could further bridge the gap between theoretical models and robust, real-world robotic deployment.
