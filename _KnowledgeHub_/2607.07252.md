# Safe Reinforcement Learning using Ideas from Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A research framework integrates Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) with Model Predictive Control (MPC) to ensure strict safety in cyber-physical systems. It establishes a recursively feasible state-action space offline using MPC and employs an online projection filter to map DRL-proposed actions into this verified safe space, achieving stable policy convergence on a physical testbed with significantly reduced constraint violations during training.

## Problem

- Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) requires unconstrained exploration, which poses a safety risk to physical hardware in cyber-physical systems (CPSs) and robotics.
- Existing safe RL methods often provide only probabilistic or single-step safety guarantees, insufficient for preventing catastrophic failures from 'points of no return' in systems with inertia.
- Online model-based safety mechanisms (e.g., online MPC or CBFs) typically introduce prohibitive computational latency, hindering real-time control in high-frequency CPS loops.

## Method

- Developed an offline procedure using ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== as an oracle to define and pre-compute a ==recursively feasible state-action space== that guarantees long-term physical safety.
- Exploited ==control-affine system dynamics== and the ==convexity== of safety constraints to efficiently map the feasible space by determining minimum and maximum safe actions via ==bisection search==.
- Implemented a computationally lightweight online **projection filter** that intercepts actions proposed by the DRL agent, mapping any unsafe action to the nearest valid point within the pre-computed feasible space before execution.

## Results

- Successfully mapped the recursively safe operational envelope for the Quanser Aero 2 testbed offline, visually demonstrating how the feasible action space constricts near physical limits.
- An integrated PPO agent achieved high control performance and stable policy convergence on the physical testbed while operating predominantly within the defined safety constraints.
- The projection filter drastically reduced catastrophic exploration failures and significantly lowered the rate of constraint violations during physical hardware training, despite rare, intermittent violations attributed to the 'Sim-to-Real' gap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proactive safety guarantees can be achieved by offloading complex predictive calculations to an offline phase, enabling real-time, lightweight safety enforcement during online learning and deployment.
> - For control-affine systems, the feasible action space often forms a convex set, allowing for efficient mapping of safety boundaries through bisection search rather than exhaustive sampling.
> - The concept of a 'point of no return' is explicitly captured by the shrinking of the feasible action space as a system approaches physical limits at high velocities, necessitating forward-looking safety measures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of reinforcement learning (RL) into cyber-physical systems (CPSs) and robotics presents a significant challenge: ensuring strict safety guarantees during the learning process. Traditional RL paradigms emphasize unconstrained exploration to discover optimal policies, which is incompatible with real-world physical systems where constraint violations can lead to damage or failure. This work addresses the fundamental problem of safe exploration in RL for safety-critical applications.

Existing approaches to safe RL generally fall into two categories:
- **Soft Constraints (e.g., Constrained Markov Decision Processes - CMDPs):** Methods like Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO) and Lagrangian relaxation aim to maximize rewards while keeping expected cumulative costs (related to unsafe behavior) below a threshold. However, these provide probabilistic guarantees over a horizon, not deterministic, step-by-step safety, which is insufficient for preventing immediate catastrophic failures in physical hardware.
- **Hard Constraints (Architectural Interventions):**
 - **Lyapunov-based and Reachability Approaches:** These construct Lyapunov functions or compute safe backward reachable sets to restrict policy updates or intervene when approaching unsafe regions. While offering strong theoretical guarantees, computing exact reachable sets can be computationally intractable for complex systems due to the curse of dimensionality.
 - **Reactive Shielding and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs):** These methods utilize external mathematical constructs to override the RL agent's actions online. Shielding employs formal verification to monitor system states and intervene, while CBFs synthesize safe control sets online by ensuring a barrier function's derivative remains within bounds. A critical limitation of these online, reactive methods, particularly for systems with inertia, is that they might intervene too late if the system has already entered an unavoidable trajectory toward a violation, as standard Deep RL (DRL) policies lack a predictive horizon.

This research distinguishes itself by proposing a framework that shifts the heavy computational burden of ensuring recursive safety from online to offline. Unlike online CBFs or reactive shielding that evaluate instantaneous actions, this approach pre-computes "recursively safe" boundaries using Model Predictive Control (MPC) offline. This offline computation allows the online safety mechanism (a projection filter) to instantly map any potentially unsafe action proposed by the DRL policy into a globally verified, recursively safe space before execution, thereby addressing the real-time latency and "point of no return" issues. The work thus contributes to the landscape by offering a method for deterministic, forward-looking safety guarantees without sacrificing the real-time performance of the online control loop.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the inherent conflict between the exploration requirements of standard reinforcement learning and the safety imperative of real-world cyber-physical systems (CPSs) and robotics.

The key objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Address the Safety Bottleneck in RL for CPSs:** Standard RL algorithms learn optimal control policies through extensive, unconstrained exploration of the state-action space. In physical systems, such exploration can lead to violations of mechanical limits, thermal bounds, or actuator constraints, resulting in hardware damage, costly downtime, or system failure. The research aims to overcome this safety bottleneck.
- **Guarantee Strict, Hard Safety Constraints:** The primary objective is to develop a mechanism that ensures absolute constraint satisfaction throughout the entire learning process, not just after a policy has converged. This includes both the active exploration phase and deployment. This is defined as avoiding "unsafe states" where the system either directly violates constraints or enters a trajectory from which future constraint violations are inevitable, regardless of subsequent control actions.
- **Combine Adaptive Learning with Formal Safety Guarantees:** The paper aims to leverage the data-driven learning capabilities and high-performance potential of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for complex, non-linear control problems, while simultaneously incorporating the formal, forward-looking safety guarantees traditionally offered by model-based control methods like Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- **Enable Safe Exploration without Impeding Real-time Control:** A significant challenge is to provide safety guarantees without introducing prohibitive computational overhead into the high-frequency control loops characteristic of modern CPSs. The objective is to pre-compute safety information offline, allowing for efficient, real-time safety enforcement during active learning and deployment.
- **Develop a Generalized Framework:** The research seeks to propose a generalized framework that can be applied to a variety of cyber-physical systems, moving beyond ad-hoc solutions to a more systematic approach for safe RL. This involves formally defining system dynamics, constraints, and the learning objective to bridge control theory and RL.
- **Empirically Validate on Physical Hardware:** A practical objective is to systematically evaluate the proposed generalized approach on a non-linear laboratory testbed (the Quanser Aero 2) to demonstrate its efficacy in a physical system, showing successful exploration and stable policy convergence while respecting safety constraints.

In summary, the motivation is to resolve the "safe exploration paradox" – the need for exploration in RL versus the demand for absolute safety in physical systems – by integrating the predictive safety of MPC into an RL framework in a computationally efficient manner, thereby enabling the safe and reliable deployment of RL in safety-critical domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology combines the adaptive control capabilities of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) with the formal, forward-looking safety guarantees of Model Predictive Control (MPC). The core idea is to define and utilize a "feasible state-action space" offline, which then acts as an oracle for an online safety filter.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

- **Continuous System Dynamics:** The work considers generic cyber-physical systems governed by continuous-time non-linear differential equations, $\dot{x}(t) = f_{cont}(x(t), u(t))$, where $x(t)$ is the system state and $u(t)$ is the control input.
- **Operational Constraints:** Strict operational and mechanical limits define safe state ($X_{safe}$) and input ($U_{safe}$) sets. The system must operate strictly within $X_{safe} \times U_{safe}$.
- **Discrete MDP:** For the RL framework, the continuous dynamics are discretized over a sample time $\Delta t$, yielding a deterministic transition function $s_{t+1} = f_{disc}(s_t, a_t)$.
- **Safe Exploration Paradox:** Standard DRL relies on stochastic exploration (e.g., adding Gaussian noise to actions) which can inevitably propose actions outside $X_{safe} \times U_{safe}$. Moreover, single-step constraint satisfaction is insufficient due to system inertia; the agent must avoid "points of no return" where future violations become inevitable.

#### 3.2. Safe Exploration via Offline MPC

To resolve the paradox, the methodology defines a "true feasible state-action space," denoted $F$, which guarantees recursive, long-term physical safety.

- **Feasible State-Action Space (F):**
 - A control action is considered truly safe for a state $s$ if there exists an infinite sequence of future actions within $U_{safe}$ that keeps the system out of the unsafe state space $S^-$. This set of safe actions for a given state $s$ is $F_A(s)$.
 - The feasible state space $F_S$ is the set of all states where at least one safe action exists ($s \in F_S \iff F_A(s) \ne \emptyset$).
 - The global feasible state-action space $F$ is the union of all safe state-action pairs: $F = \bigcup_{s \in F_S} \{s\} \times F_A(s)$.
- **MPC as a Feasibility Oracle:**
 - The constrained MPC optimization problem (minimizing stage cost $\ell(x, u)$ over a horizon $H$ subject to system dynamics and state/input constraints) is used to approximate infinite-horizon safety.
 - For a given state-action pair $(s, a)$, the MPC oracle simulates dynamics forward over a sufficiently large finite horizon $H$, forcing the first action to be $a$. If the optimizer can find a physically valid sequence of subsequent actions respecting all constraints over this horizon, the pair is deemed safe.
 - This is formalized as a binary oracle function: $\text{Oracle}(s, a) = 1$ if $(s, a) \in F$, and $0$ otherwise. Crucially, this oracle is executed strictly offline to avoid real-time computational bottlenecks.
- **Scaling the Offline Mapping via System Convexity:**
 - To make the offline mapping of $F$ computationally tractable, especially for continuous action spaces, the method exploits the physical properties of many mechanical systems, specifically control-affine dynamics.
 - For such systems, safety constraints often map to linear inequalities with respect to the action space, meaning the set of feasible actions $F_A(s)$ for a given state $s$ forms a continuous, convex interval.
 - Instead of exhaustively evaluating the entire continuous action space via grid search, it is sufficient to determine only the minimum safe action ($a_{min}(s)$) and maximum safe action ($a_{max}(s)$) using a rapid bisection search algorithm. Any action $a$ between these boundaries ($a_{min}(s) \le a \le a_{max}(s)$) is intrinsically guaranteed to be feasible.

#### 3.3. Safe RL Architecture with Projection Filters

- **Safety Filter Mechanism:** During active RL training, the agent's raw actions are not directly executed. A deterministic projection filter $P_s: A \to F_A(s)$ intercepts the proposed action $a_t = \pi(s_t)$.
 - If $a_t$ is within the safe action set $F_A(s_t)$ (derived from the offline MPC map), it passes unaltered.
 - If $a_t$ is unsafe, it is projected to the nearest valid point in $F_A(s_t)$ via Euclidean distance, yielding a guaranteed safe action $\hat{a}_t$.
- **Closed-Loop Evaluation and Reward Shaping:**
 - Only the safe action $\hat{a}_t$ is transmitted to the CPS environment, and the subsequent state $s_{t+1}$ is computed. This maintains a continuous, state-reactive feedback loop.
 - To encourage the agent to naturally learn safe boundaries and reduce reliance on the filter, the magnitude of the projection $||\pi(s) - \hat{\pi}(s)||$ can be penalized in the RL reward function.
 - An action penalty term (e.g., based on the standard deviation of normalized applied actions) can also be added to the reward to prevent high-frequency oscillations ("bang-bang" control) common in DRL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated the proposed framework through experimental deployment on a physical cyber-physical testbed.

- **Experimental Testbed:** The Quanser Aero 2, a reconfigurable dual-rotor aerospace experiment, was used. For this validation, it was configured for 1-degree of freedom (1-DOF) pitch control, with the yaw axis mechanically locked. The system's inputs were simplified to a single voltage operating two fans in opposition.
- **System Dynamics and Constraints:** The non-linear equations of motion for pitch angle ($\Theta$) and pitch velocity ($\omega$) were derived, incorporating friction, moment of inertia, and voltage gain. Strict mechanical pitch constraints of $\pm 60^\circ$ ($\approx \pm 1.05$ rad) defined $X_{safe}$.
- **Offline Mapping Results:**
 - Using the offline MPC approach and the bisection grid search strategy (exploiting the monotonic relationship between rotor voltage and thrust), the safe operational envelope for the Quanser Aero 2 was sampled.
 - The resulting feasible state space explicitly demonstrated that at high absolute velocities approaching physical limits, the set of feasible control voltages dramatically shrinks, allowing only actions capable of maximum reverse thrust to prevent violations. This visually confirmed the abstract concept of the "point of no return."
 - The convexity property of the action space with respect to safety constraints was confirmed, enabling efficient determination of minimum and maximum safe actions.
- **Empirical Performance with Safety Filter:**
 - A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent was trained directly on the simulated and physical testbed, integrated with the safety filter.
 - Empirical observations indicated that the agent successfully explored the extreme boundaries of the state space.
 - The agent achieved high control performance while remaining within the defined feasible bounds for the majority of the training process.
- **Constraint Violations and "Sim-to-Real" Gap:**
 - While the theoretical framework guaranteed recursive feasibility and strict constraint satisfaction, rare, intermittent mechanical constraint violations were observed during empirical deployment on physical hardware.
 - These edge-case violations were primarily attributed to unavoidable model inaccuracies, also known as the "Sim-to-Real" gap, where the true physical system dynamics deviate slightly from the nominal mathematical model used by the offline MPC oracle.
 - Additional contributing factors included practical implementation artifacts such as discrete-time sampling errors (where continuous dynamics might violate bounds between evaluation steps), minor unmodeled hardware communication latencies, and numerical solver tolerances during the real-time Euclidean projection.
 - Despite these real-world challenges, the projection filter operated efficiently at high frequencies and successfully prevented catastrophic, high-velocity exploration failures inherent to unconstrained DRL. The violation rate was drastically reduced compared to baseline methods, and the hardware training phase was stabilized.

In summary, the methodology successfully demonstrated its ability to enable safe exploration and achieve stable policy convergence on physical hardware by proactively constraining actions based on offline MPC-derived safety boundaries, even with the presence of practical real-world system discrepancies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research offers a significant contribution to the field of safe reinforcement learning for cyber-physical systems, with several implications and potential impacts:

- **Bridging RL and Control Theory for Safety:** The work robustly combines the data-driven adaptability and high-performance potential of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) with the formal, forward-looking safety guarantees inherent in Model Predictive Control (MPC). This integration is crucial for deploying advanced learning algorithms in safety-critical domains where traditional RL's unconstrained exploration is unacceptable.
- **Enabling Safe Exploration in Physical Systems:** By defining and pre-computing a recursively feasible state-action space offline, the methodology directly addresses the "safe exploration paradox." This allows RL agents to actively explore dynamic environments without incurring catastrophic damage to physical hardware, a fundamental requirement for real-world robotics and industrial automation.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The approach effectively shifts the heavy computational burden of predictive safety analysis from the real-time control loop to an offline phase. The online projection filter, based on the pre-computed map, is computationally light, allowing it to operate efficiently at high frequencies. This eliminates the real-time computational bottleneck often associated with model-based safety methods like online MPC or complex reachability analysis.
- **Enhanced System Stability and Reliability:** The prevention of catastrophic failures during the initial, highly stochastic stages of learning leads to a more stable and reliable training process. This reduces costly downtime, hardware replacement, and the need for extensive manual intervention or safety mechanisms during development.
- **Generalized Framework:** The proposed generalized framework provides a systematic approach for integrating safety, moving beyond domain-specific or ad-hoc solutions. Its application to a non-linear 1-DOF laboratory testbed demonstrates its practical utility and transferability to other systems with similar characteristics.
- **Identification of "Sim-to-Real" Gap Challenges:** The empirical validation on physical hardware, and the transparent discussion of observed intermittent constraint violations due to the "Sim-to-Real" gap, highlight critical practical challenges. This contributes to the broader understanding of deploying theoretically sound methods in imperfect real-world scenarios and emphasizes the need for robust control elements.

**Potential Future Impact:**

- **Scalability for Complex Systems:** The planned future work to replace grid search with Gaussian Processes (GPs) for active sampling of the feasibility boundary is critical for scaling the methodology to higher-dimensional state-action spaces, which would significantly broaden its applicability to more complex robotic and industrial systems.
- **Robustness to Model Inaccuracies:** The intention to integrate robust MPC formulations offline will directly address the "Sim-to-Real" gap, making the safety guarantees more resilient to unmodeled dynamics and disturbances, thereby increasing trust in autonomous systems.
- **Optimizing Performance and Safety Trade-offs:** Exploring multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) frameworks could allow for a more nuanced balance between aggressive task performance and maintaining a safe distance from constraint boundaries, leading to more sophisticated and adaptable controllers.

In essence, this research provides a practical and theoretically grounded method for embedding hard safety constraints into RL systems, paving the way for the safer and more widespread adoption of advanced learning-based control in critical industrial and robotic applications.
