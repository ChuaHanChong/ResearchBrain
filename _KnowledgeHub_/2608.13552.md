# PlayWorld: Benchmarking World Models with Agent Players over Long-Horizon Objectives

> [!summary] Summary
> PlayWorld is a benchmark that evaluates interactive video world models the way a human player would: each of 171 annotated cases specifies a long-horizon objective, and a multi-modal Agent Player adapts a reference action sequence online (Keep/Stop/Extend/Correct/End) while a Gemini VQA rubric verifier scores geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, out-of-sight and insight evolution. Nine models score at best 2.12/5 overall.

## Problem

- Existing world-model benchmarks drive every model with predefined camera trajectories or action sequences, so identical controls produce different movement magnitudes and non-comparable scores across models.
- Fixed schedules cannot complete complex patterns like orbiting a sculpture at a fixed heading, since required action duration and stopping point differ with each model's granularity.
- Closed-source web-interface systems such as Genie 3 admit only manual human play, making evaluation labor-intensive and biased; video-quality metrics ignore action response entirely.

## Method

- **Agent Player**: a ==multi-modal agent== (Claude Haiku 4.5) observing generated frames plus action history each step, issuing Keep, Stop, Extend, Correct, or End to adapt a human-annotated basic action sequence.
- ==Agent interface== translating those decisions into each model's native controls, including browser automation for web-only systems, closing the loop until End or a 40-step interaction budget, yielding 10-60 second rollouts.
- **VQA rubric verifier**: Gemini 3.1 Pro answers weighted Yes/No questions on contact-sheet frames after a dimension-specific ==trajectory-validity gate==, mapping to 1-5 scores over four capability dimensions plus VBench-style basic-ability metrics.

## Results

- Across nine models, Genie 3 leads overall at **2.12/5** and HappyOyster **1.92**; out-of-sight (**1.81** best) and insight evolution (**1.95** best) lag far behind geometry consistency.
- Preset+Agent control beats Preset Only and Agent Only on both Genie 3 and HappyOyster (trajectory score **1.08**/**1.12**, human preference **65.6%**/**67.4%**), modifying only **12-15%** of actions.
- Rubric scores track 600 human pairwise judgments with Spearman rho **0.933** overall; trajectory pass rate diverges from quality, SANA-WM reaching **80.4%** validity but only **1.48** rubric score.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Objective-level goals plus online action adaptation give fairer cross-model comparison than fixed control scripts whenever systems differ in action granularity or response latency.
> - High video quality and controllability scores can mask world-model failure; a nearly static rollout inflates depth stability and subject consistency without completing any objective.
> - Persistent state across time and occlusion is the hard frontier: models regenerate landmarks as new copies rather than anchoring a global 3D representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation models has progressed significantly, with recent systems demonstrating high visual quality and photorealism. A subset of this research, video world models, aims to simulate interactive environments that allow users to explore and interact while maintaining causal consistency across space and time. These models are designed to create "open worlds" for user interaction.

As world models develop, effective evaluation methods are becoming crucial. Existing benchmarks for general video generation, such as VBench, focus on aesthetics, temporal coherence, and text alignment, but do not adequately assess a model's responsiveness to user actions. More specialized world-model benchmarks evaluate aspects like 3D consistency, memory consistency, interaction, and out-of-sight state evolution (e.g., WorldScore, WorldMark, WBench, MemoBench, Omni-WorldBench).

However, a recurring limitation in many current world-model benchmarks is their reliance on predefined, low-level action trajectories. This approach presents several challenges:
- **Varying Action Granularity:** Different world models may interpret and execute the same sequence of actions (e.g., "press right arrow three times") with different magnitudes or durations, leading to inconsistent outcomes for a given objective (e.g., a 360-degree turn in one model versus a partial turn in another). This inconsistency makes direct comparisons of performance on specific objectives unreliable.
- **Inadequate for Complex Objectives:** Fixed trajectories struggle to reliably achieve more complex, long-horizon objectives that require dynamic adjustment based on the model's response, such as orbiting an object while maintaining a fixed viewing direction.
- **Challenges with Closed-Source Models:** Many advanced world models are proprietary (e.g., Genie 3, HappyOyster) and only accessible via web interfaces, making automated, fixed-trajectory evaluation difficult and labor-intensive. Human interaction for evaluation is prone to bias and lacks scalability.

PlayWorld addresses these gaps by introducing an evaluation paradigm that more closely simulates how a human user would interact with a world model. Instead of fixed action sequences, it employs an "Agent Player" that adaptively adjusts actions in a closed loop to pursue high-level, long-horizon objectives, thereby enabling fairer and more consistent cross-model comparison, particularly for interactive and web-based systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a comprehensive and fair benchmark, named PlayWorld, for evaluating interactive video world models, especially concerning their performance on long-horizon objectives.

The motivation behind PlayWorld stems from several critical limitations observed in the existing evaluation landscape for interactive world models:
1. **Mismatch with Human Evaluation:** Human users typically evaluate world models by attempting to achieve high-level, long-horizon goals (e.g., "walk into the water," "turn 360 degrees") rather than verifying a predefined sequence of low-level actions. Current benchmarks often neglect this human-centric evaluation approach, failing to capture real-world user experience and model utility.
2. **Inconsistent Comparison due to Action Granularity:** The same set of low-level actions can produce vastly different visual and spatial outcomes across different world models due to variations in their action granularity and response dynamics. This makes it challenging to compare models fairly when they are evaluated on identical, predefined action sequences, as the target state for an objective might not be reached or might be overshot in some models. For example, a fixed number of "right turn" commands might complete a full rotation in one model but only a partial turn in another, making geometry consistency evaluations incomparable.
3. **Difficulty in Evaluating Adaptive Interactions:** Many long-horizon objectives require adaptive interaction where subsequent actions depend on the model's current visual response. Current benchmarks, with their fixed trajectories, cannot flexibly adjust to model-specific behaviors, thus limiting the types of complex interactions that can be robustly evaluated.
4. **Accessibility Challenges for Closed-Source Models:** A number of state-of-the-art world models are web-based and closed-source, making them difficult to integrate into standard evaluation pipelines that rely on direct API access or local execution. These models often require direct human interaction through a web interface, which is labor-intensive, slow, and susceptible to human bias if not automated.

To overcome these challenges, PlayWorld aims to:
- Introduce an automated evaluation protocol that simulates human-like interaction with world models.
- Enable consistent evaluation across diverse models by ensuring they are tested under the *same objectives*, even if the specific low-level actions to achieve those objectives differ.
- Provide a robust and scalable method for evaluating both open-source and web-based closed-source world models.
- Offer a detailed assessment of critical world-model capabilities beyond basic video quality, including geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, and persistent state evolution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PlayWorld introduces a novel evaluation framework centered on a multi-modal "Agent Player" that interacts with world models to achieve long-horizon objectives. The methodology can be broken down into Agent Player design, benchmark construction, a VQA rubric verifier, and basic ability evaluation.

#### 3.1. Agent Player Design

The Agent Player is designed to simulate human interaction by adaptively adjusting actions based on visual observations. It consists of two main components:
- **Agent Model:** A multi-modal agent (e.g., Claude Haiku 4.5) that observes generated frames, action history, scene descriptions, and the objective. It makes decisions on how to adapt the execution of a basic action sequence.
- **Agent Interface:** This component directly interacts with the evaluated world model. It executes the agent model's decisions, captures the resulting frames, and feeds them back to the agent model, creating a closed interaction loop. For web-based models, it uses browser automation to dispatch controls and capture frames.

**Decision-Making Process:** The Agent Player receives a long-horizon objective and a human-annotated basic action sequence as an initial reference. At each step, based on the current observation and context, the agent model decides one of five actions:
- **Keep:** Continue the current planned action or advance to the next action.
- **Stop:** Terminate the active action early if the intended visual state is reached.
- **Extend:** Increase the action duration if more movement is needed.
- **Correct:** Revise or skip the next planned action if the observed state deviates from the reference.
- **End:** Terminate the evaluation case when the long-horizon objective is completed. This includes cases where interaction fidelity involves reaching and continuing through an obstacle to reveal collision behavior.

This adaptive strategy ensures that models are evaluated on achieving the *same objective* regardless of their individual action granularities or response dynamics, making comparisons fairer.

#### 3.2. PlayWorld Benchmark Construction

The benchmark is constructed around 171 evaluation scenarios, each comprising:
- **Initial World:** A starting image (from Pexels or Google Images) covering diverse environments (natural, urban, fantasy) and subjects (human, animal, vehicle). Each image is accompanied by an environment caption generated by Gemini 3.1 Pro and verified by humans.
- **Scene-Grounded Objective:** A high-level, long-horizon goal (e.g., "orbit around the statue," "run into water") with a visually verifiable completion condition.
- **Basic Action Sequence:** A human-annotated sequence of W/A/S/D, ↑/↓/←/→, and WAIT controls that serves as the Agent Player's initial reference.
- **VQA Rubric:** Sample-specific questions tailored to the target evaluation dimension.

The benchmark spans 50 action patterns, producing rollouts of approximately 10 to 60 seconds. The evaluation of nine world models generated over 1,400 interactive videos, which are assessed using more than 820 VQA questions.

#### 3.3. VQA Rubric Verifier

To quantitatively assess model capabilities, PlayWorld employs a VQA rubric verifier, leveraging Gemini 3.1 Pro to answer sample-specific Yes/No questions for each generated rollout. The rubric is structured across four core dimensions, with dimension-specific validation gates:

1. **Geometry Consistency:** Evaluates whether the environment's structure is preserved during camera movement and revisitation.
 - *Validation:* Trajectory Validity (did the rollout follow the intended path and reach the viewpoint?).
 - *Rubric:* Scene Identity (preservation of object identity, count, texture, color) and Spatial Consistency (relative object positions, overall scene layout).
2. **Interaction Fidelity:** Assesses if intended interactions occur and elicit physically plausible responses.
 - *Validation:* Subject and Reachability (did the subject respond to actions and reach the interaction region?).
 - *Rubric:* Contact and Collision (e.g., colliding with a wall vs. passing through), Motion and Causality (e.g., grounded footsteps, realistic water ripples), and Visual Response.
3. **Out-of-Sight Evolution:** Checks if a target preserves identity and continues to change plausibly while unobserved.
 - *Validation:* Trajectory Validity (did the target leave and then reappear in view?).
 - *Rubric:* Reappearance Consistency (is the reappeared target the same entity?), Hidden-State Evolution (did the state progress plausibly during occlusion?), and Physical Causality.
4. **Insight Evolution:** Focuses on continuously observed phenomena without explicit action trajectories (stationary camera).
 - *No Validation Required:* Since the camera is stationary.
 - *Rubric:* Identity and State Progression (e.g., object state changing over time), Motion and Physical Plausibility (e.g., absence of static or repetitive non-progressive actions), and Temporal Scene Consistency (no unintended environmental changes).

Scores are aggregated using weighted Yes/No outcomes and converted to a 1–5 scale. Invalid rollouts for geometry, interaction, and out-of-sight dimensions receive a minimum score of 1.

#### 3.4. Basic Ability Evaluation

Complementary to the VQA rubric, PlayWorld incorporates automatic metrics for basic video quality and controllability:
- **Video Quality:** Includes Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality, Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering (from VBench), Temporal Consistency (from Omni-WorldBench), Depth Stability (using Depth Anything V2), and Subject Consistency (adapted from HyDRA). These are normalized to.
- **Action Controllability:** Measured by Translation Pass Rate and Rotation Pass Rate (following WorldMark), using camera poses estimated by VGGT. A rollout passes if the respective error falls below a defined threshold.

All nine metrics (seven video quality, two action controllability) are combined into a "Basic Ability Score" by converting their average rank into a higher-is-better percentage.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

Nine representative world models (five closed-source web-based, four open-source local) were evaluated. Each model received the same initial world, objective, and basic action sequence. The Agent Player adaptively controlled each model, and the resulting 10-60 second rollouts were assessed using both the VQA rubric and basic ability metrics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation across nine state-of-the-art world models using the PlayWorld benchmark yielded several observations regarding the current capabilities and limitations of interactive video world models.

#### 4.1. Sustained World Evolution Remains a Primary Bottleneck

- **VQA Rubric Scores (Table 2):** Across all evaluated models, "out-of-sight evolution" and "insight evolution" consistently received lower scores compared to "geometry consistency" and "interaction fidelity." Genie 3, while achieving the highest overall score (2.12), still demonstrated modest performance in these categories (1.81 for out-of-sight, 1.51 for insight evolution). LingBot-World2 led in "insight evolution" (1.95), but this was still a relatively low score on the 1-5 scale.
- **Implication:** This indicates that current world models struggle to maintain semantic state changes, causal progression, and object persistence over long durations, especially when objects are unobserved or when the scene requires complex, continuous evolution without explicit user action. Processes may remain static, reset, or evolve illogically.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Revisitation Exposes Global Spatial Inconsistency

- **Geometry Consistency Failures (Figure 5):** The evaluation highlighted that while models might maintain local visual plausibility, they often fail to preserve global spatial consistency over long-horizon trajectories involving camera movement and revisitation. For instance, orbiting a landmark like the Taj Mahal might result in the model repeatedly regenerating the monument at different viewpoints rather than maintaining a single, consistent 3D representation.
- **Implication:** This suggests that many models lack a robust, persistent global representation of the generated 3D world, relying more on short-range appearance continuity and local motion cues, leading to structural inconsistencies upon revisitation.

#### 4.3. Interaction Fidelity is Limited Beyond Simple Responses

- **Interaction Fidelity Scores (Table 2):** Interaction fidelity scores were generally moderate. While some models could handle simple interactions like stopping a character at an obstacle boundary in third-person views, first-person movements often resulted in characters passing directly through solid obstacles (Figure 5).
- **Complex Interactions:** More intricate interactions, such as walking into water, frequently failed to produce realistic physical or visual responses (e.g., ripples, splashes, depth changes).
- **Action Binding:** In third-person scenarios, models sometimes showed inconsistent binding of input actions to the controlled character, leading to delayed, insufficient, or unrelated motions.
- **Implication:** Current world models have basic reactive capabilities but lack sophisticated physical simulation and understanding, struggling to generate complex, causally consistent, and visually plausible responses to diverse user interactions.

#### 4.4. Trajectory Control and World-Model Capability Can Diverge

- **Trajectory Validation Pass Rates (Table 3):** Models exhibited varying pass rates for following the intended trajectories. SANA-WM, for example, achieved a relatively high overall validation pass rate (80.4%), suggesting it effectively moved the camera/subject to the target regions.
- **Discrepancy:** However, SANA-WM's VQA rubric scores (e.g., 1.72 for geometry consistency, 1.48 overall) were modest.
- **Implication:** High action controllability and the ability to reach target regions do not automatically translate to strong underlying world-model capabilities such as preserving memory, maintaining spatial consistency, or generating physically plausible interactions. A model can move correctly but generate an inconsistent or implausible world.

#### 4.5. Automatic Metrics Primarily Test Basic Abilities

- **Basic Ability Scores (Table 6):** Most evaluated models performed well on pixel-level video quality metrics (Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality, Motion Smoothness). Action Controllability (Translation Pass Rate, Rotation Pass Rate) showed more variability. HappyOyster achieved the highest Basic Ability Score (76.4%).
- **Limited Scope:** However, high scores in basic abilities or controllability metrics did not necessarily correlate with strong performance on complex, long-horizon interactive objectives assessed by the VQA rubric. For instance, HappyOyster, with the highest Basic Ability Score, ranked second overall in VQA rubric scores (1.92).
- **Implication:** While useful for foundational quality checks, traditional automatic metrics often fall short in assessing the deeper, interactive, and causal consistency aspects crucial for human-like world model evaluation. They may not guarantee the completion of objective-specific target states or long-horizon goals.

#### 4.6. Human Validation Confirms VQA Rubric Alignment

- **Correlation (Figure 6):** A human evaluation study involving 600 pairwise judgments showed strong positive Spearman correlations (ρ > 0.745 across all dimensions, with ρ = 0.933 overall) between the VQA rubric scores and human preference rankings.
- **Implication:** This validation indicates that the VQA rubric effectively captures human perception of world model quality for long-horizon interactive scenarios, lending credibility to PlayWorld's automated evaluation methodology.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PlayWorld introduces a significant advancement in the evaluation of interactive video world models, addressing key limitations of previous benchmarks and offering several potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Fairer and More Relevant Evaluation:** By replacing static, predefined action sequences with an adaptive Agent Player, PlayWorld ensures that models are evaluated on achieving the *same long-horizon objectives*, despite variations in their action granularity and response dynamics. This paradigm provides a more robust and fair basis for cross-model comparison, aligning evaluation closer to how human users interact with and judge these systems.

2. **Comprehensive Assessment of World-Model Capabilities:** The benchmark's multi-faceted VQA rubric, spanning geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, out-of-sight evolution, and insight evolution, allows for a more detailed and nuanced assessment of a world model's cognitive capabilities beyond simple video quality. It highlights aspects like persistent state management, causal reasoning, and global spatial awareness, which are critical for true "world simulation."

3. **Identification of Key Research Bottlenecks:** The experimental results clearly identify "sustained world evolution" (both observed and out-of-sight) and "global spatial consistency" as significant weaknesses across current state-of-the-art world models. This empirical evidence provides clear directions for future research and development efforts, guiding the community towards fundamental challenges rather than incremental improvements on superficial metrics.

4. **Accelerating Development of Interactive AI:** By providing an automated, scalable, and human-aligned evaluation framework, PlayWorld can significantly accelerate the research and development of interactive world models. Researchers can rapidly test new architectures and algorithms against a standardized, challenging benchmark, fostering innovation in areas like embodied AI, robotics, and immersive content generation.

5. **Bridging the Gap Between Technical Metrics and User Experience:** The design of the Agent Player and the strong correlation between the VQA rubric scores and human preferences mean that PlayWorld's metrics are more aligned with the perceived quality and utility from a human user's perspective. This helps ensure that model improvements measured by the benchmark translate into a better, more engaging, and consistent user experience in interactive applications.

6. **Accessibility for Diverse Models:** The Agent Player's ability to interact with both open-source and web-based closed-source models democratizes evaluation, allowing a broader range of systems to be benchmarked under consistent conditions, including those developed by large industrial labs.

In conclusion, PlayWorld establishes a practical and theoretically grounded benchmark that pushes the frontier of world model evaluation, serving as a critical tool for understanding current limitations and driving future advancements in the creation of truly intelligent and interactive virtual worlds.
