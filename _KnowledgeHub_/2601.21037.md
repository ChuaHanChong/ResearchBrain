# Thinking in Frames: How Visual Context and Test-Time Scaling Empower Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Video generation models demonstrate capabilities as visual reasoners, showcasing robust zero-shot generalization across diverse spatial planning tasks such as maze navigation and tangram puzzles. Researchers from the University of Cambridge and the Pioneer Center for AI identify a "Visual Test-Time Scaling" phenomenon where an increased inference budget via more generated frames enhances out-of-distribution performance, consistently surpassing text-based MLLMs in visual-native challenges.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are limited in embodied and spatial reasoning due to their reliance on low-bandwidth textual descriptions for complex visual dynamics.
- Previous visual reasoning research often focused on discrete actions and in-distribution settings, lacking comprehensive evaluation for continuous manipulation or out-of-distribution generalization.
- There was a lack of thorough quantitative investigation into leveraging video generation models specifically for visual reasoning tasks beyond qualitative observations.

## Method

- The authors repurpose a fine-tuned ==text-to-video diffusion model== (Wan 2.2 TI2V 5B) to perform visual reasoning, viewing generated frames as explicit reasoning steps.
- They evaluate this approach on two diverse visual reasoning tasks: ==MAZENAVIGATION== for sequential discrete planning and ==TANGRAMPUZZLE== for continuous geometric manipulation.
- The methodology explores =="Visual Test-Time Scaling"== by generating varied video lengths during inference to observe its effect on problem-solving complexity.

## Results

- The video generation model demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization, maintaining high performance on out-of-distribution maze sizes and unseen agent icons.
- It achieved strong zero-shot transfer to novel tangram silhouettes, with up to **68.0%** Strict Goal Completion in the Translation variant.
- The approach consistently outperformed text-based MLLMs, especially in tasks requiring precise visual-native reasoning like TANGRAMPUZZLE, where MLLMs struggled with near-**zero** strict goal completion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit visual context, rather than textual instructions, provides a more effective control mechanism for guiding generative models in visual reasoning tasks.
> - Increasing the generated video length during inference ("Visual Test-Time Scaling") improves performance on complex out-of-distribution problems, showing parallels to Chain-of-Thought in LLMs.
> - Video generation models can learn abstract planning algorithms, enabling generalization to unseen visual elements and irregular spatial layouts, indicating a focus on underlying logic.
