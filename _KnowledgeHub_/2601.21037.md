# Thinking in Frames: How Visual Context and Test-Time Scaling Empower Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Video generation models demonstrate capabilities as visual reasoners, showcasing robust zero-shot generalization across diverse spatial planning tasks such as maze navigation and tangram puzzles. Researchers from the University of Cambridge and the Pioneer Center for AI identify a "Visual Test-Time Scaling" phenomenon where an increased inference budget via more generated frames enhances out-of-distribution performance, consistently surpassing text-based MLLMs in visual-native challenges.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are limited in embodied and spatial reasoning due to their reliance on low-bandwidth textual descriptions for complex visual dynamics.
- Previous visual reasoning research often focused on discrete actions and in-distribution settings, lacking comprehensive evaluation for continuous manipulation or out-of-distribution generalization.
- There was a lack of thorough quantitative investigation into leveraging video generation models specifically for visual reasoning tasks beyond qualitative observations.

## Method

- The authors repurpose a fine-tuned ==text-to-video diffusion model== (Wan 2.2 TI2V 5B) to perform visual reasoning, viewing generated frames as explicit reasoning steps.
- They evaluate this approach on two diverse visual reasoning tasks: ==MAZENAVIGATION== for sequential discrete planning and ==TANGRAMPUZZLE== for continuous geometric manipulation.
- The methodology explores =="Visual Test-Time Scaling"== by generating varied video lengths during inference to observe its effect on problem-solving complexity.

## Results

- The video generation model demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization, maintaining high performance on out-of-distribution maze sizes and unseen agent icons.
- It achieved strong zero-shot transfer to novel tangram silhouettes, with up to **68.0%** Strict Goal Completion in the Translation variant.
- The approach consistently outperformed text-based MLLMs, especially in tasks requiring precise visual-native reasoning like TANGRAMPUZZLE, where MLLMs struggled with near-**zero** strict goal completion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit visual context, rather than textual instructions, provides a more effective control mechanism for guiding generative models in visual reasoning tasks.
> - Increasing the generated video length during inference ("Visual Test-Time Scaling") improves performance on complex out-of-distribution problems, showing parallels to Chain-of-Thought in LLMs.
> - Video generation models can learn abstract planning algorithms, enabling generalization to unseen visual elements and irregular spatial layouts, indicating a focus on underlying logic.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, particularly at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), multimodal AI, and generative models. Historically, LLMs have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in textual reasoning, decomposing complex goals into actionable steps. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have extended this success to visual semantics, yet they encounter significant limitations in embodied and spatial domains. The primary bottleneck identified is their reliance on textual descriptions for reasoning, which are often inefficient and imprecise for capturing fine-grained geometric contexts and physical dynamics, such as continuous rotations or collision-free trajectories.

Recognizing these limitations, recent research has explored "reasoning with multimodal traces" or "thinking directly in the visual domain" (e.g., MVoT, Visual Planning, latent visual reasoning) to leverage higher-bandwidth representations for spatial intelligence. Concurrently, video generation models, traditionally viewed as tools for media creation, are beginning to be explored for their potential in logical consistency and reasoning. While proprietary models like Google's Veo 3 have qualitatively hinted at video-based reasoning, a thorough quantitative investigation with open reproducibility has been largely absent.

This work directly addresses this gap by formally framing visual reasoning through the lens of video generation models. It challenges the conventional view of video generation as merely a media tool, instead positing it as a scalable and generalizable paradigm for visual reasoning. By rigorously evaluating video generation models in diverse visual reasoning tasks, the authors move beyond the limitations of text-based MLLMs and the qualitative explorations of proprietary video models. The paper specifically distinguishes itself from prior visual reasoning work that largely focused on discrete actions, minimal visual changes, and in-distribution settings (e.g., maze navigation on familiar grid sizes). Instead, it pushes the boundaries by investigating continuous manipulation, high visual change, and robust out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, thus significantly expanding the scope and ambition of visual reasoning research. It also introduces the novel concept of "Visual Test-Time Scaling," drawing a direct parallel to Chain-of-Thought prompting in LLMs, which is a significant conceptual bridge between language-centric and visual-centric reasoning paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the inherent limitations of current MLLMs in truly understanding and reasoning within complex visual and embodied environments. While MLLMs excel at semantic understanding, their reliance on text as the primary reasoning modality proves inadequate for tasks requiring precise spatial, geometric, and dynamic understanding. Textual descriptions fail to capture the nuances of continuous action spaces and fine-grained visual changes necessary for tasks like manipulating objects or navigating intricate environments.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets out with several key objectives:

1. **Reconceptualize Video Generation for Reasoning:** The primary objective is to investigate whether video generation models can be effectively leveraged not just for creating visually appealing sequences, but as a robust mechanism for visual reasoning. The hypothesis is that generated frames can act as interpretable, intermediate reasoning steps, providing a continuous proxy of the reasoning process that text-based MLLMs cannot offer.
2. **Evaluate Across Diverse Visual Reasoning Regimes:** To provide a comprehensive assessment, the authors aim to evaluate video generation models across a spectrum of challenges. This involves two distinct tasks:
 - **MAZENAVIGATION:** Represents sequential, discrete planning with low visual change, evaluating long-horizon logical consistency and collision avoidance.
 - **TANGRAMPUZZLE:** Represents continuous manipulation with high visual change and strict geometric constraints, pushing the model's ability to maintain fidelity during dynamic transformations.
3. **Investigate Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** A crucial objective is to determine the models' ability to generalize beyond their training distribution. This includes unseen maze sizes, longer path lengths, novel agent icons, and new tangram silhouettes, addressing the common criticism of visual agents overfitting to specific visual assets or in-distribution settings.
4. **Explore Visual Context as a Control Mechanism:** The research aims to understand how explicit visual context (e.g., providing an agent icon or initial tangram piece layouts) can function as a powerful control signal, potentially surpassing textual instructions in guiding visual reasoning and promoting robust generalization.
5. **Identify "Visual Test-Time Scaling" Phenomena:** A novel objective is to explore if increasing the "visual inference budget"—by generating more video frames during inference—can empower models to solve more spatially and temporally complex problems, akin to how Chain-of-Thought prompting or increased compute budget aids LLMs in complex reasoning.

Ultimately, the motivation is to uncover if video generation can move beyond media synthesis to become a fundamental, scalable paradigm for developing more capable, generalizable, and interpretable visual reasoning agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a methodology centered on formulating visual reasoning as a video generation problem, where the model implicitly learns latent rules and causal dynamics by generating dense temporal transitions.

**A. Model Backbone and Fine-tuning:**
The core model used is **Wan 2.2 TI2V 5B**, a text-to-video diffusion model pre-trained on large-scale video data, typically generating sequences of 81 frames. This model is fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for 20 epochs, making it adaptable to specific visual reasoning tasks while leveraging its powerful pre-trained generative capabilities.

**B. Reasoning Regimes and Dataset Construction:**
To rigorously evaluate the model's capabilities, two contrasting visual planning regimes are introduced:

1. **MAZENAVIGATION (Sequential Discrete Planning, Low Visual Change):**
 - **Task:** An agent navigates a grid-world maze from a start point to a goal, avoiding walls.
 - **Dataset:** Built upon Ivanitskiy et al. (2023), it includes mazes from 3x3 to 8x8, with optimal path lengths from 2 to 18 steps. Crucially, it incorporates 40 distinct agent icons, including a set of "unseen icons" for OOD visual generalization.
 - **Evaluation Settings:** In-distribution, OOD Maze Sizes (7x7, 8x8), OOD Path Length (13-18 steps), and OOD Both (larger mazes with longer paths), all tested with both seen and unseen visual icons.
 - **Metrics:** Exact Match (EM) for perfect path generation and Progress Rate (PR) for partial success, adapted from Xu et al. (2025). A robust, motion-centric evaluator based on background subtraction and speed-invariant trajectory alignment is used to handle variable agent designs and generation speeds.

2. **TANGRAMPUZZLE (Continuous Manipulation, High Visual Change):**
 - **Task:** Manipulate seven geometric tangram pieces to precisely fill a target silhouette without overlap or distortion.
 - **Dataset:** Repurposed from the Kilogram dataset (Ji et al., 2022), consisting of 692 unique silhouettes.
 - **Variants:**
 - **Fade-in:** Pieces gradually appear in target locations (no geometric transformation needed).
 - **Rotation:** Pieces start with random orientations outside the silhouette, requiring rotation and translation.
 - **Translation:** Pieces start pre-aligned outside, requiring only translation.
 - **Metrics:** Strict Goal Completion (all pieces correctly placed without overlap/distortion), Progress Goal Completion (normalized count of correctly placed pieces), and Boundary Adherence (IoU of generated pieces with target silhouette). A deterministic, rule-based pixel-level geometric evaluator is developed for precise measurement.

**C. Baseline Models:**
The video generation approach is compared against strong baselines:

- **Text-based Planning (MLLMs):** Proprietary models (GPT-5.1, GPT-5.2) in zero-shot settings, and fine-tuned open-source models (Qwen3-VL-8B) for both tasks, outputting textual actions or JSON-formatted coordinates/rotations.
- **Image-based Planning:** VPRL (Xu et al., 2025) for MAZENAVIGATION; proprietary (Nano Banana / Gemini 3 Pro Image) and fine-tuned open-source (Qwen-Image-Edit-20B) image editing models for TANGRAMPUZZLE, which predict the final state image.

**D. Specific Experimental Probes:**

- **Visual Context as Control:** Experiments in MAZENAVIGATION compare providing a visual icon in the first frame vs. a textual description for OOD agent appearances. TANGRAMPUZZLE variants naturally test the impact of varying degrees of visual context.
- **Visual Test-Time Scaling:** For MAZENAVIGATION, the model generates videos of varying lengths (e.g., 61, 81, 101, 121, 141 frames) or different "scaling factors" (κ, frames per discrete maze step) to assess the impact of increased inference budget on OOD task performance.

The approach emphasizes systematic, quantitative evaluation across varied scenarios to robustly assess the strengths and limitations of video generation models as visual reasoners.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted reveal several critical insights into the capabilities of video generation models for visual reasoning:

1. **Robust Zero-Shot Generalization:**
 - **MAZENAVIGATION:** The video generation model (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) demonstrates strong performance and graceful degradation across out-of-distribution (OOD) maze sizes (e.g., 92.0% EM on unseen 7x7 grids from a 6x6 training distribution) and OOD path lengths. Crucially, it exhibits negligible performance loss when encountering entirely unseen visual agent icons, indicating it learns the underlying planning algorithm rather than memorizing pixel patterns.
 - **TANGRAMPUZZLE:** The model shows robust zero-shot transfer to unseen target silhouettes. In the Translation setting, accuracy on unseen silhouettes (60.8%) is comparable to seen ones (68.0%), confirming it has internalized generalized geometric fitting concepts.

2. **Visual Context as Control:**
 - Explicit visual context proves more effective than textual instructions for controlling generative models in visual reasoning tasks. In MAZENAVIGATION, providing the OOD agent icon visually in the first frame allows for successful generalization, whereas text prompts often fail to generate the correct agent.
 - For TANGRAMPUZZLE, the availability of visual context significantly impacts performance. The Translation setting (pieces pre-aligned, only translation needed) yields 68.0% Strict Goal Completion, significantly higher than Rotation (22.4%), and Fade-In (0.8%). This hierarchy indicates that clear geometric priors provided through visual context are essential for robust spatial manipulation.

3. **Emergence of a Visual Test-Time Scaling Law:**
 - In MAZENAVIGATION, increasing the generated video length (visual inference budget) from 81 to 121 frames consistently improves performance on spatially and temporally OOD tasks. This parallels "System 2" thinking in human cognition and Chain-of-Thought prompting in LLMs, where more "thought" (frames) leads to better solutions.
 - Qualitatively, this scaling leads to emergent "self-correction" behaviors: agents initially making wrong turns might backtrack and correct their paths within the generated sequence, a behavior not explicitly trained.
 - However, a ceiling effect is observed at very long sequences (e.g., 141 frames), attributed to architectural limitations of positional embeddings in the video model.
 - **Fidelity-Reasoning Trade-off:** This scaling effect is task-dependent. It strongly benefits MAZENAVIGATION (low visual change, logical focus) but does not yield significant gains for TANGRAMPUZZLE (high visual change, geometric fidelity focus), where maintaining precise geometric consistency over many frames becomes a bottleneck.

4. **Superiority over Text-based Baselines in Visual-Native Tasks:**
 - Video-based reasoning consistently outperforms text-based MLLMs, particularly in TANGRAMPUZZLE, where MLLMs struggle to verbalize precise spatial manipulations and achieve near-zero strict goal completion due to difficulties in cross-modal grounding (visual to symbolic coordinates).
 - Even fine-tuned MLLMs show limited generalization to OOD maze settings and struggle to reliably capture underlying spatial layouts.

5. **Further Generalization and Insights:**
 - The video generation model exhibits remarkable zero-shot adaptation to irregular maze layouts, generating diagonal trajectories and adhering to collision constraints, despite being trained only on grid-based horizontal/vertical movements. This suggests learning an abstract policy of goal-directed movement under collision constraints.
 - While image editing models can achieve higher performance on TANGRAMPUZZLE (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit-20B with 85.7% vs. Wan2.2-TI2V-5B with 68.0% on Translation), video generation offers better interpretability and verifiability through intermediate reasoning traces. The performance gap is partially attributed to model size and optimization density (video models model the entire sequence vs. image editing focusing on the final state).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of artificial intelligence, repositioning video generation from primarily a media synthesis tool to a powerful paradigm for visual reasoning. Its implications are multi-faceted, with potential impacts across several domains:

1. **Advancing Visual Reasoning Capabilities:** The paper demonstrates that generative video models provide an expressive, high-bandwidth representation for spatial planning that text-based MLLMs currently lack. This opens a new avenue for developing AI systems that can "think" directly in the visual domain, overcoming the limitations of translating complex visual dynamics into textual tokens. This capability is crucial for tasks requiring continuous, fine-grained visual understanding.

2. **Enhancing AI Generalization and Robustness:** The observed robust zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) settings (unseen maze sizes, path lengths, agent icons, and tangram silhouettes) is a critical achievement. It suggests that video generation models can abstract underlying planning algorithms rather than merely memorizing visual patterns. This disentanglement of logic from superficial visual features makes AI systems more reliable and adaptable to novel real-world scenarios, where encountering unseen variations is common.

3. **A New Frontier in AI Cognition: Visual Test-Time Scaling:** The discovery of a "Visual Test-Time Scaling Law" is a conceptual breakthrough, drawing a direct parallel to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and "System 2" thinking in large language models. This finding suggests that simply allocating a larger "inference budget" in the form of more generated frames can empower visual models to solve more complex, long-horizon problems. This sheds light on how AI models can perform deeper, more deliberative reasoning in the visual domain, offering a new dimension for understanding and scaling AI intelligence. It also opens new research directions into resource-aware visual reasoning.

4. **Improved Controllability and Interpretability:** The demonstration that explicit visual context acts as a stronger and more natural control signal than textual instructions is highly significant. This could simplify prompt engineering for visual tasks and enable more intuitive human-AI interaction in visual domains. Furthermore, generating intermediate video frames offers superior interpretability and verifiability compared to single-step image editing or abstract textual plans, allowing users to understand the AI's "thought process" and pinpoint failure modes visually.

5. **Potential for Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to simulate complex visual dynamics and plan continuous actions robustly makes this work highly relevant for embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous systems. Robots require precise spatial understanding, collision avoidance, and the ability to manipulate objects in continuously changing environments. Video generation as a reasoning paradigm could lead to more intelligent, adaptable, and robust robotic agents capable of complex tasks.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also clearly outlines future research avenues, such as addressing the fidelity-reasoning trade-off in high visual change scenarios (e.g., TANGRAMPUZZLE) to improve geometric consistency, further exploring cross-domain adaptation (e.g., irregular mazes), and delving deeper into the mechanisms behind visual test-time scaling. These challenges will drive the next generation of visual AI research.

In conclusion, this research not only presents a novel and effective approach to visual reasoning but also contributes fundamental insights into the nature of AI intelligence. By empowering AI to "think in frames," it paves the way for more capable, generalizable, and interpretable multimodal AI systems.
