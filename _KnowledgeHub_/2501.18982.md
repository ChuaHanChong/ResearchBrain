# OmniPhysGS: 3D Constitutive Gaussians for General Physics-Based Dynamics Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> OMNIPHYSGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with learnable constitutive models, automatically generating physically accurate dynamics for a wide range of materials and complex object interactions. This framework synthesizes dynamic scenes for elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, and fluid substances, demonstrating improved text-alignment and physical consistency in multi-object scenarios.

## Problem

- Current 3D dynamic scene generation methods, particularly those leveraging diffusion models, often lack strict adherence to physical laws, leading to visual artifacts and inconsistencies.
- Existing physics-integrated methods either require time-consuming manual tuning of physical properties by experts or are limited to specific material categories (e.g., pure elastic), restricting their applicability to diverse real-world scenes.
- No general and automatic solution existed for synthesizing dynamic scenes across a broad spectrum of material behaviors and compositions within a unified system.

## Method

- **OmniPhysGS** introduces ==Constitutive Gaussians== where each 3D Gaussian particle is endowed with a learnable neural network-based ==constitutive model== for predicting material properties (hyperelastic energy, plasticity, physical parameters).
- Employs a ==physics-guided constitutive network== that uses a 3D feature encoder to process initial Gaussian attributes and a physical-aware decoder to assign expert-designed constitutive models (from an ensemble of 12 combinations) to guide material property learning.
- Utilizes a custom, memory-optimized differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) re-implemented in PyTorch for simulating dynamics, combined with ==Score Distillation Sampling== (==SDS==) from a pre-trained text-to-==video diffusion model== for high-level textual supervision.

## Results

- Achieved an average improvement of **3%** to **16%** in CLIPSIM (text-alignment) for single objects exhibiting diverse material properties (elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, fluid) compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Demonstrated superior performance in synthesizing multi-object scenes with heterogeneous materials, surpassing baselines by approximately **4%** in text-alignment metrics, and accurately modeling distinct interactions.
- The custom PyTorch MPM solver reduced training memory consumption by approximately **75%** compared to existing Warp-based solvers, facilitating efficient end-to-end training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating neural networks with expert-designed physical constitutive models allows for flexible learning of material properties while ensuring physical plausibility and stability, addressing the highly nonlinear nature of material behaviors.
> - A memory-efficient PyTorch re-implementation of the differentiable MPM solver enables end-to-end training on a single GPU, significantly reducing computational demands compared to previous solvers.
> - A specialized training strategy involving mini-batch grouping and multiple iterative optimizations within each simulation stage effectively mitigates error accumulation and stabilizes the learning process for long dynamic sequences supervised by high-level diffusion guidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D asset generation and reconstruction has advanced significantly, encompassing both static and dynamic scenes. Recent progress in 3D differentiable rendering techniques, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), has provided a foundation for learning-based synthesis of dynamic 3D (4D) scenes. Many contemporary methods for 4D generation leverage text-to-image and video diffusion models, often employing Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to distill 4D objects. These approaches utilize various dynamic 3D representations, including multi-scale grids, neural radiance fields with deformation fields, or warped Gaussian surfels. While these data-driven techniques can produce visually vivid dynamics, they often lack strict adherence to physical laws, leading to potential artifacts, inconsistencies, and a lack of physical plausibility.

To address the limitations of non-physical dynamics, a growing body of research has focused on integrating physical priors into 4D scene synthesis. The particle-based nature of 3D Gaussian Splatting makes it a suitable representation for assigning physical properties to individual elements. Previous work, such as PhysGaussian, introduced the Material Point Method (MPM) to simulate the dynamics of 3DGS. Subsequent studies have extended MPM for learning-based dynamic 3D scene synthesis, often incorporating video supervision from diffusion models.

However, existing physics-integrated methods face several challenges. Some require manual tuning of physical properties, a process that is time-consuming and demands expert knowledge, potentially resulting in suboptimal outcomes. Others are constrained to specific material categories, such as pure elastic or viscoelastic materials, limiting their applicability to complex real-world scenarios that often involve heterogeneous objects composed of diverse materials. These limitations collectively prevent existing methods from offering a general and automatic solution for dynamic scene synthesis across a broad spectrum of material behaviors and interactions.

OMNIPHYSGS positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical challenge of material property generalization. It aims to overcome the reliance on fixed or narrowly defined physical models by introducing a learnable framework for material constitutive properties, thereby enabling the generation of physically plausible dynamics for a wide range of materials and complex object compositions within a unified system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind OMNIPHYSGS stems from the limitations of current 3D dynamic scene synthesis methods, particularly their inability to accurately and automatically represent the diverse physical behaviors of real-world materials. Existing approaches typically make simplifying assumptions about material homogeneity or necessitate manual intervention for specifying physical properties. This results in animations that may lack physical plausibility when dealing with complex scenes comprising multiple heterogeneous objects or materials beyond a predefined category.

The key objectives of this research are detailed as follows:

1. **To synthesize general physics-based 3D dynamic scenes:** The primary goal is to generate dynamics for a comprehensive range of materials, including but not limited to elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, and fluid substances. This also extends to accurately modeling interactions between different material types within a single scene, moving beyond the capabilities of methods restricted to specific material categories.
2. **To incorporate strong physical constraints into the dynamic generation process:** The work aims to ensure that the generated animations adhere to fundamental physical laws, thereby enhancing realism and consistency, which is often lacking in purely data-driven approaches.
3. **To develop a flexible framework capable of handling various kinds of materials and objects:** The model is designed to adapt to diverse material behaviors without requiring extensive manual recalibration for each new material or object configuration.
4. **To enable automatic learning of material properties:** A significant motivation is to eliminate the labor-intensive and expert-dependent process of manually specifying or tuning physical parameters. The system should infer and optimize material properties based on high-level textual descriptions.
5. **To integrate expert-designed constitutive models into a learnable architecture:** The research is motivated by the observation that purely neural network-based approaches struggle to fit the highly nonlinear relationships described by constitutive models without physical priors. By embedding expert knowledge, the system aims to guide the learning process towards physically plausible material behaviors and ensure stability.
6. **To leverage pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models for supervision:** The aim is to utilize the rich prior knowledge encoded in these models to guide the optimization of learnable physical parameters, allowing users to control dynamics through natural language prompts.

In essence, OMNIPHYSGS seeks to bridge the gap between visually compelling but often non-physical data-driven 4D generation and physically accurate but often rigid or manually intensive physics-based simulations, by creating an automated, flexible, and physically plausible dynamics generation system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OMNIPHYSGS introduces a novel framework for physics-based 3D dynamic scene synthesis that extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with learnable constitutive models, integrated within a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulation, and guided by a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model.

The scene is represented by a collection of 3D Gaussian kernels, each characterized by its center (xₚ), covariance matrix (Σₚ), spherical harmonic coefficients (shₚ), and opacity (αₚ). The objective is to synthesize 3D dynamics that align with a user-specified text prompt (y) while adhering to physical laws. This is formulated as maximizing the log-likelihood of the generated video (V) given the scene (S) and prompt (y), as modeled by a pre-trained diffusion model (Φ).

#### 3.1. Constitutive Gaussians

The core innovation is the concept of "Constitutive Gaussians." Unlike vanilla Gaussians, each Constitutive Gaussian particle is endowed with a learnable constitutive model. In the Material Point Method (MPM), material properties are determined by hyperelastic energy density functions (Ψ), plasticity return functions (ψ), and associated physical parameters (γ, e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio). OMNIPHYSGS makes these elements learnable by representing them as neural networks with parameters `θ_el`, `θ_pl`, and `θ_phy` respectively. This allows the model to predict the optimal material properties for each Gaussian particle automatically, enabling the simulation of diverse behaviors (elastic, plastic, viscoelastic, fluid).

#### 3.2. Physics-Guided Constitutive Network

To address the challenge of training neural networks to predict highly nonlinear constitutive models without explicit physical supervision, OMNIPHYSGS employs a physics-guided constitutive network:

- **3D Feature Encoder:** This component extracts features from the initial 3D Gaussian scene. It uses Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) and k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN) to partition particles into local neighborhoods. A mini-PointNet then encodes the attributes (`xₚ, Σₚ, shₚ, αₚ`) within each neighborhood into a feature vector. This partitioning is performed once at initialization to maintain stability and efficiency.
- **Physical-Aware Decoder:** The encoded features are then processed by this decoder. An MLP first predicts material category logits for each neighborhood. Based on the highest logit, a specific "expert-designed constitutive model" is assigned to all particles within that neighborhood using a hardmax operation with a straight-through estimator. The paper details an ensemble of 12 constitutive model combinations, comprising 3 hyperelastic density functions (Fixed Corotated Elasticity, Neo-Hookean Elasticity, StVK Elasticity) and 4 plasticity return functions (Identity Plasticity, Drucker-Prager Plasticity, von Mises Plasticity, Fluid Plasticity). These expert models guide the calculation of the Cauchy stress tensor and the corrected deformation gradient, providing physical priors that stabilize learning and ensure adherence to physical constraints.

This hybrid approach leverages the flexibility of neural networks for feature extraction and category prediction, while anchoring the final material response in established physical models, which aids in avoiding physically implausible outcomes and convergence issues.

#### 3.3. Differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) Simulation

The Material Point Method is employed to simulate the dynamics of the 3D Gaussians. To facilitate end-to-end training with PyTorch, the authors re-implemented and optimized the MPM algorithm, transforming it into vectorized PyTorch expressions. This re-implementation significantly reduced training memory consumption (by approximately 75%) compared to existing Warp-based MPM solvers, making the framework more efficient for deep learning. During simulation, Gaussian kernels are updated by their particle positions (`xₚ,render ← xₚ`) and deformation gradients (`Σₚ,render ← Fₚ Σₚ Fₚᵀ`).

#### 3.4. Diffusion Guidance

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) is used to guide the training of the Constitutive Gaussians. The simulated states of the Gaussians are rendered into a video clip (`V_hat`), which is then supervised by a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model (ModelScope) conditioned on the text prompt (y). The gradient of the learnable parameters (`θ`) is derived from the SDS loss, enabling the optimization of the constitutive models to match the desired dynamics implied by the text prompt.

#### 3.5. Training Strategy

To manage the computational demands and stability issues associated with long simulation sequences and the first-order Markov property of MPM, a specialized training strategy is proposed:

- **Grouping:** The entire simulation sequence is not processed as a single unit. Instead, simulation states are sampled at regular intervals (every `m` frames) and grouped into mini-batches, each containing `M` frames (where `M` is significantly smaller than the total number of frames `N`). This reduces memory consumption and gradient propagation length.
- **Multiple Mini-Batch Training:** Rather than optimizing through stages sequentially, each mini-batch (representing a simulation stage) is optimized multiple times before advancing to the next. This iterative correction within each stage helps to stabilize the simulation states, as the video diffusion model does not provide direct ground truth for intermediate steps, thus mitigating error accumulation in the Markov chain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OMNIPHYSGS involved both quantitative and qualitative assessments, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art physics-based 3D dynamic synthesis methods: PhysDreamer, Physics3D, and DreamPhysics.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluations

- **MPM Solver Efficiency:** The custom PyTorch-based MPM solver developed for OMNIPHYSGS demonstrated improved efficiency. It reduced training memory consumption by approximately 75% compared to a Warp-based solver (NCLaw), while maintaining a comparable test time. This optimization facilitates end-to-end training on a single GPU.
- **Single Object in Different Materials:** OMNIPHYSGS consistently outperformed baselines in generating dynamics for a single object with various material properties (elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, fluid). The method achieved an average improvement of 3% to 16% in CLIPSIM, a metric for text-alignment, across different cases. Specifically, while baselines showed comparable performance for pure elastic materials, they exhibited significant drops in performance when tasked with materials like sand or water, due to their reliance on fixed or narrowly defined constitutive models. OMNIPHYSGS's learnable Constitutive Gaussians were able to adapt to these diverse material behaviors more effectively.
- **Multiple Objects in Different Materials:** For scenes containing multiple objects with heterogeneous material properties, OMNIPHYSGS demonstrated superior performance. It surpassed baselines by approximately 4% in text-alignment metrics. This indicates the method's ability to discern and model distinct material properties for different objects within a single scene based on text prompts, a capability where baselines struggled, often predicting homogeneous material properties for all objects.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluations

- **Diverse Material Dynamics:** Qualitative results confirmed OMNIPHYSGS's ability to generate physically plausible and visually realistic dynamics corresponding to a wide range of materials. Examples include a "swinging ficus" versus a "collapsing ficus," "rubber bear" versus "sand bear," and a "jelly cube" versus a "water cube." In contrast, baselines often produced similar dynamic behaviors for different material prompts, failing to capture the unique properties of non-elastic or fluid substances.
- **Heterogeneous Object Interactions:** The method successfully synthesized realistic interactions between objects made of different materials, such as a "rubber wolf falling on water" or a "hard duck colliding to create a dent in a breakable metal can." Baselines typically failed to model these distinct interactions accurately, highlighting the expressiveness of OMNIPHYSGS's Constitutive Gaussians.
- **Motion Generalization:** OMNIPHYSGS exhibited zero-shot generalization to various motions for a trained scene by altering boundary conditions (e.g., applying initial impulse, adding colliders, changing gravity). This demonstrates its robustness and versatility in generating diverse dynamic behaviors beyond what was explicitly trained.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Network Architecture (without Physical Prior):** Ablation experiments showed that replacing the physics-aware decoder with a simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to directly predict constitutive tensors resulted in a significant drop in performance. The MLP struggled to fit the highly nonlinear and complex constitutive models, leading to unstable MPM simulations and poor convergence under diffusion guidance. This highlights the necessity of integrating expert-designed physical priors into the network architecture.
- **Training Strategy (Grouping and Multi-Batch):**
 - Training without the "grouping" strategy (i.e., attempting to optimize over the entire long simulation sequence) led to "Out of Memory" errors, underscoring the importance of breaking down the problem for computational feasibility.
 - Training without the "multiple mini-batch" strategy resulted in a substantial performance degradation. This confirms that iteratively optimizing each simulation stage before proceeding to the next is crucial for stabilizing the optimization process and effectively correcting simulation states in the first-order Markov chain, especially when direct ground truth is unavailable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OMNIPHYSGS represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D dynamic scene synthesis by addressing the long-standing challenge of material property generalization. Its introduction of **learnable Constitutive Gaussians** marks a significant departure from previous approaches that either relied on fixed constitutive models or necessitated manual, expert-driven tuning of physical parameters.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to **automatically and flexibly model a wide spectrum of material behaviors**—including elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, and fluid dynamics—and their complex interactions within heterogeneous 3D scenes. This is achieved through a **physics-guided neural network** that intelligently integrates domain-expert constitutive models. This integration ensures that the learned material properties adhere to fundamental physical laws, leading to physically plausible and visually realistic dynamic outputs. By leveraging the expressive power of pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models for supervision, OMNIPHYSGS provides an intuitive, prompt-driven interface for generating complex physical phenomena, eliminating the need for laborious manual setup.

The potential impact of OMNIPHYSGS is multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Realism and Consistency in 4D Content Generation:** The method enables the creation of dynamic 3D scenes that are not only visually appealing but also physically consistent, a crucial factor for immersive experiences.
- **Broadened Applications in Simulation and Virtual Environments:** By making it easier to simulate diverse material interactions, OMNIPHYSGS could significantly benefit areas such as:
 - **Immersive video games:** For more realistic object deformations, destructions, and environmental interactions.
 - **Hyper-realistic virtual reality (VR) experiences:** To create believable virtual worlds where objects behave as expected.
 - **Robotic simulations:** For more accurate modeling of object manipulation, especially with deformable materials.
 - **Computer-aided design (CAD) tools:** To simulate material responses during design and prototyping.
- **Advancement in Differentiable Physics:** The proposed framework contributes to the ongoing research in differentiable physics, showcasing how neural networks can be effectively combined with classical simulation methods to enable learning of physical properties from high-level supervision.

Despite its contributions, the work acknowledges limitations. The current ensemble of expert-designed constitutive models is restricted to a representative set, and the per-scene SDS optimization is computationally intensive. Future research could focus on expanding the diversity of supported materials and developing more efficient, perhaps amortized, optimization algorithms to enable instant inference and broader scalability. Overall, OMNIPHYSGS establishes a robust foundation for future endeavors in general physics-based 3D dynamics generation, moving closer to automating the creation of physically rich and interactive virtual worlds.
