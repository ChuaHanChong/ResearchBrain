# From Seeing to Simulating: Generative High-Fidelity Simulation with Digital Cousins for Generalizable Robot Learning and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers introduced WorldComposer, a generative framework that creates high-fidelity, interactive simulation environments and diverse "Digital Cousins" from real-world panoramas for robot learning. The system enables training robot policies with enhanced generalization and demonstrates a 0.91 Pearson correlation between simulated and real-world task success rates.

## Problem

- Acquiring diverse and extensive real-world training data for robot policies is costly, labor-intensive, and constrains policy robustness and generalization.
- Current simulation environment construction methods (manual modeling, predefined templates) often lack the structural and semantic complexity found in real-world scenes.
- Prior generative models for real-to-sim reconstruction typically focus on single digital replicas ("Digital Twins"), offering insufficient diversity for comprehensive data augmentation and generalizable policy evaluation.

## Method

- The **WorldComposer** framework automates scene construction from single panoramic inputs, generating a photorealistic ==Digital Twin== (==3D Gaussian Splats==, collision mesh) and diverse ==Digital Cousins== through ==prompt-driven semantic and geometric editing==.
- A ==multi-room stitching pipeline== spatially aligns discrete 3D Gaussian Splatting rooms into cohesive floorplans using ==panoramic feature matching== for coarse alignment and ==Point-to-Plane ICP== for fine refinement.
- Generated scenes are populated with a comprehensive asset library (rigid, articulated, deformable objects, fluids) and ==semantically grounded placement priors== from an LLM, supporting high-fidelity interactive simulation.

## Results

- Augmenting training data with Digital Cousins consistently improved robot policy success rates, with up to an **85%** real-world success rate achieved for "Set Tableware" when augmenting **50** real trajectories with **1000** simulated ones.
- A strong positive linear relationship (**Pearson r=0.91**) was observed between simulation and real-world success rates across various tasks and generalization levels, confirming the platform's reliability for evaluation.
- The framework's stitched multi-room environments supported long-horizon navigation tasks, achieving a **68%** average success rate for Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation in simulation, with high sim-to-real behavioral alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging prompt-driven generative models to create diverse "Digital Cousins" from a single real-world scene can significantly expand the training data distribution, enhancing robot policy generalization.
> - Seamlessly integrating generative 3D reconstruction with advanced physics engines and semantic object placement enables the creation of interactive, high-fidelity simulation environments crucial for complex robot manipulation tasks.
> - A strong correlation between simulated and real-world policy performance can be established, positioning generative simulation as a reliable testbed for evaluating and iterating on robot policies before physical deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust robot policies requires extensive and diverse training data. However, acquiring such data in real-world environments is often costly and labor-intensive due to the need for physical assets, environment reconfiguration, and extensive trials. Simulation offers a practical alternative by enabling efficient data generation and controlled experimentation.

Current approaches to constructing simulation environments face several challenges. Manual scene modeling is labor-intensive, while predefined templates or limited domain randomization often fail to capture the structural and semantic complexity found in real-world environments. Recent advances in generative models, particularly those leveraging visual observations, have shown promise in automatically reconstructing simulation environments from real-world data. However, most prior work primarily focuses on generating single digital replicas (often termed "Digital Twins"), which limits the diversity needed for systematic variation and data augmentation. This lack of diversity can hinder the training and evaluation of robot policies that generalize across unseen scene and object variations.

This work addresses the limitations of existing methods by proposing a generative framework that not only creates high-fidelity simulation environments from real-world visual data but also systematically generates diverse variations of these environments, referred to as "Digital Cousins." This approach positions itself within the intersection of generative AI, high-fidelity simulation, and robot learning, aiming to bridge the gap between static real-to-sim reconstruction and the diverse, interactive data requirements for generalizable robot policies. It builds upon multimodal world models like Marble for scene generation and integrates advanced physics engines and asset libraries to support complex physical interactions, extending the concept of "Digital Twins" to scalable "Digital Cousins" for comprehensive data augmentation and rigorous evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generative framework that facilitates generalizable robot learning and evaluation by providing diverse and high-fidelity simulated environments derived from real-world observations. The authors articulate several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Overcome the cost and limitations of real-world data collection:** The paper highlights the significant costs and logistical challenges associated with collecting diverse training data for robots in physical environments. This includes acquiring physical assets, repeatedly reconfiguring environments, and conducting extensive trials, all of which constrain policy robustness and generalization. The work aims to provide an efficient and scalable alternative through simulation.
2. **Bridge the gap between real-world perception and high-fidelity simulation:** Existing methods for creating simulation environments often struggle to faithfully reflect the complexity of real-world scenes. Manual modeling is time-consuming, and procedural generation can lack realism. The motivation is to automate the creation of realistic simulation environments directly from visual inputs, specifically real-world panoramas, without requiring manual scene modeling.
3. **Enhance diversity for robust policy learning and evaluation:** A critical limitation of current real-to-sim approaches is their focus on generating single digital replicas, which provides insufficient diversity for training policies that need to generalize to novel scenes and objects. The authors are motivated to generate a wide range of semantically and geometrically varied "Digital Cousins" from a single real-world scene, thereby expanding the available training data and enabling more rigorous evaluation of policy generalization.
4. **Support interactive manipulation tasks:** Beyond static scene generation, the objective is to create environments that support complex, interactive robot manipulation tasks. This requires integrating the generated scenes with high-quality physics engines and diverse asset libraries that can model rigid, articulated, and deformable objects, as well as fluid dynamics.
5. **Enable long-horizon navigation in complex environments:** Recognizing that many real-world robotic tasks involve traversing multi-room layouts, a specific objective is to develop a method for seamlessly stitching individual room-level simulation scenes into consistent, large-scale multi-room environments suitable for long-horizon navigation.
6. **Establish a reliable platform for sim-to-real transfer and evaluation:** The overarching goal is to create a simulation platform where policies trained and evaluated demonstrate a strong correlation with real-world performance. This would allow for systematic assessment of policy generalization under controlled variations before real-world deployment.

In summary, the motivation is to leverage generative AI to create a scalable, diverse, and high-fidelity simulation platform that addresses the data scarcity and generalization challenges in robot learning by efficiently transforming limited real-world captures into an expansive set of interactive digital experiences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WorldComposer framework operates through three core stages to generate high-fidelity, interactive simulation environments from real-world scenarios, ultimately supporting generalizable robot policy learning and evaluation.

#### 3.1. A. Automated Real-to-Sim Scene Construction

This stage converts single-view panoramic inputs into interactive 3D environments, including both "Digital Twins" and diverse "Digital Cousins."

1. **From Panorama to Digital Twin:** A single 360-degree panorama captured from the real world serves as the input. The framework utilizes Marble, a multimodal world model, to reconstruct the scene's geometry and texture. This process yields two main components:
 - **Visual Rendering:** A set of 3D Gaussian Splats (3DGS) is generated for photorealistic rendering, capturing complex lighting and material properties.
 - **Collision Mesh:** A corresponding mesh is produced to provide rigid bodies, enabling robot-environment interaction within a physics simulation.
 These components are then converted into the Universal Scene Description (USD) format using 3DGRUT to ensure compatibility with the Isaac Sim simulator.
2. **Prompt-Driven Editing for Digital Cousins:** To address the limitation of diversity in single digital replicas, the framework leverages Marble's editing capabilities. Natural language prompts (e.g., "a kitchen with wooden textures," "modern style layout") are used to semantically and geometrically modify the reconstructed scene. This process generates "Digital Cousins" – environments that retain the structural logic of the original scene but introduce significant variations in visual appearance and semantic layout. This one-to-many generation capability is designed to facilitate domain randomization and generalizable policy learning.

#### 3.2. B. Multi-Room Stitching

To create large-scale, navigable environments beyond single rooms, the framework incorporates a multi-room stitching pipeline. This spatially aligns discrete 3D Gaussian Splatting rooms generated by Marble into a cohesive floorplan.

1. **Coarse Alignment via Panoramic Feature Matching:** Given the absence of global coordinates and potential artifacts in generative 3DGS when departing from the sampling center, traditional Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines are considered unreliable for direct registration. Instead, the method leverages overlapping visual cues between original adjacent panoramas. Local features are extracted using SuperPoint and matched with LightGlue. The resulting essential matrix is decomposed into a relative rotation ($R_{ab}$) and unit translation ($\hat{t}_{ab}$). Monocular scale ambiguity is resolved by estimating a metric scale ($\alpha$) by aligning the reconstructed ground plane with the known camera height ($h$), yielding a coarse pose $T_{coarse}$.
2. **Fine Refinement via Geometric ICP:** To minimize residual errors and ensure physical continuity, a geometric refinement step is performed using Point-to-Plane Iterative Closest Point (ICP) on dense point clouds sampled from overlapping regions of the rooms. The ICP algorithm minimizes the geometric distance between point clouds, with $T_{coarse}$ initializing $T_{fine}$. Upon convergence, the 3DGS kernels and collision meshes are merged into a unified coordinate system and converted to USD format via 3DGRUT, providing a seamless and navigable surface.

#### 3.3. C. High-Fidelity Interactive Simulation

To enable realistic robotic interaction, the static generated scenes are populated with manipulable objects supported by high-fidelity physics solvers. A comprehensive asset library is curated, categorized as:

- **Rigid Objects:** Accurate convex decomposition is applied to support stable grasping and collision handling.
- **Articulated Objects:** Precise joint limits and drive mechanisms are defined for objects with kinematic chains, enabling realistic interactions such as opening microwaves or closing drawers.
- **Deformable Objects:** Advanced physics solvers are employed for complex non-rigid dynamics: Position-Based Dynamics for garments, Finite Element Method for elastic volumetric objects (e.g., soft foods), and Dynamic Grid Method for fluids.

**Scene Composition:** An LLM provides common-sense, semantically grounded placement priors for populating the Digital Cousins (e.g., placing appliances in kitchens). The pose of each target placement is estimated, and assets are instantiated with physically valid alignment, combining visual realism with physical richness.

#### 3.4. D. Generalizable Robot Learning and Evaluation

The framework is designed as both a data engine and an evaluation platform. Prompt-driven editing of Marble synthesizes a dense neighborhood of environment variants with semantic and geometric edits, along with diverse interactive objects from the asset library. This generates an unbounded training distribution, promoting robust, semantic-aware behaviors and mitigating overfitting. For evaluation, policies are assessed across hierarchical generalization tiers, probing robustness under changes in appearance, scene structure, object instances, and combinations, providing metrics correlated with real-world performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted within WorldComposer evaluate its effectiveness in improving policy generalization, establishing sim-to-real correlation, and supporting long-horizon navigation.

#### 4.1. A. Efficacy of Digital Cousin Data

The study investigated whether augmenting training data with diverse Digital Cousin data improves policy robustness and generalization.

1. **Simulation Experiments:**
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP):** Training DP with 100 original data plus 200 Digital Cousin data consistently improved success rates across various manipulation tasks, particularly for generalization to "Unseen Scene & Object" variations. For instance, in "Pour Water" and "Cut Sausage," significant improvements were observed, indicating that the high-fidelity physics engine in simulation assists in learning robust dynamics for complex interactions (Table I). The average success rate for "Unseen Scene & Object" across all tasks improved from 0.46 to 0.63.
 - **VLA Models (π₀):** For large-scale pre-trained models like π₀, data augmentation with Digital Cousins also yielded performance gains, especially in tasks such as "Set Tableware." This suggests that the high-fidelity visual generation is beneficial for such foundation models (Table II). The average success rate for "Unseen Scene & Object" improved from 0.55 to 0.76.
2. **Real-Sim-Real Transfer:**
 - **Fidelity of Sim Data:** Policies trained solely on 50 Sim (Twin) data achieved generalization performance comparable to policies trained on 50 Real data (e.g., for "Unseen Scene & Object," average success rates were 0.25 for Sim (Twin) vs. 0.27 for Real), indicating that the framework functions as a high-fidelity proxy for real-world interaction (Table III).
 - **Augmentation Benefits:** Supplementing real-world data with generated data resulted in substantial gains. Policies co-trained with "50 Real world + 100 Sim (Twin + Cousin) data" significantly outperformed baselines, nearly doubling success rates in generalization settings (0.57 vs. 0.33 for "Unseen Scene" average success rate). This validates that "Digital Cousins" provide environmental fidelity and diversity to bridge data scarcity.
 - **Scaling Trend:** Incrementally adding up to 1,000 Digital Cousin trajectories to 50 Real trajectories for the "Set Tableware" task showed a consistent upward trend in real-world success rates, increasing from 10% to 85% in the most challenging "Unseen Scene & Object" setting (Figure 6). This indicates that large-scale, varied data generated by WorldComposer can effectively boost the generalization of robotic manipulation policies.

#### 4.2. B. Real-to-Sim Evaluation Correlation

A strong positive linear relationship was observed between simulation and real-world success rates across three tasks ("Set Tableware," "Open Microwave," "Fold Cloth") and four generalization levels (Train, Unseen Scene, Unseen Object, Unseen Scene & Object). The Pearson correlation coefficient was calculated as $r = 0.91$ (Figure 7). This high correlation suggests:
- **Real-Sim Alignment:** Simulation failures (e.g., collision, dynamic mismanagement) reliably predict real-world failures.
- **Performance Ranking Preservation:** Performance differences between various policy architectures (ACT, DP, SmolVLA, π₀) are consistently maintained across domains.
- **Generalization Consistency:** The simulation accurately reflects the difficulty gradient associated with unseen factors, with performance degradation in "Unseen Scene & Object" configurations in simulation correlating with real-world results.
This finding positions WorldComposer as a high-fidelity and reliable testbed for evaluating generalist policies prior to real-world deployment.

#### 4.3. C. Navigation in Generated Multi-Room Environments

Experiments on Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation (ZSON) using the movable XLeRobot in stitched multi-room environments demonstrated the suitability of these complex layouts for scene-level tasks.
- **Performance:** The VLFM policy achieved an average success rate of 68% and an average SPL (Success weighted by Path Length) of 0.55 across five distinct target objects (Table IV). This indicates that the stitched multi-room environments provide a physically robust and semantically consistent testbed, supporting the validation of semantic reasoning and long-horizon navigation.
- **Fidelity:** Real-world navigation experiments confirmed high sim-to-real behavioral alignment with a success rate of 12/20 in Sim and 10/20 in Real (Table V), along with minimal Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) distances and consistent Value Map topologies (Figure 9).

#### 4.4. D. Additional Experiments (Appendix E)

- **Effectiveness over Domain Randomization:** An ablation study showed that while standard Domain Randomization (lighting, texture) provides moderate gains, it does not alter scene structures. Digital Cousins (scene and object variations) independently outperformed classical DR. Integrating both types of Digital Cousins within WorldComposer achieved peak performance, confirming that gains stem from scene and object diversity rather than just data volume or simple texture randomization (Table XIII).
- **Scaling Efficiency on Additional Tasks:** Scaling curves for "Open Microwave" and "Fold Cloth" tasks demonstrated consistent data efficiency, with performance reliably scaling as the number of simulated cousins increased from 50 to 1000 trajectories (Table XIV).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, through the WorldComposer framework, contributes to the field of robotics and artificial intelligence by addressing several challenges in robot learning and evaluation.

1. **Scalable Data Generation:** The framework offers an automated and scalable method for generating diverse, high-fidelity simulation data from limited real-world panoramic captures. This capability has the potential to mitigate the prohibitive costs and labor associated with real-world data collection, providing a continuous stream of training experiences for robot policies.
2. **Improved Generalization for Robot Policies:** By leveraging "Digital Cousins" – semantically and geometrically varied versions of real-world scenes – the framework provides augmented training data that demonstrably improves the generalization capabilities of robot policies. Policies trained on this diverse data exhibit enhanced robustness to unseen scene and object variations, which is a critical step towards deploying robots in dynamic and unstructured real-world environments.
3. **High-Fidelity Interactive Simulation:** The integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting for visual realism with advanced physics engines for rigid, articulated, deformable objects, and fluid dynamics, creates a comprehensive and interactive simulation environment. This fidelity is crucial for learning complex manipulation skills that involve realistic physical interactions.
4. **Reliable Sim-to-Real Transfer and Evaluation:** The demonstrated strong correlation ($r=0.91$) between policy performance in simulation and the real world establishes WorldComposer as a reliable testbed for evaluating robot policies. This allows researchers to systematically assess policy robustness and generalization in a controlled, virtual setting before costly and time-consuming real-world deployments. This is particularly impactful for rapidly iterating on policy designs and benchmarking different learning approaches.
5. **Support for Complex Robotic Tasks:** The framework’s ability to stitch together multi-room environments for long-horizon navigation, alongside its support for diverse manipulation tasks, expands the range of robotic capabilities that can be trained and evaluated. This is a step towards developing general-purpose robots capable of operating in complex, house-scale environments.

The potential impact of WorldComposer extends to various domains, including:
- **Robotics Research:** It provides a platform for developing and testing advanced robot learning algorithms, especially those focusing on generalization, few-shot learning, and foundation models.
- **Industrial Applications:** Companies developing robots for manufacturing, logistics, or service industries could leverage this for rapid prototyping, training, and testing of robotic solutions without extensive physical setups.
- **AI Development:** The framework contributes to the broader development of embodied AI by providing tools to create realistic virtual worlds for agents to learn and interact.

Future work, as outlined by the authors, includes further instance-level decomposition and cross-scene radiance field fusion, which could further enhance the granularity and seamlessness of the generated environments. Overall, WorldComposer represents progress in generative simulation for robot learning, offering a tool for advancing the capabilities and generalizability of robotic systems.
