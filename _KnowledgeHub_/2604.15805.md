# From Seeing to Simulating: Generative High-Fidelity Simulation with Digital Cousins for Generalizable Robot Learning and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers introduced WorldComposer, a generative framework that creates high-fidelity, interactive simulation environments and diverse "Digital Cousins" from real-world panoramas for robot learning. The system enables training robot policies with enhanced generalization and demonstrates a 0.91 Pearson correlation between simulated and real-world task success rates.

## Problem

- Acquiring diverse and extensive real-world training data for robot policies is costly, labor-intensive, and constrains policy robustness and generalization.
- Current simulation environment construction methods (manual modeling, predefined templates) often lack the structural and semantic complexity found in real-world scenes.
- Prior generative models for real-to-sim reconstruction typically focus on single digital replicas ("Digital Twins"), offering insufficient diversity for comprehensive data augmentation and generalizable policy evaluation.

## Method

- The **WorldComposer** framework automates scene construction from single panoramic inputs, generating a photorealistic ==Digital Twin== (==3D Gaussian Splats==, collision mesh) and diverse ==Digital Cousins== through ==prompt-driven semantic and geometric editing==.
- A ==multi-room stitching pipeline== spatially aligns discrete 3D Gaussian Splatting rooms into cohesive floorplans using ==panoramic feature matching== for coarse alignment and ==Point-to-Plane ICP== for fine refinement.
- Generated scenes are populated with a comprehensive asset library (rigid, articulated, deformable objects, fluids) and ==semantically grounded placement priors== from an LLM, supporting high-fidelity interactive simulation.

## Results

- Augmenting training data with Digital Cousins consistently improved robot policy success rates, with up to an **85%** real-world success rate achieved for "Set Tableware" when augmenting **50** real trajectories with **1000** simulated ones.
- A strong positive linear relationship (**Pearson r=0.91**) was observed between simulation and real-world success rates across various tasks and generalization levels, confirming the platform's reliability for evaluation.
- The framework's stitched multi-room environments supported long-horizon navigation tasks, achieving a **68%** average success rate for Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation in simulation, with high sim-to-real behavioral alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging prompt-driven generative models to create diverse "Digital Cousins" from a single real-world scene can significantly expand the training data distribution, enhancing robot policy generalization.
> - Seamlessly integrating generative 3D reconstruction with advanced physics engines and semantic object placement enables the creation of interactive, high-fidelity simulation environments crucial for complex robot manipulation tasks.
> - A strong correlation between simulated and real-world policy performance can be established, positioning generative simulation as a reliable testbed for evaluating and iterating on robot policies before physical deployment.
