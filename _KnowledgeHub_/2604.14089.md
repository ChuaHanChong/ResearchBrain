# UMI-3D: Extending Universal Manipulation Interface from Vision-Limited to 3D Spatial Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Hong Kong and the University of Science and Technology of China developed UMI-3D, an enhanced robot demonstration interface that incorporates a lightweight LiDAR sensor for robust 3D spatial perception, overcoming vision-only limitations. This system allows for the reliable collection of data for complex manipulation tasks like curtain pulling and door opening, and policies trained on it show strong generalization across unseen object combinations and maintain compatibility with prior UMI systems.

## Problem

- Monocular visual SLAM in prior robot learning systems, like UMI, is vulnerable to occlusions, textureless environments, and dynamic scene changes, leading to unreliable pose estimation.
- Limitations in data collection robustness hinder the scalability and generalizability of learned robot manipulation policies for diverse real-world tasks.
- Difficulty in acquiring high-quality, metric-consistent, and temporally aligned perception-action data under varied environmental conditions.

## Method

- Integrating a ==lightweight LiDAR sensor== and an ==industrial CMOS camera== into a wrist-mounted, hardware-synchronized multimodal sensor suite on the robot gripper for the **UMI-3D** system.
- Developing a pipeline for ==temporal synchronization==, ==spatial calibration==, and ==LiDAR-inertial odometry== to generate metric-consistent and temporally aligned 3D data.
- Adopting a ==diffusion policy== framework, enhanced with ==low-latency sensing== and ==relative end-effector pose representation==, for learning manipulation skills from the improved data.

## Results

- The LiDAR-centric SLAM system consistently provided stable and drift-resistant pose estimation and 3D map reconstruction even in visually challenging environments (e.g., textureless surfaces, dynamic deformable objects).
- Policies trained with UMI-3D data achieved strong performance on previously difficult tasks, such as curtain pulling (normalized scores **0.88-0.96**) and demonstrated generalization in cup arrangement with unseen object combinations (**0.736** normalized score).
- UMI-3D showed compatibility with policies trained on the original UMI, enabling direct transfer and robust execution for tasks like mouse placement (normalized scores **0.73-1.00**) without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing monocular visual SLAM with LiDAR-centric SLAM on the robot's gripper profoundly enhances pose estimation robustness and 3D scene reconstruction reliability in challenging environments.
> - Hardware-level synchronization and a unified spatiotemporal calibration framework are critical for fusing multimodal sensor data (LiDAR, camera, IMU) into consistent perception-action pairs.
> - Representing actions as relative end-effector pose trajectories improves policy robustness by accounting for tracking errors and aligning with incremental SLAM state estimation.
