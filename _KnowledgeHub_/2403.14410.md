# GLC++: Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation through Global-Local Clustering and Contrastive Affinity Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces GLC++, a framework for Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA), demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across various category shift scenarios by adapting standard pre-trained models. This approach achieved an H-score of 75.3% on VisDA for open-partial-set DA and improved novel category discovery by 5.4% on Office-Home.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks experience performance degradation when applied to target domains with differing data distributions or label spaces (e.g., closed-set, partial-set, open-set, open-partial-set scenarios).
- Existing Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) methods typically require access to raw source data, which is often impractical due to storage, computational, and data privacy concerns.
- Most Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) methods are limited to closed-set scenarios or necessitate specialized source model architectures, restricting their general applicability to diverse real-world problems.

## Method

- Developed ==Global-Local Clustering (GLC)==, a novel ==pseudo-labeling== strategy for SF-UniDA, which employs ==one-vs-all global clustering==, ==confidence-based source-private suppression==, and ==Silhouette-based== target category estimation to identify 'known' vs. 'unknown' data.
- Integrated ==Local Consensus Clustering== into GLC to mitigate negative transfer from noisy pseudo-labels by leveraging the intrinsic local structure of the target domain.
- Enhanced GLC to **GLC++** by introducing ==Contrastive Affinity Learning==, specifically designed to improve the discrimination of novel categories within 'unknown' data and enhance feature separation.

## Results

- GLC++ achieved state-of-the-art H-scores in Open-Partial-Set Domain Adaptation, for instance, reaching **75.3%** on VisDA, surpassing prior methods like GATE by **18.9%** and UMAD by **20.2%**.
- The method significantly improved Novel Category Discovery (NCD) Accuracy, increasing NCD Acc from **50.4%** to **55.4%** on Office-Home (OPDA) and from **66.9%** to **70.8%** on VisDA (OSDA).
- The contrastive affinity learning component enhanced existing SFDA/UniDA methods, boosting UMAD's H-score on VisDA from **55.1%** to **56.8%** and yielding a **1.5%** mIoU improvement in cross-domain semantic segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation is achievable using only a standard pre-trained closed-set source model, removing a major practical barrier for existing methods.
> - Combining a one-vs-all global clustering strategy with local consensus and contrastive affinity learning effectively addresses both known-unknown separation and discovery of novel categories in a source-free setting.
> - The proposed contrastive affinity learning strategy is complementary to existing SFDA/UniDA methods and can be integrated to improve their performance, particularly in novel category discovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and increasingly complex challenge in deep learning: **Domain Adaptation (DA)**. Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), despite their success, often suffer from performance degradation when applied to data from a *target domain* that differs from their *source domain* (where they were trained). This "domain shift" can manifest as covariate shifts (different data distributions) and, more challenging, category shifts (different label spaces).

Traditional **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** methods aim to align features between a labeled source dataset and an unlabeled target dataset, assuming identical label spaces (closed-set DA, CLDA). However, real-world applications often face scenarios where label spaces differ:
- **Partial-set DA (PDA):** Target label space is a subset of source (Y_s ⊃ Y_t).
- **Open-set DA (OSDA):** Target domain has novel categories not in the source (Y_s ⊂ Y_t).
- **Open-Partial-set DA (OPDA):** A complex mix, with shared, source-private, and target-private categories (Y_s ∩ Y_t ≠ ∅, Y_s ⊈ Y_t, Y_s ⊉ Y_t).

**Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA)** was proposed to provide a unified solution that accommodates all these category shifts without prior knowledge of the target domain's label space. However, most existing UniDA methods (e.g., DANCE, DCC, GATE, OVANet) still require access to the *raw source data* during adaptation. This is often impractical due to storage, computational, and, crucially, data privacy concerns (e.g., GDPR).

This context highlights the emergence of **Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA)**, which performs adaptation using only a *pre-trained source model* and unlabeled target data, without access to the original source data. Existing SFDA methods, however, are largely restricted to closed-set scenarios or, if addressing category shifts (e.g., UMAD), often rely on specialized source model architectures (e.g., two-branch open-set recognition frameworks), which limits their general applicability.

The current paper directly targets **Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA)**, which represents the apex of these challenges: performing adaptation under both covariate and *arbitrary category shifts*, **without access to source data**, and crucially, **using only a standard pre-trained closed-set source model**. This last point is a significant differentiator, as it removes the need for custom model architectures, making the approach much more broadly applicable and practical for "upcycling" existing models. The authors explicitly state that GLC is the "first to exploit and achieve" SF-UniDA with only a standard closed-set model, positioning their work as a pioneering effort in this highly challenging and practical domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and versatile framework for **Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA)**. This encompasses adapting a pre-trained deep neural network model from a source domain to a target domain under various types of domain and category shifts, specifically:
1. **Covariate Shift:** Differences in data distribution between source and target domains.
2. **Category Shift:** Inconsistencies in label spaces, covering closed-set (CLDA), partial-set (PDA), open-set (OSDA), and open-partial-set (OPDA) scenarios.
3. **Source-Free Setting:** The adaptation must occur without direct access to the raw source data, relying solely on a pre-trained source model and unlabeled target data.
4. **Standard Model Architecture:** Crucially, the approach aims to achieve SF-UniDA using only a standard, pre-trained *closed-set* source model, avoiding the need for specialized or complex model architectures typically required by previous methods for handling open-set or universal scenarios.

The motivations behind these objectives are multifaceted:
- **Practicality and Efficiency:** Accessing source raw data is often "inefficient" and may violate "increasingly stringent data privacy policies" (e.g., GDPR). SFDA addresses this by enabling model "upcycling" with only the pre-trained model.
- **Real-world Applicability:** In practical scenarios, the exact nature of category shifts (e.g., which classes are shared, source-private, or target-private) between source and target domains is rarely known beforehand. UniDA, and particularly SF-UniDA, offers a solution that does not require such prior knowledge.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - Most SFDA methods are limited to closed-set scenarios.
 - Existing UniDA methods typically require source data access.
 - Prior SFDA methods addressing category shifts often necessitate "dedicated source model architectures," restricting their general deployment.
- **Enhanced "Unknown" Data Handling:** Beyond merely rejecting "unknown" (target-private) data, the authors are motivated to improve the model's ability to discern distinct categories *within* this "unknown" data, which is a common limitation of existing approaches that treat all "unknowns" as a single monolithic class.

In essence, the authors aim to bridge the gap between practical constraints (data privacy, unknown shift types, reliance on standard models) and the need for robust domain adaptation across diverse, complex real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology consists of two main techniques: Global and Local Clustering (GLC) and its enhanced version, GLC++. The overall approach builds upon existing source-free closed-set domain adaptation (SFDA) principles, where a pre-trained source model (feature encoder $g_s$ and classifier $h_s$) is adapted by fine-tuning only a target-specific feature module $g_t$ while freezing the classifier $h_s$ (used as $h_t$).

#### 3.1. One-vs-all Global Clustering (L_glb_tar)

This is a novel pseudo-labeling strategy designed for SF-UniDA across inconsistent label spaces.
- **Core Idea:** For each source category $c$, it aims to distinguish "what is $c$" from "what is not $c$" within the target domain.
- **Procedure:**
 1. For each source category $c$, target instances with the highest top-K softmax probabilities $\delta_c(f_t(x_t))$ are identified as a positive subset $P_c$. The rest form the negative subset $N_c$.
 2. A positive prototype $p_c$ is computed as the mean feature of $P_c$.
 3. Multiple negative prototypes $\{n_i^c\}_{i=1}^M$ are obtained by applying K-means clustering on the features of $N_c$. $M$ is set to $C_t$ (estimated total target categories), assuming negative samples contain distinct categories and hard samples from known classes.
 4. A target sample $x_t$ is pseudo-labeled as belonging to category $c$ if its feature is more similar (cosine similarity $S$) to $p_c$ than to any of the negative prototypes $\{n_i^c\}$.
 5. A filtering strategy resolves semantic ambiguity, where only the category with maximum similarity is assigned. Samples not assigned to any source category are considered "unknown" and given a uniform encoding.
- **Loss:** Cross-entropy loss ($L_{glb}^{tar}$) is applied using these derived pseudo-labels.

#### 3.2. Confidence-based Source-private Suppression

This addresses scenarios like PDA and OPDA where source-private categories might mislead the global clustering.
- **Mechanism:** A suppression weight $\epsilon_c$ is introduced for each category $c$. $\epsilon_c$ is a combination of a hyper-parameter $\rho$ and the mean prediction confidence of the positive data group $P_c$. Source-private categories tend to have lower mean prediction confidence, thus receiving a smaller $\epsilon_c$, reducing their influence in pseudo-label assignment. $\rho$ is empirically set to 0.75.

#### 3.3. Silhouette-based Target Domain C_t Estimation

To make the one-vs-all clustering truly "universal" without prior knowledge of $C_t$ (number of target categories):
- **Approach:** The Silhouette criterion is used to estimate the optimal number of clusters $C_t$ in the target domain. The Silhouette value measures how similar an object is to its own cluster compared to other clusters. A high average Silhouette value indicates good clustering.
- **Implementation:** Potential values for $C_t$ are enumerated (e.g., $[1/3 C_s, 1/2 C_s, C_s, 2C_s, 3C_s]$) and $C_t$ is estimated initially and kept constant for efficiency.

#### 3.4. Local Consensus Clustering (L_loc_tar)

This component aims to mitigate negative transfer caused by noisy pseudo-labels from global clustering.
- **Mechanism:** A memory bank stores target features and their prediction scores. For each target sample, its k-nearest neighbors ($L_i$) in the embedding space are identified.
- **Loss:** Cross-entropy loss ($L_{loc}^{tar}$) is minimized to encourage prediction consistency between a sample and its nearest neighbors, thereby exploiting the intrinsic local structure of the target domain.

#### 3.5. Contrastive Affinity Learning (L_con_tar) - for GLC++

This is the key enhancement for GLC++, specifically designed to improve the discrimination of novel categories within "unknown" data.
- **Motivation:** GLC treats all "unknown" data as a single entity due to the closed-set source model, limiting its ability to identify distinct clusters within them.
- **Novelty:** Unlike traditional contrastive learning that uses data augmentation for positive pairs and treats others as negatives, this method defines pairs based on manifold proximity.
 - **Positive Pairs:** Constructed at the dataset level using the memory bank established by local consensus clustering, preserving intrinsic semantic structure.
 - **Negative Pairs:** A "hard-negative mining strategy" is employed at the batch level, selecting samples that are similar in embedding space but belong to different categories/clusters.
- **Loss:** A contrastive loss ($L_{con}^{tar}$) is defined, maximizing similarity between positive pairs and minimizing it for negative pairs.

#### 3.6. Optimization and Inference

- **Overall Loss:**
 - GLC: $L_{GLC}^{tar} = \eta L_{glb}^{tar} + L_{loc}^{tar}$
 - GLC++: $L_{GLC++}^{tar} = \eta L_{glb}^{tar} + L_{loc}^{tar} + L_{con}^{tar}$
 where $\eta$ is a trade-off hyper-parameter.
- **Inference:** For a given target sample, the normalized Shannon Entropy $I(x_t)$ is computed from the model's output probabilities. A pre-defined threshold $\omega$ (set to 0.55) is used: if $I(x_t) \geq \omega$, the sample is classified as "unknown"; otherwise, it's classified as the argmax of the softmax probabilities.

#### 3.7. Advancing Existing Methods

The contrastive affinity learning strategy ($L_{con}^{tar}$) is shown to be complementary to existing SFDA methods. It is integrated into source-available OVANet and source-free UMAD by adding $L_{con}^{tar}$ to their respective overall optimization objectives, with a weighting factor $\gamma$.

#### 3.8. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical understanding of the method based on "expansion" and "separation" assumptions, inspired by prior work. Theorem 1.3 states that the error of the target classifier $\hat{G}$ is bounded by the accuracy of the pseudo-labeler $G_{pl}$ and a separation factor $\epsilon$. The proposed components (reliable pseudo-labels, local consensus, contrastive learning) are argued to minimize this error, leading to robust results.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the significant effectiveness and versatility of GLC and GLC++ across various domain adaptation scenarios and benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance across Category Shift Scenarios (H-score / Accuracy)

- **Open-Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (OPDA):**
 - GLC and GLC++ consistently achieve state-of-the-art H-scores across Office-31, Office-Home, VisDA, and DomainNet.
 - **VisDA:** GLC (73.2%) and GLC++ (75.3%) significantly surpass GATE (56.4%) by 16.8% and 18.9%, and UMAD (55.1%) by 18.1% and 20.2%, respectively, highlighting their robust performance in this challenging scenario.
 - GLC++ generally improves upon GLC, e.g., a 2.1% H-score increase on VisDA and 6.6% on Office-Home (Ar→Cl).
 - The contrastive learning strategy ($L_{con}^{tar}$) also improves OVANet and UMAD, e.g., boosting UMAD's H-score on VisDA from 55.1% to 56.8%.
- **Open-Set Domain Adaptation (OSDA):**
 - GLC (69.6% on Office-Home, 71.5% on VisDA) outperforms UMAD by 6.9% and 4.7% respectively.
 - GLC++ further boosts performance, reaching 70.1% on Office-Home and 73.1% on VisDA.
 - $L_{con}^{tar}$ shows similar improvements for OVANet and UMAD.
- **Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (PDA):**
 - GLC outperforms SHOT-P (a PDA-specific method) on Office-31 and VisDA.
 - GLC significantly outperforms UMAD, with gains of 6.2% (Office-31), 4.5% (Office-Home), and 7.5% (VisDA).
 - GLC++ shows further improvements, and $L_{con}^{tar}$ substantially boosts OVANet (6.1%) and UMAD (3.8%).
- **Closed-Set Domain Adaptation (CLDA):**
 - GLC demonstrates strong performance, achieving 70.5% (Office-Home) and 88.0% (Office-31), outperforming UMAD by 7.4% and 6.6% respectively, validating its versatility.
 - $L_{con}^{tar}$ also contributes to OVANet (2.6%), SHOT (0.6%), and UMAD (1.0%) on Office-Home.

#### 4.2. Novel Category Discovery (NCD Acc)

- GLC++ significantly elevates the NCD Accuracy of GLC. In OPDA, NCD Acc increases from 65.2% to 70.6% on Office-31 and from 50.4% to 55.4% on Office-Home. In OSDA, it increases from 53.6% to 57.7% on Office-Home and from 66.9% to 70.8% on VisDA.
- The contrastive affinity learning strategy is crucial for distinguishing distinct categories among "unknown" data. It also improves NCD Acc for UMAD and OVANet, showcasing its complementary nature.

#### 4.3. Experiments Beyond Standard Object Recognition

- **Cross-Domain Semantic Segmentation (GTA-5 → Cityscapes):** Integrating $L_{con}^{tar}$ into CROTS (a source-free semantic segmentation method) yields a 1.5% mIoU improvement, indicating the versatility of the contrastive strategy. Visualizations show more precise segmentation masks with fewer spurious areas.
- **Open-set Wild-animal Classification (I2AWA benchmark):** GLC (79.1%) and GLC++ (80.9%) significantly outperform baseline methods like DANN, OVANet, OSBP, and DANCE in a real-world open-set application, further validating their effectiveness.

#### 4.4. Experiment Analysis Insights

- **Ablation Study:** All three components ($L_{glb}^{tar}$, $L_{loc}^{tar}$, $L_{con}^{tar}$) are effective and complementary. $L_{glb}^{tar}$ alone provides a massive boost (e.g., from 25.7% to 66.2% on VisDA), confirming its importance for "known"/"unknown" separation. $L_{loc}^{tar}$ mitigates negative transfer, and $L_{con}^{tar}$ enhances novel category discovery and general discrimination.
- **Hyper-parameter Sensitivity:** The model's performance is stable around the chosen hyper-parameters ($\eta$, $\rho$, $|L_i|$, $\gamma$), indicating robustness. The range of effective $\omega$ (uncertainty threshold) is also broad, suggesting clear separation between "known" and "unknown" categories.
- **Training Stability:** Both GLC and GLC++ exhibit stable training convergence, with H-scores stabilizing rapidly. GLC++ shows better stability in NCD Acc compared to GLC, reinforcing the benefit of contrastive learning for novel category discovery.
- **Noisy Label Learning:** GLC and GLC++ demonstrate robustness to source label noise, outperforming source-only models. This is attributed to the combination of global one-vs-all clustering, local k-NN, and contrastive learning, which enhances pseudo-label reliability and feature alignment.
- **Varying Unknown Classes:** Both H-score and NCD Acc tend to decline with an increasing number of "unknown" classes, which is expected. However, GLC and GLC++ maintain robust H-score performance. GLC++'s NCD Acc performs comparably or better than source-available OVANet, demonstrating its effectiveness in challenging scenarios.
- **Confusion Matrix & Feature Visualization:** Confusion matrices show reduced misclassification of common data into source-private categories for GLC/GLC++. t-SNE visualizations confirm better feature alignment and, for GLC++, clearer separation of different clusters within target-private "unknown" data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper presents a significant advancement in the field of domain adaptation, particularly for real-world deployment scenarios. Its core contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Pioneering SF-UniDA with Standard Models:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration that Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA) can be achieved using **only a standard pre-trained closed-set source model**. This removes a major barrier (the need for specialized model architectures) that limited the practicality of prior UniDA/SFDA methods, making "upcycling" existing models much more feasible and cost-effective.
2. **Robust "Known" vs. "Unknown" Separation:** The novel **one-vs-all global clustering strategy**, combined with confidence-based source-private suppression and Silhouette-based $C_t$ estimation, provides an adaptive and robust mechanism to identify "known" data and separate it from target-private "unknown" data across diverse category shift scenarios (CLDA, PDA, OSDA, OPDA) without prior knowledge of the target label space.
3. **Enhanced Novel Category Discovery:** The introduction of **contrastive affinity learning** (in GLC++) is crucial. It addresses the common limitation of treating all "unknown" data as a single class, enabling the model to discover and differentiate distinct categories within the "unknown" data. This is particularly valuable for applications where identifying emerging or novel entities (e.g., new species in wild-animal classification, new anomaly types) is important.
4. **Versatility and Generalizability:** The extensive experimental validation across four benchmark datasets (Office-31, Office-Home, VisDA, DomainNet) and various category shift scenarios confirms the broad applicability and state-of-the-art performance of GLC and GLC++. Furthermore, its success in cross-domain semantic segmentation and open-set wild-animal classification highlights its potential beyond traditional image classification.
5. **Complementary Strategy:** The demonstration that the contrastive affinity learning strategy can effectively enhance *existing* SFDA/UniDA methods (like OVANet and UMAD) underscores its modularity and potential for broader impact within the community.
6. **Robustness to Real-world Imperfections:** The analysis of robustness against noisy labels and varying numbers of unknown classes further strengthens the practical appeal of the proposed methods, suggesting their reliability in less-than-ideal data environments.

In summary, GLC and GLC++ address critical gaps in domain adaptation by offering a highly practical, robust, and versatile solution for model adaptation in complex real-world settings where source data is unavailable and target domain characteristics are unknown. By enabling efficient "upcycling" of standard pre-trained models and improving the discovery of novel categories, this research significantly pushes the boundaries of deployable AI systems, with potential impact across industries from autonomous vehicles and robotics to environmental monitoring and data analytics.
