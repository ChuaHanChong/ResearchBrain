# GLC++: Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation through Global-Local Clustering and Contrastive Affinity Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces GLC++, a framework for Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA), demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across various category shift scenarios by adapting standard pre-trained models. This approach achieved an H-score of 75.3% on VisDA for open-partial-set DA and improved novel category discovery by 5.4% on Office-Home.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks experience performance degradation when applied to target domains with differing data distributions or label spaces (e.g., closed-set, partial-set, open-set, open-partial-set scenarios).
- Existing Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) methods typically require access to raw source data, which is often impractical due to storage, computational, and data privacy concerns.
- Most Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) methods are limited to closed-set scenarios or necessitate specialized source model architectures, restricting their general applicability to diverse real-world problems.

## Method

- Developed ==Global-Local Clustering (GLC)==, a novel ==pseudo-labeling== strategy for SF-UniDA, which employs ==one-vs-all global clustering==, ==confidence-based source-private suppression==, and ==Silhouette-based== target category estimation to identify 'known' vs. 'unknown' data.
- Integrated ==Local Consensus Clustering== into GLC to mitigate negative transfer from noisy pseudo-labels by leveraging the intrinsic local structure of the target domain.
- Enhanced GLC to **GLC++** by introducing ==Contrastive Affinity Learning==, specifically designed to improve the discrimination of novel categories within 'unknown' data and enhance feature separation.

## Results

- GLC++ achieved state-of-the-art H-scores in Open-Partial-Set Domain Adaptation, for instance, reaching **75.3%** on VisDA, surpassing prior methods like GATE by **18.9%** and UMAD by **20.2%**.
- The method significantly improved Novel Category Discovery (NCD) Accuracy, increasing NCD Acc from **50.4%** to **55.4%** on Office-Home (OPDA) and from **66.9%** to **70.8%** on VisDA (OSDA).
- The contrastive affinity learning component enhanced existing SFDA/UniDA methods, boosting UMAD's H-score on VisDA from **55.1%** to **56.8%** and yielding a **1.5%** mIoU improvement in cross-domain semantic segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation is achievable using only a standard pre-trained closed-set source model, removing a major practical barrier for existing methods.
> - Combining a one-vs-all global clustering strategy with local consensus and contrastive affinity learning effectively addresses both known-unknown separation and discovery of novel categories in a source-free setting.
> - The proposed contrastive affinity learning strategy is complementary to existing SFDA/UniDA methods and can be integrated to improve their performance, particularly in novel category discovery.
