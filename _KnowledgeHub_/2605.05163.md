# PhysForge: Generating Physics-Grounded 3D Assets for Interactive Virtual World

> [!summary] Summary
> A research collaboration including the National University of Singapore developed PhysForge, a two-stage framework for generating physics-grounded 3D assets that are directly usable in interactive virtual worlds and embodied AI. The system employs a Vision-Language Model for hierarchical physical planning and a diffusion model for precise geometry and kinematic parameter generation, supported by the new PhysDB dataset, and demonstrates superior accuracy in part structure planning, physics property generation, and kinematic parameter estimation.

## Problem

- Existing 3D generative models create static "hollow shell" assets lacking physics information, making them non-interactive and unsuitable for simulators or game environments.
- Generating interactive 3D content for embodied AI and virtual games is labor-intensive, bottlenecked by manual physical property annotation.
- Prior part-aware 3D generation methods often overlook functional and physical properties, and procedural articulation methods suffer from generalization limitations and suboptimal kinematic accuracy.

## Method

- **PhysForge**, a decoupled ==two-stage framework==, leverages a VLM for abstract "physical planning" and a ==diffusion model== for precise "physical realization" of geometry and continuous kinematic parameters.
- Introduced **PhysDB**, a large-scale dataset of 150,000 3D objects with a novel four-tier hierarchical physical annotation system (holistic, static, functional, interactive).
- Developed a ==Kinematic Voxel Injection== (**KVI**) mechanism within the diffusion stage to synergistically generate continuous kinematic parameters and geometry, guided by VLM-predicted joint types.

## Results

- PhysForge achieved state-of-the-art results in part structure planning, demonstrating superior semantic understanding and general planning accuracy through physics-guided VLM.
- The method showed higher quality geometry generation and more accurate prediction of diverse physics properties (e.g., material, function, interaction) compared to baselines on PhysXNet and PhysDB datasets.
- Generated articulated objects exhibited higher fidelity to input images and more precise joint axis and pivot predictions, achieving lower errors (e.g., Joint-Axis-Err-5 of 0.101) than previous methods.
- The generated assets were demonstrated to be simulation-ready, enabling realistic interactions in robotic simulators, virtual game engines, and agent-environment interaction via natural language queries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For objects to be physically interactive, their generation must be driven by functional logic and hierarchical physics rather than solely holistic shape or visual appearance.
> - Co-predicting physical properties alongside bounding boxes within the VLM significantly enhances its semantic understanding, enabling more robust and semantically coherent part decompositions.
> - A decoupled framework effectively harnesses the complementary strengths of VLMs for high-level semantic planning and diffusion models for precise geometric and kinematic synthesis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D content generation has experienced rapid advancements, with models capable of synthesizing high-fidelity geometric details and diverse appearances. These generative models hold promise as a data engine for the increasing demands of embodied AI and virtual game environments. However, a significant limitation of most existing 3D generation methods is their predominant focus on static geometry and textures. The generated assets typically lack crucial physics information, rendering them non-interactive and unsuitable for direct deployment in simulators or game environments that require realistic physical interactions, such as grasping or manipulation. These assets are often described as "hollow shells."

Recognizing these limitations, a subset of research has explored "part-aware 3D shape generation." These methods aim to decompose complex objects into meaningful components, primarily based on geometric or visual boundaries, to create visually decomposable assets. While this introduces a level of control beyond holistic generation, it still generally overlooks the functional and physical properties of individual parts.

More recently, a nascent area of research has begun to address "physics-grounded 3D shape generation." This includes approaches that integrate generative modules for creating interactive scenes or those that annotate physical properties onto existing datasets. Specific research on articulation, a key component of interaction, has focused on reconstructing articulated objects (often termed "Digital Twins") or procedurally generating them. However, these procedural methods frequently rely on external, predefined content (e.g., part repositories, code templates, VLM-predicted connectivity graphs), which can constrain their generalization capabilities to novel object categories and may result in suboptimal accuracy in kinematic parameter generation.

PhysForge positions itself to bridge the gap between high-fidelity 3D geometry generation and the critical need for physically interactive assets. It addresses the limitations of purely geometric or visually part-aware methods by grounding asset generation in functional logic and hierarchical physics. By introducing a comprehensive dataset with multi-tier physical annotations and a decoupled generative framework, this work aims to provide a robust solution for synthesizing simulation-ready 3D assets directly from minimal input, thereby contributing to the development of more realistic and interactive virtual worlds and embodied AI applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a pipeline for generating physics-grounded 3D assets that are directly usable in interactive virtual worlds and embodied AI applications. The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and unmet needs in the current landscape of 3D content generation:

1. **Addressing the "Hollow Shell" Problem**: Existing 3D generative models, despite their progress in appearance and geometric detail, predominantly create static objects. These "hollow shell" assets lack inherent physical properties (e.g., material, mass, joint types, motion limits) necessary for interaction. This makes them unsuitable for tasks requiring physical manipulation by agents in simulators or for dynamic environments in games. The researchers are motivated to overcome this fundamental limitation by embedding physics directly into the generation process.

2. **Bridging the Gap for Interactive Environments**: Embodied AI and virtual game environments demand a large scale of high-quality, interactive 3D content. Current manual creation or modification of assets to include physical properties is labor-intensive and constitutes a significant bottleneck. Automating the generation of "simulation-ready" assets is a key motivation to serve as a scalable data engine for these domains.

3. **Shifting to Physics-Centric Synthesis**: The core insight motivating PhysForge is that for an object to be physically interactive, its generation must be driven by its functional logic and hierarchical physics, rather than solely by its holistic shape or visual appearance. For instance, a cabinet door requires specific material, function, and kinematic definitions beyond its mere geometry. This necessitates a shift from traditional holistic shape generation to a process where the object's structure is a manifestation of its intended physical functions.

4. **Leveraging Complementary Strengths of Generative Models**: The authors are motivated to explore a decoupled two-stage framework to harness the distinct capabilities of different generative architectures. They propose that large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are well-suited for abstract "physical planning" due to their world knowledge, while diffusion models excel at the precise "physical realization" of geometry and continuous kinematic parameters. Decoupling these stages aims to ensure both visual realism and physical consistency.

5. **Addressing Data Scarcity**: A significant challenge in physics-grounded 3D generation is the lack of large-scale datasets with detailed physical annotations. The motivation includes creating such a dataset, PhysDB, to facilitate effective training and evaluation of models capable of understanding and generating complex physical properties. This dataset is designed to capture physics hierarchically across multiple tiers (holistic, static, functional, interactive).

In summary, the key objectives are to formulate a novel approach for physics-grounded 3D generation, develop a two-stage framework (PhysForge) that integrates VLM planning with diffusion-based generation, construct a large-scale, part-aware dataset with fine-grained physical annotations (PhysDB), and extensively validate the framework's performance and direct applicability in practical interactive scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysForge is a decoupled two-stage framework designed to generate physics-grounded 3D assets from a single input image. This framework is supported by a newly constructed large-scale dataset, PhysDB.

#### 3.1. PhysDB: A Physics-Grounded Dataset

To facilitate the training of a model capable of understanding and generating complex physical properties, the researchers introduce PhysDB, a dataset comprising 150,000 3D objects.

- **Source and Coverage**: Objects are sourced from Objaverse, covering seven major categories including household items, industrial objects, and vehicles. Objects are selected for their amenability to physics annotation and existing part structure.
- **Annotation System**: A novel four-tier annotation system is defined to capture hierarchical physical properties:
 - **Holistic Tier**: Object-level properties such as real-world scale, object category (e.g., "Faucet"), and intended usage scenes (e.g., "kitchen").
 - **Static Properties Tier**: Part-level attributes including semantic labels (e.g., "faucet body"), physical materials (e.g., "metal", "wood"), and mass.
 - **Functional Tier**: Part-level intrinsic functions (e.g., "to contain", "to control water flow") and potential state machines (e.g., Button: \[pressed, released]; Faucet: \[on, off]).
 - **Interactive Tier**: Specifies how an agent can interact with the object, including an atomic affordance library (e.g., pushable, rotatable) and, for movable parts, their complete kinematic definition (parent part, joint type like revolute or prismatic, and precise joint parameters: axis origin, direction, and limits).
- **Annotation Pipeline**: A human-in-the-loop process is employed. Initial annotations are generated by a multi-modal LLM from rendered whole objects and per-part images, followed by manual screening and correction to ensure accuracy and consistency.
- **Addressing Kinematic Precision**: Due to the challenge of scaling precise 3D articulation annotation to 150,000 objects, PhysDB primarily focuses on rich physical properties and joint type identification. To provide ground-truth articulation parameters for training the diffusion stage, the dataset is supplemented with PartNet-Mobility and Infinite-Mobility.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: VLM as a Physical Blueprint Planner

The first stage leverages a fine-tuned Vision-Language Model (VLM) to act as a "physical architect," planning a "Hierarchical Physical Blueprint."

- **VLM Model**: Qwen2.5-VL is chosen as the base model, fine-tuned to acquire 3D spatial understanding and part-structure planning capabilities while retaining its world knowledge.
- **Inputs**: The VLM receives a single image (I), an optional 2D part mask (M) for granularity control, and a 3D voxel representation (V) of the object (obtained from the first stage of TRELLIS).
- **Feature Encoding**:
 - Image (I) and 2D mask (M, converted to a color map) are processed by Qwen's image encoder.
 - For the 3D voxel input (V), a Part-Field encoder extracts features per voxel, which are then downsampled by a position-aware 3D convolutional network into a 512-dimensional voxel embedding.
- **Blueprint Generation**: The VLM autoregressively generates the complete part structure and physical properties. This includes:
 - Bounding box layouts for all parts, defined using 66 new special tokens (<boxs>, <boxe>, and 64 discrete tokens for quantized coordinates). This allows efficient structural planning with 6 tokens per 3D axis-aligned bounding box.
 - Detailed physical properties for each part (parent nodes, articulation types, material, function, etc.), as defined in PhysDB.
- **Physics-Guided Planning**: A key aspect is the discovery that co-predicting physical properties alongside bounding boxes enhances the VLM's semantic understanding and resolves part ambiguity. This "physics-guided planning" allows the model to produce semantically coherent part decompositions even without an explicit 2D mask input.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Diffusion-based Generation with KineVoxel Injection (KVI)

The second stage is responsible for "forging" the high-fidelity geometry and precise kinematic parameters based on the blueprint from Stage 1. The challenge here is to synergistically generate continuous kinematic parameters within a diffusion pipeline primarily designed for geometry.

- **Core Mechanism**: A novel KineVoxel Injection (KVI) mechanism is introduced.
- **Articulation Parameter Representation**: For each movable part *i*, articulation parameters are represented as an 8-dimensional vector P_i = (O_i, A_i, L_i), where O_i ∈ R³ is the joint origin, A_i ∈ R³ is the joint axis, and L_i ∈ R² denotes the motion limits.
- **KineVoxel Encoding**: P_i is transformed into a "KineVoxel" (z_k,i) using independent Kinematic Encoders (E_kine) and Decoders (D_kine), implemented as lightweight 2-layer MLPs. Scaling factors (S_O, S_A, S_L) are applied before encoding to ensure numerical stability. This maps kinematic data into a unified latent space with geometric latents.
- **KineVoxel Injection**: The KineVoxel (z_k,i) is injected into the diffusion network. After downsampling, it is concatenated with the sequence of geometry voxel latents (Z_g = {z_g,i}) before being fed into the main denoising transformer.
- **Joint Type Embedding**: To enable the transformer to differentiate between latent types and leverage the VLM's planning, a joint type embedding (E_type), derived from the VLM's predicted joint type (e.g., "revolute"), is added to z_k,i. This embedding provides a strong functional prior, guiding the transformer to learn correlations between part geometry and its corresponding joint parameters.
- **Training Objective**: The entire model is trained by minimizing a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective. A composite loss function is used: L = E_{t,Z₀,c} \[L_geo + λ_kine · L_kine]. L_geo and L_kine are standard L2 losses for geometry and kinematic latents, respectively. A weighting factor λ_kine = 10 is applied to L_kine, placing higher importance on accurate prediction of articulation parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PhysForge focused on assessing its capabilities in part structure planning, physics property generation, and kinematic parameter accuracy, along with demonstrating its practical applications.

#### 4.1. Part Structure Planning (VLM Stage)

- **Performance Metrics**: Evaluated on the PartObjaverse-Tiny dataset using BBox IoU, Voxel Recall, and Voxel IoU.
- **Key Result**: PhysForge achieved state-of-the-art results in part structure planning, demonstrating the strongest capability compared to baselines like OmniPart and PartField.
- **Impact of Physics Guidance**: Ablation studies (Table 3) revealed that training the VLM to co-predict physical properties alongside bounding boxes (PhysForge w/o mask) significantly improved semantic understanding and planning. This version outperformed "PhysForge-bbox" (trained only on bounding boxes without physics) and even OmniPart using SAM-generated masks, highlighting the robustness and effectiveness of physics-guided planning in producing semantically reasonable part decompositions without explicit 2D mask inputs.

#### 4.2. Physics-Grounded Generation (Diffusion Stage)

- **Geometry Generation Quality**: PhysForge demonstrated superior geometry generation quality compared to baselines (PhysXGen, TRELLIS) on both the PhysXNet and PhysDB datasets, as indicated by lower Chamfer Distance (CD) and higher F1-Scores (at 0.1 and 0.05 thresholds). For instance, on PhysXNet, PhysForge achieved CD of 9.21 (vs. 9.81 for PhysXGen and 10.10 for TRELLIS) and F1-0.1 of 89.24 (vs. 87.91 and 86.53). On PhysDB, the improvements were even more significant.
- **Physics Property Accuracy**: The method significantly outperformed baselines in predicting various physics properties. For instance, on PhysXNet, it achieved a lower Absolute Scale MAE (11.04 cm vs. 25.83 cm for PhysXGen), lower Material MAE (0.81 vs. 1.59), lower Affordance MAE (1.22 vs. 3.69), and higher CLIP-Similarity for Description (0.87 vs. 0.38). Similar trends were observed on the more diverse PhysDB dataset for Material, Function, and Interaction properties.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 3)**: Showcase that PhysForge generates high-quality, physics-grounded, and part-aware 3D assets from a single image, with accurate bounding box plans and detailed object/part-level physical attributes.

#### 4.3. Kinematic Parameters Generation

- **Accuracy of Joint Parameters**: When evaluated against articulated object generation baselines (Articulate Anything, Singapo, URDFormer) on datasets including PartNet-Mobility and Infinite-Mobility objects, PhysForge achieved higher fidelity to the input image and more accurate joint axis and pivot predictions (Table 4). For example, PhysForge (Ours) recorded a Joint-Axis-Err-5 of 0.101 and Joint-Pivot-Err-5 of 0.071, which were lower than all baselines.
- **Qualitative Results (Figures 4 and 5)**: Visual examples confirm that the articulated objects generated by PhysForge exhibit superior consistency with the input image and more precise joint parameters, both for validation sets and in-the-wild images.
- **Ablation Studies on Kinematic Parameters**:
 - **Joint Type Embedding**: Removing the joint type embedding ("PhysForge (w/o joint type emb)") led to a degradation in joint accuracy (e.g., Joint-Axis-Err-5 increased from 0.101 to 0.157). This indicates that the semantic joint type predicted by Stage 1 provides a strong functional prior, crucial for guiding the precise parameter estimation in Stage 2.
 - **Kinematic Encoder/Decoder**: Removing the independent kinematic encoder and decoder ("PhysForge (w/o kinetics enc)") also negatively impacted accuracy, demonstrating the necessity of a dedicated sub-network for synthesizing precise mechanical constraints.

#### 4.4. Applications

The generated assets were demonstrated to be simulation-ready across three applications (Figure 6):

- **Robotic Simulation**: Assets were successfully imported into the RoboTwin simulation environment, allowing robotic manipulators to realistically interact with and manipulate functional parts due to detailed geometry and precise kinematics.
- **Virtual Worlds**: Assets were imported into game engines (e.g., Unity/Unreal Engine 5), enabling complex, physics-based interactions without requiring manual rigging, owing to the integrated physics-grounded attributes.
- **Agent-Environment Interaction**: The VLM-based framework allows embodied agents to query the model in natural language, receiving text-based physical blueprints (including bounding boxes) to plan manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "PhysForge" addresses a recognized bottleneck in the creation of interactive 3D content, contributing to several domains with significant potential impact:

1. **Enabling Advanced Embodied AI**: By generating simulation-ready assets with comprehensive physical properties, PhysForge provides a foundational data engine for embodied AI research. This allows AI agents to learn and practice complex manipulation tasks in realistic virtual environments, accelerating the development of more capable and dexterous robots. The ability to directly import assets into simulators like RoboTwin demonstrates practical utility.

2. **Revolutionizing Virtual World and Game Development**: The demand for high-quality, interactive 3D assets in game development and virtual reality environments is substantial. PhysForge offers a pipeline for automating the creation of such assets directly from images, significantly reducing the manual effort of modeling, rigging, and assigning physical properties. This can lead to richer, more dynamic virtual experiences and faster content creation workflows.

3. **Novel Data Resource for Physics-Grounded Learning**: The introduction of PhysDB, a large-scale dataset with a four-tier hierarchical physical annotation system, fills a critical data gap. This dataset provides a valuable resource for future research in physics-grounded 3D understanding, generation, and interaction, fostering further advancements in the field.

4. **Innovative Decoupled Framework**: The proposed two-stage "VLM Planning + Diffusion Realization" framework represents an innovative approach to complex generative tasks. By leveraging the complementary strengths of VLMs for high-level semantic planning and diffusion models for precise geometric and kinematic synthesis, the method establishes a robust architecture for multi-modal generation that could inspire similar approaches in other areas.

5. **Enhanced Semantic Understanding in Generation**: The finding that physics-guided planning within the VLM significantly improves structural decomposition and resolves part ambiguity, even without explicit mask inputs, indicates a deeper semantic understanding achieved by the model. This capability can lead to more coherent and functionally plausible generated assets.

6. **Facilitating Agent-Environment Interaction through Language**: The VLM's ability to generate hierarchical physical blueprints in natural language opens new modalities for human-agent or agent-agent interaction. Embodied agents can query the model for an object's functional logic, materials, or articulation, enabling more intelligent and context-aware task planning.

In summary, PhysForge moves beyond generating static 3D appearances to creating functionally plausible and physically interactive 3D assets. This advancement directly impacts the scalability and realism of interactive virtual environments and contributes to the foundational capabilities required for sophisticated embodied intelligence.
