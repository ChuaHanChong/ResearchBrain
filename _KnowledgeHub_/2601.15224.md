# PROGRESSLM: Towards Progress Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northwestern University developed PROGRESSLM, a framework that enables Vision-Language Models to perform progress reasoning for robotic tasks by emulating human-like episodic retrieval and mental simulation. This approach, supported by a new benchmark, allows a 3B-parameter model to surpass larger VLMs like GPT-5 in estimating task completion and recognizing unanswerable scenarios.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with long-horizon, dynamic reasoning to estimate task progress from a single, partial observation.
- Existing progress estimation methods are often task-specific or rely on indirect VLM approaches, lacking generalizable and direct intermediate progress quantification.
- VLMs typically fail to recognize unanswerable cases in progress estimation, providing arbitrary scores even when information is insufficient or inconsistent.

## Method

- Introduces ==PROGRESS-BENCH==, a systematic benchmark for progress reasoning across demonstration modalities (vision/text), viewpoint correspondence (same/cross-view), and answerability (answerable/unanswerable cases).
- Proposes a human-inspired ==two-stage reasoning paradigm==: episodic retrieval for a coarse anchor, followed by mental simulation for fine-grained progress estimation.
- Develops ==PROGRESSLM==, a model based on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, trained using a combination of ground-truth guided chain-of-thought supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on the PROGRESSLM-45K dataset.

## Results

- PROGRESSLM-3B-RL achieved the best macro-averaged Normalized Score Error (NSE) and Progress Rank Correlation (PRC) on PROGRESS-BENCH, outperforming stronger general-purpose VLMs like GPT-5 and Qwen2.5-VL-**72B**.
- The trained PROGRESSLM demonstrated improved robustness to cross-view observations, maintaining smaller performance gaps between same-view and cross-view settings.
- PROGRESSLM consistently identified unanswerable cases across modalities, with PROGRESSLM-3B-RL achieving the strongest or near-strongest unanswerable recognition accuracy among all evaluated models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-inspired two-stage reasoning (episodic retrieval and mental simulation) is effective for teaching VLMs robust dynamic progress understanding for long-horizon tasks.
> - Targeted supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning can enable smaller VLMs to acquire specialized, generalizable progress reasoning capabilities that outperform much larger, general-purpose models.
> - Systematic evaluation across demonstration modalities, viewpoint variations, and explicit unanswerable cases is crucial for comprehensively assessing VLM capabilities in dynamic reasoning and uncertainty awareness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a gap in the capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by focusing on "progress reasoning," defined as the ability to estimate how far a task has advanced based on a single observation. While current VLMs are proficient at describing static visual content, they often lack the capacity for long-horizon, dynamic reasoning necessary to infer temporal progress within an ongoing task.

Prior research on progress estimation has typically fallen into several categories:
- **Task-specific regression models:** These approaches are tailored to specific tasks or environments and do not generalize broadly (Yang et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025).
- **Indirect inference via surrogate objectives:** Some methods infer progress through tasks like trajectory reordering or pairwise comparisons (Ma et al., 2024b; Zhai et al., 2025). These methods often couple individual progress estimates within a trajectory, making each prediction dependent on the entire sequence context, rather than providing an independent estimate from a single observation.
- **Latent feature space distances:** Approaches that measure distances in latent feature spaces can struggle with fine-grained intermediate progress (Ma et al., 2022, 2023; Escontrela et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2021).
- **Coarse task completion judgments:** Many VLM-based approaches simplify progress estimation to binary decisions (e.g., complete/incomplete), overlooking the need for reasoning about intermediate progress from a single observation (Fan et al., 2022; Cui et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025; Duan et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2025; Dai et al., 2025).

This research differentiates itself by studying progress estimation as a structured reasoning capability, emphasizing the inference of a numerical progress score from a *single observation*. It posits that humans achieve this by combining episodic retrieval (locating a coarse anchor in a task trajectory) and mental simulation (reasoning about state evolution). The paper introduces a benchmark to systematically evaluate this capability and proposes a human-inspired two-stage paradigm to enhance VLMs in this area, moving beyond static perception towards dynamic, long-horizon understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Systematically study progress estimation in VLMs:** The core motivation is to determine whether VLMs can infer task progress from partial information (a single observation), a capability that extends beyond their demonstrated strength in static visual description. This involves moving from snapshot-level perception to long-horizon, dynamic reasoning.
2. **Introduce a comprehensive benchmark:** A key objective is to create **PROGRESS-BENCH**, a benchmark designed to systematically evaluate progress reasoning under various conditions. This benchmark aims to probe the robustness and generalizability of VLMs across different demonstration modalities, viewpoint changes, and scenarios requiring uncertainty awareness (answerability). This is motivated by the absence of a standardized evaluation framework for this specific reasoning task.
3. **Investigate effective learning paradigms for progress reasoning:** The research aims to explore how VLMs can effectively acquire and improve progress reasoning. Inspired by human cognitive processes, the authors investigate a two-stage approach involving episodic retrieval and mental simulation. This motivation stems from the observed limitations of direct prediction by existing VLMs and the need for more robust, interpretable reasoning mechanisms.
4. **Analyze failure patterns and success conditions in VLMs:** A detailed analysis of VLM performance on the benchmark is motivated by the desire to understand *why* existing models struggle with progress reasoning and *when* and *why* specific approaches succeed or fail. This diagnostic understanding is crucial for guiding future research and development in this domain.
5. **Develop a progress-reasoning-enhanced model:** The research aims to instantiate the proposed human-inspired paradigm into a concrete model, **PROGRESSLM-3B**, using both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. This is motivated by the goal of demonstrating that targeted training can instill robust progress reasoning capabilities even in smaller models, challenging the reliance solely on model scale.

In essence, the motivation is to elevate VLMs from mere visual describers to dynamic temporal reasoners, capable of understanding and predicting the progression of tasks in a human-like, interpretable, and robust manner, even from incomplete information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research comprises two main components: the development of **PROGRESS-BENCH** for evaluation and the proposed **Two-Stage Progress Reasoning Paradigm** for model enhancement.

#### 3.1. PROGRESS-BENCH Construction

PROGRESS-BENCH is designed to evaluate a model's ability to infer task progress from a single observation, situating it within the temporal structure of an ongoing task. It features over 3,000 instances built upon the robotic manipulation domain (RoboMind; Wu et al., 2025c), which provides temporally coherent trajectories. Each instance consists of a task demonstration (D) and a single observation (o), requiring the model to predict a normalized progress score `p ∈ [0, 100%]` or "N/A" if unanswerable.

The benchmark construction varies along three main axes:

- **Demonstration Modality:**
 - **Vision-based demonstrations:** Consist of key frames `{(f_a_j, p_j)}` from expert executions, depicting complete task states.
 - **Text-based demonstrations:** Provide stepwise action descriptions `{(t_j, p_j)}`, requiring models to integrate action semantics and infer implicit state changes.
- **Observation Sampling:** Observations `(o_i, p*i)` are sampled from intermediate execution stages.
 - **Interval sampling:** For uniform coverage of intermediate progress.
 - **Boundary sampling:** For finer resolution near step transitions.
 - **Viewpoint Robustness:** For vision-based demonstrations, observations are categorized into:
 - **Same-view:** Observations captured from the same camera viewpoint as the demonstration.
 - **Cross-view:** Observations captured from a different camera viewpoint.
- **Answerability Augmentation:** To assess uncertainty awareness, unanswerable samples are explicitly introduced. These occur when the observation is inconsistent with the demonstration, and the correct output is "N/A". Such cases are created by either modifying the demonstration or editing the observation while keeping the other fixed (see Figure 2c and Appendix A).

**Data Details:** The benchmark utilizes standardized human teleoperation data from RoboMind, comprising 240 task trajectories and 3,325 sampled observations across four robot platforms (Franka, UR5e, TienKung Humanoid, AgileX Dual-Arm) as shown in Figure 3. A two-level labeling scheme defines discrete step-level progress and uses linear interpolation for fine-grained supervision within steps.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Progress Reasoning Paradigm

Inspired by human cognition (Schacter et al., 2008), progress reasoning is framed as a two-stage process:
1. **Episodic Retrieval:** Identifying a representative reference step (anchor) within the demonstration that is most similar to the current observation. This provides a coarse localization along the task trajectory.
2. **Mental Simulation:** Reasoning about how the task state evolves from the retrieved anchor to the current observation, leading to a fine-grained progress estimate.

This paradigm is instantiated through two approaches:

- **4.2.1. Training-Free Approach:**
 - This approach uses structured prompting to guide VLMs to follow the two-stage reasoning pattern without parameter updates. The prompt schema includes four explicit fields: `<ref_think>` (episodic retrieval reasoning), `<ref>` (retrieved reference step index), `<score_think>` (mental simulation reasoning), and `<score>` (final progress estimate).

- **4.2.2. Training-Based Approach (PROGRESSLM-3B):**
 - A dedicated dataset, **PROGRESSLM-45K**, is constructed from non-overlapping manipulation tasks (25K Chain-of-Thought (CoT) samples for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and 20K for Reinforcement Learning (RL) refinement). This ensures generalizable reasoning.
 - **Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - The model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B serves as the base) is fine-tuned on PROGRESSLM-25K-COT. Each instance contains a demonstration (D_i), observation (o_i), and a ground-truth reasoning sequence (r_i*) including `<ref>` and `<score>`.
 - The model is guided to generate the intermediate `<ref_think>` and `<score_think>` fields. Training minimizes the autoregressive negative log-likelihood: `L_SFT = - (1/N) Σ log P_θ(r_i* | D_i, o_i)`.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - A second-stage RL procedure, based on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), is applied to improve robustness and calibration.
 - The RL objective is `L_RL = -E_{r~P_θ(r|D,o)} [R(r)]`, where the reward function `R(r) = αR_format + βR_ref + γR_score` encourages structured reasoning, accurate reference retrieval, and precise progress estimation. `R_ref` and `R_score` are negative relative errors between predicted and ground-truth values. Training uses 20K samples with a reward ratio `α:β:γ = 1:6:3`.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Design

Models are evaluated using four metrics:
- **Normalized Score Error (NSE)↓:** Measures pointwise error on answerable samples.
- **Progress Rank Correlation (PRC)↑:** Spearman rank correlation between predicted and ground-truth progress sequences, indicating temporal ordering.
- **Answerable False Rejection Rate (AFRR)↓:** Fraction of answerable samples incorrectly predicted as unanswerable.
- **Unanswerable Detection Accuracy (UDA)↑:** Fraction of unanswerable samples correctly identified.

Experiments benchmark 14 VLMs (2B–72B parameters) against direct prediction, training-free reasoning, and the training-based PROGRESSLM-3B (SFT/RL). All training tasks are strictly disjoint from the benchmark to ensure generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments reveal several key findings regarding the capabilities of VLMs in progress reasoning:

#### 4.1. Performance on Answerable Scenarios (Table 1)

- **VLM Limitations:** Most current VLMs struggle with reliable progress estimation under direct prediction. Performance is highly sensitive to the demonstration modality, with vision-based demonstrations consistently yielding better results than text-based ones. Many models exhibit low, negative, or undefined Progress Rank Correlation (PRC), indicating fragmented or distorted temporal understanding rather than coherent ordinal reasoning. Some models also show high Answerable False Rejection Rate (AFRR), suggesting overly conservative rejection behaviors.
- **Training-Free Reasoning:** This approach provides only conditional benefits, primarily for larger models (e.g., GPT-5, Qwen2.5-VL-72B/32B), where it offers moderate improvements in PRC and occasionally in NSE. Smaller models often see marginal or negative effects.
- **Training-Based PROGRESSLM-3B:** Explicit training leads to robust progress reasoning. PROGRESSLM-3B-RL achieves the strongest macro-averaged NSE and PRC among all evaluated models, demonstrating that targeted supervision and optimization can lead to significant improvements, even for smaller models.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Viewpoint Changes (Table 2)

- **VLM Degradation:** Most VLMs degrade substantially when transitioning from same-view to cross-view observations, especially smaller and medium-sized models. This suggests a reliance on viewpoint-dependent visual similarity rather than viewpoint-invariant progress reasoning.
- **Training-Free Benefits:** Limited and conditional, primarily improving PRC for stronger models.
- **Training-Based PROGRESSLM-3B:** PROGRESSLM-3B-RL consistently exhibits smaller performance gaps between same-view and cross-view settings, demonstrating enhanced robustness to viewpoint variations beyond surface-level visual correspondence.

#### 4.3. Unanswerable Case Recognition (Figure 4 and 13)

- **VLM Struggles:** Most VLMs fail to reliably recognize unanswerable cases, often producing progress scores even when the input is ambiguous.
- **Training-Free Thinking:** Enabling explicit reasoning at inference time consistently improves Unanswerable Detection Accuracy (UDA) for most models, particularly in text-based settings (Figure 13).
- **PROGRESSLM-3B:** Consistently identifies unanswerable cases under both vision-based and text-based demonstrations, avoiding forced predictions. PROGRESSLM-3B-RL achieves the highest or near-highest unanswerable recognition accuracy, indicating improved uncertainty awareness.

#### 4.4. Further Analyses (Section 5)

- **Predicted Score Distribution (Figure 5):** Existing VLMs often produce clustered or degenerate score distributions (e.g., collapse to 0%, 100%, or 50%, or a few heuristic anchors). In contrast, PROGRESSLM-3B-SFT and PROGRESSLM-3B-RL consistently exhibit smooth, continuous distributions across the entire [0, 100%] range, reflecting fine-grained sensitivity to intermediate task states.
- **Per-Sample Error Distribution (Figure 6):** Smaller models show broad, heavy-tailed error distributions. PROGRESSLM-3B-SFT and PROGRESSLM-3B-RL significantly tighten the error distribution, suppressing extreme errors and improving per-sample robustness.
- **Coupled Progress Reasoning (Figure 7 and 12):** Diagnostic heatmaps confirm a strong diagonal coupling between the episodic retrieval anchor index and the score-aligned demonstration index for PROGRESSLM. This indicates that the first-stage anchor actively guides the second-stage fine-grained progress estimation, affirming the two-stage paradigm.
- **Implicit State Accumulation (Figure 8):** Text-based demonstrations are harder because they require models to integrate action semantics and accumulate implicit state changes over time, rather than relying on direct visual state matching.
- **Generalization Across Model Scales (Table 3):** The training-based approach shows consistent improvements on a larger (7B) model, suggesting that the proposed supervision scales effectively with model size.
- **In-the-Wild Generalization (Table 4):** PROGRESSLM-RL-3B maintains robust performance on human-performed activities (HUMAN-BENCH), exhibiting lower NSE and competitive PRC even under substantial domain shifts in embodiment, motion, and appearance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Systematic Evaluation of Progress Reasoning:** The introduction of **PROGRESS-BENCH** provides a standardized and comprehensive benchmark for evaluating a critical, yet underexplored, aspect of VLM capabilities. This benchmark facilitates systematic analysis across diverse conditions, including demonstration modalities, viewpoint changes, and answerability, setting a new standard for assessing dynamic reasoning.
2. **Identification of VLM Limitations and Failure Modes:** The extensive evaluation of 14 VLMs revealed consistent struggles, strong sensitivities, degenerate prediction patterns, and poor handling of uncertainty. This diagnostic analysis clarifies the specific weaknesses of current VLMs in progress reasoning, highlighting areas for future research.
3. **Validation of a Human-Inspired Reasoning Paradigm:** The work successfully translates a human-inspired two-stage reasoning paradigm (episodic retrieval + mental simulation) into effective training and inference strategies for VLMs. The training-based **PROGRESSLM-3B** demonstrates that explicit learning of this structured reasoning process can yield substantial and robust improvements, even at a smaller model scale, surpassing larger general-purpose VLMs. This suggests that targeted architectural and training designs can be more effective than brute-force scaling alone for certain complex reasoning tasks.
4. **Advancement in Dynamic and Long-Horizon Reasoning:** By framing progress estimation as a dynamic, long-horizon reasoning problem rather than static perception, the research pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities towards a more nuanced understanding of temporal processes and latent state transitions.
5. **Practical Applications:** Progress reasoning has several direct practical applications:
 - **Anomaly Detection:** It can serve as an anomaly detector for long-horizon systems, identifying stagnating or inconsistent task progress.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Reward:** It can provide a dense, interpretable online reward signal for RL agents, improving learning efficiency and feedback quality.
 - **Data Engine for Reward Models:** It can act as a data engine for training task-specific reward models, further enhancing robotic control and automation.
6. **Broader Impact on General Agents:** The paradigm extends beyond embodied tasks to general agents, such as web agents, where progress signals can support inference-time scaling through iterative refinement and potentially enable more sophisticated self-improving behaviors in complex environments. This indicates a potential pathway for developing more intelligent and autonomous AI systems.
