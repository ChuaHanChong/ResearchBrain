# PROGRESSLM: Towards Progress Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northwestern University developed PROGRESSLM, a framework that enables Vision-Language Models to perform progress reasoning for robotic tasks by emulating human-like episodic retrieval and mental simulation. This approach, supported by a new benchmark, allows a 3B-parameter model to surpass larger VLMs like GPT-5 in estimating task completion and recognizing unanswerable scenarios.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with long-horizon, dynamic reasoning to estimate task progress from a single, partial observation.
- Existing progress estimation methods are often task-specific or rely on indirect VLM approaches, lacking generalizable and direct intermediate progress quantification.
- VLMs typically fail to recognize unanswerable cases in progress estimation, providing arbitrary scores even when information is insufficient or inconsistent.

## Method

- Introduces ==PROGRESS-BENCH==, a systematic benchmark for progress reasoning across demonstration modalities (vision/text), viewpoint correspondence (same/cross-view), and answerability (answerable/unanswerable cases).
- Proposes a human-inspired ==two-stage reasoning paradigm==: episodic retrieval for a coarse anchor, followed by mental simulation for fine-grained progress estimation.
- Develops ==PROGRESSLM==, a model based on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, trained using a combination of ground-truth guided chain-of-thought supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on the PROGRESSLM-45K dataset.

## Results

- PROGRESSLM-3B-RL achieved the best macro-averaged Normalized Score Error (NSE) and Progress Rank Correlation (PRC) on PROGRESS-BENCH, outperforming stronger general-purpose VLMs like GPT-5 and Qwen2.5-VL-**72B**.
- The trained PROGRESSLM demonstrated improved robustness to cross-view observations, maintaining smaller performance gaps between same-view and cross-view settings.
- PROGRESSLM consistently identified unanswerable cases across modalities, with PROGRESSLM-3B-RL achieving the strongest or near-strongest unanswerable recognition accuracy among all evaluated models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-inspired two-stage reasoning (episodic retrieval and mental simulation) is effective for teaching VLMs robust dynamic progress understanding for long-horizon tasks.
> - Targeted supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning can enable smaller VLMs to acquire specialized, generalizable progress reasoning capabilities that outperform much larger, general-purpose models.
> - Systematic evaluation across demonstration modalities, viewpoint variations, and explicit unanswerable cases is crucial for comprehensively assessing VLM capabilities in dynamic reasoning and uncertainty awareness.
