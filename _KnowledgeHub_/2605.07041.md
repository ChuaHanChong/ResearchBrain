# Dr-BA: Separable Optimization for Direct Radar Bundle Adjustment & Localization

> [!summary] Summary
> Lisus et al. introduce Dr-BA, the first direct radar bundle adjustment framework, which uses separable optimization to create dense, locally, and globally consistent maps from raw spinning radar intensity images. This method enabled robust direct radar localization (DRL), achieving an ATE of 0.54m and EPE of 0.25m on structured environments like "Suburbs" routes and state-of-the-art localization errors within 10 cm and 0.1 degrees.

## Problem

- Existing radar-based mapping techniques produce either sparse maps (feature-based) or globally consistent trajectories but locally inconsistent maps (PGO-based), limiting their utility for precise applications.
- A direct Bundle Adjustment (BA) framework for spinning radar, operating on raw intensity images to generate dense, locally, and globally consistent maps, was previously unavailable.
- Direct methods, like BA, faced computational intractability when applied to large-scale autonomous driving environments due to their complexity.

## Method

- A direct radar bundle adjustment (**Dr-BA**) framework is proposed, directly optimizing raw 2D spinning radar intensity images to jointly estimate sensor poses and a dense map.
- The optimization problem is formulated as a separable optimization using ==Variable Projection (VarPro)==, decoupling map intensity estimation from pose estimation for computational efficiency.
- Dr-BA integrates pre-processing steps like ==motion/Doppler distortion compensation==, ==adaptive Gaussian blurring== for sparse environments, and cumulative image processing to handle occlusions and saturation.

## Results

- Dr-BA produced superior average trajectory accuracy (**0.54m** ATE, **0.25m** EPE on "Suburbs" route) and significantly enhanced translational self-consistency (**0.14m** on "Suburbs") compared to previous methods.
- The generated maps enabled Direct Radar Localization (DRL) to achieve state-of-the-art performance with errors within **10 cm** and **0.1 degrees** on structured routes and ensured no unrecoverable failures across the challenging Boreas-RT dataset.
- The separable optimization formulation maintained accuracy while substantially reducing computational demands and memory footprint, allowing for practical mapping of large-scale environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The linear relationship of map intensities within the direct bundle adjustment objective function enables efficient separable optimization via Variable Projection (VarPro).
> - Decoupling pose and map intensity optimization allows map intensities to be computed optimally in closed form, and reduces the Hessian's size to scale with poses rather than map points, making direct BA tractable for large datasets.
> - Generating locally and globally consistent dense radar maps, rather than just accurate trajectories, is essential for robust performance in downstream applications like precise localization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous systems require robust environmental awareness, often achieved through simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) or localization within pre-existing maps. Radar sensors offer a unique advantage over cameras and lidar due to their resilience in adverse weather conditions, such as rain, snow, or fog, which are critical for all-weather autonomous operation.

Current state estimation approaches using spinning radar typically fall into two main categories:
1. **Feature-based methods:** These extract sparse point clouds or features from range-azimuth-intensity radar measurements and then apply point cloud alignment or feature matching techniques. While efficient, these methods discard a significant amount of data, often resulting in sparse maps that necessitate specific feature extraction for downstream applications like localization. Examples include ORORA, NDT-based methods, and ICP-like registrations.
2. **Direct methods:** These leverage the full radar return, operating directly on dense intensity images (Cartesian or polar). Early direct approaches often used cross-correlation for odometry but frequently overlooked motion and Doppler distortion. More recent advancements, like DRO, address these distortions by optimizing continuous cross-correlation with local maps.

For global consistency, SLAM methods incorporate loop-closure detection and pose-graph optimization (PGO). However, existing PGO-based radar SLAM frameworks, like Dr-PoGO and TBV-SLAM, primarily focus on accurate trajectory estimation. While they provide globally consistent trajectories, the maps generated by "stitching together" radar data based on these trajectories are not guaranteed to be locally consistent. This local inconsistency can be problematic for applications requiring high-fidelity environmental representations, such as precise localization or planning.

Bundle Adjustment (BA) is a widely used technique in computer vision and robotics to jointly optimize sensor poses and scene structure, aiming for both local and global consistency. However, a direct BA framework for spinning radar data, operating on raw intensity images to produce dense, consistent maps, has not been previously established. Existing BA formulations, particularly those in medical imaging, have demonstrated the potential for separable optimization in direct BA, but their application to radar data in the context of autonomous driving, with its unique challenges and scale, remained unexplored.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing Dr-BA, the first direct radar bundle adjustment framework. It aims to generate dense, locally, and globally consistent radar maps by directly optimizing raw radar intensity images from multiple scans. Furthermore, it explicitly formulates the problem as a separable optimization, seeking to overcome the computational intractability often associated with direct methods as map size grows. By demonstrating a direct radar localization (DRL) method using Dr-BA-generated maps, the authors also highlight the practical utility and robustness of their mapping output in a critical downstream application, particularly under challenging environmental conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for introducing Dr-BA are multifaceted, addressing fundamental challenges in robust autonomous system operation:

1. **Overcoming Radar Limitations for Reliable Mapping:** While radar offers all-weather robustness, existing radar-based mapping techniques have limitations. Feature-based methods produce sparse maps that may not be sufficiently detailed for all downstream applications and require consistent feature extraction. Pose-graph optimization (PGO) methods, while improving global trajectory consistency, do not guarantee local consistency of the resulting stitched maps. The primary motivation is to leverage radar's all-weather capability to create dense, high-quality environmental maps that are both locally and globally consistent, a characteristic vital for robust autonomous navigation and decision-making.

2. **Developing a Direct Radar Bundle Adjustment (BA) Framework:** The paper aims to introduce the first bundle adjustment framework that operates directly on 2D spinning radar intensity images. This "direct" approach is motivated by the desire to utilize the full information content of radar scans, avoiding the information loss inherent in feature extraction. The objective is to simultaneously estimate sensor poses and a dense map by optimizing the intensity differences across all overlapping radar measurements.

3. **Achieving Computational Efficiency through Separable Optimization:** Direct methods, particularly BA, can become computationally intractable for large-scale environments, such as those encountered in autonomous driving, which involve thousands of poses and kilometers of mapping data. A key motivation is to address this by formulating the BA problem as a separable optimization. This approach seeks to decouple the optimization of sensor poses from the optimization of map intensities, leading to a more efficient and tractable solution, with complexity scaling linearly with map size rather than cubically.

4. **Enabling Downstream Applications with High-Quality Maps:** The ultimate goal of accurate mapping is to support robust downstream tasks. The paper is motivated to demonstrate the practical value of the Dr-BA-generated maps by extending the framework to a novel direct radar-only localization (DRL) method. This objective serves to validate the utility and accuracy of the created maps in a real-world scenario, particularly for cross-session localization in diverse weather conditions.

5. **Promoting Open Science and Reproducibility:** The authors are motivated to contribute to the robotics community by making their implementation of Dr-BA and DRL publicly available. This objective facilitates further research, comparison, and application of direct radar state estimation techniques.

In summary, the core motivation is to enhance the robustness and reliability of autonomous systems in all weather conditions by developing a computationally efficient, direct radar BA framework that produces high-fidelity, consistent maps suitable for demanding applications like autonomous driving, and to demonstrate its utility through a novel localization method.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dr-BA's methodology centers on formulating the direct radar bundle adjustment problem as a separable optimization. This allows for an efficient decoupling of pose estimation from dense map construction.

**A. Direct Bundle Adjustment Formulation:**
The core idea is to define map variables as intensities at predetermined sample locations, rather than optimizing the locations themselves.
1. **Objective Function:** The problem is formulated as a weighted least-squares minimization (Eq. 2):
 $i^{\star}, T^{\star} = \arg \min_{i,T} \sum_{v=1}^{P} \sum_{n \in V_v} w_{v,n} (i_v - \tilde{i}_{v,n})^2$
 Here, $i_v$ are the intensity values of $P$ map points $m_v$, $T_n$ are $N$ sensor poses, $\tilde{i}_{v,n}$ is the measured intensity at $m_v$ from scan $n$ (obtained via a sampling function $\beta(\cdot)$), and $w_{v,n}$ are residual weights. The set $V_v$ contains indices of states that observe map point $m_v$.

2. **Separable Optimization via Variable Projection (VarPro):** A critical observation is that the intensity values ($i_v$) enter linearly into the error terms. This linearity enables the application of VarPro.
 - **Map Intensity Estimation (given fixed poses):** By treating the poses $T$ as fixed, the optimal value for each map intensity $i_v$ can be derived in closed form (Eq. 3):
 $i_v(T) = \frac{1}{w_v} \sum_{n \in V_v} w_{v,n} \tilde{i}_{v,n} \equiv \bar{i}_v$
 where $w_v = \sum_{n \in V_v} w_{v,n}$. This means optimal map intensities are simply weighted averages of observed scan intensities. This step allows for efficient, parallelized, per-location map intensity generation.
 - **Pose Estimation (reduced problem):** The closed-form solution for $i_v(T)$ is then substituted back into the original objective function (Eq. 2), projecting out the explicit dependence on map intensities (Eq. 4):
 $T^{\star} = \arg \min_{T} \sum_{v=1}^{P} \sum_{n \in V_v} w_{v,n} (\bar{i}_v - \tilde{i}_{v,n})^2$
 This reduced objective now depends only on the sensor poses $T$. This formulation aims to find an optimal $T$ that minimizes the weighted variance of intensity measurements at all map locations. The first pose is fixed to anchor the fully relative problem.

3. **Iterative Solution for Poses:** Equation (4) is a non-linear least squares problem solved iteratively using the Gauss-Newton method. This involves linearizing the objective function and solving a linear system (Eq. 5): $H \Delta T = b$, where $H = J^T J$ is the Hessian and $b = -J^T e$ is the gradient vector. $H$ and $b$ can be efficiently computed by summing contributions from individual map points (Eq. 6). The sparsity of the Jacobian and Hessian, due to limited co-observability between states, means the linear system size does not depend on the number of map points, only the number of poses, making it scalable.

**B. Direct Localization Extension (DRL):**
The separable optimization framework naturally extends to direct radar localization. Given a pre-built map with fixed intensity values $i_v$, the objective is to optimize for a single sensor pose $T_n$ (Eq. 7):
$T^{\star}_n = \arg \min_{T_n} \sum_{v=1}^{Q} w_{v,n} (i_v - \tilde{i}_{v,n})^2$
Here, $Q$ represents the number of map intensities observable by the scan at $T_n$. This is also solved iteratively using Gauss-Newton.

**C. Implementation Details for 2D Spinning Radar:**
1. **Map Representation:** A 2D grid of locations $m \in \mathbb{R}^2$ with intensities $i \in$ is used, sampled regularly at a chosen resolution $r_v$.
2. **State Representation:** Sensor poses are represented as elements of SE(2), $T_n \in SE(2)$, which are optimized directly on-manifold. A keyframing approach (motion-based threshold of $\ge 5$ m or $\ge 30^{\circ}$) is employed to select poses and control the problem size.
3. **Initialization:** As Dr-BA performs no explicit loop closure or feature association, a high-quality initial trajectory is required. This is provided by Dr-PoGO.
4. **Scan Representation and Pre-processing:**
 - **Cartesian Images:** Radar scans are treated as Cartesian intensity images. A map point $m_v$ is converted to pixel coordinates $p^n_v$ within a scan captured from $T_n$ using a transformation (Eq. 8), and bilinear interpolation obtains $\tilde{i}_{v,n}$.
 - **Uncertainty Weighting:** Weights $w_{v,n}$ are derived from a constant per-pixel uncertainty and a range-dependent uncertainty.
 - **Motion and Doppler Distortion:** Raw spinning radar data is subject to motion and Doppler distortion. To obtain undistorted scans, the Doppler-Aware Direct Radar Odometry (DRO) method is leveraged. DRO outputs undistorted scans and smoothed local maps, which are used as inputs to Dr-BA.
 - **Adaptive Gaussian Blurring:** In sparse environments with low gradient information, an adaptive blurring approach is applied. If the percentage of pixels above a certain intensity threshold is low, progressively stronger Gaussian blur is applied to the image until a bound is met. This enhances the convergence basin for optimization.
 - **Cumulative Image Processing:** To address issues like occlusions (which cause "fake" zero intensities) and radar saturation ("ringing"), a "cumulative" radar scan is constructed. Zero measurements with a cumulative score above a threshold are ignored, as are measurements with a score above a higher threshold (indicating saturation).

**D. Experimental Setup:**
The methods are validated on the Boreas Road Trip (Boreas-RT) dataset, consisting of 20 sequences (206.4 km) across five diverse routes (Suburbs, Industrial, Skyway, Forest, Farm). Metrics include Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE), End-Pose-Error (EPE), and a novel "self-consistency" metric (relative error for revisited areas) to evaluate map quality. Localization performance is assessed using longitudinal, lateral, and yaw RMSE. Comparisons are made against DRO, TBV-SLAM, Dr-PoGO, and radar teach & repeat (RTR) for localization. Ablation studies examine the impact of pre-processing, initialization sensitivity, and VarPro.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the performance of Dr-BA in direct radar bundle adjustment and its utility for direct radar localization.

**A. Direct Radar Bundle Adjustment Performance:**
- **Trajectory Accuracy (ATE & EPE):**
 - Dr-BA consistently achieved the best average ATE and EPE results on structured environments like "Suburbs" and "Industrial" routes, demonstrating refined trajectory estimates compared to baselines. For example, on "Suburbs", Dr-BA achieved an ATE of 0.54m and EPE of 0.25m, outperforming Dr-PoGO (0.75m/0.42m) and significantly surpassing TBV-SLAM (6.11m/0.55m) and DRO (9.53m/38.9m).
 - In challenging unstructured environments ("Forest", "Farm"), Dr-BA performed comparably to Dr-PoGO, with slight variations. For instance, on "Forest", Dr-BA had ATE/EPE of 4.36m/0.78m, while Dr-PoGO had 4.31m/0.75m.
 - On the "Skyway" route, Dr-BA showed improved performance (ATE 2.46m, EPE 0.17m) compared to Dr-PoGO (3.16m/0.23m), while TBV-SLAM failed.
- **Map Consistency (Self-Consistency Metric):**
 - Dr-BA significantly improved translational self-consistency across most routes with substantial pose overlap, such as "Suburbs" (0.14m vs. Dr-PoGO's 0.34m) and "Industrial" (0.10m vs. Dr-PoGO's 0.36m). This indicates that Dr-BA produces maps with better local alignment of observations from different poses.
 - Rotational self-consistency remained generally similar to Dr-PoGO, except for "Skyway," where adaptive blurring improved translational consistency at the expense of some rotational precision (0.24$^\circ$ for Dr-BA vs. 0.10$^\circ$ for Dr-PoGO).
- **Qualitative Map Quality:** Visual comparisons (Figure 4) demonstrated that while Dr-PoGO could yield accurate trajectories (low ATE), it often lacked local map consistency (e.g., longitudinal misalignment of scans). Dr-BA, in contrast, enforced local consistency, resulting in better-overlapping scans critical for downstream tasks.
- **Computational Performance:** The use of $r_v = 1.0$ m map resolution allowed Dr-BA to process maps of up to four million points in half an hour to a few hours on a standard laptop, which is within practical limits for mapping kilometers of data.

**B. Direct Radar Localization (DRL) Performance:**
- **Localization Accuracy:**
 - On structured routes ("Suburbs", "Industrial"), DRL-Dr-BA achieved state-of-the-art localization performance, with errors within 10 cm and 0.1$^\circ$. It performed comparably to or slightly better than DRL-GT (using ground truth poses for map creation) and substantially outperformed DRL-Dr-PoGO and the baseline RTR, particularly in rotational accuracy. For "Suburbs", DRL-Dr-BA had 0.076m longitudinal, 0.049m lateral, and 0.061$^\circ$ yaw RMSE.
 - In challenging environments where RTR failed entirely ("Skyway", "Forest", "Farm"), DRL-based methods demonstrated robustness. DRL-Dr-BA had no unrecoverable failures across the entire dataset, a result not matched even by DRL-GT.
 - While DRL-Dr-BA prevented failures, its average localization performance in successful runs on "Farm" was worse than DRL-GT or DRL-Dr-PoGO. On "Forest", all DRL methods showed similar performance, likely limited by ground truth noise.
- **Real-time Capability:** DRL achieved an average processing time of 200 ms per frame on a single thread, making it "real-time" for the 4 Hz radar data used.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
- **Processing Impact:** Adaptive blurring and the use of local maps from DRO were crucial for good performance, particularly in sparse environments like the "Skyway" route. The cumulative image processing also contributed to improved ATE.
- **Initialization Sensitivity:** Dr-BA demonstrated robustness to initialization errors, recovering perturbations up to 3.0 m and 1.50$^\circ$ without significant ATE degradation, although convergence naturally took more iterations. This suggests that while global consistency from an initial PGO is helpful, local consistency can be relatively poor.
- **VarPro Impact:** The separable optimization approach (Dr-BA) yielded near-identical ATE to a combined, non-separable formulation but required substantially less compute time and memory (lower Hessian non-zeros). The combined approach became prohibitively slow for larger problem sizes, highlighting the efficiency benefits of VarPro.

**D. Limitations Identified:**
- Performance degradation was observed in environments with very sparse features, diffuse natural structure, or poor scan co-visibility ("Skyway", "Forest", "Farm").
- The current formulation ignores the dependency of radar intensity returns on viewpoint, which could potentially improve accuracy if incorporated.
- In some challenging sequences like "Farm", maps generated with Dr-PoGO (which incorporates relative motion from Doppler and gyroscope) led to better localization results for successful runs than Dr-BA, suggesting the importance of additional interoceptive data in feature-poor environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Dr-BA represents a notable advancement in radar-based state estimation for autonomous systems, with several significant implications:

1. **Enabling Robust All-Weather Autonomy:** By providing the first direct radar bundle adjustment framework, Dr-BA facilitates the creation of high-fidelity, dense maps directly from radar intensity images. Given radar's robustness to adverse weather, this directly addresses a critical challenge for autonomous vehicles: reliable operation in conditions (e.g., heavy rain, snow, fog) where lidar and cameras often degrade or fail. The developed DRL further demonstrates the practical applicability of these maps for cross-session localization, suggesting enhanced navigation capabilities in diverse weather.

2. **Producing High-Quality, Consistent Maps:** Unlike many existing radar SLAM methods that prioritize trajectory accuracy but may yield locally inconsistent maps, Dr-BA explicitly optimizes for both local and global map consistency. This results in maps that are suitable for a wider array of downstream applications, including precise localization, path planning, and environmental perception, where fine-grained geometric and intensity consistency is crucial. The dense nature of the maps, as opposed to sparse point clouds, offers more comprehensive environmental representation.

3. **Advancing Direct Methods and Separable Optimization:** Dr-BA's formulation as a separable optimization problem using Variable Projection (VarPro) is a significant methodological contribution. It demonstrates how to make direct methods computationally tractable for large-scale, real-world problems. By decoupling pose and map optimization, the framework achieves linear scaling with map size, addressing a major bottleneck for direct BA in extended environments. This approach has the potential to be generalized to other sensors or direct state estimation problems where linear variables are present in the objective.

4. **Foundation for Future Radar Perception Systems:** The framework lays a foundation for future research in direct radar perception. The pre-processing steps, such as motion/Doppler distortion compensation, adaptive blurring, and cumulative image processing, are valuable contributions for handling specific challenges of radar data. The identified limitations regarding sparse features and viewpoint dependency also open avenues for further research and integration with other sensor modalities.

5. **Facilitating Research and Development:** The public release of the Dr-BA and DRL implementation provides a valuable resource for the robotics community. This allows other researchers to build upon this work, compare new methods against a robust baseline, and accelerate the development of all-weather autonomous capabilities.

In summary, Dr-BA offers a practical, efficient, and robust solution for radar mapping and localization, directly contributing to the goal of reliable autonomous navigation in all environmental conditions, and advances the methodological understanding of direct, separable optimization for large-scale robotic perception.
