# Evolution through Large Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Evolution through Large Models (ELM) uses a code-trained LLM diff model as an intelligent mutation operator inside a MAP-Elites quality-diversity loop. From one hand-written seed program it generated hundreds of thousands of working Python programs for Sodarace walking robots, a domain absent from pretraining. That data trained a language model, and RL then made it output a suitable walker for a given terrain.

## Problem

- Genetic programming mutates code randomly, so discrete edits usually break functionality, and the chance of a useful multi-part change falls exponentially with the number of coupled edits needed.
- Genetic programming needs domain-specific setup and cannot operate on modern human programming languages at scale without hand-designed function sets, limiting what can be evolved.
- RL cannot bootstrap into a new domain where the LLM has no prior data, since it cannot emit even valid artifacts, leaving no gradient signal and no training examples.

## Method

- **ELM** mutation: a 300M-parameter ==diff model==, prompted with commit messages such as 'Changed make walker function', proposes ==code diffs== that make coherent, human-like edits to Python programs.
- ==MAP-Elites== outer loop over a 12x12x12 grid of robot height, width and mass ==niches==, seeded from single hand-written programs, with the diff model fine-tuned on its own accepted diffs.
- Three-stage invention pipeline: **ELM** data generation, pretraining a code LLM on the outputs, then ==PPO== with a ==terrain embedding network== (discrete codes or ResNet50) for ==terrain-conditional invention==.

## Results

- On 4-Parity bug fixing, GP mutation solved **none** of five-bug cases in **100,000** trials, while the diff operator fixed **all five**; edit-mode and prompting models beat the diff model.
- Each ELM run used **1,024,000** evaluations and filled most of the **1,728** niches; fine-tuning the diff model raised niches filled, QD score, and the share of valid, runnable diffs.
- ELM data gave **280K** examples at a **50%** threshold and **95K** at **80%**; models trained on it matched dataset quality-diversity, random initialization hurt, and PPO yielded terrain-specific walkers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs trained on code diffs give evolutionary search a mutation operator that makes correlated multi-part edits, so search can stay on the manifold of working programs.
> - Search can manufacture training data for domains with none: evolution outputs a dataset that pretrains an LLM, which then lets RL start, avoiding the cold-start problem.
> - Self-improvement helps but has limits: one round of fine-tuning the mutation operator gave large gains, further rounds gave diminishing returns, and RL training proved brittle.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Artificial intelligence research has historically developed along distinct paradigms, with evolutionary computation and deep learning often progressing in parallel with limited interaction. The paper "Evolution Through Large Models" by researchers at OpenAI proposes a novel approach to bridge these paradigms by leveraging large language models (LLMs) to enhance evolutionary algorithms, specifically genetic programming (GP).

This synergy addresses a fundamental challenge in AI: how to explore and generate innovative solutions in domains where training data is limited or nonexistent. The researchers introduce Evolution through Large Models (ELM), a technique that uses LLMs to intelligently guide the evolution of computer programs, enabling exploration of solution spaces beyond the reach of either approach alone.

### 2. Combining Evolutionary Computation with Large Language Models

Evolutionary computation, particularly genetic programming, has long been used to automatically generate computer programs through principles inspired by biological evolution. However, traditional GP faces significant challenges:

1. The mutation operators typically make random changes to program code, often producing non-functional results
2. The approach struggles to scale to complex programs in modern programming languages
3. Domain expertise is required to define appropriate function sets and operators

Meanwhile, large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in code generation but are limited by their training data. They excel at tasks similar to what they've seen during training but struggle with exploration of truly novel solutions.

ELM combines these approaches by using LLMs as intelligent mutation operators within an evolutionary framework. The LLM can understand and manipulate code in a structured, semantically meaningful way that respects syntax and programming patterns, while the evolutionary algorithm guides the exploration of diverse solutions.

### 3. The ELM Framework

The core innovation of ELM is the use of a diff model—an LLM trained on code changes from GitHub—to serve as an intelligent mutation operator. This model accepts a program and a commit message describing a desired change, then produces a modified version of the program. By generating semantically meaningful mutations, the diff model dramatically outperforms traditional GP operators.

The ELM process works as follows:

1. Begin with a population of seed programs
2. For each program, generate multiple mutations using the diff model with various commit messages
3. Evaluate each mutated program's fitness and diversity
4. Select the best programs to populate a map of diverse solutions
5. Continue this process iteratively, potentially fine-tuning the diff model on successful mutations

The approach employs MAP-Elites, a quality diversity algorithm that maintains a map of solutions organized by meaningful features (in this case, the dimensions of the resulting robot). This ensures that ELM explores diverse solutions rather than simply optimizing for a single objective.

A crucial finding is that the performance gap between traditional GP mutations and LLM-based diff mutations is substantial:

```
Traditional GP mutation success rate: ~0.1-1% (decreasing with program complexity)
LLM-based diff mutation success rate: ~10-20% (relatively stable with complexity)
```

### 4. Sodarace as a Testing Ground

To demonstrate ELM, the researchers chose Sodarace, a domain where two-dimensional ambulating robots (Sodaracers) compete across various terrains. Sodaracers consist of point masses connected by springs, creating structures that can move in interesting ways when simulated with physics.

This domain is ideal for testing ELM because:

1. It's computationally efficient to simulate
2. The robots can be encoded as Python programs
3. It allows for diverse designs with varying performance characteristics
4. There was no existing large dataset of Sodaracers to train an LLM directly

The encoding of Sodaracers as Python programs allows ELM to work directly with standard code rather than requiring specialized representations. Here's a simplified example of how a Sodaracer might be defined:

```python
def make_walker():
    points = []
    springs = []

    # Add points (point masses)
    points.append(Point(0, 0))
    points.append(Point(10, 0))
    points.append(Point(5, 10))

    # Connect points with springs
    springs.append(Spring(points[0], points[1]))
    springs.append(Spring(points[1], points[2]))
    springs.append(Spring(points[2], points[0]))

    return Walker(points, springs)
```

Different seed designs were used to initialize the ELM process, including square, radial, and more complex CPPN-based designs. The system was tasked with evolving walkers for flat terrain initially, with later experiments extending to more complex environments such as bumpy terrain, tunnels, and walls.

### 5. Key Experimental Results

The researchers found that ELM significantly outperforms traditional approaches in several key metrics:

1. **Mutation Efficacy**: The diff model achieves a much higher rate of successful mutations than traditional GP operators, particularly as program complexity increases. While GP success rates decay exponentially with program complexity, the diff model maintains relatively stable performance.

2. **Model Performance**: Fine-tuning the diff model on successful mutations from previous ELM runs substantially improved performance. The fine-tuned model discovered approximately 90% of available niches in the solution space, compared to 65% for the pretrained model.

3. **Diverse Solutions**: ELM generated various types of Sodaracers with distinct locomotion strategies, demonstrating its ability to explore the solution space effectively.

4. **Adaptation to Environments**: The system evolved diverse walkers adapted to specific terrains, such as walls, tunnels, and bumpy surfaces.

### 6. The Invention Pipeline

Beyond demonstrating ELM as a standalone technique, the paper proposes a three-stage "invention pipeline" for open-ended innovation:

1. **Stage 1 (ELM)**: Generate diverse, functional examples in a novel domain using ELM
2. **Stage 2 (LLM Training)**: Train a language model on these examples to create a domain-specific generator
3. **Stage 3 (RL Fine-tuning)**: Fine-tune the model with reinforcement learning to produce conditional solutions

This pipeline addresses a fundamental challenge: how to train AI systems to generate solutions in domains where no training data exists. By bootstrapping with ELM, the researchers demonstrate that an LLM can be trained to generate Sodaracers despite never having seen examples during its pretraining.

### 7. From Exploration to Conditional Creation

A particularly significant result is the progression from unconditional generation to conditional creation. After the initial ELM phase, the system was trained to produce Sodaracers tailored to specific terrains:

1. Using the diverse Sodaracers generated by ELM as training data, a new language model was trained to generate Sodaracers given a terrain description
2. This model was then fine-tuned with reinforcement learning to maximize performance on specific terrains
3. The resulting system could analyze a terrain and generate an appropriate Sodaracer design for that environment

This capability demonstrates the power of the complete invention pipeline, which enables:

### 8. Implications for Open-Ended AI

The researchers frame ELM and the invention pipeline as steps toward open-ended AI—systems capable of unbounded innovation and adaptation. They introduce the concept of "detached conditional things" (DCTs) as a key ingredient for open-endedness. DCTs are artifacts created in response to environmental conditions but which persist independently and can change the environment itself.

In this framework, the Sodaracers are simple DCTs. More sophisticated DCTs might include tools, technologies, or even cultural artifacts that:

1. Are created in response to environmental conditions
2. Persist beyond their initial creation
3. Modify the environment, potentially leading to new challenges
4. Trigger the creation of new DCTs

This perspective connects ELM to broader questions of artificial life, cultural evolution, and potentially recursive self-improvement in AI systems.

### 9. Conclusion

Evolution Through Large Models represents a significant advance in combining evolutionary computation with modern deep learning approaches. By leveraging LLMs as intelligent mutation operators, ELM overcomes limitations of traditional genetic programming while enabling exploration beyond the training distribution of the language model.

The three-stage invention pipeline demonstrates how this approach can bootstrap innovation in domains without existing training data, potentially opening new frontiers for AI research. The ability to generate diverse, functional programs and then train conditional generators has implications for automated programming, design, and problem-solving across domains.

As AI research continues to evolve, approaches like ELM that bridge traditionally separate paradigms may prove crucial for developing systems capable of open-ended innovation. The synthesis of evolutionary principles with the semantic understanding of large language models offers a promising direction for creating AI systems that can invent, adapt, and potentially participate in the open-ended process of cumulative innovation that has characterized human technological development.
