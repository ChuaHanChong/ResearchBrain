# GR-RL: Going Dexterous and Precise for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance Seed developed GR-RL, a multi-stage reinforcement-augmented training pipeline, to specialize generalist Vision-Language-Action policies for dexterous and precise long-horizon robotic manipulation. The framework achieved an 83.3% success rate in autonomously lacing a shoe by effectively filtering suboptimal human demonstrations and aligning policy learning with real-world deployment through online reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies struggle with millimeter-level precision and dexterous manipulation of deformable objects in long-horizon tasks.
- Current VLA policies rely on human demonstrations, which often contain suboptimal, hesitant, or erroneous behaviors, leading to policies that mimic these flaws.
- A mismatch exists between raw actions learned during offline training and optimized actions executed during real-world deployment, degrading performance in precise tasks.

## Method

- A multi-stage reinforcement-augmented training pipeline is introduced, comprising data filtering, morphological symmetry augmentation, and online reinforcement learning.
- The framework utilizes a Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT) architecture, which includes a VLA policy with a Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct backbone and an action diffusion transformer, alongside a multi-task distributional critic.
- Online steering via a learned latent space noise predictor and critic distillation addresses the training-deployment mismatch during fine-tuning through closed-loop interactions.

## Results

- GR-RL achieved an **83.3%** success rate in autonomously lacing a shoe, demonstrating capabilities in long-horizon reasoning, millimeter-level precision, and compliant soft-body interaction.
- Ablation studies confirmed that data filtering boosted success from **45.7%** (base) to **61.6%**, while filtering combined with augmentation reached **72.7%**, and the full GR-RL pipeline achieved **83.3%**.
- The distributional critic proved highly robust and sensitive in reflecting task progress, accurately capturing subtle failures and long-term positive effects, outperforming regression-based and non-distributional critics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A distributional critic, used as a robust task progress evaluator, can effectively identify and filter suboptimal segments from human demonstrations to improve policy learning.
> - Morphological symmetry augmentation during offline training significantly enhances generalization and robustness for bimanual tasks by expanding data diversity.
> - Online reinforcement learning with latent space noise prediction is crucial for adapting policies to real-world execution, aligning behaviors with deployment, and achieving performance beyond offline capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

GR-RL is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within robotics and artificial intelligence: generalist robotic foundation models, real-world reinforcement learning, and dexterous manipulation of deformable objects.

The introduction of the paper clearly articulates its grounding in the recent advancements of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-1, RT-2, π0, and ByteDance's own GR-3. These models leverage "scaling laws" observed in vision-language models to achieve impressive generalization across diverse tasks, objects, and environments, given high-level natural language instructions and visual observations. They represent a paradigm shift towards building "generalist robotic agents."

However, the paper identifies a critical gap in this landscape: while these generalist models excel in broad applicability, they "still fall short in two fundamental aspects for real-world deployment: (1) Dexterity with precision – millimeter-level control over deformable objects remains unsolved. (2) Long-horizon robustness – errors accumulate over steps..." This highlights a common challenge in AI: the transition from general capability to reliable, high-performance specialization for complex real-world tasks. GR-RL directly addresses this by proposing a framework to specialize these generalist policies.

Furthermore, GR-RL contributes significantly to the field of real-world reinforcement learning (RL). Pure imitation learning, which forms the basis of many VLA policies, is prone to compounding errors and cannot surpass the quality of its demonstration data. This paper aligns with a growing body of work that utilizes online data collection and real-world RL to enhance manipulation robustness [2, 26, 28, 43–45, 53, 54]. It differentiates itself from simulation-based RL approaches and world-model-based RL [18–22, 46, 62, 67] by focusing on efficient, off-policy real-world RL. This choice acknowledges the challenges of sample inefficiency and noise in physical environments while seeking to maximize the utility of real-world interactions. The concurrent work π*0.6 is also referenced, indicating a shared direction in adopting distributional critics for high-precision manipulation, though GR-RL distinguishes its approach through filtered behavior cloning and a stronger base offline policy.

Finally, the choice of shoe lacing as a benchmark task places GR-RL at the forefront of tackling deformable object manipulation. Tasks involving soft, compliant materials are notoriously difficult for robots due to their high dimensionality, complex physics, and sensitivity to contact forces. Traditional methods for shoelacing often rely on predefined action primitives and motion planning [39–41], lacking generalization and robustness to failure. GR-RL's learning-based approach, demonstrating success in such a complex, real-world soft-body interaction, represents a significant step towards enabling robots to perform intricate tasks involving flexible materials, which has broad implications for manufacturing, healthcare, and domestic robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GR-RL is to transform a generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy into a highly specialized and capable agent adept at long-horizon, dexterous, and precise robotic manipulation. This overarching goal is driven by the recognition of two fundamental bottlenecks inherent in current generalist VLA policies when applied to complex real-world scenarios:

1. **Suboptimal Human Demonstrations:** Existing VLA policies, including ByteDance's GR-3, largely rely on the assumption that human demonstrations are optimal or near-optimal. However, for tasks demanding extreme precision and dexterity, such as threading a shoelace, human teleoperators often exhibit hesitant, inconsistent, and even erroneous behaviors. These "noisy suboptimal demonstrations" can lead to policies that learn to mimic these flaws, resulting in suboptimal performance and limited skills. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism to automatically identify and rectify these imperfections in the training data, thereby learning from a higher-quality subset of human expertise.

2. **Demonstration and Inference Mismatch:** During standard offline training, VLA policies learn to predict fixed-length action chunks based on human demonstrations. However, in real-world deployment, these raw action predictions are often subjected to system-level optimizations like temporal ensembling or asynchronous receding horizon control to ensure smooth and safe execution. This crucial difference between the training environment (raw actions) and the deployment environment (optimized actions) creates a "mismatch," which can severely degrade a policy's performance, especially in tasks requiring millimeter-level precision and compliant interaction. The motivation is to align the policy's learning process with its actual deployment behaviors, allowing it to adapt and improve through real-world trial and error.

To address these bottlenecks, GR-RL sets out to achieve several specific, challenging capabilities:
- **Millimeter-level Precision:** The ability to execute actions with extreme accuracy, crucial for tasks like threading a shoelace through a tiny eyelet.
- **Dexterous Manipulation of Deformable Objects:** Handling soft, flexible objects (like shoelaces and shoes) that change shape under interaction, requiring adaptive and compliant control.
- **Long-Horizon Robustness and Reasoning:** Performing multi-step tasks that extend over time, requiring sequential decision-making, error recovery, and adaptation to diverse and unexpected scenarios without accumulating failures.
- **High Success Rates:** Demonstrating a significant improvement in reliability and performance compared to baseline generalist policies, making robotic manipulation practical for complex tasks.

The ultimate aim is to provide a "step toward enabling generalist robot foundation models to specialize into reliable real-world experts," thereby bridging the gap between broad AI capabilities and practical, high-performance robotic deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GR-RL proposes a novel **multi-stage reinforcement-augmented training pipeline** designed to filter, augment, and reinforce suboptimal human demonstrations, thereby turning a generalist VLA policy into a highly capable specialist. The framework adopts a **Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT) architecture** with a 5B parameter count, comprising a VLA policy (πθ) and a multi-task critic (Qϕ).

**Architecture Details:**
- **Policy (πθ):** This component controls a bi-manual robot by generating k-length action chunks. It leverages **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct** as its Vision-Language-Model (VLM) backbone, indicating a transfer of knowledge from a powerful, pre-trained large language model. Action prediction is handled by an **action diffusion transformer (DiT)** trained with flow matching objectives, a method known for generating high-quality, continuous actions.
- **Critic (Qϕ):** Similar to the policy, the critic is a causal transformer that evaluates actions. Crucially, it employs **distributional reinforcement learning** (rather than simple regression), treating Q-values as a discrete distribution with upper (1) and lower (0) bounds. This design choice is vital for robustness in sparse reward settings, as it naturally reflects task progress and captures uncertainty.

**Multi-Stage Training Pipeline:**

1. **Data Filtering with a Learned Task Progress Evaluator:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Suboptimal and noisy human demonstrations containing hesitations and mistakes.
 - **Approach:** Instead of direct behavior cloning on raw data, GR-RL first trains the critic (Qϕ) using **offline reinforcement learning (TD3+BC)**. A sparse binary reward is defined: 1 for successful completion, 0 otherwise. Failed trajectories are augmented by identifying "retry keyframes" in successful demonstrations, creating additional negative examples.
 - **Key Insight:** The mean value of the categorical distribution predicted by the distributional critic, Qϕ, naturally serves as a robust **task progress evaluator (ρt)**. When a teleoperator makes a mistake, the predicted progress (Q-value) sharply drops.
 - **Filtering Mechanism:** Transitions are deemed suboptimal and excluded from the policy training dataset if the progress ρt exhibits a value drop greater than a predefined threshold δ within a sequence. This ensures the policy learns only from high-quality, positively contributing actions.

2. **Imitation Learning with Morphological Symmetry Augmentation:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Limited generalization and robustness of the offline policy.
 - **Approach:** A simple yet effective **morphological symmetry augmentation** paradigm is applied during offline training.
 - **Technique:** For bimanual tasks, image observations are horizontally flipped, and images from the left and right wrist cameras are swapped. Proprioception states and actions are converted via mirror symmetry in the world frame and then transformed back to local wrist frames. Corresponding language instructions are also flipped (e.g., "left" becomes "right"). This augmentation significantly expands the diversity of the training data without additional human effort.

3. **Online Steering for Policy Deployment Alignment:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Mismatch between raw actions seen during offline training and optimized actions executed during real-world deployment (due to control-level post-processing like temporal ensembling or receding horizon control).
 - **Approach:** After offline pre-training, GR-RL performs **online reinforcement learning** to fine-tune the policy through closed-loop interactions.
 - **Structured Exploration:** Instead of adding arbitrary noise to actions (which is ineffective for millimeter-precision tasks), GR-RL learns a lightweight **latent space noise predictor (πθ')** that generates initial noise (ϵt) for the action DiT. This steers the denoising process towards high-return regions. A penalty is imposed if the noise diverges too much from the original normal distribution to prevent arbitrary action generation.
 - **Critic Distillation:** A separate Q-function (Qϕ') is distilled over the noise space from the original action space critic (Qϕ) to facilitate policy optimization without backpropagating through the complex flow model. This distillation uses a mix of noise sampled from a normal distribution and the noise predictor itself to ensure good coverage.
 - **Sample Efficiency:** An off-policy buffer (warmed up with initial rollouts of offline-trained checkpoints) and an on-policy buffer (storing recent trajectories) are maintained. Batches are sampled evenly from both to improve sample efficiency and stabilize training. Teleoperated trajectories are not mixed into the online buffers to prevent training on mismatched dynamics.

**Robot and System:** The framework is validated on **ByteMini-v2**, a custom-built wheeled mobile manipulation robot. ByteMini-v2 features 7-DoF dual robotic arms with unique wrist spherical joints, head and hand cameras (RGB and RGB-D), and a 3D LiDAR. It represents an upgrade from ByteMini-v1 (used for GR-3), with enhanced load capacity, improved mobility in confined spaces, and a more polished, user-friendly design. The experimental task is shoe lacing, performed with a binary sparse reward for successful threading and placement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that GR-RL achieves a significant breakthrough in long-horizon dexterous and precise robotic manipulation, specifically evidenced by its ability to autonomously lace a shoe.

**Overall Performance:**
- GR-RL achieves an **83.3% success rate** in autonomously lacing a shoe by threading shoelaces through multiple eyelets. The authors claim this is the "first learning-based policy" to accomplish this task, which demands long-horizon reasoning, millimeter-level precision, and compliant soft-body interaction.

**Effectiveness of the Multi-Stage Training Pipeline (Ablation Study):**
The research provides a clear ablation study illustrating the contribution of each stage to the final performance:
- **GR-3 (Base Behavior Cloning):** The initial generalist VLA policy, trained on all human teleoperated data, achieved a success rate of **45.7%**. This highlights the limitations of directly imitating suboptimal human demonstrations for precise tasks.
- **Filtered BC:** By applying the learned task progress model to filter out suboptimal transitions, the success rate significantly boosted to **61.6%**. This confirms the critical importance of data cleaning and quality for high-precision manipulation.
- **Filtered BC + Augmentation:** The addition of morphological symmetry data augmentation further improved performance, raising the success rate to **72.7%**. This indicates the effectiveness of this simple yet powerful augmentation scheme in enhancing generalization and robustness for bimanual tasks.
- **GR-RL (Filtered BC + Aug + Online RL):** The complete GR-RL pipeline, incorporating online steering reinforcement learning, yielded the highest success rate of **83.3%**, demonstrating that online adaptation is crucial for aligning the policy with real-world deployment and surpassing offline performance.

**Online RL Performance:**
- During online finetuning, after an initial phase where the success rate might slightly decrease due to distribution shift, the performance rapidly recovers and grows, eventually exceeding 90% in average success rate. This rapid improvement underscores the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed online steering mechanism in latent space.

**Detailed Stage-wise Success Rates:**
- Analysis of intermediate stages (picking up shoelace, threading, handover, pulling tight) reveals that **data filtering and online RL largely reduce failure during the critical "threading into the eyelet" stage**. Data augmentation, while generally beneficial, provides a smaller magnitude of improvement across all stages. This suggests that precision-demanding actions particularly benefit from higher-quality data and real-world adaptation.

**Ablation on Progress Evaluator:**
- **Distributional Critic (GR-RL):** The proposed distributional critic (bounded 0-1) proved to be highly robust and sensitive, accurately reflecting task progress. It effectively captured subtle failures (e.g., missing an eyelet) and long-term positive effects (e.g., intentionally dropping and regrasping the shoelace for a better pose).
- **Regression-based Progress:** A baseline using direct regression of temporal progress produced overly smooth predictions, lacked sensitivity to subtle failures, and failed to capture long-term strategic actions.
- **Non-distributional Critic:** Standard non-distributional critics suffered from severe over-estimation, especially in early parts of trajectories due to weak sparse reward signals, indicating a lack of robustness in this challenging setting.

**Visualization of Learned Behaviors (Qualitative Results):**
GR-RL exhibits highly robust and adaptive behaviors:
- **Generalization:** Handles shoes of different colors and sizes.
- **Error Recovery:** Automatically retries when the shoelace drops or misses an eyelet.
- **Proactive Manipulation:** Can actively adjust its grasp point by dropping and regrasping the shoelace, reorient the shoe, or pull the shoe closer to simplify the task.
- **Complex Situations:** Successfully identifies and manipulates the correct shoelace even when they are crossed.

These findings collectively affirm that GR-RL successfully addresses the critical challenges of achieving dexterity, precision, and long-horizon robustness in robotic manipulation by effectively leveraging and refining generalist VLA policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GR-RL represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, offering a concrete pathway to imbue generalist robot policies with the specialization required for complex real-world tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Breakthrough in Dexterous and Precise Manipulation:**
The achievement of autonomously lacing a shoe with an 83.3% success rate is a landmark. This task is a canonical benchmark for complex manipulation, requiring:
- **Millimeter-level Precision:** Essential for threading shoelaces through small eyelets.
- **Long-Horizon Reasoning:** Involving multiple sequential steps, planning, and error recovery over an extended period.
- **Compliant Soft-Body Interaction:** Handling deformable objects like fabric shoes and flexible shoelaces, which traditionally pose immense challenges for rigid robot control.
GR-RL’s success in this domain demonstrates capabilities that were previously elusive for learning-based methods, opening doors for robots to perform intricate tasks beyond simple pick-and-place.

**2. Methodological Innovation for VLA Policy Refinement:**
GR-RL introduces a robust and practical multi-stage training pipeline that addresses key limitations of current VLA models:
- **Effective Handling of Suboptimal Demonstrations:** The RL-based data filtering mechanism is a crucial contribution. By using a distributional critic as a robust task progress evaluator, it intelligently filters out noisy and suboptimal segments from human demonstrations, enabling the policy to learn from high-quality data. This is vital for tasks where perfect human demonstrations are unrealistic to acquire.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real / Training-to-Deployment Gap:** The online reinforcement learning stage, particularly through latent space noise prediction, directly tackles the mismatch between actions generated during offline training and those executed during deployment due to low-level control optimizations. This closed-loop adaptation is critical for achieving reliable performance in the real world.
- **Robust Progress Evaluation with Distributional Critics:** The demonstration of distributional critics' superior robustness and sensitivity in sparse reward, long-horizon settings compared to regression-based or non-distributional critics is a significant technical insight. This method provides a more accurate and stable signal for task progress.
- **Simple yet Effective Data Augmentation:** The morphological symmetry augmentation for bimanual tasks is a practical and easily implementable technique that significantly boosts policy performance and generalization, maximizing the utility of collected data.

**3. Enabling Specialist Agents from Generalist Foundations:**
Perhaps the most far-reaching impact of GR-RL is its demonstration of how generalist VLA foundation models (like GR-3) can be specialized into "reliable real-world experts." This offers a compelling paradigm for future robotic development:
- **Scalability:** Start with broadly capable, pre-trained models.
- **Efficiency:** Focus fine-tuning efforts on specific, challenging tasks using targeted methods like those in GR-RL.
- **Practicality:** Achieve the high reliability and precision needed for industrial, service, or domestic applications.
This approach suggests a future where a single foundational model can be rapidly adapted and optimized for diverse, specialized tasks with fewer bespoke solutions.

**4. Broadened Real-World Application Potential:**
The capabilities demonstrated by GR-RL extend beyond shoe lacing. The ability to perform long-horizon, dexterous, and precise manipulation of deformable objects has vast implications for:
- **Manufacturing:** Assembly tasks involving cables, fabrics, or flexible components.
- **Healthcare:** Assisting with delicate procedures, handling medical instruments, or aiding in patient care involving clothing or bedding.
- **Domestic Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform household chores like laundry folding, bed-making, or indeed, dressing assistance.
- **Logistics:** Handling diverse packaging materials or sorting complex items.

**5. Future Research Directions:**
The paper also highlights limitations, such as the behavior-drifting problem during online RL and the potential for distilling improved policies back into base VLAs for combined capability and generality. These insights pave the way for future research to further refine online adaptation strategies and create an iterative cycle of improvement for robotic intelligence.

In conclusion, GR-RL pushes the boundaries of what learning-based robotic policies can achieve in complex, real-world scenarios. By meticulously addressing the nuances of human demonstration quality and the training-deployment mismatch, it provides a crucial step toward creating truly capable, reliable, and deployable robotic systems.
