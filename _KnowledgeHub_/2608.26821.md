# TemporalFlow-VLA: Learning Physically Grounded Execution History for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TemporalFlow-VLA adds a parallel temporal module to a pi-0.5 VLA backbone. Robot-surface temporal flow, rendered offline from joint states, URDF geometry, and camera calibration, supervises two chunk-aligned temporal queries that are the action expert's only channel to history. Asynchronous feature caching hides historical encoding. It reaches 97.63% average LIBERO success and 84.2% randomized RoboTwin success, with the largest gains on long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Most VLA policies condition action chunks on the current observation alone, so visually similar states arising from different execution histories become ambiguous in multi-stage manipulation.
- Simply appending historical frames does not yield action-usable representations: a diagnostic shows shuffling three past frames barely changes action loss while removing them raises it 4.6%.
- Motion-prompting and latent-memory methods either need trajectory or flow estimators at test time, or leave latents with no prescribed physical content beyond generic action supervision.

## Method

- ==Kinematics-grounded robot-surface temporal flow==: forward kinematics transports depth-visible robot pixels between frames, projected via calibrated cameras into a 16x16x2 patch-level flow target with coverage masking.
- Two ==hierarchical temporal queries== at t-15 and t-8 offsets, supervised by ==FiLM-modulated== per-interval flow decoders; a directed mask lets Q15 read Q8, and action tokens see only these queries.
- ==Asynchronous historical-feature cache== writes head-camera features into a timestamped ring buffer during chunk execution, so replanning encodes only the current frame instead of three.

## Results

- On RoboTwin 2.0, **85.5%** clean and **84.2%** randomized average success across **12** tasks, exceeding the best published baseline by **8.0** points, with **87.5%** at horizon 3 (**+14.5** over runner-up).
- On LIBERO, **97.63 ± 0.26%** average over three seeds and **96.60 ± 0.87%** on LIBERO Long, **2.1** points above the strongest listed prior result on the long suite.
- Ablations: dropping flow supervision costs **2.3/0.6** points clean/randomized versus the same two-query architecture; real-robot cup stacking rises **57.8%** to **77.8%**; caching cuts sampling latency **7.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Auxiliary supervision can define what a latent must encode; assigning history tokens an explicit physical target beats letting a backbone infer temporal cues from extra frames.
> - Free geometric labels from robot proprioception, URDF, and calibration can train perception modules without object annotations, then be discarded entirely at deployment.
> - Benefits concentrate at long horizons and are near zero on saturated short-horizon suites, so history mechanisms should be validated on multi-stage tasks, not aggregate averages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot control has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to transfer pre-trained vision-language representations to end-to-end robot control, demonstrating capabilities in generalization across various tasks and robot embodiments. Many existing VLA models, however, primarily base their action predictions on the current RGB observation, language instruction, and robot state. This approach presents a challenge in multi-stage manipulation tasks, where visually similar states can require different actions depending on the preceding execution history. For instance, a robot may encounter a visually identical pre-grasp state after a failed grasp attempt, necessitating a different subsequent action. Without awareness of recent temporal evolution, a policy might misidentify the local task phase, overlook prior outcomes, or repeat actions, leading to error accumulation.

Previous attempts to address the incorporation of temporal context generally fall into several categories. One straightforward method involves providing raw historical observations to the VLM backbone. However, this approach has shown limitations, as simply adding more frames does not reliably capture action-usable representations of physical change, and these models can be largely insensitive to the correct temporal order of historical frames. Diagnostic studies indicate that while history can be beneficial, its unstructured inclusion often lacks the specificity required for complex dynamics.

A second line of work focuses on explicit motion representation. Methods such as TraceVLA, MotionVLA, FlowVLA, and HiF-VLA aim to expose historical motion more directly to the VLA, often by estimating image-space trajectories, converting video into trajectory-field tokens, or using optical flow. These approaches typically involve motion estimation or geometric processing at inference time, which can introduce computational overhead or require additional sensing.

A third category involves learning compact latent temporal representations from VLM features, as seen in CronusVLA, HAMLET, MemoryVLA, and ReMem-VLA. These methods aggregate multi-frame motion features or generate memory tokens. While they can improve temporally dependent control, their temporal content is largely shaped by generic VLM features, action supervision, or recurrence, without explicitly prescribing which specific physical change each latent representation should encode. This can lead to representations that are not optimally aligned with continuous physical dynamics or control requirements.

TemporalFlow-VLA positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing methods. It proposes a novel approach that does not rely on merely adding raw historical frames or on inference-time motion estimation. Instead, it introduces physically grounded, robot-surface temporal flow as a *training-only* supervision target. This allows the model to learn compact, structured temporal queries that explicitly encode recent physical changes relevant for control, without incurring the computational cost of geometric processing or flow estimation during deployment. The method aims to provide a compact and physically grounded interface for exploiting ordered execution history, particularly for longer-horizon and multi-stage manipulation tasks where such history is critical for successful progression.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for TemporalFlow-VLA stems from the observed shortcomings of current VLA models in handling multi-stage robot manipulation tasks, particularly those involving long horizons. These tasks frequently present scenarios where the robot encounters visually similar states that, due to differing execution histories, demand distinct future actions. For instance, a robot attempting to pick up an object might reach a pre-grasp pose; if the preceding grasp failed, the required action is to re-attempt the grasp, whereas if the object was successfully grasped and released elsewhere, the same pre-grasp pose would imply a different task phase. Existing VLA models, which often prioritize current visual observations, struggle to reliably infer the correct local task phase, the outcome of prior actions, or to avoid repeating already executed behaviors in such ambiguous situations. Errors arising from this ambiguity can accumulate, hindering task completion.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Develop a mechanism to reliably capture recent physical change:** The authors motivate that simply feeding historical frames to a VLA does not guarantee an action-usable representation of physical change, and that unconstrained baselines are often insensitive to the correct temporal order of history. The objective is to move beyond raw historical observations and learn a more structured and robust representation of how the robot's physical state has evolved.

2. **Introduce physically grounded temporal supervision:** Instead of relying on generic feature learning or explicit motion estimation at inference time, the work aims to use deterministic, physically grounded motion information as a *training-only* supervision signal. Specifically, robot-surface temporal flow, derived from known robot kinematics (joint states, URDF geometry) and camera calibration, is proposed to provide explicit and interpretable guidance for learning historical representations. This bypasses the need for complex object annotations or additional deployment-time sensors.

3. **Learn compact, execution-aligned temporal queries:** The goal is to distill complex historical visual information into a compact, actionable format for the VLA's action expert. The paper proposes two "chunk-aligned" temporal queries (Q8 and Q15) that are supervised to represent robot-surface flow over distinct, relevant intervals (t-8 to t and t-15 to t, corresponding to recent and longer-range history relative to a 16-step action chunk). These queries are intended to serve as the *sole* historical interface to the action expert, ensuring a compact and focused representation.

4. **Integrate history efficiently into the VLA architecture:** A major concern with incorporating historical data is the potential for increased inference latency. The objective is to integrate the learned temporal representations without significantly burdening the real-time inference critical path. This involves designing an asynchronous caching mechanism for historical features to minimize synchronous computational overhead.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of history-agnostic and generically history-aware VLA models by providing a physically grounded, structured, and efficiently integrated representation of execution history, thereby enhancing robot manipulation performance, particularly for longer-horizon and multi-stage tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TemporalFlow-VLA extends a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, specifically the π0.5 model, by introducing a parallel temporal pathway. This pathway is designed to learn and integrate compact, physically grounded execution history while preserving the core action-generation mechanism of the base policy.

**A. Overall Architecture:**
The base policy continues to predict action chunks based on the current RGB observation, language instruction, robot state, and diffusion timestep. The temporal pathway additionally processes head-camera observations from three points in time: t-15, t-8, and t. These specific intervals are chosen to represent the full duration of the previous 16-step action chunk (t-15 to t) and its more recent half (t-8 to t), providing both longer-range and immediate historical context.

**B. Kinematics-Grounded Robot-Surface Temporal Flow (Supervision Target):**
The core of the methodology lies in generating explicit, physically grounded temporal flow as a supervision signal *during training only*. This flow represents the motion of visible robot surfaces in the camera's image plane. The process involves:
1. **Robot Pose and Geometry:** Using recorded robot joint configurations (qτ), the robot's URDF geometry, and calibrated camera parameters, the system determines the 3D position of each visible robot surface pixel (X_B_s(p)) in the robot's base frame at time s (t-ρ).
2. **Link-Frame Transformation:** Each 3D point is transformed into its owning link's local frame (x_l(p)(p)).
3. **Future Projection:** This link-frame point is then transported to time t using the robot's forward kinematics at time t (T_l(p)_B(q_t)) and projected back into the target image plane using the camera intrinsics (Π_K) and base-to-camera transform (T_C←B).
4. **Correspondence and Filtering:** A nearest-pixel lookup in a robot-only rendering at time t establishes correspondence. Only projections that remain within bounds, belong to the same robot link, and have a 3D residual displacement below 5mm are considered valid.
5. **Flow Calculation and Normalization:** For each valid pixel p, the 2D displacement from its source location u_s(p) to its projected target location u_t(p) is calculated (u_t(p) - u_s(p)). This displacement is normalized by the image size (224x224) to obtain the flow vector f_ρ(p).
6. **Grid Aggregation:** These per-pixel flow vectors are aggregated into a 16x16x2 grid (F_ρ(k)) by taking the masked arithmetic mean over non-overlapping 14x14 regions. A mask M_ρ(k) is generated to indicate patches with sufficient valid robot-surface pixel support (at least 10% coverage). The auxiliary loss is only computed for patches where M_ρ(k) = 1.
This entire process generates labels offline, without requiring manual annotation or deployment-time geometric computation.

**C. Hierarchical Temporal Queries and Joint Attention:**
TemporalFlow-VLA introduces two learnable temporal tokens, q8 and q15, corresponding to the Q8 and Q15 queries. These tokens are appended to the standard VLA prefix, which includes current-image tokens (V0), language tokens (L), and current robot state (s). Historical image patches (V15, V8) also receive learned frame-identity embeddings.

1. **Masked Attention Hierarchy:** A directed, query-specific attention mask structures the interaction between these queries and historical information:
 - **Q8:** Can only attend to language tokens (L), the t-8 observation (V8), the current observation (V0), and itself. This specialization allows Q8 to summarize recent motion.
 - **Q15:** Can additionally attend to the t-15 observation (V15) and Q8. This allows Q15 to integrate earlier history building upon Q8's short-range summary, establishing a Q8 → Q15 hierarchical path.
2. **Action Expert Interface:** Crucially, action tokens in the VLA can obtain historical visual information *only* through Q8 and Q15, and are explicitly blocked from directly accessing V8 and V15. This enforces a compact, supervised bottleneck.
3. **Flow Reconstruction Decoders:** Each temporal query (Qρ) is paired with a separate query-conditioned decoder (Dρ). This decoder takes the query's final state (zρ) and the VLM spatial patch states of its source frame (Vρ) to predict the reconstructed flow (F̂ρ). The query (zρ) FiLM-modulates the source spatial states, and a two-layer MLP predicts the flow. The loss (Lρ_flow) is computed using Huber loss, masked by Mρ.
4. **Joint Optimization:** The final objective function combines the original action flow-matching loss (L_action) with the temporal-flow reconstruction loss (L_temp) using a weighting factor (λ_temp), where λ_temp is set to 1.0. This ensures that Q8 and Q15 remain tied to their defined temporal scales and motion semantics throughout training.

**D. Asynchronous Historical-Feature Caching:**
To mitigate the inference overhead of re-encoding historical frames at every replanning step, TemporalFlow-VLA employs an asynchronous caching mechanism:
1. **Background Encoding:** While the robot executes the current action chunk, a background process asynchronously encodes incoming head-camera observations into visual features (Vτ). These features are stored in a timestamped ring buffer.
2. **Cached Retrieval:** At the subsequent replanning step (time t), the required historical features for t-15 (V15) and t-8 (V8) are retrieved directly from this cache.
3. **Synchronous Encoding for Current Frame:** Only the current observation (I_t) needs to be encoded synchronously.
This strategy ensures that the two additional visual forward passes for historical frames do not fall on the critical inference path, reducing the overall synchronous latency for action generation. At the beginning of an episode, if insufficient history is available, the cache is bypassed until the required historical frame tokens are present.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
The methodology was evaluated through extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks and real-robot settings:
- **Simulation Benchmarks:** LIBERO (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long suites) and RoboTwin 2.0 (12 tasks, categorized by manipulation horizon H1-H3).
- **Training Details:** Policies were trained on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs with a batch size of 32 per GPU. Temporal-flow labels were precomputed offline. The temporal module added approximately 20% to wall-clock training time compared to the baseline.
- **Evaluation:** LIBERO evaluations involved three seeds with 500 rollouts per suite and seed. RoboTwin 2.0 tasks used 50 clean and 500 randomized demonstrations per task, with 100 rollouts per task in each setting.
- **Ablation Studies:** Controlled experiments were conducted to assess the impact of history content, temporal order, query bottleneck, flow supervision, and temporal scale on performance.
- **Inference Efficiency:** Measured server-side policy sampling latency on an RTX 4090 using successful LIBERO Long rollouts, comparing cached vs. non-cached historical feature processing.
- **Real-Robot Evaluation:** Tested on an AgiBot A3 robot for two three-stage manipulation tasks (Three-Cup Stacking, Two-Bottle Packing). Each task used 280 demonstrations and policies were evaluated over three rounds of 15 trials (45 trials total per method/task).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings across simulation and real-robot evaluations:

**A. Simulation Benchmark Performance:**

- **RoboTwin 2.0:** TemporalFlow-VLA achieved an average success rate of 85.5% under clean evaluation and 84.2% under randomized evaluation across 12 tasks. In the randomized setting, this represented an 8.0 percentage point improvement over the best reported baseline. The performance advantage was more pronounced on longer-horizon tasks:
 - H=1 (shorter horizon): Average 84.3% success (randomized).
 - H=2 (medium horizon): Average 80.8% success (randomized), outperforming the runner-up average by 5.5 points.
 - H=3 (longer horizon): Average 87.5% success (randomized), outperforming the runner-up average by 14.5 points.
 This horizon-dependent improvement supports the hypothesis that explicit execution history is more valuable for multi-stage manipulation.
- **LIBERO:** The method achieved an average success rate of 97.63 ± 0.26% across the LIBERO suites. Its performance was particularly strong on LIBERO Long, reaching 96.60 ± 0.87%, which was 2.1 percentage points above the strongest listed prior mean result for that suite. This indicates benefits for multi-stage tasks where sequential progress is critical.

**B. Ablation Studies:**

- **History Content and Order (Offline Diagnostic):** Experiments on six RoboTwin tasks investigated the impact of historical perturbations on action flow-matching loss. Both removing historical frames and shuffling their temporal order (swapping t-15 and t-8) increased the loss. Shuffling history was generally more detrimental than removing it on five of the six tasks, suggesting that action prediction is sensitive to both the presence and the correct temporal sequencing of historical content.
- **Architectural Components (Online RoboTwin):**
 - The use of two temporal queries without flow supervision (2Q w/o Flow) outperformed a baseline that used raw multi-frame history (Multi-frame) (83.2%/83.6% vs. 79.8%/81.5% for Clean/Randomized average SR, respectively). This suggests that the compact query bottleneck provides benefits over unstructured history.
 - Adding the kinematics-grounded flow supervision (Ours) further improved performance over "2Q w/o Flow," indicating the value of the explicit physical target. For example, at H=2, it added 3.7 percentage points (Clean) and 1.5 percentage points (Randomized).
 - Comparing single-query variants, Q8 (short-range) alone generally outperformed Q15 (long-range) alone, but the combination of both supervised scales (Ours) achieved the best overall performance, demonstrating complementary benefits from different temporal horizons.

**C. Inference Efficiency:**

- **Asynchronous Caching:** On an RTX 4090, asynchronous historical-feature caching reduced the mean server-side policy sampling latency from 68.10 ms to 62.78 ms per replan on LIBERO Long tasks, representing a saving of 5.32 ms. Over a segment of 15 replans, cumulative sampling time decreased by 7.8% (from 1.021 s to 0.942 s). This confirms that caching effectively reduces the synchronous inference overhead associated with processing historical observations.

**D. Real-Robot Evaluation:**

- **Physical Transfer:** TemporalFlow-VLA demonstrated consistent improvements over the baseline on an AgiBot A3 robot for two three-stage manipulation tasks.
 - **Three-Cup Stacking:** Mean success rate increased from 57.8% to 77.8% (+20.0 percentage points).
 - **Two-Bottle Packing:** Mean success rate increased from 86.7% to 97.8% (+11.1 percentage points).
 The larger gain on Three-Cup Stacking, a task requiring precise sequential placements and alignments, aligns with the simulation findings that the method is particularly beneficial for multi-stage manipulation. These results indicate that the learned temporal representation is robust to real-world variations without requiring geometric inputs at deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TemporalFlow-VLA introduces a method for incorporating execution history into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that addresses recognized limitations of prior approaches. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several points:

1. **Enhanced Performance in Long-Horizon and Multi-Stage Manipulation:** The most evident impact is the demonstrated performance improvement on complex, multi-stage, and long-horizon tasks, particularly in challenging randomized environments. The clear gains on RoboTwin 2.0's H=2 and H=3 tasks, and on LIBERO Long, suggest that explicitly learning and utilizing physically grounded execution history is critical for robust sequential decision-making in robotics. This contributes to the development of more capable and reliable robot manipulation systems that can handle complex, multi-step procedures.

2. **Novel and Efficient Temporal Supervision:** The approach of using kinematics-grounded robot-surface temporal flow as a *training-only* supervision target is a notable contribution. It provides explicit, physically interpretable guidance for learning historical representations without requiring costly real-time motion estimation, object annotations, or additional sensors during deployment. This design makes the method practical and efficient for real-world applications, as the geometric pipeline is absent during inference.

3. **Structured and Compact History Representation:** The design featuring two hierarchically organized, chunk-aligned temporal queries (Q8 and Q15) that serve as the *sole* interface for historical visual information to the action expert is significant. This compact, supervised bottleneck proves more effective than feeding raw historical frames or relying on generic latent memories. It demonstrates that providing structured, semantically defined historical cues, rather than raw data, can lead to more effective action prediction and better understanding of the robot's interaction with its environment.

4. **Improved Inference Efficiency:** The implementation of asynchronous historical-feature caching addresses a common challenge in history-aware policies: increased inference latency. By encoding historical observations in a background process, the method effectively removes this overhead from the critical inference path, maintaining competitive real-time performance. This is crucial for practical robot deployment where low-latency decision-making is often required.

5. **Robustness and Generalizability:** Validation across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0) and successful transfer to a real robot (AgiBot A3) under varying conditions (clean vs. randomized) indicates the robustness and potential for generalizability of TemporalFlow-VLA. This suggests the learned temporal representations are not overly specific to simulation artifacts but capture fundamental aspects of physical interaction relevant in the real world.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for further investigation into the optimal temporal scales and sampling granularities for historical information in VLA models. Systematically exploring these parameters could lead to even more adaptive and effective temporal representation learning, further advancing the state of the art in robot control.

In conclusion, TemporalFlow-VLA provides a principled and effective method for incorporating physically grounded execution history into VLA policies, significantly enhancing their capabilities in complex manipulation tasks while maintaining inference efficiency. Its contributions are relevant for pushing the boundaries of autonomous robotic systems towards more intelligent and adaptable behavior in dynamic environments.
