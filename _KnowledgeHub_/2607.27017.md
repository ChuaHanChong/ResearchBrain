# What Can Latent World Models Know? Physical Parameter Identifiability in Multimodal Predictive Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> This research investigates which physical quantities multimodal predictive representations acquire and the factors governing their internalization, introducing a controlled simulation environment and a certificate-gated protocol. Findings reveal that explicit prediction targets are causal for parameter acquisition, exposing both a "lazy equilibrium" in vision-only models and specific "blind regions" for certain physical parameters, even with sufficient raw sensor data.

## Problem

- Identifying which specific physical quantities are encoded within the latent state of world models trained for future prediction.
- Understanding the mechanisms and factors that determine whether a given physical quantity is acquired by the latent state.
- A lack of systematic investigation linking sensor streams, objective function structures, and data distribution to the identifiability of individual physical parameters under controlled interventions.

## Method

- Developed **POKEWORLD**, a 2D interactive simulation with known, hidden physical parameters (mass, drag, contact stiffness) and multimodal sensor data, where object properties are not visually discernible.
- Implemented a ==certificate-gated protocol== to rigorously certify the recoverability of each hidden parameter from raw observations using supervised probes, ensuring observed limitations are not due to environmental observability.
- Trained a family of ==Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures== (**X-JEPA**) with factorial variations in input modalities (vision-only vs. multimodal) and prediction targets (future latents only vs. additional cross-modal forecasts).

## Results

- Contact stiffness (log k) was acquired by the latent state only when touch was included as a prediction target (R² **0.40-0.57**), while mere input fusion of touch yielded chance-level acquisition (R² **-0.02**).
- Object position decoding improved from R² **0.04** (lazy equilibrium) to R² **0.89** with multi-horizon prediction and R² **0.98** when combined with cross-modal targets.
- Drag (γ) consistently plateaued near R² **0.13** across all tested deterministic prediction objectives, despite achieving a raw observation recoverability certificate of R² **0.70-0.89**.
- Real-robot validation on RH20T confirmed the transferability of these mechanisms, demonstrating that objective structure dictates *what* physical parameters are learned, while data volume primarily affects *how well* they are learned.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prediction targets, not just input fusion, causally determine the acquisition of specific physical parameters (e.g., stiffness requires touch as a prediction target) by the latent representation.
> - Vision-only models can exhibit a "lazy equilibrium" where predictable object states are ignored, which can be overcome by multi-horizon prediction or the inclusion of cross-modal targets.
> - A "blind region" exists for slow, ratio-type physical parameters like drag, which deterministic prediction objectives struggle to acquire, even when highly recoverable from raw sensor data, suggesting a conditional-mean collapse mechanism.
> - The strength of anti-collapse regularization (SIGReg) directly influences the metric precision of physical readouts in the latent space, with lighter regularization improving precision for low-dimensional physical parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of latent world models, particularly Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). These models, including I-JEPA, V-JEPA, LeJEPA, and LeWorldModel, are premised on the idea that training a representation to predict future states compels it to internalize the underlying physics of its environment. Such models have demonstrated capabilities in visual understanding, prediction, and planning.

Despite the promise of these models, a fundamental question remains regarding the specific physical quantities represented within their latent states and the mechanisms determining their acquisition. Prior work has often focused on post-hoc evaluation of trained models or demonstrated the necessity of modalities like touch for specific forms of evidence. However, a systematic investigation linking sensor streams, objective function structures, and data distribution to the identifiability of individual physical parameters under controlled interventions has been largely absent.

This paper addresses this gap by charting an "identifiability map." It builds upon existing literature concerning multimodal representations, which highlights the role of touch in providing physical evidence beyond vision (e.g., Sparsh, TacGen, Touch-World, Kepler-Encoder, HPT). Crucially, this work distinguishes the causal roles of input fusion versus explicit prediction targets, a distinction often obscured in studies focused on static encoders. The methodology incorporates principles from classical system identification regarding parameter identifiability and input excitation, adapting them to the context of learned latent representations. The findings on "lazy equilibrium" relate to the concept of shortcut learning in deep neural networks, where models achieve satisfactory performance by ignoring task-relevant underlying structure. Theoretically, the paper provides empirical phenomena (lazy equilibrium, blind region, scale-flat degeneracy) that can inform and test existing theories of JEPA objectives and regularization, such as their characterization as low-rank factorization of co-occurrences or active-inference free energy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to answer two questions:
1. Which specific physical quantities are encoded within the latent state of a world model trained for future prediction?
2. What factors determine whether a given physical quantity is acquired by this latent state?

The motivation for this investigation is multifaceted:
- **Practical Relevance**: In domains such as robotics, particularly in contact-rich manipulation tasks, an agent's ability to estimate physical parameters like contact stiffness is critical for robust performance. A model that fails to internalize these properties will be limited in its practical application.
- **Theoretical Understanding**: From a theoretical standpoint, understanding the content of latent representations is essential. The authors hypothesize that prediction objectives will acquire only those parameters that are directly required by their designated prediction targets. This work aims to establish empirical evidence for such causal relationships.
- **Methodological Innovation**: To enable a rigorous investigation, the authors sought to develop a controlled experimental framework. This involves creating an environment where hidden physical parameters are known by design, and implementing a "certificate-gated protocol." This protocol ensures that any failure of a model to acquire a parameter can be attributed to the model's objective or architecture, rather than limitations in the environment's observability or the sensing instrumentation. This approach differentiates between what is *recoverable* from raw observations and what is *acquired* by the latent representation.
- **Systematic Exploration**: The study deliberately selects three physical parameters—mass (m), drag (γ), and contact stiffness (k)—that span a spectrum of observability, from primarily visual to primarily tactile-dependent. This design choice aims to provide diagnostic insights into how learning dynamics influence parameter acquisition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a controlled experimental design involving a custom-built simulation environment, a specific model architecture family, and a certificate-gated evaluation protocol.

**Environment: POKEWORLD**
- **Design**: POKEWORLD is a 2D interactive simulation where a force-controlled circular finger interacts with a circular object.
- **Hidden Parameters**: For each episode, the object's mass (m, [0.5, 3.0]), drag coefficient (γ, [0.5, 4.0]), and contact stiffness (k,) are resampled. Crucially, objects are rendered identically across episodes, meaning their physical properties are not visually discernible.
- **Observability Spectrum**: The parameters were chosen to represent different observability profiles: drag is primarily visual (affecting glide decay), mass is cross-modal (impulse vs. velocity change), and stiffness is almost purely tactile (requiring sub-step impact transients).
- **Dynamics**: The simulation uses semi-implicit Euler dynamics with Hertzian-style penalty contacts, featuring underdamped oscillations (ζ=0.25) to preserve stiffness-relevant impact transients in tactile data.
- **Observations**: The model receives three types of sensor data:
 - **Vision**: 64x64 grayscale images, augmented with a temporal-difference channel.
 - **Proprioception**: 4-dimensional data (finger position and velocity).
 - **Touch**: 7-dimensional data capturing sub-step statistics of reaction forces on the finger, including mean force (2D), peak magnitude, end-of-step force (2D), contact duration fraction, and a contact flag. This high-frequency tactile data is critical for stiffness detection.
- **Behavior Policies**: A mix of pursuit, strike-retreat cycles, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift, and random launches ensures that diverse interactions (contacts, impacts, glides) occur, providing sufficient data for identifying the different parameters.

**Certificate-Gated Protocol**
- **Purpose**: To ensure that any observed limitation in a model's latent representation is not due to the environment or sensor data lacking the necessary information.
- **Mechanism**: Before evaluating any world model, each hidden parameter's recoverability from *raw observations* is certified. This is achieved using a supervised recurrent probe (a 2-layer GRU with 96 hidden units) trained on 16-step windows of raw observations (vision, proprioception, touch). A physics-informed estimator is also used for drag.
- **Thresholds**: A parameter must achieve a minimum R² of 0.4 for mass, 0.4 for drag, and 0.25 for stiffness on contact windows to be considered "recoverable."
- **Separation of Concerns**: This protocol enables distinguishing between environment/instrument limitations and representation/objective limitations.

**Model Architecture: X-JEPA Family**
- **Foundation**: The models are based on the stable LeJEPA/LeWM recipe, which uses the Sliced Isotropic-Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg) to prevent collapse.
- **Components**:
 - **Encoders**: Per-frame encoders (4-stage CNN for vision; small MLPs for proprioception and touch) process raw inputs.
 - **Predictor**: A 4-layer causal transformer (192 width, 8 heads) processes encoded states and is conditioned on actions.
 - **Prediction Heads**: Separate heads predict future latent embeddings at multiple horizons (∆ ∈ {1, 4, 16}).
 - **Latent Dimension**: 128.
- **Factorial Variants**: Models are trained by factorially varying:
 - **Input Modalities**: Vision-only (V) versus all modalities (vision, proprioception, touch; VFX, VF).
 - **Prediction Targets**: Predicting only future latent embeddings (V, VF) versus adding specific cross-modal forecasts like touch (VXt), proprioception (VXp), or both (VX, VFX).

**Measurement and Evaluation**
- **Probing**: After training, linear (ridge regression) and nonlinear (early-stopped MLP) probes are applied to the *frozen predictor state* to assess what information is decodable from the latent.
- **Metrics**: Evaluation includes R² for hidden parameters (m, k, γ), object position and velocity. On the real-robot dataset, metrics include open-loop rollout error, direct-∆ prediction error, force readout, contact-onset anticipation (AUC), and performance on held-out tasks.
- **Data Splits**: All probe evaluations use episode-split data, ensuring probes are tested on episodes disjoint from those used for training the model and fitting the probe.
- **Functional Tests**: For certain parameters like drag, functional tests assess whether the model's *own predictions* effectively utilize the parameter.

**Real-Robot Validation**
- **Dataset**: RH20T (KUKA and Flexiv arms, 10 Hz sampling) is used, lacking ground truth for hidden parameters.
- **Focus**: Validation focuses on whether the identified mechanisms (lazy equilibrium, target pressure, fusion, scaling behavior) transfer to real multimodal sensor streams and influence observable outcomes (e.g., end-effector position, force prediction, contact anticipation).
- **Scaling Study**: A four-arm factorial (inputs × targets) investigates the effect of data volume (800–4,258 episodes) on learning and generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded an "identifiability map" characterized by four primary findings and validated through real-robot experiments.

**1. Target Pressure is Causal and Modality-Specific (§4.1)**
- **Stiffness Acquisition**: Contact stiffness (log k) was acquired by the latent only when touch was included as a *prediction target*. Variants forecasting touch (VXt, VX, VFX) achieved R² values ranging from 0.40 to 0.57 for stiffness. In contrast, simply fusing the full tactile stream into the input without explicit touch forecasting (VF) resulted in chance-level R² values for stiffness (e.g., -0.02). This indicates that passive input fusion alone does not ensure parameter acquisition.
- **Modality Specificity**: The effect was not due to generic auxiliary prediction pressure; adding an equally sized proprioception-forecasting target (VXp) did not improve stiffness acquisition (R² -0.01). The parameter's dependence on the target content was critical.
- **Target Composition**: Different targets could combine to yield more comprehensive representations. While individual targets yielded limited object localization (position R² 0.17 for touch target, 0.09 for proprioception target), their combination (VX) significantly improved object position decoding to R² 0.58.
- **Conclusion**: Inputs define the potential information, but prediction targets dictate what information is retained and encoded in the latent state.

**2. The Lazy Equilibrium and Its Two Escapes (§4.2)**
- **Lazy Equilibrium**: Under single-step prediction, a vision-only latent world model (V) exhibited a "lazy equilibrium" where it largely discarded even directly visible and predictable object state (object position R² 0.04). This occurred because a static object contributed minimal prediction loss, leading the encoder to ignore it.
- **Escape Mechanisms**: Two independent mechanisms were found to break this degeneracy:
 - **Cross-modal targets**: Incorporating cross-modal prediction targets (e.g., touch or proprioception) restored object position decoding to R² 0.58.
 - **Multi-horizon prediction**: Using action-conditioned prediction heads at multiple horizons (∆ ∈ {1, 4, 16}) also restored object position to R² 0.89, even without additional modalities. The longer horizon forced the model to account for larger, action-dependent positional effects.
 - **Composition**: Combining both mechanisms (VFX) resulted in near-perfect object position decoding (R² 0.98).
- **Long-Horizon Prediction**: Direct long-horizon prediction (e.g., ∆=16) was found to be more accurate than autoregressive composition of short steps (0.10 arena units error vs. 0.19).

**3. The Anti-Collapse Regularizer Sets Metric Precision (§4.3)**
- **SIGReg Effect**: The study demonstrated that the weight (λ) of the Sliced Isotropic-Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg), designed to prevent latent collapse, directly influenced the metric precision of physical readouts.
- **Dose-Response**: Sweeping λ across a 60x range revealed a monotonic dose-response curve: lightening the regularization (decreasing λ) consistently sharpened every physical readout (e.g., stiffness R² increased from 0.12 to 0.57) and halved long-horizon prediction error.
- **Tuning Rule**: The practical implication is that for data with low intrinsic dimensions (like physical parameters), a significantly lighter regularization strength than default settings for image-scale data may be required to achieve optimal metric precision in the latent space.

**4. The Frontier: A Certificate-Gated Blind Region (§4.5)**
- **Drag Identifiability Challenge**: Drag (γ) represented a "frontier" where the tested prediction objectives struggled. Despite carrying a high recoverability certificate from raw observations (R² 0.89 by a physics-informed estimator, 0.70 by recurrent probe), γ consistently plateaued near R² 0.13 under all deterministic prediction objectives tested, across various probe families and functional tests.
- **Objective-Specific Failure**: This failure was attributed to the objective structure rather than environmental or probing limitations. A supervised system-identification head on the *same model trunk* could decode γ at R² 0.45, indicating the underlying information was available in the trunk's representation. A pixel-reconstruction objective variant also showed similar blindness (R² 0.03–0.08).
- **Attribute Matrix**: An attribute matrix characterized parameters based on "fast" versus "slow" effects on observations, and "ratio-type" versus "linear-trace" readout.
 - **Fast Parameters (Stiffness)**: Acquired effectively (0.6–0.7 of its certificate) even without a linear observational trace, suggesting the objective can learn complex computations when effects are rapid.
 - **Slow Parameters with Linear Trace (Additive Force Bias)**: Acquired only to the extent of their *linear observational trace*, tracking the linear recoverability certificate rather than the full recurrent certificate.
 - **Slow, Ratio-Type Parameters (Drag, Actuation Gain)**: These parameters, having no linear observational trace and slow effects on observations, were not acquired at all by the deterministic prediction objectives.
- **Conditional-Mean Collapse Hypothesis**: This blind region is consistent with a "conditional-mean collapse" mechanism, where under squared-error point prediction, the optimal strategy for parameters not strongly constrained by a short history is to predict based on their population mean, thus removing pressure to encode episode-specific values.
- **Coordinate-Level Validation**: The hypothesis was supported by a coordinate transformation: providing a log-speed input channel, which linearizes drag's effect, raised drag's linear certificate to 0.52 and its readout to 0.33, demonstrating that making its trace linear allowed for partial acquisition.

**Real-Robot Validation at Scale (§5)**
- **Mechanism Transfer**: The core mechanisms observed in POKEWORLD transferred to real-robot data (RH20T dataset).
 - **Lazy Equilibrium**: Replicated, with vision-only models losing end-effector state and performing worse than a static baseline in prediction.
 - **Target Pressure**: Cross-modal targets improved dynamics prediction (up to 29% reduction in rollout error) and contact anticipation (AUC 0.70 → 0.80).
 - **Force Acquisition**: Force was retained and accurately forecast beyond a persistence baseline only when touch data was both *fused into the input* and *forecast as a target*.
- **Viewpoint as Selector**: Camera viewpoint acted as an observable selector; a distant view captured pose but not contact details, while a close-up captured contact but not global pose. A two-view model effectively combined this information.
- **Scaling Factorial**: A four-arm factorial experiment (varying inputs and targets) across a 5x data range (800 to 4,258 episodes) showed that:
 - Arms missing either sufficient input information or appropriate prediction pressure remained flat in performance metrics, regardless of increased data volume. For instance, a fused-input arm without cross-modal targets discarded force information it was given.
 - Only the full multimodal objective (VFX) successfully converted additional data scale into gains on held-out tasks, such as improved future-force forecasting (R² 0.39 → 0.51).
- **Conclusion**: Objective structure fundamentally determines *what* physical parameters a latent representation can learn, while data volume primarily influences *how well* those parameters are learned, but cannot compensate for structural deficiencies.
- **Cross-Embodiment**: A single model jointly trained on data from two different robots performed comparably to specialists and improved force retention for the data-poorer robot, suggesting that pooled, diverse experience can stabilize retention.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the understanding of latent world models and their ability to internalize environmental physics, offering both fundamental insights and practical design principles.

- **Reusable Methodology**: The introduction of POKEWORLD and the certificate-gated identifiability protocol provides a standardized, controlled methodology for empirically determining "what a model learned." This protocol offers a robust way to disentangle limitations stemming from the environment's observability from those arising from the learning objective or model architecture, paving the way for more rigorous research in this area.
- **Comprehensive Identifiability Map**: The "four-factor identifiability map" systematically characterizes the mechanisms governing physical parameter acquisition in latent world models. This includes:
 - Demonstrating the causal role of **prediction targets** over mere input fusion.
 - Identifying and providing solutions to the **lazy equilibrium** in vision-only models.
 - Quantifying the role of **anti-collapse regularization** in setting metric precision.
- **Identification of the "Frontier" (Blind Region)**: The discovery and characterization of the "blind region" for slow, ratio-type parameters like drag is a key contribution. By using the certificate-gated protocol and an attribute matrix, the authors precisely define the types of parameters that current deterministic prediction objectives struggle to acquire. This identifies a concrete target for the development of future objectives (e.g., those not based on conditional mean prediction, like belief states or episode-level latent variables) that are crucial for capturing properties such as viscosity and friction, which are essential for advanced contact-rich robotics.
- **Real-Robot Validation and Design Rules**: The extensive real-robot validation on RH20T confirms the transferability of the identified mechanisms from simulation to real-world scenarios. The scaling factorial experiment definitively shows that **objective structure dictates what can be learned, while data volume only influences how well it is learned**. This leads to a set of distilled design rules for training effective multimodal predictive representations, emphasizing the importance of:
 1. Making every modality a prediction target.
 2. Sensing at the physics' intrinsic bandwidth.
 3. Asking for the future directly at multiple horizons.
 4. Tuning anti-collapse regularization to the data's intrinsic dimension.
 5. Prioritizing objective structure over mere data scale.

The findings have direct implications for robotics, highlighting how to design world models that can acquire the specific physical understanding necessary for complex manipulation tasks, particularly those involving intricate contact dynamics. By providing a clear roadmap of what current models can and cannot learn, this research guides future efforts towards developing more capable and physically intelligent robotic systems.
