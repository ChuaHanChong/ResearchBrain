# MARC: Memory-Augmented RL Token Compression for Efficient Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> MARC introduces a "retrieve then compress" framework for visual language models, leveraging a visual memory retriever and reinforcement learning to achieve a 95% reduction in visual tokens for video understanding tasks. This method maintains performance comparable to models processing 64 times more frames while reducing GPU memory usage by 72.4% and overall model generation latency by 15.9%.

## Problem

- Visual Language Models (VLMs) encounter substantial computational overhead for video processing due to high frame rates and long durations, leading to increased inference costs, latency, and GPU memory consumption.
- Existing training-free token compression methods for video often result in considerable information loss, causing a noticeable degradation in VLM performance.
- The high computational demands of VLMs hinder their practical deployment in real-world, resource-constrained, and latency-sensitive applications like real-time surveillance or autonomous driving.

## Method

- A ==Visual Memory Retriever== (VMR) first segments video into semantically coherent, event-based fragments and retrieves the most query-relevant ones, reducing redundant information before compression.
- A memory-aware temporal compression layer applies iterative merging within and across selected segments, prioritizing intra-segment coherence to reduce tokens.
- Compression Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO), an RL-based distillation strategy, trains a low-token "student" model to match the reasoning quality of a high-token "teacher" model, with correctness-gated rewards for performance retention.

## Results

- MARC achieved a mean performance of 42.20 across six diverse benchmarks, nearly identical to the 42.21 mean accuracy of the 64-frame Qwen2.5-VL-**3B** baseline, while reducing visual tokens by **95%**.
- The method demonstrated a **72.4%** reduction in GPU peak memory usage (from **41.63 GB** to **11.48 GB**) and a **15.9%** reduction in overall model generation latency.
- MARC substantially outperformed existing training-free compression strategies, showing mean accuracy improvements up to **65%** over baselines like DynamicVLM and **31%** over MovieChat.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-selecting task-relevant, event-level video segments through a VMR is highly effective in focusing downstream processing and mitigating information loss during subsequent compression.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically the C-GRPO framework, can successfully distill complex reasoning capabilities from uncompressed teacher models to aggressively compressed student models, enabling robust performance under extreme token reduction.
> - Aggressive token compression, reducing video input to a single-frame token count, can maintain VLM performance levels comparable to models processing significantly more frames, challenging the necessity of full-context processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has extended their capabilities into multimodal domains, giving rise to visual language models (VLMs) that can process both text and visual information. While VLMs have demonstrated proficiency with static image tasks, their application to video data introduces substantial computational challenges. High frame rates and long video durations lead to a significant increase in the number of visual tokens, resulting in high inference costs, increased latency, and considerable GPU memory consumption. These issues impede the practical deployment of VLMs in scenarios requiring rapid responses and operating under computational resource constraints, such as real-time video question answering, surveillance, and autonomous driving.

Current approaches to mitigate these computational burdens primarily involve token compression techniques. Existing compression methods are often training-free, relying on strategies like token merging in either the spatial or temporal dimension. While these methods can reduce computational overhead, their inherent lack of training can lead to information loss during compression, which in turn causes a noticeable degradation in model performance. This trade-off between efficiency and accuracy remains a significant challenge.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a trained compression framework that aims to overcome the limitations of training-free methods. It integrates structured video retrieval with a reinforcement learning (RL)-based distillation process. This approach seeks to maintain performance levels comparable to uncompressed, full-context models while drastically reducing the computational footprint. The paper situates itself within the broader context of Video Retrieval Augmented Generation (Video-RAG), specifically adopting a video corpus retrieval scheme to efficiently identify and process relevant video segments. By developing a method that learns an optimal compression strategy rather than applying heuristic-based reductions, the research aims to bridge the gap between aggressive compression and robust VLM performance, thereby advancing the scalability and practical utility of VLMs for video understanding tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the substantial computational overhead encountered by visual language models (VLMs) when scaled from image processing to video understanding. This overhead, characterized by sharp increases in inference cost, latency, and GPU memory usage due to high frame rates and long video durations, severely limits the deployment of VLMs in real-world, resource-constrained, and latency-sensitive applications.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Mitigate Computational Overhead:** The core motivation is to significantly reduce the computational burden associated with processing extensive video data. Existing VLMs generate a large number of visual tokens, leading to high memory consumption and slow inference times, which are impractical for applications like autonomous driving or real-time surveillance. The goal is to enable efficient video understanding without requiring extensive computational resources.
- **Overcome Limitations of Training-Free Compression:** Current token compression methods are predominantly training-free, often relying on simple token merging heuristics. While these methods offer some efficiency gains, they frequently result in a considerable loss of critical information, leading to a notable decline in model performance. The researchers are motivated to develop a trained compression strategy that can intelligently select and retain essential information, thereby minimizing performance degradation.
- **Maintain Performance Under Aggressive Compression:** A key objective is to achieve extreme compression ratios, specifically reducing video input to a token count equivalent to that of a single image frame, while preserving the reasoning capabilities and accuracy of models that process substantially more frames. This involves distilling the knowledge and reasoning ability from a high-token "teacher" network to a low-token "student" network, ensuring that the student can perform comparably despite aggressive input reduction.
- **Enhance Information Relevance through Retrieval:** Motivated by principles from cognitive science and neuroimaging, the researchers observed that human visual memory is temporally organized and context-aware, segmenting continuous experiences into discrete events. This inspired the development of a mechanism to first identify and retrieve only the most query-relevant event-level segments from a video. This "retrieve then compress" strategy aims to reduce the amount of redundant information processed, thereby improving the efficiency and quality of subsequent compression.
- **Enable Deployment in Resource-Constrained Environments:** The ultimate motivation is to provide a robust and practical solution that allows large-scale VLMs to be deployed in real-world scenarios with strict latency and memory constraints. By drastically reducing GPU memory usage and generation latency, the framework aims to unlock new applications for VLMs in areas where current computational demands render them impractical.
- **Introduce a Novel RL-based Distillation Framework:** The work is motivated by the need for a sophisticated compression algorithm that goes beyond simple heuristics. By proposing Compression Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO), the aim is to leverage reinforcement learning to learn an optimal compression policy that explicitly aligns the performance of the compressed student model with that of an uncompressed teacher, directly addressing the performance retention challenge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, MARC (Memory-Augmented Reinforcement Learning-based Token Compression), is a "retrieve then compress" framework designed for efficient video understanding. It integrates a Visual Memory Retriever (VMR) with a novel reinforcement learning-based distillation strategy called Compression Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO).

#### 3.1. Visual Memory Retriever (VMR)

The VMR's primary function is to prioritize the retrieval of the most task-relevant video segments before any compression takes place. This strategy aims to reduce the computational load and mitigate the negative effects of redundant information.

- **Event-Based Video Segmentation:** Instead of employing fixed-length temporal windows, VMR utilizes an event-based video segmentation module. This module, based on a deep event detection network, analyzes the video stream to identify significant temporal boundaries, such as scene changes or shifts in action. Each identified segment, termed a visual memory fragment, is designed to encapsulate a complete and semantically coherent event, preserving contextual integrity and reducing the search space for subsequent retrieval.
- **Memory Retrieval:** Both the inferred query representation and the visual memory fragments are mapped into a shared, high-dimensional latent space using an embedding model trained with a contrastive learning framework. This ensures that semantically similar query-fragment pairs are spatially proximate in the latent space. An optimized nearest-neighbor search algorithm is then applied to the pre-indexed fragment embeddings to efficiently filter and retrieve the top-k most relevant visual memory fragments. These selected fragments are subsequently rearranged and sampled, functioning as structured "episodic memories" for downstream reasoning.

#### 3.2. RL-Based Video Token Compression

Building upon the VMR-retrieved segments, this component first applies a memory-aware temporal compression layer, followed by C-GRPO to sustain performance under extreme token compression.

- **Memory-Aware Temporal Compression Layer:** This layer is specifically tailored to the structured nature of the retrieved segments. It operates in two stages:
 - **Intra-Segment Coherence Preservation:** Within each retrieved segment, an iterative merging process is applied. The two most similar consecutive frame embeddings (similarity measured by averaged patch-aligned cosine scores) are merged into a single mean representation. This process repeats until the number of frames in a segment reaches a predefined budget, calculated as a fraction of the original segment length based on a compression ratio `ρ`.
 - **Cross-Segment Consolidation:** If the total number of frames after intra-segment merging still exceeds the overall target frame budget, a lightweight global consolidation (using the same averaging rule) is performed across the compressed segments. This ensures that local event structures are preserved primarily, with global pruning serving as a secondary measure. The output is a temporally compressed, memory-aware token sequence aligned with the VMR-selected evidence.

- **Compression Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO):** The compression process is framed as a distillation problem where a "full-frame teacher" model (e.g., operating on 64 frames) provides reference behavior, and a "single-frame student" model learns to match the teacher's reasoning quality under a drastically reduced token budget.
 - **Retention Alignment Reward:** C-GRPO introduces a specific retention alignment reward (`r_c`) to explicitly encourage the student's output to match the teacher's performance. This reward is calculated as `r_c = α · max(0, η - τ)`, where `η` is the retention ratio (student's average reward divided by teacher's average reward) and `τ` is a minimum acceptable retention threshold (empirically set to 0.6). The scaling factor `α` adjusts the incentive strength.
 - **Correctness Gating:** To prevent the model from learning to confidently produce incorrect answers for the sake of higher `η`, the `r_c` reward is gated by correctness. A positive `r_c` is only awarded if the student's generation is semantically valid (i.e., correct). The total reward `R_i` for a given instance is `r_i + 1[correct] r_c`, where `r_i` is the base task reward.
 - **Optimization:** Advantages are normalized within each group to reduce variance. The policy is optimized using a clipped objective function with a KL anchor to a reference policy, similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), but adapted for group relative policy optimization with the added compression-specific reward.

#### 3.3. Experiment Settings

Experiments were conducted on six video benchmarks: VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU (multiple-choice), MVBench, TempCompass, and VideoMME. The baseline model was Qwen2.5-VL-3B, processing videos uniformly sampled at 1 frame per second (fps) up to a maximum of 64 frames. MARC compressed inputs to a single frame's worth of tokens. Comparisons were also made against Qwen2.5-VL-3B with 16 frames, other small-scale vLLMs (InternVL3.5-2B/4B, Gemma-3-4B), and training-free compression methods (ByteVideoLLM, MovieChat, VidCom). A 5K subset of the Video-R1-260K dataset, containing both video and image samples, was used for C-GRPO training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated MARC's effectiveness in achieving significant token compression while maintaining competitive performance and improving efficiency.

- **Performance Comparison:**
 - MARC achieved a mean performance of 42.20 across six diverse benchmarks, which is nearly identical to the 42.21 mean accuracy of the 64-frame Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline. This was accomplished using only one frame's worth of tokens as input, representing a 95% reduction in visual tokens (from an average of 2589.93 to 122.69 tokens per sample).
 - On specific benchmarks, MARC surpassed the 64-frame Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline, particularly on MMVU, MVBench, and TempCompass. The improvement on TempCompass was attributed to enhanced instruction-following from the training process.
 - The average score of MARC-3B also exceeded that of larger models like InternVL3.5-4B (42.00) and Gemma-3-4B (38.87).
 - For long videos in the VideoMME benchmark, MARC retained 74% of the baseline performance while processing only 3.21% of the original visual tokens.
 - MARC substantially outperformed existing training-free compression strategies, achieving mean accuracy improvements of 65% over DynamicVLM, 31% over MovieChat, and 52% over VidCom. MovieChat, which also uses short-term memory compression, showed competitive results on VSI-Bench but lagged on other benchmarks.

- **Efficiency Comparison:**
 - MARC demonstrated a 72.4% reduction in GPU peak memory usage, decreasing from 41.63 GB (for the 64-frame baseline with a batch size of 15) to 11.48 GB.
 - The method led to a 23.9% reduction in LLM generation latency and a 15.9% reduction in overall model generation latency.
 - The end-to-end latency for processing a single MMVU sample was reduced by 11.1%.
 - Additional analysis (Appendix D.2) indicated that latency improvements become more pronounced with larger initial token counts, with a 58.3% speedup observed when reducing from 64,512 tokens (512 frames) to 1,008 tokens.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Visual Memory Retriever (VMR) Effectiveness:** Using VMR alone with the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model (without additional training or compression) boosted mean accuracy from 42.21 to 45.56. This improvement, particularly noticeable on MVBench (a 27.85% increase), was attributed to VMR's ability to select and focus on the most important video clips rather than relying on uniform sampling.
 - **Comparison to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** MARC-3B (integrating VMR and C-GRPO) achieved a mean score of 42.20, outperforming a standard SFT baseline (38.50) and an SFT+VMR variant (40.16). This indicates that the RL-based distillation via C-GRPO is crucial for performance retention under aggressive compression.
 - **Effect of `τ` in C-GRPO:** Experiments with varying `τ` values (minimum acceptable retention ratio) showed that `τ = 0.6` yielded optimal overall performance (mean score 42.20). A lower `τ` (0.4) was too lenient, providing insufficient incentive, while a higher `τ` (0.8) was overly strict, limiting learning signals and slightly reducing performance.
 - **Adjustable Compression:** When MARC was configured for 8-frame equivalent compression on VideoMME, performance improved to 45.33, compared to 39.44 for single-frame compression. This demonstrates a controllable trade-off between token budget and performance, indicating that the framework can adapt to different efficiency-accuracy requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MARC framework addresses a critical challenge in the deployment of visual language models (VLMs), specifically the high computational demands associated with processing video data. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Scalable VLM Deployment:** By achieving a 95% reduction in visual tokens while maintaining performance comparable to models processing significantly more data, MARC significantly lowers the barrier for deploying VLMs in real-world applications. This allows VLMs to operate efficiently within environments characterized by limited computational resources and strict latency requirements.
- **Advancement in Efficient Video Understanding:** The "retrieve then compress" paradigm, inspired by cognitive science, represents a novel approach to handle the inherent redundancy in video data. By first identifying and retrieving semantically relevant event-level segments, the framework ensures that downstream compression operates on a more focused and informative subset of data. This approach has implications for designing more intelligent and context-aware video processing systems.
- **Introduction of a Robust RL-based Distillation Strategy:** The Compression Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO) is a notable contribution as the first post-training reinforcement learning strategy specifically designed for video token compression. It provides a principled method for distilling complex reasoning abilities from a high-resource teacher to a low-resource student, allowing for aggressive compression without sacrificing critical capabilities. This methodology can be extended to other domains requiring efficient knowledge transfer under resource constraints.
- **Practical Applications in Resource-Constrained Domains:** The demonstrated reductions in GPU memory usage (72%) and generation latency (23.9%) have direct and substantial practical implications. This efficiency makes VLMs viable for applications such as:
 - **Real-time video question answering:** Enabling quicker responses to queries about live or streaming video feeds.
 - **Surveillance systems:** Processing continuous video streams with less hardware, making intelligent monitoring more feasible.
 - **Autonomous driving:** Reducing the processing load on onboard systems, which are critical for real-time decision-making and safety.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Compression:** The ablation studies on adjustable compression ratios indicate that the framework can be adapted to different operational needs, balancing efficiency and accuracy. This potential for dynamic and adaptive token allocation based on video content or task requirements opens avenues for future research in creating even more intelligent and flexible video understanding systems.
- **Benchmark for Future Compression Research:** MARC sets a new performance and efficiency benchmark for video token compression, demonstrating that high compression ratios do not necessarily equate to significant performance degradation when a trained, intelligent compression strategy is employed. This encourages further exploration into RL-based and knowledge distillation techniques for optimizing multimodal model efficiency.
