# MRAC Track 1: 2nd Workshop on Multimodal, Generative and Responsible Affective Computing

> [!summary] Summary
> Overview of the 2nd MRAC workshop at ACM-MM 2024, scoping responsible, multimodal, generative affective computing across privacy, bias, and deepfake concerns. Introduces four accepted papers spanning linear-attention affect prediction, macro/micro-expression spotting, hierarchical emotion-aware fake detection, and window-based temporal deepfake localization, plus keynote talks and future-direction topics.

## Problem

- Emotion AI research on multimodal human data rarely addresses privacy, consent, and bias jointly, leaving ethical gaps unresolved as systems scale from lab to real-world deployment.
- Generative technology enables realistic synthetic data and deepfakes, but responsible-use safeguards against misuse in affective computing pipelines remain underdeveloped.
- Deepfake detection work mostly classifies whole videos as real or fake, with little research on fine-grained temporal or segment-level forgery localization across audio-visual modalities.

## Method

- Workshop scope defines sixteen topics spanning ==generative AI== for affect, ==privacy-preserving recognition==, bias in multicultural datasets, and ==few-shot== Emotion AI learning strategies.
- **THE-FD** introduces a ==task-hierarchical architecture== with an ==emotional feature space==, ==fake-frame heatmap module==, and ==multiscale pyramid transformer== for segment-level forgery localization.
- **W-TDL** combines a ==window-based audio temporal deepfake localization== method with an ==EVA visual transformer== frame-based model to jointly localize manipulated audio-visual segments.

## Results

- The linear-attention multimodal Transformer achieves competitive affect-prediction performance on the K-EmoCon dataset while improving memory efficiency over standard self-attention baselines.
- The expression-sensitivity spotting algorithm surpasses prior macro- and micro-expression spotting methods under MEGC 2020 and MEGC 2021 evaluation protocols, balancing precision and recall.
- THE-FD ranks among top-performing methods and W-TDL achieves competitive results in the 2024 1M-Deepfakes Detection Challenge on the AV-Deepfake1M dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining recognition and detection objectives, as in expression spotting, can repurpose narrow-task datasets into broader benchmarks without new data collection.
> - Emotion-aware feature spaces exploiting sentiment perturbation act as a useful signal for detecting subtle manipulations, generalizing beyond pixel-level forgery cues.
> - Responsible affective computing requires treating privacy, bias, and transparency as first-class design constraints, not post-hoc additions to model development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented by the MRAC 2024 workshop positions itself at the intersection of three significant and evolving areas within artificial intelligence: Affective Computing, Generative AI, and Responsible AI.

Affective Computing (AC) is a multidisciplinary field focused on enabling AI systems to recognize, interpret, and respond to human emotions. Historically, this field has relied on the collection and analysis of vast amounts of multimodal data, including RGB images, video, audio, text, and physiological signals. The primary goal of AC is to enhance human-computer interaction by making machines more emotionally intelligent and empathetic, thereby augmenting human abilities rather than replacing them.

In parallel, the rapid advancements in generative technology have introduced new capabilities and complexities to AI. Generative AI can create novel data or multimodal digital content, such as realistic avatars for human-like interaction or synthetic data for training emotion-AI models. This technological progress presents opportunities for overcoming data scarcity but also introduces challenges related to the authenticity and potential misuse of generated content, such as deepfakes.

The integration of AC and Generative AI, while promising, necessitates a strong emphasis on Responsible AI. The development and deployment of emotionally intelligent AI systems raise critical ethical considerations, including data privacy and consent, bias and fairness, and transparency and interpretability. Training emotionally intelligent models on large-scale human data, deploying them in various applications, and ensuring their beneficial impact on users without infringing on rights or well-being are ongoing challenges. The research landscape is increasingly concerned with how to transition responsible AI principles, traditionally developed in controlled laboratory settings, to large-scale, real-world applications.

The MRAC workshop series, now in its second iteration, addresses these combined challenges by specifically focusing on the multimodal, generative, and responsible aspects of Affective Computing. It aims to be a platform that comprehensively examines the full spectrum of these integrated fields, distinguishing itself by its explicit focus on responsible AI within this context. This positions the workshop as a timely and relevant response to the need for ethical guidelines and robust technical solutions as these interconnected AI domains mature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Affective Computing, as outlined in the paper, is to design, evaluate, and implement Emotion AI systems and related technologies with the overarching goal of improving human lives. This includes developing computational models capable of genuinely enhancing human communication and interactions through emotional intelligence, while ensuring these systems augment human abilities safely and responsibly.

The MRAC 2024 Track 1 workshop is motivated by several factors arising from the current state and future trajectory of AI:
- **Rapid Advancements in Multimodal Generative Technology:** The increasing sophistication of generative AI tools necessitates a dedicated forum to explore their application within Affective Computing and to address the associated implications.
- **Ethical Implications of Emotionally Intelligent AI:** The deployment of AI systems with emotional intelligence has provoked discussions regarding their potential societal consequences. There is a recognized need to guide the development of these systems responsibly, considering their impact on humans from inception to deployment.
- **Data Requirements and Challenges:** The creation of computational models in Affective Computing demands extensive multimodal data (e.g., RGB images, video, audio, text, physiological signals). Ensuring the ethical sourcing, representativeness, and diversity of this data is critical to mitigate biases and ensure fairness. The emergence of generative AI offers a means to produce synthetic data, which can alleviate scarcity but also introduces new concerns regarding its origin and potential for misuse.
- **Bridging Lab-to-Real-World Gaps:** A significant motivation is to facilitate the transfer of responsible AI principles from controlled, small-scale laboratory environments to large-scale, real-world applications. This involves considering the practical challenges and ethical implications of deploying Emotion AI systems in diverse, unconstrained settings.
- **Addressing Potential Misuse of Generative Technology:** The capability of generative AI to create synthetic data, including deepfakes, highlights a pressing need to explore detection and prevention mechanisms to ensure responsible use and prevent deception or harm.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The complexity of these challenges requires a collaborative approach involving researchers and industry professionals from various disciplines to ensure that the development of Emotion AI is guided by diverse perspectives and inclusivity.

To address these motivations, the workshop aims to:
- Encourage and highlight novel strategies for estimating and predicting affective phenomena with an emphasis on robustness, accuracy, and responsibility across various parameters (spatial, temporal, spatiotemporal).
- Promote the application of novel neural network architectures, generative AI, anatomical insights, new challenging datasets, and multimodal training techniques.
- Showcase real-world applications of Affective Computing in sectors such as healthcare, education, and entertainment, while discussing design principles for effective, ethical, transparent, explainable, and user-empowering Emotion AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MRAC 2024 workshop serves as a platform to coalesce research at the confluence of multimodal, generative, and responsible affective computing. The approach of the workshop is to gather and disseminate current research, stimulate discussion, and define future directions in this evolving field.

The workshop's methodology centers on:
- **Thematic Focus:** The workshop explicitly defines its scope by focusing on multimodal, generative, and responsible Affective Computing. This tripartite focus ensures that contributions address both the technological advancements and the ethical considerations integral to the field.
- **Call for Papers and Peer Review:** The workshop invites submissions of research papers addressing specific topics within its scope. This process involves a peer-review mechanism to select contributions that align with the workshop's objectives and demonstrate scientific rigor. Topics of interest included, but were not limited to:
 - Large-scale data generation and inexpensive annotation for Affective Computing.
 - Application of Generative AI for Affective Computing using multimodal signals.
 - Development of multi-modal methods for emotion recognition.
 - Techniques for privacy-preserving, large-scale emotion recognition in unconstrained environments.
 - Generative aspects of affect analysis, including contextual gesture generation.
 - Methods for deepfake generation, detection, and temporal localization using Generative AI.
 - Advanced multimodal data analysis.
 - Applications of Affective Computing in education, entertainment, and healthcare.
 - Approaches for Explainable or Privacy-Preserving AI in affective computing.
 - Generative and responsible personalization of affective phenomena.
 - Zero-shot and few-shot learning strategies for Emotion AI data.
 - Mitigation of bias in affective computing datasets, particularly addressing the lack of multi-cultural datasets.
 - Affective video understanding.
 - Development of semi-supervised, weakly supervised, unsupervised, and self-supervised learning methods.
 - Domain adaptation methods specifically for Affective Computing.
- **Expert Keynote Presentations:** The inclusion of invited expert talks from prominent researchers, such as Prof. Julian Epps (University of New South Wales) and Prof. Mohammed Bennamoun (University of Western Australia), is designed to provide broader perspectives, introduce foundational concepts, and highlight significant advancements relevant to the workshop's themes.
- **Presentation of Accepted Research:** The workshop provides a forum for accepted papers to be presented, allowing researchers to share their findings, discuss methodologies, and engage with the community. The summary of accepted papers in this overview document serves as a snapshot of the contemporary research being conducted within the workshop's domain.
- **Discussion on Research Impact and Future Directions:** Beyond presenting individual research, the workshop aims to facilitate discussions on the broader impact, relevance, and future trajectories of Affective Computing, particularly in the context of integrating generative technologies and upholding responsible AI principles.

This multi-faceted approach ensures that the workshop not only showcases specific research advancements but also addresses the systemic challenges and ethical considerations inherent in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The overview paper presents a summary of four accepted papers for the MRAC 2024 workshop, reflecting the workshop's focus areas, particularly generative AI threats, deepfakes, and affect understanding in videos. These papers contribute to advancing the technical capabilities and ethical considerations within multimodal, generative, and responsible affective computing.

1. **"Are You Paying Attention? Multimodal Linear Attention Transformers for Affect Prediction in Video Conversations" by Jia Qing Poh et al.:**
 - This paper addresses the need for unintrusive affect recognition in video-based communication, especially relevant in the post-COVID-19 era.
 - The authors proposed a multimodal emotion recognition system leveraging a linear attention-based Transformer framework, integrating audio-visual cues. The approach explored various linear attention mechanisms and compared them with traditional self-attention.
 - Using the K-EmoCon dataset, the method demonstrated competitive performance in predicting affective states while achieving increased memory efficiency. Ablation studies indicated that optimized simple fusion techniques could be as effective as more complex alternatives.
 - The research contributes to the development of efficient and accessible multimodal emotion recognition systems suitable for video conversations, with potential applications in enhancing remote communication and monitoring digital well-being.

2. **"Can Expression Sensitivity Improve Macro- and Micro-Expression Spotting in Long Videos?" by Mengjiong Bai and Roland Goecke:**
 - This study focused on the detection of both macro- and micro-expressions in long videos, which is crucial for psychological analysis.
 - A novel algorithm was introduced that integrates recognition and detection tasks using an expression-sensitive model. This approach utilized a pre-trained Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and a confidence value-based mechanism for labeling training samples. The method also extended the utility of datasets originally intended for micro-expression recognition.
 - Evaluated against benchmarks and metrics from MEGC 2020 and MEGC 2021, the proposed algorithm achieved performance metrics that compare favorably to existing advanced techniques. It demonstrated a balance between precision and recall across various databases encompassing both macro- and micro-expressions.
 - This work advances facial expression analysis by offering a robust solution for detecting subtle emotional cues in long video sequences, thereby enhancing psychological and behavioral analysis.

3. **"THE-FD: Task Hierarchical Emotion-aware for Fake Detection" by Wu yang Chen et al.:**
 - This paper addressed the challenge of deepfake detection and localization, particularly the identification of fine-grained manipulations within video segments, moving beyond whole-video classification.
 - The proposed Task Hierarchical Emotion-aware for Fake Detection (THE-FD) architecture processes video-level data and adapts for segment-level analysis, allowing for feature inheritance and sharing. Innovations included an emotional feature space to represent sentiment perturbations, a fake-frame heatmap module for hidden fake patterns across multimodal data, and a multiscale pyramid transformer for learning features across various levels and time scales.
 - The approach achieved a significant improvement in performance and ranked among the top methods in the 2024 1M-Deepfakes Detection Challenge.
 - This research contributes to the development of precise tools for detecting deepfake content by enabling fine-grained temporal localization, which is relevant for combating digital manipulation.

4. **"W-TDL: Window-Based Temporal Deepfake Localization" by Luka Dragar et al.:**
 - This study tackled the problem of distinguishing realistic synthetic data, especially deepfakes, from genuine content and accurately identifying the temporal boundaries of manipulations in both audio and visual modalities. It also addressed the issue of unbalanced training labels in spoofed audio datasets.
 - A novel window-based method for audio (W-TDL) was proposed, complemented by a visual frame-based model. This dual-model strategy leveraged an EVA visual transformer for precise frame-level video analysis and a modified TDL method for audio to improve temporal boundary detection.
 - Comprehensive experiments on the AV-Deepfake1M dataset demonstrated the effectiveness of the approach, yielding competitive results in the 1M-DeepFakes Detection Challenge.
 - This work provides a robust solution for detecting and localizing deepfake manipulations by integrating both visual and auditory cues, representing an advancement in identifying synthetic content across various media types.

Collectively, these accepted papers illustrate the active research areas within multimodal, generative, and responsible Affective Computing, focusing on enhancing emotional intelligence in AI, improving the detection of subtle human expressions, and developing advanced methods to counter the misuse of generative technologies like deepfakes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MRAC 2024 workshop, and the research it highlights, holds significance for the advancement of Affective Computing by integrating it with emerging generative AI capabilities and a strong emphasis on responsible development. The potential impact extends across several critical domains.

**Enhancing Human-AI Interaction and Well-being:**
Affective Computing aims to imbue AI systems with "empathy," enabling them to recognize and interpret human emotions. This can lead to more intuitive, personalized, and supportive human-AI interactions. Potential applications include:
- **Healthcare:** Forecasting and preventing mental health issues (anxiety, stress, depression), monitoring patient emotional states, and providing better care.
- **Education:** Personalized learning environments that adapt to student emotional states.
- **Entertainment:** More engaging and responsive virtual characters and experiences.
- **Customer Service:** Bots that can detect and respond to customer frustration, leading to improved satisfaction.
- **Robotics:** Enhancing robotic empathy and improving human-robot collaboration.
These advancements have the potential to significantly improve human life by making machines more responsive and empathetic, thereby augmenting human capabilities.

**Addressing Ethical Challenges in AI Deployment:**
A core aspect of the workshop is its focus on "Responsible Affective Computing," which directly confronts the ethical challenges inherent in emotionally intelligent AI. The potential impact includes:
- **Data Privacy and Consent:** By emphasizing informed consent and user control over multimodal data, the workshop promotes practices that respect individual privacy, especially concerning sensitive data like facial recognition or physiological signals.
- **Bias and Fairness Mitigation:** The focus on addressing biases in training data (demographic, cultural, etc.) and the propagation of these biases into computational models is critical. This work contributes to developing fair and inclusive Emotion AI systems that minimize discriminatory outcomes. The discussion on the increasing risk of generated data being used without proper provenance further highlights the need for vigilance.
- **Transparency and Interpretability:** Encouraging transparency in how AI systems operate, what data they use, and how decisions are made is essential for building trust in these technologies.
- **Responsible Generative AI:** While generative AI offers benefits like synthetic data generation for training, its potential for misuse, such as creating deepfakes, necessitates robust detection and prevention mechanisms. The workshop's focus on deepfake detection and temporal localization directly contributes to this effort, aiming to secure digital interactions and content integrity.

**Shaping Future Research Directions:**
The workshop articulates several future directions, indicating areas of ongoing and prospective impact:
- **Advanced Multimodal/Cross-modal Analysis:** Developing systems that can synthesize gestures and analyze motion using noisy multimodal data, especially in challenging situations where visual stimuli are limited.
- **Robustness in Unconstrained Settings:** Improving Emotion AI performance in real-world, less controlled environments, moving beyond lab-centric datasets.
- **Reduced Supervision and LLM Integration:** Exploring unsupervised, self-supervised, weakly supervised learning, and leveraging large language models (LLMs) to reduce reliance on labor-intensive, error-prone ground truth labels.
- **Enhanced Bias and Fairness Mitigation:** Continuing the development of advanced techniques like adversarial debiasing and fairness-aware machine learning.
- **Human-AI Collaboration:** Fostering closer, more integrated partnerships between humans and AI systems across various domains.
- **Privacy-Preserving AI:** Advancing techniques such as federated learning, differential privacy, and synthetic data generation to protect sensitive data while enabling AI development.
- **Ethical AI Research:** Promoting dedicated research into novel ethical theories, frameworks, and guidelines for AI, ensuring that ethical development becomes a competitive advantage and adapts to emerging technologies like quantum computing and biotechnology.
- **Generative AI for Inclusion:** Addressing cultural and linguistic variability in generative AI by focusing on universal features, building language-agnostic systems, and using diverse datasets to ensure global applicability and prevent misinterpretation across different cultural contexts.

In summary, the MRAC 2024 workshop and its presented research contribute to the responsible evolution of Affective Computing by integrating cutting-edge generative AI while addressing critical ethical and societal considerations. The work aims to foster the development of emotionally intelligent AI systems that are not only technologically advanced but also fair, transparent, privacy-preserving, and ultimately beneficial for human-AI interaction and societal well-being.
