# Learning what you can do before doing anything

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Pennsylvania, University of Southern California, Ryerson University, and Samsung AI Centre Toronto developed CLASP, a method for learning an agent's action space purely from unlabeled visual observations. This approach achieves disentangled, minimal, and composable action representations, enabling accurate action-conditioned video prediction and effective planning in the latent space with minimal labeled data.

## Problem

- Learning robust and generalizable action representations from visual observations is challenging without explicit action labels or active environment interaction.
- Traditional reinforcement learning and imitation learning methods often require extensive, costly data or direct motor commands, limiting their application to passive observation scenarios.
- Existing models struggle to disentangle specific action information from the static content of a scene, hindering the transferability of learned representations.

## Method

- Proposed the Composable Learned Action Space Predictor (**CLASP**), an extension of recurrent latent variable models for stochastic video prediction.
- Introduced a novel composability objective (L_comp) to encourage latent variables (z) to represent state-independent actions, capturing cumulative effects over multiple timesteps.
- Incorporated the Information Bottleneck principle to enforce minimality in the latent representation, focusing on dynamic changes caused by actions rather than static visual details.

## Results

- The learned latent action space (z) demonstrated clear disentanglement from static content, with the first PCA dimension capturing **99%** of variance for a 1-DOF robot, strongly correlating with ground truth actions.
- Achieved superior action-conditioned video prediction, enabling 'action transplantation' across varied initial states and reducing relative error on the BAIR dataset by **30%** compared to baseline models.
- Enabled effective planning for visual servoing in the latent space, reaching a final distance to goal of 1.6 ± 1.0 degrees on the reacher dataset, comparable to fully supervised methods with significantly fewer labeled examples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective action representations can be learned solely from passive visual observation by enforcing minimality (through Information Bottleneck) and composability in the latent space.
> - The composability objective is crucial for ensuring that learned action representations are disentangled from instantaneous visual states, promoting generalizability across different scenes.
> - An unsupervised pre-training of visual dynamics and action structure allows for highly data-efficient semi-supervised mapping between latent actions and real control commands.
