# Distributionally Robust Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning via Robust Value Factorization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Caltech and Tencent AI Lab propose Distributionally Robust IGM (DrIGM), a new principle for cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) that extends the Individual-Global-Maximum (IGM) to uncertain environments. This framework integrates seamlessly with value factorization architectures and delivers consistent out-of-distribution performance gains, showing 10-40% higher average rewards in HVAC control simulations under climatic and seasonal shifts, and improved win rates in StarCraft II with observation noise.

## Problem

- The Individual-Global-Maximum (IGM) principle, fundamental for decentralized execution in cooperative MARL, becomes unreliable due to environmental uncertainties like sim-to-real gaps and model mismatch.
- Directly extending single-agent Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning (DR-RL) to cooperative MARL is challenging, as individual adversarial models might not align with the global worst-case for the team.
- A principled framework was needed to integrate distributional robustness into cooperative MARL without compromising the benefits of decentralized execution and established value factorization methods.

## Method

- Introduced Distributionally Robust IGM (**DrIGM**), a principle that ensures individual agents' robust greedy actions align with the robust team-optimal joint action under uncertainty.
- Proposed defining individual robust action-value functions with respect to a *globally* identified worst-case joint action-value function, guaranteeing DrIGM and compatibility with VDN, QMIX, and QTRAN architectures.
- Developed DrIGM-based robust Bellman operators for ρ-contamination and Total Variation (TV) uncertainty sets, enabling practical robust TD-learning for training agents.

## Results

- DrIGM-based algorithms consistently outperformed non-robust baselines and a GroupDR baseline in SustainGym (HVAC control) across various climatic and seasonal shifts, yielding 10-**40%** higher average rewards in extreme OOD conditions.
- In StarCraft II, robust VDN and QMIX significantly improved out-of-distribution test win rates on the **3s** vs 5z map under Gaussian noise to enemy observations, for appropriate robustness parameter settings.
- Robust training, particularly with TV uncertainty sets, demonstrated that explicitly modeling robustness can simultaneously improve both in-distribution performance and adaptability to unforeseen environmental changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving distributional robustness in cooperative MARL requires individual robust Q-functions to be defined based on a shared, global worst-case model, rather than independent local worst-cases, to maintain action alignment.
> - The DrIGM principle is theoretically compatible with popular value factorization methods, allowing for robust extensions without fundamental architectural changes or bespoke reward engineering.
> - Incorporating distributional robustness can not only enhance out-of-distribution performance but, in some cases, also improve in-distribution performance by stabilizing learning in partially observable multi-agent systems.
