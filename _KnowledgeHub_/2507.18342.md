# EgoExoBench: A Benchmark for First- and Third-person View Video Understanding in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and collaborating universities developed EgoExoBench, the first benchmark to evaluate multimodal large language models on their ability to integrate and reason across first-person and third-person video viewpoints. State-of-the-art models achieved an average accuracy of 51.7%, significantly trailing human performance of 90.1% on these cross-view understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks assess video understanding from either first-person or third-person views in isolation.
- No systematic framework existed to evaluate MLLMs' capacity to integrate and reason across these distinct egocentric and exocentric perspectives.
- The absence of cross-view reasoning capabilities limits the development of robust embodied AI and seamless human-robot collaboration.

## Method

- Introduced **EgoExoBench**, the first benchmark specifically designed for ==cross-view video understanding== in MLLMs.
- Structured the benchmark with three core dimensions (==semantic alignment==, ==view transition==, ==temporal reasoning==) and eleven subtasks.
- Generated over 7,300 multiple-choice questions using a hybrid pipeline of annotation-derived, LLM-generated, and human-annotated QA with extensive quality assurance.

## Results

- The top-performing MLLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieved an average accuracy of **51.7%** on EgoExoBench, falling significantly short of human performance at **90.1%**.
- MLLMs demonstrated particular weakness in Egocentric Wearer Identification, a task where humans achieved **perfect accuracy**, indicating a major challenge in cross-view spatial and identity inference.
- Chain-of-Thought prompting unexpectedly degraded MLLM performance on most tasks, while cross-perspective guidance improved Action Prediction but showed inconsistent effects on Skill Evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a substantial gap compared to human performance in cross-view video understanding, achieving less than 52% accuracy versus 90%.
> - Specialized single-view pretraining (e.g., on egocentric data) offers only marginal benefits for cross-perspective reasoning, indicating current MLLMs lack explicit cross-view integration.
> - Traditional Chain-of-Thought prompting often degrades MLLM performance on cross-view tasks, suggesting it interferes with the necessary visual-linguistic interplay.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of EgoExoBench positions itself at the forefront of research into embodied AI and multimodal understanding, addressing a critical gap in the capabilities of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

**Rapid Progress in MLLMs and Video Understanding:** The field has witnessed rapid advancements in MLLMs, enabling them to process and understand video content. Existing benchmarks like EgoSchema, Video-MME, MLVU, and CG-Bench have been instrumental in driving progress in various aspects of video understanding, such as temporal reasoning in egocentric streams, multi-modal question answering (QA) on third-person footage, and long-form video comprehension. These benchmarks have successfully pushed the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve within *single-view* contexts.

**The Unaddressed Gap: Cross-View Reasoning:** Despite these advancements, the paper highlights a significant limitation: all existing benchmarks operate on either egocentric (first-person) or exocentric (third-person) data in isolation. They do not systematically evaluate an MLLM's ability to integrate and reason across these distinct perspectives. This is the crucial gap that EgoExoBench aims to fill.

**Human Intelligence as Inspiration:** The authors frame this problem by drawing parallels to human intelligence. Humans intrinsically possess the ability to transfer and integrate knowledge across first- and third-person viewpoints. We learn new skills by observing others (exocentric) and mapping those actions to our own experiences (egocentric). Conversely, we articulate our experiences (egocentric) to guide others (exocentric). This dual-view reasoning is fundamental for robust perception, skill acquisition, navigation, and understanding procedural tasks in dynamic environments.

**Implications for Embodied AI and Human-Robot Collaboration:** Replicating this ego-exo reasoning in artificial systems is deemed essential for developing more capable embodied agents, such as robots. Robots that can align large-scale exocentric knowledge (e.g., instructional videos) with their own egocentric observations can perform tasks more effectively and engage in better imitation learning. Furthermore, bridging these perspectives is critical for seamless human-robot collaboration, where understanding the human's perspective (first-person) and the robot's own view (first-person for robot) in relation to a shared task (often described in third-person) is paramount.

**Distinction from Prior Ego-Exo Work:** The paper acknowledges existing "ego-exo datasets" (e.g., Ego-Exo4D, EgoExoLearn, LEMMA) that provide paired first- and third-person video collections. It also references "ego-exo video understanding" methods, which typically involve techniques like joint view-invariant learning, domain adaptation, and knowledge distillation. However, the authors emphasize that these prior efforts primarily focus on recognition tasks or specific applications (like pose estimation or navigation) and do not systematically evaluate an MLLM's general *reasoning* ability across perspectives. EgoExoBench, therefore, pioneers the evaluation of semantic alignment, spatial correspondence, and temporal reasoning explicitly within a cross-view context, making it the first comprehensive benchmark for this purpose.

In summary, EgoExoBench intervenes at a crucial juncture where MLLMs have demonstrated impressive single-view capabilities but lack a robust framework to assess and develop their cross-view intelligence—a cornerstone of human-like perception and interaction. It aims to accelerate research towards truly embodied and intelligent AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to introduce **EgoExoBench**, the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate and drive the development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) capable of cross-view video understanding and reasoning.

The motivations behind this objective are deeply rooted in both mimicking human intelligence and advancing the capabilities of artificial intelligence systems, particularly in embodied contexts:

1. **Bridging the Gap in MLLM Capabilities:** Despite the rapid progress of MLLMs in single-view video understanding (e.g., egocentric or exocentric video analysis in isolation), there is a significant unexplored area: their ability to integrate and reason about information presented simultaneously from both first-person (egocentric) and third-person (exocentric) viewpoints. This benchmark aims to systematically expose and measure this critical missing capability.

2. **Mimicking Intrinsic Human Intelligence:** The authors highlight that the ability to transfer and integrate knowledge across first- and third-person perspectives is a fundamental aspect of human intelligence. Humans routinely learn new skills by observing others (exocentric demonstrations) and mapping those actions onto their own experiences (egocentric), and conversely, can articulate their own experiences to guide others. This dual-view reasoning enables robust perception, skill acquisition, and interaction in complex environments. The motivation is to challenge MLLMs to develop a similar human-like capacity.

3. **Advancing Embodied AI and Human-Robot Collaboration:** Replicating human-like ego-exo reasoning in artificial systems promises more capable and adaptable embodied agents (e.g., robots, virtual assistants). Such agents could effectively learn from diverse sources, perform complex imitation learning, and navigate/interact with their environment more intelligently. Specifically, bridging these perspectives is crucial for seamless human-robot collaboration, where understanding shared tasks from different viewpoints is paramount. The benchmark aims to provide a tool for researchers to develop and evaluate AI systems for these applications.

4. **Comprehensive and Systematic Evaluation:** A key objective is to provide a rigorous and comprehensive evaluation suite. This involves:
 - **Defining Core Challenges:** Breaking down cross-view reasoning into three fundamental dimensions: semantic alignment, viewpoint association, and temporal reasoning.
 - **Developing Diverse Sub-tasks:** Creating eleven specific sub-tasks to thoroughly probe these dimensions.
 - **Leveraging Existing Data:** Building the benchmark from publicly available, paired egocentric-exocentric video sources to ensure accessibility and facilitate future research.
 - **Ensuring Quality and Reliability:** Implementing a meticulous question-answer generation pipeline with automated and human-based quality assurance to produce high-quality, unambiguous multiple-choice questions that genuinely require visual cross-view understanding.

By achieving these objectives, EgoExoBench seeks not only to identify the current limitations of MLLMs in cross-view understanding but also to serve as a catalyst for new research directions, inspiring novel architectures and training strategies that can bridge the gap towards human-level cross-view intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for creating EgoExoBench is meticulously designed to construct a comprehensive and high-quality benchmark for cross-view video understanding in MLLMs. The approach can be broken down into task suite design, data construction pipeline, and experimental evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Task Suite Design

EgoExoBench is structured around three core dimensions of ego-exo reasoning, which collectively encompass eleven distinct subtasks:

1. **Ego-Exo Relation (Semantic Alignment):** Evaluates a model's ability to semantically align content across perspectives.
 - **Task Relation (TR):** Matches high-level activities across views (e.g., "assembling a device").
 - **Action Relation (AR):** Identifies corresponding fine-grained actions across views.
 - **Object Relation (OR):** Matches interactions with the same object across views.
 - **Person Relation (PR):** Identifies the same individual across different viewpoints.

2. **Ego-Exo View Transition (Spatial Correspondence):** Measures a model's ability to translate spatial information between first- and third-person perspectives.
 - **Egocentric Wearer Identification (EWI):** Identifies the camera wearer in a third-person view given their egocentric footage.
 - **Direction Prediction (DP):** Projects egocentric motion into global scenes, predicting direction of movement.
 - **Body Part Action Understanding (BPA):** Maps fine-grained limb movements and their roles across views.

3. **Ego-Exo Temporal Reasoning (Sequence Integration):** Examines a model's capacity to align and infer the flow of events across paired video streams.
 - **Action Prediction (AP):** Forecasts subsequent egocentric actions based on an exocentric demonstration.
 - **Action Ordering (AO):** Determines the temporal relationship between two short clips (ego and exo).
 - **Sequence Alignment (SA):** Assesses if egocentric and exocentric videos share the same action ordering or differ at key steps.
 - **Skill Evaluation (SE):** Assesses the skill level in a video from one viewpoint against a reference expert demonstration from another.

All questions are presented in a **4-way multiple-choice question (MCQ) format** to ensure reliable and scalable assessment, mitigating the difficulties of evaluating free-form responses.

#### 3.2. Data Construction Pipeline

The benchmark utilizes a three-stage pipeline (Video Collection, QA Construction, Quality Assurance & Filtering) to generate over 7,300 MCQs.

1. **Video Collection:** The benchmark aggregates videos from six publicly available ego-exo paired datasets: Ego-Exo4D, EgoExoLearn, LEMMA, EgoMe, TF2023, and CVMHAT. These datasets offer diverse environments and activities, encompassing both synchronized and asynchronous multi-view recordings, which are crucial for probing various aspects of cross-view reasoning.

2. **Question-Answer Construction:** Diverse strategies are employed based on task requirements:
 - **Annotation-Derived QA:** For tasks with structured and deterministic annotations (e.g., task IDs, action labels), QA pairs are directly constructed using predefined templates (e.g., Task Relation, Action Relation from LEMMA).
 - **LLM-Generated QA:** For open-ended reasoning tasks, MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B, DeepSeek-V3) are used to generate context-aware QA pairs. They are prompted with task definitions, detailed annotations, exemplar QAs, and specific constraints (e.g., Body Part Action Understanding, Action Prediction).
 - **Human-Annotated QA:** For fine-grained spatial understanding where current MLLMs may not produce reliable results, human annotators are employed to create QA pairs (e.g., Direction Prediction).

3. **Quality Assurance and Filtering:** A rigorous process ensures the quality and visual grounding of the QA pairs:
 - **Consistency Verification:** An LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) is used for automated logical correctness checks against original video annotations, discarding ambiguous or inconsistent questions.
 - **Vision-Grounded Filtering:** To ensure questions *require* visual understanding, an LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) is presented with text-only questions, and any QA pairs solvable solely by textual input are discarded.

#### 3.3. Experimental Evaluation Protocol

- **Models Evaluated:** 13 state-of-the-art MLLMs are evaluated in a zero-shot MCQ format, including both closed-source (GPT-4o, GPT-o4-mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Pro) and open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-OV, LLaVA-Video, NVILA, EgoGPT).
- **Metrics:** Accuracy is the primary metric.
- **Prompting:** Models receive standardized prompts with questions and labeled options, instructed to return only the chosen letter. A rule-based approach extracts the answer.
- **Human Performance Baseline:** Two graduate students, independent of the data construction, answer a sampled subset of 330 questions to establish a human accuracy baseline under both quick (1 min) and deliberate (3 min) response conditions.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Investigates if linguistic reasoning prompts ("Let's think step by step") improve performance, by comparing baseline vs. CoT-augmented prompts on a subset of 100 questions per subtask.
 - **Cross-Perspective Guidance:** Assesses the impact of providing an additional reference video from a different perspective for Action Prediction and Skill Evaluation tasks by comparing performance with and without this additional context.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that EgoExoBench is a robust, diverse, and well-curated benchmark, capable of rigorously evaluating cross-view video understanding and reasoning in MLLMs and providing clear insights into their current limitations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs on EgoExoBench revealed significant limitations in their cross-view video understanding capabilities, contrasting sharply with human performance.

1. **Significant Gap Between MLLMs and Human Performance:**
 - The best-performing model, Gemini 2.5 Pro (from Appendix B.2), achieved an average accuracy of 51.7%. GPT-o4-mini was the best among reported models in the main paper, with 48.0%.
 - In stark contrast, human evaluators, under deliberate conditions (3 minutes per question), achieved an average accuracy of 90.1%. This represents a substantial gap of over 38-42 percentage points, indicating that current MLLMs are far from human-level cross-view intelligence.
 - The largest performance discrepancy was observed in the **Egocentric Wearer Identification (EWI)** subtask, where humans achieved 100% accuracy, while models struggled to infer spatial relationships and identity across perspectives.
 - The smallest discrepancy was in **Skill Evaluation (SE)**, where even humans found domain-specific assessments challenging, suggesting that integrating domain knowledge is crucial even for human performance and potentially a different kind of challenge for MLLMs.

2. **Struggles Across All Core Dimensions:**
 - While some models showed relative strengths in certain subtasks, none excelled consistently across the three core dimensions (Ego-Exo Relation, View Transition, and Temporal Reasoning).
 - Models that demonstrated strong performance on single-view benchmarks experienced a notable drop when confronted with cross-view challenges.
 - **Ego-Exo Relation:** Closed-source models like GPT-o4-mini outperformed open-source counterparts, indicating a lag in open-source architectures for explicit semantic alignment across views.
 - **Ego-Exo View Transition & Temporal Reasoning:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B showed competitive performance in these categories, attributed to its relative strength in spatial and video grounding, yet still lagged human ability.

3. **Limitations of Specialized Pretraining:**
 - EgoGPT, a model fine-tuned on a large egocentric video corpus (EgoIT-99K), offered only marginal gains over similarly sized counterparts. This suggests that specialized pretraining on a single viewpoint, even if extensive, is insufficient to master cross-perspective reasoning without explicit task-specific objectives or architectural designs geared towards integration.

4. **Ineffectiveness of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:**
 - Counter-intuitively, applying CoT prompting ("Let's think step by step") generally *degraded* MLLM performance on most EgoExoBench tasks.
 - For subtasks like Person Relation and Action Relation, models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B experienced substantial accuracy drops (e.g., 20% and 19%).
 - The hypothesis for this failure is that standard, text-oriented CoT cues disrupt the *interleaved visual-linguistic workflow* required for cross-view inference. Models struggle to maintain consistency between visual interpretation from one view, linguistic translation, and application to a second view during a purely text-based reasoning process. A concrete example illustrated GPT-4o misidentifying a wearer despite correctly deducing spatial relationships from the first-person view.

5. **Mixed Impact of Cross-Perspective Guidance (Reference Videos):**
 - **Action Prediction:** Providing an additional reference video from a different perspective *consistently boosted* performance (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL gained 8.2%). This validates the practical value of cross-perspective cues for improving action understanding, especially in predictive tasks.
 - **Skill Evaluation:** The impact of reference videos was *inconsistent or even negative* (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL saw a 1.5% drop). This suggests that for tasks relying on subtle quality judgments or deep domain-specific expertise (like assessing basketball proficiency), simply adding visual context from another view may not be sufficient without a deeper integration of domain knowledge.

In summary, the experiments unequivocally demonstrate that current MLLMs possess a profound deficiency in cross-view video understanding and reasoning, highlighting a critical bottleneck in the path toward truly intelligent, embodied AI systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoExoBench holds significant implications for the fields of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and robotics, promising to accelerate research in multimodal understanding and embodied AI.

1. **Pioneering a New Frontier in MLLM Evaluation:** As the "first benchmark explicitly designed to evaluate cross-view video understanding in multimodal LLMs," EgoExoBench carves out a new and essential research direction. It systematically identifies and quantifies a critical missing capability in state-of-the-art MLLMs that was previously overlooked by single-view benchmarks. This foundational work will guide future model development.

2. **Unveiling Fundamental Limitations:** The benchmark's findings clearly demonstrate that despite impressive progress in general video understanding, current MLLMs fundamentally struggle with integrating and reasoning across distinct first- and third-person perspectives. This includes challenges in semantic alignment, spatial correspondence, and temporal integration. This insight is crucial because it indicates that merely scaling up existing MLLMs or pretraining them on larger single-view datasets may not inherently resolve this deficiency.

3. **Catalyst for Architectural and Training Innovations:** The observed limitations, particularly the ineffectiveness of standard Chain-of-Thought prompting in a cross-modal context, highlight the urgent need for novel architectural designs and training paradigms. Future research will need to focus on:
 - Developing mechanisms that can robustly interleave visual and linguistic inference across multiple, distinct viewpoints.
 - Designing prompting strategies specifically tailored for multimodal, cross-view reasoning, rather than relying on purely textual decomposition.
 - Exploring new ways to integrate spatial and temporal reasoning modules that explicitly account for perspective shifts.

4. **Advancing Embodied Agents and Human-Robot Collaboration:** The ability to understand and transfer knowledge across egocentric and exocentric views is paramount for developing truly intelligent embodied agents and fostering seamless human-robot collaboration.
 - **For Embodied Agents:** EgoExoBench provides a crucial tool for evaluating robots and virtual assistants that need to learn from human demonstrations (exocentric) and perform tasks from their own perspective (egocentric), or understand their environment from multiple camera feeds.
 - **For Human-Robot Collaboration:** It will aid in building systems where robots can better understand human intentions from an observer's perspective and translate them into actionable plans from their own operational viewpoint, enhancing interaction and task efficiency.

5. **A Valuable Resource for the Research Community:** By aggregating videos from multiple publicly available ego-exo datasets and providing over 7,300 high-quality multiple-choice questions, EgoExoBench offers a comprehensive and accessible resource. The open availability of the benchmark (via GitHub) fosters reproducibility, encourages wider participation from the research community, and ensures that future progress can be benchmarked against a common, challenging standard.

6. **Highlighting the Complexity of Human-like Intelligence:** The substantial gap between MLLM and human performance on EgoExoBench underscores the profound complexity of human cross-view intelligence. Tasks like "Egocentric Wearer Identification" (where models struggle to infer identity from subtle spatial relationships) reveal that a deeper understanding of embodied perception and cognitive mapping is still required for AI.

In conclusion, EgoExoBench is more than just a dataset; it is a strategic research instrument that redefines the scope of multimodal video understanding for MLLMs. By setting a rigorous standard and exposing current shortcomings, it will undoubtedly inspire and guide future innovations towards building more human-like, intelligent, and perceptually robust AI systems.
