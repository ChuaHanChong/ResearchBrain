# EgoExoBench: A Benchmark for First- and Third-person View Video Understanding in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and collaborating universities developed EgoExoBench, the first benchmark to evaluate multimodal large language models on their ability to integrate and reason across first-person and third-person video viewpoints. State-of-the-art models achieved an average accuracy of 51.7%, significantly trailing human performance of 90.1% on these cross-view understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks assess video understanding from either first-person or third-person views in isolation.
- No systematic framework existed to evaluate MLLMs' capacity to integrate and reason across these distinct egocentric and exocentric perspectives.
- The absence of cross-view reasoning capabilities limits the development of robust embodied AI and seamless human-robot collaboration.

## Method

- Introduced **EgoExoBench**, the first benchmark specifically designed for ==cross-view video understanding== in MLLMs.
- Structured the benchmark with three core dimensions (==semantic alignment==, ==view transition==, ==temporal reasoning==) and eleven subtasks.
- Generated over 7,300 multiple-choice questions using a hybrid pipeline of annotation-derived, LLM-generated, and human-annotated QA with extensive quality assurance.

## Results

- The top-performing MLLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieved an average accuracy of **51.7%** on EgoExoBench, falling significantly short of human performance at **90.1%**.
- MLLMs demonstrated particular weakness in Egocentric Wearer Identification, a task where humans achieved **perfect accuracy**, indicating a major challenge in cross-view spatial and identity inference.
- Chain-of-Thought prompting unexpectedly degraded MLLM performance on most tasks, while cross-perspective guidance improved Action Prediction but showed inconsistent effects on Skill Evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a substantial gap compared to human performance in cross-view video understanding, achieving less than 52% accuracy versus 90%.
> - Specialized single-view pretraining (e.g., on egocentric data) offers only marginal benefits for cross-perspective reasoning, indicating current MLLMs lack explicit cross-view integration.
> - Traditional Chain-of-Thought prompting often degrades MLLM performance on cross-view tasks, suggesting it interferes with the necessary visual-linguistic interplay.
