# RuN: Residual Policy for Natural Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> The RuN framework introduces a decoupled residual learning approach for natural humanoid locomotion, enabling a Unitree G1 robot to achieve stable, human-like gaits and smooth walk-run transitions across a continuous velocity range of 0-2.5 m/s in both simulation and physical deployment. This method combines an autoregressive Conditional Motion Generator (CMG) with a reinforcement learning-based residual policy for dynamic corrections.

## Problem

- Current DRL methods often struggle to produce anthropomorphically natural humanoid locomotion, with generated motions appearing stiff or mechanical, due to the complexity of reward function design.
- Existing DRL approaches integrating motion priors typically use a direct-tracking strategy, forcing a single policy to simultaneously learn motion imitation, dynamic stability, and task execution, leading to complex learning problems, large action spaces, and long training times.
- Achieving versatile and dynamic gaits (e.g., seamless walk-run transitions across a broad speed range) while maintaining both naturalness and stability remains a significant challenge for humanoid robots.

## Method

- A decoupled residual learning framework (**RuN**) is introduced, separating kinematic style generation from dynamic corrections to simplify the control task.
- An autoregressive ==Conditional Motion Generator (CMG)== is trained offline on human motion data to produce natural kinematic reference trajectories conditioned on user commands (e.g., target velocities).
- A lightweight reinforcement learning policy learns online to output small residual actions that provide dynamic corrections to the CMG's output, ensuring stability and precise task completion.

## Results

- The RuN framework achieved superior motion naturalness (FID of **0.8753**) and imitation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art baselines like AMP and GMP, while maintaining competitive velocity tracking.
- Training efficiency improved significantly, with RuN converging faster and achieving higher final rewards than baseline methods due to its residual learning approach.
- The simulation-trained policy successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating stable, natural gaits and smooth walk-run transitions across a continuous velocity range of 0-**2.5** m/s.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the control problem into kinematic style generation and dynamic correction significantly reduces learning complexity, leading to faster training convergence and improved overall performance.
> - An autoregressive Conditional Motion Generator (CMG) with a Mixture-of-Experts architecture effectively generates high-quality, diverse, and natural kinematic priors, surpassing direct-tracking generative models in naturalness and accuracy.
> - Leveraging an asymmetric actor-critic architecture with privileged information for the critic during training enhances the robustness of the residual policy for real-world deployment while maintaining limited sensory input for the actor.
