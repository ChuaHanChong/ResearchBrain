# CodeV: Code with Images for Faithful Visual Reasoning via Tool-Aware Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> CodeV, a code-based visual agent, employs Tool-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO) to address unfaithful visual reasoning in Vision-Language Models. This method provides dense, process-level rewards for visual tool use, leading to an increase in faithful tool-use rates and competitive performance on multimodal reasoning and perception tasks.

## Problem

- Existing agentic visual reasoning systems often achieve high final-answer accuracy despite exhibiting unfaithful visual reasoning, where visual tools are used irrelevantly or their outputs are ignored.
- Current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) designs suffer from "outcome dominance" and "reward sparsity," leading to reward hacking and unstable optimization dynamics.
- The lack of faithful visual reasoning compromises the interpretability and trustworthiness of agentic AI systems, making it difficult to ascertain genuine visual evidence processing.
- How to incentivize vision
language models to produce faithful reasoning grounded in tool outputs?

## Method

- CodeV, a code-based visual agent, generates Python programs for image operations within a restricted ==Python sandbox==, allowing flexible interleaving of textual reasoning and visual tool use.
- A two-stage curriculum training pipeline is employed, starting with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for cold-starting the model, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- ==Tool-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO)== is implemented in the RL stage, using a ==hybrid reward function== that combines final answer accuracy with dense, process-aware rewards for faithful visual tool use, assessed by an external judge model.
- The Python sandbox is designed for security, usability, and robust error handling, providing read-only access to image and mathematical utilities, while returning execution observations to the agent.

## Results

- CodeV-7B-RL substantially increased the faithful tool-use rate on the V* benchmark to **68.0%**, a marked improvement over baselines like DeepEyes-7B (**49.7%**) and Pixel-Reasoner-7B (**34.1%**).
- CodeV achieved competitive or superior accuracy across multimodal benchmarks, scoring **84.8** on V* (surpassing GPT-4o's **64.4**) and attaining the highest score of **71.8** on MathVista among all tested models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full TAPO reward provided the strongest overall performance, yielding an additional **~2-point** gain over simpler reward designs, and demonstrated the critical role of SFT initialization for effective tool-using agents.
- Analysis showed CodeV uses focused tool calls, with one or two calls per trajectory being most effective, and exhibits significantly larger behavioral changes when tool outputs are perturbed, indicating genuine reliance on visual evidence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High final-answer accuracy in existing VLMs often masks unfaithful visual reasoning, as models can correctly answer questions by exploiting textual cues rather than genuinely processing visual evidence.
> - Dense, step-level supervision on visual tool outputs, enabled by TAPO's judge-based evidence checking, is more effective at promoting faithful visual reasoning than sparse, outcome-only rewards.
> - A cold-start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage is crucial for initializing models with effective coding knowledge and robust tool-use trajectories, serving as a vital foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning.
> - CodeV's reasoning and tool-use decisions are genuinely conditioned on returned visual evidence, as shown by significant behavioral changes when tool outputs are perturbed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are increasingly being developed to address complex tasks that necessitate multi-step reasoning and integration with external tools. This paradigm, referred to as agentic reasoning, involves models interacting with various modalities and tools, such as web search, code interpreters, and visual operations. In the visual domain, agentic systems are designed to "think with images" by dynamically calling image manipulation tools like cropping or segmentation to ground their reasoning in visual evidence. This approach has demonstrated performance gains on tasks requiring compositional visual reasoning, visual search, and chart analysis, often outperforming single-shot prediction methods and offering a scalable path towards evidence-based solutions.

Despite advancements, particularly with proprietary models like GPT-4o, a challenge in open-source visual agents has been the reliability and faithfulness of their visual reasoning. Existing systems, while achieving high final-answer accuracy on benchmarks, have been observed to exhibit "unfaithful visual reasoning." This phenomenon occurs when models invoke visual tools on irrelevant image regions, misinterpret tool outputs, or disregard them entirely, yet still manage to infer the correct answer, often by exploiting textual cues or superficial patterns. This behavior compromises the interpretability and trustworthiness of these agentic systems, making it difficult to ascertain whether the model genuinely processes visual evidence or merely guesses.

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has been employed to enhance complex reasoning in LLMs, and its application has extended to agentic visual reasoning. However, limitations exist within current RLVR reward designs, specifically "outcome dominance" and "reward sparsity." Outcome-dominant rewards prioritize final answer accuracy or tool invocation without supervising *how* tools are used, leading to "reward hacking" where models generate superfluous or meaningless tool calls. Sparse rewards, common in difficult tasks, can result in unstable optimization dynamics as early-stage rollouts often receive zero feedback. This research aims to address these identified shortcomings by proposing a framework that explicitly incentivizes faithful visual tool use, thereby improving the alignment between a model's reasoning process and its reliance on visual evidence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from an observed discrepancy in current agentic visual reasoning systems: high final-answer accuracy frequently masks a lack of faithful visual reasoning. The paper demonstrates that models can correctly answer visual questions even when their intermediate visual tool operations (e.g., image crops) are performed on irrelevant regions or when the outputs of these tools are ignored. This unfaithful behavior undermines the transparency and reliability of agentic AI systems, making it unclear whether the model genuinely "sees" and uses the visual evidence or if it is merely exploiting textual cues or other superficial patterns to produce plausible outputs.

Specifically, the authors identify two key issues in existing reward designs that contribute to this problem:
1. **Outcome Dominance**: Current reward functions often prioritize the final accuracy of an answer or simply whether a tool was invoked, without providing specific supervision on the quality or relevance of *how* the tool was used. This lack of step-level credit assignment can lead policies to "reward hack," where they might hallucinate tool use or perform operations that are logically unsound but contribute to a high final score through indirect means. This parallels observations in web-based agents that issue superficial search queries but largely ignore retrieved evidence.
2. **Reward Sparsity**: Rewards in current systems can be sparse, especially in complex tasks where many initial rollouts yield zero rewards. This sparsity can destabilize optimization dynamics and discourage the faithful use of tools, even when partial progress is made. While techniques like reward shaping or invocation bonuses can address sparsity, they often introduce new avenues for reward hacking by incentivizing unnecessary or trivial computations, further exacerbating unfaithful behaviors.

These observations lead to the central research question: "How do we incentivize vision–language models to produce faithful reasoning grounded in tool outputs?" To address this, the paper proposes developing a method to encourage models to produce reasoning that is genuinely supported by the outputs of visual tools. This involves a shift from purely outcome-based rewards to a process-level reward framework that can verify and reward the faithfulness of intermediate tool steps, thereby fostering more trustworthy and interpretable agentic visual reasoning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, CodeV, is a code-based visual agent trained using a two-stage curriculum framework that incorporates Tool-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO). The methodology is designed to incentivize both accuracy and faithful visual tool use.

#### 3.1. Code-based Agentic Rollouts

CodeV operates by generating a trajectory of actions, `τ = (x, a₁, o₁..., a_T)`, for an input `x = (V, Q)` comprising an image `V` and a textual query `Q`. Each action `a_t` is conditioned on the input and historical actions and observations. Actions can be one of three types:
- `<think>`: Free-form text for internal reasoning.
- `<code>`: A delimited Python program designed to operate on the image `V`.
- `<answer>`: The final answer, which terminates the trajectory.

Only completed `<code>` blocks are executed within a restricted Python sandbox. This sandbox provides read-only access to the image and a set of deterministic image and mathematical utilities. Upon execution, the sandbox returns an observation `o_t` (e.g., logs, derived images) which is appended to the context. This setup allows for a flexible interleaving of textual reasoning and visual tool use, with tool execution fully represented within the token-level policy.

#### 3.2. Python Sandbox Design

The Python sandbox is designed for security, usability, and robust error handling. It isolates code execution from the host system, routes file I/O to a controlled workspace, and statically scans for dangerous operations. Each tool call is subjected to a strict time limit to prevent stalled training. For usability, the sandbox normalizes working directories, auto-formats code, predefines common variables, and pre-imports frequently used libraries. It also automatically infers and clamps cropping box coordinates to valid image boundaries. For visualization, plotting backends are monkey-patched to capture figures as images, returning them as observations to the agent. Error messages from failed executions are returned to the model, allowing it to revise and regenerate code or proceed with alternative reasoning.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Curriculum Training Pipeline

1. **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
 - This initial stage is crucial for "cold-starting" the model. It involves training CodeV on high-quality demonstration traces where visual tools are invoked, and their outputs are integrated into the reasoning process.
 - The SFT corpus is built from Thyme-SFT, a curated dataset containing multi-step reasoning examples that involve code execution for image operations (e.g., cropping, rotation, contrast enhancement) and mathematical computations.
 - The SFT model serves as both the initialization for the subsequent RL stage and as the reference policy (π_ref) for regularization in the policy gradient optimization. The SFT stage is observed to be critical because raw instruct-tuned models often fail to translate their coding knowledge into effective problem-solving with tool use.

2. **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Tool-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO)**
 - TAPO extends the GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization) framework by incorporating dense, process-aware rewards for tool use.
 - For each image-question pair (prompt group `g`), `K` trajectories are sampled. A group baseline `b(g)` is computed as the average reward of these trajectories.
 - The trajectory-level advantage `A_t = R(τ) - b(g(τ))` is calculated and broadcast to all time steps within a trajectory.
 - TAPO optimizes a clipped GRPO objective, similar to PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization), using importance ratios to stabilize training and maintain proximity to the SFT-trained reference policy.

#### 3.4. Reward Design for TAPO

TAPO employs a hybrid reward function `R(τ) = λ_acc * r_acc(τ) + λ_tool * r_tool(τ)`, combining final-answer correctness with the faithfulness of visual tool use. `λ_acc` and `λ_tool` are weighting coefficients, with `λ_acc` being dominant.

- **Answer Reward (`r_acc`)**: This measures the quality of the final answer. It uses exact or programmatic matching for tasks with ground-truth answers (e.g., numeric, categorical VQA) or an LLM-as-a-judge for open-ended tasks.
- **Tool Reward (`r_tool`)**: This evaluates the faithfulness of intermediate tool steps. It is aggregated as an average of step-level rewards `r_tool_t` for each `<code>` action.
 - **Evidence Checking**: Instead of directly judging the model's internal thoughts or generated code, TAPO inspects non-model-generated context: the original question (`Q`), the sandbox output (`o_t`, e.g., cropped image, its description, coordinates), and metadata. A separate judge model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B or GPT-5-nano in ablations) is prompted to assess whether the tool result (e.g., a cropped image) provides any useful evidence for answering `Q`.
 - **Rubric for Tool Reward**: The judge encourages crops that clearly contain a relevant object or region and does not require the crop to include all relevant objects. This evidence-based approach is more verifiable than diagnosing internal reasoning.
 - **Discouraging Lazy Crops and Redlines**: To prevent reward hacking and trivial tool uses (e.g., overly large or uninformative crops), several mechanisms are in place:
 - SFT shaping already teaches localized, high-resolution cropping.
 - The judge rubric specifically focuses on whether the *main content* of the cropped image clearly contains the queried object.
 - Obvious misuses of tools (e.g., invalid coordinates, repeated no-op crops, sandbox errors) are assigned negative rewards.
 - The weighting `|λ_tool| < |λ_acc|` ensures that overall answer correctness remains the primary signal.
- **Tasks without explicit visual search**: For tasks where localized visual search is not critical, `r_tool_t` is set close to zero by default, applying negative rewards only for clear redlines, to avoid incentivizing unnecessary tool calls.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented empirical results across faithfulness evaluations, performance benchmarks, and ablation studies to substantiate the effectiveness of CodeV and TAPO.

#### 4.1. Evidence of Unfaithful Tool Use in VLMs (Section 3 & 5.3)

Initial evaluations on existing state-of-the-art open-source models like DeepEyes and Pixel-Reasoner revealed that high final-answer accuracy did not correlate with faithful visual reasoning. On the V* benchmark, only 57% of DeepEyes' and 43% of Pixel-Reasoner's correct answers were supported by faithful tool use (i.e., the cropped image contained the queried object). Thyme-RL-7B showed even lower faithfulness, with single-digit rates, often relying on textual reasoning instead of executing visual tools. This indicates that current benchmarks can overestimate genuine visual tool-use capability.

CodeV, trained with TAPO, demonstrated substantial improvements in faithfulness. On V*, CodeV-7B-RL achieved a 68.0% faithful tool-use rate (Table 5), a notable increase compared to DeepEyes-7B (49.7%), Pixel-Reasoner-7B (34.1%), and Thyme-RL-7B (7.0%). Similar gains were observed on HRBench-4K (CodeV: 33.5% vs. DeepEyes: 24.7%, Pixel-Reasoner: 20.1%, Thyme: 1.0%) and HRBench-8K (CodeV: 13.3% vs. DeepEyes: 6.7%, Pixel-Reasoner: 7.6%, Thyme: 1.2%). These results indicate that TAPO effectively encourages the model to ground its decisions in actual visual evidence provided by tools. The faithfulness rate was observed to decrease with increasing image resolution and task difficulty, but CodeV exhibited greater robustness under these challenging conditions.

#### 4.2. Performance of CodeV (Section 5.2)

CodeV was evaluated against open-source baselines (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Pixel-Reasoner-7B, Thyme-VL-7B-RL) and proprietary models (GPT-4o) across a suite of multimodal benchmarks (Figure 4, Tables 3 and 4).

- **Primitive Perception (VLMBlinds)**: CodeV achieved 46.6, outperforming Thyme-RL-7B (38.6) and DeepEyes-7B (41.2), and slightly surpassing GPT-4o (45.1). This suggests that incentivizing faithful tool use did not negatively impact low-level perception robustness.
- **Large-Image Visual Search (V*, HRBench-4K-all, HRBench-8K-all)**: CodeV obtained the highest score on V* (84.8), surpassing GPT-4o (64.4) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (75.0). On HRBench-4K-all and HRBench-8K-all, CodeV matched or slightly exceeded the best open-source baselines while maintaining a margin over GPT-4o.
- **Math-Heavy and Reasoning Benchmarks**: CodeV achieved 71.8 on MathVista, the highest among all models, and 49.2 on MathVerse-Mini, closing most of the gap to GPT-4o (50.2). On MathVision-Mini, CodeV (33.6) substantially improved over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (21.4) and remained competitive with GPT-4o (35.9).

Overall, CodeV demonstrated competitive or superior accuracy across a range of perception and reasoning tasks, consistently narrowing the performance gap between open-source and proprietary systems.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Section 5.4)

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of the training stages and reward design (Tables 1, 2, 7, and 8).

- **Training Stage Ablation**: Starting from Qwen2.5-VL-7B, training without the cold-start SFT stage (Zero-RL) showed an initial performance gain but quickly collapsed to pure text reasoning with minimal tool use. The cold-start SFT model, while slightly underperforming Zero-RL, produced richer tool-use trajectories. CodeV, built on this SFT initialization with TAPO, further improved average scores, with the largest gains on perception benchmarks, indicating the crucial role of SFT and code/sandbox feedback.
- **Reward Design Ablation**: An accuracy-only outcome reward yielded minor gains over SFT but drove the policy towards text-only reasoning, as indicated by a low tool invocation count (Figure 6). Adding a "Consistency" reward (similar to previous works) had negligible impact. Using GPT-5-nano as a judge within TAPO improved performance by approximately 2 points in reasoning and 1 point in perception. The full TAPO reward in CodeV provided an additional ~2-point gain, achieving the strongest overall performance, suggesting that step-level supervision on tool use is more effective than purely outcome-based rewards.

#### 4.4. Tool-Output Perturbation Analysis (Section 11)

To further assess genuine reliance on tool outputs, CodeV's behavior was evaluated when tool outputs were perturbed (masked, noised, random, or empty) at inference time (Table 6). CodeV exhibited significantly larger behavioral changes (in reasoning tokens, subsequent tool actions, and final answers) compared to baselines like Pixel-Reasoner and DeepEyes. For example, CodeV showed 85.9-90.1% change in "Think" tokens and 44.5-50.8% change in "Action" when tool outputs were perturbed, compared to much lower rates for baselines. This indicates that CodeV's reasoning process and tool-use decisions are genuinely conditioned on the returned visual evidence.

#### 4.5. Tool Use Analysis (Section 12)

Analysis of tool invocation frequency showed that CodeV primarily uses one or two tool calls per trajectory across V*, HRBench-4K, and HRBench-8K benchmarks (Figure 8). Trajectories with zero tool calls were less accurate than those with one or two, and additional tool calls beyond two rarely yielded further gains. This suggests CodeV learns to employ focused and necessary tool calls rather than over-invoking tools.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work addresses a critical issue in the development of agentic visual reasoning systems: the disconnect between high final-answer accuracy and the faithfulness of intermediate visual tool use. By explicitly identifying and evaluating "unfaithful visual reasoning," the authors highlight a fundamental challenge for building trustworthy AI agents.

The introduction of CodeV and its training framework, Tool-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO), represents a notable step toward more reliable and interpretable agentic systems. TAPO's novel approach of providing dense, process-level rewards directly on visual tool outputs, rather than relying solely on sparse, outcome-based rewards or unverifiable chain-of-thought tokens, offers a mechanism to incentivize genuine engagement with visual evidence. This method leads to a substantial increase in faithful tool-use rates and competitive or superior performance across a diverse set of multimodal reasoning and perception benchmarks, narrowing the gap with proprietary systems.

The significance and potential impact of this research include:
- **Enhanced Trustworthiness**: By ensuring that models' reasoning is truly grounded in verifiable visual evidence, CodeV contributes to the development of more trustworthy AI systems. This is particularly important for applications where explainability and reliability are paramount.
- **Improved Interpretability**: When models faithfully use tools, their intermediate actions become more meaningful and interpretable. This allows developers and users to better understand the decision-making process, moving beyond "black box" outcomes.
- **Robust and Generalizable Agents**: The process-level supervision provided by TAPO fosters more stable and generalizable learning, preventing reward hacking and promoting effective tool integration. This is demonstrated by CodeV's consistent performance gains across various task domains and resolutions.
- **New Evaluation Standards**: The proposed faithfulness evaluation protocol offers a quantitative metric beyond traditional accuracy, providing a more comprehensive way to assess the quality of agentic visual reasoning. This can guide future research and development in the field.
- **Open-Source Contributions**: The release of CodeV's training recipe, TAPO implementation, datasets, and model facilitates reproducibility and encourages further innovation within the open-source community, accelerating progress in agentic VLM research.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the reliance on a static judge model for tool rewards and the current focus on image-centric operations. Future work could explore fine-tuning a dynamic reward model or self-critic to approximate the judge and extend the framework to a broader range of tool ecosystems (e.g., structured knowledge bases, dynamic web environments) by developing scalable and verifiable notions of tool faithfulness for diverse contexts.
