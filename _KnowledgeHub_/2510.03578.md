# Latent Mixture of Symmetries for Sample-Efficient Dynamic Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Mixture of Symmetries (Latent MoS) introduces a framework for sample-efficient dynamic learning by embedding Lie group theory into latent space dynamics, which captures a flexible mixture of local and multi-scale symmetries. This approach achieves superior performance in data-scarce environments and yields interpretable latent representations for complex systems.

## Problem

- Accurately learning dynamic models from sparse or low-resolution data, crucial for control and Reinforcement Learning (RL) in engineering systems.
- Existing Neural ODEs lack inductive biases for complex dynamics, while prior symmetry-based methods often assume a single, global symmetry or decouple symmetry discovery from dynamic modeling, leading to errors.
- Real-world systems frequently exhibit "symmetry breaking," where dynamic symmetries are multi-modal, context-dependent, or vary across different temporal scales.

## Method

- The Latent Mixture of Symmetries (**Latent MoS**) framework integrates ==Lie group== theory into latent ODEs, structuring the latent space dynamics with geometric priors.
- A Mixture-of-Experts block, featuring a time-variant gating mechanism and predefined Lie group experts (e.g., planar rotation, translation, scaling), models a flexible combination of local symmetries.
- A multi-scale hierarchical architecture captures symmetries across different temporal resolutions, addressing both short-term and long-term equivariance.

## Results

- Latent MoS consistently surpasses state-of-the-art Neural ODE and Transformer baselines in interpolation and extrapolation, showing **15%** to **97%** performance improvements, particularly under high data scarcity (e.g., **90%** data drop).
- The framework successfully learns interpretable latent trajectories that reflect geometric properties and effectively identifies relevant underlying symmetries through its gating mechanism, even with **90%** data drops.
- When integrated into model-based policy optimization for power system frequency control, Latent MoS achieves superior stability (deviation of -**0.024**) and lower control costs (**0.081**) compared to other model-based and model-free approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding latent dynamics in Lie theory, specifically through decomposing nonlinear group actions, simplifies the preservation of complex system symmetries within the latent space.
> - Equivariance is maintained by ensuring commutativity between latent dynamic functions and symmetry transformations, with practical sufficient conditions derived for affine Lie subgroups.
> - Capturing a mixture of local and multi-scale symmetries using a gated expert system is essential for modeling complex real-world dynamics that exhibit "symmetry breaking" rather than rigid global symmetries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Learning accurate dynamic models from limited measurements is a foundational challenge in various engineering disciplines, underpinning advancements in model-based control and Reinforcement Learning (RL) in fields like robotics and power systems. Real-world systems often suffer from data scarcity due to low-resolution sensors, communication constraints, or device aging, making sample-efficient learning paramount.

Existing approaches to dynamic modeling under data scarcity generally fall into four categories:
1. **Interpolation techniques:** These methods assume underlying data structures like sparsity, low-rankness, or manifold geometry to reconstruct missing or low-resolution data. However, they typically rely on strong, often domain-specific, assumptions.
2. **Physics-informed learning:** This category incorporates known physical laws as hard or soft constraints during the learning process. While powerful, it requires explicit knowledge of governing equations, which are not always available.
3. **Bayesian frameworks:** These methods integrate prior distributions to quantify uncertainty and improve learning [13–15]. Their effectiveness, however, is highly dependent on the accuracy of the specified priors.
4. **Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs):** This family of methods [16–19] constructs continuous-time dynamic processes using ODE solvers. While well-suited for sparse data, generic Neural ODE architectures often lack inductive biases, leading to poor generalization and overfitting on complex dynamics.

To overcome the limitations of generic Neural ODEs, the research community has increasingly explored the use of **symmetry as an inductive bias**. Symmetry, defined as transformations that leave a system's behavior equivariant, can significantly enhance sample efficiency by allowing a single state to represent a multitude of equivariant states. This has led to two main lines of work:
- **Enforcing known symmetries:** Equivariant neural networks [27–30] and symmetry-based data augmentation/regularization [34–37] require prior knowledge of the relevant symmetry groups.
- **Discovering unknown symmetries:** More recent methods attempt to learn symmetries directly from data [38–42], often relying on restricted search spaces or GAN-based frameworks.

This paper critically identifies two key limitations in existing symmetry-based dynamic learning, particularly when applied to complex systems:
1. **Decoupled learning and accumulated errors:** Symmetry discovery and dynamic modeling are often treated as separate processes, leading to error propagation and suboptimal integration.
2. **Limited expressiveness of symmetry representations:** Most approaches assume a single, global symmetry group governs the dynamics. In reality, complex systems exhibit "symmetry breaking," where transformations are multi-modal, context-dependent, or vary across different temporal scales (short- vs. long-term).

The presented work, Latent Mixture of Symmetries (Latent MoS), directly addresses these limitations. It bridges the gap between generic Neural ODEs and rigid symmetry-enforcing methods by offering a flexible, expressive framework that integrates the learning of dynamics with the *local and adaptive preservation of a mixture of symmetries*. By grounding its approach in Lie theory and introducing hierarchical mixture models, Latent MoS significantly advances the state-of-the-art in sample-efficient dynamic learning for complex, real-world systems that exhibit nuanced, rather than strict, symmetric behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the pressing need for sample-efficient dynamic learning in modern physical and control systems, especially when data is scarce or of low resolution. Real-world applications, such as robotics and smart grids, often face challenges like infrequent sensor measurements or communication failures, which hinder the accurate modeling of system dynamics. Without precise models, robust control, Reinforcement Learning, and critical safety analyses become difficult or impossible.

Against this backdrop, the authors propose Latent MoS with the following key objectives:

- **Enhance Sample Efficiency:** The overarching goal is to improve the ability of dynamic learning models to generalize and predict accurately with significantly less training data than traditional methods. This is crucial for real-world scenarios where data collection can be expensive, time-consuming, or physically constrained.
- **Integrate Symmetry Preservation into Dynamic Learning:** Unlike prior approaches that separate symmetry discovery from dynamic modeling, Latent MoS aims to intrinsically preserve underlying symmetric transformations directly within the dynamic learning process. This avoids error accumulation and fosters a more coherent model.
- **Capture Complex, Context-Dependent Symmetries:** To address the "symmetry breaking" observed in many real-world systems, the model seeks to go beyond the assumption of a single, global symmetry group. It aims to model a *mixture* of symmetry-governed latent factors that can vary locally and depend on the system's state or time.
- **Preserve Multi-Scale Equivariance:** The model intends to capture symmetry relations that manifest across different temporal scales – both short-term, localized transformations and longer-term, coarser-grained equivariances – through a hierarchical architecture.
- **Provide Interpretable Latent Representations:** By embedding geometric priors and distinct symmetry components into the latent space, the model aims to generate physically interpretable latent trajectories. This interpretability is vital for engineering applications, enabling deeper geometric and safety-critical analyses.
- **Outperform State-of-the-Art Baselines:** Empirically demonstrate that Latent MoS yields superior performance in standard dynamic learning tasks, such as interpolation and extrapolation, particularly under high data scarcity, compared to existing Neural ODE-based and Transformer-based models.

By achieving these objectives, Latent MoS aims to provide a powerful, flexible, and interpretable tool for modeling complex dynamical systems, especially those operating with limited observational data and exhibiting diverse, non-global symmetric properties.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Latent Mixture of Symmetries (Latent MoS) framework builds upon the principles of Latent ODEs but injects geometric priors to structure the latent dynamics, thereby enhancing sample efficiency. Its methodology can be broken down into several core components:

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundations in Lie Theory

The approach is grounded in Lie theory, which provides a mathematical framework for continuous symmetries.
- **Nonlinear Group Action Decomposition:** The paper adopts a decomposition of nonlinear group actions `π_s'(g, ·)` into `f_dec ◦ π_s(g) ◦ f_enc`. This allows complex nonlinear symmetries in the observation space (X) to be transformed into simpler, often linear, matrix multiplications `π_s(g)` in a learned latent space (Z). This simplifies the problem of learning symmetry-preserving dynamics.
- **Equivariance Preservation via Commutativity:** The core theoretical insight is that if a dynamic function `f_z` in the latent space commutes with a symmetry transformation `π_s(g)`, then equivariance is preserved. The paper formally defines the "centralizer" `C_G(h)` for a Lie group `H` of dynamic transformations within a symmetry group `G`. Lemma 1 proves that if the centralizer is nontrivial, then for any `g` in `C_G(h)`, the latent space equivariance `π_s(g)z(t_{i+1}) = f_z(π_s(g)z(t_i))` holds.
- **Sufficient Conditions for Commutativity:** Proposition 1 provides practical sufficient conditions for affine Lie subgroups (e.g., rotation, translation, scaling) to ensure a nontrivial centralizer. Specifically, if the linear parts of two affine transformations commute, and one's linear part leaves the other's translational component invariant, then the transformations commute. Corollaries 1-3 extend this to planar rotation, translation, scaling, and their second-order compositions, providing a rich set of predefined symmetry experts.

#### 3.2. Latent MoS Architecture

The Latent MoS model adapts the Latent ODE framework by replacing its generic ODE update rule with a structured Mixture of Symmetries (MoS) block.
- **Encoder (ODE-RNN):** Similar to Latent ODEs, an ODE-RNN serves as the encoder (`f_enc`), processing irregularly sampled time series data `{x(t_i)}` to infer the initial latent state `z(t_0)`.
- **Decoder with MoS Block:** The critical innovation lies in the decoder, which predicts future latent states (`˜z(t_{i+1})`). Instead of a single, unstructured `h_θ` function, the MoS block employs:
 - **Gating Mechanism (Mixture-of-Experts):** A gating neural network, whose output `h ∈ R^K` (Softmax activated) determines the weights for `K` different symmetry "experts." This mechanism is *time-variant* (`h(t^(l), z(t^(l)))`), allowing the model to adapt to different local symmetries across time segments.
 - **Symmetry Experts (`π_k`):** Each expert `k` is a parameterized Lie group action (represented as an affine matrix in homogeneous coordinates `[A_h b_h; 0 1]`). The paper specifically implements experts for:
 - **Planar Rotation:** A learned projection matrix `P ∈ R^(m×2)` defines a 2D subspace for rotation, combined with a learnable `R_θ ∈ SO(2)`. This ensures commutativity.
 - **Translation:** A learnable velocity vector `v ∈ R^m`.
 - **Scaling:** A learnable scaling ratio vector `γ ∈ R^m` for diagonal scaling.
 - **Second-Order Compositions:** Multiplications of these basic Lie group actions, such as `π_tra · π_sca`, are also included as experts, leveraging their inherent commutativity properties.
 - **Linear Mixture of Latent Flows:** The next latent state is computed as a linear combination of the outputs from these experts: `˜z(t_{i+1}) = Σ_{k=1}^K h[k] · ˆπ_k · ˜z(t_i)`. This linear mixing allows the model to capture scenarios where multiple latent symmetry flows act simultaneously (e.g., damping combined with oscillation).
- **Discrete vs. Continuous Updates:** While the primary formulation uses discrete updates, the framework supports continuous-time integration by computing the Lie algebra elements (infinitesimal generators) `ξ_k = log(π_k)` and approximating the matrix integral for discrete steps. This allows handling irregular sampling and interpolation.

#### 3.3. Multi-scale Latent MoS (Hierarchical Architecture)

To address short- and long-term equivariance, the model introduces a hierarchical structure (Algorithm 1).
- The initial latent state `˜z(t_0)` is partitioned into `S` sub-states `˜z^(s)(t_0)`.
- Each `˜z^(s)` is fed into a separate MoS block, operating at a different temporal resolution (`L^(s)`), meaning higher levels model dynamics over coarser time intervals (`∆T`).
- The trajectories generated by these multi-level MoS blocks are then concatenated along the feature dimension to form the final high-resolution latent sequence.

#### 3.4. Training

The model is trained by maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for probabilistic inference or minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) for deterministic dynamic learning, similar to Latent ODEs. Regularization techniques and specific initialization strategies are used to encourage sparse gating and smooth Lie group transitions.

In essence, Latent MoS replaces the "black-box" continuous function in Neural ODEs with a geometrically informed, interpretable, and flexible mixture of Lie group transformations. This design explicitly leverages the underlying symmetries to enhance sample efficiency and model complex, "symmetry-breaking" dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive numerical experiments across a diverse set of physical and real-world systems to validate the effectiveness of Latent MoS. The evaluation focused on interpolation (reconstructing full series from sparse observations) and extrapolation (predicting future states from sparse past data), particularly under varying data drop rates (30%, 60%, 90%).

#### 4.1. Effective Symmetry Preservation in the Latent Space

- **Interpretability:** Experiments on synthetic ODE systems (Spiral, Glycolytic Oscillators, Lotka-Volterra) demonstrate that Latent MoS successfully learns and preserves the correct underlying symmetries in the latent space. As shown in Fig. 2, even with 90% data drop, Latent MoS generates highly interpretable latent trajectories that clearly reflect the system's geometric properties (e.g., spiral or oscillatory patterns). In contrast, Latent ODE often yields unstructured and less interpretable latent representations.
- **Gating Mechanism Validation:** For the Glycolytic system, despite including multiple symmetry experts, the gating mechanism strongly concentrates its weight (0.97) on the correct underlying symmetry (a second-order transformation combining rotation and scaling), highlighting its ability to identify and prioritize relevant transformations.

#### 4.2. Overall Evaluations on Diverse Datasets

- **Superior Performance:** Latent MoS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including various Neural ODE derivatives (ODE-RNN, NCDE, Latent ODE, Contiformer) and Transformer-based models (Informer, Autoformer), in both interpolation (Fig. 3, Table 3) and extrapolation (Table 1) tasks.
- **Robustness to Data Scarcity:** The performance gains are particularly pronounced under high data drop rates (e.g., 90%), demonstrating Latent MoS's superior sample efficiency. Relative improvements range from 15% to an impressive 97% across different datasets.
- **Handling High-Frequency Dynamics:** Latent MoS effectively models systems with high-frequency oscillations (e.g., Glycolytic oscillators), a task where generic models like Contiformer and Latent ODE struggle due to the lack of structured inductive biases.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Contiformer and Latent ODE** perform relatively well due to their ability to reconstruct continuous latent trajectories, but Latent MoS's geometrically structured latent space provides a significant edge.
 - **Informer and Autoformer** (Transformer-based methods) show weaker performance at high drop rates because they rely on pre-interpolation and are not designed to directly model continuous underlying dynamics.
 - **EqSINDy and EqGP** are generally less competitive, as they involve decoupled symmetry discovery and symbolic regression, prone to error accumulation.

#### 4.3. Sensitivity Analysis

- **Noise Robustness:** Latent MoS demonstrates robustness to increasing levels of Gaussian noise, exhibiting a gradual rise in MSE and effectively approximating the underlying average trend, similar to Latent ODE.
- **Latent Dimensionality:** Latent MoS shows a sharp performance improvement when the latent dimensionality `m` increases beyond a certain threshold (e.g., `m > 5`), indicating that sufficient capacity is needed to approximate nonlinear Lie group actions while maintaining linear operations in Z. Latent ODE's performance is less sensitive to `m`.
- **Gating Mechanism Impact:** An optimal number of active experts (`K_0`) in the gating mechanism is crucial. While sparse gating (e.g., `K_0 = 4`) yields strong performance, allowing too many experts can lead to overfitting, confirming the importance of selective activation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Importance of Sparse Gates:** The study confirms that sparse gating is essential for the MoS framework, effectively selecting relevant symmetries.
- **Role of Individual Symmetries:** Removing necessary symmetry experts (e.g., rotational or scaling in the Glycolytic system) significantly degrades performance. Conversely, removing *unnecessary* experts (e.g., translational expert in Glycolytic dynamics) can further improve efficiency and robustness, indicating the potential for validation-based expert selection.

#### 4.5. Dynamic Learning for Control

- **Practical Application:** In an optimal frequency control problem in power systems, Latent MoS integrated into a model-based policy optimization (MBPO-MoS) framework significantly outperforms model-free PPO, MBPO with a Gaussian process (MBPO-GP), and MBPO with idealistic swing equations (MBPO-SE).
- **Improved Stability and Cost:** MBPO-MoS achieves superior stabilization of generator frequency (deviation of -0.024) and significantly lower control costs (0.081), demonstrating the direct practical impact of learning more accurate, symmetry-preserving dynamics for safety-critical control tasks.

Overall, the results strongly support the claims that Latent MoS provides a powerful, sample-efficient, and interpretable framework for dynamic learning in complex systems, especially those characterized by sparse data and nuanced, non-global symmetries.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Latent Mixture of Symmetries (Latent MoS) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of sample-efficient dynamic learning, particularly for complex physical and engineering systems. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

#### 5.1. Scientific and Technical Significance

- **Unified Framework for Symmetry Breaking:** Latent MoS addresses a critical gap by providing a principled, unified framework to handle "symmetry breaking" in real-world dynamics. It moves beyond the restrictive assumption of a single global symmetry, instead modeling a flexible mixture of local, time-variant, and multi-scale symmetries. This enhanced expressiveness makes it applicable to a broader range of complex systems.
- **Integration of Geometric Priors and Deep Learning:** By intrinsically embedding Lie group theory into the latent space dynamics of Neural ODEs, Latent MoS effectively combines the power of deep learning with the rigorous mathematical structure of symmetries. This integration leads to more robust, data-efficient, and interpretable models.
- **Provable Equivariance Guarantees:** The theoretical foundations laid out in the paper, particularly the conditions for commutativity in Lie groups, provide provable guarantees for local equivariance preservation. This is crucial for developing trustworthy models in safety-critical applications.
- **Interpretability of Latent Representations:** The structured nature of the latent space, governed by identifiable symmetry transformations (e.g., rotation, scaling), offers inherently interpretable latent trajectories. This interpretability is invaluable for engineers and scientists to understand the underlying physical mechanisms, which is often lacking in generic black-box deep learning models.

#### 5.2. Practical and Application Impact

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency for Data-Scarce Environments:** The most immediate practical impact is the demonstrated superior performance in low-resolution and sparsely sampled data scenarios. This directly addresses a pervasive challenge in fields like robotics, smart grids, and biomedical monitoring, where data acquisition can be costly, difficult, or limited.
- **Improved Model-Based Control and Reinforcement Learning:** By providing more accurate and robust dynamic models from limited data, Latent MoS can significantly improve the performance of model-based control and RL algorithms, as demonstrated in the power system frequency control problem. This has implications for developing more stable, efficient, and safe autonomous systems.
- **Foundation for Future Geometric and Safety Analyses:** The interpretable latent representations open avenues for advanced analyses of dynamical systems. Understanding the geometric properties in the latent space can inform stability analyses, safety critical assessments, and the characterization of contraction properties, which are essential for certifying system behavior.
- **Modular and Extensible Design:** The modular architecture of Latent MoS, particularly the mixture-of-experts design, allows for the flexible incorporation of new or more complex Lie group experts. This ensures the framework can evolve and adapt as new domain knowledge or computational techniques emerge.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors)

- **Applicability Scope:** Latent MoS is primarily designed for systems exhibiting at least local symmetries. While this covers a vast majority of real-world physical, biological, and engineered systems (due to conservation laws or structural regularities), purely chaotic or stochastic processes entirely lacking symmetry might fall outside its optimal application.
- **Predefined Symmetry Candidates:** The current framework relies on a predefined set of candidate Lie group transformations. While this set is rich, it may not encompass the full spectrum of possible Lie group representations relevant to all complex systems.
- **Future Directions:** The authors propose two key areas for future work:
 1. **Automated Symmetry Selection:** Developing mechanisms to automatically refine and constrain the candidate set of symmetries (e.g., via validation-based selection or search strategies) to optimize performance when true symmetries are uncertain.
 2. **Broader Geometric Analyses:** Expanding the application of the interpretable symmetry structures for more comprehensive geometric analyses, including stability, safety, and contraction properties, further enhancing the trustworthiness and utility of the learned models.

In conclusion, Latent MoS offers a principled, robust, and empirically validated approach to tackle the fundamental problem of dynamic learning under data scarcity. By innovatively integrating Lie theory with deep learning to capture a rich mixture of symmetries, it paves the way for more efficient, interpretable, and reliable AI systems in a wide array of engineering and scientific domains.
