# Latent Mixture of Symmetries for Sample-Efficient Dynamic Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Mixture of Symmetries (Latent MoS) introduces a framework for sample-efficient dynamic learning by embedding Lie group theory into latent space dynamics, which captures a flexible mixture of local and multi-scale symmetries. This approach achieves superior performance in data-scarce environments and yields interpretable latent representations for complex systems.

## Problem

- Accurately learning dynamic models from sparse or low-resolution data, crucial for control and Reinforcement Learning (RL) in engineering systems.
- Existing Neural ODEs lack inductive biases for complex dynamics, while prior symmetry-based methods often assume a single, global symmetry or decouple symmetry discovery from dynamic modeling, leading to errors.
- Real-world systems frequently exhibit "symmetry breaking," where dynamic symmetries are multi-modal, context-dependent, or vary across different temporal scales.

## Method

- The Latent Mixture of Symmetries (**Latent MoS**) framework integrates ==Lie group== theory into latent ODEs, structuring the latent space dynamics with geometric priors.
- A Mixture-of-Experts block, featuring a time-variant gating mechanism and predefined Lie group experts (e.g., planar rotation, translation, scaling), models a flexible combination of local symmetries.
- A multi-scale hierarchical architecture captures symmetries across different temporal resolutions, addressing both short-term and long-term equivariance.

## Results

- Latent MoS consistently surpasses state-of-the-art Neural ODE and Transformer baselines in interpolation and extrapolation, showing **15%** to **97%** performance improvements, particularly under high data scarcity (e.g., **90%** data drop).
- The framework successfully learns interpretable latent trajectories that reflect geometric properties and effectively identifies relevant underlying symmetries through its gating mechanism, even with **90%** data drops.
- When integrated into model-based policy optimization for power system frequency control, Latent MoS achieves superior stability (deviation of -**0.024**) and lower control costs (**0.081**) compared to other model-based and model-free approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding latent dynamics in Lie theory, specifically through decomposing nonlinear group actions, simplifies the preservation of complex system symmetries within the latent space.
> - Equivariance is maintained by ensuring commutativity between latent dynamic functions and symmetry transformations, with practical sufficient conditions derived for affine Lie subgroups.
> - Capturing a mixture of local and multi-scale symmetries using a gated expert system is essential for modeling complex real-world dynamics that exhibit "symmetry breaking" rather than rigid global symmetries.
