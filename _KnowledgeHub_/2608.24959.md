# GaussVLA: Geometry-Aware Spatial Reasoning for Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> GaussVLA is a Mamba-based vision-language-action policy that replaces flat 2D patch tokens with 3D Gaussian spatial tokens carrying position, covariance and confidence, plus a non-autoregressive depth-aware chain-of-thought module that conditions the flow-matching action head. With 200M parameters it reaches 93.5% average LIBERO success and 100% on the Spatial suite, beating far larger VLA baselines.

## Problem

- VLA visual front-ends emit flat 2D patch tokens lacking metric geometry, treating all patches alike regardless of depth, surface orientation or geometric reliability.
- Prior geometry injection uses per-pixel scalar depth or pseudo point clouds, encoding distance but not surface structure or the depth estimator's spatially varying confidence.
- Autoregressive chain-of-thought reasons in 2D image space, adds seconds of latency per step, and stays decoupled from action decoding without direct spatial supervision.

## Method

- ==Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer== (**GST**) lifts frozen SigLIP semantic and Depth Anything V2 features into ==3D Gaussian primitives== with learnable mean offset, log-scale covariance and opacity, refined by ==spatial attention pooling==.
- ==Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought== (**DA-CoT**) uses a few learnable queries that cross-attend to Gaussian tokens under joint language and flow-time conditioning, producing supervised ==non-autoregressive reasoning tokens==.
- A ==Mamba== selective ==state-space backbone== gives linear-time multimodal sequence modeling, with global cross-attention to the 256-primitive Gaussian field and a ==flow-matching== velocity-field action head.

## Results

- On LIBERO it averages **93.5%** success with **100.0%** Spatial, **95.8%** Object, **95.3%** Goal, **83.0%** Long, exceeding the best prior average by **4.9** points at **200M** trainable parameters.
- Ablation: vanilla 2D-token model scores **78.1%** LIBERO / **11.2%** LIBERO-PRO; adding GST alone gives **90.5%** / **29.0%**, DA-CoT alone **82.1%** / **16.7%**, both **93.5%** / **33.3%** at **12.97ms** latency.
- Real-world SO-101 multi-task success averages **58.8%** versus **46.3%** for SpatialVLA and **35.7%** for ACT; Pick-Place reaches **81.0%** in-distribution and **46.7%** out-of-distribution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured geometry beats raw depth: concatenating scalar depth cut the LIBERO average from 78.1 to 73.3, while Gaussian primitives with confidence-weighted pooling lifted it to 90.5.
> - Reasoning need not be autoregressive text; a handful of supervised latent queries conditioned on flow time captures spatial reasoning at millisecond cost.
> - Strong in-distribution geometry gains do not guarantee robustness: LIBERO-PRO position and task perturbations still drive success near zero for every method tested.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen significant advancements through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which learn policies from demonstrations by leveraging visual observations and language conditioning. These models often build upon pretrained vision-language backbones, enabling generalizable skills. However, a common limitation in existing VLA models is their visual front-end, which typically projects RGB observations into a flat sequence of 2D patch tokens. This representation, while capturing appearance and semantic information, intrinsically lacks explicit metric 3D geometry, surface orientation, or geometric reliability. Consequently, this can restrict a policy's capacity for precise spatial reasoning, particularly in tasks demanding fine-grained manipulation.

Previous attempts to address the geometric limitations include augmenting visual tokens with monocular depth estimates or incorporating additional reasoning mechanisms. However, monocular depth is often treated as a scalar per-pixel signal, encoding primarily distance without explicit surface orientation or geometric reliability, and typically neglects the confidence of depth predictions. Reasoning mechanisms, such as autoregressive Chain-of-Thought (CoT), frequently operate in 2D image space, incurring latency due to sequential token generation and remaining decoupled from direct action decoding. Furthermore, many state-of-the-art VLA backbones, especially those based on transformers, suffer from quadratic computational costs with respect to sequence length and growing memory requirements for their key-value caches, limiting their efficiency for long, structured multimodal sequences.

GaussVLA positions itself within this landscape by proposing a method that fundamentally re-structures how VLA models perceive and reason about 3D space. It directly tackles the limitations of 2D visual tokens and scalar depth by introducing a structured 3D spatial representation using Gaussian primitives. It also integrates a novel reasoning mechanism, Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT), designed to perform structured geometric reasoning efficiently and non-autoregressively, anchoring it directly to action prediction. By adopting a Mamba-based backbone, GaussVLA aims to overcome the computational inefficiencies of transformer architectures, enabling linear-time sequence modeling for handling rich 3D and reasoning traces without prohibitive latency. This work thus represents an effort to enhance geometric understanding and reasoning capabilities within VLA models while improving computational efficiency, aiming to bridge the gap between perception of 3D geometry and precise robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of advanced geometric reasoning for robotic manipulation tasks, while maintaining computational efficiency. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations that address identified limitations in existing VLA frameworks:

1. **Overcome Limitations of 2D Visual Representations**: Current VLA models predominantly encode visual observations as flat 2D patch tokens. These tokens effectively capture appearance and semantic content but inherently lack explicit metric 3D geometry, surface orientation, or information regarding geometric reliability. This absence of structured spatial data limits the policy's capacity for precise spatial reasoning, which is crucial for complex manipulation tasks. The motivation is to introduce a representation that explicitly encodes these 3D properties.

2. **Enhance the Utility of Depth Information**: While some VLA models integrate monocular depth estimates, this information is typically treated as a per-pixel scalar value. Such scalar representations primarily convey distance, failing to capture critical spatial cues like local surface orientation or the confidence associated with depth predictions. The motivation is to leverage depth in a more structured and confidence-aware manner, allowing the model to interpret local geometry and its reliability.

3. **Integrate Structured and Efficient Spatial Reasoning**: Existing VLA pipelines often encode spatial relationships implicitly, requiring downstream policies to infer complex geometric structures from unstructured data. Furthermore, explicit reasoning mechanisms, such as autoregressive Chain-of-Thought (CoT), can introduce significant latency and are frequently decoupled from the action decoding process, operating primarily in 2D image space. A key motivation is to devise a structured, non-autoregressive reasoning module that explicitly processes 3D spatial relationships and is tightly integrated with action generation, thereby resolving the trade-off between interpretability and inference latency.

4. **Improve Computational Efficiency of the Policy Backbone**: Standard transformer-based VLA backbones incur a quadratic computational cost with respect to sequence length and require a key-value cache that grows proportionally with the chunk horizon. This can impede real-time performance and limit the integration of long, diverse input sequences (e.g., mixing language, visual patches, and 3D tokens). The motivation is to employ a more efficient architectural backbone, such as a state-space model, to enable linear-time sequence modeling and constant-size hidden states, thereby improving throughput and computational footprint.

5. **Achieve Superior Performance and Reliability in Robot Manipulation**: The ultimate motivation is to translate these representational and architectural improvements into higher success rates and greater robustness in robotic manipulation tasks. This includes demonstrating strong performance in simulation benchmarks requiring precise spatial understanding, generalization across varying task difficulties and long horizons, and reliable execution in real-world settings under sensor noise and distribution shifts.

By addressing these points, GaussVLA aims to provide a more robust, geometrically aware, and efficient framework for robot learning that can generalize across diverse manipulation challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GaussVLA is proposed as an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework designed to enhance robotic policy learning through structured 3D spatial representation and reasoning-aware action generation. The methodology integrates three key components within a unified, efficient architecture.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system learns a policy π_θ to predict a future action sequence ˆA based on a window of RGB observations (O), a language instruction (s), and expert action trajectories (A). The action generation is framed as learning a conditional velocity field for flow-matching, interpolating between the expert action (A) and Gaussian noise (ε) to form an interpolated action state Z_τ. The model predicts a velocity field v_θ(Z_τ, O, l, τ) to guide action generation.

#### 3.2. Dual-Stream Visual Encoding

To overcome the limitations of flat 2D visual tokens, GaussVLA employs a dual-stream approach for visual perception:
- **Semantic Stream**: A frozen, pretrained patch encoder (SigLIP-SO400M/14) processes each RGB observation x_t^(m) to extract patch-level visual features F_t^(m) ∈ R^(P×d_s), capturing appearance and high-level semantic information.
- **Geometric Stream**: A frozen, pretrained monocular depth encoder (Depth-Anything-V2 ViT-L) estimates a depth map from the same RGB input. This depth map is then sampled at the centers of the P image patches to obtain patch-aligned depth values d_t^(m) ∈ R^P.
This process yields a combined representation (f_t,r^(m), d_t,r^(m)) for each patch.

#### 3.3. Gaussian Spatial Tokenization (GST)

GST converts the dense 2D patch and depth features into structured 3D Gaussian tokens:
- **Depth Correction and Back-projection**: A learnable affine correction (λ_d, β_d) is applied to the raw monocular depth d_t,r^(m) to compensate for scale bias, yielding ˜d_t,r^(m). This corrected depth, along with the 2D image coordinates u_r and the camera intrinsic matrix K^(m), is used to back-project each patch center into 3D camera coordinates c_t,r^(m).
- **Gaussian Parameter Prediction**: A learnable Gaussian parameter head (f_par), conditioned on the semantic feature f_t,r^(m), predicts three components for each Gaussian primitive:
 - A residual mean offset ∆μ_t,r^(m) ∈ R^3.
 - A log-scale vector η_t,r^(m) ∈ R^3, which defines the Gaussian's spatial extent and local geometric structure (covariance).
 - An opacity score α_t,r^(m) ∈ (0,1), representing a depth-aware confidence in the local geometry.
- **Gaussian Primitive Definition**: The Gaussian mean μ_t,r^(m) is computed by adding the bounded residual offset to the back-projected 3D coordinate. The Gaussian scale σ_t,r^(m) is derived from exp(η_t,r^(m)).
- **3D Positional Encoding**: A 3D Fourier positional encoding γ(μ_t,r^(m)) is applied to each Gaussian mean to explicitly incorporate 3D spatial awareness.
- **Geometry-Aware Patch Token Formation**: The semantic feature f_t,r^(m), 3D positional encoding γ(μ_t,r^(m)), and log-scale vector η_t,r^(m) are concatenated to form a geometry-aware patch token g_t,r^(m).
- **Spatial Attention Pooling**: These tokens are projected into a pooling space. N_g learnable spatial queries (Q) then cross-attend to these tokens. Crucially, the opacity term α_t,r^(m) is incorporated as a reliability bias (log(α_t^(m))) during the attention computation, allowing the pooling mechanism to weigh geometrically reliable regions more heavily. This yields a compact set of pooled GST features Z_t^(m). All such tokens across observations and views form the structured GST representation Z_GST.

#### 3.4. Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT)

DA-CoT is introduced to extract and integrate task-relevant spatial relations into the action decoding process:
- **Context Generation**: The flattened GST representation ¯Z_GST, along with the language embedding l and flow time τ (encoded as φ_τ), are projected and concatenated to form a multimodal context C_τ.
- **Reasoning Query Refinement**: N_r learnable reasoning queries (Y^(0)) are iteratively refined through multi-head self-attention, cross-attention to C_τ, and a feed-forward network. This process produces R_τ, a set of structured, non-autoregressive reasoning tokens. These tokens are designed to summarize 3D scene reasoning, conditioned on both the task instruction and the current flow time, allowing the policy to focus on critical spatial relationships.

#### 3.5. GaussVLA (Unified Framework)

All components are integrated into a unified Mamba-based policy backbone:
- **Token Alignment**: GST observation tokens, DA-CoT reasoning tokens, language embedding, flow time embedding, and interpolated action state tokens are all mapped to a common backbone dimension d_b and concatenated to form the multimodal policy input Y_τ.
- **Mamba Backbone**: A Mamba-based architecture processes Y_τ. Mamba's linear-time selective scan and constant-size hidden state provide computational efficiency compared to attention-based transformers, which is beneficial for handling the long, structured sequences.
- **Action Decoder**: An action decoder refines the action-specific backbone states by cross-attending to the contextual states (non-action tokens), the DA-CoT tokens, and the time embedding.
- **Velocity Prediction**: A velocity head then predicts the conditional flow velocity v_θ, which is used for flow-matching action generation.

#### 3.6. Training Objectives

GaussVLA is trained with a multi-objective loss function:
- **Flow-Matching Loss (L_FM)**: The primary objective, minimizing the L2 distance between the model's predicted velocity field v_θ and the ground-truth target velocity U.
- **GST Depth-Consistency Loss (L_GST)**: An auxiliary opacity-weighted L1 loss that enforces consistency between the z-coordinate of each Gaussian mean μ_t,r,z^(m) and its affine-corrected depth ˜d_t,r^(m). The opacity α_t,r^(m) weights this loss, implicitly encouraging reliable geometric predictions.
- **DA-CoT Supervision Loss (L_CoT)**: An auxiliary L2 loss that encourages the DA-CoT tokens to be predictive of the target flow velocity. A mean summary of the projected DA-CoT tokens is used to predict an auxiliary velocity ˆU_CoT, which is then supervised against the flow-matching target velocity U.
The final training objective is a weighted sum of these three losses.

The implementation utilizes frozen SigLIP for semantic encoding, Depth-Anything-V2 for depth, and CLIP for language. Parameters for GST include N_g=128 Gaussian queries, and DA-CoT uses N_r=4 reasoning queries. The Mamba backbone has 5 blocks with a dimension of 512. The model predicts 7-DoF actions over a horizon of H=10.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GaussVLA was conducted across various benchmarks, including simulation environments (LIBERO, Meta-World, CALVIN) and real-world robot platforms (SO-101), assessing performance in spatial manipulation, generalization, robustness, and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **LIBERO Benchmark**: GaussVLA achieved an average success rate of 93.5%, which is an improvement over the strongest prior average (QueST at 88.6%). Notably, it obtained 100.0% success on the Spatial suite, 95.8% on Object, and 95.3% on Goal tasks, indicating proficient geometric reasoning. On the Long suite, it achieved 83.0%. These results were accomplished with 200M total parameters (179M trainable), a substantially lower count compared to several larger VLA baselines ranging from 3.3B to 7B parameters.
- **Meta-World**: The model demonstrated strong generalization, achieving 92.7% success on the Easy split. Its performance remained competitive as task difficulty increased, securing the second-best average performance among compared methods and a balanced profile across Easy (92.7%), Mid (47.9%), Hard (37.3%), and Very Hard (41.6%) tasks.
- **CALVIN**: For long-horizon sequential control, GaussVLA ranked second in completing 1-4 consecutive tasks, achieving an average sequence length of 1.474. It showed improved 1, 2, 3, and 4-task completion rates compared to some baselines, suggesting enhanced temporal robustness across multi-step rollouts.

#### 4.2. Robustness and Real-World Evaluation

- **LIBERO-PRO**: On this benchmark, designed to test robustness under substantial distribution shifts (object appearance, position, semantics, task, environment), GaussVLA achieved an average success rate of 0.33. While not the highest score, this indicates meaningful transfer capabilities under distribution shift with its relatively lightweight architecture.
- **Real-World SO-101 Robot**: GaussVLA achieved the highest success rate across real-world multi-task categories, with an average of 58.8%, surpassing SpatialVLA (46.3%) and ACT (35.7%). Specific gains were observed in Sorting (15.7 points over SpatialVLA) and Pick-Place tasks (81.0% on in-distribution and 46.7% on out-of-distribution trials, outperforming SpatialVLA by 12.0 and 10.9 points, respectively). These results indicate the model's stability and effectiveness for physical robot execution under real camera noise and execution uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

A series of ablation studies investigated the individual contributions of the Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer (GST) and Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT) modules:
- **GST Contribution**: Removing both GST and DA-CoT (Vanilla GaussVLA) yielded 78.1% on LIBERO and 11.2% on LIBERO-PRO. Adding GST alone significantly improved performance to 90.5% on LIBERO and 29.0% on LIBERO-PRO, indicating that geometry-aware Gaussian tokens are the dominant source of improvement, especially for robustness.
- **DA-CoT Contribution**: Adding DA-CoT alone (without GST) to the vanilla model improved LIBERO performance to 82.1% and LIBERO-PRO to 16.7%. This suggests that structured depth-aware reasoning provides a complementary benefit to action prediction, even when the visual representation is 2D.
- **Combined Effect**: The full GaussVLA, combining both GST and DA-CoT, achieved the highest performance (93.5% on LIBERO, 33.3% on LIBERO-PRO), demonstrating their synergistic effect.
- **Efficiency Analysis**: The full GaussVLA increased inference latency only modestly from 10.85 ms (vanilla) to 12.97 ms, with trainable parameters increasing from 158M to 179M, suggesting that performance gains are attributable to the design rather than solely increased capacity.
- **Sensitivity to Query Numbers**: Experiments confirmed that N_g=128 for GST queries and N_r=4 for DA-CoT reasoning queries represent a near-optimal trade-off between accuracy and efficiency.
- **GST Design Ablation**: Simply concatenating scalar depth to 2D patch tokens actually reduced overall LIBERO performance (78.1% to 73.3%), while full GST with 3D lifting, Gaussian parameterization, and confidence-aware pooling yielded significant gains. This indicates that structured, geometry-aware tokens are critical, rather than raw depth features.
- **DA-CoT Design Ablation**: Structured reasoning alone improved performance, while adding depth-aware grounding to DA-CoT provided a larger boost. Explicit CoT supervision further refined these gains, emphasizing the importance of both depth grounding and supervised reasoning.
- **Confidence Bias**: Removing the opacity-weighted pooling in GST resulted in performance drops on both LIBERO and LIBERO-PRO, confirming its role in robustness. The learned opacity α was also shown to correlate strongly with independent depth-uncertainty proxies, supporting its interpretation as a depth-aware reliability signal.
- **Representation Probing**: Linear probes revealed that DA-CoT tokens encoded richer 3D geometric structure (e.g., object center, gripper-object distance, object orientation) than flat 2D patches or pooled GST. The learned Gaussian covariance also showed a non-trivial correlation with ground-truth surface normals, providing mechanistic evidence for geometry-aware representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GaussVLA introduces a novel approach to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly incorporating geometry-aware spatial reasoning, presenting several significant implications for robot learning and manipulation.

Firstly, the core contribution of GaussVLA lies in its **structured 3D spatial representation** through the Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer (GST). By lifting conventional 2D visual observations into compact, anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives that encode spatial position, local geometry, and depth-aware confidence, GaussVLA addresses a fundamental limitation of current VLA models that rely on flat 2D patch tokens. This explicit geometric encoding enables the policy to operate with a richer understanding of the physical environment, which is critical for tasks requiring fine-grained contact and precise object interaction.

Secondly, the introduction of **Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT)** offers a structured, non-autoregressive reasoning mechanism. This module allows the policy to perform intermediate geometric reasoning conditioned by language and flow-time, integrating these spatial relations directly into the action generation process. This design resolves the trade-off between interpretable reasoning and inference latency that often characterizes autoregressive 2D reasoning approaches. The ability to anchor reasoning representations to the action-prediction objective through supervised multimodal tokens can lead to more intentional and robust robot behaviors.

Thirdly, the **integration of these modules within an efficient Mamba-based backbone** is a notable technical advancement. By replacing attention-based architectures with a linear-time selective scan, GaussVLA achieves improved computational efficiency and real-time inference capabilities (12.97 ms/step) while processing complex multimodal sequences. This efficiency, combined with its parameter-light design (200M total parameters), makes advanced VLA capabilities more accessible for deployment on hardware with limited computational resources, promoting broader applicability.

The **empirical results** demonstrate the practical impact of these innovations. Achieving 93.5% average success on LIBERO, including 100.0% on the Spatial suite, signifies a substantial improvement in handling geometrically demanding manipulation tasks. The strong performance on Meta-World and CALVIN benchmarks, coupled with successful real-world deployment on an SO-101 robot, including superior robustness in pick-and-place and sorting tasks, indicates that GaussVLA's approach generalizes effectively across diverse settings and bridges the sim-to-real gap more reliably.

From a broader perspective, GaussVLA provides an effective step towards **geometry-grounded VLA models** for reliable robot manipulation. Its focus on explicit 3D spatial awareness and integrated reasoning offers a foundational framework for future research. While the study identifies ongoing challenges in robustness under severe distribution shifts (e.g., LIBERO-PRO position and task-level perturbations), particularly regarding viewpoint generalization and language-side robustness, it also outlines clear avenues for future exploration, such as enhanced camera calibration, viewpoint augmentation, and adaptive geometric reasoning. Ultimately, this work contributes to the development of more capable and deployable robotic systems that can operate intelligently and robustly in complex physical environments.
