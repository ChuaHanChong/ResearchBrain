# 4DSynth: Controllable Procedural World Synthesis for Dynamic Embodied Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> 4DSynth is a controllable procedural system that turns text, blueprint masks, or a single photograph into editable 4D environments: explicit geometry, animated actors, collision-free trajectories, and physics-ready OpenUSD export. Four scene-realization routes converge on one shared Stage and provenance-preserving WorldState, so animation, camera planning, rendering, and task generation reuse the same geometry. Its 4DSynth-Nav benchmark of 333 tasks defeats both tested vision-language agents.

## Problem

- Procedural simulators, language-guided scene tools, and dynamic human-motion simulators each live in separate scene representations and authoring pipelines that do not compose.
- Text-to-4D generators output deformable radiance or Gaussian fields meant for novel-view rendering, lacking explicit objects, walkability, editable parameters, and simulator collision geometry.
- Existing real-to-sim reconstruction preserves appearance but drops openings and secondary furniture, and yields scenes that cannot be re-parameterized, animated, or physics-exported.

## Method

- Unified authoring architecture: four realization routes (native indoor, native outdoor, layout-conditioned from text or masks, single-image real-to-sim) map into one shared ==Stage== plus a persistent provenance-preserving ==WorldState==.
- Geometry-grounded 4D synthesis measures each imported character (stature, radius, sole offset, root speed), erodes the walkable set per actor, and plans ==A* collision-free actor tracks== plus validated camera trajectories from nine composable primitives.
- Real-to-sim compiler fuses ==monocular metric depth==, ==open-vocabulary segmentation==, and object-centric 3D proxies into a ==PerceivedScene==, grounding each instance to a procedural factory, then aligns materials after freezing geometry.

## Results

- 4DSynth-Nav contains **333** automatically validated tasks over **113** unique scenes drawn from **122** physics-enabled scenes, spanning three tiers and three task types with animated obstacle configurations.
- Gemini 3.1 Pro reaches **33.3%** success and **40.7%** subtask progress versus Qwen3-VL-30B's **13.2%** and **22.4%**, cutting mean final goal distance from **4.27 m** to **2.68 m**.
- Both models stall mid-episode: subtask progress far exceeds success (Qwen **15.0%** vs **4.5%** on L3), and the hardest tier combines a blind start with two-phase objectives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Keeping the scene representation explicit and procedural, rather than purely visual, is what lets object identity, support relations, and physics annotations survive from generation through simulation.
> - Physical properties can be read off procedural material parameters instead of hand-labeled, so appearance and physics annotation share one source with no separate labeling pass.
> - Success rate alone hides failure structure: partial subtask progress, collision counts, and looping trajectories expose that agents misjudge task completion rather than fail navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied artificial intelligence (AI) agents necessitates environments that are visually diverse, physically interactive, and dynamic. Existing research has addressed various aspects of this requirement:
- **Embodied Simulation Platforms:** Platforms like AI2-THOR, Habitat, iGibson 2.0, and SAPIEN provide environments for navigation, interaction, and robot learning, primarily focusing on execution speed, articulated objects, and reusable task APIs over pre-existing scene collections.
- **Procedural and Language-Guided Environments:** Systems such as ProcTHOR and Infinigen generate large collections of interactive or photorealistic scenes (indoor and outdoor) procedurally. Holodeck utilizes language models to translate prompts into relational constraints for 3D asset retrieval. These approaches focus on scene generation but often lack integration with downstream animation and simulation pipelines.
- **4D Generation and Real-to-Sim:** Recent text-to-4D methods produce visually dynamic representations using diffusion guidance and deformable fields for novel-view rendering. Concurrently, real-to-sim approaches like Digital Cousins and VIGA aim to reconstruct scenes for policy learning or through inverse-graphics reasoning. These methods either prioritize visual dynamics without explicit geometry or reconstruct scenes without ensuring editability or physics-readiness.
- **Embodied Simulation with Dynamic Agents:** Benchmarks like BEHAVIOR-1K, OmniGibson, and Habitat 3.0 incorporate physics-rich activities, humanoids, and collaborative tasks.

4DSynth positions itself as complementary to these existing efforts. Instead of focusing on simulation execution, pre-existing scene collections, purely visual 4D generation, or broad activity benchmarks, it addresses the challenge of creating the editable, physics-ready 4D world itself. It aims to unify various scene authoring methods (procedural generation, layout-conditioned synthesis, real-to-sim compilation) into a single, geometry-grounded pipeline that supports animation, camera planning, rendering, and task generation, preserving explicit object identity, support relations, and collision geometry throughout. The resulting benchmark, 4DSynth-Nav, specifically targets navigation and pick-and-place tasks with animated obstacles, serving as a diagnostic tool for visual agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for 4DSynth stems from the observation that while individual capabilities for generating interactive scenes (procedural generation), dynamic visuals (4D generators), or language-guided scene specification exist, combining these properties into a single, editable, and controllable environment still requires extensive manual effort. Furthermore, the diverse scene representations and authoring pipelines used by different systems often prevent seamless integration and reuse at scale.

The key objectives of the 4DSynth research are:

1. **To develop a unified procedural authoring architecture:** The goal is to integrate multiple scene realization routes—native indoor/outdoor generation, layout-conditioned synthesis from text or masks, and single-image real-to-sim compilation—into a single, shared Stage and a provenance-preserving WorldState. This aims to ensure that all routes feed into a consistent downstream pipeline for animation, camera planning, rendering, and task generation, avoiding the fragmentation of capabilities.
2. **To achieve geometry-grounded 4D synthesis:** The system aims to measure animated assets accurately, plan collision-free actor and camera trajectories based on the final scene geometry, and derive physics-ready OpenUSD output directly from procedural scene semantics. This objective ensures that the generated environments are not just visually dynamic but also physically consistent and suitable for simulation.
3. **To construct a scalable, automatically validated benchmark:** The research seeks to create 4DSynth-Nav, a suite of navigation and pick-and-place tasks with animated obstacles, generated entirely from 4DSynth's procedural scenes. This benchmark is intended to be used for evaluating embodied agents, specifically to diagnose failure modes and to enable independent tuning of difficulty axes due to its procedural controllability.

In essence, 4DSynth aims to bridge the gap between diverse environment generation methods and the requirements of dynamic, interactive embodied simulation, providing a practical foundation for developing and evaluating advanced embodied AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

4DSynth operates as a two-tier execution pipeline designed to transform user conditions into editable, animated 4D environments, complete with consistent observations and physics-ready simulation states.

**System Architecture Overview:**
The pipeline begins with a **PARSE** stage, which normalizes user conditions (natural-language instructions, optional blueprint masks, or single photographs) into a schema-validated 4D specification, covering scene, actors, actions, goals, and camera intent. This specification feeds into Tier 1 and Tier 2. A persistent **WorldState** maintains all intermediate results and their provenance throughout the pipeline.

**Tier 1: Scene Construction**
This tier focuses on realizing the 3D scene geometry.
- **Scene Realization Routes:** 4DSynth supports four routes, falling into two families:
 - **Native Generative Source:** For category-level requests, the system synthesizes scenes procedurally. This includes indoor scenes (single rooms or multi-room homes furnished via constraint-based placement using Infinigen Indoors) and outdoor scenes (one of eight terrain biomes with vegetation and scatter assets using Infinigen). Random seeds are recorded for reproducibility.
 - **Prebuilt-Scene Compilation:** This family processes scenes derived from external inputs:
 - **Text- and Mask-Conditioned Indoor Synthesis:** This route accepts scene-layout descriptions (processed by a constrained language model to propose rooms, openings, object footprints, and relations) or annotated masks (where spatial layout is fixed, and missing semantics are inferred). Both are normalized into a **Canonical Layout Intermediate Representation (IR)**. This IR guides a three-stage process:
 1. **Structure construction:** Builds the room shell and openings.
 2. **Placement:** Grounds categories to procedural factories, sizes assets to footprints, and instantiates them under constraints (orientation, containment, support).
 3. **Completion:** Optionally adds compatible content not specified in the input.
 This is followed by geometry validation, material assignment, and lighting.
 - **Real-to-Sim Compilation from a Single Photograph:** This route aims to approximate a specific room from an RGB image. Hybrid perception extracts monocular metric depth, open-vocabulary segmentation identifies objects and openings, and object-centric reconstruction lifts instances to posed 3D proxies. A multimodal agent maps observed instances to procedural factories. Metric and semantic reasoning combine this evidence into a **PerceivedScene**, which anchors reconstructed proxies to depth, corrects scale bias, estimates support relations, and defines room boundaries and openings. This is then mapped to the procedural backend. Realization instantiates assets to their observed footprints, resolves collisions, and then aligns appearance by selecting native material implementations and calibrating albedo/exposure against the source view. The compiled scene remains fully parametric and editable.
- **Outdoor Finalization:** Native outdoor scenes introduce a coarse-to-final loop. A coarse proxy scene supports provisional actor routes and camera views. Fine terrain, ground cover, population, and camera-local creatures are then materialized only within the view frusta of these provisional plans. A new **Stage** (the internal record of geometry, walkability, and content) is extracted, and actors and cameras are replanned on this detailed geometry. Failures in planning lead to constraint relaxation or re-planning within the same scene, with a different scene seed only attempted if initial physical support is absent.

**Tier 2: 4D Realization**
This tier integrates animation and camera motion with the finalized scene geometry.
- **Stage Extraction:** The evaluated scene geometry is distilled into a **Stage**, which includes a metric support grid (ground height, walkability), an object inventory, and geometry for collision and visibility queries. Walkability is defined based on stable, level support, avoiding submersion or solid geometry occupied by an actor. Actor-specific walkable sets are derived by eroding this set based on the actor's radius.
- **4D Agent Assets:** Requested actions are mapped to motion-captured humanoid clips from a curated library. Each character is measured post-import (stature, collision radius, ground speed) to drive Stage clearance, route erosion, inter-agent separation, and terrain grounding.
- **Collision-Aware Trajectory Planning:** Actors are placed and routed on the Stage within a feasible region, which can be constrained by preference layers. Goal-directed actors use A\* search between reachable anchors identified from the Stage inventory. Paths are smoothed but validated to remain collision-free.
- **Multi-Agent Coordination and Baking:** For multiple actors, reactive separation coordinates motion, treating stationary actors as obstacles. Tracks are then "baked" by removing root displacement, repeating animation cycles, aligning heading, and grounding feet to the terrain height field.
- **Geometry-Valid Observation Synthesis:** Camera intent (composed of primitives like orbit, turn, zoom) is separated from geometric feasibility. Two planning modes are available:
 - **Geometry-exact mode:** Requires the camera origin to stay above support and outside solid scene geometry; invalid poses are projected to feasible ones.
 - **Default fast mode:** Evaluates 11 deterministic whole-trajectory transforms. Terrain is a hard constraint. Occlusions are treated as soft costs, preferring clear views but allowing foreground occluders. Target framing thresholds (e.g., 80% for fast, 90% for exact) are enforced.
- **Physics-Ready Export to Simulation:** The finalized 4D scene is exported to an **OpenUSD** stage for simulators like Isaac Sim. Light sources are repaired, and outdoor scenes receive sun lighting. Physical properties (density, friction) are derived from procedural materials. Static colliders are assigned to all meshes, while movable objects become rigid bodies with convex-hull collision and density-derived mass. Animated humans are exported as kinematic capsule obstacles that follow generated tracks. Each exported scene undergoes headless verification in Isaac Sim to ensure proper loading, dynamic object settlement, and actor displacement reproduction.

**4DSynth-Nav Benchmark Construction:**
The benchmark is built from 122 physics-enabled Infinigen Indoors scenes in Isaac Sim. Automated task generation proceeds in three phases:
1. **Scene Probing:** Extracts object prim paths, bounding boxes, floor heights, and support relations. PhysX flood fill on a 0.25m grid determines per-object reachability for a 0.40m-radius agent.
2. **Task Generation:** Selects navigation and pick-and-place targets based on five invariants (e.g., rejecting sealed pickups, preferring unique semantic classes, capping floor pickups, ensuring targets are within reachability radii).
3. **Spawn Validation:** Loads each candidate task in Isaac Sim, validating agent spawn points against room boundaries, PhysX sphere sweeps, initial target visibility (L1/L3 vs. L2), forward clearance, and flood-fill reachability to the target.

The benchmark comprises 333 tasks across three difficulty tiers (L1-L3) varying in number of subtasks and initial target visibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Qualitative Scene Synthesis**
- **Layout-conditioned synthesis (Fig. 5):** The system successfully compiled multi-room homes from blueprint masks. Room topology, openings, and furniture arrangements were preserved, while procedural factories supplied 3D geometry, materials, and lighting. The resulting scenes retained editability of individual assets and room surfaces.
- **Real-to-sim compilation (Fig. 6):** Comparison with VIGA on shared single-image inputs demonstrated that 4DSynth's compiler more consistently recovered explicit room surfaces, openings, secondary furniture, and separately editable object instances. It maintained the dominant layout and appearance, producing procedural scenes suitable for downstream animation and editing, rather than just textured reconstructions.
- **4D environments (Fig. 7):** Frames from synthesized 4D scenes illustrated that moving actors followed collision-free trajectories, in-place actions maintained validated placements, and the camera maintained its framing against the final geometry. The environments, actors, and camera were baked into editable 4D scenes without per-scene mesh authoring.

**B. 4DSynth-Nav Benchmark Evaluation**
The 4DSynth-Nav benchmark differentiated model capabilities, indicating significant room for improvement in current vision-language models (VLMs) for dynamic embodied navigation.

- **Overall Performance (Table II):**
 - **Qwen3-VL-30B:** Achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 13.2% and Subtask Progress (SP) of 22.4% across all tasks, with a mean Goal Distance (GD) of 4.27m, 39.2 collisions, and 113.8 steps per episode.
 - **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Demonstrated improved performance with an SR of 33.3% and SP of 40.7%, reducing GD to 2.68m, collisions to 19.0, and steps to 104.5 per episode. While an improvement, it still failed two-thirds of the tasks.

- **Performance Across Difficulty Tiers:**
 - **Gemini's Advantage on Blind Starts:** Gemini 3.1 Pro showed better navigation capability, particularly on L1 tasks (one phase, initially hidden target), achieving 57.5% SR compared to Qwen's 16.8%. This was achieved with fewer steps (79.5 vs. 120.2) and closer final goal distances (2.41m vs. 4.74m). On L3 tasks (two phases, initially hidden), Gemini's SR was 18.2% compared to Qwen's 4.5%.
 - **Stalling After Subtask Completion:** For both models, Subtask Progress (SP) was consistently higher than Success Rate (SR). For Qwen, SP was 35.5% vs. 18.2% SR on L2, and 15.0% vs. 4.5% SR on L3. For Gemini, SP was 36.4% vs. 23.6% SR on L2, and 27.7% vs. 18.2% SR on L3. This pattern suggests that agents frequently complete initial subtasks but struggle with subsequent phases, indicating compounding errors in target search, spatial memory, manipulation, or re-navigation.

- **Trajectory Example (Fig. 8):** A qualitative example contrasted a successful L2 navigation by Qwen (finding a large plant, then a desk) with an L3 failure (finding a plant, but then looping away from a shelf despite objective prompt updates). This demonstrated the model's capacity for multi-stage goals but also highlighted issues with task completion judgment and re-navigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The 4DSynth system and its associated benchmark represent several significant contributions and have the potential for considerable impact on embodied AI research:

1. **Unified and Scalable Environment Generation:** By providing a single, unified procedural authoring architecture that integrates diverse scene realization routes (native generation, layout-conditioned, real-to-sim), 4DSynth addresses the current fragmentation in environment creation. This unification streamlines the process of generating complex, dynamic 4D worlds at scale, reducing the manual effort typically required.
2. **Geometry-Grounded and Editable Environments:** The emphasis on explicit geometry, physics-ready output (OpenUSD), and preservation of procedural parameters ensures that the generated environments are not merely visual but also physically consistent, interactive, and fully editable. This capability supports a wide range of downstream applications, from rendering and simulation to fine-grained scene modification and task generation without requiring per-scene mesh authoring.
3. **Foundation for Dynamic Embodied Simulation:** The system's ability to synthesize 4D environments with animated actors and collision-free trajectories directly supports the development and testing of embodied agents in dynamic settings. The explicit representation of object identity, support relations, and collision geometry is crucial for realistic physical interactions.
4. **Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied AI:** 4DSynth-Nav provides a scalable, automatically validated benchmark with controlled difficulty axes and animated obstacles. Its design specifically targets navigation and pick-and-place tasks, offering a focused tool for diagnosing VLM failures. The results from Qwen3-VL-30B and Gemini 3.1 Pro highlight existing limitations in current models, particularly regarding multi-stage objectives and navigation in initially unseen environments. The ability to reproduce and tune difficulty axes procedurally enables systematic analysis of agent capabilities and shortcomings.
5. **Practical Tool for Researchers:** 4DSynth offers a practical foundation for researchers to generate a large diversity of scenarios for embodied agents, facilitating studies on perception, planning, interaction, and learning in dynamic, interactive environments. The emphasis on auditability and regenerability means researchers can reliably create and iterate on experimental setups.

In summary, 4DSynth provides a robust, flexible, and scalable framework for synthesizing controllable 4D worlds, which is a critical infrastructure component for advancing the capabilities of embodied AI agents. The accompanying benchmark serves as a valuable diagnostic tool, pointing towards areas where current vision-language models require further development.
