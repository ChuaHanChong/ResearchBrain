# Reinforcing Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models with Interwoven Thinking and Visual Drawing

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces VILASR, a Vision-Language Model capable of spatial reasoning by generating and interpreting visual drawing operations directly within its reasoning process. The model, developed by researchers at Institute of Automation, CAS, Ant Group, CUHK MMLab, and Nanjing University, consistently outperforms existing methods by an average of 18.4% across various spatial reasoning benchmarks, including maze navigation and video analysis.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) exhibit limited spatial reasoning capabilities, often relying on text-centric processing that loses fine-grained visual details.
- Existing approaches that integrate external visual tools for LVLMs suffer from black-box limitations, offering fixed perception capabilities and leading to fragmented reasoning.
- LVLMs lack intrinsic visual manipulation abilities, hindering their capacity for iterative refinement and dynamic interaction with visual information during reasoning.

## Method

- A novel =="drawing to reason in space" paradigm== enables LVLMs to decompose complex spatial tasks into interleaved steps of natural language reasoning and visual drawing operations (bounding boxes and auxiliary lines).
- The model utilizes two fundamental drawing operations: ==`T_box` for localizing objects with bounding boxes== and ==`T_line` for visualizing spatial relationships and trajectories with auxiliary lines==.
- A ==three-stage training framework== progressively builds capabilities: cold-start training with synthetic data, reflective rejection sampling to cultivate self-correction, and reinforcement learning (GRPO) to optimize for accuracy and efficient visual thinking using a rule-based reward function.

## Results

- VILASR consistently outperforms existing methods across **five diverse spatial reasoning benchmarks**, achieving an average improvement of **18.4%** over strong baselines.
- The model achieves **98.2%** on MAZE navigation and state-of-the-art results on video and multi-view spatial reasoning benchmarks like VSI-Bench (**45.4%**), SPAR-Bench (**37.6%**), and MMSI-Bench (**30.2%**).
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of each training stage: cold-start for foundational drawing, reflective rejection sampling for self-correction (reducing reflection patterns by **96.5%** without it), and reinforcement learning for efficiency and precise numerical reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct visual manipulation via elementary drawing operations allows Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to reason about fine-grained spatial details and dynamic changes more effectively than text-centric methods.
> - A progressive three-stage training framework, incorporating supervised fine-tuning, reflective rejection sampling, and reinforcement learning, is essential for cultivating robust, self-correcting, and efficient spatial reasoning.
> - Empowering LVLMs with an intrinsic visual workspace enables them to express and analyze spatial relationships more naturally, drawing parallels to human cognitive processes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of large vision-language models (LVLMs): their limited spatial reasoning capabilities, especially compared to their impressive textual reasoning abilities. The current research landscape in LVLMs is largely dominated by models that primarily process multimodal inputs (images/videos + text) and then conduct reasoning almost exclusively within the textual domain. This text-centric approach, while successful for many tasks, inherently struggles with spatial tasks that require precise geometric understanding, continuous spatial tracking, and dynamic visual manipulation—capabilities humans often achieve through mental visualization and interaction with their environment.

The authors position their work against existing paradigms in several ways:
- **Beyond Text-Centric Reasoning**: The paper directly challenges the dominant approach where LVLMs translate visual information into textual semantics for reasoning. It argues that such translation inevitably leads to loss of fine-grained spatial details and makes dynamic spatial changes prohibitively complex to describe textually. This sets it apart from methods that heavily rely on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or similar techniques applied purely in text.
- **Improving Tool-Integrated Reasoning**: While recent efforts have explored integrating external visual tools (e.g., grounding, OCR) with LVLMs to enhance vision-centric reasoning, this paper points out their limitations. These limitations include dependence on black-box perception tools (fixed capabilities, fragmented reasoning) and reliance on oversimplified human-curated reasoning data lacking critical reflection. VILASR offers an alternative by empowering the LVLM with *intrinsic* visual manipulation capabilities through elementary drawing operations, avoiding dependence on external, specialized tools. This moves towards a more integrated and flexible "thinking with images" paradigm.
- **Addressing Spatial Reasoning Gaps**: Previous works have identified spatial reasoning as a significant weakness for LVLMs, with benchmarks like GQA and SPARTQA revealing challenges in relational reasoning and perspective transformations. This paper directly tackles these gaps by providing a systematic framework to cultivate spatial understanding that generalizes across various scenarios, including maze navigation, static image reasoning, video-based reasoning, and multi-view integration. It aims to fill the gap of systematic approaches for enhancing LVLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities across both images and videos.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning**: The work builds upon the success of "slow thinking" paradigms (e.g., OpenAI o1, DeepSeek R1) and reinforcement learning (RL) in enhancing textual reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs). It extends these ideas to the multimodal domain, using RL to refine and optimize visual reasoning processes, specifically emphasizing self-reflection and efficient tool use. This aligns with a growing trend of using advanced training techniques to unlock deeper reasoning in large models.

In essence, this paper proposes a fundamental shift in how LVLMs approach spatial reasoning, moving from a "describe-and-reason" (text-centric) or "call-tool-and-reason" (black-box tool) paradigm to an "interact-and-reason" (visual drawing) approach, mirroring human cognitive processes. It pushes the boundaries of LVLM capabilities by providing them with a flexible, intrinsic visual workspace to augment their reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by enabling them to "reason through elementary drawing operations in the visual space."

The motivation stems from several critical observations and limitations of current LVLMs:
- **Limitations of Text-Centric Reasoning**: While LLMs have made remarkable progress in textual reasoning (e.g., mathematical problem-solving, code generation) often leveraging "slow thinking" paradigms with self-reflection, applying these purely text-based methods to multimodal scenarios falls short. Spatial reasoning, by its nature, requires precise geometric understanding and the ability to track continuous changes. Translating such rich visual information into discrete textual descriptions inevitably leads to loss of detail and becomes prohibitively complex for dynamic scenes. This inherent challenge motivates a shift from textual to a more visual-centric reasoning.
- **Inadequacy of Black-Box Perception Tools**: Recent attempts to augment LVLMs with external visual tools (like object detectors or OCR systems) have shown some promise. However, these tools are often black-boxes, offering fixed perception capabilities and leading to fragmented, disconnected reasoning steps. They lack the flexibility for critical reflection or iterative refinement based on intermediate visual outputs, which is crucial for complex spatial problems where initial perceptions might be imperfect.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition**: Humans excel at spatial reasoning through mental visualization and dynamic manipulation of spatial information. This paper seeks to emulate this cognitive process in LVLMs, arguing that direct visual interaction and manipulation allow models to express and analyze spatial relationships more naturally and effectively.
- **Need for Intrinsic Visual Manipulation**: The goal is to move beyond relying on external tools or pure textual descriptions. By equipping LVLMs with basic, intrinsic drawing operations (bounding boxes for localization, auxiliary lines for relationship analysis), the authors aim to empower the models to directly manipulate and analyze visual information as part of their reasoning process, thereby overcoming the performance ceiling imposed by external tools and fragmented reasoning.
- **Cultivating Self-Reflection and Efficiency**: Beyond merely enabling drawing, a key motivation is to cultivate robust self-reflection behaviors and efficient visual thinking. The authors observe that models often lack the ability to reflect on and revise their visual operations. Therefore, the training framework is explicitly designed to foster self-correction and optimize for both accuracy and coherent, efficient reasoning processes.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a novel paradigm and a principled training framework that allows LVLMs to "think with images," making their spatial reasoning more robust, precise, and human-like, thereby unlocking advanced capabilities for real-world applications in robotics, augmented reality, and beyond.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **"drawing to reason in space,"** a novel paradigm that enables LVLMs to perform spatial reasoning through iterative visual drawing and thinking. This paradigm is supported by a three-stage training framework to cultivate the necessary capabilities.

#### 3.1. Drawing to Reason in Space Paradigm

The paradigm allows an LVLM (`M`) to decompose complex spatial reasoning tasks into a sequence of interpretable visual drawing and thinking steps. Given a visual input (`I`) and a question (`Q`), the model generates a multi-step reasoning path (`R`). Each step `t` in `R = {(r_t, e_t, o_t)}` interleaves:
- `r_t`: Natural language reasoning.
- `e_t`: Drawing operations (a set of `m_t` operations).
- `o_t`: Observed results (annotated images) from executing the drawing operations.

This iterative process continues until a conclusive answer is reached.

**Drawing Operations:** VILASR is equipped with two fundamental drawing operations:
- **`T_box` (Object Mapper)**: For annotating bounding boxes. This anchors object positions and helps in localization.
- **`T_line` (Path Tracer)**: For drawing auxiliary lines. This visualizes spatial trajectories, relationships, or measurements.

Each operation `τ ∈ {T_box, T_line}` requires three parameters:
- `k`: Index of the target image (from original input or previous drawing outputs).
- `p`: Spatial coordinates (single or multiple points/rectangles).
- `l`: Semantic labels describing the annotated content.
The execution of an operation outputs an annotated version of the target image, which becomes part of the available visual context for subsequent steps.

**Per-Step Spatial Reasoning:** At each step `t`, the LVLM generates both natural language reasoning `r_t` and drawing operations `e_t` based on the entire interaction history (`I, Q, R_<t`). The model can target different images (`k_j^t`) crucial for multi-image or video reasoning. The process terminates when `r_t` contains the final answer, at which point `e_t` and `o_t` are empty.

#### 3.2. Training Framework

The training framework is composed of three progressive stages:

##### 3.2.1. Reward Function Design

A rule-based reward function `S` guides all three stages, combining `S_correct` (answer correctness) and `S_format` (reasoning format adherence).
- `S_correct`:
 - For multiple-choice: 1 if predicted answer (`A_hat`) exactly matches ground truth (`A`), else 0.
 - For numerical: Uses Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA), which checks relative error against multiple confidence thresholds, providing a more robust measure than simple exact match.
- `S_format`: 1 if all operations in the reasoning path are executable, else 0.
A threshold `β` ensures format rewards are only given when a minimum correctness is met, preventing reward hacking.

##### 3.2.2. Stage 1: Cold-Start Training

- **Objective:** To establish basic visual interaction and drawing abilities.
- **Approach:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a synthetic dataset `D_cold`.
- **Data Generation:** Qwen2.5-72B-VL is used to generate initial reasoning paths following the "drawing to reason in space" paradigm. These paths are then rigorously filtered based on rule-based correctness and format checking (including a minimum of three reasoning steps to encourage depth).
- **Loss:** Minimizes the average negative log-likelihood over all reasoning and operation tokens.

##### 3.2.3. Stage 2: Reflective Rejection Sampling

- **Objective:** To cultivate self-reflection behaviors and the ability to revise drawing operations based on observed results.
- **Approach:** Further fine-tuning the `M_cold` model using a selective sampling mechanism.
- **Reflection Definition:** Reflection occurs when identical semantic labels (`l`) are used for different drawing operations (`e`) at different time steps, indicating a revision or re-examination (e.g., drawing a bounding box for "object A" in step 1, then redrawing or refining "object A" in step 5).
- **Mechanism:** From a batch of examples, the `M_cold` model samples reasoning paths. Only paths that not only yield the correct answer and meet format criteria but also *satisfy the reflection condition* (Eq. 5) are used for training. This selectively reinforces self-correcting reasoning.

##### 3.2.4. Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **Objective:** To further optimize the model for accurate spatial understanding and coherent, efficient visual thinking, leveraging dense reward signals.
- **Approach:** Policy optimization using GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization) without a KL penalty.
- **Rollout Policies:** During policy rollout, early termination conditions are applied to prevent inefficient or circular reasoning: (1) no drawing operations generated while not yet answering, (2) accumulated images exceed threshold (`α`), or (3) duplicate drawing operations.
- **Reward Signal:** The combined `S` (correctness + format) is used as the reward. An advantage function normalizes scores within a rollout group, enabling the model to learn from relatively better reasoning trajectories.

The full training process ensures that VILASR not only learns to generate drawing operations but also to use them reflectively and efficiently, leading to more robust and accurate spatial reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experiments across five diverse spatial reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating VILASR's effectiveness and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- VILASR consistently outperforms existing methods across all benchmarks, achieving an average improvement of **18.4%** over strong baselines like Qwen2.5-VL-7B (without reasoning capabilities).
- On **Image Spatial Reasoning** (MAZE, SpatialEval-Real): VILASR achieves exceptional performance on MAZE (98.2%), showcasing its ability to break down multi-step navigation into interpretable visual steps. It also performs strongly on SpatialEval-Real (63.9%), indicating robust static spatial understanding.
- On **Video and Multi-View Spatial Reasoning** (VSI-Bench, SPAR-Bench, MMSI-Bench): VILASR achieves state-of-the-art results (VSI-Bench 45.4%, SPAR-Bench 37.6%, MMSI-Bench 30.2%). This highlights its capability to track temporal relationships and integrate information from multiple perspectives, surpassing even significantly larger open-source models.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - **Proprietary LVLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini series, OpenAI o3/o4-mini)**: These models generally leverage their internal reasoning capabilities to outperform their non-reasoning counterparts. VILASR is competitive, particularly strong in maze navigation where it significantly outperforms even GPT-4o (98.2% vs 48.8%).
 - **Open-source LVLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/72B, LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B/72B, LLaVA-OneVision-7B/72B, Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct)**: These models show minimal or even negative improvements with reasoning enabled, suggesting a gap in their fundamental visual reasoning capabilities. VILASR significantly outstrips them.
 - **Representative Multimodal Reasoning Methods (COGCOM, VisCoT, SpaceR)**: VILASR demonstrates superior performance due to its flexible visual operations and reflective capabilities. COGCOM and VisCoT are limited by single-image input or black-box perception tools, while SpaceR's text-centric reasoning struggles with full visual temporal exploitation.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study Insights (on VSI-Bench)

The ablation studies reveal the critical role of each training stage:
- **Cold-start training (Stage 1) is essential**: Without it, the model performs worse than the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, indicating that prompting alone is insufficient; dedicated training is needed to build a foundational understanding of spatial reasoning with drawing operations.
- **Reflective Rejection Sampling (Stage 2) is crucial for self-correction**: Removing this stage leads to a **96.5% decrease** in observed reflection patterns. This dramatically impacts tasks requiring precise object localization and measurement (e.g., "Absolute Distance," "Object Size"), as the model tends to make quicker, less verified spatial judgments without self-correction. It leads to a 85.0% decrease in bounding box usage but a 70.5% increase in auxiliary line usage, implying a shift from precise localization to simpler directional judgments.
- **Reinforcement Learning (Stage 3) enables fine-grained learning and efficiency**: Without RL, consistent performance decreases are observed across most subtasks. RL optimization helps the model use drawing operations more selectively, leading to more efficient reasoning (response length decreases from ~2,500 to ~1,800 tokens). It particularly boosts performance on numerical questions (e.g., "Object Count," "Absolute Distance," "Object Size") due to its ability to provide dense reward signals based on numerical proximity to ground truth.

#### 4.3. Inference-Time Scaling (Pass@k Analysis)

The pass@k analysis demonstrates the progressive enhancement of model capability across training stages:
- **Cold-start training** significantly improves both single-attempt (pass@1) and empirical upper-bound (pass@8) performance compared to the base model.
- **Reflective rejection sampling** further elevates both metrics, indicating an expanded reasoning potential.
- **RL optimization** not only pushes the empirical upper bound but also **significantly narrows the gap between pass@8 and pass@1**. This indicates that RL effectively consolidates multi-attempt capabilities into strong single-attempt performance, making the model more robust and reliable in a single inference run.

In summary, VILASR's success stems from its novel "drawing to reason in space" paradigm, which provides an intrinsic visual workspace, and its meticulously designed three-stage training framework that progressively cultivates fundamental drawing abilities, self-correction, and efficient, precise reasoning through reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Reinforcing Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models with Interwoven Thinking and Visual Drawing" represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, particularly in addressing the long-standing challenge of spatial reasoning in LVLMs.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Novel Reasoning Paradigm ("Drawing to Reason in Space")**: This is arguably the most significant contribution. By proposing that LVLMs directly interact with and manipulate visual information through elementary drawing operations (bounding boxes and auxiliary lines), the authors introduce a paradigm that mirrors human cognitive processes more closely than traditional text-centric or black-box tool-integrated approaches. This shift enables LVLMs to capture and reason about fine-grained spatial details and dynamic changes more effectively, moving beyond the limitations of textual descriptions.
2. **Principled Multi-Stage Training Framework**: The three-stage training framework (cold-start, reflective rejection sampling, and reinforcement learning) is a meticulously designed pipeline that progressively cultivates sophisticated visual reasoning abilities.
 - **Cold-start training** provides the foundational ability to generate and interpret drawing operations.
 - **Reflective rejection sampling** is a clever mechanism to explicitly foster self-correction and iterative refinement, which is critical for complex reasoning tasks where initial perceptions might be erroneous. This addresses a common weakness in current AI systems.
 - **Reinforcement learning** effectively consolidates these learned behaviors, optimizing for both accuracy and efficiency, bridging the gap between potential (pass@k) and single-attempt (pass@1) performance.
3. **State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Spatial Tasks**: VILASR's consistent and substantial outperformance across various spatial reasoning benchmarks (maze navigation, static image, video, and multi-view reasoning) empirically validates the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of the proposed approach. Its particular strength in tasks requiring sequential spatial planning and precise numerical answers is noteworthy.
4. **Insights into Visual Reasoning Development**: The detailed ablation studies provide valuable insights into *how* LVLMs develop visual reasoning abilities, highlighting the importance of each training stage for distinct aspects like foundational drawing, self-correction, and efficient, precise problem-solving. This contributes to the broader understanding of learning dynamics in multimodal models.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Enhanced Real-World Applications**: Improved spatial reasoning is fundamental for a wide range of real-world applications.
 - **Robotics**: Better navigation, object manipulation, and understanding of dynamic environments.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality**: More accurate scene understanding, object placement, and user interaction.
 - **Healthcare**: More precise interpretation of medical images (e.g., tumor localization, anatomical measurements).
 - **Automated Driving**: Superior understanding of road layouts, object distances, and trajectory predictions.
 - **Education**: Interactive tools for teaching geometry, physics, and other spatially intensive subjects.
2. **Advancing Multimodal AI Research**: The "drawing to reason in space" paradigm opens up new avenues for research into more integrated and human-like multimodal intelligence. It encourages exploration of richer visual interaction modalities and potentially more generalizable visual reasoning architectures.
3. **Foundation for More Complex Visual Tasks**: By mastering fundamental spatial reasoning, VILASR lays the groundwork for tackling even more complex visual understanding tasks, such as causality in videos, predictive modeling of physical interactions, and even creative visual design.
4. **Increased Transparency and Debuggability**: The interpretable nature of drawing operations makes the model's reasoning process more transparent. Researchers and developers can visually inspect the intermediate steps, making it easier to debug errors, understand failure modes, and build trust in the model's decisions.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (e.g., focus on 2D operations, computational cost), its contributions lay a strong foundation for future research in intrinsic visual reasoning, potentially leading to a new generation of LVLMs that can truly "think with images" and interact with the visual world in more profound and intelligent ways.
