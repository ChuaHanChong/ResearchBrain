# VideoMAE V2: Scaling Video Masked Autoencoders with Dual Masking

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoMAE V2 introduces a dual masking strategy and a progressive training paradigm to successfully scale video masked autoencoders, training the first video Vision Transformer with over a billion parameters and achieving new state-of-the-art results across various video understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Existing video Masked Autoencoders faced computational and memory bottlenecks, preventing the training of billion-parameter models due to the high dimensionality of video data.
- Billion-parameter models require vast, diverse datasets, but public video datasets are significantly smaller and less diverse compared to those available for image or language tasks.
- Effective adaptation strategies were lacking for deploying large pre-trained video models, as direct fine-tuning on smaller, task-specific datasets often led to overfitting and suboptimal performance.

## Method

- **VideoMAE V2** introduces a ==dual masking strategy==, applying high-ratio ==tube masking== to the ==ViT encoder== and ==running cell masking== to the decoder of the ==masked autoencoder==, drastically reducing computational and memory costs during pre-training.
- Comprehensive data scaling was achieved by compiling a large 1.35 million video 'UnlabeledHybrid' dataset for ==unsupervised pre-training== and a 0.66 million video 'LabeledHybrid' dataset for supervised post-pre-training.
- A ==progressive training paradigm== was developed, involving unsupervised pre-training, supervised post-pre-training (intermediate fine-tuning), and specific fine-tuning for downstream tasks.

## Results

- Dual masking provided a **1.48x to 1.79x** speedup in training time per epoch for large models, maintaining comparable or slightly better accuracy than encoder-only masking, while reducing FLOPs by ~**30%** and memory by **40-50%**.
- The paper successfully trained the first video transformer model with over a **billion parameters** (ViT-g), showing consistent performance improvements from ViT-B to ViT-g.
- VideoMAE V2 achieved new state-of-the-art results across various video understanding tasks, including **90.0%** on Kinetics-400, **77.0%** on Something-Something V2, **42.6% mAP** on AVA, and **69.6% mAP** on THUMOS14.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dual masking significantly improves the efficiency of video MAEs, reducing FLOPs by 30% and memory by 40-50%, making billion-parameter video model training feasible without compromising accuracy.
> - Video masked autoencoders are a highly scalable self-supervised pre-training paradigm for video, demonstrating consistent performance gains as model capacity and data scale increase.
> - A multi-stage progressive training approach, including intermediate supervised fine-tuning, is essential for effectively adapting large-scale pre-trained models to diverse downstream tasks and improving generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "VideoMAE V2: Scaling Video Masked Autoencoders with Dual Masking" is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of **foundation models** in artificial intelligence. Inspired by the transformative success of large-scale pre-trained models in natural language processing (NLP) like BERT and GPT-3, the AI community has increasingly focused on developing similar general-purpose models for other modalities, including vision.

In the vision domain, **Masked Autoencoders (MAEs)** have emerged as a conceptually simple yet remarkably effective self-supervised learning paradigm. Following the pioneering work of MAE for images, a surge of research extended this approach to video data, with notable contributions including MaskFeat, BEVT, and the authors' own VideoMAE V1 and MAE-ST. These methods demonstrated that reconstructing masked portions of images or video frames could effectively learn robust visual representations without explicit human labels.

However, a significant challenge remained: **scaling these video foundation models to truly massive sizes (e.g., billions of parameters) and datasets.** While image MAEs have seen some scaling efforts, the inherent complexity of video data—with its additional temporal dimension and high redundancy—introduces unique computational and memory bottlenecks. Existing video MAE approaches, including VideoMAE V1, while efficient, were not designed to seamlessly handle models with billion-level parameters or pre-training on truly vast, diverse video datasets. The paper explicitly states that "very few work... has tried to scale up this masked autoencoder pre-training to the billion-level models in image domain, partially due to the high data dimension and the high computational overhead. This issue is even more serious for scaling up video masked autoencoder pre-training owning to its extra time dimension and strong temporal variations."

VideoMAE V2 positions itself as a direct answer to this scaling challenge. It aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical potential of scaled video MAEs and their practical realization. By introducing novel efficiency improvements and a comprehensive progressive training strategy, this work seeks to demonstrate that video masked autoencoders can indeed be scaled to unprecedented levels, thereby paving the way for more powerful and generalizable video foundation models. This effort is critical for advancing various video understanding tasks, from action recognition to spatial and temporal action detection, where robust and generic visual features are paramount. The work also contributes to the understanding of scaling laws for video transformers, a relatively unexplored area compared to images and language.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of VideoMAE V2 is to **study the scaling properties of video masked autoencoders and push their performance limits across a variety of video downstream tasks** by scaling both the model capacity and the size and diversity of the pre-training data. Specifically, the authors aim to successfully train a video Vision Transformer (ViT) model with a **billion parameters**, a scale previously unexplored in the video domain using MAE-based self-supervision.

This ambitious objective is driven by several key motivations, which also highlight the challenges addressed by the paper:

1. **Overcoming Computational and Memory Bottlenecks for Billion-Parameter Video Models:** Despite VideoMAE V1's efficiency through its asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture, it remained insufficient for training billion-level video transformers within practical timeframes (e.g., over two weeks on 64 A100 GPUs for a ViT-g model). The sheer volume of video data (with its extra time dimension) exacerbates the computational and memory demands. The motivation here is to devise **further efficiency improvements** to make billion-parameter video MAE pre-training feasible under current hardware constraints.

2. **Addressing Limited Labeled and Unlabeled Video Data Scarcity for Large Models:** Unlike images, public video datasets are significantly smaller (e.g., 0.24M videos in Kinetics400 vs. 14.2M images in ImageNet-22k). A billion-parameter model requires vast amounts of diverse data to prevent overfitting during pre-training and to learn truly generalizable representations. The motivation is to **build and leverage larger, multi-sourced video datasets** to unleash the full potential of these massive models.

3. **Developing Effective Adaptation Strategies for Large Pre-trained Video Models:** Directly fine-tuning a billion-parameter model on relatively small, task-specific datasets can lead to overfitting and suboptimal performance. Drawing parallels from image domain practices, there's a need for a more nuanced transfer scheme to effectively adapt the powerful pre-trained representations. The motivation is to **introduce a progressive training paradigm** that includes intermediate fine-tuning on a larger, labeled hybrid dataset, serving as a bridge between unsupervised pre-training and specific downstream tasks.

In essence, VideoMAE V2 is motivated by the desire to demonstrate that the MAE paradigm, when appropriately scaled and optimized for the unique characteristics of video data, can yield video foundation models comparable in scale and generalization capability to their counterparts in NLP and image vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoMAE V2 introduces a simple yet highly effective framework to scale video masked autoencoders, addressing the challenges of computational efficiency, data scale, and model adaptation. Its methodology revolves around three core technical designs: **dual masking, comprehensive data scaling, and a progressive training paradigm.**

#### 3.1. Dual Masking Strategy

The most significant technical innovation in VideoMAE V2 is the **dual masking strategy**, designed to further enhance the computational and memory efficiency of the masked autoencoder pipeline.
- **Revisiting VideoMAE (V1):** The original VideoMAE (V1) employed an asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture, where the encoder only processes a small subset of visible video tokens (e.g., 10% after a high masking ratio, ρ=0.9). However, the decoder still needs to process all original video tokens (the unmasked visible tokens from the encoder *plus* the masked tokens to be reconstructed), leading to a bottleneck for billion-level models.
- **Dual Masking Concept:** VideoMAE V2 proposes to mask the decoder as well. It generates two masking maps:
 - **Encoder Masking (`M_e`):** Same as V1, this is a random tube masking with an extremely high ratio (e.g., 90%) to reduce encoder input. This focuses the encoder on learning from sparse, non-redundant information.
 - **Decoder Masking (`M_d`):** This is a *new* masking applied to the decoder's input. Instead of reconstructing *all* masked pixels (those dropped by `M_e`), the decoder now only processes and reconstructs a *subset* of those masked pixels. The choice of `M_d` is crucial. The authors found that **running cell masking** (a strategy that selects diverse cubes to ensure information complement, inspired by efficient action recognition transformers) is most effective. This allows for a substantial reduction in the decoder's input length.
- **Reconstruction Loss:** Critically, the MSE loss is only computed between the normalized original pixels and reconstructed pixels for the tokens that were **invisible to the encoder** *and* are selected by the decoder mask (`i ∈ M_d ∩ M_e`). This specific loss formulation, combined with the running cell masking, avoids information leakage and maintains a challenging self-supervised task while significantly cutting down on computation.
- **Efficiency Gains:** This dual masking scheme reduces FLOPs by approximately 30% and memory consumption by 40-50% compared to V1's encoder-only masking, enabling faster training for huge models like ViT-g.

#### 3.2. Model Scaling

The framework systematically scales the Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone from ViT-B (Base) to ViT-g (Giant), focusing on the **ViT-g model with over one billion parameters**.
- **Backbone Choice:** Vanilla ViT is chosen for its simplicity and established scaling properties in images.
- **Encoder Design:** The encoder capacity is scaled by increasing depth, width, and number of attention heads (e.g., ViT-g has 40 blocks, 1408 dimension, 16 heads).
- **Decoder Design:** Crucially, the decoder is kept **relatively shallow and lightweight** (e.g., 4 transformer blocks, 512 channels), and its size does not scale with the encoder. This maintains the asymmetric architecture's efficiency benefits, further amplified by dual masking.

#### 3.3. Data Scaling

To effectively train billion-parameter models and ensure robust generalization, the authors developed a two-tiered data scaling strategy:
- **UnlabeledHybrid Dataset:** For unsupervised pre-training, a large-scale, diverse dataset of 1.35 million unlabeled video clips is compiled from multiple public sources: Kinetics, Something-Something V2, AVA, WebVid2M, and a self-collected Instagram dataset. This multi-source approach aims to capture broad video characteristics and reduce bias. This is the largest dataset ever used for video masked autoencoding.
- **LabeledHybrid (Kinetics-710) Dataset:** For supervised post-pre-training, a dataset of 0.66 million labeled video clips is created by merging and aligning different versions of Kinetics (K400, K600, K700), resulting in 710 action categories. This dataset provides rich semantic supervision.

#### 3.4. Progressive Training Paradigm

To effectively adapt the large pre-trained models to diverse downstream tasks and mitigate overfitting, a **three-stage progressive training pipeline** is devised:
1. **Unsupervised Pre-training:** The VideoMAE V2 model (with dual masking) is pre-trained from scratch on the large **UnlabeledHybrid** dataset. This stage learns generic spatiotemporal features.
2. **Supervised Post-pre-training (Intermediate Fine-tuning):** The pre-trained encoder from Stage 1 is then fine-tuned on the **LabeledHybrid (Kinetics-710)** dataset. This stage incorporates human semantic supervision from multiple sources, bridging the gap between unsupervised feature learning and task-specific fine-tuning. This helps stabilize and refine features for general video understanding.
3. **Specific Fine-tuning:** Finally, the model from Stage 2 is fine-tuned on individual, smaller target datasets for specific downstream tasks (e.g., Kinetics-400 for action recognition, AVA for spatial action detection, THUMOS14 for temporal action detection).

This comprehensive methodology allows VideoMAE V2 to efficiently train unprecedentedly large video transformers on vast and diverse datasets, leading to highly generalized and performant video representations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness of VideoMAE V2's core designs and its ability to establish new state-of-the-art performance across a wide array of video understanding tasks.

#### 4.1. Dual Masking Performance

- **Efficiency:** Dual masking significantly reduces computational cost (FLOPs) and memory consumption. For ViT-B, FLOPs decreased from 35.48G to 25.87G, and memory from 631M to 328M, leading to a 1.79x speedup in training time per epoch. For ViT-g, FLOPs reduced from 263.93G to 241.61G, and memory from 1753M to 1050M, resulting in a 1.48x speedup.
- **Accuracy:** Despite the substantial efficiency gains, dual masking maintained comparable or slightly better performance than the original VideoMAE's encoder-only masking (e.g., 70.15% vs. 70.28% Top-1 accuracy on Something-Something V2 for ViT-B). The running cell masking strategy proved superior for decoder masking compared to frame or random masking.

#### 4.2. Data Scaling Performance

- **Improved Performance:** Pre-training on the larger `UnlabeledHybrid` dataset (1.35M videos) consistently outperformed VideoMAE V1 models pre-trained on smaller, single-source datasets (e.g., Kinetics-400 or Something-Something V2).
- **Scaling Synergy:** The performance gap between V2 (large-scale data) and V1 (small-scale data) became more pronounced as model capacity increased. For instance, on Something-Something V2, V2's ViT-H model surpassed V1 by 2.0%.
- **Data Quality:** VideoMAE V2 (ViT-L) pre-trained on `UnlabeledHybrid` achieved 85.4% on K400, outperforming MAE-ST (ViT-L) pre-trained on a similar 1M IG-uncurated clips by 1.0%, suggesting the importance of data diversity and quality in the multi-source `UnlabeledHybrid` dataset.

#### 4.3. Model Scaling Performance

- **Consistent Gains:** The paper observed consistent performance improvements as model capacity scaled from ViT-B to ViT-g.
- **Billion-Parameter Milestone:** VideoMAE V2 successfully trained the **first video transformer model with a billion parameters (ViT-g)**, demonstrating the feasibility of such large-scale video models.
- **Performance Saturation:** While improvements were consistent, the gains from ViT-L to ViT-H and then to ViT-g became smaller, indicating a potential saturation of performance on current benchmarks around 87.0% on Kinetics-400 and 77.0% on Something-Something V2 for methods without extra labeled data.

#### 4.4. Progressive Training Performance

- **Effectiveness of Post-pre-training:** The supervised post-pre-training stage on the `LabeledHybrid` (K710) dataset consistently boosted performance. For example, on Kinetics-400, ViT-H improved from 86.9% to 88.6% (multi-view fusion) after post-pre-training.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** VideoMAE V2's progressive training with its large-scale unsupervised pre-training and intermediate fine-tuning outperformed VideoMAE V1's and MAE-ST's intermediate fine-tuning approaches.

#### 4.5. State-of-the-Art Performance on Downstream Tasks

VideoMAE V2 models achieved new state-of-the-art results across a diverse set of video understanding tasks:

- **Action Classification:**
 - **Kinetics (K400/K600):** Achieved 90.0% on K400 and 89.9% on K600 (ViT-g with larger input), setting new SOTA among methods without in-house labeled data and becoming highly competitive with methods using significantly larger proprietary datasets.
 - **Something-Something (V1/V2):** Achieved 68.7% on V1 and 77.0% on V2 (ViT-g), significantly outperforming previous bests (e.g., >7% improvement on V1).
 - **UCF101/HMDB51:** Achieved 99.6% on UCF101 and 88.1% on HMDB51 (ViT-B distilled from ViT-g), showcasing excellent transferability to smaller benchmarks.
- **Spatial Action Detection (AVA, AVA-Kinetics):**
 - Outperformed previous SOTA methods, including TubeR and MaskFeat, by a significant margin (e.g., 42.6% mAP on AVA, +3.1% over previous masked modeling; 43.9% mAP on AVA-Kinetics, +3.4% over challenge winners).
- **Temporal Action Detection (THUMOS14, FineAction):**
 - This is the first time masked pre-training has been thoroughly tested on these tasks. VideoMAE V2 (using ActionFormer pipeline) achieved SOTA results (e.g., 69.6% mAP on THUMOS14, even without optical flow; 18.2% mAP on FineAction, +5% over previous best). This demonstrates its ability to learn effective temporal representations for precise boundary localization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "VideoMAE V2: Scaling Video Masked Autoencoders with Dual Masking" paper represents a significant advancement in the field of video understanding and the broader development of AI foundation models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Demonstrating Scalability of Video MAEs:** This work conclusively proves that video masked autoencoders are a **scalable and general self-supervised pre-training paradigm** for building powerful video foundation models. By successfully training the **first billion-parameter video transformer** and showcasing consistent performance gains with increased model and data scale, VideoMAE V2 solidifies MAE's position as a leading approach for learning video representations, mirroring its success in image and language domains.

2. **Innovating for Efficiency:** The introduction of the **dual masking strategy** is a crucial methodological innovation. It provides a simple yet highly effective solution to the long-standing computational and memory bottlenecks associated with training massive video models. By intelligently masking the decoder, VideoMAE V2 makes billion-parameter video pre-training practical on current hardware, dramatically reducing training time and resource requirements. This efficiency is critical for fostering further research and development in large-scale video modeling.

3. **Establishing New State-of-the-Art Performance:** VideoMAE V2 sets new benchmarks across a broad spectrum of video understanding tasks, including action classification, spatial action detection, and temporal action detection. Its superior performance on diverse datasets like Kinetics, Something-Something, AVA, THUMOS14, and FineAction underscores the robustness and generalizability of the learned representations. This advancement will likely serve as a new baseline for future research in these areas.

4. **Providing a Comprehensive Training Paradigm:** The proposed **progressive training pipeline**, involving unsupervised pre-training on a multi-source unlabeled dataset, followed by supervised post-pre-training on a labeled hybrid dataset, offers a robust strategy for effectively adapting large-scale pre-trained models. This approach mitigates overfitting and maximizes the utility of both vast unlabeled data and curated semantic supervision, offering valuable guidance for training future video foundation models.

5. **Accelerating Video AI Research and Applications:** The release of high-performing, large-scale pre-trained models, such as the billion-parameter ViT-g, has the potential to become foundational backbones for numerous downstream video tasks. Researchers can now leverage these powerful representations, significantly reducing the need for extensive task-specific training and data collection. This will accelerate progress in diverse applications, including video content analysis, autonomous systems, surveillance, and human-computer interaction.

6. **Inspiring Future Scaling Efforts:** While VideoMAE V2 pushes the boundaries, the paper openly discusses the observed performance saturation and the remaining gap in data scale compared to image and language foundation models (billions vs. millions of videos). This acknowledgement, combined with the successful demonstration of what is achievable, will likely inspire further research into even more efficient pre-training frameworks and strategies for curating and utilizing truly massive video datasets, pushing towards a future where video foundation models are as ubiquitous and impactful as their language counterparts.

In conclusion, VideoMAE V2 represents a landmark achievement in self-supervised video learning, paving the way for a new generation of highly capable and generalizable video AI systems.
