# LadderMan: Learning Humanoid Perceptive Ladder Climbing

> [!summary] Summary
> LadderMan introduces a unified depth-based visuomotor system enabling the Unitree G1 humanoid robot to robustly climb diverse ladders and perform stable on-ladder manipulation via zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The system achieves a climbing speed of approximately 3.4 seconds per rung, comparable to human performance.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust ladder climbing due to challenges in precise whole-body coordination on sparse, discontinuous rungs and poor generalization across diverse ladder geometries.
- Sim-to-real transfer for contact-rich tasks like ladder climbing is difficult due to discrepancies in depth perception, sensor noise, and robot dynamics.
- Maintaining stability during on-ladder manipulation is challenging, as general teleoperation policies often lead to balance loss in highly constrained environments.

## Method

- A two-stage learning pipeline, starting with hybrid ==motion tracking== to learn diverse state-based expert policies from a single reference motion, then distilling them into a unified visuomotor policy using hybrid DAgger and reinforcement learning.
- Bridging the sim-to-real perception gap by employing a Vision Foundation Model (Fast-FoundationStereo) for depth processing and introducing Rung-Focused Masking (RFM) to focus perception on ladder rungs.
- A dual-agent learning framework for on-ladder manipulation, decoupling a lower-body stabilization agent from an upper-body manipulation agent to maintain balance while performing teleoperated tasks.

## Results

- The Unitree G1 humanoid robot demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, robustly climbing diverse real-world ladders with varying geometries and performing stable on-ladder manipulation tasks via teleoperation.
- LadderMan achieved over **95%** success in simulation across a range of ladder configurations, significantly outperforming a motion tracking baseline that peaked at **49%** success, and a climbing speed of 3.4 seconds per rung, comparable to human performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the hybrid DAgger + RL approach, the Vision Foundation Model for depth processing, and Rung-Focused Masking were crucial for achieving robust sim-to-real transfer and high success rates (e.g., 9/10 on Ladder A compared to 0/10-3/10 without these components).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex, diverse locomotion skills can be learned from a single reference motion by employing hybrid motion tracking with asymmetric rewards, allowing flexible adaptation of certain body parts.
> - Vision Foundation Models combined with targeted masking effectively bridge the sim-to-real perception gap for contact-rich tasks on sparse structures like ladders, reducing the need for extensive manual depth randomization.
> - A dual-agent learning framework, which decouples lower-body stabilization from upper-body manipulation, enables stable and robust whole-body manipulation on inherently unstable platforms like ladders.
