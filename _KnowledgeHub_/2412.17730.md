# Mimicking-Bench: A Benchmark for Generalizable Humanoid-Scene Interaction Learning via Human Mimicking

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces a comprehensive benchmark for teaching humanoid robots to interact with environments by mimicking human demonstrations

## Problem

- Existing humanoid benchmarks lack large-scale human demonstration data needed to study scene geometry generalization
- Current methods are limited by small-scale, manually collected demonstrations
- Technical challenges in transferring skills from humans to humanoids remain unsolved

## Method

- Created **Mimicking-Bench**, the first benchmark for learning generalizable ==humanoid-scene interaction== through ==human mimicking==.
- Integrated a large dataset of **20K synthetic** and **3K real** human interactions across **11K** diverse object shapes.
- Constructed a complete skill-learning pipeline combining ==motion retargeting==, ==tracking==, and ==imitation learning==.
- Provided a standardized evaluation framework for comparing different approaches across the pipeline.

## Results

- Best combination achieves **54%** average success rate across **6** household interaction tasks.
- Performance improves approximately linearly with the **log** of training data size.
- Tasks requiring precise hand control remain challenging even with human demonstrations.
- Demonstrated effectiveness of the human-mimicking paradigm while identifying areas needing improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using large-scale human demonstration data significantly improves task success rates compared to learning from scratch
> - Appropriate algorithm selection for each pipeline stage (retargeting, tracking, imitation) is critical for performance
> - Human mimicking approach enables more natural motion generation than pure reinforcement learning
> - Elevation map visual inputs outperform RGBD camera inputs for spatial understanding

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Humanoid robots have the potential to assist humans in various everyday tasks, but teaching them to interact with diverse environments remains a significant challenge. The paper "Mimicking-Bench: A Benchmark for Generalizable Humanoid-Scene Interaction Learning via Human Mimicking" introduces a novel benchmark designed to advance research in humanoid skill acquisition through human motion mimicking.

The benchmark addresses critical limitations in current humanoid learning research: small-scale datasets, limited generalizability, and insufficient integration of human knowledge. By providing a large-scale, diverse dataset of human-scene interactions and a standardized evaluation platform, Mimicking-Bench aims to accelerate progress in creating humanoid robots that can effectively learn from human demonstrations.

### 2. Background and Motivation

Humanoid robots offer tremendous potential for real-world applications due to their human-like structure and ability to operate in environments designed for humans. However, teaching these complex systems to interact with diverse scenes and objects remains challenging for several reasons:

1. **Exploration Complexity**: The high-dimensional action spaces of humanoids make random exploration inefficient.
2. **Physical Constraints**: Maintaining balance and physical stability during complex interactions is difficult.
3. **Generalization**: Learning algorithms often struggle to adapt to novel object geometries and interaction scenarios.

Traditional approaches to skill learning, such as reinforcement learning from scratch, face significant challenges when applied to complex humanoid systems. These methods typically require excessive training time and struggle with the exploration problem in high-dimensional spaces.

Human mimicking presents a promising alternative by leveraging human motion data as a reference, effectively guiding the skill acquisition process. This approach aligns with how humans learn - by observing and imitating others' actions - and can potentially accelerate humanoid learning by bypassing inefficient exploration.

### 3. The Mimicking-Bench Framework

The core of Mimicking-Bench is a three-stage skill learning paradigm that encompasses different techniques for transferring human skills to humanoid robots:

1. **Motion Retargeting**: Transfers human motions to humanoid skeletons, accounting for differences in body proportions and joint structures.
2. **Motion Tracking**: Enables humanoids to track skill animations in physical simulation environments, generating physically plausible demonstrations.
3. **Imitation Learning**: Trains humanoid agents to replicate skill demonstrations, generalizing to new scenarios and objects.

This modular design allows researchers to evaluate each component independently, facilitating targeted improvements and methodological innovations at different stages of the skill learning process.

### 4. Dataset and Tasks

Mimicking-Bench includes a dataset of 23,000 human-scene interaction motion sequences covering 11,000 diverse object geometries. The data comes from three main sources:

1. **UniHSI and ROAM**: Synthetic human-scene interaction generation networks
2. **CORE4D**: Real-world motion capture data

The benchmark defines six household full-body humanoid-scene interaction tasks:
- Sitting on chairs
- Lying on beds
- Sitting on sofas
- Lying on sofas
- Touching points
- Lifting boxes

Each task presents unique challenges, from maintaining stability when sitting to precise hand control when lifting objects. The dataset includes considerable geometric variation to test generalization capabilities:

The simulations are implemented in Isaac Gym, a physics-based simulator that enables fast parallel training of multiple humanoid agents. The environment provides both visual observations (RGB-D images, segmentation masks) and structured representations (elevation maps) to support different perception approaches.

### 5. Methodology

#### 5.1. Motion Retargeting

The first stage transfers human motion to the humanoid skeleton. Three main approaches are evaluated:

1. **Copy-Rotation**: Directly copies joint rotations from human to humanoid
2. **Optimization**: Optimizes humanoid poses to match the positions of key human body parts
3. **Optimization with Skeleton Shape Alignment**: Extends optimization with skeleton shape constraints

These methods address the challenge of adapting motions between bodies with different proportions and joint structures. The goal is to preserve the semantics and style of the original human motion while accommodating the humanoid's physical structure.

#### 5.2. Motion Tracking

The tracking stage transforms unphysical animations into physically plausible demonstrations. Two tracking controllers are evaluated:

1. **HST (Humanoid Skill Tracking)**: A transformer-based controller that predicts joint targets
2. **PHC (Proportional Humanoid Controller)**: A PD controller with manually designed feedback gains

This stage is critical for generating realistic reference motions that obey physical constraints such as balance, contact dynamics, and inertia.

#### 5.3. Imitation Learning

The final stage trains humanoid agents to replicate skill demonstrations. Two algorithms are evaluated:

1. **ACT (Action-Conditioned Transformer)**: A transformer-based policy that predicts actions based on state and demonstration
2. **HIT (Hierarchical Imitation Transformer)**: A hierarchical approach with separate high-level and low-level policies

These methods must learn to generalize from demonstrations to novel environments, adapting to different object geometries while maintaining the core task semantics.

The perception module significantly impacts an agent's ability to generalize across different object geometries and understand spatial relationships.

### 6. Key Findings

The extensive experiments conducted with Mimicking-Bench revealed several important insights:

1. **Human mimicking is effective**: The human mimicking paradigm substantially outperforms learning from scratch, demonstrating the value of human references for skill acquisition.

2. **Algorithm selection matters**: Different algorithms excel at different stages of the pipeline:
 - **Retargeting**: Optimization consistently outperforms other methods across all tasks
 - **Tracking**: HST significantly outperforms PHC on tasks requiring precise hand control
 - **Imitation Learning**: ACT outperforms HIT in most scenarios

3. **Perception representation is crucial**: Elevation maps significantly outperform multi-view RGB-D images across all tasks, suggesting the importance of structured and focused environmental representation.

4. **Data scale significantly impacts performance**: Increasing data scale from 10% to 100% boosts performance dramatically, with success rates more than doubling for some tasks:

The results demonstrate that while human mimicking significantly improves humanoid skill learning, the choice of algorithms, data scale, and environmental representation all play crucial roles in determining success.

### 7. Challenges and Future Research

Despite the progress demonstrated by Mimicking-Bench, several challenges remain:

1. **Hand Control**: Tasks involving precise hand interactions (touching points, lifting boxes) consistently proved more challenging, with lower success rates than other tasks.

2. **Dynamic Object Manipulation**: Manipulating dynamic objects (like boxes) remains difficult, suggesting the need for advanced controllers that can adapt to object physics.

3. **Perceptual Understanding**: While elevation maps outperformed RGB-D images, there remains significant room for improvement in how humanoids perceive and understand their environment.

4. **Adaptation to Novel Scenarios**: Even with large-scale training data, generalization to unseen objects and scenarios remains challenging, particularly for objects with unusual dimensions.

Future research directions might include:
- Developing specialized hand controllers for precise manipulation
- Incorporating tactile feedback for improved object interaction
- Exploring multimodal perception approaches
- Investigating meta-learning techniques to improve adaptation to novel scenarios
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for deployment on physical humanoid robots

### 8. Conclusion

Mimicking-Bench represents a significant advancement in humanoid skill learning research by providing a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating human mimicking approaches. The large-scale, diverse dataset and standardized evaluation platform address key limitations of existing benchmarks and facilitate research on generalization across different object geometries and interaction scenarios.

The findings from this benchmark validate the effectiveness of human mimicking as a paradigm for humanoid skill learning while highlighting important challenges and research opportunities. By enabling more efficient and natural skill acquisition, Mimicking-Bench contributes to the long-term goal of developing humanoid robots capable of assisting humans in various real-world tasks.

The modular design of the benchmark allows researchers to focus on specific components of the skill learning pipeline, promoting targeted improvements and innovations. As research in this area continues to evolve, Mimicking-Bench will serve as a valuable tool for measuring progress and comparing different approaches to humanoid skill learning.

By bridging the gap between human skill demonstrations and humanoid skill acquisition, this benchmark takes us one step closer to humanoid robots that can effectively learn from and collaborate with humans in diverse real-world environments.
