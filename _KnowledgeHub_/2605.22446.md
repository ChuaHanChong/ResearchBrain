# Pre-VLA: Preemptive Runtime Verification for Reliable Vision-Language-Action and World-Model Rollouts

> [!summary] Summary
> Pre-VLA, a runtime verification architecture, preemptively assesses candidate action chunks from Vision-Language-Action models and World Models. It improves closed-loop robotic task success rates by an average of 6.83 percentage points on LIBERO benchmarks and mitigates error accumulation in world model imaginations by filtering low-quality actions.

## Problem

- Learning-based VLA models and generative World Models suffer from inherent uncertainty, leading to low-quality actions that can cause physical failures in robots (e.g., collisions, object drops) or generate misleading, computationally expensive world model rollouts (e.g., blurry frames, target position drift).
- Existing runtime verification mechanisms struggle with the high dimensionality of multimodal sensory inputs, often evaluate states or prediction results *post-action*, and do not preemptively assess the validity of candidate actions.
- Training data for robotic failures often suffers from severe class imbalance, with safe trajectories dominating, making it difficult for models to learn from rare but critical failure samples and leading to unstable decision boundaries.

## Method

- **Pre-VLA** generates safety-aware supervision signals by transforming online PPO Critic advantage estimates into absolute safety constraints, incorporating a failure-aware penalty and task-level standardization.
- A lightweight dual-branch runtime monitor, built on an improved WorldVLA backbone, extracts modality-aware features and uses a compact FFN with dual classification and regression heads for proactive safety assessment.
- A dual-mode preemptive resampling scheduler integrates **Pre-VLA** into the inference pipeline, intercepting unsafe actions for VLA resampling in physical execution or truncating unreliable ==world model== imaginations, with a regression-guided fallback mechanism.

## Results

- Achieved an F1 score of 0.8303 and an invalid-action false pass rate of 0.0200, demonstrating reliable discrimination between valid and invalid actions.
- Improved average closed-loop robotic task success rates on LIBERO benchmarks by **6.83** percentage points (from **30.79%** to **37.62%**) while also reducing average task steps, indicating more efficient execution.
- Mitigated error accumulation and propagation in world model rollouts by filtering low-quality actions, leading to more stable scene structures and preserved spatial relationships in imagined futures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preemptive action-level verification can effectively prevent physical robot failures and significantly improve the fidelity and efficiency of world model-based planning by filtering low-quality actions before execution or imagination.
> - A multi-task training objective combining Focal Loss for class imbalance, MSE for continuous advantage regression, and a soft-threshold loss for decision boundary smoothing enables robust and calibrated safety predictions from multimodal inputs.
> - Integrating a lightweight monitor that reuses foundation model representations and a controlled resampling strategy allows for practical, real-time safety enforcement without excessive computational overhead or indefinite loops.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has seen advancements with the development of large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and action-conditioned generative World Models (WMs). VLA models leverage multimodal sensory inputs (language, vision, proprioception) to generate control policies, enabling robotic agents to perform long-horizon tasks and exhibit generalization and reasoning capabilities in open-world environments. Concurrently, generative world models function as neural simulators, predicting future visual states based on candidate actions, which supports proactive planning and multi-step lookahead verification in policy optimization.

Despite these developments, a critical challenge in their practical deployment is the uncertainty inherent in learning-based action generation. Actions generated by VLA policies can be compromised by distributional shifts, temporal error accumulation, or insufficient confidence calibration. If low-quality actions are executed, they can lead to physical failures in robotic systems (e.g., collisions, object drops, kinematic violations) or cause misleading and computationally expensive rollouts in world models (e.g., blurry frames, target position drift, distorted physical relationships, false-success predictions). This uncertainty undermines the reliability and safety of embodied intelligent systems.

Existing runtime verification mechanisms in robotics often face several limitations. Traditional formal verification and control-theory-based barriers struggle with the high dimensionality of multimodal sensory inputs. Learning-driven approaches typically focus on evaluating environmental states or prediction results, rather than preemptively assessing the validity of candidate actions before they are executed or simulated. Furthermore, designing such systems for multimodal, dynamic robotic environments introduces considerable computational overhead. The data for training these systems also frequently suffers from severe class imbalance, with safe trajectories dominating, making it difficult for models to learn from rare but critical failure samples.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a unified runtime verification architecture, Pre-VLA, designed to perform preemptive action validity assessment. It aims to integrate safety constraints directly into the foundational inference pipeline, assessing action chunks before physical execution or world model imagination. By focusing on a lightweight, multimodal, action-level verification, Pre-VLA seeks to enhance the trustworthiness and efficiency of VLA and world model applications in embodied AI, bridging the gap between advanced generative capabilities and robust, safe deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and implement Pre-VLA, a unified runtime verification architecture capable of preemptively assessing the validity of candidate action chunks generated by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models or intended for World Model (WM) imagination. This overarching objective is driven by several detailed motivations and aims to overcome specific challenges:

**Motivation for Preemptive Action Verification:**

1. **Mitigating Physical Failures in Robotic Execution:** VLA models, being learning-based, can generate low-quality actions due to inherent generative uncertainty, distributional shifts, or calibration issues. If these actions are executed on a physical robot, they can lead to tangible, undesirable outcomes such as collisions, object drops, or irreversible kinematic violations. Such failures not only pose safety risks but also halt task progress, trapping the system in invalid states and wasting computational resources and time. Preemptive verification aims to intercept these dangerous actions before they cause physical harm or disrupt operations.
2. **Preventing Misleading and Costly World Model Rollouts:** World models are used as neural simulators for planning and lookahead. However, feeding them biased or unsafe action inputs can lead to erroneous future predictions. This includes visual artifacts like blurry frames, target position drift, distorted physical relationships, or even predictions of false success. Such inaccurate rollouts compromise the integrity of planning, waste significant GPU rendering costs, and propagate visual and physical errors, accumulating inaccuracies over imagination steps. Pre-VLA seeks to filter actions at the input to the WM, thereby preserving the fidelity and efficiency of simulated planning.
3. **Enhancing Trustworthiness in Embodied AI:** As embodied intelligent systems are deployed in dynamic and unpredictable real-world scenarios, ensuring their adherence to physical constraints and safety boundaries is paramount. A mechanism for preemptive safety assessment is considered a fundamental prerequisite for building trustworthy embodied intelligence.

**Challenges Addressed by Pre-VLA's Design:**

1. **Computational Overhead with Multimodal Data:** Robotic tasks involve high-dimensional, heterogeneous multimodal information (language, vision, proprioceptive states, actions). Processing and verifying such data typically introduces substantial computational overhead, which can affect the real-time control stability of robotic pipelines. A key motivation is to design an efficient and lightweight verification framework to avoid excessive latency.
2. **Lack of Direct Action Validity Assessment:** Existing runtime evaluation modules in embodied AI often focus on assessing environmental states or the results of predictions (e.g., collision detection after an action is taken). There is a gap in mechanisms that can directly and preemptively assess the *safety and validity of candidate actions* *before* they initiate physical execution or world model imagination.
3. **Severe Class Imbalance in Training Data:** Real-world offline exploration data for robotic tasks is often limited and heavily skewed. Safe trajectories typically form the vast majority of the data, while catastrophic failure samples are rare. This imbalance poses a challenge for standard machine learning optimization, as models trained on such data may struggle to accurately identify high-risk actions, leading to over-confidence or unstable decisions near safety boundaries. The motivation includes developing a training objective that explicitly addresses this imbalance.

In summary, Pre-VLA is motivated by the need for a robust and efficient safety layer that can operate preemptively at the action chunk level, handling multimodal data and imbalanced failure cases, to improve both the physical safety and the computational efficiency of modern VLA and world model systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Pre-VLA is presented as a unified runtime verification architecture designed for dual-mode deployment, supporting both VLA execution in physical actuators and World Model imagination. The methodology encompasses three core components: generating safety-aware supervision signals, developing a lightweight dual-branch runtime monitor, and implementing a dual-mode preemptive resampling scheduler.

**A. Safety-aware Supervision Signal**

To train Pre-VLA, supervision signals are derived from online exploration rollouts of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning, transforming relative value signals into absolute safety constraints.

1. **Chunk Advantage Estimation:**
 - The system jointly models multimodal context (language instruction `l`, visual observation `o_t`, proprioceptive state `q_t`, and VLA-generated candidate action chunk `C_t = {a^1_t..., a^K_t}`) as `x_t`.
 - A pre-trained PPO Critic network computes a K-step guided chunk advantage, `A^K_t`. This calculation incorporates discounted future rewards `r_{t+i}` and a bootstrapped value `V_ψ(x_{t+K})`.
 - A **safety mask `H_{t,K}`** is introduced, which is `0` if any `d_collision_{t+i} = 1` (collision or object drop within the chunk), effectively truncating future value estimation and preventing post-failure value signals from propagating.

2. **Failure-Aware Advantage Refinement:**
 - A **failure-aware penalty term `F_{t,K}`** is added to address delayed catastrophic failures (actions that don't immediately cause `H_{t,K}=0` but lead to failures later). This penalty applies an exponentially decayed weight to historical actions within a backtracking window `W` if an episode fails at `T_fail`.
 - The original advantage `A^K_t` is revised to include this penalty: `A^K_t = A^K_t - λ_{fail}F_{t,K}`.
 - The safety-aware advantage `A^K_t` is then **standardized** (`~A^K_t`) within each task-level data buffer (using mean `μ_task` and standard deviation `σ_task`) to stabilize monitoring thresholds across diverse tasks.

3. **Threshold-Based Safety Label Generation:**
 - The continuous `~A^K_t` is converted into a binary supervision label `y^{binary}_t` (safe/unsafe) using a unified safety pass threshold `τ_A`.
 - A continuous auxiliary supervision label `y^{cont}_t` is also retained, which is simply `~A^K_t`.

**B. Lightweight Dual-Branch Runtime Monitor Pre-VLA**

The monitor is designed for low computational overhead by reusing representations from a foundation model.

1. **Multimodal Backbone and Feature Extraction:**
 - Pre-VLA integrates an improved WorldVLA unified multimodal backbone, built upon the Chameleon vision-language model.
 - It uses dedicated action and state tokenizers to represent text, visual observations, proprioceptive states, and candidate action chunks as a unified token sequence.
 - The backbone's parameters are frozen during Pre-VLA training to preserve its generative capability.
 - Final-layer hidden states `H_t` are extracted as high-dimensional feature representations.

2. **Modality-Aware Feature Pooling:**
 - To disentangle multimodal information, a modality-aware pooling layer uses predefined masks (`M_m`) to extract token representations for each modality (text, image, state, action).
 - Masked Mean Pooling is applied independently to obtain modality-level summary vectors `z_m`.
 - These `z_m` are concatenated to form a unified global feature `z_t`. Missing modalities are handled with zero vectors.

3. **Dual-Branch Safety Prediction Head:**
 - The global feature `z_t` is fed into a minimalist dual-branch Feed-Forward Network (FFN).
 - **Classification Branch:** A two-layer FFN (`FFN_cls`) with a Sigmoid activation outputs the safety confidence `p_t ∈` for the action chunk.
 - **Regression Branch:** An equally lightweight regression network (`h_ϕ`) predicts the continuous chunk advantage `~A^K_t`.
 - This compact design enables proactive safety assessment with low inference time (100–200 ms).

4. **Multi-Task Training Objective:**
 - To address severe class imbalance (safe vs. failure actions) and unstable decision boundaries, a multi-task joint loss function `L` is formulated:
 - **Focal Loss (`L_cls`):** Replaces standard cross-entropy, using `α_focal` for class balancing and `β_focal` as a focusing parameter to down-weight easy safe samples and emphasize rare hard negatives.
 - **Regression Loss (`L_reg`):** A Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss on `~A^K_t` to regularize `z_t` with continuous dynamics-aware supervision.
 - **Soft-threshold Loss (`L_soft`):** Constructs a soft target `s_t` from `~A^K_t` (using `τ_A` and `τ_temp`) to smooth the decision boundary and improve confidence calibration for ambiguous actions.
 - The total loss is `L = L_cls + λ_{soft}L_{soft} + λ_{reg}L_{reg}`, with `λ` coefficients for balancing.

**C. Dual-Mode Preemptive Resampling Scheduler**

This mechanism integrates Pre-VLA into the inference pipeline, supporting both physical execution and world model imagination, while managing resampling attempts.

1. **Warm-up Horizon (`T_w`):** Pre-VLA is activated only after an initial warm-up period (`t ≥ T_w`) to avoid premature constraints on preparatory actions.

2. **VLA Execution Scheduling in Physical Actuator:**
 - **Preemptive Resampling:** The VLA generates candidate action chunks `a^K_{t,i}` with noise `ξ_i`. Pre-VLA computes `p_{t,i}`. If `p_{t,i} < τ_p` (below safety threshold), the action is intercepted, and the VLA is instructed to resample a new action (up to `N` retries).
 - **Regression-Guided Safe Fallback:** If `N` resamples fail to produce an action above `τ_p`, the scheduler selects the action chunk `a^{K,*}_{t}` with the highest continuous advantage score `bA^K_{t,i}` from the sampled candidates for execution. This prevents indefinite resampling loops while mitigating risk.

3. **World Model Imagination Scheduling:**
 - **Preemptive Compute Filtering:** Before the world model `f_ω` predicts future states `ô_{t+K}` conditioned on `a^K_t`, Pre-VLA evaluates `p_{t,i}`. If `p_{t,i} < τ_p`, the action is rejected, preventing unnecessary WM inference and GPU computation.
 - **On-Demand Imagination Truncation:** If, after `N` resamples, the highest advantage action is still risky, the current imagination rollout is truncated to avoid unreliable predictions and release computational resources.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Pre-VLA was conducted on the LIBERO robotic manipulation benchmark, covering action validity discrimination, closed-loop physical execution, world model rollouts, and ablation studies.

**A. Discrimination Capability of the Pre-VLA**

On an independent test set, Pre-VLA demonstrated the following discrimination capabilities:
- **F1 Score:** 0.8303
- **Accuracy:** 0.9542
- **Invalid-Action Precision:** 0.7200
- **Invalid-Action Recall:** 0.9800
- **False Pass Rate (invalid actions incorrectly passed):** 0.0200
- **False Reject Rate (valid actions incorrectly rejected):** 0.0491
These metrics indicate Pre-VLA's ability to reliably distinguish between valid and invalid actions, with a low tendency to pass unsafe actions.
Further analysis on full rollout trajectories showed a correlation between Pre-VLA's predictions and task outcomes: successful trajectories exhibited an average action-chunk pass rate of 0.8424, significantly higher than the 0.5167 for failed trajectories, demonstrating a 32.6% gap.

**B. Closed-Loop Execution Evaluation of Pre-VLA**

Pre-VLA was integrated into the RynnVLA-002 baseline and evaluated against RynnVLA-002 (no verification) and Random Resampling (randomly selecting one from candidate chunks) across four LIBERO suites.

1. **Suite-Level Performance Comparison:**
 - **Success Rate:** Pre-VLA consistently outperformed RynnVLA-002 and Random Resampling across all four suites. The average closed-loop success rate across LIBERO-10, LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Object, and LIBERO-Spatial improved from 30.79% (RynnVLA-002) to 37.62% (Pre-VLA), representing an absolute gain of 6.83 percentage points. Random Resampling did not yield consistent improvements, suggesting the gain is from intelligent filtering, not just additional sampling.
 - **Task Step Efficiency:** Pre-VLA achieved lower average task steps across all suites compared to RynnVLA-002 (e.g., 262.28 steps for LIBERO-10 with Pre-VLA, compared to higher for RynnVLA-002). This indicates that Pre-VLA aids in more efficient policy execution by guiding the agent towards valid trajectories.
 - **Resampling Attempts Analysis:** The average number of attempts per step for Pre-VLA ranged from 1.2841 to 2.0038 across the suites, indicating that the system generally finds valid actions within a limited number of resamples.
 - **Closed-Loop Behavior:** Visual analysis showed Pre-VLA mitigated target-deviating actions during grasping, leading to more stable execution trajectories and consistent progress toward the task goal.

2. **Runtime Efficiency Analysis:**
 - The average per-step inference time for Pre-VLA was 1097.73 ms, which is higher than RynnVLA-002's 613.77 ms due to the verification and potential resampling processes. Most suites were kept within 1000 ms, satisfying real-time requirements for many robotic manipulation tasks.
 - The average forward verification time for a *single candidate action chunk* by Pre-VLA was approximately 183.9 ms, indicating that the verifier itself has manageable computational overhead. The increased total inference time is primarily attributed to additional VLA sampling attempts.

**C. World Model Rollout Analysis**

Visual analysis of world model rollouts on a pick-and-place task demonstrated Pre-VLA's impact on WM imagination. Without Pre-VLA, erroneous actions led to accumulated prediction errors, manifesting as target-deviating robot arm motions, object position drift, blurring, ghosting artifacts, and ultimately false task completion in the imagined future. With Pre-VLA, low-quality actions were filtered before entering the WM, effectively suppressing error accumulation and propagation. This resulted in more stable scene structures, preserved spatial relationships among objects, and reasonable trajectories leading to task completion in the rollouts, improving the reliability of WM predictions.

**D. Ablation Study**

An ablation study on an independent test set evaluated the contributions of different training and decision strategies:
- **Raw Score Thresholding:** Achieved high invalid-action recall (0.9163) but low precision (0.2651) and high false reject rate (0.3271), indicating many valid actions were incorrectly rejected.
- **Imbalance-aware Loss Scoring:** Improved F1 (0.5078), accuracy (0.7900), and reduced false pass rate (0.0500), but precision (0.3466) and false reject rate (0.2306) remained moderate.
- **Ours (Pre-VLA with imbalance-aware loss and probability threshold calibration):** Achieved the best performance with an F1 score of 0.8303, accuracy of 0.9542, and significantly reduced false pass rate (0.0200) and false reject rate (0.0491). This validates the effectiveness of combining specialized loss functions with calibrated decision thresholds.

**E. Case Study (Stronger Baseline Setting)**

Pre-VLA was evaluated on six tasks where the RynnVLA-002 baseline already performed well.
- Pre-VLA achieved an average success rate of 90.72% across these tasks, outperforming RynnVLA-002's 86.51% by 4.21 percentage points.
- It also reduced the average number of task steps from 174.83 (RynnVLA-002) to 171.70, indicating improved efficiency even with strong base policies.
- The average decision time was 950.23 ms. These results suggest Pre-VLA provides stable gains in task success and execution efficiency even when applied to capable base policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Pre-VLA, a runtime verification architecture that contributes to the reliability and efficiency of embodied intelligent systems utilizing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World Models (WMs).

The significance of this work lies in its preemptive approach to action validity assessment. By evaluating candidate action chunks before they are physically executed or used for world model imagination, Pre-VLA directly addresses a core challenge of learning-based generative models: their inherent uncertainty. This is particularly impactful in two main areas:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Robustness in Physical Embodiment:** Pre-VLA's ability to filter out low-quality actions prevents physical failures such as collisions, object drops, and kinematic violations. This directly improves the safety of robotic operations, reducing the risk of damage to robots, environments, or objects, and preventing tasks from entering unrecoverable states. Such a safety layer is crucial for the deployment of advanced VLA-driven robots in real-world, dynamic environments.
2. **Improved Fidelity and Efficiency of World Model-based Planning:** By intercepting unsafe or biased actions before they enter a world model, Pre-VLA mitigates the accumulation of errors in future state predictions. This leads to more reliable and physically consistent rollouts, preventing misleading simulations that could lead to poor planning decisions or false task completion. Furthermore, by filtering actions that would otherwise lead to irrelevant or erroneous simulations, Pre-VLA reduces unnecessary GPU rendering costs and computational overhead, making world model-based planning more efficient.

The methodological contributions, including the use of safety-aware supervision signals derived from PPO Critic advantages, a lightweight multimodal dual-branch monitor with modality-aware pooling, and a multi-task training objective incorporating Focal Loss, advantage regression, and soft-threshold calibration, demonstrate a comprehensive approach to tackle the challenges of multimodal data, real-time constraints, and class imbalance. The dual-mode preemptive resampling scheduler further highlights a practical strategy for integrating verification into continuous control pipelines while managing computational budgets.

The empirical results on the LIBERO benchmark support the effectiveness of Pre-VLA, showing improvements in closed-loop success rates, reductions in task execution steps, and mitigation of world model hallucinations. The manageable average forward verification time of 183.9 ms per action chunk indicates a practical overhead for real-time systems.

Potential future impacts of Pre-VLA include:
- **Accelerating Trustworthy AI Deployment:** By providing a robust and efficient mechanism for ensuring action validity, Pre-VLA can accelerate the deployment of VLA and world model technologies in safety-critical applications.
- **Enabling More Complex Long-Horizon Tasks:** Increased reliability at the action level can enable the development of more complex, multi-step, and long-horizon tasks that are currently limited by the fragility of generative models.
- **Informing Future Model Design:** The insights gained from preemptive verification can feedback into the design of VLA and world models themselves, encouraging the development of inherently safer and more calibrated generative policies.

While limitations such as potential bias from PPO Critic estimates and accumulated inference costs under extreme OOD states are acknowledged, the work proposes future directions, including online self-calibrating verification and generalization across diverse robot embodiments and task distributions. This research represents a step towards developing more robust, safe, and efficient embodied intelligent systems.
