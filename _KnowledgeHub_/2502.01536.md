# VR-Robo: A Real-to-Sim-to-Real Framework for Visual Robot Navigation and Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> The VR-Robo framework introduces a Real-to-Sim-to-Real pipeline that creates photorealistic and physically interactive digital twins using 3D Gaussian Splatting and mesh representations for training legged robot navigation. Policies trained within these high-fidelity simulations achieve zero-shot transfer to real robots, demonstrating robust performance with success rates up to 100% in diverse real-world visual goal-tracking tasks.

## Problem

- Existing simulators lack the visual realism and geometric accuracy of real-world environments, creating a "Sim-to-Real gap" for vision-based robot policies.
- Training legged robots directly in the real world is risky, inefficient, and limits exposure to diverse environmental conditions required for robust learning.
- Traditional simulation methods struggle to support high-level visual navigation tasks that require sophisticated RGB-based perception and detailed environmental understanding.

## Method

- A Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework reconstructs real-world scenes into photorealistic 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) representations, enhanced with geometric priors from foundation models.
- These 3DGS scenes are integrated into a physics simulator (Isaac Sim) as a hybrid GS-mesh representation, where 3DGS handles visual rendering and meshes enable physical interaction and collision detection.
- High-level visual navigation policies are trained using Reinforcement Learning (PPO) in these reconstructed simulations, incorporating extensive domain randomization for robust Sim-to-Real transfer.

## Results

- Simulation experiments demonstrated a **100%** success rate for VR-Robo in a red cone reaching task, significantly outperforming baselines like imitation learning, random background, and textured mesh rendering.
- Real-world zero-shot deployment on Unitree Go2 and Galaxea R1 robots achieved high success rates (e.g., **100%** for Easy/Hard, **93.33%** for Medium tasks) across 6 diverse indoor scenes, showcasing robust transfer.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the ViT encoder and comprehensive domain randomization are crucial for successful Sim-to-Real transfer, as their exclusion led to substantially degraded performance in real-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining photorealistic neural rendering (3DGS) with physically interactive mesh representations in a unified simulation effectively bridges the Sim-to-Real gap for complex visual navigation tasks.
> - Leveraging foundation models for geometric priors and multi-view consistency constraints significantly improves the accuracy of reconstructed scenes, even in texture-less regions.
> - The use of a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) for image encoding and comprehensive domain randomization during RL training are critical for achieving successful zero-shot policy transfer to physical robots.
