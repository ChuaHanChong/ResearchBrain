# VR-Robo: A Real-to-Sim-to-Real Framework for Visual Robot Navigation and Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> The VR-Robo framework introduces a Real-to-Sim-to-Real pipeline that creates photorealistic and physically interactive digital twins using 3D Gaussian Splatting and mesh representations for training legged robot navigation. Policies trained within these high-fidelity simulations achieve zero-shot transfer to real robots, demonstrating robust performance with success rates up to 100% in diverse real-world visual goal-tracking tasks.

## Problem

- Existing simulators lack the visual realism and geometric accuracy of real-world environments, creating a "Sim-to-Real gap" for vision-based robot policies.
- Training legged robots directly in the real world is risky, inefficient, and limits exposure to diverse environmental conditions required for robust learning.
- Traditional simulation methods struggle to support high-level visual navigation tasks that require sophisticated RGB-based perception and detailed environmental understanding.

## Method

- A Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework reconstructs real-world scenes into photorealistic 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) representations, enhanced with geometric priors from foundation models.
- These 3DGS scenes are integrated into a physics simulator (Isaac Sim) as a hybrid GS-mesh representation, where 3DGS handles visual rendering and meshes enable physical interaction and collision detection.
- High-level visual navigation policies are trained using Reinforcement Learning (PPO) in these reconstructed simulations, incorporating extensive domain randomization for robust Sim-to-Real transfer.

## Results

- Simulation experiments demonstrated a **100%** success rate for VR-Robo in a red cone reaching task, significantly outperforming baselines like imitation learning, random background, and textured mesh rendering.
- Real-world zero-shot deployment on Unitree Go2 and Galaxea R1 robots achieved high success rates (e.g., **100%** for Easy/Hard, **93.33%** for Medium tasks) across 6 diverse indoor scenes, showcasing robust transfer.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the ViT encoder and comprehensive domain randomization are crucial for successful Sim-to-Real transfer, as their exclusion led to substantially degraded performance in real-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining photorealistic neural rendering (3DGS) with physically interactive mesh representations in a unified simulation effectively bridges the Sim-to-Real gap for complex visual navigation tasks.
> - Leveraging foundation models for geometric priors and multi-view consistency constraints significantly improves the accuracy of reconstructed scenes, even in texture-less regions.
> - The use of a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) for image encoding and comprehensive domain randomization during RL training are critical for achieving successful zero-shot policy transfer to physical robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous legged robots capable of navigating and interacting with complex real-world environments is a significant challenge in robotics. A major hurdle is the "Sim-to-Real gap," where policies trained in simulated environments often fail to transfer effectively to physical robots due to discrepancies in physics, visual fidelity, and environmental geometry. VR-Robo directly addresses this critical issue, positioning itself at the intersection of several rapidly advancing research areas: robot locomotion, visual perception, neural scene representation, and reinforcement learning.

Traditionally, efforts to bridge the Sim-to-Real gap have involved domain randomization or system identification to make simulators more robust. Recent advancements have explored using depth images or generative models to enhance visual perception in simulation for low-level locomotion tasks like parkour. However, these methods often struggle with high-level tasks requiring rich RGB-based perception and detailed environmental understanding because standard simulators lack the visual realism and complex geometry of the real world.

Concurrently, neural reconstruction techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have revolutionized view synthesis and scene representation, enabling the creation of highly photorealistic "digital twins" from real-world data. Previous works, such as NeRF2Real, attempted to integrate NeRF with simulation for bipedal locomotion but faced limitations in real-time rendering, physical interaction, and environment exploration. More recent efforts have explored 3DGS for robot manipulation and navigation, focusing primarily on visual fidelity.

VR-Robo distinguishes itself by uniquely combining the photorealism of 3DGS with physically interactive mesh representations in a "Real-to-Sim-to-Real" framework. Unlike previous works that either prioritize photorealism without robust physical interaction or focus on low-level locomotion tasks, VR-Robo aims to create environments that support *both* ego-centric visual perception *and* mesh-based physical interactions for *high-level visual navigation and locomotion learning*. This unified approach allows for the training of policies directly from RGB observations in a highly realistic simulated environment, minimizing the Sim-to-Real gap for zero-shot deployment. By leveraging recent advancements in 3DGS and incorporating foundation model priors (e.g., DepthAnything v2) for geometric consistency, VR-Robo pushes the boundaries of creating interactive digital twins that are truly suitable for training complex, vision-based robot behaviors in dynamic, unstructured settings. The integration of domain randomization further cements its position as a robust solution for real-world transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for VR-Robo stems from the inherent difficulties and limitations associated with training legged robots directly in real-world environments. Real-world experiments are often:
- **Risky**: Physical robots can be damaged, or they can pose safety hazards to surrounding personnel and property.
- **Inefficient**: Data collection is slow and labor-intensive, and recovering from failures can be time-consuming.
- **Limited in Diversity**: It is challenging to expose robots to a sufficiently diverse range of environmental conditions and failure scenarios required for robust policy learning.

Simulators offer a compelling alternative, providing a safe, efficient, and scalable platform for robot training. However, existing simulators present their own set of challenges, primarily the "Sim-to-Real gap." Specifically:
- **Lack of Visual Realism**: Traditional simulators often struggle to replicate the intricate visual details, textures, and lighting complexities of real-world environments, making policies trained on synthetic visuals less effective in reality.
- **Inaccurate Geometry**: Simulators may simplify complex real-world geometries, leading to discrepancies in physical interactions and navigation planning.
- **Limited High-Level Task Support**: Policies trained in these simulators are often constrained to low-level locomotion, failing to support high-level tasks that require sophisticated RGB-based perception, such as ego-centric visual navigation or goal-tracking in complex, unstructured spaces.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of the VR-Robo project are:
1. **Develop a Real-to-Sim-to-Real Framework**: To create a comprehensive pipeline that can reconstruct real-world scenes into high-fidelity simulations, train policies within these simulations, and then transfer these policies back to real robots with minimal performance degradation.
2. **Generate Photorealistic and Physically Interactive Digital Twin Simulations**: To leverage advanced neural reconstruction techniques (specifically 3D Gaussian Splatting) to create "digital twin" environments that accurately reflect the visual appearance and geometric complexity of real-world scenes, while also supporting realistic physical interactions (e.g., collision detection, terrain traversability) in a simulator like Isaac Sim.
3. **Enable Robust Visual Navigation and Locomotion Learning**: To facilitate the training of reinforcement learning policies for complex, high-level tasks like visual goal-tracking, where the robot must rely solely on RGB-based perception and proprioception to explore and navigate diverse environments.
4. **Achieve Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer**: To demonstrate that policies trained entirely within the photorealistic and interactive simulated environments can be deployed directly onto real robots without further fine-tuning, thereby significantly improving the efficiency and applicability of robot learning.
5. **Facilitate Rapid Adaptation in New Environments**: To show that the framework allows robots to quickly adapt and learn effective exploration strategies in previously unseen, complex real-world environments, such as households and factories.

In essence, VR-Robo aims to close the Sim-to-Real gap by creating a visually and physically accurate simulated bridge between the real world and robot learning, enabling more capable and versatile autonomous robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VR-Robo presents a Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework that systematically addresses the challenges of bridging the reality gap for visual robot navigation and locomotion. The methodology is structured into three main phases: Geometry-Consistent Reconstruction (Real-to-Sim), Building Realistic and Interactive Simulation, and Reinforcement Learning in Reconstructed Simulation (Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer).

**A. Geometry-Consistent Reconstruction (Real-to-Sim)**
The first phase focuses on transforming multi-view RGB images of a real-world scene into a high-fidelity digital twin suitable for simulation.
- **Gaussian-based 3D Scene Representation**: The core of the visual reconstruction is 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which represents the scene as a set of anisotropic Gaussian primitives. This allows for photorealistic rendering of view-dependent colors from any viewpoint.
- **Flattening 3D Gaussians for Geometric Modeling**: To enhance geometric accuracy, the authors flatten the 3D Gaussian ellipsoids into 2D planar structures by minimizing the shortest axis scale. This refined representation allows for the rendering of high-quality depth and normal maps, crucial for accurate geometry.
- **Foundation Model Geometric Prior Constraints**: To address challenges in texture-less regions and improve geometric fidelity, off-the-shelf monocular depth estimators (e.g., DepthAnything v2) and normal estimators are utilized. The inherent scale ambiguity of monocular depth maps is resolved by aligning them with sparse Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points using linear regression, providing a strong geometric regularization.
- **Multi-view Consistency Constraints**: A patch-based normalized cross-correlation (NCC) loss is applied between gray renders from different views to ensure photometric consistency across multiple viewpoints, further refining the scene reconstruction.

**B. Building Realistic and Interactive Simulation**
This phase integrates the reconstructed scene into a physics simulator (Isaac Sim) to support both visual perception and physical interaction.
- **GS-mesh Hybrid Representation**: VR-Robo employs a hybrid representation: 3D Gaussians are used for rendering photorealistic visual observations from the robot's ego-centric viewpoint, while a mesh representation is used for physical interactions and collision detection. The mesh is extracted by rendering depth maps from the reconstructed 3DGS scene and fusing them into a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) field.
- **Coordinate Alignment**: A critical step is aligning the reconstructed COLMAP coordinate system with the Isaac Sim environment. This is achieved by manually matching four non-coplanar points, typically using a regular reference object, to compute a homogeneous transformation matrix. Gaussian attributes (mean, scale, rotation, and spherical harmonics coefficients) are then adjusted to match the new coordinate system.
- **Occlusion-Aware Composition and Randomization**: For dynamic environments, individual objects (like target cones) are separately reconstructed. Their 3D Gaussians are cropped using their mesh bounding boxes and merged with the environment Gaussians. An occlusion-aware rendering process using z-buffering ensures correct visibility. This modular composition, combined with randomization of agent and object positions/orientations at the start of each episode, significantly increases environment diversity for robust RL policy training.

**C. Reinforcement Learning in Reconstructed Simulation (Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer)**
This phase details the training of the robot's locomotion and navigation policy within the reconstructed simulation.
- **Two-Level Asynchronous Control**: A hierarchical control strategy is used: a high-level policy (5 Hz) outputs velocity commands, which are then executed by a pre-trained low-level policy (50 Hz) that controls joint positions. The low-level policy enables basic agile locomotion and can handle moderate terrain (e.g., climbing slopes/stairs up to 15cm).
- **High-Level Policy Training**: The high-level policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within the GS-mesh hybrid simulation.
 - **State Space**: Inputs include a feature vector extracted from ego-view RGB images by a frozen, pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT), a one-hot encoded RGB command for the target cone color, the last action, and proprioceptive robot data (angular velocity, projected gravity, joint positions/velocities). An asymmetric actor-critic structure is used, with the critic having access to ground truth world coordinates.
 - **Action Space**: The policy outputs raw linear and angular velocity commands (Vx, Vy, V_yaw), which are then scaled and sent to the low-level policy.
 - **Reward Design**: A comprehensive reward function encourages goal-reaching (distance reduction, z-axis distance, heading alignment), penalized by regularization terms for stable and efficient behavior (e.g., stopping at the goal, tracking velocities, action L2 penalty).
- **Domain Randomization**: To minimize the Sim-to-Real gap, several domain randomization techniques are applied during training:
 - **Camera Pose Noise**: Uniform noise is added to the robot's camera pose before image rendering.
 - **Image Augmentation**: Random adjustments to brightness, contrast, saturation, hue, Gaussian blur, and occasional Gaussian noise are applied to rendered images.
 - **Image Delay**: A 0 or 1-step delay is randomly applied to the rendered images to simulate latency.

This systematic approach, from photorealistic reconstruction to physics-aware interaction and robust reinforcement learning with domain randomization, forms the backbone of the VR-Robo framework, aiming for seamless transfer to the real world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for VR-Robo comprehensively evaluate its capabilities in both simulated and real-world environments, focusing on its ability to reconstruct realistic simulations and achieve zero-shot Sim-to-Real policy transfer for visual robot navigation.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
The simulation experiments assessed the performance of VR-Robo against several baselines and ablation studies in a 'red cone reaching' task within a reconstructed scene.
- **Comparison Baselines**:
 - **Imitation Learning (IL)**: Trained on real-world teleoperated trajectories.
 - **Random Background**: Re-implementation of LucidSim, using random ImageNet backgrounds while maintaining the target object.
- **Ablation Baselines**:
 - **Textured Mesh**: Using a reconstructed textured mesh (e.g., from SuGaR) for direct rendering in Isaac Sim.
 - **CNN Encoder**: Replacing the ViT image encoder with a CNN.
- **Metrics**: Success Rate (SR) and Average Reaching Time (ART).
- **Results (Table II & III)**:
 - **VR-Robo (Ours)** achieved a perfect **100.00% SR** with an ART of **4.94 seconds**, significantly outperforming all baselines.
 - **Imitation Learning** performed very poorly (8.67% SR, 14.01s ART), highlighting its limitations in exploration and generalization due to insufficient data.
 - **Random Background** showed moderate performance (43.33% SR, 11.75s ART), indicating that generic visual augmentation is insufficient without scene-specific features.
 - **Textured Mesh** also struggled (22.00% SR, 12.73s ART). The paper notes that textured meshes from RGB-only input often suffer from "bulges" in texture-less regions, degrading rendering quality and hindering robot movement (Table IV shows lower PSNR for Textured Mesh: 27.73 vs. Planar 3DGS: 29.73). This validates the choice of a GS-mesh hybrid where GS handles visual fidelity.
 - **CNN Encoder** had substantially lower performance (54.67% SR, 10.04s ART) compared to ViT, confirming that the pre-trained ViT's ability to extract precise semantic features is crucial for visual policy training (Figure 8).

**B. Real-World Experiments**
Real-world experiments validated the zero-shot transferability and robustness of VR-Robo's trained policies.
- **Qualitative Experiments**: The policies were deployed on Unitree Go2 quadruped and Galaxea R1 humanoid robots across 6 distinct indoor scenes, under varying conditions including different target cone colors, changing lighting, random interference, and background variations (Figure 2). These qualitative results demonstrated remarkable robustness and adaptability to diverse, unstructured environments.
- **Quantitative Experiments**: Conducted on a Unitree Go2 robot in Scene 1 for a 'red cone reaching' task, categorized into Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulties based on the robot's initial position relative to the target (Figure 10).
- **Additional Real-World Baselines**:
 - **SARO**: A vision-language model (VLM)-based navigation method.
 - **w/o Domain Randomization**: An ablation where domain randomization techniques were excluded during training.
- **Metrics**: Success Rate (SR) and Average Reaching Time (ART).
- **Results (Table I)**:
 - **VR-Robo (Ours)** achieved exceptional performance: **100.00% SR** for Easy and Hard tasks, **93.33% SR** for Medium tasks, with ARTs of **4.96s, 6.28s, and 9.09s** respectively. This highlights the successful zero-shot transfer and robustness of the framework.
 - **Imitation Learning** and **Textured Mesh** failed completely (0.00% SR) in real-world deployment, emphasizing the difficulty of direct transfer from these approaches.
 - **SARO** showed moderate success on Easy tasks (66.67% SR) but performed very poorly on Medium and Hard tasks (26.67% and 0.00% SR, respectively). This was attributed to the VLM's long inference time and limited exploration capabilities in complex scenarios where the goal is not initially visible.
 - **CNN Encoder** and **w/o Domain Randomization** both showed significantly degraded performance compared to VR-Robo, particularly on harder tasks (e.g., w/o Domain Randomization achieved only 6.67% SR on Medium tasks and 0.00% on Hard tasks). This underscores the critical importance of both effective visual encoders (ViT) and comprehensive domain randomization for successful Sim-to-Real transfer.

In summary, VR-Robo successfully demonstrates the efficacy of its novel GS-mesh hybrid representation and integrated learning pipeline, achieving superior performance in both simulated training and zero-shot real-world deployment across diverse and challenging visual navigation tasks for legged robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VR-Robo framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for legged robots operating in complex, visually rich environments. Its significance stems from successfully bridging a persistent gap between highly realistic visual perception and physically accurate interaction within simulated environments, leading to robust real-world policy transfer.

**Key Significance:**
- **Novel Real-to-Sim-to-Real Paradigm**: VR-Robo is the first framework to integrate 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic rendering with mesh-based physical interaction in a unified Real-to-Sim-to-Real pipeline specifically for visual robot navigation and locomotion. This goes beyond prior work that focused on either photorealism (without interaction) or low-level tasks.
- **True Digital Twins for Robot Learning**: By creating photorealistic and physically interactive "digital twin" simulations directly from multi-view RGB images, the framework offers an unprecedented level of environmental fidelity for training, enabling robots to learn in conditions closely mirroring the real world.
- **RGB-Only Zero-Shot Transfer**: The ability to train policies solely on RGB observations within these simulated environments and then deploy them directly to real robots without further fine-tuning is a major breakthrough. This eliminates the need for expensive and risky real-world data collection and policy adaptation.
- **Robustness and Generalizability**: The extensive experiments, including deployment across six diverse real-world scenes, varied lighting conditions, object interference, and even different robot types (quadruped and humanoid), demonstrate the exceptional robustness and generalizability of the learned policies. This indicates the framework's capacity to handle unstructured and dynamic environments.
- **Efficient Exploration in Complex Scenarios**: The framework allows for the rapid adaptation of robot policies with effective exploration capabilities in new, complex environments. This is particularly valuable for tasks requiring high-level understanding, such as identifying and reaching specific targets that may not be initially visible.
- **Empirical Insights**: The ablation studies provide valuable empirical findings regarding architectural choices (e.g., the superiority of ViT encoders over CNNs) and the importance of components like domain randomization and avoiding artifacts from direct textured mesh rendering.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Development**: VR-Robo has the potential to dramatically accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous legged robots for a wide array of applications. By moving the bulk of training to a highly realistic simulation, it reduces development cycles, costs, and safety concerns.
- **Expanded Application Domains**: The framework can enable robots to perform more complex, high-level visual tasks in challenging environments like:
 - **Household Services**: Navigation, object retrieval, and interaction in cluttered, visually diverse home settings.
 - **Industrial Automation**: Inspection, logistics, and assistive tasks in dynamic factory floors.
 - **Search and Rescue**: Navigating disaster zones where visual perception is crucial for identifying objectives and safe paths.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: VR-Robo lays the groundwork for future advancements, such as integrating generative models for even larger-scale and more diverse scene reconstruction, potentially enabling robots to learn in infinitely varied virtual worlds. It also opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated perception-action paradigms based on highly realistic visual inputs.
- **Reduced Dependence on Specialized Sensors**: The success with RGB-only input for visual navigation is impactful, as RGB cameras are ubiquitous and inexpensive compared to depth sensors or LiDAR, simplifying hardware requirements for deployment.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
Despite its impressive achievements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations. The current framework is limited to indoor scenes with static terrains, and reconstructing scenes from RGB-only input can lead to structural imperfections requiring manual post-processing. Training a policy from scratch still takes approximately three days. Future work aims to extend the framework to larger-scale and more complex dynamic environments, incorporating generative models for scene reconstruction to enhance generalizability and potentially reduce the reliance on manual reconstruction and post-processing.

Overall, VR-Robo significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robot learning by providing a robust, visually realistic, and physically interactive simulation environment that demonstrably closes the Sim-to-Real gap for complex visual navigation tasks, paving the way for more capable and autonomous robots in the real world.
