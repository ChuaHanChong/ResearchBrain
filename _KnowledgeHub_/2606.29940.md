# WARP: Whole-Body Retargeting for Learning from Offline Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> The WARP framework from Georgia Institute of Technology enables robots to learn whole-body mobile manipulation from offline human demonstrations by providing a precise and consistent retargeting system. It significantly reduces palm position error by over 150 times and improves policy task-execution success by an average of 12% in simulation compared to optimization-based baselines.

## Problem

- Robot data collection through teleoperation is expensive, labor-intensive, and coupled to specific robot embodiments, requiring fresh data for each new platform.
- The 'embodiment gap' between humans and robots, especially for whole-body actions like torso orientation and base shifts, hinders direct transfer of human demonstrations.
- Existing offline motion retargeting methods often yield imprecise or inconsistent solutions, leading to action multi-modality and unreliable supervision signals for policy learning.

## Method

- **WARP** (Whole-body-Aware Retargeting from human Pose) employs a geometry-based, ==closed-form Shoulder-Elbow-Wrist (SEW) solver== that ensures unique joint configurations for consistent supervision.
- The system uses a two-stage process for robot SEW representation: an ==adaptive offset== for palm centroid alignment, followed by ==per-arm palm alignment== that enforces exact palm position matching.
- A =="lazy" mobile-base tracking== mechanism is integrated to provide smooth, low-frequency base motion, allowing the torso to absorb small upper-body adjustments and enhancing manipulation stability.

## Results

- WARP reduced palm position error by over **150 times** and achieved machine precision for hand orientation in simulation, while producing the most feasible motions with lowest joint-limit violation rates and self-collision fractions.
- Policies trained on WARP-retargeted data demonstrated an average of **12%** higher task-execution success in simulation compared to those trained on optimization-based baseline data.
- In real-world tasks, WARP consistently outperformed end-effector-centric baselines, enabling manipulation using arbitrary links (e.g., pushing with an elbow) and preserving implicit base-arm coordination, leading to improved policy learnability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A 'skeleton-first' approach that constructs a target robot SEW skeleton before solving for joint angles effectively decouples cross-embodiment skeleton alignment from joint-angle inverse kinematics.
> - Enforcing exact palm position matching as a hard constraint, rather than balancing end-effector accuracy with pose similarity, is critical for generating precise and learnable manipulation trajectories.
> - Closed-form retargeting solutions, as opposed to iterative optimization, eliminate action multi-modality by providing deterministic outputs, which is crucial for the reliable convergence of supervised learning policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has increasingly focused on learning policies from demonstrations, with behavior cloning being a prominent paradigm. Traditionally, these demonstrations are collected through teleoperation, utilizing various systems such as leader-follower rigs (e.g., GELLO, Mobile ALOHA), virtual reality (VR) interfaces (e.g., Open-TeleVision, Bunny-VisionPro), and hand-held grippers (e.g., UMI, DexCap). While these methods have shown progress in training expressive policies and vision-language-action models for tasks, they inherently tie data collection to the specific robot embodiment. This coupling necessitates fresh data collection for every new robot platform, a process that is particularly resource-intensive and costly for whole-body mobile manipulation due to the complexity of the hardware and effort involved in teleoperation.

An alternative approach involves motion retargeting, which translates human skeleton keypoints into robot joint trajectories. This method addresses the "embodiment gap" between humans and robots. Prior work in retargeting includes vision-based teleoperation systems (e.g., AnyTeleop, Open-TeleVision) that map human upper-body motion to robots, often trading generality for geometric fidelity. For humanoid robots, retargeting has been integrated with learned dynamics (e.g., HOMIE, CLONE) for locomotion-aware whole-body execution, and studies have shown that retargeting quality significantly influences downstream motion tracking (e.g., GMR). However, existing retargeting methods, especially when applied to the offline setting without real-time human correction, often yield imprecise or inconsistent solutions. This leads to issues such as action multi-modality, where similar human poses map to divergent robot configurations, which can hinder the reliable convergence of supervised learning policies.

WARP positions itself within this landscape by seeking to decouple data collection from robot embodiment, specifically for whole-body mobile manipulation. It aims to replace expensive robot teleoperation with scalable offline human demonstrations by providing a precise and consistent retargeting system. Unlike previous methods that primarily focus on end-effector tracking or rely on weighted optimization problems that introduce tradeoffs between precision and whole-body similarity, WARP introduces a geometry-based, closed-form approach to extract whole-body actions. This work targets the challenges of imprecision and inconsistency in offline retargeting, which are critical for generating high-quality supervision data for behavior cloning policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable scalable whole-body mobile manipulation by allowing robots to learn directly from offline human demonstrations. This approach is motivated by several factors:

1. **Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness of Human Data**: Human data is comparatively inexpensive and straightforward to record. It does not require a robot in the loop during data collection, which reduces hardware dependency and operational costs. Furthermore, human demonstrations naturally capture unconstrained whole-body behaviors in diverse, natural environments. This contrasts with teleoperation data, which is expensive, labor-intensive, and inherently limited by the specific robot embodiment and teleoperation hardware used.

2. **Addressing the Embodiment Gap**: A direct transfer from human demonstrations to robot actions is hindered by significant kinematic and dynamic differences, known as the "embodiment gap." Human motion, particularly for manipulation, carries intricate task-relevant structures involving torso orientation, elbow routing, and base shifts for reach extension. Retargeting methods that merely aim to place the robot end-effector near the human hand often discard this crucial whole-body information, rendering the robot's motion ineffective or infeasible for complex tasks.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Retargeting in Offline Settings**: The requirements for retargeted trajectories are significantly stricter in an offline setting compared to online teleoperation.
 - **Absence of Online Correction**: In teleoperation, a human operator can provide real-time compensation for robot wrist drift, missed contacts, or undesirable inverse kinematics solutions. Offline policy learning lacks this corrective mechanism; the retargeted trajectory serves as the sole supervision signal. This means retargeting errors are less tolerable and can lead to policy failures.
 - **Imprecision in Prior Methods**: Existing offline retargeting methods often face a fundamental tension. Prioritizing whole-body similarity to the human can degrade end-effector tracking accuracy, which is critical for manipulation tasks. Conversely, prioritizing end-effector accuracy can discard essential torso, elbow, and base structures necessary for feasible manipulation. Both scenarios transform retargeting errors into supervision errors, negatively impacting policy learning.
 - **Inconsistency (Action Multi-modality)**: Humanoids are highly redundant systems. Prior optimization-based retargeting methods can map nearly identical human poses to different robot configurations due to variations in solver initialization or local tradeoffs. This inconsistency results in similar observations being paired with divergent actions, which makes it challenging for supervised policies to learn a reliable and unimodal action mapping, thereby impeding their convergence.

To address these issues, the key objective of WARP is to develop an offline pipeline that explicitly models embodiment differences to extract precise, unique, and consistent whole-body actions. This involves ensuring that the robot actions are both physically replayable on the robot and statistically learnable by a policy, without requiring human-in-the-loop teleoperation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WARP (Whole-body-Aware Retargeting from human Pose) is an offline human-to-robot retargeting and policy-learning framework designed for whole-body mobile manipulation. It aims to produce precise, feasible, and consistent robot action data from human demonstrations.

**Overall Framework:**
The system receives an offline human whole-body trajectory (observation and kinematic skeleton, captured by MoCap or VR). An offline retargeter, `Ret`, maps the human skeleton at each timestep to a robot action. This retargeted trajectory then serves as both the proprioceptive state and supervision for a behavior cloning policy, which maps environment observations and state history to future actions.

**Core Mechanism: Shoulder-Elbow-Wrist (SEW) Representation and Closed-Form Solver:**
WARP leverages the Shoulder-Elbow-Wrist (SEW) representation, which describes each arm through the geometric positions of its shoulder (s), elbow (e), wrist (w), palm offset (t), and palm-center orientation (H). This representation is expressed in an upper-body-centric frame, making it scale-invariant and allowing comparison across different embodiments. The key abstraction separates arm shape from embodiment scale by representing arm configuration through limb directions (u = unit(e-s), l = unit(w-e)). For a humanoid arm, SEW constraints allow for a closed-form inverse kinematic solution, providing a unique joint configuration, which ensures consistency in the supervision signal.

**WARP's Formulation - Two Design Axes:**
WARP distinguishes itself from prior methods by:
1. **Skeleton-first approach**: It constructs a target robot SEW skeleton (s_rb, e_rb, w_rb, H_rb) *before* solving for joint angles, rather than optimizing directly in redundant joint space. This decouples cross-embodiment skeleton alignment from joint-angle inverse kinematics (IK).
2. **Hard constraint for palm matching**: WARP enforces exact palm position matching as a hard constraint, instead of balancing end-effector accuracy and pose similarity through weighted optimization objectives.

**Two-Stage Robot SEW Representation Solving:**
WARP computes the desired robot SEW representation in two stages:
1. **Adaptive Offset**: To account for link length differences, WARP first calculates how much the robot palm drifts from the human palm if the robot were to align its links in the human arm directions. It computes a two-arm centroid offset (p_offset) that nullifies the net palm displacement, then shifts the robot upper-body origin by this offset (Figure 2a). This aligns the palm centroids.
2. **Per-Arm Palm Alignment**: With the adaptive offset applied, each arm is solved independently:
 - **Hand Orientation**: Robot hand orientation (H_rb) directly transfers from the human (H_hm).
 - **Wrist Position**: The desired robot wrist position (w_rb) is recovered by transforming the human palm target (t) to the robot frame and accounting for the robot's wrist-to-palm offset (p_WT).
 - **Elbow Position**: To determine the robot elbow (e_rb) consistent with s_rb, w_rb, and link lengths (ℓ_SE, ℓ_EW), WARP preserves the human elbow configuration. It extracts the SEW elbow angle (ψ) from the human using `Stereo-sew.FwdKin` and then uses `Stereo-sew.InvKin` to transfer a unique elbow half-plane normal parametrization (n̂) to the robot frame. Subproblem 3 (SP3) from is then used to find the rotation angle (θ_SEW) about n̂ that positions the upper-arm vector correctly, ensuring the elbow lies at the correct distances from the shoulder and wrist while matching the human elbow half-plane.
 - **Joint Angles**: The corrected robot skeleton (s_rb, e_rb, w_rb, H_rb) is then passed to `SEW-Mimic` to recover joint angles (q_rb) in closed form.
 - **Torso Pose**: The 6-DoF torso pose (T_torso) is solved using `ik-geo`, typically choosing the knee-forward solution.

**Lazy Mobile-Base Tracking:**
Human data implies a base pose, but directly tracking every small upper-body shift with the robot base can lead to instability. WARP introduces a "lazy" base target (q_b) instead of directly tracking the desired base pose (q_d). The torso absorbs most small upper-body adjustments, while the base moves only for significant relocation. This mechanism uses deadbanded error and integrates a second-order system to ensure smooth, low-frequency base motion, enhancing manipulation stability without sacrificing mobility.

**Data Collection and Robot System:**
Human demonstrations are collected using a Meta Quest headset, logging whole-body motion (root, 6-DoF upper-body kinematic tree, full hand articulation) at 60 Hz. The robot platform is an RB-Y1 humanoid (holonomic base, 6-DoF torso, two 7-DoF arms, 12-DoF XHands), commanded via joint-impedance control at 100 Hz. For object tracking, AprilTags are used with head-mounted Project Aria Gen 2 Glasses. A Vicon system provides precise robot base localization, decoupling it from wheel-odometry drift for reliable replay.

**Policy Learning:**
The policy is instantiated using HPT, a transformer-based action-chunk model. WARP introduces a body-hierarchy bias (proximal-to-distal: base, torso, arm, hand) by using a block-causal attention mask during policy training. This promotes structured information flow in whole-body action prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WARP covered retargeting motion quality, downstream policy learnability in simulation, and real-world task execution, with comparisons against end-effector-only (MINK-EF) and body-constrained (MINK-TE) optimization-based baselines, as well as unconstrained SEW-Mimic (SEW-M).

**H1: Retargeting Motion Quality**
- **Accuracy**: In simulation, WARP demonstrated substantial improvements in tracking accuracy. It reduced palm position error by over 150 times compared to MINK-EF and achieved machine precision for hand orientation error. This precision is attributed to WARP's closed-form SEW solver and its design to enforce exact palm matching as a hard constraint. While some "joint-limit on" (JL-on) scenarios showed increased error due to human-like postures pushing targets outside the robot's reachable workspace, WARP's overall accuracy remained competitive or superior.
- **Feasibility**: WARP produced the most feasible motions, exhibiting the lowest joint-limit violation rates and self-collision fractions. Its human-like solutions effectively act as a self-collision avoidance prior, which was less captured by baseline methods that sometimes drove the robot into physically infeasible configurations (Figure 4a).
- **Consistency**: WARP demonstrated superior solution consistency, achieving orders of magnitude better Nearest-Neighbor Action Disagreement (NNAD), PCA, and RMS solver-variation scores. Unlike optimization-based approaches that can yield inconsistent solutions based on initialization (Figure 4b), WARP's closed-form nature ensures deterministic and initial-condition-independent outputs, providing a consistent target for policy learning.
- **Speed**: WARP solved retargeting problems approximately 30 times faster than iterative optimization-based retargeters, enabling efficient processing of large datasets.

**H2: Downstream Learnability (Simulation Evaluation)**
- **Policy Success Rate**: On tasks from the DexMimicGen dataset (e.g., coffee, pouring, can-sort), WARP and MINK achieved comparable open-loop replay success rates, indicating that both could generate trajectories that the robot could physically execute. However, policies trained on WARP-retargeted data significantly outperformed those trained on MINK data, showing an average of 12% higher task-execution success. This margin was particularly pronounced in fine-grained manipulation tasks like "coffee," suggesting that the underlying motion quality and consistency provided by WARP are crucial for policy learning, even if replay metrics appear similar.
- **Embodiment Transfer**: The study also indicated WARP's capacity to retarget robot motions to different robot embodiments, such as Dual Kinova3, RBY1, and OpenArm (Figure 6).

**H3: Necessity of Whole-Body Retargeting (Real-World Evaluation)**
- **Outperformance on Real-World Tasks**: Across real-world tasks (Pick-up-laundry, Push-cart, Rotate-box, Fridge-door-closing), WARP consistently outperformed the end-effector-centric MINK baseline (Figure 7).
- **Preservation of Human-like Posture and Constraints**: WARP's ability to coordinate the torso and arms in a manner consistent with human demonstration resulted in maintained palm contact and recoverable postures. In contrast, MINK often led the robot's upper body into extreme or colliding configurations, making the motions feasible only during open-loop replay, but not robust for policy learning. Examples included MINK over-rotating the torso, hitting the cart, or accumulating wrist-orientation errors that caused grasp failures (Figure 9).
- **Arbitrary Link Manipulation**: WARP demonstrated the capability to support manipulation using links other than the end-effector, such as pushing a fridge door shut with the elbow (Figure 8d, Figure 9d). This functionality is outside the scope of end-effector-only formulations and validates the necessity of whole-body retargeting.
- **Implicit Base-Arm Coordination**: WARP preserved implicit base-arm constraints that end-effector- or keypoint-based retargeting methods tended to discard (e.g., Push-cart task).
- **Improved Learnability**: While WARP achieved high replay success rates (e.g., 90% for the fridge task), its main advantage was observed in policy training. Even when replay rates were comparable, policies trained with WARP-generated data were stronger (e.g., 65% vs. 40% on "Rotate Box"). This finding underlines that motion quality and consistency, rather than mere replay completion, are decisive for downstream policy learning success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of WARP signifies a step towards more scalable and robust robot learning from human demonstrations for whole-body mobile manipulation. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Enabling Scalable Data Collection**: By providing a method to accurately and consistently translate offline human demonstrations into robot actions, WARP offers a pathway to bypass the cost and complexity associated with traditional robot teleoperation. Human data is inherently cheaper, easier to collect, and capable of capturing a wider variety of natural whole-body behaviors. This could significantly accelerate the data acquisition process for developing complex manipulation policies.

2. **Achieving Zero-Shot Whole-Body Mobile Manipulation**: WARP represents a framework capable of enabling zero-shot whole-body mobile manipulation directly from offline human demonstrations. This eliminates the need for human-in-the-loop teleoperation data, thereby streamlining the deployment of learned skills onto new robot platforms without extensive re-demonstration or calibration.

3. **Enhancing Policy Learnability and Robustness**: The core contribution of WARP – producing precise, unique, and consistent robot trajectories – directly addresses the challenges of action multi-modality and imprecision that hinder supervised policy learning. By providing a reliable and unimodal supervision signal, WARP facilitates the training of more robust and successful policies for mobile manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring intricate whole-body coordination, precise end-effector control, and interaction with the environment using various body parts.

4. **Advancing Retargeting Methodology**: WARP's geometry-based, closed-form approach to retargeting, especially its use of the SEW representation with hard palm constraints and adaptive offsets, provides an analytical solution to a problem typically tackled with iterative optimization. This method offers inherent consistency and speed benefits over prior art, which can be critical for real-time applications or large-scale data processing.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: This work lays a foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Perception-Agnostic Learning**: The current system relies on simplified perception (AprilTag tracking). Future work can integrate WARP with more advanced, marker-free visual-motor policies, exploring how high-quality human pose data can aid in learning directly from raw visual observations.
 - **Dynamics-Aware Retargeting**: While WARP is primarily kinematic, future extensions could incorporate robot dynamics and controller models to improve real-world execution robustness, especially in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
 - **Human Data Quality**: Addressing the inherent noise and artifacts from VR headset-based human motion capture could lead to even higher quality robot trajectories and policy performance.

In summary, WARP's capability to generate high-fidelity, learnable robot actions from readily available human demonstrations has the potential to democratize the development of mobile manipulation skills, making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible and scalable across a variety of robot embodiments and tasks.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
- **Kinematic Focus**: The current formulation is primarily kinematic, assuming ideal controller tracking without explicitly modeling robot dynamics, actuator limits, or contact forces.
- **Simplified Perception**: The policy relies on low-dimensional object states (AprilTags), limiting its generalization to marker-free environments and complex visual scenes.
- **Human Data Quality**: The use of a VR headset for human data collection can introduce jitter, drift, or discontinuities, which propagate into the retargeted robot trajectories.
