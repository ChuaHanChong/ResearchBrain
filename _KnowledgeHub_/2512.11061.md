# VDAWorld: World Modelling via VLM-Directed Abstraction and Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Cambridge researchers introduce VDAWorld, a framework that leverages Vision-Language Models to synthesize executable "world programs" from static images and text, creating dynamic, interactive simulations. The system generates physically plausible models, demonstrating superior adherence to physical laws and logical rules compared to pixel-prediction models, and enables direct user interaction with the simulated environment.

## Problem

- Generative video models produce visually realistic but physically implausible simulations, frequently violating fundamental laws and lacking interactivity or queryable internal states.
- 3D/4D scene reconstruction methods prioritize visual fidelity over inferring physical properties, leading to representations unsuitable for tractable physical simulation or abstract rule-based environments.
- Current AI systems struggle to distill complex visual scenes into abstract, physically grounded, and interactive world models necessary for robust reasoning and planning.

## Method

- A Vision-Language Model (VLM) is used to synthesize a Python "==world program==" that abstracts a static visual scene and text into a dynamic, executable simulation.
- The VLM generates a program specifying a ==grounded abstract scene representation== (2D/3D), infers ==latent dynamics==, and selects an appropriate ==physics or logic-based simulator==.
- A comprehensive ==prompting strategy== guides the VLM, incorporating task principles, environment structure, and access to a ==perception and geometry toolbox==.
- A two-stage ==self-correction mechanism==, including a VLM-based "critic" and "code refiner," along with ==automated debugging==, enhances the robustness and accuracy of the generated programs.

## Results

- VDAWorld generated physically consistent and plausible simulations across diverse scenarios, including rigid body dynamics, thermodynamics, and abstract 2D logical systems like Conway's Game of Life.
- The framework achieved a perfect F1 score on Conway's Game of Life, significantly outperforming pixel-prediction models in adherence to explicit logical rules by synthesizing rule-based programs.
- It enabled fine-grained user intervention and control over simulation kinematics, dynamics, and appearance through direct modification of its interpretable, programmatic output, unlike black-box generative models.
- Performance on a modified PhysicsIQ benchmark was comparable to leading video generation models, while fundamentally avoiding their common physical inconsistencies and violations of object permanence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs can act as intelligent orchestrators to synthesize explicit, interpretable "world programs" that abstract scenes and select appropriate simulators, offering a robust alternative to opaque generative models.
> - Explicit physics engines and logical rule systems are essential for guaranteeing physical plausibility and logical consistency in dynamic world models, overcoming statistical failure modes of pixel-prediction approaches.
> - Abstracting away visually complex but physically irrelevant information into simulation-optimized representations is key for efficient, accurate physical reasoning and diverse dynamic scenarios.
> - Programmatic world models enable unparalleled interactivity and controllability, allowing users to inspect, modify, and explore diverse hypothetical scenarios through direct code manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in VDAWorld addresses fundamental challenges within the field of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the creation of robust and interpretable world models. In recent years, a dominant paradigm for world modeling has emerged based on large-scale generative video models, such as Genie and Wan2.2. These models, trained on extensive visual datasets, have demonstrated capabilities in synthesizing complex, dynamic scenes with visual realism. However, VDAWorld identifies critical limitations in this approach. Generative video models frequently violate physical and logical rules, such as object permanence, collision physics, and causality, because they learn statistical correlations from pixels without explicit physical enforcement. Furthermore, they operate as "black boxes," producing pixel sequences that lack a structured, queryable world representation, thereby limiting interpretability and the ability to apply novel physical interactions beyond those observed during training.

A separate line of research has focused on reconstructing 3D or 4D scene representations from images, exemplified by foundational models like DUST3R for dense geometry and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) for photorealistic novel views. While these methods excel at capturing geometry and appearance, their primary objective is not to model underlying physical dynamics. This focus also renders them unsuitable for abstract 2D environments governed by logical rules, such as cellular automata like Conway’s Game of Life. Some approaches, like PhysGen3D and PhysGen, attempt to reconstruct scenes as simulations within fixed simulators. However, their rigid pipelines restrict their applicability to specific types of scenes and dynamics (e.g., 3D material point or 2D rigid body scenes), leading to a lack of generalization and robustness to reconstruction failures.

VDAWorld positions itself as a new paradigm that diverges from direct pixel prediction and rigid reconstruction methods. It builds on advances in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) used as agents for program synthesis, a field where VLMs generate plans or actions for agents within existing environments. VDAWorld extends this concept by using a VLM not just to plan, but to actively synthesize a complete, executable, and abstract world model from an image and caption pair. This approach aims to combine the representational power of VLMs with the explicit physical and logical consistency afforded by dedicated simulation engines, addressing the limitations of both pixel-space video models and specialized 3D reconstruction-for-simulation methods by providing a more versatile, interpretable, and physically/logically grounded world model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of VDAWorld is to develop a novel framework for world modeling that transcends the limitations of existing approaches, particularly large-scale video generation models. The researchers identify several key motivations and specific problems that VDAWorld aims to address:

1. **Overcoming Physical Implausibility:** A central motivation stems from the observation that current generative video models, despite their visual fidelity, frequently produce physically implausible scenarios. These models learn statistical correlations at the pixel level and do not inherently enforce fundamental physical laws like object permanence, collision response, or causality. This leads to outputs where objects might unnaturally merge, disappear, or exhibit unphysical motion. VDAWorld aims to ensure physical plausibility by distilling visual inputs into a structured, abstract representation that can be acted upon by robust physics simulators.

2. **Enhancing Interpretability and Queryability:** Generative video models are typically "black boxes," outputting sequences of pixels without providing an explicit, structured representation of the scene's underlying state. This opacity makes it impossible to inspect the environment's internal state, understand the causal factors behind observed changes, or query specific properties of objects within the simulated world. VDAWorld's objective is to create an explicit, structured world model that is inherently interpretable, allowing for direct inspection of object properties, relationships, and dynamics.

3. **Enabling General Interactivity and Control:** Most video models lack native mechanisms for complex physical interaction. While some incorporate limited action conditioning, the action space is often constrained to simple transformations (e.g., camera viewpoint) or narrow application domains (e.g., driving). This limits a user's ability to apply novel forces, modify object properties, or explore alternative outcomes. VDAWorld aims to provide a framework for general interactivity, allowing users to define and apply diverse interventions to the simulated world through its programmatic representation.

4. **Achieving Versatility Across Diverse Domains:** Existing simulation-based approaches, such as PhysGen and PhysGen3D, often rely on rigid pipelines and fixed simulators, limiting their generalizability across different types of scenes and dynamics. For instance, a method designed for rigid body dynamics may not apply to fluid dynamics or abstract logical systems. VDAWorld is motivated to develop a versatile world model capable of handling a wide range of scenarios, from complex 3D physical interactions to abstract 2D logical environments like Conway's Game of Life, by adaptively selecting appropriate representations and simulators.

5. **Inferring Latent Dynamics from Static Scenes:** Humans can observe a static scene and infer not only its current structure but also how it might evolve over time or in response to actions. VDAWorld seeks to replicate this capability in AI by enabling the model to infer latent dynamics from a static image-caption pair, predicting plausible future states without explicit temporal input.

In summary, VDAWorld's key objectives are to build general-purpose world models through structured, simulation-ready representations rather than pixel prediction, using a VLM as an intelligent agent and inverse dynamics engine to construct grounded scene representations, leverage a diverse toolbox of computer vision tools, and demonstrate the advantages of this approach in terms of physical plausibility, general interactivity, and computational efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VDAWorld introduces a framework where a Vision-Language Model (VLM) orchestrates the transformation of a static image and a descriptive text caption into a dynamic, interactive, and interpretable Pythonic simulator of the scene. This process is conceptualized as the VLM acting as an inverse dynamics model that synthesizes a "world program."

**Core Components of the World Program:**
The generated Python world program encapsulates three key elements:
1. **Grounded Abstract Representation (P_s):** The VLM constructs a 2D or 3D model of the scene, optimized for simulation. This involves selecting from a suite of computer vision tools, such as segmentation, 3D reconstruction, and 3D mesh and primitive fitting. The abstraction intentionally discards physically irrelevant information (e.g., fine-grained textures) to create a structured model. The choice of representation is grounded in the scene's native dimensionality, allowing for a full 3D pipeline for spatial environments or a simpler 2D abstraction for planar ones.
2. **Inferred Action Dynamics (a_0:T):** The VLM predicts the most probable abstract action sequence (e.g., an impulse, velocity, or geometric transformation) that would drive the scene's evolution. This inference is based on the visual cues from the image and information provided in the caption, effectively making the VLM an action inference engine. Actions can range from explicit physical forces to camera movements or can be null if dynamics evolve naturally from initial conditions.
3. **Simulator Program (P_τ):** The VLM determines the most compatible physics simulator to act upon the abstracted representation. This choice is adaptive and co-dependent with the representation: for a scene with blocks, a rigid body model is paired with a rigid body solver; for water, a particle system is used with a fluid dynamics engine; and for logical systems like Conway's Game of Life, a logical rule engine is employed.

**Formal Formulation:**
The methodology is formally represented as a two-stage process:
- **Abstraction Phase:** A generative function Φ interprets the input image (I) and caption (C) to produce the structured world program components: `P_s`, `a_0:T`, `P_τ`, and a rendering function `R`. The initial state `s_0` is then derived by executing `P_s` on the image: `s_0 = Exec(P_s, I)`.
- **Simulation Phase:** The generated programs are executed alongside the predicted actions to roll out future states. Subsequent states are generated by the transition program `P_τ` using the current state `s_t` and action `a_t`: `s_t+1 = Exec(P_τ, s_t, a_t)`. A rendering function `R` visualizes the state `s_t` as `Î_t = R(s_t)`, although this is separate from the core world model state.

**Vision API Usage (Toolbox):**
A critical enabler for VDAWorld is a comprehensive suite of computer vision tools exposed via an API, which the VLM can selectively call. This toolbox includes:
- **Perception tools:** E.g., SAM 3 for segmentation.
- **Geometry tools:** E.g., MoGe-2 for general geometry, SAM 3D for mesh extraction, and RANSAC-based methods for fitting geometric primitives (circles, squares, cubes, cylinders) to point clouds.
The VLM is designed to leverage these existing, state-of-the-art implementations without needing to re-implement them.

**Prompting for World Program Generation:**
The VLM (Gemini 3.1 Pro is used) is guided at inference time by a multi-part prompt rather than fine-tuning:
- **Task Specification:** High-level natural language instruction outlining the objective: analyze the image and caption, then produce a self-contained Python script to simulate the scene's future, encompassing `P_s`, `P_τ`, and `a_0:T`.
- **Environment Specification:** Provides the VLM with a Python `Simulator` base class to inherit from, defining the core methods (`initial_state`, `transition_function`, `inferred_action`) that must be implemented. It also lists available Python libraries (e.g., `numpy`, `mujoco`, `pygame`, `pymunk`) for simulation.
- **API Specification:** Details the functionality and usage of the available computer vision toolkit.

**Agentic Loop for Refinement:**
To enhance robustness and accuracy, VDAWorld employs an agentic workflow where the VLM iteratively refines its generated code. The VLM has access to several agentic tools:
- **Implementational Tools:** Write code, edit code using unified diff blocks, read API documentation, and execute code.
- **Debugging Tools:** Read its own generated code, interpret error messages from the terminal, read standard output, and access metadata from API calls (e.g., number of objects segmented).
- **Critique Tools:** View individual frames of the simulation output and, crucially, analyze a **motion history image**. This image visualizes all dynamic activity over the simulation duration, encoding the time of motion (hue), frequency of motion (saturation), and whether any motion occurred (value). This allows the VLM to identify discrepancies between expected and simulated motion and refine its code accordingly.

**Interactivity and Control:**
The programmatic nature of VDAWorld facilitates explicit user intervention and counterfactual reasoning through four primary modes:
1. **Caption Modification:** Altering the input prompt (C) to generate revised programs and actions.
2. **Action Modification:** Directly editing the code defining the inferred action sequence (`a_0:T`).
3. **Transition Function Modification:** Modifying the physical rules or engine logic within `P_τ`.
4. **Initial State Modification:** Changing the starting configuration or geometry instantiated by `P_s`.

This methodology ensures that the generated dynamics are consistent, interpretable, and amenable to structured user interventions, moving beyond the limitations of opaque, pixel-based world models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VDAWorld's experimental evaluation demonstrates its capabilities across diverse domains, emphasizing flexibility, physical plausibility, and interpretability compared to existing methods.

**Qualitative Observations:**
VDAWorld can generate abstractions and simulations for complex scenarios, including solid body dynamics and thermodynamics. For simple scenes like a whiteboard drawing, it correctly infers a 2D abstraction is sufficient, intentionally discarding high-frequency visual details to prioritize accurate, physically plausible simulations over photorealism.

**Benchmark Performance:**
The method was evaluated on three benchmarks: the existing PhysicsIQ benchmark, and two newly introduced benchmarks, HSPBench and ConwayBench, alongside the MME-CoF-Pro benchmark. Comparisons were made against leading video generation models (Wan2.2, Lumiere, VideoPoet, Veo3, Sora2, SeedDance 1.0, Kling 2.1, Cosmos Transfer) and physical simulation approaches (PhysGen, PhysGen3D).

- **PhysicsIQ Benchmark (Table 1):**
 - VDAWorld demonstrated superior performance, achieving a combined score of 49.7 in the "best-of-three trials" setting, outperforming the strongest video baseline, Wan2.2 (46.2).
 - In the "one-trial" setting, VDAWorld slightly outperformed Wan2.2 and significantly surpassed other baselines.
 - Qualitative analyses (Figure 4) indicated that VDAWorld consistently generated physically plausible results, whereas video models like Wan2.2 often produced non-physical artifacts such as object merging, indicating a lack of true underlying physics modeling.
 - Fixed-pipeline simulation methods like PhysGen and PhysGen3D struggled with generalization across diverse dynamics, with PhysGen3D exhibiting a 69.9% failure rate due to rigid simulator reliance. VDAWorld is highlighted as the first simulation-based method competitive with state-of-the-art video models.

- **HSPBench (High School Physics Benchmark) (Table 2):**
 - This benchmark, composed of 40 high-school level deterministic physics problems, explicitly tested adherence to physical rules.
 - VDAWorld achieved significantly lower VQA (Visual Question Answering) error (75) compared to baselines like Wan+Gemini (227) and Veo+Gemini (291), indicating more accurate predictions about future scene states.
 - It also yielded higher scores on spatial (0.66), spatiatiotemporal (0.25), and weighted spatial (0.73) IoU metrics, confirming superior physical accuracy in deterministic settings.

- **ConwayBench (Conway's Game of Life Benchmark) (Figure 7, Right; Table 5):**
 - In this purely logical setting, VDAWorld achieved a perfect F1 score of 1.000 across all frames, acting as a guaranteed correct rule engine. This demonstrated its flexibility in generating correct simulations in domains governed by abstract rules rather than physical laws.
 - Video models consistently achieved low performance, failing to capture the dynamics, and Gemini Pro struggled with parsing complex initial states, leading to diverging simulations.

- **MME-CoF-Pro Benchmark (Table 3):**
 - VDAWorld outperformed a wide range of state-of-the-art video models, including Veo 3.1, Sora2, and Kling 2.1, in this challenging benchmark evaluating visual logic and scientific reasoning across 16 categories. It achieved the highest reasoning, consistency, and average scores (e.g., an average score of 73.6 compared to the next best, Kling-v2.1 at 65.1).

**Computational Efficiency (Figure 7, Left):**
VDAWorld's simulation execution time scales with the physical complexity of the scene, unlike video models which incur a large, fixed cost for pixel generation. For instance, Game of Life simulations run approximately 100 times faster than complex thermodynamic simulations. Considering total computation time (including VLM inference and refinement), VDAWorld processed PhysicsIQ scenes in an average of 11 minutes compared to 30 minutes for Wan2.2. For Conway's Game of Life, VDAWorld achieved a perfect simulation in 5 minutes on average, while Wan2.2 took 30 minutes for a diverging video.

**Ablation Study (Table 4):**
An ablation study on the PhysicsIQ benchmark confirmed the significance of each component:
- Ablating the Motion History Image (MHI) had the smallest impact (-3.8%), as some information is redundant with frame viewing.
- Ablating the Vision API (-10.7%), Caption (-13.2%), or Image (-14.6%) led to performance drops.
- Ablating "Tool Use" (removing agentic capabilities) resulted in a substantial performance decrease (-33.5%).
- Using Gemini 3.1 Flash instead of Gemini 3.1 Pro reduced performance by 20.1%.
- A VLM-only baseline, directly generating code without the API, tool use, or MHI, performed poorly (-56.4%), indicating that the structured world-program interface, simulation-grounded vision API, action/transition decomposition, and agentic refinement are crucial.

**Applications Demonstrated:**
- **Counterfactual Generation (Figure 8):** The code-based representation allows for intuitive, zero-shot human intervention by directly editing the generated Python code. Examples include modifying the caption to change dynamics (e.g., from collision to camera pan), altering transition functions (e.g., inventing new Conway rules), adjusting action sequences (e.g., precise robotic arm trajectory control), and modifying initial state geometry (e.g., adding dominoes).
- **Visual Reasoning:** The reported VQA and MME-CoF-Pro results underscore VDAWorld's enhanced capabilities in visual reasoning, derived from its explicit understanding of scene dynamics.

**Limitations:**
The study acknowledges that VDAWorld's quality is dependent on the fidelity and coverage of its perception tools, simulator library, and the underlying VLM's coding capabilities. Errors in segmentation or 3D reconstruction can lead to simulations that are internally consistent but do not accurately reflect the input scene. Initial exploration into photorealistic rendering from VDAWorld simulations using content-transfer methods like Cosmos Transfer (Figure 10) suggests feasibility but highlights it is not the primary focus and appearance is not perfectly maintained.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VDAWorld represents a significant advancement in the field of world modeling by proposing a new paradigm that addresses key limitations of prevailing approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in World Modeling:** By moving away from direct pixel prediction, VDAWorld establishes a novel approach that constructs explicit, structured, and simulation-ready representations of the world. This is a fundamental shift from learning implicit, opaque models to synthesizing interpretable, executable programs that govern world dynamics.

2. **Enhanced Physical and Logical Plausibility:** The framework's ability to ground simulations in explicit physics engines and logical rules ensures that generated scenarios are physically and logically consistent. This directly resolves a major shortcoming of generative video models, which frequently produce implausible artifacts due to their reliance on statistical correlations rather than underlying physical laws. This improved plausibility is critical for applications requiring reliable predictions of real-world phenomena.

3. **Increased Interpretability and Explainability:** The output of VDAWorld is a Python program, offering inherent interpretability. Researchers and users can directly inspect the code to understand the underlying scene representation, inferred dynamics, and simulation rules. This transparency contrasts with the "black box" nature of pixel-space models, facilitating debugging, trust, and deeper scientific understanding of intelligent systems.

4. **Unprecedented Interactivity and Control:** The programmatic nature of VDAWorld enables intuitive, zero-shot human intervention through direct code editing. This capability for fine-grained control over initial states, action sequences, and even the fundamental rules of the simulated world (e.g., changing gravity or cellular automata rules) opens new avenues for counterfactual reasoning, planning, and interactive agent training that are beyond the scope of current pixel-based or fixed-pipeline methods.

5. **Versatility Across Diverse Domains:** VDAWorld's adaptive selection of scene representations and compatible simulators allows it to generalize across a broad spectrum of dynamic scenarios, from rigid body mechanics and fluid dynamics to abstract logical systems like Conway's Game of Life. This versatility makes it a more general-purpose world model suitable for varied AI tasks.

6. **Computational Efficiency for Varied Complexities:** The method's computational efficiency, where simulation time scales with the intrinsic physical or logical complexity of the scene, represents a practical advantage. This is particularly impactful for simpler dynamics, which can be simulated much faster than the fixed computational cost associated with generating pixels in video models.

7. **Foundation for Advanced AI Agents:** VDAWorld contributes to the vision of creating more grounded, interactive, and compositional autonomous agents. By equipping VLMs with the ability to reason about the world and synthesize code to model it, the research points towards a future where AI systems can construct and manipulate explicit representations of their environment, leading to more robust planning, decision-making, and causal reasoning capabilities.

In conclusion, VDAWorld pushes the boundaries of world modeling by offering a robust, interpretable, and highly controllable framework. Its programmatic approach has the potential to influence the development of next-generation AI systems that are not only intelligent but also understandable, adaptable, and capable of operating within a physically and logically coherent world.
