# Learning to See Before Seeing: Demystifying LLM Visual Priors from Language Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta Superintelligence Labs and University of Oxford empirically demonstrate that Large Language Models acquire separable visual perceptual and reasoning abilities from text-only pre-training, identifying optimal data mixtures for cultivating these "visual priors" and showing improved multimodal performance. The work introduces the Multi-Level Existence Bench (MLE-Bench) for fine-grained perceptual evaluation.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs), despite text-only training, surprisingly develop latent visual capabilities or "visual priors."
- The specific textual data sources and pre-training mechanisms contributing to these emergent visual priors remain largely unexamined.
- There is a need for a systematic methodology to deliberately cultivate and enhance visual priors within LLMs for building more capable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

## Method

- The research employs a rigorous ==data-centric controlled ablation study== methodology, training over **100** LLMs across five scales (340M to 13B parameters) and **16** diverse text sources, consuming over **500,000** GPU-hours.
- LLMs are adapted into MLLMs using a standard two-stage process (visual alignment and supervised multimodal fine-tuning) and evaluated on a comprehensive suite of ==16 public VQA benchmarks==, categorized by task type.
- Pre-training data is classified into =="reasoning-centric" and "visual world"== categories to conduct grid searches on mixing ratios, identifying optimal blends for cultivating visual priors, and introducing the ==Multi-Level Existence Bench (MLE-Bench)== for fine-grained perception evaluation.

## Results

- An optimal "vision-favorable" data mixture, comprising approximately **60%** reasoning and **15%** visual content, maximizes downstream MLLM VQA performance.
- Perception priors, loosely correlated with general and OCR VQA, emerge from broad data and show scale-dependency, while reasoning priors, related to knowledge and vision-centric VQA, are robustly cultivated by reasoning-centric data and are universally transferable.
- A 7B LLM pre-trained on 1T tokens using the proposed "balanced mixture" achieved superior visual task performance and improved language proficiency compared to a "language-favorable" baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual priors in LLMs are not monolithic but decompose into separable perception and reasoning components, each with distinct origins and scaling trends.
> - Reasoning-centric language data (e.g., code, math) primarily cultivates visual reasoning abilities, while diverse language descriptions of the visual world contribute to basic perception, with different saturation points.
> - The findings strongly support the Platonic Representation Hypothesis, suggesting that LLMs learn an underlying, modality-agnostic statistical model of reality from text alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fascinating and increasingly critical area at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal AI. In recent years, a paradoxical phenomenon has gained traction: LLMs, despite being trained exclusively on vast textual datasets, demonstrably acquire rich implicit knowledge about the visual world, termed "visual priors." This emergent capability allows them to perform, or rapidly adapt to, vision tasks with surprising efficacy, even without direct visual input during their initial training.

The work directly aligns with and provides empirical support for the **Platonic Representation Hypothesis** (Huh et al., 2024b; Jha et al., 2025). This hypothesis posits that as AI models scale across diverse data, their latent representations converge towards a shared, underlying statistical model of reality, where different modalities (text, images) are merely "projections" of this unified internal world model. This paper investigates how LLMs recover this internal world model from text alone, specifically focusing on visual aspects.

Historically, the AI landscape has seen a rapid evolution from text-only LLMs (e.g., GPT, LLaMA) to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like Flamingo, BLIP-2, and LLaVA. Early MLLMs primarily focused on connecting pre-trained vision encoders with frozen LLMs using various adapter-style architectures, followed by multimodal instruction tuning. While these approaches have shown remarkable success, leading to MLLMs comparable to proprietary counterparts, they often treated the LLM as a static component, leveraging its linguistic knowledge. Recent research (Tong et al., 2024a; Laurençon et al., 2024) has highlighted that improvements in the underlying language model are often the most impactful means to enhance multimodal performance. This observation shifts the focus to understanding and optimizing the LLM itself to be "vision-aware" even before multimodal adaptation.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself within the broader context of **data-centric AI**, a paradigm shift where research moves beyond simply scaling data to strategically curating it to unlock specific capabilities. While previous work has explored the role of data mixtures for improving core language proficiency (e.g., code data enhancing reasoning), this paper extends that investigation into the multimodal domain. It fills a critical gap by systematically analyzing how text-only pre-training choices influence an LLM's visual priors and its potential for multimodal capabilities. By demystifying the textual origins of visual priors, this work contributes to a more fundamental understanding of how complex, modality-specific capabilities are encoded within language, thereby offering a clearer picture of the internal "world models" that foundation models learn from text alone.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the intriguing observation that Large Language Models (LLMs), despite their text-only training, surprisingly develop latent visual capabilities—rich "visual priors." This phenomenon is paradoxical and suggests that statistical patterns within language might be sufficient to encode fundamental principles of vision, such as object properties, spatial relationships, and scene understanding. The authors highlight several manifestations of these visual priors:
- **Programmatic Visual Knowledge:** LLMs can generate code to render complex 2D and 3D scenes, effectively demonstrating a grasp of visual concepts without direct image exposure.
- **Data-Efficient Visual Adaptation:** LLMs can be efficiently adapted to vision tasks with minimal multimodal data, implying that a strong visual foundation is already present.
- **LLMs as Strong Vision Encoders:** Repurposed LLM layers can achieve competitive performance on pure vision tasks, suggesting modality-agnostic general-purpose computational motifs.

Motivated by these compelling observations, the paper sets forth a systematic investigation with the following key objectives:
1. **Demystify the Origins of Visual Priors:** To understand *how* and *where* these implicit visual knowledge and capabilities emerge within LLMs during language pre-training.
2. **Dissect the Structure of Visual Priors:** To determine if visual priors are a monolithic block of knowledge or composed of distinct, separable abilities, such as perception and reasoning.
3. **Explore How to Leverage Visual Priors:** To identify strategies for deliberately cultivating and enhancing these priors during language pre-training to build more capable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
4. **Propose a Data-Centric Recipe:** To develop and validate a practical data-mixing strategy for pre-training vision-aware LLMs at scale.

Essentially, the paper aims to move beyond merely observing the emergent visual capabilities to systematically uncovering their underlying mechanisms and providing a roadmap for their deliberate cultivation, thereby paving the way for the next generation of multimodal LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a rigorous, **data-centric and controlled ablation study methodology** to systematically investigate the nature and origins of visual priors. The core approach involves training numerous LLMs under varying conditions and then evaluating their downstream multimodal capabilities after adaptation to MLLMs. This exhaustive experimentation consumed **over 500,000 GPU-hours** across more than 100 controlled experiments, spanning the entire MLLM construction pipeline.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **LLM Pre-training Setup:**
 - **Model Architecture:** Decoder-only Transformer models adhering closely to the Llama-3 architecture.
 - **Model Scales:** Five distinct scales were investigated: 340M, 1B, 3B, 7B, and 13B parameters.
 - **Data Volumes:** LLMs were pre-trained on varying amounts of text, from 0B to 1T tokens (up to 100B tokens for initial studies, then 1T for large-scale validation).
 - **Default Settings:** A 3B parameter model trained on 30B tokens served as the default for many ablation studies to isolate specific variables.
 - **Data Sources:** An extensive pool of 16 diverse text sources, including academic, code, math, literature, web-crawl, etc., each containing at least 50B tokens, was utilized.

2. **MLLM Adaptation Setting:**
 - **Two-Stage Adaptation:** A standard approach involving visual alignment (connecting a frozen vision encoder to the LLM via an MLP projector) followed by supervised multimodal fine-tuning (SMFT) on a mixture of vision-language and language-only instruction data.
 - **Vision Encoder:** MetaCLIP-B/16 was the default, with DINOv2-G and MAE-H used to test universality.
 - **Data Reduction for Ablation:** Strategic data reductions in alignment (1M image-caption pairs) and SMFT (3.5M curated subset) datasets were used to amplify the effects of LLM visual priors rather than external multimodal data.

3. **Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **LLM Evaluation:** Assessed raw language modeling quality (perplexity on Wikitext, LAMBADA) and reasoning abilities (zero-shot accuracy on diverse commonsense and QA tasks like PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC).
 - **MLLM Evaluation:** A comprehensive suite of 16 public benchmarks categorized into:
 - **General VQA:** Visual perception and commonsense (GQA, MME, MMBench, SEED).
 - **Knowledge VQA:** Visual information with multi-step reasoning (ScienceQA, MMMU, AI2D, MathVista).
 - **OCR & Chart VQA:** Fine-grained perception, text/chart interpretation (TextVQA, ChartQA, OCRBench).
 - **Vision-Centric VQA:** Abstract visual reasoning, spatial/3D understanding (RealWorldQA, Blink, COCO, ADE, Omni3D).
 - **Robust Parsing:** A hierarchical parsing strategy was developed for VQA evaluations to extract answers from free-form text, ensuring fair assessment across models with varying response styles.
 - **LLM-Vision Alignment:** Mutual Nearest-Neighbor (mNN) metric was used to quantify representational convergence between LLM and pre-trained vision model feature spaces using image-caption pairs from the WIT dataset.

4. **Targeted Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
 - **Impact of Scale:** Varying model and data sizes with web-crawl data.
 - **Impact of Data Sources:** Training models on single, specific data sources.
 - **Impact of Reasoning and Visual Data Proportions:** Classifying pre-training data into fine-grained "reasoning-centric" (code, math, science) and "visual world" (visual concept, attribute, relationship) categories using an LLM, then conducting grid searches on mixing ratios.
 - **Deriving Optimal Mixtures:** Identifying language-favorable, vision-favorable, and balanced data mixtures.
 - **Structure and Origin of Priors:** Analyzing correlation matrices of VQA performance categories and the impact of different vision encoders and multimodal instruction tuning data removal.
 - **Hypothesis Testing:** Introduction of the **Multi-Level Existence Bench (MLE-Bench)** to probe perception prior structure (scale-dependency) and qualitative analysis of visual reasoning traces to verify reasoning universality.

This systematic and extensive methodology allowed the authors to isolate variables, establish clear scaling trends, and uncover the distinct components and origins of visual priors in LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several critical findings that demystify the nature and origins of visual priors in LLMs:

1. **Scaling Laws for Visual Abilities (Finding 1):** Both model size and pre-training data volume positively impact downstream multimodal performance. However, this scaling is not uniform. General and Knowledge VQA tasks scale similarly, while OCR & Chart VQA is more sensitive to model size than data volume. Vision-Centric VQA shows disproportionate benefits for larger models with more data, indicating distinct scaling behaviors for different visual capabilities.

2. **Impact of Pre-training Data Sources (Finding 2):** Specific categories of language pre-training data significantly enhance certain visual capabilities. Reasoning-centric data (e.g., code, mathematics, academia) and corpora rich in visual world descriptions (e.g., arts, food) are potent drivers, particularly for Vision-Centric VQA tasks. This highlights that not all text data contributes equally to visual priors.

3. **Distinct Contributions of Reasoning and Visual Data (Finding 3):**
 - **Visual World Data:** A small amount of text describing the visual world is crucial for establishing a baseline perception, but its contribution saturates quickly, yielding diminishing returns with further increases.
 - **Reasoning-centric Data:** Increasing the proportion of reasoning-centric data progressively and significantly enhances visual abilities, with performance gains observed up to a 75% ratio before plateauing. This indicates a strong reasoning foundation is critical for robust visual capabilities.

4. **Optimal Data Mixture for Vision-Aware LLMs (Finding 4):** Maximizing MLLM VQA performance is best achieved by pre-training on a data mixture heavily skewed towards reasoning-centric content, complemented by necessary visual world knowledge. The optimal "vision-favorable" blend contained approximately 60% reasoning and 15% visual content. Through interpolation between a "language-favorable" and "vision-favorable" mixture, a "balanced mixture" (mix6) was identified, demonstrating that a calibrated data mixture can cultivate powerful visual priors without significantly compromising core language abilities.

5. **Structure and Origin of Visual Priors (Finding 5):** The learned visual prior is not a single entity but decomposes into at least two separable components:
 - **Perception Prior:** Loosely correlated with General and OCR VQA, it emerges diffusely from broad, diverse data (e.g., web-crawl data yielding best performance on these tasks). It appears to be a general byproduct of large-scale language modeling, sensitive to varied linguistic descriptions. The MLE-Bench further revealed this perception prior to be scale-dependent, with web-crawl pre-training being most effective for small-to-medium object perception.
 - **Reasoning Prior:** Loosely correlated with Knowledge and Vision-Centric VQA, it is predominantly cultivated by and scales progressively with reasoning-centric data. This prior is shown to be highly transferable and universally applicable to visual reasoning, benefiting multimodal systems regardless of the specific vision encoder used.

6. **Deconstructing Multimodal Abilities (Finding 6):**
 - **Visual Reasoning:** Primarily shaped by the reasoning prior acquired from language pre-training. It is a foundational, modality-agnostic prior that benefits reasoning-centered VQA across different vision encoders.
 - **Perception Ability:** More dependent on the specific characteristics of the vision encoder and subsequent visual instruction tuning. Removing perception-targeted instruction tuning data leads to significant drops in perception-heavy benchmarks, while removing reasoning-targeted data has only modest impacts on perception tasks.

7. **Scaling Up Validation:** Pre-training a 7B LLM on 1T tokens with the proposed "balanced" data mixture resulted in a model that outperformed a "language-favorable" baseline on all visual tasks, while also achieving better language proficiency (lower perplexity, higher accuracy). This validates the findings at a larger scale.

8. **Novel Resources and Techniques:** The paper introduces the **Multi-Level Existence Bench (MLE-Bench)** for fine-grained perceptual evaluation (especially for small objects) and the concept of **"Blind visual instruction tuning"** as a probe for models leveraging language priors to "hack" visual tasks, although acknowledging its potential to induce hallucinations if not used cautiously.

In summary, the paper provides a clear empirical framework for understanding the internal structure and textual origins of visual priors in LLMs, demonstrating that different types of language data contribute distinctly to separable perceptual and reasoning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work carries profound significance for the future of AI, particularly in the development of multimodal foundation models. Its potential impacts are multi-faceted:

1. **Data-Centric Roadmap for MLLMs:** The most immediate impact is providing a **data-centric roadmap** for pre-training vision-aware LLMs. By explicitly identifying the types of textual data that cultivate specific visual priors (reasoning-centric for reasoning, diverse general text for perception), the research shifts the paradigm from "serendipitous emergence" of visual capabilities to their "deliberate cultivation." This allows researchers and developers to strategically curate pre-training corpora to imbue LLMs with stronger visual foundations *before* any multimodal training, potentially leading to more efficient and capable MLLM development.

2. **Stronger Empirical Support for Platonic Representation Hypothesis:** The findings strongly support the **Platonic Representation Hypothesis**, illustrating that models can learn a unified internal world model—including visual aspects—from text alone. This deeper understanding of cross-modal representation learning suggests that powerful LLMs are not just statistical pattern matchers but are learning abstract structures of reality that transcend modality-specific superficial features. This insight could guide future efforts in building truly general-purpose AI.

3. **Improved MLLM Performance and Efficiency:** By optimizing the LLM pre-training phase for multimodal capabilities, the work promises to yield **more powerful and data-efficient MLLMs**. A well-tuned LLM with robust visual priors requires less multimodal instruction tuning data and potentially less reliance on complex vision encoders to achieve high performance on visual tasks. This efficiency can reduce computational costs and accelerate the development cycle for new multimodal systems.

4. **Guidance for Future LLM Architecture and Training:** The deconstruction of visual priors into separable perception and reasoning components, each with unique scaling trends and origins, offers critical guidance for designing future LLM architectures and training regimes. It suggests that specialized modules or training objectives could be developed to specifically enhance either perceptual or reasoning priors, depending on the target application.

5. **Novel Research Tools and Avenues:** The introduction of the **Multi-Level Existence Bench (MLE-Bench)** provides a valuable new tool for the MLLM community to conduct fine-grained evaluations of perceptual abilities, especially for small objects. The "blind visual instruction tuning" trick, while highlighting challenges like hallucination, also serves as a diagnostic tool to understand how models leverage language priors as "shortcuts" in visual tasks, opening new avenues for research into robustness and grounding.

6. **Ethical Considerations (Implicit):** While the paper focuses on capability, it implicitly raises important ethical considerations. Understanding the textual origins of visual priors also means understanding how biases embedded in language corpora could manifest as biased visual associations in downstream MLLMs. This knowledge can be leveraged in future work to mitigate such risks by carefully curating pre-training data.

In conclusion, "Learning to See Before Seeing" marks a significant step forward in understanding the internal workings of large foundation models. By providing a principled and empirically validated approach to cultivate visual intelligence from text, it lays essential groundwork for building a new generation of more robust, efficient, and truly multimodal AI systems.
