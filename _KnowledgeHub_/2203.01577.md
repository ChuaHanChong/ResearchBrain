# HOI4D: A 4D Egocentric Dataset for Category-Level Human-Object Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua and Peking Universities, with additional affiliation to the Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, introduce HOI4D, the first large-scale 4D egocentric dataset for category-level human-object interaction. The dataset features 2.4 million RGB-D frames, 800 object instances across 16 categories (including articulated objects), and comprehensive annotations like 3D hand and category-level object/part poses, revealing significant challenges for existing state-of-the-art methods.

## Problem

- Existing human-object interaction (HOI) datasets primarily focus on instance-level interactions with rigid objects, limiting model generalizability to novel objects within a category.
- A lack of large-scale 4D egocentric datasets with rich, granular annotations (e.g., precise 3D hand/object/part poses, fine-grained actions) for dynamic scenes from a first-person perspective.
- Current computer vision systems struggle with challenges posed by real-world egocentric HOI, such as severe self-occlusion, rapid ego-motion, diverse object appearances, and complex functional interactions with articulated objects.

## Method

- The HOI4D dataset was developed using a head-mounted dual RGB-D sensor setup to capture diverse human interactions with 800 unique object instances from 16 categories (7 rigid, 9 articulated) across 610 natural indoor rooms.
- A sophisticated semi-automatic annotation pipeline was implemented, combining manual labeling with automated propagation and optimization for 4D panoptic segmentation, 3D MANO hand pose, and category-level 9D object/part pose.
- The dataset provides reconstructed 3D meshes for all objects and frame-wise fine-grained action labels, enabling both vision benchmarks and robotics applications like imitation learning.

## Results

- State-of-the-art object pose tracking methods, such as BundleTrack, experienced a performance drop from 86.5% accuracy on synthetic data to 19.3% on HOI4D for the 'bottle' category.
- Leading 4D semantic segmentation methods achieved only 44.6% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for 'object' categories in HOI4D's indoor scenes, significantly lower than typical outdoor benchmarks.
- Fine-grained action segmentation methods like ASFormer, which achieve 85.6% accuracy on coarse-grained datasets, only managed 46.8% accuracy on HOI4D's detailed action labels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Category-level HOI perception in real-world egocentric 4D data presents significantly greater challenges for existing state-of-the-art methods compared to synthetic or instance-level datasets, resulting in substantial performance drops.
> - Models trained on HOI4D demonstrate improved generalization capabilities when transferred to other HOI datasets, indicating that its diverse and complex nature fosters more robust feature learning.
> - HOI4D's comprehensive annotations and reconstructed assets prove valuable for robot imitation learning, significantly boosting success rates for dexterous manipulation tasks compared to pure reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The HOI4D dataset is positioned within the rapidly evolving fields of **Human-Object Interaction (HOI) understanding**, **egocentric vision**, and **4D (3D + time) dynamic scene analysis**. These areas are crucial for developing advanced AI systems capable of interacting intelligently and naturally with the physical world, such as human-assistant robots, augmented reality (AR) applications, and intelligent personal assistants.

Historically, computer vision research on object and activity recognition has made tremendous progress, often facilitated by large-scale 2D image and video datasets. However, these datasets primarily provide semantic labels without the detailed 3D geometric and temporal information necessary for truly understanding interaction. Early HOI datasets often captured interactions from a third-person perspective, which can simplify some perception tasks but fails to capture the nuances of how a human experiences and initiates interaction.

The shift towards **egocentric vision**—capturing data from a first-person perspective—is vital because it directly reflects a user's sensory input during interaction. However, egocentric data presents its own set of challenges, particularly severe self-occlusion (e.g., hands occluding objects or other parts of the hand) and rapid ego-motion.

A critical limitation of many existing 3D HOI datasets (e.g., H2O, HO-3D, DexYCB) has been their focus on **instance-level interactions**. This means interactions are limited to a small, predefined set of object instances for which exact CAD models or specific properties are known beforehand. This severely restricts the generalization capabilities of models trained on such data, as real-world applications require systems to interact with a vast and diverse range of novel objects, even if they belong to a known category. Furthermore, many prior datasets neglected **articulated objects**, focusing solely on rigid ones, despite articulated objects (like drawers, laptops, scissors) being prevalent in daily life and offering richer interaction patterns. Datasets relying on markers or motion capture setups also compromise the naturalness of human motion and object appearance, creating a domain gap with real-world scenarios. While synthetic datasets (e.g., ObMan) offer control, they often struggle with sufficient realism for sim-to-real transfer, particularly for natural human motion and functional grasping.

HOI4D addresses these significant gaps by offering the **first large-scale 4D egocentric dataset specifically designed for category-level human-object interaction**. This distinction is key: it enables research into perceiving interactions with *potentially unseen objects* from a given category, a crucial step towards robust, general-purpose AI. By providing rich 4D annotations (RGB-D, panoptic segmentation, motion segmentation, 3D hand pose, *category-level* object/part pose, hand action, reconstructed meshes, scene point clouds), HOI4D elevates the complexity and realism of the available data. It also explicitly includes both rigid and articulated objects, further broadening the scope of HOI research. By capturing real-world data in diverse indoor environments without markers, HOI4D aims to bridge the gap between academic benchmarks and practical applications, pushing the boundaries of what computer vision systems can understand about human interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the HOI4D project is to **catalyze research in category-level human-object interaction from 4D egocentric visual signals**. The authors are motivated by the recognition that despite tremendous progress in object and activity recognition, current computer vision systems fall short in meeting the demands of critical applications such as human-assistant robots and augmented reality. These applications require a more profound and detailed understanding of human interaction, encompassing:

1. **Semantic understanding of 4D dynamic scenes:** Moving beyond static 2D or 3D recognition to comprehend scenes that evolve over time with interactive elements.
2. **3D pose of human hands under occlusion:** Accurately tracking hand pose despite self-occlusion, common in egocentric views, and occlusion by objects.
3. **3D pose and functionality of novel objects:** The ability to understand and estimate the pose of objects that have not been seen before, but belong to a known category, and to infer their functional affordances.
4. **Action and intention of humans:** Precisely recognizing fine-grained human actions during interaction and inferring underlying human intentions.

To tackle these challenges, the authors identified a critical need for **large-scale and annotation-rich 4D egocentric HOI datasets** that move beyond the limitations of existing resources. Specifically, their motivations include:

- **Bridging the Instance-to-Category Gap:** Current datasets largely focus on instance-level HOI, which limits generalizability. HOI4D aims to propel HOI perception to handle diverse object variations at the category level.
- **Addressing Articulated Objects:** Previous works primarily focused on rigid objects. HOI4D extends to articulated objects, enabling the study of richer and more complex interaction patterns.
- **Enhancing Realism and Naturalness:** Many existing datasets use lab settings or synthetic data, which may not accurately reflect real-world scenarios or natural human motion. HOI4D is collected in diverse indoor environments with participants performing natural tasks without markers.
- **Providing Comprehensive Annotations:** The project aims to deliver unprecedented levels of detail in annotations, including frame-wise panoptic segmentation, motion segmentation, 3D hand pose, category-level rigid and articulated object pose, and fine-grained action segmentation.
- **Establishing Challenging Benchmarks:** By providing a rich and complex dataset, HOI4D seeks to establish new benchmarking tasks that push the boundaries of existing computer vision algorithms, revealing their limitations and fostering new research directions.
- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The inclusion of reconstructed object meshes and human interaction trajectories provides valuable resources for robot learning and imitation learning in simulation environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of HOI4D involves a sophisticated multi-modal data collection setup and a meticulously designed, semi-automatic annotation pipeline to handle the scale and richness of the desired labels.

#### 3.1. Hardware Setup and Data Collection

The data capturing system consists of a simple head-mounted suite. Participants wear a bicycle helmet equipped with two synchronized and pre-calibrated RGB-D sensors:
- **Kinect v2:** A Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensor, effective for long-range depth capture.
- **Intel RealSense D455:** A structured light-based camera, advantageous for short-range (approx. 1m) depth sensing, crucial for close-up hand-object interactions.
This dual-sensor approach ensures comprehensive 3D scene capture and offers a testbed for cross-sensor transfer learning.

**Data Collection Protocol:**
- **Participants:** Initially stated as 4, later updated to 9 participants.
- **Objects:** 800 unique object instances from 16 categories (7 rigid, 9 articulated), with 50 instances per category.
- **Scenes:** 610 diverse indoor rooms, reflecting natural, cluttered environments, rather than sterile lab settings. Participants operate markerless.
- **Tasks:** 54 functionality-oriented tasks are pre-defined across categories, going beyond simple pick-and-place to reveal object functionality (e.g., opening a drawer to place a mug). Tasks are categorized into "simple" (clean background, focused interaction) and "complex" (cluttered scenes, multiple objects) to support varied research.
- **Volume:** 2.4 million RGB-D egocentric video frames, across 4000 sequences, captured at 15 frames per second (fps) for 20 seconds each.

#### 3.2. Data Annotation Pipeline

The annotation process is a hybrid of human annotation and automated algorithms, crucial for scaling up such a complex dataset.

- **4D Panoptic Labeling:**
 1. **2D Motion Segmentation:** Annotators manually segment moving objects in 10% of frames. An off-the-shelf 2D mask propagation tool is used to extend these annotations to the remaining 90% of frames, with annotator refinement.
 2. **3D Static Scene Reconstruction:** Moving objects are masked out from single-frame point clouds. A SLAM algorithm (e.g.,) then reconstructs a 3D static point cloud of the indoor scene (background, static furniture).
 3. **3D Static Scene Panoptic Segmentation:** The reconstructed static scene is manually annotated for all object instances and background "stuff." These 3D labels are projected back to each video frame.
 4. **4D Dynamic Scene Panoptic Segmentation:** The 2D motion segmentation and 3D static scene segmentation masks are merged to provide comprehensive frame-wise panoptic labels for the entire dynamic scene.

- **3D Hand Pose Annotation:**
 1. **Manual 2D Annotation:** Annotators mark 2D positions of 11 keypoints (wrist, 5 fingertips, 5 second knuckles) on 20% of frames, estimating occluded points. These correspond to a 21-joint MANO parametric hand model.
 2. **Initialization:** A 3D MANO hand pose is estimated for each annotated frame by minimizing an objective function that considers joint angle limits, 2D keypoint projection errors, depth errors, point cloud alignment (Chamfer distance), and mask errors.
 3. **Propagation:** Coarse hand poses for intermediate frames are obtained via linear interpolation.
 4. **Refinement:** All frames undergo further optimization using the loss function from initialization, augmented with `contact loss` (to handle hand-object interaction) and `temporal consistency loss` (to ensure smooth motion). Optimization is performed in batches (6-11 frames), and failure cases are manually rectified.

- **Category-Level Object Pose Annotation (Rigid and Articulated):**
 1. **Object Measurement & Manual Annotation:** Physical measurements are taken for each object instance to define its coordinate system and tight amodal bounding box. Annotators then manually fit these 9D bounding boxes (rotation, translation, scale) to objects (or parts for articulated objects) in depth point clouds every ten frames.
 2. **Model Scanning & Reconstruction:** All 800 object instances are 3D-scanned using multi-view high-resolution RGB images (with stickers to enrich texture/hide specularities) and commercial software (e.g., RealityCapture, Agisoft Metashape) to reconstruct detailed object meshes. For articulated objects, part annotations (similar to PartNet) are also provided.
 3. **Label Propagation:** Manual object poses are converted to world coordinates and linearly interpolated for intermediate frames.
 4. **Pose Optimization:** Propagated poses are refined using multi-modal data. A differentiable renderer (SoftRas) computes `silhouette discrepancy` (between rendered and ground truth 2D masks) and `depth residual`. Auxiliary losses include `Chamfer distance` between reconstructed and observed point clouds, and `temporal consistency`. For articulated objects, joint angle physical limits are also enforced.

- **Action Annotation:**
 - Fine-grained, frame-wise action labels are directly annotated on the original videos, distinguishing HOI4D from datasets with coarser action categories.

#### 3.3. Benchmarking Tasks

HOI4D proposes three core benchmarking tasks to evaluate and promote category-level HOI research:
1. **Semantic segmentation of 4D dynamic point cloud sequences:** Understanding changing environments.
2. **Category-level object and part pose tracking:** Generalizing pose estimation to novel instances within a category, including articulated parts.
3. **Egocentric hand action segmentation:** Recognizing fine-grained human actions during interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HOI4D paper presents both comprehensive dataset statistics and rigorous benchmark evaluations, revealing significant challenges for existing state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Diversity

- **Scale:** 2.4M RGB-D frames, 4000 sequences, 800 object instances across 16 categories (7 rigid, 9 articulated), 610 unique indoor rooms.
- **Rich Annotations:** Frame-wise panoptic segmentation, motion segmentation, 3D hand pose, category-level object/part pose, hand action, reconstructed object meshes, and scene point clouds.
- **Object Diversity:** Categories include common daily items like bottles, mugs, laptops, chairs, safe, etc., chosen from ShapeNet and Sapien Assets to facilitate sim-to-real transfer.
- **Task Diversity:** 54 tasks defined, encompassing pick-and-place and functionality-based interactions (e.g., pouring water, opening drawers), with "simple" and "complex" task levels to vary scene clutter and difficulty.
- **Sensor Diversity:** Uses both Kinect v2 (ToF) and RealSense D455 (structured light) for data capture, offering opportunities for cross-sensor transfer learning.

#### 4.2. Cross-Dataset Evaluations

To assess its generalizability and challenge level, HOI4D was cross-evaluated against relevant datasets (H2O for hand pose, NOCS for object pose, GTEA for action segmentation) using state-of-the-art backbones (Mesh Graphormer, CAPTRA, ASFormer).
- **Generalizability:** Models trained on HOI4D demonstrated better generalization to H2O for 3D hand pose estimation than vice-versa. For instance, an H2O-trained model showed a 3.5x error increase when tested on HOI4D, whereas a HOI4D-trained model only had a 2.2x increase on H2O.
- **Challenge Level:** Training models on combined datasets sometimes improved performance on the simpler dataset but could degrade performance on HOI4D itself. This indicates HOI4D's inherent diversity and complexity, making it a tougher benchmark than existing datasets. It suggests that models learned on HOI4D acquire stronger generalizability due to exposure to more varied real-world data.

#### 4.3. Benchmark Task Results

- **Category-Level Object and Part Pose Tracking:**
 - Evaluated on rigid objects (toy car, mug, bottle, bowl) and an articulated object (laptop) using BundleTrack (SOTA) and an ICP baseline.
 - **Significant Performance Degradation:** BundleTrack's 5°5cm accuracy on the "bottle" category dropped drastically from 86.5% on the synthetic NOCS dataset to only 19.3% on HOI4D.
 - **Challenges:** This highlights the severe difficulties posed by real-world interactive scenarios in HOI4D, including heavy hand occlusion, sensor noise, complex backgrounds, and fast object motions, which existing methods developed on simpler or synthetic data cannot robustly handle.

- **4D Point Cloud Videos Semantic Segmentation:**
 - Evaluated two SOTA outdoor 4D semantic segmentation methods (PSTNet, P4Transformer) on 38 indoor categories (objects, background).
 - **Poor Indoor Performance:** The best method (P4Transformer) achieved only 61.2% mIoU overall, with particularly low performance on "object" categories (44.6% mIoU) compared to "background" (77.7% mIoU).
 - **Reasons:** Indoor scenes feature smaller object sizes, more flexible movements, and greater occlusion, posing unique challenges not well addressed by methods tuned for outdoor LiDAR data.

- **Fine-grained Video Action Segmentation:**
 - Evaluated three SOTA methods (MS-TCN, MS-TCN++, ASFormer) on HOI4D's fine-grained action labels.
 - **Substantial Performance Drop:** ASFormer, which achieves 85.6% accuracy on the coarse-grained 50Salads dataset, managed only 46.8% accuracy on HOI4D.
 - **Qualitative Analysis:** Failure analysis suggested that networks often learn the *sequence* of actions but struggle to perceive the *specific fine-grained action* itself, especially when these fine-grained actions break inherent temporal commonalities.

#### 4.4. Robot Dexterous Manipulation (Supplementary)

- HOI4D data was used to generate state-action demonstrations for a "Pick Up" task with a simulated Adroit dexterous robot hand.
- **Imitation Learning Advantage:** Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) achieved a 17.4% success rate using HOI4D demonstrations, significantly outperforming a pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach (Soft Actor-Critic, SAC) which managed only 3.5% success.
- **Implications:** This demonstrates HOI4D's utility beyond vision benchmarks, providing valuable demonstrations to boost robot learning, particularly for challenging category-level dexterous manipulation tasks where pure RL struggles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HOI4D dataset represents a significant advancement in the fields of computer vision, robotics, and human-computer interaction, with the potential for substantial impact across several research fronts:

- **Filling a Critical Data Gap:** HOI4D is the *first* large-scale 4D egocentric dataset providing rich annotations for *category-level* human-object interaction, encompassing both rigid and articulated objects. This directly addresses a long-standing need for data that enables models to generalize beyond specific instances to novel objects within a category, a prerequisite for real-world deployment.
- **Catalyzing Foundational Research:** By revealing the substantial performance gaps of existing state-of-the-art methods across various tasks (category-level pose tracking, 4D semantic segmentation, fine-grained action segmentation), HOI4D clearly delineates new and challenging research directions. It compels researchers to develop more robust algorithms that can handle real-world complexities like heavy occlusion, fast motion, sensor noise, and diverse object appearances.
- **Advancing Human-Centric AI:** The egocentric perspective and detailed annotations on hand pose, object pose, and fine-grained actions are crucial for building AI systems that truly understand human intentions and how humans interact with their environment. This is fundamental for creating intuitive human-robot collaboration systems and immersive augmented/virtual reality experiences.
- **Promoting Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robot Learning:** The inclusion of reconstructed object meshes and the ability to extract human interaction trajectories for simulation environments makes HOI4D invaluable for robot learning. As demonstrated in the supplementary material, the dataset provides high-quality demonstrations that can significantly accelerate imitation learning for complex dexterous manipulation tasks, helping to bridge the notorious sim-to-real gap.
- **Fostering Cross-Disciplinary Innovation:** The dataset's comprehensive nature and the diverse challenges it presents will likely draw attention from multiple research communities, including computer vision, robotics, machine learning, and human-computer interaction, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration and innovative solutions.
- **Enabling New Applications:** Directly empowers the development of more capable and adaptable human-assistant robots, intelligent household robots, smart manufacturing systems, and realistic AR/VR environments where AI agents can fluidly understand and participate in human activities involving physical objects.
- **Facilitating Cross-Sensor Research:** The use of two different RGB-D sensors (Kinect v2 and RealSense D455) offers a unique opportunity to study cross-sensor transfer learning and develop methods robust to varying sensor characteristics and noise patterns.

In summary, HOI4D is more than just a dataset; it is a foundational research infrastructure that sets new benchmarks and provides the raw material for a future where AI systems can perceive, understand, and engage in the intricate world of human-object interaction with unprecedented sophistication.
