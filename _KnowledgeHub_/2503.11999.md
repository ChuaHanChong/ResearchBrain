# Diffusion Dynamics Models with Generative State Estimation for Cloth Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The UniClothDiff framework, developed at the University of California San Diego, integrates diffusion models for robust state estimation and dynamics prediction, achieving accurate 3D cloth state reconstruction from occluded observations and reducing dynamics prediction error by an order of magnitude. This enables higher success rates in complex robotic cloth manipulation tasks, such as folding and unfolding various garments.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable objects like cloth is challenging due to their high-dimensional state spaces, complex non-linear dynamics, and frequent self-occlusions.
- Existing state estimation methods struggle to infer full 3D configurations from partial observations, while traditional dynamics models face scalability issues, struggle with long-range dependencies, or are sensitive to noisy inputs.
- Model-free approaches often lack precise shape control, and model-based methods are hindered by difficulties in accurate state estimation and robust dynamics prediction.

## Method

- Researchers developed UniClothDiff, a unified framework comprising a ==Diffusion Perception Model (DPM)== for state estimation and a Diffusion Dynamics Model (DDM) for dynamics prediction, integrated with Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- Both DPM and DDM leverage Transformer-based diffusion models to formulate state estimation and dynamics prediction as conditional generation tasks, reconstructing full 3D cloth states and predicting future states respectively.
- The DDM is incorporated into an MPC framework for real-world robotic planning, utilizing informed action sampling and probabilistic grasp point selection to optimize manipulation sequences.

## Results

- The Diffusion Perception Model (DPM) significantly outperformed baselines in state estimation, reducing MSE for general cloth by over **50%** (2.32e-1) compared to TRTM (5.07e-1) and for T-shirts (2.76e-1) compared to GarmentNets (18.6e-1).
- The Diffusion Dynamics Model (DDM) demonstrated an order of magnitude reduction in long-horizon dynamics prediction error with ground truth states (e.g., 0.05e-3 MSE vs. GNN's 0.75e-3) and maintained robust performance with noisy DPM inputs.
- UniClothDiff achieved substantially higher success rates in real-world cloth manipulation tasks, showing an improvement of 30-50 percentage points for challenging objects like T-shirts under various occlusion conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative diffusion models, combined with Transformer architectures, can effectively learn and represent complex, high-dimensional cloth configurations and their intricate deformation patterns from data.
> - Framing state estimation as a conditional denoising diffusion process allows for robust inference of complete 3D object states even from severely occluded and partial observations.
> - Diffusion models are capable of accurately predicting long-horizon dynamics for deformable objects, demonstrating resilience to noisy perceptual inputs and outperforming traditional dynamics modeling approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects, such as cloth, represents a persistent challenge in robotics. This is primarily due to their high-dimensional state spaces, complex non-linear dynamics, and the frequent occurrence of self-occlusions during manipulation, which complicate both accurate state estimation and robust dynamics modeling. The current research landscape in deformable object manipulation can be broadly categorized into model-free and model-based approaches.

Model-free methods, including reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL), learn direct observation-to-action mappings. While these methods can achieve various manipulation tasks, they often encounter difficulties with precise shape control due to their lack of explicit dynamics reasoning.

Model-based approaches, in contrast, rely on accurate state estimation and predictive dynamics models. However, obtaining precise state estimates from partial observations is challenging due to occlusions. Traditional dynamics models, often utilizing structured representations like particles or meshes coupled with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), face scalability issues with a large number of graph nodes and may struggle to capture long-range dependencies crucial for complex cloth deformations. Pixel-based dynamics models, which treat the problem as action-conditioned video prediction, are often sample-inefficient and vulnerable to occlusions. Analytical simulators, while providing physical realism, are highly sensitive to noisy perceptual inputs.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing generative models, specifically diffusion models, to overcome the challenges in both state estimation and dynamics modeling. Diffusion models have demonstrated capabilities in capturing complex data distributions across various domains, including computer vision, science, and more recently, robotics for policy learning and world modeling. By leveraging these models, the paper positions itself as an advancement in enabling robust model-based control for deformable object manipulation, particularly under conditions of partial observability and intricate dynamics, by providing a unified framework for perception and dynamics prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to develop methods for accurate state estimation and robust dynamics prediction for cloth, with the ultimate goal of enabling effective robotic cloth manipulation. The motivation stems from several inherent difficulties associated with deformable objects like textiles:

1. **Highly Complex Dynamics and Near-Infinite Degrees of Freedom:** Cloth exhibits non-rigid, continuous deformation patterns that are difficult to model deterministically. Its high number of degrees of freedom makes it challenging to capture its state and predict its future behavior accurately. Existing dynamics models, such as GNNs, often struggle with the scalability required for fine-grained interactions and the capture of long-range dependencies critical for predicting how deformations propagate across the material.
2. **Frequent Self-Occlusions:** The pliable nature of cloth means that it frequently folds over itself, leading to severe self-occlusions from typical camera viewpoints. This partial observability complicates the process of inferring the full 3D configuration of the cloth from sensory inputs. Traditional state estimation methods often fail to accurately reconstruct the hidden parts of the object.
3. **Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - **State Estimation:** Most prior methods struggle with the complex mapping from highly partial observations to high-dimensional object states, lacking the ability to "imagine" or infer unobserved shapes akin to human perception.
 - **Dynamics Modeling:** GNN-based models, while offering advantages in data-scarce domains, scale inefficiently with increasing graph nodes and their inherent locality can restrict their ability to model long-range interactions, which are crucial for large-scale cloth deformations. Pixel-based models are often sample-inefficient and lack physical grounding. Analytical simulators are prone to errors when provided with noisy input data.

To address these challenges, the authors hypothesize that expressive generative models, particularly diffusion models combined with scalable architectures like Transformers, can effectively capture the intricate cloth configurations and deformation patterns from data. This hypothesis motivates the formulation of both state estimation and dynamics prediction as conditional generation processes, leveraging the capabilities of diffusion models to reconstruct full states from partial observations and predict future states given current states and robot actions. The overarching motivation is to create a unified, robust framework that can overcome the perceptual and modeling limitations, thereby enabling more precise and reliable robotic manipulation of deformable objects in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces UniClothDiff, a unified framework for cloth manipulation that integrates a Diffusion Perception Model (DPM), a Diffusion Dynamics Model (DDM), and Model Predictive Control (MPC). The core idea is to frame both state estimation and dynamics prediction as conditional generation tasks using Transformer-based diffusion models.

#### 3.1. State Representation

The state of the cloth ($s_t$) is represented as a mesh, defined by its vertices ($V_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N_v \times 3}$) and invariant edge connectivity ($E_t$). Observations ($o_t$) are multiview RGB-D data, which are converted into partial point clouds.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Perception Model (DPM)

DPM addresses the challenge of reconstructing the full 3D cloth state from partial, occluded RGB-D observations. It formulates this as a conditional denoising diffusion process.

- **Conditional Diffusion Process:** The model learns the conditional distribution $p(s|s_c, e_{pc})$, where $s$ is the full cloth state, $s_c$ is the canonical state of the template mesh, and $e_{pc}$ is an embedding of the observed partial point cloud. A standard Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) is used, which involves a forward process of gradually adding Gaussian noise to an initial state and a reverse process where a denoising network ($\epsilon_p_\theta$) iteratively removes noise to reconstruct the original state.
- **Point Cloud Embedding:** The partial point cloud is processed by first sampling $M$ center points using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) and then clustering points using K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) to form patches. Each patch is then fed through a PointNet to obtain its embedding representation ($e_{pc}$).
- **Model Architecture:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture serves as the backbone.
 - **Tokenization:** Input mesh vertices are tokenized into non-overlapping patches. FPS is used to sample $N$ patch centers, and a Voronoi diagram divides the vertices into these patches. Each Voronoi cell's points are processed by a PointNet layer for feature extraction.
 - **Conditioning:** Conditional information from the point cloud embedding is incorporated via two mechanisms: (1) Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN), which modulates normalization parameters based on the point cloud embedding, and (2) a cross-attention layer, where the hidden states serve as queries and the conditional information acts as keys and values.
 - **Decoding:** The transformed hidden states are upsampled using distance-weighted interpolation to match the full vertex count and then mapped to 3D vertex coordinates via a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict the noise added during the forward diffusion process.
- **Training:** The denoising model $\epsilon_p_\theta$ is trained to minimize the L2 loss between the predicted noise and the actual noise added during the diffusion forward process.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Dynamics Model (DDM)

DDM predicts future cloth states given the current estimated state and robot actions. It extends the DPM architecture to incorporate temporal information and action conditioning.

- **Conditional Diffusion Process:** DDM learns the conditional posterior distribution $p(s_{t+1:t+j+1}|a_t, s_{t-i:t})$, predicting $j$ future states conditioned on the current action ($a_t$) and $i$ historical states ($s_{t-i:t}$).
- **Action Embedding:** Robot actions, represented as delta end-effector positions, are encoded using a Fourier feature-based embedding, followed by an MLP. This high-dimensional encoding is then injected into the model as a condition through cross-attention layers.
- **Temporal Modeling:** The architecture includes additional temporal attention layers to enhance its ability to model dependencies across time steps.

#### 3.4. Model-Based Planning

The DDM is integrated with Model Predictive Control (MPC) for robotic cloth manipulation.

- **Optimization:** Given a sequence of current cloth states and a target state, MPC optimizes an action sequence over a horizon by minimizing a cost function. The cost function combines a weighted mean squared error (MSE) and Chamfer Distance (CD) for state matching, and an action smoothness term.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) is used as a sampling-based optimizer.
- **Action Representation:** Actions are defined as relative end-effector displacements applied to a selected cloth grasp point.
- **Planning Efficiency:**
 - **Informed Action Sampling:** The initial sampling distribution for actions is guided by a prior direction computed from the K vertices exhibiting the largest displacement between the current and target states.
 - **Probabilistic Grasp Point Selection:** Grasp points are selected using a temperature-controlled softmax distribution based on the displacement of each vertex between the current and target states, allowing for exploration while prioritizing regions with high error.
- **Control Loop:** After executing each action, the robot updates its state estimate using the DPM before replanning the next action sequence.

#### 3.5. Data Collection and Experimental Setup

Training data for both DPM and DDM is collected in a SAPIEN simulation environment.
- **Simulation Environment:** SAPIEN, leveraging a PhysX-based simulator for rigid bodies and a projective dynamics (PD) solver for cloth, is used. The systems are coupled by alternating updates.
- **State Estimation Data:** Multi-view RGB-D cameras with randomized angles generate fused point clouds paired with ground-truth mesh states. Simulated real-world conditions include augmented camera extrinsic parameters (rotational/translational variations), depth sensor noise, and random point dropout.
- **Dynamics Data:** A dataset of 500K transitions is collected using diverse action sampling strategies, including directionally-randomized displacements for folding/relocating and pair-wise vertex manipulation, with actions ranging from 15 to 35 steps to assess long-horizon performance.
- **Real-World Validation:** The system is deployed on two robotic platforms: a single UFactory xArm-6 with a soft gripper and a bimanual system with two UFactory xArm-7 robots equipped with Ability hands, both using Intel RealSense RGB-D cameras.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to evaluate the DPM's effectiveness in handling self-occlusions, the DDM's improvement in dynamics prediction, and the overall system performance in real-world cloth manipulation.

#### 4.1. State Estimation Results (DPM)

- **Quantitative Performance:** DPM demonstrated superior performance compared to baseline methods (GarmentNets, MEDOR, TRTM, and a Transformer ablation) in terms of MSE, Chamfer Distance (CD), and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD). For general cloth, DPM achieved 2.32 (10$^{-1}$) MSE in simulation, outperforming TRTM's 5.07. For T-shirts, DPM achieved 2.76 (10$^{-1}$) MSE, significantly better than GarmentNets (18.6) and MEDOR (21.0). Similar improvements were observed in real-world metrics (CD and EMD).
- **Occlusion Handling:** The results indicate that the diffusion-based generative approach effectively infers full cloth configurations from partial and highly crumpled observations, outperforming template-based and direct transformer approaches, highlighting the benefits of diffusion models for reconstructing hidden states.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** TRTM and the Transformer baseline generally outperformed GarmentNets and MEDOR, indicating the value of topological information from template meshes. DPM further improved upon these, underscoring the contribution of diffusion models.

#### 4.2. Dynamics Prediction Results (DDM)

- **Oracle Perception as Input (Ground Truth States):** DDM consistently outperformed all baselines (GNN, Transformer, Analytical Simulator) in long-horizon dynamics prediction. It reduced prediction error by approximately an order of magnitude (e.g., MSE of 0.05 vs. GNN's 0.75 for cloth and 0.35 vs. GNN's 6.36 for T-shirts, all $\times 10^{-3}$). DDM exhibited minimal temporal error accumulation.
- **DPM Perception as Input (Noisy States):** When using states estimated by DPM (which introduce noise), DDM maintained its robust performance, achieving approximately 2x lower error than other baselines. The Analytical Simulator, while initially low error for simpler objects, showed rapid degradation and higher error accumulation due to its sensitivity to inconsistent inputs, especially for complex T-shirts. GNN and the Transformer baseline also showed increased errors with noisy inputs.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Qualitative visualizations showed DDM's predictions to be physically plausible, maintaining cloth integrity and deformation patterns with significantly lower point-wise L2 error compared to baselines over extended time horizons.

#### 4.3. Real-World Planning Results

- **Manipulation Success Rates:** UniClothDiff (DPM + DDM) consistently achieved higher success rates across various cloth folding and unfolding tasks, including square cloth, T-shirts, and long-sleeve shirts, and under different occlusion types (self, external, combined). For instance, in combined occlusion scenarios for cloth, UniClothDiff achieved 6/10 success rate compared to GNN's 3/10. For T-shirts, it reached 6/10 compared to GNN's 2/10. The improvement in success rate was up to 30-50 percentage points for challenging objects like T-shirts.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The framework demonstrated successful transfer of manipulation capabilities from a parallel gripper to a dexterous hand, attributed to the embodiment-agnostic action representation.
- **Ablation Study:** The ablation study confirmed the synergistic effect of DPM and DDM. The combination of DDM + DPM yielded the highest success rates (e.g., 9/10 for cloth, 8/10 for T-shirt) compared to using either module with a less capable counterpart (e.g., GNN + DPM: 6/10 for cloth, 1/10 for T-shirt). The study indicated that for simpler topologies (cloth), accurate perception is paramount, while for more complex topologies (T-shirts), accurate dynamics modeling becomes equally crucial.
- **Qualitative Real-World Performance:** The system successfully manipulated diverse fabric items from arbitrary initial configurations to challenging target folding states, even with severe self-occlusions.
- **Intra-class Generalization:** The model demonstrated the ability to generalize across garments with varying physical attributes (size, material, and color), successfully executing precise folding trajectories across different sized square cloths.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents UniClothDiff, a unified framework that addresses two foundational challenges in robotic manipulation of deformable objects: state estimation under partial observability and robust dynamics prediction. By leveraging Transformer-based diffusion models, the work demonstrates several significant contributions:

1. **Advancement in Deformable Object Perception:** The Diffusion Perception Model (DPM) provides a method for reconstructing full 3D cloth configurations from highly occluded, partial observations. This capability is critical for enabling robots to operate effectively in cluttered or complex environments where deformable objects frequently obscure their own form.
2. **Enhanced Dynamics Modeling:** The Diffusion Dynamics Model (DDM) offers a method for predicting long-horizon cloth dynamics with notably reduced error compared to existing approaches, including GNN-based methods and direct transformer models. Its robustness to noisy perceptual inputs, derived from the DPM, is a key advancement for real-world deployment.
3. **Improved Robotic Manipulation Performance:** The integration of DPM and DDM into a Model Predictive Control framework leads to substantially higher success rates in complex cloth manipulation tasks, such as folding and unfolding various garments under different occlusion conditions. This directly translates to more reliable and precise robotic execution for tasks involving textiles.
4. **Generative Models for Robotics:** The work demonstrates the applicability and efficacy of generative diffusion models, coupled with Transformer architectures, as a powerful paradigm for learning complex data distributions in robotics. This extends the utility of these models beyond typical image/video generation to physical interaction tasks.
5. **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The embodiment-agnostic action representation design allows for the transfer of learned manipulation skills across different robot end-effectors (e.g., parallel gripper to dexterous hand), contributing to the development of more versatile robotic systems.

The potential impact of this research is broad, including:
- **Household Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform laundry, organize clothing, and assist in daily tasks involving fabrics.
- **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Automating tasks in industries that handle textiles, such as garment production or packaging.
- **Healthcare:** Assisting with patient dressing or handling medical drapes.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Providing a robust framework and demonstrating the utility of generative models, which could be extended to other deformable materials or contact-rich rigid body interactions, paving the way for further advancements in general-purpose deformable object manipulation.

While computational costs and the current focus on cloth manipulation are noted limitations, the demonstrated improvements in perception, dynamics, and control provide a foundation for future work addressing these aspects and further solidifying the role of generative models in complex robotic applications.
