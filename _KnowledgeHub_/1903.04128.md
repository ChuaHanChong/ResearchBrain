# Manipulation by Feel: Touch-Based Control with Deep Predictive Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Facebook AI developed Deep Tactile Model Predictive Control, a framework that uses high-resolution tactile sensors and deep predictive models to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks based solely on touch feedback. This method achieved an 86.6% success rate in die rolling and demonstrated superior performance over a hand-engineered baseline in ball repositioning and analog stick deflection tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robotic systems underutilize tactile sensing for continuous, non-prehensile manipulation, struggling with fine motor control due to sparse sensor data.
- Developing accurate physics models for complex contact dynamics involving friction and deformation is difficult and computationally intensive.
- Visual sensing is often insufficient for fine manipulation tasks due to occlusions at contact points by the robot's end-effector.

## Method

- A **Deep Tactile MPC** framework combines a high-resolution ==GelSight==-style tactile sensor with a ==deep recurrent convolutional network==.
- The deep network is autonomously trained on diverse exploratory interactions to predict ==future tactile observations==, implicitly learning complex contact dynamics.
- An ==MPC planner== uses the trained predictive model and ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)== to optimize action sequences, minimizing the pixel-wise ==Mean Squared Error (MSE)== to a user-specified goal tactile image.

## Results

- The deep predictive model accurately forecasted future GelSight sensor observations over multiple time steps, demonstrating effective learning of complex tactile dynamics.
- The Deep Tactile MPC framework successfully executed three distinct manipulation tasks (ball repositioning, analog stick deflection, 20-sided die rolling) relying solely on tactile feedback.
- Quantitatively, the method achieved a median L2 distance of **2.10 mm** for ball repositioning and **5.31 mm** for analog stick deflection, and an **86.6%** success rate in die rolling, consistently outperforming a hand-engineered centroid-based baseline across all tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-resolution tactile sensors, when combined with deep predictive models, allow robots to implicitly learn and leverage complex contact dynamics for fine manipulation.
> - Goal specification through desired tactile images simplifies interaction and offers a general, intuitive method for defining manipulation objectives without requiring explicit pose or force estimation.
> - Unsupervised learning from diverse exploratory interactions enables the system to acquire robust tactile control policies, reducing reliance on manual supervision or explicit reward functions.
