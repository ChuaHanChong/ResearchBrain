# Harmon: Whole-Body Motion Generation of Humanoid Robots from Language Descriptions

> [!summary] Summary
> HARMON is a framework that generates diverse and natural whole-body motions for humanoid robots from free-form language instructions. It achieves expressive movements by combining human motion priors with iterative refinement using vision-language models, demonstrating high text-motion alignment and successful execution on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack large-scale datasets for language-driven motion generation, making it difficult to generate natural and expressive whole-body movements from text.
- A significant 'embodiment gap' exists between human and humanoid robot bodies, complicating the direct transfer of human motion data to robots.
- Existing human motion datasets often lack detailed finger and head movements, which are essential for expressive communication on humanoid robots.

## Method

- ==The HARMON== framework generates initial human motion from text using a diffusion-based model, followed by inverse kinematics (IK) for retargeting to the humanoid robot's kinematic structure.
- A VLM-based motion editing stage refines the retargeted motion by generating specific finger and head movements based on the language description.
- An iterative motion adjustment loop, powered by GPT-4V, assesses alignment between the current motion and the text, suggesting and applying refinements until the motion accurately reflects the command.

## Results

- HARMON achieved an **81.2%** normalized score for text-motion alignment in human evaluations, outperforming VLM-based generation (**38.3%**) and human motion retargeting (**68.5%**).
- The framework demonstrated improved specific body part expressiveness, scoring **80.7%** for finger and head motion and **79.4%** for arm motion, while also showing strong body coordination (**83.3%**).
- Generated motions were successfully deployed and executed on a real Fourier GR1 humanoid robot, showcasing diverse and expressive movements in response to various language instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining human motion priors with vision-language models (VLMs) effectively bridges the data scarcity gap for humanoid robot motions and enhances expressiveness beyond direct retargeting.
> - Iterative motion adjustment guided by VLMs enables precise refinement of retargeted motions to ensure high alignment with free-form language descriptions.
> - Specific VLM-based modules for generating finger and head movements overcome limitations of general human motion datasets, providing crucial details for expressive humanoid gestures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Humanoid robots are designed to operate in environments built for humans, making them ideal candidates for various applications, from assistance tasks to social interactions. For these robots to be truly effective, they must be able to understand and respond to natural language commands with appropriate, human-like movements. However, generating expressive whole-body motions from language descriptions remains a significant challenge in robotics.

This paper introduces HARMON, a novel framework that enables humanoid robots to perform diverse and natural motions in response to free-form language instructions. HARMON combines the strengths of human motion data with the reasoning capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs) to generate expressive movements that fully utilize a humanoid robot's degrees of freedom.

### 2. Background and Challenges

Creating language-driven motion for humanoid robots faces several key challenges:

1. **Data Scarcity**: Unlike human motion datasets which are abundant, there is a significant lack of large-scale datasets pairing language descriptions with humanoid robot motions.

2. **Embodiment Gap**: Human and humanoid bodies differ substantially in their structure, degrees of freedom, and physical capabilities, making direct transfer of motions problematic.

3. **Expressiveness Limitations**: Standard human motion datasets often lack detailed finger and head movements, which are crucial for expressive gestures on humanoid robots.

4. **Physical Constraints**: Humanoid robots have unique kinematic and dynamic constraints that must be respected for stable motion execution.

Previous approaches have attempted to address these challenges through direct motion planning, imitation learning, or text-to-motion generation, but none have successfully combined human motion priors with VLM-guided refinement to create expressive whole-body humanoid movements.

### 3. The HARMON Framework

HARMON addresses these challenges through a multi-stage pipeline:

1. **Initial Motion Generation**: Generate human motion from textual descriptions using a diffusion-based model trained on human motion data.

2. **Motion Retargeting**: Adapt the human motion to the humanoid robot's kinematic structure using inverse kinematics (IK).

3. **VLM-Based Motion Editing**: Refine the retargeted motion using VLMs to add expressiveness and ensure alignment with the language description.

4. **Motion Execution**: Execute the generated motion on a real humanoid robot with separate control strategies for lower and upper body movements.

This approach leverages existing human motion data while addressing the specific requirements and constraints of humanoid robots, resulting in more natural and expressive movements.

### 4. Human Motion Generation and Retargeting

The first stage of HARMON uses PhysDiff, a diffusion-based generative model trained on human motion data, to generate SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) parameters representing human motion from a textual description.

For example, given a description like "Clap the hands above the head," PhysDiff generates a sequence of human body poses that perform this action. However, these poses cannot be directly applied to a humanoid robot due to differences in body structure.

To bridge this gap, HARMON employs an IK-based retargeting approach:

1. First, it minimizes the difference between human and humanoid body shapes.
2. Then, it computes joint positions for the humanoid that match the corresponding positions on the human model.
3. Finally, it solves the IK problem to obtain joint angles for the humanoid robot.

This process creates a baseline motion for the humanoid that approximates the human movement but may still lack expressiveness and precise alignment with the language description.

### 5. VLM-Based Motion Editing

The core innovation of HARMON lies in its VLM-based motion editing stage, which addresses the limitations of direct retargeting. This stage consists of three main components:

#### 5.1. Finger Motion Generation

Since most human motion datasets lack finger movement data, HARMON uses GPT-4 to generate appropriate finger gestures:

1. GPT-4 extracts finger-related descriptions from the original text prompt.
2. The system renders videos of the retargeted humanoid motion without finger movements.
3. GPT-4 observes these videos and generates finger movements that coordinate with the arm motion.

For example, if the description mentions "with a thumb-up," GPT-4 will generate the appropriate finger configuration for a thumbs-up gesture.

#### 5.2. Head Motion Generation

Similarly, head movements are often missing or limited in human motion datasets. HARMON addresses this by:

1. Extracting head-related descriptions from the text.
2. Using GPT-4 to generate appropriate head movements directly from these descriptions.

For instance, for "nods the head," GPT-4 would generate a sequence of pitch rotations for the robot's head.

#### 5.3. Iterative Motion Adjustment

To ensure the generated motion aligns well with the language description, HARMON implements an iterative refinement process:

```
Algorithm: Iterative Motion Adjustment
Input: Motion description D, Humanoid motion M
Output: Refined humanoid motion M'

1. While not aligned and iterations < max_iterations:
   2. Render video of current motion M
   3. Use GPT-4V (judgment agent) to assess alignment with D
   4. If GPT-4V determines motion is aligned, return M
   5. Get adjustment suggestions from GPT-4V
   6. Use GPT-4 (adjustment agent) to predict necessary adjustments
   7. Apply adjustments to M using IK-based editing
   8. M ← adjusted motion

9. Return final motion M
```

This iterative process allows HARMON to refine motions until they accurately reflect the language description, addressing misalignments and enhancing expressiveness.

### 6. Experimental Results

HARMON was evaluated through both quantitative human studies and qualitative analyses:

#### 6.1. Text-Motion Alignment

Human evaluators rated different methods on how well the generated motions aligned with text descriptions. HARMON achieved a normalized score of 81.2%, significantly outperforming:

- VLM-based motion generation (38.3%)
- Human motion retargeting (68.5%)
- HARMON without finger or head refinement (64.4%)

The evaluation also examined specific body parts:

- **Finger & Head Motion**: HARMON scored 80.7%, compared to just 51.9% for VLM-based generation and 72.4% for human motion retargeting.
- **Arm Motion**: HARMON achieved 79.4%, while VLM-based generation scored only 14.3%.
- **Body Coordination**: HARMON scored 83.3%, showing its ability to generate well-coordinated whole-body movements.

#### 6.2. Qualitative Analysis

The qualitative results demonstrated how HARMON's multi-stage approach improves motion quality:

For example, for the instruction "Clap the hands above the head," the refinement process corrected the hand positions to enable proper clapping. Similarly, for "Raise the right hand with a thumb-up," the system added the precise thumb-up gesture that was missing in the retargeted motion.

### 7. Real-World Applications

HARMON's generated motions were successfully deployed on a real Fourier GR1 humanoid robot. The implementation strategy involved:

1. **Motion Segmentation**: Separating lower-body locomotion from upper-body gestures for more robust execution.

2. **Locomotion Control**: Extracting locomotion commands from the pelvis trajectory and implementing them using a Zero Moment Point (ZMP) based controller.

3. **Upper-Body Control**: Executing upper-body motions including arm, head, and finger movements using joint position control.

The real-robot demonstrations showcased HARMON's ability to generate diverse and expressive movements for a range of language instructions, from simple gestures to complex whole-body motions.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, HARMON has several limitations:

1. **Control Primitives**: The current set of motion adjustment primitives is relatively simple. More sophisticated control primitives could enable finer adjustments.

2. **Real-World Robustness**: The system occasionally produces motions that, while visually correct in simulation, may be challenging to execute on real robots due to physical constraints.

3. **Dynamic Motions**: HARMON primarily focuses on quasi-static movements and could be extended to more dynamic actions through reinforcement learning techniques.

4. **Computational Efficiency**: The iterative VLM-based refinement process is computationally intensive, which could limit real-time applications.

Future work could address these limitations by:

- Incorporating reinforcement learning for more robust whole-body control
- Developing more comprehensive motion primitives for finer adjustments
- Integrating physical constraints directly into the motion generation process
- Optimizing the VLM inference pipeline for faster processing

### 9. Conclusion

HARMON represents a significant advancement in language-driven motion generation for humanoid robots. By combining human motion priors with VLM-based refinement, it bridges the gap between natural language understanding and expressive robot movement.

The framework's ability to generate diverse, expressive, and well-coordinated whole-body motions opens new possibilities for human-robot interaction. As humanoid robots become more integrated into everyday environments, systems like HARMON will be crucial for enabling natural and intuitive communication between humans and robots.

The multi-stage approach demonstrates how foundation models like VLMs can complement traditional robotics techniques, leveraging the strengths of both to solve complex challenges in motion generation. This hybrid methodology represents a promising direction for future research in robotics, potentially extending beyond humanoids to other robotic platforms that require expressive and language-driven behaviors.
