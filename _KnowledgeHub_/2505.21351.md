# EquAct: An SE(3)-Equivariant Multi-Task Transformer for Open-Loop Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> An SE(3)-equivariant multi-task transformer, EquAct, from Northeastern University, integrates geometric understanding with language conditioning for open-loop robotic manipulation. This architecture demonstrates enhanced robustness and sample efficiency, achieving a 53.3% average success rate on 10-demonstration SE(3) randomized simulation tasks, a 15.4% improvement over a strong baseline.

## Problem

- Standard transformer policies for robotic manipulation lack inherent geometric understanding, leading to poor generalization to varied 3D scene configurations.
- Learning SE(3) equivariances from scratch requires extensive data, making existing methods data-inefficient and prone to unpredictable behavior under rigid-body transformations.
- Current approaches do not explicitly enforce SE(3)-invariance for natural language instructions when conditioning robotic policies.

## Method

- Introduces **EquAct**, an SE(3)-equivariant multi-task transformer that integrates continuous SE(3)-equivariance and invariance into its architecture.
- Employs an SE(3)-==Equivariant Point Transformer== U-Net (EPTU) with spherical Fourier features and novel pooling/upsampling operations for geometrically consistent observation encoding.
- Incorporates Invariant Feature-wise Linear Modulation (iFiLM) layers to enforce SE(3)-invariance for natural language conditioning.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **53.3%** in the 10-demonstration SE(3) randomized simulation tasks, outperforming the best baseline by **15.4%**.
- Demonstrated superior real-world performance, with a **65.0%** average success rate across 4 physical multi-task scenarios, compared to **12.5%** for the baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that geometric equivariance is critical, with even minor architectural changes leading to a performance drop from **52.8%** to **12.3%** in relevant tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding SE(3)-equivariance into transformer architectures dramatically enhances geometric consistency and generalization to novel 3D scene configurations.
> - Incorporating geometric priors significantly reduces the data required for learning robust robotic manipulation policies.
> - Formally enforcing SE(3)-invariance for language instructions through iFiLM layers provides a theoretically sound method for conditioning equivariant policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the adoption of deep learning, particularly transformer architectures. These models excel at multi-modal tasks, allowing policies to be conditioned on natural language instructions and visual observations, leading to breakthroughs in learning multi-task keyframe action policies from demonstrations. However, a persistent challenge has been the lack of inherent geometric understanding in standard transformer models. Robotic tasks inherently involve rich 3D geometric structures, from the robot's end-effector poses to object configurations in the scene and even the implicit geometry in language instructions (e.g., "turn the tap clockwise"). Standard transformers often fail to generalize to novel 3D scene configurations because they must learn these geometric invariances and equivariances from scratch, requiring vast amounts of data and often resulting in unpredictable behavior under rigid-body transformations (SE(3) transformations: 3D rotations and translations).

EquAct addresses this critical gap by explicitly incorporating SE(3)-equivariance and invariance directly into the transformer architecture. This work builds upon and significantly extends several lines of research:

- **Multi-task Keyframe Action Policies:** Papers like PerAct established transformer-based agents for multi-task, language-conditioned keyframe action learning. EquAct adopts this successful framework but enhances its geometric robustness.
- **Equivariant Policy Learning:** Prior work has explored equivariance, primarily focusing on SE(2) (2D rigid-body transformations) for planar tasks or discrete approximations of SO(3). Some methods have extended to full SE(3) but often for single-policy tasks, pick-and-place specific actions, or with high computational costs due to iterative refinement. EquAct distinguishes itself by achieving continuous SE(3)-equivariance for *multi-task* keyframe policy learning within a single, unified model and an efficient, one-shot forward pass.
- **Equivariant Neural Networks:** The architecture leverages advanced techniques from equivariant neural networks, particularly those operating in the spherical Fourier domain, such as EquiformerV2. EquAct scales these models to larger point clouds and adapts them for full SE(3) action space prediction, including a novel U-Net style architecture with spherical Fourier maxpooling and upsampling.
- **Language Conditioning in Equivariant Models:** While some recent works have explored combining equivariant graph neural networks with language embeddings, EquAct is the first to explicitly identify and enforce the SE(3) invariance of natural language instructions in the context of robotic policies, introducing specialized invariant FiLM (iFiLM) layers.
- **3D Generalization:** Most manipulation policies focus on tasks with SE(2) randomization. EquAct specifically targets and evaluates performance on scenes with SE(3) randomization, which more accurately reflects real-world variability.

In essence, EquAct pushes the boundaries of multi-task robotic manipulation by seamlessly integrating geometric priors (SE(3) equivariance) with language conditioning, offering a more robust, sample-efficient, and geometrically consistent approach than previous state-of-the-art methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the EquAct research are driven by the limitations of existing transformer-based multi-task robotic manipulation policies:

1. **Enhance Geometric Consistency:** Standard transformers, despite their prowess in modality bridging, lack built-in guarantees of geometric consistency. This often leads to unpredictable behavior when the scene undergoes SE(3) transformations. EquAct aims to rectify this by embedding SE(3)-equivariance directly into the policy network's architecture.
2. **Improve Generalization to Novel 3D Configurations:** Current methods struggle to generalize to novel 3D scene configurations and object poses because they have to implicitly learn underlying geometric structures from extensive data. EquAct seeks to improve this by leveraging theoretical guarantees of equivariance, thereby reducing the need for large amounts of data to capture these fundamental symmetries.
3. **Ensure Language Invariance:** Natural language instructions for robotic tasks often describe actions that are invariant to the scene's rigid-body transformation (e.g., "pick up the red cube" should yield a transformed action if the cube is moved, but the instruction itself doesn't change). EquAct aims to formalize and enforce this SE(3)-invariance for language conditioning within the policy.
4. **Develop an Efficient and Unified Multi-Task Model:** Prior equivariant models for manipulation were often limited to specific actions (e.g., pick-and-place) or required separate network models for each task, often relying on discrete equivariance or computationally expensive iterative processes. EquAct aims to create a single, unified model capable of continuous SE(3)-equivariance for diverse multi-task keyframe actions in an efficient, one-shot manner.
5. **Address SE(3) Scene Randomization:** Many benchmarks primarily use SE(2) (planar) object randomization. EquAct aims to evaluate and demonstrate superior performance in more challenging scenarios involving full SE(3) randomization, which better reflects real-world variability.

In summary, the motivation is to create a robotic manipulation policy that is not only proficient at language-conditioned multi-task learning but also fundamentally understands and respects the 3D geometry of its environment, leading to superior generalization, robustness, and data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EquAct proposes a novel SE(3)-equivariant multi-task transformer architecture for open-loop robotic manipulation, built upon two core components: an SE(3)-equivariant point cloud-based U-net with spherical Fourier features and SE(3)-invariant Feature-wise Linear Modulation (iFiLM) layers for language conditioning.

**Policy Formulation:** EquAct models the multi-task keyframe action policy, π(o, n) = a, as an implicit action value function Q_a(o, n, a), where 'o' is the observation (point cloud 's' and gripper pose 'e'), 'n' is the natural language instruction, and 'a' is the keyframe action. The action 'a' consists of translational (a_t), rotational (a_r), and gripper open/close (a_open) components. The policy predicts the action with the highest value.

**Equivariance and Invariance Assumptions:**
The core methodological innovation lies in enforcing:
- **SE(3)-Equivariance for Observation-Action Mapping:** If the observation 'o' is transformed by a rigid-body transformation 'g' (g · o), the predicted action 'a' also transforms accordingly (g · a).
- **SE(3)-Invariance for Natural Language Instruction:** For a fixed language instruction 'n', the action transformation depends *solely* on the observation transformation. This implies that the language instruction's semantic meaning is preserved regardless of the scene's orientation or position.

**Architecture Components:**

1. **SE(3)-Equivariant Point Transformer U-Net (EPTU):**
 - **Purpose:** To encode the input point cloud 's' and gripper pose 'e' into SE(3)-equivariant latent spherical features 'h'.
 - **Structure:** A U-Net-style architecture, inspired by standard U-Nets, but adapted for 3D point clouds and spherical Fourier features.
 - **Feature Representation:** Uses spherical Fourier features in its hidden layers, allowing for continuous SE(3)-equivariance.
 - **Core Operations:**
 - **Spherical Fourier Maxpooling:** A novel operation that reduces feature map resolution by aggregating features from k-nearest neighbors and selecting the spherical Fourier coefficient with the largest magnitude at each degree 'l'. This operation is mathematically proven to be SE(3)-equivariant.
 - **Spherical Fourier Upsampling:** A novel interpolation method for spherical Fourier features, performing coefficient-wise interpolation over k-nearest neighbors of a query point. Also mathematically proven to be SE(3)-equivariant.
 - **EquiformerV2 Graph Attention Blocks:** Interleaves pooling/upsampling layers with these blocks, which construct k-nearest-neighbor graphs and apply equivariant attention-based message passing.
 - **Skip Connections:** Incorporates skip connections between downsampling and upsampling stages, typical of U-Net architectures, to preserve detail.

2. **Invariant Feature-wise Linear Modulation Layers (iFiLM):**
 - **Purpose:** To condition the policy on natural language instructions ('n') while enforcing SE(3)-invariance with respect to the language input and SO(3)-equivariance with respect to the policy features.
 - **Mechanism:** Takes a spherical Fourier feature 'c' and a type-0 (invariant) language condition feature 'k' (obtained from a CLIP tokenizer and transformer encoder). A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) projects 'k' into type-0 modulation scales (α, β) and bias (γ).
 - **Modulation:** For type-l features (l > 0), iFiLM scales them by α_l. For type-0 features (l = 0), it applies an affine transformation using β and γ. This design ensures that the language modulation itself does not break the equivariance of higher-order features.
 - **Proof:** Mathematically proven to be SO(3)-invariant with respect to the condition input 'k' and SO(3)-equivariant with respect to the input feature 'c'.

3. **Equivariant Field Network:**
 - **Purpose:** To evaluate action values across the continuous pose action space by propagating features from the latent point cloud 'h' to any query action pose.
 - **Translational Action (Q_t):** For a query translational action 'a_t', the network builds a graph, aggregates spherical Fourier features from 'h' to 'a_t' using an EquiformerV2 attention block, and outputs an invariant (type-0) action value. It uses a coarse-to-fine sampling approach.
 - **Gripper Open Action (Q_open):** Similar to Q_t, but outputs two type-0 feature channels for open/close values.
 - **Rotational Action (Q_r):** Aggregates features at the predicted translation 'a_t' to obtain spherical Fourier features 'ϕ'. Then, a spherical CNN with a learnable filter 'ψ' calculates the action value for a rotational query 'a_r' by performing a spherical convolution, making it SE(3)-equivariant.

**Training:** EquAct is trained using a cross-entropy loss, treating policy learning as a classification problem over sampled actions. Data augmentation (randomly rotating point cloud and action simultaneously) is applied during training to further enhance robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EquAct's performance was rigorously evaluated across simulation and physical tasks, demonstrating significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (18 RLBench Tasks)

- **Experimental Settings:** Benchmarked on 18 RLBench tasks with increasing difficulty:
 - **100-demo SE(2) setting:** 100 demonstrations per task, objects initialized with random SE(2) poses (standard).
 - **10-demo SE(2) setting:** 10 demonstrations per task, objects initialized with random SE(2) poses (low-data).
 - **10-demo SE(3) setting:** 10 demonstrations per task, objects initialized with random SE(3) poses (low-data, high geometric variability).
- **Baselines:** SAM2ACT (SOTA, leverages pre-trained vision models, multi-view transformer) and 3DDA (point-cloud based diffusion policy).
- **Results (Table 1):**
 - EquAct consistently outperformed all baselines across all settings.
 - **100-demo SE(2):** EquAct achieved an average success rate of 89.4%, outperforming SAM2ACT (86.8%) by 2.6% and 3DDA (81.3%) by 8.1%.
 - **10-demo SE(2):** EquAct achieved 60.1%, surpassing SAM2ACT (52.2%) by 7.9% and 3DDA (50.3%) by 9.8%.
 - **10-demo SE(3):** This is where EquAct's advantage was most pronounced, achieving 53.3%, a substantial 15.4% lead over SAM2ACT (37.0%) and 3DDA (37.9%).
 - **Task-Specific Performance:** EquAct particularly excelled in tasks requiring high precision (e.g., "place_cups," "sort_shape"), where its geometric reasoning proved crucial. It showed strong generalization to SE(3) variations, which proved challenging for other methods.
 - **Efficiency:** EquAct matched the training/inference time and GPU memory consumption of baselines, despite its theoretical guarantees, indicating computational practicality.

#### 4.2. Physical Experiments (4 Multi-Task Settings)

- **Experimental Setup:** Evaluated on 4 real-world multi-task scenarios ("disassemble_pipe," "pluck_flower," "pick_fruit," "install_toilet_roll") with 11 variations and 135 total demonstrations. Objects in most tasks underwent random SE(3) transformations.
- **Baseline:** 3DDA (the best baseline from the 10-demo SE(3) simulation).
- **Results (Table 2):** EquAct achieved an average success rate of 65.0%, significantly outperforming 3DDA (12.5%). This demonstrates EquAct's ability to learn effective SE(3) multi-task policies from limited real-world demonstrations, handling noise and real-world complexities.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Settings:** Conducted on the 10-demo SE(2) setting across 4 RLBench tasks.
- **Key Findings (Table 3):**
 - **Equivariance is Critical (`equ. → no equ.`):** Replacing even a single equivariant layer with a non-equivariant one led to the largest performance drop (from 52.8% to 12.3% average success rate), emphasizing that enforcing geometric structure is paramount.
 - **Fourier Feature Resolution (`l = 3 → 2`):** Reducing the spherical harmonic degree from 3 to 2 resulted in a noticeable performance drop (from 52.8% to 45.5%), indicating the importance of high-resolution spherical Fourier coefficients for precise action reasoning.
 - **Invariant FiLM (`iFiLM → FiLM`):** While generally comparable, ablating iFiLM layers led to degradation in precision-demanding tasks like "place_cups," validating the generalization advantage of the proposed invariant language conditioning.
 - **Data Augmentation (`aug. → no aug.`):** Removing data augmentation also led to a slight performance decrease (from 52.8% to 50.5%), suggesting it complements the architectural equivariance by potentially reducing numerical errors.

Overall, the results strongly support EquAct's claim of superior spatial generalization and sample efficiency, particularly in scenarios involving complex 3D pose variations and limited data, due to its theoretically grounded SE(3) equivariance and invariance properties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EquAct represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, primarily by offering a principled and effective way to integrate 3D geometric reasoning into multi-task language-conditioned policies. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** By embedding continuous SE(3)-equivariance and invariance, EquAct intrinsically understands how to adapt actions to transformed observations. This dramatically improves the policy's robustness to varying object poses and scene configurations, a major hurdle for deploying robots in unstructured, real-world environments. Its superior performance on SE(3) initialized tasks is a direct testament to this.
2. **Increased Sample Efficiency:** Learning geometric priors from data alone requires enormous datasets. By incorporating these priors directly into the architecture, EquAct reduces the amount of data needed to learn a capable policy. This is crucial for robotics, where collecting large, diverse datasets is expensive and time-consuming. The strong performance with only 10 demonstrations highlights this benefit.
3. **Unified and Efficient Multi-Task Learning:** EquAct introduces a single, unified model that can handle diverse multi-task keyframe actions with continuous SE(3) variations, avoiding the need for task-specific models or iterative, computationally intensive refinement processes. This simplifies policy development and deployment.
4. **Theoretical Foundation for Language Conditioning:** The formalization and implementation of SE(3)-invariant Feature-wise Linear Modulation (iFiLM) layers provide a novel and theoretically sound approach to condition equivariant policies with natural language, ensuring that semantic meaning remains geometrically independent. This could pave the way for more robust language-robot interactions.
5. **New Benchmarking Standard:** The proposal of 18 RLBench tasks with SE(3) initialization addresses a gap in current benchmarks, providing a more challenging and realistic evaluation environment for robotic manipulation policies. This will likely encourage the development of more geometrically aware learning methods.
6. **Broader Impact on Equivariant AI:** The architectural innovations, particularly the SE(3)-equivariant Point Transformer U-Net with spherical Fourier maxpooling and upsampling, contribute to the broader field of equivariant neural networks. These components could be adapted for other 3D data processing tasks where rigid-body symmetries are important.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Open-loop Control:** EquAct relies on a keyframe action formulation, which assumes tasks can be decomposed into a sequence of discrete gripper poses. This might not be suitable for fine-grained, closed-loop tasks requiring continuous feedback.
- **Lack of Pre-trained Vision Models:** EquAct does not leverage large pre-trained vision models (like CLIP or SAM) which have shown benefits for data efficiency and semantic generalization in other works. Integrating such models while preserving equivariance is a potential avenue for further improvement.
- **Computational Speed:** While matching baselines, the authors note that EquAct's training and inference speed could be further optimized with a more efficient equivariant backbone.

In conclusion, EquAct provides a robust, sample-efficient, and geometrically consistent solution for multi-task robotic manipulation. Its theoretical underpinnings and strong empirical results, especially in challenging 3D-randomized and physical environments, mark a significant step towards more capable and generalizable robotic systems.
