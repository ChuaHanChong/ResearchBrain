# Online Experiential Learning for Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research introduced an Online Experiential Learning (OEL) framework that allows large language models to continuously refine their capabilities directly from real-world textual interactions, circumventing the need for explicit reward signals or human annotations. This iterative approach to extracting and consolidating experiential knowledge into model parameters yielded progressive gains in task accuracy, reduced response lengths by up to 30% in experiments, and maintained out-of-distribution performance.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are primarily trained offline and become static artifacts post-deployment, unable to learn or adapt from real-world interactions.
- The extensive and diverse experience accumulated during actual usage in dynamic, open-ended environments typically remains unexploited.
- Current methods struggle to extract actionable training signals from unstructured textual feedback without human annotations, explicit reward models, or direct server-side environment access.

## Method

- The **OEL** (Online Experiential Learning) framework establishes an iterative, reward-free online learning loop comprising two stages: ==knowledge extraction== and ==knowledge consolidation==.
- Experiential knowledge is extracted from multi-turn interaction trajectories using a designated language model, accumulating transferable insights from raw textual environment feedback without requiring ground-truth labels.
- This extracted knowledge is consolidated into the model's parameters via ==on-policy context distillation==, optimizing the student model to match a knowledge-conditioned teacher model through ==token-level reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence==, performed entirely server-side.

## Results

- The OEL framework led to progressive increases in task pass rates on text-based game environments, such as Frozen Lake and Sokoban, over successive online learning iterations.
- Models trained with OEL demonstrated improved inference efficiency, with average per-turn response lengths decreasing by approximately **30%** on Frozen Lake while concurrently enhancing task accuracy.
- On-policy context distillation effectively maintained out-of-distribution (OOD) accuracy on IF-Eval benchmarks, showing better preservation of general capabilities compared to off-policy distillation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuously extracting and consolidating experiential knowledge from real-world interactions enables language models to achieve progressive improvements in performance and efficiency within an iterative online learning cycle.
> - Employing on-policy context distillation for knowledge consolidation helps mitigate catastrophic forgetting, preserving out-of-distribution capabilities while internalizing new experiences.
> - Distilled experiential knowledge is substantially more effective for model improvement than directly using raw interaction trajectories, highlighting the importance of the extraction process.
