# Multi-agent Architecture Search via Agentic Supernet

> [!summary] Summary
> MaAS replaces the search for one best multi-agent workflow with an agentic supernet, a probability distribution over layered operator choices such as CoT, debate, ReAct and early exit. A query-conditioned controller samples a tailored system for each query. Distribution and operators are trained jointly with cost-aware gradients. On six benchmarks it beats prior systems by 0.54-16.89% at 6-45% of their inference cost.

## Problem

- Existing automated methods (ADAS, AFlow, AgentSquare) search for one static, complex workflow, which cannot match compute to query difficulty. Easy questions get token-heavy pipelines with dozens of LLM calls.
- A single system is rarely best across domains. On GAIA, no one workflow suits both file reading and web search, so practitioners must split benchmarks and optimize each part separately.
- Prior search paradigms (MCTS, evolution, heuristic or Bayesian search) are black-box and costly to train. Their training bills are high (AFlow costs 22.50 dollars on MATH) and they optimize accuracy, not inference resources.

## Method

- **Agentic supernet**: a layered ==probabilistic distribution== over ==agentic operators== (CoT, debate, ReAct, self-consistency, ensemble, refine) plus an ==early-exit operator==, so sampled ==DAG workflows== have query-dependent depth and width.
- ==Query-conditioned controller==: a ==mixture-of-experts==-style network scores operators from embeddings of the query and earlier layers. It activates operators in score order until a ==cumulative threshold== is met, so each layer's width tracks difficulty.
- ==Cost-constrained joint optimization==: ==Monte Carlo estimation== with ==cost-aware importance weights== updates the distribution. LLM-generated ==textual gradients== rewrite operator prompts, temperature and structure. A ==penalty term== trades accuracy against token cost.

## Results

- With gpt-4o-mini, MaAS averages **83.59%** over GSM8K, MATH, MultiArith, HumanEval and MBPP. That beats AFlow (**82.25%**) and hand-crafted systems by **3.90-6.40%**. On GAIA it reaches **20.69%** versus **16.34%** for the best automated baseline.
- On MATH, training costs **3.38 dollars** versus **22.50** for AFlow (**6.8x** cheaper), taking **53 minutes** versus **184**. Inference costs **0.42 dollars** versus **1.66**, and MaAS also gets the highest accuracy, **51.82%**.
- Removing the textual gradient drops MATH accuracy from **51.82%** to **48.23%**. Removing early exit or the cost term barely changes accuracy but raises HumanEval cost from **1.01** to **1.67** or **1.38** (10^-3 dollars). Supernets transfer to Qwen-2.5-70b with **4.98-5.50%** gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimize a distribution over architectures conditioned on the input, not a single best system. Per-query allocation gives accuracy and cost gains that one static pipeline cannot reach.
> - Borrowing the weight-sharing supernet idea from neural architecture search (as in DARTS) makes agent design search far cheaper than black-box MCTS or evolutionary search over whole workflows.
> - Learned early exit and a cost penalty mostly buy efficiency, not accuracy, while prompt-level textual gradients drive quality. Cost-aware objectives should be built in from the start.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have led to their application in various domains, from question answering to code generation. A significant extension of individual LLMs is the concept of LLM-based agents, which augment LLMs with capabilities like persona, tools, planning, and memory. Building on this, researchers have explored **multi-agent systems (MAS)**, where multiple LLM agents collaborate or compete to collectively surpass individual capabilities, mimicking emergent collective intelligence.

Early MAS, such as CAMEL, AutoGen, and MetaGPT, proved the concept but heavily relied on **labor-intensive manual designs**. This involved extensive prompt engineering, agent profiling, and defining complex inter-agent communication pipelines, severely limiting their adaptability and scalability across diverse tasks and domains.

The research community has since shifted towards **automating multi-agent system design**. Initial efforts focused on automating specific aspects, like prompt optimization (e.g., DsPy, EvoPrompting), inter-agent communication (e.g., GPTSwarm, G-Designer), or agent profiling (e.g., EvoAgent, AutoAgents). More recently, frameworks like ADAS, AgentSquare, and AFlow have broadened the design search space, aiming to automate the entire multi-agent workflow. These state-of-the-art methods typically employ search paradigms like heuristic search, Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), or evolutionary algorithms to identify a single, complex, and high-performing multi-agent workflow for a given dataset.

However, this prevailing paradigm of searching for a "one-size-fits-all" multi-agent system presents two critical dilemmas:
1. **Resource Inefficiency (Dilemma 1):** Such static complex systems are often resource-intensive, involving numerous LLM API calls and external tool usages, even for simple queries. This leads to high token costs, LLM calls, and inference latency, making them impractical for dynamic, real-world deployment where query difficulty varies. For instance, a complex mathematical problem might require a sophisticated multi-agent debate, while a simple arithmetic question could be solved with a single zero-shot LLM call.
2. **Lack of Adaptability Across Domains (Dilemma 2):** A single optimal system optimized for one task domain (e.g., math reasoning) may perform poorly or inefficiently in another (e.g., web navigation). This forces practitioners to manually split benchmarks and optimize systems separately, which is cumbersome and limits generalizability.

This paper, "Multi-agent Architecture Search via Agentic Supernet" (MaAS), directly addresses these two dilemmas. It marks a significant departure from the current research trajectory by moving beyond the pursuit of a single optimal multi-agent architecture. Instead, MaAS draws inspiration from Neural Architecture Search (NAS) in AutoML, particularly the concept of "supernets," to propose optimizing a *distribution* of agentic architectures. This allows for dynamic, query-dependent allocation of resources and adaptation to varied task domains, positioning it at the forefront of automated, adaptive, and resource-efficient multi-agent system design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the MaAS framework are to:

1. **Overcome the "One-Size-Fits-All" Limitation:** The core motivation is to move away from the current paradigm of optimizing a static, monolithic multi-agent system that is resource-intensive and struggles to adapt to varying query complexities and domains.
2. **Enable Dynamic Resource Allocation:** To address Dilemma 1, the goal is to develop a system that can dynamically allocate inference resources (e.g., LLM calls, tool calls, token cost) based on the specific difficulty and domain of each incoming query. Simple queries should trigger simpler, more cost-efficient workflows, while complex ones can invoke more sophisticated, multi-agent collaborations.
3. **Achieve Query-Dependent Adaptability:** To tackle Dilemma 2, the objective is to create a framework that can automatically sample and deploy tailored multi-agent architectures for queries across diverse task domains, without requiring separate, manual optimizations for each domain.
4. **Jointly Optimize Performance and Cost:** The framework aims to discover multi-agent systems that not only deliver high-quality solutions but also do so in a token-economical manner, balancing utility (performance) and cost (inference resources).
5. **Propose the Agentic Supernet Concept:** Introduce and formalize the novel concept of an "agentic supernet" – a probabilistic and continuous distribution of agentic architectures – as the foundational mechanism to achieve query-dependent adaptability and resource efficiency.

In essence, MaAS seeks to automate the design of multi-agent systems to make them more intelligent, adaptive, and practical for real-world deployment by optimizing for a *distribution* of solutions rather than a single fixed one.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MaAS introduces a novel framework centered around the concept of an **agentic supernet** to achieve query-dependent multi-agent architecture search.

#### 3.1. Search Space Definition

The foundation of MaAS is built upon formally defined components:
- **Agentic Operator (O):** This is the basic building block, representing a composite LLM-agent invocation process. An operator can involve multiple LLM calls (`M`), specific prompts (`P`), and tool usages (`T`). Examples include Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Self-RAG, Multi-agent Debate, Self-Consistency, Self-Refine, Ensemble, Testing, ReAct, and a crucial "Early-exit" operator.
- **Multi-agent System (G):** A multi-agent system is defined as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of selected agentic operators (`V`) and their connectivity (`E`).
- **Agentic Supernet (A):** The core innovation. It's an L-layer probabilistic, continuous distribution of agentic architectures. It comprises a set of feasible operators (`O`) and their parameterized probability distributions (`π`). Specifically, `π_ℓ(O)` represents the probability of operator `O` being present at layer `ℓ`, conditioned on previously selected operators. This supernet induces a joint distribution over all possible multi-layer operator configurations.

#### 3.2. Problem Formulation

Unlike previous methods that optimize for a single `G`, MaAS aims to optimize a conditional probability distribution `P(G|q)`. The objective is to maximize the expected utility `U(G; q, a)` while minimizing the cost `C(G; q)` for a given query `q` and its oracle answer `a`, where `G` is sampled from `P(G|q)`. A trade-off parameter `λ` balances performance and cost.

#### 3.3. Agentic Architecture Sampling (Query-Dependent)

The key mechanism for dynamic allocation is the **controller network (Q_ϕ)**. This network takes the input query `q`, the current parameterized distribution `π`, and the available operators `O` to sample a customized multi-agent architecture `G`.
- **Conditional Sampling:** The controller `Q_ϕ` samples operators layer by layer, where the selection at each layer is dependent on the query `q` and operators chosen in preceding layers.
- **Mixture-of-Expert (MoE)-style Network:** `Q_ϕ` is implemented as an MoE-style network. For each layer, it computes activation scores for all feasible operators based on the query embedding and embeddings of previously selected operators. Operators are activated sequentially based on their scores until a cumulative score threshold (`thres`) is met. This ensures that the number of selected operators per layer is dynamically determined by the query's complexity. Lightweight models (e.g., MiniLM, Sentence-BERT) are used for embedding queries and operators to keep the controller efficient.
- **Early-Exit Operator (O_exit):** A critical feature for cost efficiency. If the `O_exit` operator is sampled at any layer, the multi-agent architecture sampling process terminates early, preventing unnecessary deep computations for simpler queries.

Once a task-specific multi-agent system `G` is sampled, it is executed to produce an answer `ea`.

#### 3.4. Cost-constrained Supernet Optimization

The optimization objective is to minimize a loss function that balances performance (maximizing the probability of generating the correct answer) and cost (minimizing token cost). The optimization process involves jointly updating the distribution parameters (`π`) and the agentic operators (`O`).

- **Optimizing Distribution (π) via Monte Carlo:** Since the utility function (producing a correct answer) involves non-differentiable external API calls, the gradient with respect to the distribution `π` is estimated using an empirical Bayes Monte Carlo procedure. This procedure computes cost-aware importance weights for each sampled architecture, favoring those that generate high-quality solutions with minimal token cost.
- **Optimizing Operators (O) via Textual Gradient:** Agentic operators involve natural language prompts and black-box tool usage, making numerical gradient computation infeasible. MaAS employs an **agent-based textual gradient** mechanism. An LLM agent analyzes the performance and cost feedback from the environment and generates "gradients" in textual format. These textual gradients provide instructions for updating:
 - **Prompts (T_P):** Suggestions for refining prompts within operators.
 - **Temperature (T_T):** Adjustments to LLM temperature settings for stability or exploration.
 - **Operator Node Structure (T_N):** Recommendations for structural changes to operators (e.g., merging, splitting, altering internal logic).
This approach enables the self-evolving capability of the agentic supernet, allowing both the selection probabilities and the internal workings of the operators to be optimized.

In summary, MaAS intelligently constructs a flexible supernet of multi-agent components, learns a policy (via a controller network) to dynamically select and compose these components based on query characteristics, and then refines both the selection policy and the components themselves using a combination of Monte Carlo sampling and textual gradient feedback, all while explicitly considering resource costs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MaAS was rigorously evaluated across six diverse public benchmarks: GSM8K, MATH, MultiArith (mathematical reasoning), HumanEval, MBPP (code generation), and GAIA (tool use). The key findings demonstrate MaAS's superior performance, cost efficiency, and adaptability.

#### 4.1. Performance Analysis

- **Overall Superiority:** MaAS consistently achieved optimal performance across all tested task domains. It surpassed manually designed multi-agent systems by an average of 3.90% to 6.40% and existing automated methods by 2.07% to 8.26%.
- **Mathematical Reasoning & Code Generation:** For these domains (Table 1), MaAS achieved an average best score of 83.59% (compared to the runner-up AFlow at 82.25%), showcasing its versatility. Specific improvements over the SOTA AFlow were notable: 1.14% on GSM8K (92.30% vs 91.16%), 0.54% on MATH (51.82% vs 51.28%), 2.58% on MultiArith (98.80% vs 96.22%), 1.92% on HumanEval (92.85% vs 90.93%), and 0.50% on MBPP (82.17% vs 81.67%).
- **Complex Tool Use (GAIA):** For the GAIA benchmark (Table 2), which requires diverse tool usage and is challenging for single optimal systems, MaAS achieved significant improvements. It reached an average accuracy of 20.69%, considerably outperforming the next best (AgentSquare at 16.34% and GPTSwarm at 16.33%) and SOTA AFlow (8.00%). The adaptive sampling of MaAS led to 18.38% and 17.61% improvements on Level 1 and 2 tasks, respectively, demonstrating its ability to customize systems for different problem types.

#### 4.2. Cost Analysis

MaAS demonstrated remarkable resource efficiency in terms of token cost, API cost, and wall-clock time (Table 3, Figure 4).
- **Training Cost Efficiency:** MaAS achieved high accuracy with significantly lower training token consumption. Its training cost on the MATH benchmark was merely $3.38, which is approximately 6.8 times less than AFlow ($22.50) while achieving better accuracy. The training wall-clock time for MaAS was 53 minutes, substantially faster than DyLAN (508 minutes) and GPTSwarm (129 minutes).
- **Inference Cost Efficiency:** During inference, MaAS maintained the highest accuracy with an API cost of $0.42 on MATH, outperforming AFlow's $1.66. It had the lowest prompt/completion token consumption and the shortest wall-clock time (19 minutes vs. AFlow's 23 minutes). This underlines the benefit of the agentic supernet's ability to dynamically allocate resources based on query difficulty, avoiding over-computation for simpler tasks.

#### 4.3. Case Study & Intrinsic Mechanisms

- **Query-Aware Early Exit:** Visualizations (Figure 5, Figure 8) confirmed that MaAS learns to adaptively terminate the multi-agent architecture sampling process. For "easy" queries, MaAS frequently samples the "early-exit" operator at shallow layers (e.g., second layer with 37-47% probability), while for "medium" and "hard" queries, it samples additional layers, leveraging more complex operators. This dynamically adjusts the computational depth based on task complexity.
- **Dynamic Operator Allocation:** For "easy" queries, MaAS could still select multiple first-layer operators (e.g., direct I/O and ReAct), showing its ability to dynamically allocate different operators at each layer.

#### 4.4. Framework Analysis

- **Sensitivity Analysis (Figure 7):**
 - **Number of Layers (L):** Performance significantly improved from L=2 to L=4, but gains were marginal beyond L=4 while incurring higher costs, leading to L=4 being the optimal choice.
 - **Cost Penalty (λ):** Larger `λ` values reduced costs but slightly degraded performance, confirming the trade-off.
 - **Sampling Count (K):** K=4 provided a satisfactory low-variance estimation, while K=2 led to suboptimal performance with high variance.
- **Ablation Study (Table 4):**
 - **w/o ∇_O L (Textual Gradient):** The largest performance drop occurred without textual gradients, highlighting its critical role in the self-evolving capability of operators.
 - **w/o O_exit (Early-Exit Operator):** Removed early-exit, performance was minimally affected, but inference costs significantly increased, confirming its role in resource efficiency.
 - **w/o C(·) (Cost Constraint):** Removing the cost constraint also had minor performance impact but increased inference costs, validating the cost-aware optimization.
- **Transferability Analysis (Tables 7 & 8):**
 - **Cross-Model Transferability:** The agentic supernet optimized with `gpt-4o-mini` transferred well to other LLM backbones (Qwen-2.5-72b and Llama-3.1-70b), yielding performance improvements of 4.98% to 5.50% over their vanilla counterparts.
 - **Cross-Dataset Generalization:** The optimized supernet demonstrated strong transferability across different datasets (e.g., trained on MATH, evaluated on GSM8K), outperforming GPTSwarm and AFlow in these transfer scenarios.
- **Inductive Analysis (Figures 8, 9, 10):** MaAS showed inductive capabilities, meaning it could generalize to unseen agentic operators. Even when the Debate operator was withheld during training and introduced only at inference, MaAS could still reasonably activate and utilize it, demonstrating its robustness and flexibility in handling new components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Multi-agent Architecture Search via Agentic Supernet" paper represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM-based multi-agent systems and automated AI design.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Reformulation

The most profound contribution is the **paradigm shift** from optimizing a single, static multi-agent system to optimizing a **probabilistic, continuous distribution of agentic architectures** (the agentic supernet). This moves the field closer to truly adaptive and intelligent AI systems. It acknowledges that no single system can be optimal for all situations, introducing a flexible framework that can adapt its "cognition" based on the specific demands of a query. This is analogous to how human experts adjust their problem-solving strategies based on the complexity and domain of a task.

#### 5.2. Practical Solution for Real-World Deployment

MaAS offers a highly practical solution to critical challenges in deploying LLM-based agents:
- **Dynamic Resource Allocation:** By learning to sample query-dependent multi-agent systems, MaAS ensures that simpler tasks consume fewer resources (LLM calls, tokens, time) while complex tasks receive the necessary computational depth. This addresses the high inference costs and latency that plague current sophisticated multi-agent systems, making them more economical and efficient for real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalizability:** The framework not only achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks but also demonstrates strong transferability across different LLM backbones and datasets. This means a single optimized supernet can be deployed more broadly, reducing the need for domain-specific re-optimization.
- **Self-Evolving Capability:** The use of textual gradients for operator optimization enables the system to "self-evolve," continuously improving its building blocks and their interactions based on environmental feedback. This moves towards a vision of self-organizing and self-improving collective intelligence.
- **Inductive Generalization:** The ability to effectively utilize unseen operators signifies a robust framework that can gracefully integrate new tools or reasoning patterns without requiring full re-training, which is crucial for future-proofing AI systems.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact

- **Democratization of Intelligent Automation:** By significantly reducing inference costs and enhancing flexibility, MaAS could democratize access to advanced intelligent automation. It lowers the barrier for integrating sophisticated multi-agent capabilities into various applications, from enterprise solutions to educational tools, even for organizations with limited computational budgets.
- **Foundation for Future AI Agents:** The agentic supernet concept and the methodology of joint optimization with textual gradients provide a new research direction for designing more robust, adaptive, and autonomous AI agents. This could lead to the development of truly self-organizing AI systems that can learn and adapt their internal architectures on the fly to tackle unforeseen challenges.
- **Sustainable AI Development:** The focus on resource efficiency aligns with the growing emphasis on sustainable AI, reducing the carbon footprint associated with large-scale model inference and complex AI deployments.

In conclusion, MaAS represents a significant leap forward by rethinking the fundamental approach to multi-agent system design. Its emphasis on dynamic, cost-aware, and self-evolving architectures positions it as a pivotal work that could accelerate the development and widespread adoption of more intelligent, adaptable, and efficient AI systems.
