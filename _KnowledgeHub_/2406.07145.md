# Failures Are Fated, But Can Be Faded: Characterizing and Mitigating Unwanted Behaviors in Large-Scale Vision and Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Arizona State University developed a three-stage, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework to characterize and mitigate unwanted behaviors in pre-trained large-scale deep neural networks. This approach constructs a "failure landscape" by mapping operating conditions to model failures, which can then be restructured using limited human feedback to reduce or shift specific undesirable modes across diverse AI tasks.

## Problem

- Large deep neural networks exhibit vulnerabilities such as accuracy issues, social biases, and misalignment with human values, which often remain opaque until after deployment.
- Exhaustive testing of deep learning models is infeasible due to the vast and complex high-dimensional concept spaces involved.
- Existing reliability methods, like formal verification or out-of-distribution detection, face scalability challenges or do not provide a comprehensive characterization of failure landscapes for targeted mitigation.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline (==Discover==, ==Summarize==, ==Restructure==) utilizes ==deep reinforcement learning (DRL)== to explore the ==failure landscape== of a model across defined ==concepts== or operating conditions.
- DRL agents are trained with specific reward functions to efficiently identify combinations of concepts that trigger significant deviations from human-expected outputs in image classification, text summarization, and image generation tasks.
- Limited post-hoc human feedback is integrated to prioritize discovered failures, followed by targeted ==fine-tuning== (e.g., last layer fine-tuning, ==LoRA==) to reduce or shift undesirable failure modes without full model retraining.

## Results

- The DRL-based approach consistently identified a **higher number of failure instances** across AlexNet, ResNet50, and EfficientNetV2 compared to baseline search strategies while maintaining competitive computational times.
- Fine-tuning successfully reduced the frequency of targeted undesirable actions and improved model performance, such as increasing **BLEU scores** in text summarization and reducing gender bias in image generation from a male-to-female ratio of **1.65 to 1.16**.
- **Wasserstein distance** measurements quantitatively confirmed the localized mitigation effect and the successful shift of failure distributions within the concept space after applying mitigation strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating failure discovery as a deep RL problem enables efficient and scalable exploration of vast, high-dimensional concept spaces, outperforming random, greedy, and Bayesian optimization search methods.
> - The framework distinguishes between macroscopic exploration for identifying diverse failure regions and microscopic exploration for pinpointing critical individual factors influencing specific failures (e.g., image darkness).
> - Targeted fine-tuning, guided by minimal human feedback, can effectively restructure the failure landscape by mitigating specific unwanted behaviors, even revealing vulnerabilities in models previously considered adversarially robust.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The widespread deployment of large deep neural networks in various applications highlights their performance capabilities but also exposes their inherent vulnerabilities, including issues related to accuracy, social biases, and misalignment with human values. Understanding and addressing these failure modes before deployment is a critical concern for both model developers and regulatory bodies.

This work addresses limitations in existing approaches to model reliability and safety:
- **Formal verification and validation:** While important for rigorous analysis, these methods face scalability challenges when applied to state-of-the-art deep neural networks due to their reliance on specific assumptions about model architectures and functions.
- **Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection:** Research in OOD detection aims to identify inputs that deviate from the training distribution. The presented work differentiates itself by focusing on characterizing specific regions where failures occur within a defined concept space, rather than merely flagging inputs as OOD.
- **Adversarial attacks:** These techniques involve small perturbations to inputs to induce model errors. The paper views adversarial attacks as a specific instance of microscopic exploration, concentrating on sensitivity to minor input changes. It argues that its approach offers a more comprehensive characterization of the overall failure landscape, identifying vulnerabilities beyond those near decision boundaries, as evidenced by comparisons with methods like the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM).
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) in other domains:** RL has been successfully applied to control and design tasks. However, existing RL-based stress-testing methods, particularly in fields like aeronautics, may not scale efficiently to the high-dimensional, continuous action spaces characteristic of modern foundation models.
- **"Red teaming" and failure discovery:** While the practice of "red teaming" large language models to find failures is gaining traction, the authors note that many current efforts do not frame failure discovery as an RL problem. The scalability of the proposed method is attributed to the inherent capabilities of deep RL in managing complex action spaces. The authors also highlight the tendency of Bayesian Optimization (BO) to get trapped in local minima in high-dimensional spaces, a limitation addressed by RL's long-horizon exploration.
- **Human-in-the-loop learning and human-aligned models:** These fields incorporate human feedback into model training. The presented work distinguishes itself by using limited, post-hoc human feedback to guide the restructuring of an already characterized failure landscape, focusing on specific application-relevant concepts rather than learning a reward function through extensive iterative human comparisons.

By proposing a post-hoc, deep RL-based framework, this research offers an empirical and scalable approach to systematically characterize and mitigate failures in complex deep learning systems, integrating targeted human feedback to enhance model alignment with desired outcomes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The principal objective of this research is to develop a post-hoc methodology for characterizing and mitigating unwanted behaviors in pre-trained, large-scale deep neural networks. The work focuses on constructing a "failure landscape," which maps specific operating conditions or "concepts" to the likelihood of model failure. Following characterization, the aim is to demonstrate how this landscape can be restructured, with limited human feedback, to reduce or shift undesirable failure modes.

The motivation for this research arises from several practical and ethical considerations surrounding contemporary deep learning models:
- **Inherent model limitations:** All models, regardless of size, are approximations of the real world and are expected to fail under certain conditions not fully represented in their training data.
- **Opacity of deep learning models:** Unlike models built from first principles with explicitly defined assumptions, the factors contributing to failures in deep neural networks are often not transparent and typically manifest only after deployment in diverse, unpredictable environments.
- **Consequential failures:** Failures in deep learning systems can have severe consequences, ranging from safety risks in autonomous systems (e.g., incorrect object detection) to ethical concerns in generative AI (e.g., perpetuating social biases or stereotypes).
- **Operational and regulatory needs:** Engineers require effective tools to understand and debug the complex interplay of factors that affect model performance. Concurrently, legislative bodies and auditors need accessible methods to assess model capabilities, identify risks, and ensure compliance with ethical and legal standards.
- **Impracticality of exhaustive testing:** The vast number of potential test cases, involving combinations of continuous, discrete, or hybrid factors, renders exhaustive testing of large deep learning models infeasible.
- **Stochastic nature of failures:** Model failures can be stochastic, occurring under specific conditions while being absent in seemingly similar scenarios, which complicates their systematic identification.

To address these challenges, the research proposes an efficient, automated system that can explore failure landscapes, summarize findings for human review, and enable targeted mitigation strategies, thereby supporting the development and responsible deployment of robust AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper is structured as a three-stage pipeline: Discover, Summarize, and Restructure. This framework utilizes deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for failure landscape exploration, integrates human feedback for preference specification, and employs fine-tuning for targeted failure mitigation.

#### 3.1. Defining Failures

A "failure" is defined when a deep neural network $f_\theta$ produces an output $y$ that deviates significantly from a human's expected output $y_{human}$ under a specific set of operating conditions, termed "concepts" ($C$). This deviation is quantified by a discrepancy $\Delta(m(y_{human}), m(y)|C)$, where $m(.)$ is a scoring function evaluating model and human outputs. A failure occurs if this discrepancy exceeds a non-negative threshold $\epsilon$. $y_{human}$ can originate from annotated ground truth or real-time human evaluations. The objective is to efficiently identify concept combinations that trigger such failures.

#### 3.2. Discovering Failures with Deep Reinforcement Learning

The process of discovering failure-inducing concept combinations is formulated as a deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) problem. A policy $\pi$ is learned to modify the values of concepts by executing actions $a$, aiming to maximize rewards associated with model failures.

- **Markov Decision Process (MDP):** The problem is modeled as an MDP $(S, A, P, R, \gamma)$:
 - **States ($S$):** Represent the current input (e.g., image, text prompt) from the dataset.
 - **Actions ($A$):** Represent modifications applied to the concepts affecting the input.
 - **Transition Probability ($P$):** Describes the likelihood of observing a new state $s'$ after applying action $a$ to state $s$.
 - **Reward Function ($R$):** Designed to assign higher rewards for actions that lead to a greater probability of model failure.
 - **Discount Factor ($\gamma$):** A standard parameter influencing the weight of future rewards.
- **Deep Q Networks (DQNs):** Given the large state and action spaces involved with large-scale neural networks, DQNs are utilized. A fully-connected neural network is employed as the policy network. An exploration schedule, which gradually decreases the $\epsilon$ parameter from 1.0 to 0.6 during training, is implemented to balance initial broad exploration with subsequent focused search in high-failure regions.
- **Exploration Strategies:** Two distinct strategies provide varying degrees of granularity in landscape exploration:
 - **Macroscopic Exploration:** This strategy encompasses all possible combinations within the concept value set $C$. It is designed for rapid, wide-ranging exploration to identify diverse failure regions across the action space. The Q-value update rule for this mode is $Q'_{s,a} = Q_{s,a} + \alpha [r_{t+1} - Q_{s',a'}]$.
 - **Microscopic Exploration:** This strategy uses a compact set of actions that incrementally alter the system's state. Starting from a known state, small, fixed-size concept changes are applied to converge towards regions where discrepancy $\Delta$ is within a tolerable range $\epsilon$. This approach allows for a detailed examination of specific sub-regions in the concept space. The Q-value update rule is $Q'_{s,a} = Q_{s,a} + \alpha[r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q_{s',a'} - Q_{s,a}]$.

#### 3.3. Machine Learning Models to Debug or Audit

The framework is instantiated across three DRL environments, each tailored to different machine learning tasks:

- **Accuracy in Image Classification:**
 - **Models:** Pre-trained AlexNet, ResNet50, and EfficientNetV2 Large on ImageNet-1K.
 - **Concepts:** Image transformations (rotation, darkness, saturation).
 - **Action Space:** Discrete combinations of three transformations (e.g., 5-degree rotation, 0.1 darkness, 0.05 saturation increments), yielding 125 actions for macroscopic exploration.
 - **Reward Function:** Penalizes correct predictions and rewards actions that diminish classification performance. $R_{macro-vision}(s, a) = K \cdot \ln(\frac{score}{1-score+const})$ if prediction is incorrect, and -1 otherwise. For microscopic exploration, a penalty for steps taken is included: $R_{micro-vision}(s, a) = R_{macro}(s, a) - \alpha \times \text{steps}$.
- **Effectiveness of Text Summarization:**
 - **Models:** BART and T5 summarization models, utilizing the OpenAI summarize-from-feedback dataset.
 - **Observation Space:** 1024-dimensional BART embeddings of summaries.
 - **Action Space:** 16 distinct text alteration operations (e.g., verb tense changes, misspellings, sentence structure modifications).
 - **Reward Function:** Rewards actions that reduce summary quality. $R_{macro-nlp}(s, a) = (1 - \text{BLEU}) \times \text{len}(\text{prediction})$. For microscopic exploration, $R_{micro-nlp}(s, a) = (1 - \text{BLEU}) - \text{steps}$.
- **Algorithmic Bias in Image Generation:**
 - **Model:** Stable Diffusion v1-4 (SD v1-4) text-to-image model.
 - **Concepts:** Words from sets of personal attributes, professions, and places, integrated into base prompts.
 - **Action Space:** Combinations of these attribute, profession, and place terms.
 - **Reward Function:** Designed to identify combinations leading to poor image quality or high bias. Two variants:
 - **Human Feedback:** $R_{macro-vlm-hf}(s, a) = \frac{1}{N_{feedback}} \sum_{feedback} (\text{bias} \times \text{quality})$.
 - **CLIP Embeddings:** $R_{macro-vlm-clip}(s, a) = 100 \times (1 - \frac{e_{word} \cdot e_{image}}{\|e_{word}\| \|e_{image}\|})$.

#### 3.4. Obtaining Human Preferences

Post-discovery, human feedback is integrated to contextualize and prioritize the discovered failures based on application-specific relevance and ethical considerations. This involves a limited number of post-hoc feedback instances (typically 1-4), distinct from iterative Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF).

- **Qualitative Summary:** The discovered failures are categorized into regions of consistent failure, regions of normal operation, and uncertain regions. Q-values are transformed into action probabilities $P(a_i|s) = \frac{\exp(Q(s, a_i))}{\sum_{j=1}^n \exp(Q(s, a_j))}$. These probabilities are visualized in a 3D space, where color indicates the mean probability of failure (reflecting propensity) and size represents confidence (inverse standard deviation of visits). Users interact with this visualization to select specific points and view associated sample failure cases.
- **Quantitative Summary:** For high-dimensional action spaces where 3D visualization is insufficient, the Wasserstein barycenter (Eq 11) is utilized to summarize failure characteristics within specific regions, offering marginalized views across dimensions.

#### 3.5. Restructuring the Failure Landscape

Once undesirable failure modes are identified through human preference, the goal is to reduce their occurrence. "Reduced failures" are defined as a decrease in the expected discrepancy of scores for a modified model $f_{\theta^*}$ compared to the original $f_\theta$ for the targeted actions $A^*$. To avoid costly retraining, fine-tuning is employed, acknowledging that mitigating one failure mode might impact others, thus supporting an iterative process.

- **Image Classification:** Fine-tuning is applied only to the final layer of the neural networks using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with a learning rate of 0.001 and momentum of 0.9, aiming to preserve learned features and overall accuracy.
- **Text Summarization:** Standard fine-tuning parameters for Large Language Models (LLMs) are used, including a learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-5}$ and a weight decay of $10^{-2}$ for regularization, using the Hugging Face Transformers library.
- **Image Generation:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used to fine-tune SD v1-4. LoRA freezes the original model weights and introduces trainable rank-decomposition matrices, allowing the model to acquire new knowledge while retaining its foundational capabilities. A small, unbiased dataset (e.g., synthetically generated with balanced gender terms from DALL·E3) is used for training the LoRA matrices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of the proposed framework demonstrates its efficacy across image classification, text summarization, and image generation tasks.

#### 4.1. Image Classification

- **Failure Discovery Performance:** The DRL-based approach consistently identified a higher number of failure instances compared to baseline search strategies (random, greedy, threshold-based) across AlexNet, ResNet50, and EfficientNetV2 Large. DQN exhibited lower entropy in its discoveries, indicating a more concentrated identification of failure modes (Table 1).
- **Failure Mitigation Efficacy:** Post-fine-tuning, the count of discovered failures decreased across all models. EfficientNet, being the most accurate baseline, showed the least change, suggesting fewer inherent failure points. Fine-tuning successfully reduced the frequency of targeted undesirable actions (Figure 4a).
- **Quantifying Impact:** Wasserstein distance measurements indicated that fine-tuning localized the mitigation effect, with points closer to the targeted failure node experiencing a more significant shift in the failure landscape (Table 2, Figure 11).
- **Microscopic Exploration Insights:** This exploration revealed that darkness levels exerted a stronger influence on image classification failures compared to saturation or rotation, identifying the most critical factors contributing to model inaccuracies (Figure 14).

#### 4.2. Text Summarization

- **Failure Shift and Quality Improvement:** The DRL agent identified specific text alteration actions leading to summarization failures. After fine-tuning, the dominant failure mode shifted (e.g., from "delete random word" to "repeat random word" for BART, and "repeat random word" to "remove punctuations" for T5). This shift correlated with a significant reduction in cumulative rewards (indicating fewer faults) and an improvement in BLEU scores (Figure 16, 17).
- **Landscape Restructuring:** Wasserstein distance metrics quantitatively confirmed the shift in the failure distribution within the action space post-fine-tuning (Table 3).

#### 4.3. Image Generation

- **Bias Reduction and Quality Enhancement:** For Stable Diffusion v1-4, the framework identified gender bias, manifested as a higher frequency of male image generations for specific prompts. LoRA-based fine-tuning, guided by a balanced dataset, reduced the male-to-female bias ratio from 1.65 to 1.16. Simultaneously, an overall improvement in generated image quality was observed, alongside a 43% reduction in ambiguously gendered images (Figure 5, 30).
- **Failure Mode Reconfiguration:** The prominent failure mode shifted from one combination of attributes, profession, and place (e.g., "distinct, artist, research center") to another ("distinct, scientist, corporate office") after mitigation (Figure 4c).

#### 4.4. Comparative Analysis

- **Search Strategy Efficiency:** DQN demonstrated superior failure discovery rates compared to random, greedy, and threshold-based search methods, while maintaining competitive computational times (Table 1, 4).
- **Adversarial Robustness:** Even in models trained with adversarial techniques (e.g., FGSM), the method revealed persistent vulnerabilities at points in the concept space farther from the decision boundaries, suggesting that adversarial training alone does not fully characterize or eliminate all failure modes (Figure 6, 31).
- **DRL vs. Bayesian Optimization:** Bayesian Optimization was found to be susceptible to local minima and less effective in high-dimensional or discrete action spaces, highlighting DRL's advantage in comprehensive exploration (Figure 32, 33).
- **Reward Mechanism Correlation:** In image generation, the learned policies derived from human feedback and CLIP embeddings showed strong similarity, indicating that CLIP can serve as a viable proxy for human evaluation in some contexts (Figure 34).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for systematically understanding and mitigating undesirable behaviors in large-scale deep neural networks, offering contributions to the robustness, interpretability, and responsible deployment of AI systems.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Enhanced Model Reliability:** By providing a structured approach to characterize and address failure modes, the framework can contribute to the development of more robust and reliable AI models, particularly in high-stakes applications like autonomous systems and critical decision-making.
- **Actionable Debugging for Engineers:** The deep RL-based method offers an automated tool for engineers to efficiently explore the complex factors contributing to model failures. The identification of physically meaningful concepts provides actionable insights for targeted debugging and model improvement.
- **Facilitating AI Auditing and Governance:** The framework provides a tangible method for policymakers and legislative bodies to audit AI model capabilities and limitations, assess risks, and ensure compliance with ethical guidelines and regulations, fostering greater accountability in AI deployment.
- **Improved Human-AI Alignment:** The integration of limited human feedback enables the explicit shifting of failure modes away from undesirable outcomes, such as social biases. This iterative process offers a mechanism to embed human ethical considerations and preferences directly into the behavior of AI systems.
- **Scalability for Foundation Models:** By formulating failure discovery as a deep RL problem, the method efficiently navigates vast, high-dimensional, and often hybrid (continuous and discrete) concept spaces, making it suitable for analyzing complex, pre-trained foundation models where exhaustive testing is impractical.
- **Broad Applicability:** The demonstration across diverse modalities—computer vision, natural language processing, and vision-language tasks—suggests the framework's versatility and potential for application across a wide range of deep learning domains.
- **Post-hoc Efficiency:** As a post-hoc method, it can be applied to existing pre-trained models, which is crucial given the substantial computational and financial costs associated with retraining large models from scratch.

This research indicates that the proposed approach is effective in identifying hidden failures within seemingly well-performing models, making it a valuable tool for pre-deployment assessments. Future development aims to unify fine-tuning approaches for broader applicability. The authors do not anticipate any direct societal harm from the method itself.
