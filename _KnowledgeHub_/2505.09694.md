# EWMBench: Evaluating Scene, Motion, and Semantic Quality in Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> EWMBENCH introduces the first world generation benchmark for embodied tasks, providing a multi-dimensional framework to evaluate scene, motion, and semantic quality in embodied world models. It systematically assesses models against criteria vital for robotic applications, demonstrating that domain-adapted models outperform general video generators while highlighting motion correctness as a key area for further development.

## Problem

- Existing general video generation benchmarks primarily focus on perceptual metrics (visual fidelity, language alignment) which are insufficient for evaluating embodied world models (EWMs).
- EWMs require evaluation criteria specific to physical plausibility, action consistency, and structured realism for robotic manipulation and other embodied AI applications.
- There was no standardized, comprehensive framework to determine if a video generation model truly qualifies as an effective embodied world model beyond general video generation.

## Method

- **EWMBench** introduces a comprehensive evaluation framework with a unified world initialization and a specialized dataset built on real-world robotic manipulation data (==Agibot-World==).
- It proposes a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol covering ==Visual Scene Consistency== (using ==DINOv2==), ==Motion Correctness== (using fine-tuned ==YOLO-World== with HSD, NDTW, and DYN metrics), and ==Semantic Alignment/Diversity== (using MLLMs and CLIP).
- The framework provides systematic evaluation tools and a curated dataset with diverse task trajectories, enabling rigorous assessment of embodied world models.

## Results

- Domain-adapted models (**EnerVerse_FT**, **LTX_FT**) consistently achieved the highest overall scores, outperforming general open-source and commercial video generators.
- **Motion correctness** emerged as the most challenging evaluation dimension for all models, with scores indicating significant room for improvement across the board.
- EWMBench's automated metrics showed a **strong correlation with human judgments** for embodied tasks, validating its specialized design over general benchmarks like VBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Domain-specific fine-tuning substantially enhances the performance of embodied world models, particularly in motion correctness and task understanding.
> - Current video generation models face significant challenges in generating physically plausible and action-grounded motions for embodied tasks.
> - A multi-dimensional evaluation covering visual scene consistency, motion correctness, and semantic alignment is essential for robustly assessing embodied world models, aligning well with human perception.
