# EWMBench: Evaluating Scene, Motion, and Semantic Quality in Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> EWMBENCH introduces the first world generation benchmark for embodied tasks, providing a multi-dimensional framework to evaluate scene, motion, and semantic quality in embodied world models. It systematically assesses models against criteria vital for robotic applications, demonstrating that domain-adapted models outperform general video generators while highlighting motion correctness as a key area for further development.

## Problem

- Existing general video generation benchmarks primarily focus on perceptual metrics (visual fidelity, language alignment) which are insufficient for evaluating embodied world models (EWMs).
- EWMs require evaluation criteria specific to physical plausibility, action consistency, and structured realism for robotic manipulation and other embodied AI applications.
- There was no standardized, comprehensive framework to determine if a video generation model truly qualifies as an effective embodied world model beyond general video generation.

## Method

- **EWMBench** introduces a comprehensive evaluation framework with a unified world initialization and a specialized dataset built on real-world robotic manipulation data (==Agibot-World==).
- It proposes a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol covering ==Visual Scene Consistency== (using ==DINOv2==), ==Motion Correctness== (using fine-tuned ==YOLO-World== with HSD, NDTW, and DYN metrics), and ==Semantic Alignment/Diversity== (using MLLMs and CLIP).
- The framework provides systematic evaluation tools and a curated dataset with diverse task trajectories, enabling rigorous assessment of embodied world models.

## Results

- Domain-adapted models (**EnerVerse_FT**, **LTX_FT**) consistently achieved the highest overall scores, outperforming general open-source and commercial video generators.
- **Motion correctness** emerged as the most challenging evaluation dimension for all models, with scores indicating significant room for improvement across the board.
- EWMBench's automated metrics showed a **strong correlation with human judgments** for embodied tasks, validating its specialized design over general benchmarks like VBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Domain-specific fine-tuning substantially enhances the performance of embodied world models, particularly in motion correctness and task understanding.
> - Current video generation models face significant challenges in generating physically plausible and action-grounded motions for embodied tasks.
> - A multi-dimensional evaluation covering visual scene consistency, motion correctness, and semantic alignment is essential for robustly assessing embodied world models, aligning well with human perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "EWMBENCH" positions itself at the critical intersection of generative AI, particularly text-to-video synthesis, and embodied AI. Recent advancements in "creative AI," driven by innovations in diffusion models and large datasets, have enabled the generation of high-fidelity images and videos from language instructions. The paper notes the evolution of these text-to-video diffusion models into "embodied world models (EWMs)," which are capable of synthesizing physically plausible scenes and action sequences based on language commands or action policies. This progression is vital for bridging the "vision and action" gap in embodied AI applications, such as robotic manipulation, where generated trajectories must adhere to physical and kinematic constraints.

However, the authors identify a significant void in the existing evaluation landscape. While general video generation benchmarks (e.g., VBench, PhyGenBench, T2V-CompBench) have focused on perceptual metrics like visual fidelity, language alignment, and human preference, these criteria are inherently insufficient for EWMs. Embodied generation tasks demand a unique set of considerations, including:
- **Coherence in embodiment motion:** The generated movements of a robot or agent must be physically realistic and consistent.
- **Plausibility in action execution:** Actions must make sense within the physical environment and achieve task objectives.
- **Structured realism:** Unlike general videos where anything can change, embodied scenarios often require static backgrounds, consistent object configurations, and unchanging embodiment structures (e.g., robot morphology), with only the robot's pose and interactions evolving.

"EWMBENCH" directly addresses this critical evaluation gap. It is proposed as the "first world generation benchmark tailored for embodied tasks," aiming to provide a systematic and comprehensive framework for assessing EWMs based on criteria specifically relevant to their application in physical environments. By highlighting the limitations of current benchmarks and proposing a more suitable evaluation paradigm, this work contributes to a more rigorous and meaningful assessment of progress in embodied AI, steering future research towards physically grounded and action-consistent generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind "EWMBENCH" stems from a fundamental unresolved question in the field of embodied AI: "How can we determine whether a video generation model qualifies as a good embodied world model, beyond merely serving as a general-purpose video generator?" This question underscores the inadequacy of existing evaluation metrics for the unique demands of embodied tasks.

The paper argues that current video generation benchmarks primarily emphasize perceptual quality, which fails to capture critical aspects such as physical plausibility, action consistency, and interaction logic—all paramount for EWMs. For instance, in a robotic manipulation task, a generated video must not only look realistic but also accurately depict the robot's end-effector interacting correctly with objects, maintaining scene integrity, and executing movements that conform to physical laws.

Given this gap, the key objectives of the "EWMBENCH" project are multifaceted:
1. **Formalize EWM Benchmarking:** To establish a clear and standardized setup for evaluating embodied world models, specifying inputs (initial video segment, language instructions) and expected outputs (autoregressively generated future frames).
2. **Propose a Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Protocol:** To design a comprehensive set of criteria that go beyond perceptual metrics, focusing on three core aspects: Visual Scene Consistency, Motion Correctness, and Semantic Alignment and Diversity.
3. **Develop Systematic Evaluation Tools:** To create and open-source the necessary tools and metrics, leveraging advanced AI models like multimodal large language models (MLLMs), trajectory detectors, and fine-tuned visual foundation models, to automate the assessment process.
4. **Curate a Specialized Dataset:** To construct a high-quality, diverse benchmark dataset derived from real-world robotic manipulation data (Agibot-World), specifically designed to challenge EWMs on tasks requiring logical reasoning, action-ordering, and precise manipulation.
5. **Identify Limitations and Guide Future Research:** Through systematic evaluation, to pinpoint the shortcomings of current video generation models when applied to embodied tasks, thereby providing actionable insights and directions for future advancements in physically grounded and action-consistent embodied AI.

In essence, the motivation is to elevate the evaluation of embodied world models from a general visual assessment to a rigorous, task-centric appraisal that truly reflects their utility and safety in real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EWMBENCH is designed as a comprehensive evaluation framework comprising a unified world initialization, a meticulously curated dataset, and a suite of systematic evaluation metrics.

**Unified World Initialization:**
The framework begins by providing generative models with an initial context, including up to four initial scene images (`I`), corresponding language task instructions (`L`), and optionally, an action trajectory (`T`) formatted as a sequence of 6D poses. The generative model (`g_world`) is then tasked with autoregressively producing future video frames. To ensure fair evaluation, raw generated videos (`V`) are normalized (`v_norm`) for resolution (640x480) and frame rate (30 FPS) before metric computation. Task prompts also include specific constraints like "Keep the first-person view of the robot unchanged" and "Keep the first frame of this video unchanged" to enforce consistency.

**Dataset Construction:**
The evaluation dataset is built upon **Agibot-World**, a large-scale real-world robotic manipulation dataset. Ten diverse tasks (e.g., retrieving toast, pouring water, setting cutlery) were selected, covering household and industrial contexts, chosen for their clear operational goals, sequential dependencies, and diverse object interactions (rigid, deformable, articulated).
To ensure motion diversity, 100 video instances were initially sampled per task. Each high-level task was decomposed into 4 to 10 atomic sub-actions, each with a step-level caption, ensuring a one-to-one alignment between video segments, action labels, and linguistic descriptions. Action trajectories of the end-effectors (left and right) were encoded into voxel grids. A pairwise similarity matrix based on 3D Intersection over Union (IoU) was computed between these voxel grids. A greedy algorithm was then employed to select the 10 most diverse trajectories for each task, ensuring the benchmark includes both common and atypical motion patterns.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
EWMBENCH evaluates generated videos across three critical dimensions: Scene, Action Motion, and Semantics, with detailed metric definitions provided in the appendix.

- **A. Scene (Visual Scene Consistency):**
 - **Objective:** To ensure static elements (background, objects, embodiment structure) remain unchanged.
 - **Approach:** Leverages **DINOv2** (fine-tuned on the Agibot-World dataset for embodied scenes) to extract patch-level frame representations. This fine-tuning is crucial as it enables DINOv2 to consistently focus on task-relevant agents and tools, overcoming limitations of general visual models (like VBench's DINO-ViT-B/16 which struggles with foreground-background separation and viewpoint changes).
 - **Metric:** Cosine similarity between patch embeddings of consecutive frames and initial frames. Higher scores indicate stable scene structures and coherent viewpoints.

- **B. Action Motion (Motion Correctness):**
 - **Objective:** To assess the coherence, physical plausibility, and task alignment of the generated embodiment trajectory.
 - **Approach:** Extracts 2D end-effector (EEF) trajectories using a fine-tuned YOLO-World model for per-frame detection and BoT-SORT for temporal association. Models are required to generate three candidate trajectories per task, with the best chosen based on Hausdorff distance.
 - **Metrics (complementary and validated for necessity):**
 - **Symmetric Hausdorff Distance (HSD Consistency):** Measures spatial alignment by calculating the maximum deviation between points on generated and ground truth (GT) trajectories (reported as 1/HSD).
 - **Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (NDTW Consistency):** Captures spatio-temporal alignment and sequencing, ensuring correct order and timing of motions (reported as 1/NDTW).
 - **Dynamic Consistency (DYN):** Evaluates motion dynamics (velocity and acceleration) by computing the Wasserstein distance between predicted and GT distributions of these kinematic properties. An amplitude normalization factor is introduced to prevent numerical amplification from low-amplitude trajectories.

- **C. Semantics (Semantic Alignment and Diversity):**
 - **Objective:** To assess alignment with linguistic instructions and the model's ability to generalize across diverse tasks.
 - **Approach (MLLM Prompt Suite Design):** Utilizes a multi-modal large language model (MLLM) to perform language analysis at three levels:
 - **Global Video Caption Representation:** MLLM summarizes the entire video, and this summary is compared with the raw task instruction using the **BLEU score** to evaluate overall alignment.
 - **Key Steps Description:** MLLM produces a detailed step-by-step description, which is compared with GT step descriptions using **CLIP score**. This evaluates fine-grained task understanding.
 - **Logical Error Punishment:** The MLLM explicitly identifies and penalizes logical errors, commonsense violations, hallucinations, or spatial inconsistencies in the generated video, ensuring realistic and coherent task execution.
 - **Semantic Diversity:** Uses the **CLIP model** to extract global video features, with diversity computed as (1 - similarity), reflecting the model's capacity for varied outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation benchmarked seven video generation models: three open-source (OpenSora 2.0, LTX, COSMOS-7B), two commercial (Kling-1.6, Hailuo I2V-01-live), and two domain-adapted (LTX_FT, EnerVerse). The experiments focused on the Image-Text-to-Video setting, generating three videos per model per episode across 10 tasks (10 GT episodes each), totaling 2,100 videos.

**Evaluation Results (Table 2 & Figure 4):**
- **Overall Performance:** Domain-adapted models, specifically **EnerVerse_FT** (4.7010) and **LTX_FT** (4.5493), consistently outperformed all other categories. This highlights the substantial benefits of domain-specific fine-tuning for embodied tasks, as it significantly enhances task understanding and motion alignment.
- **Dimension-wise Performance:**
 - **Scene Consistency:** All models generally performed well, with scores ranging from 0.79 to 0.94. Domain-adapted models slightly led, indicating good scene stability.
 - **Motion Correctness:** This dimension presented the most significant challenge for all models. Scores were generally low, especially for open-source and commercial models (e.g., OpenSora at 0.3442). Domain-adapted models showed a clear advantage (EnerVerse_FT at 1.6676, LTX_FT at 1.6163), demonstrating better capture of motion dynamics and trajectory alignment, though still indicating room for improvement.
 - **Semantic Alignment:** Models performed relatively well in semantic understanding. EnerVerse_FT and Kling demonstrated particularly strong semantic alignment, reflecting a solid grasp of task logic.
- **Interpretability and Robustness:** Visual examples (Figure 5) confirmed that EWMBENCH's scores align well with human perception of scene and motion accuracy. The benchmark effectively discriminates between good and bad examples, emphasizing the necessity of a multi-dimensional approach, as visually plausible but static videos might score high in scene consistency but fail in motion.

**Human Evaluation (Figure 6A & 6B):**
A human evaluation, where annotators ranked videos from representative models (LTX_FT, Kling, Hailuo, OpenSora), demonstrated a strong correlation between EWMBENCH's automated rankings and human judgments. In contrast, VBench, a popular general video generation benchmark, showed weaker alignment with human perception for embodied tasks, validating EWMBENCH's specialized design.

**Complementarity of Trajectory Metrics (Figure 6C):**
Controlled experiments confirmed that HSD, nDTW, and DYN are indeed complementary:
- **Sequence Reversal:** Only nDTW showed a significant drop, confirming its sensitivity to temporal order.
- **Outlier Insertion:** HSD and DYN declined substantially, proving their effectiveness in detecting spatial inaccuracies and issues with motion integrity.
- **Frame Repetition:** nDTW increased (due to repeated alignment), while DYN decreased (due to lack of motion smoothness), demonstrating their unique sensitivities.
This analysis reinforces that all three metrics are essential for a comprehensive evaluation of trajectory quality in embodied contexts.

**Characteristics of SOTA Models (Further Analysis):**
- **Domain-adapted Models:** While leading in overall performance, they sometimes exhibited "empty grasping" behaviors, revealing limitations in fine-grained action grounding despite strong semantic and dynamic understanding.
- **Kling:** Demonstrated robust capabilities and the best overall performance among general commercial/open-source models.
- **Hailuo:** Performed reasonably in zero-shot scenarios, but its generated scenes often appeared "cartoon-like," reducing visual realism.
- **COSMOS and LTX:** Showed a bias towards human hand representations instead of robotic arms, highlighting semantic adaptation failures. LTX also struggled with abrupt scene transitions and inconsistent task execution, often generating static action sequences. COSMOS had issues with maintaining consistent viewpoints.
- **OpenSora:** Showed partial understanding but suffered from "jittery robotic arm movements" and a tendency to generate static videos, indicating significant challenges in motion control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EWMBENCH marks a pivotal contribution to the field of embodied AI, addressing a critical and previously underserved area of evaluation. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Pioneering Embodied World Model Evaluation:** EWMBENCH is the first dedicated benchmark suite specifically tailored for evaluating embodied world models. This fills a crucial gap left by existing general video generation benchmarks, which fail to capture the unique requirements of physically grounded and action-consistent behaviors in robotic and embodied contexts.
2. **Comprehensive and Human-Aligned Metrics:** The multi-dimensional evaluation approach, encompassing visual scene consistency, motion correctness, and semantic alignment, provides a holistic assessment. The empirical validation through human evaluation confirms that EWMBENCH's automated metrics align more closely with human judgments for embodied tasks than general benchmarks, ensuring meaningful and reliable performance measurement.
3. **Guidance for Model Development:** By systematically identifying the strengths and weaknesses of current state-of-the-art video generation models in embodied scenarios, EWMBENCH offers invaluable insights. For instance, the consistent struggle of models with "motion correctness" and "action grounding" (e.g., empty grasping, jittery movements) highlights key areas for future research and development. This will guide researchers and engineers in designing more effective architectures and training strategies for EWMs.
4. **High-Quality, Real-World Dataset:** The curation of a diverse, real-world robotic manipulation dataset (based on Agibot-World) with fine-grained task decomposition and diverse motion patterns is a significant asset. This dataset provides a challenging yet realistic testing ground, pushing models beyond synthetic environments towards real-world applicability.
5. **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the dataset and evaluation tools (https://github.com/AgibotTech/EWMBench) is a major catalyst for collaborative research. It democratizes access to a robust evaluation framework, enabling wider participation in EWM development and fostering rapid iteration and progress within the community.
6. **Advancing Embodied AI Safety and Reliability:** By emphasizing physical plausibility, action consistency, and logical error detection, EWMBENCH implicitly contributes to the development of safer and more reliable embodied AI systems. Models that perform well on this benchmark are more likely to generate behaviors that are not only visually convincing but also physically executable and free from commonsense violations, which is paramount for real-world robotic deployment.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors candidly acknowledge several limitations, which also serve as clear directions for future research:
- **Limited Trajectory Scope:** The current method primarily focuses on the end-effector's trajectory. Future work will expand to incorporate the state and configuration of the entire robotic arm, which is crucial for comprehensive kinematic and dynamic evaluation.
- **Fixed Viewpoint Scenes:** The current evaluation is conducted in scenes with fixed viewpoints. Future research will explore flexible viewpoints, such as dynamic camera setups, to assess models' robustness to varying observational perspectives.
- **Task Domain Expansion:** The current scope is limited to robotic manipulation tasks. Future work aims to extend the benchmark to a broader range of embodied tasks, including navigation and mobile manipulation, to provide a more comprehensive assessment of general embodied world modeling capabilities.

In conclusion, EWMBENCH is a timely and essential contribution that provides the much-needed infrastructure for rigorously evaluating the next generation of embodied world models. By establishing a robust, multi-dimensional, and human-aligned benchmark, it is set to accelerate progress towards truly capable and physically grounded embodied AI systems.
