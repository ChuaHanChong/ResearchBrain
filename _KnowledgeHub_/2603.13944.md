# ToMPC: Task-oriented Model Predictive Control via ADMM for Safe Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel Task-oriented Model Predictive Control (ToMPC) framework integrates full-body dynamics, whole-body collision avoidance, and compliant interaction for robotic manipulation, utilizing ADMM to decompose the optimization problem for real-time performance and strict safety constraint satisfaction. The method demonstrated reduced computation times by an order of magnitude and achieved superior task execution quality compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Integrating versatile motion, compliant physical interaction, and comprehensive collision avoidance into a single control framework for robots operating in complex and obstructed environments.
- Achieving real-time performance for dynamics-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) while rigorously satisfying hard safety inequality constraints (e.g., obstacle avoidance).
- Existing efficient nonlinear solvers like DDP struggle with hard inequality constraints, and methods using barrier functions may compromise strict constraint satisfaction.

## Method

- A unified Task-oriented ==Model Predictive Control== (**ToMPC**) framework incorporating full-body dynamics, whole-body collision avoidance, and compliant environment interaction.
- A task-oriented avoidance strategy that utilizes kinematic redundancy to proactively maneuver robot links, enhancing manipulation efficiency in obstructed spaces.
- The Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) decomposes the complex optimization into a DDP-solved dynamics subproblem and parallel QP-solved constraint subproblems for real-time performance and strict constraint satisfaction.

## Results

- ToMPC rigorously satisfied hard safety constraints (e.g., obstacle avoidance) and demonstrated an order of magnitude faster computation times (e.g., 0.0009s for T=10) compared to benchmark solvers.
- In hardware, the task-oriented avoidance strategy enabled robots to leverage kinematic redundancy for proactive obstacle navigation, resulting in enhanced manipulation efficiency and lower position errors (e.g., 0.0201m mean error).
- The framework achieved accurate and smooth force tracking during compliant environment interaction and successfully performed hybrid motion-force tasks while avoiding obstacles, expanding the robot's effective workspace.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ADMM-based decomposition effectively separates the complex MPC problem into dynamic (DDP) and constraint (QP) subproblems, enabling real-time control with strict satisfaction of hard inequality constraints.
> - Task-oriented avoidance, utilizing kinematic redundancy, allows robots to proactively navigate obstacles without compromising task performance, extending the effective manipulation workspace.
> - Incorporating full-body dynamics and an impedance-based interaction model within an MPC framework leads to smoother and more accurate force tracking during compliant robot-environment interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The increasing demand for robotic manipulators in open and complex workspaces necessitates advanced control strategies that can ensure both versatile motion and safe interaction with the environment. This work addresses challenges within the field of robotic manipulation control, particularly focusing on the integration of collision avoidance and compliant interaction capabilities while maintaining real-time performance.

Traditional motion planning approaches, such as random sampling-based methods, are proficient at generating collision-free paths but often do not effectively incorporate task execution requirements or physical interaction dynamics. Optimization-based methods, in contrast, offer a framework for integrating diverse objectives and complex constraints. Among these, Model Predictive Control (MPC) is noted for its ability to perform online replanning, which is beneficial for adaptability in dynamic and uncertain environments.

Existing MPC applications in safe robotic manipulation have explored various aspects: including velocity damping for singularity avoidance, extended prediction horizons to manage velocity constraints, and distance constraints for obstacle avoidance. However, many MPC implementations face challenges in handling force-based manipulation when relying solely on kinematic information. While dynamics-based formulations can address force control, they often incur increased computational burden, making real-time implementation difficult. Few studies have successfully combined obstacle avoidance with force application, especially while considering the quality of manipulation.

Computational efficiency is a persistent concern in MPC for robotics. Common acceleration techniques include linearizing the system model for Quadratic Programming (QP) solvers, parallel processing, and multiple-shooting methods. Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP) and its variants have gained traction for solving nonlinear optimization problems due to their efficiency in handling approximate linear-quadratic regulator problems over a prediction horizon. A known limitation of DDP, however, is its difficulty in directly incorporating hard inequality constraints. Solutions for this include active-set methods, quadratic barrier functions, or Augmented Lagrangian (AL) techniques. The Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM), which emerged from AL, is particularly relevant due to its decomposability, allowing large-scale optimization problems to be broken down into smaller, simpler subproblems. While ADMM has been applied in various robotics contexts (autonomous cars, drones, contact-aware manipulation), its exploitation for robotic manipulation tasks that couple full-system dynamics with comprehensive collision avoidance has been less explored.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by proposing a unified MPC framework that integrates full-body dynamics for compliant contact, whole-body collision avoidance, and a task-oriented avoidance strategy, all while ensuring real-time execution and strict adherence to safety constraints. The novel use of ADMM in this context facilitates the decomposition of the complex, constrained nonlinear optimization problem into subproblems suitable for DDP and QP, thereby leveraging their respective computational strengths.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a task-oriented Model Predictive Control (ToMPC) framework that enables safe and efficient robotic manipulation within open and potentially obstructed workspaces. The motivation for this development stems from several challenges identified in contemporary robotic control:

- **Versatility in Open Workspaces:** Modern robotic manipulators are increasingly required to perform complex tasks beyond simple pick-and-place operations, often in environments shared with obstacles and requiring physical interaction. Existing methods frequently struggle to provide a unified solution for these diverse scenarios.
- **Integration of Safety and Interaction:** There is a need for robotic control systems that can simultaneously ensure safety (e.g., collision avoidance) and facilitate controllable physical interactions with the environment (e.g., applying desired forces). Current approaches often treat these aspects separately or lack robustness in their combined execution.
- **Enhancing Manipulation Efficiency in Obstructed Environments:** When robots operate near obstacles, avoidance strategies can sometimes compromise the primary manipulation task by restricting the robot's movement excessively. The motivation is to develop a method that allows robots to navigate obstructed spaces while preserving, and potentially enhancing, the efficiency and range of manipulation tasks by intelligently utilizing kinematic redundancy.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-time Control:** Integrating full-body dynamics, complex constraints, and a long prediction horizon typically leads to computationally intensive optimization problems. The demand for real-time control in dynamic environments necessitates efficient solvers that can handle nonlinearity and hard constraints without significant delays. Existing solvers, such as DDP, are effective for nonlinear problems but often struggle with hard inequality constraints, while methods using barrier functions may compromise strict constraint satisfaction.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth three key objectives:
1. **Develop a Unified MPC Framework:** To propose a single MPC framework capable of online planning for both collision-free motion and environment interaction tasks. This framework is intended to operate in workspaces with obstacles and leverage full-body dynamics to enable compliant contact without requiring additional force sensors.
2. **Introduce Task-oriented Obstacle Avoidance:** To define an avoidance strategy that uses the robot's kinematic redundancy to achieve a balance between obstacle avoidance and task execution. This strategy aims to increase the effective manipulation range of the end-effector by enabling proactive link movements.
3. **Implement ToMPC using Distributed Optimization (ADMM):** To utilize the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) to solve the resulting complex constrained nonlinear optimization problem. The ADMM approach is designed to decompose the problem into subproblems (solved by DDP for dynamics and QP for constraints) to achieve real-time performance and ensure strict satisfaction of safety-related hard constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Task-oriented Model Predictive Control (ToMPC) framework integrates collision-free motion and robot-environment interaction within a unified MPC structure, solved efficiently using ADMM.

**A. Robot and Interaction Modeling**
The system dynamics are modeled using the rigid body dynamics of redundant robotic manipulators:
$M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + g(q) = \tau + \tau_{ext}$
where $q$ is the joint position, $M$ is the inertia matrix, $C$ represents Coriolis and centrifugal forces, $g$ is the gravity vector, $\tau$ is the commanded joint torque, and $\tau_{ext}$ is the external torque.
For interaction tasks, $\tau_{ext}$ is defined as $J_c^T F$, where $F$ is the external wrench and $J_c$ is the Jacobian in the contact frame. An impedance control-inspired interaction model defines the external wrench $F$:
$F(q, \dot{q}) = K_{env}(X_{ref} \ominus X) + D_{env}V$
where $K_{env}$ and $D_{env}$ are stiffness and damping matrices, $X_{ref}$ is the reference end-effector pose, $X(q)$ is the current pose, and $V$ is the end-effector twist. This external wrench is treated as a state, allowing the system to model interaction behavior.
The overall system is then formulated as a state-space model $x_{t+1} = f(x_t, u_t)$, where $x = (q, \dot{q})$ are states and $u = \tau$ are control inputs. The nonlinear forward dynamics are leveraged by the DDP algorithm, requiring analytical derivatives of the dynamics.

**B. Safety Constraints**
To ensure safe operation, several constraints are incorporated:
- **Whole-Body Obstacle Avoidance:** This is achieved by enforcing a minimum allowable distance $d_{th1}$ between robot links and obstacles. The shortest distance $d_i(q)$ for each collision pair is calculated, and a linear approximation $d_i(q_t) \approx d_i(q) + \hat{n}_i^T J_{iA}(q)(q_t - q)$ is used for subsequent states within each MPC cycle after an initial computationally expensive distance query. The hard constraint is $d(q_t) \geq d_{th1}$ for all time steps.
- **Joint and Control Limits:** Standard inequality constraints are applied to joint angles ($x_{min} \leq x_t \leq x_{max}$), joint velocities, and control inputs ($u_{min} \leq u_t \leq u_{max}$).

**C. Cost Function Design**
The MPC objective function comprises stage costs $\ell_t(x_t, u_t)$ and a terminal cost $\ell_T(x_T)$:
$\ell_t(x_t, u_t) = \ell_m(x_t) + \ell_f(x_t) + \ell_{rep}(x_t) + \ell_u(x_t, u_t)$
$\ell_T(x_T) = \ell_m(x_T) + \ell_f(x_T)$

- **Motion and Force Tracking ($\ell_m, \ell_f$):**
 - Motion tracking penalizes deviation from a reference end-effector pose and regularizes joint velocities: $\ell_m(x_t) = ||X_{ref} \ominus X(q_t)||^2_{Q_m} + ||\dot{q}_t||^2_{Q_s}$.
 - Force tracking penalizes deviation from a reference wrench: $\ell_f(x_t) = ||F_{ref} - F(q_t, \dot{q}_t)||^2_{Q_f}$.
 - Weights $Q_m$ and $Q_f$ are complementary, allowing each Cartesian direction to be either motion- or force-controlled.

- **Task-oriented Avoidance ($\ell_{rep}$):** This strategy introduces a "repulsive region" around obstacles (distance threshold $d_{th2} > d_{th1}$). When a link enters this region, a virtual repulsive velocity $v_{rep}$ is generated. To prevent this from interfering with the main task, kinematic redundancy is leveraged. The cost term penalizes deviations from applying this repulsive motion in the null space of the task Jacobian: $\ell_{rep}(x_t) = ||N_{task}(\dot{q}_t - \bar{J}_{iA}v_{i+})||^2_{Q_{rep}}$, where $N_{task}$ is the null space projection matrix. Additionally, a goal relaxation term $\xi(d_i)$ is introduced (exponentially decreasing from 1 to 0 as distance $d_i$ approaches $d_{th1}$ from $d_{th2}$), which dynamically reduces the weights $Q_m$ and $Q_f$ for motion and force tracking as the robot gets closer to an obstacle, balancing avoidance and task objectives.

- **Control Regularization ($\ell_u$):** This term penalizes the control input deviation from gravity compensation: $\ell_u(x_t, u_t) = ||u_t - g(q_t)||^2_R$.

**D. Distributed Optimization via ADMM**
The complex constrained nonlinear MPC problem is reformulated for ADMM. It is expressed as a general optimization problem with two independent decision variables, $y$ (states and inputs, subject to dynamic equality constraints and some limits) and $z$ (projection of $y$, subject to inequality constraints), linked by an equality constraint $Gy - z = 0$. The augmented Lagrangian function is then minimized iteratively.

- **Step 1: Task Execution (y-update):** This subproblem minimizes the augmented Lagrangian with respect to $y$, keeping $z$ and $\lambda$ fixed. It primarily involves the motion, force, and control regularization costs, along with the dynamic equality constraints. This nonlinear problem is efficiently solved using the Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP) algorithm.
- **Step 2: Safety Guarantee (z-update):** This subproblem minimizes the augmented Lagrangian with respect to $z$, given fixed $y$ and $\lambda$. It incorporates the task-oriented avoidance cost and all hard inequality constraints (joint/velocity limits, torque limits, and whole-body obstacle avoidance). This problem can be decomposed into $T$ smaller, independent Quadratic Programming (QP) problems that are solved in parallel, leveraging linear approximation for distances.
- **Step 3: Lagrangian Multiplier Update ($\lambda$-update):** The Lagrangian multipliers $\lambda$ are updated based on the consistency error between $Gy$ and $z$: $\lambda^{(k+1)} = \lambda^{(k)} + \rho(Gy^{(k+1)} - z^{(k+1)})$.

The ADMM iterations continue until a desired convergence residual is achieved. The solution from the previous MPC cycle is used as a warm start to accelerate convergence. A low-level Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller ($ \tau_{des} = \tau_{mpc} + K_p(q_{des} - q) + K_d(\dot{q}_{des} - \dot{q})$) is used to execute the commands from ToMPC, with the feedforward term $\tau_{mpc}$ being the primary contributor to the control torque.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ToMPC framework was validated through simulations and hardware experiments on a Franka Emika Panda robot.

**A. Scenario #1: Obstacle Avoidance (Simulation)**
- **Constraint Satisfaction:** The ADMM-based ToMPC strictly adhered to the minimum distance threshold ($d_{th1} = 0.02$ m) for obstacle avoidance. In contrast, a benchmark FDDP solver, which used quadratic barrier functions for obstacle avoidance, exhibited minimum distances below the threshold, indicating safety constraint violations. This demonstrates ADMM's effectiveness in enforcing hard inequality constraints.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The ToMPC method exhibited lower computation times compared to the FDDP benchmark, even with longer prediction horizons. For a prediction horizon $T=10$, ADMM's average time was 0.0009 s versus FDDP's 0.0091 s. For $T=50$, ADMM's average time was 0.0043 s. This efficiency is attributed to the distributed nature of ADMM, which separates inequality constraints into parallel QP problems, simplifying the DDP step.

**B. Scenario #2: Task-oriented Avoidance (Hardware Experiment)**
- **Enhanced Manipulation Efficiency:** When manipulating a block along a Lemniscate of Bernoulli trajectory in the presence of a ball obstacle, the ToMPC enabled the robot to proactively twist its body using kinematic redundancy. This maneuver optimized the goal-reaching task, resulting in smaller Cartesian tracking errors compared to the benchmark "oaMPC" (obstacle-avoidance MPC) which did not feature task-oriented avoidance.
- **Safety Compliance:** Both ToMPC and oaMPC maintained robot safety by ensuring minimum distances above the threshold. However, ToMPC achieved this while preserving better task execution quality.
- **Control Authority:** Analysis of joint torques demonstrated that the MPC-generated feedforward torque ($\tau_{mpc}$) constituted the majority of the total desired torque, indicating that the model-based control was the primary driver of robot behavior.

**C. Scenario #3: Environment Interaction (Hardware Experiment)**
- **Accurate Force Tracking:** The ToMPC framework successfully tracked sinusoidal force reference trajectories ($f_{ref_z}$) at various frequencies (0.4, 0.8, 1.5 Hz) when interacting with an environment. The measured contact force closely followed the commanded references.
- **Smooth Interaction:** Compared to an instantaneous inverse dynamics controller, the ToMPC produced smoother force trajectories with reduced noise during contact with a rigid surface. This attributed to the long-horizon dynamics optimization property of DDP, which predicts future behavior and computes minimal control inputs, thereby filtering out noise.

**D. Scenario #4: Hybrid Motion-Force Task (Hardware Experiment)**
- **Combined Capabilities:** The robot successfully performed a hybrid motion-force task (wiping a whiteboard along a circular trajectory with a constant contact force) while avoiding a ball obstacle. Both ToMPC and oaMPC maintained collision-free motion and managed contact dynamics.
- **Increased Workspace Exploration:** The ToMPC, by leveraging its proactive link avoidance, allowed the robot to explore a larger portion of the Cartesian workspace. This resulted in successfully reaching two marker points on the whiteboard, whereas the benchmark oaMPC was only capable of cleaning one marker, demonstrating enhanced manipulation efficiency in cluttered environments.
- **Performance Trade-off:** Quantitatively, ToMPC achieved a lower mean position error norm (0.0201 m vs 0.0303 m for oaMPC) and RMS position error (0.0236 m vs 0.0355 m). The force tracking error for ToMPC was slightly higher (mean 2.0335 N vs 1.0505 N for oaMPC), but remained within an acceptable range, indicating a beneficial trade-off between motion accuracy and force control for overall task performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework that addresses several challenges in advanced robotic manipulation, offering a unified and computationally efficient solution for complex tasks in dynamic and cluttered environments.

The **significance** of this work stems from several key contributions:
- **Unified Control Paradigm:** The ToMPC framework unifies collision-free motion planning, compliant environment interaction, and hybrid motion-force tasks within a single MPC formulation. This integration is crucial for robots operating in unstructured or semi-structured workspaces where diverse capabilities are required.
- **Real-time Performance with Strict Constraint Satisfaction:** By employing the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM), the proposed method effectively decomposes a complex, nonlinear, and constrained optimization problem into subproblems suitable for Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP) and Quadratic Programming (QP). This distributed optimization approach allows for real-time planning while rigorously enforcing hard inequality safety constraints (e.g., obstacle avoidance and joint limits), which is a notable improvement over methods relying solely on barrier functions that may lead to constraint violations.
- **Enhanced Manipulation Efficiency via Task-oriented Avoidance:** The introduction of a task-oriented avoidance strategy, which leverages kinematic redundancy, enables the robot to proactively maneuver its links to avoid obstacles without unduly compromising the primary manipulation task. This capability improves the effective manipulation range of the end-effector in obstructed environments.
- **Robust Force Control:** The framework incorporates full-body dynamics and an impedance-based interaction model, enabling compliant contact tasks. The long-horizon optimization provided by MPC, particularly through DDP, contributes to smoother force trajectories and reduced noise compared to instantaneous control methods.

The **potential impact** of this research is substantial, particularly for advancing the capabilities and deployment of robotic manipulators:
- **Increased Autonomy and Safety:** The ability to simultaneously handle complex motion, force interaction, and strict collision avoidance in real-time can significantly enhance the autonomy and safety of robots in various applications, reducing the risk of damage to the robot or its surroundings.
- **Wider Application Scope:** This framework can facilitate the deployment of robots in more complex industrial scenarios (e.g., flexible assembly, material handling in confined spaces), service robotics (e.g., domestic tasks, caregiving), and human-robot collaboration where dynamic obstacles and compliant physical interactions are common.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** The ADMM-based decomposition strategy provides a template for tackling other complex robotic control problems that involve multiple objectives and diverse constraints. The work also explicitly identifies future research directions, such as learning-based methods for initial trajectory generation and automatic tuning of cost weights, which could further improve the robustness and practical applicability of such advanced control systems.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling more natural and compliant physical interactions, alongside robust obstacle avoidance, the research contributes to the development of robots that can operate more effectively and safely alongside human co-workers.
