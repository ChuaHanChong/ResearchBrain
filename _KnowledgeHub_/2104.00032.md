# Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks for Interpretable Classifications

> [!summary] Summary
> Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets) are introduced as a family of neural networks designed for both high classification performance and inherent interpretability, generating faithful contribution maps that highlight discriminative input patterns. These models demonstrate superior performance over post-hoc attribution methods on localization and pixel removal metrics across datasets like CIFAR-10 and TinyImagenet.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks frequently operate as 'black boxes,' hindering trust and adoption in critical applications due to their opaque decision-making processes.
- Existing post-hoc explanation methods for neural networks often provide unfaithful approximations of the model's true internal computations and may fail basic sanity checks.
- Developing inherently interpretable models typically involves a trade-off, where interpretability comes at the cost of reduced classification performance compared to state-of-the-art black-box models.

## Method

- The paper introduces **Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets)**, a new architecture built upon ==Dynamic Alignment Units (DAUs)== that possess '==dynamic linearity==' and '==alignment maximization==' properties.
- **DAUs** are designed to allow direct decomposition of network output into faithful input contributions and encourage their internal weight vectors to align with discriminative input patterns during optimization.
- The training procedure incorporates ==temperature scaling== and ==L1 regularization== to explicitly enhance interpretability by increasing 'alignment pressure' within DAUs and promoting sparsity in the resulting contribution maps.

## Results

- CoDA-Nets achieved competitive classification accuracies on CIFAR-10 (**96.5%**) and TinyImagenet (**58.4%**), demonstrating performance comparable to VGG and ResNet architectures.
- The inherent contribution maps generated by CoDA-Nets consistently outperformed various post-hoc attribution methods on quantitative interpretability metrics, including localization (pointing game) and pixel removal strategies.
- Temperature scaling was shown to consistently improve the interpretability of CoDA-Nets, leading to higher quality contribution maps, with a controllable trade-off against minimal reductions in classification accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Neural network architectures can be designed to be inherently interpretable, providing faithful explanations directly from their internal computations, eliminating the need for post-hoc approximations.
> - The 'dynamic linearity' of CoDA-Nets enables a direct and faithful decomposition of the network's output into input contributions, offering a more reliable explanation than implicit linearities in traditional models.
> - By incorporating 'alignment maximization' into Dynamic Alignment Units, the network is trained to ensure that its internal explanations highlight truly discriminative and task-relevant features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in deep learning: the interpretability of neural networks. While neural networks have achieved high performance across a wide array of tasks, their complex, non-linear nature often renders their decision-making processes opaque. This lack of transparency is a significant barrier to their adoption in critical applications where trustworthiness, accountability, and the ability to understand and diagnose failures are paramount.

The current research landscape for interpretability broadly comprises two main categories:
1. **Post-hoc explanation methods:** These techniques attempt to explain the predictions of a pre-trained "black-box" model after it has made a decision. Examples include model-agnostic methods like LIME and RISE, which analyze model behavior under different inputs, and model-specific methods like Grad, Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients, and DeepLIFT, which leverage internal computations (e.g., gradients). While these methods are widely used, their faithfulness to the model's true internal computations can be questionable, and some have been shown to fail basic "sanity checks."
2. **Inherently interpretable models:** This category focuses on designing neural network architectures that are interpretable by design, meaning their internal computations directly provide insights into their predictions. Examples include prototype-based neural networks, BagNets, and Self-Explaining Neural Networks (SENNs). These models aim to integrate interpretability into the network's architecture and training objective, rather than approximating explanations afterward. The proposed Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets) fall into this latter category, aiming to advance the development of neural networks that are both performant and inherently interpretable, thus offering a different approach compared to most existing attribution methods. The work positions itself as a new direction for building architectures with high modeling capacity and inherent interpretability.

The paper differentiates CoDA-Nets from existing approaches by combining "dynamic linearity"—a property shared by many ReLU-based networks—with a structural bias towards alignment between internal computations and discriminative input patterns. This aims to overcome limitations observed in methods like Input×Gradient on piece-wise linear models, which often yield unsatisfactory explanations despite their inherent linearity. The authors also draw architectural comparisons to dynamic local filtering layers, attention networks, low-rank matrix approximations, and capsule networks, highlighting both similarities and key distinctions of their approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a new family of neural network models, called Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets), that simultaneously achieve high classification performance and a high degree of *inherent interpretability*. The authors are motivated by the challenges associated with understanding the decision-making processes of complex neural networks and the limitations of existing post-hoc explanation methods.

The key motivations and objectives can be detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the "Black-Box" Problem:** Neural networks, despite their success, are often considered "black boxes," making it difficult to understand *why* they make a particular prediction. This opacity hinders trust and application in sensitive domains. The work aims to develop models that provide transparent and faithful explanations directly from their internal computations.
- **Improving Explanation Faithfulness:** Many existing interpretability methods provide approximations of a model's behavior, and some have been shown to be unfaithful to the underlying model. The authors seek to develop an architecture where the "explanation" (i.e., contribution maps) is an intrinsic and faithful projection of the model's internal computations.
- **Combining Performance and Interpretability:** A common challenge in interpretable AI is that often, models designed for interpretability may sacrifice some level of performance compared to state-of-the-art black-box models. A key objective of CoDA-Nets is to demonstrate that high classification accuracy can be achieved concurrently with inherent interpretability.
- **Leveraging Dynamic Linearity for Decomposability:** The paper notes that many modern neural networks (e.g., ReLU-based networks) implicitly model their output as a linear transformation of the input, making them piece-wise linear. However, the resulting input contributions from these implicit linear transformations often do not reflect the internal computations well. The CoDA-Nets explicitly design their architecture to be "dynamic linear," enabling direct decomposition of the output into individual input contributions, similar to linear regression models.
- **Enabling "Optimized for Interpretability" Explanations:** Beyond simple linearity, the core motivation is to ensure that these inherent contribution maps are actually *meaningful*. This is achieved by introducing a structural bias within the network's building blocks (Dynamic Alignment Units) that encourages their weight vectors to align with *discriminative* and *task-relevant* patterns in their inputs during optimization. This alignment property aims to ensure that the generated explanations highlight the most important features used by the model for its predictions.
- **Introducing Novel Building Blocks:** The paper's specific contributions include the introduction of Dynamic Alignment Units (DAUs) as fundamental components that exhibit dynamic linearity and alignment maximization properties. The objective is to demonstrate that networks constructed from these DAUs, termed CoDA-Nets, inherit these desirable properties, leading to inherently interpretable classification.
- **Developing Principled Interpretability Enhancement Mechanisms:** The authors also aim to show that interpretability can be explicitly promoted through modifications to the training procedure, such as "temperature scaling" of the final output, which increases the "alignment pressure" within the DAUs, leading to higher quality contribution maps.

In essence, the overarching objective is to move beyond post-hoc explanations by designing a new class of neural networks that inherently explain their classifications through faithful, high-quality contribution maps that reflect discriminative input patterns, while maintaining competitive predictive performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology lies in the introduction of a new neural network architecture family: Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets), built upon novel Dynamic Alignment Units (DAUs). The approach is structured around three key properties of DAUs and their inheritance in CoDA-Nets: Dynamic linearity (P1), Alignment maximization (P2), and Inheritance (P3).

#### 3.1. Dynamic Alignment Units (DAUs)

DAUs are the fundamental building blocks. A single DAU takes an input vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and computes an output $o$ as:
$DAU(\mathbf{x}) = g(A\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})^T \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x})^T \mathbf{x}$
where:
- $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$ are trainable transformation matrices. The use of $\mathbf{AB}$ allows for controlling the maximum rank $r$ of the transformation and reducing parameters, with $r$ referred to as the rank of the DAU.
- $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is a trainable bias vector.
- $g(\mathbf{u}) = \alpha(||\mathbf{u}||)\mathbf{u}$ is a non-linear scaling function. Two specific choices are explored:
 - **L2:** $g(\mathbf{u}) = \frac{\mathbf{u}}{||\mathbf{u}||_2}$ (rescales to unit norm)
 - **SQ (Squashing function):** $g(\mathbf{u}) = \frac{\mathbf{u}}{||\mathbf{u}||_2} \times \frac{||\mathbf{u}||_2^2}{1+||\mathbf{u}||_2^2}$ (from Capsule Networks)

The DAUs possess two critical properties:
- **P1: Dynamic linearity:** The output $o$ is an input-dependent linear transformation of $\mathbf{x}$. This implies that $o$ can be decomposed into contributions from individual input dimensions, given by $\mathbf{w}_j(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{x}_j$.
- **P2: Alignment maximization:** Due to the norm-constrained nature of $\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x})$ (i.e., $||\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x})|| \leq 1$), the output of a DAU is upper-bounded by $||\mathbf{x}||$. Maximizing the average output of a DAU over a set of inputs forces alignment between the input $\mathbf{x}$ and the dynamic weight vector $\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x})$. This effectively causes the DAU to encode the most frequent patterns in its inputs as eigenvectors in the linear transform $\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$, akin to a low-rank matrix approximation or principal component analysis (PCA), but optimized for angular reconstruction.

#### 3.2. DAUs for Classification

For classification, multiple DAUs are applied in parallel to produce an output vector $\hat{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{x}) = [DAU_1(\mathbf{x})..., DAU_k(\mathbf{x})]$. This can be written as a linear transformation $\hat{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{x}$, where each row of $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times d}$ is a DAU's weight vector. The network is trained using a binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss on the sigmoid-activated outputs. This loss function, by maximizing the output for the correct class and minimizing for incorrect classes, implicitly leverages the alignment maximization property (P2). The contribution of each input dimension $j$ to the output for class $l$ is then computed as the element-wise product: $s_l(\mathbf{x}_i) = \mathbf{w}_l(\mathbf{x}_i) \odot \mathbf{x}_i$.

#### 3.3. Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets)

To overcome the limited modeling capacity of a single DAU layer, CoDA-Nets stack multiple convolutional DAU layers. A convolutional DAU layer applies dynamic filters to different spatial patches of the input.
- **P3: Inheritance:** CoDA-Nets are designed such that they inherit both dynamic linearity (P1) and alignment maximization (P2) across all layers. If $\mathbf{a}_{l-1}$ is the input to layer $l$, the output $\mathbf{a}_l = \mathbf{W}_l(\mathbf{a}_{l-1})\mathbf{a}_{l-1}$. For an entire network of $L$ layers, the output $\mathbf{a}_L$ can be expressed as a linear transformation of the initial input $\mathbf{a}_0=\mathbf{x}$: $\mathbf{a}_L = \mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{x}$, where $\mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}(\mathbf{x})$ is a product of all dynamic layer-specific matrices. This "global matrix" allows for linear decomposition of the final output into input contributions: $s_j(\mathbf{x}_i) = [\mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}(\mathbf{x}_i)]^T_j \odot \mathbf{x}_i$.

#### 3.4. Temperature Scaling and Regularization

The authors identify that standard BCE loss can lead to saturation, reducing the "alignment pressure" within DAUs once high confidence is achieved. To mitigate this, they introduce **temperature scaling ($T$)** to the network logits: $\hat{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{x}) = T^{-1}\mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{x}$. Lowering the effective upper bound on network output by increasing $T$ forces DAUs to accumulate more signal, thereby enhancing the alignment of weights with discriminative patterns (P2) and improving interpretability.
Additionally, an L1-regularization term is added to the loss function, applied to the absolute values of the entries in the global transformation matrix $\mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}(\mathbf{x})$. This encourages sparsity in the signal alignments, potentially leading to sharper contribution maps. The overall loss function is:
$L(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{y}_i) = BCE(\sigma(T^{-1}\mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}(\mathbf{x}_i)\mathbf{x}_i + \mathbf{b}_0) , \mathbf{y}_i) + \lambda\langle|\mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}(\mathbf{x}_i)|\rangle$

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Shared Matrix B:** The matrix $\mathbf{B}$ is shared across all DAUs within a given layer to improve parameter efficiency and fix the maximum rank of each DAU.
- **Input Encoding:** Inputs are augmented with their negative image (e.g., for RGB images, $[r, g, b, 1-r, 1-g, 1-b]$) to mitigate a bias towards pixels with high RGB values.
- **Architecture:** CoDA-Nets typically consist of 9 convolutional DAU layers followed by a global sum-pooling layer (which is also a linear operation). They do not employ standard techniques like residual connections, dropout, or batch normalization. Models of various sizes (S, M, L, XL) are used depending on the dataset.

#### 3.6. Evaluation

The models are evaluated on CIFAR-10 and TinyImagenet for classification accuracy. Interpretability is assessed both qualitatively (visual inspection of contribution maps on Imagenet-100) and quantitatively using:
1. **Localization Metric (Adapted Pointing Game):** Measures how well positive attribution aligns with the correct object in multi-image grids.
2. **Pixel Removal Strategy:** Analyzes how the model's target class probability changes when pixels are removed in order of least or most importance as determined by the attribution method.

These evaluations compare CoDA-Net's inherent contribution maps against a range of popular post-hoc attribution methods (Grad, IxG, DeepLIFT, IntG, GradCam, LIME, RISE, Occlusion).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the performance and interpretability of Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets).

#### 4.1. Classification Performance

- CoDA-Nets achieve competitive classification accuracies on standard datasets, performing on par with models from the VGG and ResNet families. For instance, on CIFAR-10, an M-CoDA-SQ model with data augmentation achieved 96.5% accuracy, comparable to WideResNet-28-2 (95.8% with augmentation). On TinyImagenet, an XL-CoDA-SQ model achieved 58.4% with augmentation, comparable to ResNet-110 (56.6%).
- The performance of CoDA-Nets was found to be robust across different non-linearities (L2 vs. SQ), temperature (T) values, and regularization strengths ($\lambda$). The SQ non-linearity generally performed better than L2, attributed to its property of avoiding up-scaling vectors with low norms.
- Regarding efficiency, CoDA-Nets show comparable inference times to typical ResNets but have significantly longer training times in their current, non-highly-optimized implementations.

#### 4.2. Interpretability – Qualitative Analysis

- **High Visual Quality and Detail:** The model-inherent contribution maps generated by CoDA-Nets exhibit a high degree of visual quality and detail (Figure 5, Figure A.1, A.2). They effectively highlight discriminative patterns relevant to the predicted class, distinguishing relevant features from background noise. For example, for a "goldfinch" prediction, the maps accurately emphasize the bird's body and head, showing positive contributions in red and negative in blue.
- **Alignment with Discriminative Patterns:** The visualizations confirm that the dynamic weight vectors of DAUs align with discriminative features in their inputs, reflecting the task-relevant patterns used by the model for classification (Figure 2, Figure 3).
- **Comparison to Post-hoc Methods:** When qualitatively compared to other prominent post-hoc attribution methods (LIME, GradCam, Occlusion, RISE), CoDA-Net's inherent contribution maps were observed to provide more detail while highlighting broadly similar regions of importance (Figure 8, Figures A.3, A.4). Unlike perturbation-based methods, CoDA-Net maps do not require multiple model evaluations.
- **Effect of Regularization ($\lambda$):** Explicit regularization of the global linear mapping $\mathbf{W}_{0 \rightarrow L}$ was shown to impact the quality of contribution maps. Without regularization, maps could be noisy, but even small amounts of regularization made them sharper and better aligned with discriminative parts of the input (Figure A.5).
- **Sanity Check:** The model-inherent contribution maps successfully passed the "sanity check" proposed by Adebayo et al. (Figure A.6). When CoDA-Net layers were progressively randomized from output to input, the contribution maps significantly changed, indicating that they faithfully reflect the learned parameters of the specific model, rather than just general architectural properties or input statistics. In contrast, "Input×Gradient" on piece-wise linear ResNets yielded noisier and less relevant features (Figure A.7).

#### 4.3. Interpretability – Quantitative Analysis

- **Localization Metric (Pointing Game):** CoDA-Nets consistently outperformed all other evaluated attribution methods under the localization metric on both TinyImagenet and CIFAR-10 (Figure 6, top row). This indicates that the positive contributions identified by CoDA-Nets are more accurately concentrated within the target class's image region compared to other methods.
- **Pixel Removal Strategy:** CoDA-Nets performed well in the pixel removal setting (Figure 6, bottom row). When least important pixels were removed first, the model's confidence for the target class either increased or remained stable, suggesting that the inherent contribution maps accurately rank pixel importance. When most important pixels were removed first, CoDA-Net's rankings resulted in the most rapid drop in confidence, indicating a superior ability to identify truly critical pixels (Figure B.4a).
- **Effect of Temperature Scaling ($T$):** Increasing the temperature parameter $T$ (which decreases the effective upper bound of the network output) consistently improved the interpretability of CoDA-Nets across both the localization and pixel removal metrics (Figure 6, right column). This confirms that increased "alignment pressure" due to temperature scaling enhances the alignment of DAU weights with discriminative patterns, leading to more meaningful explanations. A trade-off was observed where excessively high temperatures could slightly reduce classification accuracy (Figure B.1).
- **Comparison to ResNet-56 Baseline:** The CoDA-Net contribution maps demonstrated strong performance even when compared to attribution methods applied to a pre-trained ResNet-56. CoDA-Net's inherent maps outperformed these methods under the localization metric, and its pixel importance rankings were comparable to the best occlusion methods on the ResNet (Figure B.4b).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Convolutional Dynamic Alignment Networks (CoDA-Nets) represents a significant contribution to the field of interpretable artificial intelligence, offering a novel architectural paradigm that directly integrates interpretability into the network's design and optimization process. The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:

- **Advancement in Inherently Interpretable AI:** CoDA-Nets offer a promising direction for building inherently interpretable neural networks that do not rely on post-hoc approximations. By ensuring that contribution maps are faithful projections of internal computations and optimized for interpretability through the DAU design, this work moves towards more trustworthy and transparent AI systems.
- **Enhanced Trust and Reliability:** The ability to generate high-quality, discriminative contribution maps directly from the model's predictions can increase user trust in AI systems, particularly in sensitive applications such as medical diagnosis, autonomous driving, or financial risk assessment, where understanding the basis of a decision is critical. This could facilitate greater adoption of deep learning in domains currently hesitant due to black-box concerns.
- **Improved Model Understanding and Debugging:** By providing clear, detailed, and aligned explanations, CoDA-Nets can assist researchers and practitioners in better understanding model behavior, identifying biases, and diagnosing failure modes. This inherent transparency could streamline the debugging and refinement process for neural networks.
- **New Architectural Design Principles:** The Dynamic Alignment Units (DAUs) and the principles of dynamic linearity and alignment maximization offer new building blocks and design considerations for neural network architectures. This could inspire further research into developing other inherently interpretable components or network designs that prioritize transparency alongside performance.
- **Principled Control over Interpretability:** The demonstration that interpretability can be explicitly enhanced through mechanisms like temperature scaling provides a principled way to tune the trade-off between performance and explanation quality. This explicit control offers a novel avenue for engineering AI systems that meet specific interpretability requirements.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future research into optimizing CoDA-Nets for larger datasets (like full ImageNet), improving their training efficiency, and exploring their applicability to other tasks beyond classification. The detailed comparisons with existing attribution methods also provide a benchmark and highlight the strengths of architectural interpretability.

In summary, CoDA-Nets contribute to the growing body of work advocating for interpretability by design, providing a robust and performant neural network family that offers faithful, high-quality, and inherently generated explanations, thereby fostering greater understanding and trust in complex AI models.
