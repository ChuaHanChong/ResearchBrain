# The Moving Eye: Enhancing VLA Spatial Generalization via Hybrid Dynamic Data Collection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Lion Rock AI Lab introduce "The Moving Eye," a Hybrid Dynamic Data Collection framework that enhances the spatial generalization of Vision-Language-Action models in robotic manipulation. This framework systematically injects multi-dimensional diversity into training data, effectively mitigating shortcut learning caused by spurious correlations and achieving robust performance across varied camera poses and object configurations.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit fragile spatial generalization, with performance degrading under minor deviations in camera pose or object configurations from their training distributions.
- Models often succumb to "shortcut learning," leveraging spurious correlations like Camera-Base, Camera-Object, or Object-Position couplings rather than acquiring robust spatial relationships.
- Prior architectural or algorithm-focused solutions, including simply increasing viewpoint diversity, frequently fail to prevent these shortcut learnings, limiting real-world deployment.

## Method

- **The Moving Eye**, a ==Hybrid Dynamic Data Collection== framework, is proposed, utilizing a ==dual-arm robotic setup== where one arm manipulates and the other acts as a mobile environmental camera.
- The framework employs a ==Hierarchical Data Decoupling== strategy, collecting training data across Fixed, Multi-Fixed (discrete static views per episode), and continuously Moving View configurations.
- ==Multi-dimensional diversity== is explicitly injected by varying environmental camera viewpoint, relative object configurations, and ensuring ==camera-base decoupling==, with an empirically determined optimal ==mixing ratio== of Multi-Fixed and Moving data (e.g., 1:3 for Gr00t n1).

## Results

- The hybrid method achieved an **83.0%** success rate on out-of-distribution moving views for Camera-Base coupling tasks, a substantial improvement over the **43.0%** of the fixed-view baseline.
- It effectively mitigated Object-Position coupling, maintaining **90.6%** success on tasks with shifted object holders, where a Multi-Fixed baseline saw performance drop from **95.0%** to **71.9%**.
- The data strategy demonstrated universality, leading to performance gains across diverse VLA architectures (e.g., Gr00t **+8.5%**, Diffusion **+26.8%** over their pure Multi-Fixed baselines), with an optimal mixing ratio enhancing overall robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models are highly susceptible to shortcut learning from implicit couplings in training data, such as Camera-Base and Object-Position correlations, even when some viewpoint diversity is present.
> - Purely dynamic (Moving View) data, while crucial for generalization, hinders policy convergence due to high visual input variance, highlighting the necessity of combining it with more stable data.
> - A hybrid data collection strategy optimally combines the stability provided by Multi-Fixed view data with the decorrelating and regularizing effect of Moving View data, effectively breaking shortcut learning and enhancing spatial invariance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated capabilities in robotic manipulation by integrating visual perception with language understanding and action generation. However, a significant challenge within this field is the fragility of their spatial generalization. Existing VLA models often exhibit performance degradation when faced with minor deviations in camera pose or object configurations from their training distributions. This limitation hinders their deployment in dynamic real-world environments.

Prior research has attempted to address viewpoint robustness through several avenues. One approach involves incorporating explicit 3D or depth cues into the perception pipeline, utilizing techniques such as differentiable neural rendering, combining geometry-aware encoders, or generating orthographic views from multi-view RGB-D data. Another strategy focuses on injecting camera information directly into the policy, for instance, by adapting policies via visual feature modulation, distilling knowledge from multi-view teachers, or encoding camera extrinsics. A third line of work employs self-supervised learning to enforce cross-view consistency, often through view-masked representations or point-cloud alignment.

Despite these architectural and learning-algorithm-focused advancements, the paper identifies that merely increasing viewpoint diversity can still lead to "shortcut learning," where models exploit superficial correlations rather than learning robust spatial relationships. This work positions itself within an emerging data-centric paradigm that emphasizes strategic data collection and organization. While some prior efforts have explored data augmentation (e.g., novel-view synthesis) or active perception (learning to control the viewpoint online), this paper focuses on explicitly reshaping training data distributions to mitigate spurious correlations. It extends concepts like motion-based data collection, which was previously explored primarily in simulation, by implementing and validating such a system on real robotic platforms and introducing additional dimensions of diversity. Thus, this research contributes to the data-centric aspect of improving VLA model robustness, specifically by designing a physical data collection methodology that directly targets the identified problem of shortcut learning in spatial generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation. This is motivated by the observed fragility of current VLA models, which struggle with even slight perturbations in camera pose or object configurations, leading to a substantial gap in robustness and generalization when visual inputs deviate from the training distribution.

The authors identify "shortcut learning" as a core contributing factor to this fragility. Shortcut learning occurs when models leverage spurious correlations present in the training data instead of acquiring true, task-relevant spatial relations. This results in models that appear competent in controlled training environments but fail in varied real-world scenarios. The paper categorizes these problematic implicit couplings into three types:

1. **Camera-Base Coupling:** The model memorizes the robot's appearance relative to a static background. If the camera's relationship to the robot base changes (e.g., due to camera movement), the learned policy may fail.
2. **Camera-Object Coupling:** The model learns to recognize objects based on fixed camera angles. When an object is viewed from new perspectives, the model's recognition and manipulation capabilities degrade.
3. **Object-Position Coupling:** The model overfits to fixed relative positions between objects (e.g., a pen and its holder). Even if camera views are diverse, if the target and receptacle maintain static relative positions during training, the model struggles to generalize when these relative positions change in deployment.

Addressing these couplings is crucial for the real-world applicability of robotic systems, where visual stability is rarely guaranteed across different scenarios, including static camera setups (prone to minor shifts), multi-fixed view scenarios (mixing data from different static camera rigs), and dynamic moving view scenarios (e.g., handheld operation, mobile manipulation).

To overcome these issues, the work proposes a data-centric solution: a Hierarchical Data Decoupling strategy realized through a Hybrid Dynamic Data Collection framework. The key motivation is that simply increasing the number of viewpoints is insufficient; instead, the data collection methodology must actively disrupt these spurious correlations. This involves utilizing a dual-arm setup where one arm manipulates while the other acts as a mobile environmental camera, systematically evaluating different data distribution patterns (Fixed, Multi-Fixed, and Moving Views), and crucially, injecting multi-dimensional diversity into the data collection process by explicitly varying relative object positions. The overarching goal is to enable VLAs to generalize to unseen camera poses and object configurations by learning robust spatial representations, rather than relying on superficial cues.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a data collection strategy designed to break shortcut learning through hierarchical viewpoint sampling and multi-dimensional diversity injection, implemented using a dual-arm robotic setup.

**A. Problem Setup**
The system considers a VLA policy, $\pi(a|o, l)$, which maps observations ($o$, from wrist and environmental cameras) and language instructions ($l$) to actions ($a$). Observations include visual input from a wrist-mounted camera on the manipulation arm and a mobile environmental camera controlled by a second robot arm. The pose of the environmental camera ($P_c$) is constrained within a defined workspace ($W$).

**B. Camera Viewpoint Configurations**
Three distinct camera viewpoint configurations are defined to systematically vary the training data distribution:
- **Fixed View:** The camera maintains a single, static pose throughout all episodes. This configuration represents a common, constrained baseline.
- **Multi-Fixed View:** The camera pose remains static within each episode but varies discretely across different episodes within a bounded region. This introduces viewpoint diversity between episodes but not within them.
- **Moving View:** The camera moves continuously along trajectories within a bounded region (the same region as Multi-Fixed View) during data collection. This "decoupling zone" is hypothesized to maximize variation in relative poses among the camera, robot, and objects, thereby breaking spurious correlations.

**C. Multi-dimensional Diversity Injection**
To explicitly prevent shortcut learning, diversity is enforced along three axes during data collection:
1. **Viewpoint Diversity:** Achieved by sampling $P_c$ using Multi-Fixed and Moving configurations (random camera poses per group or continuous trajectories per episode).
2. **Object Configuration Diversity:** Achieved by randomizing the relative poses between target objects and their receptacles (e.g., varied positions for a pen and its holder). This directly targets Object-Position Coupling.
3. **Base-Camera Decoupling:** Ensured by viewpoint variation (environmental camera motion in Moving and Multi-Fixed Views), preventing fixed relationships between the camera frame and the robot base frame.

**D. Hybrid Dynamic Data Collection Strategy**
The proposed "Hybrid Dynamic Data Collection" strategy integrates the above components:
1. **Hierarchical Viewpoint Sampling:** Data is collected across Fixed, Multi-Fixed, and Moving View configurations. Multi-Fixed data is theorized to provide stability for policy convergence, while Moving View data acts as a regularizer to enforce spatial invariance.
2. **Multi-dimensional Diversity Injection:** Beyond camera motion, explicit variation in relative object positions (e.g., target and receptacle) is introduced. Camera-Base decoupling is addressed through the variation of environmental camera viewpoints.
3. **Optimal Composition:** The strategy investigates the optimal mixing ratio between Multi-Fixed and Moving data. The training dataset $D_{train}$ is formed as a weighted sum:
 $D_{train} = \frac{k}{k+1} D_{MultiFixed} + \frac{1}{k+1} D_{Moving}$
 where $k$ represents the Multi-Fixed part in the Moving:Multi-Fixed ratio of 1:k. For the primary model (Gr00t n1), an empirically optimal ratio of 1:3 was identified. Algorithm 1 formally outlines this strategy, which samples a random number to decide between Moving View (1/(k+1) probability) or Multi-Fixed View (k/(k+1) probability) for an episode, then randomizes object configuration and collects data.

**E. Experimental Setup**
- **Platform:** A So-101 robot arm with a wrist camera for manipulation, and an Airbot arm controlling the environmental camera. WowRobo 2 MP USB cameras capture 640x480 resolution at 30 FPS.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Pen Task:** Grasping a pen and inserting it into a holder (contact-rich alignment).
 - **Five-Category Multi-Object Task:** Pick-and-place for watch, lollipop, nail clipper, tape, and cube, covering varied geometries.
- **Sensing & Camera Motion:** Environmental camera follows continuous trajectories at 0.05 m/s linear speed (mean angular speed 0.198 rad/s) for Moving View collection. Data recorded as continuous MP4 video.
- **Baselines:** Fixed View (single static camera), Multi-Fixed View (discrete static viewpoints).
- **Data Collection Settings:**
 - **Pen Task (Multi-Fixed for Coupling Test):** Camera and pen holder fixed within groups, pen position varied.
 - **Pen Task (General):** Pen and holder at diverse positions, camera moves along random trajectories for Moving data.
 - **Multi-Task:** Single Fixed View only, with diverse object types and positions.
 - Emphasis on maximizing diversity (object poses, instances, orientations) except for specific coupling tests.
- **Evaluation Settings:** Fixed sets of 40 moving trajectories (Moving Test), 40 static poses (Multi-Fixed Test), and a predefined fixed pose (Fixed Test). Also, 5 fixed target-container relative positions and 8 pen orientations. Success rates computed over 400 (Pen) or 100 (Multi-Task) evaluation episodes. In-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) vary per experiment to probe specific shortcut couplings.
- **Training Details:** Gr00t models trained on 8 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, batch size 4, initial LR 1e-4 with cosine decay. Pen task (2400 episodes) took 34-37 hours; Multi-Task (2400 episodes) took 14 hours.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments systematically evaluated the proposed hybrid dynamic data collection strategy, demonstrating its effectiveness in mitigating shortcut learning and enhancing spatial generalization across various VLA architectures.

**A. Exp. 1: Shortcut Learning (Camera-Base Coupling)**
This experiment investigated Camera-Base Coupling by training on a Fixed View dataset and testing on both Fixed (ID) and Moving (OOD) viewpoints.
- **Results:** The Baseline (Fixed View Data) achieved an 85.0% success rate on the ID-Test (Fixed View) but collapsed to 43.0% on the OOD-Test (Moving View). This indicated severe overfitting to the static camera-base relationship.
- **Finding:** The proposed method (Mixed Data) maintained high robustness, achieving 86.0% on ID-Test and 83.0% on OOD-Test, demonstrating effective mitigation of Camera-Base coupling.

**B. Exp. 2: Shortcut Learning (Object-Position Coupling)**
This experiment was a diagnostic test to verify Object-Position Coupling, training a model on Multi-Fixed View data where the camera and pen holder positions were fixed within groups, but the pen (target object) position varied. It was tested on fixed holder (ID) and shifted holder (OOD) conditions.
- **Results:** The Baseline (Multi-Fixed) achieved 95.0% on ID-Test (fixed holder) but dropped significantly to 71.9% on OOD-Test (shifted holder).
- **Finding:** This confirmed that even with diverse camera viewpoints, a lack of variation in relative object positions leads to shortcut learning. Our method (Mixed 1:3 ratio) maintained high performance in both settings (91.9% ID, 90.6% OOD), successfully breaking this coupling.

**C. Exp. 3: The Hybrid Dynamic Data and The Golden Ratio of Composition**
This experiment focused on identifying an optimal mixing ratio between Multi-Fixed View and Moving View data for the Gr00t n1 model on the Pen Pick-and-Place task.
- **Results:**
 - Training solely on pure Moving data yielded poor performance (54.8% success rate on Moving Test), suggesting difficulty in policy convergence due to high visual input variance.
 - Pure Multi-Fixed data performed better (80.5% success rate on Moving Test) than pure Moving data, indicating its role in providing training stability.
 - A strategic mixture significantly improved performance. For Gr00t, the optimal ratio was found to be Moving:Multi-Fixed = 1:3, achieving the highest success rate of 89.0%.
- **Finding:** This demonstrates that Multi-Fixed data provides stability for convergence, while Moving View data acts as a critical regularizer for spatial invariance. A hybrid strategy leverages the benefits of both.

**D. Exp. 4: Transfer, Sample Efficiency & Universality of the Data Strategy**

1. **Cross-Task Spatial Generalization Transfer:**
 - **Objective:** To evaluate if spatial knowledge learned from a simple auxiliary task (Pen Task) using the hybrid data strategy could transfer to a new multi-object task, enhancing sample efficiency and generalization to moving views.
 - **Results:** The Baseline, trained purely on Fixed-View multi-task data, showed low performance (e.g., 43.0% at 2400 episodes) on the Moving Test set. Our method, which mixed 50% Fixed-View multi-task data with 50% auxiliary Pen data (collected with a 1:3 Moving:Multi-Fixed ratio), consistently achieved higher performance (e.g., 83.0% at 2400 episodes) with fewer task-specific episodes.
 - **Finding:** This confirms that spatial perception capabilities (hand-eye coordination, viewpoint invariance) learned from low-cost, auxiliary hybrid dynamic data can be effectively transferred to robustly guide manipulation actions on new tasks, even under dynamic viewpoints.

2. **Universality of the Data Strategy:**
 - **Objective:** To determine if the benefits of the hybrid dynamic data collection strategy extend to diverse VLA architectures. Models tested included ACT, Diffusion, Pi0, and Gr00t.
 - **Results:** Across all evaluated architectures, pure Moving data consistently led to convergence difficulties, while Multi-Fixed data performed better. However, all models achieved substantial performance gains by incorporating Moving-View data in a mixed strategy. For example, Diffusion gained +26.8%, Pi0 +13.8%, ACT +8.1%, and Gr00t +8.5% compared to their pure Multi-Fixed baselines. While the optimal mixing ratio varied between architectures (e.g., 1:1 for Diffusion, 1:11 for Pi0), the overall pattern of benefit from mixed data was universal.
 - **Finding:** The susceptibility to shortcut learning and the benefit of the hybrid dynamic data collection strategy are universal characteristics across diverse VLA architectures, highlighting the data-centric solution's broad applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically addressing the fragility of spatial generalization in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work provides a significant real-world validation of a dynamic viewpoint data collection system, bridging a gap between simulation-based paradigms and physical deployment.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

- **Mitigation of Shortcut Learning:** The research empirically demonstrates that VLA models are susceptible to shortcut learning, relying on spurious correlations (Camera-Base, Camera-Object, and Object-Position couplings). The proposed Hybrid Dynamic Data Collection strategy effectively mitigates these couplings, enabling models to learn more robust spatial representations. This is a crucial step toward building more reliable and trustworthy robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Spatial Generalization:** By combining the stability provided by Multi-Fixed view data with the decorrelating effect of Moving View data, the hybrid strategy significantly improves VLA models' ability to generalize to unseen camera poses and object configurations. This is essential for deploying robots in real-world environments where visual conditions are dynamic and unpredictable.
- **Universality Across Architectures:** The finding that the benefits of the hybrid data strategy extend to diverse VLA architectures (ACT, Diffusion, Pi0, Gr00t) suggests a broad applicability of this data-centric approach. This indicates that the problem of shortcut learning is not specific to particular model designs but a general challenge in how training data is presented.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Transferability:** The demonstration that spatial knowledge learned from a low-cost auxiliary task using the hybrid data can be efficiently transferred to new tasks with better performance on dynamic viewpoints highlights a path for more efficient data collection and model training. This could reduce the resources and time required to develop generalized robotic policies.
- **Practical Implications for Data Collection:** The research offers a practical, systematic methodology for collecting data that directly targets common failure modes in robotic learning. Roboticists and researchers can adopt this hybrid dynamic data collection approach to build more robust datasets, thereby improving the generalization capabilities of their VLA models.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The current evaluation primarily focuses on table-top pick-and-place tasks and contact-rich alignment. While the data strategy is considered task-agnostic, systematic validation on longer-horizon or more complex contact-rich tasks remains future work. Methodologically, a future direction involves integrating moving viewpoints into an online module within the VLA, enabling policy-conditioned active viewpoint control to resolve occlusions while benefiting from the learned viewpoint invariance. This would combine the data-centric approach with active perception for a more comprehensive solution.
