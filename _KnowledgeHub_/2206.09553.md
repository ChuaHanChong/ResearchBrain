# Capturing and Inferring Dense Full-Body Human-Scene Contact

> [!summary] Summary
> RICH is a multiview 4K capture of 22 subjects in 5 laser-scanned indoor/outdoor scenes, yielding 577K images with SMPL-X fits and vertex-level human-scene contact labels. Trained on it, BSTRO (Body-Scene contact TRansfOrmer) regresses dense per-vertex contact straight from one RGB image via a transformer, reaching 0.708 F1 versus 0.464 for POSA.

## Problem

- Prior contact methods restrict themselves to feet-and-ground or a handful of joints, and often assume a flat ground plane that stairs or uneven terrain violate.
- Physics-based and MoCap-trained contact detectors reduce the body to boxes, cylinders or spheres, and predict contact from pose or velocity while ignoring image evidence entirely.
- Existing contact datasets cover self-contact, hand-object or person-person, or are indoor RGB-D only, where occlusion-corrupted body fits propagate into noisy contact labels.

## Method

- **RICH** capture pipeline: markerless multiview ==SMPL-X== fitting with a bone-orientation term, a triangulation-based multiview-consensus reweighting of noisy keypoints, and ==PARE==-initialized batch optimization with motion smoothness.
- Contact labeling by point-to-surface distance from body vertices to a Leica RTC360 laser scan with normal-compatibility check, thresholded at 5cm under the feet and 2.5cm elsewhere.
- **BSTRO**: ==HRNet== image features concatenated with SMPL template vertex coordinates as positional encoding, fed to a multi-layer ==transformer== that emits per-vertex contact logits trained with ==binary cross-entropy== plus ==Masked Vertex Modeling==.

## Results

- On RICH-test BSTRO reaches **0.699** precision, **0.774** recall, **0.708** F1 and **10.98cm** geodesic error, beating POSA with ground-truth bodies (**0.464** F1, **19.96cm**) and HuMoR (**0.314** F1).
- Generalization splits show interaction type matters most: withholding the interaction drops F1 to **0.520-0.705**, while the fully unseen scene/interaction/subject subset still scores **0.721** F1.
- PIXIE degrades under contact on RICH: **214.0mm**/**172.81mm** TR-MPJPE/TR-V2V with meaningful scene contact versus **161.81mm**/**121.71mm** without, and **84.15mm** versus **63.67mm** PA-MPJPE.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contacting body regions are self-occluded by definition, so contact inference is a hallucination task, favoring non-local attention over architectures reading only visible local evidence.
> - Ground-truth quality bounds learned contact: distance-thresholded labels inherit every body-fit error, so multiview fitting beats monocular RGB-D as a supervision source.
> - Recall-heavy contact predictors trade away precision; scene contact also measurably degrades pose-and-shape regressors, so contact-aware benchmarks expose failures standard datasets hide.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has seen substantial progress in human pose and shape estimation (HPS) from single images and 2D human-object interaction (HOI) detection. However, these advancements often treat the human body in isolation from its surrounding environment. Existing HPS methods frequently produce physically implausible results, such as body parts penetrating the ground or other objects, because they largely disregard the scene and the physical interactions within it.

Reasoning about 3D human-scene contact (HSC) from a single image presents a persistent challenge. Current approaches to HSC detection typically suffer from several limitations:
- They often only consider a limited number of predefined contact types, such as foot-ground contact.
- The representations of the human body and the scene are frequently simplified, reducing them to a small set of geometric primitives (e.g., body as boxes, ground as a flat plane).
- Many methods infer contact indirectly, either as a post-processing step after reconstructing a 3D body or by deriving it from body pose parameters alone, without directly utilizing visual evidence from the input image. This often overlooks rich contextual information present in the image.
- A fundamental bottleneck is the scarcity of datasets that provide natural images coupled with accurate 3D ground-truth contact labels, particularly for dense, full-body interaction in complex, real-world scenes. Existing datasets either lack 3D contact information or are limited to specific indoor environments, offering lower quality or less comprehensive data.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by introducing both a novel dataset and a new algorithmic approach. It seeks to enable direct, dense, and image-driven inference of 3D human-scene contact, thereby fostering a more comprehensive understanding of human interaction within diverse environments and contributing to the development of physically plausible human motion and interaction models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of inferring dense, full-body human-scene contact (HSC) directly from a single RGB image. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed motivations that highlight the limitations of existing research:

- **Enhancing Physical Plausibility in Human Pose and Shape Estimation:** Current 3D HPS methods typically reconstruct human bodies in isolation, without considering their interaction with the environment. This often leads to physically implausible outcomes, such as foot skating, ground penetration, or bodies floating in space. A detailed understanding and estimation of HSC are critical first steps towards generating physically consistent human poses and motions within a scene.
- **Addressing the Scarcity of 3D Contact Data:** A major impediment to advancing HSC research is the lack of rich, accurately annotated datasets. Existing datasets either do not provide 3D contact labels, offer them at a coarse level (e.g., joint-level), or are limited in scope (e.g., indoor-only, simplified scenes). The motivation is to create a high-quality, comprehensive dataset that includes natural images, precise 3D scene geometry, and dense, vertex-level ground-truth contact labels for the full body.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing HSC Inference Methods:** Previous methods for HSC detection often rely on restrictive assumptions. They might:
 - Only detect a few predefined types of contact (e.g., foot-ground).
 - Approximate human bodies or scenes using simplified geometric primitives, which may not capture the nuances of real-world interactions.
 - Infer contact through post-processing steps or solely from reconstructed body poses, effectively neglecting direct visual cues from the input image. The researchers are motivated to develop an approach that leverages image evidence directly to infer contact.
- **Handling Occlusion in Contact Inference:** Regions of the body in contact with a scene are inherently occluded from the camera's view. This presents a significant challenge for direct inference from images. A key motivation is to design an algorithm that can "hallucinate" or infer unobserved contact by effectively exploring non-local relationships and contextual information across the entire image.
- **Developing a Body-Centric Contact Detector:** Many approaches require prior 3D scene reconstruction or known scene geometry to infer contact. The objective is to develop a body-centric method that can infer contact for any given human in an image, without explicitly reconstructing the full 3D scene first.
- **Benchmarking Human Pose and Shape Estimation in Interactive Contexts:** Current HPS benchmarks primarily focus on body reconstruction accuracy in isolation. This work aims to provide a dataset that enables the evaluation of HPS methods specifically in the context of human-scene interaction, allowing researchers to analyze how accurately bodies are estimated when they are in contact with or occluded by a scene.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research addresses the challenges of human-scene contact (HSC) inference through two main contributions: the creation of a novel dataset named RICH, and the development of a neural network called BSTRO.

#### 3.1. RICH Dataset: Real scenes, Interaction, Contact and Humans

The RICH dataset is designed to provide high-quality data for dense, full-body HSC estimation. Its creation involved a multi-stage capture and annotation process:

- **Multiview Video Capture:** The dataset comprises multiview video sequences captured at 4K resolution in both indoor and outdoor environments. This multiview setup is crucial for resolving occlusions and obtaining robust 3D reconstructions.
- **3D Scene Acquisition:** High-resolution 3D geometry of the scenes was acquired using an industrial laser scanner (Leica RTC360). This provides accurate static scene meshes and HDR textures. The rigid transformation between camera coordinates and scan coordinates was established through manually identified correspondences.
- **3D Human Body Capture and Modeling:**
 - **Subject-Specific Shape:** High-resolution 3D scans of all subjects in minimal clothing were captured. The SMPL-X body model was then fitted to these scans to obtain precise, subject-specific body shapes (parameter β). This shape parameter is pre-estimated and held constant during the body posing process for each subject.
 - **Markerless Motion Capture:** For each video sequence, subjects were first tracked temporally in each view using AlphaPose for 2D keypoint detection, and then tracklets were matched across views using MvPose.
 - **Multiview SMPL-X Fitting:** The SMPLify-X framework was adapted for multiview data. Instead of fitting in individual views, all 2D landmarks from multiple cameras were combined into a multiview energy term (∑c EcJ). To enhance fitting quality, two additional terms were introduced:
 - **Bone Orientation Term (EO):** This term focuses on the relative orientation of bone vectors derived from detected landmarks and estimated SMPL-X joints, effectively factoring out accumulated errors from parent joints in the kinematic chain. It aims to align the orientation of estimated bones with those implied by 2D keypoint detections.
 - **Multiview Consensus:** To counter noisy 2D keypoint detections, a soft majority voting mechanism was implemented. For each joint, 3D points were triangulated from pairs of views and projected into a third view. The reprojection error between the projected point and the actual detection in the third view was used to downweight the contribution of views with inconsistent detections, improving robustness.
 - **Initialization and Temporal Smoothing:** To avoid local minima, the optimization process was initialized using fused results from a state-of-the-art monocular body regressor (PARE) applied to each view. After frame-wise optimization, a batch of frames was jointly refined with additional smoothness terms for body (Eb_sm) and hand (Eh_sm) motions.
- **Dense Contact Labeling:** With the precisely reconstructed SMPL-X bodies placed within the scanned 3D scenes, dense per-vertex scene contact labels were automatically computed. For each vertex on the body mesh, the point-to-surface distance to the scene scan was calculated. A vertex was classified as being in contact if this distance was below a specific threshold (5cm for vertices at the bottom of the feet, 2.5cm for the rest of the body) and the normal vectors were compatible. This approach, leveraging multiview data, improves accuracy over methods relying on monocular RGB-D, which are more susceptible to occlusion errors.
- **Dataset Characteristics:** RICH contains 142 multiview videos, amounting to 577K 4K images, 90K posed 3D SMPL-X bodies, and 90K dense full-body scene-contact labels (in both SMPL-X and SMPL topology). It features diverse outdoor and indoor environments, single and multiple subjects, dynamic backgrounds, and varied viewpoints, making it suitable for training and evaluating monocular HSC methods.

#### 3.2. BSTRO: Body-Scene contact TRansfOrmer

BSTRO is a deep learning model designed to estimate dense vertex-level HSC directly from a single RGB image, bypassing the conventional multi-stage approach of first reconstructing a 3D body.

- **Core Idea:** The fundamental insight is that contact regions are frequently occluded in images. Therefore, the network needs to explore the entire image for contextual evidence and "hallucinate" the unobserved contact. A transformer architecture is leveraged for its ability to capture non-local relationships.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Input:** A single color image containing a person.
 - **Feature Extraction:** An HR-Net backbone extracts 2048-dimensional image features.
 - **Positional Encoding:** The 3D vertex locations of a canonical SMPL template (V=6890 vertices, excluding the head, face, and hands of SMPL-X to reduce output dimensionality) are appended as positional encoding to the image features, forming query vectors for each vertex.
 - **Multi-layer Transformer:** A multi-layer transformer takes these query vectors as input. It processes the information to learn complex, non-local relationships between visual evidence across the image and the potential contact status of each body vertex.
 - **Output:** The transformer outputs logits for each vertex. A sigmoid function is applied to these logits to produce per-vertex probabilities (pv ∈) of being in contact.
 - **Dense Contact Vector:** A binary scene-contact vector (c) is obtained by thresholding these probabilities at 0.5.
- **Training:**
 - **Loss Function:** The model is trained end-to-end using a binary cross-entropy loss between the ground-truth binary contact labels from RICH and the predicted contact probabilities.
 - **Masked Vertex Modeling (MVM):** To enhance robustness to occlusion, MVM is employed. During training, a subset of query vectors corresponding to certain body vertices is randomly masked out. The network is still required to predict contact for all vertices, forcing it to infer information about occluded or missing parts from the remaining visible cues.
 - **Optimization:** The model is trained using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1e-4 for 100 epochs, utilizing 24K (Image, HSC) pairs sampled from RICH-train. The HR-Net backbone is initialized with weights pre-trained on ImageNet, Human3.6M, or 3DPW.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results, including a generalization study and an analysis of HPS performance in the presence of scene contact.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics and Baselines

- **HSC Evaluation:** Standard detection metrics of precision, recall, and F1 score were used. Additionally, a "geodesic error" was introduced, measuring the shortest geodesic distance from a predicted contact vertex to any ground-truth contact vertex, to quantify spatial error.
- **Baselines:** Three HSC baselines were evaluated:
 - **Zou et al.:** Infers foot contact using 2D keypoint velocities.
 - **HuMoR:** Estimates contact for 8 sparse joints during human motion reconstruction.
 - **POSA:** A generative model that samples dense body contact given a 3D body mesh in canonical space. POSA was evaluated in two configurations:
 - **POSA_GT:** Using ground-truth 3D body meshes (from RICH).
 - **POSA_PIXIE:** Using 3D body meshes estimated by PIXIE, a state-of-the-art HPS regressor.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results on RICH-Test

Table 2 presents the performance of the methods on the RICH-test set:

| Methods | Precision (↑) | Recall (↑) | F1 (↑) | Geo. Error (↓) |
| :---------------------- | :------------ | :--------- | :-------- | :------------- |
| a. Zou et al. | 0.277 | 0.609 | 0.359 | 17.48cm |
| b. HuMoR | 0.248 | 0.527 | 0.314 | 25.35cm |
| c. POSA (GT) | 0.375 | 0.768 | 0.464 | 19.96cm |
| d. POSA (PIXIE) | 0.312 | 0.699 | 0.399 | 21.16cm |
| **e. BSTRO (Ours)** | **0.699** | **0.774** | **0.708** | **10.98cm** |

- **HuMoR** exhibited the lowest detection scores and highest geodesic errors, potentially due to its assumption of an even ground plane, which is often violated in the diverse RICH scenes.
- **POSA** methods generally showed higher recall but lower precision, indicating a propensity for false positives. The performance of POSA significantly improved when provided with ground-truth bodies (POSA_GT) compared to PIXIE-estimated bodies (POSA_PIXIE), highlighting the impact of upstream HPS accuracy.
- **BSTRO** achieved substantially higher precision and the highest F1 score (0.708) across all methods, along with the lowest geodesic error (10.98cm). This indicates that BSTRO strikes a robust balance between accurately identifying contact and minimizing false positives, producing predictions closer to the ground truth.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples (Figure 6) demonstrated that RICH provides accurately fitted SMPL-X bodies and HSC ground truth. BSTRO's estimations of scene contact were observed to be closer to the ground truth compared to POSA_PIXIE, which frequently produced false positives or missed contact regions (e.g., on hands). BSTRO also showed an ability to generalize its performance to in-the-wild images not present in the training set.

#### 4.4. Generalization Study

To assess BSTRO's generalization capabilities, the RICH-test set was subdivided based on whether the scene, human-scene interaction (HSI), and subject attributes were "seen" (3) or "unseen" (7) during training (Table 3):

| Scenario (Scene, HSI, Subject) | Precision (↑) | Recall (↑) | F1 (↑) | Geo. Error (↓) |
| :----------------------------- | :------------ | :--------- | :----- | :------------- |
| a. (7, 3, 3) | 0.766 | 0.819 | 0.769 | 4.77cm |
| b. (3, 3, 7) | 0.734 | 0.756 | 0.718 | 9.64cm |
| c. (3, 7, 7) | 0.556 | 0.593 | 0.520 | 25.11cm |
| d. (7, 3, 7) | 0.753 | 0.870 | 0.790 | 6.61cm |
| e. (7, 7, 3) | 0.644 | 0.820 | 0.705 | 16.64cm |
| f. (7, 7, 7) | 0.682 | 0.814 | 0.721 | 9.00cm |
| g. Full RICH-test set | 0.699 | 0.774 | 0.708 | 10.98cm |

- **Impact of HSI:** Observing similar HSI during training proved to be the most influential factor for performance. Scenarios where HSI was seen (e.g., a, b, d) generally yielded higher scores and lower geodesic errors. A notable drop in performance occurred when HSI was unseen (e.g., e, c).
- **Challenging Scenarios:** The most challenging subset (f), where scene, HSI, and subjects were all unseen, still yielded competitive results, indicating BSTRO's capacity for generalization. Subset (c), with unseen HSI and subjects but a seen scene, also proved challenging, partly due to complexities like person-person occlusion.

#### 4.5. HPS Evaluation on RICH-Test

RICH also functions as a benchmark for monocular HPS methods, allowing for the analysis of performance in the presence or absence of scene contact. PIXIE, a state-of-the-art HPS method, was used to regress SMPL-X bodies from RICH-test images. Discrepancies were measured using Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Vertex-to-Vertex Error (V2V). Errors were reported with Procrustes alignment (PA) for moving cameras and pelvis-aligned translation-only (TR) for calibrated cameras, excluding ubiquitous foot-ground contact.

- Images containing meaningful scene contact (beyond foot-ground contact) consistently yielded higher errors for PIXIE compared to images with no significant contact.
 - For calibrated cameras: 214.0mm TR-MPJPE and 172.81mm TR-V2V for images with contact, versus 161.81mm TR-MPJPE and 121.71mm TR-V2V for images without contact.
 - For moving cameras: 84.15mm PA-MPJPE and 83.16mm PA-V2V for images with contact, versus 63.67mm PA-MPJPE and 64.37mm PA-V2V for images without contact.
- This demonstrates that the presence of scene contact, often accompanied by scene occlusion, increases the difficulty for monocular HPS methods, impacting both body pose and global orientation/translation estimates. This finding highlights a specific area for future improvement in HPS research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer vision through the introduction of a new dataset and a novel method for inferring human-scene contact, with implications for a broader understanding of human behavior and advancements in related applications.

#### 5.1. Significance of RICH Dataset

- **Comprehensive Data for 3D HSC:** RICH is positioned as the first dataset to provide multiview images of people interacting with complex, laser-scanned 3D scenes, including outdoor environments, along with accurate, dense, vertex-level 3D human-scene contact labels. This addresses a critical gap in existing data resources, which were often limited in scope (e.g., indoor-only, sparse contact, or lower-quality RGB-D based fits).
- **Robust Benchmarking:** The dataset enables the rigorous training and evaluation of novel monocular 3D HSC detection methods. Its detailed annotations, including pseudo-ground-truth human poses and shapes, also establish it as a valuable benchmark for monocular or multiview HPS methods, particularly for analyzing their performance in scenarios involving human-scene interaction and occlusions.

#### 5.2. Significance of BSTRO Model

- **Direct Contact Inference:** BSTRO represents an advancement as it directly estimates dense 3D body-scene contact from a single RGB image, without requiring an intermediate 3D body reconstruction. This streamlines the inference process and potentially makes it more robust to errors inherent in multi-stage pipelines.
- **Addressing Occlusion Challenges:** The use of a transformer architecture, combined with a Masked Vertex Modeling training strategy, specifically targets the challenge of inferring contact in occluded regions of the body. This approach leverages non-local image evidence effectively, allowing the model to "hallucinate" unobserved contact.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** BSTRO demonstrates superior performance compared to prior art in dense HSC detection on the challenging RICH dataset, achieving higher F1 scores and lower geodesic errors.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Understanding of Human Actions:** Accurate 3D human-scene contact inference is fundamental for a more detailed and physically consistent understanding of human actions, behaviors, and interactions within complex environments.
- **Advancements in Robotics and AI:** The capabilities developed could inform various applications in robotics (e.g., human-robot collaboration, navigation in human-centric spaces), virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) (e.g., creating more realistic virtual humans and their interactions with virtual environments), and animation (e.g., generating physically plausible human movements).
- **Improved Human Pose and Shape Estimation:** The finding that current HPS methods struggle in the presence of significant scene contact (often due to occlusion) highlights a clear direction for future research in HPS. By providing the means to analyze and address these limitations, this work can contribute to the development of more robust HPS algorithms.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Work

- **Static Scene Interactions:** A current limitation of the RICH dataset is its focus on contact with static scenes, thereby excluding interactions with dynamic elements like hand-held objects or other humans.
- **Future Extensions:** Future work could involve extending the dataset and methods to address contact with dynamic scenes, potentially by jointly estimating the rigid-body pose of objects and the interacting hand/body. Another direction involves integrating body pose, shape, and scene contact estimation into a single, unified network.
