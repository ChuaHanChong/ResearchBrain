# SRFT: A Single-Stage Method with Supervised and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Supervised Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (SRFT), a single-stage method that unifies supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs). SRFT achieves an average accuracy of 59.5% on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, improving upon zero-RL methods by 9.0% and demonstrating enhanced out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) can lead to models memorizing patterns, overfitting, and potentially failing to develop true reasoning abilities or generalize well to unseen problems.
- Pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods often suffer from sample inefficiency due to vast solution spaces, struggle with effective exploration, or experience issues like mode collapse, leading to repetitive or suboptimal outputs.
- Conventional two-stage approaches (e.g., SFT then RL) often face issues such as error propagation, limited efficacy of RL if SFT induces excessive deviation from optimal policies, or catastrophic forgetting of SFT-acquired knowledge during the RL phase.

## Method

- Proposes Supervised Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (**SRFT**), a novel ==single-stage== method that unifies SFT and RL into a single optimization process to enhance LLM reasoning capabilities.
- Integrates learning from both high-quality demonstrations and self-exploration rollouts within a unified loss function, building upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.
- Introduces two entropy-aware weighting mechanisms to dynamically adjust the influence of the SFT loss and the positive self-exploration samples, ensuring stable training while preserving exploration capabilities and managing the trade-off between knowledge distillation and policy optimization.

## Results

- SRFT achieved an average accuracy of **59.5%** across five competition-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming zero-RL methods by **9.0%** and other integrated SFT+RL methods like LUFFY (**55.5%**) and TAPO (**55.7%**) by **3.4%** and **3.8%** respectively.
- Demonstrated strong out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, achieving an average score of **62.5%** on three OOD benchmarks (ARC-C, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), outperforming the best baseline by **4.7%**.
- Maintained more stable entropy during training compared to pure RL, confirming the efficacy of its entropy-aware weighting mechanisms in preserving exploration diversity and contributing to stable learning dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT induces "coarse-grained global changes" to an LLM's policy distribution, broadly shifting token probabilities.
> - RL performs "fine-grained selective modifications," refining probabilities for specific subsets of tokens without drastic global shifts.
> - Entropy serves as a critical indicator of training effectiveness, revealing that sequential SFT
RL approaches lead to reduced model plasticity after RL, especially when followed by SFT again due to rapid entropy increase and performance drop.
> - Single-stage integration of SFT and RL proves superior in training efficiency and avoids transient performance degradation (catastrophic forgetting) observed in sequential methods by directly optimizing the policy while leveraging demonstrations and self-exploration signals concurrently.
> - The authors empirically show that the order of application matters, with RL followed by SFT yielding suboptimal results and reduced plasticity, motivating a single-stage approach to overcome these issues and enable direct optimization towards better solutions across an expanded space, avoiding the pitfalls of sequential transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has marked a revolutionary period in AI, with models demonstrating unprecedented capabilities in various natural language processing tasks, including complex reasoning. However, effectively fine-tuning these models to achieve optimal performance, especially in specialized domains like mathematical reasoning, remains a key challenge. This paper directly addresses the ongoing debate and practical difficulties in integrating Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms.

Historically, SFT and RL have often been treated as distinct, sequential stages in LLM development. SFT, typically involving training on high-quality human-annotated data, is effective for imparting instruction-following abilities and broad knowledge. However, it can lead to models that memorize patterns or overfit, lacking true generalization and reasoning capabilities. RL, conversely, excels at optimizing models for specific objectives (e.g., reward maximization through problem-solving), fostering exploration and dynamic adaptation. Yet, RL methods are notoriously sample-inefficient, prone to exploration challenges in vast solution spaces, and can suffer from mode collapse, where the model generates limited, suboptimal outputs.

Recent advancements have shifted towards more integrated or interleaved approaches, recognizing the complementary strengths of SFT and RL. Researchers have explored combining SFT and RL to leverage the structured guidance of demonstrations while benefiting from the explorative power of reinforcement learning. This work directly builds upon and contributes to this trend, specifically by proposing a *single-stage* unification of SFT and RL. Previous attempts at integration often involved sequential stages (e.g., SFT followed by RL) or dynamic switching mechanisms. The authors identify critical issues with sequential methods, such as catastrophic forgetting or suboptimal policy shifts, which prompt their investigation into a more seamless, concurrent integration.

The paper's contribution lies in its comprehensive analysis of the differential effects of SFT and RL on LLM policy distributions (SFT induces coarse-grained changes, RL performs fine-grained modifications) and its proposal of an entropy-aware weighting mechanism. This mechanism attempts to dynamically balance the influence of supervised and reinforcement signals, addressing a fundamental trade-off that practitioners face. By demonstrating superior performance on challenging reasoning benchmarks and out-of-distribution tasks, SRFT positions itself as a significant advancement in the landscape of LLM fine-tuning, pushing the boundaries of how SFT and RL can be harmoniously combined to unlock enhanced reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **optimal strategy for integrating Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enhance the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs)**. The authors observe that while both SFT and RL have contributed significantly to LLM progress, their separate or sequential application presents fundamental challenges.

The key motivations driving this work stem from a comprehensive analysis of the limitations inherent in existing fine-tuning paradigms:
1. **Limitations of SFT:** SFT, while effective for initial instruction following, can lead to models that merely memorize training patterns, potentially overfitting and failing to develop true reasoning abilities. This results in models that might not generalize well to unseen problems.
2. **Limitations of RL:** Pure RL methods, though powerful for optimizing specific objectives, often suffer from sample inefficiency due to vast solution spaces, struggle with effective exploration, or experience issues like mode collapse, leading to repetitive or suboptimal outputs.
3. **Challenges of Sequential Integration:** Conventional two-stage approaches (e.g., SFT then RL) often face issues such as error propagation, limited efficacy of RL if SFT induces excessive deviation from optimal policies, or catastrophic forgetting of SFT-acquired knowledge during the RL phase. The authors empirically show that the order of application matters, with RL followed by SFT yielding suboptimal results and reduced plasticity.

Through their preliminary analysis, the authors uncover crucial insights:
- SFT induces "coarse-grained global changes" to the LLM's policy distribution, broadly shifting probabilities across tokens.
- RL, conversely, performs "fine-grained selective modifications," refining probabilities for a smaller subset of tokens without drastic global shifts.
- **Entropy** is identified as a "critical indicator" of training effectiveness, reflecting the model's plasticity and the nature of distribution changes.

Based on these findings, the core objective of the paper is to propose **Supervised Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (SRFT)**, a novel **single-stage method** that unifies both SFT and RL. The motivation for a single-stage approach is to enable direct optimization towards better solutions across an expanded space, avoiding the pitfalls of sequential transitions. SRFT aims to effectively leverage high-quality demonstrations (often the strength of SFT) while simultaneously benefiting from self-exploration and policy optimization (the strength of RL), thereby achieving stable learning and superior reasoning performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper, Supervised Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (SRFT), is a single-stage fine-tuning approach that unifies Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) using entropy-aware weighting mechanisms. The core idea is to simultaneously learn from both human demonstrations and self-exploration rollouts.

SRFT builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework, a memory-efficient variant of PPO that operates without a learned value function. GRPO computes relative advantages for a group of generated responses and uses a clipped surrogate objective to ensure stable updates.

The SRFT approach integrates four key components into a single unified loss function:

1. **Learning from Demonstrations (Dual-Pronged Strategy):**
 - **Coarse-grained SFT on Demonstrations (`L_demo.SFT`):** SRFT utilizes high-quality demonstrations (e.g., reasoning responses from expert LLMs like DeepSeek-R1) to perform a coarse-grained approximation of the expert's behavior policy. This is achieved by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the demonstration data, similar to standard SFT:
 `L_demo.SFT = E_(x,y)~D_demo. [-log(pi_theta(y|x))]`
 Crucially, the authors introduce an **entropy-aware weighting mechanism** for this loss: `w_SFT = 0.5 * stop_grad(exp(-H(pi_theta)))`. This weight dynamically adjusts the influence of the SFT loss based on the current policy's entropy (`H(pi_theta)`). When the policy's entropy is high (indicating uncertainty or deviation from the expert), `w_SFT` is diminished, mitigating performance degradation due to distribution mismatch and allowing more flexibility for RL to guide.
 - **Fine-grained Off-policy RL on Demonstrations (`L_demo.RL`):** SRFT augments the LLM's on-policy rollouts with demonstration data to create a heterogeneous training batch. The advantage estimation is computed across this combined group. To account for the distribution shift between the behavior policy (demonstrations) and the current training policy, an importance sampling term is introduced, similar to GRPO or PPO. The behavior policy (`pi_beta`) is simplified to `1` to avoid tokenization complexities, allowing for easy integration of off-the-shelf datasets without recomputing probabilities. The clipping operation is omitted in this case due to potential instability with `pi_beta = 1`.

2. **Learning from Self-Exploration (`L_self-rollout.RL`):**
 - SRFT enables the LLM to learn from its own on-policy rollouts. The standard RL objective function with binary rewards (1 for correct, -1 for incorrect) is naturally decomposed into two parts: maximizing the likelihood of positive samples and minimizing the likelihood of negative samples.
 - The positive sample objective (`E_x~D, y+~pi_theta(·|x) [-log(pi_theta(y+|x))]`) structurally resembles SFT, but it operates on samples generated by the current policy. To prevent rapid entropy reduction and preserve exploration diversity during self-exploration, another **entropy-adaptive weighting mechanism** is introduced for this positive sample component: `w_RL = 0.1 * stop_grad(exp(H(pi_theta)))`. This weight increases with higher entropy, promoting stability and preventing premature convergence to deterministic outputs. The negative sample component (`E_x~D, y-~pi_theta(·|x) [log(pi_theta(y-|x))]`) simply minimizes the likelihood of incorrect responses.

3. **Total Loss Function:** The final SRFT objective combines all four components, leveraging the complementary strengths of demonstration-based learning and self-exploration in a single optimization process:
 `L_SRFT(theta) = L_demo.SFT(theta) + L_demo.RL(theta) + L_self-rollout.RL(theta)`

This integrated, single-stage approach, particularly with its novel entropy-aware weighting mechanisms, allows SRFT to dynamically balance knowledge distillation from demonstrations with active policy exploration, leading to stable training and enhanced reasoning capabilities. The authors use Qwen2.5-Math-7B as the base model and train on the OpenR1-Math-46k-8192 dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings regarding the dynamics of SFT and RL, culminating in the superior performance of the proposed SRFT method.

**Analysis of SFT and RL Effects:**
- **Token Distribution Effects:** Through visualization of token probability changes, the authors demonstrate a fundamental asymmetry: SFT induces "coarse-grained global changes" across the entire response sequence, significantly altering the probability distribution. In contrast, RL performs "fine-grained selective modifications," primarily adjusting probabilities for a small subset of tokens, leaving much of the numerical and mathematical content unchanged. This suggests SFT globally reshapes the policy, while RL refines specific behaviors.
- **Learning Dynamics and Entropy as an Indicator:**
 - A novel visualization in 3D probability space, using reference models (Qwen-2.5-Math-7B, DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B) as coordinate frames, reveals that SFT causes larger distribution changes from the base model and achieves higher performance. RL, on the other hand, fine-tunes within a localized neighborhood.
 - **Entropy Dynamics:** Sequential SFT→RL approaches show that RL significantly reduces policy entropy, leading to more deterministic outputs. However, subsequent SFT after RL causes a rapid increase in entropy and a sharp performance drop, indicating reduced model plasticity. This highlights that entropy serves as a crucial indicator: while RL enhances performance, it can limit the model's ability to adapt further via SFT.
 - **Single-Stage Superiority:** Preliminary experiments comparing pure RL, sequential SFT→RL, and single-stage SFT+RL (the precursor to SRFT) reveal that single-stage integration achieves superior training efficiency. It avoids transient performance degradation (catastrophic forgetting) observed in sequential SFT→RL, especially after extensive SFT pre-training. This is attributed to the single-stage method's ability to directly optimize the policy while leveraging demonstrations.

**Experimental Results of SRFT:**
- **Superior Performance on Reasoning Benchmarks:** SRFT achieves an average accuracy of **59.1%** (Table 2 shows 59.5% average) across five competition-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AMC, MATH500, Minerva, Olympiad), outperforming zero-RL methods by a substantial **9.0%** (59.5% vs. 50.1% for Oat-Zero, the best zero-RL method). It also improves upon direct SFT by 4.8% and outperforms other SFT+RL integrated methods like LUFFY (55.5%) and TAPO (55.7%) by 3.4% and 3.8% respectively, demonstrating the effectiveness of its single-stage design and entropy-aware weighting.
- **Strong Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** On three OOD benchmarks (ARC-C, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), SRFT achieves an average score of **62.5%**, outperforming the best baseline (LUFFY at 57.8%) by **4.7%**. This indicates SRFT's enhanced generalization ability due to its balanced learning approach.
- **Stable Training Dynamics:** Analysis of training dynamics (Figure 6) shows that SRFT achieves faster performance improvement compared to pure RL. While RL tends to generate concise responses for challenging data, SRFT progressively lengthens responses, suggesting more thorough reasoning. Critically, SRFT maintains **more stable entropy** compared to the rapid decline seen in RL, confirming the efficacy of its entropy-aware weighting mechanisms in preserving exploration diversity during training.
- **Ablation Study Validation:** An ablation study confirms the individual contributions of the two entropy-aware weighting mechanisms. Removing the SFT weighting (`w/o w_SFT`) results in a 4.0-point average accuracy drop, and removing the RL weighting (`w/o w_RL`) leads to a 2.9-point decrease. This empirically validates the theoretical importance of these components in dynamically balancing the SFT and RL signals for optimal and stable training.

Overall, the findings robustly support the authors' hypotheses about the differential nature of SFT and RL, the importance of entropy as a training indicator, and the superior performance and stability of their single-stage, entropy-aware SRFT method for LLM reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Supervised Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (SRFT) method carries significant implications for the field of Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuning, particularly for enhancing complex reasoning capabilities.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
1. **Fundamental Insight into SFT vs. RL:** The detailed analysis distinguishing SFT's "coarse-grained global changes" from RL's "fine-grained selective optimizations" offers a crucial conceptual framework for understanding how these two paradigms influence LLM policy distributions. This insight, along with the identification of entropy as a critical indicator, provides a more nuanced understanding of LLM learning dynamics, moving beyond a simplistic view of "more data, better model."
2. **Novel Single-Stage Unification:** SRFT introduces a truly single-stage fine-tuning approach that effectively unifies supervised and reinforcement learning. This is a significant departure from prevalent sequential or interleaved methods, addressing issues like catastrophic forgetting and sub-optimal policy shifts that often plague multi-stage pipelines. By concurrently leveraging demonstrations and self-exploration, SRFT offers a more cohesive and efficient training paradigm.
3. **Entropy-Aware Weighting Mechanisms:** The development of adaptive, entropy-aware weighting mechanisms for both demonstration-based SFT and positive self-exploration samples is a novel contribution. These mechanisms dynamically balance the influence of different learning signals, ensuring stable training while preserving exploration capabilities. This provides a principled way to manage the trade-off between knowledge distillation and policy optimization, which is a core challenge in integrated fine-tuning.
4. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** SRFT's demonstrated superior performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (9.0% improvement over zero-RL baselines) and strong generalization on out-of-distribution tasks (10.9% improvement) validates its practical effectiveness. This suggests that SRFT can equip LLMs with more robust and adaptable reasoning skills.
5. **Improved Generalization:** The significant gains on out-of-distribution benchmarks underscore SRFT's ability to foster genuine reasoning capabilities rather than just overfitting to training data. This improved generalization is critical for deploying LLMs in real-world scenarios where problems may deviate from the training distribution.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** SRFT can lead to the development of more capable and reliable LLMs for complex problem-solving, particularly in domains requiring systematic logical inference, such as mathematics, science, and engineering.
- **More Efficient Fine-Tuning:** By offering a single-stage approach, SRFT can streamline the LLM development lifecycle, potentially reducing computational costs and time associated with multi-stage training and hyperparameter tuning for transitions between SFT and RL.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The findings on entropy dynamics and the distinction between SFT and RL effects lay a foundation for future research into more sophisticated adaptive control mechanisms, beyond the current simple exponential weighting functions. This could lead to more principled hybrid training algorithms.
- **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, the principles of single-stage integration and entropy-aware balancing could potentially be applied to other LLM fine-tuning tasks that benefit from both expert guidance and self-improvement, such as code generation, scientific discovery, or complex decision-making.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
The authors acknowledge that the current utilization of entropy dynamics is relatively simple, suggesting opportunities for more sophisticated entropy-based control mechanisms or multi-timescale entropy analysis. Additionally, the approach assumes access to high-quality demonstrations, indicating a need for future research into training with imperfect demonstrations to broaden the method's applicability. Despite these limitations, SRFT represents a significant step towards more effective and robust LLM fine-tuning for reasoning.
