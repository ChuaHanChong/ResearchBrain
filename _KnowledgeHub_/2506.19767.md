# SRFT: A Single-Stage Method with Supervised and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Supervised Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (SRFT), a single-stage method that unifies supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs). SRFT achieves an average accuracy of 59.5% on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, improving upon zero-RL methods by 9.0% and demonstrating enhanced out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) can lead to models memorizing patterns, overfitting, and potentially failing to develop true reasoning abilities or generalize well to unseen problems.
- Pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods often suffer from sample inefficiency due to vast solution spaces, struggle with effective exploration, or experience issues like mode collapse, leading to repetitive or suboptimal outputs.
- Conventional two-stage approaches (e.g., SFT then RL) often face issues such as error propagation, limited efficacy of RL if SFT induces excessive deviation from optimal policies, or catastrophic forgetting of SFT-acquired knowledge during the RL phase.

## Method

- Proposes Supervised Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (**SRFT**), a novel ==single-stage== method that unifies SFT and RL into a single optimization process to enhance LLM reasoning capabilities.
- Integrates learning from both high-quality demonstrations and self-exploration rollouts within a unified loss function, building upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.
- Introduces two entropy-aware weighting mechanisms to dynamically adjust the influence of the SFT loss and the positive self-exploration samples, ensuring stable training while preserving exploration capabilities and managing the trade-off between knowledge distillation and policy optimization.

## Results

- SRFT achieved an average accuracy of **59.5%** across five competition-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming zero-RL methods by **9.0%** and other integrated SFT+RL methods like LUFFY (**55.5%**) and TAPO (**55.7%**) by **3.4%** and **3.8%** respectively.
- Demonstrated strong out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, achieving an average score of **62.5%** on three OOD benchmarks (ARC-C, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), outperforming the best baseline by **4.7%**.
- Maintained more stable entropy during training compared to pure RL, confirming the efficacy of its entropy-aware weighting mechanisms in preserving exploration diversity and contributing to stable learning dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT induces "coarse-grained global changes" to an LLM's policy distribution, broadly shifting token probabilities.
> - RL performs "fine-grained selective modifications," refining probabilities for specific subsets of tokens without drastic global shifts.
> - Entropy serves as a critical indicator of training effectiveness, revealing that sequential SFT
RL approaches lead to reduced model plasticity after RL, especially when followed by SFT again due to rapid entropy increase and performance drop.
> - Single-stage integration of SFT and RL proves superior in training efficiency and avoids transient performance degradation (catastrophic forgetting) observed in sequential methods by directly optimizing the policy while leveraging demonstrations and self-exploration signals concurrently.
> - The authors empirically show that the order of application matters, with RL followed by SFT yielding suboptimal results and reduced plasticity, motivating a single-stage approach to overcome these issues and enable direct optimization towards better solutions across an expanded space, avoiding the pitfalls of sequential transitions.
