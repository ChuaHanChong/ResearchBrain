# FurnitureBench: Reproducible Real-World Benchmark for Long-Horizon Complex Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FurnitureBench introduces a real-world benchmark for complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation, focusing on furniture assembly with 3D-printable models, a standardized software stack, and a large teleoperated dataset. Initial evaluations reveal that current state-of-the-art algorithms struggle significantly, failing to complete any full assembly tasks.

## Problem

- The lack of standardized, reproducible, and sufficiently complex real-world benchmarks for robotic manipulation research.
- Existing real-world benchmarks often focus on simple, short-horizon tasks, creating a 'sim-to-real' gap for complex, multi-step operations.
- Difficulties in reproducing robotics research due to variations in hardware, environments, and software configurations.

## Method

- Developed ==FurnitureBench==, a reproducible real-world benchmark for furniture assembly using a Franka Emika Panda robot and 3D-printable furniture models.
- Provided a Dockerized robot control software stack, a systematic task initialization tool, and comprehensive setup guides to ensure ease of use and reproducibility across labs.
- Released a large ==teleoperated demonstration dataset== (200+ hours, 5000+ demonstrations) and a high-fidelity simulator (==FurnitureSim==) to support offline learning and sim-to-real transfer research.

## Results

- Reproducibility analysis showed that new environments set up by different participants achieved **75-93%** relative performance compared to the original setup.
- Neither Behavioral Cloning (BC) nor Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) policies successfully completed any full furniture assembly tasks across all eight models or randomness levels.
- The 'inserting' skill proved to be the most challenging (**0-20%** success rate), and 'screwing' for round parts also showed very low success (**0-10%**), highlighting critical bottlenecks for existing algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Furniture assembly serves as an effective long-horizon, complex manipulation challenge, requiring diverse dexterous skills like precise insertion and robust screwing under varying conditions.
> - Reproducibility in real-world robotics can be achieved through standardized hardware, 3D-printed components, and a robust software stack, with independent setups showing high performance consistency.
> - Current state-of-the-art offline reinforcement learning and imitation learning algorithms are significantly limited and unable to solve full, real-world long-horizon assembly tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation research has seen impressive advancements with deep reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning (IL), and task and motion planning (TAMP). However, a significant bottleneck has been the lack of standardized, reproducible, and sufficiently complex real-world benchmarks. Existing simulated benchmarks, like Atari or OpenAI Gym, while accessible, often abstract away the complexities and stochasticity of the real world, leading to a "sim-to-real" gap. In robotic manipulation, many simulated environments focus on primitive, short-horizon tasks (e.g., pick-and-place, peg insertion), or composite tasks with limited variations. While environments like BEHAVIOR and Habitat offer long-horizon mobile manipulation, they often prioritize high-level planning over low-level, dexterous control challenges. The previous IKEA furniture assembly simulation by some of the current authors highlighted the potential of assembly tasks but remained in simulation.

Real-world benchmarks, summarized in Table I of the paper, have emerged to address the sim-to-real gap, offering reproducible setups with affordable robots or cloud-based evaluation platforms. Examples include ROBEL, RGB-Stacking, REPLAB, and the Real Robot Challenge. However, a common limitation across these benchmarks is their focus on relatively simple, short-horizon tasks such as stacking blocks or re-orienting objects. Robotic assembly benchmarks, like the NIST Assembly Task Board, exist but often lack a standardized experimental setup, including specific robots, hardware, and observation/action spaces, making cross-comparison difficult.

FurnitureBench strategically positions itself at the intersection of these challenges by offering a *reproducible real-world benchmark* for *long-horizon, complex manipulation tasks*, specifically furniture assembly. It bridges the gap between simple real-world tasks and complex simulated ones by bringing the difficulty of multi-step assembly into a standardized physical environment. By providing 3D-printable models, a comprehensive setup guide, a standardized software stack, and a large dataset, FurnitureBench aims to lower the barrier to entry for real-world robot learning, making it easier for researchers globally to test algorithms and compare results reliably. This work directly pushes the frontier of robot learning by providing the necessary infrastructure to tackle real-world tasks that demand advanced planning, dexterous control, and robust perception – capabilities essential for deploying autonomous robots in everyday environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective behind the development of FurnitureBench is to accelerate research in autonomous robots performing complex, long-horizon, everyday physical tasks. The authors identify a crucial gap in current robotic manipulation benchmarks: while RL, IL, and TAMP have shown promise, their real-world application has been limited to simple behaviors due to the short-horizon and often artificial nature of existing tasks. To enable robots to perform more human-centric tasks like doing laundry, tidying rooms, or assembling furniture, there is an urgent need for benchmarks that reflect these challenges.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives are:

- **Addressing Long-Horizon Complexity:** To move beyond simple pick-and-place tasks and confront the challenges of multi-step, hierarchical tasks that require reasoning over extended time spans and composing diverse behaviors. Furniture assembly is chosen as a prime example due to its inherent long-horizon structure and the variety of skills required.
- **Enhancing Reproducibility:** To overcome the notorious reproducibility crisis in robotics research. Many impressive results are difficult to replicate due to non-standardized hardware, environments, and software. FurnitureBench aims to provide a low-barrier-to-entry and easily reproducible setup using widely available commercial off-the-shelf components and 3D-printable models.
- **Facilitating Research in Dexterous Manipulation and Perception:** Furniture assembly demands precise alignment, delicate force control for insertion and screwing, and robust visual perception under varying conditions. The benchmark is designed to expose and evaluate these critical manipulation skills.
- **Providing Comprehensive Resources:** To make the benchmark truly accessible, the authors commit to providing a full suite of resources:
 - 3D printable furniture models.
 - A robotic environment setup guide.
 - A robot control software stack (Dockerized).
 - A systematic task initialization tool.
 - A large teleoperated demonstration dataset (200+ hours, 5000+ demonstrations) to aid offline RL and IL research.
 - A fast and realistic simulator, FurnitureSim, for rapid iteration and preliminary testing.
- **Identifying Algorithmic Limitations:** By benchmarking state-of-the-art algorithms on these complex tasks, the authors aim to highlight current weaknesses in RL, IL, and TAMP, thereby guiding future research directions towards more capable and generalizable robotic policies.

In essence, FurnitureBench seeks to be a foundational tool that enables the robotics community to reliably measure progress and foster innovation in solving truly complex, real-world robotic manipulation problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FurnitureBench is meticulously designed to be reproducible, easy-to-use, and challenging. The methodology encompasses hardware, software, task design, data collection, and simulation.

#### 3.1. Reproducible Real-World Environment Design

- **Hardware:** The core system utilizes a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot arm with its default parallel gripper, a widely used and affordable research robot. Three Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D cameras provide visual input. A black IKEA TOMMARYD table serves as the workspace, selected for its global availability. 3D-printed obstacles are affixed to the table to aid single-arm manipulation. A green photography backdrop and adjustable LED lighting ensure consistent visual conditions.
- **Furniture Models:** Eight distinct furniture models, inspired by IKEA designs (e.g., lamp, square_table, chair), are provided as 3D-printable files. This ensures identical physical properties across different labs. The designs are slightly modified to be feasible for single-arm manipulation. AprilTags are integrated into the 3D models for robust, multi-camera pose estimation, which is used for reward computation and task initialization, not as direct policy input.
- **Reproducibility Analysis:** To validate reproducibility, 10 participants independently set up environments. A pre-trained IQL-R3M policy for a simple subtask (one_leg assembly) showed consistent performance (84% average performance relative to the original setup), confirming the benchmark's reproducibility despite slight variations in camera views or backgrounds.

#### 3.2. Easy-to-Use Software and Data

- **Robot Control Stack:** A Dockerized, Python-based control stack built on Polymetis and utilizing Operational Space Control (OSC) is provided. This "plug-and-play" setup lowers the technical barrier for new users. The control operates at 10 Hz for end-effector delta-pose commands, which are then translated to 1000 Hz joint torques.
- **Task Initialization Tool:** A GUI tool guides users to precisely set up initial furniture part configurations, sampling from pre-defined distributions (low, medium, high randomness). This ensures consistent evaluation conditions across experiments.
- **Teleoperated Demonstration Dataset:** A massive dataset of 200+ hours (5000+ successful demonstrations) was collected using an Oculus Quest 2 VR controller and keyboard. This large dataset is crucial for training offline RL and IL algorithms and reducing the need for costly real-world interactions. The data includes RGB-D frames, robot states, actions, rewards, and AprilTag poses.
- **Observation and Action Space:** The policy receives RGB images from front-view and wrist cameras (224x224 pixels) and proprioceptive robot states (end-effector position, orientation, velocity, gripper width). The action space is 8D (delta end-effector position and orientation, plus gripper action).

#### 3.3. FurnitureSim: Simulated Environment

- **Simulator Design:** FurnitureSim, a high-fidelity simulator, is built upon Isaac Gym and Factory, providing fast and accurate physics simulation (especially for screw interactions) and realistic rendering. It uses the same 3D models and robot controller as the real world to minimize sim-to-real gaps.
- **Sim-to-Real Correlation:** Empirical evaluation shows a positive correlation between performance in FurnitureSim and FurnitureBench, suggesting the simulator serves as a useful proxy for rapid prototyping and verifying algorithms before real-world deployment.

#### 3.4. Task Definition and Evaluation

- **Furniture Assembly Hierarchy:** Furniture assembly is modeled as a hierarchical task involving iterative 1-step assemblies: selecting parts, grasping, moving, aligning, and firmly attaching (screwing/insertion). This structure creates long task horizons (60-230 seconds, 600-2300 low-level steps).
- **Diverse Dexterous Skills:** The benchmark tests a range of skills: robust grasping, precise re-orientation, collision avoidance, accurate alignment for insertion, and deliberate force control for screwing (including re-grasping for rotation beyond wrist limits).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Beyond overall task success (completing assembly), a "phase completion" score is introduced, counting completed subtasks (e.g., "grasping leg," "inserting leg"). This provides finer-grained progress measurement, especially for difficult tasks.
- **Randomness Levels:** Three initialization randomness levels (low, medium, high) are defined for evaluation, testing an agent's generalization capability under increasing uncertainty in initial object placement.
- **Baselines:** State-of-the-art offline RL (Implicit Q-Learning, IQL) and imitation learning (Behavioral Cloning, BC) algorithms, typically with R3M or VIP visual encoders, are used as baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The benchmarking results reveal significant challenges for current state-of-the-art robot learning algorithms in tackling complex, long-horizon furniture assembly tasks.

- **Reproducibility Validated:** The reproducibility analysis confirmed that new environments set up by different participants consistently achieved 75-93% of the performance of the original environment. This demonstrates the robustness of the benchmark's design and setup instructions.

- **Single-Skill Benchmark:**
 - Algorithms (BC and IQL) performed well on "grasping" (e.g., 70-90% success for lamp, square_table) and "placing" skills.
 - "Pushing" (e.g., drawer) showed moderate success (30-60%), attributed to stochastic non-prehensile interactions.
 - **"Inserting" proved to be the most challenging skill**, with success rates consistently ranging from 0% to 20%. This highlights the difficulty in precise alignment and robust recovery from misalignments.
 - "Screwing" showed variable performance: high success for square/flat parts (e.g., 90% for square_table, 70% for desk at low randomness) but struggled significantly for round parts (e.g., 0-10% for lamp, round_table), indicating sensitivity to object geometry and contact dynamics.

- **Full-Assembly Benchmark:**
 - Neither BC nor IQL achieved a single full furniture assembly across any of the eight furniture models or randomness levels.
 - Policies often succeeded in initial phases like grasping the first part and placing it, but consistently failed at more complex subsequent phases, particularly insertion and screwing.
 - IQL generally outperformed BC, and performance degraded with increasing randomness in initial configurations.
 - These results starkly illustrate that current algorithms are far from solving real-world, long-horizon assembly tasks, even with high-quality demonstration data.

- **Tractable One_leg Assembly Task:**
 - To demonstrate tractability, a simplified "one_leg" assembly task (5 phases: grasp tabletop, place, pick leg, insert, screw) was introduced, with 1000 demonstrations per randomness level.
 - On this simpler task, IQL-R3M achieved 4 phases on average and a 40% success rate on the low randomness level, indicating that the benchmark *can* be solved.
 - The primary failure point remained "inserting," reinforcing its status as a critical bottleneck.

- **Impact of Data and Vision:**
 - **More data improves performance:** Policies trained with 2000 demonstrations (mixed low/medium randomness data) significantly improved phase completion (e.g., from 3.8 to 4.6 for IQL-R3M on one_leg at low randomness).
 - **Diversity of data matters:** Policies trained *only* on low randomness data performed better on low randomness evaluation than policies trained on medium randomness data, suggesting current algorithms struggle to generalize from diverse data distributions.
 - **Wrist camera is crucial:** Policies trained without wrist camera input showed significant performance drops (e.g., from 3.8 to 2.0 phases on one_leg at low randomness), highlighting its importance for observing occluded areas and close-up manipulation.
 - **AprilTag exploitation is minimal:** Policies performed reasonably well even when AprilTags were removed or randomized, confirming they do not "cheat" by directly exploiting marker information.

- **Simulation Benchmark (FurnitureSim):**
 - Simulation results for one_leg assembly mirrored real-world trends, with IQL outperforming BC and demonstrating the utility of the simulator as a proxy.
 - Different visual encoders (ResNet-18, R3M, VIP) showed varying performance, with IQL-VIP performing best in simulation but IQL-R3M being more robust in the real world. This further highlights the existing sim-to-real gap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FurnitureBench represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, with the potential to drive substantial advancements in several key areas:

- **Advancing Long-Horizon, Complex Manipulation:** By presenting a compelling and tangible challenge, FurnitureBench directly encourages the robotics community to move beyond simplified tasks and tackle the complexities inherent in real-world, multi-step operations like furniture assembly. Solving this benchmark will require breakthroughs in hierarchical planning, robust error recovery, and complex skill composition.
- **Standardized and Reproducible Research:** The meticulously designed setup, complete with 3D-printable models, comprehensive guides, and a Dockerized software stack, provides an unprecedented level of standardization and reproducibility for real-world robotics experiments. This will enable fairer comparisons between different algorithms and foster a more collaborative research environment, reducing wasted effort on replication issues.
- **Benchmarking Algorithmic Limitations:** The initial benchmarking results clearly demonstrate that even state-of-the-art imitation learning and offline reinforcement learning methods struggle profoundly with the full furniture assembly tasks. This stark finding serves as a critical indicator, highlighting the current deficiencies of algorithms in areas like dexterous manipulation (especially insertion and complex screwing), generalization across diverse initial conditions, and robust contact-rich interactions. It effectively pinpoints specific research gaps, guiding future algorithmic development.
- **Resource for Data-Driven Methods:** The provision of a large, high-quality teleoperated demonstration dataset (200+ hours, 5000+ episodes) is invaluable for data-driven robot learning. It significantly lowers the entry barrier for researchers working on offline RL and IL, allowing them to train and test algorithms without the prohibitive cost and time of collecting their own real-world data from scratch.
- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer Research:** The inclusion of FurnitureSim, a high-fidelity simulator, alongside the real-world benchmark creates a fertile ground for research into sim-to-real policy transfer. While domain gaps exist, the correlation between sim and real performance provides a foundation for developing better randomization techniques, domain adaptation methods, and sim-to-real learning strategies.
- **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines limitations and future work, such as extending to multi-arm or mobile manipulation, incorporating diverse object categories/materials, tackling larger-scale furniture, and further closing the sim-to-real gap. This proactive guidance helps researchers identify promising avenues for impactful contributions.

In summary, FurnitureBench is more than just a new task; it's a foundational infrastructure designed to systematically challenge and advance robot learning towards more intelligent, autonomous, and capable robots that can assist humans in complex, everyday environments.
