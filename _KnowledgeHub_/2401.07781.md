# Towards A Better Metric for Text-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National University of Singapore and Tencent PCG introduce T2VScore, a novel automatic metric for evaluating text-to-video generation by separately assessing text-video alignment and video quality. The metric utilizes multimodal large language models and a mixture-of-experts structure, achieving higher correlation with human judgments on a new dataset compared to existing automated methods.

## Problem

- Existing automated metrics for text-to-video (T2V) generation often fail to capture temporal dynamics and show low correlation with human perception, being ill-suited for open-domain evaluation.
- Subjective human evaluations, while accurate, are resource-intensive, slow, and prone to bias, hindering large-scale and consistent benchmarking of T2V models.
- A lack of fine-grained, automatic metrics that can independently and comprehensively assess both the semantic fidelity (text alignment) and the perceptual quality of generated videos.

## Method

- The paper proposes **T2VScore**, a novel automatic evaluation pipeline composed of two distinct components: **T2VScore-A** for text-video alignment and **T2VScore-Q** for video quality.
- T2VScore-A employs a ==question-answering== framework leveraging ==multimodal large language models (MLLMs)== and integrates auxiliary trajectory data to precisely assess spatial and temporal alignment with the input text prompt.
- T2VScore-Q utilizes a ==mixture-of-limited-experts== structure with ==progressive optimization== and ==list-wise learning objectives== to provide a robust and generalizable video quality score, even for videos from unseen generative models.
- A new human-annotated dataset, **TVGE**, was created and open-sourced to validate the T2VScore and provide a benchmark for future T2V evaluation metric development.

## Results

- T2VScore-A, when powered by GPT-4V and auxiliary trajectory data, achieved a Spearman 's of **0.486**, showing substantially higher correlation with human judgment than existing text-video alignment metrics.
- T2VScore-Q demonstrated over **20%** improvement in all correlation coefficients (e.g., Spearman 's improved to **0.5029**) over state-of-the-art video quality assessment methods in a zero-shot setting.
- The adaptation strategy for T2VScore-Q yielded an average gain of **11.2%** in Spearman 's across cross-model scenarios, demonstrating robust generalization to generative models not seen during adaptation.
- Analysis of the TVGE dataset indicated that current text-to-video models often produce videos with lower-than-average human ratings for both alignment (mean **2.59**) and quality (mean **2.77**) on a 5-point scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human perception of text-video alignment and video quality are largely independent, showing a very low correlation (Spearman 's = 0.223), justifying their separate evaluation.
> - The accuracy of text-video alignment metrics heavily relies on the capabilities of underlying MLLMs, with advanced models like GPT-4V demonstrating superior performance in video question answering.
> - Incorporating auxiliary trajectory data significantly enhances MLLMs' fine-grained temporal understanding, leading to improved accuracy in identifying subtle object and camera movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has made considerable advancements, particularly in synthesizing high-quality text, images, and videos. Text-to-video (T2V) generation models, in particular, have demonstrated capabilities in creating visually compelling video content. However, the evaluation of these generated videos presents significant challenges, which this paper aims to address.

**Current State of T2V Generation and Evaluation:**
Contemporary T2V models, such as VDM, Make-A-Video, Imagen Video, PYoCo, LVDM, Show-1, and commercial products like Gen-2, Pika, and Floor33, have become increasingly sophisticated. These models leverage diffusion-based architectures and learning from vast text-video datasets to generate diverse and complex video content.

Despite these advancements, the reliable evaluation of generated videos remains an active area of research. Existing evaluation approaches can be broadly categorized into automated metrics and subjective user studies.
- **Automated Metrics**: Common metrics include Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and Video Inception Score (IS) for video quality, and CLIP Score for text-video alignment.
 - **Limitations**: FVD and Video IS, being full-reference metrics, are often considered unsuitable for open-domain video generation where a direct "ground truth" reference video is not available. CLIP Score, which averages per-frame text-image similarities, frequently overlooks crucial temporal dynamics and motion changes specific to video, leading to a recognized mismatch with human perception. Image-level metrics like PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID are frame-wise and do not comprehensively assess video quality, particularly temporal consistency. Other video metrics like Kernel Video Distance (KVD) and Video Inception Score (Video IS) also primarily focus on general video quality rather than text-conditional fidelity.
- **Subjective User Studies**: While capable of accurately reflecting human perception, these studies are resource-intensive, laborious, and often influenced by subjective biases, making them impractical for large-scale or consistent benchmarking.

**Related Areas of Research:**
The paper draws inspiration from and builds upon several related domains:
- **Video Quality Assessment (VQA)**: Traditional VQA approaches often rely on learning-based methods, employing pre-trained deep neural networks and human opinions to regress quality scores. Recent advancements involve leveraging large VQA databases (e.g., LSVQ, MaxWell) for better feature representation and transferring these learnings to diverse video types, including computer-generated content. Multi-modality foundation models like CLIP have also been explored for VQA, using text prompts as implicit quality indicators in zero-shot or few-shot settings.
- **Question Answering (QA)-based Evaluation**: Recent studies have utilized Visual Question Answering (VQA) to assess the accuracy of AI models, particularly for text-to-image generation (e.g., TIFA, VQ2A, VPEval). These methods typically involve generating questions based on text prompts and using VQA systems to verify image content. However, these prior works have largely focused on static images, neglecting the spatial and temporal elements crucial for video evaluation.

**Gap Addressed by This Work:**
This research addresses the critical need for fine-grained, automatic, and human-aligned evaluation metrics specifically tailored for text-guided generated videos. It seeks to overcome the limitations of existing automated metrics in capturing temporal aspects and the impracticalities of large-scale human evaluations by proposing a novel pipeline that integrates state-of-the-art vision-language understanding with a robust video quality assessment framework, validated by a purpose-built human-annotated dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the recognized deficiencies in existing methods for evaluating text-to-video (T2V) generation models. As T2V models become more sophisticated and widely adopted, the absence of reliable and comprehensive evaluation metrics poses a significant impediment to their further development and comparison.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Investigate Limitations of Existing Metrics**: A fundamental objective was to empirically demonstrate and analyze the shortcomings of currently employed automated metrics (such as FVD, IS, and CLIP Score) in accurately assessing the quality and text-fidelity of generated videos. The paper specifically highlights their inadequacy in temporal analysis and their unreliability as indicators of true video quality and alignment.
2. **Introduce a Novel Evaluation Pipeline (T2VScore)**: The central objective is to develop and propose a new, automatic evaluation metric named T2VScore. This pipeline is designed to offer a more holistic and reliable assessment of text-conditioned generated videos by integrating two critical dimensions:
 - **Text-Video Alignment**: To scrutinize how faithfully the generated video represents the given text description, including all spatial and temporal elements. This dimension aims to ensure semantic consistency and adherence to the prompt.
 - **Video Quality**: To evaluate the overall technical and perceptual production caliber of the synthesized video. This includes assessing visual fidelity, absence of distortions, and naturalness.
3. **Develop T2VScore-A for Text-Video Alignment**: A specific objective is to design the T2VScore-A component, which evaluates the correctness of both spatial and temporal elements derived from the text prompt. This involves leveraging cutting-edge vision-language models for a question-answering approach to verify content.
4. **Develop T2VScore-Q for Video Quality**: Another key objective is to create the T2VScore-Q, engineered to predict a robust and generalizable quality score. This metric aims to effectively assess videos generated by diverse models, including those not encountered during its training, through a combination of structural design and training strategies.
5. **Create and Open-Source the TVGE Dataset**: To facilitate the development and validation of evaluation metrics, an essential objective is to compile the Text-to-Video Generation Evaluation (TVGE) dataset. This dataset collects extensive human judgments on generated videos across the two core criteria (text-video alignment and video quality) that T2VScore addresses. The dataset is intended to serve as an open benchmark for correlating automatic metrics with human perception and for adapting automatic metrics to the specific domain of T2V-generated content.
6. **Demonstrate Superiority and Alignment with Human Judgment**: The ultimate objective is to conduct comprehensive experiments on the TVGE dataset to validate that the proposed T2VScore, encompassing T2VScore-A and T2VScore-Q, achieves higher correlation with human judgments compared to existing baseline metrics, thus offering a more effective and reliable means of evaluating text-to-video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces T2VScore, a novel evaluation pipeline for text-to-video generation, comprising two primary components: T2VScore-A for text-video alignment and T2VScore-Q for video quality. A new dataset, TVGE, is also presented for validation and future development.

#### 3.1. T2VScore-A: Text Alignment

This component assesses how accurately a generated video reflects its input text prompt, employing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and a structured question-answering framework.

1. **Entity Decomposition in Text Prompt**:
 - The input text prompt (P) is parsed into distinct semantic elements ($e_i$).
 - Hierarchical semantic relationships among these elements are identified, forming entity tuples (e.g., (dog, a), (cat, playing soccer)).
 - Global elements (e.g., style, camera motion) are categorized separately. This structured decomposition implicitly prioritizes elements based on their hierarchical significance, impacting the penalty for mismatches.

2. **Question/Answer Generation with LLMs**:
 - Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically GPT-3.5, are used to generate question-choice-answer (QCA) tuples {Q_i, C_i, A_i}.
 - The generation process employs a single-pass inference with in-context learning, utilizing a few manually curated examples to guide the LLM.
 - Emphasis is placed on generating questions that cover temporal aspects (e.g., object trajectory, camera motion), which are critical for video but often overlooked by image-centric QA methods.

3. **Video Question Answering with Auxiliary Trajectory**:
 - The generated video (V) and the generated QCA pairs {Q_i, C_i} are fed into MLLMs.
 - To compensate for the limitations of most open-domain MLLMs in fine-grained temporal comprehension, auxiliary trajectory data (V_track) generated by off-the-shelf point tracking models (e.g., CoTracker, OmniMotion) is integrated. This enhances understanding of object and camera movements.
 - The MLLMs provide answers (Â_i = VQA(V, V_track, Q_i, C_i)).
 - **T2VScore-A Calculation**: The Text-to-Video alignment score is defined as the accuracy of the video question answering process, ranging from 0 to 1, where higher values indicate better alignment.

#### 3.2. T2VScore-Q: Video Quality

This component aims to provide a robust and generalizable quality score for generated videos, capable of evaluating videos from unseen generation models. This is achieved through a mixture-of-experts structure, progressive optimization, and list-wise learning objectives.

1. **Mix-of-Limited-Expert Structure**:
 - **Technical Expert**: Adopts the structure of FAST-VQA, pre-trained on the large LSVQ database and fine-tuned on the MaxWell database (which contains diverse spatial and temporal distortions). This expert captures distortion-level quality relevant to naturally captured videos.
 - **Semantic Expert**: Based on MetaCLIP, this expert assesses semantic degradations (e.g., incorrect object structures) common in generated videos. It is calculated via a confidence score on a binary classification between "good, high quality" and "poor, low quality" prompts. An additional adapter is used to tailor the CLIP-based evaluator to video quality assessment.
 - **Fusion**: The scores from the technical (Q_tech(V)) and semantic (Q_sem(V)) experts are remapped using an ITU-standard perceptual-oriented function and then averaged to produce the final T2VScore-Q(V), ranging from 0 to 1.

2. **Progressive Optimization Strategy**:
 - The training of T2VScore-Q is conducted in three stages, with gradually decreasing dataset scales and progressively reduced optimizable parameters to enhance generalization.
 - **Pre-training**: Technical expert is end-to-end pre-trained on LSVQ (28K videos). Semantic expert directly uses official MetaCLIP weights.
 - **Fine-tuning**: Technical expert is multi-layer fine-tuned on MaxWell (3.6K videos). Semantic expert trains a lightweight adapter on MaxWell.
 - **Adaptation**: The technical expert is frozen to prevent overfitting to generated videos. For the semantic expert, an additional prefix token is trained to robustly adapt it to the domain of generated videos, which often exhibit specific semantic distortions (Fig. 5 in paper).

3. **List-wise Learning Objectives**:
 - To improve reliability and generalizability, especially for smaller datasets, the training uses list-wise learning objectives.
 - The objective function combines a rank loss (L_rank) and a linear loss (L_lin), emphasizing the rank relations among scores rather than just independent score values.

#### 3.3. TVGE Dataset: Text-to-Video Generation Evaluation Dataset

1. **Motivation**: To evaluate T2VScore's reliability, to adapt the quality metric to the unique distortion patterns of generated videos, and to provide an open benchmark.
2. **Collection**: 2543 text-guided videos were collected, generated by 5 popular T2V models (Floor33, Gen2, ModelScope, Pika, ZeroScope) using a diverse set of prompts from EvalCrafter.
3. **Subjective Studies**:
 - Each video was independently annotated by 10 experienced human subjects.
 - Ratings were on a five-point Likert-like scale for both text alignment and video quality.
 - Annotators received training with clear criteria and examples for each scale and for identifying common video distortions.
 - Crucially, the text prompt was *only* displayed for alignment annotation, ensuring quality scores focused solely on visual fidelity.
4. **Analysis**: The dataset revealed generally low human ratings for generated videos and a low correlation (0.223 Spearman’s ρ, 0.152 Kendall’s ϕ) between perceived alignment and quality, supporting their independent assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the TVGE dataset provided several key insights into the limitations of existing metrics and demonstrated the performance of the proposed T2VScore.

#### 4.1. Inconsistency of Existing Metrics with Human Judgment

- Traditional objective metrics for text-video alignment (CLIP Score, X-CLIP Score, BLIP-BLEU, mPLUG-BLEU) exhibited low correlation with human judgments on the TVGE dataset.
- Video-based CLIP models (X-CLIP Score) sometimes showed even lower correlations than image-based counterparts, attributed to their training scope on specific datasets (e.g., Kinetics) that are insufficient for broad-domain video understanding.
- BLEU scores, while standard in NLP, were ineffective for text-video alignment due to the inherent difficulty of accurate video captioning. These findings reinforce prior research indicating a mismatch between automated metrics and human perception in T2V evaluation.

#### 4.2. T2VScore-A (Text Alignment) Performance

- **Model Agnostic Nature**: T2VScore-A's performance was observed to be dependent on the capabilities of the underlying multimodal large language models (MLLMs) utilized for visual question answering.
- **MLLM Comparison**: While open-source image LLMs (InstructBLIP, mPLUG-OWL2-I) showed decent results, they were less effective than advanced open-source video LLMs (mPLUG-OWL2-V) in video-based QA tasks. However, a notable performance disparity existed between open-source MLLMs and proprietary models.
- **Superiority of GPT-4V**: GPT-4V demonstrated superior performance in video question answering, achieving the highest correlation with human judgment among all tested models (Spearman’s ρ = 0.486, Kendall’s τ = 0.360), significantly outperforming other models.
- **Effect of Auxiliary Trajectory**: The incorporation of auxiliary trajectory data (e.g., from CoTracker) was shown to enhance fine-grained temporal understanding. This capability allowed models to accurately identify subtle camera movements (e.g., panning, rotating) and object motions, leading to improved VQA accuracy and better alignment with human judgment compared to models without trajectory input. For GPT-4V, trajectory data improved VQA accuracy from 0.4791 to 0.5765 and increased Spearman's ρ from 0.4454 to 0.4859.

#### 4.3. T2VScore-Q (Video Quality) Performance

- **Zero-shot Setting Performance**: In a zero-shot setting (without training on any generated videos), T2VScore-Q demonstrated substantial improvements over existing state-of-the-art VQA methods.
 - Both the fine-tuned technical and semantic experts within T2VScore-Q notably improved upon their corresponding baselines (FAST-VQA, MaxVQA, CLIP variants).
 - The ensemble of these limited experts resulted in a significant performance gain, with T2VScore-Q achieving over 20% improvements in all correlation coefficients (Spearman’s ρ, Kendall’s τ, Pearson’s ρ) compared to other VQA approaches. For instance, Spearman’s ρ improved from baselines ranging 0.3164-0.4110 to 0.5029.
- **Cross-model Generalization (Adaptation)**: To address concerns about evaluator generalization to unseen models, T2VScore-Q was adapted using videos from one generative model and tested on videos from the remaining four.
 - The prefix-tuning-based adaptation strategy proved effective, demonstrating an average gain of 11.2% in Spearman’s ρ, 11.2% in Kendall’s τ, and 10.6% in Pearson’s ρ across various cross-model scenarios. This indicates T2VScore-Q's ability to robustly generalize to novel generation models not seen during its adaptation, functioning as a reliable open-set quality metric.
- **Ablation Studies**: Experiments confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed components within T2VScore-Q. Fine-tuning both the technical and semantic experts significantly improved their individual accuracy and the overall accuracy of the combined T2VScore-Q.

#### 4.4. TVGE Dataset Insights

- Analysis of human annotations on the TVGE dataset indicated that current text-to-video generation methods generally face challenges, receiving lower-than-average human ratings for both alignment (mean = 2.59) and quality (mean = 2.77) on a 5-point scale.
- A very low correlation (Spearman’s ρ = 0.223, Kendall’s ϕ = 0.152) was observed between human-rated text alignment and video quality scores. This finding supports the independent consideration and evaluation of these two dimensions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the evaluation of text-to-video (T2V) generation models, addressing several critical limitations of current assessment methodologies.

#### 5.1. Addressing Critical Evaluation Gaps

The T2VScore offers a more comprehensive and reliable automated evaluation metric by distinctly assessing both text-video alignment and video quality. This dual-criteria approach directly addresses the shortcomings of existing metrics that often neglect temporal consistency or fail to accurately capture the fidelity of generated content against textual prompts. By providing a metric that aligns more closely with human perception, the research fills a crucial gap in the T2V development pipeline.

#### 5.2. Advancing Generative AI Research

The introduction of T2VScore provides a standardized and robust benchmark for comparing and evaluating T2V models. This enables researchers and developers to more accurately identify the strengths and weaknesses of different generation architectures and techniques. For instance, the T2VScore-A's reliance on multimodal large language models (MLLMs) highlights the need for continued improvement in MLLMs' temporal understanding capabilities, thereby driving advancements in foundational models.

#### 5.3. Enabling Open Benchmarking and Future Adaptability

The open-sourcing of the TVGE dataset is a significant contribution. It establishes the first dedicated open-source dataset with human judgments for benchmarking and enhancing T2V evaluation metrics. This resource will foster collaborative research, allowing the community to develop and validate new metrics against a consistent human-annotated standard. Furthermore, the demonstrated cross-model generalization of T2VScore-Q suggests its potential to remain effective as new T2V generation models emerge, providing a continually relevant evaluation tool.

#### 5.4. Guiding Model Development

By offering fine-grained scores for alignment and quality, T2VScore can provide actionable insights to T2V model developers. This allows for targeted improvements, helping to ascertain whether a model's limitations lie in faithfully interpreting the text prompt or in synthesizing high-quality, coherent video content. This diagnostic capability can accelerate the iterative refinement of T2V systems.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact on Content Creation

Ultimately, by improving the evaluation of T2V models, this work indirectly contributes to the development of more controllable, accurate, and high-quality video generation tools. Such tools have potential applications across various domains, including entertainment, education, content marketing, and creative industries, by enabling more efficient and precise automated video creation.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges that T2VScore-A's performance is heavily dependent on the current capabilities of MLLMs, particularly their limitations in fine-grained temporal understanding. Future improvements in MLLMs are anticipated to enhance the stability and reliability of T2VScore-A. Additionally, the authors plan to continuously update the TVGE dataset with results from new open-source T2V models, ensuring its relevance as the field evolves.
