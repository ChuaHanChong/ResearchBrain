# VoLN: Vision-Only Long-Horizon Navigation---Paradigm, Benchmark, and Method

> [!summary] Summary
> VoLN introduces a paradigm and benchmark for vision-only long-horizon navigation using aerial agents in complex 3D environments, evaluating agents' ability to infer routes solely from visual cues. Their VoLN-MLLM method, a two-stage visual-semantic planner, achieved superior success rates and trajectory quality compared to baselines on this challenging task.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) benchmarks confound an agent's visual navigation ability with its capacity to leverage explicit, externally supplied route-level information.
- There is a lack of focus on long-horizon navigation where agents must actively detect, interpret, and select route-relevant information online from locally observable in-scene visual cues.
- Applying existing navigation paradigms to Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) introduces challenges due to continuous 3D space, out-of-view destinations, and large viewpoint variations.

## Method

- Proposes the ==Vision-Only Long-Horizon Navigation (VoLN)== paradigm, requiring agents to infer routes solely from visual goal sets, egocentric RGB observations, and proprioception.
- Establishes **VoLN-UAV**, a benchmark for aerial navigation featuring long-horizon goal-directed flight, continuous 3D motion, and context-dependent semantic beacons (active cues and passive distractors) in diverse simulated environments.
- Introduces **VoLN-MLLM**, a ==two-stage visual-semantic planning== framework that aligns ==DINO== visual features with a structured ==CLIP== semantic space and uses a ==LoRA==-adapted Vicuna LLM to predict short-horizon waypoint segments.

## Results

- VoLN-MLLM consistently achieved the highest reported success rates (SR) and superior trajectory quality (nDTW, SPL) across all difficulty levels on the VoLN-UAV benchmark, outperforming existing visual-goal navigation baselines.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that visual-semantic alignment via CLIP-teacher supervision was the most critical component, with its removal causing SR to drop from **5.7%** to **2.3%**, the most significant performance degradation observed.
- The DINO backbone provided more robust visual representations and efficient inference compared to a direct CLIP encoder (cycle time **1.42s** vs **1.98s**), while LoRA adaptation was essential for reliable planner output and improved trajectory fitting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Removing external guidance, such as language instructions or GPS, is crucial for isolating and advancing an agent's intrinsic visual reasoning and perception for autonomous navigation in unknown environments.
> - Aligning self-supervised visual features with a structured semantic space is critical for effective visual grounding and interpretation of locally observable in-scene cues for long-horizon planning.
> - Adapting large language models (LLMs) with LoRA enables them to serve as effective planners by integrating observational history, goal views, and retrieved semantic tokens for robust waypoint prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied navigation has seen substantial progress, particularly in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), where agents interpret natural-language instructions to perform navigation tasks. Much of this prior work has focused on indoor, ground-level agents or aerial agents operating in structured environments with explicit route instructions. These instructions commonly encode spatial information such as orientation, metric distance, or route structure, often derived from pre-planned trajectories or global scene knowledge.

A primary challenge in this existing paradigm is that these route-level priors are frequently not directly observable through onboard sensing during real-world deployment in open, GPS-denied environments. Consequently, benchmark performance in such VLN settings can confound an agent's fundamental visual navigation capabilities with its ability to leverage externally supplied route information. This makes it difficult to isolate and improve the core visual reasoning and perception required for autonomous navigation.

A complementary line of research, visual-goal navigation, focuses on guiding agents to a visually specified destination. However, these methods often emphasize terminal goal grounding rather than the detection, interpretation, and selection of route-relevant information encountered online throughout a long trajectory. Similarly, advancements in aerial navigation have addressed motion planning and semantic task execution, but existing aerial VLN benchmarks commonly provide explicit route information.

This work introduces Vision-Only Long-Horizon Navigation (VoLN) as a paradigm intended to address the limitations of relying on explicit, externally supplied route information. By removing language instructions and global guidance, VoLN aims to shift the focus to an agent's capacity to infer navigation cues purely from locally observable in-scene visual evidence. This aligns with the broader objective of developing more robust and generalizable embodied AI systems capable of operating in unknown or unstructured environments without prior maps or detailed instructions, thereby advancing autonomous navigation in conditions where external aids are limited or unavailable. The work thus complements existing VLN research by focusing on a distinct and challenging subset of the navigation problem, specifically emphasizing reactive visual interpretation over proactive instruction following.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of embodied navigation by proposing a new paradigm and an associated benchmark that focuses on vision-only, long-horizon navigation in complex 3D environments. This initiative is motivated by several limitations observed in existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) frameworks:

- **Confounded Performance Evaluation:** Current VLN benchmarks often provide agents with explicit route-level information (e.g., orientation, distance, layout) through natural-language instructions or global guidance signals. While effective for specifying routes, these quantities are typically not directly available from onboard sensing during real-world deployment, especially in open, GPS-denied environments. As a result, benchmark performance jointly reflects an agent's visual navigation ability and its capacity to utilize these explicit route structures, making it challenging to disentangle and assess each contribution independently. The authors aim to remove this confounding factor to enable a clearer evaluation of pure visual navigation capabilities.
- **Lack of Online Cue Interpretation:** Many existing navigation tasks focus on reaching a terminal visual goal or following pre-defined instructions. There is a comparative lack of research into long-horizon navigation scenarios where agents must actively detect, interpret, and select route-relevant information *online* from locally observable in-scene cues as they traverse an environment. This capability is crucial for truly autonomous agents operating in dynamic or unfamiliar settings.
- **Specific Challenges of Aerial Navigation:** Applying existing navigation paradigms to Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) introduces unique challenges. UAVs operate in continuous 3D space, where destinations frequently lie outside the current field of view, and long-range trajectories involve substantial changes in position, altitude, and viewpoint. These conditions stress cross-view re-identification, robust cue selection, and stable closed-loop control, which existing benchmarks may not fully capture when providing explicit route guidance.

To address these motivations, the research establishes three key objectives:

1. **Formulate VoLN Paradigm:** To introduce Vision-Only Long-Horizon Navigation (VoLN), a paradigm where goal views visually specify the destination, and all route-relevant information must be detected, interpreted, and selected by the agent solely from locally observable in-scene cues, without external instructions or global signals.
2. **Establish VoLN-UAV Benchmark:** To instantiate the VoLN paradigm specifically for aerial navigation by creating VoLN-UAV. This benchmark is designed to feature:
 - Long-horizon goal-directed flight.
 - Continuous 3D motion with large viewpoint and scale variations.
 - Context-dependent beacon selection, incorporating active (task-relevant) and passive (distractor) semantic beacons within diverse simulated environments.
 - Robust evaluation metrics for goal convergence, trajectory quality, and closed-loop reliability.
3. **Provide Initial Methodology (VoLN-MLLM):** To propose VoLN-MLLM, a two-stage visual-semantic planning framework as an initial reference baseline. This method aims to align self-supervised visual features with a structured semantic space and integrate observational history, goal views, retrieved semantic tokens, and proprioception to predict short-horizon waypoint segments in a closed-loop manner. This baseline is intended to provide an initial evaluation of the VoLN paradigm and highlight existing challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the VoLN paradigm, instantiates it with the VoLN-UAV benchmark, and provides an initial solution, VoLN-MLLM.

#### 3.1. The VoLN Paradigm

VoLN is formulated as a goal-directed, long-horizon embodied navigation paradigm. During execution, the agent's policy receives only a visual goal set (images near the destination), egocentric RGB observations, and proprioceptive states (IMU measurements, altitude, velocity, orientation). Crucially, it is deprived of externally supplied task-level route instructions, GPS, global maps, or shortest-path annotations. The agent must process locally observable in-scene cues (including semantic beacons), interpret their meaning in relation to the goal views and interaction history, and select relevant cues online to generate actions. An episode is successful if the agent issues a stop action within a predefined goal region within a maximum number of steps.

#### 3.2. The VoLN-UAV Benchmark

VoLN-UAV instantiates the VoLN paradigm for aerial navigation in continuous 3D space.
- **Simulation Environments:** Built using Unreal Engine and Microsoft AirSim, the benchmark includes 17 diverse environments. These scenes, adapted from existing aerial VLN benchmarks (AerialVLN, OpenUAV) and custom-built, cover natural and built settings (deserts, forests, mountains, urban canyons, tunnels, industrial corridors) with variations in layout, visibility, altitude, and landmark density.
- **Benchmark Construction Pipeline:**
 - **Trajectory Collection:** Reference routes are sampled from a pool of predefined routes and custom routes recorded by human operators. Episodes are stratified into Easy (<300m), Normal (300-450m), and Hard (≥450m) based on reference-path length.
 - **Beacon Augmentation:** Each reference trajectory is augmented with 3-5 active semantic beacons placed at decision points, serving as task-relevant cues. Additionally, approximately 150 passive beacons are populated in each environment as fixed semantic clutter and distractors. Beacons fall into four categories: directional guidance, warning cues, environmental distractors, and contextual cues. Agents perceive beacons solely through egocentric observations.
 - **Multimodal Rollout and Annotation:** For each reference trajectory, synchronized egocentric RGB observations and proprioceptive states are recorded at 2-second intervals. The final three RGB observations form the episode-level visual goal set. Each step's observation and proprioception are paired with the goal set to form a training sample, with the subsequent H (8) states along the reference route defining the short-horizon waypoint target.
 - **Dataset Split:** The dataset comprises 7,210 episodes. It is split into Train (5,047 episodes from 12 environments), Validation-Seen (1,082 episodes from 5 training environments with disjoint trajectories), and Test-Unseen (1,081 episodes from 5 held-out environments).

#### 3.3. The VoLN-MLLM Method

VoLN-MLLM is a two-stage visual-semantic planning framework designed as a reference baseline:
- **Phase I: Visual-Semantic Alignment:**
 - A frozen DINOv3 ViT-B16 backbone extracts visual features from an observation.
 - A lightweight trainable adapter maps these features into the CLIP image-embedding space, producing a normalized student embedding (`E_stu`).
 - This adapter is trained using a distillation objective (cosine distance loss) against the normalized teacher embedding (`E_clip`) from a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder for the same image.
- **Phase II: Trajectory Decoding:**
 - **Semantic Tokenization:** A fixed semantic bank of textual category descriptors, pre-encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, is used. At each step, the aligned visual embedding (`E_stu`) is compared with bank embeddings. The top-k similar entries' category identifiers are encoded with the planner's tokenizer, and their similarity scores are mapped to learned confidence embeddings, forming visual-semantic tokens.
 - **Planner Input:** Aligned visual features from a fixed window of recent observations and the goal views (projected to the planner's embedding dimension), proprioception (mapped by a separate projector), retrieved visual-semantic tokens, and learned structural tokens are concatenated.
 - **Planner Architecture:** A frozen Vicuna-7B-v1.5 language model serves as the planning backbone. Its attention and feed-forward projections are adapted using low-rank adaptation (LoRA) modules.
 - **Output:** The hidden state of a planning token is fed to a trajectory head, which predicts eight relative three-dimensional waypoints in the UAV's body frame, and a binary stopping head, which predicts a stop signal.
 - **Training and Inference:** The visual/state projectors, trajectory/stopping heads, and LoRA parameters are trained jointly. The trajectory head is supervised with an ℓ1 loss against demonstrated waypoint sequences, and the stopping head uses binary cross-entropy. During inference, the policy stops if the stopping probability exceeds a threshold (tuned on Validation-Seen); otherwise, it executes the predicted waypoint segment via a low-level flight controller and replans.

#### 3.4. Baselines and Evaluation Metrics

VoLN-MLLM is compared against a random policy and three visual-goal (VG) variants of instruction-following architectures: Seq2Seq-VG, CMA-VG, and LAG-VG. All learned methods share the same visual encoder, waypoint action space, and training targets, receiving identical observation history, goal views, proprioception, and semantic tokens.
Evaluation metrics include Success Rate (SR), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), Navigation Error (NE), normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW), and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VoLN-MLLM and baselines was conducted on the Validation-Seen (VS) and Test-Unseen (TU) splits of the VoLN-UAV benchmark, across Easy, Normal, and Hard difficulty levels.

- **Overall Performance:** VoLN-MLLM consistently yielded the highest reported point estimates across all evaluation metrics (SR, OSR, NE, nDTW, SPL) for all difficulty levels on both Validation-Seen and Test-Unseen splits. On the Test-Unseen split, VoLN-MLLM achieved success rates (SR) of 7.4% for Easy, 4.5% for Normal, and 1.8% for Hard episodes. This compares with the strongest baseline, LAG-VG, which achieved SRs of 2.3% (Easy), 1.2% (Normal), and 0.4% (Hard) on the same split. While VoLN-MLLM exhibited a notable advantage over the baselines, the absolute success rates, particularly for the Hard episodes, indicate that vision-only long-horizon navigation remains a challenging task.

- **Trajectory Quality and Efficiency:** Beyond terminal success, VoLN-MLLM demonstrated superior trajectory quality and efficiency. It recorded lower Navigation Error (NE), indicating that its final positions were closer to the goal. A higher normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW) score for VoLN-MLLM suggested better agreement between its executed trajectories and the reference paths. Furthermore, VoLN-MLLM achieved the highest Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), reflecting a strong combined performance in both navigation success and path efficiency across all difficulty levels and evaluation splits.

- **Ablation Studies:** An ablation study on the Test-Unseen split investigated the contribution of key components of VoLN-MLLM:
 - **No-Align:** Removing the CLIP-teacher supervision for visual-semantic alignment resulted in the most significant performance degradation, with SR dropping from 5.7% to 2.3% and nDTW decreasing from 45.8 to 29.6. This highlights the importance of aligning DINO visual features with a structured semantic space for effective grounding.
 - **No-LoRA:** Freezing the planner backbone by removing the LoRA adaptation branches led to the highest Execution Error Rate (EER) of 5.8% and the lowest nDTW of 27.2. This suggests that adapting the language model planner through LoRA contributes to output reliability and improved trajectory fitting.
 - **CLIP-Input:** Replacing the DINO backbone with a CLIP image encoder increased the cycle time from 1.42s to 1.98s and reduced the SR to 2.9%. This indicates that the DINO backbone contributes to more robust visual representations and efficient inference for the task.

- **Qualitative Analysis and Physical Demonstration:** Qualitative examples illustrated successful rollouts where the agent accurately followed active beacons and reached the goal region, contrasting with failure cases where trajectories diverged near passive distractors (billboards). A preliminary physical demonstration in a controlled indoor testbed (a scaled urban scene with miniature beacons) provided qualitative evidence for the potential instantiation of the VoLN task interface and closed-loop navigation pipeline on a physical platform.

In summary, VoLN-MLLM demonstrated improved performance over existing visual-goal navigation baselines on the newly introduced VoLN-UAV benchmark, showing better success rates, closer goal proximity, and higher trajectory fidelity. The ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of visual-semantic alignment, planner adaptation, and robust visual feature extraction. Despite these advancements, the overall low success rates underscore the inherent challenges in long-horizon, vision-only navigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel paradigm and benchmark that contributes to the field of embodied navigation by addressing a fundamental challenge in current approaches.

- **Reframing Embodied Navigation:** The VoLN paradigm offers a distinct and rigorous formulation for long-horizon navigation. By explicitly removing external guidance such as natural-language instructions or global positioning systems (GPS), the work focuses on the agent's intrinsic ability to interpret locally observable visual cues for route planning and execution. This re-formulation isolates the critical problem of visual reasoning and perception, enabling a more direct study and advancement of navigation capabilities in GPS-denied or unknown environments.
- **Comprehensive Benchmark for Aerial Autonomy:** VoLN-UAV provides a comprehensive and challenging benchmark specifically designed for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). Its features, including continuous 3D motion, large viewpoint changes, long-horizon trajectories, and the presence of both active (task-relevant) and passive (distractor) semantic beacons, closely mimic complexities encountered in real-world aerial navigation. The inclusion of held-out unseen environments in the Test-Unseen split promotes the development of generalizable navigation policies. This benchmark is poised to drive significant research in robust and autonomous UAV operation, particularly for applications like search and rescue, surveillance, and exploration in environments where external navigation aids are unreliable or unavailable.
- **Initial Methodology and Performance Insights:** VoLN-MLLM serves as a valuable initial reference baseline for the VoLN-UAV benchmark. Its two-stage visual-semantic planning framework demonstrates the potential of aligning self-supervised visual features with semantic spaces and leveraging large language models for waypoint prediction. The performance of VoLN-MLLM, while outperforming adapted baselines, highlights that the problem remains largely unsolved. The low absolute success rates, especially for harder episodes, explicitly reveal substantial remaining challenges in areas such as long-horizon evidence integration, robust cross-view goal matching, and maintaining closed-loop stability over extended periods. These identified challenges provide clear directions for future research efforts in embodied AI.
- **Foundation for Generalizable Autonomy:** The focus on vision-only navigation is critical for developing truly autonomous agents that can adapt to diverse and unpredictable real-world scenarios without relying on pre-existing maps or human intervention. The insights gained from VoLN, particularly concerning the interpretation of in-scene cues and robust decision-making, could be transferable to other embodied agents beyond UAVs, such as ground robots navigating unstructured terrains.
- **Proof-of-Concept for Real-World Implementation:** The preliminary physical testbed demonstration, while qualitative, provides encouraging evidence that the core principles and task interface of VoLN are not purely theoretical and can be instantiated on physical robotic platforms, suggesting a path towards real-world applicability.

In essence, this work does not merely introduce a new dataset or model; it proposes a fundamental shift in how embodied navigation is formulated and evaluated, emphasizing intrinsic visual intelligence over reliance on explicit external guidance. This shift has the potential to accelerate the development of more capable, adaptable, and truly autonomous robotic systems.
