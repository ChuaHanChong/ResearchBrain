# Visual Prompt Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Visual Prompt Tuning enables efficient adaptation of large pre-trained vision Transformers to diverse downstream tasks by introducing a small set of learnable prompt tokens. This approach achieves performance that often exceeds full fine-tuning while using less than 1% of the backbone parameters, significantly reducing per-task storage and computational costs.

## Problem

- Large pre-trained Vision Transformers incur prohibitively high storage costs when fully fine-tuned for numerous downstream tasks, requiring a full copy of the model per task.
- Full fine-tuning of massive models demands significant computational resources and time, making deployment across many tasks expensive.
- Existing parameter-efficient tuning (PET) methods for vision models often compromise accuracy, failing to match the performance of full fine-tuning.

## Method

- **Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT)** introduces a small number of ==learnable, task-specific continuous prompt embeddings== into the input sequence of a pre-trained ==Vision Transformer==.
- The large Vision Transformer backbone is kept entirely ==frozen==, and only these prompts along with a lightweight classification head are updated during downstream training.
- Two architectural variants, **VPT-Shallow** (prompts at the first layer) and **VPT-Deep** (prompts at every layer), are proposed and evaluated.

## Results

- VPT-Deep achieved higher accuracy than full fine-tuning on **20 out of 24** downstream recognition tasks when adapting a ViT-B/16 model.
- The method adapted large Vision Transformers using **less than 1%** of the backbone's parameters per task, drastically reducing storage costs.
- VPT maintained its performance advantages over full fine-tuning in low-data regimes and scaled effectively with larger Transformer models (ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VPT, particularly its deep variant, often achieves higher accuracy than full fine-tuning on many vision tasks, differentiating it from typical findings in NLP prompt tuning.
> - Inserting prompts into the latent input space of multiple Transformer layers (VPT-Deep) is generally more effective than a shallow approach, with prompts in earlier layers being particularly impactful.
> - Even with a tiny fraction of trainable parameters (under 1% of backbone), VPT can effectively adapt large vision Transformers, demonstrating superior parameter efficiency compared to other methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of deep learning, particularly concerning the adaptation of large, pre-trained foundation models. The current research landscape is dominated by massive Transformer-based models, such as Vision Transformers (ViT) and Swin Transformers, which achieve state-of-the-art performance across a wide array of computer vision tasks. These models are typically pre-trained on enormous datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21k) and then adapted to specific downstream tasks.

The conventional and often most effective adaptation strategy is **full fine-tuning**, where all parameters of the pre-trained backbone are updated. While powerful, this approach has significant drawbacks:
- **High Storage Costs:** Each downstream task requires storing a separate copy of the entire model backbone, which can be hundreds of millions or even billions of parameters. This becomes prohibitively expensive and infeasible for deploying many task-specific models. For instance, a ViT-Huge model has 632 million parameters, making per-task storage a major concern.
- **Computational Expense:** Fine-tuning such massive models demands substantial computational resources (GPUs, energy), both during training and deployment.

To mitigate these issues, researchers have explored **parameter-efficient tuning (PET)** methods. In the era of Convolutional Neural Networks (ConvNets), techniques like fine-tuning only the classifier head, bias terms, or adding lightweight adapter modules were developed. However, these methods generally underperformed full fine-tuning in accuracy when applied to ConvNets. The landscape for adapting the newer, larger Transformer architectures in vision was less explored, with many existing PET methods (like Adapters and BitFit, originally from NLP) not fully demonstrating their efficacy or superiority in the vision domain.

A pivotal inspiration for Visual Prompt Tuning comes from **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**. The concept of "prompting" and "prompt tuning" revolutionized the adaptation of large language models (LLMs) like GPT-3, BERT, and T5. Instead of fine-tuning the entire LLM, prompt tuning involves prepending a small number of learnable, task-specific continuous vectors (prompts) to the input sequence, keeping the large language model backbone frozen. This approach achieved performance comparable to full fine-tuning with a tiny fraction of trainable parameters.

This paper is a significant contribution because it successfully **translates and adapts the powerful concept of prompt tuning from NLP to vision Transformers (image encoders)**. Prior work on prompting in vision-language models typically focused on the text encoder part. By demonstrating that visual prompts can effectively adapt vision Transformers, the authors open a new avenue for efficient transfer learning in computer vision. The work distinguishes itself by showing that Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) can not only match but *outperform* full fine-tuning in many scenarios, a result that notably differs from some findings in NLP prompt tuning literature. It directly addresses the scalability and deployment challenges of next-generation vision foundation models, positioning itself as a highly relevant and impactful solution in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) paper are driven by the practical challenges of adapting increasingly large and powerful pre-trained vision Transformer models to a multitude of downstream tasks.

The primary motivations are:
1. **Addressing the Storage Burden of Full Fine-Tuning:** With Vision Transformers (ViT, Swin) growing to hundreds of millions or even billions of parameters, storing a separate, fully fine-tuned copy of the backbone for every single downstream task becomes economically impractical and infeasible, especially for scenarios involving numerous applications or resource-constrained environments. The authors explicitly state their aim to "reduce per-task storage cost."
2. **Improving Efficiency Without Sacrificing Performance:** Existing parameter-efficient tuning (PET) methods for vision models (and even some adapted from NLP) often fall short of the accuracy achieved by full fine-tuning. The authors seek an alternative adaptation strategy that is both highly efficient (minimal trainable parameters per task) and highly effective (maintaining or surpassing the performance of full fine-tuning).
3. **Leveraging Advances from NLP:** Inspired by the success of "prompting" in adapting large language models, the authors were motivated to explore if a similar mechanism—inserting small, task-specific, learnable parameters into the input space while freezing the backbone—could be successfully applied to vision Transformers. This cross-modal transfer of a powerful technique is a key driver.
4. **Enabling Practical Deployment of Foundation Models:** Large pre-trained models are often referred to as "foundation models" due to their general-purpose capabilities. An efficient adaptation mechanism like VPT is crucial for unlocking their full potential and facilitating their widespread deployment across diverse vision applications without incurring prohibitive costs.
5. **Seeking a Superior Adaptation Paradigm:** The paper implicitly challenges the default "full fine-tuning" paradigm by asking, "what is the best way to adapt large pre-trained Transformers to downstream tasks in terms of effectiveness and efficiency?" The motivation is to establish a new, more optimal protocol.

In summary, the key objective is to introduce Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) as a novel, highly parameter-efficient, and surprisingly effective method for adapting large pre-trained Vision Transformers to a wide range of downstream recognition tasks, significantly reducing storage costs while often outperforming traditional full fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) methodology lies in injecting a small set of learnable, task-specific parameters, termed "prompts," into the input space of a frozen pre-trained Transformer backbone. Only these prompts and a lightweight classification head are updated during downstream training.

#### 3.1. Vision Transformer Preliminaries

The paper first establishes the standard Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, which takes an input image, divides it into fixed-sized patches, embeds these patches into a d-dimensional latent space with positional encoding, and processes them through a series of Transformer encoder layers. An additional learnable [CLS] token is appended to the sequence for classification, whose final embedding `x_N` is fed into a classification head.

#### 3.2. Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT)

VPT introduces `p` continuous embeddings (prompts), each of dimension `d`, into the Transformer's input sequence. The crucial aspect is that **only these prompts and the classification head are learnable parameters**; the entire pre-trained Transformer backbone (all its weights) remains **frozen**. This dramatically reduces the number of trainable parameters per task.

Two variants of VPT are proposed:
- **VPT-Shallow:** Prompts `P` are inserted *only into the input of the first Transformer layer (L1)*. The input to L1 becomes `[x0, P, E0]`, where `x0` is the initial [CLS] token embedding and `E0` is the sequence of image patch embeddings. For subsequent layers, the sequence propagates as usual.
- **VPT-Deep:** Prompts `P_i` are inserted *into the input of every Transformer layer (L_i)*. This means each layer `L_i` receives `[x_i-1, P_i-1, E_i-1]` as input, where `P_i-1` is a distinct set of learnable prompts for that specific layer.

#### 3.3. Storing Visual Prompts

A key advantage of VPT is the minimal storage cost per task. For a ViT-Base (86M parameters, `d=768`), 50 shallow prompts (`p=50`) add only `p * d = 0.038M` parameters, and 50 deep prompts (`N * p * d = 0.46M` for `N=12` layers) add only `0.53%` of the backbone parameters. This drastically reduces the total storage required for multiple tasks.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Pre-trained Backbones:** The study primarily uses Vision Transformers (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14) and Swin Transformers (Swin-B), all pre-trained on ImageNet-21k.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of transfer learning protocols are compared:
 - **Full Fine-tuning:** Updates all backbone and head parameters.
 - **Head-oriented methods:** Linear (only linear head), Partial-k (fine-tune last k backbone layers), Mlp-k (MLP head).
 - **Backbone-oriented methods (subset tuning/additive parameters):** Sidetune (side network), Bias (only bias terms), Adapter (insert lightweight MLP modules).
- **Downstream Tasks:**
 - **FGVC (Fine-Grained Visual Classification):** 5 datasets (e.g., CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars).
 - **VTAB-1k (Visual Task Adaptation Benchmark):** 19 diverse tasks divided into Natural, Specialized, and Structured groups, each with 1000 training examples.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Grid search for learning rate and weight decay using validation sets. Prompt length (`p`) is the only VPT-specific hyperparameter tuned.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Average test accuracy for classification, mean IoU (mIoU) for segmentation.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

The authors conduct extensive ablations to understand VPT's design choices:
- **Prompt Location:** Comparing prepending (default) versus adding prompts element-wise to embeddings, and inserting prompts in latent space (default) versus pixel space.
- **Prompt Length (`p`):** Varying the number of prompt tokens.
- **Prompt Depth:** Investigating which layers to insert prompts (e.g., only first, last few, all, or specific ranges).
- **Final Output:** How the classification head consumes the final outputs (e.g., [CLS] token, image patch pooling, prompt pooling).
- **Effect of Expanding Input Sequence Length:** Analyze if benefits come from learnable prompts or just longer sequences.
- **Sharing Prompts:** Investigate sharing prompt parameters across layers or within a layer.
- **Prompt Initialization:** Comparing random initialization (default) with class-prototype-based initialization.
- **Combining with Bias Tuning:** Exploring if VPT complements other PET methods.
- **Prompt Ensembling:** Evaluating performance of multiple prompts.
- **Computational Cost:** Measuring latency and GPU memory usage.

#### 3.6. Generalization Studies

To assess broader applicability, VPT is tested on:
- **Semantic Segmentation:** ADE20K dataset with SETR-PUP model.
- **Different Pre-training Objectives:** ViT-B trained with self-supervised MAE and MoCo v3.
- **ConvNets:** ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-Base.

This comprehensive approach allows for a thorough evaluation of VPT's effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness across various model architectures, tasks, and training conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a compelling case for Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) as a highly effective and efficient method for adapting large vision Transformer models. The main findings are:

#### 4.1. Superior Performance and Parameter Efficiency

- **Outperforms Full Fine-tuning in Many Cases:** Surprisingly, VPT-Deep achieved higher accuracy than full fine-tuning on 20 out of 24 downstream recognition tasks when adapting a ViT-B/16 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-21k. This is a significant finding, as prompt tuning in NLP typically only matches full fine-tuning, but rarely exceeds it.
- **Dramatic Reduction in Trainable Parameters:** VPT achieves these results while using a tiny fraction of the total model parameters—less than 1% of the backbone parameters for each task. This translates to significantly reduced per-task storage costs.
- **Dominates Other Parameter-Efficient Methods:** VPT-Deep consistently outperformed all other parameter-efficient tuning protocols (Linear, Partial-k, Mlp-k, Sidetune, Bias, Adapter) across all task groups, establishing it as the most effective sparse tuning strategy evaluated.

#### 4.2. Robustness Across Scales and Data Regimes

- **Low-Data Regime Superiority:** VPT-Deep maintained its advantage over full fine-tuning and other baselines, particularly in scenarios with limited downstream training data, where it demonstrated significant performance gains.
- **Scales with Model Capacity:** The benefits of VPT-Deep persisted and even strengthened with larger Transformer models (ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14), outperforming full fine-tuning on most VTAB task groups for these larger models.
- **Applicability to Hierarchical Transformers:** VPT successfully adapted to Swin Transformers, outperforming other parameter-efficient methods, though full fine-tuning still yielded the highest overall accuracy for some Swin tasks (e.g., Specialized VTAB group). Interestingly, VPT-shallow performed very well on Swin-B for Natural tasks.

#### 4.3. Insights from Ablation Studies

- **Optimal Prompt Location:** Prepending prompts to the sequence of image patch embeddings in the *latent input space* of the Transformer layers (after embedding but before attention) was found to be most effective. Introducing prompts in the pixel space or adding them element-wise significantly reduced performance.
- **Prompt Depth Matters:** Performance generally improved with increased "prompt depth" (inserting prompts into more Transformer layers), with deeper prompts being more effective. Prompts in earlier layers were found to be more crucial.
- **Prompt Length Sensitivity:** The optimal number of prompt tokens (`p`) varied by task, but even a small number of prompts (e.g., `p=1`) yielded competitive results against other baselines and sometimes full fine-tuning.
- **Random Initialization is Effective:** Contrary to some findings in NLP, random initialization of prompts generally performed better than initialization based on class prototype embeddings in vision.
- **Non-Complementary with Bias Tuning:** Combining VPT with Bias tuning did not consistently yield superior results and could sometimes even undermine VPT's performance, suggesting they might not be complementary methods.
- **[CLS] Token for Classification:** Using the final [CLS] token embedding as input to the classification head (as in standard ViT) was more effective than pooling prompt outputs or all image patch outputs.

#### 4.4. Generalization Beyond Classification and Supervised Pre-training

- **Semantic Segmentation:** VPT was shown to be competitive for semantic segmentation (ADE20K dataset with SETR-PUP), achieving results comparable to a fully fine-tuned state-of-the-art ConvNet while using significantly fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning a Transformer.
- **Self-Supervised Pre-training:** When applied to ViTs pre-trained with self-supervised objectives (MAE, MoCo v3), VPT still outperformed linear probing, but its lead over other parameter-efficient methods was less conclusive, suggesting different fine-tuning dynamics for these models.
- **ConvNets Applicability:** The concept of adding learnable prompts in the input space (as pixels) also showed promise for larger ConvNets like ConvNeXt-B, sometimes outperforming full fine-tuning, but was less consistently effective for smaller ConvNets like ResNet-50.

#### 4.5. Efficiency and Visualization

- **Practical Computational Cost:** Despite the theoretical quadratic complexity of self-attention with input sequence length, empirical measurements showed that doubling the sequence length (e.g., with more prompts) only approximately doubled inference latency and GPU memory, not quadrupled. An alternative implementation (VPT-prefix) can significantly reduce inference cost.
- **Feature Separability:** t-SNE visualizations demonstrated that VPT, particularly VPT-Deep, produced linearly separable representations of classes, similar to or even better than full fine-tuning, indicating its effectiveness in learning task-specific features.

In essence, the main findings establish VPT as a highly efficient and often superior alternative to full fine-tuning for large vision Transformers, robust across various conditions, and a strong candidate for practical deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) represents a significant advancement in the field of transfer learning for computer vision, with far-reaching potential impacts:

#### 5.1. A Paradigm Shift in Vision Transformer Adaptation

VPT challenges the long-standing dogma that full fine-tuning is the gold standard for adapting pre-trained models. By demonstrating that a tiny fraction of learnable parameters (less than 1% of the backbone) can not only match but often *outperform* full fine-tuning, VPT proposes a new, more efficient, and effective paradigm for leveraging large vision Transformers. This result is particularly impactful as it differentiates visual prompting from its NLP counterpart, where prompt tuning typically only *matches* full fine-tuning performance.

#### 5.2. Dramatically Improved Resource Efficiency

The most immediate and tangible impact of VPT is the drastic reduction in storage costs per downstream task. For large foundation models with hundreds of millions of parameters, storing a separate copy for each task is prohibitive. VPT allows for the re-use of a single frozen backbone, storing only task-specific prompts and a small head. This democratizes access to and deployment of state-of-the-art large models for organizations with limited computational or storage resources, making multi-task model serving much more feasible. It also lowers the carbon footprint associated with training and deploying numerous large models.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Scalability and Accessibility of Foundation Models

As vision foundation models continue to grow in size and complexity, efficient adaptation methods are crucial for their practical utility. VPT ensures that these ever-growing models can be readily adapted to diverse applications without necessitating proportional increases in resource investment for each new task. This will accelerate the adoption of foundation models across various industries and research domains.

#### 5.4. Strong Performance in Low-Data Regimes

VPT's demonstrated strength in low-data settings is particularly impactful for real-world applications where large, labeled datasets are scarce or expensive to acquire. This allows high-performing models to be deployed even in scenarios with limited training data, a common challenge in specialized fields like medical imaging or remote sensing.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research and Understanding

The paper raises several intriguing questions and opens new avenues for research:
- **Discrepancies between Visual and Textual Prompting:** The differences observed (VPT often outperforming full fine-tuning, efficacy of random initialization, importance of early layer prompts) suggest fundamental differences between how vision and language Transformers process and learn from prompts. This warrants deeper theoretical and empirical investigation.
- **Fine-tuning Dynamics of Self-supervised Models:** The varied performance of VPT on models pre-trained with different self-supervised objectives highlights that optimal adaptation strategies may be dependent on the pre-training method, offering a rich area for future exploration.
- **Optimal Prompt Design and Strategies:** Further research can explore more sophisticated prompt designs, adaptive prompt lengths, or dynamic prompt generation for different vision tasks, including beyond classification (e.g., detection, generation).
- **Broadening Applicability:** While shown promising for ConvNeXt, fully understanding VPT's transferability to diverse architectures and a wider array of vision tasks (e.g., video understanding, 3D vision) remains an open challenge.

In conclusion, "Visual Prompt Tuning" provides a powerful, efficient, and surprisingly effective method for adapting large vision Transformers. Its ability to outperform full fine-tuning while drastically reducing resource requirements marks it as a pivotal development, promising to accelerate the deployment and practical impact of foundation models across the computer vision landscape.
