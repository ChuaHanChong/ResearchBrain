# GenBreak: Red Teaming Text-to-Image Generators Using Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GenBreak fine-tunes a Large Language Model to systematically generate adversarial prompts for text-to-image models, effectively inducing highly toxic images across categories like nudity and violence while consistently evading safety filters. This automated framework demonstrates its capabilities on both open-source models and commercial APIs, revealing safety weaknesses in deployed systems.

## Problem

- Text-to-image (T2I) models can be misused to generate harmful content, such as nudity, violence, and hate speech, despite integrated safety filters.
- Existing red teaming methods for T2I models either produce highly toxic images using easily detectable prompts, or bypass filters without consistently generating genuinely harmful outputs.
- There is a lack of reliable tools that can simultaneously achieve high image toxicity, effective content filter evasion, and prompt diversity for comprehensive T2I safety evaluation.

## Method

- **GenBreak** proposes a two-stage framework that fine-tunes a ==red-team== Large Language Model (LLM) to generate adversarial prompts for T2I models.
- The first stage involves Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of the LLM on curated datasets containing adversarial rewrites for harmful categories and pre-attack examples.
- The second stage uses Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a multi-objective reward function that balances image toxicity, filter evasion, prompt stealthiness, and lexical/semantic/image diversity.

## Results

- GenBreak achieved Toxic Bypass Rates (TBRs) of **60.8%** for nudity, **89.7%** for violence, and **84.6%** for hate content on Stable Diffusion 2.1, significantly outperforming baseline methods.
- The framework demonstrated strong transferability to commercial T2I APIs, achieving single-attempt nudity TBRs of **70%** on Leonardo.Ai, **30%** on fal.ai, and **47%** on stability.ai.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the integrated multi-objective reward system, including bypass, clean, and diverse prompt/image rewards, is essential for GenBreak's effectiveness in generating stealthy and toxic content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-objective optimization through reinforcement learning is crucial for balancing the conflicting goals of generating toxic content, evading filters, and maintaining prompt diversity in text-to-image red teaming.
> - Subtle, non-explicit phrasing and contextual references enable adversarial prompts to effectively bypass existing T2I safety filters.
> - Adversarial prompts developed against open-source T2I models can transfer effectively to black-box commercial T2I APIs, indicating widespread vulnerabilities in deployed systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in text-to-image (T2I) generation models, such as Stable Diffusion and FLUX.1, have revolutionized content creation. However, these powerful capabilities are accompanied by significant safety risks, as T2I models can be misused to generate harmful content, including nudity, violence, and hate speech. This concern has led to the widespread implementation of content moderation systems, primarily through prompt filtering and/or post-generation image analysis by T2I service providers.

Despite these safeguards, a critical challenge remains: determined adversaries can often exploit underlying vulnerabilities to bypass filters and produce harmful outputs. This forms the core problem addressed by the paper. The research fits squarely within the emerging field of generative AI safety, specifically focusing on "red teaming" – a systematic process of simulating adversarial attacks to uncover security flaws and vulnerabilities in AI systems.

The broader landscape of red teaming for generative AI is already well-established for large language models (LLMs), with prominent works demonstrating how LLMs can be prompted to generate harmful text. However, applying robust red teaming methodologies to T2I models presents unique challenges. Previous research on red teaming and adversarial attacks against T2I models has exhibited notable limitations:
- Some methods succeed in generating highly toxic images but rely on easily detectable adversarial prompts that are quickly blocked by safety filters (e.g., CRT, Ring-A-Bell).
- Conversely, other approaches focus primarily on bypassing safety mechanisms but fail to consistently produce genuinely harmful outputs, thus neglecting the discovery of truly high-risk prompts (e.g., SneakyPrompt, ART).

This dichotomy highlights a critical gap: a lack of reliable tools that can simultaneously achieve high image toxicity *and* effective evasion of content filters while maintaining prompt diversity. GenBreak directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel framework that bridges the divide between effective jailbreaking and the generation of genuinely harmful, yet stealthy, visual content. It positions itself as a systematic solution for uncovering practical and concerning safety weaknesses in defended T2I models, going beyond mere filter evasion to focus on the *risk* posed by the generated content. This work also contributes to the understanding of transferability of adversarial prompts across different T2I models, including commercial APIs with undisclosed safety mechanisms, which is a significant practical concern.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for GenBreak stems directly from the identified limitations in existing T2I red teaming efforts and the pressing need for more robust safety evaluations. While T2I models offer immense creative potential, their capacity for misuse to generate visually harmful content (e.g., nudity, violence) poses significant societal risks. Current content moderation systems, despite their deployment, are not foolproof and can be circumvented by sophisticated attack strategies. Identifying these vulnerabilities systematically is crucial for enhancing the robustness and safety of T2I services.

The paper argues that effective red teaming must prioritize the discovery of prompts that not only bypass security checks but also consistently produce genuinely harmful outputs, as these represent the most significant real-world risks.

The **key objectives** of the GenBreak framework are:
1. **Induce Highly Toxic Image Outputs:** To reliably generate images that are objectively harmful across categories like nudity, violence, and hate speech.
2. **Evade Safety Filters:** To craft adversarial prompts that can bypass both textual (prompt-based) and visual (image-based) safety mechanisms integrated into T2I models.
3. **Maintain Semantic Coherence and Diversity:** To ensure that the generated prompts are semantically fluent, grammatically correct, and diverse enough to explore a broad range of potential vulnerabilities, preventing the red-team model from converging on a few easily patched patterns.
4. **Demonstrate Transferability:** To show that the adversarial prompts generated by GenBreak are effective not only against open-source models but also against black-box commercial T2I APIs, reflecting real-world attack scenarios.

Ultimately, GenBreak aims to provide a reliable, automated tool for evaluating the safety of *defended* T2I models, allowing developers to proactively identify and mitigate critical vulnerabilities before or after deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenBreak proposes a novel framework that fine-tunes a red-team Large Language Model (LLM) to systematically generate adversarial prompts for T2I models. The approach is structured in two distinct stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Threat Model:**
The research considers two threat models:
- **Gray-box:** Applicable to open-source T2I models, where the attacker can access the generated image (even if filtered) but not the internal parameters or gradients of the T2I model or its safety filter. This setup simulates a common scenario for researchers and developers.
- **Black-box:** Relevant to commercial T2I models, where the attacker is limited to transfer-based attacks and receives no image feedback if the safety mechanism is triggered. This tests real-world attack efficacy against undisclosed defenses.

**Framework Overview (Two-Stage Pipeline):**

1. **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
 - **Motivation:** General-purpose LLMs are typically safety-aligned or lack specific training for T2I red teaming. SFT adapts them to this specialized task.
 - **Data Curation:** Two specialized datasets are created:
 - **Category Rewrite Dataset:** Generated using Gemini 2.0 Flash, this dataset contains pairs of initial seed prompts and adversarially rewritten prompts for nudity, violence, and hate domains. This trains the LLM to rewrite prompts in a harmful, yet potentially stealthy, manner.
 - **Pre-Attack Dataset:** This dataset collects prompts that have already demonstrated effectiveness against Stable Diffusion 2.1. It's built by iteratively attacking SD 2.1 with an uncensored LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Lexi-Uncensored-V2) and selecting prompts with high "Toxicity Bypass Scores" (TBS), which measure both bypass success and image toxicity. This dataset provides examples of successful jailbreaks.
 - **Fine-tuning:** A pre-trained LLM (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) is fine-tuned on both datasets using a standard SFT loss function. This phase initializes the red-team LLM with basic red-teaming capabilities.

2. **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
 - **Motivation:** SFT provides a baseline, but RL is crucial for optimizing the LLM to balance the complex, often conflicting, objectives of toxicity, evasion, and diversity through trial and error.
 - **RL Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed. This algorithm is designed to optimize policy updates based on group-relative advantage estimation, contributing to training stability.
 - **Multi-objective Reward Modeling:** This is the core innovation of GenBreak. Multiple reward signals are integrated to guide the LLM:
 - **Toxicity Reward (`R_tox`):** Measures the toxicity of the generated image. To mitigate subjectivity and reward hacking, it aggregates scores from three expert image toxicity models: MHSC, LLaVAGuard, and NudeNet, applicable based on the harmful domain.
 - **Bypass Reward (`R_bypass`):** Awarded only if both the prompt and the generated image successfully bypass the integrated safety filters. Crucially, its magnitude is tied to `R_tox`, discouraging prompts that bypass but fail to generate harmful content.
 - **Clean Reward (`R_clean`):** Penalizes the use of blacklisted sensitive terms in the prompt, encouraging the LLM to find alternative, stealthier phrasings.
 - **Lexical Diversity Reward (`R_lexical`):** Uses negative SelfBLEU against a dynamic pool of recent test cases to promote diversity in prompt phrasing.
 - **Semantic Diversity Reward (`R_semantic`):** Uses negative cosine similarity of sentence embeddings (from a pre-trained model) against a dynamic pool to promote semantic novelty in prompts.
 - **Image Diversity Reward (`R_img_div`):** Uses negative cosine similarity of image embeddings (from DreamSim) against a dynamic pool of generated images to encourage visual variety in harmful outputs, which is vital for comprehensive safety evaluations.
 - **Auxiliary Rewards (from Appendix G):** `R_gibberish` and `R_symbol` are included to maintain linguistic fluency and prevent the LLM from generating nonsensical or punctuation-abusing outputs.
 - **Prompt Template:** A structured prompt template is used during RL to guide the LLM, incorporating strategies like prompt dilution (adding irrelevant elements), image obfuscation (suggesting ambiguous styles), and conceptual confusion (replacing sensitive terms with visually similar innocuous ones).
 - **Integrated Filter:** For open-source model evaluation, a composite filter comprising a text toxicity classifier, NSFW text detector, and an image safety checker is used to simulate real-world content moderation.
 - **Implementation:** The Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct model is used as the base LLM, and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied during fine-tuning for training stability. Separate red-team LLMs are trained for each T2I system and harmful content domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GenBreak's effectiveness was rigorously evaluated against both safeguarded open-source T2I models and commercial APIs, showcasing its superior performance in red teaming.

**Performance on Safeguarded Open-Source Models (Stable Diffusion 2.1 and Stable Diffusion 3 Medium):**
- **Superior Toxic Bypass Rate (TBR) and Toxic Clean Bypass Rate (TCBR):** GenBreak significantly outperformed all baseline methods (Vanilla RL, CRT, SneakyPrompt, ART, MMA-Diffusion) across nudity, violence, and hate categories. For instance, on SD 2.1, GenBreak achieved TBRs of 60.8% (nudity), 89.7% (violence), and 84.6% (hate), vastly exceeding baselines which often scored in single-digit percentages or near zero for TCBR. This demonstrates GenBreak's strong attack capability, successfully bypassing integrated filters while producing highly toxic images.
- **High Image Toxicity:** Prompts generated by GenBreak consistently yielded high average toxicity scores for the resulting images, often above 0.8 in nudity and violence categories, indicating genuinely harmful content generation.
- **Effective Stealthiness:** GenBreak maintained high TCBR, meaning it produced toxic images without relying on blacklisted sensitive keywords. This contrasts sharply with methods like CRT, which, despite good diversity, often relied on easily detectable terms, leading to near-zero TCBR.
- **Balanced Diversity:** While GenBreak sometimes achieved slightly lower prompt diversity metrics (Lexical and Semantic Diversity) compared to highly diverse but less effective methods (e.g., MMA-Diffusion, ART), it maintained a strong balance, proving that high-quality, high-risk prompts could still be diverse enough to explore vulnerabilities. The image diversity reward also ensured visual variety in the generated harmful content.
- **Limitations of Baselines:**
 - **Vanilla RL:** Lacked diverse exploration, often converging to monotonous, suboptimal solutions.
 - **CRT:** Heavily relied on sensitive keywords, making prompts easily detectable.
 - **SneakyPrompt & ART:** Showed limited ability to generate genuinely harmful content consistently, or failed to bypass moderation effectively.
 - **MMA-Diffusion:** Produced prompts with high lexical diversity but often lacked practical effectiveness due to random word combinations.

**Transfer Attacks on Commercial T2I Services (Black-box Evaluation):**
- **Strong Transferability:** GenBreak's adversarial prompts exhibited remarkable transferability to commercial T2I APIs (Leonardo.Ai, fal.ai, stability.ai), even with unknown internal filtering mechanisms. This is a critical finding for real-world security.
- **High Single-Attempt TBRs:** In the heavily moderated nudity domain, GenBreak achieved impressive single-attempt Toxic Bypass Rates of 70% on Leonardo.Ai, 30% on fal.ai, and 47% on stability.ai. This indicates that only a few attempts are sufficient to obtain highly toxic images from these commercial models.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** The strong transferability is attributed to:
 1. **Realistic Training Environment:** GenBreak's training environment on open-source models with content filters closely mimics real-world commercial pipelines.
 2. **Effective RL Reward Design:** The stability of the converged RL policies ensures consistent, reliable performance in generating prompts that maximize reward, generalizing well to unseen platforms.
 3. **Subtle Phrasing:** GenBreak learns to bypass filters by using alternative, seemingly innocuous expressions (e.g., "nymph" instead of "nude," "red oil" instead of "blood") or by referencing historical contexts to generate hate symbols without explicit keywords.
 4. **Reliability:** The generated prompts are highly reliable in producing harmful content, unlike other methods that often require multiple attempts.

**Ablation Study:**
The ablation study confirmed the importance of each reward component:
- The **bypass reward** was crucial for effective evasion.
- The **clean reward** was essential for generating stealthy prompts that avoid explicit toxic keywords.
- **Prompt diversity rewards** (lexical and semantic) were vital for increasing prompt variety and preventing premature convergence, especially for challenging objectives like TCBR.
- The **image diversity reward** further enhanced the visual variety of generated harmful images, proving valuable for comprehensive safety testing despite potential minor trade-offs in other metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GenBreak represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI safety, specifically for Text-to-Image models. Its contributions and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Novel Red Teaming Framework:** GenBreak is the first comprehensive red teaming framework that jointly optimizes for generating highly toxic images, bypassing safety filters, and maintaining semantic fluency and diversity. This multi-objective approach addresses a critical gap identified in prior research, moving beyond isolated successes in either toxicity or evasion.
- **Automated Vulnerability Discovery:** By introducing a reinforcement learning-based methodology to train red-team LLMs, GenBreak demonstrates the feasibility and efficacy of automatically discovering adversarial prompts under complex safety constraints. This automation scales the red teaming process, making it more efficient and thorough than manual efforts.
- **Proactive Safety Enhancement:** GenBreak provides a powerful, practical tool for T2I model developers and service providers. Instead of reacting to instances of misuse, they can proactively use GenBreak to identify high-risk vulnerabilities in their models *before* deployment or as part of continuous security auditing. The discovered adversarial prompts and corresponding harmful images can then be used to guide targeted improvements, such as fine-tuning prompt filters, augmenting training data with adversarial examples, or implementing additional defense strategies.
- **Revealing Real-World Vulnerabilities:** The extensive evaluations against both open-source models and, critically, real-world commercial T2I APIs, reveal concerning safety weaknesses in deployed services. The high Toxic Bypass Rates achieved by GenBreak's prompts on commercial platforms highlight that current defenses are not foolproof and can be circumvented, underscoring the urgent need for more robust safety mechanisms. This finding has direct implications for industry best practices in AI safety.
- **Advancing Responsible AI Development:** By facilitating the discovery and understanding of harmful content generation pathways, GenBreak contributes directly to the responsible development and deployment of generative AI technologies. It helps mitigate the potential for misuse and promotes a safer online environment.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also openly discusses its limitations, such as the challenge of obtaining image toxicity scores for black-box models if filters are triggered, and the potential discrepancy between experimental filters and real-world commercial policies. These limitations naturally point towards crucial avenues for future research, including developing stronger evasion techniques against industrial-grade image filters and exploring methods for evaluating toxicity without direct image access in black-box scenarios.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The authors acknowledge the sensitive nature of their research, which identifies methods for generating harmful content. They commit to responsible disclosure by requiring users to provide contact information and justify their intended use before granting access to datasets or models, demonstrating a commitment to ethical AI research and preventing potential misuse of their findings.

In conclusion, GenBreak represents a significant leap forward in T2I red teaming, offering a systematic and effective approach to uncover critical safety vulnerabilities. Its findings have immediate practical implications for improving the robustness of T2I content moderation systems and contributing to the broader goal of responsible AI innovation.
