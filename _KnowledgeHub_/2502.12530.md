# Translate Policy to Language: Flow Matching Generated Rewards for LLM Explanations

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework trains large language models to provide natural language explanations for decisions made by various AI agents, utilizing Rectified Flow models to generate distributional rewards that effectively capture nuanced human judgments. The approach yields explanations enabling higher prediction accuracy of agent actions, outperforming other methods by up to 18.2% on diverse tasks, and demonstrates improved logical soundness and lower cognitive load in human evaluations.

## Problem

- Existing natural language explanation methods for AI agents are highly task- and agent-specific, limiting their generalizability across diverse applications.
- Human judgments regarding the quality of AI explanations are inherently subjective, pluralistic, and probabilistic, a complexity not adequately captured by traditional scalar reward models.
- Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) struggles with the inherent noise and potential inaccuracy of proxy rewards generated by auxiliary LLMs, requiring better management of these proxy errors.

## Method

- A framework trains an EXPLANATION LLM to generate ==natural language explanations== for any agent decision based solely on contextual information, without prior knowledge of the specific action.
- Generative Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs), specifically Rectified Flow, are employed as a reward model to capture the probabilistic nature of human judgments and denoise proxy rewards from other LLMs.
- The Rectified Flow model's architecture incorporates cross-attention mechanisms to selectively integrate linguistic and contextual cues from the decision context and generated explanation.
- The EXPLANATION LLM is fine-tuned using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with dense, sentence-level rewards derived from the flow-matched reward model.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **1.9%** to **18.2%** higher accuracy across various RL tasks (SMAC, AI2-THOR) and LLM benchmarks (MMLU, MathQA) compared to existing baselines.
- Human evaluations validated the quality of generated explanations, showing **25.7%** higher accuracy on MathQA than the best baseline and being rated as more logically sound and less cognitively demanding by **60%** of participants.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the cross-attention mechanism in the reward model and the use of dense, sentence-level rewards are crucial, with their removal significantly reducing performance (e.g., over **5%** accuracy drop).
- The Rectified Flow reward model successfully generalized to distinguish between true and false negative explanations, and the framework maintained robust performance across different LLM backbones and under noisy reward conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous Normalizing Flows can effectively model the probabilistic and pluralistic nature of human judgments, providing a mechanism to denoise LLM-generated proxy rewards and theoretically bound errors.
> - Training an explanation LLM to infer decisions from context alone, rather than directly stating them, fosters genuine reasoning and promotes a more generalizable explanation capability.
> - Cross-attention within the flow-based reward model is essential for its ability to effectively process linguistic and contextual cues, allowing it to generalize to negative samples and assign accurate reward distributions.
> - Providing dense, sentence-level rewards during RL fine-tuning offers more effective guidance to the EXPLANATION LLM compared to sparse, end-of-explanation rewards, leading to higher quality outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Explainable AI (XAI), Reinforcement Learning (RL), Large Language Models (LLMs), and AI Alignment. The paper identifies a crucial need for natural language explanations of intelligent agent policies to foster transparency, safety, and alignment, especially as AI agents become more ubiquitous.

**Contribution to Explainable AI (XAI) and Explainable Reinforcement Learning (XRL):**
The paper positions itself within the broader XAI/XRL landscape (Arrieta et al., 2020; Carvalho et al., 2019). It acknowledges existing methods but highlights their limitations, particularly their "task- and agent-specific engineering." This work addresses the need for a **generalizable and task-agnostic explanation method** by training LLMs to generate explanations solely from contextual information, without prior knowledge of the agent's specific decisions or task details. This is a significant departure from many prior XRL methods that rely on interpretable surrogate models (e.g., decision trees, finite state machines) or saliency maps, which often have limited capacity or fail to capture deeper reasoning. By generating explanations that allow a third party to *infer* the decision, the method aims for a form of "behavioral faithfulness," which is critical for trust and intervention.

**Extension of Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF):**
The work explicitly extends Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) (Bai et al., 2022b), a paradigm that economizes costly human feedback by using auxiliary LLMs to generate proxy rewards. The paper identifies a critical gap in current RLAIF approaches: the lack of rigorous study on how to generate *distributional rewards* that explicitly manage proxy errors relative to true human reward distributions. This paper closes that gap by introducing Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) as reward models. It directly tackles the inherent "pluralistic and probabilistic" nature of human judgments about explanations, a challenge that simple scalar rewards or pairwise preferences struggle to capture comprehensively.

**Advancement in Distributional Reward Modeling for LLMs:**
While some recent RLHF/RLAIF studies have explored distributional reward models (e.g., QRM, DPRM, URM), this paper distinguishes itself by modeling *continuous* feedback distributions using generative CNFs (specifically Rectified Flow), rather than discretizing the feedback space or assuming specific parametric forms (like Gaussian). This allows for a more flexible and accurate representation of complex human preferences, especially for open-ended tasks like explanation generation. The theoretical analysis backing the error bounds for deviation from true human reward distributions is a novel and important contribution in this specific sub-field.

**Integration of Generative Models (CNFs/Flow Matching) in LLM Training:**
The innovative use of generative continuous normalizing flows, particularly Rectified Flow, as a reward model within an LLM training framework is a key differentiating factor. While diffusion models (a related class of generative models) have been increasingly integrated with Transformers for tasks like image/text generation, their application to generating *reward distributions* for RL-based LLM fine-tuning, especially with cross-attention mechanisms, appears to be novel. This integration leverages the strengths of generative models in capturing complex distributions and Transformers in processing linguistic and contextual cues.

In essence, this paper pushes the boundaries of XAI by proposing a generalizable, LLM-centric approach to explanation generation that moves beyond task-specific solutions. It significantly advances RLAIF by providing a theoretically-grounded and empirically effective method for modeling the nuanced, distributional nature of human (or proxy-AI) preferences, thereby enhancing the reliability and quality of AI-generated explanations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research stems from the increasing integration of intelligent agents (RL agents, LLMs, robotics) into human environments, necessitating transparent and understandable decision-making. The authors identify that for reliable coexistence, agents must be able to **explain their policies in natural language**.

Based on this overarching need, the paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Generalizable and Task-Agnostic Explanation Framework:** A primary goal is to overcome the limitations of existing natural language explanation methods, which are often "tailored to specific application scenarios." The authors aim to create a framework that trains an EXPLANATION LLM to generate reasoning for *any* agent decision (RL actions, LLM choices) across diverse tasks, using only contextual information and *without* knowing the actual decision beforehand. This "concealing agent decisions" forces the LLM to reason from context alone, promoting generalizability.
2. **Capture the Pluralistic and Probabilistic Nature of Human Judgments:** The authors recognize that human evaluations of explanations are inherently subjective, "pluralistic," and "probabilistic." Traditional single-point rewards or pairwise preferences often fail to capture this complexity. The objective is to design a reward mechanism that explicitly models these nuanced human judgments, represented as a distribution of plausible rewards.
3. **Rigorously Manage Proxy Errors in RLAIF:** While RLAIF offers a cost-effective alternative to human feedback, proxy rewards generated by LLMs can be noisy and deviate from true human preferences. A critical objective is to "rigorously study how to generate distributional rewards that explicitly manage proxy errors relative to human reward distributions." This includes providing theoretical guarantees for bounding these deviations.
4. **Leverage Generative Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) as Reward Models:** To achieve the above objectives, the paper aims to introduce CNFs, specifically Rectified Flow, as a novel mechanism for generating distributional rewards. The CNF is intended to "capture the pluralistic and probabilistic nature of human judgments" and act as a denoising mechanism for proxy rewards.
5. **Design a Specialized CNF Architecture for Linguistic and Contextual Cues:** To effectively generate rewards from text, the CNF reward model needs to understand the linguistic nuances of decision contexts and explanations. An objective is to design a specialized architecture that can selectively attend to these cues, enabling it to assign accurate reward distributions based on textual input.
6. **Train Explanation-Generating LLMs Effectively using Flow-Matched Rewards:** Ultimately, the framework's goal is to train an EXPLANATION LLM (via RL, specifically PPO) to produce high-quality, logically sound, actionable, and low-cognitive-load explanations that enable accurate prediction of true agent decisions. The generated distributional rewards should provide superior feedback compared to existing RLHF/RLAIF methods.

In essence, the research seeks to enhance the interpretability and trustworthiness of AI agents by enabling them to provide robust, high-quality, and human-understandable natural language explanations, even in the face of complex, probabilistic human feedback and noisy AI-generated proxy rewards.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, "TRANSLATE POLICY TO LANGUAGE: FLOW MATCHING GENERATED REWARDS FOR LLM EXPLANATIONS," is a general-purpose framework that trains an EXPLANATION LLM using Reinforcement Learning (RL) guided by distributional rewards generated by generative Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs).

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The framework aims to train an EXPLANATION LLM (denoted π_e(θ_e)) to generate natural language explanations (y_e_j) for an agent's decisions (a_j) given only the decision context (c_j). Crucially, the actual decision a_j is *excluded* from the prompt provided to the EXPLANATION LLM to prevent superficial explanations and encourage genuine reasoning from context. The training of π_e(θ_e) occurs via RL, with rewards provided by a Rectified Flow model (φ(θ_φ)).

#### 3.2. Rectified Flow Reward Model

To address the cost of human feedback and the noise in direct LLM-generated proxy rewards, the paper proposes using a generative Continuous Normalizing Flow (CNF), specifically Rectified Flow (RF), as the reward model.

- **Proxy Reward Generation:**
 - Initially, multiple independent PROXY LLMs are queried with a prompt consisting of the decision context (c_j) and an incremental explanation (y_e_j, up to sentence k). For each candidate decision 'a', the PROXY LLM measures the likelihood (via token-level logits M(a|x_g_j)) of 'a' being a plausible outcome.
 - This process generates multiple sets of logits ({M_j,k(·|x_g_j)}_K_k=1), capturing the probabilistic nature of judgments.
- **CNF Training (Rectified Flow):**
 - The Rectified Flow model φ(θ_φ) is trained to minimize a loss function (L_FLOW) that pushes an initial random sample (z_0) from a base noise distribution (Z_0, e.g., Gaussian) towards the logit vectors (M_j,k) generated by the PROXY LLMs. The objective is to make the flow follow a linear path between z_0 and M_j,k as much as possible, effectively learning to map noise to the target reward distribution.
 - **Theoretical Advantage:** A key theoretical contribution is that, under the assumption that the noise in PROXY LLM rewards shares the same functional form as the CNF's base distribution (e.g., Gaussian), the CNF provably bounds its deviation from the *underlying human reward distribution*. This positions the CNF as a "denoising" mechanism for the noisy proxy rewards.
- **Specialized Rectified Flow Model Architecture:**
 - To enable the RF model to understand linguistic and contextual cues, it's embedded within an LLM structure.
 - **Embedding:** The RF model's inputs (current state z_t and positional encoding PE(t)) are projected into "flow tokens" via MLPs (φ_EMB).
 - **Cross-Attention:** These flow tokens then perform cross-attention with the latent embeddings (last layer hidden states) of the decision context (c_j) and the explanation (y_e_j) from the EXPLANATION LLM. This allows the RF model to selectively integrate linguistic and contextual information when generating reward distributions.
 - **Projector:** A fully-connected network (φ_PROJ) then generates the vector field (φ) from the cross-attention output, incorporating skip-layer connections for stability.

#### 3.3. Overall Training Procedure

The training involves an iterative, alternating process:

1. **Rectified Flow Training:** The φ model is trained using the proxy rewards from multiple PROXY LLMs, as described above. Rejection sampling is used, where only samples where PROXY LLMs assign the highest likelihood to the true decision are used for training the flow model. Softmax activation is applied to logits before use in the loss.
2. **EXPLANATION LLM Training (via PPO):**
 - Once φ is trained, it's used to generate rewards for the EXPLANATION LLM. For a given explanation, the flow model is solved (ODE dz_t = φ(t, z_t)dt) starting from z_0, yielding an estimated logit distribution z_1 = M_FLOW.
 - The reward for the EXPLANATION LLM is defined as the *average logit of the true decision* (M_FLOW(a_j|x_g_j)).
 - Crucially, the method uses **dense, sentence-level rewards**. Per-sentence rewards are defined as the *changes* in the true decision's logit after each newly added sentence (r(s_e(k)_j) = p(a_j|x_g(k)_j) - p(a_j|x_g(k-1)_j)), providing finer-grained feedback than sparse, end-of-explanation rewards.
 - The EXPLANATION LLM (π_e) is then fine-tuned using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with LoRA to maximize these rewards.
3. **Alternating Training:** The rectified flow model and the EXPLANATION LLM are trained alternately for several rounds until convergence.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **Backbone Models:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (default), with tests on Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and Gemma-2-2B-It to assess robustness.
- **Data Generation:** A synthetic dataset of preferred and rejected explanations is generated by o1-mini (an OpenAI model) and verified by human evaluators.
- **Initial SFT:** All methods (except o3-mini) undergo three epochs of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the preferred subset. This acts as an initialization stage.
- **PROXY LLMs (for Flow Training):** K=3 independent PROXY LLMs are used: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and Gemma-2-2B-It.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates its proposed method against a comprehensive set of baselines across diverse benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements.

#### 4.1. Benchmarks and Evaluation Metrics

- **RL Tasks:** SMAC (StarCraft Multiagent Challenge, multi-agent micromanagement), AI2-THOR (embodied AI, complex multi-robot tasks).
- **LLM Tasks:** MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding, multiple-choice QA for professional knowledge), MathQA (mathematical word problems).
- **LLM Evaluation:** A separate GPT-4o model is used to infer the ground-truth decision based on the generated explanation, with **Accuracy (ACC)** (or Success Rate for AI2-THOR) as the primary metric.
- **Human Evaluation:** 45 participants evaluated explanations for prediction accuracy, logical soundness, actionability, and cognitive load.

#### 4.2. Key Findings

1. **Superior Performance Across Benchmarks:**
 - The proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines (SFT, Proxy LLM, PPO, DPO, KTO, CoT+SFT, ReFT, Skywork, DeepSeek, o3-mini) by **1.9%-18.2%** across all tasks and backbone models.
 - On SMAC, MMLU, and MathQA, it achieves 12.4%, 6.9%, and 11.0% higher ACC, respectively, compared to the "Proxy LLM" baseline (which directly uses proxy rewards). This strongly validates the theoretical analysis that the Rectified Flow model effectively handles noisy proxy rewards and provides better optimization signals.
 - On the challenging AI2-THOR embodied AI benchmark, it improves the success rate by 7.4% over KTO, 6.0% over ReFT, and 2.5% over o3-mini.

2. **Validation by Human Evaluation:**
 - Human evaluators confirmed the high quality of explanations generated by the proposed method. On MathQA, it achieves the highest accuracy, exceeding the strongest baseline (ReFT) by **25.7%** (0.892 vs 0.635).
 - Approximately 60% of participants rated its explanations as the most logically sound and the least cognitively demanding, indicating significantly improved decision comprehension.

3. **Effective Generalization to Negative Samples (Distinguishing True and False Negatives):**
 - The Rectified Flow reward model successfully differentiates between "true negatives" (invalid explanations) and "false negatives" (effective explanations misclassified by the PROXY LLM). During training, it learns to assign low rewards to true negatives and high rewards to false negatives, which is crucial for informative reward signals.
 - This generalization capability is attributed to the **cross-attention mechanism** in the RF model. Ablation studies ("Ours w/o Attn") show that removing cross-attention causes performance stagnation on negative samples, unable to reproduce 100% accuracy on positive samples or generalize to negative ones.

4. **Impact of Design Choices (Ablation Studies):**
 - **Cross-Attention:** Removing the cross-attention layer in the RF reward model ("Ours w/o Attn") leads to a minimum of 5.1% lower ACC, highlighting its critical role in capturing complex contextual information and generalizing to negative samples.
 - **Sentence-Level Rewards:** Using only sparse rewards for the last token ("Ours (Sparse Reward)") results in at least 1.2% lower ACC compared to sentence-level rewards, confirming that dense, sentence-level rewards provide better guidance for learning high-quality explanations.
 - **Backbone Model Robustness:** The method consistently improves ACC (up to 19.5% over SFT) across different LLM backbones (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-2B-It), demonstrating its robustness.
 - **Sensitivity to σ in q0:** Performance remains stable across a range of σ values, confirming robustness predicted by theoretical analysis.
 - **Necessity of SFT:** The method requires initial SFT to achieve strong performance, particularly on complex RL tasks, as SFT provides crucial positive samples for training the rectified flow model. Even without SFT, the method still significantly outperforms baselines without SFT on SMAC.
 - **Robustness to Noisy Reward:** The method's performance only decreases slightly (e.g., from 77.2% to 74.3% on MMLU) under conditions of 3% random noise in final token rewards, and still outperforms baselines trained without noise, showcasing its robustness to imperfect feedback.

5. **Masked Explicit Decisions:** Masking explicit mentions of the decision in generated explanations caused only a slight performance drop (1.8-3.6%), indicating that the explanations still provided sufficient reasoning for inference, and the method wasn't merely learning to restate decisions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions to the fields of Explainable AI, Reinforcement Learning, and Large Language Models, with profound potential impact:

1. **Advancing Generalizable and Faithful Explanations for AI Agents:** The primary significance lies in introducing a novel, general-purpose framework that can generate natural language explanations for diverse AI agents (RL agents, LLMs) across various tasks, *without* needing prior knowledge of the agent's explicit decisions. This addresses a major limitation of existing, often task-specific, XAI methods and moves towards truly generalizable and "behaviorally faithful" explanations, where the explanation allows an external observer to accurately infer the agent's actions. This significantly enhances the transparency and interpretability of complex AI systems.

2. **Robustness to Noisy and Pluralistic Feedback:** The innovative use of Rectified Flow models to generate *distributional rewards* is a breakthrough in managing the inherent noise of AI-generated proxy feedback and the pluralistic nature of human judgments. The theoretical guarantees for bounding deviations from true human reward distributions offer a strong foundation for building more reliable RLAIF systems. This method enables LLMs to learn from preferences that are not simply binary or scalar but reflect a spectrum of plausibility and human variability.

3. **Enhanced Trust and Alignment in Human-AI Interaction:** By producing explanations that are not only accurate but also logically sound, actionable, and impose low cognitive load (as evidenced by human evaluations), this work directly contributes to building greater trust in AI systems. In high-stakes applications (e.g., healthcare, finance, autonomous driving), human understanding and the ability to intervene are paramount. Improved explanations facilitate better human comprehension, meaningful feedback loops, and safer deployment of AI.

4. **Broader Impact on LLM Training Frameworks:** The paper envisions extending this approach "into a general-purpose LLM training framework that automatically generates high-quality reward signals." This suggests a future where LLM alignment and fine-tuning could be significantly streamlined and improved by leveraging sophisticated generative models for dynamic and nuanced reward generation, moving beyond static datasets or simpler reward models.

**Ethical Considerations:** The authors include an ethics statement, noting the recruitment of diverse human participants for evaluation, ensuring standardized instructions, and anonymizing all participant data. This commitment to ethical research practices, particularly in human-in-the-loop evaluations, is crucial for responsible AI development.

**Reproducibility:** The inclusion of a reproducibility statement, detailing the provision of source code, theoretical proofs, and data processing steps, reinforces the scientific rigor and allows the broader research community to build upon this work.

In conclusion, this paper presents a significant advancement in generating robust and human-understandable explanations for AI systems. By cleverly integrating generative flow models with reinforcement learning from AI feedback, the authors have developed a powerful and versatile framework that has the potential to make AI agents more transparent, trustworthy, and aligned with human expectations across a multitude of applications.
