# AbductiveMLLM: Boosting Visual Abductive Reasoning Within MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Beijing Institute of Technology and collaborators introduce AbductiveMLLM, an architecture designed to enhance Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) capacity for Visual Abductive Reasoning (VAR). The approach integrates human-inspired verbal and pictorial thinking, achieving state-of-the-art results on VAR and YouCookII benchmarks, with improvements such as +15.38 CIDEr over previous traditional models on the VAR dataset.

## Problem

- Humans readily infer plausible explanations for incomplete visual observations, a process known as Visual Abductive Reasoning (VAR).
- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with high-level abductive inference, often providing textually consistent but visually inconsistent outputs.
- Prior specialized VAR models are typically small-scale, task-specific, and rely exclusively on verbal forms of abduction, limiting their generalizability and reasoning depth.

## Method

- Developed AbductiveMLLM, a unified multimodal network that incorporates two synergistically trained components: ==REASONER for verbal abduction== and ==IMAGINER for pictorial abduction==.
- REASONER employs a ==causality-aware hypothesis generation module== to create diverse verbal hypotheses, then prunes them using a cross-modal contrastive learning mechanism to select causally relevant priors for the MLLM.
- IMAGINER adapts a text-to-image diffusion model with lightweight visual, temporal, and FFN adapters to 'imagine' plausible visual scenes conditioned on observed video and REASONER's output, providing rich visual context to the MLLM.

## Results

- AbductiveMLLM achieved state-of-the-art performance across all five evaluation metrics (BLEU@4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, BERT-S) on both the VAR and YouCookII benchmarks.
- The method substantially outperformed traditional specialized models (e.g., **+1.14** BLEU@4, **+15.38** CIDEr on VAR over UPD-Trans) and general MLLMs (e.g., **+6.34** ROUGE-L, **+27.79** CIDEr over zero-shot MLLMs on VAR).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both REASONER and IMAGINER components contribute individually to performance gains, with their synergistic combination yielding the best results and optimal performance achieved with k=**3** selected hypotheses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mimicking human dual-mode cognition, integrating both verbal and pictorial thinking, significantly boosts MLLMs' capabilities in visual abductive reasoning.
> - Causality-aware selection of verbal hypotheses, informed by cross-modal alignment, is essential to filter out low-quality or hallucinated explanations generated by LLMs.
> - Leveraging adapted text-to-image diffusion models for pictorial thinking provides crucial visual contextual grounding, complementing verbal reasoning within the MLLM framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within artificial intelligence: computer vision, natural language processing, multimodal learning, and advanced reasoning. Specifically, it addresses the challenging task of **Visual Abductive Reasoning (VAR)**, which requires AI systems to infer the most plausible explanation for incomplete visual observations.

Historically, VAR research has evolved from analyzing static images to processing dynamic video sequences. Early approaches to VAR, such as those employing the Sherlock dataset or models like RCA and BiGED, primarily focused on static image-based inference. While these methods laid foundational groundwork, they were often limited by the incomplete nature of single-frame observations and struggled with complex spatiotemporal causal structures. More recent endeavors have shifted towards video-based VAR, exemplified by models like REASONER, UPD-Trans, Conan, and Videoabc. These models, along with approaches incorporating external knowledge bases like KN-VLM, MAR, and COIN, have focused on developing specialized architectures for verbal reasoning based on video input. However, a common thread among these traditional models is their small-scale, task-specific nature, and their exclusive reliance on verbal abduction.

Concurrently, the emergence of **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**, such as VideoChat2, Qwen2VL, and others building upon pre-trained LLMs, has revolutionized video understanding. These MLLMs leverage vast amounts of human knowledge and sophisticated architectures (like dedicated video encoders and instruction tuning) to achieve impressive performance across a wide array of multimodal tasks, including visual question answering, multimodal dialogue, and video captioning. Despite their general-purpose multimodal reasoning capabilities, recent studies (e.g., Wang et al. 2024b; Chinchure et al. 2024) have highlighted a significant limitation: current MLLMs exhibit a considerable gap compared to human ability in abductive inference, particularly when understanding ambiguous observations and causal relationships. They often struggle with high-level, human-like reasoning that requires inferring unobserved events or explanations.

The "ABDUCTIVEMLLM" paper directly addresses this critical gap. It represents a pioneering effort to enhance the abductive capabilities of general-purpose MLLMs by drawing inspiration from human cognition. Instead of developing another specialized small-scale model, this work integrates sophisticated abductive reasoning directly into the more powerful and generalizable MLLM framework. By proposing a unified multimodal network that incorporates both verbal and pictorial thinking—a process humans routinely employ—the paper pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities beyond superficial understanding to deeper causal inference. This approach distinguishes itself by being the first to explicitly incorporate pictorial thinking into VAR within the MLLM paradigm, bridging the divide between abstract reasoning and concrete imagination in AI systems. It seeks to bring MLLMs closer to human-level cognitive flexibility in interpreting complex, incomplete visual narratives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the recognized disparity between human and AI capabilities in Visual Abductive Reasoning (VAR). While humans effortlessly infer the most likely explanations for incomplete visual observations by engaging in a dynamic interplay between verbal and pictorial thought, advanced Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), despite their prowess in general multimodal tasks, often fall short in such abductive inference. This limitation prevents MLLMs from achieving truly human-like high-level reasoning.

The **key objectives** of this work are thus:

1. **Enhance MLLMs' Abductive Reasoning Capabilities:** To specifically address the noted deficiency of current MLLMs in visual abductive reasoning, moving beyond their existing general multimodal understanding to facilitate deeper causal inference.
2. **Mimic Human Dual-Mode Cognition:** To draw inspiration from human cognitive processes, where verbal and pictorial thinking synergistically contribute to abduction, and to translate this dual-mode behavior into an AI architecture. This involves developing mechanisms that allow AI to "verbally abduce" (formulate hypotheses) and "pictorially abduce" (mentally "imagine" plausible visual scenes).
3. **Develop Synergistic Verbal and Pictorial Components:**
 - **REASONER:** Design a component that operates in the verbal domain to explore a broad space of possible explanations, prune visually incongruent hypotheses based on cross-modal causal alignment, and then steer the MLLM's reasoning with these refined verbal priors.
 - **IMAGINER:** Create a component that emulates human-like pictorial thinking by conditioning a text-to-image diffusion model on both input video and the REASONER's output to "imagine" corresponding visual scenes, thereby enriching the MLLM's contextual grounding.
4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To validate the proposed AbductiveMLLM by demonstrating its superior performance on standard VAR benchmarks, consistently outperforming existing specialized solutions and advanced MLLMs.
5. **Pioneer Integration of Pictorial Thinking in VAR MLLMs:** To establish the first instance of explicitly incorporating pictorial thinking capabilities to improve verbal abductions within an MLLM framework for VAR, thus opening new avenues for research in cognitively-inspired AI.

In essence, the research aims to bridge the "abduction gap" between MLLMs and human beings by carefully engineering an architecture that reflects the complementary nature of language and imagination in human reasoning, thereby pushing MLLMs closer to advanced, causally-aware cognitive abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **AbductiveMLLM** architecture is designed to boost Visual Abductive Reasoning (VAR) in MLLMs by mimicking human-like verbal and pictorial thinking. It consists of two primary, synergistically trained components: **REASONER** (verbal mode) and **IMAGINER** (pictorial mode).

#### 3.1. REASONER: Abduction in Verbal Mode

The REASONER component focuses on enhancing MLLMs' abductive capabilities through verbal processing.

#### 3.1.1. Causality-aware Hypotheses Generation (CHG)

1. **Candidate Verbal Hypotheses Generation:**
 - First, a pre-trained MLLM generates descriptive captions for each observed video clip in an incomplete video sequence.
 - These captions are then fed into a powerful Large Language Model (LLM), specifically GPT-4o-mini, prompted to infer plausible missing events at a masked position. To ensure diversity and reduce redundancy, GPT-4o-mini is queried multiple times with a high sampling temperature (e.g., 1.4), yielding a collection of diverse candidate verbal hypotheses.

2. **Causality-aware Cross-modal Hypotheses Selection:**
 - To address the potential for low-quality or hallucinated hypotheses generated solely from text, a contrastive learning module is introduced. This module comprises a vision encoder ($\Phi_V$) and a text encoder ($\Phi_T$).
 - **Negative Sample Generation:** Unlike standard contrastive learning, which often uses random negatives, this method employs GPT-4o-mini to generate "hard negatives." These negatives are semantically distinct from the ground-truth explanation but remain logically consistent with the observed captions, thus challenging the model to discern true causal relevance.
 - **Causal Alignment:** The vision encoder ($\Phi_V$) projects observed initial (I) and final (F) video segments into a joint causal space, while the text encoder ($\Phi_T$) embeds positive and negative hypotheses.
 - **Contrastive Loss:** The module is optimized using the NT-Xent loss, maximizing the cosine similarity between the sum of the initial segment and a positive hypothesis ($X_I + X_P^+$) and the final segment ($X_F$), while minimizing similarity with negative hypotheses ($X_I + X_P^-$ vs. $X_F$). This encourages the model to learn causal relevance rather than superficial similarity.
 - **Hypothesis Pruning:** During inference, each candidate hypothesis is scored for its causal relevance using this module, and only the top-k highest-scoring hypotheses are selected.

#### 3.1.2. Hypotheses Guided Verbal Abduction in MLLMs

- The selected top-k causally aligned hypotheses are incorporated into the prompt for the main MLLM, serving as "targeted priors."
- The input video for the MLLM consists of concatenated observed events, with placeholder frames (random pixels) indicating the position of the unobserved event.
- Event-level indices, rather than frame-level, are added to the video frames to guide the MLLM on the temporal ordering and the location of the missing event.
- The output embeddings from the REASONER (denoted as $c_t$) are then passed to the IMAGINER and also decoded to generate the final verbal abductive explanation.

#### 3.2. IMAGINER: Abduction in Pictorial Mode

The IMAGINER component facilitates pictorial thinking, providing rich visual contextual cues to guide the MLLM. Its objective is to provide visual guidance rather than to produce high-fidelity video generation.

- **Backbone:** An existing text-to-image diffusion model, Stable Diffusion-v1-4, is adapted for video generation. Its original weights are frozen.
- **Lightweight Adapters:** Three types of lightweight adapters are integrated into each U-Net block of Stable Diffusion, and only their parameters are updated during training:
 1. **Visual Cross-attention Adapter (V-Adapter):** This adapter injects informative visual priors from the observed video events (O). It extracts a "local-global hybrid representation" ($c_v$) by selecting high-scoring frames based on their similarity with the textual explanation (using CLIP embeddings) to form a local representation and a weighted average for a global representation. The V-Adapter operates in parallel with the original textual cross-attention within the U-Net block, allowing the model to attend to both visual and textual cues simultaneously.
 2. **Temporal Convolution Adapter (T-Adapter):** Positioned after spatial convolution layers, this adapter models temporal dependencies across frames using depth-wise 3D convolutions. It helps maintain structural consistency and improves temporal modeling efficiency.
 3. **FFN Adapter (F-Adapter):** This adapter enhances spatial representation by working in parallel with the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layer, preserving the original FFN's integrity while adapting to video's spatial features.

- **Conditioning:** The IMAGINER is conditioned on both the textual output embeddings from the REASONER ($c_t$) and the visual local-global hybrid representations ($c_v$) extracted from the observed videos.
- **Loss:** Training uses a conditional latent diffusion loss ($L_{Diffusion}$) with Min-SNR weighting to accelerate convergence.

#### 3.3. Network Training

A two-stage training paradigm is employed:

1. **Stage I (Independent Training):**
 - The MLLM within REASONER is finetuned with LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) using a standard cross-entropy loss ($L_{CE}$).
 - The adapters within IMAGINER are trained using the $L_{Diffusion}$ loss.
 - The cross-modal contrastive learning module (for hypothesis selection) is trained independently for 10 epochs.

2. **Stage II (Joint End-to-End Optimization):**
 - REASONER and IMAGINER are jointly finetuned for one epoch. The overall loss function is a combination of the cross-entropy loss and the diffusion loss: $L = L_{CE} + \alpha L_{Diffusion}$, where $\alpha$ is a balancing coefficient.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Network Architecture:** Qwen2VL-7B-Instruct (for MLLM), ResNet200/BN-Inception with a 2-layer Transformer encoder (for $\Phi_V$), CLIP text encoder with 2-layer MLP (for $\Phi_T$), and Stable Diffusion-v1-4 (for IMAGINER). A 2-layer MLP acts as a bridge layer.
- **Datasets:** VAR (Liang et al. 2022) and YouCookII (Zhou, Xu, and Corso 2018), covering diverse real-world video events and cooking procedures.
- **Competitors:** Traditional specialized models (REASONER, KN-VLM, UPD-Trans) and various MLLMs (GPT-4o-mini, VideoChat2-7B, Qwen2VL-7B), including a fine-tuned version of Qwen2VL-7B ($Qwen2VL-7B_{FT}$).
- **Metrics:** BLEU@4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, and BERT-S, standard metrics for evaluating text generation quality and semantic similarity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the VAR and YouCookII benchmarks demonstrate the significant effectiveness of AbductiveMLLM, establishing new state-of-the-art performance across various metrics.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Overall Superiority:** AbductiveMLLM consistently achieved the highest scores across all five evaluation metrics (BLEU@4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, BERT-S) on both the VAR test set (Table 1) and the YouCookII test set (Table 2).
- **Outperforming Traditional Models:** The proposed method substantially surpassed the best traditional specialized models. For instance, on the VAR dataset, AbductiveMLLM improved BLEU@4 by +1.14, METEOR by +2.25, ROUGE-L by +2.33, CIDEr by +15.38, and BERT-S by +6.00 compared to UPD-Trans, the previous leading traditional model. This highlights that MLLMs, when augmented with specialized abductive reasoning modules, can offer a more powerful and scalable solution than task-specific architectures.
- **Surpassing Zero-shot MLLMs:** AbductiveMLLM demonstrated significant gains over general zero-shot MLLMs (GPT-4o-mini, VideoChat2-7B, Qwen2VL-7B). On VAR, it showed improvements of over +6.34 ROUGE-L, +27.79 CIDEr, and +6.79 BERT-S. Similar substantial gains were observed on YouCookII, confirming the inherent deficiency of general MLLMs in abductive reasoning without specific enhancements.
- **Advancing Beyond Fine-tuned MLLMs:** Even when compared to a fine-tuned MLLM baseline ($Qwen2VL-7B_{FT}$), AbductiveMLLM achieved consistent improvements on both datasets (e.g., +0.84 ROUGE-L, +6.22 CIDEr on VAR; +1.42 ROUGE-L, +9.26 CIDEr on YouCookII). This crucial finding validates that the integration of human-inspired verbal and pictorial abductive processes provides complementary reasoning capabilities that go beyond what simple fine-tuning can offer.
- **Persistent Human-AI Gap:** Despite the impressive gains, the results acknowledge that a significant gap still remains between AI models and human abductive capabilities, indicating ample room for future research and development.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- The qualitative example provided (Fig. 4) illustrates AbductiveMLLM's ability to generate plausible and causally coherent hypotheses for unobserved events. For an observation of a man and a dog with a frisbee, the model correctly inferred: "The man throws the frisbee again and the dog catches it."
- In contrast, competitor models often yielded unsatisfactory results: REASONER misidentified the person's gender, GPT-4o-mini provided overly general descriptions lacking fine-grained reasoning, and the $Qwen2VL-7B_{FT}$ baseline failed to accurately infer the man's action. This highlights AbductiveMLLM's superior ability to understand the cause-effect chain and generate precise, contextually relevant explanations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Key Component Analysis (Table 3):** Both the Causality-aware Hypotheses Generation (CHG) module and the IMAGINER component individually contributed to performance improvements across all metrics. IMAGINER particularly yielded larger gains on semantics-oriented metrics (METEOR, ROUGE-L) and embedding-based BERT-S, suggesting it enriches visually grounded verbal results. The synergistic combination of both components led to the best overall performance.
- **Top-k Hypotheses Selection (Table 4):** The study found an optimal value for $k=3$ (selecting the top 3 hypotheses). Both an insufficient number of hypotheses ($k<3$) and an excessive number ($k>3$) led to performance drops, indicating that precise pruning of candidate hypotheses is crucial.
- **Coefficient $\alpha$ (Table 5):** The model demonstrated relative stability across different values of $\alpha$ (the balancing coefficient between $L_{CE}$ and $L_{Diffusion}$), with $\alpha=5$ yielding the best performance. This suggests robustness to hyperparameter selection.
- **IMAGINER Adapters (Table 6):** Removing any single adapter from IMAGINER (V-Adapter, T-Adapter, F-Adapter) resulted in a performance decrease, particularly affecting the CIDEr metric. This confirms the necessity of each adapter in effectively integrating visual context and temporal information for pictorial thinking, further underscoring the importance of this modality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ABDUCTIVEMLLM" research carries substantial significance and holds potential for broad impact across several domains within artificial intelligence and beyond.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Visual Abductive Reasoning and MLLMs

- **New State-of-the-Art:** By consistently outperforming both specialized traditional models and advanced MLLM baselines, AbductiveMLLM sets a new benchmark for visual abductive reasoning, particularly in complex video-based scenarios.
- **Bridging the MLLM Reasoning Gap:** The work successfully addresses a known weakness of existing MLLMs in high-level abductive inference. It demonstrates a concrete and effective method for enhancing their causal reasoning capabilities, moving them beyond mere pattern recognition and descriptive generation towards explanatory intelligence.
- **Cognitively Inspired AI:** By explicitly drawing inspiration from and mimicking the human cognitive interplay between verbal and pictorial thought, this research validates the utility of "cognitively inspired AI." This approach could pave the way for more human-like reasoning systems across various AI tasks, fostering models that not only process information but also understand and explain it more intuitively.

#### 5.2. Broader Impact and Applications

The enhanced abductive reasoning capabilities offered by AbductiveMLLM have numerous potential applications:

- **Enhanced Situational Awareness for AI Agents:** In robotics, autonomous driving, or surveillance systems, the ability to infer unobserved events or their causes from partial visual data could drastically improve decision-making, predictive capabilities, and anomaly detection. For instance, a robot could infer why a machine stopped working based on observed visual cues, or an autonomous vehicle could deduce a potential hazard unseen directly.
- **Forensic Analysis and Event Reconstruction:** In security or investigative contexts, AI systems equipped with abductive reasoning could assist in reconstructing events from incomplete or ambiguous visual evidence (e.g., damaged surveillance footage), helping to piece together sequences of occurrences.
- **Content Generation and Storytelling:** AI systems could generate more coherent and causally plausible narratives or augment existing content by inferring missing scenes or motivations based on visual context, leading to richer and more engaging creative applications.
- **Medical and Scientific Diagnosis:** While the current work focuses on visual data, the underlying principle of abductive reasoning (inferring the most likely explanation for observations) is critical in medical diagnosis or scientific discovery. Future extensions could apply similar dual-mode reasoning to interpret complex multi-modal data in these fields.
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** An AI that can explain its inferences, even for unobserved events, will be a more trusted and effective collaborator for humans, particularly in high-stakes environments where understanding "why" is as important as "what."

#### 5.3. Future Research Directions

- **Further Reducing the Human-AI Gap:** Despite the advances, the acknowledged gap between AI and human abductive reasoning suggests ongoing research is needed, potentially by exploring more nuanced cognitive models or incorporating even richer forms of common-sense knowledge.
- **Complexity and Scale:** Applying AbductiveMLLM to even more complex, longer-duration, or open-world reasoning tasks would be a natural next step, pushing the boundaries of its robustness and scalability.
- **Generative Quality vs. Reasoning Guidance:** While IMAGINER currently focuses on guidance, exploring scenarios where higher-fidelity imagined visual outputs are also desired, and how this impacts reasoning, could be an interesting avenue.
- **Interactivity and Active Abduction:** Integrating active learning or interactive components, where the AI can query for more information or perform actions to resolve ambiguities, could lead to more robust abductive systems.

In conclusion, AbductiveMLLM represents a significant leap forward in equipping MLLMs with more sophisticated reasoning capabilities, particularly in the realm of visual abduction. By successfully integrating human-inspired dual-mode thinking, it not only pushes the state-of-the-art but also lays crucial groundwork for future AI systems that can reason, explain, and understand the world with greater intelligence and human-like intuition.
