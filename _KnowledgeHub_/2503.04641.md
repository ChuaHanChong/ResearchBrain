# Simulating the Real World: A Unified Survey of Multimodal Generative Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a unified framework for understanding multimodal generative models for real-world simulation, systematically categorizing advancements from 2D appearance to 4D representations that integrate appearance, dynamics, and geometry. It highlights interdependencies across modalities and proposes future directions for building comprehensive world simulators.

## Problem

- Existing research on generative models often treats different modalities (2D images, videos, 3D) as independent domains, overlooking their inherent interdependencies.
- Current approaches to real-world simulation lack a systematic framework that integrates various dimensions of reality, such as appearance, geometry, and dynamics, in a coherent manner.
- The absence of a unified perspective hinders a comprehensive understanding of progress and the development of advanced 'world models' for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

## Method

- Proposing a ==unified conceptual framework== that organizes **multimodal generative models** by the progression of data dimensionality: 2D (appearance), Video (appearance + dynamics), 3D (appearance + geometry), and 4D (appearance + dynamics + geometry).
- Systematically reviewing existing algorithms, ==representations==, datasets, and evaluation metrics within each dimensional category, emphasizing their connections and evolutionary pathways.
- Identifying key trends, challenges, and future research directions to guide progress towards integrating disparate research areas into a cohesive vision for ==real-world simulation==.

## Results

- A comprehensive **taxonomy** categorizing major 2D, video, 3D, and 4D generative models, detailing their architectural approaches, underlying representations, and practical applications.
- Identification of prevailing trends such as the **dominance of diffusion models** across modalities, the critical role of pre-trained 2D models, and the persistent challenge of high-quality 3D/4D dataset scarcity.
- Articulation of open research problems and strategic future directions, including the need for enhanced computational efficiency, better human-aligned evaluation metrics, and the integration of physical plausibility into generative processes for unified spatiotemporal world models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lower-dimensional generative models, particularly 2D image models, serve as critical priors and supervisory signals (e.g., through SDS loss) for generating higher-dimensional content.
> - Higher-dimensional representations (e.g., 3D geometry, 4D physical laws) can provide valuable consistency and regularization feedback to refine lower-dimensional generation tasks.
> - Future advancements require 'cross-dimensional synergy' and the development of unified generative backbones that jointly model spatial reconstruction and temporal evolution to achieve consistent and controllable 4D synthesis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of systems that can understand and replicate the physical world is a central aspiration in Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) research. Within this broader goal, "world models" aim to capture fundamental principles of reality to enable more accurate simulations and interactions. Historically, real-world simulation has relied on graphics techniques for geometry, texture, and dynamics, often requiring extensive manual design and computational resources. The advent of learning-based approaches, particularly multimodal generative models, has offered a data-driven alternative, reducing manual effort and improving scalability.

Existing research in generative models has largely progressed by treating different modalities—such as 2D images, videos, and 3D representations—as independent domains. This compartmentalized approach has often overlooked the inherent interdependencies and the systematic integration of various dimensions of reality. For instance, previous surveys on generative models typically focus on text-to-image, text-to-video, or text-to-3D generation in isolation. This survey distinguishes itself by proposing a unified framework that traces the progression of data dimensionality, from 2D appearance to 4D representations that integrate appearance, dynamics, and geometry. This perspective aims to bridge previously fragmented research areas and establish a coherent view of real-world simulation through the lens of multimodal generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a unified and systematic review of multimodal generative models for real-world simulation, specifically by investigating the progression of data dimensionality. The authors aim to integrate the study of 2D, video, 3D, and 4D generation within a single conceptual framework, which they identify as a gap in current literature.

The motivation for this unified approach stems from several observations:
- **Addressing Disjointed Research:** Current methods often treat different modalities (2D images, videos, 3D, and 4D representations) as independent, failing to account for their inherent interdependencies. This leads to fragmented progress in understanding and replicating the real world.
- **Integrating Dimensions of Reality:** Existing approaches typically focus on isolated dimensions of reality, such as appearance, geometry, or dynamics, without systematically integrating their connections. The real world is inherently high-dimensional, requiring a holistic perspective that combines these aspects.
- **Advancing AGI and Transformative Applications:** Simulating the real world is considered a cornerstone for achieving AGI. A more comprehensive understanding and unified approach to multimodal generation can unlock transformative applications in virtual reality, gaming, robotics, and autonomous driving.
- **Leveraging Generative Model Advances:** Recent advancements in learning-based generative models, such as Sora, demonstrate early-stage understanding of physical laws and significant capabilities in realistic content creation. This progress necessitates a structured overview that elucidates how these models contribute to simulating complex realities.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The authors intend for this survey to serve as a foundational resource for new researchers entering the field and to foster critical analysis among experienced researchers. By identifying open challenges and potential future directions within a unified framework, the survey aims to guide further exploration and accelerate progress toward advanced world simulators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a survey methodology, synthesizing existing research on multimodal generative models through a specific lens: the progression of data dimensionality in real-world simulation. The core approach is structured around a conceptual taxonomy that outlines the growth from lower-dimensional to higher-dimensional representations.

The survey's methodological progression is as follows:

1. **Dimensional Growth Framework:** The central organizing principle is the evolution of data dimensionality:
 - **2D Generation:** Focuses on modeling appearance (e.g., images).
 - **Video Generation:** Extends 2D by incorporating dynamics (appearance + dynamics).
 - **3D Generation:** Extends 2D by incorporating geometry (appearance + geometry).
 - **4D Generation:** Integrates all dimensions: appearance, dynamics, and geometry.

2. **Unified Schematic:** A unified framework (illustrated in Fig. 2 of the paper) connects these modalities through text-guided synthesis, demonstrating how higher-dimensional content is synthesized by extending foundational modalities along spatial and temporal axes. This includes transformations like 2D→3D (spatial lifting), 2D→Video (temporal inflation), Video→4D (spatial reconstruction of dynamic sequences), and 3D→4D (temporal animation of static geometry).

3. **Detailed Paradigms Section (Section II):** This section forms the core of the survey, dedicated to each dimension:
 - **2D Generation (Sec. II-A):** Reviews algorithms like Imagen, DALL-E, DeepFloyd IF, Stable Diffusion, and FLUX.1, highlighting their capabilities in producing realistic and semantically accurate images from textual descriptions.
 - **Video Generation (Sec. II-B):** Classifies methods into VAE- and GAN-based, Diffusion-based (U-Net and Transformer architectures), and Autoregressive-based approaches. It details representative models (e.g., Sora, Make-A-Video, VideoPoet), discusses evaluation metrics (e.g., FVD, VBench, human studies), and outlines applications (video editing, novel view synthesis, human animation).
 - **3D Generation (Sec. II-C):** Begins with an exposition of 3D representations (Explicit: Point Clouds, Voxel Grids, Meshes, 3D Gaussian Splatting; Implicit: SDF, NeRF; Hybrid: Hybrid Voxel Grids, DMTet, Triplane). It then categorizes generation algorithms based on input modalities: Text-to-3D, Image-to-3D, and Video-to-3D, each further divided into Feedforward, Optimization-based, and MVS-based approaches. Key models and their interactions with 2D/video priors are discussed, along with evaluation metrics (e.g., CLIP Score, Chamfer Distance, IoU).
 - **4D Generation (Sec. II-D):** Focuses on synthesizing dynamic 3D scenes. It introduces 4D representations, typically decomposed into a canonical 3D representation and a deformation model (e.g., D-NeRF, 4DGS). Algorithms are categorized into Feedforward and Optimization-based methods, detailing models like MAV3D, 4D-fy, and Control4D. Evaluation involves time cost, CLIP, and LPIPS, and applications include 4D editing and human animation.

4. **Supportive Elements:**
 - **Datasets (Appendix C, Table VII):** A comprehensive review of commonly used datasets for 2D, video, 3D, and 4D generation, outlining their properties and contributions.
 - **Evaluation Metrics (Appendix C, Table VIII):** A unified summary of quantitative metrics (quality and alignment) and qualitative human preference indicators. It also emphasizes practical challenges like computational efficiency.
 - **Glossary (Appendix A) and Preliminaries (Appendix B):** Definitions of technical terms and an overview of deep generative models (GANs, VAEs, AR Models, NFs, Diffusion Models) for non-specialists.

The overall approach emphasizes the interdependent nature of these modalities, proposing that advancements in lower dimensions provide priors for higher-dimensional synthesis, while higher-dimensional structures can regularize lower-dimensional generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, the "findings and results" consist of the comprehensive synthesis and categorization of existing methodologies, along with observed trends, challenges, and comparisons across different generative model types and dimensions.

#### 4.1. 2D Generation (Appearance)

- **Algorithms:** The field is dominated by diffusion models, exemplified by Imagen, DALL-E (versions 2 and 3), DeepFloyd IF, Stable Diffusion (including SDXL), and FLUX.1. These models achieve high-quality and semantically accurate image generation from text by leveraging techniques like latent diffusion, pre-trained language models (e.g., T5, CLIP), and transformer architectures.
- **Observation:** Scaling up language models in text-to-image generation has been shown to enhance image fidelity and text congruence more effectively than simply enlarging image diffusion models.

#### 4.2. Video Generation (Appearance + Dynamics)

- **Evolution:** Early video generation methods adapted 2D image models by adding temporal layers. State-of-the-art models now frequently use diffusion transformer architectures operating on compressed spatio-temporal latent spaces, processing spatial and temporal information simultaneously (e.g., Sora).
- **Algorithm Categories:**
 - **VAE- and GAN-based:** Early approaches focused on stochastic dynamics and decomposing content/motion (e.g., MoCoGAN, StyleGAN-V). These provided foundational contributions but often faced limitations in resolution and temporal coherence.
 - **Diffusion-based (U-Net & Transformer):** U-Net-based models (e.g., VDM, Make-A-Video, Imagen Video) extended image diffusion, often using joint image-video training or cascaded super-resolution. Transformer-based models (e.g., VDT, Sora, CogVideoX) utilize spatiotemporal attention mechanisms on tokenized latent spaces, showing strong performance in diverse tasks and resolutions.
 - **Autoregressive-based:** Inspired by LLMs, these methods encode visual content into discrete latent tokens and model token distribution (e.g., CogVideo, VideoPoet).
- **Evaluation:** Primarily uses Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) for spatiotemporal feature distributions. More recent benchmarks like VBench offer granular analysis. However, automated metrics often misalign with human perception, leading to an increasing shift towards human studies for holistic assessment. Challenges include measuring long-horizon coherence, narrative structure, and physical plausibility.
- **Applications:** Video editing (e.g., Tune-A-Video, AnimateDiff), novel view synthesis (e.g., ViewCrafter, SV3D), and human animation (e.g., ControlNet, Animate-Anyone) have benefited from video diffusion models.

#### 4.3. 3D Generation (Appearance + Geometry)

- **3D Representations:**
 - **Explicit:** Point clouds, voxel grids, meshes, and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offer precise visualizations but can be memory-inefficient or lack explicit connectivity. 3DGS is highlighted for its efficiency in training and rendering.
 - **Implicit:** Signed Distance Fields (SDF) and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) define 3D spaces with continuous functions, enabling smooth surfaces but with higher training/inference overhead.
 - **Hybrid:** Combine explicit and implicit methods (e.g., Hybrid Voxel Grids, Deep Marching Tetrahedra (DMTet), Triplane) for improved efficiency and flexibility.
- **Algorithm Categories (by Input):**
 - **Text-to-3D:** Leverages 2D image models (e.g., SDS loss) or synthesizes multi-view images.
 - *Feedforward:* Directly synthesize 3D from text (e.g., Point·E, Shap·E). Efficient but often suffer from inferior detail.
 - *Optimization-based:* Optimize 3D representations using text-to-image models for supervision (e.g., DreamFusion, Magic3D, DreamGaussian). Produce finer details but are computationally expensive.
 - *MVS-based:* Synthesize multi-view images first, then reconstruct 3D (e.g., Instant3D, Sherpa3D). Aims to leverage 2D models effectively but faces challenges in 3D consistency.
 - **Image-to-3D:** Generates 3D assets consistent with a single input image.
 - *Feedforward:* Encode 3D assets into latent codes (e.g., 3DGen, CraftsMan, Clay). Achieve fine geometric details but are limited by 3D dataset size and diversity.
 - *Optimization-based:* Optimize 3D assets using SDS loss from image-to-image or text-to-image models (e.g., Zero123, Magic123). Strong generalization and texture precision but can suffer from overly smooth geometries and long training times.
 - *MVS-based:* Generate multi-view images from a single image, then reconstruct 3D (e.g., One-2-3-45, InstantMesh, CRM). Significantly improve 3D consistency and reduce inference time but may lack high-quality geometry details due to model scale limitations.
 - **Video-to-3D:** Builds on 2D diffusion, extending image priors into time to learn implicit 3D structure from motion (e.g., SV3D, CAT3D). A promising approach for 3D-consistent object synthesis, especially with camera-controllable video generation models.
- **Evaluation:** Quantitative evaluation remains challenging due to the lack of groundtruth 3D data. Metrics like CLIP Score, Chamfer Distance (CD), and IoU are used, alongside human preference studies. Pretraining on large-scale 3D datasets is shown to improve geometry quality significantly.
- **Applications:** Avatar generation (head and full-body, animated), scene generation (object-centered, room-scale, outdoor/city-scale), and 3D editing (appearance, geometry, object-level manipulation).

#### 4.4. 4D Generation (Appearance + Dynamics + Geometry)

- **4D Representations:** Extend 3D spatial representations with time, often decomposing into a canonical 3D shape and a deformation model (e.g., D-NeRF, 4DGS). Challenges include high computational cost, addressed by explicit and hybrid methods like planar decompositions and hash-based representations.
- **Algorithm Categories:**
 - **Feedforward:** Directly synthesize dynamic scenes in a single pass (e.g., Control4D, Animate3D, L4GM). Prioritize speed and adaptability, suitable for real-time applications, but may lack detail and diversity compared to optimization-based methods.
 - **Optimization-based:** Employ iterative techniques like Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to adapt pre-trained diffusion models for dynamic 4D scenes (e.g., MAV3D, 4D-fy, AYG). Achieve high quality and temporal coherence by leveraging rich priors but are computationally demanding.
- **Evaluation:** Focuses on generation time, CLIP score for image similarity, and LPIPS for perceptual similarity between generated and ground truth novel views. A trade-off exists between speed (feedforward) and fidelity/editability (optimization-based).
- **Applications:** 4D editing (instruction-guided editing of dynamic scenes) and human animation (generating realistic 3D human motion from sparse controls or multi-modal conditions like text and music).

#### 4.5. Cross-Dimensional Trends and Challenges

- Image generation models serve as critical components across all higher dimensions, providing supervision (e.g., SDS loss) or synthesizing multi-view data.
- The trade-off between generation speed/efficiency and output quality/consistency is a recurring theme across 3D and 4D generation.
- The scarcity of high-quality 3D/4D datasets compared to 2D datasets remains a bottleneck, motivating methods to leverage 2D priors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey contributes to the field by providing a structured and comprehensive overview of multimodal generative models, unifying disparate research areas under a single framework of data dimensionality progression. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Understanding:** By systematically unifying the study of 2D, video, 3D, and 4D generation, the survey offers a coherent perspective that highlights the interdependencies and evolutionary pathways across modalities. This unified view can facilitate a deeper understanding of the underlying principles governing real-world simulation.
- **Guidance for Researchers:** The comprehensive review of methodologies, datasets, and evaluation metrics serves as a foundational resource for newcomers, clarifying the landscape and providing practical references. For experienced researchers, it promotes critical analysis by outlining cross-domain relations and identifying open problems.
- **Advancing Real-World Simulation:** The explicit focus on integrating appearance, dynamics, and geometry in an escalating dimensional hierarchy directly addresses the challenge of building more comprehensive "world simulators." This is crucial for developing intelligent systems that can accurately capture the complexities of reality.
- **Catalyzing Cross-Dimensional Synergy:** The survey proposes "cross-dimensional synergy" as a key future direction, where knowledge is explicitly transferred between modalities. This includes:
 - **Semantic Offloading:** Suggesting that 2D foundation models, with their abundant data and strong semantic capabilities, can serve as the primary engine for semantic and diversity generation, with higher-dimensional models focusing on geometric and motion lifting. This could alleviate the data scarcity issue in 3D/4D.
 - **Consistency Back-propagation and Physics-grounded Regularization:** High-dimensional structural constraints (e.g., coherent geometry, physics laws in 4D) can provide valuable regularization for lower-dimensional generation tasks, addressing issues like temporal inconsistency in video models. This suggests a feedback loop where higher-dimensional understanding refines lower-dimensional synthesis.
- **Towards Unified Spatiotemporal World Models:** The insights from the survey point towards the development of unified generative backbones that jointly model spatial reconstruction (2D → 3D) and temporal evolution (2D → video). Such architectures could enable consistent 4D synthesis with enhanced controllability, moving closer to the vision of generative world models that integrate appearance, geometry, and dynamics into a single spatiotemporal representation. This would significantly impact fields like embodied AI, virtual content creation, and autonomous systems.
- **Highlighting Practical Challenges:** By emphasizing the importance of computational efficiency, runtime, memory usage, and training costs in addition to visual quality, the survey encourages future research and benchmarks to consider the practical deployability and scalability of generative models.

In conclusion, this survey provides a structured framework for understanding the evolution of multimodal generative models, identifies critical interdependencies between modalities, and outlines strategic directions for future research aimed at building more coherent, controllable, and physically grounded real-world simulators.
