# ActionUNet: Improving Robustness of VLA Models with Efficient Multi-scale Fine-tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> ActionUNet is a lightweight fine-tuning head for pre-trained VLA policies that inserts a temporal U-Net into the action-feature space to inject multi-scale motion priors, then decodes trajectories with a conditional SIREN whose local fields are blended by Matérn-5/2 kernel weights into C2-smooth actions. On π0.5 it lifts hard-setting success by 9.8 points on RoboTwin 2.0, 6.1 on LIBERO-Plus, and 11.4 in real-world tests.

## Problem

- VLA action experts map coarse multi-modal semantics directly to fine-grained action steps through a single-scale head, ignoring that per-step end-effector displacement varies widely across and within tasks.
- Prior multi-scale policies either discretize actions via vector quantization, creating an information bottleneck, or need specialized heads and full retraining, which prevents reuse of strong pre-trained VLA priors.
- Naively fusing actions across temporal scales creates discontinuities at scale boundaries, producing non-smooth trajectories and mechanical oscillations that further degrade execution robustness under visual distractors.

## Method

- ==Temporal U-Net==: a strided Conv1D encoder halves temporal resolution at each stage, then nearest-neighbor upsampling with skip-connection linear fusion restores the horizon, injecting hierarchical coarse-to-fine priors into backbone action features.
- ==Conditional SIREN decoder==: per-anchor implicit fields with time-dependent biases map continuous query time to actions, aggregated by normalized ==Matérn-5/2 kernel== weights, provably yielding a C2-continuous action trajectory.
- Matérn-5/2 correlated flow noise replaces white Gaussian noise in ==flow-matching== training; the backbone stays frozen and only the action expert is fine-tuned under the base policy's regression or flow objective.

## Results

- RoboTwin 2.0 (12 bimanual tasks): π0.5 average success rises from **59.6%** to **68.4%** easy and **29.8%** to **39.6%** hard; 20 of 24 sequential A/B tests are significant.
- LIBERO-Plus robustness improves from **65.8%** to **71.9%** for π0.5 and from **60.9%** to **64.6%** for OpenVLA-OFT; LIBERO reaches **97.7%** and **98.6%**; real-world hard success goes from **57.3%** to **68.7%**.
- A parameter-matched single-scale variant gains only 4.0 hard points versus 12.0 for ActionUNet; DCT and Laplacian action-space pyramids fail; per-call latency rises only **3.9 ms** (**6.5%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-scale structure helps when injected into the backbone's temporally aligned feature space, but hurts when imposed as output-space decomposition that overrides the pre-trained action mapping.
> - Smoothing alone does not buy robustness: SIREN alone gives the lowest jerk but no hard-setting gain. Multi-scale aggregation drives robustness, and smoothing repairs the discontinuities that fusion introduces.
> - Extra capacity mainly improves fitting in clean settings, while robustness to distractors comes from architectural inductive bias, so robustness claims need parameter-matched controls.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Stacking blocks requires a robot to move above the work area and then make finer adjustments to place a block. A vision-language-action (VLA) model must turn broad visual and language cues into actions precise enough for physical execution, even though the required movement scale can change from task to task and within one task. ActionUNet adds a temporal multi-scale feature-refinement step and a smooth continuous decoder to an existing VLA policy, without replacing the policy's backbone. On RoboTwin 2.0, averaged over the paper's 12 selected tasks, the reported hard-setting success rate rises from 29.8% for π0.5 to 39.6% for π0.5+ActionUNet.

### 2. Refining the action features at several scales

The authors characterize scale using the sum of left- and right-end-effector displacement between successive recorded steps. Larger displacements indicate coarser motion, but this measure is a dataset-level proxy, not velocity.

ActionUNet responds in two stages. First, a temporal U-Net downsamples the sequence of action features and then restores it. Its skip connections retain local detail while it combines temporal scales. Second, a conditional SIREN decoder maps query times to actions. For a flow-based policy, it maps them to the flow-matching vector field instead. Matérn-weighted aggregation combines the decoder's local fields.

The paper proves that the decoder output is twice continuously differentiable ($C^{2}$). This is a property of the continuous output function. It is not evidence that every executed robot path has zero jitter. During fine-tuning, the VLA backbone stays frozen. ActionUNet is added to the action expert, and training retains the backbone's original objective.

Schematic, not a data plot. RGB imagery, proprioception, and the instruction enter the VLA backbone. Temporal-aligned action features pass through the ActionUNet temporal U-Net and then the conditional SIREN decoder to produce predicted actions. The diagram shows compatibility with flow-based policies such as π0.5 and regression-based policies such as OpenVLA-OFT.

### 3. Testing whether the multi-scale design matters

In the main RoboTwin evaluation, each of the 12 single-task policies uses 50 clean demonstrations. Each policy is evaluated over 100 rollouts in both settings. The hard setting adds stronger environmental perturbations.

One skeptical alternative is that ActionUNet helps only because it adds parameters. To test this, the authors compare full ActionUNet with a parameter-matched r=1 single-scale variant. This variant removes temporal downsampling and cross-scale fusion. On a four-task RoboTwin ablation, hard-setting mean success is 38.5% for r=1 versus 46.5% for ActionUNet. This comparison weakens the explanation that the improvement is due only to adding parameters. It does not prove that multi-scale structure alone explains every performance difference.

A second alternative applies multi-scale decomposition in the output action space instead of the feature space. Under the same four-task protocol, DCT decomposition averages 33.0% hard success and a Laplacian residual pyramid averages 32.0%. ActionUNet reaches 46.5%, and π0.5 scores 34.5%. These controls favor the authors' feature-space multi-scale design over capacity alone or these tested output-space decompositions. They do not identify every cause of the gain.

### 4. Scale before execution

The tested combination improved robustness in these experiments: refining temporal structure in the action features, then decoding it continuously. That result comes from a selected subset of RoboTwin's 50 tasks. It is not a claim about every task or every robot.

The added module also has a cost. On the paper's compiled RTX 4090 measurement, one policy call rises from 60.947 ± 0.251 ms to 64.886 ± 0.376 ms, an increase of 3.939 ms, or 6.46%. These measurements cover policy computation, not end-to-end robot execution times.
