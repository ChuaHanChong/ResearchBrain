# ViSurf: Visual Supervised-and-Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Large Vision-and-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ViSurf introduces a unified, single-stage post-training framework that integrates supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large vision-and-language models (LVLMs). The method consistently outperforms existing paradigms across diverse visual perception and reasoning tasks, achieving an average relative gain of 38.6% and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often leads to sub-optimal performance and catastrophic forgetting in Large Vision-and-Language Models (LVLMs).
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles with tasks requiring knowledge beyond the model's pre-existing base, particularly when self-generated rollouts are unreliable.
- Sequential SFT RLVR pipelines are computationally expensive, complex, and still prone to catastrophic forgetting during the initial SFT phase.

## Method

- ==ViSurf== proposes a unified, single-stage post-training paradigm that integrates ==SFT and RLVR objectives==, leveraging a theoretical analysis of their gradient similarity.
- The method constructs an ==augmented rollout set== that includes both model-generated rollouts and ground-truth labels, with rewards computed for both to influence advantage calculations.
- Three novel ==reward control strategies== are introduced: aligning ground-truth labels with model preference, eliminating thinking reward for ground-truth, and adaptively smoothing ground-truth rewards to balance external supervision with internal reinforcement.

## Results

- ViSurf achieved superior performance across diverse visual-language tasks, demonstrating an average relative gain of **38.6%** over the baseline and outperforming SFT, RLVR, and sequential SFT RLVR methods.
- The framework effectively mitigated catastrophic forgetting, maintaining or improving performance on VQA tasks while fine-tuning for new perception tasks, unlike SFT and sequential methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of each reward control strategy to improved performance, training stability, and entropy behavior, additionally showing reduced dependence on meticulous prompt design.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The gradients of SFT and RLVR objectives share a similar structural form, which enables their effective integration into a single, unified objective function.
> - Adaptive reward control strategies, particularly smoothing the ground-truth reward, allow ViSurf to dynamically adjust the influence of external supervision based on the model's internal generative capabilities.
> - Injecting ground-truth labels directly into the RL framework within a single training loop provides robust guidance, mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enhancing performance on out-of-distribution tasks more effectively than multi-stage approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Vision-and-Language Models (LVLMs) capable of performing diverse visual perception tasks represents a significant area of research. Post-training optimization of these models typically employs two primary paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

SFT involves directly optimizing models using expert-annotated data, providing explicit external guidance. This approach has been widely adopted in prominent LVLM series such as LLaVA, QwenVL, MGM, Eagle, and InternVL, and has shown efficacy in tailoring models for specialized downstream tasks like image quality assessment and autonomous driving. However, SFT has been observed to lead to sub-optimal generalization, particularly in new or out-of-distribution scenarios, and is prone to catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge.

Conversely, RLVR, utilizing on-policy methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO), leverages internal reinforcement signals derived from rollout evaluations to optimize model policies. This paradigm is known for mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enhancing performance by aligning models with desired behaviors. Recent advancements like SegZero and VisualRFT have further underscored its utility for LVLMs. A key limitation of RLVR, however, is its dependence on the model's internal knowledge base; its performance may stagnate when tasks extend beyond the model's initial policy distribution or require external knowledge acquisition. Specifically, RLVR struggles with tasks that demand novel knowledge or "no-object" responses where self-rollouts cannot provide a correct signal.

A common attempt to combine the strengths of these two paradigms involves a sequential SFT → RLVR pipeline. While this approach aims to leverage SFT for initial knowledge injection and RLVR for subsequent performance refinement, it introduces significant computational overhead. Furthermore, it remains susceptible to catastrophic forgetting during the initial SFT phase, which can compromise the knowledge gained. This research addresses the limitations of these existing methods by proposing a unified, single-stage framework that aims to synthesize the complementary advantages of SFT and RLVR, thereby enhancing both knowledge acquisition and performance optimization while mitigating associated drawbacks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified, single-stage post-training paradigm that effectively integrates the benefits of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Vision-and-Language Models (LVLMs), while addressing their individual limitations and the challenges of sequential approaches.

The motivation for ViSurf stems from several identified shortcomings of current post-training methodologies:

1. **Sub-optimal Performance and Catastrophic Forgetting in SFT:** SFT, while effective for injecting explicit knowledge from annotated data, often leads to sub-optimal generalization capabilities. Figure 1(c) illustrates that for tasks exceeding an LVLM's knowledge base, SFT may perform better than RLVR, but it still struggles with out-of-distribution knowledge acquisition. Additionally, SFT is susceptible to catastrophic forgetting, where fine-tuning on new tasks degrades performance on previously learned ones. Table 2 and Figure 2 corroborate this, showing SFT's performance degradation on benchmarks like ChartQA and DocVQA when fine-tuned without specific VQA data.

2. **Knowledge Boundary Limitations of RLVR:** RLVR, relying on internal reinforcement signals, is effective at refining existing capabilities and mitigating forgetting. However, its efficacy is fundamentally constrained by the model's inherent knowledge boundaries. As depicted in Figure 1(b), RLVR generally performs well on tasks within the LVLM’s existing knowledge base. Yet, when tasks extend beyond these pre-existing capabilities, RLVR's performance can stagnate or even degrade, as it lacks a mechanism for acquiring entirely new, externally guided knowledge. The case study on non-object referring expression segmentation (Figure 3) exemplifies this: RLVR achieves high overall gIoU but fails to correctly identify the absence of objects, often generating masks even when no relevant object exists. This highlights RLVR’s struggle when self-exploration cannot yield the correct "no object" output, necessitating external grounding.

3. **Computational Overhead and Forgetting in Sequential Pipelines:** A straightforward approach to combine SFT and RLVR is a two-stage pipeline (SFT → RLVR). However, this sequence introduces significant computational overhead due to two separate training phases. Furthermore, it remains vulnerable to catastrophic forgetting during the initial SFT phase, undermining the very knowledge injection it aims to achieve. Figure 2's bar chart specifically points out that the SFT → RLVR pipeline also exhibits catastrophic forgetting, similar to standalone SFT.

To address these limitations, the research posits that a unified framework is necessary to leverage both external supervision (from SFT) and internal reinforcement (from RLVR) simultaneously and efficiently. The core motivation is to synthesize these complementary strengths within a single, coherent training stage to improve knowledge acquisition, enhance performance, maintain stability, and mitigate catastrophic forgetting, without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ViSurf (Visual Supervised-and-Reinforcement Fine-Tuning) methodology proposes a unified, single-stage paradigm to integrate Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). This approach is grounded in a theoretical analysis of the objective functions and gradients of SFT and RLVR, aiming to combine their strengths.

**Preliminary Analysis of SFT and RLVR:**
The paper begins by defining the objective functions for both SFT and RLVR.
- **SFT (Equation 1)** aims to minimize the negative log-likelihood of ground-truth labels `y` given visual `v` and textual `t` inputs: $L_{SFT}(\theta) = -\text{E}_{(v,t)\sim D_{input}, y\sim D_{label}}[\log \pi_\theta(y | v, t)]$. Its gradient (Equation 4) is $∇_\theta L_{SFT}(\theta) = -\text{E}_{(v,t)\sim D_{input}, y\sim D_{label}}[∇_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y | v, t)]$.
- **RLVR (Equation 3)**, specifically utilizing an on-policy method like GRPO, optimizes the policy $\pi_\theta$ based on verifiable rewards. For an input $(v,t)$, the old policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$ generates `G` rollouts $\{o_j\}_{j=1}^G$. Each rollout $o_j$ is evaluated by a reward function $r(\cdot)$, and advantages $\hat{A}_j$ are calculated (Equation 2) relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards for the group of rollouts. The RLVR objective minimizes a term involving these advantages and the policy ratio. Its gradient (Equation 5) is approximately $∇_\theta L_{RLVR}(\theta) = -\text{E}_{(v,t)\sim D_{input}, \{o_j\}_{j=1}^G\sim \pi_{\theta_{old}}}[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{j=1}^G \hat{A}_j ∇_\theta \log \pi_\theta(o_j | v, t)]$.

**Unification in ViSurf Objective:**
The core insight of ViSurf is to include the ground-truth label `y` as a high-reward sample directly within the RLVR framework. This is achieved by constructing an augmented rollout set that includes both the ground-truth label `y` and the `G` self-generated rollouts $\{o_j\}_{j=1}^G$. The reward for the ground-truth label, $r(y)$, is then incorporated alongside the rewards for the self-generated rollouts. The advantage calculation for both rollouts ($\hat{A}_j$, Equation 6) and the ground-truth ($\hat{A}_y$, Equation 7) is modified to be relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards from this augmented set.

The objective of ViSurf (Equation 8) then extends the RLVR objective to include a term for the ground-truth label:
$L_{ViSurf}(\theta) = -\text{E}_{(v,t), \{o_j\}, y} [\frac{1}{G+1} (\sum_{j=1}^G \min(\dots \hat{A}_j) + \min(\dots \hat{A}_y))]$.
The gradient of ViSurf (Equation 9) reveals its composite nature:
$∇_\theta L_{ViSurf}(\theta) = -\text{E}_{(v,t), \{o_j\}, y} [\frac{1}{G+1} (\sum_{j=1}^G \hat{A}_j ∇_\theta \log \pi_\theta(o_j | v, t) + \hat{A}_y ∇_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y | v, t))]$.
This gradient can be interpreted as a sum of an RLVR term and an SFT term (Equation 10), scaled by their respective advantages. This formulation enables simultaneous optimization, leveraging internal reinforcement from rollouts and external guidance from ground-truth labels.

**Reward Control Strategies for Ground-Truth Labels:**
To ensure training stability and optimal balancing between external supervision and internal reinforcement, ViSurf introduces three novel reward control strategies for ground-truth labels:

1. **Aligning Ground-truth Labels with Rollouts Preference:** This strategy involves reformatting ground-truth data to match the model's preferred stylistic patterns (e.g., JSON whitespace). This minimizes distribution shift between the current policy ($\pi_\theta$) and the old policy ($\pi_{\theta_{old}}$), which is crucial for maintaining the approximation $\pi_\theta \approx \pi_{\theta_{old}}$ used in gradient derivation.

2. **Eliminating Thinking Reward for Ground-truth Labels:** When reasoning paths are required for a task, ground-truth labels typically lack such annotations. ViSurf assigns a reasoning format score of zero to ground-truth labels in these cases. This encourages the model to generate its own reasoning traces through self-rollouts, preventing bias or penalty from the absence of external reasoning annotations.

3. **Smoothing the Reward for Ground-truth Labels:** If the maximum reward among generated rollouts (max$\{\{r(o_j)\}_{j=1}^G\}$) is greater than or equal to the ground-truth reward $r(y)$, it indicates that the policy has already produced a high-quality output. In such cases, $r(y)$ is set to the mean of the rollout rewards. This makes the ground-truth advantage $\hat{A}_y$ approximately zero, effectively disabling the external supervision signal when it's no longer needed, preventing reward hacking and unnecessary SFT-like updates.

**Optimization Dynamics:**
The paper analyzes the self-adaptive nature of the ViSurf objective. When the policy fails to generate high-quality rollouts, $\hat{A}_j$ decreases (potentially becoming negative), and $\hat{A}_y$ remains high, causing the SFT term to dominate the policy update, providing strong external guidance. Conversely, when the policy generates desirable rollouts, the reward smoothing mechanism sets $\hat{A}_y \approx 0$, leading the RLVR term to dominate the optimization. This dynamic shifting between learning modes is a key feature of the single-stage ViSurf.

**Implementation Details:**
ViSurf is instantiated with Qwen2.5VL-7B as the base Large Vision-and-Language Model, and SAM2 is integrated when segmentation capabilities are required. A constant learning rate of 1e-6 is used across all methods, with specific batch sizes (32 for SFT, 16 for RLVR and ViSurf) to ensure fair comparison. Reward functions for various tasks are adapted from VisionReasoner, incorporating measures of format accuracy, point accuracy, and bounding box accuracy, along with specific MathVista rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ViSurf across diverse benchmarks and training paradigms revealed several key findings:

#### 4.1. Comparison of Different Training Paradigms

- **Superior Performance:** ViSurf consistently outperformed standalone SFT, RLVR, and the two-stage SFT → RLVR pipeline across all evaluated domains (Non-Object Segmentation, Reasoning Segmentation, GUI Grounding, Industrial Anomaly Detection, Medical Imaging, Mathematical Reasoning). Table 1 shows ViSurf achieving an average relative improvement of 38.6% over the baseline.
- **Domain-Specific Effectiveness:** The performance gains were most pronounced in challenging domains such as Non-Object Segmentation (gRefCOCO) and Anomaly Detection (RealIAD), where baseline models initially struggled. This suggests ViSurf's effectiveness in expanding model capabilities beyond their inherent knowledge base. In contrast, in domains where the baseline already demonstrated high proficiency, the incremental improvements from ViSurf were more marginal.
- **Addressing SFT Degradation and RLVR Limitations:** SFT exhibited performance degradation on OmniACT, attributed to potential overfitting during pre-training. Both RLVR and ViSurf preserved foundational performance in such cases. Notably, pure RLVR underperformed the original model in RealIAD and gRefCOCO (non-object detection), indicating that incorrect self-generated rollouts introduced noise. ViSurf successfully mitigated this issue.

#### 4.2. Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation

- Evaluations on ChartQA and DocVQA (Table 2) after fine-tuning without specific VQA data demonstrated that ViSurf, along with RLVR, exhibited robustness against catastrophic forgetting. In contrast, SFT and the SFT → RLVR pipeline suffered from performance degradation, suggesting their susceptibility to forgetting previously acquired knowledge.

#### 4.3. Generalizability Across Models

- When applied to Qwen2VL-7B (Table 3), ViSurf consistently outperformed its counterparts (SFT, RLVR, SFT → RLVR), indicating its generalizability beyond the primary Qwen2.5VL-7B + SAM2 setup.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study of Reward Control Strategies

- **Aligning Ground-truth Labels with Rollouts Preference:** Ablating this strategy led to consistent performance degradation across datasets (Table 4), empirically validating its importance for minimizing distribution shift and maintaining the $\pi_\theta \approx \pi_{\theta_{old}}$ approximation.
- **Eliminating Thinking Reward for Ground-truth Labels:** This strategy was crucial for tasks requiring complex inference, such as ReasonSeg and MathVista, encouraging the model to generate reasoning processes. However, for simpler tasks like gRefCOCO, omitting the reasoning trace yielded superior performance, suggesting task-dependent utility.
- **Smoothing the Reward for Ground-truth Labels:** The performance decline observed across datasets after ablating reward smoothing (Table 4) underscored its necessity. This mechanism ensures that the SFT term becomes superfluous when the model's self-rollouts already achieve high-quality solutions, preventing redundant external supervision.

#### 4.5. In-depth Analysis

- **Entropy Analysis (Figure 5):** ViSurf exhibited an initial entropy drop, indicating efficient fitting to external guidance, followed by a slower convergence rate compared to RLVR and SFT → RLVR. This slower convergence implies ViSurf effectively avoids entropy collapse, promoting more stable learning.
- **Training Stability (Figure 6):** Models trained with ViSurf demonstrated greater stability over extended training periods compared to pure RLVR and SFT → RLVR, whose performance declined with longer training. The introduced external guidance in ViSurf appears to act as a constraint, stabilizing the optimization process.
- **Boundary Analysis:** The performance gain of ViSurf correlated with the baseline model's initial performance (Table 1). When the baseline performed poorly (e.g., below 50%), ViSurf yielded substantial improvements. When the baseline already achieved high performance (e.g., above 50%), ViSurf's upper bound aligned with that of RLVR alone, corroborating the theoretical analysis in Section 3.5.
- **Reduced Prompt Design Burden (Table 5):** ViSurf achieved satisfactory results even without detailed formatting prompts, which are typically critical for RLVR paradigms to guide model outputs. This indicates that ViSurf's integration of external guidance can reduce the dependency on extensive manual prompt engineering.
- **Training Cost (Table 6):** While RLVR and ViSurf offered greater memory efficiency per GPU compared to SFT, they incurred higher computational time per step due to the overhead of generating rollouts. ViSurf’s per-step time was comparable to RLVR.

#### 4.6. Comparison with State-of-the-Art

- ViSurf achieved state-of-the-art performance on gRefCOCO and ReasonSeg benchmarks (Table 7) when compared against other advanced models such as LISA, GSVA, SAM4MLLM, SegZero, and VisionReasoner.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Results

- Visualizations (Figure 7) demonstrated ViSurf's capability to successfully address various visual perception tasks, including non-object segmentation, anomaly detection, GUI grounding, medical image analysis, and mathematical reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViSurf introduces a novel, unified post-training paradigm for Large Vision-and-Language Models (LVLMs) that effectively integrates Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). This unification is a significant contribution to the field of LVLM optimization, addressing several limitations of existing standalone or sequential approaches.

The primary significance lies in its capacity for **simultaneous knowledge injection and internal reinforcement** within a single, efficient training stage. This means models can acquire new, externally guided knowledge (a strength of SFT) while concurrently refining existing capabilities and mitigating catastrophic forgetting (strengths of RLVR). The theoretical derivation, showing how ViSurf's objective gradient combines elements of both SFT and RLVR, provides a robust foundation for this approach.

The introduction of **three novel reward control strategies** for ground-truth labels contributes to **enhanced training stability and optimization efficiency**. These strategies dynamically balance the influence of external supervision and internal reinforcement, ensuring that the model receives appropriate guidance without being over-constrained or hindered by redundant signals. This self-adaptive learning mechanism represents an advancement in fine-tuning methodologies for complex multimodal models.

Empirically, ViSurf's **consistent outperformance** across diverse benchmarks, including challenging visual perception and reasoning tasks, indicates its practical utility. Its ability to expand model capabilities in domains where baselines struggle, while preserving foundational performance and mitigating catastrophic forgetting, suggests a more robust and generalizable fine-tuning method. The reduction in reliance on highly detailed prompt engineering also has implications for the practical deployment and accessibility of LVLMs.

The potential impact of ViSurf is multi-faceted:
- **Improved LVLM Performance and Generalization:** By combining the strengths of SFT and RLVR, ViSurf can lead to LVLMs with enhanced accuracy, better generalization to novel scenarios, and increased robustness to out-of-distribution data.
- **Enhanced Training Efficiency:** The single-stage nature of ViSurf eliminates the computational overhead associated with traditional two-stage SFT → RLVR pipelines, offering a more streamlined and resource-efficient training process despite the per-step cost of rollouts.
- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** The demonstrated resilience against catastrophic forgetting contributes to the development of more stable and adaptable LVLMs that can continuously learn new tasks without compromising previously acquired knowledge.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across various domains, from non-object segmentation to mathematical reasoning, suggests its potential as a general-purpose fine-tuning solution for a wide range of vision-and-language tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper notes ViSurf's inherent compatibility with incorporating explicit reasoning traces and advanced techniques like knowledge distillation. This provides a foundation for future work in post-training LVLMs, potentially leading to more interpretable and powerful models.

While the current implementation relies on specific frameworks and model architectures, the underlying principles of ViSurf offer a promising direction for advancing the field of machine learning, particularly in the domain of large multimodal models.
