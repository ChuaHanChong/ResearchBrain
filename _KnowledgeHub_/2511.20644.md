# Vision-Language Memory for Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM² adds view-consistent 3D-aware representations plus a dual-memory module (sliding-window working memory + fixed-capacity episodic memory) to a video VLM, resolving semantic-geometric misalignment and enabling bounded-cost spatial reasoning across frames; achieves state-of-the-art on VSI-Bench, VSTI-Bench, ScanQA, and SQA3D.

## Problem

- Semantic 2D features give strong categorical understanding but lack metric grounding, causing distinct objects across viewpoints to be wrongly matched as the same instance.
- Naive fusion of geometric features from 3D visual geometry models with semantic features produces view-dependent instabilities and global spatial inconsistency under camera motion.
- Existing VLMs rely on token-based context windows that overwrite prior observations, so objects seen earlier and later revisited cannot be recalled for tasks like counting.

## Method

- ==Adaptive 3D Position Injection==: a ==learnable gate== selectively injects predicted per-patch 3D coordinates into visual tokens, filtering noisy monocular depth predictions instead of applying them uniformly.
- ==Viewpoint-Aware Geometry Alignment==: fuses geometry tokens with projected view tokens at patch and frame level to disambiguate viewpoint-ambiguous geometric features before fusion.
- ==Dual-memory module==: ==cross-attention working memory== (sliding window over recent frames) fused via a learnable gate with a fixed-capacity ==episodic memory== updated by cosine-similarity replacement.

## Results

- On VSI-Bench, VLM²-7B scores **68.8** average, beating VLM-3R-7B (**60.9**) and GPT-5 (**55.0**), with a **+28.2** point gain on Appearance Order.
- On VSTI-Bench, VLM² reaches **65.3** average versus VLM-3R's **58.8**, an **11.1%** relative improvement, with **+16.2** on Camera Movement Direction.
- Ablations show 3D-aware representation adds **+8.6%** over baseline, and combining both memory modules adds **+4.0%** further, totaling **+12.6%** over baseline (**55.2** to **67.8**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing semantic and geometric features without explicit viewpoint-alignment leaves misalignment unresolved regardless of fusion strategy sophistication (concat-MLP vs cross-attention).
> - Gating noisy predicted 3D coordinates rather than injecting them uniformly is necessary; unfiltered noisy depth/position injection can degrade rather than help performance.
> - Bounded-capacity memory (sliding window plus fixed-size similarity-pruned episodic bank) can match or beat unbounded-growth memory designs while keeping compute constant over long videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial reasoning is an essential capability for intelligent agents, particularly in robotics, enabling comprehension and interaction with physical environments. Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs), despite advancements, exhibit limitations in video-based spatial reasoning when compared to human performance. This deficit is primarily attributed to two challenges: semantic-geometric misalignment and the absence of persistent memory.

Existing research has explored various approaches to enhance spatial reasoning in VLMs. Early methods often incorporated explicit 3D information, such as point clouds or depth maps, from modalities like 3D-LLM, Chat-3D, and LL3DA. While effective, these approaches are often constrained by the availability and scale of 3D datasets and may require explicit 3D inputs. More recent methods, including Spatial-MLLM, VG-LLM, and VLM-3R, have leveraged geometric priors from 3D Visual Geometry Foundation Models (VGFMs). However, a common limitation among these is their reliance on direct feature fusion, which does not explicitly address semantic-geometric misalignment, often resulting in view-dependent instabilities or an inability to maintain global spatial consistency across camera movements. Furthermore, some methods necessitate accurate depth labels, which are frequently unavailable or noisy in real-world scenarios.

Memory mechanisms have been a long-standing area of research in computer vision, applied to tasks such as video understanding, 3D reconstruction, and embodied navigation (e.g., MTU3D, 3D-Mem, 3DLLM-Mem). While 3DLLM-Mem has integrated memory with 3D LLMs, its design often involves unpruned memory accumulation, leading to increased computational costs and potential redundancy over extended video sequences.

This work, VLM², positions itself by directly addressing both semantic-geometric misalignment and the absence of persistent, bounded memory. It aims to develop a view-consistent, 3D-aware representation purely from 2D video inputs, thereby reducing reliance on explicit 3D data. Simultaneously, it proposes a dual-memory architecture designed to retain critical spatial information efficiently and persistently across frames without unbounded growth, thus contributing to more robust and scalable spatial reasoning in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance video-based spatial reasoning capabilities in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for applications such as intelligent robotics, aiming for performance levels closer to human understanding. This overarching goal is driven by the need to overcome specific limitations observed in current VLMs:

**Addressing Semantic-Geometric Misalignment:**
- **Motivation:** Contemporary VLMs often struggle with consistent 3D understanding from 2D video inputs. Semantic features derived from 2D encoders, while providing strong categorical understanding, frequently lack metric grounding and precise positional awareness. This deficiency can lead to spatial inconsistencies, such as incorrectly identifying distinct objects across different viewpoints as the same instance. Conversely, geometric features from 3D visual geometry models offer reliable structural cues, but their naive fusion with semantic representations often results in view-dependent instabilities under camera motion, compromising global spatial coherence. Additionally, even when predicted 3D point clouds are used as explicit inputs, they do not inherently establish semantic-geometric correspondence with 2D semantic tokens, necessitating additional alignment mechanisms. The reliance on accurate depth maps or 3D point clouds as input further limits applicability in real-world scenarios where such data may be noisy or unavailable.
- **Objective:** To construct a 3D-aware representation purely from monocular 2D videos by explicitly aligning semantic and geometric features before fusion. This involves grounding visual features into 3D space and enforcing cross-view consistency to ensure a coherent 3D understanding that is stable under camera motion, without requiring explicit and accurate depth inputs.

**Addressing the Absence of Persistent Memory:**
- **Motivation:** Intelligent agents, particularly mobile robots, operate in dynamic environments characterized by viewpoint changes and occlusions, where objects may temporarily disappear and reappear. Many existing VLMs rely on short-term, token-based context windows that are insufficient for preserving coherent spatial representations across extended video sequences. As new visual information arrives, previously observed but still relevant spatial data can be overwritten or lost, hindering tasks that require reasoning over historical observations, such as accurate object counting across viewpoint shifts. Current memory mechanisms, if present, often suffer from unbounded growth, leading to high computational costs and inefficient storage for long videos.
- **Objective:** To equip the VLM with a persistent, attention-based memory mechanism that can effectively preserve and update 3D-aware representations across frames and viewpoints. This mechanism is designed to support consistent spatial reasoning by allowing the model to recall both immediate and earlier observations, while maintaining bounded memory usage and computational cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLM² is a Vision-Language Model incorporating persistent memory for spatial reasoning. It is designed to process monocular video sequences (V) and language instructions (Q) to generate answers through a memory mechanism built upon view-consistent, 3D-aware representations. The model is implemented on top of LLaVA-Video and its architecture is structured around two primary innovations: the construction of a view-consistent 3D-aware representation and the integration of a dual-memory module.

#### 3.1. View-Consistent 3D-Aware Representation (Section 3.1)

The initial step involves processing the input video by uniformly sampling `N` frames {I_t}. For each frame `I_t`, the model extracts:
- **Visual tokens (F_t):** Capturing semantic information from a pre-trained vision encoder.
- **Geometric priors:** Obtained from a 3D foundation model (specifically, π₃). These include:
 - **Geometry tokens (G_t):** Raw geometric features.
 - **View tokens (Z_t):** Representing viewpoint information.
 - **Per-pixel point maps (X_t):** Providing 3D coordinate data.

To address semantic-geometric misalignment, three core modules are introduced:

1. **Adaptive 3D Position Injection (A3PI):**
 - **Purpose:** To ground visual tokens in 3D space while mitigating the impact of noisy 3D coordinate predictions derived from monocular estimation.
 - **Process:** Per-patch 3D coordinates (C_t) are obtained by pooling the predicted point maps (X_t). These coordinates are then encoded into 3D position embeddings (C'_t) using sinusoidal position encoding followed by a two-layer MLP (ϕ). A learnable gate (α_t) adaptively modulates the injection of these 3D position embeddings into the visual tokens: F_t^pa = F_t + α_t ⊙ ϕ(C_t). This mechanism selectively incorporates reliable 3D cues, filtering out irrelevant or noisy information.

2. **Viewpoint-Aware Geometry Alignment (VAGA):**
 - **Purpose:** To resolve the inherent viewpoint ambiguity of raw geometric features.
 - **Process (Patch-level):** Geometry tokens (G_t) are enriched with their corresponding view tokens (Z_t). The view tokens are first projected through a linear layer (ψ_v) to align their dimensions, resulting in ˆZ_t. These are then concatenated with G_t and processed by an MLP to produce viewpoint-aware features (G_t^va).
 - **Process (Frame-level):** To provide global viewpoint context, a global view descriptor (¯Z_t), derived by pooling Z_t and projecting via a linear layer (ψ_g), is appended to G_t^va. This yields the viewpoint-consistent geometric tokens (G_t^vc), which are informed by both local and global perspective cues.

3. **Semantic-Geometric Fusion:**
 - **Purpose:** To integrate the position-aware visual tokens and viewpoint-aware geometry tokens into a coherent 3D-aware representation.
 - **Process:** A cross-attention mechanism is employed, where the position-aware visual tokens (F_t^pa) act as queries, and the viewpoint-aware geometric tokens (G_t^vc) serve as both keys and values. The output, H_t = Attn(Q(F_t^pa), K(G_t^vc), V(G_t^vc)), represents a sequence of 3D-aware tokens for frame I_t. This fusion binds visual semantics to consistent geometric structure, addressing the semantic-geometric misalignment.

#### 3.2. Dual-Memory Module (Section 3.2)

The dual-memory module is designed to store and update the 3D-aware representations (H_t), facilitating temporally aware spatial reasoning with bounded computational and storage resources.

1. **Working Memory (W_t):**
 - **Function:** Operates as a sliding window, storing the `L_w` most recent 3D-aware representations. This provides immediate context for the current observation.
 - **Mechanism:** The current 3D-aware representation (H_t) queries the working memory (W_t) via cross-attention to produce an enhanced representation (M_t^w) that incorporates relevant short-term context. The working memory is updated by adding H_t and removing the oldest entry if its capacity is exceeded.

2. **Episodic Memory (E_t):**
 - **Function:** Acts as a fixed-capacity bank (L_e entries) for storing the most salient observations across frames. This enables recall of information beyond the immediate working window.
 - **Mechanism:** H_t queries the episodic memory (E_t) through cross-attention, yielding an episodic-enhanced representation (M_t^e). The model learns implicitly what constitutes "task-relevant" memory through end-to-end training on QA tasks.

3. **Memory Fusion and Update:**
 - **Fusion:** The working-enhanced representation (M_t^w) and episodic-enhanced representation (M_t^e) are combined using a learnable gate (γ_t) to produce the final memory-enhanced feature (M_t): M_t = γ_t ⊙ M_t^w + (1 − γ_t) ⊙ M_t^e.
 - **Update:** To maintain a diverse and non-redundant episodic memory, the most similar existing entry (E_i*_t) in E_t to the current memory-enhanced representation (M_t) is identified via cosine similarity and replaced with M_t. This similarity-based update ensures bounded memory usage while preserving salient information.

#### 3.3. Training Details

VLM² is initialized from the LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2 checkpoint. The vision encoder (siglip-so400m-patch14-384) and the 3D foundation model (π₃) are kept frozen during training. The language backbone and the VLM²-specific modules are updated. Training involves one epoch on a mixed dataset comprising spatial reasoning, spatial-temporal reasoning, and 3D scene understanding QA pairs sourced from VLM-3R, ScanQA, and SQA3D. The AdamW optimizer is used with a global batch size of 128, a peak learning rate of 1e-5 for the LLM during warmup, and uniform sampling of 32 frames per scene. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with a rank of 128 and a scaling factor of 256 is applied for fine-tuning. DeepSpeed ZeRO-2 is employed for memory optimization. Experiments are conducted on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLM² was conducted across multiple spatial reasoning and 3D understanding benchmarks, demonstrating its performance against proprietary models, open-source VLMs, and spatial-enhanced models.

#### 4.1. Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks

- **VSI-Bench:** VLM² achieved an average accuracy of 68.8%, establishing state-of-the-art performance among video-based models. This performance surpassed prominent proprietary models like GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro, as well as various open-source VLMs. Notably, the model demonstrated pronounced gains in tasks requiring fine-grained 3D understanding, such as Absolute Distance and Relative Direction, and achieved state-of-the-art results in Route Plan and Appearance Order tasks, indicating proficiency in reasoning over dynamic viewpoints and consistent object relationships.
- **VSTI-Bench:** On tasks assessing spatial reasoning under temporally evolving dynamics, VLM² attained an average accuracy of 65.3%. This represented an 11.1% relative improvement over the previously top-performing method, VLM-3R (58.8%), with consistent gains observed across both spatial and temporal reasoning categories.

#### 4.2. 3D Scene Understanding Benchmarks

- **ScanQA and SQA3D:** VLM² demonstrated state-of-the-art performance among video-based models, achieving a CIDEr score of 105.5 on ScanQA and an exact match (EM) accuracy of 60.4% on SQA3D. The model outperformed most 3D/2.5D-input models, with the exception of Ross3D, which utilizes additional point clouds for reconstructive supervision. These results indicate that VLM²'s 3D-aware representation, constructed solely from video, provides effective geometric cues for 3D scene understanding.

#### 4.3. Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** VLM² achieved an average accuracy of 71.1% on CV-Bench, SPAR-Bench, and BLINK without additional fine-tuning. This suggests that the learned 3D-aware representations exhibit transferability beyond the specific training benchmarks.
- **Performance Across Video Durations:** When videos were categorized into short, mid, and long durations, VLM² maintained more stable performance on VSI-Bench compared to VLM-3R, which showed degradation with increasing video length. On VSTI-Bench, VLM² achieved higher accuracy across all duration groups, with larger gains observed on longer videos. This indicates the robustness of the view-consistent 3D-aware representation and dual-memory design for extended sequences.
- **Overall Component Analysis:** Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of each proposed module. Incorporating the 3D-aware representation into the LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B baseline yielded an 8.6% accuracy gain. Adding the working memory module further improved performance by 2.1%, while the episodic memory module contributed an additional 2.3% gain. The full VLM² model, combining all components, achieved a 12.6% improvement over the baseline, with a particularly notable gain of 12.4 points on the Route Plan task, which demands spatial structure awareness.
- **Effectiveness of 3D-Aware Representation:** Comparisons of fusion strategies revealed that simple concatenation with an MLP for semantic-geometric fusion resulted in lower performance (40.4–43.3%). A cross-attention fusion strategy with 3D backbones improved performance (59.4–61.0%). The combination of Viewpoint-Aware Geometry Alignment (VAGA) and Adaptive 3D Position Injection (A3PI) further boosted accuracy from 61.0% to 63.8%. Removing the adaptive mechanism from A3PI led to a performance reduction to 58.9%, highlighting the importance of selectively injecting position information to mitigate noise.
- **Ablation of Dual-Memory Length:** Varying the working memory window (Lw) and episodic memory capacity (Le) showed that increasing Le up to 32 generally improved performance. An optimal Lw of 8 was identified, with larger windows potentially diluting attention. The configuration (Lw=8, Le=32) yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating a balance between immediate and long-term memory components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents VLM², a vision-language model that advances video-based spatial reasoning by addressing key limitations in existing approaches. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** VLM² demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple spatial and spatial-temporal reasoning benchmarks, indicating progress towards more robust and human-like understanding of 3D environments from 2D video. This advancement is foundational for intelligent systems that must operate in complex physical spaces.
- **Practicality for Real-World Applications:** By constructing view-consistent 3D-aware representations purely from monocular 2D videos, VLM² reduces the dependency on explicit, often unavailable or noisy, 3D depth maps or point clouds. This enhances the applicability of such models in real-world scenarios, particularly for embodied agents and robotics, where sensing modalities might be limited.
- **Efficient Long-Term Memory Management:** The introduction of a dual-memory module with bounded working and episodic memory capacities offers an efficient solution for retaining critical spatial information over extended video sequences. This addresses a significant challenge in previous VLM memory mechanisms that faced issues with unbounded growth and computational inefficiency, enabling more scalable and persistent spatial reasoning.
- **Robustness to Dynamic Environmental Conditions:** The view-consistent 3D-aware representation, coupled with the dual-memory, allows the model to maintain object identity and understand spatial relationships despite common real-world challenges such as changing camera viewpoints, occlusions, and object reappearance. This robustness is crucial for reliable operation in dynamic environments.
- **Foundational for Embodied AI:** Improved spatial reasoning and persistent memory are fundamental building blocks for developing more capable embodied AI agents. These capabilities are critical for tasks such as autonomous navigation, object manipulation, environmental exploration, and nuanced human-robot interaction, contributing to the broader goal of generalist intelligent agents.
- **Contribution to Multimodal AI Research:** This work offers a method for effectively and consistently integrating visual semantics with geometric cues, resolving semantic-geometric misalignment. This contributes to the broader field of multimodal large language models by demonstrating a principled approach to combining different modalities for enhanced environmental understanding.
