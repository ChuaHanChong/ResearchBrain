# Multimodal Fused Learning for Solving the Generalized Traveling Salesman Problem in Robotic Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Central South University and the National University of Singapore developed Multimodal Fused Learning (MMFL), a framework that integrates graph and image representations to solve the Generalized Traveling Salesman Problem in robotic task planning. The approach achieved superior solution quality and computational efficiency across various problem scales and configurations, including successful validation on a physical mobile robot.

## Problem

- Exact GTSP algorithms are computationally expensive, and metaheuristics require extensive parameter tuning, hindering real-time robotic deployment.
- Existing neural combinatorial optimization methods primarily rely on graph-based inputs, often neglecting crucial geometric and spatial information.
- Autonomous robots need efficient task planning to visit exactly one location from each of several defined target clusters.

## Method

- **MMFL** introduces a framework that processes both topological (graph) and spatial (image) representations of GTSP instances.
- An ==Adaptive Resolution Scaling (ARS)== image builder component constructs spatially-aware images from node coordinates and cluster assignments, ensuring consistent information density.
- A ==multimodal fusion module== leverages learnable ==bottleneck tokens== and ==bidirectional cross-attention== to effectively combine features from independent graph and image encoders, feeding into a multi-start decoder.

## Results

- **MMFL** achieved a **0.00%** optimality gap for smaller instances and produced tour lengths **5.51%** shorter than LKH and **4.13%** shorter than OR-Tools for n=200, m=40 instances.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization, outperforming baselines across diverse grouping configurations and group size distributions, consistently achieving **0.00%** optimality gaps in most test cases.
- **MMFL** was successfully deployed and validated on a physical mobile robot, demonstrating its practical effectiveness for multi-zone exploration tasks in real-world indoor environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding and integrating geometric information via image representations alongside graph structures significantly enhances GTSP solution quality.
> - Dynamically adjusting image resolution based on problem size (ARS) is crucial for maintaining critical spatial details and generalizability.
> - A specialized multimodal fusion mechanism, employing bottleneck tokens and cross-attention, effectively synergizes diverse data modalities for a more comprehensive problem understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of effective and efficient task planning for autonomous mobile robots, a critical component in various applications such as warehouse logistics, environmental monitoring, healthcare, manufacturing, and disaster response. A common requirement in these applications is for a robot to visit precisely one location from each of several defined target clusters, which is formally modeled as a Generalized Traveling Salesman Problem (GTSP). The objective of GTSP is to find the minimum-cost tour that visits exactly one node from every cluster.

Historically, solving GTSP has involved several approaches, each with limitations:
- **Exact algorithms:** Methods like branch-and-bound or cutting-plane algorithms can guarantee optimal solutions. However, their computational cost scales exponentially with problem size, rendering them impractical for real-time, on-board execution in robotic systems.
- **Metaheuristic techniques:** Approaches such as genetic algorithms, ant-colony optimization, memetic algorithms (MA), and Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) offer improved scalability compared to exact methods. Nevertheless, they often necessitate extensive parameter tuning and domain-specific adaptations, which restricts their portability across diverse robotic platforms and environments.
- **Neural approaches:** More recently, methods based on neural networks and reinforcement learning, such as Policy Optimization with Multiple Optima (POMO), have emerged, demonstrating potential for learning heuristics directly from data. These models typically operate on graph-based inputs, representing nodes and their connectivity.

The authors identify a significant gap in existing neural approaches: their predominant reliance on purely graph-based inputs. This approach, according to the authors, fails to adequately leverage the rich geometric and spatial information that often critically influences solution quality in GTSP instances. Such information is particularly relevant when candidate nodes within a given cluster are spatially distributed, where the geometric relationships between potential visitation points can significantly impact tour length and efficiency. This research positions itself to bridge this gap by introducing a multimodal learning framework that integrates both topological (graph) and spatial (image) representations to achieve improved GTSP solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient method for solving the Generalized Traveling Salesman Problem in the context of robotic task planning, specifically addressing the limitations of existing approaches. The motivations and specific objectives include:

- **Addressing Computational Intractability:** A core motivation stems from the computational burden of exact GTSP solvers and the parameter-tuning complexities of metaheuristics. For real-time robotic operations, solutions must be generated both accurately and swiftly, a challenge that conventional methods often struggle with as problem size increases.
- **Leveraging Complementary Information:** The authors observe that while graph-based representations capture topological connectivity, they often lack explicit spatial information crucial for GTSP. In scenarios where nodes within a cluster are geographically dispersed, the geometric arrangement of these nodes significantly impacts optimal path selection. The motivation is to integrate this overlooked spatial context to enhance solution quality.
- **Developing a Multimodal Representation Strategy:** A key objective is to design a framework capable of processing and fusing diverse data modalities. This involves:
 - **Image Construction:** Creating an informative, spatially-aware image representation directly from GTSP instance coordinates and cluster assignments. This aims to explicitly encode geometric relationships that are implicit or difficult to extract from pure graph structures.
 - **Adaptive Resolution Scaling (ARS):** Implementing a mechanism to ensure that the image representation scales appropriately with varying problem sizes. This is motivated by the need to maintain consistent information density and prevent loss of critical spatial details as the number of nodes changes.
 - **Dedicated Fusion Mechanism:** Designing a module to effectively combine features extracted independently from graph and image modalities. The motivation is to enable a synergistic understanding of the problem by leveraging the strengths of both topological and spatial perspectives.
- **Achieving High-Quality and Efficient Solutions:** The overarching goal is to develop a policy capable of generating high-quality task planning schemes (i.e., shorter tour lengths) while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for real-time deployment on mobile robots.
- **Demonstrating Generalization and Practical Effectiveness:** The research aims to demonstrate that the proposed framework not only outperforms existing methods across various problem scales and configurations in simulations but also exhibits practical effectiveness and robustness when deployed on physical robotic platforms in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a Multimodal Fused Learning (MMFL) framework, which integrates graph and image-based representations through a policy network designed for solving GTSP. The framework consists of several key components:

- **Overview:** The MMFL architecture comprises a coordinate-based image builder, a graph encoder, an image encoder, a multimodal fusion module, and a multi-start decoder.
- **Image Builder:**
 - **Representation:** GTSP instances, characterized by `n` nodes with 2D coordinates `(v_i,1, v_i,2)` within `^2` and cluster assignments `c_i` from `1` to `m`, are transformed into a single-channel image `I`.
 - **Mapping:** Node coordinates are discretized into pixel positions `(v'_i,1, v'_i,2)` by scaling with image dimensions `W` and `H`. The pixel value at these coordinates is set to `c_i + 1`, while other pixels are `0`.
 - **Adaptive Resolution Scaling (ARS):** To address varying problem sizes, the image dimensions `W` and `H` are dynamically adjusted. The formula `W = H = floor(alpha * sqrt(n/w)) * w` ensures that image resolution scales proportionally with the square root of the number of nodes `n`, where `w` is the patch size and `alpha` is a scaling factor. This maintains an approximately constant node density.
 - **Positional Encoding:** For these variable-sized images, a flexible positional encoding is implemented using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that maps normalized patch coordinates `(i/W, j/H)` to embeddings.
- **Image Encoder:**
 - A specialized Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture processes the generated instance images.
 - **Patch Embedding:** The input image `I` is divided into non-overlapping `w x w` patches, each projected into a `d`-dimensional embedding space using a convolutional operation.
 - **Positional Encoding:** The MLP-generated positional encodings are added to the patch embeddings.
 - **Transformer Encoder Stack:** The combined embeddings are fed into multiple transformer encoder layers. Each layer contains a Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) sublayer and a Feed-Forward Network (FFN), with layer normalization and residual connections. The output is a sequence of enhanced patch embeddings `z^(L)`.
- **Graph Encoder:**
 - This component processes the node coordinates and cluster assignments.
 - **Initial Embedding:** For each node `i`, an initial embedding `h^(0)_i` is created by concatenating its coordinates `[v_i,1, v_i,2]` and cluster index `c_i`, followed by a linear projection.
 - **Self-Attention Layers:** The graph encoder uses multiple self-attention layers, each applying MHSA to capture relationships between nodes, followed by an FFN, with residual connections and normalization. The output consists of refined node embeddings `h^(L)_i`.
- **Multimodal Fusion Module:**
 - This module integrates information from both graph and image modalities through a bidirectional cross-modal fusion mechanism utilizing learnable bottleneck tokens.
 - **Structure:** Multiple fusion layers iteratively refine the representations.
 - **Bottleneck Tokens:** Learnable parameters `b_graph` and `b_image` (with `n_b` tokens) act as information conduits, augmenting the node (`h_graph`) and patch (`h_image`) embeddings.
 - **Cross-Attention:** Multi-Head Attention (MHA) is applied bidirectionally: `G_out = MHA(G_in, I_in, I_in)` and `I_out = MHA(I_in, G_in, G_in)`, where `G_in = [h_graph; b_graph]` and `I_in = [h_image; b_image]`.
 - **Refinement:** After attention, layer normalization and FFNs with residual connections are applied to both graph and image features.
 - **Final Fused Representation:** The ultimate fused representation `h_fused` is computed as `h_out_graph + alpha * Mean(h_out_image)`, with `alpha` set to 0.5.
- **Multi-start Decoder:**
 - This component constructs a tour by auto-regressively selecting one node from each cluster.
 - **Initial Step:** The depot node (node 0) is always selected first.
 - **First Selection (t=1):** The model selects `k` nearest neighbors to the depot, initiating multiple parallel rollouts for diverse exploration.
 - **Subsequent Steps (t>=2):**
 - The embedding of the previously selected node `h_pi_t-1` is retrieved.
 - Query vectors `q` are computed by combining `h_pi_t-1` with a global context `g` (derived from graph and averaged image features).
 - Compatibility scores `alpha_i` for each available node are calculated using a scaled dot-product attention mechanism, incorporating a learnable projection matrix `W_K` and a clipping parameter `C`.
 - Masking ensures that already visited nodes or clusters are not selected again.
 - A softmax function converts compatibility scores into policy probabilities `p(pi_t = i|pi_1:t-1)`.
 - **Inference:** Nodes are either sampled from this policy or greedily selected based on the evaluation mode.
- **Training Procedure:**
 - **Algorithm:** The model is trained using the REINFORCE algorithm.
 - **Variance Reduction:** A shared baseline, computed as the average reward across all `k` sampled rollouts for each instance, is used to reduce policy gradient variance.
 - **Optimization:** Model parameters `theta` are updated using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of `1 x 10^-4`, `1 x 10^-6` weight decay, and a cosine annealing scheduler. Gradient clipping is set to 1.0. The model is trained for 200 epochs, each processing 100,000 instances with a batch size of 128.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the MMFL framework involved extensive comparisons against state-of-the-art algorithms across various GTSP instances, problem scales, grouping configurations, and group size distributions. Physical robot tests were also conducted.

- **Comparison Analysis (Table 1):**
 - **Superior Performance Across Scales:** MMFL consistently demonstrated superior performance across all tested problem scales, from `n=20, m=4` to `n=200, m=40`.
 - **Optimality Gap:** For smaller instances (`n=20, m=4` and `n=50, m=10`), MMFL achieved a 0.00% optimality gap, matching MA and POMO. As problem sizes increased, MMFL maintained a 0.00% optimality gap, indicating it found the best solutions among the compared methods.
 - **Significant Improvement for Larger Instances:** For larger instances, MMFL's advantage became substantial. For example, at `n=200, m=40`, MMFL achieved tour lengths 5.51% shorter than LKH and 4.13% shorter than OR-Tools. At `n=100, m=20`, its performance was 21.33% better than LKH and 26.22% better than OR-Tools.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** MMFL maintained efficient inference times, comparable to POMO (approximately 0.11-0.14 seconds per test dataset), and significantly faster than traditional heuristic methods like MA and LKH, whose computational costs increased dramatically with problem size.
- **Generalization Analysis (Tables 2 and 3):**
 - **Diverse Grouping Configurations (Table 2, n=100):** MMFL exhibited strong generalization capabilities across different ways nodes were grouped. It achieved the best objective values in 3 out of 4 configurations (Random, Proximity-Based, and Hybrid Groups), demonstrating a 0.00% optimality gap in these cases. In Density-Based Groups, MMFL had a slight 1.38% gap but still performed competitively.
 - **Varying Group Sizes (Table 3, n=100):** MMFL consistently delivered superior solutions across instances with Uniform, Small, Large, and Mixed group sizes. It achieved a 0.00% optimality gap for all these configurations. Notably, for Large Groups, MMFL was 35.83% better than POMO and 45.00% better than LKH, and for Small Groups, it was 5.10% better than POMO and 7.40% better than ALNS. This indicates MMFL's robustness to diverse structural properties of GTSP instances.
- **Ablation Study (Figure 4, Table 4):**
 - The ablation study confirmed the critical contribution of both the image encoder and the multimodal fusion module.
 - **Without Image Encoder:** The model showed slower convergence in early epochs and consistently higher tour lengths (e.g., 19.56% gap for `n=100, m=20`).
 - **Without Multimodal Fusion:** This variant resulted in higher initial tour lengths and instability during training, leading to significant performance degradation (e.g., up to 35.83% gap for Large group distributions).
 - The complete MMFL model consistently maintained lower tour length values, demonstrating that both components are essential for optimal performance, contributing approximately 0.1 to tour length improvement.
- **Experimental Validation (Figures 5 and 6):**
 - The MMFL framework was successfully deployed on a physical mobile robot platform.
 - The robot successfully navigated a complex indoor environment, generating paths for multi-zone exploration tasks, thereby validating the practical effectiveness of the algorithm in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMFL framework presents an advancement in addressing the Generalized Traveling Salesman Problem, particularly in the context of robotic task planning. The integration of both graph-based and spatial representations through a multimodal learning approach offers several significant implications:

- **Improved Solution Quality for Complex GTSP:** By effectively leveraging complementary topological and geometric information, MMFL consistently generates higher-quality solutions (shorter tour lengths) compared to existing state-of-the-art algorithms, especially for larger and more complex GTSP instances with diverse spatial distributions and group configurations. This enhanced accuracy can translate directly to optimized robot performance, reducing travel time, energy consumption, and operational costs.
- **Real-time Applicability:** MMFL's ability to maintain competitive computational efficiency while delivering high-quality solutions makes it suitable for real-time robotic applications. This is critical for autonomous systems operating in dynamic environments where rapid decision-making is necessary, such as in warehouse logistics, environmental monitoring, or disaster response.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The framework demonstrates robust generalization across varying problem scales, grouping strategies, and cluster distributions. This adaptability reduces the need for extensive retraining or parameter tuning for different operational scenarios, making the solution more versatile and deployable across a broader range of robotic tasks and environments.
- **Bridging the Gap in Neural Combinatorial Optimization:** The work addresses a limitation of many current neural approaches to combinatorial optimization by explicitly incorporating spatial reasoning. This multimodal approach could inform future research in combining different data representations to solve other spatially-sensitive optimization problems.
- **Validation in Physical Systems:** The successful implementation and validation on a physical robot platform underscore the practical relevance and readiness of the MMFL framework for real-world deployment. This moves beyond purely simulated results, providing empirical evidence of its utility in tangible robotic applications.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as generalization to novel distributions, computational scalability for extremely large instances, and assumptions about static environments, the MMFL framework represents a substantial step toward more intelligent and efficient robotic task planning. Its potential impact extends to various industries reliant on mobile autonomous agents, promising more optimized and adaptable robotic operations.
