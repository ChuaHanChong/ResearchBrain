# First-Person Hand Action Benchmark with RGB-D Videos and 3D Hand Pose Annotations

> [!summary] Summary
> A first-person hand action benchmark: 1,175 RGB-D videos (105,459 frames) of 45 daily hand-object actions with 26 objects, annotated with 21-joint 3D hand poses from six magnetic sensors plus inverse kinematics, and 6D object poses for a subset. Eighteen RGB-D and pose-based recognition methods are benchmarked; pose features prove the strongest single cue, with the best method reaching 85.39% accuracy.

## Problem

- Egocentric action datasets label actions but lack 3D hand pose, while hand-pose and grasp datasets lack dynamic manipulation actions, so neither supports studying both jointly.
- Prior first-person work modeled hands only via silhouettes, skin features, or discrete grasp classes, low-level cues far weaker than viewpoint-invariant articulated pose used for full-body action recognition.
- Reliable hand pose annotation on real interaction video was unavailable, pushing researchers to synthetic renderings or rough manual labels, yielding unrealistic images and inconsistent joint annotations.

## Method

- ==Magnetic-sensor mo-cap== annotation: six 6-DoF sensors on fingertips and wrist drive inverse kinematics over a 21-joint hand model, giving near-ground-truth pose without corrupting the depth image.
- Dataset design spanning 45 actions, 26 objects, 34 grasp types, 3 scenarios, 6 subjects, plus 6D object pose and 3D mesh models for four objects covering ten actions.
- Benchmark protocol suite: 1:3, 1:1, 3:1 splits and leave-one-person-out cross-validation, an LSTM pose baseline, 18 evaluated methods, and cross-subject/cross-object pose-estimation splits.

## Results

- Pose-based methods lead: Gram Matrix **85.39%** and Lie Group **82.69%** beat depth methods (HON4D **70.61%**) and the RGB two-stream network (**75.30%**); LSTM baseline reaches **78.73%**.
- Modality breakdown with a joint heterogeneous-feature approach gives pose **74.60%** versus color **66.78%** and depth **60.17%**, with all channels combined at **78.78%**; adding 6D object pose lifts **87.45%** to **91.97%**.
- Training the pose estimator with objects cuts error from **31.03mm** to **14.34mm** and raises action accuracy **29.63%** to **72.06%**; cross-object error **19.84mm** far exceeds cross-subject **11.25mm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Articulated pose beats appearance for manipulation recognition, and object pose is complementary rather than redundant, so fusing both cues outperforms either modality alone.
> - Pose estimators generalize across people far better than across unseen objects, so occlusion patterns and object shape must be represented in training data.
> - Recognition tolerates noisy poses: thumb and index joints are least occluded and most informative, but methods with strong noise assumptions degrade sharply while recurrent models stay robust.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several computer vision and robotics domains, including egocentric vision, action recognition, 3D hand pose estimation, and human-object interaction. The broader landscape involves understanding human actions, particularly those performed with hands, in various contexts.

Previous work in first-person (egocentric) action recognition has recognized the importance of hands and manipulated objects as cues for understanding daily activities. However, many existing approaches in this area rely on low-level image features, hand silhouettes, or discrete grasp classifications. While full-body human action recognition has benefited from higher-level, viewpoint-invariant features like 3D body pose, the application of similar detailed 3D pose information to hand actions in egocentric views has been less explored. Hand actions present unique challenges, such as pronounced style and speed variations due to high finger mobility, subtle motions, and significant self-occlusion or object occlusion from the first-person perspective.

A major impediment to advancing hand action recognition using 3D pose has been the scarcity of high-quality, annotated datasets, particularly those involving real-world hand-object interactions and dynamic actions. Many existing hand pose estimation datasets are either synthetic, small, limited to static poses, or lack comprehensive action labels. Similarly, established egocentric action recognition datasets typically do not include detailed 3D hand pose annotations. Datasets that do involve hands or objects often model them using low-level features or empirical grasp taxonomies, which lack the fine-grained detail of 3D joint poses.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing a novel benchmark dataset that combines first-person RGB-D videos of dynamic hand actions with precise 3D hand pose annotations and, for a subset, 6D object pose annotations. By providing this resource, the authors aim to facilitate research that leverages detailed kinematic information to improve the understanding and recognition of complex hand-object interactions, similar to how body pose has advanced full-body action recognition. The research also aims to evaluate the current capabilities of 3D hand pose estimation algorithms when confronted with the occluded and dynamic nature of egocentric hand-object manipulations, and to assess the utility of estimated poses for action recognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the efficacy of using 3D hand poses for recognizing first-person dynamic hand actions that involve interaction with 3D objects. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed motivations and specific objectives:

1. **Address the Data Gap:** A significant motivation stems from the recognized lack of a comprehensive dataset that combines first-person dynamic hand action sequences with high-quality 3D hand pose annotations in real-world videos. Existing datasets often fall short by being synthetic, limited to static poses, lacking detailed hand pose labels, or focusing on third-person views or full-body actions. The authors aim to fill this gap by creating such a benchmark.

2. **Enable Fine-Grained Action Understanding:** The motivation is to move beyond recognition methods based on low-level image features, hand silhouettes, or discrete grasp classifications, which have been prevalent in previous first-person hand action recognition. By providing fine-grained 3D hand pose features, the research seeks to enable a deeper understanding of how hands are used during daily life dynamic actions, akin to the benefits seen from body pose in full-body action recognition.

3. **Support High-Precision Applications:** Understanding hand actions with high precision is critical for a range of practical applications. These include hand rehabilitation, where detailed kinematic feedback is essential; virtual and augmented reality, which demand accurate hand interaction; teleoperation, requiring precise remote control; and robot imitation learning, where robots learn complex manipulation skills by observing human demonstrations. The dataset aims to provide the necessary data for developing advanced systems in these areas.

4. **Evaluate Current Action Recognition Methods:** The research aims to conduct an extensive experimental evaluation of existing RGB-D and pose-based action recognition algorithms using the newly collected dataset. This includes assessing the impact of different data modalities (appearance features, depth, 3D hand poses, and their combinations) and evaluating various training and testing protocols to establish baselines and identify effective approaches for this specific problem.

5. **Assess 3D Hand Pose Estimation in Challenging Settings:** A key motivation is to evaluate the current state of 3D hand pose estimation techniques when hands are severely occluded by objects in egocentric views. This scenario presents unique challenges for pose estimators. The research seeks to quantify the performance of a state-of-the-art hand pose estimator under these conditions and, crucially, to understand how the accuracy of estimated hand poses influences the performance of action recognition. This assessment is intended to highlight areas for improvement in hand pose estimation research.

6. **Provide Resources for Related Fields:** Beyond action recognition and hand pose estimation, the dataset, with its 6D object pose annotations and 3D object models for a subset of interactions, aims to encourage research in related areas such as 6D object pose estimation and the emerging field of joint hand-object tracking.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome data limitations and research gaps to foster the development of more robust and precise methods for understanding complex first-person hand-object interactions, with direct implications for both fundamental research and real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers around the collection of a novel RGB-D video dataset with extensive 3D hand pose and, for a subset, 6D object pose annotations, followed by a comprehensive evaluation of existing action recognition and hand pose estimation techniques on this dataset.

#### 3.1. Dataset Collection and Annotation (First-Person Hand Action Benchmark)

- **Visual Data Acquisition:** An Intel RealSense SR300 RGB-D camera was mounted on the subject's shoulder to capture first-person video sequences. The camera recorded at 30 frames per second, with RGB images at 1920x1080 resolution and depth images at 640x480 resolution.
- **Action Categories and Objects:** The dataset comprises 1,175 action video sequences, encompassing 45 distinct daily hand action categories. These actions involve the manipulation of 26 different objects across three scenarios: kitchen (25 actions), office (12 actions), and social (8 actions). Each hand-object manipulation was considered a distinct action category.
- **Subjects:** Six right-handed actors participated in the data collection. They were given safety instructions but no specific guidance on style or speed, aiming to capture realistic inter-subject and intra-subject variability in action performance.
- **Hand Pose Annotation:** To achieve high-quality 3D hand pose annotations, a custom motion capture (mo-cap) system was utilized. Six magnetic sensors (2mm wide) were attached to the user's hand: five on the fingertips (thumb, index, middle, ring, pinky) and one on the wrist. Each sensor provided 6 degrees of freedom (position and orientation). The full 3D pose of a 21-joint hand model was then inferred using inverse kinematics. These sensors minimally affected the depth images but were visible in the color stream. The dataset contains over 100,000 RGB-D frames with these accurate hand pose annotations.
- **Object Pose Annotation:** For a subset of 4 objects (milk bottle, salt, juice carton, liquid soap) involved in 10 different action categories, 6-dimensional object pose (3D location and orientation) ground truth was recorded. This was achieved by attaching an additional magnetic sensor to the object, typically at a point close to its center of mass. Corresponding 3D mesh models for these objects are also provided.
- **Dataset Characteristics:** The actions were designed to cover a wide range of hand configurations (34 identified grasp types) and temporal dynamics, from short, atomic movements (e.g., "put sugar") to longer, more complex sequences (e.g., "open letter"). The first-person perspective naturally leads to significant hand self-occlusion and object occlusion, with an average of only 10 out of 21 hand joints visible per frame across actions. The distribution of hand viewpoints (angle between camera direction and palm) shows a high prevalence of self-occluding perspectives.

#### 3.2. Action Recognition Evaluation

- **Evaluated Methods:** The study evaluated 18 baseline and state-of-the-art approaches for action recognition, covering RGB-D and pose-based methodologies. These included:
 - **RGB-based:** Two-stream network.
 - **Depth-based:** HOG2, HON4D, Novel View.
 - **Pose-based:** 1-layer and 2-layer LSTMs (custom baseline inspired by), Moving Pose, Lie Group, HBRNN, Gram Matrix, TF.
 - **Hybrid (multi-modal fusion):** JOULE for combining color, depth, and pose.
- **Pose Normalization:** For pose-based methods, hand poses were normalized to account for anthropomorphic differences and viewpoint variations. This involved standardizing the distance between joint pairs and setting the wrist as the coordinate origin.
- **Training and Testing Protocols:** Two main protocols were employed:
 - **Sequence-level splits:** Data was partitioned into training and testing sets at the sequence level with ratios of 1:3, 1:1, and 3:1. The 1:1 ratio (600 training, 575 testing sequences) was primarily used for method comparisons.
 - **Cross-person cross-validation:** A 6-fold "leave-one-person-out" protocol was used, where 5 subjects' data were used for training and one for testing in each fold, to assess generalization across subjects.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Action recognition accuracy (percentage of correct video classification) was the primary metric. Further analysis included per-category accuracy, top-k accuracy (for action and manipulated object), and the contribution of individual fingers to recognition performance.
- **Object Pose Utilization:** For the subset of actions with 6D object pose annotations, an experiment was conducted using the LSTM baseline to evaluate the contribution of hand pose, object pose, and their combination to action recognition.

#### 3.3. Hand Pose Estimation Evaluation

- **Evaluated Estimator:** A Convolutional Neural Network (ConvNet) approach for hand pose estimation, similar to, was selected for evaluation due to its interpretability and reported performance in cross-benchmark settings. This is a discriminative, frame-by-frame method.
- **Training Scenarios:** To assess the impact of training data on hand pose estimation in egocentric, occluded settings, the ConvNet was trained under several conditions:
 - Using object-free egocentric samples from a large dataset.
 - Using the training set of the proposed hand action dataset (which includes objects).
 - Cross-subject protocol: Half of the users for training and half for testing, with all objects seen in both splits.
 - Cross-object protocol: Half of the objects for training and half for testing, with all subjects seen in both splits.
- **Impact on Action Recognition:** The estimated hand poses from these different scenarios were then used as input to the action recognition models (specifically the LSTM baseline, Lie Group, and Gram Matrix) to quantify the degradation in action recognition performance due to estimation errors.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Mean 3D distance error (in mm) between estimated and ground truth (magnetic sensor) 21-joint poses was used. Further analysis included error distribution per frame, per action class, and per finger.

This multi-faceted methodology ensures a thorough examination of first-person hand action recognition, the role of 3D hand pose, and the challenges of hand pose estimation in realistic, interactive scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the proposed dataset, the performance of various action recognition methods, and the current state of hand pose estimation in egocentric, object-interacting contexts.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics and Annotation Quality

- The collected dataset successfully provides over 100,000 RGB-D frames of 45 dynamic hand action categories involving 26 objects, annotated with high-quality 3D hand poses obtained via magnetic sensors and inverse kinematics.
- For a subset of actions, 6D object pose ground truth and 3D object models are also provided.
- The dataset exhibits high inter-subject and intra-subject variability in action style, speed, scale, and viewpoint, reflecting realistic daily interactions.
- A significant characteristic of the egocentric view is the high degree of hand occlusion by objects or self-occlusion, with an average of only 10 out of 21 hand joints being visible per action class.

#### 4.2. Action Recognition Performance (Using Ground Truth Hand Pose)

- **Superiority of Pose-based Methods:** Methods leveraging 3D hand pose features consistently outperformed those relying solely on RGB or depth modalities.
 - Gram Matrix achieved the highest accuracy at 85.39%, closely followed by Lie Group at 82.69%. These results align with observations in full-body action recognition, indicating the effectiveness of pose dynamics.
 - The 2-layer LSTM baseline achieved 80.14% accuracy, demonstrating robustness.
- **Modalities Comparison:**
 - RGB-only methods (e.g., Two-stream-all at 75.30%) showed competitive performance, particularly in scenarios with larger objects or less hand motion, benefiting from pre-training on image recognition datasets.
 - Depth-only methods (e.g., HON4D at 70.61%) generally performed slightly worse, suggesting challenges in extracting discriminative features from noisy, textureless depth images for subtle hand actions.
 - JOULE analysis showed that hand pose features (74.60%) are the most discriminative among individual modalities, but combining all three (RGB, depth, pose) further improved accuracy to 78.78%, indicating complementary information.
- **Training Protocols:** The "leave-one-person-out" (cross-person) protocol resulted in significantly lower accuracy (62.06% for LSTM) compared to sequence-level splits (e.g., 78.73% for 1:1 split), highlighting substantial inter-subject variability in hand action styles.
- **Finger Contribution:** Analysis of finger motion indicated that the index finger is the most informative for action recognition. Combining thumb and index finger poses significantly boosted accuracy, attributed to their role in most grasp types. Fingertips collectively also provided a high source of information due to their high articulation.
- **Object Pose Contribution:** On the subset of actions with 6D object pose annotations, combining object pose with hand pose achieved the highest action recognition accuracy (91.97%), outperforming hand-pose-only (87.45%) and object-pose-only (74.45%), demonstrating the complementary value of object kinematic information.

#### 4.3. Hand Pose Estimation Performance and Impact on Action Recognition

- **Importance of Training Data with Objects:** Training the hand pose estimator with hand-object interaction images was critical. A model trained on object-free egocentric data showed a high average pose estimation error of 31.03mm, leading to poor action recognition performance (29.63%). In contrast, training with object-present images from the dataset reduced the error to 14.34mm and yielded significantly better action recognition (72.06%).
- **Generalization Challenges:** The pose estimator generalized well to unseen subjects (11.25mm error) but struggled considerably with unseen objects (19.84mm error). This finding suggests that recognizing the shape and interaction with specific objects is crucial for accurate hand pose estimation, especially under occlusion.
- **Robustness of Action Recognition to Noisy Poses:** Despite pose estimation errors, the action recognition models (particularly the LSTM baseline) demonstrated robustness. The accuracy drop when using estimated poses (72.06%) compared to ground truth poses (78.73%) was 6.67%, which the authors deemed acceptable. Other methods, like Gram Matrix, showed a more significant performance degradation with noisy poses.
- **Occlusion Impact:** Actions with higher degrees of hand occlusion correlated with increased pose estimation error, subsequently affecting action recognition accuracy. However, the LSTM could still achieve reasonable recognition rates by inferring actions from temporal patterns and correctly estimated visible joints. The thumb and index fingers, typically less occluded, exhibited lower estimation errors and were identified as highly informative for action recognition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented by Garcia-Hernando et al. offers a significant contribution to multiple research domains, with potential impacts across several application areas.

#### 5.1. Novel Benchmark for Egocentric Hand-Object Interactions

The primary significance lies in the introduction of the first benchmark dataset that combines first-person dynamic hand actions with precise 3D hand pose annotations and, for a subset, 6D object pose annotations, all captured in real-world RGB-D videos. This addresses a critical void in publicly available datasets. Previous benchmarks either focused on third-person views, lacked detailed 3D pose information, were restricted to static poses, or used synthetic data. This new dataset provides a realistic and comprehensive resource for studying complex hand manipulations, paving the way for more data-driven research.

#### 5.2. Advancement in Action Recognition

The study demonstrates a clear benefit of leveraging fine-grained 3D hand pose features for recognizing first-person dynamic hand actions. By showing that pose-based methods outperform those relying on lower-level RGB or depth cues, the research shifts the focus towards incorporating kinematic understanding into action recognition frameworks. This could lead to more robust and precise action recognition systems capable of distinguishing subtle differences in hand movements, which is crucial for distinguishing between similar actions or understanding intention.

#### 5.3. Insights for 3D Hand Pose Estimation

The evaluation of a state-of-the-art hand pose estimator on this challenging dataset highlights key difficulties in estimating hand poses during egocentric, object-occluded interactions. The finding that training with hand-object images is crucial for performance, and that generalization to unseen objects is particularly challenging, underscores the need for future research to focus on robust pose estimation in highly occluded and interactive scenarios. This can drive the development of novel algorithms that explicitly model occlusions and hand-object contact.

#### 5.4. Facilitation of Joint Hand-Object Understanding

By providing both 3D hand and 6D object pose annotations (for a subset), the dataset directly supports the emerging field of joint hand-object pose estimation and tracking. The finding that object pose provides complementary information to hand pose for action recognition further motivates the development of integrated models that understand the interplay between the hand and the manipulated object. This is a critical step towards richer scene understanding and more intelligent robotic manipulation.

#### 5.5. Potential for Real-World Applications

The advancements enabled by this benchmark have direct implications for several high-precision applications:
- **Robotics:** Enhanced robot imitation learning, where robots can learn manipulation skills by observing human demonstrations with greater fidelity. Teleoperation systems could benefit from more accurate hand kinematic feedback.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** More natural and precise hand interaction within virtual environments, improving user experience and immersion.
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** Development of more intuitive gesture-based interfaces.
- **Medical and Rehabilitation:** Detailed analysis of hand movements for rehabilitation assessment and training, particularly for stroke patients or individuals with motor impairments.

In conclusion, this work not only provides a valuable new dataset but also offers significant empirical evidence and insights that can guide future research directions in computer vision, robotics, and human-computer interaction, ultimately contributing to a more nuanced understanding and utilization of human hand actions.
