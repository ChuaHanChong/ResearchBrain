# Cache-to-Cache: Direct Semantic Communication Between Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Cache-to-Cache (C2C) lets two LLMs communicate through KV-Cache instead of text. A neural cache fuser projects a Sharer model's per-layer KV-Cache into a Receiver's space, merges it residually, and a learnable gate picks which Receiver layers accept the injection, with both LLMs frozen. C2C beats single models by 6.4-14.2% average accuracy and text communication by 3.1-5.4%, with 2.5x lower latency.

## Problem

- Multi-LLM systems exchange information only as text, compressing high-dimensional internal representations into linear strings, so signals the receiver lacks vocabulary for become irrecoverable.
- Natural language stays ambiguous and every exchange needs token-by-token decoding of intermediate explanations, so rigid agent protocols still pay heavy latency for flexible open-domain collaboration.
- Existing KV-cache sharing targets compute reuse within one model or identically structured models, never semantic transfer across different model families, sizes and specializations.

## Method

- ==Cache fuser== concatenates Receiver and Sharer per-layer ==KV-Cache==, passes them through a projection plus feature-fusion layer, then adds the result back to the Receiver cache residually.
- A ==dynamic weighting module== reweights projected heads, and a per-layer ==learnable gate== with ==Gumbel-sigmoid temperature annealing== stays differentiable in training but binary at inference, selecting beneficial layers.
- ==Model alignment== re-encodes Receiver tokens with the Sharer tokenizer using ==maximal string coverage==, aligns layers terminally from last to first; training freezes both LLMs under next-token loss.

## Results

- With a Qwen3-0.6B Receiver, C2C raises accuracy **9.64-11.88%** over Receiver-only and **3.06-5.36%** over text-to-text across MMLU-Redux, OpenBookQA, ARC-C and C-Eval.
- Latency falls **1.51-14.41x** versus text-to-text, since **90ms** cache fusion replaces **1312ms** of Sharer decoding; LongBench gains persist beyond 8k tokens (**30.72** vs **25.64**).
- Ablations show residual fusion beats pure cache replacement by **24.18%** and gating adds **3.07%**; heterogeneous Sharers beat both identical-model fusion and plain Receiver fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Richer cache semantics, not longer context, drive accuracy: an enriched same-length cache already improves answers, making representation quality a lever separate from context budget.
> - Layer selectivity matters: injecting external representations into badly chosen layers degrades accuracy, so gated per-layer fusion beats uniform merging of cross-model representations.
> - Cache-level transfer inherits the Sharer's mistakes and costs O(N) training per pair, so gains hinge on Sharer quality and many-model scaling stays unsolved.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement and specialization of Large Language Models (LLMs) have led to the development of multi-LLM systems. These systems aim to combine the distinct strengths of various models, whether for general-purpose applications or specialized tasks such as coding, mathematics, or visual understanding.

Existing multi-LLM systems generally fall into two categories:
1. **Collaborative Multi-LLM Systems:** These systems assign distinct roles to LLMs, which then exchange information through text messages to collectively solve complex problems. Examples include Chain-of-Agents and MetaGPT, which rely on natural language interfaces or, more recently, formalized text protocols like Anthropic's Model Context Protocol (MCP) or Google's Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol. This approach fundamentally operates at the text level, requiring one model to generate text token-by-token and another to ingest it.
2. **Routing-based Multi-LLM Inference Systems:** These systems coordinate models of varying parameter sizes or reasoning depths to optimize efficiency. They typically involve dynamically selecting or switching between models based on query complexity or context, with downstream models inheriting context from preceding ones. However, these systems often lack direct semantic understanding sharing between models, primarily focusing on efficiency through strategic model switching rather than deep collaboration.

Parallel to these, research on **KV-Cache sharing and reuse** has primarily focused on computational efficiency within single LLMs or models fine-tuned from the same base. This involves reusing a portion of KV-Cache (e.g., common prefix) for the same model across multiple queries or for shallow layers to accelerate deeper layers. DroidSpeak extended this to models fine-tuned from the same base.

This work identifies a critical limitation in current multi-LLM communication paradigms: the reliance on text-to-text (T2T) interfaces. This approach is characterized by an information bottleneck, inherent ambiguities in natural language, and high latency due to token-by-token decoding. Existing KV-Cache reuse methods, while efficient, do not address the need for semantic transfer between *different* and *heterogeneous* LLMs.

The presented research introduces a novel paradigm, Cache-to-Cache (C2C) communication, which aims to bridge this gap. By proposing KV-Cache as a direct medium for semantic transfer between diverse LLMs, this work moves beyond the limitations of text-based communication and expands the utility of KV-Cache from mere computational efficiency to deep semantic collaboration. It addresses the challenge of enabling heterogeneous LLMs (different families, sizes, specializations) to share rich, internal representations, thereby enhancing collective performance and efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing communication mechanisms in multi-LLM systems, particularly the pervasive use of text-to-text (T2T) interfaces. The paper highlights several inherent constraints of T2T communication:

1. **Information Bottleneck and Semantic Loss:** Text is a low-bandwidth medium compared to the high-dimensional internal representations of LLMs. During T2T communication, the rich, deep semantic information encoded within an LLM's internal states must be compressed into a linear sequence of tokens. This compression inevitably leads to a loss of valuable information that may be irrecoverable by the receiving LLM, especially when models possess different knowledge bases or roles (e.g., misinterpreting technical terms like `<p>` in code).
2. **Ambiguity of Natural Language:** Natural language is inherently ambiguous, prone to underspecified references, idioms, and vague expressions. Even with recent efforts to standardize text messages through agent protocols, these rigid templates often prove insufficient for flexible, open-domain collaboration required in complex tasks. Misinterpretations by a receiving LLM can lead to incorrect or suboptimal responses.
3. **Incurred Latency:** T2T communication is a sequential process. Every exchange necessitates token-by-token decoding for the sender LLM to generate contextual explanations, followed by sequential encoding for the receiver LLM to process them. This iterative generation and ingestion process introduces significant latency, hindering the efficiency of collaborative LLM systems.

Motivated by these limitations, the central objective of this work is to address the question: "Can LLMs communicate beyond text?" The authors propose exploring KV-Cache as an alternative medium for LLM communication, positing that it offers a richer representation and enables parallel, rather than sequential, information transfer.

To validate this motivation and the feasibility of KV-Cache based communication, two oracle experiments were conducted:

1. **Cache Enrichment Oracle:** This experiment investigated whether the semantic quality of a model's KV-Cache could be improved without increasing its overall size. By comparing a "Direct" setup (decoding with a KV-Cache derived only from the question) with an "Oracle" setup (prefilling with exemplars and the question, but then discarding the exemplar's KV-Cache before decoding, leaving only the semantically enriched question KV-Cache), the researchers demonstrated that enriching the KV-Cache's semantic content alone—independent of context length—improves response quality. This supported the hypothesis that KV-Cache holds valuable semantic information beyond mere token sequences.
2. **Cache Transformation Oracle:** This experiment aimed to verify if the KV-Cache from one LLM could be effectively utilized by another, potentially heterogeneous, LLM. By training an MLP to project the KV-Cache from a source LLM (Qwen3-4B) into the representation space of a target LLM (Qwen3-0.6B), the results indicated that KV-Caches are convertible between different models. T-SNE visualizations showed that the transformed KV-Cache from the source model occupied a subspace within the target model's representation. Crucially, the observation that different models encode distinct semantic understandings and that their correct-answer sets have limited overlap further motivated the potential to harness complementary strengths through direct semantic transfer.

These findings collectively provided the empirical foundation and strong motivation for proposing Cache-to-Cache (C2C) communication as a new paradigm for richer and faster multi-LLM interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Cache-to-Cache (C2C)** paradigm aims to enable direct semantic communication between LLMs by extracting contextual understanding or knowledge from a source model (Sharer) and fusing it into a target model (Receiver) via their KV-Caches.

The C2C design involves several key components and strategies:

**C2C Paradigm Overview:**
During the prefill stage, for each transformer layer `n`, a dedicated fuser `F_n` takes the `n`-th layer cache of the Receiver model `C_n(X)` and the corresponding `G(n)`-th layer cache of the Sharer model `C_S_G(n)(X)`. It then generates a fused cache `C_F_n` using a residual connection: `C_F_n = C_n(X) + F_n(C_n(X), C_S_G(n)(X))`. This process is applied across all relevant layers. During decoding, the Receiver predicts the next token `y_{i+1}` conditioned on this fused input cache `C_F(X)` concatenated with the generated prefix `C(Y_[0:i])`.

**Fuser Structure:**
The fuser `F_n` is designed based on a residual integration principle to enhance the Receiver’s KV-Cache without destructive overwriting. It comprises three modules:
1. **Projection Module:** Concatenates the Receiver’s KV-Cache with the Sharer’s KV-Cache. This concatenated feature is then processed through a projection layer, followed by a feature fusion layer, to map it into a suitable representation space.
2. **Dynamic Weighting Module:** Applies an input-aware head modulation layer. This mechanism dynamically reweights the projected information, allowing the fuser to adaptively control the influence of the Sharer's input based on the specific context.
3. **Learnable Gate:** A trainable, per-layer gate value determines whether to inject the Sharer's context into that specific layer of the Receiver. The gate employs a Gumbel-sigmoid function with temperature annealing, enabling smooth differentiation during training and a binary (on/off) decision at inference time. This mechanism is informed by the oracle experiment finding that not all layers benefit from cache enrichment.

**Model Alignment:**
Fusing KV-Caches between different LLMs, potentially from different families or of varying sizes, requires alignment at two levels:
1. **Token Alignment:** Given that different tokenizers might produce varied token sequences for the same input, the process aligns them by decoding each Receiver token into its string form and re-encoding it using the Sharer's tokenizer.
 - **Template Sections:** Structural tokens (e.g., role delimiters) are aligned by simple length padding.
 - **Message Sections:** For actual textual content, if re-encoding a target token yields multiple source tokens (one-to-many), a "Maximal-coverage selection" strategy is used, choosing the source token with the maximal string coverage to preserve information.
2. **Layer Alignment:** A **terminal alignment strategy** is adopted. This strategy aligns the final layers of both models first, then the penultimate layers, and so on, working backward to the first layer of the shallower model. This prioritizes aligning the deeper layers, which are typically responsible for higher-level semantic representations.

**Training Scheme:**
During the training of the C2C module, both the Sharer and Receiver LLMs are kept frozen. Only the C2C fuser modules (projection, dynamic weighting, and gates) are trained. The training objective is standard next-token prediction loss, similar to supervised fine-tuning (SFT), applied to the Receiver’s response predictions. The key distinction is that the Receiver predicts responses conditioned on the *fused* KV-Cache rather than its own original cache.
The training procedure consists of three stages for each step:
1. **Forward:** Both Sharer and Receiver models encode the input context independently to generate their respective KV-Caches.
2. **Fusion:** The C2C module processes these KV-Caches, fusing them according to its architecture, and then replaces the Receiver's original KV-Cache with the newly fused version.
3. **Supervision:** The Receiver then uses this fused cache to prefill the response. Gradients are backpropagated through the C2C module to minimize the next-token prediction loss, thereby optimizing the fuser's parameters.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology involves evaluating C2C across various model families (Qwen2.5, Qwen3, Llama3.2, Gemma3), sizes (0.6B to 14B), specializations (general, code, math), and training stages (pretrained, instruction fine-tuned). Baselines include individual model performance, Text-to-Text (T2T) communication (Sharer generates an analytical text for Receiver), and Query-level routing (selecting the appropriate LLM per query). Evaluation is performed on benchmarks covering reasoning (OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge), knowledge (MMLU-Redux, C-Eval), and long-context tasks (LongBenchV1), using average accuracy and inference time as metrics. Training of C2C fusers utilizes general finetuning datasets like OpenHermes2.5 or task-specific datasets like MMLU's auxiliary train split to ensure generalizability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents comprehensive experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of the Cache-to-Cache (C2C) communication paradigm.

**Oracle Experiment Results:**
- **Cache Enrichment Oracle:** The "Oracle" setup, where KV-Cache was semantically enriched without increasing sequence length, improved response quality. As shown in Table 1, the Oracle method achieved 62.34% accuracy compared to 58.42% for the "Direct" baseline. Further layer-wise analysis (Table 10, Figure 4) indicated that while enriching certain layers improved accuracy, enriching others could degrade performance, providing a rationale for the learnable gating mechanism.
- **Cache Transformation Oracle:** T-SNE visualizations (Figure 3) demonstrated that KV-Caches from different LLMs (Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-0.6B) are convertible, with the transformed source cache residing within the target model’s representation space. The effective rank analysis (Table 2, Figure 12) revealed that C2C fusion increased the average effective rank of both Key and Value caches in the Receiver, signifying a semantic enrichment of its internal representations.

**Main Performance and Efficiency:**
- **Performance (Table 4):** C2C consistently improved the Receiver model's performance across all tested benchmarks (MMLU-Redux, OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, C-Eval) and Sharer configurations. It achieved an average of 6.4-14.2% higher accuracy than individual models. Crucially, C2C outperformed the Text-to-Text (T2T) communication paradigm by approximately 3.1-5.4% on average. C2C also demonstrated robustness by allowing a weaker instruction-tuned Receiver to leverage knowledge from a stronger base model (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Base), even when the base model failed to follow instructions in T2T settings.
- **Efficiency (Table 4, Table 3):** C2C delivered significant speedups, averaging 2.5× faster than T2T. Specific speedups ranged from 1.51× to 14.41×, with a 3.46× speedup noted with Qwen2.5-0.5B as Sharer. This efficiency gain is attributed to replacing T2T’s sequential, token-by-token decoding (e.g., 1312ms for 80 tokens) with a parallel cache fusion process (e.g., 90ms).

**Scaling Behavior:**
- **Sequence Length (Table 5):** On long-context tasks from LongBenchV1, C2C consistently outperformed T2T across all sequence-length intervals (0-4k, 4-8k, 8k+), highlighting its advantages for longer inputs.
- **Model Sizes (Figure 6):** As Sharer model sizes increased, the accuracy improvements of C2C over Receiver-only baselines generally increased faster than those of T2T. This indicates that C2C is more effective at transmitting richer knowledge from larger Sharers. The gains were less pronounced for larger Receivers, likely due to their stronger baselines and increased knowledge overlap with Sharers.
- **Different Model Combinations (Table 7):** C2C demonstrated generalizability across diverse Sharer-Receiver pairs, including heterogeneous model families (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B with Gemma3-1B) and specialized models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B). It outperformed T2T on all five tested combinations, achieving an average accuracy increase of 8.59% on MMLU-Redux. Swapping Sharer and Receiver roles also showed C2C providing robust performance increases.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Sources of Improvement (Table 6):** An ablation study confirmed that C2C's performance gains primarily originate from the complementary contextual understanding provided by a *heterogeneous* Sharer. C2C with a different Sharer significantly outperformed both "Single" (Receiver fine-tuned alone) and "Identical" (C2C with the same model as Sharer and Receiver), indicating it's not merely due to added trainable capacity or self-communication.
- **Fuser Architecture (Table 8):** The "fusing" component (combining both caches residually) was critical, yielding a 24.18% accuracy increase over pure projection. The learnable gate further contributed, adding 3.07% to the average accuracy.

**Behavior Analysis:**
- **Accuracy Breakdown (Figure 7, 8, 9, 10):** Venn diagrams of correctly answered questions showed that C2C not only leveraged the Sharer's capabilities but also solved additional questions by integrating understanding from both models. When a stronger Sharer was used, C2C integrated a larger proportion of its understanding. Gains varied across MMLU-Redux subcategories, with C2C outperforming T2T in most.
- **Progressive Behavior (Figure 11):** Gradually increasing the proportion of C2C-fused KV-Cache generally improved performance, particularly when exceeding 50% of the context, with later segments having a more substantial impact.
- **Gate Behavior:** Analysis of learnable gates (Appendix A.4.2) revealed adaptive strategies. General-purpose training led to nearly fully open gates with dynamic weights modulating information flow. Task-specific training resulted in sparser gate activation, with stronger reliance on the selected useful layers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of multi-LLM systems by demonstrating that large language models can communicate effectively beyond traditional text-based interfaces. The introduction of **Cache-to-Cache (C2C)** direct semantic communication through KV-Caches represents a practical alternative to token-based methods, offering several implications:

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Overcoming Text-to-Text Limitations:** C2C directly addresses the inherent limitations of text communication—namely, the information bottleneck, ambiguity, and high latency. By enabling the transfer of rich, high-dimensional internal representations, C2C facilitates a more comprehensive and precise exchange of semantic understanding between LLMs.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The consistent improvements in task accuracy (3.1-5.4% over T2T) and substantial speedups (average 2.5×) demonstrate C2C's ability to achieve both better quality and faster responses in collaborative LLM setups. This dual benefit is critical for developing more capable and practical multi-LLM applications.
- **Enabling Heterogeneous Collaboration:** C2C's ability to fuse KV-Caches across different model families, sizes, and specializations is a notable achievement. It allows systems to leverage the complementary strengths of diverse LLMs, potentially combining a specialized model's in-depth knowledge with a general-purpose model's reasoning capabilities, even when models have varying instruction-following abilities.
- **Foundation for Deeper LLM Understanding:** The oracle experiments provide empirical evidence for the semantic richness and convertibility of KV-Caches, contributing to a deeper understanding of how LLMs encode and process contextual information internally. The effective rank analysis further supports that C2C enriches the semantic space of the Receiver model.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
The C2C paradigm opens several promising avenues for future research and development:
- **Multi-Agent Systems:** C2C could serve as a fundamental communication primitive for more sophisticated multi-agent systems, particularly in complex, multi-round reasoning tasks, coding, and tool-use scenarios, where real-time, nuanced semantic exchange is crucial. Early explorations into mathematical problem-solving demonstrate its potential.
- **Cross-Modal Collaboration:** Extending C2C to fuse caches among vision-language models (VLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) models could enable richer multi-modal collaboration, allowing for direct semantic sharing of visual or action-related context.
- **Inference Acceleration Techniques:** C2C's principles could be integrated into inference optimization techniques like speculative decoding or token-level routing across heterogeneous models, leading to further reductions in latency and computational costs.
- **Privacy-Aware Collaboration:** By transmitting KV-Cache segments rather than explicit text, C2C might offer a mechanism for privacy-aware collaboration, limiting direct exposure of sensitive content during inter-model communication.

**Limitations:**
The study also acknowledges certain limitations:
- **Sharer Quality Dependency:** Similar to T2T communication, C2C performance can degrade when a significantly weaker Sharer provides noisy or inaccurate information to a stronger Receiver, highlighting the importance of Sharer semantic quality.
- **Scalability of Multi-LLM Communication:** While pairwise KV-Cache communication is shown to be feasible and advantageous, scaling up to an arbitrary number of communicating LLMs (where training cost might grow with O(N^2) fusers) remains an open challenge. Preliminary efforts toward O(N) scaling through a shared latent space are discussed as future work.

In conclusion, this work establishes C2C as a robust and efficient new paradigm for direct semantic communication between LLMs, offering a practical pathway toward building more collaborative, intelligent, and resource-efficient multi-LLM systems.
