# Characteristics, Management, and Utilization of Muscles in Musculoskeletal Humanoids: Empirical Study on Kengoro and Musashi

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Tokyo empirically categorize muscle characteristics and propose an integrated management system for full-body musculoskeletal humanoids, Kengoro and Musashi. This system combines low-level reflex controls with high-level body schema learning to manage properties like redundancy and nonlinear elasticity, enabling adaptive grasping, robust muscle rupture handling, dynamic manipulation, and autonomous navigation in complex tasks.

## Problem

- A lack of a unified and comprehensive discussion on how to effectively manage and utilize the diverse properties of musculoskeletal structures in real full-body robotic systems.
- Musculoskeletal humanoids possess complex characteristics (e.g., redundancy, nonlinear elasticity, variable moment arms) that lead to modeling difficulty, high internal forces, and computational costs.
- Most prior studies addressing musculoskeletal characteristics rely on simulations or simplified 2D models, lacking empirical validation on actual full-body musculoskeletal robots.

## Method

- The study classified muscle characteristics into five fundamental properties: Redundancy, Independency, Anisotropy, ==Variable Moment Arm==, and Nonlinear Elasticity, and systematically organized their resulting advantages and disadvantages.
- An integrated hierarchical software system was developed, comprising low-level reflex controls (e.g., Stretch Reflex, Muscle Thermal Control) for adaptability and high-level body schema learning (Static and Dynamic Body Schemas) for internal modeling.
- Methods for muscle grouping (manual and automatic) and body schema adaptation (muscle addition, rupture adaptation) were introduced to reduce computational cost and enhance system resilience to physical changes.

## Results

- Kengoro achieved adaptive object grasping by refining its body representation through online learning of the static body schema, overcoming initial failures.
- Musashi demonstrated robust adaptation to muscle rupture, detecting the failure and relearning its altered body schema to continue object manipulation tasks.
- Musashi-W successfully executed dynamic, flexible cloth manipulation, integrating low-level reflexes and body schemas, with adaptive stiffness control improving maximum joint velocity by **12%** for wider cloth spreading.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Musculoskeletal structures inherently present both challenges (e.g., modeling difficulty, high internal forces) and advantages (e.g., variable stiffness control, muscle rupture handling) that necessitate a comprehensive, integrated control and learning framework.
> - Online learning of static and dynamic body schemas is crucial for real-time state estimation, adaptive control, anomaly detection, and robust adaptation to changes within complex biomimetic robots.
> - A combination of high-frequency, low-level reflex controls and slower, high-level learning-based body schemas is effective for managing the diverse and complex properties of musculoskeletal humanoids for robust and versatile operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of musculoskeletal humanoids has been an active area of research, driven by objectives such as gaining a constructive understanding of the human body, validating the advantages of biomimetic structures, and transferring human anatomical principles to robotic technologies. These robots typically feature complex musculoskeletal structures comprising bones, joints, ligaments, and numerous muscles, which exhibit characteristics distinct from conventional axis-driven robots.

Prior research has explored various aspects of musculoskeletal systems, particularly focusing on muscle redundancy. Studies have investigated variable stiffness control leveraging redundancy and nonlinear elasticity, the relationship between redundancy and muscle synergies, the impact of redundancy on contact force prediction, and methods for addressing muscle rupture. Some research has also treated redundancy as a drawback, exploring control methods to mitigate its complexities. Additionally, the contribution of biarticular muscles to balance and energy efficiency, and learning the intricate relationships between muscles and joints, have been areas of focus.

However, much of this existing research has been conducted through simulations or on simplified 2D models. A notable gap in the literature is a comprehensive and unified discussion specifically pertaining to actual full-body musculoskeletal robots. There has been a lack of empirical analysis on how the diverse properties inherent in these complex structures should be managed and effectively utilized in real-world robotic systems. Discussions often remain fragmented, addressing isolated aspects rather than an integrated approach.

This study distinguishes itself by focusing on musculoskeletal humanoids that do not use pulleys at the joints, meaning their moment arms are variable, mimicking human anatomy more closely. It also specifically addresses systems where electric motors wind wires, rather than pneumatic artificial muscles. By empirically categorizing muscle characteristics and proposing an integrated system for their management and utilization on actual full-body robots, Kengoro and Musashi, this work aims to provide a more unified and practical framework for advancing the field of musculoskeletal robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this study is to empirically categorize and analyze the characteristics of muscles, as well as their management and utilization methods, based on extensive research conducted on the full-body musculoskeletal humanoids, Kengoro and Musashi.

The motivation behind this research stems from several identified gaps and challenges in the field:
- **Lack of Unified Discussion:** Despite the development of various musculoskeletal humanoids and numerous studies on control mechanisms, there has not been a sufficient or unified discussion regarding the diverse properties of musculoskeletal structures and how to effectively manage and utilize them in real robotic systems. Existing discussions tend to be fragmented.
- **Complexity of Muscle Properties:** Muscles in musculoskeletal humanoids possess diverse and complex characteristics—such as redundancy, nonlinear elasticity, variable moment arms, and anisotropy—that are not found in general axis-driven robots. These properties lead to both advantages (e.g., variable stiffness) and disadvantages (e.g., modeling difficulty, high internal forces, high computational cost). Understanding and systematically addressing this complexity is crucial for the effective deployment of such robots.
- **Empirical Validation on Full-Body Systems:** Most prior studies addressing musculoskeletal characteristics have relied on simulations or simplified 2D models. There is a need for empirical investigations on actual full-body musculoskeletal robots to validate theoretical concepts and develop practical control strategies.
- **Integrated System Development:** The intricate interplay of muscle properties necessitates an integrated system that can collectively manage the disadvantages and leverage the advantages for robust and versatile control. This includes developing solutions for state estimation, control, anomaly detection, and adaptation to bodily changes.

To address these motivations, the study specifically aims to:
1. **Classify Muscle Characteristics:** Categorize the features of the musculoskeletal structure into five fundamental properties: Redundancy, Independency, Anisotropy, Variable Moment Arm, and Nonlinear Elasticity.
2. **Organize Advantages and Disadvantages:** Systematize the various advantages and disadvantages that arise from the combinations of these five muscle properties.
3. **Develop Management and Utilization Strategies:** Propose and detail methods for managing and utilizing these muscle characteristics through low-level reflex control, high-level body schema learning, muscle grouping, and body schema adaptation.
4. **Demonstrate Integrated System Performance:** Showcase the effectiveness of the proposed integrated system through motion experiments on Kengoro and Musashi, performing complex tasks that require robust control and adaptability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this study involves an empirical analysis of musculoskeletal humanoids, specifically Kengoro and Musashi, focusing on their structural characteristics, control strategies, and adaptation mechanisms. The approach integrates low-level reflex controls with high-level learning-based body schema to manage the inherent complexities and leverage the unique advantages of these systems.

**A. Basic Musculoskeletal Structure and Modeling:**
The study focuses on musculoskeletal humanoids consisting of bones, joints, muscle actuators, wires, and nonlinear elastic elements. Muscle actuators wind wires, driving joints, and nonlinear elastic elements mimic muscle elasticity. Measurements include muscle length ($l$), muscle tension ($f$), muscle temperature ($c$), and in some cases, joint angle ($\theta$). Kengoro, designed for human-like anatomy, relies on vision-based estimation for joint angles due to complex joints, while Musashi, with its modular design, incorporates joint encoders for direct measurement.
Fundamental equations relate muscle lengths to joint angles ($l = g_m(\theta)$) and joint torques to muscle tensions ($\tau = -G^T(\theta)f$), where $G(\theta)$ is the muscle Jacobian.
Joint angle estimation in Kengoro utilizes an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) to estimate $\theta$ from changes in muscle length ($\Delta l$), further corrected by visual recognition of AR markers for enhanced accuracy, addressing the challenge of unmodeled factors like muscle stretch and friction. For generating desired joint torques, a quadratic programming approach is formulated to calculate muscle tensions, allowing for minimization of tension while satisfying torque constraints, or minimizing error if exact solutions are not feasible.

**B. Classification of Muscle Characteristics:**
The study classifies muscle characteristics into five properties:
1. **Redundancy:** Multiple muscles acting on a single joint, including antagonistic and synergistic arrangements.
2. **Independency:** The ability to arrange joints and muscles independently, unlike coupled actuator-joint systems.
3. **Anisotropy:** Muscles exert force primarily in the contraction direction and can loosen in the opposite direction.
4. **Variable Moment Arm:** The moment arm from muscle to joint varies with joint angle, mimicking human biomechanics.
5. **Nonlinear Elasticity:** Presence of nonlinear elastic elements within the muscle-wire system.

**C. Advantages and Disadvantages of Musculoskeletal Structures:**
These five characteristics contribute to eight identified advantages and disadvantages:
- **Disadvantages:** Modeling Difficulty (from Redundancy, Anisotropy, Variable Moment Arm, Nonlinear Elasticity), High Internal Force (from Redundancy), High Computational Cost (from Redundancy).
- **Advantages:** Easy Muscle Arrangement (from Independency), Variable Stiffness Control (from Redundancy, Nonlinear Elasticity), Muscle Rupture Handling (from Redundancy), Nonlinearity Utilization (from Variable Moment Arm), Free Joint Utilization (from Independency, Anisotropy).

**D. Integrated Management and Utilization System (Fig. 4):**
A hierarchical software system is proposed to manage these properties, comprising low-level reflex controls and high-level body schema learning.

1. **Reflex Control:** A set of low-level, high-frequency controllers that modify muscle stiffness ($k_{msc}$) and target muscle length ($l_{ref}$) based on sensory feedback to enhance adaptability and safety.
 - **Muscle Stiffness Control (MSC):** The base control, setting target muscle tension based on bias and stiffness-weighted length difference.
 - **Stretch Reflex Control (SRC):** Mimics human stretch reflex, contracting muscles in response to sudden stretches (detected by tension changes beyond a threshold, due to nonlinear elasticity). Addresses Modeling Difficulty, risk avoidance, posture stabilization.
 - **Antagonist Inhibition Control (AIC):** Mimics human reciprocal innervation, suppressing antagonist muscle stiffness based on moment arm to enable smoother, wider range movements, addressing Modeling Difficulty and High Internal Force.
 - **Muscle Thermal Control (MTC):** Suppresses muscle activity (by relaxing $l_{ref}$) as motor core temperature rises, preventing overheating based on a two-resistor thermal model and predictive limits. Addresses High Internal Force.
 - **Maximum Speed Control:** Enables rapid joint movements despite muscle velocity limits.
 - *Inhibition Control (IC):* Sets motor current of antagonist muscles with large moment arms to zero, utilizing backdrivability.
 - *Elongation Control (EC):* Pre-elongates antagonist muscles by simulating movement to ensure muscles do not reach max speed. Leverages Free Joint Utilization.
 - **Muscle Relaxation Control (MRC):** Gradually relaxes non-essential muscles (low $f_{nec}$) without affecting posture, primarily in static states. Addresses High Internal Force, aids continuous movement and environmental interaction.

2. **Body Schema Learning and Control:** A high-level learning approach represented by neural networks that model intersensory relationships.
 - **Static Body Schema:** Represents correlations between sensors (joint angle $\theta$, muscle tension $f$, muscle length $l$) at the same time point (joint-muscle mapping). It uses a network with masked inputs/outputs to learn relationships (e.g., estimate $l$ from $\theta, f$). Online learning updates network weights for adaptation. Used for state estimation, control (e.g., optimizing $l$ for target $\theta$ and minimal $f$), anomaly detection, and Variable Stiffness Control. Addresses Modeling Difficulty.
 - **Dynamic Body Schema:** Represents time-series state transitions of sensor values (e.g., $\theta, f, l$, contact force, muscle temperature). Utilizes recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to predict future sensor states from current states and control inputs (e.g., $\Delta l_{ref}$). Online learning can update network weights or parametric biases. Enables contact simulation, anomaly detection, and even object recognition. Handles Modeling Difficulty and temporal changes.

3. **Muscle Grouping:** Methods to reduce High Computational Cost by organizing redundant muscles.
 - **Manual Grouping:** Manually partitions the static body schema into functional groups of joints and muscles based on their anatomical and kinematic connections.
 - **Automatic Grouping:** Automates grouping using functional connections (derived from network weights of static body schema) and spatial connections (geometric proximity), combined into a graph for partitioning.

4. **Body Schema Adaptation:** Mechanisms to adjust the body schema in response to physical changes.
 - **Muscle Addition:** When new muscles are added, a new static body schema is constructed, original weights are copied, and new muscle weights are relearned through random movements while preserving existing relationships. Leverages Easy Muscle Arrangement.
 - **Muscle Rupture Adaptation:** If a muscle ruptures, the anomaly is detected via body schema prediction error. A mask variable is introduced to ignore the ruptured muscle in the body schema, allowing remaining muscles to compensate and tasks to continue. Leverages Muscle Rupture Handling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's main findings revolve around the empirical demonstration of an integrated system designed to manage and leverage the complex characteristics of muscles in musculoskeletal humanoids. The application of this framework to Kengoro and Musashi allowed for the realization of complex robotic behaviors, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed control and learning strategies.

- **Integrated System Effectiveness:** The combined low-level reflex control and high-level body schema learning system was demonstrated to be capable of handling the inherent disadvantages (Modeling Difficulty, High Internal Force, High Computational Cost) and utilizing the advantages (Easy Muscle Arrangement, Variable Stiffness Control, Muscle Rupture Handling, Nonlinearity Utilization, Free Joint Utilization) of musculoskeletal humanoids.
- **Adaptive Object Grasping (Kengoro):** Experiments on Kengoro demonstrated that online learning of the static body schema, which maps joint states to muscle parameters, enabled the robot to gradually acquire an accurate body representation. This learning process allowed the robot to overcome initial failures and successfully achieve object grasping through iterative refinement of its internal model, highlighting the ability to adapt to its own complex structure.
- **Robustness to Muscle Rupture (Musashi):** The system proved capable of adapting to muscle failures. Upon detecting a muscle rupture as an anomaly through the prediction error of the static body schema, Musashi could relearn its altered body schema through random movements. This adaptation enabled the robot to continue performing the intended object manipulation task despite the damage, showcasing the utility of redundancy for Muscle Rupture Handling.
- **Dynamic and Precise Manipulation (Musashi-W):** The musculoskeletal wheeled robot Musashi-W successfully executed a table-setting task involving flexible cloth manipulation. This task integrated both low-level reflex controls (muscle relaxation control, muscle thermal control) and high-level body schemas. A static body schema was employed for regular movements, while a dynamic body schema, which took cloth images as input and output, facilitated dynamic flexible cloth manipulation. Adaptive modulation of body stiffness through Variable Stiffness Control improved maximum joint velocity by 12%, allowing the cloth to be spread more widely, demonstrating enhanced dexterity and adaptability in complex interaction tasks.
- **Integrated Autonomous Navigation (Musashi):** Musashi performed an autonomous driving experiment, integrating reflex controls (muscle relaxation, muscle thermal control) with body schema learning. A static body schema was used for steering control, while a dynamic body schema, taking vehicle speed as input and output, managed pedal operations. The robot demonstrated the ability to recognize traffic signals, operate pedals, and steer through intersections, illustrating the framework's applicability to complex, real-world navigation tasks.

These experimental results indicate that the proposed framework provides a robust foundation for enabling musculoskeletal humanoids to perform long-term and stable movements in diverse scenarios, by actively managing their complex intrinsic properties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study offers a unified and empirical perspective on the characteristics, management, and utilization of muscles in musculoskeletal humanoids, addressing a notable gap in the existing literature that often relies on simulations or fragmented discussions. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Consolidation of Knowledge:** By categorizing muscle properties (Redundancy, Independency, Anisotropy, Variable Moment Arm, Nonlinear Elasticity) and systematically linking them to advantages and disadvantages, the study provides a comprehensive framework for understanding musculoskeletal structures. This unified discussion can serve as a foundational reference for future research in biomimetic robotics.
- **Advancement of Musculoskeletal Humanoid Control:** The proposed integrated system, combining low-level reflex control with high-level body schema learning, muscle grouping, and body schema adaptation, offers a robust and adaptable approach to control. This system effectively manages intrinsic challenges like modeling difficulty, high internal forces, and computational costs, while leveraging inherent advantages such as variable stiffness and muscle rupture handling. This could lead to the development of more versatile, resilient, and human-like robotic systems.
- **Empirical Validation for Real Robots:** The empirical validation on full-body musculoskeletal humanoids, Kengoro and Musashi, provides practical insights and demonstrates the feasibility of applying these complex control strategies to hardware systems. This moves the field beyond theoretical and simulation-based studies, offering a blueprint for real-world implementation.
- **Inspiration for Future Research Directions:** The discussion section outlines several critical areas for future investigation, including:
 - **Context-Dependent Reflex Switching:** Developing mechanisms for switching reflex controls based on task requirements (e.g., disabling reflexes for precise manipulation).
 - **Enhanced State Estimation:** Integrating a wider range of internal sensors (IMUs, joint capsule pressure, skin stretch) for more reliable and accurate state estimation, reducing reliance on external sensors like vision.
 - **Energy Management:** Further research into the design and utilization of nonlinear elastic elements for effective energy storage and release, addressing current material and size limitations.
 - **Robustness to External Disturbances:** Expanding control and learning strategies to account for dynamic obstacles and external environmental forces, moving beyond internal body dynamics.
 - **Hardware-Software Co-Design:** Deeper exploration of hardware aspects, such as complex joint designs and reconfigurable passive elements, to fully capitalize on musculoskeletal advantages.
 - **Cognitive and Developmental Robotics:** Integrating developmental and cognitive perspectives, including studies on internal states, human comparisons, and muscle synergies, once the fundamental engineering challenges are further addressed.
- **Potential for Biomimetic Robotics:** The insights gained from managing and utilizing biomimetic muscle characteristics could inform the design and control of next-generation robots that exhibit greater dexterity, adaptability, and robustness in unstructured human environments, potentially impacting areas like personal assistance, disaster response, and exploration.

In essence, this study provides a structured and empirically supported approach to navigating the complexities of musculoskeletal humanoids, paving the way for more sophisticated and functionally capable biomimetic robots.
