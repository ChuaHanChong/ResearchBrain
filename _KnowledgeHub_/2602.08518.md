# Characteristics, Management, and Utilization of Muscles in Musculoskeletal Humanoids: Empirical Study on Kengoro and Musashi

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Tokyo empirically categorize muscle characteristics and propose an integrated management system for full-body musculoskeletal humanoids, Kengoro and Musashi. This system combines low-level reflex controls with high-level body schema learning to manage properties like redundancy and nonlinear elasticity, enabling adaptive grasping, robust muscle rupture handling, dynamic manipulation, and autonomous navigation in complex tasks.

## Problem

- A lack of a unified and comprehensive discussion on how to effectively manage and utilize the diverse properties of musculoskeletal structures in real full-body robotic systems.
- Musculoskeletal humanoids possess complex characteristics (e.g., redundancy, nonlinear elasticity, variable moment arms) that lead to modeling difficulty, high internal forces, and computational costs.
- Most prior studies addressing musculoskeletal characteristics rely on simulations or simplified 2D models, lacking empirical validation on actual full-body musculoskeletal robots.

## Method

- The study classified muscle characteristics into five fundamental properties: Redundancy, Independency, Anisotropy, ==Variable Moment Arm==, and Nonlinear Elasticity, and systematically organized their resulting advantages and disadvantages.
- An integrated hierarchical software system was developed, comprising low-level reflex controls (e.g., Stretch Reflex, Muscle Thermal Control) for adaptability and high-level body schema learning (Static and Dynamic Body Schemas) for internal modeling.
- Methods for muscle grouping (manual and automatic) and body schema adaptation (muscle addition, rupture adaptation) were introduced to reduce computational cost and enhance system resilience to physical changes.

## Results

- Kengoro achieved adaptive object grasping by refining its body representation through online learning of the static body schema, overcoming initial failures.
- Musashi demonstrated robust adaptation to muscle rupture, detecting the failure and relearning its altered body schema to continue object manipulation tasks.
- Musashi-W successfully executed dynamic, flexible cloth manipulation, integrating low-level reflexes and body schemas, with adaptive stiffness control improving maximum joint velocity by **12%** for wider cloth spreading.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Musculoskeletal structures inherently present both challenges (e.g., modeling difficulty, high internal forces) and advantages (e.g., variable stiffness control, muscle rupture handling) that necessitate a comprehensive, integrated control and learning framework.
> - Online learning of static and dynamic body schemas is crucial for real-time state estimation, adaptive control, anomaly detection, and robust adaptation to changes within complex biomimetic robots.
> - A combination of high-frequency, low-level reflex controls and slower, high-level learning-based body schemas is effective for managing the diverse and complex properties of musculoskeletal humanoids for robust and versatile operation.
