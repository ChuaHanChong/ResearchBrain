# Thompson Sampling via Fine-Tuning of LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at IBM Research and ETH Zürich developed Thompson Sampling via Fine-Tuning (TOSFIT), an approach that leverages fine-tuned Large Language Models (LLMs) to directly parameterize the probability of maximality in Bayesian Optimization. This method addresses the challenge of intractable acquisition function maximization in large, unstructured discrete search spaces, achieving state-of-the-art sample and computational efficiency across tasks such as natural language generation, protein design, and quantum circuit synthesis.

## Problem

- Maximizing acquisition functions in Bayesian Optimization is computationally intractable in large, unstructured discrete search spaces due to the absence of gradients and combinatorial complexity.
- Existing discrete Bayesian Optimization methods often rely on structural assumptions or extensive task-specific pre-training, limiting their scalability and general applicability.
- Traditional Bayesian Optimization struggles to effectively integrate and leverage the rich prior knowledge embedded within large pre-trained generative models like LLMs for principled candidate generation.

## Method

- **TOSFIT** employs a prompt-conditioned generative LLM to directly parameterize the ==posterior probability== of maximality (PoM), eliminating the need for explicit acquisition function maximization.
- The LLM policy is iteratively fine-tuned using a Variational Bayesian Optimistic Sampling (VBOS) objective, guided by a scalable Gaussian Process (GP) reward model operating on deep features extracted from candidates.
- A novel theoretical cumulative regret bound is derived for the variational Thompson Sampling formulation, providing stronger guarantees for high-dimensional discrete domains by accounting for reward correlation through maximal information gain.

## Results

- TOSFIT consistently achieved state-of-the-art sample efficiency and computational efficiency across benchmark tasks including FAQ response refinement, thermally stable protein search, and quantum circuit design, outperforming various Bayesian Optimization, Reinforcement Learning, and evolutionary search baselines.
- Empirical evidence validated the importance of strong initial priors and careful fine-tuning, demonstrating that aggressive fine-tuning could degrade performance by causing the model to 'forget' beneficial prior knowledge.
- The method inherently supports batched observations, showing that while larger batch sizes slightly reduced sample efficiency, they significantly improved iteration efficiency, which is beneficial for time-consuming evaluations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly parameterizing the probability of maximality with a fine-tuned generative LLM provides an effective and scalable solution to the acquisition function maximization bottleneck in complex discrete spaces.
> - The inherent prior knowledge from LLM pre-training and precise prompt conditioning is crucial for initializing an effective optimization policy, and this knowledge should be carefully preserved through cautious, low-learning-rate fine-tuning.
> - The uncertainty-aware entropy regularization within TOSFIT's objective promotes a stable exploration-exploitation trade-off, leading to more consistent policy diversity compared to methods with constant or undirected exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of Bayesian Optimization (BO), a framework for optimizing expensive black-box reward functions by maintaining a probabilistic model of the unknown rewards and using it to guide the search for optimal configurations. A central challenge in BO, particularly in large unstructured discrete spaces, is the computational expense of maximizing acquisition functions. In such domains, the absence of gradients prevents efficient search, and iterating over all possible points is combinatorially intractable.

Thompson Sampling (TS), one of the earliest BO strategies, is noted for its strong theoretical convergence guarantees and empirical performance. Traditionally, TS operates by drawing a function from the reward posterior and then finding the point that maximizes this sampled function, effectively treating it as an acquisition function. However, this maximization step remains problematic in large discrete spaces.

The paper introduces Thompson Sampling via Fine-Tuning (TOSFIT) as an approach to circumvent this challenge. It builds upon Variational Bayesian Optimistic Sampling (VBOS) but differentiates itself by leveraging large language models (LLMs) to directly parameterize the probability of maximality (PoM). This places the work at the intersection of Bayesian optimization, generative AI, and deep learning.

Existing approaches to BO in discrete spaces include:
- **Relaxation to continuous spaces:** Methods using variational autoencoders (VAEs) map discrete spaces to continuous latent spaces where acquisition functions can be maximized via gradient ascent. However, these require task-specific pre-training of the autoencoder.
- **Structured discrete domains:** Some methods assume domain factorization (e.g., Bal et al., 2025) or use local mutations with learned policies (Swersky et al., 2020) for acquisition function maximization, but these are limited to domains with specific structures and may still incur high computational costs.
- **In-context Bayesian optimization:** Approaches like FIBO (Sutter et al., 2025) attempt to perform Thompson sampling within the context of an LLM without explicit parameter updates, often facing limitations related to context window size and scalability.
- **Deep kernel learning:** Methods like GOLLuM (Rankovi´c & Schwaller, 2025) learn deep kernel features for GPs, which can improve optimization trajectories but do not directly address acquisition function intractability.

TOSFIT differentiates itself by directly fine-tuning a prompt-conditioned generative LLM to represent the posterior PoM, thereby avoiding explicit acquisition function maximization and utilizing the prior knowledge embedded in the LLM's pre-training. This contrasts with methods that treat LLMs as fixed-feature extractors or rely solely on in-context learning. The work also provides a novel theoretical regret bound for its variational TS formulation, aiming to match the strong guarantees of standard TS and GP-UCB while being applicable to high-dimensional, unstructured discrete domains. The empirical comparisons against reinforcement learning and evolutionary search methods further contextualize TOSFIT within a broader landscape of search and optimization algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and computationally efficient method for Bayesian optimization (BO) in large, unstructured discrete search spaces. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current BO methodologies when applied to such domains:

- **Intractability of Acquisition Function Maximization:** In classical BO, candidate selection involves maximizing an acquisition function over the search space. In high-dimensional Euclidean spaces, this can often be made tractable using gradient-based optimization, projection techniques, or local optimization. However, in discrete spaces, particularly those that are unstructured and combinatorially large (e.g., sequences of amino acids or quantum circuit programs), gradients are unavailable. Exhaustive search is infeasible for spaces exceeding practical limits, rendering acquisition function maximization intractable. This computational bottleneck restricts the applicability of BO to many problems of scientific and economic relevance.
- **Leveraging Prior Knowledge in Generative Models:** The authors recognize that large language models (LLMs) are trained on vast datasets and inherently encode significant prior knowledge about complex data structures, including natural language, code, and molecular sequences. This embedded knowledge is a valuable resource that traditional BO methods typically do not directly exploit for candidate generation in a principled, adaptive manner.
- **Scalability Challenges of Existing Discrete BO:** While some methods exist for discrete BO, they often impose structural assumptions on the search space (e.g., factorizations for local search) or require task-specific pre-training phases (e.g., VAEs to map to continuous latent spaces). These limitations hinder their zero-shot applicability and scalability to truly unstructured, general discrete domains, especially when leveraging large-scale pre-trained models.
- **Theoretical Guarantees for Variational Thompson Sampling:** The work is motivated to provide stronger theoretical guarantees for approximate Thompson Sampling methods. Previous variational formulations, like VBOS, had regret bounds that could become vacuous in combinatorially large domains due to a dependence on the size of the search space (`|X|`). A key objective is to derive a regret bound that accounts for reward correlation across the search space through maximal information gain, thereby providing tighter and more meaningful guarantees.
- **Empirical Performance and Efficiency:** Beyond theoretical motivations, the researchers aim to empirically demonstrate that their proposed method can achieve state-of-the-art sample and computational efficiency across diverse real-world-inspired tasks, encompassing natural language generation, protein design, and quantum circuit synthesis. This involves comparing against a range of contemporary optimization paradigms, including other Bayesian optimization variants, reinforcement learning, and evolutionary algorithms.

In essence, the core motivation is to bridge the gap between the principled uncertainty-aware decision-making of Bayesian optimization and the powerful generative capabilities of LLMs, specifically addressing the computational hurdle of acquisition function maximization in high-dimensional, unstructured discrete spaces, while simultaneously providing robust theoretical underpinnings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on **Thompson Sampling via Fine-Tuning (TOSFIT)**, which re-conceptualizes Thompson sampling by directly parameterizing the probability of maximality (PoM) using a generative large language model (LLM). This approach aims to eliminate the need for explicit acquisition function maximization, which is often intractable in large unstructured discrete spaces.

#### 3.1. Core Framework (Figure 1)

TOSFIT treats the candidate generation process as Thompson sampling. Instead of drawing a function and maximizing it, the method directly learns a policy (parameterized by an LLM) that generates candidates according to the posterior PoM. The LLM is initialized with prior knowledge from pre-training and in-context conditioning, and then incrementally fine-tuned.

#### 3.2. Algorithm 1: TOSFIT with Gaussian Process Reward Model

The algorithm outlines an iterative process:
1. **Initialization:** A pre-trained LLM (`π_θ`) is used as the initial policy. A feature map (`ϕ`) extracts embeddings for candidate solutions, forming the basis for a Gaussian Process (GP) reward model.
2. **Burn-in Period:** An initial set of `m` candidates are sampled from `π_θ` and evaluated to obtain rewards (`y`). These observations are used to perform GP marginal likelihood maximization, establishing initial prior parameters.
3. **Optimization Loop:**
 - **GP Update:** The Gaussian posterior (mean `μ_ϕ` and covariance `K_ϕ`) is computed in closed form based on all accumulated observations.
 - **Policy Fine-tuning:** For `c` gradient ascent steps:
 - `B` candidates are generated from the current LLM policy `π_θ`.
 - The gradient of the Variational Bayesian Optimistic Sampling (VBOS) objective, `V(π_θ)`, is estimated using these generated candidates.
 - The LLM parameters `θ` are updated (fine-tuned) using a learning rate `η`, moving `π_θ` towards the VBOS maximizer.
 - **Observation:** A batch of `b` candidates (typically `b ≤ B`) generated in the previous step are evaluated, and their rewards are observed.
 - **GP Update:** The GP model is updated with these new observations, including another marginal likelihood maximization step.
4. **Termination:** The loop continues until a computational budget is exhausted, and the candidate with the highest observed reward is returned.

#### 3.3. Scaling Gaussian Processes (Appendix D)

To ensure computational and memory efficiency, especially with growing numbers of observations:
- **Linear Kernel:** The method assumes a linear kernel `K(x, z) = ϕ(x)^T ϕ(z)` over deep features `ϕ(x)` extracted from candidates. This allows for scalable GP inference.
- **Constant-Time Updates:** By maintaining specific aggregate statistics (`Ψ^-1`, `Φy_O`, etc.) rather than the full covariance matrix, the GP mean, covariance, and marginal likelihood maximization can be computed in `Θ(d^2)` time (where `d` is the dimension of the feature space `H`), independently of the number of observations. Each new observation requires `Θ(d^2)` compute and memory to update these statistics.

#### 3.4. Gradients of Variational Bayesian Optimistic Sampling (Proposition 1, Section 3.2)

- The VBOS objective `V(π_θ) := E_x~π_θ [μ_x + sqrt(-2 ln(π_θ_x)) * σ_x]` is shown to be concave (strictly so if `σ_x > 0`).
- The gradients `d/dθ V(π_θ)` are derived using a generalized score trick, accounting for the policy's dependence within the reward surrogate. The gradient expression incorporates an "advantage function" based on the difference between the expected reward and a term related to the inverse of the `v` function, effectively pushing up probabilities for samples where the LLM's expected reward underestimates the true reward.

#### 3.5. Stabilizing Gradients of VBOS (Section 3.3)

To mitigate high variance in gradient estimation:
- **Reinforce Leave-One-Out (RLOO) Baseline:** The baseline `ξ_i` for each sample `x_i` in a batch is set to the average of the reward surrogates of all *other* samples in the batch.
- **Advantage Normalization:** The advantage function is normalized by its empirical standard deviation (a variance-adaptive learning rate). This is mathematically equivalent to the advantage function used in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

#### 3.6. Theoretical Framework (Section 4)

- **Improved Regret Bound (Theorem 1):** The work derives a novel cumulative regret bound for VBOS. For exact VBOS, the bound is improved from `~O(sqrt(T|X|))` to `~O(sqrt(T γ_T))`, where `γ_T` is the maximum information gain, reflecting reward correlation and kernel structure. This bound matches guarantees of standard Thompson sampling and GP-UCB.
- **Inexact VBOS Analysis:** For gradient-based approximate VBOS, the regret bound includes an additional term, `E Σ D_σ_t(π_t, ~π_t)`, which quantifies the Bregman divergence between the current approximate policy `π_t` and the exact VBOS maximizer `~π_t`. This term highlights the importance of keeping `π_t` close to `~π_t`.
- **Insights for Policy Initialization and Adaptation:** The theoretical analysis motivates initializing the policy with strong priors (e.g., from LLM pre-training and prompt conditioning) to start close to the optimal policy in the search space. It also suggests that cautious, low-learning-rate fine-tuning is crucial to maintain this prior knowledge and prevent the policy from diverging too far from the optimal variational policy, thus minimizing the Bregman divergence term in the regret bound.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical findings that validate TOSFIT across three distinct tasks, demonstrating its performance against a suite of baselines.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Sample and Computational Efficiency (Figure 4, 7a)

- TOSFIT consistently achieved state-of-the-art sample efficiency (reaching higher best-seen rewards with fewer evaluations) across all three benchmark tasks: FAQ response refinement, thermally stable protein search, and quantum circuit design.
- It also exhibited state-of-the-art computational efficiency (reaching target rewards faster in wall-clock time) compared to other LLM-based optimization methods, despite the additional compute cost of fine-tuning. For instance, fine-tuning added approximately 19% overhead per step to candidate generation on protein search (Table 2), but the superior sample efficiency compensated for this, leading to overall faster optimization.

#### 4.2. Outperformance Against Baselines (Figure 4)

- **Unguided Generation:** Served as a non-adaptive baseline and quickly saturated at a suboptimal reward level.
- **Post-Generation TS:** While more efficient than unguided generation, its performance was bounded by the quality of the initial fixed candidate pool, confirming the need for generative methods that can explore new regions.
- **Actor Critic and Soft Actor Critic:** These reinforcement learning methods, which conduct undirected exploration or constant entropy-regularized exploration, generally performed worse than TOSFIT, indicating the benefit of TOSFIT's optimism and uncertainty-aware exploration.
- **Evolutionary Search (Character/LLM):** Both character-level and LLM-prompted evolutionary search methods were less effective, particularly in sample efficiency, suggesting that their mutation and crossover operations did not guide the search as effectively as TOSFIT's principled adaptation to the posterior PoM.
- **FIBO (In-context BO):** This method, closest in spirit to TOSFIT by using in-context learning for Thompson sampling, showed significantly lower performance. Its reliance on context window length for storing evaluated candidates also limited its scalability and computational efficiency in longer optimization runs.

#### 4.3. Impact of Model Size and Task Type (Figure 4d, e, f)

- Increasing LLM model size (e.g., from 1.7B to 8B parameters) proved highly beneficial for the natural language task (FAQ response refinement).
- However, for tasks like protein search and quantum circuit design, which may require venturing beyond the training data manifold for novel discoveries, the performance gain from larger models was less pronounced for all methods, including TOSFIT. This observation aligns with prior work on code discovery.

#### 4.4. Balanced Exploration-Exploitation and Policy Diversity (Figure 5, 10)

- TOSFIT's objective, which includes an uncertainty-aware entropy regularization term (`sqrt(-2 ln(π_θ_x)) * σ_x`), contributes to a stable exploration-exploitation trade-off.
- This mechanism prevents the diversity collapse observed in pure Actor Critic methods and provides more stable and effective diversity trajectories compared to constant entropy regularization in Soft Actor Critic. TOSFIT adapts diversity based on reward uncertainty, increasing it when uncertainty is high and decreasing it as observations become sufficient.

#### 4.5. Effectiveness in Batched Bayesian Optimization (Figure 6)

- TOSFIT inherently supports batched observations, allowing for parallel evaluation of multiple candidates.
- While increasing batch size slightly reduced sample efficiency (more total evaluations for a given reward), it significantly improved iteration efficiency (fewer rounds to achieve the same reward), which is beneficial when evaluations are time-consuming.

#### 4.6. Validation of Theoretical Insights (Figure 8, 9)

- **Importance of Strong Priors:** Empirical results confirmed that a strong initial policy from pre-training and precise prompt conditioning substantially improved performance. For instance, masking information in the prompt for quantum circuit design (weak context) led to considerably worse rewards, with uniform initialization failing to produce valid code.
- **Careful Fine-Tuning:** The experiments demonstrated that aggressive fine-tuning with a large learning rate, while potentially showing initial gains, ultimately harmed performance by causing the model to "forget" its beneficial prior knowledge and stagnate. Conversely, careful adaptation with smaller learning rates was crucial for long-term performance and effective retention of prior knowledge, aligning with the theoretical implications regarding the Bregman divergence term in the regret bound.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Thompson Sampling via Fine-Tuning (TOSFIT) presents a notable development in the application of Bayesian optimization to complex, unstructured discrete search spaces. Its significance and potential impact span both theoretical advancements and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Core Limitation in Bayesian Optimization

TOSFIT offers a principled and scalable solution to the long-standing problem of intractable acquisition function maximization in large discrete domains. By directly parameterizing the probability of maximality with a fine-tuned generative LLM, it circumvents the need for explicit maximization, thus enabling Bayesian optimization in previously challenging areas. This expands the scope of BO, making it applicable to problems in fields like drug discovery, material science, and quantum computing, where high-dimensional discrete spaces are common and evaluations are costly.

#### 5.2. Synergistic Integration of LLMs and Bayesian Optimization

The work demonstrates a method for synergistically combining the data-driven generative capabilities and embedded prior knowledge of large language models with the uncertainty-aware decision-making framework of Bayesian optimization. This integration provides a mechanism for LLMs to go beyond mere generation and actively guide an informed search for optimal solutions, leveraging their "world models" to accelerate discovery in areas where traditional search methods struggle.

#### 5.3. Improved Efficiency for Expensive Optimization Tasks

TOSFIT exhibits state-of-the-art sample and computational efficiency across diverse tasks. This means that fewer expensive black-box evaluations are required to find high-quality solutions, and these solutions can be found in less wall-clock time. Such efficiency is critical in scientific and engineering domains where experimental evaluations are resource-intensive, time-consuming, or physically demanding. The ability to perform batched optimization further enhances its practical utility in real-world settings.

#### 5.4. Stronger Theoretical Guarantees

The derivation of a novel cumulative regret bound for variational Bayesian optimistic sampling (VBOS), matching the strong guarantees of standard Thompson sampling and GP-UCB while accounting for reward correlation through maximal information gain, strengthens the theoretical foundation of approximate Bayesian optimization methods. This makes the bounds meaningful even in combinatorially large spaces, providing a deeper understanding of the algorithm's convergence properties and guiding its design, particularly concerning policy initialization and adaptation.

#### 5.5. Guidance for LLM Adaptation in Optimization

The empirical validation of theoretical insights regarding the importance of strong priors and cautious fine-tuning provides practical guidance for applying LLMs in optimization contexts. It highlights that the pre-trained knowledge of LLMs is a valuable asset that should be preserved through careful adaptation rather than being overwritten by aggressive fine-tuning, contributing to best practices for leveraging foundation models in active learning and experimental design.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The paper outlines several avenues for future work, including integrating learned, task-adaptive deep embeddings (complementing fixed ones), replacing Gaussian Processes with more expressive reward models like Bayesian neural networks, and exploring lightweight alternatives to full model fine-tuning (e.g., in-context conditioning). These directions suggest a fertile research area for further enhancing the performance, flexibility, and efficiency of LLM-powered Bayesian optimization.

In summary, TOSFIT contributes to making complex, discrete search problems more tractable, leveraging the power of LLMs in a principled manner to accelerate scientific discovery and engineering design while offering robust theoretical underpinnings for its approach.
