# Thompson Sampling via Fine-Tuning of LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at IBM Research and ETH Zürich developed Thompson Sampling via Fine-Tuning (TOSFIT), an approach that leverages fine-tuned Large Language Models (LLMs) to directly parameterize the probability of maximality in Bayesian Optimization. This method addresses the challenge of intractable acquisition function maximization in large, unstructured discrete search spaces, achieving state-of-the-art sample and computational efficiency across tasks such as natural language generation, protein design, and quantum circuit synthesis.

## Problem

- Maximizing acquisition functions in Bayesian Optimization is computationally intractable in large, unstructured discrete search spaces due to the absence of gradients and combinatorial complexity.
- Existing discrete Bayesian Optimization methods often rely on structural assumptions or extensive task-specific pre-training, limiting their scalability and general applicability.
- Traditional Bayesian Optimization struggles to effectively integrate and leverage the rich prior knowledge embedded within large pre-trained generative models like LLMs for principled candidate generation.

## Method

- **TOSFIT** employs a prompt-conditioned generative LLM to directly parameterize the ==posterior probability== of maximality (PoM), eliminating the need for explicit acquisition function maximization.
- The LLM policy is iteratively fine-tuned using a Variational Bayesian Optimistic Sampling (VBOS) objective, guided by a scalable Gaussian Process (GP) reward model operating on deep features extracted from candidates.
- A novel theoretical cumulative regret bound is derived for the variational Thompson Sampling formulation, providing stronger guarantees for high-dimensional discrete domains by accounting for reward correlation through maximal information gain.

## Results

- TOSFIT consistently achieved state-of-the-art sample efficiency and computational efficiency across benchmark tasks including FAQ response refinement, thermally stable protein search, and quantum circuit design, outperforming various Bayesian Optimization, Reinforcement Learning, and evolutionary search baselines.
- Empirical evidence validated the importance of strong initial priors and careful fine-tuning, demonstrating that aggressive fine-tuning could degrade performance by causing the model to 'forget' beneficial prior knowledge.
- The method inherently supports batched observations, showing that while larger batch sizes slightly reduced sample efficiency, they significantly improved iteration efficiency, which is beneficial for time-consuming evaluations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly parameterizing the probability of maximality with a fine-tuned generative LLM provides an effective and scalable solution to the acquisition function maximization bottleneck in complex discrete spaces.
> - The inherent prior knowledge from LLM pre-training and precise prompt conditioning is crucial for initializing an effective optimization policy, and this knowledge should be carefully preserved through cautious, low-learning-rate fine-tuning.
> - The uncertainty-aware entropy regularization within TOSFIT's objective promotes a stable exploration-exploitation trade-off, leading to more consistent policy diversity compared to methods with constant or undirected exploration.
