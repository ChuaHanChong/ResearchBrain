# DexViTac: Collecting Human Visuo-Tactile-Kinematic Demonstrations for Contact-Rich Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A portable hardware and software system, DexViTac, enables efficient, high-fidelity collection of human visuo-tactile-kinematic demonstrations for dexterous manipulation. The associated representation learning algorithm interprets tactile signals by grounding them in hand kinematics, leading to robotic policies achieving an average 85.8% success rate across diverse contact-rich tasks and accelerating data acquisition to over 248 demonstrations per hour.

## Problem

- Comprehensive, high-fidelity multimodal (especially tactile) human demonstration datasets are largely absent for contact-rich dexterous manipulation.
- Existing data collection systems are often confined to structured laboratory settings, lack multi-finger tactile feedback, or impede natural human motion.
- High-dimensional tactile signals present inherent semantic ambiguities, making their interpretation difficult without global kinematic context.

## Method

![[2603.17851-method.png]]
*Two-stage learning strategy. Stage 1: A self-supervised framework aligns high-density tactile features with visual anchors utilizing a kinematics-Grounded encoder to learn spatially anchored representations. Stage 2: The pretrained encoders are subsequently integrated into an Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy to map synchronized multimodal observations to multi-step action sequences for contact-rich dexterous manipulation.*

- Developed **DexViTac**, a portable, human-centric hardware system for symmetric, high-fidelity ==visuo-tactile-kinematic== data capture in unstructured environments.
- Implemented a ==ROS2-based software framework== for synchronized acquisition, alignment, and storage of heterogeneous and asynchronous multimodal data.
- Proposed a ==two-stage learning approach==: ==kinematics-grounded tactile representation pretraining== (self-supervised) to resolve semantic ambiguities, followed by policy learning via ==Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)==.

## Results

- Policies trained with the full DexViTac method achieved an average success rate of **85.8%** across four diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks on a real robotic platform.
- The system demonstrated high data collection efficiency, gathering over **248 demonstrations per hour**, approaching natural human demonstration speed and outperforming traditional teleoperation methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of kinematics-grounded tactile pre-training: Pipetting success rate decreased from **83.3% → 43.3%** without pre-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Kinematics-grounded representation learning effectively resolves spatial semantic ambiguity in high-dimensional tactile signals, which is essential for precise force control and multi-finger coordination.
> - Self-supervised pre-training with temporal consistency significantly enhances the policy's ability to infer object deformation and topological characteristics from tactile data.
> - Multimodal feedback, particularly the tactile modality, is critical for robust performance in tasks involving visual occlusion or requiring high-precision force regulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose dexterous manipulation capabilities in robotics is a significant area of research. This pursuit is often informed by scaling laws, which suggest that robust and generalizable robotic policies can be achieved through the acquisition and utilization of large-scale datasets. For complex physical interactions, the tactile modality is considered crucial, as it provides contact information often not captured by visual observations alone, thereby supporting stable interaction control.

Current approaches to data collection for dexterous manipulation face several challenges. Traditional teleoperation systems and Augmented Reality (AR) or Virtual Reality (VR) environments are typically confined to structured laboratory settings and often lack the ability to provide multi-finger tactile feedback. Embodiment-agnostic frameworks, such as UMI, primarily focus on low-degree-of-freedom (DOF) grippers, which limits their applicability to complex dexterous tasks. Exoskeleton solutions, while enabling kinematic tracking, can be intrusive to natural human motion or do not incorporate essential contact information.

A recurring limitation across many existing human-centric and embodiment-agnostic data collection systems, including DexCap, DexWild, DexUMI, BunnyVisionPro, Open-TeleVision, and ARCap, is their predominant focus on visual and proprioceptive information. These systems frequently overlook the tactile modality, which is necessary for fine-grained force regulation and understanding physical interactions. Consequently, current portable frameworks generally lack high-fidelity contact data, which restricts their utility in scenarios requiring precise force control.

Furthermore, effectively processing and utilizing high-dimensional tactile data for policy learning introduces another set of challenges. High-fidelity tactile signals exhibit spatial semantic ambiguity, meaning that local tactile readings lack clear physical meaning when decoupled from the global kinematic context of the hand. This necessitates a method to integrate tactile data with kinematic information to resolve such ambiguities. This paper aims to address these identified gaps by developing a system capable of scalable visuo-tactile-kinematic data acquisition in unstructured environments, coupled with integrated representation learning methods to enhance tactile signal interpretation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from the identified limitations in collecting comprehensive, high-quality multimodal demonstrations, particularly those incorporating tactile information, for robot learning of contact-rich dexterous manipulation. While human-centric data collection systems have lowered the barrier to scaling data acquisition, they often fail to capture the critical tactile feedback during physical interactions.

The key objectives of the DexViTac project are as follows:

1. **Develop a portable, human-centric data collection system:** The central objective is to design and implement a hardware system, named DexViTac, specifically engineered for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. This system aims to enable the acquisition of multimodal data without significantly impeding natural human motion.
2. **Achieve high-fidelity multimodal data acquisition:** The system is intended to capture first-person vision, high-density tactile sensing, end-effector poses, and hand kinematics with fidelity. A design goal is to permit data collection in unstructured, "in-the-wild" environments, moving beyond the constraints of laboratory settings.
3. **Resolve semantic ambiguities in tactile signals:** A significant challenge with high-fidelity tactile data is its inherent spatial semantic ambiguity, where local contact information can be difficult to interpret without global hand posture. The project aims to propose a kinematics-grounded tactile representation learning algorithm to effectively address this issue by associating tactile signals with corresponding kinematic configurations.
4. **Construct a large-scale multimodal dataset:** Leveraging the efficiency of the DexViTac hardware and software framework, a further objective is to create a substantial dataset comprising visuo-tactile-kinematic demonstrations. This dataset is intended to cover diverse tasks and environments relevant to contact-rich manipulation.
5. **Facilitate the development of generalized robotic policies:** The collected data and the proposed representation learning strategy are designed to support the training of robotic policies. A practical objective is to demonstrate that policies trained with this data can achieve high success rates in challenging, contact-rich tasks when deployed on real-world robotic platforms. The system also aims for embodiment-agnostic data collection to support cross-hardware generalization.
6. **Foster community research:** The authors state an intention to open-source the hardware designs, the large-scale dataset, and the associated codebases to contribute to further research in contact-rich manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DexViTac system employs a symmetric hardware-software synergistic architecture, encompassing hardware design, data collection software, data preprocessing, and a two-stage learning strategy.

**A. Hardware Design**
The hardware design adheres to three principles: tactile-centric data capture, anthropomorphic dexterity tracking, and embodiment-agnostic operation. The system comprises a human demonstration interface and a robotic execution platform, featuring mirrored sensor configurations and data modalities to minimize the human-robot domain gap.

1. **Human Demonstration Interface:** This component is designed for synchronous, high-dimensional, asymmetric multimodal information capture.
 - **Tactile Sensing Module:** Integrates customized modules with motion-capture gloves using HIT LongLin-96 fingertip tactile sensors. These flexible pressure sensors provide an 8 × 16 taxel array (140 mm² area) with a 0–20 N range, 0.01 N sensitivity, and 1000 Hz sampling rate, with less than 5% full-scale accuracy. Identical sensors are used on robotic end-effectors to minimize perceptual domain gaps.
 - **Hand Kinematics Capture:** Manus Quantum Metagloves, equipped with Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs), track hand poses. This approach is selected for its robustness against occlusions in close-contact scenarios, providing high-fidelity finger state acquisition.
 - **First-Person Wide-Angle Vision:** A GoPro Hero 12 Black with a Max Lens Mod 2.0 fisheye lens offers a 177° Field of View (FOV). This setup captures near-field hand-object interactions and environmental context, enhancing portability and providing higher effective spatial resolution near the image center due to distortion.
 - **Global Pose Tracking:** A RealSense T265 camera uses its built-in Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) to output 6-DOF poses and confidence levels. A custom error-compensation algorithm is integrated for stable and precise wrist trajectory tracking.

2. **Robot Setup:** An isomorphic sensing architecture supports multi-platform compatibility. For experiments, a GoPro camera is mounted below the robot wrist, aligning its optical center with the middle fingertip, with a horizontal deviation within ±12°, to maintain a consistent first-person perspective.

**B. Data Collection Software Framework**
An integrated software framework, built on ROS2, manages efficient acquisition, spatiotemporal alignment, and formatted storage of heterogeneous and asynchronous multimodal data.
- **Global Data Collection:** Independent ROS2 nodes operate for tactile (60Hz), vision (60Hz), kinematics (120Hz), and pose-tracking (200Hz). A central node handles timestamp alignment, frequency coordination, and buffer management. Kinematics are represented using Stretch (flexion-extension) and Spread (abduction-adduction) parameters for 19-DOF motion, focusing on relative states for cross-operator generalization. The system is designed for portability (Mini-PC, power bank) and plug-and-play operation.
- **Tactile-Dominant Multimodal Time Synchronization:** A two-stage "hardware buffering-software alignment" mechanism is employed. During online collection, ROS2 system clocks and thread-safe buffer queues prevent frame loss. Offline, tactile (60Hz) timestamps are used as global anchors due to the temporal sensitivity of tactile events. Vision streams (60Hz) undergo hardware frame-level alignment, hand kinematics (120Hz) use integer downsampling, and high-frequency global poses (200Hz) employ binary search for nearest-neighbor matching, ensuring causal integrity.

**C. Data Preprocessing**
Raw data from human demonstrations contains a human-robot domain gap, necessitating three preprocessing steps:
1. **Hand Retargeting:** A two-layer mapping translates human hand parameters (h_t) to robot joint commands (q_t). F_H maps h_t to the target actuator's URDF-defined kinematic joint space, and F_R maps this to the robot control space (solvable via PyBullet inverse kinematics). This decoupled representation allows adaptation to different dexterous hands by resetting F_R.
2. **Robot Arm Retargeting:** An incremental algorithm maps T265's local 6-DOF poses to the robot end-effector's pose in the base frame. An automatic calibration procedure determines the rotation matrix R_c2b for transformation. This enables cross-configuration generalization by re-solving R_c2b for different robot arm platforms.
3. **Tactile Denoising:** A soft-thresholding process based on dynamic baselines removes spatially non-uniform zero-point noise (ghost-touches). T_clean = max(0, T_raw - T_bias - ϵ) zeros out background noise while preserving subtle contact signals, preventing false positives from affecting policy learning.

**D. Pretraining and Policy Learning**
A two-stage training strategy is adopted:
1. **Kinematics-Grounded Tactile Pretraining:** This self-supervised stage learns kinematics-conditioned and temporally consistent visuo-tactile representations.
 - A ViT-B/16 extracts global visual features (z_v_t).
 - A kinematics-conditioned encoder processes tactile information (3-layer CNN followed by an MLP fusion with a kinematics vector z_h_t derived from hand state h_t) to generate a space-anchored representation (z_x_t).
 - The visual encoder is frozen, acting as a semantic anchor. A contrastive InfoNCE loss (ℓ(u, v)) optimizes perception weights: L_pre = E_t[ℓ(z_v_t, z_x_t) + λℓ(z_v_t, z_x_t+1)]. The first term aligns visuo-tactile representations, while the second term uses temporal consistency to predict tactile evolution from current visual information, aiming to filter noise and extract stable features.
2. **Policy Learning via ACT:** The pre-trained encoder is integrated into Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT). The network receives a joint observation sequence (e_t = [z_v_t, z_x_t, z_h_t]) and predicts a multi-step action sequence (â_t:t+H-1). This mitigates control latency and jitter. The model is optimized using Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents findings across policy learning and deployment, data collection efficiency, and hand pose tracking quality.

**A. Dataset and Experimental Design**
The DexViTac system was used to create a multimodal dataset comprising over 2,400 visuo-tactile-kinematic demonstrations. This dataset covers more than 40 daily tasks across over 10 real-world environments, including kitchens, homes, retail spaces, and offices. The tasks are categorized into four types: High-Sensitivity Tactile Force Control, Contact-Rich Dynamic Following, Fine-Grained Precision Manipulation, and Configuration-Adaptive Interaction.
For evaluation, four representative tasks were selected:
1. **Pipetting:** Requires precise pinch force regulation, testing high-sensitivity force control.
2. **Whiteboard Erasing:** Demands constant contact pressure along dynamic trajectories, evaluating force compliance and stability.
3. **Marker Insertion:** Involves stable grasping and in-hand manipulation of small objects.
4. **Fruit Collection:** Operates on heterogeneous objects with varying stiffness, verifying adaptability to physical properties.
Experiments were conducted using a RealMan RM-65 robot arm and an Inspire RH56DFTP five-finger dexterous hand, equipped with HIT Longlin-96 tactile sensors identical to those in the human demonstration interface.

**B. Task-Level Deployment and Generalization (Q1)**
The full DexViTac method was compared against three baseline policies over 30 trials per task:
- **Ours (Full Method):** Integrates kinematics-grounded and temporally consistent pre-trained encoders, fine-tuned with ACT.
- **B1. Vision-only ACT:** Trained with only visual observations.
- **B2. Ours w/o Pretraining:** Full method without self-supervised pre-training (randomly initialized encoder).
- **B3. Ours w/o Spatio-temporal Grounding:** Full method without the kinematics prior (h_t) and temporal consistency constraint (λ = 0) during pre-training.

The results showed the full method achieved the highest success rates:
- **Pipetting:** 83.3%
- **Whiteboard Erasing:** 86.7%
- **Marker Insertion:** 80.0%
- **Fruits Collection:** 93.3%
- **Average Success Rate:** 85.8%

Ablation analysis provided specific insights:
- **Efficacy of Perceptual Pre-training (B2 vs. Ours):** B2 (w/o Pretraining) saw success rates drop to 43.3% for pipetting and 53.3% for fruit collection. This outcome indicates that the proposed tactile-kinematic representations and self-supervised pre-training are necessary for alleviating convergence difficulties and enabling the policy to infer object deformation and topological characteristics from tactile data.
- **Necessity of Spatio-Temporal (ST) Coupling (B3 vs. Ours):** B3 (w/o ST Grounding) exhibited reduced success rates in marker insertion (36.7%) and whiteboard erasing (46.7%). This suggests that kinematic priors are important for resolving tactile semantic ambiguity in in-hand adjustments, while temporal consistency is crucial for smooth closed-loop force control during dynamic interactions.
- **Essential Need for Multimodal Closed-loop (B1 vs. Ours):** The vision-only baseline (B1) achieved success rates of only 6.7% for pipetting and 16.7% for whiteboard erasing. These tasks involve visual occlusion or high reliance on force feedback, demonstrating that the tactile modality is essential for compensating for visual information loss and enabling high-precision force-controlled manipulation.

**C. Data Collection Efficiency (Q2)**
The average data collection duration for DexViTac across the four tasks was 14.5 seconds per demonstration, corresponding to an efficiency exceeding 248 demonstrations per hour. This compares to 13.1 seconds per demonstration for natural human demonstration (275 demos/hour) and 112.3 seconds per demonstration for traditional teleoperation (84 demos/hour). DexViTac's collection efficiency is reported to be significantly higher than traditional teleoperation and approaches the speed of natural human operation, with the efficiency advantage becoming more pronounced for complex tasks.

**D. Hand Pose Tracking Quality (Q3)**
A comparison was made between DexViTac's physical motion capture and RTMPose, a vision-based tracking algorithm, particularly in scenarios with occlusion.
- **Robustness to Occlusion:** Under severe hand-object and self-occlusion (e.g., during fruit grasping, pipetting, marker insertion), vision-based methods like RTMPose exhibited high-frequency jitter, step-like jumps, pose estimation distortion, and physical interpenetration. In contrast, DexViTac, relying on physical motion-capture gloves, consistently produced smooth, continuous, and high-fidelity joint trajectories that adhere to physical constraints.
- **Quantitative Analysis:** Analyzing tracking curves of representative finger joints during fruit grasping showed that RTMPose displayed significant high-frequency noise and sudden mutations between 1.5 and 5 seconds. DexViTac, however, maintained smooth and continuous trajectories, demonstrating robustness against visual interference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexViTac system and associated methodology address several fundamental challenges in the field of robot learning for dexterous manipulation.

Firstly, its **portable, human-centric design** provides a solution for the high-fidelity, large-scale acquisition of multimodal visuo-tactile-kinematic data in unstructured, real-world environments. This capability is significant because existing data collection systems often compromise on either the fidelity of tactile information, the naturalness of human motion, or their applicability outside of structured laboratory settings. By enabling efficient data collection (exceeding 248 demonstrations per hour, approaching human speeds), DexViTac facilitates the creation of larger and more diverse datasets, which are increasingly recognized as critical for developing generalized robotic policies.

Secondly, the introduction of a **kinematics-grounded tactile representation learning algorithm** directly addresses the challenge of semantic ambiguity in high-dimensional tactile signals. By integrating local tactile features with the global kinematic configuration of the hand, the system provides physically consistent multi-finger coordination. This approach helps the policy network to accurately interpret complex contact information, overcoming a limitation that often hinders the effective utilization of tactile data in robot learning. The demonstrated improvement in success rates when this representation learning is applied, particularly in tasks sensitive to tactile feedback and in-hand manipulation, supports the effectiveness of this method.

Thirdly, the **construction of a large-scale, contact-rich dataset** (over 2,400 demonstrations across 40+ tasks in 10+ environments) is a substantial contribution. This dataset, which the authors plan to open-source along with hardware designs and codebases, has the potential to become a valuable resource for the research community. It could accelerate advancements in robot learning for dexterous manipulation by providing comprehensive, synchronized multimodal data that reflects real-world human interaction.

Finally, the **demonstrated performance** of policies trained with DexViTac data, achieving an average success rate exceeding 85% across four challenging contact-rich tasks, validates the efficacy of the system. The ablation studies clearly highlight the critical role of multimodal feedback (especially tactile), the proposed pre-training strategy, and the spatio-temporal coupling of modalities in achieving robust performance. This suggests progress towards developing more capable and generalized robotic manipulation skills that can operate reliably in complex, real-world scenarios, where visual information alone is often insufficient.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as fixed-base setup and single-arm modality, identifying future work towards dual-arm architectures and mobile platforms, the current work establishes a foundation for scalable and robust data-driven approaches to dexterous manipulation. The open-sourcing commitment further amplifies its potential impact by lowering barriers to entry for other researchers in this domain.
