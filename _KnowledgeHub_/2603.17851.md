# DexViTac: Collecting Human Visuo-Tactile-Kinematic Demonstrations for Contact-Rich Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A portable hardware and software system, DexViTac, enables efficient, high-fidelity collection of human visuo-tactile-kinematic demonstrations for dexterous manipulation. The associated representation learning algorithm interprets tactile signals by grounding them in hand kinematics, leading to robotic policies achieving an average 85.8% success rate across diverse contact-rich tasks and accelerating data acquisition to over 248 demonstrations per hour.

## Problem

- Comprehensive, high-fidelity multimodal (especially tactile) human demonstration datasets are largely absent for contact-rich dexterous manipulation.
- Existing data collection systems are often confined to structured laboratory settings, lack multi-finger tactile feedback, or impede natural human motion.
- High-dimensional tactile signals present inherent semantic ambiguities, making their interpretation difficult without global kinematic context.

## Method

![[2603.17851-method.png]]
*Two-stage learning strategy. Stage 1: A self-supervised framework aligns high-density tactile features with visual anchors utilizing a kinematics-Grounded encoder to learn spatially anchored representations. Stage 2: The pretrained encoders are subsequently integrated into an Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy to map synchronized multimodal observations to multi-step action sequences for contact-rich dexterous manipulation.*

- Developed **DexViTac**, a portable, human-centric hardware system for symmetric, high-fidelity ==visuo-tactile-kinematic== data capture in unstructured environments.
- Implemented a ==ROS2-based software framework== for synchronized acquisition, alignment, and storage of heterogeneous and asynchronous multimodal data.
- Proposed a ==two-stage learning approach==: ==kinematics-grounded tactile representation pretraining== (self-supervised) to resolve semantic ambiguities, followed by policy learning via ==Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)==.

## Results

- Policies trained with the full DexViTac method achieved an average success rate of **85.8%** across four diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks on a real robotic platform.
- The system demonstrated high data collection efficiency, gathering over **248 demonstrations per hour**, approaching natural human demonstration speed and outperforming traditional teleoperation methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of kinematics-grounded tactile pre-training: Pipetting success rate decreased from **83.3% → 43.3%** without pre-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Kinematics-grounded representation learning effectively resolves spatial semantic ambiguity in high-dimensional tactile signals, which is essential for precise force control and multi-finger coordination.
> - Self-supervised pre-training with temporal consistency significantly enhances the policy's ability to infer object deformation and topological characteristics from tactile data.
> - Multimodal feedback, particularly the tactile modality, is critical for robust performance in tasks involving visual occlusion or requiring high-precision force regulation.
