# HCLSM: Hierarchical Causal Latent State Machines for Object-Centric World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> HCLSM unifies object-centric decomposition, hierarchical temporal dynamics, and causal structure learning into a differentiable world model, employing a two-stage training protocol to foster specialized object representations. It demonstrates structured understanding of robot manipulation tasks on the PushT dataset and features a custom Triton kernel for improved computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing world models use flat latent representations that entangle distinct objects and lack explicit causal structures, limiting their utility for detailed planning; this absence of explicit causal understanding also prevents models from reasoning about interventions or predicting the consequences of alternative actions.
- Current models often disregard the multi-scale nature of real-world temporal dynamics, collapsing diverse timescales (continuous physics, discrete events, abstract goals) into a single dynamic mechanism.
- Naive joint training protocols often lead to prediction objectives dominating and forcing object slots into distributed, non-specialized representations.

## Method

- **HCLSM** introduces a five-layer architecture that integrates a ==Vision Transformer==, dynamic ==Slot Attention== with birth/death mechanisms for object decomposition, and a ==Spatial Broadcast Decoder (SBD)== for spatially specialized representations, paired with ==hierarchical dynamics== using individual ==selective State Space Models (SSMs)== for continuous per-object physics, a sparse ==Event Transformer== for discrete events, and a ==Goal Compression Transformer== for abstract, long-term goals.
- An explicit ==causal adjacency matrix== is learned using a ==Graph Neural Network (GNN)== with ==Gumbel-softmax sampling== and an ==acyclicity constraint==, enforced via augmented Lagrangian optimization.
- A ==two-stage training protocol== is employed: Stage 1 focuses on SBD reconstruction to encourage object specialization, while Stage 2 activates the full ==JEPA prediction loss== and causal losses.

## Results

- **HCLSM** achieved a next-state prediction loss (MSE) of **0.008** and an SBD reconstruction loss of **0.008** on the PushT dataset, with qualitative results showing the Spatial Broadcast Decoder's alpha masks successfully identifying and tracking distinct spatial regions corresponding to objects in PushT scenes.
- The custom Triton kernel for the selective SSM scan achieved a **38.0x** speedup compared to sequential PyTorch implementations, reducing the SSM component's forward pass time to approximately **5%**.
- The learned event detector successfully identified key state transitions, such as robot-object contact, occurring **2-3 times** per 16-frame sequence in PushT episodes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is possible to unify object-centric decomposition, hierarchical temporal dynamics, and explicit causal structure learning within a single, differentiable world model architecture, where a learned event detector identifies salient state transitions and a Spatial Broadcast Decoder effectively encourages slots to claim distinct spatial regions, together producing object-aware representation.
> - A two-stage training protocol is critical for successfully learning structured object-aware representations, as it prevents predictive losses from dominating and collapsing specialized slot codes.
> - Custom engineering optimizations, such as a Triton kernel for selective State Space Models, are essential for making complex hierarchical dynamics computationally tractable, achieving significant speedups.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of world models aims to enable artificial intelligence systems to predict future states from sensory input, typically video. Existing approaches, such as V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2, DreamerV3, and GAIA-1, commonly rely on flat latent representations. These representations tend to entangle distinct objects, disregard underlying causal structures, and collapse diverse temporal dynamics into a single processing scale. While effective for certain video understanding tasks, this lack of structure limits their utility for tasks requiring detailed planning or counterfactual reasoning.

A separate line of research focuses on object-centric representation learning, exemplified by models like Slot Attention, SAVi, DINOSAUR, SlotFormer, and Adaptive Slot Attention. These models are designed to decompose visual scenes into discrete "object slots," thereby achieving a more structured understanding of individual entities. However, many of these object-centric models either lack robust temporal dynamics or implement them at a single temporal scale, failing to capture the multi-scale nature of real-world interactions. Slot State Space Models represent an effort to integrate state space models with object-centric representations, but without a full hierarchical dynamic or explicit causal learning.

Another relevant area is the development of State Space Models (SSMs), such as S4 and Mamba, which offer efficient alternatives to transformers for long-sequence modeling, particularly with input-dependent (selective) mechanisms. HCLSM incorporates selective SSMs to model continuous per-object dynamics.

Furthermore, the paper intersects with research in causal discovery within neural networks (e.g., NOTEARS, DAG-GNN), which seeks to infer causal relationships from data. While some previous work explores causal representation learning as a foundational element for robust AI, HCLSM aims to integrate causal structure learning directly into its world model architecture through graph neural networks.

Finally, in the domain of robot learning from video (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, RT-2, Octo), the primary focus has often been on learning policies for robot control. HCLSM, in contrast, concentrates on developing a structured world model that could serve as a foundation for such policies, enabling more sophisticated planning capabilities.

HCLSM is presented as an attempt to bridge these distinct research areas by unifying object-centric decomposition, hierarchical temporal dynamics, and explicit causal structure learning within a single, differentiable world model architecture, a combination the authors claim is novel among existing systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for HCLSM stems from an observation about human perception and cognition: humans process the world not as a monolithic stream of pixels but as a composition of discrete objects interacting over multiple timescales with underlying causal relationships. Current world models, operating on unstructured latent representations, fail to capture these fundamental aspects of reality, thereby limiting their capacity for advanced reasoning tasks like planning and counterfactual "what-if" analysis.

The authors identify three specific dimensions where current world models are deficient, which directly inform HCLSM's objectives:

1. **Object-Centricity:** Real-world scenes are composed of distinct, semi-independent physical entities (e.g., a mug, a table). Existing flat latent spaces entangle these objects, making it challenging to isolate and track the state of individual components. A key objective is therefore to enable the model to discover and maintain separate representations for individual objects.
2. **Hierarchical Temporal Dynamics:** Physical processes unfold across various temporal scales. For instance, a ball's continuous trajectory involves milliseconds, a collision is a discrete event, and a strategic plan spans minutes. Current models often collapse these diverse timescales into a single dynamic mechanism. HCLSM aims to incorporate a hierarchical system capable of modeling continuous physics, discrete events, and abstract, long-term goals separately yet cohesively.
3. **Causal Structure:** Interactions in the physical world are directional and causal (e.g., a robot gripper pushes a mug, not vice versa). Without an explicit understanding of causality, a model cannot reason about interventions or predict the consequences of alternative actions. A critical objective is to enable the model to learn and represent the causal relationships between objects.

In addition to addressing these three structural deficiencies, a significant objective is to develop a training protocol that facilitates the emergence of these structured representations. The authors note that naive joint training can lead to the prediction objective dominating and forcing slots into distributed, non-specialized representations. Therefore, a specific aim is to devise a training strategy that encourages object specialization before the full dynamics prediction task is introduced.

Ultimately, HCLSM's overarching objective is to build a world model that mirrors the compositional, multi-scale, and causal structure of the physical world, thereby providing a more interpretable and robust foundation for autonomous agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HCLSM is a five-layer architecture that integrates object decomposition, hierarchical temporal dynamics, and causal reasoning through a two-stage training protocol.

#### 3.1. Architecture

1. **Layer 1: Perception:** Video frames are processed by a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder, yielding patch embeddings. Temporal position embeddings are added, and a linear projection maps these to a unified representation dimension ($d_{\text{world}}$).
2. **Layer 2: Object Decomposition:**
 - **Slot Attention with Dynamic Birth/Death:** This module dynamically identifies and tracks objects. It initializes a maximum number of slot proposals from learned Gaussian distributions. These slots iteratively compete for patch tokens from the perception layer via a softmax attention mechanism. The slot representations are refined through weighted value aggregation and a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU). An existence head predicts the probability of each slot being "alive," and a mechanism allows new slots to be "born" from regions with high residual attention energy if existing slots do not adequately cover them.
 - **Spatial Broadcast Decoder (SBD):** Each slot's representation is independently broadcast to a 14x14 spatial grid, combined with positional coordinates, and then decoded by a four-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). This decoder outputs feature predictions and an alpha mask for each slot. The alpha masks are softmax-normalized across active slots to enforce pixel-level competition for spatial ownership. The reconstruction target for the SBD is not raw pixels but frozen ViT patch features obtained from an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target encoder, as in DINOSAUR. The SBD loss ($L_{\text{SBD}}$) quantifies the reconstruction error, weighted by the alpha masks, encouraging slots to specialize in reconstructing specific image regions.
 - **Relation Graph:** A Graph Neural Network (GNN) models interactions between all pairs of slots. Edge features are constructed by concatenating slot embeddings, their difference, and their element-wise product. These features are processed by an edge Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), which produces weighted messages. These messages are then aggregated per-node. For a large number of slots (N > 32), a chunked computation strategy is employed to manage memory consumption during all-pairs tensor operations.
3. **Layer 3: Hierarchical Dynamics:** This layer models temporal evolution at three distinct scales:
 - **Level 0: Selective State Space Model (SSM) for Continuous Physics:** Each object slot is associated with an individual SSM track, sharing parameters across objects. This uses a selective scan mechanism (inspired by Mamba) where the state transition matrices (A, B, C) are input-dependent. A global SSM operates on mean-pooled object states, providing context to the per-object tracks. Specific numerical stability measures are implemented for bfloat16 precision, including tailored initialization of SSM parameters and clamping intermediate activations.
 - **Level 1: Sparse Event Transformer:** An event detector monitors the states produced by Level 0 SSMs. It uses multi-scale temporal features (e.g., frame differences) processed through causal dilated convolutions. When an "event score" surpasses a learned threshold, the corresponding timestep is identified as an event. A standard transformer then processes only these sparse event timesteps, significantly reducing computational cost compared to processing all timesteps.
 - **Level 2: Goal Compression Transformer:** This level uses learned summary query tokens to cross-attend to the sequence of event states, compressing them into a smaller set of abstract "goal-level" state tokens. These tokens are then processed by a transformer, capable of being conditioned on external language or goal embeddings for high-level planning.
 - **Hierarchy Manager:** Vectorized gather/scatter operations and learned per-level gating weights integrate the outputs from these three temporal scales.
4. **Layer 4: Causal Structure:** The GNN's edge weights implicitly encode interaction patterns. Additionally, an explicit causal adjacency matrix $W$ is learned using Gumbel-softmax sampling for differentiable edge decisions, L1 sparsity regularization, and a NOTEARS-inspired acyclicity constraint ($h(A) = tr(e^{A \odot A}) - N = 0$), enforced via augmented Lagrangian optimization.
5. **Layer 5: Continual Memory:** The architecture diagram indicates a "Hopfield + EWC" module, suggesting a mechanism for continual learning or memory, but further details are not provided in the main text.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Protocol

A crucial aspect of HCLSM's methodology is its two-stage training protocol, designed to overcome the challenge of prediction losses dominating and preventing the specialization of object slots:

- **Stage 1 (Initial 40% of training steps):** During this phase, only the SBD reconstruction loss ($L_{\text{SBD}}$) and a diversity regularizer contribute to the total loss used for gradient computation. The JEPA-style prediction loss is computed for monitoring but its gradients are not backpropagated. This explicitly forces slots to learn distinct spatial regions by minimizing their individual reconstruction errors.
- **Stage 2 (Remaining 60% of training steps):** Once slots have achieved spatial specialization, the full JEPA prediction loss is activated alongside the SBD loss (which transitions to a regularization role with a lower weight), object-level loss ($L_{\text{obj}}$), and causal loss ($L_{\text{causal}}$). With pre-specialized slots, the dynamics model can then learn to predict the future states of already-decomposed, distinct objects.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

The implementation includes several engineering optimizations:
- **Custom Triton SSM Kernel:** A custom kernel parallelizes the selective SSM scan across batch and inner dimension blocks, achieving significant speedups over sequential PyTorch execution.
- **Numerical Stability at bf16:** Specific measures address bfloat16 precision issues, such as replacing `x**2` with `x*x`, initializing SSM `A_log` in a constrained range, clamping intermediate activations, and disabling PyTorch's `GradScaler`.
- **GPU-Native Slot Tracking:** A differentiable Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm executed entirely on GPU replaces CPU-based Hungarian matching for slot tracking, eliminating CPU-GPU data transfer bottlenecks.
- **Memory-Efficient GNN:** For large numbers of slots, a chunked computation strategy is used for the all-pairs edge tensor, reducing peak memory consumption.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

HCLSM Small (68 million parameters) was trained on the PushT task from the LeRobot / Open X-Embodiment dataset. This dataset comprises 206 episodes (25,650 frames) of a robot manipulating a T-shaped block. Training used 16-frame video clips at 224x224 resolution on NVIDIA H100 GPUs with a batch size of 4, bfloat16 mixed precision, and a cosine learning rate schedule over 50,000 steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative measurements of model performance and qualitative insights into the learned representations and dynamics.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

Comparison of training protocols on the PushT dataset (Table 3) showed:
- **HCLSM (two-stage training with SBD):** Achieved a next-state prediction loss (MSE) of 0.008, a spatial broadcast decoder (SBD) reconstruction loss of 0.008, and a diversity loss of 0.132.
- **HCLSM (without SBD):** Achieved a lower prediction loss of 0.002 but a higher diversity loss of 0.154.

The authors interpret the higher prediction loss for the two-stage SBD protocol (0.008 vs. 0.002) as a trade-off for achieving spatially specialized, object-aware representations. The low SBD loss (0.008) in the two-stage approach confirms that individual slots successfully learned to reconstruct specific spatial regions, a goal not met without the SBD. The reduced diversity loss (0.132 vs. 0.154) further supports the notion of more differentiated slot representations when SBD is employed.

Regarding computational efficiency (Table 2), the custom Triton kernel for the selective SSM scan demonstrated substantial speedups. For the "Base" configuration (B=512, N=16), the Triton kernel achieved a 38.0x speedup (1.83 ms vs. 69.64 ms) over sequential PyTorch implementation. This optimization reduced the SSM component from being a dominant bottleneck to consuming approximately 5% of the forward pass time.

#### 4.2. Spatial Decomposition

Qualitative evaluation of the Spatial Broadcast Decoder's alpha masks (Figure 2) after two-stage training indicated that different slots learned to claim distinct spatial regions within the PushT scenes. This outcome is presented as the emergence of object-aware representations within the model. The segmentation maps, derived from the argmax over slot ownership probabilities, illustrated how different colors (representing different slots) segmented various parts of the scene. The authors acknowledge that while spatial structure emerged, the decomposition was not perfectly clean, with 32 slots being excessive for a scene with typically 3 distinct objects, leading to objects being split across multiple slots. In contrast, prior experiments without the SBD component reportedly resulted in uniform attention across all slots and no discernible spatial structure.

#### 4.3. Event Detection

The learned event detector (Figure 3) demonstrated its ability to identify significant state transitions in PushT episodes. The detector typically fired 2-3 times per 16-frame sequence, corresponding to moments such as the robot end-effector making contact with the T-block. The event detection mechanism was trained using a contrastive signal that rewarded alignment between high event probability and actual state-change magnitude.

#### 4.4. Latent Dynamics

Visualization of slot state trajectories projected into 2D via Principal Component Analysis (PCA) (Figure 4) revealed structured temporal evolution. Individual slots followed smooth paths, with occasional changes in direction occurring at detected event boundaries. The spread of trajectories suggested that different slots tracked distinct aspects of the scene's dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Significance

HCLSM represents a notable development in the field of world models by attempting to unify three key principles: object-centric decomposition, hierarchical temporal dynamics, and causal structure learning, within a single differentiable architecture. The paper's primary contribution is the demonstration that such a comprehensive model can be trained on real-world robot manipulation data.

The introduction of a two-stage training protocol is significant, as it addresses a fundamental challenge in learning structured representations: preventing the dominance of predictive losses from collapsing specialized slot codes into distributed ones. This protocol facilitates the emergence of spatially specialized object representations before the full dynamics prediction task is activated.

Furthermore, the work highlights practical engineering advancements, such as the custom Triton kernel for selective SSMs, which significantly enhances the computational efficiency of the model, making complex hierarchical dynamics more tractable. The open-source release of the codebase, training infrastructure, and evaluation suite contributes to reproducibility and facilitates further research in this domain.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The proposed HCLSM architecture has several potential impacts on future research and the development of autonomous agents:

- **Enhanced World Understanding for Autonomous Agents:** By providing a structured and interpretable representation of the environment, HCLSM could lay a foundation for more sophisticated planning, reasoning, and decision-making in robotic systems and other AI applications. Agents equipped with such models might better understand the consequences of their actions and engage in counterfactual reasoning.
- **Advancement in Robot Learning:** The application to real-world robot manipulation data (PushT from Open X-Embodiment) suggests that HCLSM could be integrated with model-based reinforcement learning frameworks. This could enable robots to learn complex skills more efficiently and robustly by reasoning about distinct objects, their interactions, and the temporal evolution of events.
- **Foundation for Generalizable AI:** The principles advocated by HCLSM—object-centricity, multi-scale dynamics, and causal reasoning—are considered by some to be fundamental requirements for achieving robust and generalizable artificial intelligence. HCLSM offers an architectural paradigm that explicitly incorporates these elements.
- **Stimulation of Future Research:** The paper transparently discusses its current limitations, including issues with slot count management, the robustness of causal discovery, and numerical stability challenges at larger scales. These identified challenges present clear directions for future research, potentially leading to advancements in adaptive slot mechanisms, more robust causal inference techniques, and improved numerical stability for large-scale hierarchical models. The eventual goal of integrating model-based planners for closed-loop robot control also indicates a future path towards practical applications.
