# Collaborative Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The MATTRL framework, developed by researchers from MIT, NUS, and Microsoft, introduces Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning to enhance LLM-driven multi-agent reasoning. It achieves superior accuracy on medical, mathematical, and educational benchmarks by injecting structured textual experience at inference time, avoiding costly weight fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Traditional Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) for LLMs is resource-intensive and suffers from instability due to non-stationary co-adaptation.
- Sparse, delayed, and high-variance rewards in complex reasoning tasks make effective learning challenging for multi-agent LLM systems.
- Fine-tuning LLMs extensively for specific MARL domains can erode their general reasoning capabilities, limiting versatility.

## Method

- ==MATTRL== introduces a ==Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning== framework that injects rich, turn-level, structured textual experience directly during inference.
- A three-stage multi-expert team collaboration process is established, involving team formation, ==consensus-driven dialogue== augmented by retrieved experiences, and final report synthesis.
- A ==dynamic experience construction mechanism== scores utterances, assigns credit using strategies like ==Difference Rewards==, and distills high-value turns into a retrievable experience pool.

## Results

- MATTRL achieved the strongest overall retrieval quality (average Hit@k of **0.565** and MRR of **0.51**) on the RareBench medical diagnostic task, outperforming existing multi-agent baselines.
- The framework improved exact-match accuracy on mathematical reasoning (Humanity's Last Exam) to **0.36** (**+0.09** over single-agent) and achieved a **0.33** learning gain (∆Acc) on educational tasks (SuperGPQA), nearly doubling the single-agent improvement.
- Difference Rewards consistently yielded the best strict-precision performance for credit assignment (Hit@1/3 of **0.40**/**0.53** in medicine), demonstrating its ability to isolate decisive contributions and reduce noise compared to Naive and Shapley methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time injection of structured textual experience offers a stable, efficient, and robust alternative to traditional weight-update-based MARL training for LLMs.
> - The choice of group-to-agent credit assignment significantly impacts the quality of the constructed experience pool, with Difference Rewards balancing precision and efficiency effectively.
> - An adaptive router, which dynamically switches between single-agent and multi-agent MATTRL based on task characteristics, further improves performance by leveraging complementary strengths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: multi-agent systems, large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning (RL) for reasoning, and test-time adaptation. The paper specifically addresses a critical challenge in scaling and stabilizing multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) systems that leverage LLMs for complex reasoning tasks.

**LLM-based Multi-Agent Collaboration:** The emergence of LLM-driven multi-agent systems (e.g., AutoGen, CAMEL, AgentVerse, ChatDev, Magentic-One) has demonstrated their superior performance over single-agent baselines in tasks requiring diverse perspectives, cross-checking, and robust problem-solving. This work acknowledges and builds upon this trend, aiming to further enhance the capabilities of such collaborative AI teams.

**Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning:** Recent advancements like DeepSeek-R1 have shown the immense potential of RL as a post-training paradigm to imbue LLMs with stronger reasoning abilities. Efforts such as MAPoRL and ReMA extend RL to multi-agent settings. However, the authors identify a significant hurdle: traditional MARL training is often resource-intensive, unstable (due to non-stationarity from co-adapting agents), and prone to eroding the general abilities of LLMs when fine-tuned for specific domains. MATTRL directly confronts these issues by proposing an alternative to weight-update-based MARL training.

**Test-Time Adaptation and Structured Experience:** The concept of "test-time adaptation" (e.g., TLM, TTRL) allows models to dynamically adjust to new data or domains during inference without explicit retraining. MATTRL innovates by applying this paradigm to *multi-agent systems*, specifically by injecting *structured textual experience* into the collaborative deliberation process. Unlike prior test-time RL methods that might rely on pseudo-rewards or unlabeled data for self-evolution, MATTRL focuses on curating and integrating actionable *textual experiences* derived from effective past interactions. This distinct approach aims to provide richer, more interpretable, and turn-level guidance compared to scalar rewards or implicit adaptations.

**Credit Assignment in Collaboration:** A crucial aspect of multi-agent systems is fairly attributing contributions, especially when learning from collective outcomes. This paper directly addresses this by investigating different group-to-agent credit assignment strategies (Naive, Difference Rewards, Shapley-style approximations). This aligns with ongoing research in areas like Shapley-Coop, LLM-MCA, and CollabUIAgents, which seek robust mechanisms for credit allocation in LLM-driven multi-agent environments.

In essence, MATTRL carves a niche by proposing the *first Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning framework*. It synthesizes ideas from robust multi-agent orchestration, LLM reasoning enhancement, and efficient test-time adaptation, while explicitly addressing the stability and resource challenges of traditional MARL. By leveraging textual experience at inference time, it offers a novel pathway to achieving distribution-shift-robust multi-agent reasoning without the computational overhead and stability concerns associated with fine-tuning LLM weights.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind MATTRL stems from the inherent difficulties in training effective multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) systems, especially those driven by large language models (LLMs), for complex reasoning tasks. While LLM-based multi-agent collaborations have demonstrated superior performance, their underlying MARL training mechanisms face significant challenges:

1. **Resource Intensiveness:** Training multi-agent systems, particularly with large foundation models, is computationally expensive and requires substantial resources.
2. **Training Instability:** Co-adapting teammates in MARL introduces non-stationarity, leading to shifting state and return distributions, which makes training unstable and difficult to guarantee optimal outcomes.
3. **Sparse and High-Variance Rewards:** In complex reasoning tasks, feedback (rewards) is often sparse, delayed, and can exhibit high variance, making it challenging for agents to learn effective strategies.
4. **Erosion of General Abilities:** Adapting LLMs to a single domain through extensive MARL training can inadvertently erode their original general reasoning capabilities, limiting their versatility.

To address these pain points, the primary **key objective** of this work is to introduce Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (MATTRL), a framework designed to enhance multi-agent reasoning in a stable, effective, and efficient manner, *without the need for fine-tuning LLM weights*.

More specifically, the paper outlines several objectives:

- **Inject Structured Textual Experience:** To overcome the limitations of scalar rewards and unstable training, MATTRL aims to inject rich, turn-level, structured textual experience into multi-agent deliberation directly at inference time.
- **Facilitate Distribution-Shift-Robust Adaptation:** The framework seeks to enable rapid and robust adaptation to new tasks or domains, even under distribution shifts, without compromising the LLM's original generality.
- **Establish a Multi-Expert Team Collaboration Process:** To structure multi-agent interaction, the work defines a three-stage collaborative process: team formation, consensus-driven dialogue augmented by retrieved experiences, and final report synthesis and decision-making.
- **Develop Credit Assignment for Experience Construction:** A crucial objective is to systematically study and implement various group-to-agent credit assignment strategies for selecting high-value dialogue turns and distilling them into a useful experience pool. This ensures that the generated experiences accurately reflect constructive contributions.
- **Demonstrate Empirical Superiority:** The framework aims to significantly improve reasoning accuracy over comparable multi-agent and single-agent baselines across challenging and diverse benchmarks, including medicine, mathematics, and education.
- **Analyze Credit Assignment Impact:** To provide insights for future research and practical application, the paper intends to validate the effect of different credit assignment schemes on the construction of the experience pool and the final decision outcomes.

By pursuing these objectives, MATTRL seeks to offer a practical and powerful paradigm for advancing LLM-driven multi-agent systems, making them more adaptable, reliable, and efficient for complex reasoning challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MATTRL introduces a novel framework that fundamentally shifts the paradigm of multi-agent reinforcement learning for LLMs from costly weight updates during training to efficient, structured experience injection during inference. The methodology revolves around a three-stage multi-expert team collaboration process and a sophisticated mechanism for constructing and utilizing a test-time experience pool.

#### 3.1. Multi-Expert Team Collaboration

The collaborative process is structured into three distinct stages:

- **Stage I: Team Formation:** Instead of free-form role invention, a coordinator agent (LLM_Coo) selects a specialized expert team (TEAM) from a predefined catalog (SP). This selection is based on the task record (X) and each specialist's expertise description, ensuring a targeted and relevant team for the specific task. Each specialist maintains an opinion set and a convergence flag.
- **Stage II: Consensus via Experience-Augmented Dialogue:** The team engages in synchronized, multi-turn discussions, capped by a maximum number of rounds (R_max). In each round, non-converged specialists retrieve task-relevant textual experiences (ER_s) from a shared experience pool (E). Retrieval is powered by a shared encoder (Qwen3-Embedding-4B) and a FAISS index, selecting top-K entries based on cosine similarity, which are then appended to the agent's prompt. Specialists update their opinions (O_s^(r)) conditioned on their previous state and the retrieved evidence. Incremental updates are then shared among all team members via a "MEETING" step to align beliefs and prevent redundant discussions. The process concludes when all specialists converge or the round limit is reached.
- **Stage III: Report Synthesis and Final Decision:** After the discussion, the coordinator agent synthesizes the team's cumulative evidence into a comprehensive discussion report (DR). The coordinator may also perform its own experience retrieval. Finally, it outputs the ultimate decision (A), conditioned on the task record and the aggregated evidence. This separation of evidence aggregation and decision-making enhances controllability and auditability.

#### 3.2. Test-Time Experience Construction

A critical component of MATTRL is the dynamic creation and utilization of a textual experience pool from ongoing interactions. This process involves:

- **Utterance Scoring:** For each turn `t` and agent `i`, an LLM judge evaluates the agent's utterance (`u_i,t`) based on domain-relevant rubrics (e.g., correctness, information gain, relevance, clarity). This yields an individual score `s_i,t` ∈.
- **Contribution Ratio and Terminal Shared Reward:** A single terminal team-level outcome score `G` (e.g., task success) is obtained at the end of the consultation. This `G` is allocated back to each turn `t` using a decay kernel `w_t = γ^(R-t)`, giving higher weight to later turns if `γ < 1`. Each agent's contribution ratio `c_i,t` within a turn is estimated by proportionally normalizing `s_i,t` among all agents.
- **Turn-level Reward (`r_i,t`):** Individual utterance scores and the decayed terminal team signals are fused: `r_i,t = λ * s_i,t + (1-λ) * G * w_t * c_i,t`, where `λ` balances the two signals.
- **Selection of High-Value Utterances:** Utterances generating a turn-level reward `r_i,t` above a predefined threshold `τ` are selected for experience construction.
- **From High-Scoring Utterances to Textual Experience:** An LLM summarizer then distills the context (`H_i,t`), the high-scoring utterance (`u_i,t`), and the quantitative reward (`r_i,t`) into a structured, retrievable textual experience entry (`e_i,t`). These entries form the test-time experience pool (E), designed to be compact, actionable, and easily retrievable.

#### 3.3. Credit Assignment Schemes

The paper systematically investigates different methods for attributing team-level returns to individual agents at each turn, which directly impacts the quality of the constructed experience pool:

- **Naive:** The simplest approach, where individual scores `s_i,t` directly inform contribution ratios.
- **Difference Rewards:** Calculates an agent's contribution by comparing the team's objective (`F_t`) with the objective achieved if that agent were neutralized (`F_t(TEAM \ {i})`). This aims to isolate an agent's marginal impact.
- **Shapley-style Approximations:** Estimates an agent's Shapley value by averaging its marginal effect across various permutations of agents in a coalition, theoretically providing a fairer but computationally more intensive credit allocation.

These credit assignment scores are used to normalize contribution ratios before being fed into the turn-level reward calculation. The entire framework leverages GPT-5 as the backbone LLM for agent reasoning and summarization, and Qwen3-Embedding-4B for generating embeddings for the retrieval mechanism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across diverse and challenging benchmarks in medicine, mathematics, and education consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of MATTRL.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **Medicine (RareBench):** MATTRL achieved the strongest overall retrieval quality, with an average Hit@k of 0.565, outperforming MDAgent (0.515) and RareAgents-Refined (0.528). It also yielded the highest Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of 0.51. The advantages were most pronounced at Hit@1 (better top-rank precision) and Hit@10 (more reliable shortlist coverage), indicating that test-time collaborative adaptation significantly boosts diagnostic accuracy.
- **Mathematics (Humanity's Last Exam - HLE):** While a single-agent baseline achieved 0.27 exact-match accuracy, multi-agent deliberation improved it to 0.33 (+0.06). MATTRL further boosted performance to 0.36 (+0.09 over single-agent), demonstrating that test-time experience meaningfully strengthens collaborative problem-solving.
- **Education (SuperGPQA):** All methods started with a pre-test accuracy of 0.44. The single-agent teacher improved post-test accuracy to 0.60 (∆Acc = 0.16). A multi-agent teacher achieved a much larger gain, reaching 0.73 (∆Acc = 0.29). MATTRL surpassed both, achieving the best post-test performance of 0.77 with the highest learning gain (∆Acc = 0.33), nearly doubling the improvement of the single-agent baseline. This highlights the substantial enhancement of teaching outcomes through collaboration and test-time experience.

#### 4.2. Group-to-Agent Credit Assignment Analysis

- **Difference Rewards** consistently yielded the best strict-precision performance (Hit@1/3 of 0.40/0.53) for medicine, outperforming Naive (0.39/0.51) and Shapley (0.35/0.49). This is attributed to its ability to reduce free-riding noise and isolate decisive turns by contrasting the full team's performance with a counterfactual where an agent is neutralized, leading to sharper credit attribution.
- **Shapley-style approximations** underperformed on tight metrics because they tend to spread credit across coalitions, diluting the impact of sharp, decisive moves and instead rewarding more consistent, alignment-improving meta-behaviors. It also presented higher computational cost and variance.
- **Naive** was the cheapest but sensitive to correlated noise.
- The findings suggest **Difference Rewards** as a practical and effective default, offering a good balance between precision and efficiency.

#### 4.3. Adaptive Collaboration

- An adaptive router, which dynamically routes cases to either a single-agent CoT solver or MATTRL, achieved further performance gains. It improved average accuracy by 10% over the single agent and 5.5% over MATTRL in isolation.
- Analysis revealed that single-agent methods excel for standardized cases with concentrated evidence, while multi-agent MATTRL is superior when evidence spans multiple specialties, requires cross-validation, or benefits from systematic hypothesis testing for robust differentials. This highlights the complementary strengths of different collaboration styles.

#### 4.4. Scaling with Team Size

- Increasing the number of agents did not uniformly improve performance.
- For **Hit@1 (strict precision)**, accuracy peaked at **three agents** and then declined, as larger teams introduced more divergent opinions, making consensus harder.
- For **Hit@3 and Hit@5**, modest, steady gains were observed with increasing team size.
- For **Hit@10 (broader recall)**, performance benefited the most from scaling, as wider discussions surfaced more plausible candidates.
- This suggests an optimal team size depends on the task's precision vs. recall requirements; smaller teams for high-precision, larger for broader recall.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Few-shot Learning

- Few-shot prompting (adding 3 random exemplars) resulted in only minor Hit@1 improvement while decreasing Hit@3/5/10. This indicates that MATTRL's gains are not merely from providing extra context, but from its structured experience *integration* mechanism, which selectively curates and applies high-value reasoning patterns.

Overall, the main findings strongly support MATTRL as an effective, efficient, and robust framework for enhancing LLM-driven multi-agent reasoning, with practical insights into credit assignment and team scaling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MATTRL framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multi-agent artificial intelligence, particularly for systems leveraging large language models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Novel Paradigm for Multi-Agent Learning:** MATTRL introduces the first Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning framework. This innovative approach effectively bypasses the significant challenges associated with traditional MARL training—such as resource intensiveness, instability due to non-stationarity, and the risk of eroding LLM generality—by performing adaptation during inference. This paradigm shift makes advanced multi-agent capabilities more accessible and practical.

**2. Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** By injecting structured textual experience at test time, MATTRL enables LLM-driven multi-agent systems to adapt rapidly and robustly to new tasks and distribution shifts without costly retraining. This is crucial for real-world applications where environments and data characteristics are constantly evolving. The demonstrated improvements across diverse domains (medicine, math, education) underscore its versatility and robustness.

**3. Efficiency and Generality Preservation:** A core strength of MATTRL is its ability to enhance reasoning without tuning LLM weights. This preserves the original generality of the underlying LLMs, preventing catastrophic forgetting or domain-specific over-specialization. The efficiency gain is substantial, making sophisticated multi-agent reasoning more feasible for deployment in scenarios with limited computational resources or strict latency requirements.

**4. Improved Interpretability and Auditability:** The use of structured textual experience provides a richer, more interpretable signal about reasoning quality and collaborative dynamics compared to sparse scalar rewards. This turn-level insight into *why* certain decisions were made or *how* collaboration unfolded can significantly improve the auditability and explainability of AI decisions, which is particularly vital in high-stakes domains like medicine and education.

**5. Practical Guidance for System Design:** The detailed analysis of credit assignment strategies offers invaluable practical guidance for developers building multi-agent systems. The finding that "Difference Rewards" provides a strong balance of accuracy and efficiency for experience construction informs how to curate high-quality experience pools. Similarly, the insights into optimal team sizing for different task requirements (precision vs. recall) help in designing more effective and tailored multi-agent architectures.

**6. Blueprint for Adaptive AI:** The concept of an adaptive router, which intelligently switches between single-agent and multi-agent MATTRL approaches based on task complexity, paves the way for truly intelligent and resource-aware AI systems. This enables systems to leverage the strengths of each paradigm optimally, enhancing overall performance and efficiency.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Medical Diagnostics:** The significant accuracy gains in rare disease diagnosis could lead to more reliable AI-assisted diagnostic tools, improving patient outcomes.
- **Advanced Education:** The framework can create more effective and personalized AI tutors, capable of diagnosing misconceptions and scaffolding learning more effectively, potentially revolutionizing educational technology.
- **Complex Problem Solving:** In fields like scientific discovery, engineering design, or financial analysis, MATTRL could empower AI teams to tackle problems requiring diverse expertise and dynamic collaboration more efficiently and accurately.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** MATTRL opens new avenues for research into dynamic budget controllers, confidence-based early stopping mechanisms, and sophisticated lifecycle management for experience pools (e.g., de-duplication, recency weighting, anomaly screening), further enhancing the practicality and long-term utility of test-time adaptive multi-agent systems.

In conclusion, MATTRL offers a timely and impactful solution to the limitations of current multi-agent learning paradigms, propelling LLM-driven collaborative AI towards greater stability, efficiency, robustness, and practical applicability across a wide array of critical domains.
