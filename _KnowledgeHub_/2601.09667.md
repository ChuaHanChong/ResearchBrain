# Collaborative Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The MATTRL framework, developed by researchers from MIT, NUS, and Microsoft, introduces Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning to enhance LLM-driven multi-agent reasoning. It achieves superior accuracy on medical, mathematical, and educational benchmarks by injecting structured textual experience at inference time, avoiding costly weight fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Traditional Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) for LLMs is resource-intensive and suffers from instability due to non-stationary co-adaptation.
- Sparse, delayed, and high-variance rewards in complex reasoning tasks make effective learning challenging for multi-agent LLM systems.
- Fine-tuning LLMs extensively for specific MARL domains can erode their general reasoning capabilities, limiting versatility.

## Method

- ==MATTRL== introduces a ==Multi-Agent Test-Time Reinforcement Learning== framework that injects rich, turn-level, structured textual experience directly during inference.
- A three-stage multi-expert team collaboration process is established, involving team formation, ==consensus-driven dialogue== augmented by retrieved experiences, and final report synthesis.
- A ==dynamic experience construction mechanism== scores utterances, assigns credit using strategies like ==Difference Rewards==, and distills high-value turns into a retrievable experience pool.

## Results

- MATTRL achieved the strongest overall retrieval quality (average Hit@k of **0.565** and MRR of **0.51**) on the RareBench medical diagnostic task, outperforming existing multi-agent baselines.
- The framework improved exact-match accuracy on mathematical reasoning (Humanity's Last Exam) to **0.36** (**+0.09** over single-agent) and achieved a **0.33** learning gain (∆Acc) on educational tasks (SuperGPQA), nearly doubling the single-agent improvement.
- Difference Rewards consistently yielded the best strict-precision performance for credit assignment (Hit@1/3 of **0.40**/**0.53** in medicine), demonstrating its ability to isolate decisive contributions and reduce noise compared to Naive and Shapley methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time injection of structured textual experience offers a stable, efficient, and robust alternative to traditional weight-update-based MARL training for LLMs.
> - The choice of group-to-agent credit assignment significantly impacts the quality of the constructed experience pool, with Difference Rewards balancing precision and efficiency effectively.
> - An adaptive router, which dynamically switches between single-agent and multi-agent MATTRL based on task characteristics, further improves performance by leveraging complementary strengths.
