# Advances in Embodied Navigation Using Large Language Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey by researchers at multiple Chinese institutions comprehensively reviews the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in embodied navigation. It categorizes LLMs' roles, compares LLM-based and non-LLM models, analyzes relevant datasets, and identifies persistent challenges and future research directions.

## Problem

- Integrating diverse multimodal sensor data (text, images, real-time inputs) into robust navigation systems presents a significant challenge.
- High latency and substantial computational demands of Large Language Models (LLMs) hinder their real-time application in dynamic navigation scenarios.
- A need exists for a structured understanding of how LLMs can enhance embodied navigation, considering their language understanding and planning capabilities.

## Method

- The paper systematically reviews existing LLM-based navigation models, categorizing LLMs' roles into ==grounded language understanding== and ==few-shot planning==.
- It comparatively analyzes LLM-based models against traditional ==Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN)== models, outlining their respective advantages and limitations.
- A thorough analysis of commonly used datasets relevant to embodied navigation is presented, discussing their applicability, issues, and limitations.

## Results

- LLM-based models like NavGPT and SayNav show capabilities in complex planning, sub-goal decomposition, landmark recognition, and dynamic instruction generation.
- The survey highlights that LLM-based models often excel in natural language understanding and few-shot learning but are computationally demanding and can struggle with fine-grained **spatial reasoning**.
- Identified challenges include data diversity for granular navigation, improving spatial reasoning, addressing **computational efficiency**, and ensuring privacy in embodied AI systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs primarily function in embodied navigation either by providing grounded language understanding for exploration policies or by directly generating actions as few-shot planners.
> - LLM-based agents demonstrate strong capabilities in path planning, environmental understanding, and human-agent interaction due to their natural language processing strengths.
> - While offering superior language understanding, LLMs face challenges in fine-grained spatial reasoning, integration of diverse real-time sensor data, and computational efficiency for navigation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is strategically positioned at the nexus of two rapidly evolving and highly impactful fields: Large Language Models (LLMs) and Embodied Intelligence (EI), specifically focusing on their synergistic application in navigation tasks. The broader research landscape is characterized by significant advancements in both LLMs, exemplified by models like GPT and BERT, and the increasing sophistication of intelligent agents capable of interacting with physical environments.

Traditionally, embodied navigation has relied on techniques such as machine learning, deep learning, and reinforcement learning to enable agents to perceive environments, make decisions, and execute actions. However, the advent of LLMs has introduced a transformative paradigm. LLMs, with their unparalleled capabilities in natural language understanding, generation, and few-shot planning, offer the potential to imbue embodied agents with higher-level reasoning, common-sense knowledge, and more intuitive human-agent interaction.

This paper serves as a critical comprehensive review in a burgeoning interdisciplinary area. While other reviews might cover general Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) or LLMs in robotics, this survey distinguishes itself by its precise focus on the *application of LLMs specifically within the navigation domain*. It meticulously analyzes the strengths and weaknesses of existing LLM-based navigation models, drawing explicit comparisons with non-LLM-based VLN models to highlight the unique contributions and challenges introduced by LLMs. Furthermore, it provides an in-depth analysis of the diverse datasets used in this field, which is crucial for understanding the current state and future directions of research.

The paper effectively contextualizes its contribution by providing a temporal map of significant works in embodied navigation from 2022 to 2024, emphasizing the rapid pace of development in integrating LLMs. It acknowledges the "remarkable success in few-shot planning" achieved by LLMs, but also highlights persistent challenges such as simultaneous integration of multimodal data (text, images, sensor data), latency reduction for real-time applications, and training efficiency. By addressing these aspects comprehensively, the survey provides a valuable framework for researchers, helping them navigate the complex landscape, identify promising avenues, and avoid redundant efforts. It signifies a maturation point in the field, where sufficient progress has been made to warrant a systematic categorization and forward-looking projection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey paper stems from the rapid evolution and significant potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) in the domain of embodied intelligence, particularly concerning navigation tasks. While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable success in various natural language processing applications, their integration into embodied systems for real-world interaction presents both exciting opportunities and substantial technical and theoretical challenges.

The paper identifies several critical challenges that motivate its comprehensive review:
- **Multimodal Data Integration:** The inherent difficulty in simultaneously integrating diverse sensor data streams, such as text, images, and other real-time environmental inputs, which is crucial for robust embodied navigation.
- **Latency for Real-time Applications:** The computational demands of LLMs often lead to high latency, posing a significant hurdle for real-time decision-making in dynamic navigation scenarios.
- **Training Efficiency:** The immense computational resources and extensive high-quality datasets required to train and fine-tune LLMs, which impacts their practical deployment.
- **Deep Environmental Understanding and Decision-Making:** Navigation tasks necessitate not just path planning but also a deep understanding of the environment and rapid, accurate decision-making, areas where LLMs' language and reasoning capabilities could offer substantial enhancement.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth three clear and comprehensive objectives, explicitly outlined as its contributions:

1. **Comprehensive Review and Comparative Analysis:** To provide an in-depth review of the application of LLMs in the navigation field. This involves covering a wide array of LLM-based navigation models and, critically, comparing them with non-LLM-based Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) models. The aim is to offer an integrated analysis that helps readers fully grasp current research developments and discern the specific advantages and disadvantages that LLMs bring to navigation.
2. **Dataset Analysis:** To present a thorough analysis of commonly used datasets pertinent to embodied navigation. This includes discussing their applicability to different tasks, highlighting their inherent issues, and outlining their limitations. This objective is crucial for guiding researchers in selecting appropriate datasets and for identifying gaps in existing data resources, thereby enhancing the practical relevance of the survey.
3. **Challenges and Future Directions:** To not only showcase existing research advancements but also to delve into the practical challenges and theoretical issues faced by current methods in real-world applications. By identifying these challenges, the paper aims to delineate clear directions for future research and pinpoint innovative application scenarios and emerging technological trends related to LLMs in navigation.

In essence, the paper aims to serve as a foundational resource for researchers and practitioners, synthesizing the current state of the art, pinpointing critical limitations, and charting a course for future innovation in the rapidly evolving field of LLM-enhanced embodied navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, "Advances in Embodied Navigation Using Large Language Models" employs a systematic review methodology to synthesize existing knowledge, categorize different approaches, and identify open challenges and future research directions. The approach is primarily analytical and comparative, drawing insights from a comprehensive collection of studies.

The core methodology involves:

1. **Literature Collection and Curation:** The authors have compiled an extensive list of relevant studies, explicitly mentioning its availability at `https://github.com/Rongtao-Xu/Awesome-LLM-EN`. This suggests a systematic effort to gather pertinent research, ensuring a broad and up-to-date coverage of the field, particularly from 2022 to 2024 as indicated by the temporal map.

2. **Conceptual Framework Development:** The paper establishes a conceptual framework by categorizing the roles of LLMs in navigation into two main types, illustrated by clear architectural diagrams (Figure 2 and Figure 3):
 - **LLMs for Grounded Language Understanding:** Here, LLMs analyze visual or textual data to extract goal-relevant information, which is then fed to exploration policies to generate actions. In this role, LLMs perform semantic understanding rather than direct planning.
 - **LLMs for Few-Shot Planning:** In this paradigm, LLMs directly generate actions and leverage exploration policies to control agents. This highlights their capability for zero-shot and few-shot learning in complex task planning.

3. **Detailed Model Analysis:** The survey provides in-depth descriptions of individual LLM-based navigation models (e.g., LM-Nav, CLIP-NAV, SQA3D, ESC, NavGPT, VELMA, MiC, SayNav, ETPNav). For each model, it outlines its core architecture, how LLMs are integrated, their specific functionalities (e.g., decoding verbal instructions, extracting keyphrases, generating plans), and their demonstrated capabilities.

4. **Comparative Analysis of Models:** To provide a holistic view, the paper explicitly compares LLM-based models with several notable non-LLM navigation models (e.g., CoW, ZSON, VLMAP, OVRL). This comparison is structured through tables (Table II) that classify works based on their type, multimodal design, underlying large models, design structure, and specific applications. This allows readers to understand the distinct advantages and disadvantages of integrating LLMs versus traditional approaches.

5. **Performance Evaluation and Metric Discussion:** The authors discuss common evaluation metrics used in vision-and-language navigation, such as Path Length (PL), Navigation Error (NE), Ideal Success Rate, Navigation Success Rate (SR), and Success-weighted Path Length (SPL). They provide a formula for SPL and present performance comparisons of various models across different benchmarks and datasets (Tables IV and V). Crucially, they acknowledge the limitations in comparison due to the unavailability of public code for some works, relying on reported metrics.

6. **Dataset Review and Comparison:** A significant part of the methodology involves a thorough review of prominent datasets used in embodied AI, especially for navigation (e.g., MatterPort3D, TOUCHDOWN, R2R, REVERIE, RxR, ProcTHOR, R3ED, CVDN, X-Embodiment). For each dataset, the paper describes its size, content, focus (indoor/outdoor), and key features (e.g., panoramic images, instructions, point clouds, human inclusion). Table VI provides a structured comparison of these datasets, and Figure 6 offers a chronological timeline of their development. This analysis is vital for understanding the data landscape and its implications for model training and evaluation.

7. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** Based on the comprehensive review and analysis, the authors synthesize current limitations and identify key challenges in LLM-based navigation. This forms the basis for proposing concrete future research directions, covering aspects like dynamic path optimization, algorithm and model architecture optimization, integration with autonomous driving, and multimodal instruction tuning.

In essence, the methodology is robust and systematic, characteristic of a high-quality survey. It moves from broad categorization to specific model and data analysis, culminating in a forward-looking perspective, thereby providing a structured and insightful overview of the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey "Advances in Embodied Navigation Using Large Language Models" synthesizes a wealth of information to present several key findings regarding the current state, capabilities, and limitations of LLM-based embodied navigation.

#### 4.1. Roles of LLMs in Embodied Navigation

A fundamental finding is that LLMs primarily assume two distinct, yet complementary, roles in navigation tasks:
- **Grounded Language Understanding:** LLMs act as information providers, analyzing visual or textual input to extract semantically rich, goal-relevant information. They perform semantic understanding, such as identifying targets or locations from human instructions, which is then passed to traditional exploration algorithms that guide agent movement. Examples include LM-Nav (decoding verbal instructions into textual landmarks) and SQA3D (assessing scene comprehension).
- **Few-Shot Planning:** LLMs directly serve as planners, generating actionable plans or steps, often leveraging their zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities. This allows them to process natural language queries and translate them into sequences of actions for navigation. Examples include NavGPT (complex planning, instruction subdivision, common-sense integration) and MiC (dynamic dialogues for generating step-by-step instructions).

#### 4.2. Performance and Capabilities of LLM-based Agents

The survey highlights that LLM-based agents demonstrate excellent performance in key navigation sub-tasks, including:
- **Path Planning:** Generating effective routes, even for novel tasks with minimal data.
- **Environmental Understanding:** Leveraging language and image processing for advanced perception, such as recognizing landmarks and understanding spatial relationships.
- **Dynamic Adjustment:** Adapting navigation behaviors based on real-time data and unexpected environmental changes.
- **Human-Agent Interaction:** Facilitating natural language communication and interpreting complex human instructions, thereby enhancing user experience.

Models like NavGPT show remarkable abilities in intricate planning, including sub-goal decomposition, incorporating common-sense knowledge, landmark recognition, and real-time plan adjustments. SayNav further emphasizes dynamic instruction generation and iterative refinement based on new data.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Non-LLM-based Models

The survey offers a critical comparison between LLM-based and traditional (non-LLM) navigation models:
- **LLM Advantages:** Superior natural language understanding and generation, strong few-shot and zero-shot learning, and adaptability due to their vast pre-trained knowledge. They enable more dynamic and flexible route planning compared to rule-based systems.
- **LLM Limitations:** Often rely heavily on pre-trained LLMs without extensive task-specific fine-tuning, which can impact optimal performance. They demand substantial computational resources and large, high-quality datasets. They may struggle with fine-grained navigation instructions and complex spatial reasoning, particularly in dynamic environments or for long-term planning. There is an inherent information loss when translating rich visual signals into natural language for LLM processing.
- **Non-LLM Advantages:** Tend to require less training data and avoid the computational overhead of extensive natural language understanding. Models like CoW and ZSON effectively repurpose zero-shot visual models (e.g., CLIP) for object localization and exploration without fine-tuning, demonstrating strong performance in specific visual tasks.
- **Hybrid Strengths:** The survey points to the increasing trend of multimodal integration in both LLM and non-LLM models, where visual, linguistic, and auditory data are combined to enhance environmental understanding and decision-making.

#### 4.4. Dataset Landscape Analysis

The paper meticulously reviews and compares a diverse set of datasets crucial for embodied navigation research:
- **Diversity:** Datasets cater to various environments (indoor like MP3D, R2R, REVERIE; outdoor like TOUCHDOWN; procedurally generated like ProcTHOR) and task complexities (e.g., dialogue-based navigation in CVDN, remote entity referencing in REVERIE, multilingual instructions in RxR).
- **Limitations:** While datasets provide valuable resources, they come with limitations. MP3D, though high-quality, requires high hardware. TOUCHDOWN's urban environments can lead to inconsistent language annotations. The need for diverse, high-quality, and often human-annotated data remains a challenge. The survey implicitly highlights that the choice of dataset significantly influences the capabilities and generalizability of trained agents.

#### 4.5. Identified Challenges and Future Directions

The survey explicitly outlines persistent challenges for LLMs in navigation:
- **Data Diversity and Granular Navigation:** Handling varied and real-time sensor data, and performing fine-grained navigation.
- **Spatial Reasoning Ability:** LLMs often lack intrinsic spatial awareness, struggling with complex topological and metric reasoning.
- **Interaction Adaptability:** Adapting to diverse user intentions and dynamic environmental changes.
- **Computational Resources and Efficiency:** The high computational cost and data dependency remain significant barriers.
- **Privacy and Security:** Important considerations when handling user data and real-world interactions.

Looking forward, the paper proposes future research directions focusing on:
- **Dynamic Path Optimization:** Moving beyond static environments to real-time path adjustment in dynamic spaces.
- **Algorithm and Model Architecture Optimization:** Developing more efficient, task-specific LLMs to reduce resource consumption.
- **Integration with Automated Driving Technologies:** Fusing multimodal data (GPS, radar) for high-precision navigation.
- **Multimodal Instruction Tuning:** Enhancing task performance through better integration of multimodal inputs.
- **Privacy Protection:** Prioritizing data privacy and security in embodied AI system design.

In summary, the main findings underscore the transformative potential of LLMs in elevating embodied navigation from reactive systems to intelligent agents capable of high-level reasoning and natural language interaction. However, they also clearly articulate the significant technical and theoretical hurdles that must be overcome for widespread, robust real-world deployment.
