# TrackVLA: Embodied Visual Tracking in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, BAAI, and Galbot develop TrackVLA, a unified Vision-Language-Action model that integrates target recognition and trajectory planning within a single LLM backbone for embodied visual tracking, achieving 10 FPS inference speed while outperforming existing methods on zero-shot tracking benchmarks and demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer on a quadruped robot through joint training on 855K tracking samples and 855K video question-answering samples using an anchor-based diffusion action model that generates continuous waypoint trajectories from natural language instructions.

## Problem

- Existing embodied visual tracking methods suffer from error accumulation due to decoupled recognition and planning modules, where perception mistakes cascade into poor trajectory decisions
- Most tracking systems lack natural language understanding capabilities, limiting human-robot interaction to basic category-level commands rather than fine-grained target descriptions
- Current approaches struggle with generalization to unseen environments and real-world deployment, often failing in dynamic scenarios with occlusions and distractors

## Method

- ==Unified VLA== architecture using a shared LLM backbone (Vicuna-7B) that processes both visual understanding and action generation through task-dependent decoding
- Anchor-based diffusion action model that generates waypoint trajectories by denoising from pre-clustered trajectory patterns, achieving 5x faster inference than vanilla diffusion policies
- Two-stage training pipeline combining visual-language alignment pre-training with joint training on mixed tracking and VQA datasets at optimal 1:1 ratio

## Results

- Achieves perfect scores (500/**1.00** Episode Length/Success Rate) on Gym-UnrealCV single target and unseen objects tasks in zero-shot evaluation
- Demonstrates 10 FPS inference speed with **80.7%** recognition accuracy, approximately **100x** faster than GPT-4o baselines while maintaining comparable performance
- Successfully tracks targets on real quadruped robot with **90%** success in medium difficulty scenarios and **70%** in hard scenarios, outperforming commercial DJI tracking drone

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint training on tracking and recognition tasks creates synergy that significantly improves both capabilities, with VQA co-training providing 29.53% recognition accuracy boost
> - Anchor-based diffusion starting from plausible trajectory clusters enables efficient real-time action generation while maintaining trajectory quality
> - Historical observation windows (32 frames) and future trajectory horizons (10 waypoints) are crucial for maintaining tracking persistence in dynamic environments

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) aims to create agents that can perceive, reason, and act in physical or simulated environments. A fundamental capability within Embodied AI is **Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT)**, which requires an agent to persistently follow a specific target using only its egocentric vision, particularly in dynamic and complex real-world scenarios.

Historically, approaches to EVT have largely relied on a **modular separation** of concerns: a perception module (e.g., a detection model) identifies the target, and a separate planning module (e.g., a reinforcement learning or imitation learning policy) determines the robot's actions. While these decoupled systems have benefited from advancements in visual foundation models (like Segment Anything Model, SAM, or Grounding DINO) and policy learning techniques, they suffer from inherent limitations. Chief among these is **error accumulation**, where inaccuracies in recognition propagate to planning, leading to suboptimal or failed tracking. Furthermore, many existing methods are limited to category-level tracking (e.g., "track a person") in relatively open environments and often lack support for natural language inputs, limiting their applicability in human-robot interaction. Prior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while addressing natural language, have sometimes been constrained by discrete action spaces or lower inference efficiency in highly dynamic settings.

TrackVLA directly addresses these shortcomings by proposing a **unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model**. Instead of decoupling recognition and planning, TrackVLA learns the *synergy* between these two tasks within a single framework. By leveraging a shared Large Language Model (LLM) backbone, it processes both target recognition (outputting textual descriptions) and trajectory planning (outputting continuous waypoints). This integration is a significant departure from modular designs, aiming to mitigate error accumulation and enable more robust, intelligent behavior. This work positions itself at the forefront of VLA research by demonstrating its effectiveness not just in static navigation but in the highly dynamic and challenging task of persistent tracking with fine-grained language control. It pushes the boundaries of embodied AI towards more generalist agents that can understand complex instructions and react intelligently in the "wild."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly robust and generalizable embodied visual tracking system capable of operating effectively in dynamic and challenging real-world environments. The authors articulate several key motivations and specific objectives:

- **Address the Challenge of Embodied Visual Tracking:** The task is inherently difficult, requiring both accurate target identification (recognition) and effective movement generation (trajectory planning) simultaneously, especially under real-world complexities like severe occlusion and high scene dynamics.
- **Overcome Limitations of Decoupled Systems:** Existing methods typically separate target recognition (using a detection model) and trajectory planning (using a policy model). This modularity leads to error accumulation, where a recognition mistake can cascade into incorrect planning, and vice-versa. The motivation is to achieve "synergy" between these two skills to avoid such pitfalls.
- **Integrate Natural Language Understanding:** Many previous tracking methods lack the ability to take natural language instructions, limiting their practicality in human-robot interaction. A key objective is to enable the agent to track targets based on detailed natural language descriptions.
- **Achieve Generalizability:** The authors aim for a model that performs well not only in synthetic environments but also demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to unseen scenarios and real-world deployment, including tracking unseen objects and navigating novel environments.
- **Ensure Real-Time Performance:** For practical robotic applications, the tracking system must operate at a sufficiently high inference speed. Achieving robust tracking at a high frame rate (e.g., 10 FPS) in real-world scenarios is a critical objective.
- **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark and Dataset:** Recognizing the need for diverse and challenging training and evaluation data, the authors motivate the construction of a new large-scale dataset, EVT-Bench, to facilitate research in embodied visual tracking with varying difficulty levels and detailed language inputs.

In essence, the core motivation is to move beyond specialized, brittle tracking systems towards a more intelligent, unified, and general-purpose embodied agent that can "track anything" based on human language in complex, dynamic settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TrackVLA adopts a novel **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model** paradigm, unifying object recognition and trajectory planning within a single architecture.

**Core Architecture:**
At its heart, TrackVLA leverages a **shared Large Language Model (LLM) backbone** (specifically, Vicuna-7B). This LLM acts as the central processing unit for both visual understanding and action generation.
1. **Observation Encoding:** Egocentric RGB video sequences are fed into a pre-trained **vision encoder** (EVA-CLIP). To manage token length and performance, visual features are processed with a grid pooling strategy, generating both fine-grained (for the latest observation) and coarse-grained (for historical observations) representations. A sliding window mechanism retains the latest `k` frames (32 in their implementation) to ensure consistent inference speed during tracking. These visual features are then projected into the LLM's latent space using a cross-modality projector (a 2-layer MLP).
2. **Language Model Forwarding:** The projected visual tokens are concatenated with language tokens (the natural language instruction). For tracking tasks, a special `[Track]` token is prepended to the instruction. This combined sequence is fed into the LLM.
3. **Task-Dependent Decoding:** The LLM's output hidden state is then processed differently based on the task:
 - **Recognition Task:** If no `[Track]` token is present (indicating a Video Question Answering - VQA task), a standard language modeling head decodes the output token auto-regressively into a textual response (e.g., describing a person's attire).
 - **Trajectory Planning Task:** If the `[Track]` token is present, the LLM's hidden state serves as conditional input to an **anchor-based diffusion model**, which acts as the action head. This model generates waypoint trajectories for robot navigation.

**Anchor-based Diffusion Action Model:**
This component is crucial for efficient and flexible trajectory generation. It operates by performing denoising from predefined "anchors" (pre-clustered trajectory patterns from the training data). This approach significantly speeds up the denoising process (5x faster than vanilla diffusion policies), as it starts from coarse, plausible trajectories. The model predicts both denoised trajectories and classification scores for each anchor, optimizing a joint loss that combines trajectory regression and score prediction. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) is used for denoising, requiring only two denoising steps during inference.

**Training Strategy:**
TrackVLA employs a two-stage training pipeline:
1. **Stage 1 (Pre-training):** The vision encoder's projector is trained on a large amount of image-caption data to align visual embeddings with the LLM's latent space.
2. **Stage 2 (Joint Training):** The visual projector, the LLM, and the action model are jointly trained using a mixed dataset. The overall loss is a weighted combination of the tracking loss (`L_track`) and the text prediction loss (`L_text`), formulated as `L = L_track + αL_text`. The vision encoder's parameters remain frozen throughout this stage.

**Data Collection:**
A significant part of the methodology involves collecting a large-scale, diverse dataset to enable the model to acquire both recognition and planning capabilities:
- **Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT) Data (855K samples):** Collected from a self-developed simulator, **EVT-Bench**, built upon Habitat 3.0. This simulator features:
 - Automated generation of diverse humanoid avatars with descriptive annotations.
 - Natural human behaviors (random walking speeds, ORCA for collision avoidance).
 - **EVT-Bench** comprises three sub-tasks of increasing difficulty:
 - **Single-Target Tracking (STT):** Basic following.
 - **Distracted Tracking (DT):** Requires fine-grained target recognition amidst distractors.
 - **Ambiguity Tracking (AT):** Identifies targets from ambiguous instructions (e.g., "first person you see") when identical distractors are present.
- **Video Question Answering (VQA) Data (855K samples):**
 - **Human Recognition VQA:** Leverages SYNTH-PEDES to create samples requiring identification or description of individuals in videos with randomly composed human subjects and backgrounds.
 - **Open-world VQA:** Incorporates publicly available VQA datasets (ActivityNet, MovieChat, PANDA) to enhance general object recognition abilities.
 - The authors empirically found a **1:1 ratio** between EVT and VQA samples to yield the best performance.

**Inference Details:**
During inference, a special `[Track]` token indicates a tracking task, triggering a single-step autoregression and action model output. For VQA, full autoregressive decoding is performed. The use of a DDIM update rule with only two denoising steps ensures high inference efficiency (10 FPS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TrackVLA demonstrates state-of-the-art performance and strong generalizability across both synthetic and real-world environments.

- **Zero-shot Performance on Gym-UnrealCV:**
 - TrackVLA significantly outperforms existing baselines (DiMP, SARL, AD-VAT, TS, EVT) in a zero-shot manner on the public Gym-UnrealCV benchmark.
 - It achieves perfect scores (500/1.00 for Average Episode Length / Success Rate) in "Single Target" and "Unseen Objects" tasks, indicating complete success in tracking throughout the entire episode.
 - In the more challenging "Distractor" task, TrackVLA still surpasses the previous SOTA (EVT) with improved EL and SR, showcasing its robust recognition capabilities amidst distractions. This zero-shot performance is a strong indicator of its generalization.

- **Performance on EVT-Bench:**
 - On the newly proposed EVT-Bench, TrackVLA vastly outperforms all existing approaches (IBVS, PoliFormer, EVT, Uni-NaVid) across all three difficulty levels: Single-Target Tracking (STT), Distracted Tracking (DT), and Ambiguity Tracking (AT).
 - For instance, in STT, TrackVLA achieves an SR of 85.1% and TR of 78.6% with a very low Collision Rate (CR) of 1.65, dramatically outperforming methods like Uni-NaVid (25.7% SR, 39.5% TR).
 - While performance naturally drops in DT and AT due to their inherent difficulty, TrackVLA maintains a significant lead over baselines, highlighting its improved ability to handle fine-grained recognition and ambiguity.

- **Visual Recognition Capability:**
 - TrackVLA demonstrates comparable recognition accuracy (80.7% ACC) to powerful state-of-the-art multimodal large language models like SoM+GPT-4o (82.4% ACC) on a human recognition task.
 - Crucially, TrackVLA achieves this at an inference speed of **10 FPS**, which is approximately **100 times faster** than GPT-based baselines (0.1 FPS). This makes it highly suitable for real-time embodied applications.
 - Ablation studies confirm that co-tuning with VQA samples significantly improves recognition accuracy (a 29.53% increase), validating the effectiveness of the diverse recognition dataset.

- **Qualitative Results in Real-World Deployment:**
 - TrackVLA exhibits strong **sim-to-real transfer capabilities**. Deployed on a Unitree GO2 quadruped robot equipped with an Intel RealSense D455 camera, it successfully tracks targets in challenging real-world scenarios: cluttered environments, low-lighting, pursuit-evasion games, and multi-person recognition.
 - Comparative real-world experiments against a commercial tracking drone (DJI Flip) show TrackVLA's superior robustness. While both achieved 100% success in easy open environments, TrackVLA maintained higher success rates (90% in medium, 70% in hard) compared to DJI Flip (70% in medium, 50% in hard) in scenarios involving occlusions and high-speed target motion.

- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Data Scale and Ratio:** Increasing training data scale consistently improves performance. A **1:1 ratio** of embodied visual tracking samples to open-world recognition samples yields the best performance, indicating the importance of balanced joint training.
 - **Action Model Architecture:** The proposed anchor-based diffusion model significantly outperforms alternative action models (Autoregressive, MLPs, vanilla Diffusion Policy) in terms of success rate, tracking rate, and collision rate, while maintaining high efficiency. Scaling up the Diffusion Transformer backbone further enhances performance.
 - **History Window Length:** Incorporating historical observations (optimal at 32 frames) is crucial, as removing them leads to a significant performance drop.
 - **Future Trajectory Horizon:** Predicting 10 waypoints in the future trajectory yields the best performance.
 - **Human Recognition Data Types:** The inclusion of various human recognition data types (single human, multiple human, same human) and the presence of random backgrounds all contribute positively to the model's recognition capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "TrackVLA: Embodied Visual Tracking in the Wild" holds substantial significance for the field of Embodied AI and Robotics:

- **Advancing Embodied Visual Tracking:** By proposing a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, TrackVLA represents a significant leap forward in addressing the complex task of embodied visual tracking. It moves beyond the limitations of modular, decoupled systems by learning the intrinsic synergy between target recognition and trajectory planning. This integration paves the way for more robust and intelligent tracking behaviors in dynamic environments.
- **Enabling Natural Language Interaction:** The ability to understand and execute fine-grained tracking instructions provided in natural language (e.g., "Follow the man wearing a black top and black pants") makes human-robot interaction for tracking tasks much more intuitive and versatile. This is crucial for seamless deployment in real-world human-centric environments.
- **Setting New Benchmarks for Generalizability:** TrackVLA demonstrates exceptional generalizability, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on public benchmarks and strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities. This indicates that the learned representations and policies are not merely memorized but can adapt to unseen targets, environments, and challenging conditions, which is a critical step towards truly autonomous embodied agents.
- **Practical Real-World Deployment:** The high inference speed of 10 FPS, coupled with robust performance in diverse real-world scenarios (cluttered, low-light, high-speed targets), makes TrackVLA highly practical for immediate robotic applications. Its ability to outperform commercial tracking drones in challenging settings further underscores its real-world utility.
- **Contribution of a New Dataset and Simulator:** The creation of the Embodied Visual Tracking Benchmark (EVT-Bench) is a valuable contribution to the research community. This large-scale, diverse dataset with varying difficulty levels and detailed language inputs will serve as a crucial resource for future research, fostering innovation and enabling standardized evaluation in this challenging domain.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** By showcasing the power of unified VLA models for complex tasks like embodied tracking, this research lays a strong foundation for developing more generalist embodied AI agents. The success of joint training across recognition and action modalities suggests a promising direction for other embodied tasks requiring tight coupling of perception and control.

While acknowledging limitations such as reliance on egocentric observations and the use of a waypoint controller, TrackVLA opens new avenues for developing intelligent robots capable of persistent, human-like tracking in complex, unconstrained environments. The public release of both TrackVLA and EVT-Bench will undoubtedly accelerate progress in this vital area of embodied AI.
