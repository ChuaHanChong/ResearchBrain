# SAGE: Scalable Agentic 3D Scene Generation for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> SAGE, developed by NVIDIA, UIUC, Cornell, and Stanford researchers, introduces an agentic framework for autonomously generating diverse and physically valid 3D environments from natural language for embodied AI. The framework achieves near-perfect scene stability (99.9%) with a 1.9% collision rate in Isaac Sim, enabling robot policies trained on its data to demonstrate improved generalization and scaling for various manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Embodied AI training requires vast amounts of diverse, realistic, and physically valid 3D scene data, which is costly, unsafe, and slow to collect in the real world.
- Existing 3D scene generation methods for simulation often lack realism, diversity, or physical stability, frequently producing physically invalid or unstable scenes unusable in robotics simulators without extensive manual intervention.
- Current approaches struggle with open-vocabulary scene descriptions and flexible adaptation to diverse user requests.

## Method

- An agent-driven framework leverages a Large Language Model (LLM) to orchestrate various generator and critic tools under a Model Context Protocol (MCP) for iterative scene construction and refinement.
- Generator tools (Scene Initializer, Asset Placer, Mover, Remover) synthesize 3D assets from text with collision-aware placement, while 'critics' provide feedback: a Visual Critic assesses semantic plausibility, and a Physics Critic runs simulator-in-the-loop validation via Isaac Sim for stability.
- A multi-level augmentation strategy (object configuration, category, and scene layout) creates diverse, task-consistent scene variants for robust policy learning, followed by automated action generation for training.

## Results

- SAGE-generated scenes achieve **99.9%** stability and a **1.9%** collision rate in Isaac Sim (vs. Holodeck's **63.8%**/**22.0%** and SceneWeaver's **67.7%**/**32.8%**); ablations show the physics critic alone drives collisions from **7.8%** to **1.9%** and stability to **99.6%**.
- The framework achieves higher visual quality scores across realism, functionality, layout, and completeness compared to existing methods, and successfully generates diverse, stylized scenes from open-vocabulary prompts.
- Robot policies trained on SAGE-generated data demonstrate clear scaling trends with increased data volume and show robust out-of-distribution generalization, achieving higher success rates (e.g., **39.1%**) when tested on baseline scenes than policies trained on baseline data (e.g., **13.2%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agentic framework powered by LLMs, integrating a physics critic with simulator-in-the-loop validation, enables adaptive and open-vocabulary scene generation with complex user requests and iterative self-correction without fixed pipelines, essential for generating physically stable, simulation-ready 3D environments crucial for successful sim2real transfer.
> - Multi-level scene augmentation significantly enhances data diversity beyond initial generation, leading to stronger generalization capabilities for robot policies trained on the generated data.
> - Automatic synthesis of action data within these validated and augmented scenes provides a scalable pathway for imitation learning, reducing the reliance on costly real-world data collection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

SAGE addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of Embodied AI: the scarcity of scalable, diverse, and physically valid training data. The broader research landscape for 3D scene generation can be categorized into several approaches, and SAGE carves out a unique and highly impactful niche by synthesizing the strengths of multiple paradigms while mitigating their weaknesses.

Historically, 3D indoor scene synthesis began with **rule-based or procedural systems** like ProcTHOR and Infinigen. While these methods ensure physical plausibility and geometric consistency, they suffer from limited flexibility, diversity, and an inability to handle open-vocabulary user requests. They rely on fixed recipes, making them unsuitable for the varied environments needed for generalized robot learning.

**Data-driven approaches** (e.g., ATISS, DiffuScene) emerged to improve realism by learning spatial priors from existing 3D datasets. However, these methods are constrained by the limited availability of diverse 3D training data, struggle with open-vocabulary prompts, and often lack fine-grained control over scene elements. Crucially, they typically do not attach explicit physical properties or validate scenes within a simulator, making their outputs less "simulation-ready."

More recently, the advent of **Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** has spurred interest in language- or vision-assisted scene synthesis (e.g., Holodeck, ArtiScene). These methods allow for text-driven generation and wider semantic coverage. However, many still operate with fixed, pre-ordered pipelines, offering limited self-improvement or adaptation. A significant drawback is their often-sparse 3D grounding, which leads to physically invalid or unstable scenes not directly deployable in robot simulators.

**Agentic pipelines**, such as SceneWeaver, represent a step towards more adaptive scene generation by leveraging tool use and iterative decision-making. These works acknowledge the need for self-reflection and refinement. However, as the authors highlight in Table 1, even advanced agentic systems like SceneWeaver often fall short on critical criteria for embodied AI, specifically lacking systematic attachment of physical attributes and robust simulator-in-the-loop validation, rendering their outputs not directly usable for policy training.

SAGE distinguishes itself by integrating and advancing these paradigms:
- **Agentic Framework:** It adopts an agent-driven approach, utilizing LLMs (gpt-oss-120b) as the core agent for reasoning and adaptive tool orchestration, enabling iterative self-correction.
- **Physical Grounding and Simulation-Readiness:** Unlike many prior works, SAGE prioritizes physical validity by incorporating a "Physics Critic" that performs simulator-in-the-loop validation using Isaac Sim. This ensures generated scenes are stable, collision-free, and directly deployable in modern robotics simulators. This is a crucial gap SAGE fills (as highlighted in Table 1).
- **Open-Vocabulary and Diversity:** It leverages state-of-the-art text-to-3D asset generators (TRELLIS) and VLMs (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct) to support open-vocabulary scene generation and fine-grained control, allowing for diverse and stylized environments. Furthermore, its multi-level augmentation strategy significantly boosts scene diversity beyond initial generation.
- **Task-Awareness:** The framework explicitly considers embodied tasks, generating environments tailored for robot policy learning, including automatic action synthesis.

By bridging the gap between high-fidelity 3D content generation and the stringent requirements of embodied AI simulators, SAGE offers a comprehensive solution that moves beyond "semantically plausible" scenes to "task-aligned, simulation-ready" data. This positions it as a significant advancement for scaling up robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SAGE stems from the pressing data challenge in Embodied AI. Training intelligent agents that can interact with the physical world requires vast amounts of data, but real-world data collection is inherently:
- **Costly:** Manual setup, equipment, and human effort make it expensive.
- **Unsafe:** Robots operating in unstructured environments pose safety risks to themselves and humans.
- **Slow and Constrained:** Real-world interactions are time-consuming and limited by the available physical environments.

These limitations make **simulation** a natural and necessary alternative, offering scalable, interactive, and safe environments. However, existing 3D scene generation methods for simulation fall short of satisfying four critical desiderata for effective embodied AI training:
1. **Realism:** Simulated environments must closely resemble the real world in terms of geometry, appearance, semantics, and physics to ensure policies transfer reliably.
2. **Diversity:** A wide range of assets, environments, and tasks are needed to prevent overfitting and promote generalization.
3. **Simulation-Readiness:** Objects and scenes must be physically stable, interactable, and directly compatible with modern simulators for large-scale training without manual intervention.
4. **Task-Awareness:** Environments should be adaptable and facilitative for various targeted embodied tasks (e.g., kitchens for household robots, surgical rooms for medical robots).

**The key objective of SAGE is to overcome these shortcomings by developing an agentic framework that can autonomously generate scalable, realistic, diverse, and simulator-ready 3D environments from open-ended user requests.**

Specifically, SAGE aims to:
- **Understand user intent:** Interpret natural language task descriptions (e.g., "pick up a bowl and place it on the table") to generate relevant scenes.
- **Ensure physical validity:** Guarantee that generated scenes are physically stable and interaction-ready, a major weakness of prior generative models.
- **Promote diversity at scale:** Provide mechanisms for multi-level scene augmentation to create numerous task-consistent variants, crucial for robust policy learning.
- **Facilitate embodied policy learning:** Directly support the automatic synthesis of action data within these generated environments, enabling imitation learning for robot policies that exhibit clear scaling trends and generalize well.

By achieving these objectives, SAGE intends to unlock the full potential of simulation-driven scaling for embodied AI, accelerating the development of more capable and general-purpose robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAGE adopts an **agent-driven scene generation framework** operating under the **Model Context Protocol (MCP)**, which standardizes interaction with external tools. The core idea is an iterative, self-improving process where an intelligent agent orchestrates various generative and evaluative tools until the scene meets user intent and physical validity.

The methodology can be broken down into three main components:

#### 3.1. Agent-driven Scene Generation

The SAGE agent, powered by an LLM (gpt-oss-120b), acts as the MCP client. It dynamically calls a suite of **generator tools** and receives feedback from **critics** to construct and refine 3D scenes. The agent's reasoning enables adaptive tool selection and self-correction without rigid, hard-coded pipelines.

**Generators:**
- **Scene Initializer:** Given a scene specification, it generates an empty 3D room (floor and walls), using MatFuse for textures and an LLM for size prediction. It also proposes an initial list of required objects with text descriptions, estimated physical attributes, and placement constraints, often derived from the user's embodied task description.
- **Asset Placer:** This tool takes a text string describing object placement requirements. It uses TRELLIS to synthesize 3D assets from text, estimates physical properties (height, mass, PBR values) via a VLM, and determines placement categories (floor, wall, on-top) using an LLM. It then employs a depth-first search with collision avoidance to find optimal stable placements, supporting multi-layer object relationships.
- **Asset Mover:** Locates and moves specified objects. It functions by removing an object and then re-using the Asset Placer's logic to reposition it, guided by feedback (e.g., from the visual critic).
- **Asset Remover:** Deletes objects from the scene based on LLM-based reasoning, typically invoked when critics suggest removing problematic items.

**Critics for Self-Improvement:**
- **Visual Critic:** Evaluates semantic plausibility, visual realism, and scene completeness. It analyzes the current scene configuration and multi-view renderings to propose new objects, suggest adjustments to existing placements (e.g., "The floor lamp is blocking the bookshelf access"), or identify missing details. Its feedback aids the agent's decision-making on which generator to invoke next.
- **Physics Critic:** Crucially, this critic enforces physical stability and simulation-readiness through **simulator-in-the-loop validation using Isaac Sim**. After each object addition, movement, or removal, the scene is loaded into Isaac Sim. It measures pose changes after simulation and rejects placements that lead to instability or collisions. If a stable configuration isn't found, it reports failure to the agent, suggesting alternatives. This iterative validation ensures near-perfect physical stability, making scenes directly deployable.

#### 3.2. Scaling the Scene for Embodied AI

To generate diverse data for robust policy learning, SAGE employs a **multi-level augmentation strategy**:

- **Object Configuration-level Augmentation:** Resamples the pose of task-relevant objects within the existing scene to introduce spatial variations while maintaining semantic consistency.
- **Object Category-level Augmentation:** For task-relevant objects, an LLM-based text augmentation generates variations in geometry and texture (e.g., different shapes, colors, materials of a "mug"). TRELLIS then synthesizes corresponding 3D assets, placed into the scene with physics validation.
- **Scene Layout-level Augmentation:** For tasks requiring full scene exploration, the entire background environment (room geometry and task-irrelevant objects) is regenerated using the agent-driven framework, while preserving the task specification and key objects. All augmented scenes undergo physical validation.

#### 3.3. Action Generation and Policy Learning

The augmented, simulation-ready scenes are then used to generate training data for embodied AI tasks:

- **Action Generation:** For **Pick-and-Place** tasks, M2T2 generates grasp pose candidates from depth images, and Curobo (a parallelized collision-free motion generation library) handles inverse kinematics and trajectory planning. For **Mobile Manipulation**, RRT (Rapidly-exploring Random Trees) is used for robot path planning, combined with pick-and-place actions. Failure modes in motion planning are filtered out.
- **Policy Learning:** A **Diffusion Policy** (specifically from Diffusion Policy) is trained via imitation learning using the large-scale action data. The policy takes RGB and depth images from multiple camera views and outputs continuous actions. For long-horizon tasks, policies are decomposed into sequential stages.

This holistic approach, from intelligent scene generation and rigorous physical validation to scalable data augmentation and automated action synthesis, constitutes SAGE's comprehensive methodology for addressing the embodied AI data challenge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SAGE demonstrates state-of-the-art capabilities in 3D scene generation and its direct utility for embodied AI learning, outperforming existing methods across multiple metrics.

#### 4.1. Scene Generation Performance

**Quantitative Evaluation (Table 2):**
SAGE significantly outperforms Holodeck and SceneWeaver on common indoor scene types (Bedroom, Kitchen, Living Room).
- **Visual Quality:** SAGE consistently achieves higher scores across Realism, Functionality, Layout, and Completeness, indicating more coherent, plausible, and richly detailed scenes. For instance, in Bedrooms, SAGE achieves 9.5 for Completeness compared to Holodeck's 6.1 and SceneWeaver's 7.5.
- **Physical Validity:** This is SAGE's most striking advantage. Its collision rate is drastically lower (average 1.9% vs. Holodeck's 22.0% and SceneWeaver's 32.8%), and its stability rate is near-perfect (average 99.9% vs. Holodeck's 63.8% and SceneWeaver's 67.7%). This directly demonstrates the effectiveness of the physics critic and simulator-in-the-loop validation.

**Qualitative Evaluation (Figures 3, 4, 5):**
- **Common Types:** SAGE produces more complete and realistic layouts for standard rooms compared to baselines (Figure 3).
- **Open-Vocabulary Types:** SAGE successfully generates highly diverse and stylized scenes (e.g., "Fairy-tale princess room," "Cyberpunk game den," "Starry-night bedroom") that faithfully adhere to open-ended text prompts, thanks to its text-to-3D object synthesis (Figures 3, 4).
- **Stability Verification:** Visual comparisons clearly show baseline scenes with displaced or fallen objects after Isaac Sim simulation, while SAGE-generated scenes remain fully stable, confirming its superior physical validity (Figure 5).

**Ablation Study (Table 3):**
The ablation study confirms the critical role of both critics:
- Adding the **visual critic** alone substantially improves visual quality metrics.
- Adding the **physics critic** alone drastically reduces collisions (from 7.8% to 1.9%) and significantly boosts stability (to 99.6%).
- Combining both visual and physics critics yields the best overall performance, demonstrating their complementary importance for generating realistic and physically stable scenes.

**Additional Capabilities:**
- **SAGE-10k Dataset:** The framework was used to pre-generate a large-scale dataset of 10,000 scenes across 50 room types and styles, containing 565,000 unique 3D objects, demonstrating its scalability (Figure 6).
- **Multi-room Layout:** SAGE can generate complex multi-room environments with interconnected floor plans (Figure 7).
- **Image-conditioned Generation:** It can generate scenes based on reference images by extracting style and object attributes (Figure 8).
- **Articulated Objects:** SAGE can integrate articulated assets from PartNet-Mobility, enabling tasks involving opening/closing drawers or doors (Figure 9).

#### 4.2. Embodied AI Learning Performance

SAGE-generated data significantly enhances robot policy learning, showing clear scaling trends and improved generalization.

**Scaling Trend with SAGE-generated Data (Figures 10, 11):**
- For both **Pick-and-Place** (object augmentation) and **Mobile Manipulation** (scene layout augmentation), policy success rates show clear increases with the number of generated scenes and demonstrations.
- The gap between the learned policy (using partial visual observations) and the "privileged agent" (with full 3D scene information) narrows with increased data, indicating effective learning.

**Comparison with Baselines (Figure 11, Table 5):**
- Two baselines mimicking prior methods (one omitting physics critic and object synthesis, another further replacing the agent with a fixed pipeline) show significantly slower improvement and lower final success rates compared to SAGE-trained policies, even with the same amount of data.
- **Cross-Evaluation (Table 5):** Policies trained on SAGE-generated data demonstrate robust generalization. Even when tested on held-out scenes generated by the baseline methods (representing a distribution shift), SAGE-trained policies achieve higher success rates (e.g., 39.1% on Baseline 1 scenes) than policies trained *and* tested on baseline data (e.g., 13.2% for Baseline 1). This is a strong indicator of SAGE's ability to produce high-quality, diverse data that fosters generalizable robot behaviors.

**Policy Performance vs. Motion Planning (Table 4):**
- The learned policies achieve success rates approaching those of the underlying motion planners (privileged agent), confirming the fidelity of the generated data and the effectiveness of imitation learning at scale. For example, Pick-and-Place policy rollout achieves 50.0% test success rate compared to the privileged agent's 57.7%.

These findings collectively underscore SAGE's dual contribution: it advances 3D scene generation by prioritizing physical validity and diversity through an agentic framework, and it directly translates these advancements into measurable benefits for embodied AI policy learning, demonstrating robust scaling and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SAGE represents a significant step forward in addressing the data bottleneck for Embodied AI, promising to accelerate the development of more capable and general-purpose robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unlocking Scalable Data Generation for Embodied AI:** The most immediate impact of SAGE is its ability to automatically generate large volumes of diverse, high-quality, and simulator-ready 3D environments. This fundamentally changes the paradigm from costly and slow manual data collection or limited procedural generation to highly automated, on-demand data synthesis. This scalability is crucial for training complex embodied agents that require exposure to a vast array of scenarios to achieve robust generalization.

- **Enhanced Realism and Physical Validity:** By rigorously integrating a physics critic with simulator-in-the-loop validation (Isaac Sim), SAGE ensures that generated scenes are not just visually plausible but also physically stable and interactable. This is a critical distinction from many prior generative methods, as physically invalid scenes lead to brittle policies that fail in the real world. SAGE's guarantee of physical validity means policies trained in these environments are more likely to transfer successfully to real robots.

- **Improved Generalization for Robot Policies:** The multi-level scene augmentation strategy is key to training policies that generalize to unseen objects, configurations, and even entirely new layouts. The experimental results clearly show that policies trained on SAGE-generated data exhibit better scaling trends and stronger out-of-distribution generalization compared to baselines. This directly addresses the long-standing challenge of "sim2real" transfer by providing more robust training data.

- **Adaptive and Flexible Scene Creation:** The agentic framework, powered by LLMs and MCP, allows for open-vocabulary scene generation and adaptive self-correction. Users can specify complex, open-ended tasks or stylistic preferences, and the agent intelligently orchestrates various generators and critics to fulfill these requests. This flexibility is vital for adapting to diverse research needs and rapidly prototyping new embodied AI tasks.

- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI Research:** The SAGE-10k dataset, a large collection of physically validated scenes, provides a valuable resource for the wider research community, enabling standardized benchmarking and further development in robot learning. The framework's modular design also suggests future extensions, such as incorporating deformable objects, outdoor environments, or more complex articulated object interactions, which could significantly broaden the scope of embodied AI applications.

- **Accelerating Iterative Design and Validation:** By integrating simulation directly into the generation loop, SAGE allows for rapid iterative design and validation of environments. This capability can streamline the development cycle for robot designers and AI researchers, enabling quicker testing of hypotheses and faster convergence towards effective robot behaviors.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Scope:** Current focus is on indoor scenes and rigid-body physics. Extending to outdoor settings and handling articulated/deformable objects is a promising direction.
- **Action Generation Complexity:** While flexible, the current action generation primarily targets pick, place, and navigation tasks. Expanding to a wider range of embodied tasks is a natural next step.
- **Policy Learning:** Beyond imitation learning, coupling the generator with online Reinforcement Learning (RL) and real-robot closed-loop validation could further boost performance and bridge the sim2real gap even more effectively.

In conclusion, SAGE fundamentally redefines how training data for embodied AI can be acquired. By offering an intelligent, scalable, and physically grounded approach to 3D scene generation, it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for robust robot learning, paving the way for more capable, adaptable, and generalizable embodied agents in the future.
