# GigaWorld-1: A Roadmap to Build World Models for Robot Policy Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from GigaAI and Tsinghua University introduce GigaWorld-1, a systematically designed world model, and WMBench, a benchmark for evaluating world models as robot policy evaluators. This framework allows for scalable and reliable assessment of robot policies, achieving higher fidelity in long-horizon rollouts and closer alignment with real-world task outcomes compared to general-purpose video generation models.

## Problem

- Evaluating embodied robot foundation models on physical hardware is time-consuming, expensive, and requires continuous human supervision, creating a development bottleneck.
- Traditional simulation environments are limited by the "sim-to-real gap" and high overhead in constructing scene-specific digital twins.
- There is a lack of systematic understanding regarding the properties that make a world model a reliable and trustworthy evaluator of robot policies.

## Method

- The authors developed **WMBench**, a novel benchmark providing 2,989 paired real-world and world-model trajectories across eight manipulation tasks, alongside 324,000 human-annotated rollout segments for training and evaluation.
- They designed **GigaWorld-1**, an ==autoregressive diffusion-transformer== world model incorporating architectural innovations like ==unified spatially-aligned control injection==, ==hierarchical history injection== for persistent memory, and a ==progressive multi-stage training pipeline==.
- A ==4-step closed-loop evaluation protocol== and a comprehensive metric system, including the ==World Model as Evaluator Score (WMES)== and VLM-assisted outcome evaluation, were established to rigorously assess evaluator quality.

## Results

- GigaWorld-1-Plus (**5B** parameters) achieved the highest overall average score of **0.6834** across key evaluator-relevant metrics, outperforming strong baselines like Cosmos-Predict2.5 (**0.6123**) and Wan 2.2 (**0.5948**).
- The model consistently maintained superior PSNR, FID, and FVD over **40 seconds** of autoregressive generation, demonstrating enhanced long-horizon stability and reduced failure modes.
- A VLM-based evaluator, fine-tuned with outcome-centric supervision, achieved **87.80%** exact agreement and **99.16%** adjacent agreement with human ratings, proving its reliability as a scalable proxy for human judgment, and GigaWorld-1's generated success rates aligned more closely with real-world outcomes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual and geometric fidelity, along with the ability to follow instructions, are critical for a world model to be a reliable policy evaluator, as shown by strong correlations with human-annotated WMES.
> - Effective world models for policy evaluation require transferable physical priors from diverse pretraining data and a balanced training corpus that combines broad physical videos with robot-specific demonstrations.
> - Spatially aligned action control interfaces and persistent multi-scale memory mechanisms are crucial for maintaining long-horizon stability, mitigating viewpoint drift, and preserving object identity during autoregressive rollouts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The evaluation of embodied robot foundation models presents a notable challenge in the field of artificial intelligence. Unlike large language models, which can be efficiently assessed using digital benchmarks, robotic policies typically require real-world rollouts on physical hardware. This process is often time-consuming, expensive, and necessitates continuous human supervision, establishing evaluation as a significant bottleneck in the advancement of robot policy models. This issue is particularly pronounced for emerging robot foundation models such as vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models, where reliable evaluation remains heavily dependent on physical robot execution.

Traditional simulation environments offer a partial solution by reducing cost and enabling repeatable testing. However, their utility is limited by the "sim-to-real gap," alongside substantial overhead involved in constructing scene-specific digital twins. World models have emerged as a potential alternative, leveraging advancements in video generation and action-conditioned modeling to capture visual dynamics and, to some extent, controllable physical evolution. These models could serve as efficient policy evaluators by interacting with robot policies and accurately preserving the relative success or failure of their rollouts, thereby reducing reliance on real-world testing.

While existing literature has demonstrated the feasibility of using world models for evaluation, a systematic understanding of the properties that render a world model reliable for policy assessment remains underdeveloped. This paper addresses this gap by investigating the fundamental design principles and empirical factors that contribute to a world model's efficacy as a robot policy evaluator, aiming to establish principled design rules rather than isolated proof-of-concept demonstrations. It positions itself as a systematic study to move the field towards trustworthy, scalable, and real-robot-aligned world model-based evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in evaluating embodied robot foundation models. The process of validating robotic policies in the real world is resource-intensive, requiring significant human hours, dedicated hardware, and often struggling with consistent reset states across trials. This stands in contrast to the relatively low-overhead evaluation of large language models, which benefit from standardized digital benchmarks. This disparity highlights a critical bottleneck in robotics research: evaluation impedes the rapid iterative development and improvement of robot policy models.

Recognizing the limitations of both real-world rollouts and traditional simulations (due to the sim-to-real gap and high modeling costs), the authors identify world models as a compelling intermediate solution. If world models can accurately simulate robot-environment interactions and preserve the outcomes of robot policies, they could offer a scalable and cost-effective method for policy evaluation. However, the specific characteristics that make a world model a *reliable* policy evaluator are not well understood.

To address this foundational gap, the paper sets out three core objectives:

1. **Systematic Evaluation of Evaluator Quality:** To determine how to systematically assess whether a world model is a proficient policy evaluator. This goes beyond generic video quality metrics, focusing on whether the world model can genuinely predict policy quality in a manner consistent with real-world execution.
2. **Impact of Pretraining and Training Data:** To investigate how different pretraining strategies and the composition of training data influence the quality of a world model when used as a policy evaluator. This involves understanding the balance between general world knowledge and robot-specific controllability.
3. **Influence of Architectural and Algorithmic Design Choices:** To identify which architectural and algorithmic decisions, such as action encoding, memory design, and evaluator-focused post-training, most significantly affect the reliability of a world model in aligning with real-world robot behavior during policy evaluation.

The overarching goal is to transition the field from merely demonstrating the possibility of world-model-based evaluation to establishing principled design guidelines, thereby fostering the development of scalable and trustworthy evaluation pipelines for embodied foundation models. The WMBench benchmark is introduced as a central tool to facilitate controlled comparisons and answer these research questions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic, empirical approach centered around the development and application of WMBench, a novel benchmark for evaluating world models as policy evaluators.

**WMBench Construction:**
WMBench is designed to enable rigorous assessment by providing paired real-world and world-model rollouts.
- **Data Sources:** The benchmark corpus consists of 2,989 paired trajectories across eight manipulation task families (including rigid and deformable manipulation). These trajectories are drawn from two complementary sources: a teleoperated real-world dataset and a policy-rollout dataset generated by GigaBrain checkpoints, ensuring a balance of successes and failures. The train-test split maintains episode-disjointness, diversity, and outcome balance.
- **Large-scale Rollout Dataset:** For deeper analysis, an additional 324,000 world model rollout segments from community submissions (CVPR 2026 GigaBrain Challenge) were curated. These segments were chained into long-horizon episodes (20-30 segments each) and meticulously human-annotated with a World Model as Evaluator Score (WMES).
- **WMES:** A four-level ordinal scale (0-3) is used, ranging from "incorrect outcome & low fidelity" (Score 0) to "accurate outcome & high fidelity" (Score 3), providing fine-grained assessment of outcome prediction and visual quality. Each rollout was independently scored by three annotators with senior review.
- **Training Data Corpus:** The final GigaWorld-1 model is trained on a heterogeneous corpus of approximately 12,980 hours of video, encompassing:
 - Internet and physics videos (∼1,298 hours) for generic physical dynamics.
 - Open-source robot datasets (∼5,377 hours) for diverse embodiments.
 - Egocentric human-hand data (∼2,411 hours) for hand-object interaction.
 - Giga-collected humanoid and dual-arm demonstrations (∼3,894 hours) with calibrated robot trajectories and multi-view observations.
- **Data Curation and Giga DataCrafter:** Raw data undergoes extensive cleaning, including metadata consistency checks, frame decodability, timestamp monotonicity, resolution validity, and removal of corrupted or static clips based on image quality and temporal integrity metrics. Motion and trajectory filters identify physically meaningful dynamics and reject implausible movements using optical flow and action-consistency checks. A distribution filter balances data across source type, embodiment, camera view, task family, and motion intensity. The Giga DataCrafter then converts curated videos into structured supervision: semantic masks (using SAM2), monocular depth (using Depth Anything 3), and fast-slow language captions (using VLMs) for object-level geometry and task semantics.

**Evaluation Protocol (4-step Closed-Loop):**
The protocol mimics real-world policy deployment:
1. **Real-world policy data collection:** Policies are trained on real-robot data, and physical closed-loop rollouts are collected, including initial observation, task instruction, multi-view video, and human-annotated success labels.
2. **World model training and holdout:** World models are trained on the designated training split, with test episodes strictly held out to evaluate generalization.
3. **Closed-loop rollout in world models:** Starting from a held-out test episode's first frame, the policy predicts an action. The world model uses this action and current state to predict future observations, which are fed back to the policy, forming a closed-loop execution.
4. **Metric calculation and outcome assessment:** Generated trajectories are evaluated using both WMES (human/VLM-assisted) and a suite of automatic diagnostic metrics.

**Metric System:**
The system uses both outcome-based (WMES) and diagnostic automatic metrics:
- **Outcome-based:** WMES (0-3 scale, human/VLM annotated) measures alignment with real-world task success.
- **Automatic Diagnostic Metrics:** Organized into three families from WorldArena:
 - **Frame and representation fidelity:** Image Quality, Aesthetic Quality, JEPA Similarity, Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Photometric Consistency.
 - **Geometry, semantics, and interaction:** Geometry Accuracy, Perspectivity, Semantic Alignment, Instruction Following, Interaction Quality, Trajectory Accuracy.
 - **Motion and long-horizon rollout:** Dynamic Degree, Flow Score, Motion Smoothness, PSNR, FID, FVD.
 - Pearson correlation with WMES is used to identify predictive metrics, with 95% confidence intervals from bootstrap resampling.

**GigaWorld-1 Design and Training:**
GigaWorld-1 instantiates the derived design principles:
- **Architecture:** An autoregressive diffusion-transformer, built upon Wan backbones (1.3B and 5B parameters). It reuses frozen VAE and text encoder components while employing trainable LoRA adapters and control pathways.
- **Autoregressive World Generation:** Reformulated as a video continuation problem, learning conditional distributions of future latent windows given historical latents and control conditions.
- **Unified Control Injection:** Uses view-specific control signals projected into a shared latent space. For head camera views, an end-effector (EE) pose map encodes manipulation intent. For wrist camera views, ray maps encode view-dependent camera motion. These are concatenated and injected into the denoising network.
- **Hierarchical History Injection:** Maintains multi-scale memory (short, mid, long range) and a persistent first-frame anchor latent to preserve scene identity and temporal context, mitigating drift.
- **Hierarchical History Guidance Attention:** Processes noisy future context and historical context differently in self-attention, and applies cross-attention only to the noisy window for semantic injection.
- **Relative RoPE:** Uses Relative Rotary Position Embedding for local temporal coordinate systems, preventing temporal position drift during long autoregressive rollouts.
- **Prompt Transition via SLERP:** Employs Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) to smoothly transition between text embeddings, guiding the model through different semantic stages of a task.
- **Progressive Training Pipeline:** A multi-stage curriculum:
 1. **Robot world foundation model:** Initializes from a pretrained video backbone and trains on the multi-source robot corpus using a flow-matching objective.
 2. **Autoregressive world modeling:** Converts the model to an autoregressive generator by integrating AR components, optimizing for denoising future observations conditioned on past context and actions.
 3. **Scene adaptation LoRA (optional):** Fine-tunes low-rank adapters for scene-specific appearance, lighting, and object inventory.
 4. **Few-step distillation:** Accelerates inference through an optional ODE warm-start and mandatory DMD2 distillation, matching teacher distribution, score consistency, and adversarial supervision.
- **System Efficiency Optimization:** Implements custom kernels and distributed strategies (SageAttention, TinyVAE for preview, Ulysses sequence parallelism, Flash normalization, Flash RoPE) to accelerate training and inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several empirical findings across three core questions, culminating in the design and evaluation of GigaWorld-1.

**Question I: How Should Evaluator Quality Be Assessed?**
The analysis of 324,000 manually annotated rollouts established key insights into reliable metric design and evaluation protocols:
- **Finding 1: Visual and geometric fidelity dominate WMES prediction.** Metrics such as Subject Consistency (𝜌 = 0.88), Perspectivity (𝜌 = 0.86), and Instruction Following (𝜌 = 0.84) showed strong positive correlation with WMES. This indicates that preserving recognizable subjects, viewpoint geometry, and semantic task information is crucial for an effective evaluator, more so than merely generating smooth motion.
- **Finding 2: Degenerate metrics mislead evaluator ranking.** Metrics like Background Consistency (𝜌 = −0.45), Photometric Consistency (𝜌 = −0.42), and Interaction Quality (𝜌 = −0.11) exhibited negative or weak correlation. This is attributed to static videos artificially boosting appearance stability metrics, and current VLMs not robustly judging physical realism, indicating that metrics must penalize action-ignorance and accurately assess physical consistency.
- **Finding 3: Evaluator quality must be assessed under long-horizon rollout, not single-step video generation.** Performance degrades significantly over longer horizons for generic video generators (e.g., SVD, LTX, Wan, Cosmos), which suffer from viewpoint drift, object-identity collapse, and texture accumulation. This highlights the importance of evaluating a model's ability to preserve actionable state information under repeated autoregressive feedback.
- **Finding 4: Outcome-centric supervision is essential for scalable VLM evaluation.** Fine-tuning a VLM (Qwen3-VL) with structured, outcome-focused supervision and token-type-aware loss weighting was critical for its reliability.
- **Finding 5: VLM evaluators achieve near-perfect agreement with human ratings.** The VLM-based evaluator achieved 87.80% exact agreement and 99.16% adjacent agreement with human annotations across 5,000+ videos, with low MAE (0.1304) and high Spearman (0.7574) and Kendall 𝜏𝑏 (0.7507) correlations, demonstrating its reliability as a proxy for human judgment.

**Question II: How Do Pretraining and Training Data Matter?**
- **Finding 6: Transferable physical priors matter more than raw scale in pretraining.** Comparing open-source video backbones, Cosmos-Predict2.5 (AVG 0.6123) with robotics/autonomous-driving pretraining was the strongest baseline. Wan 2.2 (5B, AVG 0.5948) surpassed the larger LTX 2.3 (22B, AVG 0.5775). SVD (AVG 0.5569) performed worst, particularly on Trajectory Accuracy (0.0926). This suggests that specific transferable physical priors are more influential than parameter count alone.
- **Finding 7: Broad physical videos provide the best overall trade-off for evaluator training.** Adding `PhysData` (internet and physics videos) to `GigaData` (robot demonstrations) improved the average score from 0.5654 to 0.6144, with significant gains in Photometric Consistency, Image Quality, and Subject Consistency. This indicates that general physical videos reinforce world knowledge necessary for diverse robotic interactions without overly specializing the model.
- **Finding 8: Robot-specific data mainly improves embodiment fidelity, but introduces a sharper trade-off.** Adding `AgiBot` data (other robot embodiments) also improved the overall score to 0.5940, particularly Aesthetic Quality, Photometric Consistency, and Subject Consistency. However, it also led to larger drops in JEPA Similarity and Trajectory Accuracy, suggesting that narrow robot-domain data can over-specialize and reduce broader structural generalization.

**Question III: How Do Model Design Choices Matter?**
- **Finding 9: Action control must be injected through a spatially aligned interface.** Comparing different control interfaces, channel-concatenated control maps achieved the best scores across all paired metrics, including Trajectory Accuracy (0.3528), Dynamic Degree (0.3566), and Subject Consistency (0.8600). This indicates that explicit, spatially aligned action representations (EE pose maps for head view, ray maps for wrist view) are critical for faithful action adherence.
- **Finding 10: Reliable evaluators require persistent memory for long-horizon rollout.** Augmenting the Wan 2.1 backbone with hierarchical history memory substantially improved long-horizon rollout quality across 40 seconds, yielding best PSNR, FID, and FVD. Memory-free models suffered from viewpoint drift and object-identity collapse, showing that persistent memory is necessary for stable and reliable long-horizon evaluation.

**Final Evaluation of GigaWorld-1:**
- **Comparison with Baselines:** GigaWorld-1-Plus (5B) achieved the highest overall average score of 0.6834 across six core evaluator-relevant metrics, outperforming GigaWorld-1-Nano (1.3B, 0.6717), Cosmos-Predict2.5 (0.6123), and Wan 2.2 (0.5948). The gains were concentrated in critical metrics such as JEPA Similarity, Semantic Alignment, and Trajectory Accuracy.
- **Long-Horizon Rollout Quality:** GigaWorld-1 consistently maintained superior PSNR, FID, and FVD over 40 seconds of autoregressive generation compared to baselines, mitigating common failure modes like viewpoint drift and identity collapse.
- **Impact of Memory and Prompt Interpolation:** Qualitative ablations confirmed that the combination of hierarchical history memory and Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) for prompt transitions enabled stable workspaces and smoother semantic evolution during multi-stage tasks.
- **OOD Generalization:** GigaWorld-1 demonstrated the ability to handle out-of-distribution shifts in object appearance, category, scene background, and crucially, accurately simulated both successful and failed policy executions.
- **Closed-Loop Policy Consistency:** GigaWorld-1's generated success rates aligned more closely with real-world success rates across WMBench tasks compared to other challenge models, exhibiting a smaller overall "Gen − Real" deviation and less optimistic bias.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic framework for understanding and developing world models specifically tailored for robot policy evaluation, addressing a critical bottleneck in embodied AI.

**Scientific Significance:**
The paper shifts the focus from merely generating visually plausible videos to preserving policy-relevant causality and action-conditioned evolution over iterative, long-horizon rollouts. It identifies that a world model's efficacy as an evaluator depends on a balanced integration of broad world knowledge, fine-grained action controllability, and persistent temporal consistency, rather than just model scale or data volume. This insight refines the theoretical understanding of what constitutes a reliable learned environment for robotic policy assessment. The formulation of world model as policy evaluator as a "first-class research problem" is a notable contribution.

**Practical Implications:**
- **Accelerated Robot Development:** By providing a robust, scalable, and cost-effective evaluation method, GigaWorld-1 can significantly accelerate the iterative development and refinement of robot foundation models. This reduces the dependence on expensive and slow real-world robot rollouts, enabling faster experimentation and performance tuning.
- **Benchmark Infrastructure:** The introduction of WMBench serves as a standardized, open-source benchmark for the community. It provides paired real-world and world-model rollout data, a structured evaluation protocol, and a comprehensive metric system. This infrastructure facilitates controlled comparisons, promotes reproducible research, and encourages community-driven exploration of diverse evaluator candidates. The VLM-assisted outcome annotation pipeline also offers a scalable approach to evaluation.
- **Principled Design Roadmap:** The empirical findings are distilled into a practical design map for building effective world models for policy evaluation. This roadmap guides researchers and developers on data curation (balancing general and robot-specific data), model architecture (explicit, spatially aligned control, hierarchical memory, relative temporal encoding), and training pipelines (progressive multi-stage learning, distillation).
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** GigaWorld-1 demonstrates improved long-horizon stability, better out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, and more accurate closed-loop policy consistency compared to general-purpose video generation baselines. This suggests that world-model-based evaluation can provide meaningful support for policy iteration in practical settings.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations and outline promising avenues for future work:
- **Benchmark Expansion:** Extending WMBench to cover a wider range of tasks, including mobile manipulation, dexterous in-hand manipulation, and safety-critical autonomy, with more diverse and challenging closed-loop rollout data.
- **Metric Refinement:** Developing more accurate metrics for outcome faithfulness, action-conditioned consistency, and overall evaluator reliability.
- **Architectural Exploration:** Investigating other structured state-space or hybrid 3D approaches, and incorporating richer multimodal training data (e.g., from omni world models like Cosmos-3) to enhance controllability and physical grounding.
- **Model Scaling:** Continuing to scale model parameters as a complementary path to improve capacity, world knowledge retention, and long-horizon robustness, alongside evaluator-oriented design.
- **Advanced Applications:** Extending world models beyond policy evaluation to richer reward modeling, counterfactual evaluation, and certified uncertainty estimation.

In conclusion, this work provides both scientific advancements in understanding learned environments for robotics and practical tools that can accelerate the development of embodied AI, moving towards a future where robot foundation models can be evaluated with efficiency and reliability comparable to their digital counterparts.
