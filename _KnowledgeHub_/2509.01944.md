# AutoDrive-R$^2$: Incentivizing Reasoning and Self-Reflection Capacity for VLA Model in Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoDrive-R² presents a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework for autonomous driving that enhances reasoning and self-reflection capabilities through a novel Chain-of-Thought dataset and physics-grounded reinforcement learning. The system achieves state-of-the-art trajectory planning, demonstrating an average L2 error of 0.19m on nuScenes and strong zero-shot performance on Waymo.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving often produce physically infeasible trajectories or exhibit model collapse when generating direct actions.
- Current VLA approaches employ simplistic reasoning strategies, failing to account for intricate road conditions, dynamic agent interactions, and precise vehicle kinematic constraints in complex scenarios.
- Many end-to-end autonomous driving models lack interpretability, making it difficult to understand the rationale behind specific driving decisions.

## Method

- A two-stage training process: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) using a novel, manually annotated and LLM-synthesized ==Chain-of-Thought== (==CoT==) dataset (nuScenesR²-6K) that includes a four-step reasoning structure and self-reflection.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) is applied in the second stage, utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==) to refine trajectories.
- A multi-dimensional physics-grounded reward framework guides the RL process, incorporating spatial alignment, vehicle dynamics (steering and velocity), and temporal smoothness to ensure physical feasibility and safety of actions.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the nuScenes dataset with the 7B model recording an average L2 error of 0.19m, outperforming leading specialized driving models like EMMA+ (0.29m) with significantly less training data.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on the Waymo dataset, achieving an average L2 error of 0.20m, which represents a **33.3%** reduction compared to EMMA+ (0.30m).
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of all proposed components: the two-stage training, the structured CoT with self-reflection, and each element of the physics-grounded reward framework were shown to be critical for performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, particularly with a four-step logical chain (Observation, Calculation, Logic Synthesis, Self-Reflection), is crucial for building robust, interpretable, and error-resilient decision-making in autonomous driving VLA models.
> - A comprehensive physics-grounded reward function, accounting for spatial, kinematic, and temporal constraints, is essential for ensuring that VLA-generated trajectories are not only accurate but also physically feasible and safe for real-world deployment.
> - Combining initial supervised fine-tuning to establish reasoning with subsequent reinforcement learning for physical plausibility yields superior performance compared to single-stage or less structured training approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving technology has progressed from traditional modular architectures, which separate tasks such as perception, mapping, prediction, and planning, to integrated end-to-end learning paradigms. Early end-to-end systems aimed to simplify system design, enhance robustness, and reduce error accumulation but often lacked contextual reasoning for complex scenarios.

More recently, the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into autonomous driving has leveraged their pre-trained reasoning capabilities to improve decision-making. These VLM-based approaches fine-tune models pre-trained on vast image-text datasets, enabling vehicles to interpret dynamic traffic situations and formulate navigation strategies. Extending beyond VLMs, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models predict specific actions from visual inputs and textual instructions, leading to developments in action tokenizers for generating precise planning trajectories in autonomous vehicles.

Despite these advancements, current VLA approaches in autonomous driving face two primary limitations. First, generated trajectories often lack physical feasibility, leading to outputs that violate vehicle kinematics or real-world constraints. Attempts to mitigate this using intermediate representations can increase model complexity and deviate from end-to-end optimization. Second, existing systems demonstrate inadequate reasoning capabilities for complex driving scenarios, often employing simplistic strategies that overlook intricate road conditions and kinematic constraints, resulting in deviations from real-world requirements.

AutoDrive-R² addresses these limitations by introducing a novel VLA framework designed to enhance both reasoning quality and physical feasibility. It integrates chain-of-thought (CoT) processing and reinforcement learning (RL) to produce more interpretable, coherent, and physically plausible action sequences. By focusing on structured reasoning and physics-grounded optimization, this work positions itself as an advancement in developing inherently trustworthy and robust AI for autonomous driving, bridging the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical constraints. The approach draws inspiration from progress in CoT reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) and RL-based post-training methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the interpretability and coherence of the decision-making process, as well as the plausibility of action sequences, for Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models in autonomous driving systems. This objective is motivated by critical limitations observed in existing VLA approaches that hinder their practical deployment and trustworthiness.

Specifically, the paper identifies two key problems motivating the development of AutoDrive-R²:
1. **Generation of Physically Infeasible Trajectories:** Current VLA models frequently produce planning outputs that are not physically achievable by a real vehicle. This issue arises from methods that directly generate textual commands or waypoints without adequately incorporating vehicle kinematic constraints. While some approaches use intermediate representations (e.g., meta-actions, latent action tokens) to improve feasibility, these often introduce complexity and contradict the principles of end-to-end optimization. The motivation here is to develop a framework that inherently ensures the physical realism of predicted trajectories without compromising architectural simplicity.
2. **Inadequate Reasoning Capabilities for Complex Driving Scenarios:** Many existing VLA systems employ simplistic reasoning strategies that fail to account for the intricacies of complicated road conditions, dynamic traffic, and the vehicle's own kinematic limitations. This can lead to predicted trajectories that significantly diverge from safe and efficient real-world driving behaviors. The motivation is to instill in VLA models a more sophisticated and structured reasoning capacity that can interpret complex contextual information and derive logically sound decisions.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose AutoDrive-R² with the following key objectives:
- To cultivate explicit reasoning and self-reflection capabilities within VLA models for autonomous driving. This aims to make the decision-making process more transparent and verifiable.
- To ensure the physical feasibility and temporal smoothness of generated trajectories by integrating real-world physics and vehicle dynamics into the planning process.
- To achieve state-of-the-art performance in trajectory planning on standard autonomous driving benchmarks while maintaining architectural simplicity and generalization capacity.
- To develop a novel Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset tailored for autonomous driving that provides not only ground-truth actions but also the underlying reasoning and self-reflection steps, thus building a cognitive bridge between input and output.
- To leverage reinforcement learning with a physics-grounded reward framework to refine and optimize trajectory planning, addressing the constraints of spatial alignment, vehicle dynamics, and temporal smoothness.

The motivation is rooted in the belief that effective autonomous driving necessitates a balance between advanced semantic understanding (reasoning) and strict adherence to physical laws (feasibility), which current VLA models have not fully achieved.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoDrive-R² employs a two-stage training process designed to enhance both reasoning and self-reflection capabilities, as well as ensure the physical feasibility of generated trajectories. The model's objective is to predict future Bird’s-Eye View (BEV) trajectory coordinates for the next 3 seconds at 0.5-second intervals, given historical vehicle states and a camera image.

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) with nuScenesR²-6K Dataset**

The first stage focuses on building foundational perception and structured reasoning capabilities. This is achieved through supervised fine-tuning using a newly constructed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset named nuScenesR²-6K.

- **Rationale:** Initial experiments indicated that models trained solely with reinforcement learning exhibited degradation in trajectory planning compared to those pre-trained with SFT, highlighting the need for a "cold start" to establish foundational understanding.
- **nuScenesR²-6K Dataset Construction:**
 - The dataset comprises 6,000 high-fidelity image-trajectory pairs, each enriched with CoT reasoning.
 - It was created using a "generate-then-validate" pipeline. An initial pool of approximately 8,000 image-trajectory pairs was curated from the nuScenes training set.
 - The Qwen2.5-VL-72B model was used to synthesize initial CoT reasoning sequences for these pairs.
 - A closed-source model, Qwen-VL-Max, acted as an "expert validator" to score and review each generated chain, discarding those with factual errors or logical inconsistencies.
- **CoT Prompt Design and Four-Step Logical Chain:** Unlike universal prompts, the CoT prompt in nuScenesR²-6K systematically decomposes trajectory planning into three interdependent reasoning stages, supplemented by a crucial fourth self-reflection step:
 1. **Image-Driven Analysis (Observation):** Establishes foundational scene understanding, including obstacle and lane localization, and traffic sign detection, to anchor subsequent reasoning.
 2. **Physics-based Calculation:** Leverages kinematic equations (e.g., for position, velocity, acceleration, steering angle) to translate abstract observations into quantifiable predictions.
 3. **Contextual Logic Synthesis (Logical Deductions):** Integrates domain-specific knowledge, such as intersection traffic rules, to ensure predictions align with real-world driving regulations and safety.
 4. **Self-Reflection (Reflection):** Inspired by mathematical reasoning frameworks, this phase allows the model to verify the coherence of its reasoning, check assumptions, and correct potential contradictions, thereby enhancing robustness and correctness.
- **Model for SFT:** The Qwen2-VL-7B model is fine-tuned on the nuScenesR²-6K dataset in this stage.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**

The second stage refines trajectory generation to ensure physical feasibility and adapt to diverse driving scenarios. This is achieved using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm within a physics-grounded reward framework.

- **GRPO Algorithm:** GRPO (Jiang et al., 2025) is employed for training. It simplifies traditional RL architectures by using a pairwise comparison mechanism among candidate responses, rather than relying on critic networks to estimate value functions.
 - For a given input, G distinct candidate responses are generated through policy sampling.
 - Each response is evaluated using verifiable reward functions.
 - Advantages for each response are normalized by the group mean and standard deviation.
 - The optimization objective includes a KL-divergence term to regularize the updated policy, ensuring it stays close to the reference policy, balancing exploration and stability.
- **Physics-Grounded Accuracy Reward (r_acc):** This is a multi-dimensional reward framework designed to guide the model in generating safe, feasible, and comfortable driving strategies. It integrates spatial, kinematic, and temporal constraints:
 1. **Spatial Alignment (r_pos):** Measures the mean squared Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth coordinates over N time steps. This ensures global path adherence, penalizing deviations from the intended route.
 2. **Vehicle Dynamics (r_ste, r_vel):** Incorporates real-world physical limitations of the vehicle.
 - `r_ste`: Penalizes deviations in steering angles between predicted and ground-truth values to ensure kinematic feasibility.
 - `r_vel`: Addresses unphysical acceleration/braking by penalizing deviations in predicted velocity from ground-truth values. These terms ensure trajectories are physically realizable and socially acceptable.
 3. **Temporal Smoothness (r_tem):** Penalizes rapid variations in consecutive control signals (steering and velocity) to prevent temporal discontinuities. This ensures coherence, stability, and comfort by minimizing jerk and oscillations.
- **Format Reward (r_format):** A binary reward (1 or 0) that enforces strict adherence to the required output format: `<think>thinking process here</think><answer>(x1, y1)...,(xn, yn)</answer>`.
- **Total Reward:** The total reward for a response is calculated as `r_i = r_i_acc + r_i_format`, where `r_acc` is a weighted sum of its components: `r_acc = λ_pos · r_pos + λ_ste · r_ste + λ_vel · r_vel + λ_tem · r_tem`. In experiments, uniform weights (λ=1) were used.
- **Implementation Details:** The RL pipeline utilized the TRL framework and was trained for 750 iterations and 1 epoch over 18 hours.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AutoDrive-R² demonstrates its performance across standard autonomous driving benchmarks and highlights the contributions of its core components.

**Performance on nuScenes Dataset:**
AutoDrive-R² (7B model) achieved the lowest L2 error and collision rate across all prediction horizons (1s, 2s, 3s).
- **L2 Error (Avg.):** 0.19m, a 34.5% reduction compared to EMMA+ (0.29m), which is trained on a substantially larger dataset (103k scenarios vs. AutoDrive-R²'s 6k curated samples per stage).
- **Collision Rate (Avg.):** 0.07%, demonstrating a lower frequency of collisions compared to competitors.
- Compared to the baseline Qwen2-VL-7B, AutoDrive-R² (7B model) reduced L2 errors by 86.9%.

**Zero-shot Performance on Waymo Dataset:**
The model demonstrated robust generalization capabilities when tested in a zero-shot setting on the Waymo dataset.
- **L2 Error (Avg.):** 0.20m, a 33.3% reduction compared to EMMA+ (0.30m) and a 90.7% reduction compared to the Qwen2-VL-7B baseline (2.13m). This indicates the model's ability to transfer its learned reasoning and planning skills to unseen environments.

**Closed-loop Experiment on NAVSIM Benchmark:**
AutoDrive-R² established new state-of-the-art performance across all metrics in closed-loop simulation:
- **Navigation Completion:** 98.5%
- **Driving Agent Comfort:** 95.9
- **Time-to-Collision:** 95.4
- **Ego-Progress:** 82.7
- **Planner-Diverse Motion Score (PDMS):** 90.3, surpassing the next-best competitor by over 6 points, suggesting superior human-like planning. These results confirm the model's ability to translate predictive accuracy into robust, safe, and efficient real-world driving actions.

**Impact of Model Size:**
Comparisons between the 3B and 7B variants of Qwen2.5-VL within the AutoDrive-R² framework showed:
- The 7B model achieved superior performance with an average L2 error of 0.19m.
- The 3B version (0.49m avg L2) still demonstrated a notable improvement over its Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (4.21m avg L2), indicating the effectiveness of the two-stage framework in compensating for models with limited capacity by enforcing strict trajectory constraints and contextual logic synthesis.

**Qualitative Visualization:**
Visual comparisons in the nuScenes dataset showed that AutoDrive-R² consistently produced more reliable and physically feasible trajectory planning under varying illumination and complex motion patterns, in contrast to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, which sometimes failed to generate accurate predictions, and EMMA+, which exhibited significant trajectory deviations.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Necessity of Two-Stage Training:** A model trained purely on RL (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + RL) underperformed the SFT-trained model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SFT) by 22.2% in average L2 error (0.33m vs 0.27m). This confirmed that SFT is crucial for establishing foundational reasoning before RL optimization.
- **SFT Component Contribution:**
 - Removing the four-step reasoning structure (`w/o. Four.`) increased average L2 error to 0.25m, a 31.5% degradation compared to the full AutoDrive-R² (0.19m).
 - Eliminating self-reflection (`w/o. Self.`) resulted in an average L2 error of 0.23m, a 21.1% decline. This highlights the interdependence and importance of both the structured logical chain and self-reflection.
- **RL Reward Component Contribution:** Each component of the physics-grounded reward framework was found to be critical:
 - Removing spatial alignment (`w/o. r_pos`) led to a significant increase in error to 0.53m.
 - Removing steering angle regulation (`w/o. r_ste`) caused a 10.5% degradation (0.21m).
 - Excluding velocity consistency constraints (`w/o. r_vel`) raised the error to 0.22m.
 - Removing temporal smoothness penalties (`w/o. r_tem`) resulted in a 26.3% increase in error (0.24m). This validates the necessity of each physics-grounded element for reliable planning.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:** Optimal hyperparameters were identified: uniform reward weights (λ=(1,1,1,1)), a KL-divergence coefficient β=0.04, and G=6 generations for GRPO, balancing performance and computational cost.

**"Aha Moment": Reasoning Self-Correction:**
The research identified an emergent "reasoning self-correction moment" where the model systematically detected and resolved contradictions in its initial trajectory planning. Through its four-stage self-reflection, the model could re-examine assumptions, iteratively recalculate, and refine predictions (e.g., recognizing an embankment was not a dead end but roadside topography, leading to a gentle right turn instead of a full stop). This demonstrates an intrinsic explainability and adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AutoDrive-R² contributes to the field of autonomous driving by addressing known limitations in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly concerning the interpretability of decision-making and the physical feasibility of planned trajectories. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** By integrating physics-grounded rewards into the reinforcement learning stage, AutoDrive-R² promotes the generation of trajectories that are not only geometrically accurate but also kinematically feasible and temporally smooth. This directly translates to safer and more reliable operation of autonomous vehicles, reducing the likelihood of unphysical maneuvers or discomfort for occupants.
- **Improved Interpretability and Trust:** The introduction of a four-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process, including a self-reflection mechanism, offers a pathway towards more transparent autonomous systems. By providing a structured thought process (Observation → Calculation → Logic → Reflection), the model's decisions become more understandable and verifiable, which is critical for building public trust and for regulatory compliance in safety-critical applications.
- **Strong Generalization Capacity:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on the Waymo dataset indicates that AutoDrive-R² possesses robust generalization capabilities. This suggests that the model can effectively transfer its learned reasoning and planning skills to diverse and unseen driving environments, reducing the need for extensive retraining on new datasets.
- **Data Efficiency:** Achieving state-of-the-art performance with a relatively smaller curated dataset (nuScenesR²-6K, 6k samples per stage) compared to some competitors (e.g., EMMA+ with 103k scenarios) highlights the efficiency of the proposed two-stage training framework. This can lower the resource requirements for developing high-performing autonomous driving models.
- **Advancement in VLA Research:** The work advances the capabilities of VLA models in autonomous driving by specifically tackling the issues of physically infeasible outputs and inadequate contextual reasoning. The combination of structured SFT and physics-informed RL represents a novel methodological contribution for integrating complex cognitive processes with real-world physical constraints.
- **Foundational for Future Work:** The established framework and insights into reasoning self-correction provide a foundation for future research in autonomous AI. The paper explicitly mentions future efforts focusing on multi-agent coordination and real-time sensor fusion integration, which are complex challenges that could benefit from AutoDrive-R²'s structured reasoning and physical grounding.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The accompanying ethics statement underscores a commitment to responsible AI development, emphasizing privacy (anonymized data), safety, fairness, and transparency. By publicly releasing the dataset, code, and training protocols, the authors encourage independent verification and ethical scrutiny, fostering a collaborative approach to ensure advancements align with societal values.

In summary, AutoDrive-R² offers a significant step towards more intelligent, reliable, and transparent autonomous driving systems by systematically integrating reasoning, self-reflection, and physics-grounded optimization.
