# Co-RL: Unsupervised Reasoning Emerges from Diverse Cohort in Multi-agent RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Co-RL trains multiple decoupled, parameter-sharing-free LLM/VLM agents via RL where each agent's reward comes from a peer's majority-vote pseudo-label, not its own. This cross-agent supervision breaks self-reinforcing bias loops that plague self-rewarding RL, yielding 3.0-8.6% gains on text benchmarks and 2.3-7.2% on multimodal ones without ground-truth labels.

## Problem

- Self-rewarding RL derives reward signals (majority vote, self-certainty, entropy) entirely from a single model's own outputs, with no external reference point.
- Without an independent signal, self-generated rewards amplify a model's existing biases and errors, causing response homogenization and eventual training collapse.
- Prior multi-agent RL methods (debate, CoMAS) need heavy agent interaction or an external LLM judge/learned reward model, adding cost and complexity.

## Method

- **Co-RL**: a cohort of N independently parameterized agents (no shared weights/gradients) each sample K completions per unlabeled prompt and are optimized with ==GRPO== or ==REINFORCE++==.
- ==Cross-agent pseudo-labeling==: each agent's reward is 1 if its answer matches the majority-vote pseudo-label from the next agent in a cyclic ring, never its own votes.
- ==Cohort diversity engineering==: mixing model families/sizes and rephrasing prompts per agent (via DeepSeek-V3 rewrites) to decorrelate errors and strengthen the corrective signal.

## Results

- Across seven text benchmarks, Co-RL improves four LLMs by **3.0-8.6%** on average, beating the strongest self-rewarding baseline (Co-rewarding-II) by **0.8-2.0%**.
- On the CoMAS multi-agent protocol, Co-RL beats CoMAS by **4.0%** average while using only half the agents and no LLM judge (**62.97%** vs **58.94%** average).
- Three-agent Co-RL matches or exceeds ground-truth-reward training on all three heterogeneous LLMs tested, while self-rewarding baselines like TTRL diverge or collapse at longer training horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decorrelated errors across independently trained models supply a genuinely external learning signal, closing the gap co-training theory predicted decades ago for modern RL reasoning.
> - Diversity is the active ingredient, not just model count: different families/sizes/prompt phrasings reduce correlated mistakes more than adding parameters or compute alone.
> - Theoretically, cross-agent supervision enlarges the basin of correct convergence (pA+pB>1 suffices) whereas self-rewarding is self-confirming and collapses whenever initial accuracy is below 50%.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) has been significantly advanced by reinforcement learning (RL). A prominent paradigm, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), relies on ground-truth supervision to provide reward signals. While effective, the acquisition of such ground-truth labels is often costly and becomes increasingly challenging as model capabilities approach or exceed human evaluative capacity. This dependency limits the scalability and applicability of RLVR for enhancing cutting-edge reasoning.

To address the limitations of ground-truth supervision, "self-rewarding RL" methods have emerged. These approaches enable models to generate reward signals from their own outputs, often using internal consistency, self-certainty, predictive entropy, or majority-vote predictions. While reducing reliance on external labels, a core challenge for self-rewarding RL is the potential for reinforcing existing biases and suboptimal behaviors, which can lead to reduced response diversity, homogenization of outputs, and ultimately, training collapse due to the lack of an independent corrective signal. The model's own feedback loop can amplify its errors rather than rectify them.

Another related area involves multi-agent RL systems, where multiple models interact to improve reasoning. Existing multi-agent debate or collaborative frameworks often necessitate external components, such as a separate LLM acting as a judge or a learned reward model, to facilitate effective learning signals. These systems introduce their own complexities and dependencies.

This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the limitations of both supervised and self-rewarding RL, particularly the absence of an independent learning signal in label-free settings. It proposes that such a signal can naturally arise from the decorrelated errors of independently trained models, thereby providing corrective feedback that single-model self-supervision cannot. The research aims to explore a multi-agent framework that does not require ground-truth labels or external judging mechanisms, but instead leverages peer-to-peer supervision to foster the emergence of unsupervised reasoning capabilities, while also addressing the problem of training stability and diversity maintenance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable unsupervised reasoning in language and vision-language models through a cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning framework, termed CO-RL. This objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Addressing the Cost and Scarcity of Ground-Truth Supervision:** Current state-of-the-art reasoning improvements via RL (RLVR) heavily depend on human-annotated, verifiable rewards. Such annotations are expensive to acquire and become increasingly scarce and unreliable as models push the boundaries of reasoning beyond human evaluative capacity. A core motivation is to develop a method that can improve reasoning without this dependency.

- **Mitigating Biases and Training Collapse in Self-Rewarding RL:** While self-rewarding RL attempts to alleviate the need for external labels by generating rewards internally (e.g., from a model's own completions or internal consistency), this approach is prone to amplifying existing biases and suboptimal behaviors. Relying solely on a single model's view can reduce response diversity and lead to "training collapse," where the model converges to homogenized or incorrect solutions due to self-reinforcing feedback loops. The researchers are motivated to find an external, yet still label-free, source of supervision that breaks these loops.

- **Leveraging Decorrelated Errors of Independent Models:** A fundamental insight motivating this work is that independently trained models, particularly those with different architectures or training histories, are likely to make decorrelated errors. This means that an error made by one model might not be replicated by another. The motivation is to harness this discrepancy by allowing models to provide corrective feedback to each other, thus generating a more objective and independent learning signal than self-generated feedback.

- **Developing a Lightweight and Symmetric Multi-Agent Framework:** The authors aim to design a multi-agent RL system that avoids the complexities and external dependencies of prior multi-agent methods, such as those requiring an external LLM judge or a separately learned reward model. The motivation is to create a framework where each agent simultaneously acts as a learner and a source of supervision for its peers, allowing for mutual improvement within a single training run without additional overhead.

- **Investigating the Role of Cohort Diversity:** A secondary but crucial objective is to demonstrate that increasing the diversity within the cohort of agents enhances the effectiveness of peer-supervision. The hypothesis is that heterogeneity in model families, sizes, and even input formulations (e.g., rephrased prompts) reduces correlated errors, thereby strengthening the corrective signal available to each agent. This is motivated by principles from co-training, which suggest that the independence of views is critical for effective mutual learning.

In summary, the key objective is to establish a robust and effective method for unsupervised reasoning improvement that sidesteps the limitations of both supervised and existing self-supervised RL approaches by introducing a novel form of multi-agent, peer-to-peer reinforcement learning, explicitly designed to exploit diversity for stable and high-quality learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CO-RL framework is a label-free multi-agent reinforcement learning method designed to enable unsupervised reasoning by having decoupled models supervise one another using pseudo-rewards derived from their peers.

#### 3.1. Co-Reinforcement Learning (Co-RL) Framework

The core of CO-RL involves a cohort of *N* agents, each with an independently parameterized policy (π_θn). These agents operate without sharing parameters or gradients, with their optimization linked only through the rewards exchanged between them. The training process for each unlabeled reasoning problem *x* proceeds as follows:

1. **Response Generation:** Each agent *n* independently generates a group of *K* completions (y_k^n) by sampling from its policy π_θn(· | x). From each completion, a final answer (a_k^n) is extracted using an answer-extraction function g(·).
2. **Pseudo-Label Construction:** For each agent *n*, a supervision target, called the pseudo-label (â_(-n)(x)), is constructed. This pseudo-label is derived exclusively from the majority-vote answer among the *K* completions generated by a *designated peer* agent. Specifically, agent *n* is supervised by agent *n-1* (with indices cycling, so agent 1 is supervised by agent *N*). This mechanism ensures that an agent's own predictions do not contribute to its supervision signal, breaking self-reinforcing loops.
3. **Reward Assignment:** The *k*-th response of agent *n* (y_k^n) receives a binary reward (r_k^n). A reward of 1 is assigned if its extracted answer (a_k^n) matches the peer-generated pseudo-label (â_(-n)(x)); otherwise, the reward is 0.
4. **Policy Optimization:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed to update each agent's policy. The assigned rewards (r_k^n) are normalized within each agent's own group of *K* rollouts to compute group-relative advantages. These advantages are then used in the GRPO objective function to optimize the policy (θn). All agents are updated simultaneously in each training step. The overall training objective is the average of individual GRPO objectives across all *N* agents.

This decoupled optimization, where policies are updated independently but receive supervision from peers, is central to CO-RL's ability to maintain distinct perspectives and avoid synchronized errors. The framework is designed to be compatible with other sequence-level reward-based policy optimization methods.

#### 3.2. Diverse Cohorts for Unsupervised Reasoning

A critical aspect of CO-RL is the active promotion of diversity among agents, which is hypothesized to reduce correlated errors and strengthen the corrective signal. Three main sources of diversity are utilized:

1. **Decoupled Policy Optimization:** The inherent design of CO-RL ensures independent parameter sets and optimizer states for each agent, with no gradient propagation between them. This architectural decoupling contributes to distinct learning trajectories and less-correlated predictions over time.
2. **Diversity Across Model Families and Sizes:** Agents are initialized from heterogeneous sources, including different pretrained model families (e.g., Qwen2.5, Llama 3, Gemma) and varying model sizes (e.g., 1.7B, 3B, 7B, 8B, 12B). These differences encompass architectural choices, tokenization strategies, pretraining data, and post-training procedures, leading to distinct inductive biases and error profiles. For vision-language models (VLMs), this extends to distinct vision encoders (e.g., InternViT, SigLIP).
3. **Diversity Across Input Formation:** To further decorrelate agent behaviors, one agent might be trained on original prompts while another is trained on semantically equivalent, rephrased versions of the same prompts (e.g., rewritten by DeepSeek-V3). This exposes agents to different linguistic forms of the same problem, mitigating errors induced by specific phrasing.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical characterization comparing CO-RL's learning dynamics with self-rewarding GRPO. The analysis simplifies the problem to two outcomes (correct vs. aggregate incorrect answer) and focuses on the probability of generating the correct answer (*p*).

- **Self-rewarding dynamics:** The theoretical analysis indicates that self-rewarding methods are "self-confirming." If an agent's probability of being correct (*p*) is below 0.5, the self-rewarding mechanism is likely to reinforce the incorrect answer, driving *p* towards 0. Conversely, if *p* > 0.5, it drives *p* towards 1. The equilibrium at *p = 0.5* is unstable.
- **Cross-agent supervision dynamics (CO-RL):** For a two-agent setting, the dynamics show that an agent's update direction is determined by its peer's majority-vote prediction. The key finding is that CO-RL significantly "enlarges the basin of correct convergence." If the sum of the initial probabilities of being correct for two agents (pA(0) + pB(0)) is greater than 1, both agents converge to the correct answer (pA(t), pB(t)) -> (1, 1). If the sum is less than 1, they converge to the incorrect answer (0, 0). This implies that CO-RL can exploit complementary strengths, allowing one agent's correct prediction to correct another's mistake, thereby correcting errors that self-rewarding methods would otherwise reinforce.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CO-RL was conducted across various LLM and VLM architectures, model sizes, and reasoning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Language Models (LLMs)

- **Outperformance of Self-Rewarding Methods:** CO-RL consistently outperformed single-agent self-rewarding baselines, including TTRL, RENT, Intuitor, and Co-Rewarding-II, across seven text-only reasoning benchmarks (mathematical reasoning, coding, knowledge-intensive reasoning).
 - For 3B models, CO-RL (Different family+) achieved average gains of 8.6% for Qwen2.5-3B and 5.2% for Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct over their respective base models. This variant also outperformed the strongest self-rewarding baseline by 2.0% for Qwen2.5-3B and 0.8% for Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct.
 - Similar trends were observed for 7B and 8B models, where CO-RL (Different family+) improved Qwen2.5-7B by 4.6% and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct by 3.0% on average over base models. For Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, CO-RL (Different family+) matched or surpassed ground-truth supervised GRPO (47.7% vs. 47.1% average).
- **Impact of Diversity:** The "Same family" CO-RL setting, training two agents from the same base model, already yielded substantial improvements over base models. However, introducing "Different family" diversity further enhanced performance, with the "Different family+" variant (incorporating data decoupling) generally achieving the strongest label-free results.
- **Comparison with Multi-Agent RL Baselines:** Under the CoMAS multi-agent RL experimental setup, CO-RL (Different family) achieved an average performance of 62.97%, outperforming CoMAS (58.94%) by 4.0%. Notably, CO-RL achieved this while using half the number of agents and without requiring an external LLM judge or learned reward model.
- **Scaling to Three Agents:** In a setup with three heterogeneous agents (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-1.7B), CO-RL consistently improved all base models, with average gains ranging from 6.0% to 8.2%. It matched or outperformed ground-truth supervised training (GT-Reward) in average performance for all three models without relying on ground-truth labels.

#### 4.2. Performance on Vision-Language Models (VLMs)

- **Generalization to Multimodal Reasoning:** CO-RL successfully generalized to multimodal mathematical reasoning, demonstrating consistent improvements for five VLMs ranging from 2B to 12B parameters across four benchmarks (MathVision, MathVerse, MathVista, We-Math). Average gains ranged from 2.3% to 7.2% over base models.
- **Outperformance of Self-Rewarding VLMs:** CO-RL consistently outperformed the multimodal self-rewarding baseline MM-UPT (which uses the TTRL reward formulation) across various VLM pairs and training datasets.
- **Competitive with Supervised Methods:** In several VLM settings, CO-RL's performance approached or surpassed ground-truth supervised training. For instance, on Gemma-3-12B, CO-RL (Different family) achieved 47.56% average accuracy, surpassing GT-Reward (45.17%) without ground-truth labels.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics and Stability

- **Stability:** CO-RL exhibited stable training dynamics across both text and vision-language models. It maintained a non-degenerate reward standard deviation and relatively stable completion lengths. This contrasts with self-rewarding baselines, which often showed reward collapse, length degeneration, or divergence during prolonged training.
- **Complementary Learning:** The observed dynamics indicated that CO-RL agents maintained partial agreement while the accuracy of their exchanged pseudo-labels steadily improved. This suggests that agents did not simply converge to identical behaviors but preserved meaningful differences while providing increasingly reliable supervision to one another, aligning with the theoretical predictions of complementary learning.

#### 4.4. Controlling for Training and Inference Budgets

A matched-budget comparison demonstrated that CO-RL's gains were attributable to the cross-agent supervision mechanism rather than merely deploying multiple models or increased computational budget. When comparing two independently trained TTRL agents (ensembled at inference) against two CO-RL trained agents (ensembled at inference) with equivalent total training and inference resources, CO-RL consistently achieved superior average performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces CO-RL, a novel framework that significantly contributes to the field of reinforcement learning for reasoning in large language and vision-language models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Breakthrough in Unsupervised Reasoning:** CO-RL provides a concrete and empirically validated method for the emergence of unsupervised reasoning capabilities in LLMs and VLMs. By demonstrating that independent learning signals can arise from peer interaction without ground-truth labels or external judges, it addresses a fundamental challenge in scaling advanced reasoning systems.
- **Reduced Dependency on Costly Supervision:** The elimination of reliance on ground-truth supervision offers substantial practical benefits. It can drastically reduce the annotation costs and logistical complexities associated with training advanced reasoning models, particularly as target capabilities surpass human evaluative abilities. This cost-effectiveness could democratize access to advanced RL fine-tuning for a broader range of applications and research.
- **Enhanced Training Stability and Diversity:** By breaking the self-reinforcing feedback loops inherent in single-model self-rewarding RL, CO-RL mitigates issues of training collapse, reduced response diversity, and homogenization. The framework's ability to maintain stable training dynamics and foster behavioral diversity among agents contributes to more robust and generalized model improvements.
- **Theoretical Validation of Complementary Learning:** The theoretical analysis supports the empirical findings, demonstrating that cross-agent supervision expands the basin of correct convergence compared to self-rewarding methods. This theoretical grounding strengthens the understanding of how independent, diverse agents can collectively overcome individual limitations.
- **Broad Applicability Across Modalities and Architectures:** The consistent performance gains observed across diverse LLMs and VLMs, different model sizes, and various reasoning domains (mathematical, coding, knowledge-intensive, multimodal) indicate the generality and robustness of the CO-RL approach. This suggests its applicability to a wide range of current and future AI systems.
- **Catalyst for Future Research in Multi-Agent Learning:** CO-RL opens new avenues for research in multi-agent reinforcement learning, particularly regarding optimal cohort design. Future work can explore the interplay of agent number, diversity characteristics (e.g., specific architectural differences, specialized pre-training), and interaction topologies in shaping the learning dynamics and outcomes. Adaptive supervision mechanisms could further exploit complementary expertise.

In essence, CO-RL represents a step towards making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and scalable, fostering the development of intelligent systems that can learn and improve robustly in environments with limited or no human supervision.
