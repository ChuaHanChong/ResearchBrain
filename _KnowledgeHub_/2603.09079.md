# GST-VLA: Structured Gaussian Spatial Tokens for 3D Depth-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework for Vision-Language-Action models, GST-VLA, introduces structured 3D Gaussian spatial tokens to encode geometric details and a Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought mechanism for explicit 3D reasoning. This approach achieves an 83.1% average success rate on the LIBERO-Pro benchmark, improving precision insertion tasks by 9.2 percentage points over previous methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models commonly process visual observations as 2D patch tokens, lacking intrinsic geometric properties like depth, surface orientation, and confidence crucial for precise robotic manipulation.
- Existing depth augmentation methods often provide pixel-uniform scalar depth values that fail to encode surface orientation or geometric confidence and allocate representational capacity inefficiently across a scene.
- VLA models typically lack explicit intermediate spatial verification steps, making their 3D scene interpretation opaque and challenging to inspect or debug before an action is executed.

## Method

- Developed a Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer (GST) to represent 3D scenes using anisotropic Gaussian primitives that capture position, surface orientation, and geometric confidence, adaptively allocating tokens to relevant regions.
- Introduced a Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT) mechanism that compels the VLM to explicitly generate structured 3D geometric targets (e.g., object centroids, grasp affordances, SE(3) waypoints) before action decoding.
- Utilized a composite training objective and a three-stage protocol to synergistically train the GST, VLM reasoning core, and a flow-matching action expert, ensuring geometric calibration and precise action generation.

## Results

- GST-VLA achieved an 83.1% average success rate on the LIBERO-Pro benchmark, surpassing SpatialVLA (76.8%) and improving precision insertion tasks by 9.2 percentage points over DepthVLA.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced generalization on the SimplerEnv benchmark, achieving an 80.2% average task progress, which is a 5.4 percentage point improvement over DepthVLA.
- Ablation studies confirmed that core components like 3D Fourier Positional Encoding and spatial attention pooling within GST, as well as the SE(3) motion plan in DA-CoT, each contributed meaningfully to the overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding 3D scenes with anisotropic Gaussian primitives, which capture position, surface orientation, and geometric confidence, significantly enhances a model's ability to perform precise manipulation tasks.
> - Explicitly supervising intermediate 3D geometric reasoning (DA-CoT) makes the model's scene interpretation verifiable and interpretable, improving debugging and potentially enabling human-robot collaboration.
> - The accuracy of the explicit 3D reasoning outputs directly correlates with the success rate of downstream robotic tasks, indicating its role as a strong predictor and diagnostic tool for action quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control. Traditional VLA models commonly process visual observations as 2D patch tokens, which inherently lack intrinsic geometric information such as depth, surface orientation, or geometric confidence. This limitation can hinder their performance in manipulation tasks requiring high millimeter-scale geometric accuracy, such as precise object insertion or grasping of thin objects.

Previous attempts to integrate geometric information, such as DepthVLA, augment VLA models with dense monocular depth. However, these approaches typically inject pixel-uniform scalar depth values, which do not inherently encode surface orientation or confidence in the depth estimate. Furthermore, they often lack mechanisms for explicit intermediate spatial verification before action decoding, relying instead on implicit spatial reasoning within the model's hidden states.

GST-VLA addresses these identified limitations by introducing two primary contributions: structured 3D Gaussian spatial tokens and an explicit Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT) reasoning process. It differentiates itself from existing work by:
- **Spatial Representations:** Unlike DepthVLA and SpatialVLA, which use pixel-uniform scalar depth or scalar 3D position offsets, GST-VLA employs anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives. These primitives are parameterized to capture not only position but also surface orientation and geometric confidence, and their allocation is adaptively managed via spatial attention pooling.
- **3D Gaussian Primitives:** While 3D Gaussian Splatting and related methods utilize Gaussian representations for rendering, 3D understanding, or image tokenization, GST-VLA's application of Gaussian primitives is distinct. Here, the Gaussian format is specifically designed as a spatial token for consumption by a VLM for manipulation policy conditioning, leveraging its ability to encode comprehensive geometric information (position, anisotropic extent, and confidence) relevant to robot action.
- **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** Prior Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches in embodied AI, such as ECoT and CogACT, focus on general spatial reasoning or decoupling cognition from action. DA-CoT distinguishes itself by supervising *explicit metric 3D coordinates, grasp contact geometry, and SE(3) motion plan waypoints*, derived directly from demonstration data, as structured intermediate generation targets within the VLM. This provides a verifiable and inspectable pathway for the model's 3D scene interpretation.

By providing richer geometric representations and explicit spatial reasoning, GST-VLA aims to advance the capabilities of VLA models toward more precise, reliable, and interpretable robotic manipulation in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the geometric precision and interpretability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several limitations observed in existing VLA architectures:

- **Lack of Intrinsic Geometric Structure in Visual Tokens:** Current VLA models typically encode visual observations as 2D patch tokens. These tokens capture local appearance statistics but lack inherent geometric properties such as depth, surface normal direction, or geometric confidence. For manipulation tasks that demand sub-centimeter accuracy, like peg insertion, edge grasping, or picking thin objects, models must implicitly derive 3D structure from these 2D features. This implicit recovery can degrade systematically as task precision requirements increase.
- **Inadequacies of Current Depth Augmentation Methods:** While approaches like DepthVLA have integrated depth information, they often do so by adding a stream of pixel-uniform scalar depth values. This method presents three specific structural limitations:
 1. **Uniform Token Budget Allocation:** Scalar depth tokens distribute representational capacity equally across all pixel locations, regardless of their geometric relevance to the task. This can lead to inefficient use of the fixed token budget, dedicating resources to geometrically irrelevant background regions rather than salient objects.
 2. **Absence of Surface Orientation Encoding:** A scalar depth value at a point provides no information about the local tangent plane or surface orientation. Consequently, a flat surface and a sharp edge at the same depth would yield identical scalar depth representations, even though they demand distinct manipulation strategies.
 3. **Lack of Geometric Confidence:** Scalar depth values do not intrinsically convey the reliability or confidence of the depth estimate. Depth measurements can be unreliable on specular, textureless, or highly occluded surfaces, and a model without confidence estimation may treat all depth values equally.
- **Absence of Explicit Spatial Reasoning and Verification:** In many VLA models, the pathway from visual and depth inputs to action generation is entirely implicit and opaque. There is no mechanism for the model to explicitly articulate, verify, or inspect its 3D scene interpretation before committing to an action. This makes debugging difficult and limits the model's ability to recover from misinterpretations.

To address these motivations, the research establishes the following specific objectives:
- **Develop a Structured 3D Spatial Representation:** Create a novel tokenization scheme, the Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer (GST), that converts dense depth and semantic features into a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives. These primitives should encode position, surface orientation (via covariance eigenstructure), and geometric confidence (via learned opacity), and adaptively allocate representational capacity to task-relevant regions.
- **Implement Explicit Depth-Aware Reasoning:** Introduce a supervised intermediate reasoning stage, Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT), within the VLM. This stage aims to force the model to generate explicit 3D geometric targets (e.g., object centroids, grasp affordances, metric relations, SE(3) waypoints) before producing action tokens, making its spatial understanding inspectable and verifiable.
- **Integrate and Optimize:** Combine the GST and DA-CoT within a comprehensive VLA architecture and develop a multi-stage training protocol with a composite loss function to ensure synergistic learning between geometric calibration, spatial reasoning, and action generation.
- **Demonstrate Performance Gains:** Evaluate the proposed GST-VLA on challenging robotic manipulation benchmarks to demonstrate significant improvements in task success, particularly for tasks demanding high geometric precision, and to analyze the contributions of individual components through ablation studies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed GST-VLA pipeline comprises five sequential stages: parallel processing of RGB and depth data by frozen encoders, structured spatial tokenization, VLM reasoning with explicit intermediate thoughts, and action decoding by a flow-matching expert.

**A. Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer (GST)**
The GST is a trainable module designed to convert frozen semantic patch features (F_sem) and frozen affine-invariant metric depth (D_hat) into N_g = 128 structured volumetric spatial tokens. This involves four sequential operations:

1. **Depth Back-Projection to Metric 3D Anchors:** Each pixel (u,v) from the input RGB observation (224x224) is lifted to a camera-frame 3D coordinate (p_uv) using the metric depth D_hat and calibrated camera intrinsics (K). For each of N_p = 256 semantic patches (derived from 16x16 pixel receptive fields), the mean of these back-projected 3D coordinates within the patch's receptive field is computed, yielding N_p metric anchors {p_k}.
2. **Per-Patch Gaussian Parameter Estimation:** A 4-layer MLP (f_θ) maps each semantic patch embedding (F_sem,k) to a 7-dimensional Gaussian parameterization: [μ_k, σ_k, α_hat_k].
 - **Residual Mean (μ_k ∈ R³):** This offset from the back-projected anchor (p_k) forms the primitive centroid c_k = p_k + μ_k. This residual formulation allows the MLP to learn fine geometric refinements beyond the coarse metric localization provided by the depth estimator.
 - **Log-Scale (σ_k ∈ R³):** This parameterizes an axis-aligned anisotropic covariance matrix Σ_k = diag(exp(2σ_k)). The eigenvalues of Σ_k encode the spatial extent of the primitive along each camera-frame axis, allowing it to represent local surface orientation (e.g., small extent perpendicular to flat surfaces, contracted extent for edges).
 - **Pre-activation Opacity Logit (α_hat_k ∈ R):** Instead of deriving opacity solely from the patch feature, a multi-scale pathway aggregates features from three spatial scales using a multi-scale image pyramid (MIP). This multi-scale context informs α_k ∈ (0,1), which represents geometric confidence. Higher texture gradients across scales lead to higher opacity, indicating more reliable depth estimates.
3. **3D Fourier Positional Encoding (PPE):** The metric centroid c_k is encoded using 6-octave 3D sinusoidal features, yielding a 36-dimensional positional code (PPE(c_k)). This 3D Fourier encoding allows the VLM to infer approximate metric distances between tokens based on their positional codes, resolving the conflation of depth variation and lateral displacement present in 2D positional embeddings.
 The raw spatial token for each patch k is formed by concatenating [F_sem,k; PPE(c_k); σ_k; α_k] and projecting it through a learned linear projection W_tok to a dimension d_g = 768.
4. **Spatial Attention Pooling:** The N_p = 256 raw spatial tokens are compressed to N_g = 128 output tokens using a single-layer cross-attention mechanism. N_g learned pooling queries (Q_pool) attend to key and value projections of the raw token set. This mechanism adaptively concentrates representational capacity on geometrically salient regions (e.g., object surfaces) rather than distributing it uniformly across all raw tokens. The full raw token set T_raw is retained for DA-CoT cross-attention.
5. **Differentiable Depth Rendering as Geometric Regularizer:** To ensure geometric consistency, a differentiable rendering loss (L_depth) is applied. This loss minimizes the difference between depth rendered from the predicted Gaussian field and the target metric depth. It provides gradients to geometrically calibrate the GST parameters, preventing degenerate solutions where Gaussian parameters might be useful for action prediction but inconsistent with the true scene geometry.

**B. VLM Reasoning Core and DA-CoT**

1. **Spatial Token Injection:** The pooled spatial tokens (Z_spatial) are projected into the VLM hidden dimension (d_VLM) via a two-layer cross-attention projector (Ψ) with a residual connection. This step re-grounds each spatial token in the full semantic feature space, fusing geometric localization with object identity. The VLM input sequence (X) consists of projected spatial tokens (Z_tilde), language embeddings (L_tokens), and proprioceptive embeddings (e_s). LoRA (r=16, α=32) adapters are used for VLM adaptation.
2. **Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT):** This supervised intermediate generation stage separates 3D scene interpretation from action generation. During DA-CoT token generation, an additional cross-attention sublayer is inserted at every VLM transformer block. This sublayer provides unfiltered access to the *full N_p=256 raw Gaussian token set (T_raw)*, allowing the model to query specific geometric regions at full resolution, which is crucial for fine-grained geometric tasks.
 The DA-CoT output is a structured chain C = (c_1, c_2, c_3, c_4), generated autoregressively before action-conditioning tokens. The four components are causally dependent:
 - **c_1: 3D object grounding:** Generation of the metric centroid of the task-relevant object in camera coordinates.
 - **c_2: Grasp affordance:** Generation of a 3D contact point offset relative to c_1 and the approach surface normal direction.
 - **c_3: Metric spatial relations:** Generation of signed metric distances between task-relevant objects and surfaces.
 - **c_4: SE(3) motion plan:** Generation of a coarse sequence of 6-DoF end-effector waypoints (pre-grasp, grasp, post-grasp retract) as deltas in camera frame.
 The sequential ordering ensures that action tokens are generated in a context that includes these explicit 3D reasoning outputs.

**C. Flow-Matching Action Expert**
The action expert is a 300M-parameter transformer. Each layer includes a self-attention block, two cross-attention blocks (one attending to VLM hidden states H_vlm, another to DA-CoT action tokens L_action), and a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) feedforward block (8 experts per layer, top-2 routing). This dual cross-attention conditioning allows the action expert to leverage both the VLM's fused semantic-visual representation and the distilled 3D geometric reasoning from DA-CoT. The action distribution is modeled via conditional flow matching, predicting 7-DoF delta action chunks (6-DoF end-effector plus 1-DoF gripper) for future poses. The MoE structure enables expert specialization across different action phases (e.g., precision reach, grasp closure).

**D. Composite Training Objective**
The overall training loss is a composite of three terms: L = L_flow + λ_CoT * L_CoT + λ_depth * L_depth, with λ_CoT = 0.5 and λ_depth = 0.1.
- **L_CoT:** Token-level cross-entropy loss for DA-CoT generation. This loss provides gradients through the VLM parameters and, critically, back-propagates through the cross-attention in Eq. (8) to adjust the GST parameters, thus geometrically supervising the Gaussian field.
- **L_depth:** Scale-invariant logarithmic loss between the rendered depth from the Gaussian field and the target metric depth, geometrically calibrating the GST parameters.

**E. Three-Stage Training Protocol**
A three-stage training protocol is employed to address ordering constraints:
1. **Stage 1 (S1): GST and action expert pretraining:** GST and action expert are trained with frozen encoders and VLM. L_depth supervision uses diverse indoor scene datasets (ScanNet, Hypersim, ARKitScenes), and L_flow supervision uses robot demonstration data without CoT. This stage establishes a geometrically calibrated Gaussian field.
2. **Stage 2 (S2): LoRA adaptation with DA-CoT supervision:** LoRA adapters are introduced on the cross-attention projector and VLM self-attention projections. The action expert is fully fine-tuned. All three loss terms (L_flow, L_CoT, L_depth) are active, utilizing offline-generated DA-CoT ground truth annotations.
3. **Stage 3 (S3): Full fine-tuning:** All non-frozen parameters are jointly refined at a reduced learning rate to achieve cross-modal alignment between GST, VLM CoT generation, and action expert conditioning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GST-VLA was conducted across three robotic manipulation benchmarks: LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO, and SimplerEnv, comparing against prominent baselines and ablation variants.

**A. Main Results**
- **LIBERO-Pro Benchmark (Table I):** GST-VLA achieved an average success rate of 83.1%, demonstrating improvements over existing methods such as SpatialVLA (76.8%), CogACT (68.7%), π0VLA (64.3%), and OpenVLA (52.3%). The gains were particularly pronounced in tasks requiring high geometric precision:
 - Precision insertion: +9.2 percentage points (pp) over DepthVLA.
 - Thin object grasping: +8.3 pp over DepthVLA.
 - Pick-and-place: +2.0 pp gain.
 Ablation variants showed that GST tokens alone (GST-VLA†, 79.2%) improved over dense depth, and DA-CoT without structured Gaussian tokens (GST-VLA‡, 76.3%) yielded limited benefits, indicating that the full model's performance exceeds the sum of individual components due to synergy.
- **LIBERO Benchmark (Table II):** GST-VLA achieved an average task success rate of 96.4%. The largest per-suite gain was observed on LIBERO-Long (+3.1 pp vs. DepthVLA), suggesting that the SE(3) waypoint supervision (c4) improves trajectory-level geometric coherence for multi-step tasks.
- **SimplerEnv Benchmark (Table III):** GST-VLA achieved an average task progress of 80.2%, an improvement of +5.4 pp over DepthVLA (74.8%). The Close-Drawer task showed the largest gain (+5.9 pp). The performance on SimplerEnv, which involves visual domain shifts, suggests that 3D metric Gaussian tokens, being decoupled from pixel-space appearance, provide more stable representations compared to pixel-aligned depth tokens.

**B. Ablation Studies**
- **GST Component Ablations (Table IV):**
 - Replacing 3D Fourier PE with learned 2D PE resulted in the largest drop within GST, -2.8 pp, highlighting the importance of metric distance encoding.
 - Replacing spatial attention pooling with uniform average pooling cost -2.1 pp, indicating the value of adaptive token allocation.
 - Fixing opacity (α_k = 1) cost -1.5 pp, confirming the contribution of geometric confidence.
 - Fixing residual mean (μ_k = 0) cost -1.9 pp, demonstrating the importance of sub-patch refinement.
 - Reducing token budget from N_g=128 to 64 cost -3.3 pp, while increasing to 256 yielded only +0.4 pp, suggesting N_g=128 as an efficient trade-off.
- **DA-CoT Component Ablations (Table V):**
 - Removing the SE(3) motion plan (c4) had the largest individual effect, -2.3 pp, emphasizing the direct constraint this prior provides to the action expert.
 - Removing 3D object grounding (c1) resulted in a -1.9 pp drop, as c1 anchors subsequent reasoning.
 - Removing all DA-CoT (L_CoT = 0) led to a -3.9 pp decrease.
- **Training Protocol and Expert Conditioning (Table VI):**
 - Ablating Stage 1 pretraining (S2+S3 only) resulted in the largest single performance drop across all ablations, -6.2 pp, confirming the necessity of geometrically calibrated Gaussian tokens before VLM reasoning.
 - Removing L_action conditioning from the action expert cost -3.1 pp, indicating that DA-CoT provides distinct geometric information not redundant with VLM hidden states.
 - Using a dense FFN instead of MoE in the action expert cost -1.7 pp, validating the benefit of expert specialization.
- **Gaussian Tokens vs. Alternative 3D Representations (Table VII):** At a fixed token budget (N_g=128), full Gaussian tokens consistently outperformed alternatives:
 - Dense depth scalars (DepthVLA-style): -4.5 pp.
 - Surface normal tokens: -3.0 pp.
 - Point cloud tokens (position only): -2.4 pp.
 - Gaussian without anisotropy: -1.6 pp (isolating orientation contribution).
 - Gaussian without opacity: -1.5 pp (isolating confidence contribution).

**C. Analysis**
- **DA-CoT Reasoning Accuracy:** On held-out demonstrations, GST-VLA achieved median 3D centroid error of 2.3 cm (c1), grasp contact point error of 1.8 cm (c2), and SE(3) waypoint position error of 3.1 cm (c4). A Pearson correlation of 0.71 between composite CoT accuracy and task success rate indicated that DA-CoT quality is a predictor of downstream action quality.
- **Gaussian Token Spatial Distribution:** Visualizations confirmed adaptive allocation, with 60-70% of pooled tokens concentrating on object surfaces and grasp-relevant edges. Opacity values were appropriately low (< 0.05) on specular or textureless surfaces.
- **Inference Latency:** The full pipeline operated at 6.2 Hz on a single A100-80GB, with DA-CoT generation being the primary source of overhead (38 ms out of 72 ms relative to DepthVLA), which is deemed acceptable for the control frequency.
- **Failure Cases:** Performance degradation was observed on highly reflective surfaces and heavily occluded targets. This was primarily attributed to unreliable metric depth estimates from the frozen encoder, which leads to inaccurate back-projected anchors. While the opacity mechanism partially mitigated this, it failed when the target itself was reflective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces advancements in robotic manipulation by addressing fundamental limitations in how Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models perceive and reason about 3D geometry.

The **Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer (GST)** represents a significant step forward in structured 3D perception for VLA models. By generating anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives, the model gains access to rich geometric information, including surface orientation (via covariance) and geometric confidence (via learned opacity), which is not available in scalar depth or 2D patch representations. The adaptive spatial attention pooling mechanism ensures that the model's representational capacity is efficiently concentrated on task-relevant geometry, optimizing resource allocation. This approach has the potential to enhance the robustness and precision of robot perception, particularly in environments with varied textures and surface properties.

The **Depth-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT)** reasoning mechanism contributes to improving the interpretability and inspectability of VLA models. By compelling the model to generate explicit 3D spatial verbalizations as intermediate targets (such as 3D object grounding, grasp affordance, metric spatial relations, and SE(3) motion plans), the research moves beyond implicit reasoning. This explicit reasoning pathway not only allows for better supervision and debugging but could also enable human operators to verify the model's understanding of a scene before task execution, thereby increasing trust and reliability in human-robot collaboration. The demonstrated correlation between DA-CoT accuracy and task success suggests that these explicit outputs could serve as valuable runtime diagnostics or confidence metrics for robotic systems.

The **composite training objective and three-stage training protocol** demonstrate an effective strategy for integrating these complex components, ensuring that geometric calibration, spatial reasoning, and action generation are synergistically optimized. This methodological contribution provides a template for developing robust end-to-end VLA systems.

The **empirical results** across multiple benchmarks, particularly the significant gains in precision-demanding tasks such as insertion and thin object grasping, confirm the practical utility of GST-VLA. The improved generalization under visual domain shifts in SimplerEnv highlights the benefit of using 3D metric-space tokens, which are less susceptible to appearance variations than pixel-aligned representations.

Collectively, GST-VLA's contributions have the potential to impact the broader field of embodied AI by providing a more geometrically aware and interpretable framework for robotic control. This could lead to the development of more capable, adaptable, and reliable autonomous agents that can perform complex manipulation tasks with higher precision and safety in diverse real-world environments. Future research may focus on mitigating failure cases on highly reflective or occluded surfaces by improving the robustness of initial depth estimation, further enhancing the model's applicability in challenging scenarios.
