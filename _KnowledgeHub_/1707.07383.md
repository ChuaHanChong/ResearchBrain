# Continuous-Time Gaussian Process Motion Planning via Probabilistic Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> GPMP represents continuous-time trajectories as samples from a structured Gaussian process with an exactly sparse Gauss-Markov prior, letting O(1) GP interpolation evaluate collision cost densely while optimizing few support states. GPMP2 recasts planning as MAP inference on a factor graph solved by sparse nonlinear least squares, and iGPMP2 replans incrementally via Bayes trees; GPMP2 plans in about 0.11 s, several times faster than CHOMP, TrajOpt and RRT-Connect.

## Problem

- Sampling-based planners like PRM and RRT find feasible paths but yield jerky redundant motion needing post-processing, and their optimal variants are computationally inefficient in high dimensions.
- Trajectory optimizers such as CHOMP and STOMP need fine time discretization to reason about thin obstacles and tight constraints, making every iteration costly and only locally optimal.
- Sparse discrete-time formulations like TrajOpt use few states but their output may need post-processing for execution and can lose collision-free guarantees between waypoints.

## Method

- **GPMP**: continuous-time trajectory as a vector-valued GP with an LTV-SDE Gauss-Markov prior whose precision matrix is exactly sparse and tridiagonal, optimized by gradient descent over a velocity- and acceleration-augmented state.
- Fast O(1) GP interpolation up-samples sparse support states through an aggregation matrix, evaluates obstacle cost and gradients densely, then projects them back so only support states are updated.
- **GPMP2** performs MAP inference on a factor graph of GP prior factors plus obstacle likelihood factors via sparse nonlinear least squares (Levenberg-Marquardt); **iGPMP2** adds iSAM2 Bayes-tree incremental replanning.

## Results

- On 24 WAM problems GPMP2-intp reaches **91.7%** success at **0.121 s** average versus CHOMP **75%** at **0.695 s** and RRT-Connect **91.7%** at **1.87 s**; interpolation cuts runtime **30-50%**.
- On 198 harder PR2 problems GPMP2-intp achieves **79.3%** success at **0.11 s**, roughly **30x** faster than RRT-Connect (**82.3%**, 3.02 s) and **9x** faster than TrajOpt-61 (**68.7%**, 0.958 s).
- Replanning: iGPMP2 averages **8.07 ms** versus GPMP2's **65.68 ms** on 72 WAM problems at equal **100%** success; on PR2 **6.17** versus **27.30 ms**, success dropping **88.9%** to **66.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured sparse GP priors decouple trajectory parameterization from cost-evaluation resolution, so few states can be optimized while collision cost is still checked densely.
> - Framing planning as probabilistic inference lets SLAM machinery, sparse least squares and incremental Bayes trees, transfer directly, giving quadratic convergence and near-free replanning.
> - Incremental reuse trades accuracy for speed: warm-starting from a stale solution degrades success when the new goal moves far, 81.5% versus 51.9%.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Motion planning is a fundamental area in robotics focused on generating trajectories for robots to achieve desired tasks while adhering to feasibility and optimality criteria. Feasibility typically involves respecting physical constraints like avoiding obstacles and joint limits, while optimality often relates to trajectory quality, such as smoothness (minimizing velocity or acceleration).

Existing motion planning approaches are broadly categorized into sampling-based algorithms and trajectory optimization algorithms.
- **Sampling-based algorithms** (e.g., Probabilistic Roadmaps - PRMs, Rapidly-exploring Random Trees - RRTs) are effective at finding feasible solutions in high-dimensional and constrained environments. However, the resulting trajectories often lack smoothness and optimality, requiring computationally intensive post-processing. While optimal sampling-based planners exist, they can be inefficient for complex problems.
- **Trajectory optimization algorithms** (e.g., CHOMP, STOMP, TrajOpt) formulate planning as minimizing an objective function that balances feasibility and optimality. These methods typically start with an initial trajectory and iteratively refine it. A common challenge is the need for fine trajectory discretization to accurately model costs from thin obstacles or tight constraints, which can lead to high computational costs. Additionally, these methods are locally optimal, potentially requiring multiple restarts with different initial conditions to find a feasible solution. Some approaches, like TrajOpt, attempt to mitigate discretization issues by parameterizing trajectories with fewer states and using continuous-time collision checking, but post-processing may still be necessary, potentially compromising collision-free guarantees.

A continuous-time trajectory representation is identified as a potential solution to some of these challenges, offering more efficient and smoother trajectories. Gaussian Processes (GPs) have been used in various robotic applications for function approximation, modeling, and estimation, but their application to motion planning has not been extensively explored in prior work. Furthermore, the concept of viewing motion planning as probabilistic inference has been investigated, often using message-passing algorithms. This paper introduces GP-based continuous-time trajectory representation into motion planning and combines it with a structure-exploiting probabilistic inference framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a novel and efficient framework for continuous-time motion planning that addresses the limitations of existing sampling-based and trajectory optimization algorithms. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Introduce a continuous-time trajectory representation:** By modeling trajectories as samples from a Gaussian Process (GP), the authors seek to inherently encode optimality (smoothness) through a prior distribution, enabling sparse parameterization while allowing for querying the trajectory at any arbitrary time with computational efficiency. This aims to overcome the computational burden and potential loss of collision-free guarantees associated with discrete-time representations and post-processing in trajectory optimization.
2. **Formulate motion planning as probabilistic inference:** The paper aims to reframe motion planning as a Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) inference problem on a factor graph, exploiting the duality between inference and optimization. This formulation allows for the use of efficient, structure-exploiting numerical optimization tools, similar to those developed for large-scale Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) problems, to achieve faster convergence and improved computational efficiency compared to traditional gradient-based trajectory optimization.
3. **Develop an efficient gradient-based optimization algorithm (GPMP):** The first part of the work focuses on a gradient-based optimization algorithm that leverages GP representation and interpolation to maintain smoothness and efficiently handle obstacle costs. This serves as a foundational step towards the more advanced probabilistic inference approach.
4. **Develop a highly efficient algorithm based on probabilistic inference (GPMP2):** The core contribution is to introduce GPMP2, which integrates the GP trajectory representation with fast, sparse, least-squares optimization on factor graphs. This is motivated by the potential for quadratic convergence rates and efficient handling of high-dimensional problems.
5. **Enable fast and efficient replanning (iGPMP2):** The authors aim to extend the GPMP2 framework to handle dynamic environments and changing conditions by introducing iGPMP2, an incremental algorithm that can efficiently update previously computed plans without recomputing from scratch. This leverages incremental inference techniques from the SLAM community.
6. **Benchmark performance against state-of-the-art methods:** A crucial motivation is to empirically demonstrate the computational advantages and robustness of the proposed algorithms (GPMP, GPMP2, iGPMP2) against established sampling-based and trajectory optimization techniques across various robot platforms and environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is divided into three main components: GPMP (a gradient-based optimizer), GPMP2 (probabilistic inference via factor graphs), and iGPMP2 (incremental replanning).

#### 3.1. Gaussian Process Motion Planner (GPMP)

GPMP frames motion planning as trajectory optimization, minimizing an objective functional $F[\theta(t)]$ subject to constraints.
- **Continuous-time trajectory representation:** Trajectories $\theta(t)$ are treated as samples from a vector-valued Gaussian Process $GP(\mu(t), K(t, t'))$. This inherently defines a prior on trajectories, $p(\theta) \propto \exp\{-\frac{1}{2} \|\theta - \mu\|^2_K\}$, where $\|\cdot\|^2_K$ is the Mahalanobis distance. This prior encourages smoothness based on the kernel $K$.
- **Structured GP Prior:** A Gauss-Markov model is employed, derived from a linear time-varying stochastic differential equation (LTV-SDE). This specific construction yields a GP with an exactly sparse, tridiagonal precision matrix ($K^{-1}$), which can be factored as $K^{-1} = B^T Q^{-1} B$. This sparsity is crucial for efficient computations. A constant-acceleration (jerk-minimizing) prior is used for robot manipulators, defining the state as configuration position, velocity, and acceleration.
- **GP Interpolation:** A key feature is the ability to parameterize a continuous-time trajectory using a sparse set of support states $\theta$, then efficiently query (interpolate) the trajectory at any time $\tau$ between these support states in $O(1)$ time. This allows for dense collision checking between sparse support states without increasing the dimensionality of the optimization problem.
- **Objective Functional:** The objective functional is defined as $F[\theta(t)] = F_{obs}[\theta(t)] + \lambda F_{gp}[\theta(t)]$, where $F_{gp}$ is the GP prior cost and $F_{obs}$ is the obstacle cost, calculated as a line integral of workspace obstacle cost over body points.
- **Gradient-based Optimization:** A gradient-based iterative approach is used, where the trajectory is updated using $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \frac{1}{\eta} K (\lambda K^{-1}(\theta - \mu) + M^T g_{up})$. Here, $M^T g_{up}$ projects the obstacle gradients ($g_{up}$) computed on the densely interpolated trajectory back onto the sparse support states. This allows rich collision information to influence the sparse parameterization efficiently.

#### 3.2. Motion Planning as Probabilistic Inference (GPMP2)

GPMP2 re-conceptualizes motion planning as finding the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) trajectory $\theta^*$ given desired events $e$ (e.g., collision-free).
- **MAP Inference Formulation:** The optimal trajectory $\theta^*$ is found by maximizing the posterior $p(\theta|e) \propto p(\theta)p(e|\theta)$, where $p(\theta)$ is the GP prior (same as GPMP, but using a constant-velocity prior for computational efficiency) and $p(e|\theta)$ is the collision-free likelihood.
- **Collision-free Likelihood:** The likelihood is defined in the exponential family as $l(\theta; e) = \exp\{-\frac{1}{2} \|h(\theta)\|^2_{\Sigma_{obs}}\}$, where $h(\theta)$ is a vector-valued obstacle cost function for the entire trajectory, and $\Sigma_{obs}$ is a diagonal hyperparameter matrix.
- **Factor Graph Formulation:** Due to the Gauss-Markov model, the posterior $p(\theta|e)$ can be factored and represented as a factor graph. The factors include:
 - **Prior Factors:** On start and goal states ($f^p_0(\theta_0)$, $f^p_N(\theta_N)$).
 - **GP Prior Factors:** Connecting consecutive support states ($f^{gp}_i(\theta_i, \theta_{i+1})$), derived directly from the sparse precision matrix.
 - **Obstacle Factors:** On individual support states ($f^{obs}_i(\theta_i)$).
 - **Interpolated Obstacle Factors:** For states interpolated between support states ($f^{intp}_{\tau_j}(\theta_i, \theta_{i+1})$), leveraging GP interpolation (Equation 17) to relate the interpolated state's cost to its adjacent support states.
- **Nonlinear Least Squares Optimization:** Maximizing the posterior is equivalent to minimizing its negative log, resulting in a nonlinear least squares problem: $\min_{\theta} \{\frac{1}{2} \|\theta - \mu\|^2_K + \frac{1}{2} \|h(\theta)\|^2_{\Sigma_{obs}}\}$. This is solved iteratively using Gauss-Newton or Levenberg-Marquardt.
- **Sparsity Exploitation:** The linear system solved in each iteration is $(K^{-1} + H^T \Sigma^{-1}_{obs} H)\delta\theta^* = \dots$. Both $K^{-1}$ and $H^T \Sigma^{-1}_{obs} H$ are proven to be block-tridiagonal (or block-banded), allowing for efficient solution via sparse Cholesky decomposition. The robot is represented by a set of spheres for collision checking against a precomputed Signed Distance Field (SDF), and a hinge loss function is used for obstacle cost. Joint and velocity limits are handled as "soft" inequality constraints using hinge loss factors.

#### 3.3. Incremental Inference for Fast Replanning (iGPMP2)

iGPMP2 extends GPMP2 to efficiently handle replanning by leveraging the Bayes tree data structure, common in SLAM.
- **Bayes Tree:** A Bayes tree is a directed graphical model derived from a Bayes net, representing the conditional dependencies in a factored probabilistic density. For planning problems, variable elimination yields a chain-like Bayes net, which corresponds to a simple chain-like Bayes tree.
- **Incremental Updates:** When conditions change (e.g., new goal, updated state, new environmental obstacles), new or replaced factors are added to the factor graph. The iSAM2 algorithm (which uses a Bayes tree) is then employed to incrementally update the solution. This involves only re-factorizing the parts of the Bayes tree (cliques) that are affected by the changes, avoiding redundant computations for unchanged parts of the trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The algorithms were evaluated on two datasets: a 7-DOF WAM arm (24 problems) and a PR2’s 7-DOF right arm (198 problems). Benchmarks were conducted against sampling-based (RRT-Connect, LBKPIECE) and trajectory optimization algorithms (TrajOpt, CHOMP).

#### 4.1. Batch Planning Benchmark

- **GPMP2 Performance:** GPMP2 consistently demonstrated superior performance.
 - **Speed:** GPMP2-intp (with interpolation during optimization) was found to be several times faster than competing algorithms. On the WAM dataset, it achieved an average time of 0.121s with 91.7% success, while TrajOpt-11 was faster (0.027s) but had a significantly lower success rate (20.8%). On the PR2 dataset, GPMP2-intp was the fastest (0.11s average time) with a 79.3% success rate, outperforming TrajOpt-11 (0.191s, 77.8% success) and being approximately 30 times faster than RRT-Connect (3.02s, 82.3% success).
 - **Success Rate:** GPMP2-no-intp achieved 100% success on the WAM dataset, and GPMP2-intp had high success rates (91.7% WAM, 79.3% PR2), comparable to or exceeding other top performers, except RRT-Connect on PR2.
 - **Interpolation Benefit:** Using interpolation (GPMP2-intp vs. GPMP2-no-intp) resulted in a 30-50% speedup in average and maximum runtime, highlighting the efficiency gains from sparse parameterization with dense collision checking.
- **Comparison Breakdown (Figure 13, Table 2):**
 - GPMP, while using a similar framework to CHOMP, was more computationally expensive per iteration due to Hessian computations but achieved convergence in fewer iterations due to the GP representation and augmented trajectory gradients.
 - GPMP2 benefited from the quadratic convergence rate of nonlinear least squares and the sparse problem structure, leading to significantly faster "Solution update" times compared to GPMP and CHOMP, which rely on slower gradient-based updates. GP interpolation further reduced the per-iteration runtime.

#### 4.2. Incremental Planning Benchmark

- **iGPMP2 Performance:** iGPMP2 was benchmarked against GPMP2 on replanning problems where the goal configuration changed mid-trajectory.
 - **Speed-up:** iGPMP2 provided an order of magnitude speed-up over GPMP2 re-planning from scratch. On the WAM dataset, iGPMP2 had an average time of 8.07ms (vs. 65.68ms for GPMP2), and on the PR2 dataset, 6.17ms (vs. 27.30ms).
 - **Success Rate Trade-off:** iGPMP2's success rate was lower than GPMP2's (e.g., 66.7% vs. 88.9% on PR2). This was attributed to using the old trajectory as initialization (potentially poor for large goal changes), linearization errors due to iSAM2's incremental updates, and the lack of step damping similar to Levenberg-Marquardt.
 - **Sensitivity to Goal Changes:** iGPMP2's success rate was higher when the difference between the original and new goal configurations was smaller, suggesting that its performance is optimized for minimal changes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for continuous-time motion planning that leverages Gaussian Processes and probabilistic inference on factor graphs, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Efficient Continuous-Time Trajectories:** By representing trajectories as GPs, the framework naturally encourages smoothness through the prior and allows for sparse parameterization. The ability to perform fast GP interpolation enables dense collision checking at a high resolution without increasing the optimization problem's dimensionality, overcoming a key challenge in traditional trajectory optimization. This results in smooth, collision-free trajectories that can be directly executed by robots without further post-processing.
- **Accelerated Planning via Probabilistic Inference:** Formulating motion planning as a Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) inference problem on factor graphs allows the use of efficient, sparse, nonlinear least squares solvers. This approach exploits the underlying structure of the problem, leading to quadratic convergence rates (compared to linear for gradient-based methods like CHOMP/GPMP) and making GPMP2 several times faster than state-of-the-art algorithms while maintaining robustness.
- **Fast Incremental Replanning:** The iGPMP2 algorithm, which builds upon incremental inference techniques (Bayes trees from SLAM), enables replanning in dynamic environments orders of magnitude faster than recomputing a plan from scratch. This capability is critical for real-time robotic applications where environmental conditions or task goals can change unexpectedly.
- **Unified Framework for Robotics Problems:** The probabilistic inference perspective provides a unifying framework that could bridge motion planning with other robotics domains like SLAM, control, and learning from demonstration. This foundational approach may facilitate the development of more integrated and intelligent robotic systems.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of `gpmp2` as an open-source C++ library and a ROS interface (`PIPER`) facilitates further research and practical application of these methods in the robotics community.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The primary limitations include the lack of global optimality guarantees inherent in iterative, local optimization methods. While GPMP-GRAPH explores overcoming this by simultaneous exploration of multiple initializations, single-shot GPMP2 is still susceptible to local minima. The framework is also limited in its ability to handle hard nonlinear inequality constraints directly, which is a strength of Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) methods like TrajOpt. Integrating SQP into the trajectory optimizer is suggested as future work.
