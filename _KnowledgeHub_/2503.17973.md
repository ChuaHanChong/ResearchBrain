# PhysTwin: Physics-Informed Reconstruction and Simulation of Deformable Objects from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework combining physics-based simulation with visual reconstruction creates interactive digital twins of deformable objects from videos, enabling real-time simulation and robotic manipulation through spring-mass models, generative shape priors, and Gaussian splatting for appearance modeling.

## Problem

- Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods lack physical realism and cannot simulate unseen interactions
- Current physics-based simulation approaches require extensive training data or manual parameter tuning

## Method

![[2503.17973-method.png]]
*PhysTwin architecture. From sparse observations, the system reconstructs a physics-informed representation with topology, geometry, physical properties, and visual appearance.*

- Multi-stage optimization framework that jointly reconstructs geometry, infers physical properties, and models appearance
- Integration of spring-mass models with generative shape priors and Gaussian splats for efficient physics-informed representation

## Results

- Accurate reconstruction and future state prediction for deformable objects under interaction
- Real-time interactive simulation capabilities for virtual exploration
- Successful integration with robotic motion planning for manipulation tasks
- Generalization to unseen interactions beyond training scenarios

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining visual perception with physics-based simulation enables more robust and generalizable digital twins
> - Hierarchical optimization strategy allows effective parameter inference from sparse video observations
> - Linear blending of Gaussian kernels enables realistic appearance modeling during deformation

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work resides at the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Dynamic Scene Reconstruction:** The paper builds upon existing work in dynamic scene reconstruction, particularly those leveraging Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS). However, it addresses a key limitation of these methods by incorporating physics-based simulation.
- **Physics-Based Simulation of Deformable Objects:** The research contributes to the field of physics-based simulation by presenting a novel inverse modeling framework that can infer physical parameters from video data. It distinguishes itself from prior work by handling sparse observations, supporting a wider range of motion types, and requiring less manual specification of parameters.
- **Learning-Based Simulation of Deformable Objects:** The paper acknowledges the advancements in learning-based simulation, particularly those utilizing graph neural networks (GNNs). However, it argues that these methods often require large amounts of training data and are limited to specific objects or motions. PhysTwin offers a more data-efficient alternative that can generalize to a broader range of scenarios.
- **Digital Twins:** The broader context is the growing interest in creating digital twins for various applications, including robotics, content creation, and XR (Extended Reality). PhysTwin specifically focuses on creating *physical* digital twins that accurately capture the geometry, appearance, and physical properties of real-world objects.

The paper positions itself as an advancement over existing methods by combining the strengths of visual perception and physics-based simulation. It aims to create a more robust, data-efficient, and generalizable framework for constructing interactive digital twins of deformable objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of the paper are:

- **To develop a novel framework (PhysTwin) that can construct physically realistic digital twins of deformable objects from sparse video observations.** This means capturing the geometry, appearance, and physical properties of objects.
- **To enable real-time interactive simulation of these digital twins.** The framework should allow users to interact with the virtual objects and observe realistic responses.
- **To create a system that can generalize to unseen interactions and predict future states accurately.** The digital twins should not be limited to the specific interactions observed during training.
- **To demonstrate the utility of PhysTwin in downstream applications, such as interactive real-time simulation and model-based robotic motion planning.** The framework should be practical and useful for real-world applications.

The motivation behind this work stems from the limitations of existing methods for dynamic scene reconstruction and simulation. Current approaches often lack physical realism, require large amounts of training data, or are limited to specific scenarios. The authors aim to address these limitations by integrating visual perception and physics-based simulation in a novel and data-efficient manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PhysTwin framework employs a multi-stage, optimization-based inverse modeling approach. The core components are:

- **Physics-Informed Representation:** This representation combines:
 - **Spring-Mass Models:** For efficient and realistic physical simulation of deformable object dynamics.
 - **Generative Shape Models (TRELLIS):** To obtain complete geometry from sparse observations, leveraging shape priors.
 - **Gaussian Splats:** For high-fidelity appearance modeling and rendering.
- **Multi-Stage Optimization Framework:** This framework reconstructs complete geometry, infers dense physical properties, and replicates realistic appearance from videos.
 - **Physics and Geometry Optimization:** Minimizes the discrepancy between predicted and observed geometry and motion. This stage uses:
 - **Generative Shape Prior:** Uses TRELLIS and Grounded-SAM to generate an initial mesh. Then refines the mesh through 2D matching, rigid registration, non-rigid deformation, and ray-casting alignment.
 - **Sparse-to-Dense Optimization:** A hierarchical strategy is used to optimize the physical parameters, using zero-order optimization for topology and sparse parameters, and first-order gradient-based optimization for dense spring stiffness.
 - **Vision Foundation Models (CoTracker3):** To extract control points and tracking information.
 - **Appearance Optimization:** Optimizes the parameters of the Gaussian splats to match the appearance of the object in the video. This stage uses a linear blending algorithm (LBS) to deform Gaussian kernels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents the following main findings and results:

- **PhysTwin outperforms competing methods in reconstruction and resimulation.** Quantitative and qualitative results demonstrate that the reconstructed digital twins align accurately with real-world observations.
- **PhysTwin achieves precise future predictions.** The framework can accurately predict the future state of deformable objects under interaction.
- **PhysTwin generates realistic simulations under diverse unseen interactions.** The digital twins can generalize to scenarios not observed during training.
- **The physics simulator enables real-time dynamics and rendering of the constructed digital twins.** This allows for interactive exploration of object dynamics.
- **The framework enables applications in real-time interactive simulation and model-based robotic motion planning.** The paper showcases these applications with examples of interactive simulation and robot manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhysTwin framework represents a significant advancement in the field of dynamic scene reconstruction and simulation. Its significance and potential impact lie in:

- **Improved Realism:** By incorporating physics-based simulation, PhysTwin creates more realistic digital twins that can accurately capture the behavior of deformable objects.
- **Data Efficiency:** The framework requires only sparse video observations, making it more practical for real-world applications.
- **Generalizability:** PhysTwin can generalize to unseen interactions, expanding its applicability beyond specific training scenarios.
- **Real-Time Performance:** The framework enables real-time simulation, allowing for interactive exploration and applications in robotics.
- **Robotics Applications:** PhysTwin can be used as a dynamic model in robot planning pipelines, enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks.
- **XR and Content Creation:** The framework can be used to create realistic and interactive virtual objects for XR experiences and content creation.

The PhysTwin framework has the potential to revolutionize how we model and interact with deformable objects in virtual environments. It can be used to create more realistic and engaging XR experiences, enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks, and provide valuable insights into the behavior of physical systems.
