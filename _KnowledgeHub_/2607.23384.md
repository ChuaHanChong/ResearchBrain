# Semantic Semi-Incremental Data-Association-Free Object SLAM

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT's CSAIL developed a generalized data-association-free object SLAM framework (skid-SLAM and skill-SLAM) that jointly estimates robot poses, landmark positions, landmark semantics, data associations, and the number of landmarks. It incorporates semantic feature vectors and a semi-incremental processing scheme, demonstrating reduced absolute trajectory error and improved computational efficiency on both synthetic and real-world datasets.

## Problem

- Traditional DAF-SLAM did not utilize semantic information, limiting its ability to robustly handle data association in geometrically ambiguous environments.
- Batch optimization in DAF-SLAM suffered from accumulated odometry drift over long trajectories, leading to less accurate initial pose estimates.
- The original framework was computationally expensive and lacked principled guidelines for setting the hyperparameter that determines the optimal number of landmarks.

## Method

- Integrated ==semantic information== (real-valued feature vectors or one-hot vectors) into the ==joint estimation process==, modifying the objective function and using ==k-means== for data association in a higher-dimensional space.
- Introduced a ==semi-incremental processing scheme== (**skid-SLAM** and **skill-SLAM**) that divides long trajectories into segments for independent processing, followed by global optimization.
- Provided a principled analysis for setting the beta (β) hyperparameter in ==landmark-number estimation==, deriving heuristics based on ==local optimality conditions== and noise models.

## Results

- Semi-incremental methods (skid-SLAM, skill-SLAM) consistently achieved lower Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) compared to batch kSLAM, particularly under high odometry noise.
- The inclusion of semantic feature vectors further reduced ATE and significantly decreased landmark semantic estimation errors compared to using raw semantic measurements.
- skid-SLAM and skill-SLAM demonstrated clear speed advantages over batch kSLAM on larger datasets and successfully recovered from errors while reducing trajectory errors in real-world environments like Kitchen and Pool datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining spatial and semantic information within a data-association-free framework substantially enhances robustness, allowing for accurate object re-identification even with noisy positional measurements.
> - A semi-incremental processing strategy effectively mitigates odometry drift and improves computational scalability in batch SLAM problems, making it practical for extended robotic operations.
> - Principled derivation of heuristics for the landmark-number estimation hyperparameter (β) improves the framework's interpretability and reduces the need for extensive empirical tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental problem in robotics, involving a robot building a map of its environment while simultaneously determining its own location within that map. A central challenge in SLAM, particularly when mapping unknown environments, is data association—the process of correctly matching sensor measurements to previously observed landmarks in the map. Incorrect data associations, often termed "false positives" or "mis-associations," can lead to significant errors in the estimated robot trajectory and map.

Traditional SLAM methods primarily rely on geometric features for mapping, such as points or lines. Object SLAM represents an advancement by building maps with objects as landmarks, thereby moving towards a more semantic understanding of the scene. The integration of semantic information, typically obtained from neural object detectors or visual foundation models, offers new opportunities to enhance data association. Semantic information, such as class labels or feature vectors, can provide stronger cues for identifying and re-identifying objects compared to purely geometric measurements.

Prior research in data association within SLAM spans various approaches, including maximum likelihood estimation, joint compatibility tests, and multi-hypothesis tracking. Modern object SLAM pipelines often combine positional and semantic information, for instance, by using Mahalanobis distance for positional components and cosine similarity for semantic feature vectors. Iterative methods, such as Max-Mixture and DC-SAM, alternately solve for discrete (data associations) and continuous (poses, landmark positions) variables. The Data-Association-Free SLAM (DAF-SLAM) framework, introduced by Zhang et al. (2023), represents a specific line of inquiry where data associations and even the number of landmarks are treated as unknown and jointly estimated from odometry and landmark position measurements. DAF-SLAM formulates the problem as an inner problem (estimating associations and landmarks given a fixed number of landmarks) and an outer problem (estimating the optimal number of landmarks).

This paper extends the DAF-SLAM framework to address several limitations, specifically by integrating semantic information, improving computational efficiency and accuracy for long trajectories, and providing a more principled understanding of landmark-number estimation. It builds upon DAF-SLAM's foundation to create a more generalized and efficient object SLAM system capable of leveraging diverse semantic cues. This work positions itself as an enhancement to robust, sensor-agnostic back-end SLAM solvers, aiming to mitigate the impact of front-end association errors that are unrecoverable in other pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalized, data-association-free object SLAM framework that jointly estimates robot poses, landmark positions, landmark semantics, data associations, and the number of landmarks. This framework is designed to operate solely on odometry, positional landmark measurements, and semantic landmark measurements.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges identified with the foundational DAF-SLAM framework and the broader context of object SLAM:

- **Exploitation of Semantic Information:** The original DAF-SLAM framework did not utilize semantic information, which is increasingly available from modern object detectors (e.g., class labels) and visual foundation models (e.g., real-valued feature vectors). Incorporating this rich semantic data could significantly improve the robustness and accuracy of data association, especially in ambiguous geometric scenarios. The motivation is to create a synergy where semantic information aids data association, and correct associations, in turn, refine semantic estimates.
- **Addressing Odometry Drift in Batch Optimization:** DAF-SLAM employs a batch optimization strategy, processing the entire trajectory at once. While this approach helps avoid propagating early association errors, it can lead to accumulated odometry drift over long trajectories. This drift results in less accurate initial pose estimates (derived from the odometry chain), potentially degrading the overall estimation accuracy for extended operations. The motivation is to devise a processing scheme that mitigates this drift without sacrificing the benefits of global optimization.
- **Improved Landmark-Number Estimation:** The DAF-SLAM framework estimates the number of landmarks (K) through an outer problem that minimizes an objective function incorporating a penalty term for K. However, the principled justification for this mechanism, particularly for setting the hyperparameter (β) that balances the data fit with the penalty on K, was considered underdeveloped. A deeper understanding and more robust guidelines for β selection are motivated to improve the interpretability and practical usability of the framework.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The batch optimization approach, especially with the outer problem's need to evaluate the inner SLAM problem multiple times for different K values, can be computationally expensive. This becomes more pronounced with increasing trajectory length (N) and number of measurements (M). The motivation is to enhance the computational efficiency of the framework, making it scalable to larger and more complex datasets.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines four main contributions:
1. A principled analysis of the landmark-number estimation mechanism, based on local optimality conditions, to provide heuristics for setting the hyperparameter β.
2. Development of a semi-incremental (or block-incremental) processing scheme using a divide-and-conquer strategy to reduce drift and improve computational efficiency.
3. Integration of semantic information (real-valued feature vectors or one-hot vectors) into the joint estimation process, fostering synergy with data association.
4. Extensive evaluation on diverse synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance against strong baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework extends the original DAF-SLAM by integrating semantic information, employing a semi-incremental processing scheme, and providing a principled analysis for landmark-number estimation.

#### 3.1. A. Local Optimality Condition for Landmark-Number Estimation

The paper provides a principled analysis for the outer problem of DAF-SLAM, which estimates the number of landmarks (K) by minimizing $f^{\star}_{slam}(K) + \beta K$. This objective function balances the SLAM objective value ($f^{\star}_{slam}(K)$) with a penalty (βK) for a higher number of landmarks.
The analysis introduces necessary local optimality conditions (Equations 4-6) which state that for the true number of landmarks, $K^{\star}$, to be the minimizer, $\beta$ must fall within a specific range: $f^{\star}_{slam}(K^{\star} - 1) - f^{\star}_{slam}(K^{\star}) \geq \beta \geq f^{\star}_{slam}(K^{\star}) - f^{\star}_{slam}(K^{\star} + 1)$. This establishes upper ($\beta_{ub}$) and lower ($\beta_{lb}$) bounds for β.
The paper then derives heuristics for setting β based on the assumed Gaussian noise model for landmark measurements.
- **Large Inter-Landmark Spacing:** When landmarks are well-separated relative to measurement noise, mis-associating measurements incurs a high penalty, leading to a large $\beta_{ub}$. The lower bound, $\beta_{lb}$, is capped by the maximum residuals of measurements associated with any single landmark. This cap is related to a $\chi^2$ distribution, allowing β to be set using an inverse $\chi^2$ distribution based on the estimated number of measurements per landmark.
- **Small Inter-Landmark Spacing:** When landmarks are close, mis-associations might not significantly increase residuals, leading to a smaller $\beta_{ub}$. In this regime, an empirical approach is suggested, with a starting value for β derived from modeling the effect of splitting Gaussian samples (approximately $\frac{2|\hat{M}(j^{\star}_k)|}{\pi}$).

#### 3.2. B. Incorporating Semantic Feature Vectors (skSLAM)

The original DAF-SLAM considers only spatial measurements. This work extends it to integrate semantic information (e.g., class labels, real-valued feature vectors) by assuming a Gaussian noise model for semantic measurements: $\bar{z}_{s,k} = y_{s,j_k} + \epsilon_{s,k}$.
The inner problem objective function is modified to include a weighted semantic residual term: $\lVert y_{s,j_k} - \bar{z}_{s,k} \rVert^2_{\Sigma_s}$. This weighted term allows for a tunable balance between spatial and semantic residuals.
During the alternating minimization, the data association step (solving for $j_k$, $y$, and $y_s$) is performed using k-means. The spatial and semantic residuals are stacked, and k-means operates in a higher-dimensional space ($d+s$ dimensions, where $d$ is spatial dimension and $s$ is semantic dimension). This allows both types of information to influence data association. The cluster assignments provide the data associations, and the cluster centers represent the estimated landmark positions and weighted semantic feature vectors.
In the estimation step, after data associations are determined, the robot poses and landmark positions are updated using a SLAM solver. The semantic landmark vectors ($y_s$) are updated by averaging the semantic measurements associated with each landmark.
The outer problem (estimating K) does not include the semantic term, as β is tuned based on the positional Gaussian assumption, which is considered more reliable than the semantic Gaussian assumption in the context of outlier handling. The algorithm incorporating semantics is named **skSLAM**. The framework also supports one-hot classification vectors by treating the weight term as a hyperparameter and using majority voting for class estimation during the averaging step.

#### 3.3. C. Semi-Incremental Processing (skid-SLAM and skill-SLAM)

To address the issue of accumulated odometry drift and improve computational efficiency in batch processing, a semi-incremental scheme, inspired by a divide-and-conquer strategy, is introduced.
- **Segment Processing:** The robot trajectory is divided into segments of length $N_n$. skSLAM is run independently on each segment. This initial processing within shorter segments reduces local odometry drift.
- **Chaining and Global Optimization:** After all segments are processed, they are chained together. A global optimization is then performed using skSLAM on the concatenated trajectory. A key efficiency gain is that the search space for K in the global optimization is reduced, as it only needs to consider the sum of estimated landmarks from each segment, which is typically much smaller than the total number of measurements (M).
- **Computational Benefits:** This scheme reduces the computational load by breaking down a large problem into smaller, more manageable ones. The complexity of the outer problem (which involves solving a nonlinear SLAM problem multiple times) is significantly reduced.
The algorithm implementing this scheme is named **skid-SLAM**.
An additional variant, **skill-SLAM**, introduces parallel global optimization. The global optimization runs in a separate thread whenever at least two segments have been processed. The output of this parallel global optimization is treated as a segment and combined with subsequent segments for ongoing optimization. This aims to provide more up-to-date pose estimates and potentially higher accuracy.

#### 3.4. D. Practical Considerations

- **One-hot Feature Vectors:** The framework accommodates one-hot vectors from object detectors. While violating the Gaussian assumption for semantic noise, the weighting term for semantic residuals acts as a tunable hyperparameter. The estimation step for one-hot vectors effectively becomes majority voting for the landmark class.
- **Detector Mistakes:** The paper discusses the handling of common object detector errors:
 - **False Positives:** Sporadic false positives spatially close to true positives are likely merged with existing landmarks because the objective function penalty for creating a new landmark (βK) outweighs the potential reduction in residuals.
 - **Missed Detections:** If a landmark is missed in some frames but observable elsewhere, the framework can still estimate its position and semantics. Post-processing might involve rendering estimated objects to correct missing detections.
 - **Mislabelling:** If an object is mislabeled in some frames, the framework can correct this if it is correctly labeled and associated using spatial information in a majority of other frames, by selecting the most-voted class.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed algorithms (skid-SLAM and skill-SLAM) were evaluated against various baselines on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating performance improvements.

#### 4.1. A. Synthetic Datasets and Ablation Study

Experiments were conducted on 2D and 3D synthetic grid datasets, augmented with one-hot and real-valued semantic feature vectors. Baselines included raw odometry, Oracle (alternating minimization with ground-truth K and initial landmark guesses), and the original batch kSLAM. Ablated versions (ikSLAM for incremental kSLAM, iskSLAM for incremental kSLAM with semantics) were also tested. Performance was measured by Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) after SIM(3) alignment.

- **Odometry Noise:** All semi-incremental methods (ikSLAM, iskSLAM, skid-SLAM, skill-SLAM) consistently outperformed the batch kSLAM, particularly under high odometry noise. The inclusion of semantic feature vectors further reduced ATE (iskSLAM, skid-SLAM, skill-SLAM). Oracle showed inferior performance, consistent with prior DAF-SLAM findings.
- **Runtime:** The naive incremental rerunning (ikSLAM, iskSLAM) exhibited higher runtimes. skill-SLAM was slightly slower than skid-SLAM due to the parallel global optimization waiting for completion. Oracle was the fastest. While kSLAM was faster on smaller datasets, skid-SLAM and skill-SLAM showed significant runtime advantages on larger datasets or those with a high number of measurements per landmark, validating the efficiency of the divide-and-conquer strategy.
- **Semantic Noise:** Varying detector mislabeling probability (for one-hot vectors) and Gaussian noise standard deviation (for real-valued vectors) showed that algorithms using semantic information (iskSLAM, skid-SLAM, skill-SLAM) generally achieved lower ATE compared to those without (kSLAM, ikSLAM). The benefits diminished with unreliable semantic information. However, joint estimation of semantics consistently yielded substantial reductions in landmark semantic estimation errors compared to raw semantic measurements.

#### 4.2. B. Kitchen Datasets with One-Hot Feature Vectors

These real-world indoor datasets used an RB-Y1 robot with a ZED stereo camera, Mask R-CNN for object masks, and RGB-D visual odometry. AprilTags provided pseudo-ground-truth trajectories. Two layouts were tested: clusters of objects from different classes and clusters of objects from the same class. In this low inter-landmark spacing scenario, β was set empirically using the $\frac{2|\hat{M}(j^{\star}_k)|}{\pi}$ heuristic.

- **Performance:** skid-SLAM and skill-SLAM consistently recovered from errors (missing/extra landmarks) made by kSLAM and reduced trajectory error in both layouts. They also demonstrated a clear speed advantage over kSLAM on these larger datasets.
- **Challenges:** The "Same Class" layout was more challenging, sometimes leading to landmarks of the same class being merged, though estimated labels were consistently correct. An object-centric SLAM baseline performed worse, indicating sensitivity to manually set covariances and thresholds.

#### 4.3. C. Pool Dataset with Real-Valued Feature Vectors

This dataset involved a BlueROV2 underwater robot with a monocular camera and sonar, navigating an indoor pool with diverse objects. DINO features, extracted via unsupervised segmentation, provided real-valued semantic descriptors. Ground-truth trajectory was unavailable, so results were qualitatively visualized. For this dataset, landmarks were well-separated, and the $\chi^2$ heuristic for β was applied.

- **Error Handling:** The raw data contained errors from small, hard-to-see objects. Both kSLAM and skill-SLAM merged these erroneous measurements into nearby landmarks, suggesting the penalty term β prevented the creation of new landmarks for sporadic outliers.
- **Loop Closure and Data Association:** Due to noisy depth and accumulated odometry drift, re-observing landmarks after a loop was challenging. kSLAM erroneously merged distinct objects (e.g., cage and seaweed). In contrast, skill-SLAM correctly associated these measurements, attributing this success to its semi-incremental scheme (reducing drift) and the critical role of semantic feature vectors in distinguishing objects where positional measurements were highly noisy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work significantly advances the DAF-SLAM framework by broadening its applicability and enhancing its performance in real-world object SLAM scenarios. The key contributions offer several significant implications:

- **Enhanced Data Association Robustness:** By integrating semantic information, the framework can leverage high-level cues from object detectors and visual foundation models. This creates a synergy where semantic data aids in disambiguating geometrically similar landmarks, and accurate data associations, in turn, lead to better estimates of object semantics. This is particularly impactful in environments with noisy positional measurements or visually similar objects, as demonstrated in the pool dataset.
- **Improved Accuracy and Efficiency for Long-Term Operation:** The proposed semi-incremental processing scheme (skid-SLAM and skill-SLAM) directly addresses the limitations of batch processing by mitigating odometry drift and improving computational scalability. This makes the framework more suitable for long-duration robotic missions where accumulated errors in pose estimation can be critical. The divide-and-conquer strategy offers a practical solution to balancing global consistency with computational tractability.
- **Principled Landmark-Number Estimation:** Providing a detailed analysis and guidelines for setting the β hyperparameter in the outer problem for K estimation makes the framework more interpretable and easier to configure for different environments. This principled approach reduces the reliance on extensive empirical tuning, potentially accelerating deployment and increasing the reliability of map building.
- **Generalizability Across Semantic Information Types:** The framework's ability to handle both one-hot classification vectors and real-valued feature vectors makes it versatile, compatible with a wide range of modern deep learning models, from traditional object detectors to sophisticated visual foundation models. This broadens its utility across various object recognition and scene understanding tasks.

The potential impact of this research extends to various robotic applications requiring robust and semantic scene understanding, such as autonomous navigation, mobile manipulation, and long-term environmental monitoring in complex or challenging settings (e.g., underwater, cluttered indoor spaces). The improvements in data association, accuracy, and efficiency contribute to the development of more capable and reliable autonomous systems.

Future directions highlighted by the authors include developing more robust optimization objectives to handle both positional and semantic outlier measurements (e.g., spurious depths, false positives) and incorporating shape estimation into the framework. Moving beyond point-landmark representations to include object shapes could further enhance data association and map richness, enabling even more sophisticated robotic interactions.
