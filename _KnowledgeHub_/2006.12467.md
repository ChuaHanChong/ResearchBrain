# The Depth-to-Width Interplay in Self-Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Hebrew University of Jerusalem established that self-attention networks operate within distinct "depth-efficient" and "depth-inefficient" scaling regimes, challenging prior assumptions of uniform depth-inefficiency. Their work provides quantitative architectural guidelines for optimally balancing depth and width, particularly recommending massive widening for future trillion-parameter language models.

## Problem

- The prevailing hypothesis suggested "depth-inefficiency" in self-attention networks, implying that increasing width was generally as effective as increasing depth.
- A nuanced understanding of how network depth and width jointly contribute to expressiveness and performance in self-attention architectures was lacking.
- There was a need for quantitative guidelines to efficiently scale self-attention networks, especially for the development of multi-billion and trillion-parameter language models.

## Method

- Conducted theoretical analysis using the ==separation rank==, a tensor-based measure of input dependency modeling capacity, on a simplified ==self-attention== variant.
- Performed extensive empirical validation through systematic ablation studies on full self-attention networks across a wide range of depths (6-48 layers) and widths (1M-600M parameters).
- Identified empirical transition points between scaling regimes and extrapolated these findings to provide architectural recommendations for extremely large language models.

## Results

- Theoretical analysis showed separation rank growing double-exponentially with depth in the depth-efficient regime, while growing exponentially with both depth and width in the depth-inefficient regime.
- Empirical studies quantitatively validated the predicted log-linear relationship between optimal depth and width for a given parameter count, achieving a high **R^2 of 0.998**.
- Extrapolated optimal architectures suggest that for a **1-Trillion** parameter model, the ideal configuration is **L=95** and **d_x=30,100**, indicating a strategy of massive widening for future very large language models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-attention networks exhibit two distinct expressiveness regimes: a "depth-efficient" one (L < log_3(d_x)) where capacity grows double-exponentially with depth, and a "depth-inefficient" one (L > log_3(d_x)) where width contributes comparably.
> - A critical width-dependent depth threshold, L_th(d_x) ~ log(d_x), precisely delineates the transition between these two scaling behaviors.
> - Empirical findings revealed an unexpected "width-efficiency" regime for smaller models, where shallower, wider networks could outperform deeper ones, suggesting an optimal depth rather than just a minimal one for a given parameter budget.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work by Levine et al. addresses a nuanced aspect of neural network architecture design, specifically the trade-off between network depth (number of layers) and width (dimension of internal representation) in self-attention models.

Historically, the deep learning community has often emphasized "depth-efficiency," where increasing the number of layers was considered more advantageous for network expressiveness than increasing the width. This notion was supported by early empirical successes in convolutional networks (ConvNets), such as VGG (Simonyan and Zisserman, 2014) and ResNet (He et al., 2016), and by various theoretical studies (Cohen et al., 2016; Eldan and Shamir, 2016; Raghu et al., 2017; Daniely, 2017). Optimization challenges in very deep networks were identified as a primary limitation, and advancements in optimization techniques allowed depths to increase significantly, advancing the state-of-the-art in computer vision. However, subsequent research on ConvNets, including empirical studies (Zagoruyko and Komodakis, 2016; Wu et al., 2019) and theoretical analyses (Lu et al., 2017), suggested a more complex interplay between depth and width. This led to the development of models like EfficientNet (Tan and Le, 2019), which demonstrated performance gains by scaling depth and width in tandem.

In the realm of natural language processing, self-attention-based architectures, particularly the Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) and its variants like BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) and GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020), have become dominant. Despite the rapid increase in overall parameter count for these models (e.g., from 0.3 billion in BERT-large to 175 billion in GPT-3), the increase in depth has been relatively modest (factors of 2 to 4), with the majority of the parameter growth attributed to increased layer widths. This observation initially challenged the established depth-efficiency paradigm.

A prior empirical study by Kaplan et al. (2020) explicitly investigated this, concluding that the performance of self-attention networks primarily depends on the total number of parameters, irrespective of the specific depth-to-width ratio. This suggested a "depth-inefficiency" in self-attention, implying that widening a network was generally as effective as deepening it.

This paper by Levine et al. positions itself directly against this prevailing interpretation. It argues that the relationship between depth and width in self-attention is more subtle than previously assumed. By proposing and validating the existence of distinct "depth-efficiency" and "depth-inefficiency" regimes, the authors aim to provide a more nuanced theoretical framework and quantitative guidelines for the architectural scaling of self-attention networks. This is particularly relevant given the ongoing development of extremely large language models, with targets reaching beyond 1-Trillion parameters, where informed architectural decisions are critical for efficient resource utilization and performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate and elucidate the intricate interplay between network depth and width in self-attention architectures. The authors aim to refine the understanding of how these architectural dimensions contribute to model expressiveness and performance, particularly in contrast to previous findings that suggested a general depth-inefficiency in self-attention.

The key motivations driving this work include:

- **Challenging and Refining the "Depth-Inefficiency" Hypothesis:** The research is motivated by a desire to re-evaluate and provide a more nuanced perspective on the conclusions drawn by prior studies, such as Kaplan et al. (2020), which indicated that increasing self-attention network width is as effective as increasing depth, leading to an overall characterization of "depth-inefficiency." This paper hypothesizes that this observation might only be valid within specific parameter regimes.
- **Theoretical Prediction of Distinct Regimes:** A core objective is to theoretically predict the existence of two distinct behavioral regimes in self-attention: one where depth-efficiency is prominent, and another where depth-inefficiency (or width-efficiency) becomes apparent. The authors aim to establish a mathematical basis for this transition, specifically predicting a width-dependent depth threshold (L_th(d_x) ∼ log(d_x)) that marks the shift between these regimes.
- **Empirical Validation of Theoretical Predictions:** The theoretical predictions motivate a comprehensive empirical investigation. A key objective is to conduct systematic ablation studies on practical self-attention networks across a wide range of depths (from 6 to 48 layers) and widths to observe whether the predicted depth-efficiency and depth-inefficiency behaviors manifest in real-world models.
- **Developing Quantitative Architectural Guidelines:** The study seeks to translate its theoretical and empirical findings into explicit, quantitative guidelines for optimal parameter allocation between depth and width for a given self-attention network size. This includes providing practical recommendations for scaling architectures, particularly for the development of contemporary and future very large language models.
- **Informing the Design of Trillion-Parameter Models:** With the ongoing "race" towards language models exceeding 1-Trillion parameters, understanding the optimal way to scale network dimensions is crucial. The research is specifically motivated by the need to provide "informed guidelines" on how to increase depth and width in tandem, to ensure efficient resource allocation and maximize performance for these extremely large and costly models.
- **Fundamental Understanding of Self-Attention:** Beyond practical implications, the work is driven by a fundamental scientific curiosity to understand the underlying mechanisms of self-attention, particularly how its core connectivity fosters the modeling of input dependencies and how this capacity evolves with increasing architectural scale. The goal is to shed light on why self-attention operates as it does regarding its depth and width scaling properties.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a dual methodology, combining theoretical analysis with extensive empirical validation, to investigate the depth-to-width interplay in self-attention networks.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Approach

The theoretical analysis focuses on a simplified version of the self-attention mechanism, deliberately excluding certain components found in full Transformer architectures to isolate the core connectivity properties.

- **Analyzed Architecture:** The model analyzed is a deep multi-headed self-attention network variant. Crucially, it excludes:
 - Layer normalization.
 - Softmax normalization in the attention mechanism.
 - ReLU activation in the position-wise feed-forward sublayer.
 - Residual connections are initially omitted for cleaner presentation, with their impact later discussed and shown to be insignificant to the theoretical bounds.
- **Rationale for Simplifications:** The authors justify these relaxations by citing prior empirical evidence suggesting that the fundamental input integration and correlation ability of self-attention is primarily driven by its core connectivity, rather than these specific non-linearities or normalizations. This simplification strategy is a common technique in deep learning theory to analyze fundamental connectivity traits.
- **Tool for Expressiveness: Separation Rank:**
 - The primary theoretical tool is the "separation rank," a measure derived from tensor theory.
 - **Definition:** The separation rank quantifies a function's ability to model dependencies between subsets of its inputs. A higher separation rank indicates a greater capacity to capture complex, non-separable relationships.
 - **Application:** This measure has previously been used to gain theoretical insights into convolutional and recurrent neural networks regarding their ability to model input dependencies.
- **Analysis of Separation Rank:**
 - **Proposition 1:** The paper first establishes that, for self-attention, the separation rank with respect to balanced partitions of inputs is invariant to the specific identity of the partition. This characteristic highlights self-attention's flexible, non-local information integration, contrasting with the local bias of ConvNets.
 - **Regime-Specific Bounding:** The main theoretical contribution involves deriving tight lower and upper bounds on the separation rank based on network depth (L) and width (d_x):
 - **Theorem 1 (Depth-Efficiency):** For networks where L < log_3(d_x) (i.e., shallower networks with sufficient width), the separation rank grows *double-exponentially* with depth and polynomially with width. This suggests a significant advantage to deepening in this regime. The proof sketch involves using grid tensors to lower-bound separation rank with matrix rank, and constructing specific weight assignments to achieve the desired rank.
 - **Theorem 2 (Depth-Inefficiency):** For networks where L > log_3(d_x) (i.e., deeper networks where depth exceeds a width-dependent threshold), the separation rank grows exponentially with both depth and width. This implies that the clear advantage of depth diminishes, and widening becomes comparably effective. The proof sketch for the upper bound involves analyzing the polynomial degree of the function and bounding the number of independent monomials.
 - **Corollaries 1 and 2:** These corollaries translate the separation rank bounds into statements about the width required for a shallower network to replicate the expressiveness of a deeper one, clearly delineating the different depth-efficiency behaviors across the regimes.

#### 3.2. Empirical Approach

The empirical work is designed to validate the theoretical predictions using full, practically relevant self-attention architectures.

- **Network Architectures:** The experiments utilize common self-attention architectures, which *include* all the non-linearities (ReLU, softmax) and normalization layers (layer-norm) that were simplified in the theoretical analysis. These are decoder-only (unidirectional) models.
- **Systematic Ablations:**
 - **Depth Range:** Models were trained with depths L = 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, and 48 layers.
 - **Width Variation:** For each depth, multiple widths (d_x) were explored, resulting in network sizes ranging from approximately 1 million to 600 million parameters.
 - **Head Count:** The number of attention heads per layer was kept constant at 2, based on prior indications that it has a less critical impact on performance compared to d_x.
- **Training Setup:**
 - **Task:** Autoregressive log-likelihood optimization (language modeling).
 - **Datasets:** English Wikipedia, BookCorpus, and OpenWebText.
 - **Vocabulary:** A smaller vocabulary size of 2000 was used to ensure that input embedding parameters did not dominate the total parameter count, allowing the focus to remain on the self-attention layer parameters.
 - **Optimization:** Adam optimizer for 1 million steps, with a batch size of 512 sequences of 128 tokens. A learning rate schedule with a 12,000-step linear warm-up followed by a cosine decay to zero was used.
 - **Evaluation:** Performance was measured by the loss on a held-out test set of 170,000 sequences. Training and evaluation variance were estimated by rerunning 11 architectures three times, showing low variability.
- **Data Analysis and Extrapolation:**
 - **Identifying Transition Points:** The experimental results were used to empirically determine the "transition width" for each depth, defined as the point where a shallower network's performance statistically diverges from that of a deeper network with the same total parameter count.
 - **Fitting:** The empirically identified transition points (depth vs. log-width) were fitted to a linear model, corresponding to the theoretically predicted exponential dependence of width on depth (log(d_x) = a + bL).
 - **Extrapolation:** The fitted parameters were then used to extrapolate optimal depth-to-width allocations for much larger, state-of-the-art language models, including models up to 1-Trillion parameters. This involved calculating projected optimal depths and widths, along with their statistical uncertainties.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical derivations and empirical validations, leading to a refined understanding of self-attention network scaling.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Two Distinct Regimes of Depth-Efficiency:** The central theoretical finding is the prediction of two qualitatively different regimes governing the expressiveness of self-attention networks, delineated by a width-dependent depth threshold.
 - **Depth-Efficiency Regime (L < log_3(d_x)):** For networks where depth is below a logarithmic threshold relative to width, the self-attention mechanism exhibits strong depth-efficiency. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) demonstrates that the network's capacity to model input dependencies, quantified by its separation rank, grows *double-exponentially* with depth (L) and only polynomially with width (d_x). This implies that adding layers in this regime provides a substantial, rapid increase in expressiveness that cannot be matched by merely widening a shallower network without an extraordinarily large (double-exponential) increase in width (Corollary 1). This suggests that self-attention is highly effective at compounding interactions across layers, rapidly saturating the expressiveness supportable by a given width.
 - **Depth-Inefficiency Regime (L > log_3(d_x)):** Once the network's depth exceeds this width-dependent threshold, the expressive advantage of depth becomes less pronounced. In this regime, the separation rank is found to grow exponentially with both depth and width (Theorem 2). This signifies that depth and width contribute more equivalently to expressiveness, and increasing width becomes a comparably effective strategy to increasing depth (Corollary 2).
- **Role of Width Threshold (L_th(d_x) ∼ log(d_x)):** The theoretical analysis quantifies a critical transition point where the dominant scaling behavior shifts, with the threshold depth (L_th) depending logarithmically on the network's width (d_x).
- **Invariance to Partition Identity:** The separation rank of self-attention functions for balanced input partitions is shown to be invariant to the specific identity of the partition (Proposition 1). This aligns with the understanding that self-attention can dynamically learn to correlate any inter-dependent subsets of inputs, unlike architectures with fixed local connectivity.
- **Robustness to Relaxations:** The theoretical conclusions regarding separation rank bounds are shown to hold even with the re-introduction of residual connections, indicating that these architectural details do not significantly alter the fundamental scaling properties identified.

#### 4.2. Empirical Findings

- **Validation of Predicted Regimes:** The systematic empirical ablations on full self-attention architectures (including non-linearities and normalizations) clearly corroborated the theoretical predictions (Figure 2).
 - For relatively small network sizes (and thus typically lower depths for a given parameter count), deepening did not offer a clear advantage; often, shallower and wider networks performed comparably or better.
 - As network sizes (and thus widths) increased, a distinct transition occurred where deeper networks began to outperform shallower ones for the same parameter budget, demonstrating the predicted depth-efficiency.
- **Quantitative Match of Transition Points:** The empirically identified transition points between the depth-efficiency and depth-inefficiency regimes showed a strong quantitative agreement with the theoretically predicted exponential relationship between width and depth.
 - A linear fit to the log of the transition width versus depth yielded a high R^2 value of 0.998 and a low reduced chi-squared (χ^2_red) of 0.854 (Figure 3a), confirming the exponential dependence (d_x ∝ e^(bL)) predicted by the theory.
- **Discovery of "Width-Efficiency" Regime:** The empirical results uncovered an additional phenomenon not explicitly predicted by the theoretical model: for certain small network sizes, deeper self-attention networks performed *worse* than shallower, wider ones of the same parameter count (Figure 2b,c). This "width-efficiency" implies that a network can be "too deep" for a given parameter budget, suggesting an optimal depth rather than just a minimal one (Figure 3c).
- **Extrapolated Guidelines for Large Models:** The robust empirical fit allowed for extrapolation to much larger network scales, providing practical architectural guidelines for contemporary and future huge language models (Figure 4, Table 1).
 - **GPT-3 Scale Analysis:** The analysis suggests that large models, such as GPT-3 175B (L=96, d_x=12288), might be "too deep" relative to their parameter count according to the optimal scaling curve. The optimal depth for a 175B parameter model is projected to be around L=80, with a wider d_x of 13,500.
 - **Path to 1-Trillion Parameters:** The extrapolation strongly indicates that for models approaching and exceeding 1-Trillion parameters, the optimal scaling strategy involves *massive widening*. The optimal architecture for a 1-Trillion parameter network is projected to have a depth of 95 and an unprecedented width of 30,100.
 - **General Recommendation:** For self-attention architectures up to the scale of 100 billion parameters, current designs generally appear "too shallow" and could benefit from deepening alongside appropriate widening. Beyond this scale, widening becomes the predominantly recommended strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this research carry significant implications for both the theoretical understanding of deep learning architectures and the practical design of large-scale self-attention models.

- **Refined Theoretical Understanding of Self-Attention:** The work moves beyond a generalized "depth-inefficiency" claim for self-attention, offering a nuanced theoretical framework that identifies distinct depth-efficiency and depth-inefficiency regimes. By demonstrating a double-exponential growth of expressiveness with depth in the early layers, the paper reveals a powerful compounding mechanism in self-attention that quickly saturates width capacity. This is a fundamental theoretical contribution to understanding the expressive power of self-attention.
- **Quantitative Architectural Guidelines:** The study provides concrete, data-driven guidelines for allocating parameters between depth and width in self-attention networks. This is particularly valuable in an era of rapidly growing model sizes where architectural choices have substantial performance and resource implications. The high confidence of the extrapolated predictions (e.g., within 20% for 1-Trillion parameter models) enhances their practical utility.
- **Impact on Large Language Model Design:** The analysis directly informs the development of state-of-the-art and future large language models (LLMs).
 - **Challenging Current Designs:** The finding that models like GPT-3 175B might be "too deep" for their size, or conversely, smaller GPT-3 variants "too shallow," challenges existing architectural design heuristics and could prompt a re-evaluation of current scaling strategies by leading research institutions.
 - **Guidance for Trillion-Parameter Models:** The strong recommendation for "massive widening" as the optimal path to 1-Trillion parameter models and beyond is a crucial insight. From an engineering perspective, increasing width is often more amenable to parallelization than increasing depth, potentially leading to more efficient training and inference, and reduced computational costs.
- **Motivation for Novel Architectural Innovations:** By pinpointing network width as a limiting factor for depth-efficiency, the research provides theoretical motivation for exploring new architectural designs aimed at increasing effective width while managing computational expenses. Examples include techniques like those seen in ShuffleNet (for ConvNets) or sparse activation patterns (as in Switch Transformers), which enable large effective widths without proportional increases in computation. This could foster innovation in more efficient self-attention variants.
- **Economic and Resource Efficiency:** Given the substantial financial and energy costs associated with training multi-billion and trillion-parameter models, informed architectural guidelines can lead to significant optimizations. Avoiding suboptimal depth-to-width ratios can save millions of dollars in compute resources and reduce the environmental footprint of large-scale AI research.
- **Foundation for Comparative Architectural Studies:** The explicit comparison of self-attention's depth-efficiency with that of ConvNets (conjecturing that ConvNets might benefit more from depth because their expressiveness grows less rapidly, thus saturating width thresholds slower) opens avenues for future theoretical work comparing the fundamental scaling properties across different deep learning architectures.
- **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The robust empirical validation of theoretical predictions, even after removing simplifying assumptions in the theoretical model, reinforces the value of theoretical analysis in guiding practical deep learning design. This work demonstrates a successful synergy between abstract theoretical insights and concrete experimental observations.
