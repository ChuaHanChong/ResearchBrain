# APIVOT: Adaptive Planning with Interleaved Vision-Language Thoughts

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed APIVOT, a VLM-based robot planner that adaptively interleaves language with visual thoughts to improve long-horizon task planning in spatially complex environments. The system achieved an 8.1 percentage point higher average task success rate over leading baselines and demonstrated efficient reasoning by selectively invoking visual thoughts for geometric challenges.

## Problem

- Integrating semantic reasoning (task structure) and geometric reasoning (spatial constraints) remains a challenge for long-horizon robotic manipulation.
- Existing VLM-based planners often generate semantically plausible plans that are geometrically infeasible due to a lack of internal spatial awareness.
- There is a need for adaptive modality selection, enabling planners to efficiently choose between language-only or vision-enhanced reasoning based on task demands.

## Method

- **APIVOT** is a VLM-based planner that generates multimodal reasoning traces, combining language with ==visual thoughts== to internally verify geometric feasibility for future states.
- Visual thoughts are fixed-length sequences of visual tokens representing imagined future observations, allowing the VLM to reason about spatial constraints and object placement.
- The model is trained using a three-stage ==supervised fine-tuning== curriculum, progressing from visual thought comprehension and generation to ==adaptive modality selection== based on geometric utility.

## Results

- APIVOT achieved an average task success rate of **0.419**, outperforming the top-performing VLM baseline by **8.1 percentage points** and the strongest planning baseline by **9.0 percentage points**.
- Performance gains were more pronounced in geometrically constrained settings, with APIVOT maintaining success rates more effectively as occupancy ratios increased.
- Adaptive modality selection allowed APIVOT to retain **91%** of the task success of an 'Always-Image' approach while reducing token usage by **39%**, selectively employing visual thoughts for **48.4-56.2%** of geometrically relevant steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly integrating geometric reasoning within the VLM's internal planning process via 'visual thoughts' significantly improves planning robustness compared to relying on external geometric feedback.
> - Language is effective for semantic task decomposition, while visual representations are crucial for precise geometric reasoning, and leveraging both adaptively is key.
> - Learning to selectively generate visual thoughts only when geometrically necessary enhances reasoning efficiency, offering a favorable balance between performance and computational cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Long-horizon robotic manipulation necessitates the integration of both semantic and geometric reasoning. Semantic reasoning involves understanding task structures, decomposing goals, and sequencing actions based on logical prerequisites. Geometric reasoning, conversely, deals with spatial constraints, object collisions, and available free space. Historically, these two forms of reasoning have been challenging to integrate seamlessly within robot planning frameworks.

Existing planning methods can be broadly categorized. Classical Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) methods combine symbolic search with geometric feasibility checks. While principled, these approaches typically rely on hand-specified symbolic abstractions and domain models, limiting their flexibility. More recent approaches leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to translate natural language instructions into action sequences or high-level plans. These LLM/VLM-based planners excel at generating semantically plausible plans but frequently encounter difficulties when geometric feasibility becomes critical. For instance, a plan might be logically sound but physically impossible due to spatial constraints or potential collisions.

To address this geometric grounding deficiency, a segment of contemporary research integrates LLM/VLM planners with external components such as motion planners, feasibility checkers, or learned dynamics models. These external modules provide geometric feedback, often triggering replanning. However, a limitation of this paradigm is that the geometric reasoning largely remains external to the VLM's internal thought process; the model itself does not directly incorporate spatial constraints into its initial planning or decision-making.

Within the realm of multimodal reasoning, recent advancements in unified multimodal models have focused on visual understanding and generation. Related work in multimodal chain-of-thought or latent visual reasoning employs intermediate visual representations. While these approaches enhance visual and spatial reasoning, they typically operate on current observations or static problem solving, rather than explicitly planning for the evolution of a scene over multiple actions. Furthermore, vision-language-action (VLA) models and embodied foundation models are emerging, integrating multimodal reasoning into robotics for policy execution rather than high-level semantic planning. A consistent challenge across these multimodal approaches is developing mechanisms for *adaptive modality selection*—determining when to engage visual reasoning versus relying solely on language.

This paper positions APIVOT as a VLM-based planner that directly addresses the gap of *internal, adaptive, interleaved* semantic and geometric reasoning. It aims to overcome the limitations of external geometric feedback and uniform multimodal reasoning by enabling the planner to strategically leverage language and visual "thoughts" as complementary modalities within its own internal planning trace. The work builds on the idea that language is efficient for semantic decisions (task decomposition, preconditions), while visual representations are more appropriate for geometric reasoning (fitting objects, free space evaluation), but seeks to learn *when* to use each modality efficiently.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to develop a VLM-based robot planner capable of robustly handling long-horizon tasks by effectively integrating semantic task structure and geometric feasibility. The core motivation stems from the observed limitations of existing LLM/VLM-based planners that, despite generating semantically plausible action sequences, frequently produce plans that are geometrically infeasible.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **To Internally Integrate Geometric Reasoning:** Prior approaches often rely on external modules (e.g., motion planners, feasibility checkers) to provide geometric feedback for replanning. The authors contend that for optimal performance, geometric constraints should actively shape the planner's *internal* reasoning process from the outset, rather than being a post-hoc verification step. APIVOT is motivated to bring this geometric awareness directly into the VLM's thought process.

2. **To Leverage Complementary Modalities:** The paper postulates that language and vision offer distinct strengths for different aspects of planning. Language is highly effective for semantic reasoning, such as decomposing complex goals, selecting task-relevant objects, and establishing action prerequisites. Conversely, visual representations are naturally suited for encoding spatial layouts, object shapes, and available free space, which are critical for geometric reasoning. A primary objective is to develop a system that can exploit these complementary strengths.

3. **To Enable Adaptive Modality Selection:** While multimodal reasoning offers benefits, uniformly applying visual processing can be computationally expensive and unnecessary for all planning steps. A significant motivation is to enable the planner to *adaptively* choose the appropriate modality—language or vision—based on the specific demands of each planning step. This involves learning when a decision requires geometric precision (warranting a visual thought) versus when it can be effectively handled by semantic reasoning alone (using language).

4. **To Improve Planning Success in Spatially Constrained Settings:** A direct consequence of poor geometric reasoning in current planners is a failure to succeed in tasks involving tight spaces, obstacle avoidance, or precise object arrangement. A key objective for APIVOT is to significantly enhance planning success rates, particularly in environments characterized by high geometric complexity and constraints.

5. **To Enhance Reasoning Efficiency:** Beyond improving planning success, the authors aim to demonstrate that adaptive modality selection can also lead to more efficient reasoning. By selectively generating visual thoughts only when needed, APIVOT seeks to minimize unnecessary computational overhead associated with processing or generating visual representations, which are generally more costly than language tokens.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more intelligent, robust, and efficient robot planner that mimics a more human-like planning process, wherein both abstract semantic understanding and concrete spatial visualization are selectively brought to bear on complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

APIVOT (Adaptive Planning with Interleaved Vision-Language Thoughts) is a VLM-based planner designed to perform long-horizon robot planning by adaptively interleaving language with visual thoughts.

#### 3.1. Planner Overview

At inference, APIVOT generates a multimodal reasoning trace followed by a sequence of actions.
- **Multimodal Reasoning Trace:** The trace `R` is a concatenation of reasoning steps `r_j`, each associated with an intermediate subgoal. Each `r_j` consists of text tokens `t_j` and may optionally include visual tokens `v_j`. Language is used for semantic reasoning (subgoal decomposition, action ordering), while visual thoughts represent imagined future states for geometric feasibility verification.
- **Visual Thought:** A visual thought is a fixed-length span of `K=16` visual tokens, delimited by `<|image_start|>` and `<|image_end|>` tokens. The decoder's hidden states at these `K` "image_pad" tokens form the latent visual state `H_j ∈ R^(K×d)`, encoding visual content that subsequent tokens can attend to for reasoning and plan generation.
- **Action Plan:** The model outputs a plan (a sequence of actions `a_i`) conditioned on the reasoning trace. Actions belong to `A_plan`, which includes discrete primitives (e.g., `open`, `pick`, `place`) and continuous parameters (e.g., `(u, v)` image-space coordinates for `place` actions, lifted to world-frame using depth and camera extrinsics).

#### 3.2. Supervision from Reference Outputs

APIVOT is initialized from a pretrained VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) and trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on reference outputs.
- **Reference Output Construction:**
 1. **Demonstration Decomposition:** Successful demonstrations from the KitchenWorlds simulator are decomposed into sequences of subgoals. `open` actions become `open` subgoals, while consecutive `pick`/`place` calls are grouped into `place` subgoals.
 2. **Subgoal Annotation:** Each subgoal is annotated with:
 - **Constraint Type:** Categorized as `symbolic precondition` (independent of continuous parameters), `current geometric constraint` (parameter restricted by current scene), or `future geometric feasibility` (parameter choice affects downstream feasibility). Geometric metrics (surface areas, object footprints, free-space margins) derived from simulator replay are used to compute constraint tightness.
 - **Modality Label:** Determines whether the reference trace uses text-only or includes a visual thought. `Symbolic precondition` steps are text-only. Geometric steps receive a visual thought if tightness exceeds a threshold.
 - **Purpose:** Tags the subgoal's role (e.g., advancing task, satisfying precondition, clearing obstacle).
 3. **Trace Expansion:** A high-capacity language model (gemini-3-flash-preview) expands the annotated subgoal skeleton into a natural-language reasoning trace. It adds connective reasoning while preserving the structure and grounding the trace in physical constraints. For visual thought steps, the discrete visual token sequence (`<|image_start|>...<|image_end|>`) is inserted, paired with a target future RGB observation of the environment after the subgoal is achieved.
- **Visual Alignment:** The latent visual states `H_j` generated by the VLM are supervised by aligning them with target features `Z_gt_j` obtained by encoding the corresponding ground-truth future images `I_gt_j` with a frozen vision encoder `E`. A learned projection head `f_ϕ` maps `H_j` into `E`'s feature space, and a cosine similarity loss `L_vis = 1/|B_vis| Σ (1 - cos(Z_j, Z_gt_j))` is applied.
- **Training Objective:** The total loss `L_total = L_CE,plan + λ_CE,trace L_CE,trace + λ_vis L_vis` comprises cross-entropy losses for the reasoning trace (`L_CE,trace`) and the plan (`L_CE,plan`), and the visual alignment loss (`L_vis`).

#### 3.3. Training Curriculum

APIVOT is trained using a three-stage curriculum:
1. **Stage 1: Visual Thought Comprehension:** Teaches the model to use visual thoughts for planning. All reasoning steps include a visual thought, and target latent visual states (`Z_gt_j`) are directly injected into the decoder input (disabling `L_vis`). This allows the model to learn to ground planning in provided visual states.
2. **Stage 2: Visual Thought Generation:** Teaches the model to generate visual thoughts. All reasoning steps include a visual thought, but the model now generates its own latent visual states. All loss components (`L_CE,trace`, `L_CE,plan`, `L_vis`) are enabled.
3. **Stage 3: Adaptive Modality Selection:** Teaches the model to adaptively choose the reasoning modality. Training uses traces with adaptive modality selection (visual thoughts only when geometrically useful, based on simulator-derived heuristics). All loss components are enabled, encouraging performance while promoting selective visual thought generation.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Environment:** KitchenWorlds simulator, a PyBullet-based environment.
- **Tasks:** Long-horizon kitchen tasks requiring joint semantic and geometric reasoning. Three families:
 - `CONTAINMENT`: Placing objects into constrained storage spaces.
 - `SORTING`: Assigning objects to containers by type, often with capacity constraints.
 - `STORING LEFTOVERS`: Held-out compositional task combining `SORTING` and `CONTAINMENT`.
- **Data:** 2,000 examples per training task family per stage. Evaluation on 100 examples for in-distribution (ID) tasks and the held-out `STORING LEFTOVERS` task. Out-of-distribution (OOD) settings test longer horizons and increased complexity.
- **Evaluation:** Closed-loop, receding-horizon setup. Metrics: task success rate and token usage (reasoning cost).
- **Baselines:**
 - **General-purpose VLMs:** Gemini-3.1-Pro, Gemini-ER-1.5, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (APIVOT's base model).
 - **Planning Baselines:** FastDownward (symbolic, with symbolic states inferred by BLADE and random placement grounding), Reflect-VLM (VLM using external imagined observations for critique), VLM-TAMP (VLM proposes symbolic structure for downstream planner).
- **Implementation:** APIVOT uses Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, finetuned with LoRA on attention and MLP projections. `K=16` visual tokens per visual thought.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate APIVOT's performance and adaptive behavior yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. Task Planning Performance

- **Overall Success Rate:** APIVOT achieved an average task success rate of 0.419 across all task families. This represents an 8.1 percentage point improvement over the top-performing VLM baseline, Gemini-ER-1.5 (0.338), and a 9.0 percentage point improvement over the strongest planning baseline, VLM-TAMP (0.329).
- **Performance Across Task Families:** APIVOT demonstrated consistent gains across both training tasks (`CONTAINMENT`: 0.472 vs. VLM-TAMP's 0.370, and `SORTING`: 0.421 vs. VLM-TAMP's 0.311).
- **Compositional Generalization:** On `STORING LEFTOVERS`, a held-out compositional task, APIVOT achieved a 0.365 success rate, outperforming VLM-TAMP (0.307) by 5.4 percentage points. This suggests the learned planning strategy transfers to novel compositions of semantic and geometric constraints.
- **Geometric Complexity:** APIVOT's performance was notably more robust in geometrically constrained settings (higher occupancy ratio). While baseline performance declined significantly, APIVOT's success rate dropped less, widening the performance gap from 0.07 to 0.17 points as geometric complexity increased. This indicates its advantage in handling precise placements and collision avoidance.
- **Reasoning Budgets:** Under matched reasoning-token budgets, APIVOT consistently outperformed VLM baselines. It showed the largest gains at low- and medium-token budgets, suggesting that visual thoughts provide a more token-efficient representation for encoding spatial information compared to lengthy language descriptions.

#### 4.2. Role of Visual Thoughts

- **Text-Only SFT Ablation:** A text-only variant of APIVOT, trained using standard SFT on language traces, achieved an average success rate of 0.24. While this was an improvement over the base Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model (0.188), it was substantially lower than APIVOT's 0.419. This indicates that while domain-specific language supervision is beneficial, the primary gains of APIVOT stem from its ability to represent and reason with intermediate future states visually.
- **Modality Ablations:** Training with multimodal (text+image) supervision improved planning performance even when inference-time reasoning was text-only (0.282 success for Text+Image training, Text inference vs. 0.244 for Text-only training, Text inference). This implies that multimodal training encourages planning representations that are more sensitive to geometry. Using visual thoughts during inference ("Always-Image" ablation) yielded the highest success (0.459), while the "GT oracle" (conditioning on ground-truth visual thoughts) achieved an even higher 0.482, suggesting room for improvement in generated visual thought quality.

#### 4.3. Adaptive Modality Selection Behavior

- **Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** APIVOT, with its adaptive modality selection, retained 91% of the task success rate achieved by the "Always-Image" ablation (0.419 vs. 0.459), while simultaneously reducing token usage by 39%. This placed APIVOT in a favorable position on the performance-efficiency frontier, demonstrating its ability to balance task success with reduced reasoning cost.
- **Constraint-Aware Modality Selection:** Analysis of APIVOT's reasoning traces revealed meaningful modality-selection behavior. The model rarely generated visual thoughts when steps did not require geometric reasoning (e.g., symbolic preconditions, unconstrained actions). In contrast, it used visual thoughts for 48.4% of steps involving limited free space and 56.2% of steps involving downstream feasibility constraints. This indicates that APIVOT learned to selectively invoke visual reasoning when spatial precision or complex geometric interactions were relevant.
- **Heuristic-Guided Trace Ablation:** The heuristic-based approach for generating reference traces, which used visual thoughts for 75% of geometrically relevant steps, achieved the best success rate. A random visual thought insertion strategy, even with higher inference-time usage, performed worse, suggesting the effectiveness of the geometric heuristic in guiding adaptive learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of APIVOT represents a notable step in advancing autonomous robot planning capabilities, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks in physically constrained environments.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Robot Planning Paradigms

APIVOT's primary significance lies in demonstrating a mechanism for deep, internal integration of semantic and geometric reasoning within a VLM-based planner. By allowing the VLM to generate and reason with "visual thoughts" as imagined future states, the system moves beyond reactive replanning based on external geometric feedback. This internal foresight enables spatial constraints to shape planning decisions *before* execution, addressing a fundamental limitation in current LLM/VLM planning approaches.

#### 5.2. Improved Performance and Robustness in Constrained Environments

The results indicate that APIVOT achieves higher task success rates compared to both general-purpose VLMs and other VLM-based planning frameworks. This improvement is particularly pronounced in geometrically constrained settings, where precise object placements and collision avoidance are critical. The ability to maintain performance even as geometric complexity increases suggests a more robust and reliable planning capability for real-world scenarios that are inherently spatially complex.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Reasoning Efficiency through Adaptive Modality Selection

Beyond mere performance, APIVOT contributes to the field by demonstrating that intelligent, adaptive modality selection can lead to more efficient reasoning. By learning *when* to invoke computationally intensive visual reasoning and when to rely on more efficient language-based semantic reasoning, APIVOT achieves significant reductions in token usage while largely preserving planning success. This has implications for deploying VLMs in resource-constrained robotic systems or scenarios requiring faster decision-making.

#### 5.4. Learned Context-Aware Decision-Making

The analysis of APIVOT's modality selection behavior indicates that it learns to use visual thoughts systematically based on the type of constraint identified at each planning step. This suggests that APIVOT develops a form of context-aware decision-making, where it intelligently assesses the need for visual precision, rather than applying multimodal reasoning uniformly or arbitrarily. This ability to "think in the right modality" based on task demands represents an advancement towards more intelligent and flexible robotic agents.

#### 5.5. Potential for Future Generalization and Applications

The success of APIVOT on a held-out compositional task suggests that the learned planning strategy can generalize to novel combinations of semantic and geometric constraints. This indicates a potential path toward more adaptable general-purpose robot planning. Future work could explore extending this framework to more visually realistic simulators or real-world settings, scaling visual pretraining to broader datasets, incorporating reinforcement learning for optimizing adaptive modality selection, and integrating richer geometric representations (e.g., points, bounding boxes) to enhance planning at different levels of abstraction. These advancements could broaden the applicability of APIVOT's principles to a wider range of complex robotic tasks, from household chores to industrial automation.
