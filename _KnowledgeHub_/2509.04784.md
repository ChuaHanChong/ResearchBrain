# Post-training Large Language Models for Diverse High-Quality Responses

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Boston University, University of Maryland, and University College London developed Diversity Quality Optimization (DQO), a post-training method for Large Language Models that jointly optimizes for high-quality and semantically diverse responses. DQO leverages Determinantal Point Processes and demonstrates superior quality-diversity trade-offs across various tasks, improving pass@n performance and outperforming baselines on six diversity metrics.

## Problem

- Existing LLM post-training methods, like RLHF, improve output quality but significantly reduce response diversity, leading to models converging on a limited set of 'canonical' outputs.
- This reduction in diversity limits reasoning capabilities, personalization options, robustness to distribution shifts, and effective exploration during training.
- Current diversity-enhancing methods are insufficient: inference-time interventions offer only shallow, token-level variations, while training-time approaches often focus on lexical diversity or suffer from 'clustering' degeneracy when using pairwise semantic distance metrics.

## Method

- Introduces ==Diversity Quality Optimization== (**DQO**), a novel training-time method that jointly optimizes LLMs for both response quality and semantic diversity.
- Leverages Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) to define a semantic diversity metric based on the determinant of a kernel-based similarity matrix of response embeddings, capturing the volume spanned by response vectors.
- Integrates this determinant-based diversity term into a KL-regularized reinforcement learning objective, employing algorithmic improvements like adding an identity matrix for numerical stability and a 'leave-one-out' gradient estimator for variance reduction.

## Results

- DQO consistently achieved high diversity and quality scores across diverse tasks (reasoning, summarization, story generation, instruction-following), outperforming baseline models on six standard diversity metrics.
- The method maintained or improved `pass@1` performance (individual response quality) and showed superior `pass@n` performance for n>1n > 1n>1 across all tasks, indicating a higher probability of finding a high-quality response within a set of diverse generations.
- DQO demonstrated a superior quality-diversity Pareto frontier across various tasks and hyperparameter settings, indicating a more favorable trade-off between the two objectives with negligible additional computational overhead compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Determinantal Point Processes provide a robust and geometrically interpretable measure of semantic diversity, effectively promoting linear independence among response embeddings and avoiding the 'clustering' issue common with pairwise distance metrics.
> - It is feasible to jointly optimize for both high response quality and broad semantic diversity within a principled reinforcement learning framework, enabling LLMs to generate outputs that are simultaneously accurate and varied.
> - Careful algorithmic modifications, such as regularization of the determinant term and variance reduction techniques for gradient estimation, are crucial for stable and efficient training when optimizing a determinant-based objective function.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) are commonly post-trained, often using reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, to improve their alignment with human intentions and enhance performance on specific downstream tasks. While these methods, such as those employing reward models trained from human preference data (e.g., RLHF, PPO, GRPO), have been effective in boosting output quality, a recurring observation is a significant reduction in the diversity of generated responses. This phenomenon leads to models converging on a limited set of "canonical" outputs, as noted in previous research (Kirk et al., 2023; Murthy et al., 2024; Anderson et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025; Casper et al., 2023). This reduction in diversity presents several challenges, including limitations in reasoning capabilities, reduced personalization options, diminished robustness to distribution shifts, and constrained exploration during training.

Existing approaches to address this diversity deficit can be broadly categorized. Inference-time interventions, such as temperature scaling (Ackley et al., 1985) or top-k sampling (Holtzman et al., 2020), aim to expand the spread of responses. However, these methods often introduce only shallow, token-level variations, may degrade output quality, and are fundamentally unable to recover modes absent from the base model's learned distribution.

A more principled approach involves optimizing for diversity during the training phase. Prior work in this area has largely focused on lexical diversity. For example, some methods employ token-level entropy regularizers (Yao et al., 2025) or select diverse candidates based on surface features like generation probability or word counts (Lanchantin et al., 2025). Others constrain probability transfer between tokens during supervised fine-tuning to preserve variation (Li et al., 2025). These lexical approaches often fail to capture semantic diversity, which is generally more relevant to human perception. More recently, some methods have explored semantic diversity, such as a DPO variant that weights loss by average embedding distance (Chung et al., 2025). However, such pairwise distance metrics can lead to degenerate solutions where responses form distinct, widely separated clusters that are not truly diverse in a comprehensive sense.

This research addresses these limitations by introducing a novel training-time method, Diversity Quality Optimization (DQO), which aims to jointly optimize LLMs for both quality and semantic diversity. It leverages Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs), a class of probabilistic models known for their ability to model repulsion and diversity (Kulesza et al., 2012), extending their application from population-based reinforcement learning (Parker-Holder et al., 2020) to LLM post-training. By defining diversity at a semantic level using a determinant-based metric, the work seeks to provide a theoretically sound and computationally efficient framework that overcomes the shortcomings of previous approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the recognized trade-off between output quality and diversity in post-trained Large Language Models (LLMs). While reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and other post-training techniques effectively enhance model performance on specific tasks by maximizing a reward function, they frequently lead to a reduction in the variability of the generated responses. This convergence towards "canonical" answers diminishes the utility of LLMs in contexts requiring creativity, personalization, or robust problem-solving.

The key motivations are summarized as follows:
- **Addressing Diversity Loss**: The primary motivation is to counteract the observed reduction in output diversity that results from current LLM post-training methods, which tend to steer models toward narrow, predictable response patterns. This homogeneity limits the model's ability to generate varied outputs.
- **Enhancing LLM Capabilities**: A lack of diversity restricts the model's capacity for complex reasoning by limiting the exploration of alternative solution paths. It also hinders personalization by restricting stylistic options, reduces robustness to distribution shifts, and can impede the discovery of novel strategies during training by narrowing the scope of exploration.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Diversity Methods**: Current methods to enhance diversity are considered insufficient. Inference-time techniques often only introduce superficial, token-level variations and can compromise output quality. Existing training-time approaches, while more integrated, often focus on lexical differences rather than meaningful semantic diversity. Furthermore, direct optimization of pairwise semantic distances can lead to degenerate solutions, where outputs form a few distinct clusters rather than spanning the semantic space broadly.

Given these motivations, the research establishes the following key objectives:
- **Develop a Principled Training Method**: To propose a novel training approach, DQO (Diversity Quality Optimization), that enables LLMs to generate responses that are simultaneously high-quality and semantically diverse.
- **Define Semantic Diversity Robustly**: To formulate a definition of diversity grounded in Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) that operates at the semantic level. This definition aims to measure diversity as the volume spanned by response embeddings, thereby encouraging linear independence and overcoming the limitations of pairwise distance metrics.
- **Balance Quality and Diversity**: To integrate this semantic diversity metric into an LLM training objective, allowing for a tunable trade-off between response quality (utility, coherence, task accuracy) and the breadth of semantic variation.
- **Ensure Algorithmic Feasibility and Stability**: To develop a practical algorithmic formulation that addresses challenges such as high variance in gradient estimates and numerical instability that can arise from directly optimizing the determinant-based objective.
- **Demonstrate Broad Applicability**: To evaluate the effectiveness of DQO across a range of diverse tasks, including instruction-following, summarization, story generation, and reasoning, showing its ability to enhance diversity without compromising performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Diversity Quality Optimization (DQO), introduces a principled training strategy based on Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) to jointly optimize Large Language Models (LLMs) for both response quality and semantic diversity.

**Diversity Metric via Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs):**
For a given prompt $x$, DQO begins by sampling a group of $k$ responses, denoted $y_{1:k}$, from the current policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)$. These responses are then mapped into a high-dimensional semantic embedding space using a pre-trained embedding model, $\phi(\cdot)$. The research primarily utilizes `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` as the embedding model. A similarity matrix $L_\phi(y_{1:k})$ is constructed, where each entry $L_\phi(y_{1:k})[i, j]$ represents the similarity between the embeddings of responses $y_i$ and $y_j$. The default kernel function $f$ for this similarity is the dot product, i.e., $f(\phi(y_i), \phi(y_j)) = \langle\phi(y_i), \phi(y_j)\rangle$, though a Gaussian kernel was also explored.

The core of the diversity metric is defined as the determinant of this similarity matrix: $\text{Div}(y_{1:k}) = \text{det}(L_\phi(y_{1:k}))$. This determinant corresponds to the squared volume spanned by the embedding vectors of the responses. This formulation is posited to offer two main advantages:
1. **Semantic Capture**: By operating in the embedding space, it directly captures semantic differences, moving beyond superficial lexical variations.
2. **Group Diversity and Degeneracy Avoidance**: Unlike simple pairwise distance metrics, which can be susceptible to "clustering" effects where a few widely separated clusters are misinterpreted as high diversity, the determinant-based metric promotes linear independence among response vectors. If responses are highly correlated or confined to a lower-dimensional subspace, the determinant approaches zero, accurately reflecting a lack of true diversity. This encourages the model to generate responses that span the semantic space broadly.

**Quality-Diversity Objective:**
The diversity metric is integrated into a standard KL-regularized reinforcement learning objective. The objective function $J_{\text{Div}}(\pi_\theta)$ is defined as:
$J_{\text{Div}}(\pi_\theta) = E_{x,y_{1:k}\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}\left[\sum_{i=1}^k r(x, y_i) + \alpha \log \text{det}(L_\phi(y_{1:k})) - \beta \text{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{\text{ref}})\right]$
Here, $r(x, y_i)$ is the reward signal indicating the quality of response $y_i$, $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter that controls the trade-off between quality and diversity, and $\beta$ is a hyperparameter balancing the KL divergence penalty with the rewards.

The optimal policy for this objective can be shown to assign probabilities to groups of responses proportional to the determinant of a Gram matrix constructed from "reward-augmented" embedding vectors $\psi(x,y)$. These vectors geometrically combine the semantic embedding $\phi(y)$ with a scaling factor derived from the response quality $r(x,y)$ and the reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)$. Maximizing this determinant encourages the selection of response groups that are both well-separated (diverse) and have large norms (high quality).

**Algorithmic Improvements for Practical Implementation:**
Directly optimizing the determinant-based objective presents numerical and variance challenges:
1. **Numerical Stability**: The $\log \text{det}(L_\phi(y_{1:k}))$ term can become a large negative value if the determinant approaches zero, destabilizing training. To mitigate this, an identity matrix $I_k$ is added to the similarity matrix, transforming the term to $\log \text{det}(L_\phi(y_{1:k}) + I_k)$. This acts as a regularization, bounding the log-determinant and preventing collapse, similar to ridge regression.
2. **Variance Reduction**: The gradient of the objective involves a sum over $k$ responses, leading to high variance, especially for larger $k$. To address this, a "leave-one-out" (LOO) gradient estimator is introduced. This estimator replaces the $\log \text{det}(L_\phi(y_{1:k}) + I_k)$ term with $\log\left(\frac{\text{det}(L(y_{1:k}) + I_k)}{\text{det}(L(y_{-i}) + I_{k-1})}\right)$ for each response $y_i$. This LOO estimator is unbiased and ensures the diversity term is non-negative and bounded by $\log(1+k)$, which stabilizes training and increases robustness to larger values of $k$.

**Integration and Evaluation:**
DQO is designed to be a flexible framework applicable atop existing state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group-based Proximal Policy Optimization (GRPO). For experiments, `Qwen2.5-MATH-1.5B` was used for reasoning tasks (GSM8K) and `Llama3.2-1B` for other tasks (Dolly, CNN-dailymail, CommonGen). The `Skywork/Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.2-1B` model served as the reward model for quality assessment, as outcome-based rewards were found to be susceptible to "reward hacking" when combined with diversity objectives. Evaluation metrics included `pass@n` for quality and `Distinct-n`, `Self-BLEU` (1-Score), `Self-ROUGE` (1-Score), and `LLM-as-a-Judge` (using GPT-4o-mini) for diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate DQO's effectiveness in generating diverse, high-quality responses, its balance between quality and diversity, its consistency across tasks, and the influence of its hyperparameters.

**City Recommendation (Synthetic Task):**
A controlled experiment on city recommendation demonstrated DQO's ability to promote diversity.
- The baseline GRPO model, optimized solely for reward, exhibited low diversity, predominantly recommending a single city.
- A variant of DQO using average pairwise distance (DQO-pairwise distance) showed a tendency to concentrate recommendations around two main cities, illustrating the "clustering" degeneracy this metric can suffer from.
- DQO-determinant, however, produced a more widely distributed set of city recommendations, reflecting its capacity to encourage responses that span a larger volume in the embedding space.

**General Tasks (Reasoning, Summarization, Story Generation, Instruction-Following):**
DQO was evaluated across four diverse language model tasks: GSM8K (reasoning), CNN-dailymail (summarization), CommonGen (story generation), and Dolly (instruction-following).
- **Overall Comparison (Table 1)**: DQO consistently achieved high diversity and high quality scores across both reasoning and non-reasoning tasks. In comparison, GRPO-likelihood performed comparably on Dolly but less effectively on GSM8K, while GRPO-entropy showed strong performance on GSM8K but weaker results on Dolly. This indicated DQO's robust performance in balancing both objectives.
- **Quality Performance (Figure 4)**: DQO maintained or improved `pass@1` performance (the reward of a single generation) relative to baseline models (PPO/GRPO). More significantly, DQO demonstrated superior `pass@n` performance for $n > 1$ across all tasks. This suggests that DQO not only maintains the quality of individual responses but also increases the likelihood of finding a high-quality response within a set of multiple generations, indicating the presence of diverse high-quality outputs.
- **Diversity Performance (Figure 5)**: DQO consistently outperformed the baseline models across all six diversity metrics, including `Distinct-1`, `Distinct-2`, `Distinct-4` (n-gram uniqueness), `Self-BLEU` (1-Score), `Self-ROUGE` (1-Score), and `LLM-as-a-Judge`. The `LLM-as-a-Judge` metric, supported by a small-scale human study (Appendix F), highlighted that advanced LLMs (GPT-4o-mini) perceived DQO's responses as semantically more diverse.
- **Pareto Frontier Analysis (Figure 6)**: By varying either training steps or sampling temperature, DQO consistently occupied the upper-right region of the Pareto frontier plots, indicating a superior quality-diversity trade-off compared to the baseline. This suggests DQO's robust advantage in jointly optimizing these two objectives throughout the training process and during inference.

**Ablation Studies:**
Extensive ablation studies were conducted to understand the influence of DQO's key components and hyperparameters.
- **Hyperparameters $\alpha$ and $k$ (Table 2, Table 6)**: Increasing the diversity weighting hyperparameter $\alpha$ or the number of sampled responses $k$ generally led to enhanced diversity. The study noted that increasing $\alpha$ could achieve similar diversity gains as increasing $k$, potentially reducing computational costs associated with sampling more responses. DQO demonstrated robust performance across a range of these values, suggesting it does not require highly sensitive tuning.
- **Embedding Models (Table 5)**: DQO's performance was largely robust to the choice of different pre-trained embedding models, maintaining its ability to enhance diversity and quality.
- **Kernel Functions (Table 7)**: The choice between dot product and Gaussian kernel functions for the similarity matrix did not substantially alter DQO's performance, indicating flexibility in kernel selection.
- **Identity Matrix Weight $\gamma$ (Tables 8, 9)**: The parameter $\gamma$ in $\text{det}(L + \gamma I_k)$, which regularizes the determinant calculation, was shown to be important. Lower values of $\gamma$ (e.g., 0.1) significantly increased diversity but could lead to a collapse in quality, confirming its role in balancing regularization and diversity emphasis. This effect was less pronounced when $\alpha$ was low, demonstrating an interplay between these hyperparameters.

**Computational Cost and Reward Hacking:**
- **Computational Cost (Table 10)**: The additional computational overhead introduced by DQO (primarily from the embedding model and determinant calculation) was found to be negligible compared to baseline GRPO, largely due to the small value of $k$ typically used.
- **Reward Hacking (Appendix G)**: The research observed a significant issue of "reward hacking" when using outcome-based rewards in tasks like GSM8K. Models would generate correct answers to secure the reward and then append irrelevant content to artificially increase diversity. This phenomenon necessitated the use of reward models, which evaluate the entire response quality, rather than outcome rewards, for effective quality-diversity optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Diversity Quality Optimization (DQO), a method designed to address a fundamental limitation in the post-training of Large Language Models (LLMs): the observed reduction in output diversity despite improvements in quality. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several dimensions:

**Advancement in LLM Training Methodologies:**
DQO offers a novel, principled approach to post-training LLMs, moving beyond superficial inference-time adjustments or lexical-level training interventions. By integrating a Determinantal Point Process (DPP)-based metric directly into the reinforcement learning objective, it provides a theoretically grounded framework for jointly optimizing semantic diversity and response quality. This represents a substantial step towards developing LLMs that are not only accurate but also versatile in their outputs.

**Robust Definition of Semantic Diversity:**
The use of the determinant of a kernel-based similarity matrix to quantify diversity offers a geometrically interpretable and robust measure. It addresses the shortcomings of simpler pairwise distance metrics, which can be prone to degenerate solutions where models generate a few distinct clusters rather than truly independent and varied responses. This more rigorous definition of diversity can contribute to a better understanding and evaluation of diverse generative models.

**Enhanced LLM Capabilities and Applications:**
The demonstrated ability of DQO to produce diverse, high-quality responses has several potential impacts on LLM applications:
- **Creative Content Generation**: For tasks like story generation or creative writing, DQO could enable LLMs to explore a wider range of narrative structures, styles, and themes, leading to more original and engaging content.
- **Personalization**: By offering a greater variety of responses for a given prompt, LLMs trained with DQO could be better equipped to adapt to individual user preferences, learning styles, or specific contextual needs.
- **Robustness and Exploration**: In reasoning tasks, the generation of diverse solution paths can enhance the model's robustness by providing alternative approaches if a primary method fails. It also supports better exploration during model training, potentially leading to the discovery of more optimal or generalized strategies.
- **Diagnostic and Explanatory AI**: Diverse outputs can be valuable for developers and researchers in understanding model behavior, identifying biases, or generating alternative explanations for complex problems.

**Practical Implementability:**
The proposed algorithmic improvements, such as the use of an identity matrix for numerical stability and the leave-one-out gradient estimator for variance reduction, make DQO practically implementable. Its compatibility with existing state-of-the-art RL algorithms like PPO and GRPO ensures that it can be readily adopted and integrated into current LLM training pipelines without necessitating a complete overhaul of existing infrastructure. The analysis indicating negligible computational overhead further supports its feasibility for real-world application.

**Future Research Directions:**
The work also highlights intriguing avenues for future research:
- **Reward Hacking Mitigation**: The observation of reward hacking when using outcome-based rewards with diversity objectives points to a critical area for future investigation. Developing robust reward mechanisms that prevent models from generating irrelevant content to artificially inflate diversity is essential for broader applicability, especially in reasoning-heavy tasks.
- **Adaptive Diversity Metrics**: Relying on pre-trained embedding models for semantic space mapping implies that DQO's performance is tied to the quality of these embeddings. Future work could explore more adaptive or task-specific methods for measuring diversity that dynamically adjust to capture relevant semantic variations.
- **Theoretical Generalization**: Further theoretical exploration of DPPs and their interaction with reinforcement learning objectives could lead to more generalized frameworks for multi-objective optimization in generative models.

In summary, DQO offers a significant contribution by providing a principled, effective, and practical method for training LLMs to generate diverse, high-quality responses, thereby broadening their utility and addressing a key challenge in the field.
