# Refinement via Regeneration: Enlarging Modification Space Boosts Image Refinement in Unified Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Tencent HY introduce Refinement via Regeneration (RvR), a new approach for improving text-to-image (T2I) outputs by reframing refinement as conditional image regeneration. This method, which discards editing instructions and relaxes pixel-level consistency, achieves state-of-the-art performance, boosting scores on benchmarks like Geneval to 0.91 and UniGenBench++ to 77.41.

## Problem

- Text-to-Image (T2I) generation struggles with reliably adhering to complex prompts involving multiple objects, intricate attributes, or fine-grained relationships, often leading to semantic misalignments.
- Existing UMM-based "Refinement via Editing" (RvE) methods rely on coarse, incomplete editing instructions, leading to error accumulation and suboptimal corrections.
- The RvE paradigm unnecessarily restricts the modification space by enforcing strict pixel-level consistency, hindering the model's ability to make holistic, semantically correct adjustments.

## Method

- The authors propose **Refinement via Regeneration (RvR)**, reformulating image refinement as a ==conditional image regeneration task== within ==Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs)==.
- A new ==data construction pipeline== generates diverse prompt-image pairs and selects misaligned and aligned images without content-consistency constraints, using LLMs and VLMs.
- The training and inference strategy ==discards low-level VAE tokens== of the input image from conditioning to remove bias towards appearance preservation, instead relying on the target prompt and high-level semantic tokens.

## Results

- RvR achieved state-of-the-art performance across T2I benchmarks: boosting **Geneval from 0.78 (base BAGEL) to 0.91**, **DPGBench from 84.02 to 87.21**, and **UniGenBench++ from 61.53 to 77.41**.
- Qualitative results demonstrate RvR's ability to correct complex semantic misalignments such as object quantity, relative position, world knowledge, and negation grammar more effectively than previous methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the performance gains are primarily due to the RvR training approach and its enlarged modification space; incorporating editing data or VAE conditioning actually **degrades** refinement performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating refinement as conditional image regeneration, rather than editing, significantly enlarges the modification space, allowing for more comprehensive semantic corrections.
> - Discarding intermediate editing instructions and strict pixel-level preservation prevents error accumulation and enables the model to revise any region hindering prompt satisfaction.
> - The system can intelligently reuse compatible semantics from the initial image while discarding conflicting ones, demonstrating robustness and effective leveraging of input context without being constrained by it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Text-to-Image (T2I) generation has advanced significantly, enabling the synthesis of high-fidelity images from natural language prompts. However, a persistent challenge lies in reliably adhering to complex prompts that involve multiple objects, intricate attributes, or fine-grained relationships. This often leads to semantic misalignments between the generated image and the user's intent.

To address these limitations, a growing area of research focuses on refinement approaches, particularly leveraging Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). UMMs integrate capabilities for visual understanding, generation, and often editing within a single framework. This unified architecture allows them to analyze an initially generated image in the context of a target prompt and subsequently improve it.

Previous UMM-based refinement methods have largely adopted a "Refinement via Editing" (RvE) paradigm. In this approach, a UMM first identifies prompt-image semantic mismatches and then generates explicit or implicit editing instructions. These instructions guide the UMM to perform localized image modifications, aiming to correct identified errors while preserving other parts of the image. This work positions itself by identifying inherent limitations within the RvE paradigm and proposing an alternative, "Refinement via Regeneration" (RvR), to achieve more complete semantic alignment and expand the potential performance ceiling for T2I refinement. It aims to demonstrate that a regeneration-based approach can yield superior results compared to editing-based methods for image refinement tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more effective framework for refining images generated by Text-to-Image (T2I) models, particularly for complex prompts where initial generations may contain semantic misalignments. The authors aim to improve prompt-image alignment by addressing the limitations observed in existing UMM-based refinement methods, which predominantly follow a "Refinement via Editing" (RvE) paradigm.

The key motivations for proposing a new approach are rooted in two identified limitations of RvE:

1. **Coarse and Incomplete Editing Instructions:** In the RvE paradigm, a Unified Multimodal Model (UMM) first generates an editing instruction that summarizes the semantic mismatch between the initial image and the target prompt. The authors argue that these intermediate editing instructions are often coarse, incomplete, or ambiguous. For example, an instruction like "include a third bench" might address only one aspect of a misalignment, failing to specify other necessary corrections such as removing extra armrests, adjusting layout for composition, or harmonizing the appearance of existing elements. This incompleteness can lead to error accumulation, as the subsequent image editing step is guided by an imperfect specification, potentially resulting in suboptimal refinement.

2. **Unnecessary Restriction of Modification Space:** The editing formulation inherently enforces strict pixel-level consistency in unedited regions. While this constraint is crucial for traditional image editing tasks (where specific, localized changes are desired while preserving the rest of the content), it is considered unnecessary and restrictive for image *refinement*. The goal of refinement is to produce a final image that better aligns with the target prompt, which may sometimes necessitate broader structural changes or re-composition rather than just localized edits. Forcing pixel-level preservation in areas not explicitly targeted by an editing instruction can limit the model's ability to make more holistic, semantically correct adjustments. For instance, if adding an object requires significant spatial rearrangement, strict preservation constraints might result in unnaturally small or visually discordant insertions. The authors contend that refinement should prioritize semantic correctness and overall visual plausibility, even if it requires modifications beyond localized changes.

Motivated by these observations, the paper proposes Refinement via Regeneration (RvR) to overcome these inherent limitations. The objective of RvR is to reformulate refinement as conditional image regeneration, thereby discarding the reliance on intermediate editing instructions and relaxing the constraint of strict pixel-level content preservation, which is hypothesized to enlarge the effective modification space and enable more complete semantic alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on reformulating image refinement from an editing task to a conditional image regeneration task within Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). The approach involves a novel data construction pipeline and a distinct training and inference strategy.

**Background on Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) and Refinement via Editing (RvE):**
UMMs, such as BAGEL, integrate visual understanding, image generation, and image editing capabilities. They typically use a semantic visual encoder (e.g., Vision Transformer, ViT) to extract high-level semantic features ($Z_{\text{ViT}}$) from an image for understanding and a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to map images into low-level latent tokens ($Z_{\text{VAE}}$) for generative modeling.

Existing Refinement via Editing (RvE) methods operate in two stages:
1. **Instruction Generation:** The UMM compares an input image ($I$) with a target prompt ($T_{\text{prompt}}$) and generates an editing instruction ($\hat{T}_{\text{edit}}$) describing necessary modifications. This uses the UMM's image understanding capability: $\hat{T}_{\text{edit}} = M(T_{\text{prompt}}, Z_{\text{ViT}})$.
2. **Image Editing:** The UMM then edits the image ($I$) using the generated instruction ($\hat{T}_{\text{edit}}$), conditioned on both semantic ($Z_{\text{ViT}}$) and low-level VAE tokens ($Z_{\text{VAE}}$) of the original image: $\hat{I}' = M(\hat{T}_{\text{edit}}, Z_{\text{ViT}}, Z_{\text{VAE}})$.
Training involves an autoregressive text loss for instruction generation and a flow matching (FM) objective for image editing, where $Z_{\text{VAE}}$ of the original image is a conditioning input.

**Critique of RvE Constraints and Rationale for RvR:**
The paper argues that RvE's reliance on intermediate editing instructions introduces potential errors due to their incompleteness, and the enforcement of pixel-level consistency via $Z_{\text{VAE}}$ conditioning unnecessarily restricts the modification space for refinement. Image refinement, unlike editing, should prioritize overall semantic alignment with the prompt, allowing for more extensive changes if needed.

**Refinement via Regeneration (RvR) Formulation:**
RvR reformulates refinement as conditional image regeneration. Instead of generating editing instructions or strictly preserving input content, RvR regenerates images conditioned directly on the target prompt and the semantic tokens of the initial image. This is intended to enlarge the modification space, enabling the model to revise any regions that hinder prompt satisfaction.

**RvR Data Construction:**
The data construction pipeline for RvR is simplified and scalable, focusing on semantic alignment rather than strict content consistency. It consists of three steps:
1. **Prompt Generation:** A Large Language Model (LLM, e.g., Gemini) generates diverse textual prompts covering various semantic dimensions (e.g., style, world knowledge, quantity), with each prompt incorporating 1-5 randomly selected dimensions.
2. **Image Generation:** For each prompt, multiple T2I generators (e.g., BAGEL, GPT-4o) independently synthesize candidate images. This step explicitly avoids content-consistency constraints, allowing for diverse outputs relative to the prompt.
3. **Alignment Evaluation:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM, e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro) evaluates the semantic alignment of each candidate prompt-image pair. Finally, for each prompt, one "misaligned" image ($I$) and one "aligned" image ($I'$) are selected to form a training triplet $\langle I, I', T \rangle$. The aligned image is not necessarily an edited version of the misaligned image, but rather an independently generated image that better fits the prompt.

**RvR Pipeline: Training and Inference:**
A crucial aspect of RvR is the use of a specific **system prompt** during both training and inference: "Analyze the potential misalignment between the generated image and the user’s prompt. Then, re-generate the image to precisely align with the user’s prompt." This prompt defines the UMM's role as a regenerator, not an editor.

- **Training:** RvR takes four inputs: the system prompt ($T_{\text{system}}$), the semantic tokens ($Z_{\text{ViT}}$) of the misaligned image ($I$), the user prompt ($T_{\text{T2I}}$), and the noisy VAE tokens ($x_t$) of the *aligned* image ($I'$). The UMM predicts the velocity field and is optimized with the flow matching (FM) objective.
 - **Key Distinction:** Unlike RvE (Eq. 8), RvR's training formulation *discards* the low-level $Z_{\text{VAE}}$ tokens of the input image from the conditioning context (Eq. 9). This is done to remove the bias towards appearance preservation and encourage the model to make larger, semantically driven modifications. The prompt used for conditioning is $T_{\text{prompt}} = T_{\text{system}} \oplus T_{\text{T2I}}$.

- **Inference:** Given a target prompt ($T_{\text{prompt}}$) and a misaligned image ($I$), RvR directly regenerates an improved image ($\hat{I}'$) conditioned on the combined prompt and the semantic tokens ($Z_{\text{ViT}}$) extracted from $I$: $\hat{I}' = M(T_{\text{prompt}}, Z_{\text{ViT}})$.
 - **Advantages:** This formulation avoids error accumulation from intermediate editing instructions and operates solely on high-level semantic representations, removing unnecessary content-preservation constraints.

**Implementation Details:**
RvR is built upon BAGEL, trained using 16 NVIDIA H800 GPUs for 15K steps. The training data combines 100k RvR refinement samples, 60k text-to-image samples (from BLIP-3o) to preserve basic T2I ability, and 1k image understanding samples (from BAGEL repository) to maintain visual reasoning. These are mixed in a 2:1:1 ratio. Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is applied during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of Refinement via Regeneration (RvR) compared to existing methods.

**Qualitative Comparison:**
RvR exhibits advantages in correcting various semantic misalignments. Examples provided illustrate improvements in:
- **Object Quantity:** Correctly rendering "four clocks" when the initial image had a different count.
- **Relative Position:** Accurately placing "a bed right of a frisbee" where previous methods preserved an irrelevant window, preventing correct placement.
- **World Knowledge:** Correctly depicting "physicist Newton sitting under an apple tree."
- **Negation Grammar:** Interpreting "has no tail" for a cat with butterfly wings.
- **Object Composition:** Properly composing elements like the "Eiffel Tower composed of countless shining, translucent jellyfish."

The qualitative analysis suggests that RvR's larger modification space allows it to address misalignments more comprehensively, often involving broader structural changes rather than localized edits, leading to images that are semantically correct, spatially coherent, and better aligned with the target prompt.

**Quantitative Comparison:**
RvR was evaluated on three established T2I benchmarks: Geneval, DPGBench, and UniGenBench++.
- **Geneval:** RvR boosted the score from 0.78 (base BAGEL) to 0.91, outperforming existing RvE methods (e.g., UiG at 0.80, Uni-CoT at 0.83, IRG at 0.85).
- **DPGBench:** RvR improved the score from 84.02 (base BAGEL) to 87.21, also surpassing RvE methods (e.g., UiG at 85.11, Uni-CoT at 83.17).
- **UniGenBench++:** RvR achieved a significant improvement from 61.53 (base BAGEL) to 77.41, again outperforming RvE methods (e.g., UiG at 64.91, Uni-CoT at 69.86).

These results indicate that RvR consistently achieves leading performance across benchmarks, particularly for prompts involving compositional and dense semantics. The improvements are attributed to the enlarged modification space, which allows for more effective semantic correction than strict pixel-level consistency constraints. The BAGEL-RvR model also demonstrated state-of-the-art performance when compared with other T2I models and unified multimodal models.

**Multi-round Generation:**
The experiments demonstrated that RvR supports iterative refinement:
- **Further Correction of Misalignments:** For semantics that were not fully aligned after the first round (e.g., a "brown orange" that was partially non-brown, or an unexpected duplicated Saturn for a "whale's body consists of glowing nebulae in Saturn's rings"), additional RvR iterations led to further correction.
- **Preservation of Aligned Semantics:** When semantics were already correctly aligned after the first round, subsequent rounds preserved these correct results while potentially improving minor visual details (e.g., refining a bench with one armrest into a more natural bench without armrests).

**Robustness to Initial Image Semantics:**
Experiments investigated RvR's ability to selectively reuse or discard semantics from the initial image:
- **Reusing Compatible Semantics:** When initial images contained elements compatible with the prompt (e.g., grass for a "dog is lying," city background for a "spaceship is flying"), RvR preserved these unmentioned but contextually appropriate elements in the refined images. This suggests RvR leverages compatible semantics from the input image.
- **Discarding Conflicting Semantics:** When initial image semantics strongly conflicted with the prompt (e.g., grass and trees for a "shark in the sea," a city scene for a "waterfall in a jungle"), RvR discarded these conflicting elements and generated new content aligned with the prompt. This indicates strong pipeline robustness and the capability to make significant semantic transformations when necessary.

**Ablation Studies (on DPGBench):**
- **Refinement Training Benefit:** The primary performance gain of RvR was attributed to the refinement training process itself, rather than solely improvements in basic T2I generation. The BAGEL-RvR-T2I model, evaluated for its T2I capability, showed performance comparable to the original BAGEL model (84.08 vs. 84.02), indicating T2I capability was preserved but not the main source of refinement improvement.
- **RvR vs. SFT:** Conducting Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on only the aligned image-prompt pairs (converting refinement triplets to T2I pairs) yielded only a minor improvement over BAGEL (84.62 vs. 84.02), suggesting that the explicit refinement mechanism of RvR, rather than just higher-quality finetuning data, is responsible for the substantial gains.
- **Editing Data Degradation:** Incorporating additional editing data (where source and target images have strong pixel-level consistency) into RvR training led to a performance drop (85.70 vs. 87.21). This suggests that the pixel-level preservation bias inherent in editing data restricts the modification space needed for effective semantic correction in RvR.
- **VAE Conditioning Degradation:** Introducing low-level VAE tokens of the input image into RvR's conditioning context also slightly harmed performance (86.41 vs. 87.21). This validates the design choice to remove $Z_{\text{VAE}}$ conditioning, as these features are largely irrelevant to the goal of semantic correction in a regeneration context and can inappropriately bias the model towards appearance preservation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Refinement via Regeneration (RvR) as an alternative paradigm to existing Refinement via Editing (RvE) methods for improving text-to-image (T2I) generation within unified multimodal models (UMMs). Its significance lies in challenging the conventional understanding of image refinement as an editing task and demonstrating the advantages of a regeneration-based approach.

The primary impact is the **redefinition of image refinement**. By reframing refinement as conditional image regeneration, RvR moves beyond the limitations of generating explicit editing instructions and enforcing strict pixel-level consistency. This shift enables a "larger modification space," allowing the model to make more comprehensive semantic corrections rather than being restricted to localized, appearance-preserving edits. This directly addresses a critical bottleneck in T2I generation, where models struggle with complex, compositional prompts leading to semantic misalignments.

The consistently superior performance across multiple T2I benchmarks (Geneval, DPGBench, UniGenBench++) suggests that regeneration is a more effective and principled foundation for image refinement within UMMs. This finding could influence the design of future multimodal models and refinement pipelines, encouraging a focus on holistic semantic understanding and transformation rather than incremental modifications.

The methodology's robustness, demonstrated by its ability to iteratively improve results and intelligently reuse compatible semantics while discarding conflicting ones, indicates a strong foundation for building reliable and flexible image generation systems. The ablation studies further reinforce the importance of these architectural and training choices, specifically highlighting that editing-specific constraints can hinder overall refinement performance.

Potentially, RvR could lead to T2I systems that are more adept at following intricate user prompts, reducing the need for multiple generation attempts or manual post-editing. This could enhance the user experience for various applications, from creative content generation to specialized design tasks. Furthermore, the underlying principles of RvR—emphasizing high-level semantic conditioning and a wider modification scope—may find applications in other generative AI tasks that require substantial content transformation or correction based on abstract directives. The work thus contributes to the ongoing development of more intelligent and adaptable multimodal AI systems capable of deeper contextual understanding and more precise content generation.
