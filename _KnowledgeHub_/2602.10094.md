# 4RC: 4D Reconstruction via Conditional Querying Anytime and Anywhere

> [!summary] Summary
> 4RC, developed by researchers at Nanyang Technological University and the University of Oxford, presents a unified, feed-forward framework for 4D reconstruction from monocular video, allowing conditional querying of dense geometry and motion from a compact latent representation. The system demonstrated improvements in dense tracking and camera pose estimation compared to previous methods, operating efficiently across diverse dynamic scenes.

## Problem

- Existing 4D reconstruction methods often decouple 3D geometry and motion estimation, leading to fragmented, multi-stage pipelines that are computationally intensive.
- Current feed-forward 4D approaches are restricted to pairwise views, specific reference frames, or struggle with modeling long-term and complex non-rigid motion.
- Prior work frequently relies on iterative optimization or expensive global aggregation schemes, contributing to high computational costs and slow inference times.

## Method

- Introduces **4RC**, a unified, feed-forward Vision Transformer-based architecture that processes an entire video sequence to generate a compact ==4D spatio-temporal latent== representation.
- Employs a ==conditional decoder== for an "encode-once, query-anywhere and anytime" paradigm, allowing flexible retrieval of dense 3D geometry and motion for any query frame at any target timestamp.
- Utilizes a factorized 4D representation, decomposing each frame's attributes into a viewpoint-invariant base 3D geometry and time-dependent relative 3D displacements, to enhance temporal consistency.

## Results

- Achieved a **36%** improvement in Average Percentage of Points (APD) on the Waymo dataset for dense tracking compared to V-DPM.
- Obtained the best Absolute Translation Error (ATE) and Relative Translation Error (RPEt_tt) on the TUM-dynamics dataset for camera pose estimation, outperforming specialized 3D reconstruction methods.
- Demonstrated strong performance in 3D sparse point tracking, outperforming SpatialTrackerV2 on 3 out of 4 WorldTrack benchmark datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling dense geometry and motion dynamics within a single feed-forward network, rather than through decoupled or multi-stage pipelines, significantly improves efficiency and completeness for 4D reconstruction.
> - A minimally factorized 4D representation, separating base geometry from explicit 3D displacements, simplifies the learning task and maintains temporal consistency across dynamic scenes.
> - Conditioning a decoder on learned time tokens from a global latent representation enables flexible and efficient querying of specific 3D geometry and motion information for arbitrary timestamps and viewpoints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D reconstruction has advanced significantly, transitioning from classical geometric pipelines such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) to more recent learning-based approaches, exemplified by models like DUSt3R and VGGT. These contemporary methods facilitate direct feed-forward inference of dense 3D geometry, enhancing efficiency, scalability, and generalization in general-purpose 3D perception. However, the majority of these advancements primarily address static scene geometry.

Real-world environments are characterized by dynamic elements, necessitating systems that can model not only 3D structure but also its evolution over time. This requirement motivates the task of 4D reconstruction, which aims to jointly capture 3D geometry and motion. Such representations are foundational for applications including video synthesis, scene understanding, and robotics, where understanding object trajectories, deformations, and interactions is critical.

Current approaches to 4D reconstruction exhibit fragmentation and limitations in flexibility. A common strategy involves decomposing the problem into sequential subtasks, typically separating motion estimation from 3D reconstruction. For instance, methods like SpatialTracker and MonST3R involve staged processes, often requiring iterative refinement or post-hoc optimization, and frequently yield only sparse 3D trajectories. While some feed-forward methods, such as ST4RTrack and Dynamic Point Map, have emerged for direct 4D prediction, they are often restricted to pairwise views, limiting their ability to model long-term or complex motion. Other concurrent efforts, including TraceAnything, which uses B´ezier curves for motion, may compromise geometric quality for trajectory smoothness. Any4D predicts scene flow for only the first frame, lacking flexibility for motion from arbitrary viewpoints, and V-DPM, while extending 4D reconstruction, is noted for slow inference and limited flexibility due to its reliance on aggregating information from all views.

4RC aims to address these limitations by proposing a unified, feed-forward framework that integrates both geometry and motion modeling within a single network. It seeks to provide a comprehensive and flexible 4D representation that supports querying geometry and motion across arbitrary frames and timestamps, departing from multi-stage pipelines, sparse outputs, or constrained motion predictions of existing methods. This work positions itself as a development towards more unified and efficient 4D scene understanding systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified, feed-forward model, named 4RC (4D Reconstruction via Conditional Querying Anytime and Anywhere), capable of performing complete and flexible 4D reconstruction from monocular video sequences. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and addresses perceived limitations in the current state of 4D reconstruction:

1. **Addressing Fragmentation and Decoupling:** Existing 4D reconstruction methods frequently decouple the estimation of motion from 3D geometry. This often leads to multi-stage pipelines that are computationally intensive, require iterative refinement, and may produce limited 4D attributes, such as sparse 3D trajectories or two-view scene flow. The motivation is to overcome this fragmentation by developing a single network that jointly models both dense scene geometry and motion dynamics.

2. **Enhancing Flexibility and Completeness:** Many current feed-forward 4D methods are restricted in their scope. For example, some predict only pairwise motion or motion relative to a fixed reference frame (e.g., the first frame). Other approaches might struggle with long-term or complex motion patterns or compromise geometric accuracy to achieve motion smoothness. The objective is to create a system that can flexibly query dense 3D geometry and motion from any source frame to any target timestamp within the video sequence, providing a more complete 4D understanding of dynamic scenes.

3. **Improving Efficiency through a Unified Feed-Forward Approach:** The reliance on iterative optimization or expensive global aggregation schemes in prior work contributes to high computational costs and slow inference. A key motivation for 4RC is to achieve real-time or near real-time performance by employing a fully feed-forward architecture. This aims to eliminate the need for auxiliary estimators or per-scene optimization, streamlining the 4D reconstruction process.

4. **Developing a Holistic and Compact 4D Representation:** Directly predicting full 3D point clouds for every frame at every possible timestamp is redundant and computationally prohibitive. The researchers are motivated to learn a holistic and compact 4D representation that efficiently encodes the entire video sequence into a latent space. This latent representation should serve as a centralized source from which geometry and motion can be efficiently queried and decoded.

5. **Introducing a Novel "Encode-Once, Query-Anywhere and Anytime" Paradigm:** To support the desired flexibility and efficiency, the research aims to establish a paradigm where the video is encoded once into a spatio-temporal latent space. Subsequently, a conditional decoder can efficiently retrieve specific 3D geometry and motion information for any query frame at any desired target timestamp without re-processing the entire video or performing additional optimization steps.

6. **Proposing a Minimally Factorized Output Formulation:** To manage the complexity of representing dynamic scenes, the work is motivated to adopt a factorized representation. This involves decomposing per-view 4D attributes into a viewpoint-invariant base geometry and time-dependent relative motion, parameterized as 3D displacements. This factorization aims to provide clear motion cues, avoid redundant geometric re-prediction, and maintain temporal consistency, particularly in static or rigidly moving regions.

In summary, 4RC aims to provide a robust, efficient, and versatile solution for 4D reconstruction, overcoming the limitations of fragmentation, inflexibility, and computational overhead present in existing methods by integrating geometry and motion into a unified, query-based feed-forward framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

4RC adopts a unified, feed-forward transformer-based architecture to reconstruct the full 4D attributes of a dynamic scene from a monocular video. The methodology revolves around an "encode-once, query-anywhere and anytime" paradigm, utilizing a compact 4D representation and a conditional decoder.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to recover time-indexed 3D point maps, $P^{t_i \to \tau}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$, which denote the 3D positions of points observed in a query frame $I_i$ (at time $t_i$) as they appear at an arbitrary target timestamp $\tau$. When $\tau = t_i$, this represents the static 3D geometry of the frame. When $\tau \ne t_i$, it describes the dynamic point map corresponding to point motion.

#### 3.2. Factorized 4D Attributes

To avoid redundant prediction for all possible source-target pairs, 4RC employs a factorized representation: $P^{t_i \to \tau}_i = P^{t_i}_i + \Delta P^{t_i \to \tau}_i$.
- $P^{t_i}_i$: Represents the base 3D geometry at time $t_i$. This is reconstructed from image $I_i$ under a perspective camera model.
- $\Delta P^{t_i \to \tau}_i$: Represents the 3D displacement from time $t_i$ to $\tau$. This explicitly captures temporal motion.
All point maps are viewpoint-invariant and expressed in a world coordinate system defined by the first frame's camera. This factorization facilitates temporal consistency and avoids re-predicting complex geometry at every timestep.

#### 3.3. 4D Representation Encoder

The encoder, $E$, processes the input video sequence $V = \{I_i\}^N_{i=1}$ to generate a unified 4D representation, $F = E(V)$.
- **Architecture:** A Vision Transformer (ViT)-based architecture, similar to DINOv2, is employed. It alternates between frame-wise and global self-attention.
- **Input Augmentation:** Each view's patchified tokens are augmented with a dedicated *camera token* (similar to VGGT for camera geometry) and a *time token* $T_i$. The time token aggregates temporal information specific to that view and serves as a conditioning signal for motion decoding.
- **Output:** The encoder produces $F = \{F_i\}^N_{i=1}$, where each $F_i$ consists of $M$ patch tokens, an encoded camera token $\hat{C}_i$, and a time token $\hat{T}_i$.

#### 3.4. Conditional 4D Decoder

The unified 4D latent representation $F$ is then processed by a conditional decoder with two main heads:

- **Geometry Head ($D_g$):**
 - **Function:** Recovers the base 3D geometry for each input frame.
 - **Input:** The encoded spatial tokens $\hat{Z}_i$ (from $F_i$) and camera tokens $\hat{C}_i$.
 - **Output:** Predicts the per-frame depth map ($\hat{D}_i$), ray map ($\hat{R}_i$), and camera parameters ($\hat{\theta}_i$). The base point map $P^{t_i}_i$ is then derived from these outputs.
 - **Design:** Follows a dual-DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer) design, augmented with a lightweight camera head.

- **Motion Head ($D_m$):**
 - **Function:** Recovers dense 3D displacement fields for any query frame $I_q$ at a target timestamp $\tau$.
 - **Architecture:** A lightweight transformer-based decoder with $K$ layers of alternating self-attention and cross-attention.
 - **Query Initialization:** Query tokens $\hat{Z}_q$ are initialized from the encoder's output $F$.
 - **Conditioning:** The target time is conditioned by injecting the time embedding $\hat{T}_{\tau}$ (from $F_{\tau}$) via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) in the self-attention blocks. Cross-attention is then applied to the target spatial token set $\hat{Z}_{\tau}$ to obtain context.
 - **Output:** Generates the dense 3D displacement field $\Delta \hat{P}^{t_q \to \tau}_q$. This design supports both dense motion estimation and point tracking.

#### 3.5. Training Scheme

4RC is trained end-to-end with joint supervision for geometry and motion attributes.
- **Scale Normalization:** Ground-truth scene scale is normalized such that the average Euclidean distance of valid 3D points to the origin is 1.
- **Overall Objective:** The total loss is $L = L_{depth} + L_{ray} + L_{cam} + L_{motion}$.
- **Uncertainty Formulation:** An aleatoric uncertainty formulation, $\ell(\hat{y}, y, \Sigma)$, is used for most loss terms, where $\Sigma$ is a predicted pixel-wise uncertainty map that adaptively down-weights unreliable regions.
- **Gradient-Based Constraints:**
 - **Geometry:** Spatial smoothness is enforced on predicted depth maps $\hat{D}$ using image-space gradients ($\nabla_x \hat{D}$) within $L_{depth}$.
 - **Motion:** An additional temporal gradient term ($\nabla_t \Delta \hat{P}$) is incorporated into $L_{motion}$ to constrain the first-order temporal derivative of displacement (velocity), promoting temporally consistent motion.
- **Datasets:** Training uses a diverse collection of large-scale public datasets covering dynamic and static scenes, both synthetic and real-world (e.g., PointOdyssey, Dynamic Replica, Waymo, Kubric, DL3DV, ScanNet++, MVS-Synth).
- **Implementation Details:** The encoder uses a DINOv2-based ViT-Giant. Encoder and geometry decoder are initialized with pretrained weights from DA3. Input images are resized to varying resolutions and aspect ratios for generalization. Training sequence lengths vary from 2 to 18 frames. Motion supervision employs a probabilistic sampling strategy, focusing on larger displacements for 80% of iterations. AdamW optimizer is used for 50 epochs on 16 A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated 4RC across various 4D reconstruction tasks, comparing its performance against prior and concurrent methods.

#### 4.1. 4D Reconstruction Performance

- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations demonstrate 4RC's ability to model complex motion patterns, including occlusions, non-rigid motion, and large movements (e.g., motorcycle tracking on DAVIS dataset). The method also generates accurate geometry reconstruction and motion modeling in diverse in-the-wild videos, covering both static and dynamic scenes.
- **Dense Tracking:** Quantitative evaluation on Kubric and Waymo test sets, using the middle view as a query, indicates that 4RC achieves competitive performance among concurrent 4D reconstruction methods. On the Waymo dataset, which features dynamic scenes, 4RC demonstrated a 36% improvement in Average Percentage of Points (APD) compared to V-DPM. This performance is achieved using a flexible per-frame decoding mechanism, in contrast to V-DPM's global aggregation, which has higher computational costs.
- **Sparse Point Tracking:** On the WorldTrack benchmark datasets (PointOdyssey, Dynamic Replica, Aerial Digital Twin, Panoptic Studio), 4RC exhibits strong performance in 3D sparse point tracking, even when compared to methods specifically designed for point tracking, such as CoTracker3 and SpatialTrackerV2. 4RC outperforms SpatialTrackerV2 on 3 out of 4 datasets in this benchmark. An extended evaluation using global median scale alignment further corroborates 4RC's strong performance, positioning it as a competitive method on 4 out of 6 datasets under this protocol.

#### 4.2. 3D Reconstruction Performance

- **Camera Pose Estimation:** On the TUM-dynamics and ScanNet datasets, 4RC achieves strong camera pose estimation results. On the TUM-dynamics dataset, it obtains the best Absolute Translation Error (ATE) and Relative Translation Error (RPE$_t$) among all evaluated methods, including specialized 3D reconstruction methods like Pi3. This indicates that the unified 4D representation effectively supports accurate camera trajectory estimation. 4RC also performs well among concurrent feed-forward 4D reconstruction methods.
- **Multi-View Reconstruction:** Evaluated on 7-Scenes and NRGBD datasets, 4RC demonstrates competitive performance in scene-level multi-view 3D reconstruction. It achieves superior accuracy (Acc) and completeness (Comp) on 7-Scenes and the best Normal Consistency (NC) on NRGBD among 4D reconstruction methods. For example, its accuracy on 7-Scenes (0.034) is notably better than TraceAnything's (0.240), suggesting that 4RC maintains geometric quality while modeling motion.
- **Depth Estimation:** 4RC explicitly predicts depth maps as part of its factorized 4D representation. On the Bonn dataset, 4RC achieves the best $\delta < 1.25$ score and matches the second-best Relative error. On Sintel and KITTI datasets, its performance is competitive with specialized 3D reconstruction methods like Pi3, despite not being exclusively trained on the same large-scale 3D datasets, and notably outperforms many general video depth estimation approaches.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Motion Head Design:** Experiments on disabling components of the motion head (cross-attention, self-attention, time-token conditioned AdaLN) consistently showed performance degradation. Removing either the cross-attention or self-attention module resulted in the largest drops, indicating their necessity for effective motion decoding. Qualitative observations suggest that cross-attention is crucial for modeling complex non-rigid motions and capturing fine details, preventing over-smoothed trajectories.
- **Factorized Motion Representation:** Comparing 4RC's displacement-based factorization with direct point prediction (either in world or local camera coordinates) demonstrated the effectiveness of the factorized approach. Both point-based variants performed worse. This indicates that explicitly decoupling static geometry from time-dependent motion via displacement fields reduces learning difficulty, avoids redundant geometry recomputation, and improves temporal consistency.

#### 4.4. Streaming 4RC (S-4RC)

A streaming variant, S-4RC, built on the STream3R architecture for causal and online 4D reconstruction, was introduced. S-4RC operates sequentially and caches 4D latent representations, enabling flexible motion queries to past timestamps and point tracking to the current time. It achieved competitive performance compared to the offline 4RC, demonstrating its feasibility for online applications without access to global temporal context.

#### 4.5. Limitations

The paper acknowledges limitations, including challenges in geometric recovery in extreme depth regions, with transparent objects, or floating artifacts, and observed performance degradation in scenes with highly chaotic or extreme motion, which is attributed to insufficient diversity in current motion annotation datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "4RC: 4D Reconstruction via Conditional Querying Anytime and Anywhere" contributes to the field of computer vision and machine learning in several ways:

1. **Advancement in 4D Reconstruction:** 4RC offers a unified and feed-forward framework for 4D reconstruction from monocular videos, addressing the limitations of prior methods that often decouple geometry and motion or provide restricted 4D attributes. By jointly modeling dense scene geometry and motion dynamics within a single network, it streamlines the reconstruction process and enhances the completeness of the output.

2. **Novel Querying Paradigm:** The "encode-once, query-anywhere and anytime" paradigm represents a notable development. It allows for efficient and flexible retrieval of 3D geometry and motion from a compact spatio-temporal latent representation, supporting arbitrary query frames and target timestamps. This flexibility contrasts with methods limited to pairwise views or specific reference frames.

3. **Robustness and Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate that 4RC achieves competitive performance across a range of 3D and 4D reconstruction tasks, including camera pose estimation, video depth prediction, point cloud reconstruction, 3D point tracking, and dense motion modeling. Its strong performance on challenging datasets, including those with highly dynamic scenes, indicates the robustness of the proposed architecture and its factorized 4D representation.

4. **Efficiency:** As a fully feed-forward framework, 4RC bypasses the need for auxiliary estimators or per-scene optimization, which are common in many existing approaches. This design choice contributes to higher efficiency during inference, a critical factor for real-world applications.

5. **Factorized Representation:** The minimally factorized 4D representation, which decomposes each frame into a viewpoint-invariant base geometry and time-dependent relative motion, is significant. This explicit decoupling simplifies the learning task, improves temporal consistency, and provides clear motion cues that can be beneficial for downstream applications.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Robotics:** A comprehensive understanding of dynamic scenes, including object trajectories and deformations, is fundamental for autonomous navigation, manipulation, and interaction in complex environments. 4RC's ability to provide dense 4D information could enhance robotic perception and motion planning.
- **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Accurate 4D reconstruction can facilitate more immersive and interactive AR/VR experiences by enabling realistic integration of virtual objects into dynamic real-world scenes and precise tracking of user movements and environmental changes.
- **Content Creation:** The ability to reconstruct and model dynamic scenes with high fidelity could benefit video synthesis, special effects, and animation, offering tools for creating or manipulating dynamic digital content.
- **General-purpose Perception Systems:** By providing a unified approach to reasoning about both geometry and motion, 4RC contributes to the development of more general-purpose visual perception systems that can better understand and interact with the dynamic nature of the real world.
- **Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as challenges with extreme depth or highly chaotic motion, point towards avenues for future research, including improvements in depth estimation and scaling training data to cover a broader range of complex dynamics. The streaming variant (S-4RC) also opens possibilities for online and causal 4D reconstruction, which is relevant for real-time applications.
