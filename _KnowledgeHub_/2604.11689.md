# LARY: A Latent Action Representation Yielding Benchmark for Generalizable Vision-to-Action Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> The LARY Benchmark provides a quantitative framework for rigorously evaluating latent action representations, demonstrating that general visual foundation models, even without explicit action supervision, outperform specialized embodied models in both semantic action understanding and low-level robotic control tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic learning and embodied AI face a "data island" problem due to the scarcity of explicitly labeled action datasets.
- Leveraging large-scale human action videos requires transforming raw visual signals into robust, ontology-independent latent action representations.
- There is a lack of thorough and rigorous evaluation frameworks for assessing the quality and effectiveness of latent action representations, often conflating overall VLA system performance with the representation quality itself.

## Method

- Introduces the **LARY** (==Latent Action Representation Yielding==) Benchmark, a quantitative framework for directly evaluating latent action representations across high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical control.
- Curates a large-scale, high-quality dataset of over **1.2 million** videos and **595K** motion trajectories, spanning human and robotic agents in diverse environments, through an ==automated data engine==.
- Systematically benchmarks existing ==Embodied Latent Action Models (LAMs)==, general vision encoders (semantic and pixel-level), and a new class of ==General LAMs== that leverage pre-trained visual backbones.

## Results

- General visual foundation models (**V-JEPA 2**, **DINOv3**) achieved average accuracies of **76.62%** and **68.68%** respectively in semantic action classification, significantly outperforming Embodied LAMs (**17.99%–20.90%**).
- General vision foundation models also exhibited substantially lower Mean Squared Error (MSE) in action regression, with latent-based encoders showing stronger robotic control capabilities than pixel-based ones.
- General LAMs, which graft LAM training onto frozen general vision backbones, generally outperformed existing Embodied LAMs (e.g., LAPA-DINOv2 achieved **49.36%** accuracy vs. UniVLA's **17.99%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General visual foundation models trained without explicit action supervision inherently encode knowledge relevant for physical control, outperforming specialized embodied LAMs.
> - Semantic-level abstraction in visual representations provides a more effective pathway from vision to action compared to pixel-level reconstruction.
> - Self-supervised visual encoders, especially those with contrastive priors, are effective for building LAMs that capture fine-grained spatiotemporal dynamics and localization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic learning and embodied AI, often referred to as the "data island" problem. Traditional methods for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are limited by the scarcity of explicitly labeled action datasets specific to robotics. A promising alternative involves leveraging large-scale, unlabeled human action videos, which offer a more scalable data source. However, a critical hurdle in this approach is the transformation of raw visual signals from these diverse videos into ontology-independent representations, termed "latent actions."

Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs), such as LAPA, Moto, LAPO, and IGOR, have introduced unsupervised frameworks to tokenize visual changes between video frames into discrete latent action tokens or compress visual changes between initial and goal images. While these models represent progress in transforming vision to latent action, the research identifies a significant gap: a lack of thorough and rigorous evaluation frameworks for assessing the quality and effectiveness of these latent action representations. Current evaluation practices often rely on downstream manipulation task performance or qualitative visualizations, which typically conflate the performance of the VLA components with the quality of the latent action itself. Furthermore, existing methods often do not span different entities, tasks, and granularities, limiting the assessment of generalization capabilities. Benchmarks like EWMBENCH and LAWM exist but sometimes struggle with aspects like robustness to distractors or are limited in scope. This paper aims to bridge this evaluation gap by introducing a comprehensive benchmark that directly probes the semantic separability and embodied control capabilities of latent action representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current VLA model development and evaluation. The shortage of diverse, explicitly labeled action data for robotics compels researchers to explore alternative data sources, particularly large-scale human action videos. However, harnessing these videos necessitates converting their visual content into robust, ontology-independent latent action representations. The authors observe that despite advancements in generating such latent actions, a rigorous, standardized framework for assessing their quality and effectiveness has been absent. Existing evaluation methods are often indirect, relying on downstream task performance, which makes it difficult to ascertain whether performance issues arise from the latent action representation itself or from other components of the VLA system. Moreover, these evaluations typically lack comprehensive coverage across various agents, scenarios, and levels of granularity, impeding a holistic understanding of representation generalization. The influence of different architectural choices, training strategies, and usage paradigms on the learned action representations also remains underexplored.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this work are:

- To introduce the Latent Action Representation Yielding (LARY) Benchmark, a quantitative framework designed for the rigorous and direct evaluation of latent action representations.
- To establish a standardized metric that assesses both the embodied capabilities of these representations across different agents and scenarios, and their general video understanding abilities.
- To evaluate latent action representations across two complementary levels of embodied intelligence:
 - **High-level semantic understanding:** Assessing the capacity to encode "what to do," by distinguishing between atomic primitives (e.g., "move up," "close gripper") and composite behaviors (e.g., "pick up," "place," "twist") derived from diverse human and robot manipulation videos.
 - **Low-level physical control mapping:** Examining the ability to encode "how to do it," by preserving sufficient physical detail for reconstructing continuous end-effector trajectories using embodied datasets with fine-grained robot motion annotations.
- To curate a large-scale, high-quality dataset for this benchmark, addressing issues of imprecise temporal boundaries and inconsistent action annotations prevalent in existing embodied video datasets through an automated data engine.
- To systematically benchmark various model families, including purpose-built Embodied LAMs, general vision encoders (both semantic and pixel-level), and a newly proposed class of General LAMs that leverage pre-trained visual backbones. This comprehensive evaluation aims to reveal insights into effective strategies for latent action learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LARY Benchmark is designed as a unified framework for evaluating latent action representations, focusing on both high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical control. The core methodology involves extracting motion information as latent representations ($z \in Z$) from a sequence of visual observations ($o_{1:T}$) using a Latent Action Model (LAM), and then evaluating the efficacy of $Z$ through two distinct tasks: semantic action decoding via classification ($f_{sem}: Z \to C$) and robotic control construction via regression ($f_{dyn}: Z \to A$).

**Dataset Curation:**
A comprehensively curated dataset underpins LARYBench, comprising over 1.2 million short videos (totaling more than 1,000 hours), 620K image pairs, and 595K motion trajectories. This dataset spans 151 unique action categories, covering both human and robotic agents, captured from egocentric and exocentric perspectives across simulated and real-world environments. An automated data engine was developed to re-segment and re-annotate a large corpus of existing datasets, ensuring precise temporal boundaries and consistent action annotations.

- **Atomic Classification Track (Kinematic-Level Atomic Primitives):** This subset, termed "Atomic Robot," contains 25,940 high-quality exocentric image pairs with trajectories derived from the LIBERO suite. It evaluates 28 discrete kinematic primitives, such as directional translations and binary gripper states. An automated process tracks end-effector positional offsets to identify precise start and end frames for movement, assigning ground-truth labels based on directional shifts.
- **Composite Classification Track (Task-Level Composite Behaviors):** This track assesses abstract behavioral semantics using 1,190,820 video clips from diverse sources like HoloAssist, Ego4D, Something-Somethingv2, TACO, EPIC-KITCHENS (human-centric), and AgiBotWorld-Beta (robot-centric). A four-stage, API-driven (doubao-1.5-pro-vision API) data curation pipeline was employed:
 1. **Action Segmentation:** Raw videos are temporally segmented into short clips, each with a sentence-level action annotation.
 2. **Video-Description Matching:** Clips are filtered for temporal validity (0.5s-20s), strict semantic alignment between video and description, and perspective consistency (egocentric for human data). A core action verb is extracted.
 3. **Video-Verb Consistency Check:** A secondary API-based verification ensures the isolated verb accurately reflects the visual content.
 4. **Manual Sampling Inspection:** A final manual review purges verbs lacking explicit or well-defined kinematic meanings (e.g., "apply," "clean").
- **Regression Track (Physical Execution Mapping Assessment):** This track evaluates continuous low-level robotic control. It includes 595,237 image pairs with corresponding motion trajectories from CALVIN and VLABench (single-arm, exocentric, Franka), and RoboCOIN and AgiBotWorld-Beta (bimanual, ego-centric, diverse platforms). The action space heterogeneity (e.g., 16-DoF for AgiBotWorld-Beta, 12-DoF for RoboCOIN) is preserved, with dexterous hand joint data in RoboCOIN masked to focus on macroscopic arm displacements.

**Evaluation Settings and Models:**

- **Data Split & Metrics:** Classification tasks use randomized stratified sampling (Atomic: 75:25, Composite: 70:30). Regression tasks use 75:25 train-validation splits, with CALVIN using environments A, B, C for training and D for validation. Top-1 Accuracy is reported for classification, and Mean Squared Error (MSE) for regression.
- **Latent Action Extraction:** For atomic and regression tasks, continuous latent embeddings from curated image pairs are used. For composite classification, a Motion-Guided Sampler (MGSampler) selects effective frame sequences to capture dynamic variation, and latent action features are extracted from adjacent sampled frames.
- **Probing Protocol for Semantic Action Classification:** 9 frames (224x224) are uniformly sampled from video clips. A 4-layer attentive probe classifier (similar to V-JEPA) is used, with a projector to align varying latent dimensions. The probe is trained for 20 epochs with specific optimization strategies.
- **Action Experts for Low-level Control Regression:** Latent actions (derived from image pairs with stride s=5) are mapped to absolute end-effector trajectories (7-DoF/12-DoF/16-DoF per chunk) using a simple MLP-based Action Expert.
- **Taxonomy of Action Representation Paradigms:** The study benchmarks three main families of models:
 - **Embodied Latent Action Models (Embodied LAMs):** LAPA, UniVLA, villa-X, explicitly designed for robot manipulation.
 - **General Vision Encoders:**
 - *General Semantic Encoders:* DINOv3, V-JEPA 2 (contrastive alignment/latent reconstruction).
 - *Generative Pixel Encoders:* Wan2.2 VAE, FLUX.2-dev VAE (spatial-temporal compression/pixel reconstruction).
 - **General Latent Action Models (General LAMs):** A new class proposed, where the LAPA framework's encoder is replaced with frozen pre-trained general vision backbones (DINOv2, DINOv3, SigLIP2, MAGVIT2). These are trained on diverse internet video data. For DINOv2/v3 and SigLIP2, the objective is to reconstruct high-level latent features; for MAGVIT2, it remains pixel-level reconstruction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on LARYBench yielded several key findings:

- **Superiority of General Visual Foundation Models:** General visual foundation models, particularly those trained without explicit action supervision (e.g., V-JEPA 2 and DINOv3), consistently outperformed specialized Embodied Latent Action Models (LAMs) across both semantic action classification and low-level control regression tasks.
 - In **semantic action classification (Table 1)**, V-JEPA 2 and DINOv3 achieved average accuracies of 76.62% and 68.68% respectively, significantly higher than the 17.99%–20.90% range for Embodied LAMs. V-JEPA 2, which learns from visual latent features, demonstrated the highest performance, suggesting that action representations can effectively emerge from latent feature spaces rather than solely from pixel space.
 - In **action regression (Table 2)**, general vision foundation models also exhibited substantially lower Mean Squared Error (MSE) values. Latent-based vision encoders (DINOv3 and V-JEPA 2) showed better robotic control capabilities than pixel-based encoders (Wan2.2, FLUX.2-dev), indicating a stronger alignment between latent visual space and physical action space.
- **Inherent Action-Relevant Knowledge in General Visual Representations:** The strong performance of general vision encoders, without any action-specific training, suggests that large-scale visual pre-training inherently encodes knowledge relevant for physical control.
- **Effectiveness of Semantic-Level Abstraction:** The results indicate that semantic-level abstraction is a more effective pathway from vision to action compared to pixel-level reconstruction.
- **Performance of General LAMs:** The newly introduced General LAMs, which graft LAM training onto frozen general vision backbones, generally outperformed existing Embodied LAMs. For example, LAPA-DINOv2 achieved 49.36% average accuracy in classification, compared to UniVLA's 17.99%. This highlights the benefit of leveraging diverse training data and fewer task-specific constraints. However, General LAMs showed a performance decline on robot actions compared to human actions, possibly due to limited robot-specific data diversity in their pre-training.
- **Ablation Study on Latent Action Model Components:** Systematic ablations within the LAPA framework, specifically LAPA-DINOv3, revealed crucial insights for building effective LAMs:
 - Self-supervised visual encoders (like DINOv3) produced superior LAMs compared to reconstruction-based or vision-language contrastive models, implying that contrastive priors are better at capturing fine-grained spatio-temporal dynamics.
 - Codebook capacity, sequence length, and latent dimension of the quantization bottleneck require careful tuning. A moderate codebook size (e.g., 64) and sequence length (e.g., 49) were found to be optimal for balancing performance and utilization, avoiding issues like catastrophic codebook collapse. A latent dimension of 256 provided the best capacity-stability balance.
- **Error Analysis - Spatiotemporal Grounding and Robustness:**
 - **Long-Tail Dilemma (Figure 5):** Stronger models exhibited better generalization capabilities in long-tail action categories.
 - **Spatiotemporal Grounding (Figure 6):** Self-supervised models (V-JEPA 2, DINOv3) demonstrated superior attention grounding, precisely localizing interaction points. In contrast, generative pixel encoders showed dispersed attention, and Embodied LAMs often failed to localize meaningful features. General LAMs, like LAPA-DINOv2, inherited strong localization abilities from their backbones. This suggests that predictive failures often stem from an inability to focus attention on dynamically interacting objects.
 - **Stride Ablation (Table 6):** Latent Action Models (both Embodied and General LAMs) exhibited remarkable temporal stability across varying sampling strides (5, 15, 30 frames), indicating their ability to capture robust dynamic trajectories for long-horizon intents. Pure spatial generative encoders (e.g., FLUX.2-dev), while strong at short horizons, suffered catastrophic performance degradation at longer strides.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LARY Benchmark represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotic learning, with several potential impacts:

- **Standardized and Decoupled Evaluation:** LARYBench provides the first comprehensive framework that rigorously decouples the evaluation of latent action representations from the performance of downstream policy learning. This allows for a direct and standardized measurement of representation quality, which can help diagnose bottlenecks in VLA systems more effectively.
- **Robust and Scalable Dataset:** The creation of a large-scale, meticulously curated dataset (1.2M videos, 620K image pairs, 595K trajectories, 151 action categories, 11 robotic embodiments), powered by an automated data engine, offers a rich resource for training and evaluating future VLA models. Its open-source availability (GitHub, Hugging Face) promotes collaborative research and benchmarking.
- **Re-evaluation of VLA Design Paradigms:** The empirical findings challenge existing assumptions in VLA design. The consistent outperformance of general visual foundation models over specialized embodied LAMs suggests that latent actions naturally emerge from large-scale visual pre-training without explicit action supervision. Furthermore, the superiority of latent-based visual spaces over pixel-based ones for physical control is a key insight.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The research advocates for a paradigm shift in VLA system design. Instead of dedicating efforts to learning action spaces solely from limited robotic data, future research could benefit more from aligning control policies with the robust, pre-existing feature spaces of general vision models. This implies that efforts should focus on architectural challenges such as continuous signal decoding and feature alignment to effectively leverage these "universal priors" for developing more data-efficient embodied agents.
- **Deep Error Analysis:** The detailed error analysis, including the long-tail dilemma, spatiotemporal grounding, and stride ablation, offers valuable insights into the limitations and strengths of different representation paradigms, guiding targeted improvements in model development.
