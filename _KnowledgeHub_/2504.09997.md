# GenTe: Generative Real-world Terrains for General Legged Robot Locomotion Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen and Shenzhen Institute of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics for Society developed GenTe, a framework that generates physically realistic and adaptable terrains for training legged robot locomotion policies. GenTe integrates geometric and physical terrain modeling, including deformable and fluid-based environments, and leverages Vision-Language Models for scalable, contextually relevant terrain creation, enabling trained policies to achieve high success rates and stable command following on diverse, unseen terrains.

## Problem

- Existing simulation environments for legged robots primarily use rigid, predefined geometric terrains, lacking the dynamic and complex properties of natural, unstructured real-world environments.
- Current methods often struggle to integrate diverse physical terrain interactions (e.g., ground compliance, fluid dynamics, soil sinkage) into a single, comprehensive training framework.
- The limited realism and diversity of simulated terrains hinder the generalization and robustness of learned locomotion policies, restricting their applicability to real-world deployment.

## Method

- **GenTe** introduces a ==dual-level terrain simulation framework== that models both geometric features (height maps, obstacles) and physical properties (wading in water, deformable ground like sand) with realistic force interactions.
- The framework employs ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== and ==function-calling== to intelligently generate complex, contextually relevant terrains from high-level textual or visual inputs, mapping semantic interpretations to physical and geometric parameters.
- A ==curriculum learning== approach uses an ==atomic terrain library== for policy training via the ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== algorithm in Isaac Gym, incorporating penalties for obstacle collisions to encourage avoidance behaviors.

## Results

- The trained locomotion policy demonstrated a steady increase in cumulative reward over **1,500 training iterations**, maintaining stable control and accurately executing velocity-based navigation commands on unseen terrains.
- Robots successfully navigated through terrains with varied obstacles (pillars and rocks), with larger waypoint update distance thresholds leading to smoother and more efficient obstacle avoidance trajectories.
- The policy maintained consistently high success rates and sustained performance on diverse, unseen Vision-Language Model-generated terrains, even beyond initial training iterations, indicating strong generalization and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining both geometric (e.g., height maps) and physical (e.g., water resistance, soil sinkage) terrain modeling within a unified framework is crucial for generating sufficiently realistic and diverse environments to train generalized locomotion policies.
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively serve as reasoning engines for automated, scalable terrain generation, allowing for the creation of complex and contextually relevant environments from high-level descriptions.
> - Introducing dynamic and varied force modeling for terrain interactions, such as buoyancy, added mass, drag, bulldozing resistance, and friction, is vital for training policies that account for real-world physical dynamics and adapt to unpredictable conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of bipedal robots capable of traversing diverse real-world terrains represents a persistent challenge in the field of robotics. Current methodologies for training legged robot locomotion policies, primarily via reinforcement learning in simulation, often rely on two main abstractions: geometric terrains and physical terrains. Geometric terrains are typically modeled as rigid surfaces parameterized by height maps, while physical terrains incorporate dynamic force interactions such as ground compliance or fluid dynamics.

Prior approaches for terrain generalization predominantly utilize rigid, predefined environments, adjusting height maps to simulate basic surfaces like flat ground, stairs, and slopes. While these methods establish foundational locomotion capabilities, they frequently do not capture the dynamic and complex properties of natural terrains, which require adaptive behaviors in response to unpredictable conditions. Some research has focused on enhancing robustness by precisely modeling specialized forces in specific deformable terrains, such as sand or snow. However, integrating various types of complex terrains into a single, comprehensive framework remains an area requiring further development. Existing simulators, while capable of domain randomization to introduce minor variations in texture and height, often lack the capability to accurately replicate the nuanced physical interactions found in natural, unstructured environments, thereby limiting the adaptability of learned policies.

Efforts in simulation environment generation have largely concentrated on manipulation tasks, focusing on flexible layouts and object placement. Examples include systems that create varied virtual environments for robust skill learning or simulate complex interactions for dexterous manipulation. While procedural generation techniques have been employed to create diverse 3D environments for navigation and interaction policies, the application of such advanced environment generation specifically for dynamic and unpredictable locomotion terrains has received less attention. Simulators for locomotion often provide environments for basic movement tasks but typically lack the complexity required for simulating interactions with multiple environmental factors or dynamic terrain properties.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing GenTe, a framework designed to systematically generate physically realistic and adaptable terrains. GenTe aims to bridge the gap between existing static or narrowly specialized terrain models and the need for comprehensive, diverse, and dynamic environments for training generalizable legged robot locomotion policies. By integrating both geometric and physical terrain modeling within a scalable generation framework driven by Vision-Language Models, this research positions itself as a development in creating more comprehensive and realistic simulation environments for legged robot locomotion control, distinct from previous work focused on geometric simplicity or specialized physical models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to address the limitations of existing methods in training bipedal robots to traverse diverse and unstructured real-world terrains. Current approaches, which largely rely on predefined height maps and static environments, are insufficient for capturing the complexity and dynamic nature of natural landscapes. This inadequacy restricts the generalization and robustness of locomotion policies learned in simulation, hindering their applicability in real-world deployment.

The key objectives of the GenTe framework are as follows:

- **Develop a comprehensive framework for generating physically realistic and adaptable terrains:** The central objective is to create a system, named GenTe, that can produce varied and complex training environments. These environments are intended to be more representative of real-world conditions than those traditionally used in robot locomotion simulation.
- **Construct an atomic terrain library with both geometric and physical features:** This involves systematically building a library of fundamental terrain components. This library is designed to include both geometric representations (e.g., height maps for hills and obstacles) and physical properties (e.g., ground compliance, fluid dynamics, and deformable surfaces) to cover a wide spectrum of real-world surface conditions.
- **Enable curriculum training for reinforcement learning-based locomotion policies:** By providing a diverse and progressively challenging set of terrains from the atomic library, the framework aims to facilitate more effective curriculum learning. This approach allows robots to gradually acquire fundamental navigation skills and adapt to increasingly complex environments.
- **Leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and function-calling for contextual terrain generation:** A significant objective is to utilize the reasoning capabilities of VLMs, combined with function-calling techniques, to generate complex and contextually relevant terrains. This allows for the creation of specific terrain types (e.g., a beach environment) from high-level textual or graphical inputs, which then trigger the instantiation of appropriate geometric and physical properties from the terrain library.
- **Introduce realistic force modeling for terrain interactions:** The framework aims to incorporate advanced physical models that capture nuanced interactions such as soil sinkage and hydrodynamic resistance. This objective enhances the physical realism of the simulated environments, leading to more robust policies that account for dynamic terrain properties.
- **Establish a benchmark of generated terrains:** To facilitate future research and provide a standardized evaluation tool, the project aims to introduce a benchmark comprising 100 generated terrains. These terrains are intended to serve as a standardized testbed for evaluating the generalization and robustness of locomotion policies.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need for more sophisticated and realistic simulation environments to train bipedal robots for general locomotion in unstructured real-world settings. The objectives focus on creating a generative framework that integrates advanced terrain modeling, VLM-driven generation, and robust policy training, thereby advancing the state of the art in legged robot locomotion control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GenTe framework integrates a dual-level approach for terrain simulation—geometry and physics—and employs Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for intelligent terrain generation and policy training for legged robots.

**Terrain Simulation:**
The simulation environment is constructed at two complementary levels to address the complexity of real-world terrains:

- **Geometry Terrains:** These terrains are defined using height maps, which provide underlying topography for features such as hills, valleys, and uneven ground. Height maps can be generated procedurally or from real-world scan data. Geometric primitives and objects like rocks, trees, slopes, and cliffs are incorporated to add further complexity and create diverse terrain types.
- **Physical Terrains:** This level focuses on modeling dynamic force interactions based on terramechanics principles.
 - **Wading Terrain:** Simulates locomotion in water, incorporating forces such as fluid resistance, added mass effects, and buoyancy.
 - An additional flow-induced force, represented by sinusoidal, Gaussian, or zero-flow patterns, models water movement (ocean waves, streams, still water).
 - Forces are decomposed into horizontal and vertical components, with horizontal forces converted to torques and vertical forces adjusting the robot's effective weight. Gaussian noise is applied to force components to enhance generalization.
 - Drag force ($F_d$) on a leg is calculated based on water density ($\rho$), drag coefficient ($C_d$), projected area ($A(t)$), and leg velocity ($v(t)$), with $C_d$ approximated for turbulent flow.
 - Added mass ($m_{added}$) accounts for the increased inertia due to surrounding water, calculated using an added mass coefficient ($C_m$) and submerged volume ($V_{sub}(t)$).
 - Buoyant force ($F_b$) is incorporated by adjusting the robot's effective mass ($m_{eff}$), which depends on submerged volume and fluid properties.
 - Water flow force ($F_{flow}$) simulates constant water, currents, or tides, perturbed by a dynamic Gaussian noise term.
 - All these forces are dynamically modeled by treating submerged leg length as time-dependent, enabling continuous adjustments to projected area and submerged volume, and calculating resulting torques.
 - **Deformable Terrain:** Models locomotion on surfaces like sand, focusing on mechanical properties and foot-ground interaction.
 - A contact model, based on Vanderkop et al., identifies frictional forces ($F_\mu$) and bulldozing resistance ($F_B$) as dominant forces.
 - Bulldozing resistance ($F_B$) is modeled as $az^n$, where $z$ is sinkage and $a, n$ are empirical parameters.
 - Frictional force ($F_\mu$) is modeled using a Coulomb friction model distinguishing static and kinetic phases: $\mu_f F_N (1 - e^{-x/K})$, where $\mu_f$ is the coefficient of friction, $x$ is displacement, $K$ determines friction increase rate, and $F_N$ is the normal force.
 - Terrain parameters (e.g., moisture, grain size, cohesion) are sampled from Gaussian distributions to introduce natural variability and avoid overfitting.
 - The total horizontal external force ($F_x$) is summarized as an inner product combining drag, water flow, bulldozing resistance, and friction, incorporating Gaussian noise and indicator functions for specific physical properties.
- **Obstacles Avoidance:** Pillars and rocks terrains are introduced as obstacles of varying heights. Collisions with these obstacles are penalized during training to encourage the development of avoidance strategies, enabling the robot to recognize obstacle positions and adjust its path.

**Terrain Generation for Policy Training (GenTe Framework):**
The framework employs a VLM-driven approach for automated, diverse terrain generation:

- **VLM-Driven Terrain Generation:** In a zero-shot inference phase, GenTe utilizes VLMs (specifically Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct for image input and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for text input) as a reasoning engine.
 - **Semantic Interpretation:** The VLM interprets high-level textual or visual input, extracting features and relationships defining the terrain's nature and underlying properties.
 - **Function-Based Parameterization:** Interpreted features are mapped to geometric and physical parameters (e.g., surface roughness, friction, elevation gradients). Each basic terrain type is formulated as a tool with specific parameters.
 - **Terrain Construction:** These parameters are used to procedurally generate a simulator-ready environment, ensuring both geometric and physical realism for dynamic robotic simulations.
- **Policy Training:**
 - The terrain generation and control problem is modeled as a Discrete-time Markov Decision Process (DTMDP). A policy $\pi_\theta(a|s, m)$ aims to maximize the expected cumulative reward.
 - A Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller translates high-level actions from the policy network into joint torques for the robot’s actuators, ensuring smooth and stable movement.
 - The policy is trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm with curriculum learning on fundamental terrains. Training parameters include a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-4}$ and a discount factor ($\gamma$) of 0.99.
 - Simulations are performed in Isaac Gym, with generated terrains structured as height maps with physical attributes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of GenTe focused on policy training, obstacle avoidance capabilities, and performance on VLM-generated terrains.

**Policy Training:**
The locomotion policy was trained for 1,500 iterations using curriculum learning on a set of fundamental terrains.
- **Cumulative Reward:** The mean cumulative reward across three trials demonstrated a steady increase over the training iterations, indicating that the agent effectively learned and improved its performance. The variance intervals around the mean reward suggested stability in the training process.
- **Command-Following Capability:** Evaluation of the trained policy on previously unseen terrains showed its ability to generalize. The robot maintained stable control and accurately executed velocity-based navigation instructions, as evidenced by the close tracking of commanded velocities (both X-axis and Y-axis) by the robot's actual velocities.

**Obstacle Avoidance Capability:**
The agent's capacity for autonomous obstacle avoidance was assessed in a terrain featuring varied obstacles (pillars and rocks of different heights).
- **Navigation Performance:** Robots successfully navigated from a "Start" point to an "End" point while avoiding obstacles. The black and gray blocks on the height map represented obstacles.
- **Waypoint Update Frequency:** The study explored the effect of the distance threshold ($d$) for waypoint updates on trajectory smoothness. As $d$ increased (from 0.1 meters to 20 meters), the robot's trajectories exhibited smoother and more direct paths. This suggests that larger distance thresholds for waypoint updates lead to fewer course corrections and potentially more efficient navigation, while still effectively bypassing obstacles.

**Evaluation on Generated Terrains:**
The trained policy was deployed on a variety of unseen terrains generated by the Vision-Language Model (VLM) to evaluate its robustness and generalization.
- **Success Rates:** The policy consistently maintained high success rates across different VLM-generated terrain types.
- **Sustained Performance:** To further challenge the policy, tests were conducted beyond the initial 1,500 training iterations, extending to over 2,000 iterations. The results demonstrated that the policy sustained high success rates even when encountering complex and unstructured environments that were not part of its direct training curriculum. This performance indicated the policy's strong generalization capabilities.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** The sustained performance across diverse and unpredictable terrains underscored the effectiveness of the GenTe terrain generation strategy in producing meaningful variations and the robustness of the training framework in fostering adaptive and resilient navigation. The policy demonstrated stable control, precise execution of commands, and reliable decision-making across a broad range of conditions, suggesting its potential for deployment in challenging and dynamic real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces GenTe, a framework designed to enhance the generalization capabilities of bipedal robot locomotion control. The development of GenTe represents a contribution to addressing the fundamental challenge of enabling robots to traverse diverse and unstructured real-world terrains, which current simulation and training methods often struggle to replicate comprehensively.

The primary significance of this work lies in its systemic approach to generating simulation environments for legged robot locomotion. By integrating both geometric and physical terrain modeling, GenTe moves beyond the limitations of predefined height maps and static environments. The framework's ability to construct an atomic terrain library, comprising both geometric and physical features, enables the creation of a wide range of real-world surface conditions, including deformable and fluid-based terrains. This comprehensive modeling facilitates the development of more adaptable and robust locomotion policies.

A key aspect of GenTe's approach is the leverage of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and function-calling techniques. This allows for scalable and adaptive terrain creation based on high-level textual or graphical inputs, a capability that facilitates the rapid generation of contextually relevant and complex environments. This method is noted as the first framework for generalized locomotion simulation environment generation, providing a novel tool for researchers.

The introduction of realistic force modeling, capturing effects such as soil sinkage and hydrodynamic resistance, further contributes to the fidelity of the simulated environments. This realism is intended to reduce the simulation-to-real-world gap for locomotion policies trained with GenTe-generated terrains.

The experimental results validate the effectiveness of the generated terrains in improving the generalization and robustness of bipedal robot locomotion. The demonstration of consistent high success rates and stable command-following on unseen, VLM-generated terrains, even beyond the initial training iterations, indicates the robustness of the trained policies.

The potential impact of GenTe is multi-faceted:
- **Accelerating Research:** By providing an open-source framework and a benchmark of 100 generated terrains, GenTe aims to accelerate future research in legged robot learning and terrain-aware locomotion. This resource can serve as a standardized platform for developing and evaluating new control policies.
- **Enhanced Robot Adaptability:** The framework can lead to the development of bipedal robots with significantly improved adaptability and robustness, capable of navigating complex, dynamic, and unstructured environments in real-world scenarios. This includes applications in search and rescue, exploration, or industrial settings with uneven ground.
- **Reducing Sim-to-Real Gap:** By generating more physically realistic and diverse training environments, GenTe has the potential to narrow the simulation-to-real-world gap, making policies learned in simulation more directly transferable to physical robots.
- **Advanced Training Paradigms:** The VLM-driven generation capability could enable more intuitive and efficient training paradigms, allowing users to describe desired terrains naturally rather than through laborious manual parameter tuning.

Future work outlined includes exploring real-world deployment of policies trained with GenTe and further refining the physical terrain modeling for enhanced realism, indicating a trajectory towards practical application and continuous improvement of the framework.
