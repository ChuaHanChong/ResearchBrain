# MacTok: Robust Continuous Tokenization for Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MacTok, a continuous tokenizer from Fudan University, robustly learns compact and semantically rich 1D latent representations by addressing posterior collapse in KL-based VAEs. It achieves a new state-of-the-art gFID of 1.52 on ImageNet 512x512 with 128 tokens and competitive reconstruction fidelity while using significantly fewer tokens.

## Problem

- KL-based continuous image tokenizers frequently suffer from 'posterior collapse' under strong compression, leading to degenerate latent spaces that lose meaningful semantics and result in poor reconstruction and generation quality.
- Existing solutions to posterior collapse, such as delicate KL weight tuning, have not provided a fundamental resolution to latent degeneration.
- Prior representation alignment approaches in state-of-the-art tokenizers often rely on fixed token counts, perform coarse global alignment, or require multiple auxiliary objectives, limiting flexibility and consistency.

## Method

- **MacTok** employs a ==Vision Transformer==-based continuous tokenizer architecture that uses a 1D latent space for flexible token counts, preventing posterior collapse through enforced information flow.
- Dual image masking strategies, including random and DINO-guided semantic masking, compel the latent tokens to infer complete information and capture robust semantic features from partial inputs.
- A novel global and local representation alignment mechanism connects each latent token with both holistic image semantics and specific regional DINOv2 features, structuring the latent space effectively.

## Results

- Achieved a gFID of 1.52 and an IS of 316.0 on ImageNet 512x512 with 128 tokens (SiT-XL and CFG), establishing a new state-of-the-art for image generation.
- Demonstrated superior generation with fewer tokens, outperforming SoftVQ-VAE by **2.21** gFID on ImageNet 256x256 (64 tokens, without CFG).
- Exhibited strong reconstruction performance with rFID scores of 0.43 for 128 tokens and 0.75 for 64 tokens on ImageNet 256x256, competitive with or superior to VQ-based tokenizers requiring many more tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image-level masking, particularly with semantic guidance, fundamentally mitigates posterior collapse by forcing the latent space to retain essential and semantically discriminative information.
> - Multi-level representation alignment with pre-trained vision models (DINOv2) is crucial for organizing the latent space, enabling semantically similar concepts to cluster even with high compression.
> - It is possible to achieve state-of-the-art generative performance with significantly fewer tokens (e.g., 64 or 128) by learning robust, semantically rich continuous latent representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "MacTok" addresses a core challenge within the field of visual generative modeling: the efficient and high-fidelity representation of raw image data in compressed latent spaces. Modern generative models, such as diffusion models, flow-based models, and autoregressive models, rely on image tokenizers to map high-dimensional pixel data into more manageable latent representations, thereby reducing computational costs during training and inference.

Image tokenizers generally fall into two categories: discrete and continuous. Discrete tokenizers, exemplified by Vector-Quantized Variational Auto-Encoders (VQ-VAE) and its variants (e.g., VQ-GAN, TiTok), discretize the latent space using a finite codebook. While offering stability through straight-through estimation, they can suffer from quantization error and issues with codebook utilization. Recent advancements in this area include two-stage training schemes and the integration of pre-trained visual features to enhance efficiency and compactness.

In contrast, continuous tokenizers, such as Kullback–Leibler Variational Auto-Encoders (KL-VAE) and related methods (e.g., SD-VAE, MAR-VAE, MAETok, SoftVQ-VAE), define a continuous latent space regularized by a Gaussian prior via KL divergence. While these models can yield smooth representations, they are prone to a phenomenon known as "posterior collapse," particularly under strong compression. Posterior collapse occurs when the encoder fails to encode informative features into the compressed latent space, leading to a degenerate latent distribution that loses meaningful semantics and results in poor reconstruction and generation quality. Previous attempts to mitigate posterior collapse, such as KL weight tuning, have often required delicate hyperparameter adjustments and have not provided a fundamental resolution to latent degeneration.

Furthermore, state-of-the-art tokenizers, both discrete and continuous, increasingly leverage representation alignment with external semantic features (e.g., from DINOv2 or CLIP) to improve reconstruction and generation quality. However, existing alignment approaches often have limitations such as reliance on fixed token counts, coarse global alignment, or the need for multiple auxiliary objectives.

MacTok positions itself within this landscape by focusing specifically on improving KL-based continuous tokenizers. It aims to directly address the posterior collapse problem, which is identified as a primary reason for the under-exploration and limited performance of continuous tokenizers under strong compression. By introducing novel masking strategies and a refined representation alignment mechanism, MacTok seeks to enable robust and semantically rich latent representations with significantly fewer tokens, thereby advancing the efficiency and fidelity of continuous tokenization for visual generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a continuous image tokenizer that can robustly learn compact and informative latent representations, specifically addressing the challenge of posterior collapse in KL-based Variational Auto-Encoders (VAEs) when subjected to strong compression. The authors motivate their work through several observations and specific goals:

#### 2.1. Mitigating Posterior Collapse

The core motivation stems from the persistent issue of posterior collapse in KL-VAE based continuous tokenizers. When trained with strong KL regularization, these models tend to excessively constrain the latent distribution, driving it toward an isotropic Gaussian prior and causing a loss of meaningful semantics. This degeneration of the latent space leads to poor image reconstruction and limited generation quality. Existing solutions, such as fine-tuning KL weights, are described as requiring delicate adjustments and not providing a fundamental resolution to the underlying problem. The key objective is to find a mechanism that allows the model to learn a robust and discriminative latent representation that preserves semantics, even under high compression rates, by maintaining sufficient mutual information between the input and its encoded representation.

#### 2.2. Enhancing Latent Space Robustness through Masked Reconstruction

Drawing inspiration from masked representation learning, the authors hypothesize that requiring the tokenizer to reconstruct images from partial inputs could force the latent space to capture more robust and semantically discriminative information. They specifically investigate two masking schemes:
- **Addressing Latent Token Masking Limitations:** Initial experiments showed that masking latent tokens only temporarily delays posterior collapse, with the model eventually collapsing. This motivated the exploration of more effective masking strategies.
- **Promoting Robustness via Image Masking:** The observation that masking image tokens leads to consistently stable optimization and more robust representations became a key motivation. This strategy compels both the encoder and decoder to infer from incomplete visual evidence, thereby encouraging the latent space to capture global structural semantics.

#### 2.3. Injecting Semantic Priors with Guided Masking

While random masking enhances general robustness, it is agnostic to the semantic content of an image. A further motivation was to introduce semantic awareness into the masking process. By proposing a DINO-guided semantic masking strategy, the objective is to selectively occlude the most informative regions of an image (as identified by a pre-trained DINOv2 model). This targeted masking strategy is intended to enforce the reconstruction of key semantic regions from incomplete observations, thereby implicitly transferring semantic priors from the image space into the latent space and leading to more semantically rich latent representations and improved generation quality.

#### 2.4. Refining Representation Alignment for Structural Coherence

State-of-the-art tokenizers often employ representation alignment with external semantic features (e.g., from DINOv2) to improve reconstruction and generation quality. However, previous approaches were identified as having limitations, such as relying on a fixed token count, performing only coarse global alignment, or requiring multiple auxiliary objectives. MacTok's motivation in this area is to introduce a more comprehensive and flexible alignment strategy. The objective is to propose a global and local representation alignment mechanism that connects each latent token with both holistic image semantics and specific regional features. This approach aims to facilitate more consistent semantic guidance across various token lengths, leading to a well-structured latent space that enhances reconstruction and generation performance.

In summary, the detailed objectives are to: (1) fundamentally overcome posterior collapse in KL-based continuous tokenizers; (2) learn highly compressed yet semantically rich and robust 1D latent representations; (3) integrate masking strategies (both random and DINO-guided semantic masking) to enforce information flow from partial inputs; and (4) implement a novel global and local representation alignment scheme to structure the latent space effectively. These efforts are aimed at achieving a superior trade-off between compression efficiency, reconstruction fidelity, and generative quality, particularly with a minimal number of latent tokens.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MacTok, a Masked Augmenting 1D Continuous Tokenizer, is designed to learn compact and robust latent representations for image generation by addressing posterior collapse through a combination of image masking and representation alignment.

#### 3.1. Continuous Tokenizer Architecture

MacTok employs a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture for both its encoder (E) and decoder (D). It adopts a one-dimensional (1D) tokenizer design, which provides flexibility in the number of latent tokens used to represent an image.
- **Encoder (E):** An input image $I \in R^{H \times W \times 3}$ is initially partitioned into non-overlapping patches, forming image tokens $x \in R^{N \times D}$, where $N$ is the number of patches and $D$ is the embedding dimension. A set of learnable latent tokens $z \in R^{L \times D}$ (where $L$ is the desired number of latent tokens) is concatenated with the image tokens, forming the sequence $[x; z]$. This combined sequence is then fed into the encoder. The encoder outputs corresponding to the latent tokens are extracted as the latent representations $\hat{z} \in R^{L \times Z}$, where $Z$ is the latent dimensionality.
- **Latent Space:** The latent vector $\hat{z}$ is treated as a Gaussian random variable parameterized by its mean and variance. A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term regularizes the posterior distribution of $\hat{z}$ toward an isotropic Gaussian prior, promoting a smooth continuous latent space.
- **Decoder (D):** During the decoding phase, the sampled latent representations $\hat{z}$ are concatenated with learnable reconstruction tokens $h \in R^{N \times Z}$. This sequence, $[h; \hat{z}]$, is then passed to the decoder. The decoder's outputs corresponding to the reconstruction tokens $h$ are projected through a linear layer to reconstruct the image $\hat{I}$.
- **Positional Embeddings:** 2D absolute positional embeddings are used for image tokens to preserve spatial structure, while 1D embeddings are employed for latent and reconstruction tokens, which do not have explicit spatial coordinates.

#### 3.2. Image Masking for Latent Preservation

To prevent posterior collapse and ensure that the latent variables capture informative content, MacTok enforces information flow through masked reconstruction. Two complementary masking strategies are applied with equal probability during training:
- **Random Masking:** A random subset of image patches is replaced with special mask tokens before encoding. This forces the latent tokens to infer complete information about the image and reconstruct the missing regions using the visible context. The mask ratio $m$ is uniformly sampled from $[-0.1, M]$ (where $M$ is typically 0.7) and clipped to $[0, M]$. This stochastic masking encourages the latent variables to encode essential information for reconstruction.
- **Semantic Masking:** This strategy injects semantic priors into the latent space using features from a pre-trained DINOv2 model. Given the DINOv2 classification token $c$ and patch tokens $P = \{p_i\}$, the cosine similarity $s_i$ between $c$ and each $p_i$ is computed to determine semantic relevance. The top $\lfloor m \times N \rfloor$ patches with the highest similarity scores are then masked. By masking semantically important regions, the reconstruction task becomes more challenging, compelling the latent tokens to capture object-level structures and global context, thereby implicitly transferring semantic knowledge from the image space.

#### 3.3. Local and Global Representation Alignment

MacTok further aligns its latent representations with pre-trained DINOv2 features to enforce semantic consistency and improve structural coherence.
- **Feature Construction:**
 - **Local Features:** The encoder's latent tokens $\hat{z} = \{\hat{z}_i\}_{i=1}^L$ are expanded into a sequence $\tilde{z}_{loc} \in R^{N \times Z}$ by repeating each latent token $r = N/L$ times, matching the spatial granularity of DINOv2 patch features.
 - **Global Feature:** A global latent representation $\tilde{z}_{glob}$ is obtained by average pooling all latent tokens $\hat{z}$.
- **Projection and Alignment Loss:** Both $\tilde{z}_{loc}$ and $\tilde{z}_{glob}$ are projected into the DINOv2 feature space using two lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLP1 and MLP2) to produce $o_{loc}$ and $o_{glob}$. A representation alignment loss ($L_{RA}$) is then calculated using cosine similarity: $o_{loc}$ is aligned with DINOv2 patch tokens, and $o_{glob}$ is aligned with the DINOv2 classification token. This loss encourages the latent tokens to encode semantically coherent information at both patch and image levels.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

MacTok is optimized using a composite objective function $L$, which combines several terms:
$L = L_{recon} + \lambda_1 L_{percep} + \lambda_2 L_{adv} + \lambda_3 L_{KL} + \lambda_4 L_{RA}$
- $L_{recon}$: A pixel-wise reconstruction loss (e.g., L1 or L2 loss).
- $L_{percep}$: A perceptual loss (e.g., VGG-based) that enforces similarity in a pre-trained feature space.
- $L_{adv}$: An adversarial loss, employing a discriminator, to encourage the generation of realistic images.
- $L_{KL}$: The KL divergence term, regularizing the latent distribution toward a Gaussian prior.
- $L_{RA}$: The proposed global and local representation alignment loss.
The coefficients $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3, \lambda_4$ control the weighting of these different loss components.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The default MacTok architecture uses a ViT-Base backbone (176M parameters). The encoder is initialized with DINOv2 weights to integrate semantic priors. A frozen DINO-S network serves as the discriminator, augmented with DiffAug, consistency regularization, and LeCAM regularization for stable adversarial training. MacTok is trained on ImageNet at 256x256 and 512x512 resolutions. After tokenizer training, a decoder fine-tuning stage is performed where the encoder is frozen, and the decoder is trained for 10 epochs without masking. For downstream generative modeling, SiT and LightningDiT architectures are employed with MacTok's latent tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding MacTok's performance in both reconstruction and image generation, as well as an analysis of its latent space characteristics.

#### 4.1. Generation Performance

MacTok-based generative models demonstrated improvements in generation quality on ImageNet datasets.
- **ImageNet 256x256:** MacTok, when integrated with LightningDiT-XL and SiT-XL, achieved a gFID of 1.44 using 128 tokens with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), which is described as competitive. With 64 tokens, it achieved a gFID of 1.68 (with CFG), surpassing SoftVQ-VAE by 2.21 gFID without CFG.
- **ImageNet 512x512:** SiT-XL trained with MacTok using 128 tokens and CFG achieved a gFID of 1.52 and an IS of 316.0, which is reported as a new state-of-the-art result. With 64 tokens, it achieved a gFID of 1.52 (with CFG), outperforming SoftVQ-VAE by 0.69 gFID without CFG.
These results indicate that MacTok effectively addresses posterior collapse in KL-based tokenizers while maintaining strong generation fidelity, even with a reduced token count.

#### 4.2. Reconstruction Performance

MacTok exhibited strong reconstruction capabilities despite using significantly fewer tokens.
- **ImageNet 256x256:** rFID scores were 0.75 for 64 tokens and 0.43 for 128 tokens.
- **ImageNet 512x512:** rFID scores were 0.89 for 64 tokens and 0.79 for 128 tokens.
These rFID scores were competitive with or superior to VQ-based tokenizers that typically use at least 256 tokens and KL-based tokenizers, while requiring up to 64 times fewer tokens. This suggests that MacTok learns compact latent representations that retain substantial semantic information.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Other Continuous Tokenizers

When compared against other leading continuous tokenizers (e.g., VA-VAE, MAETok, SoftVQ-VAE, SD-VAE, MAR-VAE, l-DeTok) using SiT-B on the 256x256 benchmark, MacTok demonstrated a balance between compression and reconstruction quality. With 128 tokens, it achieved an rFID of 0.43, PSNR of 25.03, and SSIM of 0.806, surpassing MAETok. With 64 tokens, it maintained competitive results (rFID 0.75, PSNR 23.10, SSIM 0.738), outperforming SoftVQ-VAE. For generation, SiT-B trained with MacTok (128 tokens) achieved a gFID of 3.15, exceeding other continuous tokenizers in that comparison set.

#### 4.4. Latent Space Analysis

- **Visualization:** UMAP projections of the latent space illustrated the impact of MacTok's components. A standard KL-VAE showed severe posterior collapse, resulting in an isotropic and uninformative distribution. MacTok with masking but without representation alignment produced a more compact and less dispersed latent space, suggesting that image-level masking implicitly promotes the preservation of visual details and structural information. The full MacTok model, including global and local representation alignment, yielded a more structured and discriminative latent space where semantically similar concepts clustered together.
- **Linear Probing and Convergence:** Correlation analysis indicated that higher linear probing accuracy (reflecting better semantic retention in the latent features) corresponded with improved generative performance. MacTok also exhibited faster convergence during training compared to other baselines.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Mask Ratio:** Experiments with varying maximum mask ratios ($M$) showed that a moderate $M$ of 70% yielded optimal generation performance, suggesting that stronger masking contributes to more robust and informative latent representations. The combination of random and semantic masking further improved generation quality. Decoder fine-tuning was found to mitigate reconstruction degradation caused by high mask ratios without compromising the learned semantic structure.
- **Module Contribution:** Sequential addition of MacTok's key modules demonstrated their individual contributions:
 - Random masking mitigated posterior collapse.
 - Local alignment improved reconstruction and generation by introducing structured organization.
 - DINO-guided semantic masking strengthened semantic robustness and improved gFID and IS.
 - Global alignment further enhanced high-level semantic consistency.
 The integration of all modules resulted in the best overall performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MacTok contributes to the field of visual generative modeling by providing a method to address the long-standing challenge of posterior collapse in continuous KL-based tokenizers. This work demonstrates that with appropriate regularization and semantic guidance, continuous tokenizers can learn highly compressed and robust latent representations, which has several implications:

**Robustness and Efficiency in Continuous Tokenization:** MacTok's primary significance lies in its ability to effectively mitigate posterior collapse, a known limitation that has hindered the widespread adoption and performance of continuous tokenizers, especially under strong compression. By achieving high-fidelity image tokenization with significantly fewer tokens (e.g., 64 or 128 tokens), MacTok offers a more efficient alternative compared to many existing discrete and continuous tokenizers that often require hundreds or thousands of tokens. This token reduction can lead to substantial computational savings during the training and inference of downstream generative models.

**Enhanced Latent Space Quality:** The combined strategies of random and DINO-guided semantic masking, along with global and local representation alignment, enable MacTok to learn a latent space that is not only compact but also semantically rich, structured, and discriminative. The improved organization of the latent space, as evidenced by visualization and linear probing accuracy, means that the latent tokens effectively encode both global layout and local semantic details. This enhanced latent space quality directly translates to superior reconstruction fidelity and state-of-the-art generation performance.

**Advancing Generative Modeling:** By providing a stable and efficient continuous tokenizer, MacTok can serve as a robust foundation for various generative model architectures, particularly diffusion models and autoregressive transformers. The ability to work with fewer, yet more informative, tokens allows generative models to operate more efficiently, potentially enabling the generation of higher-resolution images or more complex scenes with reduced computational overhead. The faster convergence observed with MacTok also suggests potential for reduced training times for these large generative models.

**Broader Applicability of Masking and Alignment Strategies:** The principles demonstrated by MacTok, particularly the use of informed masking to force information flow through a latent bottleneck and multi-level representation alignment with pre-trained vision models, could be transferable to other latent variable models or representation learning tasks that face similar issues of latent space collapse or the need for compact, semantically meaningful embeddings. This could extend beyond image generation to areas such as video generation, 3D data synthesis, or even modalities beyond vision.

**Pushing the Boundaries of Compression:** The results indicate that it is possible to achieve strong generative performance with a token count significantly lower than previously considered feasible for high-quality image generation. This pushes the boundaries of compression in visual latent spaces, opening new avenues for research into ultra-efficient visual representations and large-scale generative models.
