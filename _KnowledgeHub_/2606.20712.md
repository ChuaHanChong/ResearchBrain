# Real-World Deployment of Massively Parallel Sampling-Based MPC for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A high-fidelity sampling-based Model Predictive Control framework was successfully deployed on a real robot, demonstrating Model Tensor Planning's (MTP) superior exploration capabilities for contact-rich manipulation compared to unimodal baselines. The work also provided empirical insights into the effectiveness of online domain randomization for real-time adaptation, highlighting the utility of contact-initiation parameters.

## Problem

- Gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) struggles with the non-smooth and hybrid dynamics inherent in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- Standard sampling-based MPC algorithms, such as MPPI and CEM, often get trapped in local minima due to their reliance on unimodal Gaussian proposals within the multimodal cost landscapes of contact-rich scenarios.
- Advanced sampling-based MPC methods like Model Tensor Planning (MTP) lacked real-world validation, scalability analysis under real-time constraints, and investigation into the sim-to-real gap for complex manipulation.

## Method

- A JAX-based SMPC framework, Hydrax, was developed, leveraging MuJoCo MJX for massively parallel, GPU-accelerated simulation of thousands of control trajectories.
- The Model Tensor Planning (MTP) algorithm was deployed, which augments local Gaussian samples with global, graph-based tensor-path samples to enhance exploration in multimodal cost landscapes.
- A real-to-sim-to-real pipeline was established using ROS 2 to couple the planning loop to a physical Franka Research 3 robot and a 3D-printed T-block for contact-rich pushing tasks.
- A structured domain randomization interface was implemented to analyze the impact of randomizing global physics parameters (e.g., mass, friction) and contact-initiation parameters (e.g., collision margins) using uniform or online adaptive weighting.

## Results

- MTP successfully solved the Bugtrap Escape task in all tested seeds, consistently outperforming MPPI, CEM, and PS, which failed to escape the trap in all but one instance.
- On the Push-T manipulation task, MTP achieved the lowest average final pose error and variance compared to unimodal baselines (MPPI, CEM, PS) in both simulation and real-robot experiments.
- Domain randomization improved final pose quality on the real robot compared to a non-randomized baseline, with contact-initiation parameters yielding interpretable weight evolution; however, the performance advantage of online adaptive reweighting over uniform weighting was small in the tested scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MTP's integration of global, graph-based sampling with local Gaussian perturbations effectively navigates multimodal cost landscapes in contact-rich tasks, enabling successful mode switching where unimodal SMPC methods fail.
> - Real-time deployment of high-fidelity SMPC on hardware presents a fundamental trade-off: small physics timesteps are necessary for physical stability and contact fidelity but necessitate shorter planning horizons to meet replanning frequencies, potentially degrading receding-horizon performance.
> - Online domain randomization for adaptation is more effective when applied to contact-initiation parameters (like collision margins), which provide interpretable feedback signals, rather than global physics parameters (like mass or friction) that are often non-identifiable within short online replanning intervals.
