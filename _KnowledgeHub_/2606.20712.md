# Real-World Deployment of Massively Parallel Sampling-Based MPC for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A high-fidelity sampling-based Model Predictive Control framework was successfully deployed on a real robot, demonstrating Model Tensor Planning's (MTP) superior exploration capabilities for contact-rich manipulation compared to unimodal baselines. The work also provided empirical insights into the effectiveness of online domain randomization for real-time adaptation, highlighting the utility of contact-initiation parameters.

## Problem

- Gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) struggles with the non-smooth and hybrid dynamics inherent in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- Standard sampling-based MPC algorithms, such as MPPI and CEM, often get trapped in local minima due to their reliance on unimodal Gaussian proposals within the multimodal cost landscapes of contact-rich scenarios.
- Advanced sampling-based MPC methods like Model Tensor Planning (MTP) lacked real-world validation, scalability analysis under real-time constraints, and investigation into the sim-to-real gap for complex manipulation.

## Method

- A JAX-based SMPC framework, **Hydrax**, leveraging MuJoCo MJX for massively parallel GPU-accelerated simulation of thousands of trajectories, deployed Model Tensor Planning (MTP), augmenting local Gaussian samples with global, graph-based tensor-path samples to enhance exploration in multimodal cost landscapes.
- A real-to-sim-to-real pipeline was established using ROS 2 to couple the planning loop to a physical Franka Research 3 robot and a 3D-printed T-block for contact-rich pushing tasks.
- A structured domain randomization interface was implemented to analyze the impact of randomizing global physics parameters (e.g., mass, friction) and contact-initiation parameters (e.g., collision margins) using uniform or online adaptive weighting.

## Results

- MTP successfully solved the Bugtrap Escape task in all tested seeds, consistently outperforming MPPI, CEM, and PS, which failed to escape the trap in all but one instance.
- On the Push-T manipulation task, MTP achieved the lowest average final pose error and variance compared to unimodal baselines (MPPI, CEM, PS) in both simulation and real-robot experiments.
- Domain randomization improved final pose quality on the real robot compared to a non-randomized baseline, with contact-initiation parameters yielding interpretable weight evolution; however, the performance advantage of online adaptive reweighting over uniform weighting was small in the tested scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MTP's integration of global, graph-based sampling with local Gaussian perturbations effectively navigates multimodal cost landscapes in contact-rich tasks, enabling successful mode switching where unimodal SMPC methods fail.
> - Real-time deployment of high-fidelity SMPC on hardware presents a fundamental trade-off: small physics timesteps are necessary for physical stability and contact fidelity but necessitate shorter planning horizons to meet replanning frequencies, potentially degrading receding-horizon performance.
> - Online domain randomization for adaptation is more effective when applied to contact-initiation parameters (like collision margins), which provide interpretable feedback signals, rather than global physics parameters (like mass or friction) that are often non-identifiable within short online replanning intervals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical gap in the application of Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) to contact-rich robotic manipulation. Classical gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) often struggles with contact-rich scenarios due to the non-smooth and hybrid nature of their dynamics, which violate assumptions of differentiability. SMPC, by contrast, evaluates candidate control trajectories through forward simulation, making it suitable for such complex dynamics, especially when combined with massively parallel GPU simulation and frameworks like JAX and MuJoCo MJX.

While SMPC has demonstrated success in areas like robot locomotion (e.g., quadruped jumping, climbing, drone flight), its real-world application in contact-rich manipulation has been more limited. Prior work in manipulation, such as that by Pezzato et al., has faced challenges including limited GPU throughput and real-time control constraints, which restricted effective parallelization and trajectory smoothness.

A significant limitation of standard SMPC algorithms like Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) and Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is their reliance on unimodal Gaussian proposals. In contact-rich manipulation, the cost landscape is frequently multimodal, leading these algorithms to become trapped in local minima. Model Tensor Planning (MTP) was developed to address this by integrating global, graph-based sampling with local Gaussian perturbations, thereby improving exploration capabilities. However, MTP's evaluation had previously been confined to simulation, leaving its real-world applicability, scalability under real-time constraints, and sensitivity to the sim-to-real gap unexplored.

Furthermore, the paper situates its analysis of online domain randomization within the context of improving sim-to-real robustness. While domain randomization is known to help transfer policies from simulation to reality, its real-time effectiveness in adapting to unknown physics parameters within the tight compute budgets of online SMPC, particularly for contact-rich tasks, remains an open question. This work aims to provide empirical insights into which physics parameters yield informative adaptation signals in this online setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to advance the practical deployment and understanding of sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) in real-world, contact-rich robotic manipulation. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current MPC and SMPC methodologies:

- **Addressing limitations of gradient-based MPC:** Traditional gradient-based MPC algorithms are ill-suited for contact-rich manipulation due to the non-smooth and non-differentiable nature of contact dynamics. SMPC offers a promising alternative by evaluating candidate control trajectories through simulation, but its real-world application in manipulation has been constrained.
- **Bridging the sim-to-real gap for advanced SMPC:** Despite the theoretical advantages of SMPC, most prior work, especially for advanced algorithms like Model Tensor Planning (MTP), has been confined to simulation. There is a need to evaluate the performance, scalability, and real-time applicability of these methods on physical hardware under realistic constraints. The paper seeks to deploy a high-fidelity SMPC framework using MuJoCo MJX for contact-rich tasks on a real robot.
- **Improving exploration in multimodal cost landscapes:** Standard SMPC algorithms (e.g., MPPI, CEM) often struggle with multimodal cost landscapes common in contact-rich manipulation, frequently converging to suboptimal local minima due to their reliance on unimodal Gaussian proposals. A key objective is to demonstrate that MTP, with its structured global sampling, can reliably navigate such complex landscapes and outperform these unimodal baselines in tasks requiring mode switching.
- **Empirical analysis of online domain randomization:** While domain randomization (DR) is a technique for enhancing sim-to-real transfer, its utility in real-time, online SMPC, where compute budgets are tight, needs systematic investigation. The research aims to identify which physics parameters (e.g., global parameters like friction and mass, versus contact-initiation parameters like collision margins) yield interpretable and exploitable adaptation signals within the MPC's sample budget, and to understand the challenges associated with using these signals for real-time adaptation.

In summary, the motivation is to develop and validate a robust SMPC framework capable of executing complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks on real robots, address the exploration limitations of existing SMPC methods, and provide empirical insights into the effectiveness of online domain randomization in this challenging context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes an MPC framework and evaluates it using both simulation and real-world experiments on contact-rich manipulation tasks.

#### 3.1. A. Framework and Environments

The framework is built upon Hydrax, a JAX-based SMPC framework leveraging MuJoCo MJX as its high-fidelity dynamics backend. This setup enables end-to-end JIT compilation and `vmap`-vectorized rollouts, allowing thousands of trajectories to be simulated in parallel on a GPU without requiring hand-crafted dynamics models.

Two environments are used for evaluation:
- **Bugtrap Escape:** This is a 2D point mass task within a U-shaped trap constructed from physical box primitives. The objective is for the agent to escape the trap and reach a goal, penalizing external contact forces and distance to the goal. This task is specifically designed to test the mode-switching capabilities of the algorithms.
- **Push-T:** This task involves a Franka Research 3 arm pushing a rigid T-shaped block to a target pose. The T-block is modeled as a 7 DoF free-floating rigid body, incorporating realistic friction, mass distribution, and full table-contact dynamics. The cost function combines T-pose alignment, end-effector proximity to the object, and a retraction term to prevent singularities. Control inputs are end-effector twists, which are mapped to joint velocities using differentiable inverse kinematics.

#### 3.2. B. Real-to-Sim-to-Real Pipeline

For real-robot experiments, a pipeline based on ROS 2 is implemented, coupling the planning loop to a Franka Research 3 arm and a 3D-printed T-block. This system consists of four asynchronous ROS 2 processes:
- **Control Nodes:** Two nodes run MoveIt2 Servo for 1 kHz Cartesian velocity streaming and select the current horizon step at 50 Hz.
- **Bridge Node:** Publishes Mocap poses (from an OptiTrack system) at 100 Hz.
- **Planning Node:** Executes the JIT-compiled MPC on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU at a replanning frequency of 8–10 Hz.

The planning and servo nodes are decoupled, allowing the servo node to interpolate within the published action trajectory based on elapsed time, ensuring smooth control between replanning steps. Hand-eye calibration is used to align the OptiTrack world frame with the robot's base frame.

#### 3.3. C. Sampling-Based MPC Algorithms

The research compares the following SMPC algorithms:
- **MPPI (Model Predictive Path Integral):** Uses exponential reweighting of Gaussian perturbations.
- **CEM (Cross-Entropy Method):** Employs hard elite selection (top-E trajectories).
- **PS (Predictive Sampling):** Selects the lowest-cost rollout as the next mean.
- **MTP (Model Tensor Planning):** Augments local Gaussian samples with global tensor-path samples drawn from a layered graph over the control space. These are combined into a joint batch, followed by CEM-style elite selection and MPPI reweighting over the elites. The best sample is then applied. A mixing coefficient β ∈ controls the global/local sample budget split.

#### 3.4. D. Domain Randomization

A structured domain randomization (DR) interface is implemented, supporting randomization of parameters at the per-body, per-geom, and per-joint levels. For parameters with derived quantities (e.g., mass, inertia), separate MuJoCo models are compiled per domain tuple, and the resulting arrays are extracted into the batched pytree to ensure physical consistency.

In each planning step, `N` trajectories are rolled out simultaneously over `D` randomized domains. The per-domain costs are aggregated via a weighted average, distributing the total sample budget between trajectory samples and domains. Two approaches for per-domain weights `w_d` are explored:
- **Uniform weights:** All domains are weighted equally.
- **Online adaptive weights:** Weights are updated based on the discrepancy between the predicted object pose (`T_hat^(d)`) under each domain for the elite sample and the observed object pose (`T_obs`) at the next replanning step. This discrepancy is measured using the geodesic pose error `d_SE(3)`: `w_d ∝ exp(-d_SE(3)(T_hat^(d), T_obs) / σ^2)`.

#### 3.5. E. Experimental Design

Experiments address three research questions:
- **RQ1:** Comparison of MTP against unimodal baselines (MPPI, CEM, PS) on tasks requiring multimodal exploration (Bugtrap Escape).
- **RQ2:** Evaluation of performance transfer to real hardware under real-time constraints for the Push-T task.
- **RQ3:** Analysis of which physics parameters yield informative domain-randomization feedback signals for online adaptation.

Simulation experiments for Bugtrap Escape run at 25 Hz for 10,000 steps with 128 samples per seed. Push-T simulations use 256 samples, planning at 20 Hz, over 100 steps, with both long (0.625 sec) and short (0.375 sec) horizons. Real-robot evaluations for Push-T use 1024 control samples, planning at 8–10 Hz. Domain randomization analysis involves sweeping over global physics parameters (friction, mass) and contact-initiation parameters (end-effector collision margins) across 6 seeds with 8 domains, using 160 samples per domain, planning at 5 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several findings regarding the performance of MTP, the challenges of real-world SMPC deployment, and the efficacy of online domain randomization.

#### 4.1. A. Bugtrap Escape – Simulation Experiment (RQ1)

- **Failure of Unimodal Baselines:** MPPI and CEM consistently failed to solve the Bugtrap Escape task across all tested seeds. CEM's greedy elite selection often trapped it against the inner wall in a contact-avoiding limit cycle, while MPPI's full-sample averaging led the particle to an equilibrium point in the trap's center, balancing wall-collision costs and the goal attractor. PS also mirrored CEM's behavior, with only one instance of escape.
- **MTP's Superiority:** MTP successfully escaped the trap and reached the goal in all seeds. This outcome is attributed to MTP's enhanced exploration capability, which combines local Gaussian sampling with global graph-based candidates, enabling it to navigate the multimodal cost landscape and discover the escape route.

#### 4.2. B. Push-T – Simulation and Real-World Experiments (RQ2)

- **Simulation Performance:** In simulation, MTP achieved the lowest final pose error across various initial configurations of the T-block and end-effector. The unimodal baselines (MPPI, CEM, PS) frequently stalled in local minima or exhibited degraded performance, especially with shorter planning horizons. This reinforced MTP's ability to handle complex manipulation tasks requiring mode switching.
- **Real-World Performance:** On the physical Franka Research 3 robot, MTP again demonstrated the lowest average final pose error and variance among the tested algorithms.
 - MPPI often avoided contact altogether.
 - PS initiated contact aggressively but struggled with overshooting and brittle contact exploitation.
 - CEM produced smoother motions but tended to stall in local minima after initial pose corrections.
- **Real-Time Deployment Challenges:** The primary bottleneck for real-time performance was identified as the MuJoCo MJX physics solver, which dominated runtime. This necessitated the use of coarser timesteps and shorter planning horizons to meet the required replanning frequencies (8–10 Hz). However, integration steps exceeding 0.025 s often led to physical instability due to deep single-step penetrations, triggering constraint-resolution accelerations rather than realistic physical friction. This highlights a fundamental trade-off: achieving high contact fidelity demands small timesteps, while real-time replanning mandates shorter horizons, potentially degrading receding-horizon performance for tasks requiring longer planning ranges.

#### 4.3. C. Domain Randomization Analysis (RQ3)

- **Global Physics Parameters (Mass, Friction):** Randomizing global physics parameters such as mass and friction did not yield a stable or interpretable adaptation signal within the online MPC setting. The authors found these parameters to be effectively non-identifiable within a single replanning interval. The physics was largely dominated by contact timing and constraint resolution forces rather than smooth variations in the randomized parameters. The resulting error signals were often asymmetric (e.g., low-mass domains overshot, while high-mass domains moved less), causing adaptive weights to drift spuriously towards heavy/high-friction domains.
- **Contact-Initiation Parameters (Collision Margins):** In contrast, randomizing contact-initiation parameters, such as end-effector collision margins, produced interpretable and mode-dependent weight evolution. For instance, domain weights shifted towards larger margins during turning phases of the push, and away from larger margins during straight pushing, which is consistent with how margins directly influence the onset of contact.
- **Hardware Robustness Gains:** On the real robot, a non-randomized baseline accumulated error due to kinematic misalignment. Both uniform domain randomization and adaptive reweighting (based on collision margins) improved the final pose quality compared to this baseline. However, the performance advantage of adaptive reweighting over uniform weighting was observed to be small and not statistically conclusive in the current experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing practical challenges in deploying advanced Model Predictive Control (MPC) techniques to real-world, contact-rich scenarios.

- **Advancement of SMPC in Manipulation:** The work provides a significant step forward in the real-world application of Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) for contact-rich manipulation. It presents one of the first successful real-robot deployments and systematic evaluations of the Model Tensor Planning (MTP) algorithm, demonstrating its capabilities beyond simulated environments.
- **Validation of MTP's Superiority:** The consistent outperformance of MTP over unimodal baselines (MPPI, CEM, PS) in tasks requiring mode switching, both in simulation (Bugtrap Escape) and on real hardware (Push-T), establishes MTP as a more robust and effective strategy for problems with multimodal cost landscapes prevalent in contact-rich interactions. This finding is significant for developing more general and capable manipulation policies.
- **Identification of Real-Time Deployment Challenges:** The study highlights critical practical tensions and limitations encountered during real-time deployment of high-fidelity SMPC. The trade-off between small timesteps (necessary for contact fidelity and avoiding physical instabilities) and short planning horizons (required to meet real-time replanning frequencies) exposes a fundamental challenge for receding-horizon performance, particularly in tasks demanding longer-range planning. This provides valuable insights for future framework design and algorithm development.
- **Empirical Understanding of Online Domain Randomization:** The detailed analysis of online domain randomization yields practical implications for sim-to-real transfer in time-constrained robotic systems. The finding that global physics parameters (mass, friction) are difficult to identify and adapt to in real-time, while contact-initiation parameters (collision margins) provide more interpretable and actionable feedback, informs how domain randomization should be structured for online adaptation. This suggests that future efforts might benefit from focusing randomization on parameters that directly influence observable contact events.
- **Development of a Robust Framework:** The development and release of a JAX-based, MuJoCo MJX-backed SMPC framework (Hydrax) and a complete real-robot pipeline for contact-rich manipulation represents a valuable contribution to the research community, providing a foundation for future work in this area.

The research's findings underscore the complexity of deploying high-fidelity, sampling-based MPC on real hardware and suggest directions for future research, such as integrating learned value-function surrogates or generative sampling priors to address the short-horizon problem. The insights gained regarding contact sensitivity, computational budgets, and the utility of different domain randomization parameters have implications for the design of more robust and adaptive control systems for complex robotic interactions.
