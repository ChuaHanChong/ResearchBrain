# DreamLite: A Lightweight On-Device Unified Model for Image Generation and Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ByteDance developed DreamLite, a 0.39B parameter diffusion model that unifies text-to-image generation and text-guided image editing on mobile devices. The model achieves 1024x1024 image processing in under one second on a flagship smartphone, demonstrating competitive performance against larger server-side models while operating entirely on-device.

## Problem

- Large-scale diffusion models are too computationally intensive and memory-heavy for practical deployment on consumer-grade edge devices.
- Existing on-device generative models primarily specialize in text-to-image generation, requiring separate models or additional components for instruction-based image editing.
- Achieving real-time, high-resolution (e.g., 1024x1024) image generation and editing performance on mobile hardware presents significant challenges due to latency and resource constraints.

## Method

- A compact architecture featuring a systematically compressed **0.39B** parameter UNet, a lightweight **TinyVAE**, and a **Qwen3-VL-2B** text encoder is developed.
- An ==in-context conditioning== mechanism spatially concatenates target and conditioning image latents (blank for generation, source for editing) with explicit task tokens, enabling a single network for both tasks.
- A ==task-progressive joint pretraining== strategy (T2I > Edit with ==foreground-emphasis mask== > Unified) is combined with post-training (SFT and ==Reward Feedback Learning==) and ==Distribution Matching Distillation== for 4-step inference.

## Results

- **DreamLite** achieved an overall score of **0.72** on the GenEval benchmark for image generation and **4.11** on the ImgEdit benchmark for image editing, demonstrating competitive performance with, and often outperforming, existing lightweight and some larger unified models.
- The model completed 1024x1024 image generation or editing tasks in less than **1 second** on a Snapdragon 8 Gen3 smartphone, with the UNet backbone contributing approximately **0.42 seconds** for 4 denoising steps.
- The UNet backbone occupied **389.00 MB**, enabling efficient on-device deployment and providing a unified solution for both creative functionalities on mobile devices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The in-context conditioning paradigm, guided by task tokens, successfully unifies distinct generative (T2I) and editing tasks within a single compact diffusion model.
> - A task-progressive pretraining scheme, particularly with foreground-emphasis masking during editing pretraining, is crucial for stable convergence and optimal performance in compact, multi-task diffusion models.
> - Combining a compact architecture with supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and step distillation enables real-time on-device performance while maintaining high visual quality and instruction following.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the expanding field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on diffusion models for image synthesis and editing. Large-scale diffusion models (e.g., FLUX, HunyuanImage, Qwen Image) have demonstrated significant capabilities in text-to-image (T2I) generation and text-guided image editing. However, these models typically consist of billions of parameters, leading to high computational demands, substantial memory footprints, and high inference latency, which limit their deployment on consumer-grade edge devices.

A parallel research direction has concentrated on developing efficient diffusion models. This includes efforts to optimize architectures (e.g., PixArt-Σ, SANA), attention mechanisms, and training/inference procedures to reduce model size and accelerate performance for both T2I generation and image editing (e.g., VIBE, EditMGT). Another significant area is on-device generative models, which specifically target deployment on mobile hardware. Prior works like SnapFusion, Mobile Diffusion, and SnapGen have utilized compact U-Net backbones to enable on-device T2I generation, with some exploring efficient diffusion transformers (SnapGen++). Mobile-O has attempted to unify visual generation and understanding, though with noted limitations in fine-grained visual control for editing tasks.

Despite these advancements, a notable gap exists in the provision of a single, unified on-device model that seamlessly supports both T2I generation and text-guided image editing. Existing on-device models largely specialize in T2I generation, requiring either separate models or additional components for editing tasks. This increases system complexity and resource consumption, which is particularly restrictive on memory-constrained mobile devices. This paper addresses this gap by proposing DreamLite, a compact, unified diffusion model designed for efficient on-device execution of both generation and editing tasks. It positions itself by integrating architectural efficiency with a multi-task training strategy to achieve capabilities previously associated with larger, server-side or specialized models, but within a compact on-device footprint. The work also integrates advancements in post-training alignment (RLHF) and sampling acceleration (step distillation) adapted for the constraints of mobile deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a lightweight, unified on-device diffusion model capable of performing both text-to-image (T2I) generation and text-guided image editing within a single network. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Address Deployment Challenges of Large Diffusion Models:** Existing state-of-the-art diffusion models, while powerful, typically employ billions of parameters. This scale leads to high inference latency and substantial memory requirements, making their deployment on resource-constrained consumer-grade devices (such as smartphones) impractical. The research aims to overcome these limitations by proposing a highly compact model architecture.

2. **Unify Generation and Editing on-device:** Current on-device diffusion models predominantly focus on T2I generation. Instruction-based image editing often necessitates separate models or additional editing-specific components. This separation increases system complexity, memory consumption, and development overhead for applications requiring both functionalities. The goal is to provide a single, unified network that can handle both "generate" and "edit" functionalities, offering a streamlined user experience and reducing deployment complexity.

3. **Achieve Real-time Performance on Mobile Devices:** For interactive mobile applications, low latency is critical. Traditional diffusion models require numerous iterative denoising steps, further exacerbating latency. A key motivation is to enable generation or editing of high-resolution images (e.g., 1024 × 1024) in sub-second timescales on contemporary smartphones.

4. **Maintain Performance with Compactness:** While efficiency is paramount, the model must still produce high-quality images with strong semantic alignment and accurate instruction following. The research aims to demonstrate that a compact, on-device model can achieve performance competitive with, or even surpass, existing specialized lightweight baselines and remain competitive with larger server-side models on standard benchmarks for both generation and editing tasks.

5. **Develop a Stable and Effective Training Strategy for Compact Unified Models:** Training a compact model to perform divergent tasks (generation and editing) within a unified framework presents challenges due to limited capacity and potential task interference. A motivation is to develop a robust training methodology that ensures stable convergence and optimal performance across both tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the advanced capabilities of large diffusion models and the practical constraints of on-device deployment, offering a compact, unified solution for real-time image generation and editing on mobile devices.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamLite’s methodology involves a compact model architecture, an in-context conditioning mechanism, a task-progressive joint pretraining strategy, a two-stage post-training approach, and step distillation for accelerated inference.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

DreamLite comprises three main modules: a UNet backbone, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), and a text encoder.

- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE):** An extremely lightweight VAE, TinyVAE (2.5M parameters), is employed for efficient image tokenization, mapping 4-channel images to 8x8 downsampled latent representations.
- **Compact UNet:** Building upon the mobile-efficient architecture of SnapGen, the UNet backbone is systematically compressed. Optimizations include:
 - Reducing transformer blocks (from to) and shrinking channel dimensions (from to).
 - Removing self-attention layers at high-resolution stages to mitigate quadratic complexity.
 - Replacing standard convolutions with expanded separable convolutions.
 - Setting the hidden channel expansion ratio to 3 in the feed-forward network.
 - Adopting Multi-Query Attention (MQA) with a single KV head.
 - Implementing stage alignment, QK-RMSNorm, and a light text projector.
 These optimizations reduce the UNet from a 2.5B baseline to 389M parameters.
- **Text Encoder:** Qwen3-VL-2B is used as the text encoder for robust visual-language comprehension and processing of multimodal inputs.
- **In-context Paradigm:** To unify generation and editing within a single network, an in-context conditioning framework is adopted. The model takes a two-panel latent input by spatially concatenating the target image latent (z_tgt) and a conditioning image latent (z_cond) along the width.
 - For **T2I generation**, z_cond is a blank (all-black) image latent.
 - For **image editing**, z_cond is the source image latent.
 Explicit task tokens ([Generate] or [Edit]) are prepended to text prompts to resolve task ambiguity without adding parameters or specialized branches.

#### 3.2. Task-progressive Joint Pretraining

A three-stage task-progressive joint pretraining scheme is proposed to ensure stable convergence of the compact unified model:

1. **Text-to-Image (T2I) Pretraining:** DreamLite is initially trained as a standard T2I diffusion model using the flow matching objective. This stage establishes a strong generative prior. Progressive resolution training (256x256 to 1024x1024), multi-scale training, logit-normal noise sampling, and dynamic time shifting are employed.
2. **Edit Pretraining:** The in-context conditioning mechanism is activated, and the model is trained on paired text-guided image editing data using a flow-matching loss (L_fmw). To address the challenge of small target edit regions, a **foreground-emphasis mask** is applied to re-weight the loss. This mask is derived by pixel-wise differencing, dilation, connected component filtering, and max-pooling, assigning higher loss weights (w(x) = log2(x) + 1) to edited regions. This aligns visual conditioning with the generative latent space.
3. **Unified Joint Training:** The model is jointly trained on a mixture of T2I and editing data. Task tokens ([Generate] and [Edit]) are used to guide the model, consolidating the generative priors and instruction-following capabilities into a single set of parameters.

#### 3.3. Post Training

A two-stage post-training strategy refines the model's behavior:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The model is fine-tuned on a curated dataset of approximately 0.5M high-quality samples to improve visual quality and instruction following.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Reward Feedback Learning (ReFL) is used to align DreamLite with human preferences. Scalar rewards from pre-trained reward models (HPSv3 for generation, EditReward for editing) guide the denoising trajectory via gradient backpropagation. A ReLU-truncated reward formulation (L_rl = - max(0, r(c, x̂) - b)) is applied, with task-specific hyperparameters for `b`.

#### 3.4. Step Distillation

To enable real-time mobile inference, Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD2) is applied. This technique reduces the sampling process to 4 denoising steps without significant compromise in visual quality. DMD minimizes the approximate Kullback-Leibler divergence between real and generated image distributions, and GAN loss (L_GAN) is incorporated to enhance diversity and realism.

#### 3.5. Training Data

The training corpus includes 20M text-to-image pairs (categorized into General Perception, Human & Portrait, Graphic Design, Scene Text, Artistic Styles) and 1.7M image editing samples (categorized into Understanding Edit, Local Edit, Global Edit, View Edit, Style Edit). A sampling ratio of 1:1 between T2I and editing data is used during unified joint training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DreamLite achieved competitive performance across generation and editing benchmarks, demonstrating its efficacy as a unified on-device model.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Image Generation:**
 - On the GenEval benchmark, DreamLite (0.39B parameters) achieved an overall score of 0.72. This performance is competitive with, and in some aspects surpasses, larger lightweight generative models (e.g., SANA-1.6B at 0.67, Meissonic-1B at 0.54) and prior on-device models (e.g., SnapGen at 0.70, SnapGen++ at 0.66, SANA-0.6B at 0.64, Nitro-E-GRPO at 0.70). It also remains competitive with significantly larger unified models such as FLUX.1-Dev (12B, 0.67) and OmniGen2 (4B, 0.80), though it scored lower than BAGEL (7B, 0.82) and LongCat-Image (6B, 0.87).
 - On the DPG benchmark, DreamLite scored 85.8, outperforming many lightweight (e.g., Meissonic-1B at 65.3, Hunyuan-DiT-1.5B at 78.9) and some on-device models (e.g., SnapGen at 81.1, Nitro-E-GRPO at 81.1), while being competitive with others like SnapGen++ (85.2) and SANA-0.6B (83.6).
- **Image Editing:**
 - On the ImgEdit benchmark, DreamLite achieved an overall score of 4.11, assessed by GPT-4o. This score positions it as a leading lightweight model, outperforming VIBE (1.6B, 3.85) and EditMGT (0.96B, 2.89). It demonstrated performance comparable to or exceeding several larger unified models such as BAGEL (7B, 3.42) and OmniGen2 (4B, 3.44), and was competitive with DeepGen1.0 (2B, 4.03), while scoring lower than models like Z-Image (6B, 4.30) and LongCat-Image (6B, 4.49).
 - On the GEdit-EN benchmark, using Qwen2.5-VL as the judge, DreamLite achieved an overall score of 6.88. This is competitive with VIBE (1.6B, 7.28) and EditMGT (0.96B, 6.33), and comparable to larger models like OmniGen2 (4B, 6.79).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons indicated that DreamLite maintained high structural integrity and semantic alignment in generation tasks, even with complex prompts. It demonstrated an understanding of stylistic nuances and spatial compositions, producing fewer artifacts in high-resolution synthesis compared to other lightweight models. For editing tasks, DreamLite exhibited precise understanding of spatial relationships, occlusion, and ability to apply complex style and background transformations while preserving main subject integrity. It also efficiently executed fine-grained localized edits without unintended disruptions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Condition Mechanism:** The In-context (IC) mechanism significantly improved editing performance (ImgEdit from 3.67 to 3.88) compared to the Pix2Pix-style channel concatenation, and was more suitable for unified training (GenEval from 0.61 to 0.71).
- **Training Recipe:** The proposed Task-progressive Joint (TPJ) pre-training (T2I → Edit → Unified) was crucial for performance. Direct joint training (T2I → Unified) led to a performance bottleneck (GenEval 0.65, ImgEdit 3.14), while TPJ recovered and surpassed performance for both generation (GenEval 0.71) and editing (ImgEdit 3.94).
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Integrating RLHF into the TPJ pipeline further enhanced overall performance, yielding 4.11 on ImgEdit and 0.72 on GenEval. Qualitative results showed improvements in image aesthetics, high-frequency details, background realism, and human identity maintenance during editing.
- **Step Distillation:** DMD significantly accelerated sampling, enabling 4-step inference, with a slight, expected performance penalty in complex semantic alignment tasks (ImgEdit 3.8 after distillation).

#### 4.4. On-device Deployment

- DreamLite's UNet backbone (quantized to W8A8) demonstrated an inference latency of 103.84ms per step on a Snapdragon 8 Gen3 (Xiaomi 14).
- For a 4-step sampling schedule, the total UNet inference time was approximately 0.42s for a 1024 × 1024 image.
- Including VAE encoding/decoding (approx. 22ms each) and system overhead, the end-to-end generation or editing task was completed in less than 1 second on a Xiaomi 14 smartphone.
- The overall model size for the UNet was 389.00 MB, with the VAE adding 2.45 MB. The text encoder (Qwen3-VL-2B) remains a current latency bottleneck, with common prompts pre-computed for instantaneous interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamLite represents a significant advancement in on-device generative AI, primarily through its successful integration of both text-to-image generation and text-guided image editing into a single, compact diffusion model. This work addresses a critical gap in the field by enabling a unified user experience for creative tasks directly on mobile devices, which previously required separate, larger models or server-side processing.

The key significance and potential impact are:

1. **Reduced Deployment Complexity and Resource Consumption:** By unifying two distinct but complementary functionalities within a single 0.39B parameter network, DreamLite eliminates the need to deploy multiple specialized models. This significantly reduces memory footprint and computational overhead on resource-constrained mobile hardware, simplifying application development and deployment pipelines.
2. **Enabling Real-time On-device AI Creativity:** The achievement of generating or editing 1024 × 1024 images in less than 1 second on a flagship smartphone demonstrates a new benchmark for real-time interactive generative AI on edge devices. This capability has the potential to transform mobile creative applications, offering instant visual content creation and modification without reliance on cloud infrastructure.
3. **Advancement in Compact Model Design and Training:** The paper's systematic U-Net compression strategies and the novel task-progressive joint pretraining scheme (T2I → Edit → Unified) provide a robust methodology for stably training compact, multi-task diffusion models. This approach can inform future research in efficiently scaling complex AI capabilities down to edge devices.
4. **Improved User Experience and Accessibility:** The unified "generate and edit" experience enhances user workflows, allowing for seamless iteration on creative ideas within a single application. This broadens the accessibility of advanced generative AI tools to a wider user base through mobile devices, potentially democratizing creative content production.
5. **Benchmarking for On-device Generative AI:** DreamLite establishes a new performance baseline for unified on-device diffusion models, outperforming prior specialized mobile models and remaining competitive with significantly larger server-side counterparts. This provides a valuable reference point for future research and development in this domain.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the scale of the text encoder and opportunities for improving reconstruction fidelity with a slightly larger VAE, highlight clear avenues for future work, stimulating further innovation in optimizing end-to-end on-device generative pipelines.

Overall, DreamLite contributes a practical and efficient solution for enabling comprehensive generative AI capabilities on mobile devices, paving the way for more sophisticated and integrated AI-powered applications in the consumer market.
