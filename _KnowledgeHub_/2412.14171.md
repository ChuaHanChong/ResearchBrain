# Thinking in Space: How Multimodal Large Language Models See, Remember, and Recall Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from New York University, Yale University, and Stanford University introduced VSI-Bench, a video-based benchmark, to assess the visual-spatial intelligence of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). The study found a substantial performance gap between MLLMs and human capabilities in spatial reasoning, identifying relational reasoning and egocentric-allocentric transformation as key limitations, while demonstrating that explicit cognitive map generation can enhance spatial distance understanding.

## Problem

- Most existing multimodal large language model (MLLM) benchmarks primarily focus on content-level understanding or temporal reasoning from video, neglecting 3D spatial relationships.
- MLLMs lack the intuitive human ability to perceive, remember, and reason about spaces from sequential visual observations, such as building an implicit "cognitive map."
- There was a need to diagnose the strengths and limitations of MLLMs in visual-spatial intelligence and understand how they process spatial information.

## Method

- The researchers developed ==VSI-Bench==, a novel video-based benchmark with over 5,000 question-answer pairs derived from real indoor 3D scene datasets (ScanNet, ARKitScenes), designed to evaluate MLLMs' understanding of spatial relationships.
- They evaluated 15 diverse MLLMs in zero-shot settings using metrics like Accuracy and a new ==Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA)== for quantitative tasks, comparing performance against human baselines.
- They probed MLLMs' spatial thinking by analyzing linguistic self-explanations for errors and by prompting models to generate explicit ==cognitive maps== of observed scenes.

## Results

- Human evaluators achieved **79.2%** accuracy on VSI-Bench, significantly outperforming the best MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro) which scored **48.8%**.
- Manual error analysis of MLLM self-explanations revealed that approximately **71%** of errors were due to faulty spatial reasoning capabilities.
- Explicitly generating and using cognitive maps improved MLLMs' relative distance reasoning accuracy by **10%** on VSI-Bench, highlighting the potential of integrating explicit spatial world models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial reasoning, specifically relational reasoning and egocentric-allocentric transformation, is identified as the primary bottleneck for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in visual-spatial tasks.
> - Linguistic prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought surprisingly led to performance degradation in MLLMs on spatial reasoning tasks, indicating these methods do not inherently solve visual-spatial challenges.
> - MLLMs tend to form localized, rather than globally consistent, cognitive maps from video observations, showing strong local spatial awareness but poor global understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), computer vision, and cognitive AI, specifically addressing the critical but underexplored area of *visual-spatial intelligence* in AI systems.

The past few years have seen an explosion in the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), enabling advanced linguistic reasoning and problem-solving. Simultaneously, Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) have emerged, integrating vision and language to achieve impressive performance on tasks requiring general visual understanding, open-ended dialogue, and even acting as web agents. However, as the authors highlight, most existing MLLM benchmarks and applications primarily focus on *content-level understanding* (e.g., identifying objects, describing actions) or *temporal reasoning* within videos. While these are crucial, they often treat videos as a sequence of 2D images, neglecting the inherent 3D spatial relationships and the dynamic nature of how humans perceive and remember environments.

This paper explicitly distinguishes itself from prior work by focusing on "visual-spatial intelligence," defined as the ability to perceive and mentally manipulate spatial relationships. This concept is deeply rooted in cognitive psychology, drawing parallels to human "cognitive maps" and the transformation between egocentric (first-person) and allocentric (environment-centered) perspectives. Existing benchmarks for MLLMs on video, such as Video-MME, EgoSchema, and OpenEQA, primarily test recognition, perception, and content-level understanding from egocentric videos. In contrast, "Thinking in Space" goes beyond these by requiring MLLMs to build an implicit "cognitive map" of an environment from sequential video observations and then leverage this map for complex spatial reasoning tasks like distance estimation, relative direction, object counting, and route planning.

The novelty lies in:
- **Emphasis on Spatial Reasoning from Video:** Unlike image-based spatial reasoning benchmarks, this work uses continuous video, which more closely mimics human observation of real-world environments and enables richer spatial understanding and memory.
- **Repurposing 3D Reconstruction Data:** By using datasets like ScanNet, ScanNet++, and ARKitScenes, which provide accurate object-level 3D annotations, the work subtly hints at the potential connection between MLLMs and explicit 3D reconstruction, even if MLLMs are primarily trained on 2D data.
- **Diagnostic Approach:** Beyond just benchmarking performance, the paper deeply probes *how* MLLMs reason spatially through linguistic self-explanations and generated cognitive maps, offering crucial diagnostic insights into their internal representations and limitations.
- **Addressing Embodied AI Gaps:** Visual-spatial intelligence is fundamental for embodied agents (e.g., robots, autonomous vehicles, AR/VR systems) that need to navigate and interact with the 3D world. By exposing MLLMs' shortcomings in this area, the work paves the way for more robust and spatially aware embodied AI.

In summary, this research advances the MLLM landscape by introducing a dedicated and diagnostic benchmark for a critical aspect of intelligence—spatial reasoning—that has been largely overlooked, pushing the field towards more sophisticated and human-like AI capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a fundamental human cognitive ability: visual-spatial intelligence. Humans effortlessly perceive a space from sequential visual observations, remember its layout, and retrieve this spatial information to answer questions on demand, like estimating distances or navigating. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), despite being trained on vast video datasets, lack this intuitive "thinking in space" capability. The authors pose critical questions: Can MLLMs "think spatially" when presented with a video recording of an environment? Can they build an accurate, implicit "cognitive map" that allows them to answer questions about a space? What are their strengths and limitations in visual-spatial intelligence?

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **Develop a Novel Benchmark (VSI-Bench) for Visual-Spatial Intelligence:** The primary objective is to create a comprehensive, video-based benchmark that specifically evaluates MLLMs' ability to understand, remember, and reason about spaces from sequential visual observations. This benchmark aims to fill a critical gap in existing MLLM evaluations, which often focus on content or temporal understanding rather than explicit spatial reasoning.
2. **Assess the Visual-Spatial Intelligence of State-of-the-Art MLLMs:** To quantitatively measure the current capabilities of both proprietary (e.g., Gemini, GPT-4o) and open-source MLLMs on a range of diverse spatial tasks. This includes identifying performance gaps relative to human intelligence and baseline models.
3. **Diagnose MLLMs' Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** To go beyond simple performance scores and gain a deeper understanding of *how* MLLMs think (or fail to think) in space. This involves probing their internal reasoning processes both linguistically (through self-explanations) and visually (through generated cognitive maps).
4. **Identify Bottlenecks in MLLMs' Spatial Performance:** Through detailed error analysis, pinpoint the specific sub-components of visual-spatial intelligence (e.g., visual perception, linguistic intelligence, relational reasoning, egocentric-allocentric transformation) that hinder MLLMs' performance on spatial tasks.
5. **Investigate Methods to Enhance MLLMs' Spatial Reasoning:** Explore whether established linguistic reasoning techniques (like Chain-of-Thought) or novel visual prompting methods (like generating cognitive maps) can improve MLLMs' visual-spatial intelligence.

Ultimately, the motivation is to encourage and illuminate research into grounding frontier AI models with robust visual-spatial intelligence, which is crucial for applications in embodied AI, robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented/virtual reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is multi-faceted, involving the creation of a novel benchmark, comprehensive evaluation of MLLMs, and diagnostic probing of their internal spatial reasoning.

#### 3.1. VSI-Bench Construction

**Data Collection and Unification:**
The benchmark, VSI-Bench, is built upon existing public indoor 3D scene reconstruction datasets: ScanNet, ScanNet++, and ARKitScenes. These datasets were chosen for their real-world videos and accurate object-level 3D annotations, which are crucial for generating precise spatial questions. The authors standardize various datasets into a unified meta-information structure, including object categories, bounding boxes, video specifications (resolution, frame rate), and room dimensions (calculated via Alpha shape algorithm). A curated subset of categories is used, excluding rare or extremely small objects, and category remapping ensures consistency.

**Question-Answer Generation:**
VSI-Bench comprises over 5,000 question-answer (QA) pairs derived from 288 real indoor-scene videos.
- **Task Types:** The benchmark includes eight tasks categorized into three types:
 - **Configurational:** Object Count, Relative Distance, Relative Direction, Route Plan. These test understanding of spatial layout.
 - **Measurement Estimation:** Object Size, Room Size, Absolute Distance. These require precise quantitative estimation.
 - **Spatiotemporal:** Appearance Order. This tests memory of object appearance sequence in video.
- **Automation and Human Annotation:** Most QA pairs are auto-annotated using templates and the unified meta-information. The "Route Plan" task is human-annotated, requiring annotators to define navigable paths between objects.
- **Answer Formats:** Tasks are designed for either Multiple-Choice Answer (MCA) or Numerical Answer (NA). For NA tasks, multiple-choice options are also generated with a sampling strategy to ensure diversity.

**Human-in-the-Loop Quality Review:**
To ensure high quality and minimize ambiguity, a rigorous human-in-the-loop verification protocol is implemented. Evaluators review QA pairs, flagging incorrect or ambiguous entries. Errors are traced back to their source (e.g., source dataset annotation errors, problematic data samples, question templates), and the benchmark is iteratively refined until quality standards are met.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Setup

**Benchmark Models:**
15 video-supporting MLLMs are evaluated, covering a diverse range of model families, parameter scales, and training recipes. This includes proprietary models (Gemini-1.5 Flash/Pro, GPT-4o) and open-source models (e.g., InternVL2, ViLA, LLaVA-Video, LLaVA-OneVision). All evaluations are conducted in zero-shot settings with default prompts and greedy decoding for reproducibility.

**Metric Design:**
- **Accuracy (ACC):** For MCA tasks, calculated via exact or fuzzy matching.
- **Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA):** For NA tasks, a novel metric introduced to capture the degree of proximity between predictions and ground truth. It averages relative accuracy across a range of confidence thresholds (0.5 to 0.95), where a prediction is "correct" if its relative error is below `1 - θ`.

**Baselines:**
- **Chance Level (Random/Frequency):** Random selection for MCAs; always selecting the most frequent answer for each task to identify biases.
- **Human Level Performance:** A subset of 400 questions (VSI-Bench tiny) is independently answered by human evaluators to provide an upper bound on performance.

#### 3.3. Probing MLLMs' Spatial Thinking

**Linguistic Probing via Self-Explanations:**
The best-performing MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro) is prompted to provide step-by-step linguistic explanations for its predictions, particularly for incorrect answers. A subset of 163 incorrect answers is manually reviewed, and errors are categorized into four types:
1. **Visual Perception Error:** Misrecognized or misclassified objects.
2. **Linguistic Intelligence Error:** Defects in logical, mathematical reasoning, or language understanding.
3. **Relational Reasoning Error:** Errors in understanding spatial relationships (distance, direction, size).
4. **Egocentric-Allocentric Transformation Error:** Incorrect allocentric spatial layout or improper perspective-taking.

**Limits of CoT Methods in Visuospatial Tasks:**
Three prevalent linguistic prompting techniques are applied to Gemini-1.5 Pro to assess their impact on spatial reasoning:
- **Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Adding "Let's think step by step."
- **Self-Consistency w/ CoT:** Generating multiple CoT responses and taking the majority consensus.
- **Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT):** Dividing reasoning into plan generation and answer prediction with iterative selection.

**Visual Probing via Cognitive Maps:**
MLLMs are prompted to predict object center positions on a 10x10 grid, effectively generating an explicit "cognitive map" of the scene. The quality of these generated maps is quantitatively assessed by comparing the Euclidean distances between all pairs of objects in the predicted map against the ground truth map. Accuracy is defined by deviation no more than one grid unit.

**Enhancing Spatial Reasoning with Cognitive Maps:**
An experiment is conducted where Gemini-1.5 Pro first generates a cognitive map, and then uses this predicted map to answer relative distance questions. This tests if explicit "mental imagery" improves spatial reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the visual-spatial intelligence of MLLMs:

1. **Significant Gap with Human Performance:** On VSI-Bench, human evaluators achieved an average accuracy of 79.2%, significantly outperforming the best MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro) which scored 48.8%. This highlights a substantial gap between current MLLM capabilities and human intuitive spatial understanding. Humans performed exceptionally well on configurational and spatiotemporal tasks (94-100% accuracy), while the gap narrowed slightly for precise measurement estimation tasks, suggesting a relative strength for MLLMs in quantitative estimation compared to complex spatial reasoning.

2. **Emerging Visual-Spatial Intelligence in Top MLLMs:** Despite the gap, leading proprietary models like Gemini-1.5 Pro and top open-source models such as LLaVA-Video-72B and LLaVA-OneVision-72B demonstrated competitive performance, significantly surpassing chance-level baselines. This indicates that MLLMs, even if trained primarily on 2D digital data, exhibit nascent visual-spatial intelligence. However, a majority of open-source models performed below chance level, underscoring inconsistencies in capabilities across the MLLM landscape.

3. **Spatial Reasoning as the Primary Bottleneck:** Manual error analysis of Gemini-1.5 Pro's self-explanations revealed that approximately 71% of all errors were attributable to faulty spatial reasoning capabilities, specifically relational reasoning and egocentric-allocentric transformation. This finding is crucial, as it pinpoints spatial reasoning, rather than visual perception, linguistic intelligence, or temporal processing, as the dominant limitation for MLLMs on visual-spatial tasks. MLLMs often exhibited strong video understanding and linguistic reasoning, but struggled with correctly interpreting spatial relationships or transforming perspectives.

4. **Linguistic Prompting Fails to Improve Spatial Reasoning:** Surprisingly, applying prevailing linguistic reasoning techniques—Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Self-Consistency with CoT, and Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT)—led to a performance *degradation* on VSI-Bench, averaging around 1.1% to 4% decrease. This contrasts sharply with their effectiveness in general language and visual tasks. This indicates that spatial reasoning challenges for MLLMs cannot be overcome merely by encouraging more detailed linguistic thought processes; the root cause appears to lie in the underlying spatial understanding itself.

5. **MLLMs Form Local, Not Global, Cognitive Maps:** When prompted to generate cognitive maps (object center positions on a grid), MLLMs demonstrated strong local spatial awareness, achieving 64% accuracy in positioning adjacent objects. However, this accuracy significantly deteriorated as the distance between objects increased. This suggests that MLLMs tend to form a series of localized world models in their minds from video observations, rather than a unified, globally consistent spatial representation.

6. **Explicit Cognitive Maps Enhance Spatial Distance Reasoning:** A significant finding was that explicitly generating and using cognitive maps improved MLLMs' relative distance reasoning accuracy by 10%. While still benefiting from accurate ground truth maps (20-32% gain), this result highlights the potential of integrating "mental imagery" or explicit spatial world models as a valuable strategy to enhance MLLMs' spatial intelligence, particularly for tasks requiring distance estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant contribution to the fields of multimodal AI and cognitive computing by rigorously addressing the previously underexplored domain of visual-spatial intelligence in Large Language Models.

1. **Introduction of a Foundational Benchmark:** VSI-Bench serves as a critical, high-quality, video-based benchmark tailored specifically for visual-spatial intelligence. Unlike existing benchmarks that focus on content or temporal understanding, VSI-Bench directly challenges MLLMs on their ability to perceive, remember, and reason about real-world 3D spaces from dynamic video input. Its construction, leveraging existing 3D scene datasets and incorporating a meticulous human-in-the-loop quality review, ensures its robustness and relevance. This benchmark provides a standardized tool for future research to quantitatively measure progress in this vital area.

2. **Diagnostic Insights into MLLM Capabilities:** The paper offers crucial diagnostic insights into the internal workings and limitations of state-of-the-art MLLMs. By systematically probing their linguistic self-explanations and generated cognitive maps, the research identifies that spatial reasoning—particularly relational reasoning and egocentric-allocentric transformation—is the primary bottleneck preventing MLLMs from achieving human-level visual-spatial intelligence. This finding provides a clear roadmap for future model development, indicating where architectural or training improvements are most needed.

3. **Challenges to Conventional Prompting Paradigms:** The surprising finding that linguistic prompting techniques (like CoT) can *harm* spatial reasoning performance is highly significant. It suggests that simply extending linguistic reasoning abilities does not inherently solve visual-spatial challenges, implying a deeper, non-linguistic representational or processing deficit. This necessitates a re-evaluation of current MLLM design philosophies and encourages the development of "visuospatial-tailored" prompting or training methods.

4. **Highlighting the Importance of Explicit Spatial Representations:** The demonstration that explicit cognitive map generation *improves* spatial distance reasoning is a key takeaway. It underscores the value of building and utilizing a consistent internal spatial world model within MLLMs, mirroring how humans employ mental imagery for spatial tasks. This finding points towards promising future research directions, such as integrating 3D scene understanding components, developing self-supervised learning objectives for spatial consistency, or designing MLLMs that can explicitly construct and manipulate internal cognitive maps.

5. **Direct Impact on Embodied AI:** The visual-spatial intelligence assessed by VSI-Bench is foundational for embodied AI systems like robotics, autonomous driving, and AR/VR. Robots need to accurately perceive their environment, remember layouts, estimate distances for grasping, and plan routes for navigation. By exposing MLLMs' current limitations in these areas, the research directly informs the development of more capable and reliable embodied agents, paving the way for AI systems that can seamlessly operate and interact within our 3D world. The work encourages the community to ground frontier models with this critical form of intelligence, bridging the gap between digital data understanding and real-world physical interaction.
