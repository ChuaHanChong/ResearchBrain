# Weakly-supervised VLM-guided Partial Contrastive Learning for Visual Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Weakly-supervised Partial Contrastive Learning (WPCL) framework enhances Visual Language Navigation (VLN) agents by improving viewpoint-invariant object recognition using pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for weak supervision without fine-tuning. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance on R2R, REVERIE, and SOON benchmarks, notably reaching 78% Success Rate and 70% SPL on R2R val unseen, while operating on a single 24GB GPU.

## Problem

- VLN agents struggle to robustly identify the same objects from different dynamic viewpoints due to the sensitivity of existing vision models.
- Integrating powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into VLN is computationally expensive if fine-tuned, or suboptimal if used in a zero-shot manner.
- Directly relying on VLM outputs in a fully supervised way can introduce biases from their general pre-training data that may not align with specific VLN tasks.

## Method

- Developed ==Weakly-supervised Partial Contrastive Learning (WPCL)== that leverages pre-trained VLMs (like LLaVA v1.5) to extract object lists from observations as weak supervisory signals.
- Introduced a ==Partial Contrastive Learning mechanism== that splits visual feature vectors into object-centric and flexible segments, applying contrastive loss only to the object segment to encourage viewpoint-invariant learning.
- Integrated this mechanism into a VLN agent architecture (==DUET-based==) guided by a ==sample selection matrix== generated from VLM-extracted objects, without fine-tuning the VLM itself.

## Results

- WPCL consistently achieved state-of-the-art results across R2R, REVERIE, and SOON benchmarks, with an R2R val unseen Success Rate of **78%** and SPL of **70%**, outperforming prior methods.
- The method demonstrates computational efficiency, running on a single **24GB** NVIDIA TITAN RTX GPU, in contrast to VLM fine-tuning approaches that require significantly more powerful and numerous GPUs.
- Performance remains robust across different VLM choices for generating weak supervision (e.g., LLaVA 1.5, LLaVA 1.6, OpenFlamingo), indicating its generalizability and insensitivity to specific VLM architectures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High performance in Visual Language Navigation can be achieved by carefully integrating VLM knowledge through weak supervision, circumventing the high computational cost of VLM fine-tuning.
> - A causal framework informs the design of a partial contrastive learning approach, allowing for specific invariance to shared objects across viewpoints while maintaining flexibility for other visual information.
> - Careful prompt engineering for VLM-based object extraction is crucial for generating effective weak supervisory signals, ensuring relevant object pairs for contrastive learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Language Navigation (VLN) is a fundamental and challenging task within Embodied AI, where an intelligent agent must navigate a real or simulated environment solely based on natural language instructions. This task requires a sophisticated integration of visual perception, language understanding, and decision-making capabilities. The broader research landscape of VLN has seen significant advancements, moving from early rule-based or purely reactive agents to more complex models incorporating attention mechanisms, memory, and various forms of pre-training.

Historically, VLN methods have relied on diverse approaches, including end-to-end training, VLN-specific pre-training, and fine-tuning. Key challenges persist:
1. **Dynamic Viewpoints:** Agents typically operate in 3D environments, experiencing constant viewpoint changes as they move. Traditional visual perception backbones (e.g., ResNet, CLIP) often struggle with robustly identifying the same object from different angles, which is critical for consistent environmental understanding. The paper highlights that current vision models are "highly sensitive to viewpoint changes," leading to performance limitations.
2. **Integration of Large Models:** The advent of large pre-trained models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4, LLaVA, and CLIP, has opened new avenues for VLN. These models possess vast general knowledge. However, their integration into VLN presents a dilemma:
 - **Without Fine-tuning (Zero-shot/Few-shot):** While computationally efficient, these methods often "fall short of traditional methods due to the lack of VLN-specific information." They may lack the nuanced understanding required for precise navigation instructions.
 - **With Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning LLMs/VLMs can significantly improve performance by adapting them to VLN-specific data. However, this comes at a substantial "computational cost," often requiring extensive GPU resources (e.g., multiple A100 or V100 GPUs), making it infeasible for many researchers and applications.
3. **Bias in Pre-trained Models:** Large pre-trained models, while powerful, can carry biases from their training data that may not align with the specific requirements of VLN tasks. Over-reliance on their direct outputs without careful integration can sometimes be detrimental.

This paper addresses these critical limitations by proposing **Weakly-supervised Partial Contrastive Learning (WPCL)**. It positions itself as a solution that leverages the strengths of pre-trained VLMs (their rich semantic knowledge) while mitigating their weaknesses (viewpoint sensitivity, computational cost of fine-tuning, potential biases). By focusing on enhancing visual perception specifically for dynamic viewpoints and integrating VLM knowledge in a computationally efficient, weakly-supervised manner, WPCL aims to strike a balance between performance, efficiency, and robustness, carving out a significant niche in the evolving VLN research landscape. It advances the field by demonstrating that effective VLM integration for VLN does not necessarily require costly fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are clearly articulated as addressing the common challenges faced by existing VLN methods, specifically:

1. **Enhance Object Identification from Dynamic Viewpoints:** The core motivation stems from the observation that an agent's actions in navigation environments primarily change its viewpoint, not the environment itself. Therefore, a truly intelligent agent must be able to recognize the "same objects from different angles." Existing pre-trained vision models (e.g., ResNet, CLIP) are often sensitive to these viewpoint changes, hindering an agent's comprehensive understanding of the environment. The paper introduces a "generalizable causal framework" (Figure 3) to formally illustrate how objects act as confounders between observations under varying viewpoints, motivating the need to extract mutual information between observations that share common objects.
2. **Integrate Pre-trained VLM Knowledge Effectively and Efficiently:** Current methods either achieve suboptimal performance when using zero-shot VLMs due to a lack of VLN domain knowledge or incur high computational costs when fine-tuning VLMs. The objective is to leverage the powerful "zero-shot capability" of large pre-trained VLMs to guide the learning process without the prohibitive expense of fine-tuning them.
3. **Reduce Reliance on VLM Outputs to Mitigate Bias:** While VLMs provide rich knowledge, directly relying on their outputs in a fully supervised manner can introduce biases inherent in their pre-training data. The paper aims to use a "weakly-supervised mechanism" to guide the agent's perception, thus reducing direct reliance on VLM outputs and mitigating potential biases, leading to more robust learning.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a VLN agent that possesses an enhanced, viewpoint-invariant understanding of its environment, is computationally efficient by avoiding VLM fine-tuning, and remains robust by carefully integrating VLM knowledge in a weakly-supervised manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Weakly-supervised Partial Contrastive Learning (WPCL)**, is a novel framework designed to improve VLN agents' perception by integrating pre-trained VLM knowledge efficiently and effectively. It primarily consists of three interconnected components: VLM-guided weak supervision, a Partial Contrastive Learning mechanism, and an overall workflow that integrates these into a VLN agent architecture.

**A. Weakly-supervised by Pre-trained VLM**
This module addresses the challenge of providing supervisory signals for contrastive learning without relying on costly human annotations or computationally expensive VLM fine-tuning.
- **Object Extraction:** Given an observation history $O = \{o_i\}_{i=1}^N$, each image $o_i$ is fed into a pre-trained VLM (specifically, LLaVA v1.5 is used). A carefully crafted prompt, "Give me a list of objects that appear in the given image. Ignore the general environmental objects such as walls, floors or rooftops. Please only answer with a list of objects, and separate each object by a comma," is used to extract a list of discernible objects for each observation. The prompt is designed to filter out ubiquitous environmental features and ensure a consistent output format.
- **Sample Selection Matrix (A):** Based on the extracted object lists, a symmetric binary matrix $A$ of size $N \times N$ is constructed. $A_{i,j} = 1$ if observations $o_i$ and $o_j$ share at least $m$ common objects (where $m$ is a tunable hyper-parameter, set to 1 in experiments), and $A_{i,j} = 0$ otherwise. This matrix serves as the weak supervisory signal, indicating which pairs of observations are "positive samples" because they contain shared objects.

**B. Partial Contrastive Learning**
This mechanism is designed to encourage the agent to recognize common objects across different viewpoints, inverting the data generation process identified by the causal framework.
- **Positive and Negative Sample Definition:** A pair $(o_i, o_j)$ is considered a positive sample if $A_{i,j}=1$. An observation $o_k$ is a negative sample relative to a positive pair $(o_i, o_j)$ if it does not share more than $m$ common objects with *either* $o_i$ or $o_j$ (i.e., $A_{i,k}=0$ AND $A_{j,k}=0$).
- **Partial Feature Invariance:** Unlike traditional contrastive learning that assumes full invariance between positive samples, WPCL introduces a "partial" invariance. The visual feature vector $h_{visual} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ for an observation is split into two segments: $h_{objs} \in \mathbb{R}^{\lambda d}$ and $h_{flex} \in \mathbb{R}^{(1-\lambda)d}$, where $\lambda$ is a tunable parameter (set to 0.2).
 - $h_{objs}$: This segment is designated to encode information about the common objects shared between positive samples.
 - $h_{flex}$: This segment captures other visual information, including viewpoint-specific details, noise, or unshared objects.
- **Partial InfoNCE Loss:** The standard InfoNCE loss (Equation 1, 2) is applied *only* to the $h_{objs}$ segments of the positive and negative samples (Equation 6, 7). This encourages the model to learn a representation for common objects that is invariant to viewpoint changes, while allowing flexibility in the rest of the visual representation.

**C. Overall Workflow**
The WPCL framework integrates these components into a standard VLN agent architecture, building upon the Dual-Scale Graph Transformer (DUET) framework:
1. **Visual Perception Module:**
 - Observation history $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^N$ is first processed by a pre-trained CLIP model to generate initial image features $\{z_i\}_{i=1}^N$. CLIP is chosen for its effectiveness and low computational requirements.
 - These CLIP features are then passed through a trainable "Partial Contrast Module" to produce the refined visual features $h_{visual}$, which are segmented into $h_{obj}$ and $h_{flex}$.
 - Concurrently, the VLM (LLaVA 1.5) generates object lists from $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^N$ to construct the sample selection matrix $A$, which then guides the Partial Contrast Module's training via the $L_{pcon}$ loss.
2. **Instruction Understanding Module:** The natural language instruction is converted into text features $h_{instrct}$ using a text embedding layer.
3. **Action Prediction Module:** The $h_{instrct}$ and $h_{visual}$ features are fed into an adapted DUET architecture. This includes a Global Action Prediction Module (with Topological Mapping) and a Local Action Prediction Module. A Dynamic Fusion module consolidates their predictions to output the final action $\hat{a}$.
4. **Training Objectives:** The model is pre-trained with standard VLN objectives:
 - **Single-Step Action Prediction (SAP):** $L_{SAP}$ (Equation 8), for expert action prediction.
 - **Object Grounding (OG):** $L_{OG}$ (Equation 9), if object annotations are available.
 - **Pseudo Interactive Demonstrator (PID):** $L_{PID}$ (Equation 10), for further policy refinement.
 The overall objective function combines these with the Partial Contrastive Loss: $\min_{\theta} \gamma L_{SAP} + L_{PID} + L_{OG} + \alpha L_{pcon}$ (Equation 11), where $\gamma$ and $\alpha$ are weight parameters.

This comprehensive approach allows WPCL to leverage the strengths of foundation models for perception while specifically addressing the unique challenges of dynamic viewpoints in VLN, all within a computationally efficient framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of WPCL was conducted on three widely recognized VLN benchmarks: R2R, REVERIE, and SOON. The effectiveness, robustness, and generalizability of the proposed method were assessed using standard navigation metrics: Trajectory Length (TL), Navigation Error (NE), Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), and Oracle Success Rate (OSR), across both "val seen" (training environments) and "val unseen" (novel environments) splits.

**A. Overall Performance:**
- **Consistent Outperformance:** WPCL consistently demonstrates superior performance across all three datasets and most evaluation metrics, especially on the more challenging "val unseen" splits, indicating strong generalization capabilities.
- **R2R Benchmark (Table I):** WPCL achieves SR of 82% (val seen) and 78% (val unseen), and SPL of 80% (val seen) and 70% (val unseen). Notably, it significantly outperforms methods within its category (using pre-trained LLMs/VLMs without fine-tuning) such as NavGPT, MapGPT, and DiscussNav. For instance, on R2R val unseen, WPCL achieves an SR of 78% and SPL of 70%, whereas NavGPT yields SR 34% and SPL 29%, and MapGPT SR 39% and SPL 26%. It also surpasses many traditional VLN methods and even some methods that *fine-tune* LLMs/VLMs (e.g., NavCoT, LangNav, NaviLLM, NavGPT-2 on several metrics).
- **REVERIE and SOON Benchmarks (Tables II and III):** Similar trends are observed on REVERIE and SOON, where WPCL sets new state-of-the-art results among the compared baselines across all metrics. For example, on REVERIE val unseen, WPCL achieves SR 54.03% and SPL 37.41%, outperforming the strong DUET baseline (SR 46.98%, SPL 33.73%).
- **Computational Efficiency Advantage:** A critical finding is that WPCL achieves these strong results while maintaining computational efficiency. It runs on a single 24GB NVIDIA TITAN RTX GPU, in stark contrast to other methods that fine-tune LLMs/VLMs, which often require significantly more powerful and numerous GPUs (e.g., 32GB V100, 80GB A100, or multiple V100s).

**B. Further Analysis:**
- **Sensitivity to Prompt:** The study found that careful prompt design for VLM-based object extraction is crucial. Specifically, excluding "general environmental objects" (like walls, floors) prevents the VLM from identifying ubiquitous elements that would undermine the contrastive learning by making all pairs positive. Specifying the output format (e.g., "list of objects, separated by a comma") also ensures consistent parsing. This highlights the importance of prompt engineering when leveraging zero-shot capabilities of VLMs.
- **Sensitivity to VLM Choice:** Experiments were conducted using different VLMs (LLAVA 1.5, LLAVA 1.6, OpenFlamingo, BLIP-2) to generate supervisory signals. The results (Figure 6) show "minimal differences in performance." This indicates that WPCL's effectiveness is not overly reliant on a specific VLM and demonstrates its "robustness" and "generalizability" across different foundation models for object recognition tasks. This suggests that the core mechanism of partial contrastive learning, guided by *any sufficiently capable* VLM, is effective.
- **Accuracy of VLM Object Detection:** While direct quantitative evaluation of VLM detection accuracy was not possible due to a lack of object-level labels in the VLN datasets, qualitative case studies (e.g., Figure 2) and the similar performance across different VLMs suggest "highly accurate, achieving near-perfect quality."

In essence, the main findings confirm that WPCL successfully addresses its core objectives: it significantly improves VLN performance by enhancing environmental understanding through viewpoint-invariant object recognition, effectively integrates VLM knowledge without fine-tuning, and does so with remarkable computational efficiency and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Weakly-supervised VLM-guided Partial Contrastive Learning (WPCL)" framework represents a significant step forward in Visual Language Navigation and has several important implications for the broader field of Embodied AI:

1. **Bridging the Performance-Efficiency Gap:** WPCL offers a compelling solution to the long-standing trade-off between performance and computational cost in VLN, particularly concerning large pre-trained models. By demonstrating that high performance can be achieved by leveraging VLM knowledge in a *weakly-supervised* and *inference-only* manner, WPCL opens avenues for developing advanced embodied agents without the prohibitive resource requirements of fine-tuning multi-billion parameter models. This makes state-of-the-art VLN research more accessible to a wider range of researchers and institutions.
2. **Enhanced Environmental Understanding from Dynamic Viewpoints:** The core contribution of the Partial Contrastive Learning mechanism, guided by a causal framework, is its ability to make agents more robust to viewpoint changes. This is crucial for real-world robotic applications where an agent constantly observes its environment from different angles. By enabling the agent to consistently recognize the same objects regardless of perspective, WPCL fosters a more stable and accurate internal representation of the environment, leading to more reliable navigation.
3. **Robust and Generalizable VLM Integration:** The method's demonstrated insensitivity to the specific VLM used for generating weak supervision (as shown in the VLM choice sensitivity analysis) highlights its robustness. This implies that the WPCL framework can likely benefit from future advances in VLM technology without requiring substantial re-engineering or re-training. Its generalizability makes it a flexible paradigm for future VLN development.
4. **Mitigating Bias and Over-reliance on LLMs/VLMs:** By using VLMs in a weakly-supervised capacity rather than fully supervised, WPCL effectively leverages their general knowledge while minimizing direct reliance on their potentially biased or domain-mismatched outputs. This approach provides a blueprint for integrating powerful foundation models into specific downstream tasks in a more controlled and principled manner.
5. **Potential for Real-World Robotics:** The improvements in navigation success rate, combined with computational efficiency, make WPCL particularly promising for deployment in real-world robotic systems. Robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments require robust perception and efficient decision-making. WPCL's ability to interpret complex instructions and consistently identify landmarks from varying perspectives could lead to more capable and autonomous navigation systems for tasks like assistive robotics, logistics, or exploration.
6. **Novel Learning Paradigm:** The introduction of a "partial contrastive learning framework tailored to the scenario" and a "novel causal perspective to understand VLN tasks" are significant theoretical contributions. These concepts can inspire further research into how to adapt generic learning paradigms (like contrastive learning) to the specific challenges of embodied AI, especially in handling temporal dynamics and viewpoint shifts.

In conclusion, WPCL not only achieves state-of-the-art performance but also pioneers a balanced and efficient approach to integrating large foundation models into Embodied AI. Its significance lies in offering a practical yet powerful methodology that can accelerate progress in developing more intelligent, robust, and deployable agents capable of understanding and acting in complex human environments.
