# Diffusion-Informed Probabilistic Contact Search for Multi-Finger Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Diffusion-Informed Probabilistic Contact Search (DIPS) integrates a diffusion model and discriminator with A* search and trajectory optimization to plan complex, contact-rich multi-finger manipulation. The approach discovers more efficient and robust contact sequences, outperforming training data baselines in simulation and demonstrating improved performance and reliability on a real robotic hand.

## Problem

- Planning complex, contact-rich interactions in multi-finger manipulation is challenging due to high-dimensionality and hybrid dynamics (making/breaking contact).
- Pure classical planning methods face computational intractability when exhaustively searching discrete contact modes with expensive trajectory optimization.
- Pure learning-based methods are sensitive to training data, often only imitate demonstrations, and struggle to enforce hard physical constraints critical for contact tasks.

## Method

- DIPS is a hybrid framework integrating learned generative models with classical graph search and trajectory optimization for multi-finger manipulation.
- It leverages an offline-trained diffusion model as a fast, approximate trajectory predictor and a discriminator to score trajectory realism within an A* search.
- The online phase performs A* search over contact modes, using a particle-based approach to propagate variability, and executes found sequences with a high-fidelity trajectory optimizer and replanning.

## Results

- DIPS significantly outperformed baselines in simulated card manipulation, achieving distances to goal within 0.6 cm, by planning more efficient contact sequences.
- In simulated screwdriver turning, DIPS increased screwdriver rotation by 12% over fixed sequences and 23% over methods without variability propagation, by planning sequences not present in training data.
- The method successfully transferred to hardware, turning a screwdriver 41% further and demonstrating higher robustness (10/10 successful trials) than baselines by strategically planning contact modes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining learned generative models with classical planning enables efficient exploration of discrete contact sequences while maintaining physical constraint satisfaction.
> - The hybrid approach can discover contact mode sequences that are more efficient and robust than those present in the initial training demonstrations.
> - Propagating uncertainty from the learned diffusion model using a particle-filter inspired method and a discriminator is crucial for robustness in search and execution.
