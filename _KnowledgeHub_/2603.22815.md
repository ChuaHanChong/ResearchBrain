# Focus, Don't Prune: Identifying Instruction-Relevant Regions for Information-Rich Image Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> The PinPoint framework enhances Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by identifying and refining instruction-relevant image regions, rather than pruning tokens. This approach improves visual question answering (VQA) accuracy on complex images, achieving an 18.5% higher ANLS than vanilla LLaVA-NeXT on InfoVQA while reducing computational costs by 34.7%.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) incur significant computational overhead when processing information-rich, high-resolution images due to the large volume of visual tokens.
- Existing token pruning methods often suffer from unreliable attention maps, leading to the removal of crucial visual information, semantic fragmentation, and increased hallucinations.
- LVLM reasoning accuracy and factuality degrade as the proportion of instruction-irrelevant visual tokens increases, hindering fine-grained image understanding.

## Method

- The **PinPoint** framework employs a two-stage approach: ==Region Selection== identifies coarse instruction-relevant image areas, and ==Region Refinement== re-encodes these selected regions in isolation to create a compact, dense set of visual tokens.
- A novel ==instruction-region alignment module== uses ==learnable guidance queries== and two ==contrastive losses== (L_inter and L_intra) to bridge visual and textual modalities and select the most relevant regions.
- New dataset annotations were created for VQA benchmarks, providing ==bounding boxes== for both the answer and its supporting evidence ('encompass' regions), to improve training for ==instruction-image alignment==.

## Results

- PinPoint consistently outperformed state-of-the-art token reduction methods, achieving up to an **18.5%** higher ANLS than the vanilla LLaVA-NeXT baseline and **24.5%** higher than SparseVLM on InfoVQA.
- The framework significantly reduced computational costs, cutting FLOPs by **34.7%** for LLaVA-NeXT on InfoVQA compared to the vanilla model, while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- PinPoint demonstrated reduced hallucination rates across POPE, CHAIR, and AMBER benchmarks, and showed strong cross-dataset generalization to expert-level reasoning (MMMU) and scene-text understanding (TextVQA), achieving **136.9%** relative performance on TextVQA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Focusing on relevant image regions rather than pruning tokens mitigates computational overhead while preserving semantic integrity and reducing hallucinations in LVLMs.
> - Re-encoding selected regions effectively strips away irrelevant global context, generating cleaner, more discriminative visual tokens that are crucial for accurate, fine-grained visual reasoning.
> - Training with a combination of inter-modal and intra-image contrastive losses significantly improves the model's ability to identify and align with instruction-relevant visual evidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities across a range of multimodal tasks, including visual question answering. This progress is largely attributed to increased data availability, computational resources, and larger model capacities, enabling them to leverage the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). However, a significant challenge arises when these models process visually complex and information-rich images, such as infographics, document layouts, or dense natural scenes. Such images often require high-resolution processing, which generates a substantial number of visual tokens. This leads to considerable computational overhead, making these approaches resource-intensive and potentially impractical for real-world applications.

To address this computational burden, various token pruning methods have been developed. These methods typically attempt to reduce the number of visual tokens by estimating the importance of each token, often based on attention weights derived from the LLM’s decoding layers, and subsequently discarding less informative tokens. While these approaches aim to improve efficiency, they face several limitations. Attention maps can be unreliable, potentially leading to the removal of semantically crucial tokens and contributing to model hallucinations. Furthermore, token-level pruning risks fragmenting semantic units (e.g., text spanning multiple tokens) and can lead to contextual entanglement where relevant information is blended with irrelevant surrounding details.

In contrast to token-level pruning, some methods attempt to identify and focus on relevant image regions. These approaches can be more precise but often incur high computational costs due to iterative interactions with the LLM or complex multi-stage processing. This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing a method that aims to overcome the shortcomings of existing token pruning and computationally intensive region-focusing techniques. It seeks to efficiently identify and refine instruction-relevant image regions, thereby providing a more compact, information-rich set of visual tokens to the LLM, with the goal of improving both computational efficiency and reasoning accuracy, particularly for fine-grained image understanding tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) when processing visually complex and information-rich images. The authors identify several key motivations for this work:

1. **Addressing Computational Overhead from High-Resolution Processing:** Information-rich images (e.g., infographics, documents) necessitate high-resolution input to capture fine-grained details. This generates a large volume of visual tokens, leading to significant computational costs and making LVLMs impractical for many applications. The work aims to mitigate this by reducing the number of irrelevant visual tokens processed.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Token Pruning Methods:** Current token pruning techniques, which often rely on attention weights, are noted to suffer from:
 - **Imperfect Attention Maps:** Attention weights can be unreliable, potentially leading to the removal of critical visual information and increasing the risk of hallucinations (generating factually incorrect outputs).
 - **Semantic Fragmentation:** Pruning individual tokens can disrupt the collective meaning of visual elements (e.g., text), as crucial contextual information may be distributed across multiple tokens.
 - **Contextual Entanglement:** The global nature of self-attention mechanisms can cause answer-relevant tokens to be intertwined with irrelevant contextual information, reducing their discriminative power.
3. **Improving Reasoning Accuracy and Factuality:** The paper is motivated by the observation that LVLM performance degrades as the proportion of instruction-irrelevant visual tokens increases. This suggests that providing models with a more focused, relevant set of visual inputs can lead to more factual and accurate reasoning. The goal is to move from "pruning" irrelevant tokens to "focusing" on relevant regions.
4. **Developing a Resource-Efficient and Intuitive Approach:** Inspired by human visual processing (e.g., scanning globally for relevant areas before focusing in detail), the authors aim to create a method that efficiently identifies specific image regions relevant to a given instruction. This contrasts with computationally intensive iterative methods for region identification.
5. **Enhancing Ground-Truth Supervision for Complex VQA:** The motivation also extends to improving the training data for VQA on information-rich images. Existing benchmarks often annotate only the final answer's bounding box. However, complex reasoning frequently requires multiple supporting visual elements. The authors aim to provide richer annotations that include both answer and supporting evidence regions, thereby facilitating more robust model training.

In summary, the core objective is to develop a novel, two-stage framework, PinPoint, that can selectively identify and refine instruction-relevant visual regions, delivering a compact, high-quality set of visual tokens to LVLMs. This is intended to simultaneously improve computational efficiency, reduce hallucinations, and enhance the accuracy of fine-grained visual reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named PinPoint, is a two-stage framework designed to identify and refine instruction-relevant image regions. This approach aims to provide enriched visual features to a Large Language Model (LLM) while reducing computational overhead.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

PinPoint consists of two main stages:
1. **Region Selection:** This stage extracts coarse, region-level visual representations from the entire input image and learns to align them with textual instructions to identify the most relevant regions.
2. **Region Refinement:** This stage further processes the selected regions to extract fine-grained visual contexts, re-encoding these areas in isolation to create a dense, informative, and compact set of visual tokens.
The resulting instruction-relevant visual tokens are then fed into an LLM, along with the textual instruction, to generate the final answer.

#### 3.2. Region-Level Feature Extraction

To move beyond token-level comparisons and capture richer contextual relationships, the method first extracts visual token representations from the frozen Vision Transformer (ViT) and projection layer, yielding a visual token map $V \in R^{T \times d}$, where $T$ is the total number of visual tokens and $d$ is the embedding dimension.
These visual tokens are reshaped into a 2D spatial grid. A sliding window of size $W \times H$ with stride $S$ is applied across this grid. For each window, all visual tokens within it are collected and concatenated to form a region-level representation $R_i \in R^{W \times H \times d}$. This process generates a collection of region-level token sets, $R = \{R_i\}_{i=1}^{N_r}$, where $N_r$ is the total number of regions.
Concurrently, the input textual instruction is tokenized using byte pair encoding (BPE) and embedded to form the text embedding $T \in R^{M \times d}$, where $M$ is the number of text tokens.

#### 3.3. Instruction-Region Alignment

A module is introduced to align visual and textual features, as direct comparison is challenging due to the absence of a CLS token in decoder-only LLMs and the subword nature of BPE tokenization.
The core of this alignment is a set of learnable guidance queries $E = \{E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_K\} \in R^{K \times d}$, which serve as a common feature space to bridge the visual and textual modalities. These queries interact with both modalities to extract modality-aware representations.
Region-level visual features $R_i$ and textual features $T$ are first processed by simple MLP layers to obtain $R'_i$ and $T'$.
Scaled dot-product attention is then applied between the learnable queries $E$ and the transformed visual features $R'_i$ to produce visual-aware representation $E_{v_i} \in R^{K \times d}$. A similar mechanism is used with textual features $T'$ to yield text-aware representation $E_t \in R^{K \times d}$. This process effectively grounds the representations in the shared query space.

#### 3.4. Selecting and Refining Instruction-Relevant Regions

- **Region Selection:** The aligned text features $E_t$ are used to identify instruction-relevant image regions. Candidate regions (from $R$) are ranked based on their cosine similarity to $E_t$. The top-ranked regions are adaptively selected until their combined enclosing area exceeds a predefined ratio $r$ of the total image area.
- **Region Refinement:** The visual tokens from the regions selected in the previous step are re-encoded. This re-encoding is performed in isolation, effectively stripping away irrelevant global context that might have contaminated the initial ViT features. This step generates a denser, more informative, and smaller set of visual tokens, which are then passed to the LLM.

#### 3.5. Training with Contrastive Loss

To train the Instruction-Region Alignment module, two contrastive learning objectives are employed:
- **Inter-modal Contrastive Loss ($L_{inter}$):** This loss aims to align textual features $E_t$ with paired positive region features ($E_{v_{pos}}$) from the ground truth, while pushing them away from unpaired (in-batch) negative samples. This loss is symmetric, with components for $v \to t$ and $t \to v$.
- **Intra-image Contrastive Loss ($L_{intra}$):** This loss leverages the fact that only a subset of regions within an image is relevant. It pulls the instruction $E_t$ closer to the positive region $E_{v_{pos}}$ (the region closest to the center of the ground-truth answer region) and pushes it away from irrelevant (negative) regions ($E_{v_{neg}}$) within the same image.
The total training objective is a combination of these two losses: $L_{total} = L_{inter} + \lambda L_{intra}$. During training, only the learnable queries and two MLP layers are optimized, while the LLM, vision encoder, and projector remain frozen.

#### 3.6. PinPoint Dataset

To facilitate training and evaluation, the authors created new annotations for existing VQA benchmarks (InfographicVQA, MultiPageDocVQA, SinglePageDocVQA). Unlike previous datasets that often provide a single bounding box for the answer, this dataset includes multiple bounding boxes: for the answer element itself, for supporting evidence necessary for reasoning, and a unified "encompass" bounding box covering all relevant elements.
A pipeline was developed to generate these high-quality bounding boxes, leveraging an OCR engine, a vision-language model (Qwen2.5-VL), and a large language model (GPT-4o). This pipeline handles different scenarios based on OCR outcomes (exact match, multiple candidates, or OCR failure) to minimize manual annotation effort. For InfographicVQA, an LLM also generated rationale sentences to guide localization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PinPoint utilized LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B as base LVLMs across four VQA datasets: InfographicVQA (InfoVQA), SinglePageDocVQA (SPDocVQA), MultiPageDocVQA (MPDocVQA), and GQA. The performance was assessed using Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity (ANLS) for accuracy, and FLOPs and latency for computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **Comparison to State-of-the-art Approaches (Token Reduction):**
 - PinPoint consistently outperformed all competing token reduction methods (Random sampling, ToMe, FastV, PDrop, SparseVLM) across all evaluated benchmarks for both LLaVA-NeXT and Qwen2-VL.
 - For LLaVA-NeXT on InfoVQA, PinPoint achieved 0.3024 ANLS with 25.48 TFLOPs (65.3% of vanilla cost), which was 18.5% higher accuracy than the vanilla LLaVA-NeXT model and 24.5% higher than SparseVLM (the strongest prior method) with 4.9% fewer FLOPs.
 - Similar trends were observed with Qwen2-VL, where PinPoint showed substantial performance gains compared to methods like FastV and PyramidDrop, which experienced significant accuracy degradation.
- **Comparison of Region-Focusing Methods:**
 - PinPoint exhibited superior performance compared to ViCrop, another region-focusing method. For LLaVA-NeXT, PinPoint achieved 0.3024 ANLS on InfoVQA with 25.48 TFLOPs, while ViCrop scored 0.2547 ANLS with a much higher computational cost of 147.39 TFLOPs (378.1% of vanilla).
 - The "Ours + Global" variant, where the LLM received both refined regions and initial low-resolution global tokens, further improved performance (e.g., 0.3075 ANLS on InfoVQA) while maintaining substantial efficiency relative to the vanilla model. This indicates effective localization and accurate answer generation without prohibitive computational costs.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Comparison with Token Pruning Methods:** Qualitative examples (Figure 6, Figure A1) demonstrated that token pruning methods like FastV and PyramidDrop frequently pruned tokens within answer-relevant regions, leading to hallucinations or semantically inconsistent responses. PinPoint, in contrast, successfully identified and preserved answer-critical regions, yielding more accurate and contextually grounded predictions and more coherent descriptions with fewer hallucinations.

#### 4.3. Efficiency in Training

- PinPoint’s training process is efficient. It does not involve fine-tuning the LLM or backpropagation through it. Instead, it trains only a lightweight MLP and attention operations with learnable guidance queries, operating on visual features from a frozen vision encoder.
- For LLaVA-NeXT-7B, the trainable parameters accounted for only 1.4% of the entire model. Training on the InfoVQA dataset converged in 3 epochs, requiring 1.33 GPU hours on two NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Qwen2-VL-7B training involved 0.86% of total parameters.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Intra-Image Contrastive Loss ($L_{intra}$):** Including the $L_{intra}$ loss component consistently improved both ANLS and Region Accuracy across all models and datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in aligning instruction with relevant image regions (Table 3).
- **Effectiveness of Region Refinement:** The Region Refinement stage significantly improved performance. Models utilizing this stage (re-encoding selected regions) substantially outperformed variants that relied solely on initially encoded (and potentially contaminated) region tokens, indicating its role in capturing fine-grained and discriminative visual cues (Table 4).
- **Impact of Contextual Supporting Regions:** Training with "encompass" annotations, which include both the answer and supporting evidence, consistently yielded higher ANLS and Region Accuracy compared to training with only a single, tightly bounded answer box. This suggests that broader contextual supervision improves instruction-image alignment (Table A6).
- **Sensitivity to Region Coverage Threshold ($r$):** PinPoint maintained strong performance across a range of region coverage thresholds. While larger $r$ values generally increased coverage and sometimes yielded modest ANLS gains, the instruction-region alignment remained effective even when retaining only about 40% of the image area (Table A7).

#### 4.5. Hallucination and Cross-Dataset Performance

- **Hallucination Evaluation:** PinPoint achieved the lowest hallucination rates across POPE (discriminative) and CHAIR/AMBER (generative) benchmarks compared to the vanilla baseline, demonstrating improved reliability in mitigating hallucinations while maintaining computational efficiency (Table A4).
- **Cross-Dataset Generalization:** Despite being trained exclusively on the GQA dataset, PinPoint showed strong generalization to expert-level reasoning tasks (MMMU, MMMU-Pro) and real-world scene-text understanding (TextVQA). Notably, it achieved 136.9% relative performance on TextVQA compared to the vanilla baseline, while reducing computational costs to 61.1%, indicating robust learning of instruction-relevant region identification (Table A5).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Focus, Don’t Prune: Identifying Instruction-Relevant Regions for Information-Rich Image Understanding" introduces PinPoint, a novel two-stage framework that contributes to the advancement of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by addressing critical challenges in processing complex, information-rich images.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to **simultaneously enhance the accuracy and computational efficiency of LVLMs**. By moving away from token pruning and towards a focused region selection and refinement approach, PinPoint demonstrates superior performance in visual question answering tasks compared to existing methods, including both token reduction techniques and other region-focusing strategies. The consistent improvement in ANLS scores across diverse and challenging benchmarks like InfoVQA, SPDocVQA, MPDocVQA, and GQA, often surpassing vanilla LVLM baselines, indicates a more effective mechanism for grounding language models in visual evidence.

The **significant reduction in computational overhead** (FLOPs and latency) is a key impact. By providing LLMs with a compact, yet information-rich, set of visual tokens, PinPoint makes high-resolution processing of visually complex images more practical. This efficiency is achieved through a lightweight, trainable module that does not require fine-tuning the entire LLM, making the approach resource-friendly both during inference and training. This efficiency can pave the way for broader deployment of LVLMs in applications where computational resources are constrained or real-time performance is critical.

Furthermore, the research addresses the issue of **hallucinations in LVLMs**. Qualitative and quantitative analyses indicate that PinPoint reduces the generation of factually incorrect or semantically inconsistent responses. This improved factuality stems from providing the LLM with relevant and refined visual information, thereby minimizing reliance on potentially unreliable attention patterns or sparse visual cues. This has a significant impact on the trustworthiness and reliability of LVLM outputs, which is crucial for sensitive applications.

The introduction of **new dataset annotations** that include both answer and supporting evidence regions (the "encompass" annotations) is another valuable contribution. This richer form of supervision is shown to improve instruction-region alignment, helping models learn a more comprehensive understanding of complex reasoning required for VQA. This dataset and the underlying pipeline offer a resource for future research aimed at developing more robust and context-aware LVLMs, particularly for document intelligence and infographic understanding.

The **generality and robustness** of PinPoint are demonstrated by its strong performance in cross-dataset evaluations, including expert-level reasoning (MMMU) and scene-text understanding (TextVQA), even when trained on a single natural image VQA dataset (GQA). This indicates that the method learns a transferable skill of identifying instruction-relevant regions rather than overfitting to specific data distributions.

The potential impact of PinPoint extends to various domains requiring fine-grained visual understanding:
- **Document Intelligence:** Improved processing of complex documents, invoices, and reports with diverse layouts and content.
- **Infographic Analysis:** Enhanced extraction of insights from densely packed visual data presentations.
- **Medical Imaging:** Better interpretation of intricate medical scans where precise localization of features is critical for diagnostic support.
- **Accessibility:** Making complex visual information more accessible by allowing LVLMs to accurately answer questions about it.

In conclusion, PinPoint represents a step forward in making LVLMs more accurate, efficient, and reliable for handling information-rich visual content. Its focus on effective region identification and refinement, coupled with enhanced training data, has the potential to broaden the applicability and impact of multimodal AI systems.
