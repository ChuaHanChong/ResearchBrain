# Focus, Don't Prune: Identifying Instruction-Relevant Regions for Information-Rich Image Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> The PinPoint framework enhances Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by identifying and refining instruction-relevant image regions, rather than pruning tokens. This approach improves visual question answering (VQA) accuracy on complex images, achieving an 18.5% higher ANLS than vanilla LLaVA-NeXT on InfoVQA while reducing computational costs by 34.7%.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) incur significant computational overhead when processing information-rich, high-resolution images due to the large volume of visual tokens.
- Existing token pruning methods often suffer from unreliable attention maps, leading to the removal of crucial visual information, semantic fragmentation, and increased hallucinations.
- LVLM reasoning accuracy and factuality degrade as the proportion of instruction-irrelevant visual tokens increases, hindering fine-grained image understanding.

## Method

- The **PinPoint** framework employs a two-stage approach: ==Region Selection== identifies coarse instruction-relevant image areas, and ==Region Refinement== re-encodes these selected regions in isolation to create a compact, dense set of visual tokens.
- A novel ==instruction-region alignment module== uses ==learnable guidance queries== and two ==contrastive losses== (L_inter and L_intra) to bridge visual and textual modalities and select the most relevant regions.
- New dataset annotations were created for VQA benchmarks, providing ==bounding boxes== for both the answer and its supporting evidence ('encompass' regions), to improve training for ==instruction-image alignment==.

## Results

- PinPoint consistently outperformed state-of-the-art token reduction methods, achieving up to an **18.5%** higher ANLS than the vanilla LLaVA-NeXT baseline and **24.5%** higher than SparseVLM on InfoVQA.
- The framework significantly reduced computational costs, cutting FLOPs by **34.7%** for LLaVA-NeXT on InfoVQA compared to the vanilla model, while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- PinPoint demonstrated reduced hallucination rates across POPE, CHAIR, and AMBER benchmarks, and showed strong cross-dataset generalization to expert-level reasoning (MMMU) and scene-text understanding (TextVQA), achieving **136.9%** relative performance on TextVQA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Focusing on relevant image regions rather than pruning tokens mitigates computational overhead while preserving semantic integrity and reducing hallucinations in LVLMs.
> - Re-encoding selected regions effectively strips away irrelevant global context, generating cleaner, more discriminative visual tokens that are crucial for accurate, fine-grained visual reasoning.
> - Training with a combination of inter-modal and intra-image contrastive losses significantly improves the model's ability to identify and align with instruction-relevant visual evidence.
