# Visual In-Context Prompting

> [!summary] Summary
> DINOv is a unified visual segmentation framework that extends in-context prompting to generic vision tasks using purely visual cues, demonstrating strong open-set generalization and competitive performance across various benchmarks. It unifies referring and generic segmentation within a single architecture, leveraging diverse data effectively.

## Problem

- Existing visual prompting models primarily address referring segmentation, lacking robust capabilities for generic vision tasks like open-set object detection or panoptic segmentation.
- Visual prompts, often represented as dense masks, hinder generalization to entirely novel concepts and struggle with identifying multiple instances of the same semantic concept.
- Bridging the gap between LLM-style in-context learning and pixel-level vision tasks, as current vision models have limited adaptability to new tasks via visual examples alone.

## Method

- DINOv introduces a unified encoder-decoder architecture, building on MaskDINO principles, with a specialized ==PromptEncoder== that reuses the internal vision encoder to process diverse visual prompts (masks, boxes, points).
- It formulates generic segmentation by aggregating in-context examples across a batch to form ==category representatives== and referring segmentation via a =="self-referring" strategy==.
- The model employs ==dual decoder queries== (generic queries for object proposals and interactive point queries for specific instance segmentation) and is jointly trained on semantically labeled (COCO) and pixel-annotated (SA-1B) datasets.

## Results

- DINOv-L achieves **57.7 PQ** on COCO 2017 panoptic segmentation, nearly matching the text-prompted MaskDINO-L baseline and significantly surpassing other visual-prompted models like SegGPT-L (**43.4 PQ**).
- For out-of-domain open-set segmentation, DINOv demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization on ADE20K with **23.2 PQ**, outperforming or matching previous text-prompted open-set models.
- In zero-shot video object segmentation, DINOv-T achieves **73.3 J&F** on DAVIS2017, outperforming SEEM-T (**60.4 J&F**) and other non-video-trained models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual in-context prompting can be successfully extended to generic vision tasks, enabling strong open-set generalization without relying on explicit text labels or large-scale vision-language models for semantic knowledge.
> - A dedicated prompt encoder that reuses the vision encoder and focuses on fine-grained appearance features is critical for effective visual in-context learning, outperforming external pre-trained vision-language encoders.
> - Joint training on both semantically labeled and pixel-level annotated datasets, combined with a dual-query decoder, yields synergistic improvements for both generic and referring segmentation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of DINOv is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing areas in artificial intelligence, primarily drawing inspiration from Large Language Models (LLMs) and their burgeoning "in-context learning" capabilities. In recent years, LLMs like GPT have demonstrated remarkable zero-shot performance and adaptability by learning from examples provided directly within the prompt, without requiring explicit retraining or fine-tuning. This paradigm shift, however, has been less comprehensively explored and adapted for the vision domain, particularly for complex, pixel-level tasks.

The broader computer vision landscape has witnessed a significant trend towards "vision foundation models"—large, pre-trained models capable of handling diverse tasks. Many of these models, such as GLIP, Grounding DINO, OpenSeed, and X-Decoder, have successfully integrated language understanding, allowing users to interact with vision models using text prompts for open-set detection and segmentation. These "text-prompted" models have shown impressive generalizability to novel categories.

Concurrently, a distinct line of research has emerged focusing on "visual prompting." Instead of text, these methods use visual cues—like points, boxes, scribbles, or reference masks—to guide the model. Pioneering works in this area include Segment Anything Model (SAM), which provides a promptable model for interactive segmentation, and SEEM/Semantic-SAM, which expand on interactive, semantic-aware segmentation. Other generalist models like Painter and SegGPT have framed segmentation as an "in-context coloring problem," relying on visual examples to guide tasks.

However, existing visual prompting methods have notable limitations that DINOv aims to address. Most prominently:
1. **Scope of Tasks:** They primarily focus on "referring segmentation," where the goal is to segment a single, specific object identified by the visual prompt. They often struggle with "generic vision tasks," such as open-set object detection or panoptic segmentation, which require identifying *all instances* of a particular concept or generalizing to entirely *novel concepts* in an image.
2. **Generalization to Novel Concepts:** Visual prompts, especially when represented as colorful masks directly in image pixels, can struggle to generalize effectively to novel concepts not seen during training.
3. **Handling Multiple Objects:** Many existing visual prompting models have limited ability to identify and segment multiple objects belonging to the same semantic concept, a common requirement in generic segmentation tasks.
4. **Integration of In-Context Examples:** While some models use visual examples, DINOv aims to provide a more versatile "in-context prompting" mechanism that can take an arbitrary number of reference segments as context to guide predictions on a target image.

DINOv positions itself as a pioneering effort to bridge these gaps. It is presented as the *first* framework to extend visual in-context prompting effectively to *generic vision tasks* like open-set generic segmentation and detection. By unifying both referring and generic segmentation within a single architecture that relies solely on visual in-context prompts, DINOv carves a new path towards truly general-purpose visual foundation models, directly competing with and in some cases outperforming text-prompted models on open-set benchmarks, but with the added flexibility of visual input. This work represents a significant step towards creating more intuitive and powerful visual AI systems that can learn and adapt from visual examples in a manner analogous to how humans understand and interpret visual cues.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the "Visual In-Context Prompting" paper is to introduce **DINOv**, a universal framework that extends the powerful concept of "in-context prompting" from large language models (LLMs) to the domain of computer vision. Specifically, the authors aim to develop a single, unified model capable of addressing **both referring segmentation and generic vision tasks** using purely visual in-context prompts.

The key motivations driving this research are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the LLM-Vision Gap:** Inspired by the extraordinary zero-shot and few-shot capabilities of LLMs achieved through in-context learning, the authors seek to replicate and extend this paradigm to vision. They observe that while LLMs excel at adapting to new tasks via examples, similar progress in vision, particularly for pixel-level outputs, has been limited.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Visual Prompting:** Current visual prompting methods (e.g., SAM, SegGPT) primarily focus on "referring segmentation"—segmenting a single, specific object indicated by a user prompt. They often fall short in two critical aspects:
- **Generic Vision Tasks:** They struggle to handle "generic vision tasks" like open-set object detection and segmentation, which require identifying *all* instances of a given concept or generalizing to *novel concepts*.
- **Novel Concept Generalization:** Visual prompts, when represented as dense, colorful masks, inherently limit the model's ability to generalize to completely new concepts not encountered during training.
3. **Overcoming Textual Prompting Constraints:** While textual prompting in vision models (e.g., GLIP, Grounding DINO) offers flexibility for generic tasks, it cannot directly consume segmentation masks as input for in-context examples. This limits its applicability in scenarios where visual examples are the most natural form of instruction.
4. **Unifying Segmentation Paradigms:** The authors aim to simplify model design by creating a single, unified framework that can seamlessly consume both semantically labeled data (for generic tasks) and unlabelled pixel-level annotation data (for referring tasks), leading to better overall performance and efficiency.
5. **Enhancing User Interaction and Flexibility:** The goal is to develop a versatile prompt encoder that can handle a wide variety of visual prompt formats (strokes, boxes, points, masks) and an arbitrary number of reference image segments as context, making the model highly adaptable to diverse user inputs and scenarios.

In summary, DINOv's primary objectives are to be the first to enable visual in-context prompting for generic vision tasks, achieve state-of-the-art or competitive performance on both referring and generic open-set segmentation and detection using only visual prompts, and to provide a unified, flexible, and powerful framework for a broad spectrum of visual understanding challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DINOv builds upon a robust encoder-decoder architecture, specifically leveraging the design principles of MaskDINO, a unified framework for object detection and segmentation. The model's innovative core lies in its ability to process diverse visual prompts and leverage them as "in-context examples" for both generic and referring segmentation tasks.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Overall Architecture:**
- **Vision Encoder (Enc):** Employs powerful backbone networks like Swin-T or Swin-L to extract multi-scale image features from the input images (I and I_t, for reference and target images, respectively).
- **Prompt Encoder (PromptEncoder):** This is a critical new component. Unlike methods that crop and feed image regions to external pre-trained encoders (like CLIP), DINOv's PromptEncoder reuses the internal vision encoder. It combines the image features (Z) with user-provided visual prompts (P) (e.g., masks, boxes, scribbles, points) to extract robust *reference visual prompt features* (F). It utilizes multiple Mask Cross Attention Layers to capture fine-grained visual details across different resolutions. This design choice explicitly addresses the challenges of domain shift and semantic-vs-appearance feature misalignment encountered when using external vision-language encoders. These reference features are then sampled to form *query visual prompt features* (Q_p).
- **Decoder (Decoder):** A shared, general-purpose decoder processes both *segmentation queries* (Q_s) and the *query visual prompt features* (Q_p). It performs cross-attention with the target image features (Z) to generate decoded segmentation query features (O_s) and target visual prompt features (O_p). Mask and box predictions (M, B) are derived from O_s.
- **PromptClassifier:** This module is responsible for matching the decoded segmentation query features (O_s) with the target visual prompt features (O_p). For **generic segmentation**, O_p serves as class embeddings, and the classifier (g) predicts category matching scores (C_g) for each object proposal. For **referring segmentation**, the classifier (h) matches each visual prompt to a specific instance in the target image (C_r).

2. **Unified Formulation for Segmentation Tasks:**
- **Generic Segmentation:** The model identifies all objects of a given semantic concept. During training, it constructs positive and negative visual prompt samples across a large batch of images. Prompts of the same semantic category are grouped, a random number (1 to N) of in-context examples are selected, and these are aggregated to form a single reference visual prompt feature per category. This aggregated feature guides the decoder for detection. During inference, pre-extracted features for all categories from the training set are used as class representatives.
- **Referring Segmentation:** The model segments a particular object specified by a visual prompt. During training, a "self-referring" strategy is used, where the reference image is identical to the target image. A prompt is sampled from an instance, and the model is trained to refer to that same instance. This allows leveraging large-scale, pixel-annotated datasets like SA-1B.

3. **Decoder Query Formulation:**
- **Generic Queries:** For generic segmentation, DINOv uses a set of learnable latent queries, similar to MaskDINO, to propose object segments.
- **Interactive Point Queries:** For referring segmentation, it adopts interactive point queries, following Semantic-SAM. Visual prompts (points or boxes) are converted into anchor box formats, and their positions are encoded into position queries. These are duplicated and combined with content queries of different granularities. During training on SA-1B, a subset of foreground and background points are sampled to manage computational overhead.

4. **Prompt Sampling Strategies:** Detailed strategies are provided for both generic (across-batch aggregation during training, pre-extracted category features during inference) and referring (self-referring during training) segmentation to efficiently generate and manage visual in-context examples.

5. **Training Data:** DINOv is jointly trained on a combination of:
- **Semantically Labeled Data:** COCO 2017 panoptic segmentation dataset (110K images), providing explicit category information.
- **Pixel-Annotated Data:** A 20% subset of SA-1B (2M images), offering a vast collection of high-quality pixel masks without semantic labels, ideal for training referring capabilities.

6. **Extension to Video Object Segmentation (VOS):** The framework naturally extends to VOS. Visual prompts from the first frame are used, and for subsequent frames, a "memory visual prompt" mechanism is introduced. This involves storing and averaging visual features of predicted masks from previous frames (up to a certain memory length, default 8) with the first frame's prompt, leading to more accurate tracking.

This comprehensive approach allows DINOv to learn generalizable visual representations from diverse data and tasks, enabling robust performance across a wide range of segmentation and detection challenges using intuitive visual cues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DINOv demonstrates remarkable capabilities across generic segmentation, referring segmentation, detection, and zero-shot video object segmentation, largely due to its novel visual in-context prompting framework. The key findings and results are as follows:

1. **Generic Segmentation and Detection (Open-Set Capabilities):**
- **In-domain Performance (COCO):** DINOv achieves highly competitive results on the COCO 2017 panoptic segmentation dataset. With purely visual in-context prompts, DINOv-L achieves 57.7 PQ, which is very close to its strong MaskDINO-L baseline (58.3 PQ) that relies on text labels. Crucially, DINOv significantly outperforms other visual-prompting generalist models like SegGPT-L (43.4 PQ) and Painter-L (34.4 PQ) by substantial margins (14.3 PQ and 23.3 PQ, respectively), highlighting the effectiveness of its approach.
- **Out-of-domain Open-Set Segmentation (ADE20K):** DINOv showcases strong zero-shot generalization to novel visual concepts on ADE20K. It achieves 23.2 PQ, which is comparable to or better than previous *text-prompted* open-set models like OpenSeed-T (19.8 PQ) and X-Decoder-L (21.8 PQ), despite DINOv using only COCO semantic data and no semantic knowledge from large pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP. An ablation shows that a visual-prompted FC-CLIP variant performs significantly worse (2.3 PQ), underscoring the non-trivial nature of adapting multimodal models for visual prompting and validating DINOv's dedicated design.
- **Segmentation and Detection in the Wild (SegInW, ODinW):** DINOv generalizes well to diversified and domain-specific datasets. On SegInW (25 instance segmentation datasets), DINOv-L improves the AP-Average by 4.5 points over its OpenSeed baseline (40.6 vs 36.1). For ODinW (35 bounding box datasets), DINOv-L achieves competitive performance (15.7 AP-Average) compared to text-prompted models, despite using fewer semantically labeled data.

2. **Video Object Segmentation (VOS):**
- DINOv demonstrates zero-shot VOS capabilities without being explicitly trained on video data. On DAVIS2017, DINOv-T achieves 73.3 J&F, outperforming SEEM-T (60.4 J&F) and PerSAM-L (60.3 J&F). Similar strong performance is observed on YouTube-VOS 2018.
- For interactive VOS on DAVIS2016-Interactive, DINOv-T achieves 77.0 J&F, significantly outperforming other models not trained on video data. The integrated "memory visual prompt" mechanism is crucial for this performance, as shown in ablation studies.

3. **Ablation Studies Validate Design Choices:**
- **Query Formulation:** Using both generic (learnable) and interactive point queries for their respective tasks (generic vs. referring segmentation) yields the best performance, validating the dual-query design. Removing either query type leads to performance degradation in its corresponding task.
- **Visual Prompt Encoding:** The custom PromptEncoder reusing the vision encoder is highly effective. Using a pre-trained CLIP vision encoder (by cropping prompted regions) to encode visual prompts resulted in significantly inferior generalization on open-set datasets like ADE20K (e.g., PQ dropped from 19.4 to 12.6). This confirms that a dedicated prompt encoder is essential to capture fine-grained appearance features vital for visual prompting, unlike the more semantic features of CLIP.
- **Unifying Tasks and Data:** Joint training on both semantically labeled COCO data and pixel-annotated SA-1B data significantly improves performance on *both* generic segmentation (COCO PQ) and referring segmentation (DAVIS17 J&F), demonstrating the synergistic benefits of a unified training strategy.
- **Training Batch Size for Generic Segmentation:** Larger batch sizes during training consistently improve generic segmentation performance, as they enable the model to sample a wider variety of positive and negative visual in-context examples across different images.
- **Inference In-Context Examples:** Increasing the number of in-context examples during inference generally improves results, but with diminishing returns observed beyond eight examples.

In summary, DINOv successfully extends visual in-context prompting to a broader range of vision tasks, achieving strong and competitive results, particularly in open-set scenarios, while simplifying the underlying architecture and leveraging diverse data effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DINOv represents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision, particularly in its novel approach to visual prompting and its broad applicability. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Visual In-Context Learning for Generic Tasks:** DINOv is the first framework to successfully extend visual in-context prompting beyond referring segmentation to encompass generic vision tasks like open-set object detection and panoptic segmentation. This is a crucial step towards creating vision models that can adapt to new visual concepts and tasks using purely visual examples, mirroring the intuitive way humans learn and understand. It demonstrates that visual cues alone can enable powerful generalization without relying on explicit text labels or large-scale vision-language pre-training for semantic knowledge.

2. **Unified and Simplified Architecture:** By providing a unified framework for both referring and generic segmentation within a single model, DINOv simplifies model design and training. This unification allows for efficient leveraging of diverse datasets—semantically labeled data (like COCO) and pixel-level annotated data (like SA-1B)—which are traditionally used for different tasks. The ablation studies confirm that this joint training and unified query formulation lead to improved performance on both individual tasks, highlighting the synergistic benefits.

3. **Enhanced Generalization to Novel Concepts (Open-Set Capabilities):** A major impact of DINOv is its impressive ability to generalize to novel visual concepts in open-set scenarios using only visual prompts. Its competitive performance on ADE20K, SegInW, and ODinW benchmarks, often matching or exceeding text-prompted models, signifies a breakthrough. This capability is critical for real-world applications where new objects or categories constantly emerge, and gathering extensive textual descriptions might be impractical or insufficient.

4. **Flexibility and Versatility:** DINOv's prompt encoder is designed to be highly versatile, accommodating a wide range of visual prompt formats (masks, boxes, scribbles, points) and an arbitrary number of in-context examples. This flexibility makes the model user-friendly and adaptable to various interactive scenarios, providing intuitive control over the segmentation process. The extension to zero-shot video object segmentation further demonstrates its broad utility.

5. **Inspiration for Future Research in Foundational Models:** By demonstrating the effectiveness of visual in-context prompting for generic tasks, DINOv opens new avenues for research into vision foundation models. It encourages the community to explore architectures and training strategies that can better leverage visual analogies and examples, potentially reducing the reliance on massive, manually curated semantic labels for diverse visual understanding tasks. This paradigm shift could accelerate the development of more human-centric and adaptable AI systems.

6. **Practical Applications:** The immediate practical impacts are significant:
- **Improved Interactive Tools:** Enhancing interactive annotation and editing tools for images and videos, allowing users to quickly segment complex scenes with simple visual cues.
- **Efficient Data Annotation:** Facilitating faster and more accurate annotation of new datasets, especially for rare or domain-specific categories where text descriptions might be ambiguous or scarce.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Providing more robust object perception in dynamic environments where novel objects might appear, and a robot needs to quickly adapt to visual instructions.
- **Creative Content Generation:** Enabling more precise control over object manipulation in image and video editing applications.

In conclusion, DINOv represents a pivotal step towards building more general-purpose and intuitively usable visual AI systems. Its success in extending in-context learning to the visual domain with impressive generalization capabilities positions it as a foundational work that could profoundly influence the next generation of vision models and their applications.
