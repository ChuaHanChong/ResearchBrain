# Progressive Multimodal Reasoning via Active Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces AR-MCTS, a framework that enhances multi-step multimodal reasoning in MLLMs by integrating active, dynamic retrieval into the Monte Carlo Tree Search algorithm. This approach automatically generates high-quality, step-wise reasoning annotations, leading to consistent performance improvements across various MLLMs, including a 5.3% gain on MATHVISTA for Qwen2-VL-7B and improved sampling diversity.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex, multi-step reasoning tasks due to insufficient internal knowledge and compounding errors across sequential steps.
- Existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods often retrieve all knowledge upfront, failing to provide dynamic, step-wise insights needed at granular points in a multi-step reasoning process.
- Training robust Process Reward Models (PRMs) for fine-grained, step-wise verification is hindered by the high cost and labor intensity of manually annotating reasoning paths.

## Method

- ==AR-MCTS== integrates active, dynamic multimodal retrieval into the ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== algorithm's 'expansion' phase, augmenting MLLM reasoning with relevant external knowledge at each step.
- It employs a ==unified multimodal retrieval module== to construct a high-quality corpus and perform hybrid-modal retrieval, followed by knowledge concept filtering to ensure fine-grained relevance.
- A curriculum-based ==Process Reward Model (PRM)== is aligned using automatically generated step-wise reasoning annotations, acquired through the AR-MCTS simulations, to provide robust, fine-grained correctness assessment.

## Results

- AR-MCTS consistently improved multimodal reasoning performance across various MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, LLaVA, Qwen2-VL-7B) on mathematical (MATHVISTA, WE-MATH) and general reasoning (GAOKAO-MM) benchmarks.
- The framework notably enhanced the reasoning capabilities of smaller, open-source MLLMs, with Qwen2-VL-7B showing a **5.3%** improvement on MATHVISTA (ALL) and **8.3%** on WE-MATH (AVG) compared to zero-shot performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both active retrieval and the Process Reward Model are critical components, with their removal causing the most significant performance degradation, highlighting their importance in step-wise verification and knowledge augmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically retrieving fine-grained external knowledge at each reasoning step significantly enhances the MCTS expansion phase for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), improving path diversity and accuracy.
> - Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) can be effectively adapted to automatically generate high-quality, step-wise multimodal reasoning annotations, circumventing the need for costly human labeling to train Process Reward Models (PRMs).
> - A Process Reward Model, trained with these automatically generated annotations, provides robust step-level verification, making MLLM reasoning more reliable and interpretable, particularly for complex multi-step tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): **multi-step reasoning, especially in multimodal contexts.** While LLMs and MLLMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in various tasks, their performance often falters when problems require sequential logical steps, deep factual knowledge, or cross-modal understanding.

**Existing Landscape and Challenges:**
- **Reasoning Techniques for LLMs:** Early advancements like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thought (ToT), and Program-of-Thought (PoT) have improved LLMs' ability to decompose problems and generate more coherent responses. Reflection mechanisms and self-consistency have further enhanced reasoning by allowing models to explore multiple paths and self-correct.
- **Verification Mechanisms:** To improve trustworthiness, reward models have emerged. Outcome Reward Models (ORMs) provide sparse, result-oriented feedback, while Process Reward Models (PRMs) offer finer-grained, step-wise rewards. However, training PRMs typically requires extensive, costly human annotation of reasoning paths, limiting scalability.
- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for LLMs:** Recently, MCTS, traditionally used in game AI, has been integrated into LLMs to enable autonomous sampling and evaluation of reasoning paths, generating step-level annotations without human intervention. This addresses the annotation bottleneck for text-based reasoning.
- **Multimodal Reasoning Challenges:** MLLMs face unique difficulties. Their internal knowledge for multimodal reasoning can be insufficient, leading to "misalignment" between different modalities. Errors can compound across multi-step processes. While Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has shown promise in bridging knowledge gaps for MLLMs, existing RAG approaches often retrieve all relevant knowledge at once, failing to dynamically provide specific insights needed at each granular reasoning step. Furthermore, adapting MCTS for MLLMs remains largely unexplored due to these distinct multimodal characteristics. Current MCTS methods for LLMs often rely on basic sampling strategies like beam search during the "expansion" phase, which is less effective for MLLMs due to their shallower internal knowledge.

**AR-MCTS's Position:**
AR-MCTS directly tackles these limitations by **extending MCTS to multimodal reasoning and enhancing its "expansion" phase with active, dynamic retrieval.** It moves beyond static RAG and simple beam search, recognizing that different reasoning steps require different knowledge. By automatically generating high-quality, step-wise annotations for multimodal reasoning, it aims to build a robust PRM, significantly reducing the reliance on manual efforts. This innovation bridges a crucial gap between advanced reasoning algorithms (MCTS), external knowledge integration (RAG), and the complexities of multimodal data, pushing the boundaries of reliable and scalable MLLM reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the AR-MCTS framework are rooted in addressing the fundamental challenges of multi-step multimodal reasoning for Large Multimodal Language Models (MLLMs).

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Enhance Multi-step Multimodal Reasoning:** The primary objective is to significantly improve the ability of MLLMs to perform complex reasoning tasks that require multiple sequential steps and integration of information from different modalities (e.g., text and image).
2. **Enable Automated Process-Level Reasoning Verification:** To move beyond sparse, outcome-based feedback, the framework aims to automatically acquire fine-grained, step-wise reasoning annotations. This is crucial for training a robust Process Reward Model (PRM) that can assess the correctness of each intermediate step, without requiring extensive human manual labeling.
3. **Improve Candidate Solution Sampling in MCTS:** For MCTS to be effective in MLLMs, it needs to generate diverse and reliable candidate reasoning paths. The objective is to enhance the MCTS's "expansion" phase by providing richer, dynamically relevant information beyond the MLLM's internal knowledge.
4. **Develop a Universal and Plug-and-Play Framework:** The goal is to create a framework that can be easily integrated with various existing MLLM backbones (both proprietary and open-source) to enhance their reasoning capabilities, without requiring retraining of the MLLM itself.

#### 2.2. Motivation

- **Limitations of Current MLLMs:** MLLMs often possess insufficient internal knowledge for complex, multi-step multimodal tasks. Relying solely on internal knowledge or basic sampling strategies like beam search can lead to compounding errors and a lack of diversity in reasoning paths.
- **Inefficiency of Existing Verification Methods:** While PRMs are effective for fine-grained verification, the prohibitive cost and human resource demands for manual annotation of reasoning paths limit their scalability and widespread applicability. Automated annotation is essential.
- **Static Nature of Traditional RAG:** Conventional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models often retrieve all relevant information upfront. However, complex reasoning problems demand different pieces of knowledge at different steps. There is a clear need for a dynamic, step-wise retrieval mechanism.
- **Unexplored Potential of MCTS for MLLMs:** Despite MCTS's success in text-based LLMs for autonomous path exploration and annotation, its adaptation to the unique complexities of multimodal scenarios, particularly enhancing its expansion process, remained largely unaddressed.
- **Need for Reliable and Diverse Reasoning:** To build trust in AI systems, especially in high-stakes reasoning applications, models must not only arrive at correct answers but also demonstrate reliable and interpretable reasoning processes. This requires both accurate path selection and diverse exploration of the reasoning space.

By addressing these motivations, AR-MCTS aims to unlock the full potential of MLLMs in complex reasoning tasks, making their reasoning processes more robust, verifiable, and efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AR-MCTS (Active Retrieval-Monte Carlo Tree Search) is a universal framework designed to progressively improve MLLM reasoning by integrating active retrieval into the MCTS algorithm and aligning a process reward model (PRM). The methodology consists of two main components: a unified multimodal retrieval module and a progressive multimodal reasoning annotation mechanism with curriculum-based PRM alignment.

#### 3.1. Unified Multimodal Retrieval Module

This module is responsible for providing relevant external knowledge to the MLLM at each reasoning step.
- **Hybrid-Modal Retrieval Corpus Construction:** A high-quality corpus is systematically built, combining:
 - **Mathematics-Specific Reasoning Knowledge:** Includes text-only datasets like GSM8K and MATH, and multimodal datasets like MATHVISTA, MathVerse, MathVision, and WE-MATH. These are pre-processed to combine questions, solutions, and answers into a unified text format, along with image paths. Overlapping data with test benchmarks is filtered out.
 - **General Reasoning Knowledge:** Leverages web-based sources like Wikipedia (English and Chinese versions) and the COIG question bank, which undergo cleaning and chunking.
- **Unified Retrieval Processes:** Given a multimodal query (text-image pair), two parallel retrieval processes are employed:
 - **Text Retrieval:** A dense retriever (Contriever) is used to find top-K relevant documents from the text-only corpus by computing dot-product similarity between query and document embeddings.
 - **Cross-modal Retrieval:** CLIP (a contrastive vision-language model) is used to encode both image and text components of the query and the hybrid-modal corpus samples. A mixed vector representation is derived for image-text pairs. FAISS is employed for efficient indexing and retrieval of top-K cross-modal documents.
- **Knowledge Concept Filtering:** To ensure fine-grained relevance, retrieved samples are further filtered based on their alignment with the query's knowledge concept label (e.g., "Angles and Length" for math problems). Samples must meet both original retrieval similarity and knowledge concept consistency thresholds to be considered key insights. An open-world Tagger (InsTag) is used for offline concept annotation, and captions are generated for images to facilitate consistent concept labeling across modalities.

#### 3.2. Progressive Multimodal Reasoning Annotation (AR-MCTS Algorithm)

This component leverages MCTS with an active retrieval strategy to automatically generate step-wise reasoning annotations and learn a PRM.
- **MCTS Core Operations:** The standard MCTS algorithm (Selection, Expansion, Simulation, Back-Propagation) is adapted:
 - **Selection:** MCTS traverses the tree from the root, selecting child nodes (representing intermediate reasoning steps) based on the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) formula, which balances exploration and exploitation. Each node represents a state, with the multimodal query being updated to include previous reasoning steps.
 - **Expansion with Active Retrieval Strategy:** This is the key innovation. Instead of relying solely on the MLLM's internal knowledge or traditional beam search to sample the next reasoning steps, AR-MCTS dynamically retrieves new, relevant insights (r_i) from the pre-filtered D_ins corpus for each potential reasoning path. These retrieved insights augment the MLLM's input, guiding the generation of diverse and reliable candidate actions (y_j) for the current step. This process expands the search space with external, relevant knowledge.
 - **Simulation:** A one-step rollout is performed for each newly expanded node. The "value" of a reasoning path is defined by its potential to deduce the correct answer. If the final answer derived from a partial path matches the ground truth, the node's value is 1; otherwise, it's 0.
 - **Back-Propagation:** The visit counts and Q-values (quality estimates) for each state-action pair along the explored path are updated from the terminal node back to the root, reflecting the outcomes of the simulations.

#### 3.3. Curriculum Process Reward Modeling

After acquiring step-wise reasoning annotations via AR-MCTS, a two-phase curriculum learning approach is used to align the PRM:
- **Step-wise DPO Pre-alignment:** This phase trains the PRM to distinguish between correct and incorrect reasoning steps. The MCTS process naturally generates positive (high value) and negative (low value) step-level reasoning paths. These pairs are used to train the PRM using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), maximizing the likelihood of preferred steps over dispreferred ones. Pre-alignment is done using existing text-only PRM datasets (AlphaMath, Math-Shepherd, PRM800K) to provide initial reasoning discernment.
- **Point-wise Fine-tuning:** In the second stage, the pre-aligned PRM is further fine-tuned with a step-level cross-entropy objective. This allows the PRM to assign precise scores to each step, generalizing its correctness assessment capabilities. The ground truth labels for states are (0, 1), and the PRM's sigmoid score is aligned with these.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, the fine-tuned PRM guides the AR-MCTS. The PRM scores are used as the value function in the MCTS evaluation phase. In each round, the top-scoring node among K expanded reasoning paths is selected, and lower-quality paths are discarded. An early stopping criterion (e.g., 4 rounds) is employed to reduce computational complexity.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that AR-MCTS not only dynamically augments MLLMs with relevant knowledge but also provides a robust, automated mechanism for validating and refining multi-step multimodal reasoning processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results across multiple complex multimodal reasoning benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and advantages of the AR-MCTS framework.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **Consistent Enhancement:** AR-MCTS significantly improves multimodal reasoning performance across various MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4V, LLaVA-OneVision-72B, InternVL2-8B, Qwen2-VL-7B, LLaMA3-LlaVA-NeXT-8B) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATHVISTA and WE-MATH) and a general reasoning benchmark (GAOKAO-MM).
- **Superior to Baselines:** AR-MCTS consistently outperforms traditional reasoning verification strategies like Self-Consistency and Self-Correction, as well as Outcome Reward Models (ORM).
 - **Self-Correction Instability:** Self-Correction strategies often struggle, sometimes leading to performance declines, especially for weaker open-source MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2VL-7B dropped over 8% on MATHVISTA), highlighting the instability of methods relying solely on MLLM's internal self-knowledge for correction in multimodal contexts.
 - **PRM vs. ORM:** Process Reward Models (PRMs) within AR-MCTS show more significant improvements than ORMs, particularly on multi-step reasoning tasks (e.g., WE-MATH S3 metrics). This underscores the benefit of meticulous step-wise verification provided by PRM, leading to stronger alignment in complex reasoning.

#### 4.2. Benefits for Weaker MLLMs

- **Unlocking Potential:** AR-MCTS notably enhances the reasoning capabilities of smaller, open-source MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B, InternVL2-8B). For instance, Qwen2-VL-7B with AR-MCTS showed a 5.3% improvement on MATHVISTA (ALL) and 8.3% on WE-MATH (AVG) compared to its zero-shot performance.
- **Addressing Internal Knowledge Gaps:** This suggests that weaker MLLMs might possess the latent ability to arrive at correct reasoning but struggle to decode it directly using only internal knowledge. AR-MCTS's active retrieval provides the crucial external guidance needed to realize this potential, making it a valuable "plug-and-play" solution for improving less powerful models.

#### 4.3. General Reasoning Domain Verification

- **Cross-Domain Generalization:** AR-MCTS successfully generalizes to the GAOKAO-MM benchmark (a Chinese human-level multimodal reasoning task), demonstrating improvements for both GPT-4o and Qwen2-VL-7B.
- **Mitigating Knowledge Gaps:** Beyond mathematical reasoning, AR-MCTS also shows gains in humanities subjects (e.g., history in GAOKAO-MM), indicating its ability to mitigate MLLM knowledge gaps in diverse domains through its active retrieval mechanism.

#### 4.4. Quantitative Analysis

- **Ablation Study:**
 - Removing any component (PRM, Knowledge Concept Filtering, Active Retrieval) leads to performance degradation, confirming the necessity of each design choice.
 - PRM and Active Retrieval cause the most significant drops (e.g., -3.1% and -2.2% on MATHVISTA for Qwen2-7B), emphasizing their critical role in step-wise verification and knowledge-augmented expansion.
 - Knowledge Concept Filtering provides stable gains, demonstrating its effectiveness in reducing noise and ensuring the relevance of retrieved knowledge.
- **Scaling Analysis on Inference Samplings:**
 - AR-MCTS consistently outperforms ORM and Self-Consistency as the number of candidate solution paths (N) increases. This highlights the scalability and reliability of AR-MCTS's automated process labeling, which provides high-quality feedback at a lower annotation cost.

#### 4.5. Impact on Sampling Space (Accuracy and Diversity)

- **Improved Accuracy of Candidate Solutions:** AR-MCTS significantly increases the "Correctness of questions" (i.e., the percentage of questions for which at least one correct candidate solution is sampled) compared to traditional beam search. This accuracy positively correlates with the number of candidate solutions, confirming AR-MCTS's ability to generate more reliable reasoning paths.
- **Enhanced Sampling Diversity:** Visualizations using semantic embeddings (BGE-M3, PCA, DBSCAN) reveal that AR-MCTS produces a more dispersed distribution of candidate solutions, with more distinct clusters, compared to beam search, which tends to collapse into a smaller, redundant area. This demonstrates that AR-MCTS effectively expands the diversity of sampled solutions, providing a richer problem-solving space for MCTS's simulation process.

In summary, the findings robustly support AR-MCTS as an effective, scalable, and versatile framework for enhancing the multi-step multimodal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, particularly by improving both the accuracy and diversity of the reasoning search space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AR-MCTS framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and their ability to perform complex, multi-step reasoning. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Bridging Key Gaps in MLLM Reasoning:**
 - **Automated Verification:** By enabling the automatic acquisition of high-quality, step-wise reasoning annotations through its MCTS-based mechanism, AR-MCTS bypasses the costly and labor-intensive human annotation traditionally required for training Process Reward Models (PRMs). This significantly democratizes access to robust reasoning verification.
 - **Dynamic Knowledge Integration:** It moves beyond static, all-at-once Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by introducing an "active retrieval" strategy that dynamically fetches relevant, fine-grained knowledge at each step of the reasoning process. This ensures that the MLLM receives the most appropriate insights precisely when needed, overcoming limitations of internal knowledge and external misalignment.
 - **Enhanced MCTS for MLLMs:** It successfully adapts the Monte Carlo Tree Search algorithm for multimodal contexts, addressing its limitations in the "expansion" phase by replacing traditional beam search with knowledge-augmented sampling. This innovation opens new avenues for applying sophisticated search algorithms to MLLMs.

- **Improved Reliability and Interpretability:** By enabling process-level verification, AR-MCTS makes MLLM reasoning more reliable. The ability to identify and correct errors at intermediate steps, rather than just at the final outcome, also contributes to greater interpretability and trustworthiness of AI systems, which is crucial for high-stakes applications.

- **Boosting Performance of Diverse MLLMs:** The framework is shown to be universal and "plug-and-play," demonstrating consistent performance gains across various MLLM architectures, from powerful proprietary models like GPT-4o to smaller, open-source models like Qwen2-VL-7B. Its particular effectiveness in "unlocking the reasoning potential" of weaker MLLMs has significant implications for broader accessibility and development of more capable AI.

- **Expanding the Reasoning Search Space:** The quantitative analyses confirm that AR-MCTS not only improves the accuracy of sampled solutions but also significantly enhances their diversity. This means the model explores a wider range of plausible reasoning paths, increasing its chances of finding the optimal one and making its problem-solving more robust.

- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **Deeper Integration of Retrieval and Reasoning:** The success of active retrieval suggests future work could explore even deeper feedback loops between MLLM generations and retrieval mechanisms, allowing models to dynamically query for missing knowledge based on their current state of reasoning.
 - **Multi-modal PRM Foundations:** While AR-MCTS aligns a PRM on existing MLLM backbones, future research could focus on training PRMs more natively within multimodal foundational models, further enhancing cross-modal interactions for step-wise reasoning.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Although AR-MCTS reduces manual annotation, MCTS itself can be computationally intensive. Further research into optimizing its computational cost, perhaps leveraging advances in efficient inference techniques like vLLM, would enhance scalability.

In conclusion, AR-MCTS provides a robust, verifiable, and efficient solution for complex multimodal reasoning, pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve. Its unique integration of active retrieval with MCTS and process-level reward modeling establishes a powerful paradigm for developing more intelligent and trustworthy AI agents.
