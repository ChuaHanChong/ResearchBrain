# PhysVid: Physics Aware Local Conditioning for Generative Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysVid improves the physical realism of videos generated by Text-to-Video models by introducing a local, physics-aware conditioning scheme. This approach uses Vision Language Models to annotate contiguous video chunks with physics-grounded descriptions, outperforming larger baseline models on physical plausibility benchmarks like VideoPhy and VideoPhy2.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art generative video models frequently produce outputs that violate fundamental physical laws despite high visual fidelity.
- Existing physics-aware methods often apply conditioning globally, limiting their ability to capture fine-grained physical phenomena evolving over shorter temporal segments.
- Global text conditioning can lead to spatiotemporal misalignment or static attention maps, struggling to interpret temporal logic for intricate local interactions.

## Method

- **PhysVid** segments videos into contiguous temporal chunks and uses a ==Vision Language Model (VLM)== to generate physics-grounded descriptions for each chunk during training.
- It integrates ==chunk-aware cross-attention layers== with ==Rotary Positional Embeddings== into the generative video model, enabling cross-modal position awareness for localized textual conditioning.
- During inference, an LLM generates both positive and ==counterfactual (negative) local physics prompts==, which are then used with ==Classifier-Free Guidance== to steer generation towards physical accuracy.

## Results

- PhysVid achieved a Physical Commonsense (PC) score of **0.32** on the VideoPhy benchmark, representing an approximate **33%** relative gain over the larger Wan-14B baseline (PC **0.24**) with a significantly smaller model (**1.7B** vs **14B** parameters).
- On the VideoPhy2 benchmark, PhysVid (PC **0.64**) outperformed the Wan-1.3B (**0.61**) and Wan-14B (**0.59**) baselines by approximately **5%** and **8%** respectively, consistently across various subcategories.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the chunk-aware cross-attention mechanism and the application of counterfactual guidance contribute to the observed improvements in physical commonsense scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Many critical physical phenomena manifest and evolve distinctly over local, temporally contiguous segments of a video, making chunk-level conditioning effective for enforcing physical laws; a chunk-aware cross-attention mechanism can effectively align localized physics descriptions with corresponding video segments, enhancing spatiotemporal consistency in generated content.
> - Vision Language Models can extract detailed, physics-grounded annotations from generic video data, allowing for generalizable physics awareness without requiring pre-existing specialized labels.
> - Negative physics prompts (counterfactual guidance) at inference time further improve physical commonsense by actively steering the model away from physically implausible scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative video models have exhibited substantial advancements in producing high-fidelity and aesthetically realistic videos in recent years. Models such as Sora and Genie exemplify this progress. However, a persistent challenge in this field is the inability of these models to consistently adhere to fundamental physical laws. Despite training on extensive datasets, generated videos frequently depict physically implausible scenarios, such as objects moving unnaturally or interactions violating basic principles like gravity or momentum. This limitation suggests a potential ceiling on learning physical accuracy solely from data without explicit mechanisms to incorporate underlying physics.

Prior research has attempted to address this physical inconsistency through various methods. Some approaches involve conditioning video generation on explicit physical forces or simulators, while others integrate physical constraints into loss functions during training or apply them as guidance mechanisms during inference. Modular approaches have also been proposed, incorporating physical awareness through multi-stage generation or specialized modules. Examples include "Force Prompting," which conditions generation on physical forces; DiffPhy, which uses Large Language Models (LLMs) to infer physical context from text prompts; and PhyT2V, a training-free method using LLMs for iterative prompt refinement. WISA and VideoREPA have focused on injecting decomposed physical principles or transferring physics understanding from video foundation models.

A common limitation among many existing physics-aware video generation methods is their application of physics conditioning at a global, video-wide scale. This often restricts their capacity to capture fine-grained physical phenomena that evolve over shorter temporal segments. Global text conditioning, while effective for overall scene description, can be insufficient for intricate local interactions, leading to issues like static attention maps for action words or spatiotemporal misalignment. This problem has been acknowledged in image generation research, where global cross-attention can be suboptimal for video tasks, as models may struggle with temporal logic, resulting in static or incoherent motion.

PhysVid positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitation of global conditioning. It proposes that physics information often manifests at local temporal levels within a video. The work is inspired by observations that motion dynamics, shape deformations, and optical effects often occur rapidly and are better described and enforced within smaller temporal intervals. This approach aligns with recent advancements in video-based world modeling, which increasingly leverage frame-level or short temporal fragment control to dynamically guide video generation. By focusing on local, physics-aware guidance, PhysVid aims to enhance the physical plausibility of generated videos beyond what global conditioning or static, video-long physics injections can achieve, marking a step towards more robustly physics-grounded video models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the PhysVid research is to improve the physical realism and adherence to natural laws in videos generated by Text-to-Video (T2V) models. This aim stems from a recognition that while current generative video models excel in visual fidelity and aesthetic quality, they frequently produce outputs that violate fundamental physical principles. Such violations restrict their reliability and applicability in real-world scenarios.

The primary motivations underpinning this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Physical Plausibility Gaps in Generative Video Models:** Despite being trained on vast datasets, state-of-the-art generative video models struggle to produce content that consistently obeys physics. This suggests that simply scaling data or model size may not inherently lead to a deep understanding and application of physical laws. The authors contend that an explicit mechanism to incorporate physics, beyond passive learning from data, is necessary to overcome this fundamental limitation.
2. **Limitations of Global and Frame-Level Conditioning:** Previous attempts to inject physics into video generation have often relied on either global text prompts or very short-horizon, frame-level signals.
 - **Global text prompts:** These are typically coarse and can be noisy, failing to convey the fine-grained dynamics and specific physical interactions that occur over shorter time scales within a video. A global prompt describing "a car driving" does not guarantee that the car's wheels will rotate realistically relative to its speed or that it will react appropriately to a wet road surface at specific moments. Models using global cross-attention may apply the same textual guidance across all frames, leading to a struggle in interpreting temporal logic and a failure to generate details specific to local time intervals. This can result in static or incoherent motion due to spatiotemporal misalignment.
 - **Frame-level signals:** While providing fine detail, purely frame-level conditioning might lack the sufficient temporal context necessary to observe and enforce complex physical laws that unfold over short, but not instantaneous, durations (e.g., the trajectory of a thrown object, the flow of a liquid).
3. **Necessity of Local Temporal Context for Physics:** The authors observe that many critical physical phenomena, such as object motion, deformations, and optical effects, manifest and evolve most distinctly over temporally contiguous segments of a video. By conditioning generation at this "chunk" level, the model can focus on locally relevant physical principles, ensuring that each segment adheres more closely to physical laws. This local adherence is hypothesized to contribute to an overall more coherent and realistic video.
4. **Enhancing Reliability for Real-World Applications:** For generative video models to transition from aesthetic demonstrations to practical tools, especially in high-stakes domains like robotics, healthcare simulations, or autonomous systems, their outputs must be physically plausible. A robot learning from a simulated video needs accurate physics to avoid undesirable real-world outcomes. Injecting physics-aware conditioning is seen as a crucial step towards making these models reliable "world simulators."
5. **Extending World Modeling Concepts:** The work is also inspired by progress in video-based world modeling, where dynamic control at frame-level or short temporal fragments is employed. PhysVid extends this concept to physics-conditioned video generation, aiming for enhanced control over physical realism.

In summary, the key objective is to significantly enhance the physical realism of generated videos by introducing a novel local, physics-aware conditioning scheme that complements global text prompts, thereby addressing the limitations of existing methods and paving the way for more reliable generative video technologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PhysVid methodology introduces a physics-aware local conditioning scheme for generative video models, operating over temporally contiguous chunks of frames. This approach integrates local physics-grounded descriptions with global text prompts through a chunk-aware cross-attention mechanism during training, and employs negative physics prompts for guidance during inference.

The overall approach involves several key steps:

#### 3.1. Annotation of Video Chunks (Training Data Preparation)

1. **Video Segmentation:** Each video in the training dataset is first segmented into a series of contiguous temporal chunks of fixed duration (e.g., approximately 0.7 seconds, comprising a fixed number of frames). For the experiments, WISA dataset videos (5 seconds long, 81 frames at 16 FPS) were divided into 7 chunks.
2. **Physics-Grounded Annotation via VLM:** A pre-trained Vision Language Model (VLM), specifically VideoLLama3-7B, is employed to analyze each individual chunk. The VLM is instructed to identify and describe observable physical phenomena within that segment.
3. **Instruction Design for VLM:** To ensure relevance and prevent contradictions, the VLM is provided with both the video chunk and the global Text-to-Video (T2V) prompt for the entire video. The instructions guide the VLM to:
 - Focus solely on visible elements and physical phenomena within the *specific chunk*.
 - Align its annotations with the global prompt without contradicting it.
 - Focus on three key categories of physics information: **dynamics** (motion, forces, energy), **shape** (deformations, elasticity), and **optics** (illumination, reflections, shadows).
 - Generate a structured JSON output, ensuring physical accuracy and verifiability from the video content. Constrained generation techniques are used to enforce this structure.
4. **Prompt Conversion:** The structured output from the VLM is parsed and converted into a concise text prompt, which serves as the local physics-based conditioning for that specific video chunk during training.

#### 3.2. Local Conditioning with Chunk-Aware Cross-Attention (Model Architecture and Training)

1. **Base Model Integration:** The PhysVid approach integrates new chunk-aware cross-attention layers into each transformer block of a pre-trained generative T2V model, specifically a Wan2.1 model with 1.3 billion base parameters.
2. **Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE):** RoPE is applied to both visual query tokens and textual key projections.
 - **Visual Tokens:** Video query tokens are modulated by RoPE parameterized by a 3D spatiotemporal grid (frame, height, width), encoding their position within the video.
 - **Textual Tokens:** RoPE is also applied to text key projections using an identical frequency basis. A custom 2D text grid is defined for the concatenated local text tokens, where the first dimension indexes the video chunk and the second identifies the intra-chunk position. This enables cross-modal position awareness.
3. **Cross-Attention Mechanism:** The design ensures that cross-attention logits are explicitly cross-modal position aware. A video token can attend differently to text information originating from its corresponding chunk versus text from other chunks. This contrasts with conventional global cross-attention, where text keys lack frame-aligned coupling.
4. **Local and Global Pathways:** The local cross-attention modules are added as an additional pathway, complementing a parallel global cross-attention path that preserves long-range conditioning from the global text prompt. This dual-pathway design promotes temporally grounded text-video alignment while remaining compatible with standard T2V architectures.
5. **Training Procedure:** The entire architecture is trained in two stages using a flow matching loss:
 - **Stage 1:** The base Wan2.1 model layers are frozen, and only the newly added chunk-aware cross-attention modules are trained for 1000 steps.
 - **Stage 2:** The base layers are unfrozen, and the entire model is further trained for an additional 2000 steps.

#### 3.3. Inference with Local Counterfactual Guidance

1. **Local Prompt Generation (Inference):** During inference, visual data is unavailable for VLM-based chunk annotation. Therefore, an LLM is instructed to generate a set of temporally coherent, physics-grounded local prompts for an "imagined" video clip, using *only* the global T2V prompt as input. These prompts are allowed to contain information not explicitly mentioned in the global prompt, provided they maintain temporal coherence and do not contradict the global context or preceding local prompts.
2. **Counterfactual Prompt Generation:** In addition to the "positive" local prompts, the LLM also generates corresponding counterfactual prompts for each chunk. These counterfactuals deliberately describe violations of the identified physical phenomena. This process is similar to prior work by Hao et al.
3. **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Both the positive local prompts (C) and their counterfactual counterparts (C'), along with a global negative prompt (c\_n), are used to guide the generation process during inference. The guidance formula applies CFG at both global and chunk levels, effectively steering the model away from physically inaccurate content by reinforcing correct physics and discouraging violations.

This comprehensive methodology, combining local physics annotation, a novel chunk-aware cross-attention mechanism, and counterfactual guidance, aims to instill a deeper understanding and adherence to physical laws within generative video models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhysVid focused on its ability to enhance physical plausibility in generated videos, assessed through quantitative benchmarks and qualitative comparisons.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

The study utilized two widely adopted benchmarks for evaluating physical accuracy: VideoPhy and VideoPhy2. The core metrics reported are Semantic Alignment (SA) and Physical Commonsense (PC), with higher PC scores indicating better physical plausibility.

- **VideoPhy Benchmark:**
 - PhysVid, with a total of 1.7 billion parameters (including the base Wan-1.3B model and 400M additional parameters), demonstrated a substantial improvement in physical commonsense.
 - It achieved a PC score of 0.32, marking an approximate 33% relative gain over the much larger baseline Wan-14B model (14 billion parameters), which scored 0.24. The smaller Wan-1.3B baseline also scored 0.24.
 - This performance gain was observed consistently across all subcategories of VideoPhy: "solid-solid," "solid-fluid," and "fluid-fluid" interactions.
 - Semantic Alignment (SA) for PhysVid was slightly lower (0.43) compared to Wan-14B (0.52), indicating a trade-off where improved physical realism may come with a marginal reduction in semantic adherence to the original prompt, or the physics-specific language being prioritized by the VLM.

- **VideoPhy2 Benchmark:**
 - On VideoPhy2, PhysVid (PC score of 0.64) outperformed the Wan-1.3B (0.61) and Wan-14B (0.59) baselines by approximately 5% and 8% respectively.
 - The improvement was consistent across both "object interaction" and "sports and physical activities" subcategories, as well as on manually labeled "hard" examples.
 - The authors noted that improvements on VideoPhy2 were less pronounced than on VideoPhy, attributing this to differences in score calculation methods (categorical rating in VideoPhy2 vs. continuous scale with hard thresholding in VideoPhy).

- **Comparisons with Existing Approaches:**
 - When compared to a range of existing general and physics-aware generative video methods on the VideoPhy benchmark, PhysVid (1.7B parameters, PC score 0.32) remained competitive. It matched or surpassed many larger models and other physics-aware approaches like PhyT2V (best PC 0.42, but with higher SA), WISA (0.38), VideoREPA-5B (0.40), and Hao et al. (0.40).
 - The results reinforce the observation that physical accuracy does not necessarily scale directly with model size and that evaluation scores can vary across different setups due to the subjective nature of human judgment.

- **Similarity Metrics:**
 - Additional metrics like LPIPS, FVD, SSIM, and PSNR were reported. PhysVid showed a slightly increased FVD score (318.087) compared to the finetuned baseline (302.465), suggesting a minor compromise in content fidelity. However, it still improved over the original Wan 1.3B baseline (417.352). SSIM and PSNR values were comparable to or slightly better than the finetuned baseline.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples presented in the paper (Figure 1 and Appendix 9.1) illustrate visible improvements in the physical fidelity of videos generated by PhysVid compared to the Wan-14B model. For instance, in captions like "A car gliding over a road slick with rainwater," PhysVid generates more realistic water movement and reflections consistent with a slick surface. Other examples, such as "A blender spins, mixing squeezed juice within it," show more plausible fluid dynamics.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments were conducted to isolate the impact of PhysVid's key components:

- **Baseline (Wan-1.3B):** PC scores were 0.2401 on VideoPhy and 0.6144 on VideoPhy2.
- **Finetuning (Wan-1.3B without PhysVid's architecture):** Directly finetuning the base model on the WISA dataset improved PC scores to 0.2866 on VideoPhy and 0.6261 on VideoPhy2, demonstrating some benefit from the dataset.
- **PhysVid without Counterfactual Guidance:** This configuration (PhysVid's architecture but using blank local prompts for negative guidance) yielded PC scores of 0.2924 on VideoPhy and 0.6334 on VideoPhy2, showing that the chunk-aware cross-attention and positive local conditioning contribute significantly.
- **PhysVid (with Counterfactual Guidance):** The full PhysVid method achieved the highest PC scores: 0.3169 on VideoPhy and 0.6411 on VideoPhy2. This demonstrates that applying negative physics conditioning (counterfactual guidance) to local pathways during generation further enhances physical commonsense.

Qualitative ablations (Figure 7 and Appendix 9.1) visually supported these findings, showing that PhysVid, especially with counterfactual prompting, produces more physically coherent scenes compared to the baseline and finetuned models. For example, in a "mixing spoon stirring hot chocolate" prompt, PhysVid showed more realistic liquid movement.

In summary, PhysVid demonstrates that incorporating physics-aware local conditioning, facilitated by VLM-generated chunk-level prompts and novel cross-attention, significantly improves the physical realism of generated videos, even with a smaller model size, and that counterfactual guidance further contributes to this improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhysVid research presents a significant contribution to the field of generative video modeling by directly addressing a critical limitation: the lack of physical plausibility in generated content. Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several key areas:

1. **Advancement in Physics-Grounded Video Generation:** PhysVid provides concrete evidence that incorporating local, physics-aware guidance substantially increases the physical plausibility of generative video outputs. By moving beyond global conditioning to segment-level physics descriptions, the method demonstrates a more granular and effective way to instill physical realism. This marks a meaningful step towards creating truly "physics-grounded" video models that do not merely mimic visual aesthetics but also adhere to the underlying laws governing physical interactions.
2. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness:** Current generative video models, despite their impressive visual fidelity, often produce physically inconsistent results, which limits their reliability. For applications where physical accuracy is paramount, such as robotics (for simulating environments and planning actions), healthcare (for realistic medical procedure simulations), or autonomous systems (for training and testing perception and decision-making in diverse scenarios), physically implausible outputs are unacceptable. PhysVid's approach enhances the trustworthiness of generated videos, making them more suitable for these high-stakes domains.
3. **Generalizability of Physics Extraction:** A notable aspect of PhysVid is its reliance on a Vision Language Model (VLM) to extract physics information directly from training videos, rather than depending on pre-existing, explicit physics annotations in datasets. This makes the approach highly generalizable. It can be applied to generic video datasets that lack specialized physics labels, broadening the scope of data usable for training physics-aware generative models. Moreover, the ability to extract information at a finer temporal granularity than what might be available in global dataset annotations offers flexibility.
4. **Novel Conditioning Mechanism:** The introduction of chunk-aware cross-attention layers with Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) for cross-modal position awareness is a novel architectural contribution. This mechanism effectively aligns localized textual physics prompts with their corresponding video segments, allowing the model to respond to both global context and precise local physical phenomena without redesigning the core attention module. This modular addition is compatible with existing T2V architectures, facilitating its integration into future models.
5. **Effectiveness of Counterfactual Guidance:** The demonstration that negative physics prompts (counterfactual guidance) further improves physical commonsense scores highlights the utility of actively steering generation away from implausible trajectories. This inference-time guidance mechanism offers a powerful tool for refining physical adherence without requiring additional training data for physics violations.
6. **Path Towards World Simulators:** The paper frames generative video models as embarking on a journey toward "genuine world simulators." By enhancing their ability to adhere to fundamental physical laws, PhysVid contributes directly to this ambitious long-term goal. A true world simulator must accurately model physical interactions to predict future states and enable complex reasoning, and PhysVid's focus on local physics is a foundational element for achieving this.

In conclusion, PhysVid addresses a core challenge in generative AI by prioritizing physical realism. Its methodology, which integrates VLM-driven local physics annotation, chunk-aware attention, and counterfactual guidance, offers a robust framework for building more reliable and physically plausible video generation models, with significant implications for both research and real-world applications.
