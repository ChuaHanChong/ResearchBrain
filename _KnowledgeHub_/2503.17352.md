# OpenVLThinker: Complex Vision-Language Reasoning via Iterative SFT-RL Cycles

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UCLA developed OpenVLThinker, an open-source large vision-language model, leveraging an iterative supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning cycle to achieve complex, multi-step reasoning. This model demonstrates competitive performance with proprietary LVLMs and surpasses other open-source models on challenging benchmarks, showing data efficiency with 10x less data.

## Problem

- Open-source Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) typically lack the sophisticated multi-step reasoning capabilities (e.g., planning, reflection, verification) observed in proprietary multimodal models or text-only R1 models.
- Distilling reasoning directly from text-only R1 models into LVLMs through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often leads to performance degradation due to imprecise visual grounding.
- Applying pure reinforcement learning (RL) from scratch to smaller LVLMs (e.g., 7B parameters) for reasoning is inefficient due to the prohibitively large search space for reflective behaviors to emerge.

## Method

- An ==iterative self-improvement framework== alternates between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- SFT datasets are curated by using the base LVLM for captioning, feeding captions to a text-based R1 model for CoT generation, and then self-generating higher-quality CoTs in subsequent iterations.
- A ==two-stage, source-based curriculum RL strategy== trains the model first on medium-difficulty data and then on hard-difficulty data to optimize learning efficiency and maximize performance.

## Results

- OpenVLThinker-7B achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source LVLMs across six challenging benchmarks, with average accuracy of **47.9%** and specific improvements such as **3.8%** on MathVista and **2.4%** on EMMA.
- The model performs competitively with larger proprietary models like GPT-4o (**47.3%** average) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (**48.4%** average) on Math and Perception tasks.
- Empirical analysis validates that while initial SFT might cause a temporary performance drop, the iterative SFT-RL cycle consistently yields significant gains, confirming the efficacy of the proposed self-improvement paradigm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The iterative SFT-RL cycle synergistically fosters complex reasoning, where initial SFT acts as an 'action highlighting' mechanism to surface reasoning behaviors and narrow the RL search space.
> - RL refines these reasoning actions and improves generalization from the SFT-provided foundation, playing a crucial role in enhancing overall accuracy and generating better SFT data for subsequent iterations.
> - Robust complex reasoning can be instilled in LVLMs with significantly less training data (using only 12K examples per iteration) compared to concurrent single-iteration methods that require 10x more data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "OpenVLThinker" addresses a critical gap in the burgeoning field of large language models (LLMs) and large vision-language models (LVLMs): the transfer and emergence of sophisticated multi-step reasoning capabilities in multimodal contexts.

The broader research landscape has witnessed remarkable progress in LLMs, exemplified by proprietary models such as OpenAI’s GPT-4o and Google’s Gemini-2.5 Pro, which demonstrate impressive capabilities in planning, reflection, and verification for text-only tasks. Concurrently, open-source initiatives, notably DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ-32B, have successfully reproduced and advanced these "R1-style" reasoning behaviors using reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards. This has spurred a significant line of research in open-source reproduction and analysis of complex mathematical and logical reasoning in text.

However, the effective adoption of such sophisticated reasoning strategies by open-source LVLMs has remained a significant challenge. While modern LVLMs like LLaVA-NeXT and Qwen2.5-VL exhibit strong visual instruction-following, they typically lack the deeper, reflective reasoning behaviors seen in their text-only counterparts or in proprietary multimodal systems. The paper highlights two main obstacles in this endeavor:
1. **Distillation from Text-only R1 Models:** A common approach to instill reasoning is through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on chain-of-thought (CoT) demonstrations. For LVLMs, this typically involves distilling reasoning paths from text-only R1 models using image captions as proxies. However, this often leads to performance degradation due to "imprecise visual grounding," as the text-only models cannot fully leverage visual information. Proprietary LVLMs also do not expose their internal reasoning paths for direct distillation.
2. **Pure Reinforcement Learning Challenges:** Initiating RL from scratch for reasoning in smaller LVLMs (e.g., 7B models) faces a prohibitively large search space, making it difficult for reflective behaviors to emerge efficiently.

"OpenVLThinker" positions itself directly within this complex landscape by proposing a novel solution to overcome these challenges. It aligns with a wave of very recent, concurrent works (many published within two months of its submission) that are also exploring how to equip LVLMs with R1-like reasoning, either through distillation from text-only models, direct RL, or a focus on visual math reasoning. However, OpenVLThinker distinguishes itself by providing unique insights into the synergistic roles of SFT and RL, proposing an iterative SFT-RL framework that achieves superior results with significantly less data compared to many concurrent single-iteration approaches. This work is a crucial step toward achieving robust, R1-style reasoning in open-source multimodal contexts, mirroring the advancements seen in text-only models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **OpenVLThinker**, an open-source large vision-language model (LVLM) capable of exhibiting sophisticated chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, thereby achieving notable performance gains on challenging visual reasoning tasks. The authors are motivated by the gap between the advanced reasoning capabilities of proprietary multimodal models (e.g., GPT-4o) and text-only R1 models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) versus the relatively shallow reasoning of existing open-source LVLMs.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be broken down as follows:

- **Bridging the Reasoning Gap:** To develop an open-source LVLM that can effectively perform complex, multi-step reasoning, including planning, reflection, and verification, in multimodal contexts, similar to state-of-the-art text-only R1 models and proprietary LVLMs.
- **Addressing Distillation Limitations:** To overcome the performance degradation observed when naively distilling text-based R1 reasoning into LVLMs, primarily attributed to a lack of precise visual grounding.
- **Mitigating RL Search Space Issues:** To find a way for smaller LVLMs (e.g., 7B parameters) to efficiently learn reflective behaviors via reinforcement learning, circumventing the challenges of a large search space when starting RL from scratch.
- **Exploring SFT-RL Synergy:** To investigate the hypothesis that iteratively alternating between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) can synergistically foster complex reasoning capabilities in LVLMs. The authors specifically aim to demonstrate that while initial SFT might lead to performance drops, the iterative cycle eventually yields significant gains.
- **Elucidating Roles of SFT and RL:** To analyze and understand the distinct roles of SFT and RL in this iterative process. Specifically, SFT is hypothesized to act as an "action highlighting" mechanism, surfacing latent reasoning actions and narrowing the RL search space, while RL is expected to refine these behaviors and improve generalization.
- **Achieving Data Efficiency:** To demonstrate that robust complex reasoning can be instilled in LVLMs through this iterative SFT-RL approach without requiring massive, large-scale datasets often employed in other methods.
- **Advancing Open-Source Multimodal AI:** To contribute a robust, open-source model and methodology that serves as early evidence and a strong foundation for achieving R1-style reasoning in the broader multimodal domain.

In essence, the paper seeks to not only build a high-performing open-source LVLM with advanced reasoning but also to provide fundamental insights into the training dynamics that enable such complex intelligence in multimodal AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenVLThinker-7B is developed through a novel iterative self-improvement strategy that alternates between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) cycles, specifically utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This approach is designed to overcome the limitations of purely SFT-based distillation (due to imprecise visual grounding) and purely RL-based methods (due to large search space).

The core methodology revolves around an "Iterative SFT-RL Cycle" (Figure 2), starting from a base LVLM, Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Each iteration involves two main steps:

1. **Lightweight Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Initial Iteration (SFT-Iter1):** Since proprietary LVLMs do not expose their internal reasoning paths, and text-only R1 models cannot process visual input directly, the first SFT dataset is curated indirectly. The target base LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is first used as a *captioning model* to generate detailed textual descriptions for images. These captions then serve as proxies for the images and are fed into a powerful *text-based R1 reasoning model* (QwQ-32B) to generate multiple candidate reasoning chains (CoTs). From these candidates, the shortest reasoning chain that correctly arrives at the final answer is selected for the SFT-Iter1 dataset. This step aims to distill R1-like reasoning demonstrations, even if they initially cause a performance drop due to imperfect image-to-text conversion and potential verbosity. To mitigate the issue of overly long and repetitive reasoning chains, filtering strategies (e.g., truncating repetitive reflections at keywords like "Wait") are applied during data curation.
 - **Subsequent Iterations (SFT-Iter *i* for *i* > 1):** For later iterations, the LVLM trained in the *previous iteration* (i.e., the GRPO-Iter(*i*-1) model) is used to produce higher-quality CoTs on a small, curated dataset of 3,000 data points. This self-generated data progressively enhances the model's reasoning depth and quality for the subsequent SFT phase. The base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is *re-trained from scratch* at each iteration with this newly generated dataset to maintain stability and prevent overfitting, a practice common in iterative self-improvement methods.

2. **Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (GRPO):**
 - Following each SFT stage, the model's reasoning capabilities are further enhanced through RL using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. GRPO is chosen for its effectiveness in fine-tuning LLMs with human preferences, here guided by verifiable outcomes.
 - **Two-Stage Source-Based Curriculum RL:** To maximize effective exploration and learning, the RL training employs a curriculum based on data difficulty. Data sources (e.g., FigureQA, MapQA, GeoQA) are assessed for difficulty using a composite score derived from GPT-4o ratings and the base model's error rates. These sources are then categorized into Easy, Medium, and Hard groups via k-means clustering. Within each RL stage, training proceeds sequentially:
 - **Stage 1: 𝒟_RL(Medium):** The model is first trained on a dataset constructed from medium-difficulty data sources.
 - **Stage 2: 𝒟_RL(Hard):** Subsequently, it is trained on a dataset comprising the most challenging data sources. This curriculum ensures that the model builds foundational skills before tackling more complex problems, optimizing learning efficiency.

**The Role of SFT and RL in the Cycle:**
The authors emphasize a critical insight:
- **SFT as "Action Highlighting":** SFT serves as an inductive prior that effectively "surfaces" desirable reasoning actions (e.g., planning, reflection, verification) often triggered by specific tokens like "wait," "first," or "check." It guides the model to *exhibit* these behaviors, providing a structured search space for RL.
- **RL as "Refinement and Generalization":** RL then takes this foundation to refine the reasoning behaviors, generalize from the training data, and improve the overall accuracy. It also plays a crucial role in generating higher-quality SFT data for the next iterative cycle, facilitating self-improvement.

**Training Setup:**
The model uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B as its base. The training framework is LLaMA-Factory for SFT and EasyR1 for RL. Training data is sourced from LLaVA-OneVision and 14 data sources overlapping with MathV360K. Each iteration uses 3K SFT distillation data, 3K medium-level RL data, and 6K hard-level RL data, totaling 12K examples per iteration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "OpenVLThinker" paper presents several significant findings and impressive results that underscore the effectiveness of its iterative SFT-RL approach for complex vision-language reasoning:

- **Superior Performance on Diverse Benchmarks:** OpenVLThinker-7B consistently achieves top-tier performance among open-source LVLMs of comparable scale across six challenging benchmarks. These benchmarks span mathematical reasoning (MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision), general reasoning (MMMU-Pro, EMMA), and perception/hallucination (HallusionBench).
 - It registers notable improvements on MathVista by 3.8%, EMMA by 2.4%, and HallusionBench by 1.6%. The HallusionBench improvement indicates reduced hallucination and more precise perception.
 - On average, OpenVLThinker-7B achieves an accuracy of 47.9%, representing a 2% absolute gain over its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B).

- **Competitiveness with Proprietary Models:** Remarkably, OpenVLThinker-7B performs competitively with large proprietary multimodal models such as GPT-4o (47.3% average) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (48.4% average), especially in Math and Perception tasks. This demonstrates that open-source models can approach the capabilities of commercial systems in complex multimodal reasoning.

- **Exceptional Data Efficiency:** A key finding is that OpenVLThinker achieves these superior results using significantly less training data. With only three SFT-RL cycles and a total of 12K training examples (per iteration), it outperforms concurrent reasoning methods (e.g., R1-VL-7B, R1-Onevision-7B, VLAA-Thinker-7B) that rely on substantially larger datasets (ranging from 150K to 270K examples) with single-iteration SFT-RL pipelines. This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the iterative self-improvement paradigm.

- **Validation of the Iterative SFT-RL Cycle:** The paper empirically demonstrates that while the initial SFT step on text-based R1 reasoning might cause a temporary performance drop (as seen in Figure 3 on MathVista), iteratively alternating SFT and RL gradually and consistently yields significant performance gains. This validates the core hypothesis of the proposed framework.

- **Elucidating Roles of SFT and RL:**
 - **SFT as "Action Highlighting":** Analysis of reasoning keywords (e.g., "wait," "first," "check") reveals that the base model rarely exhibits planning or reflection actions. However, SFT, even when distilled from text-based R1 models, effectively *surfaces* these behaviors, providing an inductive prior that narrows the search space for RL (Figure 6). This is a crucial insight into how reasoning behaviors are initiated.
 - **RL as "Refinement":** Subsequent GRPO-based RL training further enhances performance while largely maintaining the distribution of reasoning actions, with minor refinements like reduced repetitive reflections. Pure RL without prior SFT struggles to induce these complex behaviors efficiently.

- **Effectiveness of Curriculum RL:** The two-stage, source-based curriculum RL (training first on medium-difficulty data, then on hard data) proves more effective than training solely on hard data or including easy data. This approach maximizes performance gains by providing appropriately challenging data at each stage (Figure 8, Figure 9).

- **Reasoning Length Evolution:** While the reasoning models generally produce longer responses than the non-reasoning base model, iterative training leads to gradually more concise reasoning lengths across iterations. This suggests an increasingly efficient and beneficial utilization of reflective reasoning (Figure 10).

- **Re-training from Scratch:** The empirical comparison demonstrates that re-training the base model from scratch at each iteration (as opposed to continued training from the previous checkpoint) ensures better stability and generalization, preventing overfitting to previous iteration's data, particularly on benchmarks like HallusionBench (Table 5).

- **Scalability to Smaller Models:** The success is not limited to 7B models; an OpenVLThinker-3B model, distilled from the 7B model, also achieves state-of-the-art performance on MathVista among 3B reasoning models, suggesting the approach's robustness across model scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "OpenVLThinker" paper makes several significant contributions and has the potential for a substantial impact on the field of large vision-language models and AI reasoning research:

- **Pioneering Open-Source R1-Style LVLMs:** OpenVLThinker is one of the first open-source LVLMs to reliably demonstrate sophisticated chain-of-thought reasoning, including self-reflection, planning, and correction, in visual contexts. This is a critical step towards democratizing advanced AI capabilities that were previously largely confined to proprietary models or text-only domains. Its release as an open-source model, code, and data will empower broader research and development.

- **Novel and Data-Efficient Training Paradigm:** The proposed iterative SFT-RL loop offers a simple yet highly effective self-improvement framework. Its ability to achieve state-of-the-art results with significantly less training data compared to concurrent methods is a major breakthrough. This data efficiency makes advanced reasoning accessible to researchers with more limited computational resources and can accelerate the development cycle for new models.

- **Deeper Understanding of Reasoning Emergence:** The paper's analysis provides crucial insights into the complementary roles of SFT and RL. The concept of SFT as "action highlighting" – effectively surfacing latent reasoning behaviors – and RL as a "refinement" mechanism offers a new perspective on how complex reasoning emerges in large models. This understanding can guide future research in designing more effective training strategies for advanced cognitive abilities in AI.

- **Enhanced Multimodal AI Capabilities:** By enabling complex reasoning in LVLMs, OpenVLThinker significantly boosts their performance across a range of challenging tasks, including mathematical reasoning, general knowledge, and visual perception, while also reducing hallucination. This leads to more robust, reliable, and intelligent AI systems capable of handling complex real-world scenarios involving both visual and linguistic information.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a strong baseline and early evidence for achieving R1-style reasoning in multimodal contexts. It opens avenues for future exploration into scaling this approach to even larger LVLMs, integrating more diverse reasoning tasks, and investigating the interplay of various reasoning actions in greater detail. The insights gained could also be transferable to other domains requiring iterative self-improvement and complex problem-solving in AI.

- **Contribution to Reproducibility and Transparency:** By releasing the code, model, and data, the authors contribute to the transparency and reproducibility of AI research, allowing the community to build upon their findings and further advance the field of multimodal reasoning.

In summary, OpenVLThinker represents a significant leap forward in equipping open-source LVLMs with truly sophisticated reasoning capabilities. Its methodological innovation, impressive performance, and fundamental insights into reasoning emergence position it as a highly impactful contribution to the ongoing pursuit of more intelligent and versatile AI systems.
