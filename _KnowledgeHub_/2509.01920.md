# Dynamic Speculative Agent Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Dynamic Speculative Agent Planning (DSP) framework accelerates LLM agent planning without compromising performance by employing an asynchronous online reinforcement learning method to dynamically predict optimal speculation steps. It achieves comparable latency to fixed-step baselines while reducing total costs by up to 34.9% and provides user-controlled latency-cost trade-offs.

## Problem

- LLM-based agents suffer from high latency and prohibitive inference costs due to their autoregressive planning nature, hindering widespread deployment.
- Existing acceleration methods often lack lossless performance guarantees or require extensive pre-deployment training of additional modules.
- Current solutions provide minimal user control over the efficiency-cost trade-off, making them inflexible for varying application requirements and dynamic LLM pricing.

## Method

- **DSP** introduces an online reinforcement learning predictor to dynamically determine the optimal 'speculation step' (k) without requiring any pre-deployment training or external datasets.
- The predictor operates asynchronously with multi-threading, running concurrently with the approximation agent's planning and continuously fine-tuning itself to adapt in real-time.
- Users gain fine-grained control over the latency-cost trade-off through a tunable 'tau' (τ) hyperparameter using expectile regression during training or a direct integer offset ('beta' β) at inference time.

## Results

- DSP consistently achieves superior Pareto efficiency, matching the fastest fixed-k baselines in latency reduction while reducing total costs by up to **34.9%** on GPT-4.1-mini.
- The framework significantly reduces unnecessary computational overhead, cutting redundant costs by up to **60.65%** compared to the most aggressive fixed-k settings.
- DSP's tunable parameters allow for a smooth continuum of operating points, enabling practitioners to prioritize high-performance (faster execution) or economy (lower cost) modes to suit diverse application needs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal speculative step 'k' for LLM agent planning is highly context-dependent and variable, making fixed-step speculation inefficient.
> - An asynchronous online reinforcement learning approach, formulated as a state-value prediction problem with Temporal-Difference (TD) learning, effectively predicts dynamic 'k' without prior setup.
> - Expectile regression and direct offset parameters enable robust, user-configurable control over the latency-cost spectrum in speculative agent execution.
