# Stable Virtual Camera: Generative View Synthesis with Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A generalist diffusion model, STABLE VIRTUAL CAMERA (SEVA), synthesizes realistic and 3D-consistent novel views from flexible input configurations and arbitrary camera trajectories. It establishes new state-of-the-art performance across 10 diverse datasets, generating high-quality, temporally smooth videos up to 30 seconds without requiring intermediate 3D representation distillation.

## Problem

- Existing generative novel view synthesis methods often struggle to simultaneously achieve large viewpoint changes and maintain temporal smoothness in generated sequences.
- Many NVS models are constrained by rigid input/target view configurations, limiting flexibility for real-world scenarios.
- Ensuring 3D consistency typically requires complex post-processing steps like NeRF distillation, adding computational overhead.

## Method

- **SEVA** is a ==multi-view diffusion model== built on ==Stable Diffusion 2.1==, incorporating ==3D and 1D self-attention== to process multiple camera views.
- A ==two-stage curriculum training== recipe enabling the model to handle ==large context windows== and various input-output view configurations.
- A flexible ==procedural two-pass sampling== strategy for inference, which manages diverse input/output frame counts and uses a ==memory bank== for long-range consistency.

## Results

- Achieves new state-of-the-art performance across **10** diverse datasets, including quantitative gains of **+6.0 dB PSNR** on LLFF (P=3) and **+0.6 dB PSNR** over CAT3D on Mip360 (P=3).
- Generates high-quality, photorealistic novel view videos up to **30 seconds** with minimal temporal flickering, maintaining 3D consistency across various camera movements.
- Generated views, when distilled into 3D Gaussian Splatting, result in higher 3D reconstruction quality (higher PSNR, lower LPIPS) compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A diffusion-based approach can synthesize 3D-consistent novel views and smooth trajectories without requiring explicit 3D representation distillation.
> - Flexible procedural sampling strategies, including a memory bank of spatially nearest anchors, are critical for maintaining consistency over long and complex camera paths.
> - The model exhibits robust zero-shot generalization to varying input view counts (especially semi-dense) and different image resolutions/aspect ratios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Novel View Synthesis (NVS) is a long-standing challenge in computer vision, aiming to generate realistic and 3D-consistent images of a scene from arbitrary camera viewpoints given a set of input images. Traditionally, NVS relied on dense input views, treating the problem as a 3D reconstruction and rendering task, exemplified by methods like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). While these approaches excel with abundant data, they often fail when inputs are sparse.

The emergence of "generative view synthesis" addresses this limitation by leveraging powerful deep network priors, enabling immersive 3D interactions even in uncontrolled environments with limited input images. Despite recent advancements, the field faces several critical challenges:
1. **Limited Generalization:** Existing methods often struggle to simultaneously generate large viewpoint changes and maintain temporal smoothness, typically excelling at one but not the other.
2. **Rigid Task Configurations:** Many models are constrained by fixed numbers of input and target views, limiting their flexibility in real-world applications.
3. **Consistency Issues and Distillation:** Sampling consistency often remains a hurdle, frequently necessitating additional 3D representation-based distillation (e.g., NeRF distillation) to fuse inconsistent results into a coherent 3D representation, adding complexity and computational overhead.

STABLE VIRTUAL CAMERA (SEVA) directly addresses these limitations by proposing a generalist diffusion model for NVS. Unlike many prior works that rely on explicit 3D representations within the network or subsequent distillation, SEVA focuses on a simplified, end-to-end diffusion-based pipeline. It positions itself as a "virtual camera simulation system" capable of synthesizing novel views along arbitrary camera trajectories with any number of input and target views, without requiring intermediate 3D representation distillation. This positions SEVA as a significant step towards more practical, flexible, and high-quality generative NVS solutions for "in the wild" scenarios. The paper contrasts its approach with both regression-based models (e.g., pixelNeRF, LVSM) and other diffusion-based models (e.g., Zero123, SV3D, CAT3D, 4DiM, ViewCrafter), highlighting its unique ability to combine large viewpoint generation capacity with temporal smoothness and input flexibility within a single model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the STABLE VIRTUAL CAMERA (SEVA) project are to overcome the persistent challenges in generative novel view synthesis (NVS), thereby broadening its applicability in real-world scenarios. Specifically, the authors aim to:

- **Achieve Generalization Across Diverse NVS Tasks:** Develop a single, generalist diffusion model capable of handling a wide spectrum of view synthesis tasks. This includes both "set NVS" (generating unordered images across a large spatial range, requiring high generation capacity) and "trajectory NVS" (producing smooth video sequences along a camera path, demanding high interpolation smoothness).
- **Support Flexible Input and Target Configurations:** Address the rigidity of existing methods by allowing any number of input views (from sparse to semi-dense, e.g., 1 to 32+) and arbitrary target camera configurations.
- **Enable Large Viewpoint Changes and Temporal Smoothness Simultaneously:** Overcome the common trade-off where models either excel at synthesizing drastic viewpoint shifts or generating temporally smooth sequences, but rarely both. SEVA seeks to strike a balance, producing high-quality novel views that exhibit both attributes.
- **Eliminate the Need for 3D Representation-based Distillation:** A key motivation is to streamline the NVS pipeline by removing the necessity for additional steps like NeRF distillation, which are often used to force 3D consistency onto inconsistent generative outputs. SEVA aims to maintain high consistency intrinsically, making the process more efficient and direct.
- **Generate High-Quality, Long-Duration Videos:** Demonstrate the capability to produce photorealistic videos lasting up to half a minute with precise camera control and seamless loop closure in 3D, pushing the boundaries of temporal coherence in generative NVS.
- **Simplify the NVS Pipeline for Real-World Applications:** By achieving the above, the authors aim to make NVS more accessible and practical for content creators, developers, and researchers, facilitating applications like virtual cinematography and gaming without requiring complex 3D capture setups.

These objectives are pursued through careful model design, an optimized training recipe, and a flexible sampling strategy, all designed to leverage the power of diffusion models for robust 3D understanding and synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEVA's methodology is built upon a diffusion-based architecture, strategically designed to overcome the limitations of prior NVS methods without relying on explicit 3D representations during inference. The core approach encompasses three key aspects: model design, training recipe, and sampling strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Design and Architecture

SEVA is formulated as a multi-view diffusion model, building upon the widely recognized Stable Diffusion 2.1 (SD 2.1) as its backbone. This leverages SD 2.1's strong 2D generative priors. Key modifications to adapt it for NVS include:
- **3D and 1D Self-Attention:** The 2D self-attention within the latent denoising U-Net of SD 2.1 is "inflated" into 3D self-attention for low-resolution residual blocks, enabling the model to reason about spatial relationships across multiple views. Additionally, 1D self-attention is added along the view axis via skip connections, specifically to model relationships between different camera views. These augmentations increase the model's parameters from 870M to 1.3B.
- **Optional Temporal Pathway:** For enhanced temporal smoothness in video generation, an optional pathway incorporating 3D convolutions via skip connections is introduced, increasing parameters to 1.5B. This pathway can be activated during inference when frames are known to be spatially ordered.
- **Flexible Conditioning:** The model conditions on camera poses using Plücker embeddings, which are concatenated and adaptively layer-normalized. Camera poses are normalized relative to the first input camera, and scene scale is normalized within a `[-2, 2]^3` cube. Input frames are encoded into VAE latents, combined with their Plücker embedding and a binary mask distinguishing input from target views. Target frames use their noisy latent state. High-level semantic information from CLIP image embeddings is also injected. New weights for additional channels are zero-initialized.

#### 3.2. Training Recipe

The training strategy is crucial for SEVA's generalization capabilities:
- **Two-Stage Curriculum:** To support large context windows (total input and target frames, `T = M + N`), a two-stage curriculum is employed to prevent divergence.
 - **Stage 1:** `T = 8` frames, trained for 100K iterations with a batch size of 1472.
 - **Stage 2:** `T = 21` frames, trained for 600K iterations with a batch size of 512.
- **View Selection Strategy:** During training, the number of input frames `M` is randomly sampled within `[1, T-1]`. Frames (`I_inp`, `I_tgt`) are jointly sampled with a small subsampling stride (0.2 probability) to ensure sufficient temporal granularity and capture critical transitions.
- **Optional Video Training:** In an optional stage, temporal weights are trained using data sampled with a small subsampling stride.
- **Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) Adjustment:** The SNR is shifted across stages, requiring more noise when training with more frames to effectively destroy information, consistent with findings in other diffusion models.
- **Resolution:** Models are trained on `H = W = 576` squared images.

#### 3.3. Procedural Two-Pass Sampling Strategy (Inference)

SEVA introduces a flexible procedural two-pass sampling approach to handle varying input (`P`) and output (`Q`) configurations at test time, where `P` and `Q` need not match the training context window `M` and `N`. This strategy maintains consistency across generated views.
- **Scenario 1: `P + Q <= T`:** The task fits within a single forward pass. The model pads the forward pass to exactly `T` frames by repeating the first input image, which empirically works better than zero-shot modification of `T`.
- **Scenario 2: `P + Q > T`:** This is handled by the two-pass procedure:
 - **First Pass: Anchor Frame Generation (`I_acr`):** A subset of high-quality "anchor" views are generated.
 - **Set NVS:** `Nearest procedural sampling` is used. Anchor views are generated based on pre-defined trajectory priors (e.g., 360-degree orbits for objects). The default is `gt + nearest`, where input views (`I_inp`) are appended to each forward pass.
 - **Trajectory NVS:** `Interp procedural sampling` is used. A subset of target frames (`I_tgt`) are uniformly sampled as `I_acr` along the target camera path. This strategy is chosen as the default for its robustness in producing smooth transitions.
 - **Second Pass: Target Generation:** The remaining target views are divided into chunks and generated using the newly created `I_acr` (and optionally `I_inp`), ensuring local consistency.
- **Scaling for Large `P` and `Q`:**
 - **Large `P` (`P > T`, semi-dense views):** For dense inputs (e.g., `P=32`), the context window `T` is extended zero-shot to accommodate all input views in one pass for anchor generation. This surprisingly works well, generalizing to hundreds of views as the task shifts from generation to interpolation.
 - **Large `Q` (`Q >> T`, long trajectories):** To maintain consistency over very long sequences, a `memory bank` of previously generated anchor views and their poses is maintained. Anchors are generated autoregressively by retrieving spatially nearest ones from this bank, significantly improving long-range 3D consistency compared to reusing temporally nearest anchors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SEVA's performance is rigorously evaluated through a comprehensive benchmark across 10 diverse datasets and various experimental settings, demonstrating its strong generalization and superiority.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Setup

The benchmark covers:
- **Datasets:** Object datasets (OmniObject3D, GSO), object-centric scene datasets (LLFF, DTU, CO3D, WildRGBD), and general scene datasets (RealEstate10K, Mip-NeRF 360, DL3DV140, Tanks and Temples).
- **Input Views:** From sparse (1-8 views) to semi-dense (9-32 views).
- **NVS Tasks:** Categorized into "small-viewpoint NVS" (target views similar to inputs, emphasizing interpolation smoothness) and "large-viewpoint NVS" (target views very different from inputs, emphasizing generation capacity).
- **Metrics:** PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio), LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity), SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure) for image quality; TSED (Temporal Epipolar Distance) and Motion Smoothness (MS) for temporal quality and 3D consistency.

#### 4.2. Set NVS Results

- **Quantitative Superiority:** SEVA consistently sets new state-of-the-art (SOTA) results.
 - **Small-Viewpoint:** Achieves SOTA in most splits, with a significant +6.0 dB PSNR gain on LLFF (P=3). In the semi-dense regime (P=32), SEVA surprisingly performs favorably against specialized models like Long-LRM, despite not being specifically designed for dense inputs. It shows dramatically higher PSNR on object datasets (OO3D, GSO).
 - **Large-Viewpoint:** SEVA's advantages are even more pronounced, showcasing its superior generation capacity. It improves over SOTA CAT3D by +0.6 dB PSNR on Mip360 (P=3) and demonstrates a clear performance lead on challenging scenes like DL3DV and T&T.
 - **Single-View Limitation:** For P=1 (single input view), especially on datasets like RealEstate10K with dominant panning motion, SEVA underperforms. This is attributed to scale ambiguity during camera normalization. A practical solution involves sweeping the unit length for camera normalization.
- **Qualitative Excellence:** SEVA produces photorealistic and sharper images with better alignment to ground truth compared to baselines (e.g., LVSM, Long-LRM, DepthSplat), especially when dealing with large viewpoint changes where competitors fail to produce reasonable results.
- **3D Reconstruction Consistency:** When distilled into 3D Gaussian Splatting representations, SEVA's generated views consistently lead in 3D reconstruction quality (higher PSNR, lower LPIPS), validating its inherent 3D consistency.

#### 4.3. Trajectory NVS Results

- **Qualitative Versatility:** SEVA generates high-quality videos for diverse camera movements (orbit, spiral, zoom, dolly zoom) and image types (object-centric, scene-level, real-world, text-prompted). It handles challenging scenarios like moving close to or through objects, demonstrating strong expressiveness. The outputs maintain smoothness with minimal temporal flickering.
- **Quantitative Performance:** SEVA performs favorably in trajectory NVS. The optional temporal pathway further boosts performance and smoothness, indicating the benefits of a gated architecture for temporal consistency.
- **Ablation on Two-Pass Sampling:** The "interp" procedural sampling strategy is crucial for temporal smoothness. Ablation studies show that "interp" drastically outperforms one-pass sampling and other procedural strategies (e.g., "gt + nearest") in terms of 3D consistency (lower TSED) and motion smoothness (higher MS), visually manifesting as significantly reduced flickering artifacts.

#### 4.4. Long-Trajectory NVS Results

- **Enhanced Long-Range Consistency:** For very long trajectories (e.g., 1000 frames looping multiple times), SEVA's memory bank approach, using *spatially nearest* anchors, is critical. This significantly improves long-range 3D consistency and reduces inconsistencies/artifacts in recurring locations, outperforming methods that rely on *temporal nearest* anchors. This robustness is particularly vital for in-the-wild data not constrained by the quality of explicit 3D reconstruction tools.

#### 4.5. Discussions and Generalizations

- **Zero-Shot Context Window Generalization:** Surprisingly, the model trained on T=21 frames generalizes reasonably well to much larger T (hundreds of views) in the semi-dense regime for one-pass sampling. This suggests a shift from generation to interpolation as input views become denser. However, this generalization fails in the sparse-view regime, resulting in blurry samples.
- **Zero-Shot Resolution Generalization:** Despite being trained only on 576x576 square images, SEVA generalizes well to different image resolutions and aspect ratios (portrait, landscape) during sampling.
- **Guidance Scale (CFG):** The optimal Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scale is correlated with generation uncertainty; higher CFG is preferred for high uncertainty (e.g., single-view) to avoid blurriness, while lower CFG is better for low uncertainty (e.g., few-view) to prevent oversaturation.
- **Sampling Diversity:** The model can generate diverse and plausible predictions for unseen regions by simply varying random seeds during sampling, showcasing its generative capacity for ambiguous areas.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The STABLE VIRTUAL CAMERA (SEVA) marks a significant advancement in the field of novel view synthesis, offering a generalist, robust, and highly flexible solution that addresses several long-standing challenges. Its primary significance lies in its ability to simultaneously achieve large viewpoint changes and temporally smooth generation from a variable number of input views, critically without requiring explicit 3D representation-based distillation. This simplification of the pipeline is a major leap towards making high-quality NVS more practical and accessible.

The potential impact of SEVA is far-reaching across various domains:

- **Content Creation and Virtual Cinematography:** SEVA enables content creators to intuitively design complex, controllable camera paths and generate high-quality videos, akin to the functionality of traditional "Virtual Camera" systems but powered by generative AI. This can revolutionize pre-visualization, virtual production, and the creation of immersive 3D content for film, animation, and advertising.
- **Gaming and Interactive Experiences:** The ability to generate consistent novel views from sparse inputs can enhance immersive gaming environments, enabling dynamic camera perspectives and rich 3D interactions without needing to pre-render every possible viewpoint or rely on dense captures.
- **Digital Heritage and Virtual Tourism:** SEVA can facilitate the creation of rich, explorable 3D models from limited photographic inputs, preserving cultural artifacts and historical sites in digital form, and offering compelling virtual tourism experiences.
- **Democratization of 3D Generation:** By streamlining the NVS pipeline and providing a model that generalizes well across diverse conditions, SEVA lowers the barrier to entry for developers and researchers. The open-source release of its model weights further amplifies this impact, fostering innovation and accelerating future research in generative 3D technologies.
- **Research Advancements:** SEVA's success in jointly modeling large viewpoint changes and temporal smoothness, coupled with its flexible sampling strategies and impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities (e.g., to different input counts and resolutions), provides valuable insights for the design of future generative AI models. Its demonstration of intrinsic 3D consistency without distillation challenges conventional wisdom in the field, paving the way for more direct and efficient generative 3D approaches.

While SEVA represents a substantial step forward, the authors acknowledge limitations such as reduced quality for certain content (humans, animals, dynamic textures), highly ambiguous scenes, or trajectories intersecting objects. Nevertheless, its comprehensive benchmarking and strong performance across diverse settings firmly establish SEVA as a new state-of-the-art in generative view synthesis, setting a high bar for future research and opening new avenues for practical applications.
