# Win-Win: Training High-Resolution Vision Transformers from Two Windows

> [!summary] Summary
> Naver Labs Europe introduces WIN-WIN, a training strategy for high-resolution Vision Transformers that utilizes structured token masking during training. This approach reduces training time by 3 to 4 times and memory usage by half while enabling direct, single-pass high-resolution inference without artifacts, achieving state-of-the-art optical flow performance on the Full-HD Spring benchmark with 0.475 EPE.

## Problem

- High-resolution Vision Transformers (ViTs) for dense prediction tasks incur prohibitive computational costs due to the quadratic complexity of global self-attention.
- ViTs often exhibit poor generalization when tested at resolutions significantly different from those used during training, leading to performance degradation.
- Existing solutions like architectural modifications alter the vanilla ViT structure, while training on low-resolution crops necessitates slow and artifact-prone test-time tiling strategies.

## Method

- The ==WIN-WIN== method trains vanilla ViTs by feeding a structured subset of input tokens derived from N (typically N=2) non-overlapping rectangular windows randomly sampled from the high-resolution image.
- It leverages ==Relative Positional Embeddings (RoPE)== for improved generalization and is compatible with standard convolutional heads for dense prediction tasks.
- At inference, the masking scheme is removed, allowing the model to process the full high-resolution image in a single forward pass, eliminating the need for slow tiling strategies, and uses a ==softmax temperature factor== to compensate for token distribution changes.

## Results

- Achieves **3 to 4 times faster training** and **half the memory usage** compared to full-resolution ViT training, while yielding comparable or superior performance.
- Outperforms or matches existing methods on semantic segmentation (**6**3.6%** mIoU** on BDD100k) and sets a new state-of-the-art on the Full-HD Spring optical flow benchmark with **0.475 EPE**, demonstrating over an order of magnitude faster inference than tiling methods.
- Demonstrates superior robustness to varying test resolutions and produces smooth, artifact-free high-resolution dense predictions, unlike tiling-based approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured masking with just two non-overlapping windows ("Win-Win") effectively allows the ViT to learn both local and global interactions with a significantly reduced token budget during training.
> - Relative positional embeddings (RoPE) are critical for Vision Transformers to generalize robustly to varying resolutions and maintain translation equivariance in dense prediction tasks.
> - Direct, high-resolution inference with a trained masked-input ViT is viable, provided that potential shifts in self-attention distributions due to varying token counts are appropriately managed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as the leading architecture for various computer vision tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on both image-level classification and dense pixel-wise prediction tasks. This success is largely attributed to their ability to model long-range dependencies through the self-attention mechanism. However, the application of vanilla Vision Transformers to high-resolution images, especially for dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation or optical flow, presents a significant challenge: the computational cost. The self-attention mechanism has a quadratic complexity with respect to the number of input tokens (patches), making full-resolution training prohibitively expensive in terms of both memory and time.

Furthermore, a well-documented issue with ViTs is their poor generalization to test resolutions that are significantly different from the training resolutions. Models trained on low-resolution images often suffer a clear performance drop when evaluated on higher-resolution inputs, necessitating complex and slow post-processing schemes.

The broader research landscape has explored several avenues to address these challenges:

- **Architectural Modifications:**
 - **Hierarchical Architectures:** Approaches like Swin Transformers (Liu et al., 2021) and MViT (Fan et al., 2021) introduce hierarchical designs or windowed attention mechanisms to reduce the quadratic complexity, often by limiting attention to local windows or across different scales. While effective, these designs deviate from the "vanilla" ViT structure, potentially making it harder to leverage large-scale, task-agnostic pre-training models that are often based on standard ViT backbones.
 - **Fast/Approximate Attention:** Methods like Performer (Choromanski et al., 2021) or Linformer (Wang et al., 2020a) approximate the self-attention mechanism with sub-quadratic complexity, aiming to reduce computational overhead while maintaining performance.
 - **Token Reduction/Sparsification:** Techniques that reduce the number of tokens (e.g., Token Merging, DynamicViT, A-ViT) or sparsify the attention matrix (e.g., ViT-Det, SAM) also fall into this category. ViT-Det, for instance, uses windowed attention in most blocks but retains global attention in a few to model global interactions. However, these still involve altering the attention mechanism and may offer only moderate speed-ups (e.g., 25% for ViT-Det as noted in this paper) while potentially sacrificing some performance compared to full global attention.

- **Training Strategies (without architectural changes):**
 - **Training on Low-Resolution Crops with Test-Time Tiling:** This involves training a vanilla ViT on small, fixed-size crops of images. At inference, a sliding window (tiling) strategy is employed to process the high-resolution input, aggregating predictions from multiple overlapping crops. This approach maintains the vanilla ViT structure but leads to significantly slower inference times and can introduce visible artifacts (e.g., blocking artifacts) due to the aggregation process. Examples include CroCo-Flow (Weinzaepfel et al., 2023) and FlowFormer (Huang et al., 2022).
 - **Multi-Stage Training:** Training at lower resolutions and then fine-tuning at the target high resolution (Zheng et al., 2021) is another option, but it remains very costly and time-consuming.

This paper's work, "Win-Win," positions itself strategically within this landscape. It proposes a novel training strategy that *does not alter the vanilla self-attention mechanism or introduce hierarchical backbones*. Instead, it tackles the computational cost during training by *selectively masking out most of the high-resolution input tokens*, feeding only a structured subset to the ViT. Crucially, this strategy allows the model to *directly process full high-resolution inputs at test time in a single forward pass*, circumventing the slow and artifact-prone tiling strategies, while mitigating the train/test resolution discrepancy. By doing so, "Win-Win" aims to combine the benefits of efficient training (like crop-based methods) with efficient, artifact-free high-resolution inference (like ideal full-resolution training), effectively addressing a major bottleneck for high-resolution dense prediction with vanilla ViTs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "Win-Win" research is to develop an **efficient and effective training and inference strategy for high-resolution Vision Transformers (ViTs)**, specifically tailored for dense pixel-wise prediction tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges currently faced by the Vision Transformer paradigm:

- **Prohibitive Computational Cost of High-Resolution ViTs:** Vanilla Vision Transformers employ a global self-attention mechanism with quadratic computational complexity relative to the number of input tokens. For high-resolution images, this translates to extremely high memory consumption and lengthy training times, making it impractical to train models directly on full-resolution inputs for dense prediction tasks.
- **Poor Generalization to Varying Resolutions:** ViTs are known to generalize poorly when tested at resolutions significantly different from those seen during training. This "train/test resolution discrepancy" often leads to a clear performance drop, forcing the use of sub-optimal workarounds like downscaling test images or complex multi-pass inference strategies.
- **Inefficiencies of Existing Solutions:** Current solutions each come with their own drawbacks:
 - **Architectural modifications** (e.g., hierarchical ViTs, sparse attention) often require redesigning the backbone, making it harder to leverage large-scale pre-trained vanilla ViTs. They may also not offer sufficient speed-ups or achieve the same performance as full global attention.
 - **Training on low-resolution crops** is efficient for training but necessitates slow and often artifact-inducing test-time tiling (sliding window) strategies to produce high-resolution outputs, which are unsuitable for real-time applications and can compromise visual quality.
- **Desire for Simplicity and Generality:** The authors are motivated by the idea that a simpler, more general ViT architecture, without numerous "bells and whistles" (i.e., task-specific designs or complex modifications), should be able to achieve state-of-the-art performance efficiently. This allows for easier adaptation of pre-trained models and broad applicability across tasks.

In essence, the authors aim to bridge the gap between the high performance of vanilla ViTs and the practical demands of high-resolution, dense prediction tasks by introducing a training methodology that is both computationally efficient and enables direct, high-quality inference at the target resolution, all while preserving the fundamental ViT architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Win-Win" method introduces a novel, structured token masking strategy during training to achieve efficient high-resolution Vision Transformer learning and direct inference.

**Core Principle: Masked Input Training with Structured Windows**
The central idea is to train a vanilla Vision Transformer (ViT) on a *subset* of input tokens (P' ⊂ P) rather than the full set (P) corresponding to a high-resolution image. This reduces the quadratic computational cost from O(|P|^2) to O(|P'|^2) during training. Crucially, the model is then able to process the *full* high-resolution input (all tokens P) directly at test time without any masking or special post-processing.

**Key Components of the Win-Win Approach:**

1. **Structured Masking with N Windows:**
 - Unlike random masking strategies (e.g., in Masked Autoencoders), Win-Win employs a *structured masking* approach. It selects tokens from N ≥ 2 non-overlapping rectangular windows {R_i} randomly sampled from the input image.
 - This structured selection is vital because it allows the model to learn both **local interactions** (within each window) and **global interactions** (between tokens from different windows). The authors empirically find that a single window performs poorly due to the lack of long-range context, while N=2 windows ("Win-Win") provides the best balance for a fixed token budget.
 - The size and number of windows (N) can be adjusted based on the desired computational budget. The paper demonstrates that two squared windows of the same size (e.g., 22x22 tokens) are highly effective and robust.

2. **Compatibility with Convolutional Heads:**
 - For dense prediction tasks, ViT backbones are typically paired with convolutional heads (e.g., ConvNeXt, DPT). Win-Win is designed to be compatible with these. The token features output by the transformer backbone, corresponding to the selected windows, are split and reshaped into feature maps for each rectangle. Convolutions can then be applied separately to these window-specific feature maps.

3. **Relative Positional Embeddings (RoPE):**
 - The method emphasizes the use of relative positional embeddings, specifically Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE), over absolute positional embeddings. This choice is motivated by the translation equivariance inherent to dense vision tasks. Relative embeddings are applied directly at the self-attention computation level and are shown to be critical for generalization, with absolute embeddings leading to a significant performance drop.

4. **Direct Full-Resolution Inference:**
 - At test time, the masking scheme is simply removed. The full high-resolution image, and thus the full set of tokens P, is fed directly to the trained ViT model in a single forward pass. This eliminates the need for slow, multi-pass tiling strategies. The memory requirements at test time are generally lower than during training as intermediate tensors can be freed.

5. **Softmax Temperature Adjustment for Feature Distribution Shift:**
 - A critical aspect of generalizing from masked training to full-resolution inference is addressing the change in the number of tokens. This change can alter the statistics of the self-attention softmax distribution. To compensate, a temperature factor (τ) is introduced into the softmax attention calculation: `softmax(τ QKᵀ)V`. This hyper-parameter is tuned (initially validated on full-resolution training images and potentially fine-tuned) to ensure consistent attention behavior, especially for self-attention probabilities.

6. **Generalization to Binocular Tasks (Optical Flow):**
 - For multi-image tasks like optical flow, a specialized window sampling strategy is developed.
 - First, N non-overlapping random windows are sampled from the *first frame*.
 - Then, for the *second frame*, M non-overlapping windows are sampled. This sampling is *guided* by the ground-truth optical flow: random sampling for the second frame is weighted by the amount of matched tokens (pixels from the first frame that flow into a region in the second frame) within each potential window. This ensures that relevant correspondences between the two frames are likely to be included in the training data, preventing a sparse training signal. Experiments confirm the superiority of this flow-guided, stochastic sampling (e.g., N=2 for first frame, M=4 for second frame, with a small degree of randomness).

**Architectural Details:**
The authors use a ViT-Base encoder (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) with RoPE positional embeddings, pre-trained using self-supervised methods like CroCo (Weinzaepfel et al., 2022; 2023). This encoder is coupled with task-specific convolutional heads: a ConvNeXt head for semantic segmentation and a DPT-based head for optical flow.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "Win-Win" method demonstrates remarkable effectiveness and efficiency across various dense prediction tasks:

1. **Significant Training Efficiency and Memory Reduction:**
 - Win-Win achieves a **3 to 4 times faster training time** and **half the memory usage** compared to standard full-resolution ViT training, while yielding comparable or superior performance (Figure 1). This is a critical practical advantage for high-resolution applications.

2. **Optimal Multi-Window Configuration:**
 - **Effectiveness of Multiple Windows:** A single window training strategy performs poorly (e.g., 61.8 mIoU vs. 63.6 mIoU for semantic segmentation), highlighting the necessity of learning long-range interactions.
 - **"Win-Win" (2 Windows) Optimal:** The simplest configuration of **two non-overlapping squared windows of the same size** (N=2) consistently yields the best performance for a given token budget. Increasing the number of windows beyond two does not lead to further improvements (Table 1, left).
 - **Robustness to Window Design:** The method is robust to variations in window shapes, aspect ratios, and slight changes in token count, with two identical square windows serving as a robust default (Table 1, right). Adding isolated random tokens to the windowed samples also shows no benefit and can even degrade performance.

3. **Crucial Role of Relative Positional Embeddings:**
 - Using cosine absolute positional embeddings instead of RoPE (relative positional embeddings) results in a **significant performance drop** (from 63.6% to 57.0% mIoU for segmentation). This confirms the importance of translation equivariance and highlights the limitations of absolute embeddings when scaling resolutions.

4. **Softmax Temperature Compensation:**
 - Adjusting the softmax temperature parameter at test time provides a moderate but consistent performance improvement (0-0.5 mIoU), confirming its role in compensating for the changed token distribution between training (masked) and full-resolution inference (Table 1, left). This fine-tuning can be done quickly on the training set.

5. **Superior Performance on Monocular Tasks (Semantic Segmentation):**
 - Win-Win (with 2 windows of 22x22 tokens, ~968 tokens) achieves a **63.6% mIoU** on BDD100k, slightly outperforming both full-resolution ViT (63.2% mIoU) and ViT-Det (63.3% mIoU) while being vastly more efficient.
 - It significantly outperforms training on a single crop (32x32 tokens) and testing directly at full resolution (61.8% mIoU) or with resizing (56.1% mIoU).
 - Crucially, Win-Win **outperforms or matches single-crop training with test-time tiling** (e.g., 63.6% vs. 62.6% for similar token counts) while providing a direct, single-pass inference (Table 2).

6. **State-of-the-Art Performance on Binocular Tasks (Optical Flow):**
 - **Effective Binocular Window Strategy:** The proposed flow-guided, stochastic sampling strategy for the second frame's windows (2 windows in frame 1, 4 in frame 2, with 0.3 stochasticity) is essential for high performance in optical flow, significantly outperforming simpler strategies (Table 3).
 - **Competitive Results on MPI-Sintel:** Win-Win achieves an EPE of 1.15 on MPI-Sintel, performing closely to or better than other transformer-based models that rely on tiling at test time, such as FlowFormer and CroCo-Flow (Table 5).
 - **State-of-the-Art on High-Resolution Spring Benchmark:** On the Full-HD Spring benchmark (1920x1080 resolution), Win-Win achieves **state-of-the-art EPE (0.475)**, particularly improving on large displacements (s40+). It even surpasses CroCo-Flow, despite using a smaller ViT-Base backbone compared to CroCo-Flow's ViT-Large.
 - **Order of Magnitude Faster Inference and No Artifacts:** For optical flow, Win-Win's inference is **more than an order of magnitude faster** than tiling-based methods (Figure 1, right) and produces smooth predictions without the blocking artifacts visible in tiling-based approaches like CroCo-Flow (Figure 4).

7. **Robustness to Test Resolution Changes:**
 - Win-Win demonstrates better robustness to varying test resolutions compared to both full-resolution training and single-crop training. Its performance drop at lower resolutions is comparable to models explicitly trained at those lower resolutions, indicating better generalization properties (Figure 5).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Win-Win" training strategy represents a significant advancement in the practical application of Vision Transformers for high-resolution dense prediction tasks. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Democratizing High-Resolution Vision Transformers:** By drastically reducing the training cost (4x faster, 2x less memory) without sacrificing performance, Win-Win makes it much more feasible for researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources to train powerful, high-resolution ViT models. This democratizes access to state-of-the-art vision models for a broader community.

2. **Enabling Efficient and Clean High-Resolution Inference:** The ability to perform direct, single-pass inference on high-resolution images is a major breakthrough. It eliminates the need for slow, complex, and artifact-prone test-time tiling strategies, which are a bottleneck for real-time applications and often degrade the visual quality of dense predictions. This simplification significantly eases deployment and improves the quality of outputs for end-users.

3. **Preserving Vanilla ViT Architecture and Pre-training Benefits:** A crucial impact is that Win-Win achieves its efficiency gains without altering the core architecture of vanilla Vision Transformers. This means that researchers can continue to leverage the powerful and widely available large-scale self-supervised pre-trained ViT models (e.g., from DINOv2, MAE, CroCo) without the need for complex adaptations or finetuning to modified backbones. This accelerates research and development cycles by allowing easy transfer of pre-learned robust visual features.

4. **Broad Applicability Across Dense Prediction Tasks:** The methodology's demonstrated effectiveness across diverse monocular tasks (semantic segmentation, monocular depth) and binocular tasks (optical flow) highlights its generality. This suggests Win-Win could become a standard training paradigm for many other high-resolution dense prediction problems in computer vision, from medical imaging to autonomous driving.

5. **Setting a New Baseline for Efficiency:** Win-Win establishes a new benchmark for combining training efficiency with direct high-resolution inference in ViTs. It provides a strong alternative to existing methods that either compromise on architectural simplicity, training cost, or inference speed/quality.

6. **Fueling Further Research:** The success of structured masking as a training strategy for generalization opens new avenues for research into other forms of intelligent data sampling during training. Future work could explore dynamic window selection, adaptive token budgets, or applying similar principles to other complex modalities or tasks.

From an ethical standpoint, the authors appropriately note that this work primarily focuses on technical efficiency improvements. It does not introduce new capabilities or behaviors beyond what existing ViTs can achieve, but rather makes existing capabilities more accessible and efficient. Therefore, the societal impacts are largely positive, contributing to more sustainable and performant AI development, without introducing novel ethical concerns specific to this method.
