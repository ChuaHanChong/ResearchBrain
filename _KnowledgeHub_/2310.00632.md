# Win-Win: Training High-Resolution Vision Transformers from Two Windows

> [!summary] Summary
> Naver Labs Europe introduces WIN-WIN, a training strategy for high-resolution Vision Transformers that utilizes structured token masking during training. This approach reduces training time by 3 to 4 times and memory usage by half while enabling direct, single-pass high-resolution inference without artifacts, achieving state-of-the-art optical flow performance on the Full-HD Spring benchmark with 0.475 EPE.

## Problem

- High-resolution Vision Transformers (ViTs) for dense prediction tasks incur prohibitive computational costs due to the quadratic complexity of global self-attention.
- ViTs often exhibit poor generalization when tested at resolutions significantly different from those used during training, leading to performance degradation.
- Existing solutions like architectural modifications alter the vanilla ViT structure, while training on low-resolution crops necessitates slow and artifact-prone test-time tiling strategies.

## Method

- The ==WIN-WIN== method trains vanilla ViTs by feeding a structured subset of input tokens derived from N (typically N=2) non-overlapping rectangular windows randomly sampled from the high-resolution image.
- It leverages ==Relative Positional Embeddings (RoPE)== for improved generalization and is compatible with standard convolutional heads for dense prediction tasks.
- At inference, the masking scheme is removed, allowing the model to process the full high-resolution image in a single forward pass, eliminating the need for slow tiling strategies, and uses a ==softmax temperature factor== to compensate for token distribution changes.

## Results

- Achieves **3 to 4 times faster training** and **half the memory usage** compared to full-resolution ViT training, while yielding comparable or superior performance.
- Outperforms or matches existing methods on semantic segmentation (**6**3.6%** mIoU** on BDD100k) and sets a new state-of-the-art on the Full-HD Spring optical flow benchmark with **0.475 EPE**, demonstrating over an order of magnitude faster inference than tiling methods.
- Demonstrates superior robustness to varying test resolutions and produces smooth, artifact-free high-resolution dense predictions, unlike tiling-based approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured masking with just two non-overlapping windows ("Win-Win") effectively allows the ViT to learn both local and global interactions with a significantly reduced token budget during training.
> - Relative positional embeddings (RoPE) are critical for Vision Transformers to generalize robustly to varying resolutions and maintain translation equivariance in dense prediction tasks.
> - Direct, high-resolution inference with a trained masked-input ViT is viable, provided that potential shifts in self-attention distributions due to varying token counts are appropriately managed.
