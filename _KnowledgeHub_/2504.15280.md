# Seeing from Another Perspective: Evaluating Multi-View Understanding in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, TranscEngram, and collaborating institutions introduced All-Angles Bench, a new comprehensive benchmark to evaluate multi-view understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The study demonstrated that current MLLMs exhibit a substantial performance gap and high inconsistency compared to human performance in tasks requiring 3D geometric reasoning across multiple camera perspectives.

## Problem

- While MLLMs show promise for embodied AI, their ability to interpret and reconcile visual information across multiple, disparate viewpoints for robust 3D scene understanding remains underexplored.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on single-view, egocentric, or temporal reasoning, failing to rigorously assess cross-view correspondence and geometric consistency.
- The lack of robust multi-view understanding prevents MLLMs from achieving human-level proficiency in real-world tasks like navigation and object manipulation, where a coherent 3D mental model is crucial.

## Method

- Developed ==All-Angles Bench==, the first comprehensive benchmark designed specifically to evaluate ==multi-view understanding== in MLLMs using 90 diverse multi-view scenes from real-world datasets.
- Designed six distinct task categories (e.g., Counting, Attribute Identification, Camera Pose Estimation) and generated over 2,100 human-annotated multiple-choice questions, including =='paired questions'== to assess consistency.
- Evaluated 27 representative MLLMs, including leading closed- and open-source models, and conducted in-depth qualitative analysis of failure modes.

## Results

- Human annotators achieved an average accuracy of **82.0%** across tasks, while top MLLMs significantly lagged, with differences exceeding **50%** in tasks like Camera Pose Estimation.
- Certain open-source models (e.g., Ovis2-34B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B) unexpectedly outperformed leading closed-source models in orientation-sensitive tasks like Object Manipulation.
- GPT-4o showed approximately **70%** inconsistency on Relative Distance tasks, and all models struggled with Relative Direction, exhibiting over **40%** inconsistency, highlighting a widespread lack of robust multi-view consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs demonstrate a significant performance gap compared to human-level multi-view understanding, particularly in geometric and spatial reasoning tasks.
> - MLLMs exhibit high inconsistency on semantically equivalent 'paired questions,' indicating a lack of stable and genuine understanding across varying perspectives rather than deep comprehension.
> - Failure modes primarily stem from the inability to establish multi-view correspondence for partially occluded objects and a fundamental lack of accurate coarse camera pose estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have enabled impressive capabilities in high-level reasoning, task planning, and understanding complex visual and textual information. These models are increasingly seen as potential foundations for embodied AI agents that can interact with and navigate the real world. However, the authors argue that for MLLMs to function effectively as embodied agents, they require a robust understanding of 3D scenes, geometric consistency, and cross-view correspondence—abilities that remain largely underexplored and inadequately evaluated.

Existing benchmarks for MLLMs primarily focus on single-view or egocentric data, or emphasize temporal reasoning in videos. For instance, `VideoMME` evaluates video understanding with an emphasis on temporal aspects, `VSI-Bench` targets spatial intelligence using egocentric video scenarios, and `MV-Bench` also focuses on comprehensive multi-modal temporal understanding. While these benchmarks are valuable, they fall short in rigorously assessing an MLLM's capacity to reconcile visual information from *multiple, disparate viewpoints* simultaneously, a crucial skill for genuine 3D scene comprehension.

This paper addresses a critical gap by proposing `All-Angles Bench`, the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed to evaluate MLLMs' multi-view understanding. It shifts the focus from purely temporal or single-perspective reasoning to the geometric and semantic alignment of information across diverse camera angles. This work is pivotal because an embodied agent needs to synthesize information from various observations to build a coherent 3D mental model of its environment, identify objects consistently, estimate distances, and predict trajectories. Without strong multi-view understanding, MLLMs acting as embodied agents are prone to errors in navigation, object manipulation, and overall scene interaction. By creating a dedicated benchmark and conducting an in-depth analysis of MLLM performance in this specific domain, the research aims to guide the development of MLLMs that are truly capable of robust 3D and 4D reasoning in complex real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: while MLLMs show promise in high-level reasoning for embodied agents, their underlying multi-view understanding—the ability to interpret and reconcile visual information across different camera perspectives—is poorly understood and inadequately evaluated. This deficiency prevents MLLMs from achieving human-level proficiency in tasks requiring robust 3D scene comprehension, such as navigation, manipulation, and interaction in dynamic environments.

To address this, the authors raise two fundamental questions:

1. **Do MLLMs possess the ability to understand multiple viewpoints simultaneously?** This question probes the foundational capacity of current MLLMs to integrate and process information from various visual inputs to form a coherent understanding of a shared scene.
2. **What are the key challenges in MLLMs to gain better multi-view understanding?** Beyond a simple "yes" or "no" to the first question, this objective aims to dissect the specific failure modes and limitations that hinder MLLMs in multi-view reasoning. Identifying these challenges is crucial for guiding future research and development towards more capable and reliable MLLMs.

The primary objective is to comprehensively evaluate and diagnose the multi-view understanding capabilities of MLLMs, thereby pinpointing areas where current models fall short. This diagnosis, supported by a novel benchmark, is intended to highlight the necessity for domain-specific refinements or architectural modules that can imbue MLLMs with stronger multi-view awareness and geometric consistency. Ultimately, the research seeks to bridge the significant gap between current MLLM performance and human-level multi-view understanding, enabling the development of more effective embodied AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is the introduction of **All-Angles Bench**, a novel and meticulously constructed benchmark designed to evaluate multi-view understanding in MLLMs. The methodology involves several key steps:

#### 3.1. Data Collection and Scene Curation

The benchmark is built upon a curated selection of 90 diverse multi-view scenes, sourced from the Ego-Exo4D and EgoHumans datasets. These datasets provide real-world footage captured from multiple, spatially dispersed viewpoints, covering a wide range of activities (e.g., basketball, cooking, music playing) and environments (e.g., offices, gyms, kitchens). Each scene includes footage from at least three viewpoints to ensure sufficient multi-view context. All images were standardized to 796 × 448 pixels.

#### 3.2. Task Category Design

The authors designed six distinct task categories to comprehensively assess different facets of multi-view understanding:
1. **Counting:** Enumerating objects across viewpoints without double-counting or overlooking occluded elements.
2. **Attribute Identification:** Recognizing key properties (e.g., pose, color, shape, orientation) consistently across different viewing perspectives.
3. **Relative Distance:** Estimating object distances relative to camera positions when presented with multiple views.
4. **Relative Direction:** Understanding directional relationships between objects across different views.
5. **Object Manipulation:** Inferring changes in object positions, orientations, or configurations across views following a hypothetical action.
6. **Camera Pose Estimation:** Evaluating the capacity to estimate viewpoint arrangements or scene layouts from multi-view inputs, e.g., sorting views in clockwise order.

#### 3.3. Question Creation and Human Annotation Pipeline

- **Initial Question Generation:** An MLLM (GPT-4o) was initially used to generate three multiple-choice questions per category (except camera pose estimation, which had one question) for each multi-view scene, resulting in over 2,100 Q&A pairs. For counting and camera pose estimation, all available views were used, while other tasks employed two randomly selected viewpoints.
- **Human Annotation and Quality Control:** Eight Ph.D. students specializing in NLP or CV were hired as annotators. They rigorously reviewed, refined, and validated each question, correcting phrasing, ensuring factual accuracy, and annotating the single correct answer from three options. This meticulous manual process took over 300 person-hours, supported by a GUI-based annotation platform for efficiency and consistency. A multi-stage verification process, including sample questions, cross-checking by multiple annotators, and team discussions for resolving ambiguities, ensured high quality.

#### 3.4. Paired-Question Generation for Robustness Evaluation

A unique aspect of All-Angles Bench is the creation of "paired questions." For 85.3% of the initial questions (excluding counting tasks), a semantically equivalent but rephrased or view-altered version was generated. For example, an attribute identification question "Identify person A in View 2, given they are in View 1" would be paired with "Identify person A in View 1, given they are in View 2." This strategy evaluates not just correctness but also an MLLM's *consistency* and genuine understanding across varying perspectives, rather than rote memorization or lucky guesses.

#### 3.5. MLLM Evaluation

- **Models:** 27 representative MLLMs were evaluated, including prominent closed-source models (Gemini-2.0, Claude-3.7, GPT-4o) and open-source models (Deepseek-VL2, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5, Cambrian, LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, OVIS).
- **Setup:** Evaluations were conducted using the VLMEvalKit framework, with a greedy decoding strategy (temperature set to 0) for reproducibility. A post-processing step extracted single-letter answers using Qwen2.5-32B to standardize output.
- **Human Baseline:** A subset of 250 questions was independently answered by two additional Ph.D. students not involved in annotation, serving as a human performance benchmark. Paired data was excluded from this subset, and question order was randomized to prevent bias.

#### 3.6. In-depth Analysis of Failure Modes

Beyond quantitative performance, the authors conducted a qualitative analysis, examining failed questions and tasks. This included:
- **Counting under Partial Visibility:** Investigating MLLM performance when objects are only partially visible across multiple views, compared to complete visibility in one view.
- **Reasoning Injection (CoT):** Testing if Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategies (Zero-Shot CoT, Self-Consistency, and a novel Identification CoT) could improve multi-view counting performance, especially under partial visibility.
- **Visualization of Scene Reconstruction:** Using a visualization prompt (mapping objects and camera poses to a 10x10 grid) to understand how MLLMs internally infer scene layouts and camera orientations, particularly for orientation-sensitive tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of 27 MLLMs on All-Angles Bench revealed several critical findings:

#### 4.1. Substantial Performance Gap with Human Level

Across all six task categories, a significant performance gap persists between even the most advanced MLLMs and human evaluators. While human annotators achieved an average accuracy of 82.0% (and as high as 95.7% for relative distance), the top-performing MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash) struggled significantly. For example, in **Camera Pose Estimation**, humans achieved 88.9% accuracy, whereas state-of-the-art MLLMs like Gemini-2.0-Flash, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and InternVL2.5-38B lagged by over 50% margins, with many open-source models performing worse than random guessing. This indicates a fundamental deficiency in MLLMs' geometric and spatial reasoning.

#### 4.2. Surprising Open-Source Model Performance in Orientation-Sensitive Tasks

Interestingly, certain open-source MLLMs, particularly **Ovis2-34B** and **Qwen2.5-VL-72B**, outperformed leading closed-source models (Gemini-2.0 and Claude-3.7-Sonnet) in tasks like **Object Manipulation** and **Relative Direction**. The authors hypothesize that this might be due to the specialized, video-focused training regimes of these open-source models, which emphasize frame-by-frame orientation tracking and spatial grounding. This finding suggests that domain-specific refinements can yield better performance in tasks requiring intricate orientation and geometric reasoning.

#### 4.3. High Inconsistency on Paired Questions

Beyond raw accuracy, the evaluation using "paired questions" highlighted a significant robustness issue: MLLMs often answer semantically equivalent questions inconsistently.
- **GPT-4o** showed notably high inconsistency (around 70%) on **Relative Distance** tasks, suggesting its correct answers might sometimes be accidental or based on superficial cues rather than deep multi-view understanding.
- All models struggled with **Relative Direction**, with inconsistency scores surpassing 40%, underscoring the challenge of reasoning about orientation shifts across views.
- While **Gemini-2.0-Flash** and **Claude-3.7-Sonnet** exhibited more balanced inconsistency across tasks, the overall high inconsistency scores across models indicate that MLLMs often lack a stable, consistent understanding of multi-view scenarios.

#### 4.4. Root Causes of Multi-View Understanding Failures

The in-depth analysis revealed two primary reasons for MLLMs' struggles:

1. **Failure of Multi-View Correspondence for Partially Occluded Views:** MLLMs generally perform well when all objects (e.g., people) are completely visible within a single viewpoint. However, they frequently fail in scenarios where information is fragmented across multiple views (e.g., Person A in View 1, Person B in View 2). Models like GPT-4o sometimes resorted to simply counting objects per view and picking the highest count, failing to reconcile individuals across different perspectives.
 - **Impact of Reasoning Injection (CoT):** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategies were tested. While a novel "Identification CoT" strategy, which explicitly guides the model to describe and cross-reference entities, provided substantial gains (approx. 15% relative improvement for GPT-4o) in partial-visibility counting scenarios, its benefits diminished or even degraded performance for models already robust in multi-view counting (e.g., InternVL2.5-38B). This suggests that linguistic prompting alone is insufficient for deeply embedding multi-view awareness across all models.

2. **Failure with Coarse Camera Pose Estimation:** MLLMs consistently struggled with orientation-sensitive tasks like estimating camera poses, object directions, and tracking trajectories across views. Visualizations showed that models often failed to reconcile viewpoint transformations, misaligning camera coordinates and overlooking crucial background cues. This fundamental inability to accurately estimate camera poses directly impacted performance on related tasks like relative direction and object manipulation, where precise spatial and rotational cues are essential. The models often provided detailed, yet incorrect, reasoning for their camera pose estimations, indicating a deeper lack of geometric understanding rather than a simple failure to retrieve information.

These findings strongly suggest that current MLLMs lack robust internal representations of 3D scene geometry and cross-view consistency, highlighting a need for architectural or training data improvements specifically tailored for multi-view scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Seeing from Another Perspective: Evaluating Multi-View Understanding in MLLMs" carries significant implications for the development and future trajectory of Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly those intended for embodied AI applications.

#### 5.1. Establishing a Critical Benchmark

The most immediate impact is the introduction of **All-Angles Bench**. This benchmark fills a crucial void in the evaluation landscape, moving beyond single-view or purely temporal assessments to rigorously test multi-view understanding—a foundational capability for 3D scene comprehension. By providing over 2,100 human-annotated Q&A pairs across diverse real-world scenes, the benchmark offers a standardized and challenging platform for:
- **Comprehensive Evaluation:** Enabling researchers to systematically assess various aspects of multi-view reasoning, from object counting and attribute identification to complex tasks like object manipulation and camera pose estimation.
- **Driving Future Research:** The benchmark will serve as a valuable tool for tracking progress in MLLM development and for fostering competition to develop models with superior multi-view capabilities. Its public availability (https://danielchyeh.github.io/All-Angles-Bench/) ensures broad accessibility and impact.

#### 5.2. Highlighting Fundamental Limitations of Current MLLMs

The study conclusively demonstrates that current state-of-the-art MLLMs, both closed- and open-source, are far from human-level proficiency in multi-view understanding. The substantial performance gap and high inconsistency scores on paired questions underscore that MLLMs often lack a deep, consistent geometric understanding of scenes across different perspectives. This finding serves as a stark reminder that while MLLMs excel at linguistic reasoning, their visual-spatial intelligence, particularly concerning 3D geometry, remains a significant bottleneck.

#### 5.3. Pinpointing Specific Failure Modes

The detailed analysis of failure cases provides actionable insights for researchers. Identifying that MLLMs struggle with:
- **Cross-view correspondence for partially occluded objects:** Suggests a need for models to develop stronger object permanence and identity tracking mechanisms across views, even when information is fragmented.
- **Coarse camera pose estimation:** Indicates a critical deficiency in inferring spatial relationships between viewpoints, which is fundamental for accurate navigation and interaction in 3D environments. This points to a need for better integration of geometric priors or explicit 3D reconstruction modules.

#### 5.4. Guiding Model Development Strategies

The findings suggest that relying solely on enhanced linguistic reasoning (e.g., Chain-of-Thought prompting) is insufficient to overcome these limitations across all models. Instead, the research strongly advocates for **domain-specific refinements or modules that embed stronger multi-view awareness**. This could involve:
- **Architectural innovations:** Designing MLLM architectures that explicitly incorporate multi-view geometric principles or integrate vision modules capable of 3D reconstruction.
- **Specialized training data:** Curating and leveraging large-scale datasets with explicit multi-view annotations and tasks that force models to learn cross-view consistency.
- **Multi-task learning:** Training MLLMs on a diverse set of tasks that jointly improve both high-level reasoning and low-level geometric understanding from multiple perspectives.

#### 5.5. Advancing Embodied AI

Robust multi-view understanding is indispensable for the development of truly capable embodied agents. Robots and other AI systems operating in the real world must accurately perceive their environment from multiple angles to navigate safely, manipulate objects precisely, and make informed decisions. By exposing the current shortcomings and providing a means to measure progress, this research directly contributes to bridging the gap between current MLLM capabilities and the sophisticated perception required for advanced embodied AI. The impact will be seen in future generations of robots and AI agents that can "see from another perspective" as effectively as humans.
