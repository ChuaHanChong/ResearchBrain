# Seeing from Another Perspective: Evaluating Multi-View Understanding in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, TranscEngram, and collaborating institutions introduced All-Angles Bench, a new comprehensive benchmark to evaluate multi-view understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The study demonstrated that current MLLMs exhibit a substantial performance gap and high inconsistency compared to human performance in tasks requiring 3D geometric reasoning across multiple camera perspectives.

## Problem

- While MLLMs show promise for embodied AI, their ability to interpret and reconcile visual information across multiple, disparate viewpoints for robust 3D scene understanding remains underexplored.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on single-view, egocentric, or temporal reasoning, failing to rigorously assess cross-view correspondence and geometric consistency.
- The lack of robust multi-view understanding prevents MLLMs from achieving human-level proficiency in real-world tasks like navigation and object manipulation, where a coherent 3D mental model is crucial.

## Method

- Developed ==All-Angles Bench==, the first comprehensive benchmark designed specifically to evaluate ==multi-view understanding== in MLLMs using 90 diverse multi-view scenes from real-world datasets.
- Designed six distinct task categories (e.g., Counting, Attribute Identification, Camera Pose Estimation) and generated over 2,100 human-annotated multiple-choice questions, including =='paired questions'== to assess consistency.
- Evaluated 27 representative MLLMs, including leading closed- and open-source models, and conducted in-depth qualitative analysis of failure modes.

## Results

- Human annotators achieved an average accuracy of **82.0%** across tasks, while top MLLMs significantly lagged, with differences exceeding **50%** in tasks like Camera Pose Estimation.
- Certain open-source models (e.g., Ovis2-34B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B) unexpectedly outperformed leading closed-source models in orientation-sensitive tasks like Object Manipulation.
- GPT-4o showed approximately **70%** inconsistency on Relative Distance tasks, and all models struggled with Relative Direction, exhibiting over **40%** inconsistency, highlighting a widespread lack of robust multi-view consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs demonstrate a significant performance gap compared to human-level multi-view understanding, particularly in geometric and spatial reasoning tasks.
> - MLLMs exhibit high inconsistency on semantically equivalent 'paired questions,' indicating a lack of stable and genuine understanding across varying perspectives rather than deep comprehension.
> - Failure modes primarily stem from the inability to establish multi-view correspondence for partially occluded objects and a fundamental lack of accurate coarse camera pose estimation.
