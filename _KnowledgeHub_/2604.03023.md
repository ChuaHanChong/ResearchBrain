# Behavior-Constrained Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Credit Assignment for High-Performance Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from TU Darmstadt and Porsche AG developed a behavior-constrained reinforcement learning framework for high-performance control, utilizing a receding-horizon trajectory predictor and contextual expert behavior modeling. The system achieved competitive lap times in high-fidelity race car simulations while maintaining expert-consistent driving styles, validated through human-grounded evaluations and capable of serving as a reliable digital driver for vehicle setup testing.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) methods often yield high-performance policies but produce non-human-like or unintuitive behaviors, lacking interpretability or safety for real-world applications.
- Imitation learning (IL) produces human-like behaviors but is limited by demonstration data quality and coverage, struggling to adapt to unseen situations or surpass expert performance.
- Balancing task performance with adherence to expert-preferred control strategies in hybrid RL/IL approaches often requires challenging manual tuning of trade-off coefficients.

## Method

- A ==behavior-constrained policy optimization== framework maximizes task performance while enforcing a lower bound on trajectory consistency with expert demonstrations, using an automatically tuned ==Lagrangian multiplier== for the imitation reward.
- A lightweight ==receding-horizon trajectory predictor==, utilizing ==probabilistic Bezier curves==, provides look-ahead rewards to improve ==temporal credit assignment== and near-horizon awareness for the policy.
- ==Trajectory-conditioned expert behavior modeling==, coupled with ==Reference Trajectory Distribution== (RTD)-based data augmentation, allows the policy to represent and learn from a distribution of human-like behaviors.

## Results

- The method achieved a lap time of **66.229 +/- 0.0259** seconds with a Mean Reference Offset (MRO) of **0.2891 +/- 0.0052** meters, demonstrating a favorable balance between performance and expert behavior imitation, outperforming baselines in multi-objective optimization.
- Policies trained with the framework generalized effectively across different race tracks, consistently balancing performance and imitation requirements without the need for re-tuning trade-off coefficients.
- Human-grounded evaluations confirmed that the learned policies exhibited setup-dependent driving characteristics and control strategies consistent with professional driver feedback, proving suitable as 'digital driver models' for virtual vehicle setup testing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly enforcing behavioral constraints during reinforcement learning enables agents to exceed expert performance while maintaining desired human-like characteristics, bridging the gap between raw performance and trustworthiness.
> - Incorporating a short-term trajectory predictor as an auxiliary task and for look-ahead rewards significantly enhances temporal credit assignment, leading to more robust and sophisticated control strategies in dynamic systems.
> - Modeling expert behavior as a distribution rather than a deterministic target, through contextual conditioning and data augmentation, improves the generalization and adaptability of learned policies to varying conditions.
