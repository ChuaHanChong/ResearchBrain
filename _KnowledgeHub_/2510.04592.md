# MobRT: A Digital Twin-Based Framework for Scalable Learning in Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MobRT is a digital-twin simulation framework that autonomously generates whole-body mobile-manipulation demonstrations for articulated-object interaction and mobile-base pick-and-place. It combines annotated assets, virtual kinematic chains, and optimization-based whole-body motion planning, then trains a Transformer flow-matching diffusion policy. Success rises with trajectory count, and co-training 300 simulated with 20 real demos lifts real drawer-opening success from 20% to 60%.

## Problem

- Teleoperation for mobile manipulators is costly and labor-intensive because operators must simultaneously drive the base and the arm, capping dataset scale and diversity.
- Existing simulation data generators target fixed-base tabletop manipulators, offering little support for tasks needing tight base-arm coordination in cluttered scenes.
- Prior mobile pipelines move the base to a preset pose before manipulating, or replay human demos without whole-body control, yielding incoherent motions and replay bias.

## Method

- Digital-twin assets from PartNet-Mobility, UniDoorManip, and AIGC-generated objects, convex-decomposed with ==CoACD== and annotated with 6-DoF grasp poses plus functional axes for placement.
- Action synthesis via functional-axis alignment for rigid-object placing and ==Virtual Kinematic Chains== that map articulated-link pose trajectories to end-effector trajectories, removing hand-coded motions.
- Whole-body trajectory optimization with end-effector, smoothness, and chassis soft-constraint costs, ==TOPP-RA== time parameterization, plus a **Transformer flow-matching diffusion policy** with point-cloud tokenization.

## Results

- Success scales with data: averaged over three tasks, ACT rises **17.8% to 60.0%** from 50 to 200 demos; Container Place with ACT goes **0% to 70%**.
- The proposed policy beats all baselines: RGB variant averages **46.7%/66.7%/76.7%** at 50/100/200 demos, gains of 26.7, 23.4, and 16.7 points respectively.
- Real-world drawer opening improves from **20% to 60%** when 300 simulated trajectories augment 20 real demos; iDP3 improves 10% to 40% under the same mix.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Whole-body planning, rather than sequential base-then-arm execution, is what makes synthetic articulated-object demonstrations coherent enough to train transferable policies.
> - Point-cloud policies relying on max pooling collapse under background clutter; foreground segmentation adds up to 60 points, so tokenizing points preserves spatial detail off tabletops.
> - A small real dataset paired with abundant randomized simulated data buys most of the sim-to-real gain, making few-shot adaptation cheaper than large real collections.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of robotic systems in unstructured environments, such as homes, hospitals, and warehouses, necessitates capabilities beyond fine-grained manipulation. Mobile manipulators, which integrate mobile base locomotion with arm manipulation, are considered suitable for these challenges. Recent advancements in learning-based approaches, particularly imitation learning, have contributed to making such deployments more feasible by enabling robots to acquire complex behaviors from human demonstrations.

However, a critical challenge in imitation learning for robotics is the requirement for large-scale, high-quality demonstration datasets. For mobile manipulation tasks, collecting such data is particularly arduous. Traditional methods often rely on expert teleoperation, which is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and dependent on specific robot hardware. While remote teleoperation using virtual reality devices has been explored to alleviate some practical constraints, it still depends on manual demonstrations, limiting scalability and generalization.

Simulation-based data generation has emerged as an alternative, offering a cost-effective and scalable method for producing extensive datasets. Advances in simulation techniques, including ray tracing and physics engines, have improved the feasibility of sim-to-real transfer, although discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics persist. A notable gap in existing simulation frameworks is their primary focus on fixed-base manipulators and tabletop tasks, with limited support for complex mobile manipulation scenarios that require coordinated whole-body movements.

Previous works on mobile manipulation often simplify tasks by separating base movement from arm manipulation, which can lead to inflexible and unnatural motions. Efforts in demonstration generation have also largely relied on teleoperation or trajectory replay, which may introduce biases or lack whole-body control. For instance, MoMaGen extends data generation to mobile manipulators by replaying human demonstrations but lacks whole-body control, while RoboTwin focuses on dual-arm desktop manipulation without addressing mobile challenges.

This work, MobRT, positions itself to bridge this gap by offering a digital twin-based framework for generating scalable, high-quality demonstration data specifically for mobile manipulators. It aims to support complex whole-body tasks, such as interaction with articulated objects and mobile-base pick-and-place operations, which are under-explored due to data collection difficulties. By integrating virtual kinematic control and whole-body motion planning, MobRT seeks to generate diverse and realistic demonstrations autonomously, thereby facilitating the training of more generalizable manipulation policies. The framework also contributes to the broader field of robot manipulation learning by proposing a new baseline control policy tailored for mobile manipulators, building upon recent advances in generative model-based imitation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for developing MobRT stems from several challenges inherent in deploying mobile manipulators for complex tasks in unstructured environments:

- **Need for General-Purpose Robotic Capabilities:** To perform everyday activities in diverse environments (e.g., opening cabinets, loading dishwashers, organizing clutter), robots require capabilities beyond fine manipulation, integrating reliable object interaction with robust navigation in cluttered, dynamic, and constrained spaces. Mobile manipulators, with their ability to coordinate whole-body movements involving the base, arm, and torso, are considered promising for these tasks.
- **Data Scarcity for Learning-Based Approaches:** While learning-based methods, particularly imitation learning, have shown success in robotic control, they critically depend on large-scale, high-quality demonstration datasets. This data requirement is particularly challenging for mobile manipulation tasks.
- **Limitations of Traditional Data Collection Methods:**
 - **Teleoperation:** Expert teleoperation is the traditional method but is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and often requires robot-specific hardware.
 - **Remote Teleoperation (VR):** While improving accessibility, remote teleoperation via virtual reality devices still relies on manual demonstrations, inherently limiting scalability and diversity.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Simulation Frameworks for Mobile Manipulation:** Simulation-based data generation offers a scalable alternative, but most existing frameworks primarily cater to fixed-base manipulators and tabletop tasks. They often lack comprehensive support for mobile manipulation scenarios that demand tight coordination between locomotion and manipulation, leading to discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the MobRT framework are:

1. **Develop a Scalable Data Generation and Learning Platform:** To create a flexible framework, MobRT, specifically designed for mobile manipulators that can generate high-fidelity simulations of complex whole-body mobile manipulation scenarios.
2. **Support Diverse Whole-Body Tasks:** To enable the simulation of two primary categories of complex tasks: interaction with articulated objects (e.g., opening doors and drawers) and mobile-base pick-and-place operations.
3. **Autonomous and Realistic Demonstration Generation:** To autonomously generate large-scale, diverse, and realistic demonstrations with smooth and physically consistent execution. This is achieved through the integration of virtual kinematic control and whole-body motion planning.
4. **Establish a Comprehensive Benchmark:** To evaluate the quality and effectiveness of the MobRT-generated dataset by constructing a benchmark and assessing multiple baseline algorithms against it.
5. **Improve Policy Generalization and Performance:** To demonstrate that training with MobRT-generated data, potentially combined with real-world demonstrations, significantly improves policy generalization and performance in both simulated and real-world environments.
6. **Propose a Novel Baseline Control Policy:** To introduce a new baseline control policy, based on a Transformer neural network within the Diffusion Policy framework, that is specifically tailored for mobile manipulators and outperforms existing baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MobRT's methodology is structured around three key components: demonstration generation in simulation, adaptation with real-world demonstrations, and policy training with hybrid data.

#### 3.1. A. Demonstration Generation in Simulation

The simulation component focuses on creating diverse and realistic whole-body mobile manipulation trajectories.

1. **Digital Twin Assets and Annotation:**
 - **Asset Sourcing:** Leverages existing datasets such as PartNet-Mobility and UniDoorManip for articulated objects, and RoboTwin-OD for rigid-body objects. An Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC)-based approach is used to create digital twin assets for experimental objects.
 - **Physical Consistency:** Assets are processed using CoACD for convex decomposition to ensure physically consistent interactions.
 - **Annotation:** Each object is annotated with a 6-DoF grasp pose. Rigid-body objects like containers also receive a functional axis to indicate their intended use (e.g., placement direction).
 - **Trajectory Synthesis:** These annotations, combined with Virtual Kinematic Chains (VKC) and predefined motion primitives, are used to synthesize diverse demonstration trajectories reflecting realistic manipulation behaviors.

2. **Generating Manipulating Actions:**
 - **Rigid-Body Object Interaction:** For tasks like placing a container onto a plate, the problem is simplified by aligning the annotated functional axes of the two objects.
 - **Articulated Component Manipulation:** For tasks involving articulated objects (e.g., opening a drawer), the Virtual Kinematic Chains (VKC) framework is employed.
 - When the robot's end-effector (EE) reaches a target pose, the gripper closes to establish a rigid connection with the articulated link.
 - The simulator provides access to the articulation's kinematic model.
 - A sequence of intermediate joint values is sampled from the current state to the desired target.
 - The corresponding pose trajectory of the articulated link is computed using the kinematic model.
 - This link trajectory is then mapped to an end-effector pose trajectory, effectively reducing articulated manipulation to an EE trajectory-tracking problem.
 - This process allows both pick-and-place and articulated object interaction tasks to be represented as sequences of key end-effector poses.

3. **Whole-Body Motion Planning:**
 - **Coordinated Control:** To achieve coherent and physically consistent manipulation, integrated whole-body motion planning is used to coordinate both the mobile base and the robotic arm.
 - **Optimization Formulation:** A whole-body trajectory `x[1, T]` is computed to move the end-effector to the target pose `T_eef` by minimizing an objective function. This function considers the end-effector goal pose `C_eef`, control effort `C_smooth`, and compliance to chassis soft constraints `C_base`. Chassis constraints can include maintaining a fixed orientation or minimizing collision costs.
 - **Trajectory Post-Processing:** The computed trajectories are further processed using the Time-Optimal Path Parameterization (TOPP-RA) algorithm to generate time-optimal paths that adhere to joint velocity and acceleration limits, ensuring dynamic feasibility and continuity.
 - **Motion Primitives:** The framework also supports separate trajectory generation for the chassis and arm, forming a set of motion primitives (including gripper actions) that can be composed for diverse task workflows.

4. **Environment Reset:**
 - **Trajectory Validation:** Before saving data, each generated trajectory is validated. For pick-and-place, the distance between objects is checked; for articulated objects, the target joint angle is verified. This filters out unsuccessful trajectories.
 - **Randomization:** The environment reset involves extensive randomization to ensure data diversity and improve learning robustness. This includes randomizing object position, orientation, and size; initial robotic arm configuration; textures of the ground and objects; and lighting conditions (using textures from external datasets).

#### 3.2. B. Policy Learning

After data collection, the framework focuses on distilling the trajectories into a robust policy.

1. **Flow Matching for Action Generation:**
 - **Diffusion Model Variant:** MobRT adopts Flow Matching, an optimal transport theory-based variant of diffusion models. This method learns a time-dependent vector field to transport samples from noise to expert actions more efficiently than traditional diffusion models, enabling faster and more stable inference.
 - **Action Chunking:** To improve temporal consistency, Action Chunking, as proposed in prior work, is applied.
 - **Interpolation and Loss:** Given an action chunk `A` and Gaussian noise `Z`, interpolated samples `X_τ = τA + (1 − τ)Z` are constructed. A policy `π_θ` is trained to match the optimal flow by minimizing `L_FM = E_τ,Z,A ||v(τ, X_t, o_t) − (A − Z)||^2`.
 - This formulation allows for training with 100 diffusion steps but performing inference with only 10 steps without performance degradation.

2. **Architecture Details:**
 - **Multi-modal Encoder:** The policy model utilizes a multi-modal encoder and a Transformer-based decoder.
 - **RGB Observations:** Processed using a pre-trained ResNet-18 backbone.
 - **Point Cloud Inputs:** Outputs from different cameras are transformed into a unified coordinate frame, fused, and then jointly encoded using a shared PointEncoder.
 - **Proprioceptive State and Noisy Actions:** Encoded through separate MLPs.
 - **Transformer-based Decoder:** Follows an architecture that alternates between self-attention and cross-attention. Cross-attention handles interactions with multi-modal encoder outputs, while self-attention smooths the action sequence.
 - **Timestep Integration:** The flow time `τ` is mapped into timestep embeddings and integrated via AdaNorm.

3. **Co-training with Real-world Data:**
 - **Addressing Sim-to-Real Gap:** To mitigate discrepancies between simulated and real-world robot systems (e.g., dynamic behaviors, sensor noise, actuation delays), the synthetic data is augmented with a small amount of real-world demonstrations.
 - **Combined Training Objective:** The policy for a task `m` is trained using a combined objective that samples from both real-world demonstrations `D_m_real` and simulated demonstrations `D_m_sim` with equal probability, minimizing the Flow Matching loss for both.
 - **Training Details:** Policies are trained on an RTX A6000 GPU with a batch size of 64 for 100K steps. An Adam optimizer with weight decay is used, employing a linear warmup learning rate schedule followed by cosine decay. Gradient clipping is also applied.
 - **Real-World Setup:** The real-world platform consists of a Galaxea A1 robotic arm on a Ranger Mini 3 mobile base, equipped with three Intel RealSense D435i cameras for visual input. Camera depth output is perturbed to simulate real-world sensor noise, which is then mitigated by voxel downsampling and statistical outlier removal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated MobRT's effectiveness in two primary areas: the quality of its generated data for benchmarking and the performance of its proposed policy.

#### 4.1. A. Evaluation of MobRT Benchmark

1. **Baseline Performance:**
 - Four baseline algorithms (ACT, DP, DP3, iDP3) were evaluated across three tasks: "Open Cabinet Drawer," "Container Place," and "Open Dish Washer."
 - Policies were trained with varying amounts of expert data (50, 100, 200 demonstration sets), with 30 rollouts per condition.
 - **Result:** An increase in expert demonstrations consistently led to enhanced performance across most tasks and algorithms. For instance, ACT's success rate on "Open Cabinet Drawer" improved from 23.3% with 50 demonstrations to 60.0% with 200 demonstrations. Similarly, for "Container Place," ACT improved from 0.0% to 70.0%. This indicates a positive correlation between the volume of MobRT-generated expert data and task success rates, validating the utility of the generated data for mobile manipulation.
 - **Exception:** DP3 and iDP3 showed limited improvement or even degradation in the "Container Place" task, which was further investigated.

2. **Performance of Our Algorithm:**
 - The proposed algorithm was evaluated under the same conditions.
 - **Result:** MobRT's RGB-based and Point Cloud-based variants demonstrated consistently higher success rates compared to all baselines across all tasks and data regimes. For example, in the "Open Dish Washer" task with 50 demonstrations, the best baseline achieved 30% success, while MobRT's RGB-based method reached 60%. In the "Container Place" task with 100 demonstrations, the point cloud-based variant improved from the baseline's 20% to 76.7%. These performance gains were particularly evident in low-data regimes, suggesting robustness and data efficiency.
 - This confirms that MobRT-generated data is sufficiently diverse for robust policy learning and that the proposed algorithms consistently outperform existing baselines for complex mobile manipulation tasks.

3. **Impact of Point Cloud Preprocessing:**
 - The previous observation regarding the suboptimal performance of DP3 and iDP3 on "Container Place" was attributed to their sensitivity to background noise and clutter, which often restricts their effectiveness to tabletop scenarios.
 - **Result:** When DP3 and iDP3 were augmented with foreground segmentation (DP3* and iDP3*), their success rates on the "Container Place" task significantly improved (e.g., DP3 from 0.0% to 60.0% with 100 demos). This highlights the critical role of robust point cloud processing for mobile manipulation in complex environments.
 - The proposed MobRT point cloud-based policy tokenizes point cloud data instead of relying on max pooling (used by DP3 and iDP3), which helps preserve spatial details and contributes to its robust performance across diverse environments.

#### 4.2. B. Evaluation of Mixed Data Co-Training

To assess MobRT's effectiveness in improving real-world policy robustness, experiments were conducted on the "Open Cabinet Drawer" task using a physical mobile manipulator.

- **Setup:** Policies were trained using iDP3 and MobRT's point cloud-based algorithm as backbones. Two training conditions were compared: (1) 20 real-world demonstrations only, and (2) 20 real-world demonstrations augmented with 300 MobRT-generated synthetic trajectories.
- **Result:** Co-training with a small number of real demonstrations combined with MobRT-generated synthetic trajectories led to marked performance improvements. For iDP3, the success rate increased from 10.0% (real only) to 40.0% (real + simulated). For MobRT's point cloud-based algorithm, it improved from 20.0% (real only) to 60.0% (real + simulated).
- **Explanation:** These improvements are attributed to MobRT's modeling of real-world sensing conditions and extensive domain randomization during data generation, which collectively enhance policy robustness and mitigate overfitting to simulation-specific artifacts.
- **Adaptability:** The trained policies demonstrated adaptability to variations in environmental lighting and changes in cabinet drawer height in real-world execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MobRT addresses a fundamental challenge in the field of robotics: the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality demonstration data for mobile manipulation tasks. By providing a digital twin-based framework, this work offers a scalable and cost-effective method for autonomously generating diverse and realistic whole-body mobile manipulation trajectories.

The significance of MobRT includes:

- **Enabling Scalable Data Generation:** It provides a solution for generating high-fidelity simulation data for complex mobile manipulation tasks, overcoming the limitations of manual teleoperation in terms of scalability, cost, and diversity.
- **Advancing Mobile Manipulation:** By specifically focusing on coordinated whole-body movements for tasks like articulated object interaction and mobile-base pick-and-place, MobRT contributes to making mobile manipulators more capable in unstructured, human-centric environments.
- **Establishing a Comprehensive Benchmark:** The framework includes the creation of a benchmark dataset and evaluation methodology, providing a standardized platform for comparing and improving learning algorithms for mobile manipulation.
- **Improved Policy Learning and Generalization:** The experiments demonstrate that MobRT-generated data effectively supports policy learning, leading to improved generalization and higher success rates compared to existing baselines. The proposed Transformer-based diffusion policy also offers a robust and data-efficient approach tailored for mobile manipulators.
- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By incorporating sensor noise modeling and extensive domain randomization during data generation, MobRT facilitates more robust sim-to-real transfer, evidenced by the improved performance of policies co-trained with mixed simulated and real-world data. This directly addresses a common hurdle in deploying learning-based robotic systems.

The potential impact of MobRT is considerable, as it could accelerate the development and deployment of mobile manipulators in various real-world applications, including logistics, assistive robotics in homes and hospitals, and general service robotics. By making data generation more accessible and effective, it lowers the barrier for training complex robotic skills, paving the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems. Future work, including expanding the range and horizon of tasks and integrating reinforcement learning, suggests further potential for enhancing robustness and adaptability in real-world scenarios.
