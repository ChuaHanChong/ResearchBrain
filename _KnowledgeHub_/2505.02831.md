# No Other Representation Component Is Needed: Diffusion Transformers Can Provide Representation Guidance by Themselves

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Representation Alignment (SRA) introduces a method for diffusion transformers to leverage their own internal representations for guidance, eliminating the need for external components to accelerate training and improve generation quality. The approach consistently improves FID scores and training convergence, outperforming or matching methods that use external guidance.

## Problem

- Existing methods for representation guidance in diffusion transformers rely on auxiliary external tasks or large pre-trained representation encoders, increasing computational overhead and external dependencies.
- The reliance on external components limits the applicability and resource efficiency of training advanced diffusion models, especially for modalities lacking robust external encoders.
- A key challenge is enhancing generative model training convergence and output quality without introducing additional complex external supervisory signals.

## Method

- ==Self-Representation Alignment (SRA)== employs a ==self-distillation mechanism== where a diffusion transformer guides its own representation learning via a student-teacher setup with an EMA-updated teacher model.
- It aligns 'weaker' latent representations from the student's earlier layers/higher noise levels with 'better' representations from the teacher's later layers/lower noise levels, using a lightweight projection head and a ==patch-wise smooth-ℓ1 loss==.
- The self-alignment loss is combined with the standard generative loss, allowing the model to learn enhanced representations intrinsically during the training process without external models or tasks.

## Results

- SRA consistently improves generation quality, achieving lower FID and higher IS scores across various DiT and SiT models (B/2, L/2, XL/2) on ImageNet and COCO2014 datasets.
- The method substantially accelerates training convergence, with SRA-equipped SiT-XL/2 outperforming the original SiT-XL model with **1000 fewer epochs** on ImageNet 256x256 while achieving FID **1.58**.
- SRA achieves performance comparable to or superior to state-of-the-art methods relying on external pre-trained encoders (e.g., REPA) while eliminating all external representation components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion transformers inherently learn a 'bad-to-good' discriminative hierarchy of latent representations, with features becoming more refined and discriminative in later layers and at lower noise levels.
> - This intrinsic representation progression can be effectively harnessed for self-supervision, providing valuable guidance internally without the need for external components or pre-trained models.
> - The EMA-updated teacher model consistently maintains a higher quality of representation, ensuring stable and effective guidance to the student model throughout the training process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of generative AI, specifically diffusion models, and advanced deep learning architectures, particularly Transformers. Over recent years, diffusion models have emerged as a dominant paradigm for high-fidelity image synthesis, demonstrating superior performance over earlier generative adversarial networks (GANs). A significant development within this field has been the transition from U-Net-based architectures to Transformer-based models, such as Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) and Scalable Interpolant Transformers (SiTs), which leverage the inherent scalability and expressive power of transformers for improved performance and efficiency.

A key challenge and ongoing area of research in generative model training is accelerating convergence and enhancing generation quality. Recent studies have demonstrated that incorporating "representation guidance" can significantly aid this process. These existing approaches generally fall into two categories:
1. **External Representation Tasks**: Methods like MaskDiT and SD-DiT introduce auxiliary discriminative losses (e.g., from Masked Autoencoders like MAE or self-supervised methods like iBOT) alongside the generative loss. These tasks force the diffusion model to learn meaningful internal representations.
2. **External Pre-trained Representation Encoders**: Approaches such as REPA leverage powerful, large-scale pre-trained vision models (e.g., DINOv2, CLIP) to provide supervision signals, aligning the diffusion model's latent features with those of a robust external encoder.

The current work by Jiang et al. innovates by addressing a fundamental limitation of these existing methods: their reliance on *external* components. The paper posits that the generative mechanism of diffusion transformers inherently involves a "bad-to-good" process of refining noisy inputs into clean data, suggesting that valuable representation guidance might be extractable *internally*. By demonstrating that diffusion transformers can provide their own representation guidance, this research pioneers a novel self-sufficient training paradigm. It connects to the broader theme of self-supervised learning (SSL) where models learn meaningful representations from the data itself, but specifically applies this to the context of generative model training, circumventing the need for external supervisory signals or pre-trained models. This positions SRA as a more resource-efficient and universally applicable method, particularly in domains where powerful external encoders are scarce, such as open-domain video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the limitations of current representation guidance strategies for diffusion transformers. While it has been shown that learning high-quality internal representations can significantly accelerate generative training and improve output quality, existing techniques suffer from dependencies:
- They either require the introduction of an auxiliary, often computationally expensive, external representation task.
- Or, they necessitate the availability and integration of a large-scale, pre-trained external representation encoder, which might not always exist for all data modalities or can be prohibitively resource-intensive to leverage.

This dependency poses a significant question: **Can representation guidance be obtained without any external representation components?**

The authors hypothesize that the unique generative process within diffusion transformers, which gradually transforms noisy latents into clearer ones, inherently follows a "bad-to-good" discriminative trend. Their empirical observations validate this hypothesis, showing that latent features within DiTs and SiTs are progressively refined and learn meaningful discriminative representations as they pass through more layers and are conditioned on less noise.

Given this insight, the **key objective** of this work is to develop a simple yet effective method, Self-Representation Alignment (SRA), that capitalizes on these intrinsic properties. SRA aims to:
1. Align "weaker" internal latent representations (from earlier layers or higher noise levels) to "better" internal latent representations (from later layers or lower noise levels) *within the same diffusion transformer*.
2. Achieve this self-alignment solely during the generative training process, without introducing external tasks or pre-trained models.
3. Ultimately, enhance the overall representation learning of the model, leading to accelerated training convergence and improved generation performance.

By achieving these objectives, SRA seeks to provide a flexible and resource-efficient way to integrate representation guidance, making the training of high-performance diffusion transformers more accessible and less reliant on external factors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, Self-Representation Alignment (SRA), revolves around leveraging the inherent "bad-to-good" discriminative process observed in diffusion transformers during their generative operation. The methodology is straightforward yet powerful:

**Empirical Observation as Foundation:**
The authors first conducted an empirical analysis on existing DiT and SiT models. Using PCA visualization and linear probing on ImageNet, they confirmed that latent features within these models show a clear progression from "bad" (less discriminative) to "good" (more discriminative) as the block layers increase and the noise level conditioned on the input decreases. This observation forms the bedrock of SRA, indicating that the generative model naturally learns a hierarchy of representations.

**Self-Representation Alignment (SRA) Mechanism:**
SRA proposes a self-distillation-like mechanism where the diffusion transformer guides its own representation learning. It involves:

1. **Student-Teacher Architecture**:
 - **Student Model (`f`)**: This is the primary trainable diffusion transformer (e.g., DiT or SiT).
 - **Teacher Model (`f*`)**: This model shares the same architecture as the student but its weights are updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's weights. The EMA update rule, `ζ_t = αζ_t + (1 - α)ζ_s`, where `α` is the momentum coefficient (set to 0.9999), ensures that the teacher provides a more stable and progressively higher-quality target representation. A stop-gradient operator is applied to the teacher's output, preventing gradients from flowing back into the teacher and allowing it to serve as a fixed target for the student for each training step.

2. **Latent Representation Alignment**:
 - **Student Input**: The student model `f` processes a noisy latent input `x_t` conditioned on a higher noise level `t` and a condition `c`. It outputs a latent representation `y = f_m(x_t, t, c)` from an earlier layer `m`.
 - **Teacher Input**: The teacher model `f*` processes a slightly less noisy latent input `x_{t-k}` conditioned on a lower noise level `t-k` (where `k >= 0` and `t-k < t`) and the same condition `c`. It outputs a latent representation `y* = f*_n(x_{t-k}, t-k, c)` from a later layer `n`.
 - **Alignment Strategy**: The core idea is to align the "weaker" student representation `y` with the "better" teacher representation `y*`. The choice of `m <= n` and `k >= 0` ensures that the teacher's target representation is always from a more refined stage of the generative process.

3. **Projection Head**:
 - Before alignment, the student's output `y` is passed through a lightweight trainable MLP head `j_ψ(y)`. This projection head performs a slight nonlinear transformation, which is empirically found to be crucial for better representation extraction and to avoid disrupting the generative capabilities of the diffusion transformer's internal layers. This head can be optionally discarded after training, ensuring no architectural modification to the deployed model.

4. **Loss Function**:
 - **Self-Alignment Loss (`L_sa`)**: The alignment is achieved by minimizing the patch-wise distance between `j_ψ(y)` and `y*` using a smooth-ℓ1 objective.
 - **Combined Objective (`L`)**: The `L_sa` is added to the standard generative loss (`L_gen`, which is `L_simple` for denoise-based models like DiT or `L_velocity` for flow-based models like SiT). A hyperparameter `λ` controls the trade-off: `L = L_gen + λL_sa`.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology is validated through extensive experiments on ImageNet (256x256, 512x512) and COCO2014, using various sizes of DiT and SiT models (B/2, L/2, XL/2) as baselines. Evaluation metrics include FID, sFID, IS, Precision, Recall, and PickScore. A thorough component-wise analysis is performed to determine optimal hyperparameters, such as specific block layers for alignment, time intervals, `λ` values, and the necessity of the projection head. Comparisons are made against a wide range of state-of-the-art diffusion models, including those employing external representation tasks or encoders.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly support the effectiveness and significance of the Self-Representation Alignment (SRA) method:

1. **Confirmation of Internal Representation Trend**: The initial empirical investigation using PCA visualization and linear probing on original SiT-XL/2 (and DiT-XL/2 in the appendix) confirms the core hypothesis. Latent features in diffusion transformers indeed exhibit a "bad-to-good" progression, becoming more discriminative and semantically rich as block layers increase and noise levels decrease. This provides the foundational justification for SRA.

2. **Consistent Performance Improvement**: Applying SRA consistently leads to significant performance improvements across various DiT and SiT models (B/2, L/2, XL/2 sizes). Models trained with SRA achieve lower FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and higher IS (Inception Score), indicating superior generation quality and diversity compared to their vanilla baselines. This improvement is observed on both ImageNet 256x256 and 512x512 datasets.

3. **Accelerated Training Convergence**: SRA substantially accelerates the training process. For instance, SiT-XL/2 with SRA outperforms the original SiT-XL model using 1000 fewer epochs on ImageNet 256x256. Figure 4 clearly illustrates that SRA-equipped models achieve better FID scores at equivalent training times, demonstrating enhanced training efficiency.

4. **Scalability of Benefits**: The benefits of SRA are more pronounced in larger models (e.g., XL/2), suggesting that larger architectures can provide richer and more effective internal guidance. This scalability is a desirable property for state-of-the-art generative models.

5. **Competitive Performance Against External Methods**:
 - SRA largely **outperforms** methods that rely on auxiliary external representation tasks (e.g., MaskDiT) in terms of generation quality.
 - Crucially, SRA achieves performance **comparable to or even superior** to methods that depend on external pre-trained representation encoders (e.g., REPA) while entirely circumventing the need for such external components. For example, SiT-XL + SRA at 800 epochs achieves FID 1.58 and IS 311.4 on ImageNet 256x256, competitive with REPA.

6. **Sustained Performance Boost**: Unlike REPA, which shows quick initial convergence but saturates later, SRA provides a more sustained performance improvement over longer training durations. This is attributed to the teacher model's continually improving capacity, which in turn offers better guidance to the student as training progresses.

7. **Enhanced Internal Representation Capacity**: Linear probing results (Figure 7a, 7b) confirm that SRA genuinely enhances the quality of latent representations learned by the diffusion transformer. The SRA-trained models exhibit better linear probing accuracy across different blocks and timesteps compared to vanilla models.

8. **Tight Correlation between Representation and Generation**: A strong correlation is observed between the linear probing accuracy (a proxy for representation quality) and FID scores (generation quality) when varying teacher alignment layers (Figure 7c). This finding empirically validates that SRA's mechanism of improving internal representations directly translates to better generative capabilities.

9. **Teacher's Continuous Improvement**: The EMA teacher's representation quality consistently improves throughout training and always surpasses that of the student model at each stage (Figure 7d). This ensures that the guidance provided by the teacher remains high-quality and effective over the entire training process.

10. **Optimal Hyperparameter Settings**: Component-wise analysis (Table 1) provided practical insights:
 - Aligning student's earlier layers (e.g., layer 3) with teacher's mid-to-later layers (e.g., layer 8 for small models) yields optimal results.
 - A dynamic time interval for noise reduction (e.g., `[0, 0.2)`) for the teacher's input is effective.
 - The regularization coefficient `λ` around 0.2 provides a good balance.
 - The lightweight projection head on the student's output is critical for effective and stable alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Self-Representation Alignment (SRA) method presents a significant advancement in the training of diffusion transformers, with broad implications for generative AI research and application:

1. **Pioneering Self-Sufficient Guidance**: SRA fundamentally shifts the paradigm of representation guidance in diffusion models. By demonstrating that high-quality guidance can be derived from the model's internal processes alone, it establishes a new avenue for efficient and autonomous learning in generative architectures. This self-sufficiency reduces external dependencies, making the training process more robust and self-contained.

2. **Increased Accessibility and Resource Efficiency**: One of SRA's most impactful contributions is its ability to bypass the need for external, large-scale pre-trained representation models (like DINOv2 or CLIP) or complex auxiliary tasks. This significantly lowers the computational and resource barrier for training advanced diffusion models, especially for researchers or organizations with limited access to vast computational resources or specialized pre-trained models. This is particularly relevant for emerging modalities like open-domain video generation, where strong, publicly available external encoders are currently lacking.

3. **Enhanced Training Efficiency and Performance**: SRA consistently leads to faster training convergence and improved generation quality across various diffusion transformer architectures and sizes. This translates to quicker model development cycles and the ability to achieve state-of-the-art results with fewer computational resources and less training time, accelerating the pace of research and practical deployment.

4. **Deeper Understanding of Diffusion Model Mechanics**: The empirical validation that diffusion transformers intrinsically learn a "bad-to-good" hierarchy of representations, and that this internal progression can be harnessed for self-supervision, offers valuable insights into the learning dynamics of these complex generative models. This understanding can inspire future research into exploiting inherent model properties for various tasks.

5. **Plug-and-Play Adaptability**: The method's simplicity and architectural neutrality (requiring only a lightweight projection head that can be discarded post-training) make SRA highly adaptable. It can be easily integrated into existing and future diffusion transformer designs, including those for text-to-image (as shown with MMDiT) and potentially other modalities like video. This broad applicability ensures its utility across diverse generative tasks.

6. **Robust and Sustained Improvement**: The use of an EMA teacher whose representation quality continuously improves and consistently surpasses the student's ensures that the guidance provided by SRA remains highly effective throughout prolonged training. This sustained enhancement contrasts with methods reliant on static external models, which may saturate or even become detrimental in later training stages.

In summary, SRA represents a significant step towards more autonomous and efficient training of high-performance diffusion transformers. By proving that "no other representation component is needed," the work not only simplifies the current landscape of generative model training but also opens exciting new avenues for exploring the self-supervisory potential embedded within these powerful architectures.
