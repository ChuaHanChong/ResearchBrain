# No Other Representation Component Is Needed: Diffusion Transformers Can Provide Representation Guidance by Themselves

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Representation Alignment (SRA) introduces a method for diffusion transformers to leverage their own internal representations for guidance, eliminating the need for external components to accelerate training and improve generation quality. The approach consistently improves FID scores and training convergence, outperforming or matching methods that use external guidance.

## Problem

- Existing methods for representation guidance in diffusion transformers rely on auxiliary external tasks or large pre-trained representation encoders, increasing computational overhead and external dependencies.
- The reliance on external components limits the applicability and resource efficiency of training advanced diffusion models, especially for modalities lacking robust external encoders.
- A key challenge is enhancing generative model training convergence and output quality without introducing additional complex external supervisory signals.

## Method

- ==Self-Representation Alignment (SRA)== employs a ==self-distillation mechanism== where a diffusion transformer guides its own representation learning via a student-teacher setup with an EMA-updated teacher model.
- It aligns 'weaker' latent representations from the student's earlier layers/higher noise levels with 'better' representations from the teacher's later layers/lower noise levels, using a lightweight projection head and a ==patch-wise smooth-ℓ1 loss==.
- The self-alignment loss is combined with the standard generative loss, allowing the model to learn enhanced representations intrinsically during the training process without external models or tasks.

## Results

- SRA consistently improves generation quality, achieving lower FID and higher IS scores across various DiT and SiT models (B/2, L/2, XL/2) on ImageNet and COCO2014 datasets.
- The method substantially accelerates training convergence, with SRA-equipped SiT-XL/2 outperforming the original SiT-XL model with **1000 fewer epochs** on ImageNet 256x256 while achieving FID **1.58**.
- SRA achieves performance comparable to or superior to state-of-the-art methods relying on external pre-trained encoders (e.g., REPA) while eliminating all external representation components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion transformers inherently learn a 'bad-to-good' discriminative hierarchy of latent representations, with features becoming more refined and discriminative in later layers and at lower noise levels.
> - This intrinsic representation progression can be effectively harnessed for self-supervision, providing valuable guidance internally without the need for external components or pre-trained models.
> - The EMA-updated teacher model consistently maintains a higher quality of representation, ensuring stable and effective guidance to the student model throughout the training process.
