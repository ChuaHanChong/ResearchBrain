# IntervenGen: Interventional Data Generation for Robust and Data-Efficient Robot Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new data generation system, IntervenGen, autonomously creates diverse corrective interventions for robot imitation learning from a small set of human examples. This method improves policy robustness to perception errors, enabling up to a 39x increase in task success rates and successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with minimal human input.

## Problem

- Robot imitation learning policies exhibit brittleness and poor generalization due to distribution shift, particularly from perception errors in real-world environments.
- Interactive Imitation Learning (IIL), while effective for robustness, demands extensive and continuous human supervision to provide corrective interventions.
- Generating a sufficiently diverse dataset of recovery behaviors to cover the wide spectrum of potential policy failures with human effort alone is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Method

- Introduce **IntervenGen** (I-Gen), a system that autonomously generates diverse corrective intervention trajectories from a small seed of human-provided interventions.
- Employ closed-loop policy execution to actively generate genuine policy mistakes under varied, corrupted environmental observations.
- Adapt and replay source human recovery segments via object-centric transformations to guide the robot from generated mistake states back to task completion.

## Results

- Policies trained with IntervenGen achieved up to a **39x** increase in success rate over base policies across various tasks, utilizing only 10 initial human interventions.
- IntervenGen consistently outperformed policies trained on either equivalent amounts of full human demonstrations or MimicGen-generated full demonstrations, by **34%** to **68%**.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for a block grasping task on a physical Franka robot, achieving **90%** success for robust policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automating the generation of corrective interventional data is more impactful for policy robustness to distribution shift than simply expanding datasets of successful task demonstrations.
> - The use of closed-loop policy execution to induce and identify genuine policy mistakes in varied, corrupted environments is essential for producing relevant and effective recovery data.
> - Achieving robustness to simulated perception errors through intervention data allows for successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for state-based policies, mitigating reliance on perfect real-world sensing.
