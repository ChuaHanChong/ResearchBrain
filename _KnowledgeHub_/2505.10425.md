# Learning to Think: Information-Theoretic Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The Learning to Think (L2T) framework fine-unes large language models to achieve higher reasoning accuracy with significantly fewer tokens. It introduces a universal, information-theoretic dense process reward that guides models to adaptively optimize reasoning depth.

## Problem

- Existing LLMs fine-tuned with outcome-based rewards tend to generate excessively long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) sequences, leading to wasted computational resources and reduced efficiency.
- Reinforcement learning approaches for LLMs primarily rely on sparse, final-outcome rewards, which fail to provide specific, dense feedback on the quality or efficiency of intermediate reasoning steps.
- Prior attempts at process-based rewards often require task-specific evaluators or expensive human annotations, limiting their generalizability and scalability across diverse tasks.

## Method

- Reformulates LLM fine-tuning as a ==hierarchical, episodic reinforcement learning== problem, segmenting the reasoning process into multiple discrete episodes.
- Introduces a novel ==information-theoretic dense process reward== for each episode, which quantifies the fitting information gain (reduction in answer uncertainty) and includes a parameter compression penalty to discourage redundant information.
- Employs efficient approximation techniques, such as ==low-rank parameter proxies== and ==Fisher Information Matrix== estimation, to make the computation of the dense reward feasible for large-scale LLMs.

## Results

- Achieved an average accuracy gain of approximately **3.7%** in mathematical reasoning tasks (AIME, AMC, MATH500, MinervaMATH) compared to outcome-reward-based methods.
- Improved token efficiency by roughly **2x** compared to outcome-reward approaches and approximately **1.3x** compared to other process-reward baselines, using fewer tokens to reach comparable or higher performance.
- Demonstrated consistent performance improvements across various base models (**1.5B** to **7B** parameters) and diverse task types, including mathematical reasoning and code generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a dense, internally derived information-theoretic reward enables LLMs to learn both effective and efficient reasoning, balancing accuracy with token consumption.
> - Quantifying the information gain in model parameters and penalizing redundant parameter updates at each step effectively guides the model to learn parsimonious and relevant reasoning.
> - Decomposing complex reasoning into a sequence of episodic decisions provides granular control and feedback, allowing LLMs to adapt their reasoning depth dynamically to task requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, a critical area where LLMs demonstrate complex problem-solving capabilities across diverse tasks, from code generation to mathematical problem-solving. A key advancement in this area has been the development of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and test-time compute scaling, often coupled with reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, to achieve state-of-the-art results. These methods typically encourage LLMs to generate explicit intermediate reasoning steps, allowing them to explore solution spaces more thoroughly and improve final answer accuracy.

However, the authors identify a significant limitation: existing approaches, particularly those relying on sparse, final-outcome rewards for policy optimization, often overlook the crucial trade-off between reasoning effectiveness and efficiency. Because delayed feedback doesn't penalize unnecessary reasoning steps, LLMs tend to generate excessively long CoT chains. This "one more thought" phenomenon, as the authors term it, leads to:
1. **Wasted computational resources:** Consuming more tokens than necessary, increasing inference costs and latency.
2. **Degraded effectiveness:** On simpler tasks, overly long reasoning chains can sometimes introduce errors or dilute attention, paradoxically reducing accuracy.
3. **Lack of adaptability:** Optimal reasoning chain length varies significantly with task difficulty, but outcome-reward models struggle to adaptively manage this.

The current work directly addresses these gaps by proposing a novel information-theoretic reinforcement fine-tuning framework, Learning to Think (L2T). It differentiates itself from existing solutions in several key ways:
- **Beyond Outcome Rewards:** Unlike methods relying solely on final outcome rewards (e.g., GRPO), L2T introduces a dense, step-wise process reward to provide immediate feedback on intermediate reasoning steps.
- **Generality over Task-Specific Rewards:** While some concurrent works explore process rewards, heuristic scoring, or length penalties (e.g., ReST-MCTS, MRT), these often require task-specific evaluators or expensive human annotations, limiting their reusability and scalability. L2T, in contrast, proposes a *universal* information-theoretic process reward derived from *internal model signals*, requiring no external annotations or specialized evaluators, thus enhancing generality across tasks.
- **Balancing Effectiveness and Efficiency:** L2T explicitly optimizes for both accuracy and token efficiency by rewarding meaningful information gain at each step while penalizing redundant updates, a balance often missing in prior works that focus primarily on accuracy through increased computation.
- **Robustness through Compression:** The introduction of a parameter compression penalty, inspired by information bottleneck principles, helps prevent overfitting and makes the learned policy more robust to noisy or ambiguous inputs by discouraging the absorption of redundant information.

By reframing LLM fine-tuning as an episodic RL problem with a novel, internally-derived dense reward, L2T aims to push the boundaries of efficient and effective reasoning in LLMs, providing a more adaptive and resource-aware approach compared to current state-of-the-art methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to achieve optimal reasoning performance with fewer tokens, thereby striking a better balance between reasoning effectiveness and efficiency. The authors encapsulate this goal as "Learning to Think" – teaching LLMs to reason more intelligently and parsimoniously.

The key motivations for pursuing this objective stem from critical limitations observed in existing LLM reasoning paradigms:

1. **Inefficient Token Consumption:** Current state-of-the-art LLMs, particularly those fine-tuned with reinforcement learning based on sparse outcome rewards, tend to generate unnecessarily long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning sequences. This behavior, driven by the fact that even minimal accuracy gains from extended chains are rewarded without cost, leads to significant token waste. Empirical evidence presented in the paper (Figure 1 and Subsection 3.2) strongly supports this, showing that existing methods consume more than twice the minimum tokens required for a correct answer and that accuracy can even decline with excessive reasoning depth.
2. **Lack of Intermediate Feedback:** Most RL-based fine-tuning methods rely on a binary reward signal received only at the final outcome (i.e., whether the answer is correct or not). This delayed and sparse feedback provides no specific guidance on the quality or efficiency of intermediate reasoning steps, making it difficult for the model to learn to optimize its internal thought process.
3. **Task-Specificity of Existing Process Rewards:** While some researchers have begun exploring "process rewards" to guide intermediate steps, these often depend on task-specific evaluators, manually designed high-quality process labels, or length penalties. Such approaches are expensive to develop, difficult to scale across diverse tasks, and lack generalizability. The varying optimal chain lengths for different task difficulties further highlight the need for a universally applicable solution.
4. **Resource Constraints and Real-world Applicability:** In practical deployment scenarios, especially for real-time applications or edge devices, token budgets are often limited. The inefficiency of current LLM reasoning poses a significant challenge to their broader adoption and cost-effectiveness.

To address these motivations, L2T aims to design an effective, dense process reward that quantifies the contribution of each reasoning step, allowing the model to adaptively allocate reasoning depth. This reward must be:
- **Relevant:** Accurately measure progress towards a correct solution.
- **Efficient:** Be cheap to compute in the context of large models.
- **General:** Apply uniformly across tasks without requiring bespoke engineering or additional annotations.

By achieving these aims, L2T intends to enable LLMs to learn not just *what* to think to be correct, but also *how much* to think to be efficient, thus making them more powerful and practical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Learning to Think (L2T) framework introduces an information-theoretic reinforcement fine-tuning approach structured around three key stages to optimize LLMs for both reasoning effectiveness and efficiency.

#### 3.1. Problem Reformulation

L2T reconfigures the standard LLM fine-tuning problem from a single-episode Reinforcement Learning (RL) task into a **hierarchical, episodic RL problem**. Instead of treating each question-answer interaction as one long process, it decomposes it into a sequence of *multiple episodes*.
- **Question as a Task:** Each question `x` is considered a distinct reasoning task.
- **Episode Segmentation:** The LLM's generated output `z_0:T` for a given `x` is segmented into `K` consecutive reasoning episodes using special markers (e.g., `<think>... </think>`). Each episode represents a segment of the reasoning chain.
- **State and Action:** At episode `k`, the state `s_k` comprises the initial question `x` and the partial reasoning prefix `z_1:k-1`. The action `z_k` is the token sequence generated by the policy `π_θ` for that episode.
- **Hybrid Reward Structure:** The reward for the entire interaction combines:
 - A **sparse outcome reward `r_out`**: A binary (0 or 1) signal indicating the correctness of the final answer `z_1:K`.
 - A **dense process reward `r_prg_k`**: Calculated for each intermediate episode `k`, quantifying its contribution to answer correctness probability. This is evaluated at the episode level (not per token) to reduce variance and computational cost.

The objective is to maximize the cumulative reward (outcome plus sum of dense process rewards) while adhering to a fixed token budget `B_token`. This episodic breakdown allows for granular feedback and optimization, guiding the model to make each step count.

#### 3.2. Learning Information-Theoretic Dense Process Reward

The core innovation of L2T lies in its novel information-theoretic dense process reward, designed to be universal, efficient, and relevant. This reward quantifies the *information gain in model parameters* for each episode, consisting of two main components:

1. **Fitting Information Gain:** This term encourages the model to acquire correctness-critical information at each step. It measures the reduction in uncertainty about the correct answer `Y` given the input `X` provided by the model parameters `θ`. Formally, it relates to the change in conditional mutual information `∆I_k = I(θ_k; Y|X) - I(θ_{k-1}; Y|X)`. Due to the high computational cost of direct mutual information calculation in LLMs, this is approximated by the increase in the model's predicted correctness probability: `J_r(π_θ(·|s_k, z_k)) - J_r(π_θ(·|s_k))`. This approximation requires only two forward passes of the model per episode, significantly reducing overhead.

2. **Parameter Compression Penalty:** This term penalizes redundant information absorption, ensuring efficiency and preventing excessive updates or overfitting. It quantifies the mutual information increment between the model parameters `θ` and the historical context `s_k` (i.e., `I(θ_k; s_k) - I(θ_{k-1}; s_{k-1})`). A larger increment indicates that the model has absorbed more task-specific "idiosyncrasies," which might be redundant or lead to overfitting.

The formal definition of the dense process reward for episode `k` is:
`r_prg_k = [J_r(π_θ(·|s_k, z_k)) - J_r(π_θ(·|s_k))] - β * [I(θ_k; s_k) - I(θ_{k-1}; s_{k-1})]`
where `β > 0` is a hyperparameter balancing the two terms.

**Efficient Computation of the Compression Penalty:** Directly computing the parameter compression penalty (mutual information increment) in the high-dimensional parameter space of LLMs is intractable. L2T proposes an efficient approximation using:
- **Low-rank parameter proxy (`~θ`):** The full parameter space `θ` is projected into a much smaller `r`-dimensional subspace (where `r << d`, `d` being the original dimension) using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). This reduces the dimensionality of the problem.
- **Fisher Information Matrix (FIM):** Assuming the low-rank proxy parameters follow a Gaussian distribution, the inverse of the prior covariance matrix (`Σ_0^-1`) can be approximated by the Fisher information matrix `F_θ` which is the expectation of the outer product of the gradients of the log-likelihood.
- **Taylor Expansion:** The mutual information increment is approximated using a second-order Taylor expansion involving the FIM.

This approximation (Theorem 4.2) significantly reduces computational complexity from `Θ(d^2)` or `Θ(d^3)` to `Θ(dr^2 + r^3)`, and is theoretically guaranteed to have bounded approximation error (Theorem D.3), making the dense reward practical for LLMs.

#### 3.3. Optimizing LLM with Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

L2T optimizes the LLM (policy `π_θ`) by maximizing a combined objective function:
`max_θ E_{x~D_train} E_{z_1:K~π_θ(·|s_k)} [r(x, z_1:K) + α * Σ_{k=1 to K} r_prg_k]`
where `α` is an importance weight for the process reward.

**Practical Implementation with GRPO:** L2T is implemented on top of the Group-level Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm for stable and efficient reinforcement optimization.
- **Rollouts and Rewards:** For each query, `N` reasoning rollouts are generated using the old policy (`π_θ_old`). Each rollout is segmented into episodes, and both `r_out` and `r_prg_k` are calculated.
- **Token-level Advantage:** The per-episode reward `R_i,k` is then distributed to individual tokens within that episode using log-probability surprise as weights. These token-level rewards `r_i,t` are aggregated, truncated (e.g., 95th percentile mean), and normalized to yield group-level advantages `A_i`. These advantages are finally rescaled back to token-level advantages `A_i,t`.
- **Policy Update:** The `A_i,t` values are used in GRPO's clipped policy gradient objective, along with a KL penalty, to update `π_θ`, ensuring continuous learning while maintaining training stability.

This holistic approach allows L2T to leverage the strengths of episodic RL and information theory to guide LLM learning towards generating accurate and concise reasoning, effectively managing token budgets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of L2T demonstrates its significant advantages in both reasoning effectiveness and efficiency across diverse benchmarks and base models.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness and Efficiency Analysis

- **Superior Performance:** L2T consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24-25, AMC, MATH500, MinervaMATH) and code generation tasks (HumanEval).
 - **Accuracy Boost:** Compared to outcome-reward-based methods (e.g., GRPO), L2T delivers an average accuracy gain of approximately 3.7% in mathematical reasoning. When compared to other process-reward or length-penalty baselines (e.g., ReST-MCTS, MRT), L2T still achieves over a 2% accuracy improvement.
 - **Token Efficiency:** L2T roughly doubles token efficiency compared to outcome-reward approaches and increases efficiency by about 1.3x compared to process-reward baselines. This means L2T achieves higher accuracy using significantly fewer tokens.
- **Efficient Use of Test-Time Compute:** Visualizations (Figure 3) show that L2T achieves higher reasoning accuracy under the same token budget. Furthermore, L2T consumes only 18% of the tokens required by the base model, 50% of those used by outcome-reward fine-tuning, and approximately 20% fewer tokens than other process-reward models to reach comparable performance. This highlights L2T's ability to maximize the utility of each token.
- **Generality Across Models and Tasks:** The consistent improvements are observed across different base models (DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, DeepSeekR1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen2-7B-Instruct) and task types (mathematical reasoning, code generation), demonstrating the universal applicability of the information-theoretic dense process reward.
- **Reduced Reliance on External Search:** Supplementary experiments (Table 3) show that while outcome-reward methods benefit noticeably from additional test-time search (like MCTS), L2T's strong baseline performance means it gains only marginally, confirming its ability to achieve efficient reasoning intrinsically.
- **Effective for Reranking:** When applied as an inference-only reranking signal (Table 4), L2T reranking outperforms both log-likelihood-based and other PRM-based reranking methods, further validating the quality of its internal signal.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

Ablation studies confirm the critical contribution of each component within L2T:
- **Fitting Information Gain:** Replacing the information gain term with a task-specific reward model leads to an average accuracy drop of about 1.9% and a slight increase in token consumption. This underscores the effectiveness and universality of the proposed information-theoretic gain.
- **Parameter Compression Penalty:** Removing this penalty results in a substantial increase (around 12%) in token consumption and a drop in accuracy. This highlights the penalty's role in preventing redundant information accumulation and ensuring efficiency.
- **Low-Rank Approximation:** Substituting the low-rank approximation with random sampling of 30% of layers for Fisher information matrix estimation, while faster, significantly degrades accuracy and increases fluctuations, validating the choice of SVD-based low-rank approximation for its balance of efficiency and accuracy.
- **Parameter Sensitivity:** Hyperparameters `α` (weight of process reward) and `β` (compression penalty coefficient) were optimized, with optimal settings found at `α = 0.8` and `β = 0.6`, demonstrating the framework's robustness to reasonable parameter choices.

In summary, the results overwhelmingly confirm that L2T effectively addresses the trade-off between reasoning effectiveness and efficiency, achieving higher accuracy with significantly fewer tokens, and demonstrating strong generality and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Learning to Think" (L2T) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, offering a principled approach to address the critical challenges of efficiency and generality that plague existing methods. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Efficient and Effective Reasoning:** The core contribution of L2T is its ability to train LLMs to perform complex reasoning tasks not just accurately, but also efficiently, by adaptively allocating reasoning depth and minimizing token consumption. This dual optimization of effectiveness and efficiency is crucial for the practical deployment of LLMs, especially in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications where token costs and latency are major concerns. By "learning to think" more parsimoniously, LLMs can become more sustainable and accessible.

2. **Universal Information-Theoretic Process Reward:** L2T introduces a novel dense process reward derived from *internal model signals* (information gain in parameters) rather than external annotations or task-specific evaluators. This makes the reward function truly universal, applicable across diverse reasoning tasks (mathematics, code generation) without requiring costly manual labeling or retraining. This breakthrough in generality simplifies the development pipeline for LLM-based agents and enhances their adaptability to new domains.

3. **Principled Framework for Adaptive Reasoning:** By reformulating LLM optimization as an episodic reinforcement learning problem, L2T provides a clear framework for breaking down complex reasoning into manageable steps and assigning immediate feedback. The integration of fitting information gain and a parameter compression penalty provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for guiding the model to learn only relevant information at each step, promoting a compact and effective internal representation of knowledge.

4. **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** L2T pushes the boundaries of how reinforcement learning can be applied to fine-tune LLMs. By providing dense, informative feedback at the episode level, it overcomes the limitations of sparse outcome rewards, leading to more stable, sample-efficient, and targeted learning. The efficient approximation techniques for computing information-theoretic terms (PAC-Bayes, Fisher Information Matrix, SVD) demonstrate how complex theoretical concepts can be made practical for large-scale models.

5. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The principles introduced by L2T, particularly the concept of using internal model signals for dense feedback and adaptive compute allocation, could inspire future research in various areas:
 - **Dynamic Budget Allocation:** L2T's ability to adapt reasoning depth paves the way for more explicit and automated dynamic budget allocation, which is critical for scenarios sensitive to token costs, such as mobile deployment or real-time conversational AI.
 - **Enhanced LLM Interpretability:** By quantifying information gain at each reasoning step, L2T might indirectly contribute to understanding *why* an LLM makes certain reasoning choices and *how* new information is integrated into its parameters.
 - **Agentic AI Development:** For autonomous agents, efficient and adaptive reasoning is paramount. L2T provides a foundational mechanism for agents to learn to "think" more intelligently within computational constraints.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that the study primarily evaluates general LLM reasoning tasks (mathematical proofs, code generation) but did not include newly proposed benchmarks like web design. Additionally, experiments were mainly conducted on open-source DeepSeek models ranging from 1.5B to 7B parameters, with larger models (72B and above) not being used due to resource limitations and availability. These limitations highlight avenues for future work, including extending evaluation to a broader range of tasks and scaling experiments to even larger models to confirm the framework's generalizability across all scales.

Despite these limitations, L2T offers a robust and theoretically sound solution to a critical problem in LLM development, with the potential to significantly improve their practical utility and lead to more intelligent and resource-aware AI systems.
