# Learning to Move Before Learning to Do: Task-Agnostic pretraining for VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Task-Agnostic Pretraining (TAP) teaches Vision-Language-Action models fundamental physical competence from abundant unlabeled data using inverse dynamics, then grounds these priors with minimal expert demonstrations. This approach achieved an average success rate of 33.32% on the SIMPLER benchmark, outperforming foundational models trained on limited expert data, and demonstrated enhanced robustness in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models faces a "data wall" bottleneck due to the high cost and labor of collecting large-scale, expert-teleoperated, language-annotated trajectories.
- Current VLA models struggle with generalization and robustness in out-of-distribution environments due to reliance on task-specific, supervised data that often overfits to specific contexts.
- Abundant, cheap, task-agnostic interaction data (e.g., robot random play) is largely unused by conventional VLA training pipelines, despite its potential for learning motor skills.

## Method

- A ==two-stage== **Task-Agnostic Pretraining (TAP)** framework is introduced, which decouples the acquisition of physical competence ("how to move") from semantic alignment ("what to do").
- Stage 1 ("How to Move") leverages ==self-supervised Inverse Dynamics (ID)== on repurposed existing dataset trajectories and autonomously collected random robot play data to learn transferable ==motor priors== without any language supervision, using a ==Qwen2.5-VL== backbone with a frozen ==SigLIP== visual encoder.
- Stage 2 ("What to Do") fine-tunes the pretrained model using a minimal set of language-annotated expert demonstrations via ==behavior cloning==, effectively projecting high-level language instructions onto the previously learned dynamics space.

## Results

- TAP-20k achieved an Avg-All success rate of **33.32%** on the SIMPLER benchmark, outperforming foundational VLA models like OpenVLA (**7.75%**) and RT-1-X (**3.03%**) when fine-tuned on limited expert data (**5k** trajectories).
- Real-world experiments demonstrated that TAP maintained a **65%** success rate in a pushing task with visual distractors (vs. Standard BC's **5%**) and showed superior resilience to camera perturbations and background texture shifts compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies indicated that task-agnostic pretraining raises the upper bound of achievable performance and acts as a regularizer, with increased Stage 1 data leading to higher downstream task proficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing robot learning into task-agnostic physical competence and task-specific semantic alignment is a viable strategy to mitigate the expert data scarcity in VLA model training.
> - The Inverse Dynamics objective effectively forces the visual encoder to learn dynamics-aware representations from unlabeled data, allowing the model to acquire fine-grained motor control and physical common sense without explicit task goals.
> - Learning physical priors from diverse, task-agnostic exploration data significantly improves the model's robustness to visual distractors, background shifts, and camera perturbations in real-world environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics has advanced significantly, aiming to create general-purpose robots capable of understanding natural language instructions and executing complex physical tasks. Current state-of-the-art VLA models, such as RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, NORA, and π0, primarily rely on extensive datasets comprising millions of expert-teleoperated, language-annotated trajectories. These large-scale datasets, exemplified by Open X-Embodiment (OXE), unify diverse data sources to enable models to learn broad generalization capabilities.

Despite their performance, these models face a fundamental "data wall" bottleneck. The collection of expert demonstrations, which pair observations with both language instructions and corresponding actions, is labor-intensive, costly, and inherently passive from the robot’s perspective. This paradigm restricts the scalability of VLA learning, as the rate of data acquisition is dependent on human operators.

This work draws inspiration from developmental psychology, observing that biological agents acquire motor competence through spontaneous, task-agnostic exploration (e.g., infant play) long before explicit task instructions are given. This process builds an internal model of sensorimotor contingencies and physical affordances, decoupled from specific task goals.

Existing approaches to dynamics learning in robotics often treat it as an auxiliary objective or a tool for pseudo-labeling data. Explicit methods (e.g., MIDAS, SMART, PACT) directly predict future states or actions, while implicit methods (e.g., Vi-PRoM, MaskDP) internalize dynamics through temporal reordering or masked reconstruction. Video-based frameworks (e.g., VPT, GR-1) extend these to large unlabeled datasets.

The presented research challenges the prevailing brute-force scaling regime of expert demonstrations by proposing a two-stage learning framework. It differentiates itself by employing inverse dynamics as a standalone pretraining phase specifically to leverage abundant, cheap, task-agnostic data. This approach aims to establish a robust physical foundation, or "motor priors," prior to task-specific language grounding, thereby reducing the dependency on expensive expert demonstrations and offering a potentially more scalable path for Embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the data scarcity bottleneck in training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Current VLA models are fundamentally limited by the prohibitive cost and labor involved in collecting large-scale datasets of expert demonstrations, which consist of triplets of observations, language instructions, and corresponding actions.

The motivation for the proposed approach, Task-Agnostic Pretraining (TAP), is rooted in a "Decomposition Hypothesis." This hypothesis posits that action generation in robotics can be factorized into two distinct, learnable components:
1. **Acquiring physical competence ("how to move"):** This involves understanding fundamental dynamics, physical affordances (e.g., grasping, contact), and sensorimotor contingencies.
2. **Acquiring semantic alignment ("what to do"):** This involves grounding high-level language instructions to specific task goals and actions.

A crucial insight motivating this decomposition is the argument that only the latter objective, semantic alignment, necessitates language supervision. The researchers propose that physical competence can be learned entirely from task-agnostic data, which is considerably more abundant and cheaper to acquire than fully labeled expert demonstrations.

The specific motivations for leveraging task-agnostic data are:
- **Abundance and Low Cost:** Such data includes task-irrelevant trajectories from existing datasets (e.g., "open drawer" trajectories when the target task is "put carrot on plate") and autonomously generated "random play" data from robots exploring their environment without human supervision or specific goals. This data is typically discarded by conventional VLA training pipelines.
- **Biological Inspiration:** Emulating how biological agents, such as human infants, acquire motor skills through spontaneous exploration and interaction without explicit task instructions.
- **Enhanced Transferability and Robustness:** Learning fundamental physical priors from diverse, unlabeled interactions is hypothesized to produce more robust and transferable physical representations, less prone to overfitting to specific task contexts or visual perturbations.

To extract physical knowledge from this unlabeled, task-agnostic data, the Inverse Dynamics (ID) objective is employed. The motivation for ID is that by predicting the action ($a_t$) required to transition from an observation ($o_t$) to a future state ($o_{t+1}$), the model is compelled to focus on dynamic elements—such as end-effector motion and object displacement—while disregarding static background noise. This process is intended to foster the acquisition of "physical common sense" without the need for any language annotations.

Ultimately, the work aims to demonstrate that by first grounding robots in "how to move" using inexpensive, task-agnostic data, only a minimal set of expert demonstrations will be required in a subsequent stage to align these learned physical priors with high-level language instructions, thereby offering a scalable and efficient path forward for embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, Task-Agnostic Pretraining (TAP), is a two-stage framework designed to decouple the learning of physical competence from semantic understanding.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Task-Agnostic Pretraining ("How to Move")

This initial stage focuses on learning transferable motor priors from unlabeled interaction data.

- **Harnessing Task-Agnostic Data:**
 - **Repurposed Existing Datasets:** The framework reuses trajectories from large-scale robotic datasets (e.g., BridgeData, Open X-Embodiment) by deliberately discarding their original task-specific language annotations. These "task-irrelevant" trajectories are considered valuable for learning general physical interactions.
 - **Autonomous Random Play:** Robots generate data through self-supervised exploration, executing randomized end-effector commands to interact with objects (e.g., pushing, grasping). This data is collected without human supervision or task specification.
- **Autonomous Collection Pipeline:** To ensure the random play data is meaningful and contact-rich, a constrained procedural generation algorithm is implemented:
 - **Phase 1: Safe Pose Library Initialization:** An operator teleoperates the robot to densely cover its reachable workspace without specific tasks. Valid poses are filtered and downsampled using Voxel Grid Downsampling to create a uniform, discrete "safe pose library" ($\mathcal{P}_{safe}$).
 - **Phase 2: Constrained Trajectory Generation and Execution:** Waypoints are stochastically sampled from $\mathcal{P}_{safe}$ with a minimum distance constraint. A "contact heuristic" is applied to force descent if the end-effector remains above an elevation threshold ($z_{thresh}$) for too long, promoting physical interaction. Waypoints are connected via cosine interpolation, and boundary-aware Gaussian noise is added for diversity. The robot executes these trajectories, with human intervention limited to periodic resets and object shuffling.
 - **Data Downsampling:** Raw trajectories (e.g., 25Hz) are downsampled to 5Hz to ensure perceptible visual displacement between consecutive frames, making the Inverse Dynamics task well-posed and capturing semantically meaningful actions.
- **Self-supervised Inverse Dynamics (ID) Objective:** Given two consecutive observations ($o_t$, $o_{t+1}$), the model is trained to predict the action ($a_t$) that caused the transition. This forces the visual encoder to learn dynamics-aware representations by focusing on changes in end-effector motion and object displacement, rather than static visual features.
 - **Loss Function:** The model parameters $\theta$ are optimized by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted action ($\hat{a}_t$) and the ground-truth action ($a_t$): $\mathcal{L}_{ID}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(o_t,a_t,o_{t+1})\sim\mathcal{D}_{TAP}}[\| \hat{a}_t - a_t \|_2^2]$.
 - **Model Instantiation:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone (Qwen2.5-VL 3B) is used, coupled with a SigLIP visual encoder (400M parameters). During Stage 1, the visual encoder is frozen, and only the VLM backbone and a lightweight 2-layer MLP action head are trained.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Task-Specific Alignment ("What to Do")

After acquiring physical priors, the second stage grounds these priors in language using a minimal set of expert data.

- **Data Source:** A small set of language-annotated expert demonstrations $\mathcal{D}_{expert} = \{(o_t, l, a_t)\}$ where $l$ is the language instruction.
- **Input Representation:** The conditioning signal shifts from a future observation ($o_{t+1}$) to a language instruction ($l$). A text encoder ($\psi$) maps the language instruction to a sequence of tokens. The model input becomes $f_\theta(\phi(o_t), \psi(l))$.
- **Mechanism:** The pretrained backbone ($f_\theta$) and action head from Stage 1 are reused. This stage aims to learn a lightweight projection from the semantic space (language) to the pre-established dynamics space, requiring fewer labeled samples than training from scratch.
- **Training Objective:** Standard Behavior Cloning (BC) is used to finetune the model.
 - **Loss Function:** The MSE between the predicted action and the ground-truth action, conditioned on the current observation and language instruction: $\mathcal{L}_{BC}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(o_t,l,a_t)\sim\mathcal{D}_{expert}}[\| f_\theta(\phi(o_t), \psi(l)) - a_t \|_2^2]$.
 - **Model Instantiation:** All model parameters (visual encoder, VLM backbone, action head) are jointly finetuned.

#### 3.3. Action Representation

A delta-pose end-effector action space is adopted. Actions $a_t \in \mathbb{R}^7$ encode relative position changes ($\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z$), orientation changes (as a 3D axis-angle vector), and a scalar gripper command. This relative motion representation helps the model learn local interaction dynamics that are invariant to global workspace coordinates, which is beneficial for transferability.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:** Evaluations are performed on the simulated SIMPLER benchmark (WidowX environment) and a real-world WidowX 250s robot.
- **Model & Baselines:** TAP (Qwen2.5-VL + SigLIP) is compared against:
 - **Standard BC:** An identical architecture trained from scratch on limited expert data for direct comparison.
 - **Large-scale Pretrained VLAs:** Foundational models (OpenVLA, NORA, Octo, π0) pretrained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset (millions of expert trajectories). These serve as upper-bound references for models trained with significantly more human-annotated data.
- **Training Details:** Models are trained for 100,000 steps on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs with a global batch size of 128. AdamW optimizer is used with a cosine decay learning rate schedule and global gradient clipping. Training is performed in bfloat16 precision.
- **Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **Simulation:** Success rates on four manipulation tasks are reported, decomposed into "Partial success" (successful grasping) and "Entire success" (full task completion). Metrics are averaged over 50 episodes.
 - **Real-World:** Over 600 physical trials are conducted on two manipulation tasks ("Put carrot on plate," "Push pumpkin to the left"). A tiered protocol evaluates robustness across five conditions: Standard Setup, Initial State Perturbation, Visual Distractors, Background Texture Shifts, and Viewpoint Variations. Human supervision for finetuning is limited to 200 expert trajectories per task, and Stage 1 uses 30 hours of autonomous random play.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to verify the efficacy of task-agnostic pretraining in addressing the expert data bottleneck and improving generalization and robustness.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Effectiveness and Efficiency (Simulation Results)

- The TAP-20k model achieved an Avg-All success rate of 33.32% on the SIMPLER benchmark. This performance notably exceeded foundational models like OpenVLA (7.75%) and RT-1-X (3.03%) when fine-tuned on limited expert data (5k trajectories). These large-scale models frequently demonstrated 0% "Entire success" on complex tasks, indicating performance degradation under data-scarce finetuning conditions.
- Compared to the Standard BC baseline (23.15%), which uses an identical architecture and the same Stage 2 dataset, TAP yielded a +10% absolute gain in Avg-All performance.
- A monotonic improvement in performance was observed with increased Stage 1 pretraining (from 8k to 20k episodes), suggesting that deeper task-agnostic physical exposure translates into higher downstream task proficiency.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Mechanism via Partial Success Analysis (Simulation Results)

- Analyzing partial success rates, which rely on low-level physical competencies (e.g., end-effector alignment, grasping), TAP achieved an Avg-Partial success of 45.82%. This metric was comparable to Octo (42.30%) and approached π0 (53.10%).
- This finding indicates that Stage 1 pretraining effectively teaches the model "how to move" by coercing the visual encoder to infer actions from unlabeled observation pairs, thereby internalizing fine-grained motor control without language supervision. The high partial success rates suggest that TAP successfully establishes a physical grounding, allowing the model's representational capacity during Stage 2 finetuning to focus on semantic goals.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Robustness via Self-Exploration (Real-World Experiments)

- Models were trained on only 200 expert demonstrations, with TAP pretrained on 30 hours of autonomous self-exploration data.
- **Overcoming Spurious Correlations in Clutter:** In scenarios with visual distractors, TAP maintained a 65% success rate in the pushing task, while the Standard BC dropped to 5%, and the NORA baseline decayed to 60%. This indicated that TAP's self-exploration phase helped the model focus on causal interactive dynamics, making it less susceptible to static visual distractors.
- **Resilience to Severe Spatial and Textural Shifts:** Under camera perturbations (Viewpoint Variation), TAP retained 15-25% success, whereas NORA and Standard BC suffered catastrophic failures (0% success) due to spatial misalignment. Similarly, when background textures were shifted, TAP maintained 65% success in pushing, outperforming NORA (55%).
- **Overall Performance:** Across demanding real-world scenarios, TAP's average success rate rivaled or exceeded the NORA baseline (e.g., 61% vs. 56% in the pushing task) despite utilizing significantly less expert data and only 30 hours of autonomous random play. This demonstrated superior robustness in out-of-distribution environments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: How Task-Agnostic Data Shapes Learning

- **Convergence Dynamics:** Task-agnostic pretraining did not accelerate the initial learning rate of task semantics but significantly raised the upper bound of achievable performance. Models with pretraining continued to improve where the Standard BC baseline saturated, suggesting that physical priors prevent convergence to poor local optima and expand learning capacity.
- **Data Scaling:** Downstream performance was directly bounded by the scale of Stage 1 pretraining. Minimal Stage 1 exposure led to diminishing returns in Stage 2, while scaling Stage 1 to 100k steps unlocked higher success rates, indicating that abundant task-agnostic data acts as a regularizer.
- **Visualizing Learned Physical Priors (Attention Maps):**
 - **Stage 1:** Grad-CAM analysis showed that after task-agnostic pretraining (without language), the model's attention automatically concentrated on the robot gripper and manipulable objects, suppressing background elements. This demonstrated emergent physical saliency from the inverse dynamics objective.
 - **Stage 2:** Upon receiving language instructions, attention became intensely concentrated on the robotic gripper, suggesting that language effectively prunes irrelevant physical possibilities and focuses visual processing for precise motor execution.
 - **Cross-Domain Transfer:** Attention maps maintained consistent focus on interactive elements even in novel real-world environments, indicating that learned physical representations capture domain-invariant structures.

#### 4.5. Error Analysis (Real-World Failures)

- **Execution and Dynamics Failures (Approx. 25%):** These involved precise physical contact issues such as slipping, pre-grasp misalignment, or depth ambiguity. While mitigated by TAP, purely reactive VLA models still face challenges with complex 3D spatial reasoning under extreme visual shifts.
- **Semantic and Reasoning Failures (Approx. 75%):** These were characterized by physically correct execution directed at the wrong semantic target (e.g., grasping a distractor object) or losing track of the linguistic instruction (e.g., freezing or looping). This indicates that implicit reasoning capacity remains a bottleneck for reactive VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Task-Agnostic Pretraining (TAP) as a framework that challenges the conventional reliance on massive, human-annotated expert datasets for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The findings suggest several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness:** By demonstrating that generalizable physical competence can be learned from abundant, cheap, unlabeled interaction data (including repurposed off-task trajectories and autonomous robot self-play), TAP offers a more scalable and economically viable pathway for robot learning. This reduces the dependency on expensive and labor-intensive human teleoperation for data collection.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The real-world experiments show that pretraining with diverse, autonomous exploration data significantly improves the robot's resilience to out-of-distribution scenarios, such as visual clutter, background texture shifts, and camera perturbations. This indicates that task-agnostic physical priors contribute to domain-invariant representations, which are crucial for real-world deployment.
- **Theoretical Contribution to Embodied AI:** The "Decomposition Hypothesis," which separates "how to move" from "what to do," provides a conceptual framework for understanding and addressing different facets of robot learning. The success of learning "how to move" through self-supervised inverse dynamics validates the idea that fundamental physical understanding can be acquired without explicit task semantics or language.
- **Biological Plausibility:** The approach, inspired by infant-like motor babbling and task-unaware exploration, suggests that developmental learning principles can be effectively translated into robotic learning paradigms, potentially leading to more natural and efficient acquisition of motor skills.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By reducing the need for industrial-scale datasets and computational resources typically required for training large foundational VLA models, TAP makes advanced robot learning more accessible to academic researchers and smaller institutions. The ability to achieve competitive performance with significantly fewer expert demonstrations and manageable compute infrastructure (e.g., a single node with 8 H100 GPUs) lowers the barrier to entry.
- **Future Directions for VLA Research:** While TAP effectively addresses the physical execution bottleneck, the error analysis highlights that semantic and reasoning failures remain significant. This suggests that future research can build upon the robust physical grounding provided by TAP to further enhance high-level reasoning and planning capabilities in VLA models, potentially through complementary methods that address complex implicit reasoning.

In summary, this work provides empirical evidence that task-agnostic pretraining offers a powerful and complementary approach to scaling embodied AI, moving beyond the sole reliance on expert demonstrations by instilling robust, transferable physical priors.
