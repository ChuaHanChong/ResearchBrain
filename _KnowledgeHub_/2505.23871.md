# ADG: Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery for Corruption-Robust Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and The Chinese University of Hong Kong developed Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery (ADG), a three-stage diffusion-based framework, to restore high-quality data from partially corrupted offline reinforcement learning datasets. ADG significantly improved performance across various RL algorithms, boosting MDP-based methods by an average of 69.1% and sequence modeling methods by 17.4% under random corruption, bringing scores close to those achieved with clean data.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning algorithms are highly susceptible to data corruption (noise, errors, adversarial attacks) in real-world datasets, leading to significant performance degradation.
- Existing robust offline RL methods often struggle with recovering high-dimensional state observations and are inadequate when multiple elements (states, actions, rewards) are simultaneously corrupted.
- Traditional diffusion models, while powerful for denoising, tend to overfit when trained on partially corrupted datasets because they assume either entirely clean data or a uniform noise distribution across all samples.

## Method

- **ADG** employs a three-stage diffusion-based approach: detector training, denoiser training, and ==dataset recovery==, processing trajectory "slices" to leverage temporal information.
- An Ambient Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) from approximated distributions is trained on the partially corrupted dataset to act as a detector, overcoming overfitting to existing noise.
- A standard DDPM (denoiser) is then selectively trained using only the identified "clean" data subset and subsequently used to recover the previously detected corrupted samples, forming a high-quality dataset for RL training.

## Results

- ADG consistently restored the performance of diverse offline RL algorithms (e.g., CQL, IQL, DT) on corrupted datasets, achieving scores comparable to those on clean data and significantly outperforming unrecovered or simply filtered data.
- The framework provided an average performance boost of approximately **69.1%** for MDP-based algorithms and **17.4%** for sequence modeling algorithms under random corruption, and a **24.41%** average improvement under adversarial corruption.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of Ambient Loss for detector performance, selective denoiser training, the two-model architecture, and the importance of incorporating temporal information for optimal recovery across various corruption settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Ambient Diffusion concept can be theoretically extended to the discrete DDPM framework, enabling effective learning for noise prediction on partially corrupted data without assuming clean inputs.
> - Decoupling the detection of corrupted samples from their denoising, using two distinct diffusion models, prevents interference and ensures the denoiser learns the true data distribution from clean examples.
> - Incorporating temporal context via trajectory "slices" (parameter H) significantly enhances the performance of both the detector and denoiser, improving data recovery and subsequent RL algorithm scores.
