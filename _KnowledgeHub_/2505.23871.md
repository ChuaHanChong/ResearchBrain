# ADG: Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery for Corruption-Robust Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and The Chinese University of Hong Kong developed Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery (ADG), a three-stage diffusion-based framework, to restore high-quality data from partially corrupted offline reinforcement learning datasets. ADG significantly improved performance across various RL algorithms, boosting MDP-based methods by an average of 69.1% and sequence modeling methods by 17.4% under random corruption, bringing scores close to those achieved with clean data.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning algorithms are highly susceptible to data corruption (noise, errors, adversarial attacks) in real-world datasets, leading to significant performance degradation.
- Existing robust offline RL methods often struggle with recovering high-dimensional state observations and are inadequate when multiple elements (states, actions, rewards) are simultaneously corrupted.
- Traditional diffusion models, while powerful for denoising, tend to overfit when trained on partially corrupted datasets because they assume either entirely clean data or a uniform noise distribution across all samples.

## Method

- **ADG** employs a three-stage diffusion-based approach: detector training, denoiser training, and ==dataset recovery==, processing trajectory "slices" to leverage temporal information.
- An Ambient Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) from approximated distributions is trained on the partially corrupted dataset to act as a detector, overcoming overfitting to existing noise.
- A standard DDPM (denoiser) is then selectively trained using only the identified "clean" data subset and subsequently used to recover the previously detected corrupted samples, forming a high-quality dataset for RL training.

## Results

- ADG consistently restored the performance of diverse offline RL algorithms (e.g., CQL, IQL, DT) on corrupted datasets, achieving scores comparable to those on clean data and significantly outperforming unrecovered or simply filtered data.
- The framework provided an average performance boost of approximately **69.1%** for MDP-based algorithms and **17.4%** for sequence modeling algorithms under random corruption, and a **24.41%** average improvement under adversarial corruption.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of Ambient Loss for detector performance, selective denoiser training, the two-model architecture, and the importance of incorporating temporal information for optimal recovery across various corruption settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Ambient Diffusion concept can be theoretically extended to the discrete DDPM framework, enabling effective learning for noise prediction on partially corrupted data without assuming clean inputs.
> - Decoupling the detection of corrupted samples from their denoising, using two distinct diffusion models, prevents interference and ensures the denoiser learns the true data distribution from clean examples.
> - Incorporating temporal context via trajectory "slices" (parameter H) significantly enhances the performance of both the detector and denoiser, improving data recovery and subsequent RL algorithm scores.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) has gained prominence for its ability to learn policies from static datasets, which is crucial for real-world applications where direct environment interaction is costly or unsafe. However, the performance of offline RL algorithms is highly dependent on the quality of the collected data. Real-world datasets, whether from sensors or human inputs, are susceptible to noise, errors, or adversarial corruption. This data corruption can lead to substantial performance degradation or policy deviations, presenting a significant challenge to the practical deployment of offline RL.

Prior research has addressed aspects of robust offline RL, including theoretical properties and certification under corrupted data, and empirical efforts in developing uncertainty-weighted algorithms, robust value learning using methods like Huber loss, and sequence modeling techniques for refining noisy actions and rewards. While these methods have shown progress, a notable limitation identified in previous work, particularly, is the difficulty in effectively recovering observations (states) due to their typically high dimensionality. Furthermore, existing solutions often fall short when multiple elements within the dataset (states, actions, and rewards) are simultaneously corrupted.

Diffusion models have recently emerged as powerful generative models with inherent denoising capabilities, making them attractive for mitigating noise in data. Their application in offline RL has seen success, for instance, in state denoising during the testing phase. However, a significant constraint of current diffusion models is their assumption that the training dataset is either entirely clean or exhibits a consistent noise distribution across all data points. This assumption renders naive diffusion methods prone to overfitting when applied directly to partially corrupted datasets—a common scenario in real-world offline RL—where only a subset of data is affected by noise, or the noise distribution is non-uniform.

This research addresses these limitations by developing a diffusion-based framework specifically designed to handle partially corrupted datasets during the *training phase* of offline RL. It aims to bridge the gap between the denoising capabilities of diffusion models and the complexities of real-world corrupted offline RL data, particularly concerning high-dimensional state recovery and simultaneous multi-element corruption, without requiring clean data or consistent noise distribution assumptions. The work positions itself as the first to pioneer the use of diffusion models for comprehensive dataset recovery in corruption-robust offline RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the widespread prevalence of noisy and erroneous data in real-world offline reinforcement learning scenarios. Datasets collected from various sources, such as sensors or human interactions, are inherently prone to corruption, which can significantly undermine the efficacy and reliability of learned policies. Existing offline RL algorithms, being data-dependent, exhibit vulnerability to such data disturbances, leading to performance drops or suboptimal decision-making.

A critical observation motivating this work is that current robust offline RL methods, while addressing specific forms of corruption (e.g., in actions or rewards), often struggle with high-dimensional state space corruption. Furthermore, they are generally inadequate when multiple components of a transition (state, action, reward) are corrupted concurrently. The paper empirically demonstrates (Figure 2a-c) that simply filtering out noisy data—a seemingly intuitive approach—is insufficient, especially when datasets are limited. This is because the removal of corrupted samples leads to a loss of critical sequential information within trajectories, which is essential for decision-making and policy learning, particularly for sequence modeling-based RL algorithms. This evidence underscores the necessity of *recovering* corrupted data rather than merely discarding it.

Diffusion models offer strong denoising capabilities, presenting a promising avenue for data recovery. However, a key technical challenge is their tendency to overfit noisy samples when trained on datasets with inconsistent or partial corruption. Traditional diffusion model training typically assumes a clean dataset or a uniform noise distribution, which does not reflect the partial and heterogeneous corruption common in offline RL datasets.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery (ADG) framework are:

1. **Develop a Diffusion-Based Denoising Framework:** To introduce a novel method that leverages diffusion models to explicitly address data corruption in offline RL training.
2. **Enable Learning on Partially Corrupted Datasets:** To overcome the limitation of conventional diffusion models by introducing "Ambient Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)" from approximated distributions, thereby allowing effective training even when only a portion of the dataset is corrupted and without explicit knowledge of the noise distribution.
3. **Distinguish Clean from Corrupted Data:** To use the noise-prediction property of the Ambient DDPM to accurately identify and separate clean data points from corrupted ones within the dataset.
4. **Recover Corrupted Data:** To train a standard DDPM (denoiser) using the identified clean subset and subsequently employ this denoiser to refine and restore the previously identified corrupted data, thereby enhancing the overall data quality.
5. **Achieve Versatility and Compatibility:** To design ADG as a plug-and-play module that can be seamlessly integrated with any existing offline RL algorithm, enhancing its robustness without requiring significant modifications to the underlying RL training methodology.
6. **Demonstrate Comprehensive Robustness:** To empirically validate ADG's effectiveness under various corruption types (random, adversarial), different corruption elements (state-only, full-element), varying corruption rates and scales, and different dataset sizes and qualities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery (ADG), is a three-stage diffusion-based approach designed to recover high-quality data from partially corrupted offline RL datasets. It operates without requiring external information or supervision about the corruption.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Corruption Scenarios

The work models offline RL as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a dataset `D` of transitions `(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})`. For robust offline RL, a unified trajectory-based storage `τ = (s_0, a_0, r_0..., s_{T-1}, a_{T-1}, r_{T-1})` is adopted. The method considers two main corruption scenarios:
- **State Corruption:** Only the states `s_t` within trajectories are affected.
- **Full-element Corruption:** States, actions `a_t`, and rewards `r_t` are simultaneously corrupted.

Corruption can be either **random** (noise drawn from a uniform distribution, scaled by standard deviation) or **adversarial** (using a Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) attack with pre-trained value functions to minimize expected returns). A corruption rate `η` (proportion of corrupted data) and a corruption scale `α` are used to control the extent of corruption.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Models Background

Diffusion models involve a forward process that gradually adds Gaussian noise to a clean data sample `x_0` over `K` steps to produce `x_K` (pure noise). The reverse process learns to denoise `x_K` back to `x_0`. Standard Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) are trained by minimizing a loss (Eq. 3) that predicts the added noise `ϵ` at various timesteps `k`. A critical assumption for this training is that the original data `x_0` is clean. This assumption is relaxed in ADG.

#### 3.3. Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery (ADG) - Three Stages

ADG addresses the challenge of partially corrupted data by decoupling the detection of corrupted samples from their recovery, leveraging two distinct diffusion models. The method processes trajectory "slices" `τ_t = [z_{t-H}..., z_{t+H}]`, where `z` can be `s` or `(s, a, r)`, and `H` is the temporal slice size.

**Stage 1: Detector Training with Ambient DDPM**
- **Challenge:** Standard diffusion models overfit when trained on partially corrupted data. Ambient Diffusion Models handle uniformly noisy data but not *partially* corrupted data (where some `x_0` are clean, others noisy).
- **Solution:** ADG introduces **Ambient DDPM from Approximated Distributions**. Corollary 4.1 extends the ambient diffusion concept to the discrete DDPM framework. It states that for a manually defined noise-added diffusion timestep `k_a`, an Ambient DDPM can be trained to predict the noise `ϵ` from `x_k` (for `k >= k_a`), even if the input `x_ka` samples are already noisy.
- **Theoretical Justification:** Theorem 4.3 proves that for any bounded noise scale, a `k_a` can always be found such that the KL divergence between the true clean distribution `q(x_k|x_0)` and the partially corrupted approximated distribution `q(ˇx_k|ˇx_0)` becomes arbitrarily small for `k >= k_a`. This implies that at sufficiently high noise levels (large `k_a`), the presence of original corruption `ι·ϵ` in `ˇx_0` becomes negligible compared to the diffusion noise, allowing the Ambient DDPM to learn an effective noise predictor.
- **Training Objective:** The detector `ϵ_θ` is trained using the Ambient Loss (Eq. 6) on trajectory mini-batches `ˇτ_t` sampled from the partially corrupted dataset `D`. This objective allows learning on data that might already contain noise, making `ϵ_θ` robust to partial corruption.

**Stage 2: Denoiser Training with Naive DDPM**
- **Corrupted Samples Detection:** After training, the Ambient DDPM `ϵ_θ` acts as a detector. Proposition 4.4 explains that the squared Frobenius norm of its noise prediction, `||ϵ_θ(ˇx_k, k)||^2_F`, can differentiate between noisy and noise-free samples, with maximum Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) at `k = k_a`.
- For each trajectory slice `ˇτ_t`, ADG calculates `e_θ(ˇz_t) = ||ϵ_θ(ˇτ_t, k_a)_{H+1}||^2_F`, focusing on the central RL component `ˇz_t`.
- A manually defined threshold `ζ` is used: samples `ˇz_t` with `e_θ(ˇz_t) > ζ` are classified as corrupted (`D_n`), and others as clean (`D_c`).
- **Selective Training of Denoiser:** The identified *clean* subset `D_c` is then used to train a *standard* (naive) DDPM `ϵ_ϕ`. This selective training (Eq. 7), which includes a mask `m_t` to only consider the clean parts of the trajectory slice, prevents the denoiser from overfitting to the misclassified or remaining noisy samples in `D_c`. This ensures `ϵ_ϕ` learns the true data distribution.

**Stage 3: Dataset Recovery**
- The trained denoiser `ϵ_ϕ` is applied to all samples previously identified as corrupted (`D_n`).
- The reverse DDPM process (Eq. 2) with `ϵ_ϕ` is used to generate denoised trajectory slices `˘τ_t`.
- The central `(H+1)`-th column of `˘τ_t` replaces the original corrupted `ˇz_t`.
- Finally, the denoised `D_n` samples are combined with the original clean `D_c` samples to form a high-quality, recovered dataset for subsequent offline RL training.

**Key Innovations:** ADG's novelty lies in its theoretical extension of ambient diffusion models to the discrete DDPM framework for partially corrupted data, its two-stage detection-and-denoising architecture, and the use of selective training to ensure a clean training signal for the denoiser. The approach is designed to be versatile, enabling integration with any offline RL algorithm by simply providing a pre-processed dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ADG was conducted across various offline RL benchmarks, including MuJoCo, Kitchen, and Adroit, under both random and adversarial data corruption scenarios, and with varying data availability (full or limited dataset sizes). Baseline offline RL methods included pessimistic value estimation (CQL), policy constraint methods (IQL, RIQL), and sequence modeling methods (DT, RDT).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Restoration

- ADG consistently demonstrated its ability to restore the performance of offline RL algorithms on corrupted datasets. Figure 2 illustrates that ADG effectively brings normalized scores of various algorithms (CQL, IQL, RIQL, DT, RDT) on "Recovered" datasets close to those achieved on "Clean" datasets, significantly outperforming results on "Noised" or even simply "Filtered" datasets. This highlights that recovery, rather than mere filtering, is crucial for maintaining critical sequential information.

#### 4.2. Performance under Random Data Corruption (Table 1)

- ADG provided substantial benefits to both non-robust (CQL, IQL, DT) and robust (RIQL, RDT) algorithms across all random corruption settings.
- **MDP-based algorithms (CQL, IQL, RIQL):** Showed an average performance boost of approximately 69.1%, indicating ADG's effectiveness in completing missing information and mitigating state/full-element corruption.
- **Sequence modeling algorithms (DT, RDT):** Achieved an average performance boost of about 17.4%.
- Notably, when equipped with ADG, baseline algorithms like IQL and DT often outperformed their "Naive" robust variants (RIQL and RDT), suggesting that comprehensive dataset recovery can be more impactful than explicit robust policy learning.
- ADG also enabled most algorithms to achieve comparable performance under both state-only and full-element corruption, demonstrating its scalability to more complex corruption scenarios.

#### 4.3. Performance under Adversarial Data Corruption (Table 2)

- ADG maintained its robustness under adversarial data corruption, consistently improving baseline performance by an average of 24.41%.
- Similar to random corruption, ADG-equipped IQL and DT often surpassed their "Naive" robust counterparts (RIQL and RDT), reinforcing the finding that data recovery can be a powerful defense against adversarial attacks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Ambient Loss (Figure 3):** The Ambient Loss was critical for the detector's performance. Detectors trained with naive diffusion loss quickly overfit to corrupted training data. Ambient loss significantly improved detection capabilities, leading to lower false negative rates (undetected corrupted samples). The chosen `k_a = 30` (Ambient Nature Timestep) demonstrated optimal performance, achieving the lowest FNs, which ensured that the subsequent denoiser was trained on nearly clean data. D4RL scores directly correlated with detector performance.
- **Impact of Selective Training (Figure 4):** Selective training was found to be crucial for the denoiser. A very low `ζ` (threshold for classifying clean/corrupted) resulted in insufficient data for denoiser training and overfitting. A very high `ζ` introduced too much corrupted data, degrading performance. Moderate `ζ` values (e.g., 0.10 or 0.20) yielded the best results in terms of Mean Squared Error (MSE) between recovered and ground truth data, and correlated D4RL scores, indicating the importance and robustness of selective training.
- **Impact of Two Separate Diffusion Models (Figure 5):** Using two independent diffusion models for detection and denoising consistently outperformed a single diffusion model attempting both tasks. This suggests that decoupling these objectives prevents interference, allowing each model to optimize effectively without converging to local minima.
- **Temporal Slice Size (H) (Figure 10):** Increasing the temporal slice size `H` (from `H=0` for single-step input) significantly improved the performance of both the detector and denoiser, and subsequently the RL algorithms. Performance plateaued or remained robust for `H` values between 3 and 7, emphasizing the importance of incorporating sequential information for effective recovery.

#### 4.5. Additional Findings (Appendices)

- **Visualizations:** Visualizations of `e(ˇx)` (squared Frobenius norm of noise prediction) clearly showed distinct distributions for clean versus corrupted samples (Figure 7), confirming the detector's discriminative power. Recovered trajectories in Figure 8 demonstrated reduced discontinuity compared to corrupted ones.
- **Gaussian Noise (Table 7):** ADG consistently improved performance under Gaussian noise, further demonstrating its generalizability beyond uniform noise.
- **Dataset Quality & Scale (Table 8, Figure 11):** ADG showed effectiveness across different MuJoCo dataset quality levels (medium, expert) and various down-sampled dataset scales (2%, 10%, 20%, 50%), indicating its utility in diverse data settings.
- **Varying Corruption Rates and Scales (Figure 12):** ADG robustly enhanced performance even under increasing corruption rates and scales, mitigating degradation observed in baselines.
- **Missing Data (Table 9):** The framework proved applicable to missing data scenarios (randomly zeroing out actions), recovering a significant portion of original policy quality, thus generalizing beyond additive noise.
- **Extension to SOTA Methods (Table 10):** ADG successfully improved the performance of recent state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms like A2PR and NUNO under random state attack, highlighting its broad applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Ambient Diffusion-Guided Dataset Recovery (ADG) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robust offline reinforcement learning, addressing a fundamental challenge of real-world data corruption.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Technical Contributions

- ADG is presented as the first diffusion-based denoising framework specifically designed to tackle *training-time* data corruption in offline RL. This is a crucial distinction from prior work that mostly focuses on testing-time robustness or assumes clean training data.
- The introduction of **Ambient Denoensing Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) from approximated distributions** is a core technical contribution. It theoretically enables diffusion models to learn effectively from partially corrupted datasets, overcoming the traditional limitation of requiring uniformly clean or consistently noisy data.
- The three-stage architecture, which distinctly separates the **detection of corrupted samples** (using Ambient DDPM) from their **recovery** (using a selectively trained naive DDPM), is a novel and effective strategy. This decoupling prevents interference and ensures high-quality denoising.

#### 5.2. Versatility and Broad Applicability

- A notable strength of ADG is its **versatility and seamless compatibility** with any offline RL algorithm. It acts as a pre-processing step, transforming a corrupted dataset into a high-quality one, allowing existing and future RL algorithms to perform robustly without requiring algorithm-specific modifications. This is demonstrated by its consistent improvements across pessimistic, policy-constraint, and sequence-modeling baselines, as well as recent state-of-the-art methods.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Robustness and Performance

- The comprehensive empirical evaluation demonstrates ADG's **consistent and significant performance improvements** across a wide array of challenging scenarios:
 - Various corruption types: random, adversarial, and Gaussian noise.
 - Different corrupted elements: state-only and full-element (state-action-reward triplets).
 - Diverse data settings: varying corruption rates and scales, different dataset sizes (full, down-sampled), and different quality levels (medium, expert).
 - Generalization to **missing data**, indicating broader applicability beyond additive noise.
- In many cases, ADG enabled standard offline RL algorithms to **outperform their explicitly designed robust variants**, highlighting the power of robust data preparation over purely algorithmic robustness.

#### 5.4. Potential Impact

- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** ADG has the potential to significantly enhance the applicability of offline RL in real-world settings where perfectly clean data is rarely available. By effectively mitigating the impact of prevalent data corruption, it makes offline RL systems more reliable and trustworthy.
- **New Paradigm for Robust Learning:** This work establishes a new paradigm for robust learning from noisy or corrupted data by demonstrating the efficacy of diffusion models in this challenging domain. It opens avenues for future research into data-centric approaches to robustness.
- **Reduced Complexity for RL Developers:** By providing a generic and effective data recovery mechanism, ADG could reduce the burden on RL algorithm developers to design highly specialized robust variants for every new form of corruption, allowing them to focus on core policy learning advancements.
- **Improved Data Utility:** The ability to recover, rather than simply discard, corrupted data helps to maximize the utility of valuable, often costly-to-collect, offline datasets, especially in data-limited scenarios.

#### 5.5. Limitations (as noted by the authors)

- The three-stage diffusion process introduces **additional training time** compared to standard offline RL methods, although the authors provide a computational overhead analysis indicating it is still lower than some baselines.
- The performance of ADG is reliant on the **choice of key hyperparameters** such as the temporal slice size `H` and the corruption threshold `ζ`. While ablation studies suggest robustness within a range, optimal tuning may be required for specific datasets.
- The current **trajectory-based recovery mechanism** relies on temporal consistency. The authors acknowledge that for environments with highly discontinuous dynamics or sparse rewards, the slice-based approach might struggle to capture long-range dependencies, potentially impacting recovery quality.

Despite these limitations, ADG presents a promising direction for making offline RL more robust and practical for real-world applications by addressing a critical issue in data quality.
