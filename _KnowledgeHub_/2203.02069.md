# Sim2Real Instance-Level Style Transfer for 6D Pose Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> Sim2Real instance-level style transfer improves synthetic training data for RGB 6D pose estimation. A robot collects unlabeled real images; a synthetic-only PoseNet plus a mismatch filter renders weakly-paired synthetic counterparts; a per-object CUT-style patch translation network (StyleGAN2 generator, PatchNCE + GAN + L1 losses) restyles each instance while preserving pose and silhouette. It raised ADD accuracy on all six household objects.

## Problem

- Synthetic renderings leave texture and material domain gaps against real images, so pose networks trained purely on rendered data underperform even with photo-realistic assets.
- Global image-to-image translation methods adapt whole-scene style, causing shape collapse and style mismatch when scenes contain multiple colorful or cluttered objects.
- Depth-based self-supervised adaptation fails on transparent objects, and existing instance-level translation needs manual 2D bounding-box annotation of real data.

## Method

- Robot-driven ==weakly-paired data generation==: gantry camera poses plus a synthetic-only ==PoseNet== estimate object poses, and a ==mismatch filter== keeps only views whose detection count equals the scene modal count.
- Per-class **TransNet** trained on 64x64 patches cropped from paired sim/real object regions, with flipping removed to preserve lettering, using ==PatchNCE==, ==non-saturating GAN==, ==L1== and ==R1== losses.
- Inference pre/post-processing: crop each instance, upscale to 512x512 before translation, rescale back, then mask and overlay onto the original image to keep ==silhouette== and sharp texture.

## Results

- Adaptation improved ADD pass rate at **2cm** for all six objects, e.g. potted meat can **0.579** to **0.742**, bleach cleanser **0.196** to **0.442**, water bottle **0.089** to **0.381**.
- Competing translators were inconsistent: DRIT lifted mustard bottle AUC to **67.77** but dropped mug to **57.16**, and CUT collapsed bleach cleanser to **19.57** versus **48.32** without adaptation.
- Gains scale with domain-gap size: AUC rose **21.01** for bleach cleanser and **20.08** for the transparent water bottle, but only **0.66** for the well-modeled coffee can.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adapting style per object instance rather than per whole image preserves pose-entangled shape and texture cues, which is what downstream pose regression actually depends on.
> - Weak pairing from a coarse model plus a simple consistency filter can replace manual annotation, since translation networks need far less data than pose networks.
> - Domain adaptation pays off proportionally to the modeling gap, so it mainly helps transparent, shiny, or repackaged objects rather than already well-rendered assets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The precise determination of an object's 6D pose (location and orientation in 3D space) is a fundamental capability for robotic manipulation, particularly as robots are deployed in increasingly unstructured and complex environments such as human homes. RGB-based 6D pose estimation has seen advancements with deep neural networks; however, achieving high performance typically necessitates extensive data collection and laborious 6D pose annotation, which is a significant bottleneck.

Synthetic data generation offers a solution to this annotation challenge, providing virtually infinite data with perfect ground truth labels at a low cost. Despite these advantages, a measurable "domain gap" persists between synthetic and real-world images, often leading to suboptimal network performance when models trained on synthetic data are deployed in real environments.

Various approaches have been explored to bridge this sim2real domain gap:
- **Domain Randomization (DR) and Photo-realistic Rendering:** Methods like DOPE combine DR (randomizing parameters like object poses, backgrounds, lighting, and textures) with photo-realistic rendering. While effective with high-quality 3D models, performance can degrade with noisy assets or when the inherent difference between rendered and real images remains.
- **Domain Adaptation Techniques:** These methods aim to convert synthetic images into realistic ones using real-world data, without requiring manual annotations.
 - **Geometry-based Adaptation:** Approaches like Self6D and Rad et al. leverage geometric information (e.g., depth) to make networks domain-invariant. However, these methods are constrained when depth information is unavailable or unreliable, particularly for challenging objects like transparent ones.
 - **Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)-based Adaptation:** This category utilizes RGB information for image-to-image translation. CycleGAN (employed by Rojtberg et al.) and other advanced GANs like DRIT and MUNIT can translate unpaired images between domains and disentangle style and content. A notable limitation of many of these global style adaptation methods is their tendency to introduce inconsistencies in complex scenes containing multiple objects, as they apply a global style to the entire image rather than focusing on individual instances. INIT addresses instance-level translation but requires 2D bounding box annotations for real data. Richter et al. translate synthetic images to realistic ones at a class level, showing moderate improvements.

This work addresses the limitations of global style transfer methods by proposing an *instance-level* style transfer approach. It posits that transferring the style of individual objects, rather than the entire image, results in improved pose-level correspondences, particularly for objects with varied textures or appearances depending on the viewpoint. The paper focuses on enhancing RGB-based 6D pose estimation for challenging objects (e.g., transparent, shiny household items) by improving the quality of synthetic training data through automated, instance-level style adaptation, crucially without requiring manual annotations of real data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of 6D pose estimation networks (PoseNets) by developing an automatic simulation-to-reality (sim2real) domain adaptation technique via instance-level style transfer. This is intended to improve the quality of synthetic training data, thereby bridging the domain gap between synthetic and real-world images.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in robotic object manipulation:
- **Need for Robust Perception:** Robots operating in unstructured environments, such as human homes, require robust 6D pose estimation systems capable of recognizing and interacting with a wide variety of objects, including those with challenging characteristics (e.g., transparent, shiny, texture-rich).
- **Data Annotation Bottleneck:** Training high-performance deep neural networks for 6D pose estimation traditionally demands vast amounts of real-world data with precise 6D pose annotations. This annotation process is labor-intensive and costly, especially for RGB-only methods.
- **Limitations of Synthetic Data:** While synthetic data offers a cost-effective solution with perfect annotations, a persistent domain gap between synthetic and real images hinders the transferability and performance of networks trained solely on synthetic data.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Domain Adaptation:** Prior domain adaptation methods, particularly GAN-based approaches, often apply a global style to an entire image. In complex scenes with multiple, distinct, or texture-rich objects, this global adaptation can lead to inconsistencies, loss of object-specific details, or failures in maintaining accurate pose-level correspondences. For instance, an object's appearance, and thus its style, can significantly vary with viewpoint, which global methods struggle to capture consistently.
- **Absence of Manual Annotations:** Many domain adaptation techniques still require some form of manual annotation or pairing, even if not full 6D poses, limiting their automation and scalability.

Therefore, the key objectives are:
1. **Develop an instance-level style transfer method:** This method aims to transfer the style of individual objects from synthetic to real domains, preserving object shape, texture details, and pose-level correspondences.
2. **Automate the sim2real pipeline:** Create a comprehensive, end-to-end pipeline from real data collection to PoseNet training, minimizing human intervention.
3. **Generate weakly-paired images automatically:** Produce corresponding synthetic and real images that share similar content around target objects without manual annotation, facilitating the training of the style transfer network.
4. **Improve synthetic data quality:** Enhance the realism of synthetic images for PoseNet training, especially for challenging objects and varied household environments.
5. **Amplify PoseNet performance:** Demonstrate that the improved synthetic data leads to a measurable and consistent increase in the accuracy and robustness of off-the-shelf 6D pose estimation networks across diverse objects.

The central hypothesis is that adapting styles at the individual object instance level, rather than globally across the image, is critical for maintaining necessary details for 6D pose estimation and overcoming inconsistencies in complex, multi-object scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a multi-stage pipeline designed to automatically adapt synthetic data to real-world visual characteristics for 6D pose estimation, without requiring manual annotations of real images. The workflow is structured into data collection, weakly-paired image generation, instance-level style transfer, and final PoseNet training. The approach assumes that 3D models of target objects and the environment, along with camera poses in the global frame, are available.

**A. Real Data Collection by Robot:**
The process begins with collecting real RGB images using a robotic platform in the target environment. The robot captures images of the same target area from multiple viewpoints, and its camera poses in the environment coordinate system are recorded. This multi-viewpoint collection strategy is implemented to ensure a consistent number of object instances across views, which is beneficial for later stages. A gantry-type robot installed on the ceiling was used for this purpose in the experiments.

**B. Weakly-Paired Images Generation:**
The goal of this stage is to create "weakly-paired images" – synthetic images that visually correspond to the collected real images. Since camera poses and 3D models are known, the missing information for rendering realistic synthetic counterparts is the 6D pose of the target objects in the real scene.
1. **Initial PoseNet Training:** An off-the-shelf 6D PoseNet (PVNet in the experiments) is initially trained using *only synthetic data*. This synthetic dataset comprises two types:
 - **Domain-Randomized (DR) images:** Generated with random object placements, backgrounds, distractors, and lighting to enhance robustness and cover a wide range of poses.
 - **Domain-Specific (DS) images:** Generated by placing target objects within the 3D model of the specific target environment, considering gravitational effects to capture contextual realism.
2. **Pose Inference on Real Data:** The initially trained PoseNet is then used to infer the 6D poses of objects in the collected real images.
3. **Mismatch Filter:** A crucial step to address inconsistencies. The PoseNet, when applied to real images, might not detect a consistent number of object instances across different viewpoints of the *same scene*. To ensure quality in weakly-paired images, a mismatch filter is applied: for each object class, the system determines the most frequently detected number of instances (n̄) across multiple views of a single scene. Only images where the inferred number of instances (nᵢ) matches n̄ are retained for further processing. This eliminates images with erroneous or missing detections.
4. **Synthetic Image Rendering:** Finally, synthetic images are rendered based on the filtered estimated poses and known camera poses, resulting in weakly-paired synthetic-real image sets that share similar content around the object areas.

**C. Instance-level Style Transfer:**
An instance-level style transfer network (TransNet) is trained using the generated weakly-paired images. This TransNet is inspired by single-image translation methods like CUT but adapted for individual object style transfer to preserve shape and texture details critical for 6D pose estimation.
1. **Input Processing for TransNet Training:**
 - For each target object instance, both the synthetic and corresponding real images are cropped to a 2D bounding box area (1.2 times larger than the object's synthetic mask label).
 - These cropped images are then randomly scaled (between 170 to 512 pixels on the longest axis, maintaining aspect ratio).
 - Finally, fixed-size patches (64x64 pixels) are randomly sampled from these scaled-and-cropped images, serving as input to the TransNet.
 - Unlike some general image-to-image translation methods, random flipping is omitted to preserve asymmetrical object information (e.g., text, labels) relevant for pose estimation.
2. **TransNet Training:** A separate TransNet is trained for *each object class*, enabling "single object style translation" using multiple weakly-paired images specific to that class. The network architecture and loss function are based on CUT, utilizing a Generator and Discriminator derived from StyleGAN2. The loss functions incorporate Patch-NCE, non-saturating GAN loss, L1 regularization, and R1 gradient penalty stabilization to guide the style transfer process.
3. **Inference for Style Transfer (Algorithm 1, Fig. 4):**
 - To apply the trained TransNet to new synthetic images, a specific pre-processing and post-processing sequence is followed for each object instance:
 - **Pre-processing:** The input synthetic image is cropped around an object instance using its 2D square bounding box and then enlarged (e.g., to 512x512 pixels). This scaling operation is demonstrated to reduce blurriness in the transferred image.
 - **Inference:** The enlarged cropped image is fed to the corresponding object-specific TransNet.
 - **Post-processing:** The output from the TransNet is re-scaled back to its original size. Then, the transferred object area (masked by the original synthetic mask) is overlaid onto the input image, preserving the object's silhouette and ensuring it integrates with the original background.
 - This instance-level inference allows for style transfer in scenes containing multiple objects of the same or different classes by processing each instance sequentially and overlaying the results.

**D. Training a 6D Pose Estimation Network:**
Once the TransNets are trained, a large collection of synthetic images can be adapted. Only the Domain-Specific synthetic images are adapted by the TransNet, while the Domain-Randomized images are used without adaptation. The final 6D PoseNet is then trained using this combined dataset of both adapted Domain-Specific and unadapted Domain-Randomized synthetic images. PVNet was chosen as the PoseNet for experimental evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive evaluations to assess the effectiveness of the proposed instance-level style transfer method on 6D pose estimation performance and the realism of adapted images.

**A. Experimental Environment and Datasets:**
The experiments were conducted in an indoor dining table environment utilizing an X-Y gantry robot for data collection. Six household objects were selected, including four from the YCB Object Set (mustard bottle, potted meat can, bleach cleanser, mug) and two custom Japanese household objects (a transparent water bottle and a shiny coffee can) to specifically evaluate challenging material properties. Three real-world datasets were created, each containing 1,000 RGB images with two target objects and distractors, captured by an Intel RealSense D415 camera. Ground truth 6D poses for evaluation were obtained via manual labeling.

For PoseNet training (PVNet), 5,000 synthetic images were generated for each target object (2,500 Domain-Randomized and 2,500 Domain-Specific). For TransNet training, 1,000 real images per dataset were collected and filtered using the mismatch filter, retaining an average of 86.5% for training.

**B. Quantitative Evaluation of 6D Pose Estimation:**
The performance of 6D pose estimation was measured using the Average Distance of Distinguishable Model Points (ADD) metric and the Area Under the Curve (AUC) of the ADD threshold curves. A 2cm ADD threshold was highlighted as relevant for proficient robotic grasping.

- **Consistent Performance Improvement:** The proposed instance-level style transfer (Ours) consistently improved the 6D pose estimation performance for *all six target objects* compared to training without any domain adaptation (Non-adaptation). This is evidenced by higher ADD pass rates at the 2cm threshold and increased AUC scores across the board (Table I, Fig. 5).
- **Superiority Over Baseline Methods:** When compared against CycleGAN, DRIT, and CUT, the proposed method demonstrated superior and more consistent performance. For all objects, "Ours" achieved the highest ADD pass rates and AUC scores.
- **Inconsistency of Other Methods:** The alternative style transfer methods showed varied and often inconsistent results. For example, DRIT improved performance for the mustard bottle but resulted in lower AUC scores for the potted meat can, mug, and coffee can compared to non-adaptation. Similarly, CycleGAN and CUT also showed degradations for certain objects (e.g., CycleGAN for water bottle, CUT for bleach cleanser). This indicates a lack of robustness or generalizability of these global style transfer methods across diverse objects and scene complexities.
- **Handling Challenging Materials:** The method successfully improved pose estimation for transparent (water bottle) and shiny (coffee can) objects. Notably, significant improvements in AUC were observed for objects with initially large sim2real gaps, such as the water bottle (AUC increase of 20.08) and the bleach cleanser (AUC increase of 21.01).

**C. Qualitative Evaluation of Style Transfer:**
Visual comparisons of the style-transferred images (Fig. 7) provided qualitative evidence complementing the quantitative results.
- **Realistic and Detail-Preserving Transfers:** The proposed method generated images that appeared more realistic and retained object shape and texture details effectively (Fig. 7 (f)).
- **Deficiencies in Other Methods:** In contrast, the competing methods exhibited visual artifacts:
 - CycleGAN produced visible artifacts around objects (e.g., third row of Fig. 7 (c)).
 - DRIT showed style mismatches (e.g., mug in second row of Fig. 7 (d)) and instances of collapsing shapes.
 - CUT also demonstrated issues such as collapsing shapes (e.g., second row of Fig. 7 (e)).
 These qualitative inconsistencies are hypothesized by the authors to contribute to the poorer 6D pose estimation performance observed with these methods.
- **Bridging Significant Gaps:** The approach demonstrated its ability to bridge considerable sim2real appearance gaps, such as those arising from complex transparent material settings for the water bottle, or discrepancies between 3D model labels and real-world packaging (e.g., the bleach cleanser's label) (Fig. 8).
- **Scaling Operation Impact:** An appendix analysis confirmed that the pre-inference scaling operation within the TransNet workflow contributed to preserving texture details, resulting in less blurry text on object labels (Fig. 9).
- **Multi-Class Adaptability:** The method's ability to perform style transfer for scenes containing multiple instances of different object classes, using individually trained TransNets, was also demonstrated (Fig. 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a robust and automated approach to bridge the sim2real domain gap for 6D pose estimation, with several implications for robotics and computer vision:

- **Reduced Annotation Burden:** The most significant impact is the substantial reduction in the need for labor-intensive and costly manual 6D pose annotations on real-world data. By automating the generation of weakly-paired images and focusing on unsupervised domain adaptation, the method streamlines the data preparation phase for robotic perception systems.
- **Enhanced Robotic Robustness:** The consistent improvement in 6D pose estimation performance across diverse and challenging objects (including transparent and shiny materials) enables robots to operate more reliably and effectively in unstructured and varied real-world environments like homes, where object characteristics are highly variable. This directly supports the progression towards more capable and deployable robot systems for tasks such as object manipulation.
- **Improved Synthetic Data Utility:** The proposed instance-level style transfer elevates the utility of synthetic data. Instead of being limited by the domain gap, synthetic data can now be transformed to closely mimic real-world appearance, maximizing the benefits of its low-cost generation and perfect annotations for training deep learning models.
- **Cost-Effective Environment Adaptation:** Given existing 3D models and camera poses, the pipeline offers a low-cost, automated mechanism to adapt perception networks to new target environments. This facilitates rapid deployment and re-calibration of robot perception systems in different settings without extensive manual effort.
- **Generalizability in Complex Scenes:** By focusing on instance-level style transfer, the method mitigates issues of inconsistencies and loss of detail often encountered by global style transfer techniques in complex scenes featuring multiple, texture-rich objects. This approach maintains object-specific attributes crucial for accurate pose estimation.
- **Addressing Real-World Discrepancies:** The demonstrated capability to bridge appearance gaps stemming from imperfect 3D models (e.g., outdated labels, simplified material properties) enhances the practicality of sim2real approaches, acknowledging that perfect 3D models are not always available or up-to-date.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a strong foundation for future advancements. The authors identify extensions such as background style transfer and integration with real2sim techniques (e.g., photogrammetry, NeRF) to automatically generate 3D models from real images. Such integrations could further automate the entire pipeline, from 3D model acquisition to adapted synthetic data generation, minimizing manual intervention across the perception stack.
