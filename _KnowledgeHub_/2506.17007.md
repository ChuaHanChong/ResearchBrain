# Discrete Compositional Generation via General Soft Operators and Robust Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A robust reinforcement learning framework called Trajectory General Mellowmax (TGM) was developed by researchers at Université de Montréal and Mila for discrete compositional generation, introducing a General Mellowmax operator to manage reward uncertainty. TGM achieves peakier sampling distributions that concentrate on high-reward candidates while maintaining diversity, outperforming Generative Flow Networks in large-scale biological sequence design tasks by nearly doubling mean mode reward.

## Problem

- Searching vast combinatorial spaces for high-quality objects (e.g., in protein design) is challenging due to the size and the cost of experimental evaluation.
- Existing Generative Flow Networks (GFNs) for discrete compositional generation often distribute probability mass too broadly across candidates, potentially obscuring truly optimal solutions due to reliance on uncertain proxy reward functions.
- Standard RL regularization methods, like Shannon entropy, induce reward uncertainty sets that may not adequately capture the full range of uncertainty in proxy rewards, especially regarding potential decreases in reward.

## Method

- The problem is re-formulated as a ==robust reinforcement learning== task that compositionally models reward uncertainty at each step of object generation.
- A General Mellowmax (GM) regularizer is introduced, unifying and interpolating between existing soft RL operators to enable flexible control over sampling distribution peakiness.
- The Trajectory General Mellowmax (TGM) algorithm is proposed, which integrates the GM operator with novel trajectory-level constraints and a VarGrad objective for effective training in discrete compositional generation.

## Results

- On synthetic tasks (TF-Bind-8, Bit Sequence), TGM variants with q > 0 generated peakier sampling distributions, found more ground-truth modes, and produced samples closer to optimal modes than GFN.
- In real-world biological sequence design tasks (UTR, AMP, GFP), TGM consistently matched or surpassed GFN and other baselines, notably doubling the mean mode reward over GFN in the large GFP domain.
- TGM demonstrated enhanced robustness to hyperparameter variations (learning rates, reward exponent, inverse temperature) compared to GFN and other RL methods, particularly with q=1 configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Regularized reinforcement learning is theoretically linked to reward-robust MDPs via Fenchel-robust formulation, revealing the specific reward uncertainty sets induced by different regularization functions.
> - Shannon entropy regularization, used in many existing methods, only offers robustness to increases in reward, whereas General Mellowmax with q > 0 provides robustness to both increases and decreases, better suited for uncertain proxy rewards.
> - The General Mellowmax operator enables the generation of "peakier" sampling distributions, effectively concentrating probability mass on high-reward candidates while maintaining exploration for diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in scientific discovery: efficiently identifying promising candidates from an exponentially large set of discrete, compositionally constructed objects (e.g., proteins, molecules, DNA sequences). Traditional approaches often rely on expert knowledge or computational filtering guided by proxy reward functions.

Recent advancements have increasingly leveraged reinforcement learning (RL) to navigate these vast search spaces. A notable family of methods, Generative Flow Networks (GFNs), has gained prominence. GFNs learn an amortized policy to sample objects proportionally to the exponential of their reward, a formulation connected to maximum-entropy RL and intended to promote diversity in generated samples. GFNs have shown success in various applications, including protein design, drug discovery, and material design.

However, the paper identifies a limitation within this GFN framework: in large combinatorial spaces, sampling proportional to reward can lead to "overly diverse, suboptimal candidates." This occurs because the cumulative probability of a vast number of low-reward objects can overshadow the probability of a small set of high-reward objects, even if the individual high-reward objects have significantly higher values. This phenomenon restricts the effectiveness of GFNs in pinpointing truly optimal candidates while maintaining desirable diversity.

Concurrently, the field of RL has explored various forms of regularization, leading to "soft RL" methods that balance reward maximization with entropy maximization to encourage exploration and robustness. Operators like the soft Bellman operator (related to Shannon entropy regularization), the mellowmax operator, and the soft mellowmax operator represent different approaches to this balance. Furthermore, regularized MDPs have been shown to have connections to robust MDPs, where the goal is to optimize performance under uncertainty about the environment's parameters, such as the reward function. This connection provides a theoretical underpinning for understanding the implicit robustness properties of regularized RL algorithms.

This research aims to integrate and extend these concepts by introducing a more generalized soft RL operator and algorithm. It seeks to provide a more flexible trade-off between the quality and diversity of generated candidates, moving beyond the fixed trade-offs of existing operators, and to offer a robust RL interpretation of its approach, thereby contributing to both the practical application of generative models in scientific discovery and the theoretical understanding of regularization in RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper outlines several key objectives and motivations, driven by observed limitations in current approaches to discrete compositional generation for scientific discovery.

The overarching motivation is to overcome the "major bottleneck in scientific discovery" that involves narrowing down an exponentially large set of potential objects to a small, high-quality, and diverse set of promising candidates. Current RL-guided filtering methods, particularly those employing entropy regularization such as Generative Flow Networks (GFNs), aim to learn samplers that generate diverse candidates highly rated by a proxy function.

The specific objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **To address the issue of generating overly diverse, suboptimal candidates in large search spaces:** The paper identifies that GFNs, by aiming to sample objects proportionally to `e^Φ(x)` (where `Φ(x)` is the proxy reward), can lead to a policy that assigns substantial probability mass to a huge number of suboptimal objects. In exponentially large search spaces, even if individual suboptimal objects have low rewards, their sheer number can cause their cumulative probability to dominate that of a few high-reward objects (as illustrated in Figure 1, Left, and Figure 2). This means that despite aiming for diversity, the resulting sampler might not effectively concentrate on the truly high-quality regions of the search space. The motivation is to develop a method that can "better target peakier sampling distributions" without completely sacrificing diversity.

2. **To introduce a novel unified operator that offers more flexible control over the quality-diversity trade-off:** Existing soft RL operators, such as the soft Bellman operator and the soft mellowmax operator, offer specific ways to balance reward maximization and diversity (through entropy regularization). The soft Bellman operator is prone to "accumulation," where the value of many low-reward paths can overwhelm a single high-reward path. Conversely, the mellowmax operator can suffer from "dilution," where the high reward of an object deep in a compositional tree gets averaged out by lower-reward objects closer to the root. While the soft mellowmax operator provides some improvement, there remains a need for a more general framework that allows for a flexible interpolation between these behaviors. The motivation is to create an operator that can smoothly control the tension between accumulation and dilution, enabling a more tailored approach to discovering high-quality, diverse candidates.

3. **To provide a novel, robust RL interpretation of the filtering process:** The paper aims to reframe the problem of optimizing proxy rewards in the presence of uncertainty. It suggests that the regularization employed in soft RL can be interpreted as robustness to a "compositional form of uncertainty" in the proxy function. This means acknowledging that the proxy's evaluation might differ from the true, unobservable evaluation of a candidate. The motivation is to gain a deeper theoretical understanding of why regularization is effective and to design algorithms that are inherently robust to these real-world uncertainties. This robust perspective also aims to lead to "more interpretable" reward uncertainty sets compared to those associated with standard entropy regularization or GFNs (as analyzed in Figure 3).

These motivations collectively drive the development of the "general mellowmax" (GM) operator and the "trajectory general mellowmax" (TGM) algorithm, which are designed to address the aforementioned limitations and provide a more effective and interpretable framework for discrete compositional generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on two main contributions: the development of a novel, unified soft RL operator called General Mellowmax (GM), and the derivation of a robust reinforcement learning interpretation of this framework, leading to the Trajectory General Mellowmax (TGM) algorithm.

#### 3.1. General Mellowmax (GM) Operator

The paper starts by analyzing existing soft RL operators within the context of Discrete Compositional Processes (DCPs), which are modeled as Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with deterministic transitions and terminal rewards.
- **Soft Bellman Operator (GFNs):** This operator uses the logsumexp of Q-values, corresponding to Shannon entropy regularization. While promoting diversity, it suffers from "accumulation" in large search spaces, where the value of a high-reward object can be "drowned out" by the collective value of numerous suboptimal objects (e.g., in Figure 1).
- **Mellowmax Operator:** This operator uses the logmeanexp of Q-values. It avoids accumulation but is prone to "dilution," where high rewards can be averaged down by lower-reward options deeper in the compositional tree.
- **Soft Mellowmax Operator (SMM):** An extension of mellowmax that weights elements of the sum using a softmax of Q-values, partially addressing the dilution issue.

The authors propose the **General Mellowmax (GM) regularizer** (Eq. 7) as an interpolating operator. This regularizer combines the Shannon entropy (`-H(π_s)`) and the KL-divergence to a target distribution (`KL(π_s, d_s)`) with a hyperparameter `q ∈`:
`Ω^q_{d_s}(π_s):= (1/ω) [q KL(π_s, d_s) + (1-q)(-H(π_s))]`
Here, `ω` is a regularization coefficient, and `d_s` is a reference distribution. The paper specifies using `d_s = softmax(αQ_s)` (where `α` is another inverse temperature parameter), making the regularizer depend on the current Q-values.
- `q = 0` recovers the standard entropy-regularized/GFN operators.
- `q = 1` recovers the soft mellowmax operator.
This interpolation allows for a flexible trade-off between accumulation and dilution, with `q ∈ (0, 1)` balancing these issues to achieve lower worst-case bounds on `|∆T_s|` (deviation from max Q-value), as summarized in Table 1. The associated Bellman operator for GM is derived as `Ω^q,*_α(Q_s) = (1/ω) log⟨softmax(αQ_s))^q, e^(ωQ_s)⟩` (Eq. 8).

#### 3.2. Trajectory General Mellowmax (TGM) Algorithm

To make the GM operator practical for DCP tasks, the authors leverage **trajectory-level constraints**. These constraints have been shown to be crucial for credit assignment in terminal-reward-only tasks, as they propagate signal across multi-step trajectories (e.g., Trajectory Balance in GFNs).

The core of TGM is a **novel, equivalent trajectory constraint** for the General Mellowmax operator (Theorem 4.1, Eq. 10). This theorem establishes an equivalence between satisfying the state-wise optimality condition (Eq. 9) and satisfying a specific trajectory balance equation across all full trajectories in a DCP. The trajectory constraint for a full trajectory `s_0 ->... -> s_n = x` relates the initial value `v*_0` and a sum of state-action terms (`g(Q^θ_s_i, a_i) - r(s_i, a_i)`) to the terminal reward `r(x)`.

The practical algorithm, **Trajectory General Mellowmax (TGM)**, integrates three design decisions:
1. **GM Operator with `d_s = softmax(αQ_s)`:** This choice allows for both on-policy and off-policy training and avoids the need for a separate target distribution.
2. **Novel Trajectory Constraint:** TGM aims to satisfy the derived trajectory constraint from Theorem 4.1.
3. **Single Q-network Training:** A single network `Q_θ` of Q-values is trained by minimizing a VarGrad objective `L_{TGM,Q_θ}(τ)` (Richter et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2023), which is shown to be equivalent to minimizing the trajectory constraint (Eq. 10). From `Q_θ`, the optimal policy `softmax([αq + ω]Q^θ_s)` is easily computed. This setup is stated to be efficient and equivalent to GFN training when `q=0` and `ω=1`.

#### 3.3. Robust RL Interpretation

The paper also provides a theoretical framework interpreting regularized operators through the lens of **robust reinforcement learning**.
- **Equivalence:** Building on prior work, the authors reformulate Derman et al. (2021)'s theorem to explicitly map reward-robust MDPs to regularized MDPs (Theorem 5.1). This theorem states that for a convex regularization function `Ω_s`, there exists an equivalent reward uncertainty set `R_s` such that the robust Bellman operator is identical to the regularized Bellman operator.
- **Compositional Uncertainty:** Crucially, for DCPs, the authors model reward uncertainty as compositional, meaning the difference `δ(x)` between the proxy `Φ(x)` and the true reward `r*(x)` is decomposed into a sum of perturbations `δ_i[a_i]` occurring at each step of the generation process (Eq. 12). This "state-rectangularity" of uncertainty sets makes them independent of the executed policy.
- **Analysis of Uncertainty Sets:** The paper analyzes the specific reward uncertainty sets `R_s` induced by different regularizers (Shannon entropy, soft mellowmax, and GM, as shown in Figure 3 and detailed in Appendix D).
 - **Entropy-regularized (GFN):** The uncertainty set never contains `Φ(x)` and implies robustness only to *increases* in reward. In multi-step scenarios, these sets shift further away from the proxy, suggesting a preference for longer, potentially suboptimal, objects.
 - **Soft Mellowmax:** This operator's uncertainty set contains `Φ(x)` and is robust to *decreases* in reward for one object, offering a more meaningful notion of robustness.
 - **General Mellowmax:** GM's uncertainty sets interpolate between these extremes. While not always containing `Φ(x)` for `q ∈ (0,1)`, they contain points corresponding to decreases in reward, offering a balanced and more interpretable robustness.

This robust interpretation provides a formal motivation for the GM operator, highlighting how it implicitly handles uncertainty in the proxy reward function more effectively than standard GFNs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental section aims to answer three primary questions regarding the impact of the `q` parameter, performance in biological design tasks, and hyperparameter robustness.

#### 4.1. Impact of `q` in Small and Synthetic Environments [Q1]

- **TF-Bind-8 Task:** This task involves generating short DNA sequences. Comparisons of optimal sampling distributions (Figure 4, Left) showed that TGM variants, especially with `q > 0`, concentrate significantly more probability mass on the upper quantiles of the reward distribution compared to GFN (`q = 0`). This indicates a peakier sampling behavior, effectively targeting higher-quality candidates.
- **Bit Sequence Task:** In this larger synthetic task (2^120 possible sequences), TGM demonstrated improved performance in discovering diverse modes.
 - **Number of Modes Found:** TGM with `q > 0` found a comparable or higher number of modes than GFN (`q = 0`). This indicates that increasing peakiness does not necessarily harm the ability to find different modes (Figure 4, Middle).
 - **Average Minimum Distance to Modes:** TGM with `q > 0` consistently found samples that were closer to the true modes, as indicated by a lower average minimum distance (Figure 4, Right). This suggests that TGM achieves higher-quality samples within the discovered modes.

#### 4.2. Biological Sequence Design [Q2]

The effectiveness of TGM was evaluated on three real-world biological sequence design tasks, using proxy reward models trained on actual datasets:
- **5' Untranslated Region Sequence (UTR):** Goal is to maximize predicted gene expression.
- **Antimicrobial Peptide (AMP):** Goal is to discover peptides with antimicrobial properties.
- **Green Fluorescent Protein (GFP):** Goal is to discover proteins with high predicted fluorescence.

**Performance Comparison (Table 2):**
- **TGM vs. Baselines:** Across all three tasks, various configurations of TGM either matched or outperformed the baselines, including GFNs, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Significant Improvement in GFP:** The performance difference was most notable in the GFP task, which is the largest of the three environments. TGM (specifically `q = 0.5`) achieved an average mode reward of 2.95, significantly higher than GFN's 1.90 and SAC's 0.66. This suggests TGM's scalability benefits in larger, more complex search spaces.
- **Interpolation Benefits:** The best-performing `q` values in these tasks were often `q ∈ (0, 1)` (e.g., `q=0.75` for UTR, `q=0.5` for GFP), indicating that the interpolated GM operator effectively balances accumulation and dilution for superior results. In AMP, `q=0.75` achieved the highest average mode reward (10.43).
- **RL Baselines:** SAC and PPO generally performed poorly, hypothesized to be due to difficulties with credit assignment in tasks with only terminal rewards. PPO showed better performance in AMP, where variable-length sequences are allowed.

#### 4.3. Hyperparameter Robustness [Q3]

The study assessed the sensitivity of different algorithms to variations in hyperparameters (learning rates, `β`, and `ω`).
- **TGM Stability:** TGM variants demonstrated relative robustness to these hyperparameter changes (Figure 5). Their mean average mode reward was consistently higher than GFN across different `q` values and environments.
- **Baseline Sensitivity:** SAC showed stable but poor performance. PPO was highly sensitive to hyperparameters, with its best runs achieving strong performance in AMP but overall having high variability.
- **GFN with High `β` (Appendix B.1):** While increasing `β` (up to 8192) in GFNs could improve performance in GFP, it did not consistently lead to better results in UTR and AMP, and even in GFP, GFNs with very high `β` did not reach the best TGM performance. This indicates that TGM explores meaningfully different, higher-quality peaky distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions with potential broad impact, particularly in the domain of AI-driven scientific discovery.

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation in Generative Models:** The work directly tackles the identified issue of GFNs generating overly diverse, suboptimal candidates in vast discrete search spaces. By introducing the General Mellowmax (GM) operator, the paper provides a mechanism to better target peakier sampling distributions, thereby improving the quality of generated candidates without sacrificing their diversity. This is crucial for scientific applications where identifying a small set of truly high-potential objects is paramount.

2. **Novel and Flexible Algorithmic Framework:** The development of the General Mellowmax (GM) operator as an interpolating regularizer unifies existing soft RL operators (soft Bellman, mellowmax, soft mellowmax) under a single framework. This offers a more flexible and tunable approach to balancing the trade-offs between "accumulation" and "dilution" of value in compositional generation. The resulting algorithm, Trajectory General Mellowmax (TGM), leverages the benefits of trajectory-level constraints for improved credit assignment in terminal-reward-only tasks, which is a common characteristic of scientific discovery problems. The empirical results demonstrate that TGM consistently matches or outperforms strong baselines like GFNs, SAC, and PPO, especially in large and complex biological sequence design tasks, highlighting its practical utility and scalability.

3. **Enhanced Theoretical Understanding through Robust RL:** The paper provides a novel and interpretable robust RL perspective on regularization in discrete compositional processes. By showing the equivalence between specific regularizers and well-defined reward uncertainty sets, the work offers a deeper theoretical understanding of why certain regularization strategies are effective. The analysis of uncertainty sets reveals that the proposed GM operator induces more meaningful and balanced robustness properties compared to the standard entropy regularization used in GFNs, which primarily offers robustness against reward increases. This theoretical grounding can inform future algorithm design and provide a clearer understanding of how generative models handle uncertainty in proxy reward functions.

4. **Accelerating Scientific Discovery:** The primary potential impact lies in accelerating scientific discovery. By enabling the generation of higher-quality and appropriately diverse candidate proteins, molecules, or other discrete objects, TGM could significantly reduce the cost and time associated with experimental validation. This could lead to faster breakthroughs in areas such as drug discovery, materials science, and protein engineering, where the efficient navigation of immense combinatorial spaces is a major bottleneck.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The analytical techniques employed in deriving the GM operator and its robust interpretation, along with the formulation of general trajectory constraints, establish a foundation for further research. This includes exploring more sophisticated regularization functions, their corresponding robust interpretations, and developing new algorithms that can leverage these insights for even more efficient and reliable candidate generation in diverse scientific domains.

In conclusion, this research offers a substantial advance in the field of discrete compositional generation by providing a theoretically grounded, empirically validated, and more flexible approach to identifying high-quality and diverse candidates for scientific discovery.
