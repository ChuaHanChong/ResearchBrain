# Bayesian Ego-graph Inference for Networked Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Australian Artificial Intelligence Institute at the University of Technology Sydney developed BayesG, a decentralized actor-critic framework for networked multi-agent reinforcement learning that infers dynamic, context-aware interaction graphs using only local observations. This approach achieved higher average episode returns and faster convergence compared to existing baselines across various adaptive traffic signal control benchmarks, demonstrating superior scalability in large-scale scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing networked multi-agent reinforcement learning methods often rely on static communication graphs, leading to inefficient coordination and unnecessary message overhead.
- Methods for learning dynamic interaction graphs typically require global state access and centralized infrastructure, rendering them impractical for real-world decentralized systems.
- Decentralized agents in real-world settings operate under local sensing and communication constraints, necessitating solutions that respect these physical limitations without global supervision.

## Method

- Introduced **BayesG**, a ==decentralized actor-critic== framework enabling agents to learn stochastic, context-aware ego-graphs from their local physical neighborhoods.
- Formulated the learning of latent interaction masks as a Bayesian variational inference problem, approximating the posterior distribution of relevant communication links for each agent.
- Jointly optimized agent policies and variational parameters using an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective, which integrates task feedback into the graph inference process and utilizes Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization.

## Results

- BayesG consistently achieved higher average episode returns and faster convergence rates compared to six baseline methods across five adaptive traffic signal control benchmarks.
- Demonstrated superior scalability in large-scale NewYork traffic scenarios, exhibiting higher asymptotic returns and more stable learning curves than baselines.
- Qualitative analysis and case studies revealed that BayesG learned task-adaptive and directional communication patterns, such as prioritizing communication from less congested areas to more congested ones, effectively reducing traffic congestion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decentralized agents can dynamically adapt their communication structures using only local observations and task feedback, overcoming the limitations of static graphs or centralized dynamic graph learning.
> - Learning context-dependent interaction graphs through Bayesian inference allows for adaptive pruning of irrelevant communication links, leading to more efficient and effective coordination.
> - The dual entropy regularization over both actions and communication structures contributes to the robustness and uncertainty-aware neighbor selection in dynamic multi-agent environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is a framework applied to sequential decision-making in distributed systems across various domains, including autonomous driving, wireless communication, and urban traffic control. A prevalent training approach in MARL is centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE), where a centralized critic utilizes global state information to train decentralized policies. While CTDE methods have demonstrated empirical performance in simulation benchmarks, they typically require access to global observations and a centralized learning infrastructure. This reliance often presents a challenge for real-world applications where agents are geographically distributed and operate under local sensing and communication constraints.

The current work focuses on networked MARL, a scenario where agents interact over a fixed communication graph and are restricted to observing or exchanging information only with proximate neighbors. A notable challenge within existing networked MARL methods is their frequent reliance on static communication graphs, which pre-defines how agents exchange information with local neighbors regardless of the information's contextual relevance. This can lead to inefficiencies such as unnecessary message exchange or performance degradation in dynamic or heterogeneous environments. While some recent CTDE approaches have explored learning dynamic interaction graphs, their applicability is limited in fully decentralized settings due to the global state access requirement.

Prior research in networked MARL has explored several directions:
- **Consensus-based methods** aim to synchronize local value functions through neighborhood averaging, often with assumptions about observability.
- **Network-aware priors** incorporate structural information like distance-based decay or local reward aggregation to promote spatially coherent coordination.
- **Model-based frameworks** facilitate the exchange of predicted trajectories but still operate on static interaction graphs.
- **Communication-based methods** enable information sharing among neighbors, but typically maintain fixed communication topologies throughout training.
- **Sampling strategies** have been introduced to improve scalability by sampling from predefined or uniform distributions at either the environment or agent level.

Relatedly, advances in graph-based MARL and latent structure learning have leveraged mechanisms such as attention or graph neural networks (GNNs) to infer inter-agent dependencies. Variational approaches treat interaction graphs as latent variables, enabling joint optimization of communication structure and policy learning. However, many of these methods, particularly those based on Dec-POMDPs, also assume centralized training with global state and reward signals and permit unrestricted graph rewiring, which diverges from the constraints of real-world decentralized systems.

This research distinguishes itself by addressing the networked MARL scenario within a decentralized framework. It aims to learn dynamic latent interaction masks directly on the environment graph, leveraging only local observations and without requiring global supervision or centralized infrastructure. This approach fundamentally differs from prior sampling strategies by learning context-dependent, per-edge distributions via Bayesian inference, and from CTDE/Dec-POMDP methods by adhering to the physically constrained nature of a Spatiotemporal-MDP.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from the limitations of existing multi-agent reinforcement learning approaches in real-world decentralized systems. Current networked MARL methods often rely on static communication graphs, implying that agents exchange information with all local neighbors regardless of the contextual relevance of that information. This can lead to inefficient coordination, unnecessary message overhead, and potential performance degradation, particularly in dynamic environments such as urban traffic control where congestion patterns evolve over time. While centralized frameworks can learn dynamic interaction graphs, their dependency on global state access and centralized infrastructure renders them impractical for many real-world decentralized deployments.

The paper poses a fundamental question: "Can decentralized agents learn to dynamically adapt their interaction structure using local observations and task feedback?" Addressing this, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Stochastic Graph-based Policy:** To enable agents to condition their decisions on a sampled subgraph from their local physical neighborhood (referred to as an ego-graph). This moves beyond fixed communication structures by allowing agents to stochastically select contextually relevant neighbors.
2. **Formulate Latent Graph Learning as Bayesian Variational Inference:** To model the uncertainty associated with relevant communication links. Each agent infers a binary mask (latent variable) over its local neighborhood, conditioned on the physical subgraph and agent-specific local data. This probabilistic approach is intended to provide a principled method for discovering task-adaptive interaction patterns.
3. **Introduce a Decentralized Actor-Critic Framework (BayesG):** To jointly optimize both the agent's policy and the parameters of the variational distribution for the latent graph. This framework leverages an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective, which integrates the policy learning feedback into the graph inference process.
4. **Facilitate Task-Adaptive and Communication-Efficient Coordination:** By learning to prioritize critical communication links and prune irrelevant ones, BayesG aims to achieve more efficient and effective coordination without requiring global supervision. This is expected to lead to superior performance and scalability in complex, dynamic, and large-scale networked systems.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a decentralized MARL system that can autonomously adapt its interaction topology, ensuring efficient and robust performance in real-world environments with local observability and communication constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, BayesG, is a decentralized actor-critic framework designed for networked MARL that learns sparse, context-aware interaction graphs through variational inference. The approach is structured around a graph-based policy that integrates latent interaction structures into decision-making.

**Graph-based Policy with Latent Interaction Structures:**
Unlike conventional networked A2C methods that condition policies on fixed local observations, BayesG introduces a stochastic graph-based policy. Each agent $i$ samples a binary adjacency matrix $G_{V_i}$ (representing a subgraph) from a learned distribution $\rho(\cdot; \phi_i)$ over its closed neighborhood $V_i$. This sampled graph is constrained to be a subgraph of the environment's physical topology, $G_{V_i} \preceq G_{env,V_i}$, ensuring adherence to physical communication limits. The agent's policy then conditions its action $u_i$ on a graph-conditioned encoding $\tilde{f}_i(s_{V_i}, G_{V_i})$, which processes local state $s_{V_i}$ using the sampled subgraph. This formulation allows agents to dynamically emphasize or de-emphasize communication links based on local context.

**Graph-based A2C Objective:**
The standard A2C training objective is extended to incorporate this graph-conditioned policy. The actor loss $L_{\theta,\phi}$ includes an expectation over the sampled subgraphs $G_{V_i}$. This objective jointly optimizes the policy parameters $\theta_i$ and the variational parameters $\phi_i$ that govern the sampling distribution $\rho$. The loss function includes an entropy regularization term for the action policy to promote exploration.

**BayesG: Variational Latent Ego-graph Inference:**
To learn the stochastic graph distribution $\rho(G_{V_i} | s_{V_i})$, the method models it via a binary mask $Z_i$. This mask is applied element-wise to the physical neighborhood graph $G_{env,V_i}$ to yield an effective subgraph $G^*_i = Z_i \odot G_{env,V_i}$. The learning of this latent edge mask $Z_i$ is formulated as a Bayesian inference problem. The posterior distribution $p(Z_i | G_{env,V_i}, D_i)$ is approximated by a variational distribution $q(Z_i; \phi_i)$, chosen as a factorized Bernoulli distribution over individual edges, $q(Z_i; \phi_i) = \prod_{j \in N_i} \text{Bern}(z_{ij}; \sigma(\phi_{ij}))$. Learnable logits $\phi_i$ determine the probability of each edge. The Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization trick is employed to enable gradient-based learning through the discrete sampling of $Z_i$.

**ELBO Objective:**
The variational parameters $\phi_i$ are optimized by minimizing the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the approximate posterior $q$ and the true posterior $p$. This minimization is equivalent to maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). The negative ELBO, $L_{ELBO}$, consists of two main parts: the expected graph-conditioned policy loss $E_{q(Z_i;\phi_i)}[-L_{\theta,\phi}]$ and a KL divergence term $-\sum_{j \in N_i} KL(q(Z_{ij}; \phi_{ij}) \parallel p(Z_{ij}))$. The policy loss provides task feedback for learning relevant structures, while the KL term acts as a regularization that balances policy performance with a prior distribution $p(Z_{ij})$ (e.g., a Bernoulli prior with a retention bias $\lambda$). This formulation connects to maximum entropy RL, where the term $-\log q(Z_i; \phi_i)$ regularizes the variational distribution over communication structures, preventing premature collapse to deterministic graphs and promoting uncertainty-aware neighbor selection.

**Critic Loss and Total Objective:**
The actor-based ELBO is complemented by a standard A2C critic loss $L_{Critic}$, which computes the squared difference between the critic's output and the estimated return. The overall training objective combines these components across all agents: $L_{total} = \sum_{i \in V} (-L_{ELBO} + L_{Critic})$.

**Input Representation and Execution:**
For BayesG, the agent-specific input representation $\tilde{s}_i$ (fed into policy and critic networks) is constructed via masked message passing over the effective graph $A^*_i$. This is achieved using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), specifically Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), applied to observations, policy features (action distributions), and trajectory features (RNN hidden states) of the agent's neighborhood. During decentralized execution, each agent independently samples its subgraph based on its local observations, encodes its neighborhood via GNNs over the masked graph, and selects actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BayesG was conducted across five adaptive traffic signal control (ATSC) benchmark scenarios, including synthetic grids and real-world city layouts (Monaco, NewYork33, NewYork51, NewYork167) within the SUMO microscopic traffic simulator. BayesG's performance was compared against six multi-agent actor-critic baselines, encompassing both non-communicative methods (IA2C, ConseNet, FPrint, LToS) and explicit communication protocols (CommNet, NeurComm).

**Performance Comparison:**
- **Superiority and Convergence:** BayesG consistently achieved higher average episode returns and demonstrated faster convergence rates compared to all baseline methods across all five ATSC environments. This was particularly evident in early training stages on ATSC_Grid and Monaco.
- **Scalability:** In the large-scale NewYork scenarios (NewYork33, NewYork51, NewYork167), BayesG exhibited higher asymptotic returns and more stable learning curves. In contrast, baseline methods often experienced performance plateaus or instability, indicating BayesG's effectiveness in scaling to environments with a large number of decentralized agents.

**Qualitative Analysis on Grid Environment:**
- Visual comparisons of traffic congestion on the ATSC_Grid map indicated that BayesG produced smoother traffic flows with significantly reduced congestion (thinner, less red road segments) compared to IA2C, CommNet, and NeurComm. This suggests that the learned task-adaptive communication structures contributed to more effective coordination.

**Case Study: Interpreting Learned Interaction Structures:**
- A case study on the ATSC_Grid map at a specific time step (1400) revealed that BayesG learned to prioritize communication from intersections with lower congestion toward more congested regions. For instance, when specific areas experienced congestion, upstream intersections increased their coordination weights to regulate inflow, thereby alleviating downstream pressure. This demonstrated the model's ability to infer decentralized, context-aware, and directional cooperation patterns that adapt dynamically to evolving traffic conditions, aligning with principles of effective traffic management.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Graph Masking Strategies:** Experiments comparing different subgraph construction strategies showed that `Random Masking` severely degraded performance. `No Masking` (using the raw environment topology) performed worse than the `Learned Mask` strategy employed by BayesG. This indicated that the adaptive pruning of irrelevant links by the learned mask was crucial for performance.
- **Mask Input Features:** The study on features used to generate the variational mask (state, trajectory, policy, or a combination) indicated that trajectory-based features yielded stronger performance than state-only features, suggesting the benefit of incorporating richer temporal context. Policy-based masks also provided meaningful signals. The optimal performance was achieved when the learned mask utilized a combination of these feature types, as implemented in BayesG.

**Training Loss Analysis:**
- Analysis of training loss curves indicated stable convergence behavior for BayesG across different network scales. The policy loss generally stabilized, and the mask entropy term gradually decreased, transitioning from exploratory to more deterministic communication structures. The ELBO loss exhibited trends consistent with balancing policy confidence and KL regularization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning by introducing a decentralized framework that addresses key limitations of existing approaches. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Addressing Realistic Decentralized Constraints:** BayesG overcomes the impracticality of centralized learning for dynamic graph inference and the limitations of static communication graphs in networked MARL. By operating purely on local observations and respecting fixed physical topologies, it offers a solution more aligned with real-world decentralized systems such as traffic networks, smart grids, or sensor fields.
- **Adaptive and Efficient Coordination:** The framework enables decentralized agents to learn context-aware, task-adaptive, and communication-efficient interaction structures. This adaptivity, achieved through Bayesian variational inference of latent ego-graphs, allows agents to dynamically prioritize critical communication links and prune irrelevant ones, which can significantly reduce communication overhead and improve coordination quality in dynamic environments.
- **Enhanced Performance and Scalability:** The consistent superior performance, faster convergence, and improved stability demonstrated in large-scale traffic control benchmarks suggest that BayesG offers a robust and scalable solution for complex networked systems. Its ability to effectively manage high-dimensional decentralized settings by suppressing uninformative edges is a notable advantage.
- **Interpretability of Emergent Behavior:** The learned latent graphs provide insights into the emergent coordination strategies. The case studies illustrate how agents dynamically adjust their communication patterns in response to local conditions (e.g., directing coordination towards congested areas), offering a degree of interpretability into the learned decentralized decision-making process.
- **Principled Theoretical Foundation:** By integrating Bayesian inference into MARL, the work provides a probabilistic approach to uncertainty-aware neighbor selection. The dual entropy regularization over both actions and communication structures contributes to the robustness of the method in dynamic and non-stationary environments.
- **Broader Applicability and Future Directions:** The proposed methodology offers a principled framework for structure-aware cooperation applicable to a wider range of networked systems beyond traffic control, including logistics, robotics swarms, and other distributed control problems. It also opens avenues for future research into areas such as dynamic environment graph adaptation (beyond fixed topologies), reasoning over longer-range dependencies, and further optimization of computational efficiency and hyperparameter robustness.

In summary, BayesG represents a step towards more autonomous, efficient, and interpretable multi-agent systems capable of operating effectively in constrained, decentralized real-world settings.
