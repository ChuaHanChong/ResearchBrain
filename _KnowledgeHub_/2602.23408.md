# Demystifying Action Space Design for Robotic Manipulation Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> This research systematically investigated the impact of action space design on robotic manipulation policy learning, leveraging over 13,000 real-world rollouts and diverse platforms. It established data-driven guidelines, finding that chunk-wise delta temporal actions consistently outperform step-wise, and identified trade-offs between joint-space and task-space spatial abstractions based on task complexity and generalization requirements.

## Problem

- The field of robotic manipulation lacks a systematic understanding and consensus on action space design, with choices often based on ad-hoc heuristics.
- Unstandardized control interfaces impede reproducibility and hinder the development of generalist foundation models capable of transferring knowledge across different robot embodiments.
- Suboptimal action space design becomes a critical bottleneck as research scales to large-scale generalist robot models, impacting policy optimization and stability.

## Method

- Conducted a large-scale, systematic empirical study by formalizing action space components along spatial (joint-space vs. task-space) and temporal (absolute vs. delta, action chunking) abstraction axes.
- Evaluated policies across diverse hardware (single-arm, dual-arm, different kinematics) and simulation platforms, using multiple model architectures including regression, flow-matching, and a foundation model.
- Performed over 13,000 real-world rollouts and trained more than 500 models, conducting grid searches across data scale, model expressiveness, and training duration to derive robust design guidelines.

## Results

- Chunk-wise delta actions consistently outperformed step-wise delta by over **10%** in real-world tasks, with delta actions performing optimally at shorter horizons (e.g., k=30) and absolute actions at longer ones (e.g., k=60).
- Delta temporal abstraction consistently surpassed absolute abstraction across all tested platforms, tasks, and model variations (regression and flow-matching policies).
- Joint-space control demonstrated overall superiority, especially when combined with flow-matching generative policies; however, task-space (EEF) representations showed a pronounced advantage in cross-embodiment and transfer learning scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implementation nuances, particularly regarding action chunking, are decisive; chunk-wise delta actions consistently outperform step-wise delta due to superior error propagation properties.
> - Delta temporal abstraction generally proves superior to absolute abstraction by offering a more tractable inductive bias for policy learning, though optimal chunking horizons vary.
> - The choice between joint-space and task-space spatial abstraction involves a trade-off: joint-space benefits from powerful generative models and scales well with resources, while task-space offers better generalization across different robot embodiments.
