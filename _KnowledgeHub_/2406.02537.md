# TopViewRS: Vision-Language Models as Top-View Spatial Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Cambridge introduce TOPVIEWRS, a novel multi-scale benchmark and dataset for evaluating Vision-Language Models' top-view spatial reasoning, revealing that current state-of-the-art models perform unsatisfactorily and show a substantial gap compared to human performance across various spatial tasks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack comprehensive and fine-grained evaluation of their spatial reasoning capabilities, particularly from a top-view perspective.
- Prior spatial reasoning evaluations primarily use first-person views, which introduce occlusions and perspective distortions, making it difficult to disentangle specific spatial abilities.
- There was no standardized, fine-grained benchmark specifically designed for top-view spatial reasoning that differentiates between basic perception and complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- Developed a ==hierarchical taxonomy== of nine top-view spatial reasoning tasks, ranging from basic recognition and localization to complex static and dynamic relational reasoning.
- Created ==TOPVIEWRS==, a dataset of 11,384 multiple-choice questions featuring both photo-realistic and abstract semantic top-view maps of indoor scenes from the ==Matterport3D== dataset.
- Evaluated 10 state-of-the-art open- and closed-source Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using this benchmark, establishing human performance baselines and investigating ==Chain-of-Thought prompting==.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLMs, including large closed-source models, achieved average Exact Match scores below **50%** across the benchmark, performing best on recognition and localization tasks but struggling with complex static and dynamic reasoning.
- A substantial performance gap exists between VLMs and human annotators, with humans achieving over **90%** accuracy and top VLMs lagging by more than **50%** on average in spatial awareness.
- Chain-of-Thought prompting improved performance for GPT-4V and Gemini on Static Spatial Reasoning tasks by an average of **5.82%** (**6.50%** on realistic maps, **5.14%** on semantic maps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Increasing VLM model size does not consistently improve top-view spatial reasoning, challenging traditional scaling laws observed in other VLM capabilities.
> - VLMs struggle to effectively leverage abstract semantic maps for complex tasks, despite their simplified visual information, potentially due to out-of-distribution training data or difficulty integrating explicit symbolic cues.
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting significantly boosts VLM performance on complex static spatial reasoning tasks, suggesting that explicit step-by-step reasoning can unlock latent spatial abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "TOPVIEWRS" is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which have emerged as powerful successors to Large Language Models (LLMs) by integrating visual perception with linguistic reasoning. VLMs like LLaVA, GPT-4V, and Gemini have demonstrated impressive capabilities across various multimodal tasks, including visual question answering, image captioning, and object grounding.

However, a critical area of VLM capability that remains underexplored and often conflated with other skills is **spatial reasoning**. Spatial reasoning is fundamental for intelligent agents to understand, interact with, and navigate the physical world. Prior research into VLM spatial reasoning has predominantly focused on the *first-person perspective*, mimicking human-eye views of environments. While valuable, this perspective often introduces complexities like occlusions and perspective distortions that can make disentangling specific spatial abilities challenging.

This work addresses a significant gap by specifically investigating **top-view spatial reasoning**. The authors argue that the top-view (or bird's-eye view) offers several advantages:
1. **Natural Alignment:** It is a typical perspective for reading and reasoning over maps and floor plans, a common human task.
2. **Information Density:** A single top-view image can encapsulate a wealth of information about scene layout, object locations, and their relationships without perspective distortion, making it inherently complex yet interpretable for analysis.
3. **Controlled Evaluation:** It enables a more controlled experimental framework, particularly for indoor scenes, allowing for the disentanglement and fine-grained evaluation of distinct spatial reasoning abilities.

Existing work involving top-view images has largely been confined to niche applications such as autonomous driving, person re-identification, or embodied dialogue for localization, often without a comprehensive evaluation of fundamental spatial reasoning within VLMs. Critically, these prior efforts did not provide the fine-grained, controllable analysis of basic spatial reasoning abilities that "TOPVIEWRS" introduces. The paper also differentiates itself from text-only spatial reasoning benchmarks by grounding language in rich visual contexts, moving beyond synthetic, grid-based scenarios to realistic indoor environments. By providing a structured benchmark and disentangling perception from complex reasoning, this research offers a novel and crucial contribution to understanding and advancing the spatial intelligence of VLMs, paving the way for more robust embodied AI and navigation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to comprehensively study and evaluate the capability of state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to understand and reason over spatial relations from a top-view perspective. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing an Underexplored VLM Capability:** While VLMs have shown impressive general multimodal abilities, their specific spatial reasoning capabilities, particularly from a top-view, remain largely unattested and underexplored. The authors emphasize that top-view reasoning is crucial for tasks like localization and navigation for both human and autonomous agents.
2. **Enabling Controlled and Disentangled Evaluation:** Unlike conventional first-person perspectives, the top-view offers a unique advantage for controlled and interpretable experimental frameworks. This allows researchers to clearly disentangle and assess different facets of spatial reasoning, such as recognizing objects, localizing them, and understanding their relative positions, without confounding factors like perspective or occlusion.
3. **Highlighting the Complexity of Top-View Maps:** Top-view maps encapsulate a rich amount of information about scenes, locations, and object relationships within a single image, making them a challenging yet ideal testbed for VLM spatial intelligence.
4. **Developing a Robust Benchmark:** The existing landscape lacks a standardized, fine-grained benchmark for top-view spatial reasoning that avoids conflating basic perception with higher-level reasoning. The authors aim to fill this void by creating a novel dataset and a structured evaluation framework.

To achieve these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper include:
- Defining a clear taxonomy of top-view spatial reasoning tasks, ranging from basic perception to dynamic reasoning along navigation paths, encompassing nine distinct sub-tasks.
- Collecting and releasing **TOPVIEWRS (Top-View Reasoning in Space)**, a natural, high-quality, and multi-scale dataset comprising 11,384 multiple-choice questions with both photo-realistic and semantic top-view maps of real-world indoor scenes.
- Conducting extensive evaluations of 10 representative open- and closed-source VLMs on the TOPVIEWRS benchmark to identify current performance levels and limitations.
- Quantifying the performance gap between VLMs and human annotators to establish a clear target for future research.
- Investigating the effectiveness of reasoning strategies, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, in boosting VLM spatial reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a structured approach to evaluate VLM top-view spatial reasoning, encompassing task definition, dataset creation, and experimental setup.

**Task Definition:**
The authors define all evaluation tasks as multiple-choice Question Answering (QA) tasks. Given a top-view map (realistic or semantic) and a question, the model must select the correct option from four choices. A hierarchical structure of four main tasks and nine sub-tasks is established, designed with increasing levels of complexity:
1. **Top-View Recognition (TVR):** Assesses basic map interpretation.
 - *Sub-tasks:* Object Recognition, Scene Recognition.
2. **Top-View Localization (TVL):** Evaluates grounding entities to locations.
 - *Sub-tasks:* Object Localization, Scene Localization.
3. **Static Spatial Reasoning (SSR):** Investigates complex reasoning over static spatial relationships.
 - *Sub-tasks:* Scene Counting, Relative Spatial Relations.
4. **Dynamic Spatial Reasoning (DSR):** Introduces a novel task on reasoning along navigation paths.
 - *Sub-tasks:* Dynamic Action Counting, Dynamic Spatial Localization, Dynamic Relative Spatial Reasoning.

**Top-View Map Types:**
Two types of maps are used:
- **Realistic Maps (M_Real):** Photo-realistic images captured using a simulated orthographic camera above 3D scenes.
- **Semantic Maps (M_Sem):** Represent objects with colored bounding boxes, simplifying visual interpretation by explicitly labeling object types with colors. These also serve as a testbed for out-of-distribution (OOD) performance.

**TOPVIEWRS Dataset Collection:**
The dataset creation follows a two-stage strategy:
1. **Automatic Collection:**
 - **Source Data:** Matterport3D dataset, containing 3D meshes of 90 building-scale indoor scenes with instance-level semantic and room-level region annotations. Seven high-quality, single-floor scenes with an average of 80 objects and 12 rooms were selected.
 - **Map Generation:** Realistic maps are extracted using MeshLab. Semantic maps are constructed using the Habitat simulation environment, where objects are represented by colored bounding boxes based on 3D coordinates and annotations. Specific rules filter out semantically less meaningful or overly large objects/rooms, and a color-object mapping is established.
 - **Question Generation:** 15 structured question templates were designed for the 9 sub-tasks, with automatic generation of multiple-choice questions and golden answers based on scene information. Wrong choices are randomly selected from other valid candidates.
2. **Human Alignment:**
 - A second stage involved manual human verification of the automatically generated data. Participants reviewed question-answer pairs, with options to skip, modify (e.g., options, entities in questions), correct answers, or keep data points, ensuring naturalness, correctness, and alignment with human judgment, especially for potentially obscure top-view visuals.
 - The final TOPVIEWRS dataset comprises 11,384 multiple-choice questions (5,539 realistic maps, 5,845 semantic maps).

**Experiments and Evaluation:**
- **Models:** 10 representative VLMs were evaluated in a zero-shot inference setup: Idefics (9B, 80B), LLaVA-Next (7B, 13B, 34B), InternLM-XComposer2 (7B), Qwen-VL (7B), and closed-source models GPT-4V and Gemini.
- **Prompts:** Models received task descriptions and questions. For semantic maps, the color-object mapping was additionally provided in the prompt.
- **Evaluation Measures:** Exact Match (EM) and Partial Match (PM) accuracy were used.
- **Human Performance Baseline:** 4 human participants, not involved in dataset creation, evaluated 60 randomly selected realistic map questions, achieving substantial inter-annotator agreement (Fleiss Kappa of 0.747).
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Experiment:** For Static Spatial Reasoning, GPT-4V and Gemini were prompted to "first localize the entity and then answer the question based on the locations" to encourage step-by-step reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLMs on the TOPVIEWRS benchmark yielded several critical findings regarding their capabilities in top-view spatial reasoning:

1. **Overall VLM Performance is Subpar:** Current state-of-the-art VLMs exhibit unsatisfactory performance, with average Exact Match (EM) and Partial Match (PM) scores below 50% across all tasks. Some models, like Qwen-VL, occasionally perform worse than a random baseline, often due to difficulties in following instructions for multiple-choice selection.

2. **Performance Varies with Task Complexity:**
 - **Recognition Tasks (TVR):** Models perform best on Top-View Recognition, with Gemini achieving human-comparable EM scores (over 90%) on realistic maps.
 - **Localization Tasks (TVL):** Performance drops for Top-View Localization compared to recognition.
 - **Static Reasoning Tasks (SSR):** Static Spatial Reasoning shows even lower performance, highlighting challenges with multi-step reasoning.
 - **Dynamic Reasoning Tasks (DSR):** Performance on Dynamic Spatial Reasoning is mixed. Models perform relatively better on sub-tasks like dynamic action counting and spatial localization (potentially due to visual prompting equivalence or common-sense shortcuts for narrow answer ranges), but struggle significantly with dynamic relative spatial reasoning.

3. **Model Scale Does Not Guarantee Superior Spatial Awareness:** Surprisingly, larger model sizes do not consistently translate to better performance.
 - Closed-source models (GPT-4V, Gemini) generally outperform open-source models in simpler recognition tasks (31.10% EM lead on realistic maps for TVR).
 - However, this performance gap *narrows* considerably in more complex reasoning tasks (SSR, DSR). For instance, GPT-4V sometimes performs worse than Idefics-9B on SSR and DSR, and inconsistencies are observed even within open-source model families (LLaVANext, Idefics). This challenges the expected scaling law for spatial reasoning capabilities in VLMs, suggesting that simply increasing model size may not be sufficient.

4. **Semantic Maps Present Unique Challenges for Reasoning:**
 - For easier tasks (TVR), models generally perform better with semantic maps than realistic maps (e.g., 20.35% improvement for some models), as the explicit color-object mapping simplifies interpretation.
 - However, for more complex tasks (TVL, SSR), this advantage disappears. Models struggle to effectively utilize semantic top-view maps, often performing akin to a random baseline. This is attributed to the semantic map's abstract nature and explicit color-object mapping potentially deviating significantly from the models' training data distribution, leading to failures in instruction following or generating irrelevant outputs (e.g., RGB values).

5. **Fine-Grained Insights from Sub-Tasks:**
 - On realistic maps, models perform better on scene-level recognition and localization (larger entities) compared to object-level.
 - On semantic maps, the trend reverses for recognition (object-level better than scene-level).
 - In Static Spatial Reasoning, models are better at scene counting (likely benefiting from common-sense knowledge of typical room counts) than at reasoning over complex relative spatial relations.

6. **Significant Gap to Human Performance:** Human participants achieved over 90% accuracy across all sub-tasks involving spatial awareness, demonstrating a substantial performance gap of more than 50% compared to the best VLMs. Specifically, GPT-4V performed on average 47.78% lower than humans. This gap is more pronounced in complex reasoning tasks.

7. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Improves Spatial Reasoning:** Implementing Chain-of-Thought reasoning, which prompts models to explicitly localize entities before answering, significantly improved performance on Static Spatial Reasoning. For GPT-4V and Gemini, CoT led to an average increase of 5.82% (6.50% on realistic maps, 5.14% on semantic maps), indicating that structured, step-by-step reasoning can help elicit latent spatial abilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "TOPVIEWRS" research makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the development of Vision-Language Models and the broader field of AI:

1. **Establishes a Novel and Critical Benchmark:** The introduction of TOPVIEWRS fills a crucial void in VLM evaluation. It provides the first comprehensive, fine-grained, and controlled benchmark specifically designed for top-view spatial reasoning. This disentangled evaluation, across four main tasks and nine sub-tasks, allows researchers to precisely pinpoint the strengths and weaknesses of VLMs in different aspects of spatial intelligence, from basic perception to complex dynamic reasoning.

2. **Uncovers Fundamental VLM Limitations:** The study clearly demonstrates that despite recent advancements, even state-of-the-art VLMs—including large closed-source models—possess significant limitations in top-view spatial reasoning. Their frequent inability to perform better than random baselines on complex tasks and the substantial gap with human performance highlight that current architectures and training paradigms are insufficient for robust spatial understanding. This finding signals a critical area for future research and development.

3. **Provides Insights into Model Behavior and Scaling:** The observation that increasing model size does not consistently translate to improved spatial reasoning performance challenges conventional scaling laws in this domain. Furthermore, the difficulties VLMs face with abstract semantic maps, despite their simplified visual cues, reveal issues related to out-of-distribution generalization and effective integration of explicit symbolic information (like color-object mappings). These insights are invaluable for guiding architectural design and training strategies for future VLMs.

4. **Promotes Explainable AI and Robustness:** The structured nature of TOPVIEWRS, allowing for the disentanglement of abilities, inherently supports research into more interpretable and robust VLMs. The finding that Chain-of-Thought prompting can boost performance underscores the importance of explicit reasoning processes, moving beyond black-box predictions towards more verifiable and reliable AI behaviors.

5. **Paves the Way for Enhanced Embodied AI and Navigation:** Top-view spatial reasoning is indispensable for real-world applications such as embodied AI (e.g., robots navigating indoor environments) and advanced navigation systems. By exposing current VLM deficiencies, "TOPVIEWRS" sets a clear research agenda for developing VLMs that can achieve human-level proficiency in understanding and reasoning over complex environments. This will directly impact the development of more intelligent and autonomous agents capable of safely and efficiently interacting with the physical world.

In conclusion, "TOPVIEWRS" not only identifies a critical shortcoming in current VLM capabilities but also provides the necessary tools and insights to drive progress. It lays a foundational framework for future research aimed at improving multimodal spatial reasoning, refining reasoning techniques, and ultimately, bridging the substantial gap between artificial and human intelligence in understanding the spatial world.
