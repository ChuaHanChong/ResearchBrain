# ChainVLA: Chaining Vision-Language-Action Queries through a Unified Execution State for Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ChainVLA, a 1.2B-parameter vision-language-action policy, enhances long-horizon robotic manipulation by introducing a unified, revisable execution state that consistently carries both task progress and motion continuation across queries. This policy achieved an average success rate of 62.8% on the RMBench, outperforming prior methods, and demonstrated an average of 98.8% success on LIBERO suites.

## Problem

- Existing action-chunked Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies suffer from "incomplete state handoff" between successive queries, leading to the loss of task-level context.
- The lack of explicit state communication between queries causes motion discontinuities as unexecuted action continuations are discarded, impacting physical smoothness and subsequent observations.
- Prior methods address memory retention or motion smoothing independently, failing to integrate both task progress and motion continuation for robust long-horizon performance.

## Method

- **ChainVLA** implements a unified, revisable ==execution state== (comprising Progress Context and Motion Tail) that is explicitly passed and updated across successive queries.
- The ==Progress Context== retains observation-derived task evidence by fusing live execution tokens from a recurrent Working State with retrieved evidence from a ==Sparse Event Memory==.
- The ==Motion Tail== carries the preceding prediction's unexecuted action suffix, conditioning the Working State and initializing the action decoder to ensure motion continuity and allow for adaptive plan revision.

## Results

- ChainVLA achieved an average success rate of **62.8%** on the RMBench (memory-dependent manipulation), surpassing the strongest listed method (Mem-0, **52.8%**) with complete task coverage.
- On the 'Put Back Block' task, requiring recall of out-of-view evidence, ChainVLA demonstrated **96%** success, significantly outperforming Mem-0 (90%) and MemoryVLA (50%).
- Ablation studies on RMBench showed removing Motion Tail or Progress Context led to substantial drops in average success to **11.2%** and **3.0%**, respectively, indicating strong interdependence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified execution state, incorporating both task progress and motion continuity, is critical for successful long-horizon manipulation, as removing either component drastically reduces performance.
> - Motion continuity plays a fundamental role beyond mere smoothness, actively supporting reliable task-progress inference by maintaining a consistent observation stream, thus preventing off-distribution states that can degrade memory recall.
> - Model-side conditioning with motion tail information during action generation is more effective than post-decoding smoothing, emphasizing that contextual information must influence the decoded actions themselves, not just their refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Long-horizon manipulation tasks in robotics require an agent to execute a continuous sequence of actions, where each decision depends on both the progress established by earlier actions and the ongoing movement. This parallels human cognitive and motor control, which integrates memory of past events with feedback-revised motor predictions.

Current action-chunked Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies typically address long-horizon tasks through repeated receding-horizon queries. Each query predicts a finite action horizon, executes a prefix of these actions, and then replans from the current input. A significant limitation of this design is the "incomplete state handoff" between successive queries. This lack of explicit communication results in two main issues:
1. **Loss of Task-Level Context:** Earlier task evidence, such as initial object layouts or completed subtasks, may no longer be recoverable from the current visual observation. This leads to partial observability where identical current inputs could require different actions depending on past, unobservable events.
2. **Discontinuity at the Motion Level:** Each replanning step discards the unexecuted continuation of the preceding prediction. The newly generated action prefix might disagree with the motion it replaces, potentially causing abrupt changes in movement. This discontinuity not only impacts physical smoothness but also influences the observation stream available for subsequent task progress updates, creating a coupled problem.

Existing approaches have attempted to address these issues independently. Memory-augmented policies focus on retaining long-term task evidence by incorporating recurrent states, selected observations, or compressed task histories. However, these methods typically do not preserve the preceding prediction's unexecuted continuation. Conversely, approaches like Temporal Ensemble, real-time execution, or native continuation aim to preserve short-term motion continuity by combining or reusing actions. These methods, however, lack the capacity to retain task evidence from much earlier in an episode.

The current work addresses the central question of how a VLA policy can effectively chain both task progress and motion continuation across queries while simultaneously remaining responsive to the latest observations. It proposes a unified execution state to bridge these previously separated aspects, aiming to recover the cross-query continuity that is lost in traditional action-chunked VLA policy designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a VLA policy that can perform long-horizon manipulation tasks more effectively by addressing the fundamental limitation of incomplete state handoff between successive action-chunked queries. The authors are motivated by the observation that human manipulation relies on retaining knowledge of established progress and continuously adapting ongoing motion, a capability largely absent in existing VLA policies.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **To bridge the gap in cross-query state representation:** Existing VLA policies treat each query largely as an independent decision point, lacking an explicit mechanism to carry forward crucial context. This leads to performance degradation in tasks requiring memory or coherent, continuous motion.
2. **To unify task progress and motion continuation:** The paper argues that these two components are complementary and interdependent. Task progress, derived from observations, can be lost if critical evidence leaves the current view, while motion continuity affects the observation stream and thus the ability to infer task progress. The motivation is to integrate these aspects into a single, cohesive execution state that allows a policy to remember what has been done and smoothly transition to what needs to be done next.
3. **To maintain responsiveness to new observations:** While chaining state is important, the policy must also remain adaptable to new sensory inputs. The objective is to design a state-passing mechanism that acts as a revisable prior rather than a fixed plan, allowing the decoder to regenerate each action horizon under the latest observation.
4. **To develop a scalable VLA policy:** The proposed solution, ChainVLA, is designed as a 1.2B-parameter policy, aiming for practical applicability while enhancing performance on complex manipulation benchmarks.
5. **To empirically demonstrate the interdependence of task progress and motion continuity:** Through a series of ablations, the authors aim to show that the removal of either task progress or motion continuity components significantly degrades performance, suggesting that they operate in a consecutive rather than independent manner, with motion continuity supporting reliable task-progress estimation.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to enhance the capability of VLA policies in handling complex, memory-dependent, and long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks, moving closer to the continuous and adaptive nature of human execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ChainVLA is designed as a 1.2B-parameter VLA policy that integrates a joint and revisable execution state across successive queries. This state, denoted as $s_k = (g_k, u_k)$, comprises two primary components: Progress Context and Motion Tail. The overall architecture follows a query-transition mechanism where the state is read, updated, and passed forward at each step.

#### 3.1. Incomplete State Handoff Formulation

The paper frames the problem in a strict-prefix receding-horizon regime. At query $k$, the policy receives an input $x_k = (o_k, r_k, \ell)$ (observation, proprioceptive state, language instruction). It predicts an action sequence $A_k = (a_{k,1}, \dots, a_{k,H})$ and executes the first $h_{exec}$ actions. The critical aspect is that only a prefix of $A_{k-1}$ was executed, and the remaining suffix specifies the intended continuation. ChainVLA approximates history-conditioned generation $\Pi^*( \cdot | H_k)$ with $\Pi_\theta (\cdot | x_k, s_k)$ and updates the state as $s_{k+1} = F_\theta(s_k, x_k)$.

#### 3.2. The Execution State

The execution state $s_k$ serves as a policy-side context spanning queries.
- **Progress Context ($g_k$):** This retrospective component carries observation-derived task progress. It is recomputed at each query by fusing live execution tokens ($L_k$) from a recurrent Working State with evidence ($Z^e_k$) retrieved from a Sparse Event Memory.
- **Motion Tail ($u_k$):** This prospective component carries the preceding prediction's unexecuted suffix. It directly crosses the query boundary, providing a reference for how the previous motion was expected to continue.

The query transition is formally defined as:
$(A_k, W_k, C_{k+1}) = F_\theta(x_k, D_k, W_{k-1}, C_k, u_k)$
$u_{k+1} = \text{Tail}(A_k)$, where $\text{Tail}(A_k)$ is the predicted but unexecuted suffix $(a_{k,h_{exec}+1}, \dots, a_{k,H})$.

#### 3.3. Carrying Task Progress (Progress Context)

Progress Context aims to retain task evidence over multiple queries, even when it leaves the current view.
- **Recurrent Working State ($W_k$):** Comprises live tokens ($L_k$) and a bounded cache ($B_k$) of earlier live tokens. $L_k$ is updated by learned working queries that attend to current multimodal tokens ($X_k$) and incorporate the preceding live state ($L_{k-1}$). A pooled summary $\bar{W}_k$ is generated from $L_k$, $B_{k-1}$, executed-action tokens ($P_k$), and tail tokens ($U_k$). $\bar{W}_k$ then drives event retrieval.
- **Sparse Event Memory ($C_k$):** An ordered sequence of stored events, each containing visual features, Stage Info, temporal features, a retrieval key, and the write-query index.
 - **Event Writing:** The first valid query's event is stored as an anchor. For stage-annotated data, subsequent writes occur on task-stage transitions, supervised by ground truth and triggered by a predicted stage change with sufficient confidence during rollout. For annotation-free data (and in ablations), events are written periodically (every three queries).
 - **Event Retrieval:** A retrieval query ($q^e_k$) is formed from $\bar{W}_k$, the current Stage Info summary ($\bar{s}_k$), and query-time encoding. Each stored record receives an age-aware score based on cosine similarity. The anchor and the two highest-scoring non-anchor records are retrieved. Cross-attention fuses their bodies into readout tokens ($Z^e_k$), which are then fused with $L_k$ to form the Progress Context $g_k = \text{Fuse}(L_k, Z^e_k)$.

#### 3.4. Carrying Unfinished Motion (Motion Tail)

Motion Tail carries the continuation derived from the *immediately preceding prediction*. The unexecuted suffix of $A_k$ is cached as $u_{k+1}$. This suffix provides a reference for the next query on how motion was expected to continue.
- **Model-Side Routes:**
 1. **Working State Conditioning:** $u_{k+1}$ is encoded as tail tokens ($U_{k+1}$) and used to condition Working State construction.
 2. **Action Generation Initialization:** An aligned action-space representation ($\mu_{k+1}$) from $u_{k+1}$ initializes the full-horizon decoder state $eA^{(0)}_{k+1}$ with additive noise, acting as a continuation prior. The conditional-flow decoder then regenerates every horizon position from this state under the latest observation, Progress Context, and tail tokens, allowing for revision.
- **Execution-Side Handoff:** After decoding, the controller linearly blends overlapping steps of the newly regenerated plan and the incoming suffix before execution.

#### 3.5. Training

Training unrolls the same execution-state transition. During training, the next query may use its predecessor's detached predicted suffix with 0.5 probability; otherwise, the tail path is masked. The training objective combines a standard conditional-flow action objective, stage supervision (when annotations are available), and an overlap-consistency regularizer. This regularizer penalizes disagreement between the temporally aligned overlap of consecutive predicted horizons, encouraging coherent handoff without fixing actions. The recurrent and event states follow inference order, and the entire carried state is cleared between episodes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on RMBench and LIBERO suites aimed to assess ChainVLA's performance, the individual contributions of its components, and the relationship between motion continuity and memory-dependent task performance.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on Benchmarks

- **RMBench:** ChainVLA achieved an average success rate of 62.8% on RMBench, a benchmark designed for memory-dependent manipulation tasks. This performance surpassed the strongest listed method with complete five-task coverage (Mem-0, 52.8%). ChainVLA was either the best or second-best performer on every RMBench task. Notably, on "Put Back Block," which requires returning an object to a location no longer in view, ChainVLA scored 96% success, significantly higher than Mem-0 (90%) and MemoryVLA (50%).
- **LIBERO:** On the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), ChainVLA achieved an average success rate of 98.8%, with all individual suite scores exceeding 98%. This establishes the policy's breadth across general manipulation tasks, though LIBERO is noted to be near ceiling for recent policies.

#### 4.2. Ablation Analysis of Execution State Components

A detailed ablation study on RMBench investigated the contribution of Progress Context and Motion Tail:
- **Combined State Criticality:** Removing either Motion Tail ("w/o Motion Tail") or Progress Context ("w/o Progress Ctx.") led to a substantial drop in average success from 62.8% to 11.2% and 3.0%, respectively. Removing both ("w/o Both") resulted in 1.6% success. This indicates that neither partial state can approximate the performance of the complete model and suggests a strong interdependence rather than independent contributions.
- **Partial Removals within Progress Context:** Removing "Live Tokens" resulted in 47.2% success, and removing "Event Readout" yielded 32.2%. The "Event Readout" was particularly critical for "Put Back Block," where success fell from 96% to 25% without it, confirming its role in tasks requiring recall of out-of-view evidence.
- **Partial Removals within Motion Tail:** Removing "Tail Tokens" resulted in 52.0% success, while removing "Trajectory Initialization" led to 38.0% success, suggesting the latter is a more critical route for motion continuity.
- **Substitute Control Inadequacy:** A baseline using fixed-length observation history and post-decoding temporal ensembling ("FIFO Hist. + TE"), which approximates existing approaches to handling state and motion, achieved 35.6% success. This was significantly lower than ChainVLA's full performance, suggesting that ChainVLA's explicit state chaining mechanism offers a distinct advantage over these approximations.

#### 4.3. Relationship Between Motion Continuity and Memory

The observation that removing Motion Tail, while keeping the memory pathway intact, dramatically reduced success (from 62.8% to 11.2%) prompted further investigation into the interaction between the two state components.
- **Boundary Diagnostics:** Paired rollouts on RMBench tasks showed that "w/o Motion Tail" policies exhibited significantly higher command discontinuities (CD$_p$, CD$_R$, B2) and greater disagreement between consecutive plans (RMSE$_3$) at query boundaries compared to the full ChainVLA model. For example, on "Put Back Block," the position command discontinuity (CD$_p$) was 17.32 mm without Motion Tail versus 0.66 mm with it in an illustrative case. Pooled reductions for ChainVLA were 56.6% for CD$_p$, 40.4% for CD$_R$, 57.4% for B2, and 43.1% for RMSE$_3$.
- **Consecutive Link Hypothesis:** These findings support the hypothesis that Motion Tail's contribution extends beyond mere motion smoothness. Significant discontinuities can cause the robotic arm to visit poses not encountered during training, leading to an "off-distribution" observation stream. This degraded observation stream then compromises the reliability of task-progress inference by the Progress Context, even if the memory mechanism itself remains functionally present. Thus, motion continuity appears to be crucial for preserving the integrity of the observations needed for effective task-progress estimation, implying a consecutive rather than parallel relationship between the two components.

#### 4.4. Post-Decoding Smoothing Controls

To further test if mere boundary smoothness was the key, post-decoding control methods were evaluated:
- **Ineffectiveness of Smoothing:** "Linear Continuation" and "Temporal Ensemble" were applied to create smoother boundaries, achieving lower command discontinuity metrics than even the full ChainVLA on some tasks. However, these methods resulted in negligible task success (0-2%).
- **Model-Side Conditioning Importance:** This result indicates that simply smoothing actions *after* they have been decoded (an execution-side handoff) is insufficient. The critical factor is Motion Tail's model-side conditioning, which influences *what is decoded* by integrating the preceding prediction's continuation into the state construction and action generation process itself. Boundary metrics are therefore identified as diagnostics rather than direct objectives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents ChainVLA, a VLA policy that advances the capabilities of robotic manipulation, particularly for long-horizon and memory-dependent tasks, by introducing a unified and revisable execution state.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation in VLA Policies:** The work directly tackles the problem of "incomplete state handoff" in action-chunked VLA policies, which has been a barrier to robust performance in complex, multi-stage tasks. By explicitly modeling and carrying both task progress and motion continuation, ChainVLA offers a more comprehensive approach to state management than prior memory-augmented or motion-smoothing methods.
2. **Demonstrating Interdependence of Task and Motion Context:** The asymmetric ablation results are a key finding, suggesting that motion continuity is not merely an independent objective but actively supports the reliable inference of task progress by maintaining a consistent observation stream. This insight reframes the understanding of how VLA components interact for successful long-horizon operation.
3. **Improved Performance on Challenging Benchmarks:** ChainVLA's superior performance on RMBench, a benchmark specifically designed for memory-dependent manipulation, highlights its practical efficacy in scenarios where traditional policies struggle due to partial observability. Its strong performance on LIBERO further demonstrates its general applicability across various manipulation settings.
4. **Novel Integration of Recurrent State and Memory:** The combination of a recurrent Working State with a sparse event memory for Progress Context, along with the novel mechanism of Motion Tail to carry unexecuted intentions, represents a sophisticated approach to managing both short-term dynamics and long-term task evidence within a unified framework.
5. **Implications for Future Robot Learning:** The findings suggest that future VLA architectures could benefit from explicitly considering internal state representations that bridge past achievements with future intentions in a cohesive manner. This could lead to more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems that can operate reliably in partially observable and dynamic environments over extended periods.
6. **Guidance for Policy Design:** The clear distinction between model-side conditioning and post-decoding smoothing provides valuable guidance for the design of future VLA policies. It emphasizes that incorporating contextual information *during* the action generation process is more effective than merely refining the output afterward.

Overall, ChainVLA offers a principled solution to a critical challenge in robotic manipulation, paving the way for more capable and robust autonomous systems that can perform complex, multi-step tasks with greater dexterity and intelligence.
