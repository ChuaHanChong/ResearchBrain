# Knowledge-based Visual Question Answer with Multimodal Processing, Retrieval and Filtering

> [!summary] Summary
> The Wiki-PRF framework introduces a three-stage approach that employs a reinforcement learning-trained Visual Language Model to dynamically process visual queries, retrieve multimodal knowledge, and filter relevant information for knowledge-based visual question answering. This method established new state-of-the-art accuracy on the E-VQA and InfoSeek benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Visual Language Models (VLMs) often lack the ability to access and integrate external factual knowledge required for knowledge-based visual question answering (KB-VQA).
- Current retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods for KB-VQA struggle with fine-grained knowledge retrieval from complex visual scenes, leading to noisy or irrelevant information.
- Existing RAG techniques lack effective mechanisms for filtering and condensing retrieved knowledge, which often contains extraneous details that hinder accurate answer generation.

## Method

- Proposed **Wiki-PRF**, a three-stage (Processing, Retrieval, Filtering) multimodal ==Retrieval-Augmented== Generation (RAG) framework.
- Incorporated a VLM-PRF model trained with Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enable intelligent tool invocation and information filtering.
- Enabled dynamic processing of visual queries using specialized tools (e.g., captioning, object grounding) to generate refined multimodal queries for targeted knowledge retrieval.

## Results

- Wiki-PRF achieved state-of-the-art results on knowledge-based VQA benchmarks, reaching **39.2%** accuracy on E-VQA and **43.3%** on InfoSeek (unseen questions) using InternVL3-**8B**.
- The dynamic tool invocation in the processing stage enhanced retrieval recall, improving it from **45.56%** with direct image retrieval to **54.89%** through the use of captioning and grounding tools.
- Reinforcement learning for the filtering stage outperformed supervised fine-tuning, achieving **46.3%** accuracy versus **41.8%** on InfoSeek, demonstrating more robust learning with fewer data samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically GRPO, effectively teaches Visual Language Models to strategically invoke external tools and filter information without requiring explicit intermediate reasoning labels.
> - Dynamically generated fine-grained multimodal queries, created through tool invocation, lead to more precise and relevant knowledge retrieval from complex visual scenes.
> - A dedicated, RL-trained filtering stage is crucial for distilling task-oriented knowledge from potentially noisy retrieved documents, directly improving the accuracy and conciseness of answers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving field of **Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA)**, a challenging sub-area of VQA. Standard VLMs excel at visual content understanding but struggle with questions requiring external factual knowledge, such as "What is that statue made of?" (as illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper).

The broader landscape of VQA has seen significant advancements with large-scale Visual Language Models (VLMs) like BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA, GPT-4V, and Qwen2.5-VL. While these models possess remarkable capabilities, their performance on KB-VQA remains limited because they lack inherent access to external knowledge bases.

**Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** has emerged as a dominant paradigm to address this limitation. RAG methods augment VLMs by allowing them to query external knowledge bases (e.g., Wikipedia articles) to retrieve relevant information before generating an answer. This approach typically involves three steps: retrieving knowledge, reranking it, and generating an answer. Many recent works, such as TRiG, MuKEA, EchoSight, Wiki-LLaVA, ReflectiVA, and MMKB-RAG, fall into this category.

However, the authors identify two critical shortcomings in existing RAG methods for KB-VQA:
1. **Fine-grained knowledge retrieval in complex visual scenes:** Current methods often rely on full-image visual features, which can be imprecise when the relevant object is small or obscured, leading to retrieval being influenced by dominant but irrelevant elements.
2. **Precise filtering of irrelevant information:** Even with retrieved knowledge, filtering out noise or less relevant content from large passages remains a challenge, impacting the accuracy of generated answers.

This paper positions itself as an innovative solution to these specific RAG limitations. It leverages the power of **reinforcement learning (RL)**, which has recently gained traction in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of VLMs for specialized tasks. While RL has been applied to VLMs for general reasoning (e.g., R1-OneVision, VisualThinker-R1-Zero, MM-Eureka) and specific domains like mathematical reasoning (MathVista, Visual-RFT), the authors claim their work represents the **first application of reinforcement learning specifically to multimodal retrieval-augmented generation**. This unique combination aims to imbue the VLM with a deeper "understanding" of when and how to retrieve and filter information effectively, rather than relying solely on pre-defined retrieval pipelines or supervised fine-tuning which might struggle with unseen scenarios.

By tackling these challenges, Wiki-PRF contributes to making KB-VQA systems more accurate, robust, and capable of handling real-world complexity, pushing the boundaries of what multimodal AI can achieve in knowledge-intensive tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly advance the state-of-the-art in Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA) by overcoming critical limitations observed in existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods.

The core motivations stem from two persistent challenges in current RAG approaches:

1. **Enhancing Fine-Grained Knowledge Retrieval:** Existing RAG methods often struggle to extract precise information from complex images. When a question pertains to a specific, potentially small or non-prominent object within a busy visual scene, the retrieval process, relying on general visual features, can be swayed by larger, irrelevant elements. This leads to the retrieval of overly broad or noisy knowledge, hindering accurate answers. The authors are motivated to enable VLMs to dynamically identify and focus on the most relevant visual aspects to generate targeted retrieval queries.
2. **Improving the Relevance and Conciseness of Retrieved Information:** After knowledge is retrieved, it often contains significant amounts of extraneous or redundant information. Current re-ranking techniques, typically operating at the paragraph or section level, are often insufficient for fine-grained filtering. This abundance of irrelevant content can confuse the VLM, leading to less accurate or verbose answers. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism that can intelligently filter and condense retrieved knowledge to present only the most pertinent facts to the VLM for answer generation.

To address these motivations, the authors propose two key strategic innovations:

- **Tool-Based Preprocessing for Refined Query Generation:** Recognizing that raw visual and textual inputs might be inadequate for precise retrieval, the authors seek to empower the VLM with the ability to "interact" with the image and question by invoking specialized visual tools. This dynamic tool invocation aims to extract fine-grained multimodal information (e.g., object grounding, detailed captioning) that can form highly relevant retrieval queries.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Strategic Filtering and Reasoning:** To move beyond superficial pattern matching and truly instill reasoning capabilities for tool invocation and information filtering, the authors are motivated to train their VLM using reinforcement learning (RL). RL's ability to learn strategies from reward signals, even with limited labeled intermediate steps, is seen as crucial for teaching the model to intelligently select tools, utilize their outputs, and distill essential information from large retrieved texts. The absence of readily available datasets with intermediate reasoning steps for complex VQA tasks further motivates the choice of RL over traditional supervised fine-tuning.

In essence, the overarching objective is to build a more intelligent and adaptable KB-VQA system that can autonomously refine its understanding of the visual query, strategically retrieve knowledge, and precisely filter out noise, ultimately leading to more accurate and concise answers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed **Wiki-PRF**, is a novel three-stage multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework: Processing, Retrieval, and Filtering. It leverages a Visual Language Model (VLM-PRF) trained with reinforcement learning to enhance reasoning, tool invocation, and filtering capabilities.

#### 3.1. Overview

Wiki-PRF integrates an external knowledge base (KB) with a VLM-PRF model. The process unfolds as follows:
1. **Processing Stage:** VLM-PRF dynamically invokes external tools based on the input image (I) and question (Q) to generate precise multimodal retrieval queries.
2. **Multimodal Retrieval Stage:** Based on these refined queries, relevant information is retrieved from the KB.
3. **Filtering Stage:** VLM-PRF filters and condenses the retrieved information into task-oriented knowledge, which is then used by a base VLM to generate the final answer.

#### 3.2. Processing Stage

This stage addresses the challenge of fine-grained knowledge retrieval by dynamically augmenting the input query. Instead of relying on raw inputs, VLM-PRF autonomically invokes image-processing tools:
- **Captioning Tool:** Takes an initial caption generated by VLM-PRF and the question (Q) to produce a refined caption (C_query) for retrieval.
- **Grounding Tool:** VLM-PRF identifies an object of interest, and VLM_grounding returns its positional information. The image (I) is then cropped to create an image focusing on the object (I_grounding).
- **Flipping Tool:** Applies a left-right inversion to the image to mitigate orientation bias in retrieval.

The VLM-PRF model reasons within `<think>` tags about which tools to use and their sequence, then outputs the chosen tools and their execution order in `<tool>` tags. The actual execution of these tools (e.g., captioning and grounding) is powered by a foundational VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, referred to as VLM-base). All generated query results are aggregated to enhance retrieval.

#### 3.3. Multimodal Retrieval Stage

This stage retrieves relevant articles from the knowledge base (KB), which consists of Wikipedia articles and corresponding images.
- **Initial Retrieval:** A base retrieval is performed directly on the reference image (I) to obtain the most relevant article (D).
- **Tool Search:** The refined query (Query), generated from the processing stage (combining C_query and I_grounding), is embedded using EVA-CLIP. Faiss library is used for efficient retrieval of top-k most relevant images and their documents, based on cosine similarity.
- **Section-Level Refinement:** The retrieved articles are split into sections. For captioning queries, cosine similarity is calculated between the caption query and sections. For grounding queries, similarity is calculated between the question embedding and sections to maximize multimodal fusion. The top-k_s most similar sections are selected as final retrieval results (S_tool).
- **Aggregation:** All S_tool results are concatenated to form S_search, which is then combined with the initially retrieved article D and fed into VLM-PRF.

#### 3.4. Filtering Stage

This stage tackles the issue of irrelevant information in retrieved results. VLM-PRF, guided by reinforcement learning, processes the combined information (D and S_search) and the question (Q).
- **Reasoning and Filtering:** VLM-PRF outputs its reasoning within `<think>` tags and generates a compact, task-oriented knowledge representation (F) within `<answer>` tags. This filtering process is question-specific, removing redundancy and extracting only the most relevant knowledge.
- **Answer Generation:** Finally, the base VLM uses this filtered knowledge (F) and the original question (Q) to generate the final answer (A).

#### 3.5. Training via Reinforcement Learning

To enable VLM-PRF to intelligently select tools and filter information, it is trained using **GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization)**, a policy optimization algorithm, with removed KL divergence constraints. This is critical because training data for complex VQA often lacks explicit intermediate reasoning steps required for supervised fine-tuning.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function is designed:
 - **Answer Reward (αEM):** Evaluates the correctness of the final generated answer (a_pred) against the ground truth (a_gt) using exact matching.
 - **Format Reward (βM + γM):** Encourages VLM-PRF to adhere to the desired output structure (e.g., proper use of `<think>`, `<tool>`, and `<answer>` tags) using regular expression matching. `a_tool` and `a_filter` are model outputs during processing and filtering, while `t_tool` and `t_filter` are their respective templates.
- **Efficiency:** LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is used for fine-tuning, training only a small number of additional parameters, which enhances RAG capabilities without compromising the VLM's core question-answering abilities.

The training process uses a small amount of sample data, relying on answer accuracy and format consistency as reward signals to teach the model to generate high-quality retrieval content through accurate tool invocation and selective retention of relevant retrieval results.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on benchmark datasets E-VQA and InfoSeek, along with ablation studies, demonstrate the significant effectiveness of the Wiki-PRF framework and its VLM-PRF model.

- **State-of-the-Art VQA Performance:**
 - Wiki-PRF achieves new state-of-the-art results on both E-VQA (36.0% accuracy for all, 37.1% with Qwen-2.5VL-7B) and InfoSeek (42.8% accuracy for all, 43.3% for unseen questions, 42.7% for unseen entities with Qwen-2.5VL-7B).
 - When using InternVL3-8B as a base model, Wiki-PRF further improves to 39.2% on E-VQA, showcasing consistent gains across different powerful VLMs.
 - These scores significantly outperform previous retrieval-augmented methods like ReflectiVA and MMKB-RAG, as well as zero-shot MLLMs, highlighting the value of the proposed three-stage approach and RL training.
 - On the OK-VQA benchmark, Wiki-PRF-7B also sets a new SOTA score of 77.8.

- **Effectiveness of the Processing Stage:**
 - The use of tools in the processing stage significantly boosts retrieval recall. While direct image retrieval yields 45.56% recall, combining it with tool-based retrievals increases it to 53.44% (for Qwen2.5-VL-7B).
 - Reinforcement learning further refines this, leading to 54.89% recall for VLM-PRF-7B.
 - Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of tools: captioning and grounding tools independently increase performance by 1.94% and 0.98% respectively, with multi-tool usage yielding the best results (39.48% VQA accuracy).
 - RL training encourages greater diversity and frequency in tool selection, demonstrating the model's ability to dynamically construct tool invocation schemes. The captioning tool is noted as the most frequently invoked, indicating its direct utility for enhancing article recall.

- **Effectiveness of the Filtering Stage:**
 - In an "oracle" setting where ground-truth articles are provided, the filtering stage dramatically improves VQA accuracy to 65.8% (VLM-PRF-7B), significantly surpassing other methods like Wiki-LLaVA (51.5%) and ReflectiVA (57.6%). This indicates that the VLM-PRF, when fine-tuned, is highly effective at extracting necessary information from relevant passages.

- **Effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - A direct comparison between RL and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for the filtering stage reveals that RL significantly outperforms SFT (46.3% vs. 41.8% on InfoSeek). This supports the authors' claim that RL enables the model to understand underlying principles of information filtering, leading to more robust and generalizable performance, unlike SFT which tends to imitate superficial patterns.
 - RL allows the model to achieve comparable or superior results with significantly fewer training samples (e.g., 4K samples instead of 934K for InfoSeek). This efficiency stems from RL's ability to stimulate the model to leverage tools and integrate knowledge for correct answers, rather than memorizing patterns.
 - The training curve analysis shows consistent upward trends in answer and format rewards, alongside a progressive decrease in task-oriented knowledge tokens, confirming that RL effectively teaches the model to focus on relevant information and adhere to desired output formats.

- **Impact of Knowledge Base Scale and Retrieved Article Quantity:**
 - Both Wiki-PRF and baseline methods experience performance degradation with increasing knowledge base size, a common challenge in RAG. However, Wiki-PRF demonstrates a significantly slower rate of degradation, indicating its robustness in handling larger, noisier KBs.
 - Increasing the number of retrieved articles (Top-k) generally improves VQA accuracy, with diminishing returns, balancing inference time and accuracy (k=5 chosen as optimal).

- **Inference Time:**
 - The Processing & Retrieval stages and Filtering stage consume more time than the final Answering stage (e.g., 2.2s + 3.4s vs. 0.59s for VLM-PRF-3B). This is attributed to tool invocation and long-text processing, suggesting an area for future optimization.

In summary, the main findings strongly validate the Wiki-PRF framework, showcasing its ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance in KB-VQA by intelligently processing inputs, retrieving multimodal knowledge, and effectively filtering irrelevant content, all powered by a reinforcement learning-trained VLM-PRF.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Wiki-PRF framework introduces several significant advancements that have considerable potential impact on the field of Visual Question Answering and broader AI applications.

**Scientific Significance:**
- **First RL Application to Multimodal RAG:** The most significant scientific contribution is the pioneering application of reinforcement learning to multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation for KB-VQA. This shifts the paradigm from purely supervised or rule-based RAG methods to a more adaptive, strategy-learning approach. By using answer accuracy and format consistency as reward signals, the VLM-PRF model learns to make intelligent decisions about tool invocation and information filtering, which is a novel way to address the inherent lack of intermediate reasoning labels in typical VQA datasets.
- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Understanding:** The dynamic tool invocation in the processing stage (captioning, grounding, flipping) demonstrates a sophisticated method for a VLM to interact with and extract precise, question-relevant details from complex visual scenes. This moves beyond generic image embeddings and allows for more targeted knowledge retrieval, addressing a major bottleneck in multimodal understanding.
- **Intelligent Information Filtering:** The RL-trained filtering stage is crucial for distilling high-quality, task-oriented knowledge from noisy retrieved documents. This ability to "reason" about relevance and condense information is a significant step towards more effective knowledge integration in VLMs, leading to concise and accurate answers.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The superior performance on larger, noisier knowledge bases and the ability to achieve SOTA results with a relatively small number of training samples (due to RL's efficiency in learning strategies) indicate a more robust and generalizable KB-VQA system. This suggests that the model is learning underlying principles rather than just memorizing patterns.

**Potential Societal and Technological Impact:**
- **Advanced Intelligent Assistants:** Wiki-PRF's capabilities could lead to more sophisticated intelligent assistants that can answer complex factual questions about images with greater accuracy. This is highly beneficial for everyday users, from helping with travel planning (e.g., "What is this building made of?") to educational inquiries (e.g., "What is the conservation status of this animal?").
- **Enhanced Information Retrieval and Search:** The framework's ability to process multimodal queries and filter relevant information can significantly improve image-based search engines and knowledge discovery platforms, making it easier for users to find specific information within vast visual and textual datasets.
- **Educational Applications:** Students and researchers could leverage such systems to quickly extract specific facts from encyclopedic images, aiding in learning and research by reducing the manual effort of sifting through large texts.
- **Accessibility:** For individuals with visual impairments, a highly accurate KB-VQA system could provide detailed verbal descriptions and factual answers about their surroundings or images, significantly enhancing accessibility.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of RL in multimodal RAG opens new avenues for research into how VLMs can autonomously learn to interact with their environment (including knowledge bases and external tools) to solve increasingly complex, open-ended tasks. This could extend to more complex reasoning, multi-turn dialogue, and even proactive information gathering.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as acknowledged by authors):**
The authors acknowledge that the current study is limited to three specific retrieval tools. Future work could explore integrating a wider array of tools and optimizing inference time, which is currently higher in the processing and filtering stages due to tool invocation and long-text processing. While the paper mentions that potential harmful information from retrieval can be mitigated by restricting the knowledge base, this aspect could be further elaborated, especially in real-world deployment where controlling knowledge base scope might be challenging or biases in the KB might lead to biased outputs.

In conclusion, Wiki-PRF represents a substantial step forward in KB-VQA by introducing a principled, RL-driven approach to intelligent tool use and information filtering. Its demonstrated state-of-the-art performance and unique methodology lay a strong foundation for developing more capable, adaptable, and context-aware multimodal AI systems.
