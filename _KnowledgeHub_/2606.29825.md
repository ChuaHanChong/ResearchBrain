# Data-Driven Modeling and Control for Tethered Space Systems with Koopman-Informed Graphs

> [!summary] Summary
> A data-driven Koopman Graph Dynamics (KGD) framework is presented for modeling and control of Tethered Space Systems (TSS), combining Koopman operator theory with Graph Neural Networks. This approach facilitates accurate long-term predictive control of complex, flexible structures and enables real-time optimal control through a fast Model Predictive Control scheme with online adaptation.

## Problem

- Tethered Space Systems (TSS) exhibit spatially continuous, underactuated, and highly nonlinear dynamics due to rigid-flexible coupling and large deformations of flexible components.
- Existing high-fidelity analytical models are computationally prohibitive for real-time optimal control, while simplified models sacrifice crucial flexibility characteristics.
- Current data-driven and Koopman-based methods lack structural priors for spatially distributed flexible systems and struggle with long-term predictive stability and spatial generalization.

## Method

- The **Koopman Graph Dynamics (KGD)** framework uses a ==graph-based representation== to abstract TSS dynamics, repurposing ==Graph Neural Networks== as trainable lifting functions to a globally linear ==Koopman space==.
- A fast ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== scheme is formulated within the Koopman space, utilizing ==state condensing== techniques to convert the control problem into an efficient ==Quadratic Program==.
- An ==asynchronous dual-rate architecture== ensures robust online model adaptation by decoupling high-frequency control execution from low-frequency Koopman matrix updates.

## Results

- KGD consistently outperformed baseline data-driven methods in long-term predictive fidelity and spatial generalization for both 1D flexible tethers and 2D flexible nets in ground experiments.
- The KGD-MPC achieved precise shape control of larger, unseen flexible tethers (**n=16** controlled by a model trained on **n=10**), demonstrating successful 'zero-shot spatial transfer'.
- High-fidelity orbital simulations validated the KGD-MPC's effectiveness for practical TSS applications, including precise tether length regulation for a Tethered Space Robot and coordinated capture maneuvers for a Tethered Space Net Robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating global linear Koopman dynamics with local topological priors from GNNs provides a unified, structured data-driven model that resolves the trade-off between modeling fidelity and control tractability for flexible systems.
> - A state-inclusive embedding combined with multi-step forward and backward prediction losses ensures zero reconstruction error and enhances the long-term predictive stability and dynamic consistency of the learned model.
> - State condensing within the Koopman-based MPC significantly reduces computational complexity from cubic dependence on combined state and control dimensions to solely control dimensions, enabling real-time operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The expansion of orbital activities has generated a demand for lightweight, fuel-efficient robotic systems capable of adaptable operations. Tethered Space Systems (TSS), including Tethered Space Robots (TSR), Tethered Space Net Robots (TSNR), and Tethered Formation Systems (TFS), offer advantages in dynamic reconfigurability, minimal propellant consumption, and mission versatility, making them relevant for applications such as on-orbit servicing, active debris removal, and distributed Earth observation.

However, the modeling and control of TSS present significant challenges due to their inherent characteristics. These systems exhibit spatially continuous dynamics, are often underactuated, and involve complex rigid-flexible coupling. The flexible components, such as tethers and space nets, introduce highly nonlinear dynamics characterized by large deformations, elasticity, and spatial oscillations, which invalidate simplistic rigid-body assumptions. Existing research has explored various strategies, generally facing a trade-off between modeling fidelity and control tractability. High-fidelity analytical formulations, while accurate, can be computationally prohibitive for advanced optimal control design. Conversely, simplified control-oriented models neglect crucial flexibility characteristics, potentially leading to performance degradation.

In response to the computational intractability of high-fidelity analytical models, data-driven techniques have gained traction. Specifically, Koopman operator theory has emerged as a method to identify linear embeddings of nonlinear systems within lifted observable spaces, thereby enabling the application of linear control techniques to complex nonlinear dynamics. However, current data-driven and Koopman-based learning paradigms primarily target rigid-body dynamics. These methods often lack the structural priors necessary to effectively encode the spatially distributed topologies and continuum behaviors of flexible systems. This creates a gap in formulating a unified representation that can simultaneously address the spatial topological complexities and nonlinearities of flexible TSS to enable fast, adaptive optimal control.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a framework that integrates Koopman operator theory with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). This approach aims to leverage the global linearization capabilities of the Koopman operator with the local topological reasoning of GNNs, thereby providing a structured, data-driven model capable of representing and controlling the complex dynamics of flexible TSS.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the increasing reliance on Tethered Space Systems (TSS) for advanced orbital operations and the inherent challenges associated with their dynamics. As orbital activities expand, there is a demand for robotic systems that are lightweight, fuel-efficient, and highly adaptable across diverse workspaces. TSS, encompassing Tethered Space Robots (TSR), Tethered Space Net Robots (TSNR), and Tethered Formation Systems (TFS), offer distinct advantages for tasks such as on-orbit servicing, active debris removal, and distributed spacecraft operations due to their dynamic reconfigurability and low propellant consumption.

Despite their potential, the modeling and control of TSS remain a fundamental challenge. These systems are characterized by:
- **Spatially Continuous Dynamics:** Flexible components like tethers and nets exhibit behaviors that cannot be accurately captured by simplified rigid-body models.
- **Severe Underactuation:** Control inputs are often limited, making it difficult to precisely manipulate high-dimensional flexible structures.
- **Rigid-Flexible Coupling:** Interactions between rigid platforms and flexible tethers/nets introduce complex, nonlinear dynamics.
- **High Dimensionality and Nonlinearity:** Large deformations, elasticity, and spatial oscillations of tethers/nets result in highly complex, strongly coupled, and nonlinear dynamic behaviors.

Existing approaches typically encounter a trade-off:
- **High-Fidelity Analytical Models:** While accurate in capturing continuum behaviors, they often lead to computationally prohibitive control designs.
- **Control-Oriented Simplistic Models:** These facilitate real-time control synthesis but neglect critical flexibility characteristics, leading to performance degradation.

Recent data-driven methods, particularly those based on Koopman operator theory, offer a pathway to linearize nonlinear systems in a lifted space. However, these methods frequently lack the inherent structural priors needed to represent the spatially distributed topologies of flexible continuum systems. They also tend to struggle with long-term predictive stability and spatial generalization when applied to flexible structures.

Accordingly, the primary objective of this paper is to develop a unified data-driven control framework for TSS that simultaneously addresses these challenges. Specifically, the framework aims to:
1. **Establish a unified spatial-temporal representation:** By combining graph-based topological abstraction with Koopman operator theory, to accurately capture the complex, high-dimensional, and nonlinear dynamics of flexible tethers and nets.
2. **Formulate an efficient model predictive control (MPC) scheme:** Operating within a globally linearized Koopman space, leveraging state condensing techniques to enable real-time optimal control synthesis without compromising modeling fidelity.
3. **Design an asynchronous dual-rate architecture:** To facilitate online model adaptation, thereby guaranteeing sustained robust performance against dynamic uncertainties and unmodeled continuum transients by decoupling high-frequency control execution from low-frequency model updates.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to resolve the long-standing trade-off between accurate continuum modeling and real-time control tractability for TSS, providing a scalable and robust control solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the Koopman Graph Dynamics (KGD) framework, an integrated data-driven approach for modeling and controlling Tethered Space Systems (TSS). The framework consists of three main components: a graph-based representation of TSS dynamics, a fast Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme formulated in the Koopman space, and an asynchronous dual-rate architecture for online adaptation.

#### 3.1. A Graph-Based Representation of Tethered Space System for Model-Based Control

The KGD framework begins by spatially discretizing the continuous flexible components of TSS (tethers, nets) into a finite set of interacting point masses, which are treated as nodes in a topological graph G = (O, R). This abstraction unifies the representation of diverse TSS configurations (TSR, TSNR, TFS).
- **Graph Structure:** Nodes (O_t) represent physical entities (platforms, maneuvering units, passive tether/net segments) with attributes (o_t^i = (x_t^i, u_t^i, a_o^i)), including state, control input, and a time-invariant one-hot vector indicating node type. Edges (R) represent mechanical pairwise interactions, also with attributes (r_k = (m_k, n_k, a_r^k)) defining connected nodes and relation type.
- **Message Passing:** A standard GNN message-passing scheme (Equation 8) captures the spatial-temporal evolution, where edge attributes (e_t^k) representing latent interaction forces are propagated, and node attributes (o_{t+1}^i) are updated by aggregating messages from their neighborhood.
- **Koopman Integration:** To overcome the black-box nature of GNNs for control design, the Koopman operator theory is integrated. The GNN is repurposed as a trainable lifting function (Ψ_i) that maps the highly coupled, nonlinear nodal states (x_t^i) into a higher-dimensional feature space (z_t^i = Ψ_i(x_t^i)). This transformation allows for the construction of a globally linear state-space representation directly from input/output data.
- **Compositional KGD:** The local nodal linear dynamics (Equation 9) are aggregated to form a global linear system (z_{t+1} = Az_t + Bu_t + r_t). A significant computational advantage is derived from the block-diagonal structure of the global linear evolution matrices A and B (Equation 13), which reduces the number of unknown parameters for identification.
- **Model Identification:** The Koopman matrices A and B are estimated using a data-driven approach, specifically the Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) framework (Equation 16), by minimizing the one-step forward prediction error over a dataset of system trajectories.
- **State-Inclusive Embedding and Loss Function:** The framework employs a state-inclusive embedding where the original physical state x is explicitly concatenated into the lifted feature z (z_i = [x_i^T, Ψ(x_i)^T]^T). This design ensures that the projection matrix C = [I, 0], analytically guaranteeing zero reconstruction error and eliminating the need for an auxiliary decoder network. The training objective (L_total, Equation 21) focuses on dynamic consistency, combining a multi-step forward prediction loss (L_forward, Equation 18) and a multi-step backward prediction loss (L_backward, Equation 20). The forward loss ensures accurate long-horizon predictions, while the backward loss enhances spectral properties and stability by enforcing time-reversibility. The explicit state preservation also allows the MPC cost function to be evaluated directly on physical coordinates.

#### 3.2. Fast MPC via State Condensing in Koopman Space

To address the computational demands of controlling underactuated continuum dynamics, the KGD-MPC method embeds the globally linear KGD representation into a predictive control formulation, transforming a non-convex problem into a convex Quadratic Program (QP).
- **State Condensing:** The method utilizes a dense state condensing technique to reduce the computational complexity. The future states over the prediction horizon (N_p) are recursively derived from the nominal linear KGD dynamics (Equation 22).
- **Vectorized Dynamics:** The entire nominal state trajectory and control sequence are grouped into augmented vectors (Z and U, Equation 23), allowing the state trajectory to be compactly rewritten as Z = Mz_t + C_u U (Equation 24), where M and C_u are precomputed matrices (Equation 25).
- **Quadratic Program (QP):** The MPC objective function (J, Equation 26) minimizes a quadratic cost involving tracking error (Z - Z_ref) and control effort (U). By substituting the condensed state equation into the cost, the objective becomes a QP solely in terms of the control sequence U (J = 1/2 U^T H U + f^T U, Equation 27), where the condensed Hessian matrix H and vector f are derived (Equation 28).
- **Computational Efficiency:** Since H can be precomputed, the optimization problem (Equation 29) reduces significantly, changing the computational complexity from O((N_p(n_Z + n_U))^3) to O((N_p n_U)^3), where n_U (control dimension) is substantially smaller than n_Z (latent state dimension). This enables efficient real-time execution.

#### 3.3. Online Adaptation via Asynchronous Dual-Rate Architecture

To maintain robust performance against time-varying dynamics and unmodeled disturbances, an asynchronous dual-rate control architecture is proposed. This mechanism decouples the high-frequency real-time control loop from the computationally intensive online model adaptation process.
- **Dual Threads:** The framework operates with two parallel threads:
 - **High-Frequency Control Thread:** Executes at a faster control rate (1/∆t_c). It samples the current state, encodes it using the GNN, fetches the latest precomputed condensed matrices, solves the dense QP, and applies the optimal control input.
 - **Low-Frequency Model Update Thread:** Operates asynchronously in the background at a slower identification rate (1/∆t_id). It collects recent state-action trajectories from a sliding window buffer, performs the least-squares update for the Koopman matrices (A_new, B_new), recomputes the condensed Hessian matrices, and then seamlessly injects these updated matrices into the control thread.
- **Benefits:** This architectural design isolates heavy computational burdens from the high-frequency feedback loop, endowing the KGD-MPC algorithm with continuous online adaptability without sacrificing real-time control performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of the KGD framework through ground experiments and orbital simulations.

#### 4.1. Ground Experimental Evaluation

Ground experiments were conducted on two flexible structures: a 1D flexible tether and a 2D flexible net, using a Nokov motion capture system for precise node tracking.
- **1D Flexible Tether:**
 - **Setup:** A 0.9m tether discretized into 10 nodes for training, with one end attached to a robot arm. Validation extended to larger configurations of 12, 16, and 20 nodes with constant nodal spacing (0.1m).
 - **Baselines:** Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Interaction Network (IN), and EDO-Net.
 - **Prediction Fidelity:** KGD consistently outperformed all baselines in terms of prediction errors (MSEs) across different spatial discretizations and temporal horizons (0-150 steps and 150-300 steps). For the most challenging n=20 configuration, KGD maintained a short-term error of 8.326e-4 m, significantly lower than baselines which showed severe divergence or substantial error accumulation over extended horizons. Visual comparisons confirmed KGD's ability to accurately capture transient structural deformation and whipping dynamics.
- **2D Flexible Net:**
 - **Setup:** A 0.6m square net discretized into a 4x4 grid for training, with one edge fixed and the opposite free to fall. Validation extended to 6x6 and 8x8 node configurations (0.1m spacing).
 - **Prediction Fidelity:** KGD again demonstrated superior predictive fidelity and spatial scalability. It consistently outperformed all baselines across both spatial discretizations and temporal horizons (0-500 steps and 500-1000 steps). For the 8x8 net in the extended horizon, KGD achieved an MSE of 4.803e-01 m, effectively suppressing the catastrophic drift observed in other methods.
 - **Conclusion on Dynamics Learning:** These evaluations collectively demonstrated the KGD framework's superior long-term predictive stability and robust capacity for spatial generalization across varying system scales and complexities.

- **Shape Control of Tether:**
 - **Task:** Active shape control of an underactuated flexible mass rope (n=16 tether) using a KGD model trained exclusively on the n=10 configuration.
 - **Method:** The KGD-MPC method (Algorithm 1) was employed with specified control and identification periods, prediction horizon, and weighting matrices.
 - **Results:** The KGD-MPC successfully achieved precise shape tracking for both n=12 and n=16 configurations. This demonstrated successful "zero-shot spatial transfer," indicating that the KGD representation effectively captured control-oriented dynamics to enable robust and scalable closed-loop manipulation of complex flexible structures outside the training distribution.

#### 4.2. Orbital Simulation

The proposed framework was evaluated in a full-scale orbital environment using a high-fidelity MuJoCo physics engine simulator.
- **Tethered Space Robot (TSR):**
 - **Scenario:** Deployment of a manipulator attached via a 100m tether (discretized into 101 nodes) regulated by a reel mechanism.
 - **Results:** The KGD-MPC algorithm achieved precise tether length regulation at 100m with minor oscillation while successfully deploying the manipulator to the target location 100m away from its starting point.
- **Tethered Space Net Robot (TSNR):**
 - **Scenario:** Capture of a defunct satellite by a 5m flexible net (discretized into 11 nodes per edge) maneuvered by four units equipped with thrusters.
 - **Results:** The KGD-MPC algorithm successfully coordinated the four maneuvering units to maintain the net shape during approach and executed a successful wrapping maneuver around the defunct satellite.
- **Conclusion on Orbital Simulations:** These complex orbital tasks verified the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed KGD-MPC algorithm for practical TSS applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents the Koopman Graph Dynamics (KGD) framework, offering a data-driven approach that addresses fundamental challenges in the modeling and control of Tethered Space Systems (TSS). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Resolution of Modeling-Control Trade-off:** The work offers a unified mathematical framework that aims to resolve the long-standing contradiction between achieving high-fidelity dynamic representation and enabling tractable, real-time control synthesis for complex, high-dimensional, and nonlinear flexible systems. By integrating global linear Koopman dynamics with local topological priors from Graph Neural Networks, the framework provides an interpretable and computationally efficient model suitable for control.
- **Enhanced Predictive Fidelity and Long-Term Stability:** The KGD framework demonstrates superior long-term predictive capabilities compared to other data-driven baselines. Through the use of multi-step forward and backward prediction losses, the model is trained to maintain dynamic consistency over extended temporal horizons, which is crucial for reliable autonomous operations in space.
- **Exceptional Spatial Generalization (Zero-Shot Transfer):** A key contribution is the framework's capacity for spatial transfer without retraining. Models trained on smaller configurations of flexible tethers and nets successfully predicted and controlled significantly larger, unseen physical scales. This "zero-shot generalization" ability reduces the need for extensive data collection and model retraining for varying system sizes, making the approach highly scalable and practical for diverse mission requirements.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Control:** The KGD-MPC strategy leverages state condensing within the Koopman space, transforming complex nonlinear control problems into efficient quadratic programs. This reduction in computational complexity, from cubic dependence on combined state and control dimensions to only the control dimension, facilitates real-time closed-loop execution, which is essential for dynamic space operations.
- **Robustness through Online Adaptation:** The asynchronous dual-rate architecture enables continuous online adaptation of the Koopman model. By decoupling high-frequency control execution from computationally intensive model updates, the framework maintains robust performance against unmodeled dynamics, external disturbances, and time-varying system parameters without interrupting real-time control.
- **Broad Applicability to Space Missions:** The validation through ground experiments on flexible tethers and nets, combined with high-fidelity orbital simulations of a Tethered Space Robot (TSR) and a Tethered Space Net Robot (TSNR), indicates the framework's broad applicability. This includes advanced orbital operations such as on-orbit servicing, active debris removal, and the precise formation maintenance of multi-spacecraft systems.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The success of this data-driven, Koopman-informed graph approach lays a foundation for future developments in the control of highly flexible structures in space. The authors suggest future work will focus on real orbital experimental validation, which would be a critical step towards operational deployment.

In summary, this research offers a novel and robust solution for the modeling and control of complex tethered space systems, addressing long-standing challenges with a method that is accurate, scalable, and computationally viable for real-world space applications.
