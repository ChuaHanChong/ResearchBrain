# Plan Right, Then Plan Tight: Symbolic RL for Efficient Embodied Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University and collaborators developed SymPlan, a framework for embodied task planning that uses a Behavior Domain Definition Language (BDDL)-centric approach to provide verifiable supervision. This system, combining an 8B model with multi-granular symbolic rewards and adaptive length compression, achieves a 97.3% Strict Pass rate on BEHAVIOR-1000 while reducing plan length by 79%.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based planners lack cheap and deterministic methods to verify the validity and executability of generated plans in embodied environments.
- Current methods for building embodied planning datasets often rely on manual curation, which limits scalability for diverse, open-world tasks.
- Reinforcement learning for embodied planning struggles with sparse or uninformative reward signals, making it difficult to differentiate plan quality beyond simple success/failure.
- Achieving both correctness and conciseness in planning models is challenging, as premature length compression can compromise plan validity.

## Method

- **SymPlan** employs ==BDDL== as a unified interface for data construction, symbolic plan verification, and ==multi-granular reward== design for reinforcement learning.
- A lightweight ==symbolic engine== provides millisecond-latency feedback on plan validity, including goal completion rate and specific error traces, for efficient training supervision.
- The framework uses a ==correctness-gated length compression== mechanism, **GroupAdapt**, which dynamically adjusts plan length tolerance based on task difficulty, ensuring conciseness without sacrificing accuracy.
- ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== is initialized using ==symbolic rejection sampling== from a larger LLM, effectively distilling knowledge into a compact 8B model.

## Results

- The SymPlan framework achieved a **97.3%** Strict Pass (SP) rate on the BEHAVIOR-1000 benchmark, representing a **25.9%** relative improvement over the Qwen3-8B baseline.
- The method reduced the average response length to **207** tokens, a **79%** compression compared to uncompressed outputs, while outperforming larger frontier models in SP rate.
- Symbolic rejection-sampling distillation of Gemma-4-31B-IT into Qwen3-8B increased the latter's Strict Pass rate from **77.3%** to **92.3%** on B-1000.
- The video-to-BDDL pipeline demonstrated a **74%** LLM verifier acceptance rate, and an independent LLM judge preferred SymPlan-derived BDDL for instruction alignment in **64%** of cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single BDDL specification can serve as a comprehensive interface, unifying data construction, verifiable supervision, and reward signals across an embodied planning pipeline.
> - Multi-granular symbolic rewards, derived from a fast symbolic engine, effectively guide reinforcement learning agents toward generating strictly executable and error-free plans.
> - Dynamically adapting plan length tolerance based on in-batch task difficulty (GroupAdapt) allows for significant verbosity reduction while preserving or improving planning correctness.
> - Distilling a larger LLM's planning capabilities into a smaller model through symbolic rejection sampling provides a strong foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of embodied task planning, which involves enabling robotic agents to convert natural language instructions into executable action sequences within physical environments. Historically, this domain has seen two primary approaches. Classical PDDL-style and neuro-symbolic planners offer formal executability, often relying on hand-specified domains that can be challenging to scale to open-world scenarios. In contrast, recent advancements in Large Language Model (LLM)-based planners demonstrate fluency in generating plans for open-world tasks but typically lack a cheap and deterministic mechanism to verify the validity or executability of their generated plans within the target environment. This often necessitates reliance on string-level matching or computationally expensive full-fidelity simulations for validation, which are too slow for inner-loop training signals.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a BDDL (Behavior Domain Definition Language)-centric approach. BDDL, used in benchmarks like BEHAVIOR-1K, provides a structured yet open-vocabulary interface for defining scene objects, initial states, and goal conditions. By leveraging BDDL, the work aims to bridge the gap between the generative capabilities of LLMs and the need for formally verifiable, executable plans, offering a scalable layer for task structure, supervision, execution checks, and reward signals. It distinguishes itself from purely simulation-based verification by employing a lightweight symbolic engine for millisecond-latency feedback, and from other RL-based reasoning compression methods by gating length reduction on symbolic correctness and adapting length tolerance based on task difficulty.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method, SymPlan, that provides verifiable supervision and rewards for embodied planners without relying on string-level matching for plan validity or expensive full simulation for feedback. The work posits that a single BDDL specification can serve as a unified interface for data construction, plan verification, and reward design, thereby enhancing both the effectiveness and efficiency of embodied task planning.

The motivation for this approach stems from several challenges inherent in current embodied planning systems:

- **Lack of Deterministic Plan Verification:** Existing prompting-based and reinforcement learning planners often generate fluent action text, but a direct, cheap, and deterministic method to confirm the validity of these plans in the target physical world is frequently absent. Full-fidelity simulations, while providing high-fidelity checks, are too slow to serve as practical inner-loop training signals for large-scale reinforcement learning.
- **Scalable Data Construction:** Building robust datasets for embodied planning is challenging, particularly when requiring the recovery of typed objects, initial predicates, and goals from noisy, real-world sources like open-world videos. Existing BDDL files are often manually curated, limiting their scalability.
- **Efficient and Reliable Reward Signals:** Obtaining dense and informative feedback for training embodied planners is critical. Current methods may rely on sparse binary success signals or string-level comparisons, which do not sufficiently differentiate between partial goal achievement, plans that succeed via illegal actions, and strictly valid, error-free plans.
- **Balancing Correctness and Conciseness in Planning:** Reasoning models, including LLMs, often produce verbose outputs to solve tasks. However, applying length pressure prematurely can remove useful planning steps, compromising correctness. The challenge is to compress response length only after executable behavior has become reliable.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to enable a compact planning model to achieve high performance with short, executable responses, contributing to more practical and deployable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SymPlan is structured as a three-stage pipeline centered on verified BDDL, connecting data construction, symbolic execution, and reinforcement learning (RL) training.

#### 3.1. BDDL as the Data Interface (Video/BDDL-to-Data Synthesis and Action Discovery)

The initial stage focuses on converting task evidence, whether open-world videos or curated BDDL files, into a unified, verifier-aligned planner supervision format.

- **Video-to-BDDL Construction:** For visual input, a video-to-BDDL parser processes a task description and open-world video. It grounds task-relevant objects, infers initial predicates, and derives goal predicates. The resulting draft BDDL specification undergoes verification by an LLM verifier. This verifier checks for syntax correctness, object consistency, predicate feasibility, and goal executability. Only accepted BDDL drafts proceed.
- **Data Synthesis:** Each verified BDDL task is normalized into an "extended BDDL" format. This expansion retains core task objects and predicates while incorporating realistic scene distractors and their predicates, aiming to better reflect cluttered real environments. This structured information is then rewritten into hierarchical planning conversations, comprising environment state, robot embodiment specification, short clarification dialogue, and target steps with executable code.
- **Action Discovery:** When goal predicates are encountered that are not covered by the current action set, an automatic action builder proposes and compiles missing callable actions. This involves predicate gap analysis, LLM-assisted proposal generation, and symbolic code synthesis to expand the action library dynamically.
- **SFT Data Initialization:** A cold-start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) process uses symbolic rejection sampling. A large language model (Gemma-4-31B-IT) acts as a teacher, generating multiple rollouts for each prompt. The shortest strict-pass trajectory is kept when available, otherwise the prompt is excluded. This filtered data trains an initial compact checkpoint, D-SFT (Qwen3-8B-Gemma-Distill-SFT), which serves as the base for RL.

#### 3.2. Symbolic Verification Engine

A lightweight symbolic engine evaluates generated plans. It takes a plan, consisting of an action sequence, and executes each action against a state representation derived from the BDDL task specification.

- **State Representation:** Each task defines an initial state (s0) as a set of predicates over objects and a goal state (g) as a conjunction of target predicates. The engine maintains a mutable current state (st).
- **Execution and Verification:** For each action in the plan, the engine checks if its preconditions are satisfied in the current state. If satisfied, the state is updated according to the action’s defined effects. If preconditions are violated, the action is logged as an error, but execution continues to provide maximal feedback.
- **Metrics:** After plan execution, the engine computes:
 - **Goal Completion Rate (GCR):** The fraction of target goal predicates satisfied.
 - **Error Set (E):** A record of all precondition violations.
 - **Engine Pass (EP):** Achieved if GCR = 1.0 (all goals met, errors permitted).
 - **Strict Pass (SP):** Achieved if EP is true AND the error set is empty (|E|=0) (all goals met, no errors).

#### 3.3. SFT-Initialized RL with Multi-Granular Symbolic Rewards

The D-SFT checkpoint initializes the reinforcement learning phase. The same symbolic verifier from the previous stage provides the RL reward signals.

- **Optimizer and Reward Function:** The training uses DAPO (Dynamic Asymmetric Policy Optimization), an extension of GRPO, employing dynamic sampling, asymmetric clipping, token-level policy-gradient aggregation, and overlong reward shaping. KL regularization is disabled. The total reward R(y) for a plan y is a sum of three components:
 - R_fmt(y): A binary penalty for incorrect response format.
 - R_ans(y): The core answer reward, structured to be multi-granular: `(−0.5 + 2.5 * GCR) − 1_ (|E|>0) + 0.5 * 1_ EP`. This provides continuous credit for partial goal progress, a significant penalty for any precondition violation, and a bonus for achieving an Engine Pass. This design prevents the model from trading goal coverage for cleanliness.
 - R_len(y): A correctness-gated length penalty.

- **Correctness-Gated Length Compression (GroupAdapt):** This mechanism regulates response length after the model achieves stable correctness.
 - **Accuracy Gate:** The length reward (R_len) is applied only when the generated plan achieves Strict Pass (SP) AND the current batch's SP rate (a_b) is above a threshold relative to the running best SP rate (a_b ≥ a* − ϵ_acc). This ensures compression does not occur at the expense of correctness.
 - **Group-Adaptive Tolerance:** GroupAdapt replaces a fixed length tolerance with a task-adaptive one. It uses the instantaneous strict-pass rate (ρ_t) for a group of rollouts (e.g., G=8) of a given prompt in the current DAPO batch as a zero-cost difficulty signal.
 - If ρ_t ≥ τ (threshold, e.g., 0.5), the prompt is considered "easy," and a base length budget (δ_base = 200 tokens) is applied.
 - If ρ_t < τ, the prompt is considered "hard," and a wider length budget (2 * δ_base = 400 tokens) is granted.
 - As a hard prompt's group pass rate improves beyond τ, it automatically reverts to the easy budget.
 - **Length Reward Calculation:** When the accuracy gate is open, R_len rewards responses within δ_t tokens of the shortest known SP length (ℓ_min_t) for that task, and linearly decays for longer responses up to the observed maximum length (ℓ_max_t).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings regarding the SymPlan pipeline and its components:

- **Effectiveness of Symbolic Rejection-Sampling Distillation:** The D-SFT (Qwen3-8B-Gemma-Distill-SFT) checkpoint, initialized by symbolic rejection sampling from Gemma-4-31B-IT, demonstrated a substantial improvement over its Qwen3-8B base. On the BEHAVIOR-1000 (B-1000) benchmark, Qwen3-8B achieved a Strict-Pass (SP) rate of 77.3%, which increased to 92.3% for D-SFT. Similar gains were observed on BEHAVIOR-100 (B-100), rising from 72.0% to 87.0% SP.
- **Performance of SFT-Initialized Symbolic-Reward RL:** The application of DAPO (D-SFT+DAPO) further improved the SP rates, reaching 96.2% on B-1000 and 94.3% on B-100. This indicates that the multi-granular symbolic reward effectively guided the policy toward verifiable, error-free planning.
- **Impact of Correctness-Gated Length Compression (Short-RL and GroupAdapt):**
 - The addition of Short-RL (D-SFT+DAPO+Short-RL) maintained or improved accuracy while significantly reducing response length. For instance, on B-1000, output length dropped from 999 tokens (D-SFT+DAPO) to 194 tokens, with SP increasing to 96.9%.
 - The full method, D-SFT+DAPO+Short-RL+GroupAdapt (with τ=0.5), achieved an SP score of 97.3% on B-1000, representing a 25.9% relative improvement over the Qwen3-8B baseline. This result also exceeded the strongest large-model baseline (DeepSeek-V4-Flash at 93.8% SP) by 3.5%. Simultaneously, it compressed the average response length to 207 tokens, a 79% reduction compared to the uncompressed DAPO output.
 - Ablation studies on GroupAdapt’s threshold (τ) indicated that a higher threshold (τ=0.75), which provides temporary wider length tolerance to more prompts, could achieve a slightly higher SP of 97.6% on B-1000 with a moderate increase in length to 217 tokens.
- **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Mathematical Reasoning:** While not an optimization target, GroupAdapt variants partially recovered the performance drop in OOD mathematical reasoning observed with Short-RL on benchmarks like AIME24, AIME25, and MATH500, suggesting that adaptive length control can mitigate unintended side effects on broader reasoning capabilities.
- **BDDL Construction and Verification Robustness:** Evaluation of the video-to-BDDL pipeline on 50 BEHAVIOR-1K task videos showed a 100% engine loading rate and 74% LLM verifier acceptance rate. Furthermore, 64% of the time, an independent LLM judge preferred the SymPlan-derived BDDL over the official BEHAVIOR-1K BDDL regarding alignment with natural language instructions, primarily due to SymPlan’s focus on task-relevant objects specified in the instruction.
- **RL Headroom through Pass@k Analysis:** A pass@k analysis on D-SFT revealed significant headroom for RL. On B-1000, Strict Pass@1 was 91.4%, while Strict Pass@10 reached 99.0%, indicating that the model could often solve tasks with multiple samples, leaving 7.6 points of recoverable one-sample reliability for RL to exploit.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SymPlan framework contributes to embodied task planning by demonstrating a methodology for developing efficient and verifiable planners, with several implications:

- **Unified BDDL Interface:** The central significance lies in the demonstration that a single BDDL specification can serve as a shared, effective interface across multiple stages of an embodied planning pipeline: data construction, symbolic plan verification, SFT data filtering, and reinforcement learning reward design. This unification simplifies the overall architecture and promotes consistency.
- **Scalable and Verifiable Data Generation:** The video-to-BDDL parsing and LLM-based verification process offer a scalable approach to generate structured, verifiable task specifications from open-world visual evidence, reducing reliance on manual curation and potentially expanding the scope of tasks that can be used for training.
- **Efficient and Informative Training Feedback:** The millisecond-latency symbolic engine provides dense, multi-granular feedback (GCR, EP, SP, error traces). This rich signal is critical for guiding RL policies effectively, enabling differentiation between various levels of plan quality that binary success signals would conflate.
- **Achieving Compact and Executable Plans:** By integrating correctness-gated length compression with an adaptive tolerance schedule (GroupAdapt), the method enables planning models to generate highly accurate and strictly executable action sequences while significantly reducing their output verbosity. This efficiency is critical for real-time robotic applications and reducing computational overhead.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The improved performance of an 8B planner, surpassing larger frontier models in Strict Pass rates while generating substantially shorter responses, suggests a path towards more capable and practical household, assistive, and service robots. The ability to generate verifiable plans efficiently is a foundational requirement for robust robotic autonomy.
- **Adaptive Reasoning Compression in LLMs:** GroupAdapt's novel use of in-batch group pass rate as a zero-cost signal for dynamically adjusting length tolerance has broader implications for reasoning compression in LLMs. It offers a mechanism to protect the reasoning integrity of harder tasks while promoting conciseness for easier ones, without requiring external difficulty classifiers or expensive simulations.

Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges limitations, including its focus on high-level symbolic planning rather than low-level motor control, the ongoing challenge of robust real-time scene-to-BDDL construction from diverse perception inputs for real-world deployment, and the sensitivity to prompt engineering.
