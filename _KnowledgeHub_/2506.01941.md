# FreeTacMan: Robot-free Visuo-Tactile Data Collection System for Contact-rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University, The University of Hong Kong, and collaborators developed FreeTacMan, a robot-free visuo-tactile data collection system that enables human operators to gather high-precision, unattenuated visuo-tactile data for complex manipulation tasks. Policies trained on its large-scale dataset, utilizing a temporal-aware tactile pretraining strategy, achieved a 71% average success rate in contact-rich tasks, a substantial improvement over vision-only baselines.

## Problem

- Existing data collection systems for contact-rich robot manipulation lack efficiency, accuracy, and scalability, often providing attenuated tactile feedback and suffering from localization errors.
- Prior datasets for visuo-tactile learning are limited in scale, diversity, and precision, hindering the development of generalizable policies for robot dexterity.
- Integrating high-fidelity multimodal sensory information (vision and touch) is challenging due to intrinsic sensor limitations and domain gaps between modalities.

## Method

- **FreeTacMan** integrates a wearable ==in-situ gripper== that mounts visuo-tactile sensors directly on human fingertips, providing unattenuated tactile feedback via a precise linear transmission mechanism.
- A ==NOKOV Motion Capture System== tracks 6D end-effector poses with sub-millimeter accuracy, synchronized with visual and tactile data at 30 Hz, for robust human-to-robot data transfer.
- A two-stage learning pipeline involves ==temporal-aware multi-positive contrastive pretraining== for the tactile encoder, followed by an ==Action Chunking Transformer (ACT)== for visuo-tactile policy learning.

## Results

- The FreeTacMan system enabled the collection of a large-scale dataset with over **3000k** paired visuo-tactile images and **10k** demonstration trajectories across **50** diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- A user study showed FreeTacMan achieved higher task completion rates and efficiency (e.g., **5.05x** higher Completion per Unit Time score than ALOHA), with significantly fewer object slips and damages.
- Policies trained with the pretrained tactile encoder achieved an average success rate of **71%** in challenging tasks, outperforming vision-only (**21%**) and non-pretrained tactile integration (**55%**) baselines, and demonstrated strong generalization to unseen objects and cross-sensor environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The in-situ gripper design, featuring direct tactile sensor contact and a precise linear transmission, provides superior tactile fidelity and intuitive human control compared to previous mechanically attenuated systems.
> - Temporal-aware contrastive pretraining effectively learns robust and generalizable tactile representations, bridging the domain gap between vision and touch while improving performance, especially under data scarcity.
> - High-precision pose tracking from a motion capture system is crucial for accurate visuo-tactile data collection and reliable human-to-robot policy transfer, bypassing the limitations of SLAM/IMU-based localization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of performing contact-rich manipulation tasks with human-like dexterity remains a significant challenge in robot learning. This area necessitates the integration of multimodal sensory information, particularly vision and touch, which are fundamental to human manipulation. Vision provides global context for object recognition and pose estimation, while tactile feedback offers critical local contact information such as surface texture, in-hand object pose, material compliance, and force distribution, which cannot be acquired visually.

Prior research in robot manipulation has explored various data collection paradigms to train imitation learning policies. Early approaches involved mounting sensors directly on robots, which offered limited flexibility and scalability for diverse visuo-tactile data collection. More advanced systems, such as those relying on motion-capture with AR/VR visualization or primary-replica teleoperation rigs, could capture accurate camera views and trajectories but lacked direct, real-time tactile feedback and often involved complex calibration or high latency.

Handheld data collection systems, like UMI and FastUMI, offered increased flexibility by removing the robot embodiment from the human operator. However, these methods often depend on SLAM and IMU fusion for localization, which can introduce positioning errors detrimental to precise tactile perception. Additionally, their mechanical structures, typically using trigger-based grippers and multi-link transmissions, transmit tactile cues indirectly, leading to attenuated feedback, backlash, and inaccurate measurement of instantaneous tactile signals.

The existing visuo-tactile datasets, such as ObjectFolder, generally focus on specific perception tasks (e.g., 6DoF pose tracking, cross-modal generation) or have limited scale and diversity, often utilizing tactile array sensors with inherent limitations like low spatial resolution, crosstalk, and complex fabrication. These limitations hinder the training of generalizable visuo-tactile policies across a wide range of manipulation scenarios. The current work addresses these gaps by introducing a system designed to efficiently collect large-scale, high-precision visuo-tactile data with real-time, unattenuated tactile feedback, thus facilitating advancements in contact-rich robot manipulation and policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed data collection gap in robot learning for contact-rich manipulation tasks. Existing methods exhibit inefficiencies and limitations in their sensor setups, which collectively impede the development of robots with human-like dexterity and sensitivity. Specifically, the authors identify several shortcomings in previous data collection systems:

- **Inefficiency and Limited Tactile Feedback:** Prior handheld paradigms, while offering some flexibility, often use rigid, rod-based mechanical structures that provide indirect and attenuated tactile feedback. This makes intuitive control challenging for human operators and compromises the fidelity of collected tactile data.
- **Accuracy Limitations:** Methods relying on SLAM and IMU fusion for localization introduce significant positioning errors, which are particularly problematic for tasks requiring precise contact and tactile perception. Teleoperation systems can also suffer from complex calibration or high latency.
- **Scalability and Versatility:** Many existing setups are expensive, operationally complex, or designed for specific robot embodiments, limiting their scalability and applicability across diverse manipulation tasks and robotic platforms.
- **Data Quality and Diversity:** The aforementioned limitations result in visuo-tactile datasets that are often confined to specific perception tasks or lack the necessary diversity, precision, and scale for training generalizable policies for contact-rich manipulation. Furthermore, intrinsic limitations of common tactile sensors (e.g., low resolution, crosstalk) can constrain policy performance.

To address these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives for FreeTacMan:

1. **Develop a Human-Centric and Robot-Free Data Collection System:** The core objective was to design a system that allows human operators to intuitively control a gripper with direct, unattenuated tactile feedback, mirroring natural human interaction with objects.
2. **Ensure High Accuracy and Efficiency:** The system was designed to capture end-effector poses with sub-millimeter precision and synchronize visual and tactile feedback simultaneously, avoiding the drift and errors common in other localization methods. It also aimed for high efficiency in data collection, minimizing setup time and operational complexity.
3. **Create a Wearable Gripper with In-situ Visuo-Tactile Sensors:** This involved designing a modular, lightweight, and comfortable gripper that can be worn on human fingers, enabling direct contact and proprioception while minimizing mechanical attenuation of tactile signals.
4. **Collect a Large-Scale, High-Precision Multimodal Dataset:** A central goal was to leverage the developed system to curate an extensive dataset comprising visuo-tactile images, end-effector poses, and demonstration trajectories across a wide array of diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks.
5. **Validate Effectiveness for Policy Learning:** The research aimed to demonstrate that policies trained on the FreeTacMan dataset, especially when incorporating a temporal-aware tactile pretraining strategy, can achieve enhanced success rates in challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks compared to vision-only approaches or naive tactile integration.
6. **Promote Reproducibility and Research:** By open-sourcing both the hardware design and the collected dataset, the project aimed to facilitate wider adoption, reproducibility, and accelerated research in visuo-tactile robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FreeTacMan system integrates custom hardware design with a two-stage machine learning pipeline for data collection and policy learning.

#### 3.1. Hardware Design

The hardware was designed based on criteria for multimodal data acquisition, efficiency, scalability, and usability.

- **Multimodal Sensing:** The system incorporates two primary sensing components:
 - **Visuo-Tactile Sensors:** Based on the McTac design, these camera-based sensors are mounted on the gripper fingertips. They capture tactile images (640x480 pixels, 30 FPS) providing high-resolution deformation data upon contact.
 - **Wrist-Mounted Camera:** A fisheye camera with a 180° field-of-view lens (640x480 pixels, 30 FPS) provides a global visual context from the robot's wrist perspective.
 - **High-Precision Pose Tracking:** A NOKOV Motion Capture System is utilized for 6D pose tracking of the end-effector at 240 Hz, achieving sub-millimeter accuracy. Five retro-reflective markers are placed on the gripper interface—three on the top plate for pose measurement and two on the grippers for relative displacement.

- **In-situ Gripper:** This design is central to providing unattenuated tactile feedback:
 - **Fingertip Interface:** Visuo-tactile sensors are mounted directly on the operator’s fingertips, serving as the interface between the skin and the manipulated objects. This hinge-free operation eliminates intermediate mechanical linkages, aiming for zero mechanical attenuation and natural proprioception.
 - **Linear Transmission Mechanism:** To ensure precise control and stable motion, the gripper integrates chrome-plated steel shafts and linear bearings. An inverted crank-slider mechanism converts finger-driven rotational motion into synchronized linear output, constraining movement to highly accurate linear trajectories (axial deviation ≥ 0.02 mm). Dual parallel shafts and rolling bearings minimize friction and lateral torque, achieving over 90% transmission efficiency.

- **Modular Architecture:** FreeTacMan is structured as three plug-and-play modules to facilitate rapid setup and cross-embodiment compatibility:
 - **Sensor Perception Module:** Contains the visuo-tactile sensors.
 - **Universal Gripper Interface:** Allows the same sensor unit to be interchangeably mounted on data collection setups or various robotic arms (e.g., 6-DOF Piper, 7-DOF Franka) through customized interfaces.
 - **Camera Mounting Scaffold:** Ensures consistent visual feedback by aligning the wrist camera.

- **Human-Machine Interface Design:** For user comfort and rapid adaptability:
 - **Wearability:** Hook-and-loop straps enable quick fastening and accommodate hand sizes from the 5th to 95th percentile of adults.
 - **Ergonomics:** The design is lightweight (157.5 g) and compact (145 × 85 × 106 mm³).

#### 3.2. Human-to-Robot Data Transfer

The system is designed to seamlessly transfer human demonstrations to robot execution.
- **Pose Tracking and Synchronization:** The motion capture system tracks the 6D pose of the gripper. The raw data is downsampled to 30 Hz to synchronize with RGB images. Each collected frame includes: wrist camera RGB image, two visuo-tactile images, the end-effector pose of the in-situ gripper in the world coordinate frame, and gripper width.
- **Coordinate Transformation:** Marker coordinates are transformed from the OptiTrack's world frame to the robot's base frame. A local coordinate frame, aligned with the robot's URDF, is established at the gripper's Tool Center Point (TCP) using three top-plate markers.
- **Action Representation:** The collected embodiment-agnostic poses are mapped to robot joint positions using an inverse kinematic solver (IKPY), serving as the action representation for imitation learning. This approach avoids the limitations of SLAM and IMU fusion, such as drift and tracking errors.

#### 3.3. Tactile Pretraining and Policy Learning

A two-stage learning approach is employed to leverage the collected visuo-tactile data.

- **Tactile Representation Learning:**
 - **CLIP-style Contrastive Pretraining:** A contrastive pretraining procedure is adopted to learn effective tactile representations and bridge the domain gap between tactile and visual data.
 - **Encoder Architecture:** Both visual ($f_v$) and tactile ($f_t$) encoders share a ResNet backbone. $f_v$ is frozen, while $f_t$ is finetuned during pretraining.
 - **Projection Heads:** Vision ($g_v$) and tactile ($g_t$) projection heads convert encoder outputs into normalized embeddings. The tactile projection head ($g_t$) concatenates tactile features with a normalized 7-DOF joint position vector ($q_i$) to incorporate robot joint state as global context.
 - **Multi-Positive Contrastive Loss:** The training objective minimizes a contrastive loss that aligns tactile features with visual features. To enforce temporal awareness and capture evolving contact patterns, both a "primary positive" (time-aligned visual features $v_i$) and a "secondary positive" (visual features from the next timestep $v_{i+1}$) are used, along with negatives from a fixed-size memory bank.

- **Visuo-Tactile Action Chunking Transformer (ACT):**
 - **Policy Architecture:** The pretrained tactile encoder extracts tactile representations. These are concatenated with visual embeddings and fed into an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) model.
 - **Action Prediction:** The ACT model is trained to predict a sequence (chunk) of 6-DOF robot arm joint positions and 1-DOF gripper positions, enabling continuous and coordinated motion control for manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings concerning the system's performance, the quality of the collected dataset, and the effectiveness of the proposed policy learning approach.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics

- **FreeTacMan Dataset:** The system enabled the collection of a large-scale multimodal dataset comprising over 3000k paired visuo-tactile images with corresponding end-effector poses. This dataset includes more than 10k demonstration trajectories across 50 diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- **Task Diversity:** The tasks cover a spectrum of fundamental tactile capabilities, including high-precision positioning, high-precision force control, high-fidelity dynamic response, and high-acuity tactile perception.
- **Data Quality:** Every trajectory in the dataset underwent a replay-based validation process to ensure reliability for downstream robot learning.

#### 4.2. User Study on Data Collection System (Addressing Q1)

A user study involving 12 volunteers compared FreeTacMan against two representative data collection methods: primary-replica-based teleoperation (ALOHA) and handheld devices (UMI).

- **Completion Rate and Efficiency:** FreeTacMan consistently achieved higher completion rates (up to 100% for many tasks) and significantly shorter average task durations (e.g., 2.98s for Stamp Press compared to 8.09s for ALOHA and 3.91s for UMI).
- **CPUT Score:** FreeTacMan exhibited a higher Completion per Unit Time (CPUT) score across all tasks, demonstrating an overall advantage (e.g., 5.05x higher performance than ALOHA and 1.52x higher than UMI).
- **Slippage and Damage Prevention:** FreeTacMan recorded substantially fewer object slips (maximum 2 slips) and zero damages across all tasks, indicating superior force perception and control. In contrast, ALOHA incurred up to 14 damages (Fragile Cup) and UMI reported up to 27 slips (USB Plug).
- **User Experience:** Participants rated FreeTacMan favorably in terms of stability, comfort, ease of collection, ease of entry, and control accuracy.

These results indicate that FreeTacMan enables more efficient and accurate collection of manipulation demonstrations compared to the other evaluated systems, particularly for tasks requiring precise force feedback and control.

#### 4.3. Validation on Imitation Learning (Addressing Q2 and Q3)

Policies were trained and evaluated on challenging contact-rich tasks (Fragile Cup, Calligraphy, Texture Classification, Stamp Press, USB Plug) using three configurations: Vision-only ACT, Ours (+ Tactile w/o Pretrain), and Ours (+ Pretrain).

- **Impact of Tactile Feedback (Q2):**
 - **Vision-only Baseline:** The vision-only ACT achieved a low average success rate of 21%, struggling particularly with tasks requiring fine tactile perception (e.g., 0% for USB Plug, 20% for Calligraphy).
 - **Tactile Integration (w/o Pretrain):** Incorporating tactile feedback directly into the ACT model, even without pretraining, substantially improved performance, raising the average success rate to 55%. Notable gains were observed in Fragile Cup (75%) and Calligraphy (70%).
 - **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** In the texture classification task, tactile input (without pretraining) achieved 55% accuracy on unseen objects with out-of-distribution color, matching its performance on training objects and significantly outperforming the vision-only baseline (15%). This indicates that tactile information enables robust generalization across object variations.

- **Impact of Temporal-Aware Pretraining (Q3):**
 - **Pretrained Tactile Encoder:** Integrating the tactile encoder pretrained with the multi-positive contrastive objective (Ours + Pretrain) further boosted the average success rate to 71%. This improvement was particularly evident in tasks demanding fine-grained control and dynamic contact tracking, such as Calligraphy (90%) and Stamp Press (80%). Performance for USB Plug also improved to 20%, although it remained challenging.
 - **Benefits under Data Scarcity:** Ablation studies demonstrated that the pretrained policy achieved superior performance even with a small number of task-specific demonstrations (e.g., 55% for Fragile Cup with 50 episodes compared to 35% for the vision-only baseline with 200 episodes). This indicates that pretraining provides robust, generalized features.
 - **Cross-Sensor Generalization:** The pretrained model demonstrated strong generalization to out-of-domain visuo-tactile sensors (differing in marker angle and RGB lighting environment). For example, it maintained an 80% success rate for the fragile cup task and 75% for texture classification, closely matching in-domain performance. In contrast, the non-pretrained tactile model showed significant performance drops in challenging phases (e.g., from 80% to 40% in texture classification, and from 70% to 10% in the landing phase of fragile cup).
 - **Attention Analysis:** Visualization of attention maps confirmed that tactile integration refines visual attention and that the model’s attention dynamically focuses on regions of deformation in tactile images upon contact, enabling real-time inference of texture and geometry.

These results collectively confirm the effectiveness of the data collected by FreeTacMan and highlight the importance of high-fidelity tactile feedback and temporal-aware pretraining for learning robust and generalizable policies for contact-rich robot manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FreeTacMan system and its associated dataset represent a contribution to the field of robot learning and manipulation. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Addresses a Critical Data Collection Gap:** By providing an efficient, accurate, and human-centric method for collecting visuo-tactile data, FreeTacMan directly addresses the long-standing challenge of obtaining high-quality, large-scale datasets for contact-rich manipulation tasks. This can accelerate research that has been hindered by the limitations of previous data collection paradigms.
- **Enables More Capable Robot Manipulation:** The findings demonstrate that policies trained on FreeTacMan data, especially with tactile pretraining, achieve substantially higher success rates in complex and delicate manipulation tasks. This suggests a pathway towards developing robots that can perform intricate tasks requiring fine force control, precise alignment, and texture recognition with improved dexterity and reliability.
- **Facilitates Reproducibility and Open Science:** The commitment to open-sourcing both the hardware design and the extensive multimodal dataset is a direct contribution to the research community. It allows other researchers to replicate experiments, build upon the foundation laid, and develop new algorithms and benchmarks, thereby fostering collaborative progress in visuo-tactile robot learning.
- **Advances Multimodal Learning:** The proposed temporal-aware contrastive pretraining strategy for tactile encoders offers a method for aligning visual and tactile embeddings, enhancing the learning of robust representations. This contributes to the broader field of multimodal machine learning, particularly in bridging domain gaps between different sensory inputs.
- **Foundation for Future Robot Capabilities:** The system and dataset can serve as a foundational resource for exploring more advanced robotic capabilities, such as dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands, bimanual long-horizon tasks, and manipulation in unstructured environments. The stated future work on developing high-precision visual algorithms for in-the-wild data collection further extends this potential.
- **Informs Sensor and Gripper Design:** The "in-situ" gripper design, emphasizing direct tactile feedback and precise linear transmission, provides insights into effective hardware design principles for human-robot interaction and data acquisition systems in contact-rich scenarios.

In summary, FreeTacMan contributes to overcoming fundamental barriers in robot learning by providing both a robust methodology for data acquisition and a high-quality dataset, thereby laying groundwork for the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems capable of interacting with the physical world with greater nuance and skill.
