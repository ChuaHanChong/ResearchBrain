# Adversarial Attacks on Robotic Vision Language Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Pennsylvania, Carnegie Mellon University, and Gray Swan AI researchers demonstrate that VLA models controlling physical robots are highly vulnerable to adversarial text prompts, with Greedy Coordinate Gradient attacks achieving over 90% success rates in forcing OpenVLA-controlled robotic arms to execute arbitrary targeted actions across four fine-tuned variants (Libero-Goal: 96.5%, Libero-Object: 93.8%, Libero-Spatial: 97.5%), while attacks persist across multiple rollout steps with 28x improvement over nominal baselines and transfer from simulation to real-world environments despite reduced effectiveness (61.2% to 38.0%), revealing that fine-tuned VLAs are significantly more susceptible than base models and that standard LLM defenses like perplexity filtering prove either ineffective or impractical for robotic control, fundamentally redefining AI safety from preventing harmful text generation to preventing malicious control authority over physical actions.

## Problem

- VLA models that directly control robot actuators inherit vulnerabilities from their LLM backbones but lack targeted safety research
- Traditional LLM jailbreaking focuses on eliciting harmful text rather than controlling physical actions
- No systematic study exists of adversarial attacks on low-level robotic control systems
- Current AI safety frameworks inadequately address embodied AI systems with real-world physical consequences

## Method

- Adapt ==Greedy Coordinate Gradient== (**GCG**) attacks from LLM jailbreaking to VLA control by optimizing ==adversarial text suffixes==
- Reframe threat model from semantic harm to "==control authority==" — ability to force arbitrary physical actions
- Develop three attack variants: ==single-step targeting==, ==persistence across visual changes==, and ==cross-architecture transfer==
- Test attacks on multiple **OpenVLA** variants controlling 7-DOF robotic arms with ==256-bin discretized action spaces==

## Results

- Single-step attacks succeed in 30–110 optimization steps (3–10 minutes) with **77–97%** success rates across models
- Persistence attacks maintain targeted actions for **28×** more steps than nominal baselines
- Sim-to-real transfer maintains substantial attack effectiveness (**61.2%** → **38.0%**) despite environmental differences
- Token budget directly correlates with attack success — halving tokens reduces success by factor of two
- Current LLM-based defenses achieve **0%** attack success but corrupt legitimate robot functionality

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA action spaces are highly "reachable" with adversarial prompting achieving 90%+ success rates
> - Fine-tuned VLAs show 40-60% increased vulnerability compared to base models due to concentrated action distributions
> - Attacks persist across changing visual inputs and transfer from simulation to real-world environments
> - Standard LLM defenses (perplexity filtering, smoothing) prove ineffective or impractical for VLA outputs
> - Cross-architecture transfer shows limited success, suggesting attack specificity to model architecture
