# PhysDreamer: Physics-Based Interaction with 3D Objects via Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysDreamer endows static 3D objects with interactive dynamics by estimating their spatially-varying physical material properties from visual data. It distills dynamics priors from video generation models to drive a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator, achieving an 80.8% preference rate for motion realism over PhysGaussian in user studies.

## Problem

- Generating realistic, action-conditioned dynamics for static 3D objects is challenging, limiting immersive virtual experiences.
- Estimating accurate physical material properties (e.g., stiffness) for real-world objects from visual data is difficult due to the scarcity of ground-truth data and the need for spatially-varying parameters.
- Existing video generation models produce pre-defined motions and cannot respond to novel physical interactions, while traditional physics simulators require explicit, manually specified material parameters.

## Method

- A novel optimization loop distills dynamics priors from off-the-shelf, pre-trained image-to-video generation models into physical parameters.
- Static 3D objects are represented as 3D Gaussians, and spatially-varying Young's modulus is modeled as a neural field (triplanes + MLP).
- A differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) simulator is used to predict object deformation, enhanced by a subsampling strategy for efficient simulation on fewer 'driving particles'.

## Results

- In user studies, PhysDreamer was preferred for motion realism in 80.8% of cases over PhysGaussian and 63.5% over DreamGaussian4D.
- Achieved a 53.7% preference rate for motion realism against real captured videos across diverse objects, with specific instances (e.g., alocasia plant) showing an 86% preference.
- Demonstrated lower (better) Frechet Video Distance (FVD) and Frechet Inception Distance (FID) scores compared to baselines, indicating higher quality motion and overall visual fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale video generation models implicitly learn a 'dynamics prior' relating object appearance to plausible motion, which can be explicitly distilled to infer physical material properties.
> - Combining powerful generative models with differentiable physics simulation allows for the estimation of physical parameters without requiring direct material ground truth.
> - Inferring spatially-varying material properties enables more nuanced and realistic deformation patterns, reflecting how real objects often have heterogeneous stiffness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by PhysDreamer addresses a critical and challenging problem in computer vision and graphics: enabling static 3D objects to exhibit realistic, action-conditioned dynamics. This capability is essential for creating truly immersive virtual experiences, interactive simulations, and more realistic digital content.

The broader research landscape can be broadly categorized:
- **Dynamic 3D Reconstruction and Generation:** Recent advancements, particularly with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, have significantly improved the capture and creation of static 3D assets and even unconditioned 4D (3D + time) dynamics. Methods like DreamGaussian4D can generate fixed-length dynamic 3D scenes. However, these methods typically generate pre-defined motions and cannot respond to *novel, arbitrary physical interactions* like external forces. They lack a grounded understanding of physical material properties.
- **Physics-Based Simulation:** Traditional physics-based animation relies on manually defined or painstakingly measured material properties (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio). While methods like the Material Point Method (MPM) offer robust simulation capabilities for various materials, applying them to real-world scanned or generated 3D assets is hampered by the difficulty of obtaining accurate material ground truth. Recent works like PhysGaussian, PAC-NeRF, and DANO integrate physics simulation with neural representations, but still struggle with automatically inferring appropriate material parameters.
- **Interactive Motion Generation:** This area focuses on animating images or 3D content based on user input (text, direct manipulation). Image-space methods have shown promise, but lack true 3D physical grounding. For 3D assets, prior physics-based approaches often require explicit material parameters.
- **Video Generation Models:** The past few years have seen an explosion in the capabilities of large-scale video generation models (e.g., Stable Video Diffusion, Lumiere, and OpenAI's recent work). These models, trained on vast quantities of diverse real-world video data, implicitly learn complex dynamics and how objects move in response to various unconditioned stimuli. However, they are not designed to allow users to directly apply forces and observe physically consistent, interactive responses.

PhysDreamer innovatively bridges the gap between these areas. It leverages the "dynamics priors" implicitly learned by powerful video generation models, not to *generate* video directly, but to *infer the underlying physical material properties* of static 3D objects. By distilling this implicit knowledge into explicit physical parameters that can drive a differentiable physics simulator (MPM), PhysDreamer enables static objects to respond physically plausibly to arbitrary interactive stimuli. This approach bypasses the need for difficult-to-obtain material ground truth, representing a significant step towards truly interactive and physically intelligent virtual content. It pushes the boundaries of AI's ability to "understand" and simulate the physical world based purely on visual observation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of PhysDreamer is to transform static 3D objects into interactive entities that can realistically respond to novel physical interactions, such as external forces or agent manipulations. At its core, this entails **estimating the physical material properties** of these objects from visual data alone.

The motivation for this research stems from several critical challenges and desires in creating immersive virtual experiences:
- **Need for Realistic Interactions:** While modern 3D vision techniques excel at capturing and generating high-quality static 3D assets, they often fall short in synthesizing realistic, action-conditioned dynamics. For instance, creating a digital rose that sways naturally in a breeze or deforms plausibly when touched requires an understanding of its physical properties, not just its static appearance. Without this, virtual environments feel "dead" or unresponsive.
- **Difficulty of Material Property Estimation:** Estimating the physical material properties (e.g., stiffness, elasticity, density) of real-world objects is an extremely challenging open problem. This difficulty arises from:
 - **Lack of Ground-Truth Data:** Measuring these properties for real objects is complex, often requiring specialized equipment and destructive testing. There's a severe scarcity of large-scale datasets pairing object geometry with precise material properties.
 - **Spatially-Varying Properties:** Real objects often exhibit heterogeneous material properties, meaning their stiffness or other characteristics can vary across their structure, making a single global parameter insufficient.
- **Human Intuition as Inspiration:** Humans can effortlessly imagine how objects would react to forces, inferring dynamics from appearance. This ability is rooted in our accumulated "physical prior knowledge" gained from observing and interacting with the world. This human capability inspired the authors to look for similar implicit physical priors in large-scale video generation models.
- **Leveraging Advancements in Video Generation:** Recent large video generation models, trained on vast datasets of real-world videos, implicitly capture a rich understanding of how objects move and interact. The authors hypothesize that these models encode a "dynamics prior" relating object appearance to its probable motion, which can be distilled to infer physical properties.

By addressing these motivations, PhysDreamer aims to enable a new generation of virtual experiences where digital objects are not just visually present but also physically responsive and believable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysDreamer introduces a novel, physics-based approach to endow static 3D objects with interactive dynamics. The core methodology revolves around distilling dynamics priors from pre-trained video generation models to estimate spatially-varying physical material properties, which then drive a differentiable physics simulator.

#### 3.1. Object Representation and Physical Modeling

- **3D Gaussians:** Static 3D objects are represented using 3D Gaussians, known for their efficiency in novel view synthesis and their Lagrangian nature, making them suitable for particle-based physics simulations.
- **Material Field:** The key physical property targeted for estimation is Young's modulus E(x), which measures material stiffness. This is modeled as a *spatially-varying neural field* parameterized by triplanes followed by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP). Other properties like mass (constant density * particle volume) and Poisson's ratio (homogeneous constant) are simplified based on preliminary experiments.
- **Physics Simulation:** The differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) is employed for simulating elastic object dynamics directly on the Gaussian particles. MPM is a robust hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian method capable of handling complex material behaviors and large deformations. The simulation uses a fixed corotated hyperelastic model, linking Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio to stress-strain relationships.

#### 3.2. Distillation and Optimization Loop

The overall process is a feedback loop, illustrated in Figure 3:
1. **Static Object Rendering:** Given an input static 3D object (represented by 3D Gaussians), a static image (I0) is rendered from a chosen viewpoint.
2. **Reference Video Generation:** An off-the-shelf, pre-trained image-to-video generation model (specifically, Stable Video Diffusion) takes I0 and generates a short "reference video" clip {I0, I1..., IT}. This video implicitly encodes plausible, unconditioned dynamics for the object, providing the "dynamics prior."
3. **Differentiable Simulation and Rendering:**
 - The current estimates of the spatially-varying Young's modulus field E(x) and an initial velocity field v0(x) (also a neural field) are used to drive the differentiable MPM simulator.
 - The simulator predicts the evolution of the 3D Gaussian particles' positions and rotations over time.
 - These simulated 3D Gaussians are then fed into a differentiable renderer to produce a "rendered video" (I_hat_t).
4. **Loss Calculation and Optimization:** A per-frame loss function, combining L1 and D-SSIM, measures the discrepancy between the rendered video (I_hat_t) and the reference video (It). This loss is back-propagated through the differentiable rendering and simulation steps to update the parameters of the neural material field E(x) and the initial velocity field v0(x).
 - **Two-Stage Optimization:** To enhance stability and convergence, optimization is split:
 - **Stage 1:** Young's modulus is randomly initialized and frozen. Only the initial velocity field is optimized, using only the first three frames of the reference video.
 - **Stage 2:** The initial velocity is frozen. The spatially-varying Young's modulus is then optimized. Gradient flow is limited to the previous frame to prevent vanishing/exploding gradients.
 - **Regularization:** A total variation (Ltv) regularization is applied to the triplanes of both the material and velocity fields to encourage spatial smoothness.

#### 3.3. Accelerated Simulation with Subsampling

- **Challenge:** High-fidelity 3D Gaussian representations often involve millions of particles, making direct simulation prohibitively expensive.
- **Solution:** A subsampling strategy is employed. K-Means clustering is used to select a much smaller set of "driving particles" (typically 10-50 times fewer than Gaussian particles). The full MPM simulation is run *only* on these driving particles.
- **Interpolation for Rendering:** For rendering, the positions and rotations of the original, high-fidelity 3D Gaussian particles are interpolated. For each Gaussian particle, its 8 nearest driving particles at t=0 are identified. A local rigid body transformation is fitted between these driving particles' initial positions and their simulated current positions. This transformation is then applied to the Gaussian particle's initial position and rotation to obtain its current state.

#### 3.4. Multi-view Reference Videos (Ablation)

For objects with significant self-occlusion, relying on a single reference video can lead to incomplete supervision. PhysDreamer supports using multiple reference videos from different viewpoints to jointly optimize the material field, providing more comprehensive dynamics information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysDreamer demonstrates its ability to synthesize realistic and interactive 3D dynamics across a diverse range of elastic objects, providing both qualitative and quantitative evidence for its effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Interactive Dynamics:** The project website (https://physdreamer.github.io/) showcases the synthesized interactive motions, with objects like various flowers, an alocasia plant, a telephone cord, and a beanie hat dynamically responding to applied external forces (e.g., poking, dragging).
- **Material Field Visualization:** Figure 5 qualitatively visualizes the spatially-varying Young's modulus fields estimated by PhysDreamer. Brighter colors indicate higher stiffness, revealing how different parts of an object (e.g., the stem versus petals of a flower) can have varying material properties, leading to realistic deformation patterns.
- **Space-Time Slices (Figure 6):** These visualizations effectively compare the temporal evolution of object motion across different methods and real videos. They highlight PhysDreamer's ability to capture damping and realistic oscillation patterns.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation via User Study (2AFC Protocol)

A user study involving 100 participants making 800 two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) judgments was conducted to evaluate motion realism and visual quality.

- **PhysDreamer vs. PhysGaussian:**
 - **Motion Realism:** PhysDreamer was significantly preferred in 80.8% of cases. PhysGaussian, lacking an principled material estimation method, often produced unrealistic, slow, and overly large motions.
 - **Visual Quality:** PhysDreamer was preferred in 65.0% of cases, benefiting from more realistic motion that contributes to overall visual appeal.
- **PhysDreamer vs. DreamGaussian4D:**
 - **Motion Realism:** PhysDreamer was preferred in 63.5% of cases. DreamGaussian4D generates fixed, often periodic motions without physically plausible damping or responsiveness to arbitrary interactions. It cannot extrapolate to new forces or exhibit the natural decay of motion that PhysDreamer achieves through physics-based simulation.
 - **Visual Quality:** PhysDreamer was preferred in 70.0% of cases.
 - **Motion Amount:** An additional comparison on "motion amount" showed PhysDreamer preferred in 73.6% of samples, indicating its ability to generate more pronounced and engaging motion.
- **PhysDreamer vs. Real Captured Videos:**
 - **Motion Realism:** PhysDreamer achieved a 53.7% preference rate for motion realism against real videos. This is a strong result, indicating that its synthesized dynamics are often indistinguishable from or even preferred over real captures in a blind comparison.
 - **Unexpected Observation:** For the 'alocasia' scene, 86% of users preferred PhysDreamer's motion realism over real captures. The authors hypothesize that the Material Point Method's tendency to produce smoother, lower-frequency motions for thin geometries (like alocasia leaves) might be perceived as more "natural" by human observers, who are known to have biases in judging naturalness of motion.
 - **Visual Quality:** PhysDreamer had a 37.3% preference for visual quality against real videos, indicating that while motion realism is high, rendering details or overall fidelity might still lag behind real captures.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Multi-view Reference Videos (Figure 7)

- For self-occluding objects like the alocasia plant, providing supervision from multiple generated viewpoints significantly improved results. The multi-view approach was preferred over single-view in 86.0% for motion realism and 81.0% for visual quality, demonstrating the value of comprehensive observational data for material property estimation.

#### 4.4. Quantitative Metrics (Appendix A)

- **Frechet Video Distance (FVD):** PhysDreamer consistently achieved lower (better) FVD scores against real captured videos compared to both PhysGaussian and DreamGaussian4D, reinforcing its superior motion quality (average FVD of 270.3 vs. 482.6 and 432.6, respectively).
- **Frechet Inception Distance (FID):** Similarly, PhysDreamer showed a lower (better) FID score, indicating higher overall image quality across frames (47.7 vs. 63.2 for PhysGaussian and 52.8 for DreamGaussian4D).

These results collectively demonstrate that PhysDreamer effectively addresses the challenge of material property estimation by distilling dynamics priors from video generation models, leading to significantly more realistic and interactive 3D object dynamics compared to existing baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysDreamer represents a significant advancement in the field of interactive 3D content creation, bridging the gap between static 3D assets, implicit knowledge in generative AI, and explicit physics-based simulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Paradigm for Physical Property Estimation:** The most profound contribution of PhysDreamer is its innovative approach to inferring physical material properties. By distilling "dynamics priors" from large-scale video generation models, it bypasses the traditional reliance on painstaking manual measurement or ground-truth data, which has long been a bottleneck in creating physically-based simulations for arbitrary objects. This shifts the paradigm from explicit engineering of material properties to implicit learning from abundant visual data.
- **Enabling Truly Interactive Virtual Experiences:** For the first time, static 3D objects can be endowed with physically plausible and responsive dynamics to novel interactions. This capability is transformative for virtual environments, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and video games, where the ability of objects to react realistically to user input (e.g., touching, pushing, dropping) is crucial for immersion and engagement.
- **Enhanced Realism in Digital Content Creation:** The method allows for the creation of more believable and lively digital content. Imagine a virtual flower that sways realistically in response to a simulated breeze or a digital character whose clothing wrinkles and moves naturally with its actions, all without manual rigging or complex physics setup. This opens up new possibilities for animation, visual effects, and digital product prototyping.
- **Advancement in AI's Physical Understanding:** PhysDreamer demonstrates that complex, implicit physical knowledge embedded within large-scale generative models can be extracted and made explicit. This contributes to the broader goal of building AI systems with a deeper, more grounded understanding of the physical world, moving beyond purely visual pattern recognition to inferring underlying physical laws.
- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - The work lays the groundwork for extending this approach to more complex material types (e.g., fluids, granular materials, rigid bodies with collisions), beyond the current focus on elastic objects without collisions.
 - It motivates further research into automating the entire pipeline, including object discovery, segmentation, and boundary condition specification, which currently require manual input.
 - Addressing computational efficiency remains a key future challenge, as the current method is demanding, suggesting opportunities for optimized differentiable simulators or more efficient knowledge distillation.
 - The "surprising" user study result (preference for simulated alocasia over real) opens up interesting avenues for studying human perception of physical realism and how it might differ from objective physical accuracy.

In essence, PhysDreamer empowers the creation of dynamic, interactive digital worlds where objects behave not just realistically, but *physically plausibly*, driven by AI that has learned implicitly from vast observations of our own physical reality.
