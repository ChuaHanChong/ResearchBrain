# Learning Diffusion Policies from Demonstrations For Compliant Contact-rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at OMRON SINIC X Corporation developed Diffusion Policies For Compliant Manipulation (DIPCOM), a framework integrating diffusion models with active compliance control to enable rigid robots to perform complex, contact-rich tasks from limited human demonstrations. This approach demonstrated superior performance in long-horizon, repetitive tasks like powder grinding and pencil erasing compared to a VAE-based baseline, achieving 55.88% fine powder in grinding and a 52.3% success rate in erasing.

## Problem

- Rigid robots struggle to achieve human-like dexterity and stable, consistent force application in contact-rich manipulation tasks due to difficulties in maintaining stable contact.
- Existing Learning from Demonstration (LfD) methods, including prior VAE-ACT approaches, exhibit limitations in solving long-horizon tasks with repetitive behaviors, often leading to instability or an inability to adapt.
- Achieving adaptive force control and precision in dynamic, contact-rich environments requires policies that can predict both end-effector poses and dynamically adjust robot stiffness.

## Method

- DIPCOM integrates a classifier-free conditional diffusion model to generate absolute Cartesian end-effector poses and stiffness parameters, conditioned on multi-modal observations (RGB images, force/torque, proprioception).
- It employs a 6D continuous orientation representation to improve neural network training stability and consistency for rotational actions.
- The framework incorporates a higher action prediction frequency (48 actions per horizon) and an extended ==Temporal Ensemble== technique to mitigate error accumulation and ensure stable performance in long-horizon contact tasks.

## Results

- DIPCOM achieved **55.88%** fine powder in a grinding task, significantly outperforming the baseline (**9.96%**) and successfully reproducing complex circular motions and periodic pauses.
- For pencil erasing, DIPCOM achieved **77.32%** erased marks and a **52.3%** success rate, applying more effective force compared to the baseline's **26.0%** erased and **0.0%** success rate.
- In bimanual peg insertion tasks, DIPCOM matched the baseline's high success rates (**100%** round, **95%** cuboid) while exhibiting more diverse trajectories and introducing novel, un-demonstrated motor skills, such as continuous adjustment of the supporting arm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models, when integrated with compliance control, can effectively capture multimodal action distributions, enabling rigid robots to learn diverse and adaptive behaviors for complex contact-rich tasks from limited demonstrations.
> - A continuous 6D orientation representation significantly improves the robustness and consistency of neural network outputs for robot rotations, overcoming issues with discontinuous representations.
> - Combining higher action prediction frequency with a Temporal Ensemble technique helps stabilize control and reduce error accumulation in long-horizon, repetitive contact interactions, critical for compliant manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of performing complex manipulation tasks, particularly those involving physical contact and requiring human-like dexterity, remains a significant challenge in robotics. Traditional rigid robots, which often rely on position or velocity control, face difficulties in maintaining stable contact and applying consistent force in environments that demand intricate force-intensive interactions. Such scenarios are common in industrial settings, laboratories, and workshops.

Learning from Demonstration (LfD) has emerged as a prominent paradigm to enable robots to acquire complex skills by observing human experts. This approach bypasses the need for explicit programming of complex motion trajectories and force profiles. However, applying LfD to tasks requiring precise, contact-rich manipulation, such as grinding or delicate assembly, presents its own set of challenges. Rigid robots, even with LfD, can struggle to sustain controlled contact and force over extended durations, limiting their utility in demanding applications. Some prior works address this through mechanical compliance mechanisms, but these can complicate precise tool control.

A complementary approach involves active compliance control, which allows rigid robots to manage direct contact tasks by modulating forces through external sensors. Previous research in this area, including the authors' prior work, Comp-ACT, combined force information and compliance control with policies based on Variational AutoEncoders (VAEs) and the Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) strategy. While Comp-ACT demonstrated performance in various tasks, it encountered limitations when dealing with long-horizon tasks that involve repetitive behaviors.

Concurrently, diffusion models have gained traction as powerful probabilistic generative models capable of learning complex data distributions. In robotics, diffusion models have been shown to effectively capture multi-modal action distributions, leading to more diverse and robust behaviors compared to VAE-based policies. They have been applied across various robotic domains, including motion planning, navigation, human-robot interaction, and grasping. However, the integration of diffusion models with active compliance control for robust, contact-rich manipulation, especially for rigid robots operating with limited demonstrations, represents an active area of research.

This paper positions its work, Diffusion Policies For Compliant Manipulation (DIPCOM), as an advancement within this landscape. It aims to bridge the gap between the expressive power of diffusion models and the requirements of compliant contact-rich manipulation. By integrating diffusion policies with a compliance controller, the research seeks to overcome the limitations of prior LfD methods, particularly in tasks requiring sustained force control and long-horizon repetitive actions, using fewer demonstrations than studies that do not incorporate compliance control. It extends the foundational robot demonstration system from their previous work by adopting a diffusion-based policy architecture, thereby aiming to improve performance in force-intensive tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel diffusion-based framework, named Diffusion Policies For Compliant Manipulation (DIPCOM), that enables rigid robots to learn and perform complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks from human demonstrations with active compliance control.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current robotic manipulation:

- **Dexterity in Contact-Rich Environments:** Robots currently struggle to achieve human-like dexterity, especially in tasks requiring precise, contact-rich manipulation within dynamic environments. Rigid robots, typically controlled by position or velocity commands, face difficulties maintaining stable surface contact and applying consistent force, which are critical for many real-world applications.
- **Limitations of Existing Learning from Demonstration (LfD) Methods:** While LfD offers a promising solution for robots to acquire complex skills, tasks involving intricate maneuvers and high force, such as powder grinding, present specific difficulties. Prior LfD approaches, including the authors' VAE-ACT-based Comp-ACT method, have shown limitations in solving long-horizon tasks with repetitive behaviors, often exhibiting instability or an inability to adapt over extended periods.
- **Need for Adaptive Force Control:** To safely and effectively perform contact-rich tasks, robots must be able to adaptively regulate force while maintaining precision and stability. This necessitates an approach that can predict not only end-effector poses but also dynamically adjust robot stiffness to apply the necessary contact forces.
- **Exploiting Generative Model Capabilities:** The authors are motivated by the potential of diffusion models to capture multimodal action distributions. Unlike some other policy learning methods (e.g., VAE-ACT), diffusion models are hypothesized to generate a more diverse and adaptable range of behaviors, which is beneficial for the variability and nuance present in contact-rich tasks.
- **Improving Integration of Diffusion Policies with Compliance Control:** The research aims to explore and improve how diffusion models can be effectively integrated into compliance control schemes. This involves designing a policy that simultaneously predicts Cartesian end-effector (EE) poses and modulates the stiffness parameters of the robot arm.
- **Real-World Application and Comparison:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the effectiveness of this novel framework in real-world tasks and provide a detailed comparison with existing methods, specifically Comp-ACT. This comparison aims to highlight the scenarios where diffusion-based policies offer advantages and to establish best practices for deploying such compliance control systems.
- **Overcoming Orientation Representation Issues:** The authors are also motivated to address known issues with discontinuous and non-unique orientation representations (like axis-angles) in neural network training, opting for a continuous 6D representation to improve model fitting and output consistency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Diffusion Policies For Compliant Manipulation (DIPCOM) framework is designed to learn variable compliance control from demonstrations by predicting target end-effector (EE) poses and robot stiffness parameters conditioned on current observations. These predictions are then fed into a compliance controller to compute joint position commands, enabling compliant robot movement.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation

The system operates within a Learning from Demonstration (LfD) paradigm. The policy learns to predict a sequence of absolute Cartesian EE poses and stiffness parameters (Action $A = \{p, g, k\}$) based on current observations (Observation $O$).
- **Observations ($O$)** include RGB images ($I$), force/torque (F/T) sensor readings ($F$), and proprioception data ($S$).
- **Action ($A$)** comprises:
 - **Absolute EE pose ($p = \{r, o\}$):** A position vector $r \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and an orientation vector $o \in \mathbb{R}^6$.
 - **Gripper action ($g$):** The desired non-binary opening width of the gripper.
 - **Stiffness parameter ($k$):** Diagonal elements of the 6D stiffness matrix, controlling the robot arm's compliance.

#### 3.2. B. Data Collection

Demonstrations are collected using the teleoperation system previously presented in. This system utilizes Virtual Reality (VR) controllers, allowing a human operator to provide reference EE poses and stiffness inputs to the compliance controller. During demonstrations, the operator can switch between two pre-selected stiffness modes. The robot's observations ($O$) and the VR controller's actions ($A$) are simultaneously recorded to form the dataset.

#### 3.3. C. Orientation Representation for Cartesian Action Space

To address the issues of discontinuity and non-unique representations (e.g., axis-angles) commonly found in 3D rotations, the study adopts a six-dimensional (6D) orientation representation. This 6D representation, derived from the first two columns of the rotation matrix, provides a continuous and unique representation for each orientation, which is conducive to neural network training and avoids inconsistent outputs at inference time.

#### 3.4. D. Diffusion Policies For Compliant Manipulation (DIPCOM)

The core of the methodology is a classifier-free conditional diffusion model used to generate actions $A$ conditioned on observations $O$.

- **Diffusion Process:** The process involves two stages:
 1. **Forward Process:** Noise (Gaussian) is incrementally added to the demonstrated action data ($a_0$) over a series of timesteps, transforming it into a noisy sample ($a_n$).
 2. **Inverse Diffusion Process (Denoising):** A neural network, termed the denoising model, is trained to reverse this process, progressively recovering the original data from noise, conditioned on the input observations. This is implemented using the Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model (DDIM) formulation, which makes the denoising process deterministic, enabling more efficient inference and flexible adjustment of inference steps.

- **Architecture:** DIPCOM utilizes an encoder-decoder transformer architecture, similar to.
 - **Vision Backbone:** A ResNet18 (without pre-trained weights) processes the RGB images.
 - **Encoder:** The processed image features are concatenated with the F/T sensor readings and robot state data. This combined observation is then fed into the transformer's encoder.
 - **Decoder:** The transformer decoder block receives the noisy actions and applies cross-attention, conditioned on the encoded observations, to predict the denoised actions.

- **Training:** The model is trained to predict the original action ($a_0$) from its noisy version. The loss function used is the Mean Squared Error (MSE): $L_{sample} = \|a_0^t - \hat{a}_0^t\|^2$.

- **Inference:** During inference, the model iteratively predicts the original sample from a randomly initialized noisy sample by reversing the noise addition process. The inference formula used is: $a_{n-1}^t = \sqrt{\beta_{n-1}}\hat{a}_0^t + \sqrt{1 - \beta_{n-1}} \cdot \frac{a_n^t - \sqrt{\beta_n}\hat{a}_n^t}{\sqrt{1 - \beta_n}}$, where $a_n^t$ is the noisy data at time step $n$, $\beta$ is the cumulative product of noise scales, and $\hat{a}_0^t$ is the estimated original data.

#### 3.5. E. Action Sequence Generation

To address challenges specific to contact-rich manipulation, such as prolonged interaction and error accumulation over longer action horizons, the methodology incorporates two key strategies:

- **Higher Action Prediction Frequency:** DIPCOM operates at a higher action prediction frequency than previous works, predicting an average of 48 actions per horizon (compared to typical 16 actions). This allows for finer-grained control and smoother movements, particularly important in contact-rich scenarios.
- **Temporal Ensemble:** An extension of the Temporal Ensemble technique, originally introduced for ACT policies, is applied to the diffusion model. Instead of discarding the latter part of the predicted action horizon, the remaining actions are considered for subsequent inference steps. This smoothing mechanism helps to minimize erratic movements and ensure stable performance during transitions between action sequences, mitigating error accumulation over long horizons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The DIPCOM framework was evaluated against a baseline, Comp-ACT, on a set of challenging real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks using two UR5e robot arms equipped with F/T sensors and RealSense SR305 cameras. The policies were trained with a relatively small number of demonstrations (40-60 per task).

#### 4.1. A. Baseline Method

The baseline, Comp-ACT, utilizes a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) architecture. It consists of a transformer encoder for observational data synthesis and a transformer decoder for generating sequences of Cartesian EE poses and stiffness parameters. Similar to DIPCOM, it uses F/T observations, Cartesian EE poses, and RGB images as inputs to a compliance controller.

#### 4.2. B. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Two UR5e robot arms with integrated F/T sensors.
- **Cameras:** Two wrist-mounted RealSense SR305 cameras and one static third-person view camera.
- **Data Collection:** Demonstrations collected by three co-authors to introduce dataset variability.
- **Task Conditions:** Varied number of demonstrations (40-60), camera views (1-3), number of arms (1-2), and stiffness modes.

#### 4.3. C. Task-Specific Results

1. **Powder Grinding (Single-arm, 40 demonstrations):**
 - **Objective:** Grind granular coffee powder in a mortar with a pestle, involving circular motions and periodic checks of powder state.
 - **Metrics:** Percentage of fine powder produced, and qualitative observation of motion patterns.
 - **DIPCOM Performance:** Achieved 55.88% fine powder (SD: 13.54%), significantly outperforming the baseline. The policy successfully reproduced the demonstrated behavior, including circular pestle motions and periodic pauses to observe the powder's state (Fig. 4).
 - **Comp-ACT Performance:** Achieved 9.96% fine powder (SD: 1.39%). It struggled to reproduce circular motions, often remaining in the same place.
 - **Force Profiles (Fig. 5):** Both methods applied similar magnitudes of force, but DIPCOM's force profile better mimicked the demonstrators' patterns, despite having a larger standard deviation. Comp-ACT tended to exhibit more consistent but cyclic force patterns.

2. **Pencil Eraser (Single-arm, 60 demonstrations):**
 - **Objective:** Erase "OSX" written on a notepad by gently but firmly rubbing an eraser, without damaging the paper. Demonstrated with right-to-left linear motions, lifting, and repeating.
 - **Metrics:** Percentage of erased marks and success rate (complete erasure).
 - **DIPCOM Performance:** Achieved 77.32% erased (SD: 19.48%) and a 52.3% success rate. The policy applied more force and aimed more effectively at the pencil marks.
 - **Comp-ACT Performance:** Achieved 26.0% erased (SD: 16.6%) and a 0.0% success rate. The policy applied less overall force and struggled to completely erase marks.
 - **Force Profiles (Fig. 6):** Comp-ACT applied less overall force. DIPCOM applied more force to the paper.

3. **Bimanual Round and Cuboid Peg Insertion (Two-arm, 60 demonstrations each):**
 - **Objective:** Coordinate two arms to align, insert, and release a peg into a mating part (2mm tolerance).
 - **Metrics:** Success rate (correct insertion and release).
 - **DIPCOM Performance:** Achieved 100% success for round peg insertion and 95% for cuboid peg insertion.
 - **Comp-ACT Performance:** Achieved 100% success for round peg insertion and 95% for cuboid peg insertion.
 - **Behavioral Differences:** While success rates were similar, their execution behaviors differed. Comp-ACT consistently adhered to the demonstrated strategy (e.g., fixing one arm's pose before insertion). DIPCOM exhibited more diverse trajectories and introduced novel motor skills not present in demonstrations, such as continuously adjusting the supporting arm during insertion to assist the peg-holding arm, contributing to smooth execution.

#### 4.4. D. General Observations

- **Long-Horizon and Repetitive Tasks:** DIPCOM demonstrated superior flexibility and adaptiveness in longer-horizon, repetitive tasks like powder grinding and pencil erasure. Comp-ACT frequently struggled to maintain fluid, repetitive motions, sometimes freezing mid-task.
- **Behavioral Diversity:** DIPCOM, a diffusion-based method, exhibited greater diversity in its execution behaviors and introduced novel skills, aligning with findings from other studies on diffusion models. Comp-ACT, a VAE-based method, showed more consistent but less adaptable behavior.
- **Force Application:** Both policies applied force more conservatively than human demonstrators. However, DIPCOM's force patterns better mimicked human demonstrations, despite a larger standard deviation.
- **Limitations:** DIPCOM was noted to be more sensitive to parameter settings and required more computational resources than Comp-ACT, making it more challenging to tune and maintain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Diffusion Policies For Compliant Manipulation (DIPCOM), a novel framework that integrates diffusion models with compliant control for rigid robots performing contact-rich tasks. This contribution holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Robot Dexterity and Compliance Control:** DIPCOM advances the state of the art in robot dexterity by enabling rigid robots to execute complex, force-intensive tasks with a level of compliance and adaptability that surpasses previous methods. By effectively capturing multimodal action distributions and predicting both end-effector poses and arm stiffness, the framework allows for nuanced force control, crucial for delicate or variable-contact operations.
- **Improved Performance in Challenging Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate DIPCOM's superior performance in long-horizon, repetitive, and force-critical tasks such as powder grinding and pencil erasure. This addresses a known limitation of prior Learning from Demonstration (LfD) approaches, which often struggled with maintaining consistent and adaptive behavior over extended task durations. The ability to introduce novel, un-demonstrated motor skills, as observed in bimanual insertion tasks, indicates a higher degree of generalization or adaptability beyond direct imitation.
- **Guidelines for Diffusion Policy Deployment:** The study provides a comprehensive comparison with existing methods (Comp-ACT) and outlines specific tasks where diffusion-based policies excel, along with best practices for their application in compliant control. This offers valuable insights for researchers and practitioners looking to implement diffusion models in robotics.
- **Foundation for Future Robot Autonomy:** The framework's ability to learn complex compliant behaviors from a relatively small number of demonstrations is impactful, reducing the data collection burden. This lays a foundation for more generalizable and robust robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic human environments, such as advanced manufacturing, laboratory automation, and service robotics.
- **Addressing Fundamental Challenges in LfD:** By adopting a 6D continuous orientation representation and strategies for robust action sequence generation (higher frequency, temporal ensemble), DIPCOM mitigates common issues in LfD, leading to smoother and more stable robot movements, especially in continuous contact tasks.
- **Potential for Generalization and Robustness:** While currently sensitive to parameters, the identified potential for generalization across different task variations with larger datasets points towards a future where a single policy could perform a broader range of complex tasks. This would significantly enhance robot robustness and applicability across diverse scenarios.
- **Safety and Efficiency in Human-Robot Interaction:** Improved compliant control facilitates safer physical interaction between robots and their environment, including human collaborators. The ability to perform repetitive or hazardous tasks with human-like dexterity and consistent force application can lead to increased efficiency and safety in various industries.

In conclusion, DIPCOM represents a step toward more capable and dexterous robotic systems by effectively leveraging generative diffusion models within a compliance control framework. Its success in challenging contact-rich tasks indicates a promising direction for advancing robotic manipulation and enabling robots to tackle complex, real-world problems.
