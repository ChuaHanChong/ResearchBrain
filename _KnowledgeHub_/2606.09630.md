# ReCoVLA: VLM-Guided Reward Compilation for Failure Recovery in Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ReCoVLA is a framework that improves the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies by using a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to generate stage-gated rewards for training residual reinforcement learning policies. This approach enables robots to recover from manipulation failures in off-nominal states, increasing success rates in both simulation and physical robot experiments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are brittle and fail to recover robustly when encountering off-nominal states, such as misplacing objects or losing grasp.
- Traditional recovery methods, like fine-tuning VLAs, are costly due to data collection and risk catastrophic forgetting of nominal skills.
- Reinforcement learning for robot recovery is hampered by difficulties in designing precise, non-conflicting rewards, as generic task-level rewards are often too sparse.

## Method

- **ReCoVLA** employs a frozen base Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy and trains an additive ==residual== policy in its latent space to provide corrective actions.
- An external Vision-Language Model (VLM) analyzes visual rollouts to semantically identify specific failure categories and current recovery stages.
- A deterministic reward compiler leverages the VLM's semantic descriptor to compile stage-gated rewards, activating specific primitive reward components only when their preconditions are met.

## Results

- ReCoVLA (M4) increased the average success rate from **36.7%** to **66.7%** in simulation and achieved a **61.7%** average success rate in zero-shot physical robot experiments, surpassing baselines.
- The stage-gated reward compilation (M4) consistently outperformed simpler reward structures (M2, M3) across diverse manipulation tasks, demonstrating its critical role in effective recovery.
- The framework generalized to different VLA backbones, improving OpenVLA's average success from **23.3%** to **45.0%** in simulation, and exhibited robustness to visual and geometric out-of-distribution variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level semantic failure understanding using a VLM from low-level corrective control through a residual policy improves recovery robustness.
> - Utilizing VLMs to guide structured reward compilation for reinforcement learning is more stable and reliable than direct action generation.
> - Stage-gated reward activation, which ensures reward components are active only under specific recovery preconditions, is critical for achieving reliable and efficient recovery.
