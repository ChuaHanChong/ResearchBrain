# RoCo Challenge at AAAI 2026: Benchmarking Robotic Collaborative Manipulation for Assembly Towards Industrial Automation

> [!summary] Summary
> The RoCo Challenge established a high-fidelity benchmark for robotic collaborative manipulation in industrial assembly, focusing on planetary gearbox construction and evaluating solutions in both simulation and real-world environments. It revealed a substantial "Sim-to-Real Cliff" and demonstrated that end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures offered greater robustness for real-world tasks, particularly for error detection and recovery, compared to modular perception-action approaches.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation benchmarks primarily focus on household or tabletop settings, lacking the sub-millimeter precision, structured workflows, and collaborative manipulation required for industrial assembly tasks like gearbox construction.
- Robotic systems struggle with long-horizon, interdependent assembly sequences, coordinated bimanual manipulation, and robust autonomous recovery from intermediate failures in real-world industrial environments.
- Current models face challenges in out-of-distribution generalization, data efficiency, robustness to sensor noise, and real-time feasibility when deployed on industrial edge hardware.

## Method

- The **RoCo Challenge** introduced a standardized benchmark centered on the assembly of a simplified ==planetary gearbox==, structured into a ==Simulation Track== and an ==Onsite Track==.
- Tasks included assembly from scratch, resuming from partial states, and challenging ==error detection/recovery== scenarios, designed to test precision, coordination, and semantic reasoning.
- Comprehensive multi-modal datasets of over **300 demonstrations** (automatically generated and teleoperated) were provided, along with baseline ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== models, to support participant development.

## Results

- The challenge attracted over **60 teams** and **170+ participants**, with results highlighting a substantial performance drop when transferring solutions from simulation to real-world hardware.
- Top-performing teams utilized end-to-end VLA architectures, such as **ARC-VLA**, which combined advanced vision backbones with **L1 loss** optimization and dedicated recovery data programming, achieving robust real-world performance.
- Simulation leaders, often relying on modular perception-action pipelines with ad-hoc geometric logic, proved brittle in physical deployment due to sensitivity to calibration, unmodeled physics, and system latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A significant "Sim-to-Real Cliff" exists, where modular perception-action pipelines perform well in simulation but struggle with real-world physical robot latency, mechanical tolerances, and unmodeled complexities.
> - End-to-end VLA architectures, leveraging their reasoning capabilities, demonstrated superior robustness to domain shift and were more effective for tasks requiring semantic understanding and error recovery.
> - For high-precision industrial tasks, data quality, diversity (especially failure-recovery curriculum data), and specific loss functions like L1 loss are more critical than merely increasing model parameter counts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI), particularly concerning robotic manipulation for industrial applications. EAI is noted to be transitioning from isolated perception to integrated, continuous action, moving towards adaptive, foundation-model-driven architectures. Robotic manipulation for industrial use cases, especially intricate assembly tasks, is identified as a critical frontier to enhance manufacturing efficiency and reduce human exposure to repetitive or hazardous labor.

The paper acknowledges a range of existing benchmarks for embodied manipulation, spanning both simulation and real-world settings. In simulation, examples include LIBERO (lifelong learning, knowledge transfer), ManiSkill 3 (GPU-parallelized, contact-rich tasks), CALVIN (language-conditioned, long-horizon), and RoboTwin 2.0 (scalable synthetic data, bimanual manipulation). For real-world settings, benchmarks like LeRobot (open-source framework for real-world datasets), FurnitureBench (long-horizon furniture assembly), and the Functional Manipulation Benchmark (generalizable learning with 3D-printed objects) are cited.

However, the authors identify a gap in the current landscape: existing benchmarks primarily focus on household, tabletop, or synthetic environments. They rarely capture the precision requirements, structured workflows, and collaborative manipulation characteristics inherent in real industrial assembly processes. Specifically, high-precision mechanical assemblies, such as gearbox construction, demand coordinated bimanual manipulation, strict tolerance control, and robust recovery from intermediate failures, aspects that are largely underexplored. The RoCo Challenge is introduced to address this specific gap by providing a high-fidelity benchmark centered on industrial gearbox assembly, bridging simulation and real-world evaluation within a unified framework. It aims to develop human-centric systems capable of robust operation in industrial environments, particularly addressing challenges like out-of-distribution generalization, data efficiency, robustness to noise, and real-time feasibility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the RoCo Challenge is to benchmark and advance robotic collaborative manipulation capabilities for industrial assembly, specifically within the context of human-centered manufacturing. The motivation stems from several technical challenges and limitations in current robotic systems and benchmarks:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Industrial Precision**: Existing benchmarks are often insufficient in capturing the sub-millimeter precision, contact dynamics, and noise sensitivities encountered in real industrial assembly. The RoCo Challenge seeks to bridge this gap by defining a high-precision task and evaluating solutions in both simulation and physical environments.
- **Addressing Long-Horizon Manipulation**: Industrial assembly tasks often involve a sequence of interdependent sub-tasks over a long horizon. Failures in early stages can propagate, making robust execution challenging. The challenge aims to evaluate a robot's ability to maintain a coherent global plan and execute complex sequences reliably.
- **Enabling Coordinated Bimanual Manipulation**: Many industrial assembly operations require two arms working in coordination. This introduces increased degrees of freedom and demands sophisticated spatial reasoning, which is a focus of the challenge.
- **Developing Robust Recovery from Failure Capabilities**: A critical aspect of deployable industrial systems is the ability to detect, diagnose, and autonomously recover from errors, such as misplaced parts or incorrect components. The challenge explicitly includes tasks designed to test these recovery capabilities.
- **Benchmarking Against a Representative Industrial Task**: The chosen task, assembling a planetary gearbox, is described as a demanding yet highly representative operation in modern industry. This provides a realistic and relevant context for evaluating robotic manipulation skills.
- **Catalyzing Research in Embodied AI for Manufacturing**: By providing a standardized benchmark, dataset, and evaluation system, the challenge aims to attract global research community participation and accelerate the development of EAI solutions suitable for smart manufacturing.
- **Addressing Practical Deployment Hurdles**: The authors motivate the challenge by identifying significant hurdles in deploying current models in real factories:
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization**: Models frequently struggle with environmental variations not present in training data.
 - **Data Efficiency**: Complex, high-precision skills typically demand extensive data, which is costly to acquire in physical industrial settings.
 - **Robustness to Noise**: Real-world data is often noisy, requiring models to extract actionable features without reliance on high-fidelity tactile or force sensing.
 - **Real-time Feasibility**: Large model computational demands must be balanced with edge hardware constraints for low-latency deployment.

In summary, the RoCo Challenge was motivated by the need for a comprehensive benchmark that reflects the intricate, high-precision, and failure-prone nature of real-world industrial assembly, thereby pushing the boundaries of EAI towards practical industrial automation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoCo Challenge was structured into two distinct but interconnected tracks: a Simulation Track and an Onsite Track, both centered on the assembly of a simplified planetary gearbox (Figure 1), consisting of a sun gear, three planet gears, a finned key shaft, a ring gear component, and a planet carrier.

#### 3.1. Simulation Track: Human-in-the-Loop by State Initialization

- **Environment**: Built upon NVIDIA Isaac Sim, providing a digital twin of the assembly bench and the Galaxea R1 robot.
- **Task Definitions**: Three scenarios were defined (Figure 2):
 - **Task 1 (Assembly from Scratch)**: Robot begins with an empty planet carrier and sequentially installs three planet gears, a sun gear, and a ring gear.
 - **Task 2 (Resume from Partial State)**: Robot starts with some components already assembled (e.g., three planet gears in place) and must complete the remaining sequence.
 - **Task 3 (Error Detection and Recovery)**: Semantic errors (e.g., incorrect gear size, misplaced part) are injected. The robot must identify, remove, and correct the error before resuming assembly.
- **Evaluation and Scoring**: A single model was required for all three tasks. Success was measured by a normalized score (S ∈) based on correctly assembled parts and error recovery accuracy. The final simulation score was a weighted average: `S_sim = (4·S1 + 2·S2 + 4·S3) / 10`. Evaluation occurred on a private workstation with randomized (but consistent) lighting, textures, and gearbox colors to prevent overfitting.
- **Baseline and Data Provision**: Baselines (π0.5 VLA model and an ACT model) were provided, trained on a subset of the dataset. A comprehensive dataset of over 300 automatically generated demonstrations was supplied, including synchronized multi-modal observations (RGB, Depth, proprioception) and action streams (Figure 3). Data generation scripts were also provided for augmentation.

#### 3.2. Onsite Track: Physical Collaborative Assistance

- **Hardware**: Finalists deployed their models on the Galaxea R1 Lite, a dual-arm mobile manipulation platform equipped with head-mounted stereo cameras and wrist-mounted RGB-D sensors (Figure 4). The specific arm used for data collection was the Galaxea A1X.
- **Task Definitions (Real-World)**: Tasks were adapted to the physical setup (Figure 5):
 - **Task 1**: Assemble 3 planet gears onto carrier pins.
 - **Task 2**: Resume from a partial state where two planet gears are inserted, requiring the robot to insert the remaining one.
 - **Task 3**: Error recovery, requiring removal of an incorrectly mounted larger gear and placement of the correct one.
- **Real-World Constraints**: Physical challenges included precise meshing for contact dynamics, managing sensor noise from real-world RGB streams and depth reconstruction, and quick deployment/testing on physical hardware emphasizing sim-to-real transfer.
- **Baselines and Data Provision**: Similar to simulation, π0.5 and ACT baselines were provided, trained on a high-quality teleoperated dataset of over 300 demonstrations collected on the target hardware (Figure 6). This dataset included synchronized multi-modal observations (RGB, depth, proprioceptive states) and action streams, with a significant portion dedicated to error detection and recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RoCo Challenge attracted over 60 teams and 170+ participants from more than 10 countries. The results, summarized in Table 1, revealed a "Sim-to-Real Cliff," indicating a significant performance drop when transferring solutions from simulation to real-world hardware.

#### 4.1. General Observations

- **Simulation Leaders vs. Real-World Performance**: Teams excelling in simulation (e.g., HD-Robo, K-Lee-gends) often leveraged modular perception-action pipelines. These solutions, however, proved brittle in the real world, struggling with physical robot latency, mechanical tolerances, and unmodeled complexities.
- **VLA Advantage in Real-World Robustness**: The top three overall winners (IIGroup, RoboCola, Show Me Robot) utilized end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures. These models exhibited superior robustness to domain shift between simulation and reality, particularly in Task 3 (Error Recovery), which required semantic reasoning.

#### 4.2. Excellent VLA Solutions (Top Performers)

- **IIGroup (ARC-VLA - 1st Place Overall)**:
 - **Architecture**: A 3.6-billion-parameter model based on the π0.5 backbone, combining SigLIP and Gemma vision backbones with a dedicated action expert.
 - **"Risk-First" Perception**: Employed a wrist-camera-centric strategy for high-fidelity "near-field" data, implicitly learning contact geometry without relying on noisy global depth maps.
 - **L1 Loss Optimization**: Addressed precision bottlenecks by using L1 loss instead of L2 (MSE) loss. L1 loss maintains a constant gradient, forcing the model to minimize sub-millimeter errors during insertion, counteracting the "lazy robot" phenomenon.
 - **Recovery Correction Data Programming**: Explicitly trained the robot on failure management through a two-stage pipeline, including error types like small offsets and complete retract-recalibrate-retry scenarios. This "failure-aware" learning was crucial for Task 3 success.
 - **Deployment Alignment**: Reduced action chunking size from 50 to 20 during deployment for more dynamic closed-loop control.
- **RoboCola (Dual-Model Collaboration Framework - 2nd Place Overall)**:
 - **Dual-Model Framework**: Fused outputs from a semantic planner (OpenPi – Generalist) and a kinematic controller (GalaxeaVLA – Specialist). The final action was a weighted combination (`a_final = λ · a_spec + (1 − λ) · a_gen`), balancing high-level logic with low-level precision.
 - **Delta Action Space and Trajectory Smoothing**: Used a Delta Action Space for micro-adjustments and applied a temporal window algorithm for smoothing action chunks, reducing jitter during gear meshing.
 - **Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT)**: Utilized RFT to bridge the data gap for failure recovery. Successful simulated rollouts, including accidental recoveries, were filtered and added back to the training dataset, boosting performance in Tasks 1 and 2.
 - **Inference Optimization**: Identified an optimal 7 execution steps per inference cycle for fluid motion on physical hardware.

#### 4.3. Excellent Perception-Action Solutions (Simulation Leaders)

- **HD-Robo (Highest Simulation Score)**:
 - **Task Decomposition and Rule-Based Twisting**: Treated assembly as decoupled geometric problems. Used Foundation Pose estimation and depth-based thresholding for part identification and 6D pose estimation.
 - **Ad-hoc Twisting Logic**: Implemented "Twist-Left, Twist-Right" motion primitive near pins to allow gears to "settle" into meshed states.
 - **Sim-to-Real Failure**: Reliance on Franka-style impedance control rules, not supported by Galaxea R1 Lite, led to deployment failure, highlighting the difficulty of transferring policies coupled to specific simulation physics.
- **K-Lee-gends (High Simulation Score)**:
 - **Geometric Priors and Spiral Search**: Adopted a purely geometric approach with robust perception. Replaced Mask R-CNN with Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM 3) for high-fidelity part segmentation across varying lighting.
 - **Spiral Search Algorithm**: Implemented a spiral search when vertical resistance force was encountered, allowing the robot to find pin locations even with slight calibration offsets.
 - **Sim-to-Real Failure**: Cumulative errors in the physical perception-action loop and system latency prevented successful execution on hardware despite high precision in simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoCo Challenge has established a new benchmark for evaluating Embodied AI within the context of Human-Centric Manufacturing. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Highlighting the "Sim-to-Real Cliff"**: The competition robustly demonstrated the performance gap between simulation and real-world deployment. Modular perception-action pipelines, while effective in ideal simulation, proved brittle due to calibration sensitivity and latency jitter. In contrast, VLA-based approaches showed higher resilience to sensor noise and unmodeled physical realities, by internalizing these offsets within their visual distribution learning. This finding redirects research focus towards more robust, generalized solutions.
- **Emphasizing Data Engineering over Raw Model Scale**: The success of ARC-VLA underscored that for high-precision industrial tasks, data quality and diversity, particularly failure-recovery curriculum data and targeted loss functions (e.g., L1 loss for precision), are more decisive than merely increasing model parameter counts. This shifts the focus towards intelligent data curation and problem-specific learning strategies.
- **Identifying Critical Hardware-Software Limitations**: Task 1 revealed visual occlusion as a fundamental bottleneck. The advantage of wrist-mounted cameras was demonstrated, and the need for future solutions involving "active perception" through multi-arm collaboration was highlighted, pushing research into bimanual coordination for sensor placement.
- **Underscoring the Need for Semantic Reasoning**: Task 3 proved challenging, requiring not only motor skills but also semantic understanding of the workspace (e.g., detecting wrong parts). Only VLA models, leveraging the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), could successfully address these high-level errors. This indicates a future trajectory where low-level motor control is fused with high-level semantic reasoning for industrial robots.
- **Fostering Global Participation and Community Building**: The challenge attracted significant international interest from academic and industrial entities, facilitating a unique exchange of ideas and building a community around industrial EAI challenges.
- **Standardizing Industrial Benchmarks and Resources**: By providing a standardized hardware platform (Galaxea R1 Lite) and a unified evaluation kit, along with the "RoCo-Dataset" of high-quality teleoperated demonstrations and multi-modal sensor logs, the challenge has addressed reproducibility issues and provided a persistent resource for future research and benchmarking.
- **Shifting Research Paradigms**: The challenge has effectively shifted the research conversation from "can a robot perform a task in simulation?" to "how robustly can a robot handle the uncertainty of a physical assembly line?". This sets a new standard for what constitutes a "deployable" EAI solution in manufacturing.
- **Future Directions**: The insights gained inform future research, including expanding the simulation ecosystem for broader industrial tasks (inspection, PCB assembly, dynamic human-robot collaboration), advancing algorithmic robustness (autonomous recovery discovery, temporal memory integration, active bimanual perception), and scaling real-world deployments to varied materials and non-ideal lighting conditions.

Overall, the RoCo Challenge has provided a rigorous testbed for industrial automation, offering critical insights into the strengths and weaknesses of current EAI approaches and establishing a roadmap for developing robust, human-centric robots for future smart manufacturing environments.
