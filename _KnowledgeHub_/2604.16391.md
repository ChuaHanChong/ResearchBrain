# Disentangled Robot Learning via Separate Forward and Inverse Dynamics Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> DeFI, a robot learning framework, disentangles the pretraining of forward and inverse dynamics models, leveraging diverse action-free video data for robust action grounding. This approach achieves superior performance on robotic manipulation benchmarks, including an average task length of 4.51 on CALVIN ABC-D and an 81.3% success rate on real-world Franka Panda tasks.

## Problem

- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from unstable training due to competing objectives of 2D video forecasting and 3D action prediction.
- Existing VLA paradigms fail to fully exploit the vast quantities of action-free human and web video data for learning rich motion priors.
- Prior work often underemphasizes robust inverse dynamics, leading to bottlenecks in action inference despite powerful forward prediction models.

## Method

- **DeFI** introduces a two-stage approach: decoupled pretraining for separate forward and inverse dynamics models, followed by coupled fine-tuning.
- A General Forward Dynamics Model (GFDM), based on Stable Video ==Diffusion==, is pretrained on mixed human and robot videos to predict future video frames.
- A General Inverse Dynamics Model (GIDM), a ==Transformer== with VQ-==VAE==, is self-supervisedly pretrained on action-free egocentric and robot videos to infer latent actions from visual transitions.

## Results

- Achieved an average task length of 4.51 on CALVIN ABC-D (multi-view), outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods like OpenVLA (3.27) and VPP (4.33).
- Demonstrated enhanced data efficiency, requiring approximately **60%** of CALVIN ABC-D data to surpass the performance of previous state-of-the-art baselines.
- Attained an **81.3%** average success rate on real-world Franka Panda manipulation tasks, significantly exceeding Diffusion Policy (**48.2%**) and OpenVLA (**43.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating forward and inverse dynamics pretraining resolves inherent conflicts in end-to-end VLA models, allowing each component to specialize.
> - Large-scale, diverse action-free human and web video data can be effectively leveraged to learn generalized motion priors and robust latent action representations.
> - A strong, separately pretrained inverse dynamics model is critical for accurate action inference, which is as important as accurate future prediction for effective robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control has seen substantial advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to build generalist robots capable of understanding complex instructions and executing a wide range of tasks by leveraging large language models (LLMs) and massive action-labeled robot demonstration datasets.

Current VLA paradigms often integrate visual forecasting and action reasoning into a single, end-to-end architecture. While this approach has shown promise, it faces two primary challenges. First, the competing objectives of 2D video forecasting and 3D action prediction can lead to unstable training. Second, and more critically, this entangled training process limits the model's ability to fully utilize the vast quantities of action-free human and web video data. These human videos are considered indispensable due to their sheer scale and diversity, containing rich motion priors that could benefit robotic agents across various embodiments and tasks.

An alternative strategy involves employing a video prediction model, pretrained on mixed human and robot videos, primarily for forward dynamics learning, followed by a simpler model for inverse action inference. This approach reduces reliance on costly action-labeled data and incorporates priors from large video generators. However, this line of work often underemphasizes the importance of accurate action inference. The inverse dynamics component may be overlooked or treated with insufficient scalable pretraining, potentially becoming a bottleneck that limits the overall policy performance, despite a powerful forward prediction model.

This paper introduces DeFI (Decouples visual Forward and Inverse dynamics pretraining), a framework that addresses these limitations by disentangling forward and inverse dynamics learning. It proposes to pretrain these components separately using different data sources and objectives, and then couple them for fine-tuning. This approach positions itself as a method to mitigate the inherent conflicts in end-to-end VLA training and unlock the potential of large-scale, action-free video data for robust action grounding in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel framework for robot learning that effectively resolves the challenges associated with vision-language-action (VLA) models, particularly the misalignment between 2D image forecasting and 3D action prediction, and the limited exploitation of abundant action-free video data.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving the DeFI framework are:

1. **Address Misalignment in VLA Models:** Traditional end-to-end VLA models often implicitly learn a coupled representation of forward and inverse dynamics. However, the distinct requirements of predicting future 2D visual states and inferring 3D robot actions can lead to competing objectives and unstable training. The authors aim to explicitly disentangle these processes to allow each to specialize without interference.
2. **Harness Large-Scale, Action-Free Video Data:** The research identifies human and web videos as an immense, largely untapped resource containing rich motion priors applicable across various embodiments and tasks. Existing VLA models, constrained by their need for action-labeled data, struggle to fully exploit these "action-free" datasets. A primary motivation is to design a paradigm that enables the model to effectively learn from this massive, low-cost data.
3. **Strengthen Inverse Dynamics Learning:** While some prior work uses video prediction for forward dynamics, the inverse dynamics component, responsible for inferring actions, is often underdeveloped or lacks scalable pretraining. The authors contend that accurate action inference is as crucial as accurate future prediction. Thus, a key objective is to develop a robust and scalable pretraining method for the inverse dynamics model that can leverage heterogeneous, action-free video data.
4. **Achieve a "Win-Win" for Forecasting and Action Prediction:** The core motivation is to create a system where both visual forecasting (forward dynamics) and 3D action prediction (inverse dynamics) can excel. By allowing each module to absorb complementary knowledge from distinct data sources during separate pretraining, and then integrating them, the framework aims to achieve synergistic benefits. The forward model focuses on motion regularities from 2D forecasting, while the inverse model emphasizes 3D action reasoning grounded in state transitions.
5. **Develop a Scalable and Generalizable Policy Framework:** Ultimately, the goal is to build a robotic policy that can generalize effectively to novel tasks and environments without requiring massive robot-specific demonstration datasets. By pretraining on diverse, large-scale data and then fine-tuning efficiently with limited robot data, the framework seeks to enhance data efficiency and push the upper bounds of robot learning.

In summary, the motivation behind DeFI is to overcome inherent limitations of current VLA approaches by adopting a decoupled pretraining strategy that fully utilizes diverse video data to develop specialized and powerful forward and inverse dynamics models, which then cooperate to achieve enhanced robotic policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DeFI framework introduces a two-stage approach: a decoupled pretraining stage for separate forward and inverse dynamics models, followed by a coupled fine-tuning stage for end-to-end task adaptation.

#### 3.1. Stage I: Decoupled Pretraining

The core principle of Stage I is to allow the forward and inverse dynamics models to learn specialized knowledge from distinct data sources, without the entanglement issues of joint training.

#### 3.2. General Forward Dynamics Model (GFDM) Pretraining

- **Objective:** To learn to predict short-horizon future video frames given a current observation and a language instruction. This implicitly captures forward dynamics knowledge (how the environment evolves given an action/instruction).
- **Architecture:** The GFDM is built upon the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model, which includes a video Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for latent space definition and a denoiser (U-Net/Transformer with temporal attention) for latent diffusion. A CLIP text encoder is integrated to condition predictions on language instructions. The output video resolution is 256x256.
- **Training:** The model is pretrained using a latent diffusion objective, minimizing the noise prediction error. This involves adding Gaussian noise to a latent video sequence and training the denoiser to predict this noise, conditioned on the current encoded image and the instruction embedding.
- **Data:** A diverse mixture of large-scale human video datasets (e.g., Something-Something-v2, Ego4D) and robot manipulation video datasets (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, CALVIN-ABC) is used.
- **Inference Strategy (for fine-tuning):** To enhance efficiency and focus on motion dynamics over pixel-level details, the pretrained GFDM is frozen. During subsequent stages, its denoising process is restricted to a single step, which is observed to provide sufficient motion information for downstream action planning while being computationally less expensive than full video denoising. For multi-camera setups, future videos for each view are predicted independently.

#### 3.3. General Inverse Dynamics Model (GIDM) Pretraining

- **Objective:** To infer meaningful latent actions from observed visual transitions in a self-supervised manner, without requiring explicit action labels. This captures inverse dynamics knowledge (what action caused a state change).
- **Approach:** A proxy task is devised for unsupervised learning.
- **Input:** Pairs of consecutive video frames (o_t, o_t+n), separated by a fixed time interval (approximately 1 second), and a language instruction (l). DINOv2 visual encoder extracts semantically rich latent states (e_t, e_t+n) from the frames. T5 is used to embed instructions.
- **Architecture:** The GIDM consists of a spatial-temporal Transformer with causal temporal masks. It processes the concatenated DINO embeddings of current and future frames, instruction embeddings, and a set of learnable action queries. It integrates a Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) codebook to represent actions as discrete tokens, which quantizes the continuous latent actions.
- **Training:** The model is trained using a VQ-VAE objective. The primary loss minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted DINO features of the future frame (ˆe_t+n), generated by a Spatial Transformer decoder conditioned on the current frame and the quantized latent action, and the ground-truth future DINO features (e_t+n). A VQ-VAE loss component is also included for the quantization process.
- **Data:** Large-scale human egocentric datasets (e.g., Ego4D) and a subset of robot video datasets (Open X-Embodiment) are used. Crucially, action labels from these datasets are excluded during GIDM pretraining to enforce self-supervised learning.

#### 3.4. Stage II: Coupled Fine-tuning

In this stage, the separately pretrained GFDM and GIDM are integrated into a unified architecture and optimized end-to-end with a small amount of robot action-labeled data.

- **Frozen GFDM:** The pretrained GFDM remains frozen throughout fine-tuning to preserve its generalized dynamics priors and avoid catastrophic forgetting on smaller downstream datasets. It provides temporally consistent future video representations.
- **Feature Projection:** A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) projects the future latent embeddings generated by the GFDM onto the input manifold of the GIDM, ensuring representational compatibility.
- **Spatiotemporal Enhancement:** A video former extracts intermediate-layer features from the GFDM for richer spatiotemporal representation. These are then fused with the MLP-projected features.
- **Action Adapter:** A diffusion-based action adapter, initialized from a 30M DiT-B model, is introduced. This adapter takes the combined features and the inferred latent actions from the GIDM, conditioned by the CLIP-encoded language instruction, to generate executable robot control commands.
- **Optimization:** The GIDM and the diffusion-based action adapter are jointly optimized in an end-to-end manner on downstream tasks with robot action data. This aligns future prediction, action inversion, and low-level control.

#### 3.5. Inference Process

During inference, the GFDM takes the current observation and instruction, generating a sequence of predicted future video features (ˆz_t:t+H) via a single-step denoising. These features are projected by the MLP and combined with the current latent state to feed into the GIDM, which infers the latent action sequence. Finally, the diffusion-based action adapter conditions on these latent actions to produce the executable robot control commands.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the DeFI framework across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Manipulation Benchmarks on CALVIN ABC-D

- **Superior Performance:** DeFI achieved an average task length of 4.51 in the multi-view setting and 4.05 in the third-view setting on the CALVIN ABC-D benchmark. This represents a significant improvement over prior state-of-the-art methods, including OpenVLA (3.27), VPP (4.33), Seer (4.28), and UP-VLA (4.08).
- **Impact of Forward Dynamics:** The results indicated that leveraging a powerful forward dynamics model to predict future visual states substantially benefits current action reasoning, outperforming methods that directly project RGB images to actions (e.g., OpenVLA, π0, GR1).
- **Effectiveness of Decoupled Pretraining:** DeFI outperformed UniVLA, a method that extracts latent action labels from human videos to pretrain VLAs, suggesting that explicit decoupling of forward and inverse dynamics pretraining is more effective than solely using latent actions as pseudo-labels.
- **Importance of Inverse Dynamics:** Compared to methods like SuSIE, CLOVER, and VPP, which primarily rely on video generation for forward dynamics, DeFI demonstrated more accurate control. This finding supports the argument that robust, separately pretrained inverse dynamics are critical for translating predictions into effective actions.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** DeFI showed enhanced effectiveness in long-horizon tasks, attributed to the GFDM's ability to predict future videos and the GIDM's capability to resolve actions over extended sequences.

#### 4.2. Data Efficiency

- **Reduced Data Requirements:** DeFI consistently improved policy performance across varying data scales. With only 10% of the training data on CALVIN ABC-D, the pretrained DeFI policy achieved an 18% relative improvement in average task length compared to VPP.
- **Efficiency Milestone:** The method required approximately 60% of the data on CALVIN ABC-D to surpass the performance of previous state-of-the-art baselines, highlighting its potential in data-scarce scenarios.

#### 4.3. Manipulation Benchmarks on SimplerEnv-Fractal

- **State-of-the-Art in Simulation:** On the SimplerEnv-Fractal benchmark (Google Robot), DeFI achieved an average success rate of 51.2% for visual matching and 45.4% for variant aggregation, outperforming Octo-Base (16.8%, 1.1%), TraceVLA (42.0%, 45.0%), and OpenVLA (27.7%, 39.8%).
- **Domain Shift Observation:** The paper noted underperformance on certain SimplerEnv tasks, which was attributed to a domain shift where the GFDM, pretrained on real-world datasets, produced predictions that did not perfectly align with the simulated environment, leading to erroneous actions from the inverse dynamics model.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

- **Robust Real-World Operation:** DeFI was evaluated on a Franka Panda arm across eight manipulation tasks, achieving an average success rate of 81.3%. This significantly surpassed Diffusion Policy (48.2%), Octo-Base (34.4%), and OpenVLA (43.8%).
- **Performance Across Task Complexities:** DeFI demonstrated strong performance in simple single-task scenarios (place, open, close), moderately complex tasks (cut, stack), and complex long-horizon, accurate control tasks (pour water). Its ability to handle tasks like grasping a teapot and pouring water into a cup was attributed to the robust pretrained inverse dynamics model.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Decoupled Pretraining:** Experiments confirmed that both GFDM and GIDM pretraining were essential. GFDM without pretraining yielded 3.28 average task length, GIDM without pretraining yielded 4.16, while full decoupled pretraining achieved 4.51. This validated the importance of large-scale pretraining for stable optimization and generalization.
- **Impact of Human Video:** Incorporating human videos during pretraining consistently improved performance. Full human video incorporation increased average task length to 4.51, representing relative gains over scenarios with limited or no human video usage, underscoring the value of diverse motion priors.
- **GFDM Denoising Steps:** A single denoising step in the GFDM (approx. 150 ms inference) was found to be sufficient, capturing critical motion information. Increasing to five steps (approx. 250 ms) did not improve manipulation performance but increased latency. Using a DINO-based generative model for GFDM led to inferior performance compared to the SVD baseline, highlighting the advantage of established video generation frameworks.
- **GIDM Architectural Variants:** The proposed GIDM architecture (causal transformer with VQ-VAE for discrete action space) outperformed simpler MLP and direct Transformer baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness in learning a structured latent action space for precise action generation.
- **Discretization Strategies:** The discrete VQ-VAE method in GIDM outperformed Gaussian Mixture, Simple Binning, and Continuous Latent Action methods, suggesting it functions as an effective information bottleneck that stabilizes inverse dynamics learning by preventing future-state leakage.
- **Partial Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning only the GIDM and Action Adapter (our proposed method) yielded the best results (4.51). Fine-tuning all modules (GFDM, GIDM, Adapter) resulted in lower performance (4.40) due to representation instability and gradient interference from updating the GFDM. This supported the decision to keep the GFDM frozen.
- **Failure Cases Analysis:** Analysis of 200 failure cases indicated that 62% were due to forward-dynamics failures (e.g., hallucinated predictions, multi-view mismatch in contact-rich scenarios), and 38% were due to inverse-dynamics failures (e.g., misplacing objects, failed grasps despite accurate future predictions). This reinforced the necessity of robust inverse dynamics.
- **Inference Latency:** On an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU, the total inference time for DeFI was approximately 153.3 ms (GFDM: 86.1 ms, GIDM: 42.9 ms, Action Adapter: 24.3 ms).
- **Human Data Scaling:** Performance generally improved with increased proportions of human video data used for dynamics pretraining, indicating scalability without saturation within the tested range.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeFI framework introduces a novel paradigm for robot learning that addresses several fundamental challenges in the development of generalist robotic agents. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Resolution of VLA Model Misalignment:** By explicitly decoupling the pretraining of visual forward dynamics and inverse dynamics, DeFI effectively mitigates the inherent misalignment between 2D video forecasting and 3D action prediction that has constrained traditional end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This architectural separation allows each component to optimize for its specific objective without interference, leading to more stable and robust learning.
2. **Enhanced Data Utilization from Action-Free Videos:** A core contribution is the framework's ability to unlock and exploit the vast potential of large-scale, action-free human and web video data. This immense resource, previously underutilized due to the need for explicit action labels, is now leveraged for both forward dynamics (learning motion regularities) and inverse dynamics (learning latent action representations through self-supervision). This expands the data landscape for robot learning beyond costly robot demonstrations.
3. **Improved Generalization and Scalability:** The decoupled pretraining on diverse, Internet-scale video data instills strong priors for physical-world dynamics and action representations. This leads to significantly improved generalization capabilities across various tasks and environments, including unseen settings (e.g., CALVIN ABC-D evaluation). The framework offers a scalable pathway for VLA systems, as it can continuously benefit from the growing availability of video data.
4. **Increased Data Efficiency in Fine-tuning:** DeFI demonstrates substantial data efficiency, requiring considerably less robot-specific action-labeled data for fine-tuning compared to prior methods. This is a critical advantage, as collecting high-quality robot demonstration data is time-consuming and expensive. The ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance with a fraction of the fine-tuning data can accelerate the deployment of robotic skills.
5. **New State-of-the-Art Performance:** The framework establishes new performance benchmarks on challenging simulated environments like CALVIN ABC-D and SimplerEnv-Fractal, as well as in real-world manipulation tasks with a Franka Panda robot. This empirical evidence validates its effectiveness and potential for practical applications.
6. **Methodological Advancement:** DeFI proposes a conceptually clean and empirically effective decoupled pretraining paradigm. The careful design of the General Forward Dynamics Model (GFDM) for efficient future prediction and the General Inverse Dynamics Model (GIDM) for self-supervised latent action inference, combined with a robust fine-tuning strategy, represents a significant methodological advancement in robot policy learning.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work highlights important directions for future research, including integrating large language models for more sophisticated semantic grounding and interactive reasoning, improving precision for fine-grained manipulation, and enhancing computational efficiency for real-time deployment. It also contributes to the growing body of literature exploring the potential of world models and video generation for robotics.

In essence, DeFI offers a principled and effective approach to overcome key limitations in robot learning by disentangling dynamics modeling and action inference, thereby paving the way for more capable, generalist robotic agents that can learn effectively from the wealth of available video data.
