# Continuous Subspace Optimization for Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CoSO is a continual learning framework that fine-tunes pre-trained models within dynamically evolving, low-rank subspaces, addressing the rigidity of fixed low-rank adaptation methods. This approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art parameter-efficient continual learning techniques, achieving a 3.23% higher final accuracy on ImageNet-R (10 tasks) and demonstrating robust forgetting mitigation across diverse vision benchmarks.

## Problem

- AI models in continual learning suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where performance on previously learned tasks degrades when adapting to new tasks.
- Existing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, particularly LoRA-based approaches, restrict parameter updates to fixed low-rank subspaces, limiting the model's learning capacity and adaptability.
- Effectively leveraging the strong generalization abilities of large pre-trained models for sequential task learning requires a balance between adapting to new information and retaining old knowledge.

## Method

- CoSO optimizes pre-trained models within dynamically determined low-rank subspaces by performing truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) on orthogonalized gradients.
- Catastrophic forgetting is mitigated by projecting current task gradients onto the orthogonal complement of a historical subspace, ensuring updates minimally interfere with prior knowledge.
- The Frequent Directions algorithm efficiently consolidates and updates the historical task subspace, capturing essential learning directions from accumulated gradients for robust knowledge retention.

## Results

- CoSO consistently achieved higher final and average accuracy across CIFAR100, ImageNet-R (5, 10, 20 tasks), and DomainNet benchmarks, significantly outperforming all baseline PEFT-based continual learning methods.
- The framework demonstrated robust resistance to catastrophic forgetting in long task sequences, improving final accuracy by 3.23% and average accuracy by 2.47% over the best baseline on ImageNet-R (10 tasks).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of both orthogonal projection (e.g., an 8.52% drop in final accuracy without it on ImageNet-R 20 tasks) and the Frequent Directions algorithm for CoSO's effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting the optimization subspace based on gradient structure provides superior learning capacity and adaptability compared to static, fixed low-rank fine-tuning methods.
> - Explicitly orthogonalizing current task gradients to previous task subspaces offers an effective strategy to prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain performance on prior tasks.
> - Efficient aggregation of intermediate gradients using matrix sketching algorithms, such as Frequent Directions, captures comprehensive task-specific information vital for robust knowledge consolidation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the critical problem of **continual learning (CL)**, which aims to enable AI models to sequentially learn from a stream of tasks without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. The core challenge in CL is **catastrophic forgetting**, where adapting to new tasks significantly degrades performance on older ones.

The research landscape of continual learning has seen a shift from training models from scratch to leveraging **pre-trained models**, particularly **Vision Transformers (ViTs)**. These foundation models, due to their strong generalization abilities, offer promising avenues for mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Within this context, **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)** techniques have gained prominence, as they allow efficient adaptation of massive pre-trained models by updating only a small subset of parameters, significantly reducing computational and memory requirements.

The authors categorize existing PEFT-based CL methods into two main groups:
1. **Prompt-based techniques**: These methods (e e.g., L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, VPT-NSP2) optimize learnable tokens that modulate the pre-trained model's behavior. While efficient, they often struggle to capture complex task-specific features directly.
2. **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)-based methods**: Approaches like InfLoRA and SD-LoRA directly update model parameters within constrained low-dimensional subspaces. While more direct, a fundamental limitation identified by the authors is that these methods restrict parameter updates to *fixed* low-rank subspaces. This inherent rigidity compromises the model's full learning capacity, potentially leading to suboptimal performance, especially in long task sequences or complex scenarios.

**CoSO directly tackles this limitation.** It draws inspiration from recent work in offline learning, specifically **GaLore (Gradient Low-Rank Projection)** [Zhao et al., 2024], which demonstrated that neural network gradients often exhibit low-rank structure and can be efficiently optimized by projecting them onto dynamically determined low-rank gradient subspaces. GaLore showed that optimizing in these *gradient subspaces* can achieve higher model capacity than constraining the *weight updates* to a fixed low-rank structure (like LoRA).

By adapting and extending the dynamic, gradient-aware subspace optimization concept from GaLore to the continual learning setting, CoSO positions itself as an advanced PEFT-based method. It aims to overcome the rigid constraints of static low-rank adaptation methods by introducing dynamically updated, continuous subspaces for optimization, while simultaneously incorporating mechanisms to explicitly prevent catastrophic forgetting. This places CoSO at the forefront of research exploring how to effectively fine-tune large foundation models for real-world, sequential learning tasks, bridging the gap between efficient adaptation and robust knowledge retention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel continual learning framework, CoSO, that effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting in sequential task learning, particularly when fine-tuning powerful pre-trained models like Vision Transformers.

The key motivations driving this work are rooted in addressing the inherent limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Overcoming Fixed Subspace Limitations**: The central motivation is to overcome the performance ceiling imposed by current PEFT-based continual learning methods, especially those relying on LoRA. These methods confine parameter updates to a predetermined, fixed low-rank subspace, which inherently restricts the model's learning capacity and adaptability for new tasks. CoSO aims to enhance this by enabling optimization within a series of *dynamically determined* subspaces.
2. **Leveraging Pre-trained Model Generalization**: Acknowledging the strong generalization abilities of foundation models, the authors are motivated to harness these models for continual learning more effectively. The goal is to provide a fine-tuning mechanism that allows these models to adapt profoundly to new tasks without degrading their existing, powerful representations.
3. **Ensuring Memory-Efficient Optimization**: While aiming for greater adaptability, the practical constraint of optimizing large models necessitates memory efficiency. CoSO is motivated to achieve enhanced learning capacity without incurring substantial additional memory or computational overhead compared to existing PEFT methods.
4. **Robust Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation**: Beyond just learning new tasks, a core motivation is to robustly preserve prior knowledge. CoSO is specifically designed to enforce mechanisms that prevent new task learning from interfering with performance on previously learned tasks.
5. **Dynamic Capture of Critical Information**: The authors are motivated to develop a system that can continuously and efficiently identify and consolidate the most critical learning directions for each task, ensuring that valuable information is retained and integrated into the model's cumulative knowledge base.

In essence, CoSO seeks to strike a superior balance between **plasticity** (the ability to learn new information) and **stability** (the ability to retain old information) in continual learning, addressing the shortcomings of rigid, static optimization spaces.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoSO introduces a novel approach for continual learning by dynamically adjusting the model's optimization space, effectively fine-tuning pre-trained models within a series of continuously evolving, low-rank subspaces. The methodology is structured around three core mechanisms: orthogonal projection for forgetting mitigation, memory-efficient optimization via dynamic gradient SVD, and efficient historical task space update using Frequent Directions.

#### 3.1. Continuous Subspace Optimization with Orthogonal Projection

The core idea of CoSO is to avoid the rigidity of fixed low-rank adaptation by optimizing within multiple, dynamically determined subspaces. To explicitly address catastrophic forgetting, CoSO introduces an **orthogonal constraint**:

- **Historical Subspace Maintenance**: For each task $\tau$, an orthogonal basis matrix $M_{\tau-1}$ is maintained, spanning the combined gradient subspace accumulated from all previous tasks. This subspace encapsulates the principal directions critical for past learning.
- **Orthogonal Gradient Projection**: When processing gradients $G_{\tau,t}$ for the current task $\tau$, they are projected onto the *orthogonal complement* of the historical subspace $M_{\tau-1}$. This yields $G'_{\tau,t} = G_{\tau,t} - M_{\tau-1}M^T_{\tau-1}G_{\tau,t}$. This operation ensures that only the gradient components orthogonal to previously learned directions are used for updates, minimizing interference.
- **Dynamic Low-Rank Approximation**: To achieve memory-efficient fine-tuning, CoSO follows GaLore's principle. Instead of directly using the full orthogonalized gradient $G'_{\tau,t}$, it performs a truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) on $G'_{\tau,t}$ to obtain a low-rank projection matrix $P_{\tau,t}$. This $P_{\tau,t}$ captures the most significant directions within the *current task's new learning space*. The projection matrix $P_{\tau,t}$ is updated periodically (every $K$ steps) to adapt to the evolving gradient landscape.
- **Projected Adam Optimization**: The orthogonalized gradient $G'_{\tau,t}$ is projected into the low-rank subspace spanned by $P_{\tau,t}$ to form $R_{\tau,t} = P^T_{\tau,t}G'_{\tau,t}$. The Adam optimizer is then applied to $R_{\tau,t}$ in this lower-dimensional space to compute updates $N_{\tau,t}$. Finally, $N_{\tau,t}$ is projected back to the original parameter space ($\tilde{G}_{\tau,t} = P_{\tau,t}N_{\tau,t}$) to update the model weights $W_t = W_{t-1} - \eta \cdot \tilde{G}_{\tau,t}$. This process ensures that parameter changes occur in directions with minimal overlap with previous tasks, while maintaining memory efficiency ($O(mn + mr_1 + 2nr_1)$ instead of $O(mn + 3mr_1 + 3nr_1)$ for LoRA).

#### 3.2. Historical Task Space Update for Knowledge Consolidation

CoSO incorporates a sophisticated mechanism to update the historical task subspace, ensuring that newly acquired knowledge is consolidated without jeopardizing past learning.

- **Task-Specific Subspace Estimation using Frequent Directions**: During the training of task $\tau$, the model generates a sequence of orthogonalized gradients $\{G'_{\tau,1}, \dots, G'_{\tau,T}\}$. To efficiently capture the principal directions of these accumulated gradients, CoSO employs the **Frequent Directions (FD)** algorithm [Ghashami et al., 2016]. FD is a deterministic matrix sketching algorithm that maintains a low-rank approximation of the accumulated covariance matrix ($\sum_t G'_{\tau,t}G'^{\top}_{\tau,t}$) in a streaming fashion. For each step, a low-rank matrix $Q_{\tau,t}$ is computed from $G'_{\tau,t}$ via SVD. This $Q_{\tau,t}$ is then incrementally integrated into a sketch matrix $S_{\tau,t}$, providing an efficient approximation of the task's critical update directions with significantly reduced computational cost ($O(mnr_2T)$ instead of $O(m^2nT)$).
- **Updating Orthogonal Basis Matrix**: After task $\tau$ training is complete, the final task-specific sketch matrix $S_{\tau,T}$ is used to update the global orthogonal basis. SVD is performed on $S_{\tau,T}$ to extract its principal directions ($U_{\tau}$). A subset of these directions (determined by a variance threshold $\epsilon_{th}$) is then chosen and used to expand the historical orthogonal basis matrix: $M_{\tau} = [M_{\tau-1}, U_{\tau}[:, : k]]$. This process effectively consolidates the newly learned task's essential knowledge into the global historical subspace, laying the groundwork for subsequent tasks while maintaining orthogonality.

The methodology ensures that CoSO optimizes in dynamically evolving subspaces, explicitly prevents forgetting through orthogonal projection, and efficiently consolidates knowledge, all while being memory-efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted by the authors on various datasets and task settings demonstrate CoSO's significant superiority over state-of-the-art PEFT-based continual learning methods.

- **Consistent Outperformance**: CoSO consistently achieves higher final accuracy (ACC) and average accuracy (ACC) compared to all baseline methods, including prominent prompt-based techniques (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, VPT-NSP2) and LoRA-based methods (InfLoRA, SD-LoRA). This trend holds across CIFAR100, ImageNet-R (with 5, 10, and 20 tasks), and DomainNet benchmarks.
- **Robustness to Long Task Sequences**: CoSO's performance gains are particularly pronounced in challenging scenarios involving longer task sequences. For example, on ImageNet-R with 20 tasks, CoSO attains a final accuracy of 78.19% and an average accuracy of 83.69%, significantly outperforming the best baseline (VPT-NSP2), which achieved 75.42% and 81.32% respectively. This indicates a strong resistance to catastrophic forgetting even under demanding conditions.
- **Quantitative Improvements**:
 - **ImageNet-R (10 Tasks)**: CoSO improves final accuracy by 3.23% and average accuracy by 2.47% over the best baseline.
 - **ImageNet-R (5 Tasks)**: CoSO leads by 2.38% in final accuracy and 2.05% in average accuracy.
 - **DomainNet**: CoSO outperforms the best baseline by 1.75% in final accuracy and 1.37% in average accuracy, highlighting its ability to generalize across diverse domains.
- **Visual Evidence of Stability**: Figures illustrating accuracy evolution across tasks show that CoSO maintains a superior performance curve throughout the continual learning process, exhibiting a slower decline in accuracy compared to competing methods.
- **Ablation Study Validation**: The ablation studies conclusively validate the contributions of CoSO's core components:
 - **Without Orthogonal Projection ("w/o Orth")**: Removing the orthogonal projection mechanism leads to a substantial performance drop (e.g., 8.52% reduction in final accuracy for 20 tasks on ImageNet-R). This powerfully confirms the critical role of orthogonal constraints in preventing task interference and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.
 - **Without Frequent Directions ("w/o FD")**: Replacing the FD-based gradient consolidation with a simpler strategy (using only the final gradient subspace) also degrades performance (e.g., 1.59% reduction in final accuracy for 20 tasks). This demonstrates that aggregating all intermediate gradients via FD captures richer, more comprehensive task-specific information, which is vital for robust knowledge consolidation.
- **Efficiency Analysis**: CoSO demonstrates favorable computational and memory efficiency. It requires half the GFLOPs of prompt-based methods and maintains competitive memory usage compared to other LoRA-based methods. This suggests that CoSO achieves its superior performance without prohibitive resource demands.
- **Generality with Self-Supervised Models**: Additional experiments using a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone also showed CoSO outperforming baselines, affirming its applicability and effectiveness across different types of pre-trained models, including those trained via self-supervision.

In summary, the findings robustly establish CoSO as a highly effective and robust method for continual learning, particularly for fine-tuning pre-trained vision transformers, by dynamically adapting its optimization strategy and explicitly managing task interference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoSO represents a significant advancement in the field of continual learning, particularly in the context of leveraging large pre-trained foundation models. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

1. **Breaking the Rigidity Barrier in PEFT-based CL**: The most direct impact of CoSO is its ability to overcome a fundamental limitation of existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for continual learning. By moving beyond fixed, low-rank adaptation subspaces, CoSO enables models to learn more adaptively and capture complex task-specific features more effectively. This allows for a deeper and more meaningful integration of new knowledge, which is crucial for real-world applications where task distributions can be diverse and complex.
2. **Enhanced Foundation Model Utility**: CoSO makes powerful pre-trained Vision Transformers even more practical and effective for real-world continual learning scenarios. By providing a method that both efficiently fine-tunes these models and robustly mitigates catastrophic forgetting, CoSO facilitates their deployment in dynamic environments where models need to continuously learn and adapt without extensive retraining. This is particularly relevant for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and personalized AI where sequential data streams are the norm.
3. **Principled Forgetting Mitigation**: The introduction of orthogonal projection, coupled with dynamic subspace updates, provides a principled and effective mechanism for balancing plasticity and stability. By ensuring that new updates minimally interfere with prior knowledge, CoSO offers a robust solution to one of CL's most persistent challenges. This approach could inspire further architectural or algorithmic designs that inherently respect historical knowledge boundaries.
4. **Memory and Computational Efficiency**: Despite its sophisticated dynamic optimization strategy, CoSO maintains competitive memory and computational costs relative to other PEFT methods. This efficiency is critical for the scalability of continual learning to large-scale foundation models and resource-constrained deployment environments, making advanced CL techniques more accessible.
5. **Methodological Innovation**: The integration of concepts from gradient low-rank projection (inspired by GaLore) and streaming matrix sketching algorithms (Frequent Directions) into continual learning is a notable methodological innovation. This demonstrates how advancements in different areas of machine learning and theoretical computer science can be synergistically combined to address complex problems, potentially opening new research avenues for dynamic optimization and knowledge consolidation.
6. **Broader Applicability**: While demonstrated on visual tasks, the underlying principles of dynamic gradient subspace optimization and orthogonal learning could potentially be generalized to other modalities and types of sequential learning, such as natural language processing or reinforcement learning. The authors explicitly mention extending CoSO to multimodal task settings as future work, indicating its potential for broader impact across AI.
7. **Academic Contribution**: The paper provides a well-supported framework with both empirical evidence and theoretical justification (Proposition 1), contributing significantly to the academic understanding of how to manage knowledge and memory in continually learning systems. Its strong performance and clear ablation studies offer valuable insights for future research in CL.

In conclusion, CoSO offers a compelling solution to the long-standing challenge of catastrophic forgetting in continual learning, particularly in the era of large pre-trained models. Its innovative approach to dynamic, orthogonal subspace optimization promises to push the boundaries of model adaptability and robustness, paving the way for more intelligent and resilient AI systems.
