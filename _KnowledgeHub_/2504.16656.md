# Skywork R1V2: Multimodal Hybrid Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Skywork R1V2, developed by Skywork AI, introduces a multimodal hybrid reinforcement learning approach to enhance reasoning capabilities, achieving state-of-the-art results among open-source models on diverse reasoning benchmarks while effectively mitigating visual hallucinations. This 38B-parameter model demonstrates competitive performance against larger proprietary systems by directly acquiring reasoning skills through a novel training paradigm that combines Mixed Preference Optimization and Group Relative Policy Optimization, supported by a Selective Sample Buffer.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal models often struggle with deep, 'slow-thinking' reasoning tasks, primarily focusing on rapid generation rather than complex problem-solving.
- Models optimized for specialized reasoning frequently experience a trade-off, leading to performance degradation on general perception tasks.
- Visual hallucinations are a common issue in vision-language models, compromising factual accuracy and reliability.

## Method

- A modular architecture decouples visual-language alignment from reasoning, using a lightweight MLP adapter to connect a frozen InternViT-6B vision encoder with a QwQ-32B language model, eliminating the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage.
- A ==hybrid reinforcement learning paradigm== combines ==Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO)== for foundational alignment and ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== for fine-tuning reasoning, integrating reward model guidance with rule-based constraints.
- The ==Selective Sample Buffer (SSB)== mechanism caches and re-introduces high-quality, informative training examples to address the 'vanishing advantages' problem, enhancing GRPO training efficiency and stability.

## Results

- Skywork R1V2 achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source models across diverse benchmarks, including **73.6%** on MMMU, **74.0%** on MathVista, and **62.6%** on OlympiadBench.
- The model significantly outperforms its predecessor, Skywork-R1V1, with improvements such as **6.9** percentage points on AIME24 and **18.6** points on LiveBench, demonstrating the effectiveness of the hybrid RL approach.
- Hybrid MPO+GRPO leads to the best overall performance, notably on OlympiadBench, while reducing hallucination rates to **9.1%**, compared to **18.4%** with standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A direct reinforcement learning approach using Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) can acquire complex reasoning skills in multimodal models without supervised fine-tuning or teacher model distillation.
> - The 'adapter-only' training strategy effectively facilitates cross-modal alignment, with capabilities showing high transferability between text and vision modalities.
> - The Selective Sample Buffer (SSB) mechanism effectively addresses the 'vanishing advantages' dilemma in GRPO, maintaining over 60% effective training samples and improving optimization stability and performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Skywork R1V2 is situated at the forefront of contemporary AI research, specifically at the intersection of multimodal AI, advanced reasoning, and reinforcement learning.

**Evolution of Multimodal AI:** The field of AI has witnessed rapid advancements in multimodal models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which aim to bridge the gap between visual and textual understanding. Early VLMs primarily focused on tasks like visual question answering (VQA) and image captioning. However, the current frontier is shifting towards equipping these models with deeper reasoning capabilities, moving beyond mere perception to complex problem-solving. Skywork R1V2 is a direct response to this shift, aiming for models that can engage in "slow-thinking" processes rather than just "fast-thinking" rapid generations.

**The Rise of "Slow-Thinking" Models:** The paper highlights a critical distinction between "fast-thinking" models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.5), which prioritize rapid generation and broad general knowledge, and "slow-thinking" models (e.g., OpenAI-o1, Gemini-Thinking, Kimi-1.5, Skywork-R1V), which emulate reflective cognitive processes for complex reasoning tasks. Skywork R1V2 positions itself firmly within the latter category, building upon the foundational work of its predecessor, Skywork-R1V, which pioneered the direct application of text reasoning to vision via adapter techniques. This trend acknowledges that complex tasks like mathematical problem-solving or scientific inquiry require iterative deliberation and structured reasoning, demanding models that can allocate computational resources for extended thought processes.

**Reinforcement Learning for Advanced Capabilities:** Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), has revolutionized the alignment and refinement of Large Language Models (LLMs). This paper extends the application of RL to multimodal domains, specifically for acquiring complex reasoning skills directly, *bypassing the need for teacher model distillation*. This is a significant strategic choice, as it suggests a more scalable and potentially more robust path to training highly capable reasoning agents without relying on the outputs of even larger, pre-existing models as supervisors. The integration of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), initially developed for text-only LLMs, into a multimodal context represents a novel adaptation within this paradigm.

**Addressing Key Challenges:** The work directly addresses several pressing challenges in the development of sophisticated VLMs:
1. **Reasoning-Generalization Trade-off:** Models heavily optimized for specialized reasoning (e.g., mathematics) often suffer performance degradation on general perception tasks. Skywork R1V2 explicitly aims to balance these two aspects.
2. **Visual Hallucinations:** A common issue in VLMs, where models generate factually incorrect or inconsistent visual details. The paper acknowledges and systematically monitors this, aiming to mitigate it through calibrated reward thresholds.
3. **RL Efficiency and Stability:** The "vanishing advantages" dilemma, where reward signals diminish during GRPO training, is a practical hurdle. The proposed Selective Sample Buffer (SSB) mechanism directly tackles this, improving the efficiency and stability of RL fine-tuning.

**Open-Source vs. Proprietary AI:** By releasing its model weights, Skywork R1V2 aims to bridge the performance gap between open-source models and the leading proprietary systems (like Gemini 2.5 and OpenAI-o4-mini). This contribution enriches the open-source community, providing a powerful, efficient, and well-documented model that can serve as a new baseline for future research and applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Skywork R1V2 are multi-faceted, aiming to advance the state-of-the-art in multimodal AI reasoning.

1. **Develop a Next-Generation Multimodal Reasoning Model:** The core objective is to create Skywork R1V2 as a significant advancement over its predecessor, Skywork R1V, by enhancing its multimodal reasoning capabilities to handle complex scientific, mathematical, and abstract reasoning tasks.

2. **Balance Reasoning Capabilities with Generalization:** A central motivation is to overcome the inherent trade-off observed in previous models: that models optimized for specialized reasoning often compromise broad generalization. R1V2 seeks to achieve a harmonious balance, allowing it to excel at complex reasoning while maintaining strong performance on general multimodal understanding tasks.

3. **Acquire Reasoning Skills Directly via Reinforcement Learning:** The authors explicitly state their motivation to train R1V2 using a direct reinforcement learning paradigm. This approach aims to imbue the model with sophisticated reasoning skills without relying on distillation from a teacher model, which can be computationally intensive and may limit the "genuine" reasoning behaviors the model can develop.

4. **Mitigate Visual Hallucinations:** Recognizing that enhanced visual reasoning can inadvertently lead to an increase in visual hallucinations, a key objective is to systematically monitor and mitigate this phenomenon. The paper aims to achieve this through calibrated reward thresholds and specific optimization strategies.

5. **Enhance Reinforcement Learning Efficiency and Stability:** The authors identify the "vanishing advantages" dilemma in GRPO as a significant challenge to training efficiency. A crucial motivation is to develop mechanisms, such as the Selective Sample Buffer (SSB), to address this issue, ensuring a consistent and effective flow of gradient signals throughout the RL optimization process.

6. **Establish New Open-Source Baselines and Narrow the Performance Gap:** A major motivation is to contribute a highly performant, open-source model to the research community. By achieving benchmark-leading results among open-source models and significantly reducing the performance gap with premier proprietary systems, R1V2 aims to accelerate research and development in multimodal reasoning globally.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Skywork R1V2 employs a sophisticated, hybrid reinforcement learning paradigm, combined with an efficient modular architecture, to achieve its advanced multimodal reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.1. Efficient Multimodal Transfer via Modular Reassembly

To reduce dependence on extensive multimodal reasoning data and preserve the inherent reasoning capabilities of the language model, R1V2 adopts a modular approach:
- **Decoupled Alignment:** It decouples the alignment of visual-language representations from the preservation of reasoning capabilities.
- **Architecture:** A lightweight multi-layer perceptron (MLP) adapter (`fc`) connects a **frozen vision encoder** (InternViT-6B) with a **reasoning-capable language model** (QwQ-32B). This modularity allows for targeted optimization.
- **Elimination of SFT:** Crucially, R1V2 *eliminates the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage*. The authors argue that SFT can undermine subsequent reinforcement learning and hinder the development of genuine reasoning behaviors. By directly connecting a pre-trained reasoning language model with a visual adapter, they aim to preserve the LM's intrinsic reasoning abilities.
- **Component Activation Analysis:** Experiments showed that "adapter-only" training was most effective. This suggests that the primary bottleneck in multimodal reasoning lies in establishing effective cross-modal alignment, rather than solely enhancing visual encoding itself. Improvements in one modality (text or vision) exhibit high transferability to the other.

#### 3.2. Hybrid Reinforcement Learning Paradigm

The core of R1V2's training lies in a novel hybrid reinforcement learning approach that combines two distinct but complementary optimization strategies, supported by a specialized buffering mechanism.

##### 3.2.1. Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO)

MPO is employed as a crucial initial step to achieve a strong foundation model before the more intensive reinforcement fine-tuning. It harmonizes reward-model guidance with rule-based strategies.
- **Learning Objectives:** MPO integrates three core objectives:
 - **Relative Preferences (L_preference):** Utilizes a DPO-like loss to optimize the model based on relative preferences between chosen and rejected response pairs.
 - **Absolute Quality (L_quality):** Incorporates a BCO-like loss to help the model understand the absolute quality of individual responses, mapping chosen responses to 1 and rejected to 0. A moving average of past rewards (`δ`) stabilizes training.
 - **Generation Loss (L_generation):** A Negative Log Likelihood (NLL) loss guides the model to learn the chosen response, minimizing distribution shift.
- **Reward Model Guidance:** Preference signals are provided by the **Skywork-VL Reward model**, which is capable of evaluating both multimodal understanding and reasoning tasks.
- **Mitigation of Repetitive CoT/Overthinking:** This process helps reduce repetitive chain-of-thought (CoT) and "overthinking" in generated outputs, leading to improved performance on general vision tasks and mitigating hallucinations.
- **Hybrid Reward Structure:** MPO integrates preference signals with hand-crafted rule-based constraints (e.g., format correctness, factual consistency, step-by-step reasoning completeness), aligning outputs with stylistic preferences and factual requirements across modalities.

##### 3.2.2. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO is applied during the reinforcement fine-tuning stage to further boost reasoning capabilities.
- **Token-Level Advantage Estimates:** GRPO computes token-level advantage estimates by performing intra-group comparisons of generated responses for a given query. The advantage is normalized by the mean and standard deviation of rewards within a batch of candidate responses.
- **Hybrid Reward Signal:** To mitigate the "alignment tax" on reasoning ability, R1V2 uses a hybrid reward function:
 - `r(x, y_i) = r_rule(x, y_i) + r_θ(x, y_i) + r_format(x, y_i)`
 - `r_rule`: Rule-based reward for specific criteria.
 - `r_θ`: Preference reward signal from the Skywork-VL reward model.
 - `r_format`: Reward for adhering to the desired output format (e.g., DeepSeek R1-style chat template).
- **Optimization Objective:** A clipped surrogate loss term, augmented with a KL-penalty, ensures stable policy updates, preventing excessively large deviations from the reference policy.

##### 3.2.3. Selective Sample Buffer (SSB)

SSB is proposed to address the "vanishing advantages" phenomenon observed during GRPO training, where advantage signals diminish as responses converge, impeding effective gradient updates.
- **Mechanism:** SSB caches high-quality training examples that exhibit non-zero advantage signals. These informative samples are then strategically re-introduced during policy updates.
- **Benefits:** This mechanism increases gradient density, enhances training stability, and encourages the model to explore deeper reasoning paths. It ensures a continuous source of valuable training signals, preventing the model from plateauing.
- **Efficiency:** Incorporating SSB into the rollout of offline inference time (creating a filtered prompt pool) substantially improves training efficiency during initial optimization.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Setup

- **Benchmarks:** Extensive evaluation across diverse benchmarks for both text reasoning (AIME 2024, LiveCodebench, LiveBench, IFEVAL, BFCL) and multimodal reasoning (MMMU, MathVista, OlympiadBench, MathVision, MMMU-Pro).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Pass@1 score, averaged across 5 independent runs.
- **Evaluation Framework:** Utilized LLM Judge (OpenAI-o4) for both text and multimodal reasoning, assessing semantic equivalence and mathematical accuracy rather than strict string matching. This is particularly valuable for reasoning tasks where multiple valid solutions or paths may exist.
- **Generation Length:** Maximum 64K tokens.
- **Prompting:** Unified prompts for multiple-choice (boxed answer) and open-ended (step-by-step reasoning + boxed final answer).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Skywork R1V2 demonstrates exceptional performance across a wide array of reasoning benchmarks, setting new open-source standards and significantly narrowing the gap with proprietary models.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance

- **Open-Source Leadership:** R1V2 achieves state-of-the-art results among open-source models of similar or even larger parameter scales.
 - **Text Reasoning:** Achieves 78.9% on AIME24, 63.6% on LiveCodebench, 73.2% on LiveBench, 82.9% on IFEVAL, and 66.3% on BFCL.
 - **Multimodal Reasoning:** Scores 73.6% on MMMU, 74.0% on MathVista, 62.6% on OlympiadBench, 49.0% on MathVision, and 52.0% on MMMU-Pro.
- **Superiority over Predecessor:** R1V2 significantly outperforms its predecessor, Skywork-R1V1, with notable improvements such as 6.9 percentage points on AIME24, 6.4 points on LiveCodebench, and a substantial 18.6 points on LiveBench. This validates the effectiveness of the hybrid RL approach.
- **Efficiency with Fewer Parameters:** Despite being a 38B parameter model, R1V2 often outperforms larger open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B and QvQ-Preview-72B, both 72B parameters) on key benchmarks like MMMU and especially OlympiadBench (62.6% vs. 40.4% and 33.2%). This highlights its efficiency in learning complex reasoning.
- **Competitive with Proprietary Models:** R1V2 demonstrates competitive or superior performance against several leading commercial models, including Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 2 Flash, and Kimi k1.5 longcot, particularly on multimodal reasoning tasks. While larger proprietary models like OpenAI-o4-mini still hold an edge, the performance gap has been significantly reduced.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Methodological Insights

- **Effectiveness of Selective Sample Buffer (SSB):** The SSB mechanism proved crucial in addressing the "Vanishing Advantages" problem. With SSB, the proportion of effective training samples remained above 60% (compared to <40% without it), leading to more stable optimization dynamics and improved performance (e.g., 73.6% vs. 73.4% on MMMU). This validates SSB's role in maintaining a gradient-rich learning environment.
- **SFT vs. MPO vs. Hybrid (MPO+GRPO):**
 - **SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning)** significantly *degraded* model reasoning capabilities, performing poorly on mathematical tasks (e.g., 70.0% on AIME 2024 vs. 79.0% with MPO). This strongly motivated its removal.
 - **MPO (Mixed Preference Optimization)** alone showed strong performance, especially in reducing hallucination rates (8.7% vs. 12.1% with SFT) and achieving high scores on AIME 2024 (79.0%).
 - **Hybrid (MPO+GRPO)** approach achieved the best overall balance, notably leading to the highest score on OlympiadBench (62.6%) and maintaining competitive performance on AIME 2024 (78.9%), confirming its ability to balance reasoning and generalization. A slight increase in hallucination (9.1%) with the hybrid approach suggests a minor trade-off, but it's still significantly better than SFT.
- **Component Activation Analysis:** "Adapter-only" training yielded the best results across all evaluated benchmarks. Surprisingly, activating the vision encoder during training provided minimal additional benefits, suggesting that the critical bottleneck lies in improving cross-modal alignment rather than just visual encoding itself. This emphasizes the high transferability of capabilities between modalities.
- **MPO Threshold Analysis:** Higher MPO threshold values (e.g., 15) resulted in more stable training dynamics and better final performance with fewer iterations, indicating more selective and effective preference learning. Lower thresholds sometimes led to initial performance peaks followed by degradation, aligning with observations of "reward hacking" in iterative DPO.

#### 4.3. Hallucination and General Vision Tasks

- While R1V2 prioritizes reasoning, MPO successfully mitigated hallucination rates (reducing from 18.4% with standard SFT to 8.7% with full MPO implementation). The hybrid approach still keeps it low (9.1%).
- On general vision understanding tasks (e.g., document and video understanding), R1V2 maintains competitive performance, showing only a modest gap compared to specialized models. It demonstrates stronger capabilities when temporal reasoning is specifically required in video tasks.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples from Chinese Gaokao problems (Physics and Math) effectively showcase R1V2's ability for:
- **Systematic Problem Decomposition:** Breaking down complex problems into manageable steps.
- **Application of Principles:** Applying fundamental physical laws and mathematical principles.
- **Irrelevant Information Filtering:** Distinguishing relevant from irrelevant parameters.
- **Self-Verification:** Explicitly checking its own steps for errors, a hallmark of reflective reasoning.
- **Structured Output:** Providing clear, step-by-step reasoning processes culminating in boxed final answers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Skywork R1V2 represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with several layers of impact:

- **New Open-Source Baseline for Reasoning:** By achieving benchmark-leading performance among open-source models, R1V2 establishes a new state-of-the-art for multimodal reasoning. This provides the broader research community with a highly capable and publicly available model to build upon, accelerating future research and development in areas requiring sophisticated AI reasoning.
- **Efficient and Scalable Reasoning:** The paper demonstrates that high-level multimodal reasoning can be achieved with significantly fewer parameters (38B) compared to many leading proprietary and even larger open-source models. This efficiency makes R1V2 a more practical and deployable solution for real-world applications with computational constraints, democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities.
- **Pioneering Hybrid Reinforcement Learning:** The introduction of the novel hybrid RL paradigm (MPO + GRPO enhanced by SSB) is a methodological breakthrough. This approach effectively addresses the long-standing challenge of balancing specialized reasoning with broad generalization and provides a robust framework for training reflective AI agents without relying on distillation from teacher models.
- **Addressing Critical RL Challenges:** The Selective Sample Buffer (SSB) mechanism directly tackles the "vanishing advantages" problem in GRPO, a practical hurdle for efficient RL training. This contribution improves the stability and effectiveness of policy optimization, making RL a more viable and efficient tool for fine-tuning complex models.
- **Mitigation of AI Hallucinations:** The systematic approach to monitoring and mitigating visual hallucinations through calibrated reward thresholds and MPO is crucial. This helps build more trustworthy and factually grounded multimodal AI systems, which is vital for adoption in sensitive applications.
- **Deeper Understanding of VLM Training Dynamics:** The extensive ablation studies provide critical insights into the underlying dynamics of multimodal model training. The findings regarding the effectiveness of adapter-only training and the high transferability between textual and visual capabilities offer valuable guidance for designing more efficient and powerful VLM architectures in the future.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose AI:** R1V2’s ability to unify high-level reasoning and perceptual understanding across diverse domains paves the way for the next generation of more general-purpose AI systems. Its capabilities in complex problem-solving across mathematics, science, and coding have wide-ranging potential applications in education (e.g., personalized tutors), scientific discovery, automated programming, and expert systems.
- **Promoting Open Science:** The public release of the model weights is a significant contribution to the open-source community, fostering transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative innovation in the field of multimodal AI.
