# Described Object Detection: Liberating Object Detection with Flexible Expressions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Described Object Detection (DOD), a new task that unifies open-vocabulary and referring expression object detection by enabling detection with flexible language, existence verification, and multi-target support. A new benchmark, D³, is established, and an OFA-DOD baseline method is proposed, demonstrating improved performance over existing approaches on the challenging DOD task.

## Problem

- Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD) is limited to short category names, unable to handle complex attributes or relationships in natural language descriptions.
- Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) assumes the target object always exists, leading to false positives in real-world scenarios where the object might be absent.
- Existing bi-functional models often maintain separate training and inference for OVD and REC, leading to compromises and inheriting individual limitations rather than a truly unified solution.

## Method

- Introduces ==Described Object Detection (DOD)==, a new task unifying OVD and REC by allowing flexible language expressions, ==explicit existence verification==, and multi-target detection.
- Constructs the ==Description Detection Dataset (D³)==, featuring complete instance annotations, unrestricted language (including 'absence expressions'), and multi-instance support to benchmark DOD.
- Proposes OFA-DOD, an adapted baseline model leveraging ==Granularity Decomposition, Reconstructed Data, and Task Decomposition== to improve localization, multi-target handling, and negative instance rejection.

## Results

- OFA-DOD significantly outperforms all existing baseline methods on the D³ dataset, achieving **21.6%** intra-scenario mAP and **5.7%** inter-scenario mAP.
- Ablation studies confirm that Granularity Decomposition, Reconstructed Data, and Task Decomposition progressively improve OFA-DOD's mAP from **3.4%** to **21.6%**, with Task Decomposition being crucial for false positive rejection.
- Existing REC and bi-functional methods show high 'False Positives Per Category' on 'no-instance' images and struggle with multi-instance detection, while OVD methods degrade rapidly with longer descriptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art OVD, REC, and bi-functional methods are insufficient for Described Object Detection, particularly struggling with absence expressions, multi-instance detection, and rejecting non-existent targets.
> - Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) methods poorly distinguish between true and false positives due to their 'always positive' grounding formulation, leading to many false positives when the object is absent.
> - The ability to explicitly verify object existence and reject negative instances is a critical, unmet challenge for language-guided object detection, with most existing methods failing to achieve this effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of object detection, traditionally focused on identifying objects from a predefined set of categories, has been rapidly evolving to incorporate natural language understanding. This paper addresses a critical gap in two prominent sub-fields: Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD) and Referring Expression Comprehension (REC).

**Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD)** extends traditional object detection to identify objects based on *any given short category name* (e.g., "dog," "chair"), even those not seen during training. This leverages large-scale vision-language models like CLIP to generalize to novel categories. However, OVD is inherently limited to simple category labels, failing to handle more complex attributes or relationships (e.g., "a dog not led by ropes outside").

**Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)**, on the other hand, focuses on precisely locating a *single, pre-existing target* within an image that is described by an intricate, often lengthy, natural language expression (e.g., "the cat lying on the sofa"). A major limitation of REC is its inherent assumption that the target object *must* exist in the image; if it doesn't, REC models still produce a false-positive result, making them impractical for real-world scenarios where object existence is uncertain. Additionally, most REC datasets are not comprehensively annotated, leaving many negative instances unmarked.

Recent advancements have seen the emergence of **bi-functional models** (e.g., Grounding-DINO, UNINEXT) that attempt to combine OVD and REC capabilities. While these models are trained on data from both tasks, they typically maintain separate training procedures and inference strategies for each, and evaluate them independently. This often results in a compromise, where they might excel at one task but still inherit the limitations of the other when faced with generalized scenarios.

**Described Object Detection (DOD)**, as introduced by this paper, aims to unify and advance these two tasks into a more practical and holistic paradigm. DOD is presented as a superset that addresses the limitations of both OVD and REC:
1. **Flexible Language Expressions:** It expands beyond short category names (OVD) to include arbitrary, complex, and lengthy descriptions, similar to REC but without its single-target, pre-existence constraints.
2. **Existence Verification:** Crucially, DOD explicitly requires the model to verify the existence of the described object, rejecting predictions if the target is absent, thus overcoming a major flaw in REC.
3. **Multi-target Detection:** Unlike REC, DOD can detect multiple instances of an object described by a single expression.

By defining DOD and providing a dedicated benchmark, this work seeks to push the boundaries of language-guided object detection towards more generalized, robust, and real-world applicable solutions, bridging the gap between category-based and description-based object recognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish and advance the new task of **Described Object Detection (DOD)**. The authors are motivated by the clear limitations of existing language-based object detection paradigms (OVD and REC) in addressing practical, real-world scenarios.

The key motivations and objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming OVD's expressiveness limitations:** OVD is restricted to short category names, making it incapable of detecting objects based on nuanced attributes or complex relationships described by language. The objective is to enable detection using flexible, long, and complex expressions.
- **Addressing REC's "always positive" flaw:** REC assumes the described object always exists and outputs a bounding box regardless, leading to frequent false positives in real-world applications where target objects may be absent. A core objective is to integrate the ability to **reject negative instances** where the described object does not appear.
- **Unifying disparate functionalities:** Current bi-functional models still treat OVD and REC as distinct tasks with separate inference paths. The goal is to move towards a truly unified model capable of handling both short category names and long descriptions, with robust existence verification and multi-target detection.
- **Establishing a comprehensive benchmark:** To properly define and evaluate DOD, a new dataset is essential. A key objective is to construct the **Description Detection Dataset (D³)**, which features complete annotations, unrestricted language expressions (including "absence expressions"), and supports multi-instance detection. This dataset will serve as a foundational benchmark for future DOD research.
- **Benchmarking existing methods:** The paper aims to rigorously evaluate state-of-the-art OVD, REC, and bi-functional methods on D³ to pinpoint their specific weaknesses and "troublemakers" when faced with the demands of DOD. This analysis is critical to guide future research directions.
- **Proposing an initial baseline:** Based on the insights gained from benchmarking, the authors aim to propose a strong baseline method, OFA-DOD, that addresses some identified limitations of existing approaches and demonstrates the potential for the DOD task.

The authors highlight several compelling **real-world applications** that necessitate DOD capabilities:
- **Urban Security:** Detecting leash-less dogs, clothes hung outdoors, overloaded vehicles, or fallen trees.
- **Network Security:** Identifying sensitive images (e.g., violence, bloodshed) in large datasets.
- **(Fine-grained) Photo Album Retrieval:** Searching images using descriptive queries.
- **Autonomous Driving:** Detecting specific events like pedestrians crossing the road, or specific configurations of objects.
These examples underscore the practical demand for a more versatile and accurate language-driven object detection system that can adapt to precise, context-rich requirements and verify object existence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around introducing the Described Object Detection (DOD) task and establishing a new benchmark, the Description Detection Dataset (D³), followed by evaluating existing state-of-the-art methods and proposing an improved baseline.

#### 3.1. The D³ Dataset Construction

The D³ dataset is built upon the existing GRD dataset (for Referring Expression Segmentation) by re-annotating and expanding it. It addresses critical limitations of previous datasets:

- **Source Data:** Images are sourced from GRD, comprising 10,578 images across 106 scenarios.
- **Complete Annotation:** Unlike REC datasets that only annotate a few positive instances, D³ provides **dataset-level complete annotation**. For every description, all corresponding positive objects in every image are marked, and all images without the object are explicitly confirmed as negative. This is akin to traditional object detection datasets like COCO, enabling robust evaluation of absence detection.
- **Unrestricted Language Description:** Descriptions in D³ are flexible, varying from short category names to long, complex sentences (e.g., "a person who wears a hat and holds a tennis racket on the tennis court"). This challenges models to understand diverse linguistic nuances, including attributes and relationships.
- **Absence Expression:** A novel feature is the inclusion of "absence descriptions" (e.g., "a person without a safety helmet," "dog not led by ropes outside"). These constitute about 25% of the 422 descriptions and 7,802 positive instances, explicitly evaluating a model's ability to discern the *absence* of a feature.
- **Instance-level Annotation:** Each referred object is annotated with individual bounding boxes and instance masks, suitable for detection tasks.
- **Multi-Instance Support:** A single description can refer to multiple instances within an image, a feature typically seen in OD datasets but often lacking in REC.

**Annotation Process:**
The dataset was built by:
1. Manually adding 1-2 absence descriptions per GRD group, expanding the total to 422.
2. Automatically selecting possible positive references for each image using CLIP similarity (sampling top-N global references plus local group references).
3. Manually verifying the selection process to ensure no true positives were filtered out.
4. Human annotators meticulously marking bounding boxes and instance masks for all positive and negative instances across the entire dataset.
5. A multi-stage quality control process to ensure high annotation accuracy.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Standard **mAP (mean Average Precision)** is used, similar to COCO, but adapted for the multi-label nature of D³ (one instance can match multiple descriptions).
- **Intra-scenario mAP:** Evaluation performed using only descriptions from an image's scenario (default).
- **Inter-scenario mAP:** Evaluation performed against *all* 422 descriptions for each image, a much harder setting that necessitates robust negative rejection.
- **False Positives Per Category (FPPC):** For "no-instance" images, this metric measures the ratio of images where false positives are produced, directly assessing the ability to reject non-existent targets.

#### 3.2. Baseline Methods for Evaluation

The authors benchmarked representative SOTA methods:
- **REC Methods:** OFA (base and large variants), a multi-modal multi-task sequence-to-sequence framework, known for SOTA performance on standard REC benchmarks.
- **OVD Methods:** OWL-ViT (base and large) and CORA (R50), which leverage vision transformers and language models (e.g., CLIP) for open-vocabulary detection.
- **Bi-functional Methods:** Grounding-DINO (tiny and base) and UNINEXT (large and huge), which combine detection and REC capabilities through pre-training on diverse datasets.

All evaluations were conducted in a **zero-shot setting** as D³ images do not overlap with the training data of these baselines. Inference strategies were adapted to DOD; for REC and bi-functional methods, the default inference often involves predicting a single region, which impacts their performance on multi-instance or negative scenarios.

#### 3.3. The Proposed Baseline: OFA-DOD

To provide a stronger baseline for DOD, the authors adapted OFA, chosen for its text understanding capabilities and flexible seq2seq framework. OFA-DOD incorporates three key modifications:

1. **Granularity Decomposition (GD):** The single shared decoder of OFA is replaced with two parallel decoders. One handles "global tasks" (e.g., image captioning, VQA, MLM) and the other handles "local tasks" (e.g., object detection, REC). This disentangles conflicts between different task granularities, enhancing localization performance.
2. **Reconstructed Data (RD):** The REC training data is re-structured to unify it with object detection data. Instead of single image-text-box triples, examples now comprise one image, multiple positive and negative descriptions, and potentially zero, one, or multiple bounding boxes. This explicitly trains the model for multi-target localization and prepares it for scenarios where targets might be absent.
3. **Task Decomposition (TD):** The DOD task is broken down into two sub-tasks for training:
 - **REC:** Locating regions based on references (similar to original OFA).
 - **VQA (Binary Classification):** An auxiliary task where the model determines if a *given region* and a *given description* match. This provides explicit supervision for distinguishing positive from negative matches, crucial for rejecting false positives and handling absence expressions.

These modifications collectively aim to improve OFA's ability to handle multiple targets, reject negative instances, and provide more reliable confidence scores, making it more suitable for the complex demands of DOD.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis on the D³ dataset yielded several critical insights into the limitations of existing methods and demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed OFA-DOD baseline.

#### 4.1. Insufficiency of Existing SOTAs for DOD

- **Overall Performance:** All existing SOTA methods, despite excelling in their original benchmarks, show poor performance on D³, highlighting the significant challenge posed by the DOD task.
- **Bi-functional Advantage:** Bi-functional methods (UNINEXT, Grounding-DINO) generally outperform pure REC and OVD methods, confirming their conceptual advantage in combining diverse capabilities.
- **Inter-scenario Challenge:** The "Inter-scenario mAP" setting (detecting with all 422 references per image) is extremely challenging. REC methods (OFA) almost completely fail (mAP < 0.1%), as they are designed to always predict a region, leading to an overwhelming number of false positives. Even bi-functional and OVD methods struggle significantly, underscoring the difficulty of rejecting irrelevant descriptions.

#### 4.2. Challenges with Absence Descriptions and Confidence Scores

- **Absence Difficulty:** Most baseline methods perform consistently worse on "Absence Descriptions" (ABS) compared to "Presence Descriptions" (PRES). This indicates a general inability to accurately differentiate between the presence and absence of specific attributes in a language description.
- **REC Confidence Issues:** OFA, a REC method, produces confidence scores that poorly distinguish between True Positives (TPs) and False Positives (FPs) (Fig. 4a). Its seq2seq framework does not naturally yield robust confidence scores, and its grounding formulation prioritizes finding *a* region, not verifying existence.

#### 4.3. Multi-Instance and Negative Instance Rejection Challenges

- **Multi-Instance Detection:** OVD methods (OWL-ViT) show a strong advantage in scenarios with multiple instances, maintaining consistent performance as the number of instances increases (Table 3). REC and current bi-functional methods, however, struggle significantly in multi-instance settings, often unable to detect all targets.
- **Negative Instance Rejection:** REC and bi-functional methods demonstrate a high "False Positives Per Category" (FPPC) on "no-instance" images (Table 3, No-instance column). They frequently produce bounding box predictions even when the described object is absent, confirming the "always positive" flaw of REC paradigms. Only OVD methods and OFA-DOD show a better ability to reject these negative image-text pairs.

#### 4.4. Impact of Description Length

- **OVD Sensitivity:** OVD methods (OWL-ViT) perform well on "short" descriptions (1-3 words) but their performance degrades rapidly with increasing description length (Table 4). This confirms their limitation to simple category names.
- **REC/Bi-functional Robustness:** REC and bi-functional methods are less affected by longer and more complex descriptions, showcasing their strength in language understanding, though this doesn't guarantee overall high mAP due to other limitations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies on OFA-DOD

The ablation studies confirm the positive impact of each proposed modification:
- **Granularity Decomposition (GD):** Improves mAP from 3.4% to 10.5%, showing its effectiveness in making the model more suitable for localization.
- **Reconstructed Data (RD):** Further boosts mAP to 17.2%, emphasizing the importance of diverse data for multi-target and varied reference scenarios.
- **Task Decomposition (TD):** Leads to the final mAP of 21.6%, highlighting its crucial role in empowering the model to reject false positives.
The study on training tasks revealed that detection data and Image-to-Text (I2T) tasks are vital for OFA-DOD's performance and generalization, while Masked Language Modeling (MLM) had no significant effect.

#### 4.6. OFA-DOD Performance

The proposed OFA-DOD baseline significantly outperforms all existing methods, achieving 21.6% intra-scenario mAP and 5.7% inter-scenario mAP (Table 2). Its improved score distribution for TPs vs. FPs (Fig. 4d) indicates better confidence estimation. While still far from perfect, OFA-DOD demonstrates a stronger ability to reject irrelevant references and handles multi-target scenarios better than traditional REC, making it a robust starting point for DOD research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the fields of computer vision and natural language processing, particularly at their intersection:

- **Introduction of Described Object Detection (DOD):** The paper formally defines a new, more comprehensive, and practically relevant task that unifies and extends Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD) and Referring Expression Comprehension (REC). DOD addresses key limitations of prior paradigms, such as the inability to handle flexible language expressions and verify object existence. This is a crucial step towards more human-like, language-guided perception systems.
- **Establishment of D³ Dataset:** The creation of the Description Detection Dataset (D³) is a major contribution. Its unique characteristics—complete annotations, unrestricted language (including novel "absence expressions"), instance-level annotations, and multi-instance support—provide a robust and challenging benchmark specifically designed for the complexities of DOD. This dataset will be instrumental in driving future research and fair evaluation in this nascent field.
- **Comprehensive Benchmarking and Identification of Limitations:** The thorough evaluation of state-of-the-art OVD, REC, and bi-functional methods on D³ provides critical insights. The paper systematically exposes the "troublemakers" for each method type (e.g., REC's inability to reject negatives or handle multiple targets, OVD's struggle with long descriptions), offering clear directions for future model development.
- **Development of OFA-DOD Baseline:** The proposed OFA-DOD baseline, built on an adapted OFA model, demonstrates significant improvements over existing SOTAs for the DOD task. Its architectural modifications (granularity decomposition, reconstructed data, task decomposition) provide a blueprint for addressing some of the identified challenges, serving as a strong starting point and source of inspiration for subsequent research.
- **Practical Applicability:** By emphasizing real-world applications in urban security, network security, and autonomous driving, the paper highlights the immediate practical value of effective DOD systems. The ability to use flexible language to precisely define and locate objects, even by their absence, dramatically lowers the customization threshold for users and enhances the utility of detection models.

**Limitations:**
The authors candidly acknowledge certain limitations:
- **Dataset Scale:** Due to the high annotation cost of complete, dataset-wise annotations, D³ is not a "huge" dataset with millions of images. Larger datasets will likely be needed to fully scale DOD methods.
- **Description/Image Dependence:** The evaluation and findings might be somewhat dependent on the specific choice of descriptions and image sources in D³.
- **Baseline Imperfection:** While OFA-DOD outperforms existing methods, it is explicitly stated that it is "far from a strong DOD method," indicating ample room for further improvement.

**Future Work:**
The paper points towards future research directions, particularly mentioning the need to investigate the potential of emerging large multimodal models like Shikra, Kosmos-2, and Qwen-VL for DOD, which could leverage their advanced vision-language understanding capabilities.

In conclusion, this paper successfully introduces a crucial new task, provides a foundational benchmark, thoroughly analyzes existing methods, and offers a strong initial baseline. It significantly contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in language-guided object detection, pushing the field closer to truly flexible and practical visual perception systems.
