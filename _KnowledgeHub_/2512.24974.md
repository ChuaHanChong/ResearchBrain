# Hierarchical Deformation Planning and Neural Tracking for DLOs in Constrained Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong developed a unified framework for manipulating deformable linear objects (DLOs) in cluttered environments, integrating a hierarchical deformation planner and a neural model predictive control (MPC) tracker based on a Transformer-based deformation model. This system enables precise and collision-free DLO manipulation, demonstrating robust performance across diverse cable types and complex real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robust and efficient manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) in obstacle-dense environments remains challenging due to their high-dimensional state space and complex deformation dynamics.
- Existing DLO deformation planning methods are computationally expensive, yield physically implausible motions, or struggle with scalability in constrained environments.
- Current DLO deformation tracking approaches lack the robustness for cluttered spaces, fail to capture long-range dependencies, or incur high computational costs for real-time operation.

## Method

- A hierarchical deformation planner combines clearance-aware RRT*-based pivot path planning, passage-assisted path set generation, and DLO deformation optimization using a Mass-Spring model to synthesize physically plausible, collision-free deformation sequences.
- A neural ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) system employs a Transformer-based Deformation Model (T-DM) to predict DLO shape changes from robot end-effector actions, enabling robust, real-time, and obstacle-aware tracking.
- The T-DM is trained offline and then efficiently fine-tuned online by adapting only its decoder for generalization to new **DLOs** and real-world conditions.

## Results

- Simulations confirmed the planner's ability to generate smooth, physically feasible, and collision-free DLO deformation sequences, with the neural MPC tracker achieving low target errors (e.g., below 2 cm) in complex environments.
- The Transformer-based Deformation Model (T-DM) consistently outperformed baseline models, reducing long-horizon prediction error by **54.7%** to **70.4%** in real-world experiments.
- The framework achieved high success rates (up to 16/16 trials) and low target errors (average **1.58** cm to **1.94** cm) across diverse real-world cables and constrained tasks, including zero-shot adaptation and precise cable insertion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical approach effectively decouples global DLO deformation planning from local real-time tracking, enabling efficient generation of complex trajectories while maintaining robust execution.
> - Leveraging a Transformer-based architecture with self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms accurately models both internal DLO dynamics and long-range robot-DLO interaction effects.
> - Efficient online adaptation of only the deformation model's decoder allows for robust generalization and deployment across various deformable linear objects in real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) represents a significant and persistent challenge in robotics, primarily due to their high-dimensional state space and complex, non-linear deformation dynamics. This field is critical for various practical applications, ranging from cable packing and harness assembly in industrial settings to constrained wiring and surgical robotics. The current work directly addresses a major gap in the existing literature: robust and efficient DLO manipulation in obstacle-dense, constrained environments.

Historically, DLO manipulation research can be broadly categorized into planning and tracking. Early works often focused on free-space manipulation, where obstacle avoidance was not a primary concern. However, real-world tasks invariably involve cluttered workspaces.

In **DLO deformation planning** for constrained environments, several approaches have emerged:
- **Model-based planners** utilize explicit physics-based or data-driven deformation models to optimize robot actions. While capable of generating feasible trajectories, these methods are often computationally prohibitive for complex, long-horizon tasks due to the need for repeated, nonlinear deformation simulations.
- **Sampling-based planners** (e.g., RRT) explore the high-dimensional DLO state space. However, raw samples rarely correspond to physically feasible shapes, necessitating costly post-processing steps like equilibrium searches or energy minimization, which further exacerbates computational inefficiency and limits scalability.
- **Spatial path set planning**, a more recent paradigm, focuses on planning homotopic paths for DLO keypoints. This approach is more efficient by reducing the search space compared to sampling full DLO shapes. However, a significant limitation has been the lack of physically plausible deformation information, often resulting in coarse motion references and incoherent deformations (e.g., over-compression) when keypoints are tracked independently in confined spaces.

For **DLO deformation tracking**, robust execution of planned trajectories in cluttered environments is paramount:
- **Jacobian-based methods** employ linear controllers to reduce shape errors using local deformation Jacobians. These methods suffer from limitations in cluttered environments because their linear approximations cannot adequately account for obstacles or large deformations.
- **Learning-based approaches** leverage deep neural networks to model DLO deformations, often integrated within Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks for obstacle-aware tracking. Within this category:
 - **Latent-space dynamics models** predict rope deformation from images. These are computationally expensive and sensitive to visual variations due to heavy image encoder/decoders.
 - **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)** incorporate physical priors by modeling local interactions. However, their reliance on local neighborhood aggregation struggles to capture the long-range dependencies crucial for large global deformations.
 - **Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) networks** capture long-range dependencies but are less parallelizable, leading to slower training and inference.
 - **Self-attention-based models** have shown promise for complex deformation patterns, but prior works have sometimes excluded important factors like End-Effector (EEF) rotations, limiting generalization.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a novel, unified framework. It directly tackles the trade-off between planning efficiency and physical realism by introducing a **hierarchical deformation planner** that combines efficient spatial path set generation with a novel deformation optimization module. Simultaneously, it overcomes the shortcomings of existing tracking methods by developing a **neural MPC approach** that leverages a **Transformer-based deformation model** to accurately capture both local and global DLO-robot interaction dynamics, ensuring robust, obstacle-aware tracking. By integrating these two stages, the work aims to deliver a comprehensive solution that is both efficient and highly performant in complex, constrained DLO manipulation scenarios, thus significantly advancing the state-of-the-art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable reliable and efficient manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) in challenging, obstacle-dense environments, which are ubiquitous in real-world applications. The authors identify two primary, intertwined challenges: synthesizing globally consistent and collision-free DLO deformation trajectories (planning) and accurately executing these trajectories while actively avoiding obstacles (tracking).

The key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **To develop an efficient and robust DLO deformation planner capable of synthesizing global deformation sequences in obstacle-dense workspaces.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing planning methods face significant limitations. Model-based approaches are computationally prohibitive, making real-time and long-horizon planning impractical. Sampling-based methods struggle with the high-dimensionality of DLOs and require costly post-processing to ensure physical feasibility. While spatial path set planning offers efficiency by reducing the search space, it often lacks the fine-grained physical deformation details necessary for precise and coherent manipulation, potentially leading to issues like over-compression or topological inconsistencies. The objective is to overcome these by creating a hierarchical planner that first generates topologically sound spatial paths and then refines them into physically plausible temporal deformation sequences.

2. **To design a neural Model Predictive Control (MPC) tracking controller that accurately follows planned DLO deformations while robustly navigating constrained environments.**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional Jacobian-based controllers are limited by their local linear approximations, making them unsuitable for cluttered environments where large, global shape changes and obstacle avoidance are critical. Learning-based approaches, while promising, have their own drawbacks: image-based models are computationally expensive and sensitive to visual conditions; GNNs struggle with long-range dependencies; and Bi-LSTMs are slow. Previous self-attention models might also overlook crucial robot-DLO interactions. The objective is to develop a highly accurate and generalizable deformation model, specifically a Transformer-based architecture, that can capture complex, long-range dependencies between robot actions and DLO deformations, thereby enabling an MPC framework to perform precise, obstacle-aware tracking.

3. **To validate the proposed framework through extensive simulation and real-world experiments across diverse constrained scenarios and DLO types.**
 - **Motivation:** The true test of any robotic manipulation framework lies in its ability to perform reliably in diverse, real-world conditions. This objective aims to demonstrate the practical effectiveness, robustness, and generalizability of the integrated planning and tracking pipeline across various cable materials, complex obstacle configurations (e.g., mazes), and challenging tasks like insertion.

In essence, the research is motivated by the critical need for advanced robotic capabilities in handling flexible materials within cluttered environments, pushing beyond the limitations of current planning and control paradigms that often sacrifice either computational efficiency, physical realism, or robustness in complex settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework addresses DLO manipulation in constrained environments by integrating a hierarchical deformation planner with a neural Model Predictive Control (MPC) tracker. This unified pipeline decouples the complex problem into efficient global planning and robust local tracking.

**I. Hierarchical Deformation Planner (Offline Phase):**
This component generates an optimal temporal deformation sequence for the DLO. It consists of two main stages:

A. **Spatial Path Set Planning:** This stage focuses on generating a set of collision-free spatial paths for DLO keypoints while satisfying homotopic constraints.
1. **Clearance-Aware Optimal Pivot Path Planning:**
 - A pivot point (typically the DLO's center) is selected.
 - An RRT*-based algorithm is used to plan an optimal path for this pivot point.
 - The planning objective function balances path length and obstacle clearance, ensuring sufficient free space for the DLO's deformation.
2. **Passage-Assisted Path Set Generation:**
 - This novel method leverages "passages" (narrow regions between obstacles) to deform the pivot path for other DLO keypoints.
 - **Passage Introduction:** Shortest line segments between obstacles are identified as passages, acting as geometric cues.
 - **Path Deforming Steps:**
 - Identify passages traversed by the optimal pivot path.
 - Uniformly scale down the start and goal DLO shapes about the pivot point to ensure collision-free paths for all keypoints within the scaled shapes.
 - Apply linear interpolation of the scaled shapes to transfer the pivot path to all keypoints, identifying intersection points within the traversed passages.
 - Redistribute these intersection points along each passage, aligning them to the passage center and scaling them outward, to efficiently utilize passage width and avoid clustering, preventing overstretching.
 - Connect the redistributed keypoints to the original start and goal shapes via linear interpolation between successive passages or DLO shapes.
 - This process ensures that all keypoint paths maintain homotopic properties, are collision-free, and leverage available free space.

B. **DLO Deformation Optimization:** This stage refines the spatial path set into a physically plausible temporal deformation sequence.
1. **Smooth Parametric Path Interpolation:**
 - Each discrete keypoint path from the spatial path set is converted into a smooth, continuously differentiable curve using a weighted interpolation method based on path parameters. This allows for continuous gradients needed for optimization.
2. **Deformation Optimization Problem Formulation:**
 - The goal is to find a sequence of path parameters for all keypoints over time (decision variable `z`) that forms feasible DLO shapes.
 - A DLO configuration mapping function `h` uses the smooth parametric paths to generate DLO shapes based on the current path parameters.
 - The objective function `J` minimizes the accumulated potential energy of the DLO over the entire sequence.
 - A heuristic Mass-Spring (MS) model is employed to calculate potential energy, favoring smooth shapes and avoiding overstretching/over-compression.
 - Constraints include bounds on the change in path parameters to ensure smoothness of the deformation sequence, and start/end conditions (initial/final path parameter values).
 - The problem is solved offline using a gradient-based optimizer (IPOPT) with automatic differentiation (JAX).

**II. Neural MPC Tracking (Online Phase):**
This component enables real-time, obstacle-aware execution of the planned deformation sequence.

A. **Deformation Model Learning:** A data-driven model predicts how robot End-Effector (EEF) actions affect DLO deformation.
1. **Model Design (Transformer-based Deformation Model - T-DM):**
 - An encoder-decoder Transformer architecture is proposed.
 - **DLO Encoder:** Uses a self-attention mechanism to capture internal relationships and long-range dependencies among DLO keypoints.
 - **Robot Encoder:** Encodes EEF poses and actions.
 - **DLO Decoder:** Utilizes a cross-attention module to learn the complex interaction between EEF motions and the resulting DLO deformation, treating DLO keypoints as queries and EEF information as keys/values.
2. **Model Training:**
 - Offline: Trained on a large dataset (e.g., 50k transitions in simulation) collected from random EEF motions in unconstrained environments. The model minimizes the L2 norm of the predicted shape change.
 - Online Adaptation: For real-world deployment, the model is fine-tuned using online interaction data. Crucially, the DLO and robot encoders are frozen, and only the decoder is updated. This lightweight adaptation strategy enables efficient generalization to novel DLOs and real-world conditions with minimal computational cost and reduced overfitting.

B. **Neural MPC Design:** This framework uses the learned T-DM for real-time control.
1. **Problem Formulation:** Deformable object tracking is framed as a finite-horizon optimization problem, where a sequence of EEF actions is optimized at each timestep.
2. **Objective Functions:**
 - **Deformation Tracking:** Minimizes the L2 error between the predicted DLO shape and the reference shape from the offline planner.
 - **Obstacle Avoiding:** Penalizes the proximity of both DLO keypoints and EEFs to obstacles using a signed distance function, ensuring collision-free manipulation.
 - **Control Regularization:** Minimizes the magnitude of robot EEF movements to ensure smooth and energy-efficient control.
 - These objectives are combined with tunable weights.
3. **Optimization:** Since the T-DM is differentiable, a gradient-based optimizer is used to solve the MPC problem efficiently at each control cycle.

**Hardware and Software:** Simulations are conducted in MuJoCo. The pivot path planner uses OMPL (RRT*), and FCL handles collisions. Deformation optimization uses IPOPT and JAX for acceleration. Real-world experiments use two UR5e robots, a RealSense RGB-D camera for DLO keypoint tracking (Lucas–Kanade algorithm), and the neural MPC runs at 10 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive validation of its hierarchical deformation planning and neural tracking framework through both simulation and real-world experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in challenging constrained DLO manipulation tasks.

**A. Simulation Experiments:**
Simulations in MuJoCo provided a controlled environment to evaluate the planning and tracking components.
- **Deformation Planning Results:**
 - **Clearance-aware pivot path planning** successfully generated trajectories that provided ample free workspace, prioritizing manipulation space over shortest path length (e.g., navigating above obstacles).
 - **Passage-assisted path set generation** effectively created homotopic spatial paths for all DLO keypoints. These path sets utilized passage widths efficiently, offering comprehensive motion guidance while satisfying spatial and topological constraints.
 - **Deformation optimization** successfully synthesized smooth and physically feasible DLO deformation sequences, minimizing potential energy. The visualization demonstrated intelligent deformation, where parts of the cable advanced into gaps before the rest, avoiding excessive bending or compression.
- **DLO Manipulation Results (Tracking):**
 - The framework was tested in tasks of increasing complexity: single obstacle avoidance, multiple obstacle avoidance, and maze navigation.
 - In all scenarios, the **neural MPC tracker** consistently maintained positive clearance between the DLO (and EEFs) and obstacles, confirming collision-free operation.
 - Final target errors were consistently low (e.g., below 2 cm for single obstacle and maze, below 1 cm for multiple obstacles), demonstrating accurate tracking of the planned deformation sequences.
 - Snapshots illustrated the EEFs skillfully guiding the DLO through narrow gaps and complex corridors. The results showed robust performance across various constrained environments.

**B. Real-World Experiments:**
Experiments using UR5e robots and various real-world cables (Ethernet, Paracord, Rubber, USB, HDMI, Silicone) demonstrated the framework's practical applicability.

- **Deformation Model Performance:**
 - A critical finding was the superior performance of the **Transformer-based Deformation Model (T-DM)**. Compared to baseline models (Conv1D-based DM and Bi-LSTM-based DM), T-DM consistently achieved lower prediction errors for both short and long horizons.
 - Specifically, T-DM reduced long-horizon prediction error by 54.7% (vs. C-DM) and 70.4% (vs. B-DM) on the USB cable, showcasing its enhanced ability to capture complex, long-range dependencies. This highlights the architectural advantage of self-attention in modeling global interactions.
 - The B-DM performed worst, struggling with long-range dependencies, while C-DM showed inconsistent performance, effective for local features but weak on global ones.

- **DLO Manipulation Results:**
 - **Unconstrained Deformation Control:** T-DM MPC achieved precise shape formation with consistently low target errors (average < 2 cm across different cables), outperforming a standard Jacobian-based inverse kinematic controller and the C-DM/B-DM based MPCs. This demonstrated the neural controller's accuracy and generalizability across varying physical properties of cables.
 - **Constrained Tasks (Single/Multiple Obstacle Avoidance, Maze Navigation):**
 - All cables were successfully manipulated through complex environments, maintaining positive obstacle clearance (mean clearances ranging from 0.43 cm to 1.51 cm) and achieving low target errors (average 1.58 cm to 1.94 cm across tasks).
 - The system exhibited **robustness to varying initial conditions** and consistently adapted to different cable materials.
 - A case study on multiple obstacle avoidance revealed that the optimized deformation sequence intelligently staged the cable's movement, advancing one end rapidly through a gap before the other to prevent over-compression.
 - Overall success rates were high (14/16 to 16/16 trials), despite real-world challenges like keypoint tracking errors, noise, and action delays.

- **Case Studies:**
 - **Zero-shot Silicone Tube Manipulation:** The framework successfully manipulated a silicone tube using a deformation model initially identified from an HDMI cable, relying only on online adaptation. This demonstrated the model's powerful generalization capabilities.
 - **Obstacle-aware HDMI Cable Insertion:** The system successfully planned and executed the insertion of an HDMI cable into a port on a cluttered tabletop. This complex task, requiring precise routing, collision avoidance, and endpoint alignment, validated the framework's applicability to realistic, everyday tasks.

In summary, the main findings confirm that the proposed hierarchical planning and neural tracking framework effectively addresses the challenges of DLO manipulation in constrained environments. The Transformer-based deformation model significantly improves prediction accuracy, leading to robust and precise control, and the integrated pipeline demonstrates strong generalization and practical utility across diverse real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes substantial contributions to the field of robotic manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) and has significant potential impact across various domains.

**I. Advancing the State-of-the-Art in DLO Manipulation:**
- **Unified and Comprehensive Solution:** The paper presents a novel, unified framework that effectively bridges the gap between high-level, global deformation planning and low-level, robust online tracking in constrained environments. This integrated approach addresses a critical need in DLO manipulation research, moving beyond fragmented solutions that often excel in one aspect but falter in another.
- **Efficient and Physically Plausible Planning:** The hierarchical deformation planner introduces a crucial innovation. By combining clearance-aware pivot path planning and passage-assisted path set generation, it efficiently synthesizes globally collision-free spatial paths while maintaining homotopic and topological constraints. Subsequent deformation optimization, guided by a mass-spring model, ensures that the resulting temporal deformation sequences are not merely collision-free but also physically plausible and energy-efficient, a significant improvement over methods that yield incoherent deformations.
- **Robust and Generalizable Tracking:** The introduction of the Transformer-based Deformation Model (T-DM) is a key technological advance. Its self-attention mechanism effectively captures both fine-grained local interactions and long-range global dependencies within the DLO and between the DLO and robot end-effectors. This leads to significantly more accurate deformation predictions compared to prior learning-based models, which in turn empowers the neural MPC controller to achieve superior tracking performance, obstacle avoidance, and generalization across diverse DLO materials, even with zero-shot adaptation.

**II. Potential Impact and Applications:**
- **Industrial Automation:** The ability to precisely manipulate cables, wires, and other flexible components in cluttered industrial settings (e.g., cable harness assembly, constrained wiring, electronic device manufacturing) can lead to higher automation rates, improved product quality, and reduced manual labor. This directly addresses current limitations in tasks requiring dexterous handling of compliant materials.
- **Surgical Robotics:** In medical applications, the manipulation of sutures, catheters, and other flexible instruments within the confined and often obstructed human body is critical. This framework's robustness, precision, and obstacle-awareness could significantly enhance the capabilities of surgical robots, potentially leading to more complex, less invasive procedures and improved patient outcomes. The group's affiliation with a Multi-scale Medical Robotics Centre strongly suggests this as a primary target application area.
- **Domestic and Service Robotics:** As robots become more prevalent in homes and public spaces, the ability to handle everyday deformable objects (e.g., laundry, charging cables, packaging) will be essential for their utility. This research lays a foundation for robots to perform such tasks more autonomously and effectively.
- **Robotics Research Community:** The proposed methodology, particularly the hierarchical planning strategy and the Transformer-based deformation model, offers new avenues for research in deformable object manipulation, multi-robot coordination, and learning-based control. The detailed validation and comparison with baselines provide a strong benchmark for future developments.

**III. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors candidly acknowledge limitations, which also point to promising future research directions:
- **Perception Robustness:** Current reliance on keypoint tracking with a RealSense camera suffers from limited workspace visibility and robot-induced occlusions. Future work on robust, marker-less DLO state estimation methods (e.g., using advanced vision techniques or tactile sensing) would enhance real-world reliability.
- **Robot Configuration Constraints:** The current planner focuses on DLO deformation and is robot-agnostic, potentially leading to robot self-collisions in complex scenarios. Integrating joint reasoning over robot kinematics and DLO deformation will improve safety and expand the framework's applicability to more intricate robot movements.
- **Extended Applications:** The framework can be extended to multi-robot cable assembly and human-robot shared manipulation, opening up collaborations and more complex, cooperative tasks.

In conclusion, this research presents a robust and innovative framework that significantly advances the field of deformable object manipulation. Its strengths in efficient planning, accurate prediction, and robust control in constrained environments, coupled with strong real-world validation, position it to have a transformative impact on a wide array of robotic applications, particularly those demanding high dexterity and safety when interacting with flexible materials.
