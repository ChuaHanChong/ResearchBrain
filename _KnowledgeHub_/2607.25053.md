# Motion Generation With Environmental Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Environmental Constraint Exploitation (ECE) methods for robot motion planning, which leverage deliberate contact with the environment to simplify planning in high-dimensional spaces and enhance robustness against uncertainty. These methods were shown to efficiently generate plans for tasks like object insertion, tactile localization, and grasping from granular piles in both simulations and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Traditional robot motion planning struggles with the high dimensionality of configuration spaces, leading to computational intractability for complex robots.
- Robot operations in real-world environments are susceptible to inherent uncertainties in sensing, actuation, and modeling, which traditional planners often fail to address robustly.
- Conventional approaches prioritize collision-free paths, overlooking deliberate contact with the environment as a means to simplify planning and enhance robustness.

## Method

- Proposed **Environmental Constraint Exploitation (ECE)** as an alternative paradigm, leveraging deliberate environmental contact to simplify planning and manage uncertainty.
- Integrated **ECE** into sampling-based motion planning algorithms (e.g., ==CERRT==, ==ConCERRT==, ==CET==, ==CEET==) by defining and sequencing =='manipulation funnels'== that exploit contact manifolds.
- Addressed motion uncertainty by formulating planning as a ==belief space== problem, where contact sensing is used for ==belief partitioning== and ==contingent planning==.

## Results

- ECE-based planners efficiently computed motion policies in 7-dimensional configuration spaces for tasks like object insertion, demonstrating scalability without fixed discretization and robustness against high motion uncertainty (**σ_m = 0.1**).
- Real-world experiments validated ECE for robust object insertion, reducing initial uncertainty from **7cm** to **2cm** deviation, and for tactile localization, handling object displacements up to **4cm**.
- A novel 'granular Environmental Constraint' was identified in object piles, enabling simplified, open-loop grasping strategies with up to **100%** success rate in certain conditions and successful bin-picking of irregular objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deliberate interaction with environmental features can simplify motion planning by reducing the effective dimensionality of the configuration space.
> - Exploiting contact through manipulation funnels and contact sensing actively reduces state uncertainty, making robot motions more robust in uncertain environments.
> - Combining ECE with workspace information (e.g., sphere-based wavefront expansion) enables efficient exploration of task-relevant regions, maintaining computational practicality for high-dimensional robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Robot motion planning is a foundational area in robotics, traditionally focused on generating collision-free paths. This conventional approach, while extensively studied, faces two significant challenges: the high dimensionality of configuration spaces (C-space) and the presence of various uncertainties (e.g., sensing, actuation, modeling). The majority of research in motion planning has historically prioritized avoiding contact between the robot and its environment.

This paper explores an alternative paradigm by re-engaging with earlier, less-explored concepts of motion planning that involve deliberate contact with the environment. Pioneering work in the 1980s demonstrated how contact could enable successful plans even with substantial execution uncertainty. However, subsequent research largely shifted away from this concept.

This work positions itself as a consolidation and extension of prior research that advocates for "Environmental Constraint Exploitation (ECE)." ECE proposes that deliberate interaction with the environment can simplify planning by reducing dimensionality and computational complexity, while simultaneously leveraging contact for uncertainty reduction, thereby enhancing robustness during execution. The research integrates ECE into existing motion planning algorithms, biasing exploration towards task-relevant regions and exploiting environmental features to manage uncertainty. It addresses the limitations of traditional collision-free planning by presenting methods that are computationally practical in high-dimensional and complex C-spaces and robust under various forms of uncertainty. This approach contrasts with methods that rely on pre-defined discretization or complete search in belief space, which often become intractable in high-dimensional and continuous state-action spaces.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance robot motion planning by proposing and systematically evaluating an alternative approach called Environmental Constraint Exploitation (ECE). The motivation stems from two fundamental challenges in conventional motion planning:

1. **High Dimensionality of Configuration Space:** As robots become more complex with more degrees of freedom, the configuration space grows exponentially, making planning computationally intractable. Traditional methods struggle to explore these vast spaces efficiently. The motivation is to simplify planning by using environmental features to reduce the effective dimensionality of the problem or guide exploration more effectively.
2. **Uncertainties in Sensing, Actuation, and Modeling:** Real-world robotic operations are subject to inherent uncertainties, leading to deviations between planned and executed motions. Traditional planners often assume perfect execution, leading to failures in uncertain environments. The motivation is to develop planning algorithms that are robust against these uncertainties, particularly by leveraging contact to reduce state uncertainty.

Specifically, the paper aims to:

- **Integrate ECE into Motion Planning Algorithms:** Develop and adapt sampling-based motion planning algorithms (e.g., RRT-based planners) to explicitly incorporate and exploit environmental contacts. This includes defining and sequencing manipulation funnels as central algorithmic elements.
- **Simplify Planning by Dimensionality Reduction:** Demonstrate how deliberate contact can reduce the effective dimensionality of the planning problem. Environmental surfaces can act as lower-dimensional representations of the C-space, guiding the search.
- **Enhance Robustness by Uncertainty Reduction:** Show that contact with the environment can actively reduce state uncertainty. This is achieved through mechanisms like manipulation funnels (collapsing uncertainty onto contact manifolds) and contact sensing (partitioning belief states based on distinct contact events).
- **Maintain Computational Practicality:** Develop methods that remain efficient even in high-dimensional C-spaces and complex environments by avoiding explicit computation of full belief spaces or exhaustive search. This involves biasing exploration to task-relevant regions and reusing solved contingencies.
- **Evaluate Practical Benefits in Real-World Applications:** Validate the proposed ECE-based planners through simulations and real-world experiments, demonstrating their efficacy in scenarios such as object insertion, tactile localization, and bin-picking from homogeneous piles.
- **Consolidate and Extend Prior Work:** Bring together previous conference papers in an archival format, situating them within a broader framework of ECE-based motion generation and introducing novel contributions related to integrating ECE with workspace information and characterizing new environmental constraints.

Ultimately, the motivation is to simplify motion planning while simultaneously enhancing adaptability and performance in complex and uncertain environments, thereby enabling robots to perform tasks that are currently beyond practical solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around Environmental Constraint Exploitation (ECE), where deliberate contact with the environment is used as a central algorithmic element in motion generation. The approach can be categorized into two main sub-problems: ECE sequencing and contact-based motion planning, addressed across varying levels of C-space complexity and motion uncertainty.

#### 3.1. ECE Sequencing

This is defined as a graph search problem where nodes represent Environmental Constraints (ECEs) and edges represent directional transitions between them. An ECE (Υ) is a contiguous subset of configurations on a contact manifold (M_F) with associated exerted forces. The existence of an edge between two ECEs requires a non-empty intersection of their contact manifolds and a change in contact force. The goal is to build an ECE-graph for a given environment and task, and then find a path (sequence of ECEs) from a start to a goal region, using associated local policies.

#### 3.2. Contact-Based Motion Planning (Belief Space Planning)

To account for motion uncertainty, motion planning is cast as a belief space planning problem. A belief (b) is a probability distribution over configurations, including a mean configuration, an uncertainty covariance matrix, and a fully observable contact state. The configuration space (C) is decomposed into free space (C_free) and contact space (∂C_free). Different contact sensor models are defined: Tactile, Force, and Oracle. The objective is to find a policy (π) that brings the robot from a start belief to a goal belief with high probability, prioritizing feasibility over optimality.

#### 3.3. Integration of ECE into RRT-based Planners

The research introduces and extends several sampling-based, RRT-like planners, each designed to handle specific challenges related to C-space complexity and motion uncertainty:

- **Contact Exploiting RRT (CERRT):**
 - **Purpose:** Planning in low-to-moderate C-space complexity and under low-to-moderate motion uncertainty.
 - **Mechanism:** Extends RRT by rapidly exploring "manipulation funnels" (akin to contact-based local policies) and their connectivity. It represents beliefs using particles, which can become non-Gaussian on contact manifolds.
 - **Local Planners:** Uses free-space motion (connect), guarded moves (straight line in free space ending in contact), and sliding actions (moving in contact using task projection).
 - **Nearest Neighbor Selection:** Balances Euclidean joint distances and belief uncertainty using a weighting factor (γ) to guide exploration.
 - **Action Selection:** Biases selection between free-space and contact-exploiting actions based on γ.

- **Contingent CERRT (ConCERRT):**
 - **Purpose:** Planning under high motion uncertainty.
 - **Mechanism:** Builds upon CERRT by generating contact contingencies. It handles probabilistic transitions between funnels by anticipating contact events. When an action results in a new belief, it is partitioned into smaller beliefs based on distinct observable contact states (e.g., different sensor patches in contact).
 - **Dynamic Programming:** Simplifies contingency planning by incrementally building a policy and reusing already connected contingencies as goal regions for unsolved branches. This balances exploration (for new contingencies) and exploitation (connecting to existing solutions).
 - **Nearest Neighbor Selection:** Considers not only the current belief's uncertainty but also the uncertainty of all sibling beliefs (partitions) that could be reached from the same action.

- **Contact Exploring Tree (CET) and Contact Exploring/Exploiting Tree (CEET):**
 - **Purpose:** Planning in high C-space complexity and under moderate motion uncertainty.
 - **Mechanism:** These planners use "workspace information" to guide exploration of task-relevant funnels, reducing the search space. Workspace information is extracted via sphere-based wavefront expansion, approximating task-relevant free-space regions (S) and associated contact surfaces (neighboring ECs).
 - **CET:** Restricts random configuration sampling to be within the task-relevant spheres (S), effectively exploring a subspace.
 - **CEET:** More extensively guides exploration and action selection. It balances exploitation (following the potential field defined by sphere sequence) and exploration (sampling further from sphere centers) using a parameter β. It also balances exploring free-space (εC_task) and contact-space (δC_task) by probabilistically projecting sampled poses onto neighboring surfaces.
 - **Nearest Neighbor Selection:** Combines belief uncertainty (d_Σ) with spatial distance (d_T) between the robot's end-effector frame and a sampled target frame, rather than joint space distance.
 - **Action Selection:** Chooses between connect or slide actions based on the potential field and contact state, using the robot's Jacobian to drag the end-effector.

#### 3.4. Use Case Study: Granular ECE for Grasping from Piles

A specific application in bin-picking is explored to demonstrate ECE. This study identifies a novel "granular EC" arising from pile dynamics, which simplifies grasping from homogeneous piles without requiring visual object detection, complex grasp planning, or precise force control. The methodology involves:
- **Observation:** Observing interaction regularities when a hand pushes into a pile, leading to object stabilization and centering.
- **Explanation:** Proposing a physics-based explanation for granular ECE effects (object stabilization, object centering, interaction with geometrical ECs) based on granular material properties (penetration resistance, force chains).
- **Hypotheses Formulation:** Deriving testable hypotheses regarding the relationship between pile cardinality and grasp success, the efficacy of pile-centered vs. object-centered grasping, and the benefits of combining granular and geometrical ECs.
- **Empirical Evaluation:** Conducting real-robot and simulated experiments to evaluate the hypotheses by observing roll success (object rolling onto the hand) and grasp success rates under varying conditions (pile cardinality, hand alignment, environmental constraints).

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several key findings across simulation and real-world experiments, supporting the benefits of Environmental Constraint Exploitation (ECE) in robot motion planning.

#### 4.1. Scaling to High-Dimensional C-Spaces

- **Low-Dimensional (2DOF Gripper):** All conformant planners (CERRT, CET, CEET) successfully computed motion plans in low-dimensional C-spaces, including complex maze-like environments.
- **High-Dimensional (7DOF WAM Arm):** CERRT, CET, and CEET, along with their contingent variants (ConCERRT, ConCET, ConCEET), efficiently computed policies in a 7-dimensional configuration space for an object insertion task. These planners were shown to be efficient without requiring fixed discretization of the contact space, which typically limits POMDP-based methods in high dimensions.

#### 4.2. Benefits of Guided Funnel Sequencing in Complex C-spaces

- **Increased Efficiency with Workspace Information:** In 2D planning problems with increasing C-space volume and environmental complexity (e.g., maze environments), planners leveraging structural context from workspace information (CET and CEET) exhibited significantly improved planning efficiency compared to CERRT, which explored C-space randomly.
- **Reduced Search Space:** CET and CEET, by exploring only task-relevant regions, effectively reduced the search space volume, making planning tractable in complex environments where CERRT sometimes failed.
- **Action Space Reduction:** While C-space reduction provided significant benefits, action space reduction (as employed by CEET) offered minor additional improvements. In cases where the entire workspace was task-relevant, random exploration (CERRT) sometimes outperformed guided approaches due to overly restrictive approximations of task-relevant space by CET/CEET.

#### 4.3. Manipulation Funnels and Contact Sensing for Increased Uncertainty

- **Contingent Planning for High Uncertainty:** In the 7DOF WAM arm problem, only the contingent planners (ConCERRT, ConCET, ConCEET) were able to find solutions (even partial ones) under high motion uncertainty (σ_m = 0.1). The conformant planners failed in these conditions, demonstrating that contact event-based belief partitioning is crucial for handling significant uncertainty.
- **Complementary Approaches:** Guided exploration also enhanced contingent planning under uncertainty, indicating that workspace-aware guidance and contingency planning are complementary and should be combined for complex, uncertain scenarios.

#### 4.4. Real-World Applications

- **Robust Object Insertion (CERRT):** A real-world experiment with a 7DOF WAM arm inserting an end-effector into a wall deepening demonstrated that CERRT plans could handle unexpected environmental changes (e.g., a 7 cm wall raise). The plan, utilizing ECE, reduced an initial uncertainty of 7 cm to approximately 2 cm deviation at the goal.
- **Tactile Localization (ConCERRT):** ConCERRT policies were executed on a 7DOF Barrett WAM robot with a soft RBO Hand 2 for tactile localization of an object. The robot successfully localized a box by sequencing free-space and contact-exploiting motions, robustly handling object displacements up to 4 cm. This validated the use of contact events for belief partitioning in a real robot.

#### 4.5. Granular ECE for Grasping from Homogeneous Piles

- **Discovery of Granular EC:** A novel "granular EC" was characterized, emerging from the dynamics of object piles, offering similar benefits to geometrical ECs for grasping. This allows for open-loop grasping without complex visual perception or precise force control.
- **Grasping Strategy:** A simple shoveling-like strategy, leveraging three ECEs (lowering to tabletop, sliding on surface, pushing into pile), achieved up to 100% grasp success in an explorative study.
- **Hypothesis 1 (Object Stabilization):** Experimental results showed a strong positive correlation (Spearman coefficient 0.99) between pile cardinality and roll success rate (object rolling onto the hand). Larger piles provided sufficient opposing force for grasping, confirming that granular ECE supports grasping based on kinetic energy loss.
- **Hypothesis 2 (Object Centering):** There was no significant statistical difference in grasp success rates between a pile-centered strategy (object agnostic) and an object-centered strategy (expert-aligned) for light tennis balls and cylinders. For heavy tennis balls, the pile-centered strategy showed slightly better performance (p-value = 0.041), indicating that granular ECE can effectively center objects without active control, providing a manipulation funnel for object-hand positioning.
- **Hypothesis 3 (Combination with Geometrical ECs):** Grasp success rate increased significantly when a pile's expansion was constrained by static walls (geometrical ECs). A strong correlation (Spearman coefficient > 0.94) was observed between the degree of environmental constraint and grasp success, demonstrating that geometrical ECs complement granular ECE by increasing penetration resistance in smaller piles.
- **Real-World Bin-Picking:** The simple ECE-based grasping strategy was successfully applied to an Ocado-inspired bin-picking scenario, achieving an average of 6.3 attempts to pick and place four items, and generalizing to irregular object shapes like apples and net bags of limes.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of robotics, particularly in motion planning and manipulation, with broad potential impact:

- **Paradigm Shift in Motion Planning:** The work advocates for and empirically validates Environmental Constraint Exploitation (ECE) as a viable and advantageous alternative to traditional collision-free motion planning. By deliberately leveraging contact, it challenges the conventional constraint of obstacle avoidance, opening new avenues for motion generation.
- **Addressing Core Robotics Challenges:** ECE offers a unified framework to address two major challenges in robotics: the curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional configuration spaces and the robustness required for operating in uncertain real-world environments.
- **Enhanced Computational Tractability:** By reducing effective dimensionality through contact manifolds and biasing exploration to task-relevant regions, the proposed ECE-based planners significantly improve computational efficiency. This makes previously intractable problems in complex C-spaces amenable to solution.
- **Improved Robustness to Uncertainty:** The integration of manipulation funnels (collapsing uncertainty) and contact sensing (belief partitioning) into planning algorithms provides a robust mechanism to handle various forms of uncertainty (motion, proprioception, initial state). This capability is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic environments.
- **Scalability to Complex Systems:** The demonstrated ability of the ECE planners to scale to high-dimensional robot systems (e.g., 7DOF arms) without relying on restrictive discretizations or full belief space representations suggests broader applicability for complex manipulators and even humanoids.
- **Novel Environmental Constraint Characterization:** The discovery and characterization of "granular ECE" from pile dynamics represent a significant advancement in understanding complex physical interactions. This provides a theoretical and practical basis for simplified, open-loop grasping from piles, a common and challenging task in logistics and manipulation.
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful demonstration in real-world applications such as robust object insertion, tactile localization, and bin-picking directly illustrates the practical utility and readiness of ECE concepts for industrial and service robotics. This offers a path toward more reliable and less sensor-dependent robotic manipulation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays a foundation for future research by showing how ECE can simplify not only motion planning but also potentially control and perception. Future work could explore integrating ECE with advanced sensing, learning frameworks, and dynamic environments, potentially leading to more adaptive and intelligent robotic systems.
- **Inspiration for Bio-Inspired Robotics:** The observation that ECE aligns with human manipulation strategies (e.g., effortless grasping from piles) suggests that this paradigm could inspire more bio-inspired and robust robotic behaviors.
