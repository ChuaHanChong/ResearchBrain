# iMoWM: Taming Interactive Multi-Modal World Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> iMoWM presents an interactive multi-modal world model that predicts future color images, depth maps, and robot arm masks conditioned on actions. This model leverages an efficient multi-modal tokenizer and an autoregressive transformer to achieve enhanced visual prediction, faster learning in model-based reinforcement learning, and high-fidelity data augmentation for real-world imitation learning.

## Problem

- Existing 2D video-based world models lack explicit geometric and spatial understanding, which is crucial for effective robotic manipulation.
- Direct 4D world models, such as those using 3D Gaussian Splatting, are computationally expensive and require high-quality 3D reconstruction, making them impractical for common monocular camera setups.
- Prior models struggled to efficiently provide comprehensive, interactive multi-modal predictions (color, depth, and robot dynamics) necessary for robust downstream robotic tasks.

## Method

- A novel MMTokenizer unifies multi-modal inputs (RGB, depth, robot arm masks) into a compact, discrete token representation, optimizing for dynamic regions and incorporating modality-specific embeddings.
- An autoregressive transformer, initialized from pre-trained checkpoints, predicts future multi-modal tokens by integrating action information via 'slot tokens' representing robot end-effector poses.
- The world model is applied as an imagined simulator for model-based reinforcement learning and as a data augmentation tool for real-world imitation learning, generating synthetic multi-modal trajectories.

## Results

- iMoWM outperforms state-of-the-art baselines like iVideoGPT and GWM in action-conditioned multi-modal video generation, achieving superior FVD, PSNR, SSIM, and notably, depth accuracy (AbsRel) on BAIR and RoboNet datasets.
- The model demonstrates faster convergence and higher ultimate success rates in visual model-based reinforcement learning across Meta-World tasks compared to prior image-only and 3DGS-based approaches.
- High-fidelity multi-modal roll-outs generated by iMoWM effectively augment real-world demonstration data, enabling imitation learning policies to achieve success rates comparable to those trained solely on ground-truth data for real-robot manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating geometric information (depth maps) and dynamic attention (robot arm masks) alongside color images into world models significantly improves prediction accuracy and physical consistency for robotic interaction.
> - Developing an efficient multi-modal tokenization strategy allows for the effective incorporation of rich 3D information into transformer-based world models without incurring prohibitive computational costs.
> - Action-conditioned multi-modal world models can serve as high-fidelity neural simulators, enabling faster policy learning in reinforcement learning and providing valuable data augmentation for imitation learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of learned world models, specifically their application to robotic manipulation. Learned world models aim to predict future states of an environment based on limited observations, acting as neural simulators that can facilitate planning and adaptation in various domains, including embodied AI.

Historically, world models have advanced significantly in scene generation, with many recent models excelling at high-fidelity video generation. However, a notable limitation for robotic applications is the lack of explicit action integration. While some models have begun to incorporate action conditioning, they often remain constrained to 2D RGB pixel prediction, which inherently lacks the spatial and kinematic reasoning necessary for physical interactions in a 3D environment. This limitation can lead to physically implausible dynamics, especially under complex manipulation scenarios or occlusions.

Attempts to integrate 3D structure, such as those leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting, offer improved spatial awareness. However, these 3D models typically suffer from high computational and memory overhead, rendering them too slow for high-frequency robotic control, particularly when relying on monocular observations. Their performance can also be brittle due to dependence on high-quality dense 3D reconstructions.

The current research landscape, therefore, presents a challenge: to develop world models that possess the spatial awareness of 3D approaches while maintaining the computational efficiency characteristic of 2D video-based models. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a "2.5D" world model, which integrates visual context with geometric and physical constraints without resorting to computationally expensive dense 3D reconstructions. It aims to bridge the gap between physically agnostic 2D pixel prediction and computationally prohibitive explicit 3D world modeling for robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel world model, named RoDyn, that can effectively simulate environmental dynamics for robotic manipulation by incorporating both visual and geometric information in an efficient manner. The key motivations for this work stem from several limitations observed in existing learned world models when applied to robotic tasks:

- **Lack of Spatial and Kinematic Reasoning in 2D Models:** Prevalent 2D video-based world models, despite their generative capabilities, inherently lack the explicit spatial and kinematic understanding crucial for physical interactions. Robotic manipulation fundamentally involves continuous interactions in a 3D space. Without robust geometric understanding, 2D models often predict physically implausible dynamics, particularly during complex manipulations or occlusions, which diminishes their utility as accurate neural simulators.
- **Computational Inefficiency and Brittleness of 3D Models:** While recent 3D-aware world models address the spatial reasoning deficit, they often incur substantial computational and memory overhead due to their reliance on dense 3D reconstructions (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting). This makes them too slow for real-time robotic control and can lead to brittleness, especially when operating with monocular observations or in diverse, complex scenes where high-quality 3D reconstruction is challenging.
- **Insufficient Action Integration:** Many advanced generative models for video lack explicit action conditioning. For world models to serve as effective neural simulators for robotics, predicted future states must causally correspond to the robot's actions. Without this, the models cannot accurately inform embodied decision-making.
- **Improving Downstream Robotic Task Performance:** The overarching motivation is to translate improved predictive capabilities of world models into tangible benefits for downstream robotic tasks, such as accelerating model-based reinforcement learning (RL) and enhancing the success rates of real-world imitation learning. Current models often struggle to provide sufficiently realistic and geometrically consistent rollouts to significantly improve these tasks, especially in real-world deployments.

In response to these motivations, RoDyn is designed to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To formulate environmental dynamics within a geometry-aware latent space that combines the inference speed of 2D models with the spatial awareness of 3D models.
- To explicitly couple semantic visual appearances with spatial depth and robot-centric priors through a novel Robot-Dynamic Tokenizer.
- To guide the model's focus toward active robot-object interaction regions by injecting mask priors directly into sequence transitions using a Mask-guided Autoregressive architecture.
- To demonstrate that these predictive capabilities lead to substantial gains in downstream robotic manipulation tasks, specifically in accelerating model-based reinforcement learning and improving real-world imitation learning success rates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoDyn is introduced as a Robot-Dynamic 2.5D World Model designed to autoregressively predict future physical states by jointly synthesizing semantic visual appearances, spatial depth, and dynamic robot masks. The framework operates on an initial RGB observation, which is extended to a geometry-aware 2.5D observation space, incorporating depth and mask information.

The methodology comprises three main components:

**A. Robot-Dynamic Tokenizer**
Conventional tokenizers are primarily tailored for 2D pixels, which limits their applicability to 3D-aware robotic tasks. The Robot-Dynamic Tokenizer aims to project the 2.5D state space into a compact, physics-aware discrete representation.
- **Dual-Encoder–Decoder Framework:** The tokenizer is instantiated within a dual-encoder–decoder framework, consisting of `{(E_c, D_c), (E_p, D_p)}`. `(E_c, D_c)` handles initial observations to establish the global static scene, extracting contextual tokens `Z_c`. `(E_p, D_p)` is dedicated to modeling temporal evolution, generating dynamic tokens `Z_d` for future frames, exploiting the property that significant changes are localized to interacting entities.
- **RGB-Dominated Cross-Attention:** To align multi-modal inputs (RGB, depth, masks) without simple concatenation, an RGB-dominated cross-attention mechanism is employed. This design allows spatial depth and agent-centric masks to explicitly query and modulate the core semantic visual features.
- **Mask-Driven Dynamic Fusion:** A mask-driven dynamic fusion operation is integrated to inject mask priors, which adaptively concentrates the representation on active robot-object interactions. This coupling aims to ensure that the resulting tokens are grounded in causal robotic dynamics, rather than static background elements.
- **Training Objective:** The tokenizer is optimized using a joint training objective, `L_tokenizer`, comprising an L1 reconstruction loss, a commitment loss, a perceptual loss, and optionally an adversarial loss. This ensures both high-fidelity multi-modal reconstruction and a stable discrete latent space.

**B. Mask-guided Autoregressive Transformer**
Following tokenization, future state generation is framed as a discrete sequence modeling problem. The high-dimensional 2.5D multi-modal inputs are serialized into a unified token sequence.
- **Embodiment Transition Token [S_t]:** To enforce causal constraints during sequence transitions, an `Embodiment Transition Token`, denoted as `[S_t]`, is introduced. This token serves as a physical boundary flag, fusing the continuous action command `a_t` with current kinematic priors `m_t`. Specifically, `[S_t]` is composed of a learnable universal boundary embedding `[S_base]`, an action projection `P_act(a_t)` encoding the end-effector pose, and a mask projection `P_mask(m_t)` injecting the extracted physical mask representation. This explicit mask-guided injection is intended to ground temporal predictions on active robot-object interactions, mitigating physical hallucinations.
- **Causal Transformer Backbone:** A scalable causal transformer backbone, adopting a LLaMA-style architecture, is employed. It incorporates RMSNorm, SwiGLU activations, and Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE).
- **Spatially-Selective Cross-Entropy Loss:** The predictive objective, `L_AR`, is applied exclusively to the dynamic tokens, using a spatially-selective cross-entropy loss. Context tokens act as read-only prefix prompts. During inference, future dynamic states are generated autoregressively, with newly predicted spatio-kinematic tokens fed back into the sequence for continuous, closed-loop simulation.

**C. Downstream Tasks**
RoDyn's predictive capabilities are then applied to two downstream robotic tasks:
- **Imitation Learning:** RoDyn is used as a data augmentation engine. It synthesizes multiple geometrically grounded demonstration rollouts based on an initial observation and a successful action trajectory. These generated 2.5D multi-modal outputs are then used to train a robotic manipulation policy via 3D Diffusion Policy. The policy consumes the synthesized semantic appearances and spatial topologies, predicting actions in sequential chunks for temporal consistency.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** RoDyn functions as a physically grounded neural simulator within model-based RL frameworks, similar to MBPO. An initial policy collects real replay data `D_real` to train RoDyn. The optimized RoDyn (`P_θ`) then generates an imagined replay buffer `D_imag`. An actor-critic model is updated using the combined dataset `D_real ∪ D_imag`. An auxiliary reward head is incorporated into RoDyn to predict step-wise rewards, aligning with standard visual RL practices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate RoDyn's performance across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios, establishing its capabilities in multi-modal generation and downstream robotic tasks.

**A. Real-World Imitation Learning**
- **Setup:** Evaluated on a GALAXEA A1 robotic arm with an Intel RealSense D435i camera, performing 5 representative complex manipulation tasks (e.g., item stacking, pick-and-place) from a dataset of 10 tasks. Policies were trained using RoDyn-synthesized 2.5D rollouts, compared against policies trained on pure 2D iVideoGPT-generated data (augmented with ground-truth depth for fair comparison) and policies trained directly on real-world ground-truth (GT) demonstrations.
- **Results:** RoDyn achieved an average manipulation success rate of 87.0% across the 5 tasks, which is an absolute improvement of 42.0% over the 2D iVideoGPT baseline (45.0%). Notably, RoDyn's performance matched and slightly surpassed that of policies trained on ground-truth data (83.0%), suggesting its synthesized data effectively smoothed sub-optimal human demonstration noise. Quantitative visual fidelity metrics on high-resolution real-world data (256x256) showed RoDyn outperforming iVideoGPT with higher PSNR (38.11 vs 32.14), SSIM (0.941 vs 0.901), and lower LPIPS (0.091 vs 0.130).

**B. Action-conditioned Multi-modal Video Generation**
- **Setup:** Benchmarked on large-scale datasets including BAIR, RoboNet, and the challenging DROID dataset, as well as a self-collected high-resolution dataset. Depth maps were extracted using Video Depth Anything, and agent-centric kinematic masks were generated using Grounding DINO combined with SAM 2 to construct the 2.5D state space. Metrics included FVD, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS for visual quality and AbsRel for spatial depth accuracy.
- **Results:**
 - **BAIR and DROID:** RoDyn demonstrated superior performance across all visual and spatial metrics compared to existing baselines (e.g., MaskViT, iVideoGPT, IRASim, Cosmos, EVAC, BridgeV2W). On BAIR, RoDyn achieved an FVD of 60.9 (compared to 65.01 for iVideoGPT) and better PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and AbsRel. On DROID, RoDyn's FVD was 122.7 (compared to 145.2 for BridgeV2W), with improved PSNR (24.37 vs 22.89), SSIM (0.877 vs 0.874), and LPIPS (0.076 vs 0.111).
 - **RoboNet:** RoDyn achieved the best pixel-level fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and spatial accuracy (AbsRel), though its FVD (67.6) was slightly higher than iVideoGPT (65.8).

**C. Visual Model-Based Reinforcement Learning**
- **Setup:** Evaluated on six robotic manipulation tasks from the Meta-World benchmark, using an MBPO-inspired framework with DrQ-v2 as the actor-critic algorithm. Comparisons were made against explicit 3D-aware world models (GWM) and optimized pure 2D models (iVideoGPT, DreamerV3).
- **Results:** RoDyn consistently improved performance across the tasks, achieving an average success rate increase of over 10% compared to the strongest baseline. It secured the state-of-the-art position across all six individual sub-tasks, with a maximum absolute performance gain of approximately 40% on the hammer task. This indicates its efficacy as a stable, physically grounded neural simulator.

**D. Ablation Study**
- **Tokenizer Efficiency:** Comparing the Robot-Dynamic Tokenizer against a Naive Concat baseline (which concatenates multi-modal observations, tripling sequence length). The Robot-Dynamic Tokenizer accelerated inference by 86x (down to 10s from 860s) while stabilizing temporal generation quality.
- **Module Integration:** Incrementally integrating core modules. Adding the Robot-Dynamic Tokenizer and the Mask-Guided AR Transformer individually and together contributed to RoDyn's overall performance. Both the geometry-aware latent space and the physically constrained causal generation (via mask-guided AR) were shown to improve performance metrics (FVD, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) over a pure 2D visual baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoDyn framework offers a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and embodied AI, particularly through its novel approach to world modeling.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **bridge the critical gap between computational efficiency and spatial awareness**. By developing a Robot-Dynamic 2.5D World Model, the authors have demonstrated that it is possible to incorporate essential geometric and kinematic priors into environmental dynamics without incurring the prohibitive computational costs and brittleness associated with dense 3D world models. This enables a more nuanced understanding of physical interactions compared to purely 2D video-based approaches.

The **Robot-Dynamic Tokenizer** and **Mask-guided Autoregressive Transformer** represent architectural innovations that specifically address the challenges of robotic interaction. The tokenizer's RGB-dominated cross-attention and mask-driven fusion facilitate the construction of a geometry-aware latent space that prioritizes active robot-object interactions. The explicit injection of mask and action priors into the autoregressive process through the Embodiment Transition Token enhances the physical grounding of predictions, mitigating the generation of implausible scenarios. This design enables the model to focus its predictive capacity on relevant dynamic regions, reducing computational redundancy.

The practical impact of RoDyn is demonstrated through its **substantial performance improvements in downstream robotic tasks**:
- **Real-world Imitation Learning:** The significant increase in real-world manipulation success rates (42% improvement over 2D baselines, matching or surpassing ground-truth performance) suggests RoDyn's potential as a robust data augmentation engine. It can help bridge the reality gap by generating geometrically consistent and physically plausible rollouts, potentially smoothing out noise in human demonstrations and improving policy generalization.
- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** Its efficacy as a physically grounded neural simulator, leading to consistent performance gains across diverse Meta-World tasks, indicates that RoDyn can accelerate the learning process and enable more stable policy optimization in simulated environments. This could reduce the need for extensive real-world data collection, which is often a bottleneck in robotics.

The ability to generate high-fidelity, action-conditioned multi-modal videos (RGB, depth, masks) with improved spatial accuracy contributes to the development of more reliable and versatile visual perception systems for robots. This could lead to advancements in robot autonomy, allowing for more precise and adaptable manipulation in complex, unstructured environments.

Future work, as suggested by the authors, includes scaling up pre-training data across more diverse robotic embodiments and heterogeneous action spaces. This would further unlock the potential for universal generalizability of 2.5D multi-modal world models, potentially broadening their application beyond the current scope and impacting areas such as human-robot collaboration, industrial automation, and exploration robotics.
