# Revisiting LLM Reasoning via Information Bottleneck

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper from The University of Sydney, ByteDance, and NTU introduces IB-aware Reasoning Optimization (IBRO), an information-theoretic framework grounded in the information bottleneck principle, to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models during reinforcement learning-based post-training. The method consistently improved average `top@1` reasoning scores by 2 points across multiple mathematical benchmarks and RL algorithms compared to vanilla training.

## Problem

- Current reinforcement learning-based post-training methods for LLM reasoning are largely heuristic-driven, lacking a rigorous theoretical framework.
- Conflicting empirical findings exist regarding the optimal management of generation entropy during training, with some advocating for high entropy for exploration and others for reduced entropy for focused prediction.
- There is a need for a principled methodology to understand and optimally manage entropy and information flow within LLMs to enhance reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces ==IB-aware Reasoning Optimization (IBRO)==, an information-theoretic framework based on the ==Information Bottleneck principle==, to formally define optimal LLM reasoning as a trade-off between informativeness and generalizability.
- Derives a practical, ==token-level surrogate objective== by approximating mutual information terms using readily available token entropy and advantage, making direct optimization tractable.
- Proposes ==IB regularization==, an efficient method that seamlessly integrates into standard RL objectives (e.g., PPO, DAPO) by adding a single line of code, introducing negligible computational overhead.

## Results

- The proposed IB regularization consistently improved LLM reasoning performance, increasing average `top@1` scores by **2 points** across PPO and DAPO algorithms on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AMC23, AIME24, AIME25).
- Naive entropy regularization consistently degraded performance compared to no regularization, while IB regularization delivered substantial gains, particularly on more challenging tasks like AIME24 and AIME25.
- Analysis showed IB regularization maintained a stable and controlled entropy profile throughout training, unlike naive regularization which caused excessive entropy or entropy collapse, and effectively mitigated premature response truncation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal LLM reasoning requires a principled trade-off: maximizing informativeness about the correct answer while simultaneously reducing dependence on irrelevant input details for improved generalization.
> - Uniformly increasing generation entropy during LLM post-training is detrimental, often leading to reduced reasoning performance; instead, selective, advantage-aware entropy modulation is crucial for effective exploration.
> - IB regularization achieves this by encouraging higher entropy for tokens that significantly contribute to positive rewards (critical tokens) while suppressing entropy for less informative tokens, maintaining coherent and effective reasoning trajectories.
