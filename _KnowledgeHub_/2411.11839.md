# RoboGSim: A Real2Sim2Real Robotic Gaussian Splatting Simulator

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboGSim is a robotic simulator leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting and Isaac Sim to create photorealistic, physics-consistent environments for robotic learning. It substantially reduces data generation costs and offers a safe platform for policy evaluation, demonstrating improved performance for policies trained on its synthesized data, particularly in previously unseen environments.

## Problem

- High cost and inefficiency of acquiring real-world embodied data for robotic manipulation tasks.
- Limitations of existing simulators in providing sufficiently high visual fidelity and accurate physics modeling, leading to a significant Sim2Real gap.
- Difficulty in unifying high-fidelity demonstration synthesis with physics-consistent closed-loop evaluation within a single, efficient platform.

## Method

- Integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic visual scene reconstruction with the Isaac Sim physics engine and MDH kinematics for accurate robotic arm dynamics.
- Employs a Digital Twins Builder for precise spatial alignment between real, Gaussian Splatting, and Isaac Sim environments, enabling dynamic scene and object composition.
- Provides an Interactive Engine with a Synthesizer for generating diverse, high-fidelity training data and a closed-loop Evaluator for safe, reproducible policy assessment.

## Results

- Achieved high visual fidelity for novel pose synthesis, demonstrated by PSNR of 31.3, SSIM of 0.79, real-time rendering at 10 FPS, and a pixel distance of 7.37 between real and simulated points.
- Reduced data generation time by 10x (4 hours vs. 40 hours for 1,000 samples), with RoboGSim-trained policies achieving 90% success in novel scenes, outperforming real-data-trained models (60% success).
- Provided a safe and reproducible closed-loop evaluation platform that accurately reproduces real-world robot inference and prevents dangerous collisions or operational limit exceedances in simulated 'bad cases'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting with a robust physics engine effectively bridges the Sim2Real gap by offering high visual fidelity and physical consistency in simulated environments.
> - Generating large-scale, 3D-consistent synthetic data leads to more spatially intelligent policies, significantly outperforming real-data-trained models, especially in novel scenes and views.
> - A Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) pipeline offers a scalable, cost-efficient, and safe framework for robotic data generation and policy evaluation, mitigating the high expenses and risks of real-world experimentation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The core problem addressed by RoboGSim lies at the intersection of robotic learning, computer graphics, and simulation: the **Sim2Real gap**. This gap refers to the challenge of transferring policies learned in a simulated environment to real-world robotic systems due to discrepancies in physics, visual fidelity, and environmental dynamics. Bridging this gap is crucial for scaling up robotic learning, as collecting real-world interaction data is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and often unsafe.

Historically, efforts to bridge the Sim2Real gap have fallen into three main categories:
1. **Domain Randomization (DR):** Introducing randomness in simulator parameters (textures, lighting, physics properties) to expose the learning agent to a wide variety of conditions, making the learned policy more robust to real-world variations.
2. **Domain Adaptation (DA):** Methods that aim to align the feature spaces of simulated and real environments, often using techniques like generative adversarial networks (GANs).
3. **Learning with Disturbances:** Training policies in simulation with introduced noise and unpredictability to prepare them for real-world uncertainties.

While these methods have seen success, they often struggle with truly high-fidelity visual and physical realism, which is critical for complex manipulation tasks where subtle texture, lighting, and contact dynamics play a significant role.

More recently, the advent of **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs)** and particularly **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** has revolutionized 3D scene representation and rendering. These methods allow for photorealistic reconstruction of complex scenes from multi-view images, enabling novel view synthesis with unprecedented fidelity. This advancement has naturally led to the **Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) paradigm**, where real-world scenes are first reconstructed into a highly realistic digital twin (Real2Sim), and then policies are learned or evaluated within this high-fidelity simulation before being transferred back to the real robot (Sim2Real).

RoboGSim explicitly builds upon this R2S2R paradigm, positioning itself as a direct evolution of recent 3DGS-based robotic simulation efforts. The paper references similar works like **Robo-GS** and **SplatSim**. Robo-GS, for instance, introduced a Real2Sim pipeline using a hybrid representation for high-fidelity simulation but had limitations in demonstration synthesis for novel scenes/views/objects and closed-loop evaluation. SplatSim focused on reconstructing both robot and objects for Sim2Real tasks but lacked detailed mechanisms for generating digital twin assets for accurate manipulation.

RoboGSim distinguishes itself by aiming to unify **high-fidelity demonstration synthesis** (with novel scenes, objects, views, and trajectories) and **physics-consistent closed-loop evaluation** within a single, efficient, and low-cost platform. By addressing the limitations of prior 3DGS-based simulators, RoboGSim seeks to provide a more complete solution for the R2S2R pipeline, reducing the sim2real gap and accelerating policy learning and evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of RoboGSim can be summarized as follows:

- **Address the high cost and inefficiency of real-world embodied data acquisition:** Traditional methods of collecting large-scale robotic manipulation data through remote operation are extremely expensive and time-consuming, hindering the progress of data-driven policy learning.
- **Overcome limitations of existing simulators in high-fidelity modeling:** Current simulation environments, while useful, often lack the photorealistic texture and accurate physics modeling needed for effective Sim2Real transfer, leading to a significant domain gap.
- **Bridge the Sim2Real gap more effectively:** By leveraging the photorealistic rendering capabilities of 3D Gaussian Splatting and integrating it with a robust physics engine, RoboGSim aims to create a simulated environment that closely mirrors the real world, thereby facilitating better transfer of learned policies.
- **Enable efficient, large-scale, and diverse demonstration synthesis:** The goal is to generate massive amounts of high-fidelity synthetic data, including novel views, objects, trajectories, and entire scenes, to scale up training datasets for policy learning. This aims to overcome the single-source limitation and high cost of real robot data.
- **Provide a safe, reproducible, and fair platform for closed-loop policy evaluation:** Testing manipulation policies directly on real robots can be dangerous, costly, and difficult to reproduce for comparative analysis. RoboGSim seeks to offer a virtual environment where policies can be safely, reliably, and consistently evaluated in a physics-consistent manner.
- **Unify demonstration synthesis and closed-loop evaluation:** Unlike previous works that focused on either Real2Sim transfer or limited Sim2Real aspects, RoboGSim aims to provide a holistic platform that supports both data generation for training and robust evaluation of diverse policy networks.

In essence, RoboGSim is motivated by the need for a comprehensive R2S2R robotic simulator that can provide photorealistic, physics-consistent, and scalable data generation and evaluation capabilities, thereby significantly accelerating research and development in robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboGSim is designed as a Real2Sim2Real robotic simulator, structured around four main components: Gaussian Reconstructor, Digital Twins Builder, Scene Composer, and Interactive Engine. The overall pipeline integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with a physics engine (Isaac Sim) and a kinematic model (MDH) to achieve its objectives.

#### 3.1. Gaussian Reconstructor

This module focuses on reconstructing the visual aspects of the scene and robotic arm.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** Multi-view RGB image sequences are fed into a 3DGS pipeline to reconstruct static scenes and objects with high fidelity. 3DGS represents the scene as a collection of explicit Gaussian points, each with properties like mean, covariance matrix, opacity, and color, enabling real-time, high-fidelity rendering.
- **Robotic Arm Modeling (MDH):** The robotic arm's kinematics are modeled using the Modified Denavit-Hartenberg (MDH) convention. After segmenting the point clouds corresponding to each joint, the MDH dynamic model is used to control these Gaussian points, allowing for accurate, kinematic-driven dynamic rendering of the robot's motion. This is crucial for precise interaction.

#### 3.2. Digital Twins Builder

This component bridges the gap between the real world, the GS representation, and the physics simulation environment (Isaac Sim).
- **3D Assets Generation:** For real-world objects, high-quality multi-view images are captured, features extracted (using GIM to handle challenging textures/reflections), and then processed through the COLMAP pipeline to generate an initial point cloud for 3DGS reconstruction. For novel objects, generative 3D reconstruction methods are used to obtain 3D Gaussians and textured meshes, which can then be edited using tools like GaussianEditor.
- **Layout Alignment:** A critical innovation is the layout alignment module. This ensures precise spatial correspondence between the real world, the Gaussian Splatting scene, and the Isaac Sim environment. It involves measuring the real-world scene, aligning sizes in Isaac Sim, and then using segmentation maps rendered from specific camera viewpoints (e.g., Bird's Eye View) in both GS and Isaac Sim to adjust shifts and achieve alignment.
- **Sim2GS Alignment:** This module computes a transformation matrix (T_sim_gs) that maps coordinates between the Isaac Sim and GS environments, ensuring that simulated object and robot poses are accurately reflected in the GS rendering.
- **Camera Localization:** For transforming real-world coordinates to the GS system, a localization approach similar to GS-SLAM is used. This optimizes external camera parameters (T_W_C) for a pre-trained GS model by minimizing photometric residuals against observed images.

#### 3.3. Scene Composer

This module allows for dynamic editing and composition of scenes and objects.
- **Scene Editing:** It enables merging reconstructed objects into new scenes. This involves calculating transformations, projecting point cloud coordinates, and adjusting Gaussian point attributes (scale, rotation, position) to fit the new scene context realistically.
- **Object Editing:** Similar to scene editing, but specifically targets individual objects. It uses the aligned coordinate systems (from Eq. 7) to apply transformations (rotation, translation) to the Gaussian points of a target object within the scene.

#### 3.4. Interactive Engine

The final component serves two main functions: Synthesizer and Evaluator.
- **Synthesizer:** This function leverages the high-fidelity and physics-consistent environment to generate large volumes of diverse training data. It synthesizes realistic manipulated demonstrations with novel views, scenes, objects, and robot trajectories, providing low-cost, scalable data for downstream policy learning. Data collection can even be facilitated using VR/Xbox equipment to mimic real-world interactions.
- **Evaluator:** RoboGSim provides a safe, real-time, and reproducible platform for evaluating trained policy networks. It operates in a closed-loop manner: Isaac Sim outputs the initial state, GS renders images based on this state, the policy network predicts actions from these images, the predicted actions are sent back to Isaac Sim for physics-based parsing (kinematic inverse, collision detection), and Isaac Sim then sends updated poses to GS for the next frame's rendering, creating a continuous feedback loop until the task is complete. This allows for realistic assessment of policies without risking real hardware.

The integrated approach of RoboGSim, combining the visual fidelity of 3DGS, the physical realism of Isaac Sim, and precise spatial alignment, forms a powerful framework for addressing the Sim2Real gap in robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate RoboGSim are structured into four proxy groups, utilizing a UR5 robot arm. The results consistently demonstrate RoboGSim's ability to create high-fidelity simulations that effectively bridge the Real2Sim and Sim2Real domains.

#### 4.1. Real2Sim Novel Pose Synthesis

This experiment validated the accuracy of RoboGSim's reconstruction and robotic arm movements from real-world poses.
- **Fidelity:** RoboGSim successfully captured both the texture and physical dynamics of the robotic arm, with generated novel poses showing strong alignment with real-world captures.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** The system achieved a **PSNR of 31.3** and an **SSIM of 0.79** for rendered novel poses compared to real videos, along with **real-time rendering at 10 FPS**. This indicates high visual fidelity and practical usability.
- **Pixel Distance:** The pixel distance between the same point in real and RoboGSim images was measured at 7.37, further confirming geometric accuracy.

#### 4.2. Sim2Real Trajectory Replay

This experiment assessed whether trajectories generated in Isaac Sim could be accurately reproduced by a real robot and rendered faithfully by RoboGSim.
- **Alignment:** The comparison of video sequences from Isaac Sim, the real robot, and RoboGSim rendering for a Coke-grasping task revealed a "strong alignment" between the simulated policy, the real physical behavior, and the RoboGSim rendering. This demonstrates effective Sim2Real transfer and reliable modeling of real-world dynamics.

#### 4.3. RoboGSim as Synthesizer

This section showcased RoboGSim's capability to generate high-fidelity demonstrations for policy learning, particularly in challenging scenarios. A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model (using LLAMA3-8B and CLIP) was trained and evaluated on a ring-toss task, focusing on pick and place subtasks.
- **Data Efficiency and Cost Reduction:** Manual real data collection took 40 hours for 1,000 samples, whereas RoboGSim required only 4 hours for synthesis of the same amount of data, highlighting a 10x improvement in efficiency and cost reduction.
- **Zero-Shot Performance:** The model trained with RoboGSim data achieved "zero-shot capability" on the real robot, with performance comparable to the model trained with real data (both around 90% success rate for test view grasp/place).
- **Superiority in Novel Scenarios:** Crucially, for **novel scenes**, the RoboGSim-trained model performed significantly better (90% success) than the real-data-trained model (60% success). For **novel views**, RoboGSim also showed less bias compared to real data.
- **Comparison with 2D Augmentation:** Adding 2D augmentation to real data improved performance in novel scenes (40% to 60%) but decreased test view performance and increased bias in novel views. The authors attribute RoboGSim's superior performance to its "spatial intelligence" compared to "pure 2D AUG" methods, emphasizing the importance of 3D consistency.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations demonstrated the UR5 robot arm operating seamlessly in various novel scenes (factory, shelf, outdoor environments) with high-fidelity multi-view renderings, confirming the capability for novel scene synthesis.

#### 4.4. RoboGSim as Evaluator

This experiment demonstrated RoboGSim's effectiveness as a platform for closed-loop policy evaluation, emphasizing safety and reproducibility.
- **Real-World Consistency:** RoboGSim successfully reproduced results similar to those observed with real-world robot inference using the same VLA model.
- **Safety and Collision Avoidance:** In cases where a real robot might exceed operational limits (leading to manual shutdown) or collide with the environment due to policy errors, RoboGSim could simulate these "bad cases" and "avoid dangerous collisions" without actual physical damage. This highlights its value as a safe pre-deployment evaluation tool.
- **Fair and Efficient Evaluation:** The ability to reproduce real-world outcomes in a controlled virtual environment makes RoboGSim a "fair, safe, and efficient evaluation platform for policy networks."

In summary, RoboGSim demonstrates significant advancements in both visual fidelity and physical consistency, proving its capability as an efficient data synthesizer and a safe, reliable policy evaluator, particularly excelling in handling novel scenes and views with lower data collection costs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboGSim represents a significant stride in the domain of robotic learning and simulation, offering solutions to several long-standing challenges. Its potential impact spans both research and practical applications:

#### 5.1. Bridging the Sim2Real Gap

The most profound impact of RoboGSim lies in its enhanced ability to bridge the Sim2Real gap. By leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting for high-fidelity visual reconstruction and integrating it with a robust physics engine, RoboGSim creates a more realistic and physically consistent digital twin of the real world. This closer fidelity between simulation and reality means that policies learned or evaluated within RoboGSim are more likely to perform effectively when deployed on physical robots. The experimental results, particularly the zero-shot performance and superior effectiveness in novel scenarios compared to real data (even with 2D augmentation), strongly support this claim.

#### 5.2. Enabling Large-Scale and Low-Cost Data Generation

The high cost and laborious nature of collecting real-world embodied data has been a major bottleneck for data-driven robotic learning. RoboGSim addresses this by acting as an efficient synthesizer capable of generating "massive and photorealistic simulated datasets" with novel views, objects, trajectories, and scenes at a significantly lower cost and time investment (e.g., 10x faster than manual collection). This capability is crucial for training complex deep learning models that require vast amounts of diverse data, potentially democratizing access to large datasets for roboticists.

#### 5.3. Safe, Reproducible, and Fair Policy Evaluation

RoboGSim offers a virtual environment for policy evaluation that is safe, reproducible, and fair. Testing policies on real robots can be hazardous, expensive (due to potential damage), and difficult to set up identically for comparative studies. The closed-loop evaluation in RoboGSim mitigates these risks, allowing researchers to rapidly iterate on policy designs, evaluate their performance under various conditions, and compare different models without the constraints of physical hardware. The ability to simulate "bad cases" and avoid real-world collisions or violations is a critical safety feature.

#### 5.4. Advancing 3DGS Applications in Robotics

This work further validates and expands the application of 3D Gaussian Splatting in robotics. By demonstrating how 3DGS can be combined with kinematic models (MDH) and physics engines (Isaac Sim) to create dynamic and interactive robotic simulations, RoboGSim paves the way for more widespread adoption of neural rendering techniques in robotics.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also point to important avenues for future research:
- **Rigid Objects Only:** The current version can only simulate rigid objects. Extending this to deformable objects would significantly broaden the range of manipulation tasks it can support.
- **Lighting Consistency:** Unifying the lighting for synthesized objects with the robotic arm is an area for improvement, which would further enhance photorealism, especially when compositing objects from different sources.
- **Geometrically Consistent Object Meshes:** Generating geometrically accurate object meshes remains a challenge, which is vital for complex manipulation tasks requiring precise physical interaction. Exploring more advanced mesh extraction methods could enhance fidelity.
- **Task Categories and Benchmarks:** Expanding the variety of supported tasks and establishing comprehensive benchmarks would allow for more rigorous evaluation and comparison with future systems.

In conclusion, RoboGSim represents a robust, practical, and forward-looking solution for robotic simulation. By effectively leveraging cutting-edge neural rendering with established robotics principles, it provides a powerful platform for accelerating robotic learning, reducing development costs, and ensuring safer, more efficient policy deployment.
