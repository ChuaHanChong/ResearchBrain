# Evolving Language Models without Labels: Majority Drives Selection, Novelty Promotes Variation

> [!summary] Summary
> EVOL-RL introduces an evolutionary, label-free reinforcement learning framework for large language models, mitigating the prevalent 'entropy collapse' by balancing majority-driven selection with novelty-promoting variation. The Tencent AI Lab and University of Notre Dame collaboration achieves over 20 percentage points improvement in pass@16 accuracy on challenging benchmarks and robust out-of-domain generalization.

## Problem

- Existing label-free LLM self-improvement methods, like Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), suffer from 'entropy collapse' due to over-reliance on self-confirmation signals, suppressing solution diversity.
- This leads to degraded `pass@n` performance, shorter reasoning chains, and a loss of exploratory capability, hindering long-term autonomous learning.
- The challenge is to enable LLMs to continuously self-improve without ground-truth labels while maintaining robust diversity and avoiding premature convergence.

## Method

- EVOL-RL proposes a framework based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), guided by a novel ==three-part reward function== inspired by biological evolution.
- The reward function incorporates validity checks, ==majority-based selection== for correctness, and a ==semantically-aware novelty incentive== for reasoning paths, with separate normalization for majority and minority groups.
- Supporting mechanisms include ==asymmetric clipping== in GRPO and a ==token-level entropy regularizer== to further promote diversity and preserve beneficial 'mutation' signals.

## Results

- EVOL-RL consistently and substantially improves `pass@16` performance over TTRL, with gains often exceeding **20** percentage points on challenging benchmarks like AIME24, and enhances `pass@1` accuracy.
- The method demonstrates strong cross-difficulty robustness and out-of-domain generalization, transferring learned reasoning skills effectively across mathematical and non-mathematical tasks (e.g., GPQA).
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the novelty reward and exploration mechanisms in fostering long-term performance and maintaining diversity, even providing synergistic benefits to supervised RLVR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Label-free self-improvement requires explicitly balancing 'selection' (reinforcing majority consensus) with 'variation' (promoting novel reasoning paths) to prevent degenerative feedback loops.
> - The novelty reward creates an inherently unstable state for uniform solution populations, continuously driving the policy away from homogeneity and promoting diverse reasoning.
> - Entropy collapse observed in majority-only methods is not permanent; EVOL-RL demonstrates a distinct 'evolving point' after which policy entropy and reasoning length recover, leading to sustained performance gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in LLM reasoning capabilities have largely been driven by paradigms like Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR fine-tunes models using reinforcement learning on tasks where an automated verifier can confirm the correctness of a final answer, exemplified in domains such as mathematics and coding. While highly effective, the reliance of RLVR on external verifiers fundamentally limits its applicability to domains with deterministic, easily checkable solutions. The "next frontier of intelligence," as the authors articulate, lies in enabling LLMs to autonomously evolve—continuously learning from the vast, unstructured, and unlabeled data streams encountered in real-world environments, without constant human oversight or external judges.

This paper directly addresses the critical challenge of **label-free LLM adaptation and self-improvement**. Current label-free methods primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Intrinsic Confidence-Based Methods:** These methods derive rewards from a model's internal confidence signals (e.g., low-entropy or self-consistent outputs), training the model to become "more certain."
2. **Majority-Driven Bootstrapping:** Exemplified by Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), this paradigm bootstraps supervision from the majority-voted answer among several generated responses.

The authors diagnose a fundamental flaw in these existing approaches: they primarily rely on self-confirmation signals, which, while initially useful, create a degenerative feedback loop. By systematically rewarding conformity to existing beliefs, the learning process actively degrades the quality of its own reward signal over time. This leads to **entropy collapse**—a phenomenon where solution diversity is suppressed, reasoning complexity declines, and the policy converges to a low-entropy state, ultimately harming `pass@n` performance (the ability to solve a problem within `n` attempts) and out-of-domain generalization. This issue is well-known in classical reinforcement learning and self-training, especially in the absence of explicit entropy regularization or external supervision. The paper highlights that current attempts to mitigate this, such as simple entropy loss or "clip-high" strategies, are insufficient.

In response to this, the paper introduces a novel perspective by framing label-free learning as an **evolutionary system**. It draws parallels to established fields like genetic algorithms, novelty search, and quality-diversity (QD) methods (e.g., MAP-Elites) in evolutionary computation, where diversity preservation is recognized as essential for avoiding premature convergence and enabling robust, long-term progress. By explicitly balancing "selection" (for stability and correctness) with "variation" (for exploration and diversity), this work aims to overcome the limitations of current label-free LLM self-improvement methods and push towards truly autonomous and adaptable LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust, label-free framework that enables Large Language Models to autonomously self-improve without external supervision, crucially avoiding the "entropy collapse" observed in existing methods.

The primary motivations for this work are:

1. **The Demand for Autonomous LLMs:** Real-world deployment of LLMs necessitates continuous learning and self-improvement in dynamic, unlabeled environments. Relying on human labels or external verifiers is impractical and unsustainable for scaling intelligence.
2. **Addressing the "Entropy Collapse" Problem:** Existing label-free self-improvement methods, which reward self-confirmation (e.g., confidence, consistency, or majority vote), inadvertently create a positive feedback loop that eliminates solution diversity. This leads to a degradation of reasoning complexity, poor `pass@n` scores, and ultimately, an inability to generalize. The authors seek to diagnose and directly counter this critical failure mode.
3. **Balancing Exploration and Exploitation:** The paper re-highlights the classic reinforcement learning dilemma of balancing exploration (discovering new strategies) and exploitation (refining known good strategies). This dilemma is particularly acute in label-free settings, where internal signals can quickly become impoverished and drive the model into local optima.
4. **Promoting Generalizable Reasoning:** By maintaining solution diversity and fostering varied reasoning paths, the authors aim to develop LLMs that learn more fundamental and transferable reasoning skills, leading to better out-of-domain generalization beyond the specific training task.
5. **Drawing Inspiration from Evolution:** The paper is motivated by the biological principle of evolution—where variation generates new candidates and selection retains what works—to design a more robust self-improvement mechanism for LLMs. This analogy serves as a guiding principle for the proposed method.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond superficial self-confirmation to enable deeper, more resilient, and truly evolving intelligence in LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **EVOL-RL (EVolution-Oriented and Label-free Reinforcement Learning)**, tackles the entropy collapse problem by explicitly mirroring the evolutionary principle of balancing selection with variation. It builds upon a base optimization algorithm, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and integrates a novel reward function alongside supporting mechanisms.

#### 3.1. Optimization with GRPO

EVOL-RL utilizes GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), a policy-gradient algorithm suitable for fine-tuning LLMs without a separate value function. GRPO evaluates sampled responses relative to a group of peers generated for the same prompt, using this relative evaluation to update the policy via a PPO-style clipped objective, regularized by a KL penalty for stability. For a prompt, a group of `G` responses are sampled, each receiving a scalar reward `r_i`. These rewards are then z-score normalized to obtain a response-level advantage `A_hat_i`. The policy is optimized using a clipped surrogate objective that incorporates these advantages.

#### 3.2. Reward Design: Implementing Selection and Variation

The core innovation of EVOL-RL lies in its reward function, designed to counteract diversity collapse by integrating both selection and variation signals.

1. **Validity Check:** Responses must provide a numeric final answer in a `\boxed{·}` format; invalid responses receive a baseline negative reward (`-1`).
2. **Majority (Selection):** A binary label `y_i ∈ {+1, -1}` is assigned based on whether a response's final answer matches the majority-voted answer among all valid responses. This acts as the "selection" signal, anchoring the learning process to correctness (or consensus-based correctness).
3. **Novelty (Variation):** This is the explicit exploration incentive.
 - **Reasoning Embedding:** Embeddings are computed for the *reasoning part* of each response (excluding the final answer) using a specialized embedding model (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Embedding). These embeddings capture semantic similarity between reasoning paths.
 - **Novelty Score (`u_i`):** For each response `o_i`, a novelty score is calculated as `u_i = 1 - (α * s_bar_i + (1 - α) * m_i)`.
 - `s_bar_i`: Mean semantic similarity of `o_i` to other responses *within the same group* (i.e., other majority-aligned solutions or other minority solutions). This measures intra-group conformity.
 - `m_i`: Maximum semantic similarity of `o_i` to *any* other response in the entire batch. This flags near-duplication across the entire sample.
 - `α` is a mixing coefficient (default 0.5).
 - This score penalizes both local redundancy (high `s_bar_i`) and global redundancy (high `m_i`), promoting diverse reasoning.
 - **Intra-group Normalization:** The raw novelty scores `u_i` are min-max normalized *separately* within the majority and minority groups (`˜u_i`). This ensures that novelty is judged relative to similar-status peers, preventing the large semantic gap between correct and incorrect solutions from obscuring nuanced variations within each group.
4. **Final Reward Mapping:** The majority label and normalized novelty score are mapped into non-overlapping reward bands:
 - If `y_i = +1` (majority-aligned): `r_i = 0.5 + 0.5 * ˜u_i ∈ [0.5, 1]`.
 - If `y_i = -1` (minority-aligned): `r_i = -1 + 0.5 * ˜u_i ∈ [-1, -0.5]`.
 - Invalid responses: `r_i = -1`.
 This structure ensures that any majority solution, regardless of its novelty, receives a higher reward than any minority solution, maintaining a strong pressure towards consensus-based correctness. Within these bands, higher novelty translates to higher reward.

#### 3.3. Supporting Mechanisms

EVOL-RL incorporates two additional mechanisms to reinforce the reward design:
1. **Asymmetric PPO Clipping:** The GRPO objective uses an asymmetric clipping range (`ϵ_high > ϵ_low`). This allows promising and novel solutions with high advantages to receive larger gradient updates, preventing premature clipping and preserving strong learning signals from rare, valuable discoveries.
2. **Token-level Entropy Regularizer:** An entropy regularization term (`L_ent`) is added to the total objective. This term encourages diversity during the initial token generation process, ensuring a continuous supply of varied solutions for the novelty-aware reward to act upon.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Underpinnings

The paper provides a formal theoretical analysis (Appendix D) to justify EVOL-RL's ability to avoid collapse. It formally proves that:
- A correctness-only objective (like TTRL) allows the optimal policy to collapse onto a single solution, as it is indifferent to how probability mass is distributed among correct answers.
- In contrast, under mild assumptions (e.g., a "piecewise-homogeneous similarity-gap structure" on correct reasoning paths), the novelty-augmented objective guarantees that the optimal policy must distribute probability mass across *multiple correct modes*. In a symmetric case (equal mode sizes), it leads to a uniform distribution over all correct trajectories, thereby maximizing policy entropy and preventing collapse. This provides a rigorous foundation for the method's stability and diversity-preserving properties.

This multi-faceted approach, combining a sophisticated reward design with architectural and theoretical support, enables EVOL-RL to maintain solution diversity, foster deeper exploration, and drive more robust self-improvement in LLMs without the need for external labels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of EVOL-RL, comparing it against the majority-only TTRL baseline, reveals consistent and substantial improvements across various benchmarks, model scales, and training conditions.

1. **Reversing Entropy Collapse:**
 - Figure 1 clearly illustrates the problem: TTRL's majority-only objective causes "entropy collapse," characterized by a decline in `pass@n` (for n>1), shortened reasoning responses, and a collapse in policy entropy.
 - EVOL-RL, in contrast, successfully averts these symptoms, maintaining policy entropy and reasoning length, and improving `pass@n` performance.
 - Training dynamics (Figure 3 and B.4) further demonstrate this: after an initial collapse common to both methods, EVOL-RL reaches an "evolving point" where its policy entropy, average response length, and out-of-domain accuracy begin a coordinated and sustained recovery, eventually stabilizing at a significantly higher plateau. TTRL remains trapped in the collapsed state.

2. **Enhanced Pass@1 and Pass@16 Performance:**
 - EVOL-RL consistently and substantially improves both `pass@1` (single-shot accuracy) and `pass@16` (multi-path reliability) across all experimental settings.
 - For example, training on label-free AIME24 lifts Qwen3-4B-Base AIME25 `pass@1` from TTRL's 4.6% to 16.4%, and `pass@16` from 18.5% to 37.9%. Similar gains are observed across MATH, AIME24, AIME25, and AMC benchmarks. This indicates that EVOL-RL not only helps the model find the correct answer more often but also encourages a wider variety of correct reasoning paths.

3. **Robustness Across Model Scales and Training Data Sizes:**
 - The benefits of EVOL-RL are consistent across both 4B and 8B Qwen3 models and generalize well regardless of the training dataset size (large MATH-TRAIN vs. smaller MATH-500 or AIME24). This suggests the method's underlying principle is fundamentally sound and scales effectively.
 - The approach is also effective on a different model architecture, OctoThinker-8B-Hybrid-Base, confirming its generalizability beyond a single model family (Appendix B.1).

4. **Strong Cross-Difficulty and Out-of-Domain Generalization:**
 - EVOL-RL demonstrates powerful generalization, learning abstract reasoning skills that transfer effectively even across different mathematical difficulty levels (e.g., training on MATH-500 yields performance on AIME24/25 comparable to training directly on AIME24). This implies the model learns fundamental reasoning skills rather than merely overfitting.
 - Critically, EVOL-RL significantly improves performance on **non-mathematical reasoning tasks** such as MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, and BBEH (Table 3). On GPQA, where TTRL consistently degrades `pass@16` performance compared to the base model, EVOL-RL reliably recovers and surpasses the base model by +7% to +15% over TTRL. This indicates that the diversity-preserving reward mechanism fosters a more generalizable reasoning ability transferable across domains.

5. **Ablation Study Insights (Table 2):**
 - The **novelty-aware reward function** is the most critical component, particularly on easier datasets (e.g., MATH-500). Removing it leads to the largest performance drops, especially on difficult out-of-domain tasks like AIME24/25. This prevents the model from "template lock-in" to repetitive reasoning paths.
 - The **entropy regularizer** and **asymmetric PPO clipping** serve as crucial enablers, especially on challenging datasets like AIME24. The entropy regularizer ensures a rich supply of varied reasoning paths, while asymmetric clipping preserves strong learning signals from rare, high-value "majority-and-novel" solutions.

6. **Enhancement for Supervised GRPO (RLVR):**
 - The exploration-enhancing components of EVOL-RL (asymmetric clipping, entropy regularizer, and novelty reward) also provide synergistic improvements when applied to a standard supervised GRPO baseline trained with a ground-truth verifier (RLVR). This demonstrates the broader utility of these mechanisms beyond label-free settings.

7. **Computational Overhead and Semantic Validity:**
 - The computational overhead from embedding-based novelty reward calculation is manageable and remains a small fraction of the total training time, even as responses become longer (Appendix B.5).
 - A case study (Appendix B.6) empirically validates that embedding similarity reliably captures meaningful differences in reasoning paths, clustering similar logical steps together.

In summary, EVOL-RL successfully diagnoses and addresses a critical limitation of existing label-free self-improvement methods by fostering diversity in reasoning, leading to substantially improved and more generalizable performance across various reasoning tasks and model scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Evolving Language Models without Labels: Majority Drives Selection, Novelty Promotes Variation" holds significant implications for the future of Large Language Models and artificial intelligence, both in theoretical understanding and practical application.

1. **Averting a Critical Failure Mode in Autonomous LLMs:** The most profound significance lies in its direct and effective solution to the "entropy collapse" problem. This phenomenon, where self-confirmation signals lead to a loss of solution diversity and reasoning capability, is a fundamental barrier to truly autonomous and continuous LLM evolution. EVOL-RL provides a robust mechanism to prevent this degradation, making long-term, label-free self-improvement a more viable reality.

2. **Pioneering a New Paradigm for Label-Free Learning:** By framing label-free learning through an evolutionary lens—explicitly balancing selection (majority vote for stability) with variation (novelty reward for exploration)—the paper introduces a powerful new conceptual framework. This approach moves beyond simply "rewarding certainty" to actively fostering a diverse "population" of reasoning strategies, aligning with principles from decades of evolutionary computation research. This fresh perspective could inspire new research directions in how LLMs learn and adapt.

3. **Enhancing Generalizable Reasoning:** The demonstrated improvements in out-of-domain generalization, particularly across different mathematical difficulties and onto non-mathematical benchmarks like GPQA and MMLU-Pro, suggest that EVOL-RL helps LLMs learn more fundamental and transferable reasoning skills. This is crucial for developing truly intelligent agents that can adapt to novel situations and tasks without retraining from scratch or extensive task-specific fine-tuning.

4. **Practical and Scalable Methodology:** EVOL-RL offers a relatively simple yet highly effective methodology. It operates entirely without ground-truth labels or external human judges, relying only on the model's own generated responses and an embedding model for novelty assessment. This significantly reduces the cost and logistical challenges associated with scaling LLM training and deployment, particularly in data-scarce or rapidly evolving domains. The manageable computational overhead of the novelty reward further underscores its practical utility.

5. **Theoretical Advancement:** The formal proof provided in Appendix D, which demonstrates that the novelty-augmented objective guarantees probability mass across multiple correct modes (unlike a correctness-only objective), offers strong theoretical grounding for the observed empirical success. This contributes to a deeper understanding of the optimization landscape in label-free RL for LLMs.

6. **Broader Impact on AI Research:** The principles articulated in EVOL-RL—the strategic integration of diversity-preserving mechanisms within reinforcement learning from self-generated data—could find applications beyond LLMs. Similar challenges exist in other domains of self-supervised or self-improving AI, where degenerative feedback loops can lead to brittle and poorly generalizing models.

In conclusion, EVOL-RL represents a significant step forward in the quest for intelligent systems that can learn and evolve autonomously in complex, unlabeled environments. By embracing the power of variation alongside selection, it brings LLMs closer to exhibiting the adaptable and robust intelligence seen in natural systems, paving the way for more resilient, general-purpose AI.
