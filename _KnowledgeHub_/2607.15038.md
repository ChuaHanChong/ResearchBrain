# Video = World + Event Stream

> [!summary] Summary
> Wan-Streamer v0.3 introduces a "video = world + event stream" framework for general pretraining over ordinary video, enabling real-time interactive agents to perform open-vocabulary behaviors. This approach maintains a low model-side latency of approximately 200 ms for 640x368 video at 25 FPS while expanding expressive range.

## Problem

- Prior real-time interactive AI systems often relied on cascaded modular components for perception, response, and synthesis, leading to potential latency and synchronization issues.
- Previous versions of Wan-Streamer focused on training for specific interaction tasks, limiting the generalizability and transferability of learned competencies.
- Interactive agents typically possessed a limited, fixed action vocabulary, hindering the ability to perform nuanced, free-form behaviors alongside speech.

## Method

- Reframes video as a ==persistent world== context and a ==time-varying event stream== to establish a general pretraining task on large-scale, diverse video corpora.
- Employs an ==autoregressive Transformer== model to predict the next event unit, explicitly conditioning on the world context and causal history, coupling speech, motion, and appearance.
- Integrates ==free-form behavior records== as parenthesized directives within the language stream, enabling open-vocabulary actions realized through joint ==audio-video latent generation==.

## Results

- Preserves the established real-time operating point with approximately **200 ms** model-side latency and **550 ms** total interaction latency for **640x368** video at **25 FPS**.
- Demonstrates the agent performing open-vocabulary behaviors in real-time concurrently with speaking, including facial expressions, posture changes, and object interactions.
- Generated actions are coherent, synchronized with speech, and grounded in the configured scene, consistent with the agent's identity and appearance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing video into a persistent "world" and a dynamic "event stream" provides a scalable, general-purpose pretraining objective for real-time AI systems.
> - Learning world-event dynamics from diverse video enables the acquisition of broad underlying competence transferable to various real-time tasks like embodied control and interaction.
> - Achieving expanded agent expressiveness through open-vocabulary actions is possible without compromising critical low-latency and high-resolution real-time performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of real-time interactive artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of multimodal foundation models for audio-visual interaction and embodied agents. Prior work in this area often involved cascaded modular systems, where perception, response generation, and audio-visual synthesis were handled by separate components, potentially leading to increased latency and synchronization issues. Wan-Streamer v0.1 and v0.2 established an end-to-end, native-streaming formulation for real-time full-duplex audio-visual interaction, where all modalities (text, audio, video) are processed on a single causal timeline by a unified Transformer model. This approach aimed to learn synchronized behaviors directly rather than assembling them.

Wan-Streamer v0.3 evolves this line of research by proposing a more general conceptual framework: "any video can be read as a world plus an event stream." This reframing positions the work within the growing field of "world models" [4–7], which seek to build AI systems capable of understanding and predicting the dynamics of complex environments. It also relates to embodied AI and robot control [8–10], where agents need to interact with and manipulate objects within a defined context.

The paper distinguishes itself from previous versions by shifting the core objective from training on interaction-specific data to a general-purpose pretraining task over large amounts of ordinary video. This move aligns with the trend of developing broadly competent foundation models that can be specialized for various downstream tasks, analogous to large language models being adapted for diverse applications. The expansion of the agent's expressive range to include open-vocabulary behavior also addresses a limitation often found in interactive agents constrained by a fixed action vocabulary. The work maintains a focus on low-latency, high-fidelity real-time interaction, a challenge also explored by other systems in real-time voice and avatar generation [11–15].

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Wan-Streamer v0.3 is to reframe the native-streaming interaction model under a single organizing view: "a video is a world plus an event stream." This reframing is motivated by the desire to establish a general-purpose pretraining task that can yield a broader and more transferable underlying competence for real-time AI systems.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Establishing a General Pretraining Objective:** Previous versions of Wan-Streamer focused on a single target application (an agent that talks and reacts). Wan-Streamer v0.3 aims to identify the underlying, more general competence by decomposing video into a "world" (persistent context) and an "event stream" (time-varying changes). This decomposition allows for a pretraining task that generalizes across diverse video data, teaching the model how scenes plausibly evolve and respond in real-time.
- **Enhancing Scalability and Transferability:** By defining a pretraining task over large amounts of ordinary video (as essentially all natural video exemplifies a world with an event stream), the approach is designed to be highly scalable. The competence acquired through this pretraining is intended to be applicable to a broad family of real-time downstream tasks, including world-model control, real-time first-person interaction, embodied manipulation, and audio-visual interaction.
- **Expanding Agent Expressive Range:** For the specific downstream task of real-time full-duplex audio-visual interaction, a key objective is to widen the agent's expressive capabilities. While v0.1 and v0.2 focused on speech and visible listening behavior, v0.3 aims to enable the agent to perform "free-form behavior" or "open-vocabulary actions" alongside speech. This allows for more nuanced and situated interactions where the agent can react physically to its environment and conversational context.
- **Preserving Performance Metrics:** A significant objective is to achieve this expanded functionality and generalizability while preserving the low-latency and high-resolution operating point established by Wan-Streamer v0.2. This includes maintaining 640x368 video at 25 FPS, a 160 ms streaming unit, approximately 200 ms model-side response latency, and approximately 550 ms total interaction latency under a 350 ms bidirectional network budget. The goal is to demonstrate that the new conceptual framework and expanded capabilities do not introduce new latency-critical paths or compromise the real-time performance.
- **Developing a VLA-like Multimodal Understanding:** The work aims to develop a multimodal understanding process that functionally maps streaming multimodal user input (text, audio, video) to language-form speech and behavior actions, analogous to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These language-form outputs then condition synchronized audio-video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Wan-Streamer v0.3's methodology is centered on its "world + event stream" decomposition and a subsequent pretraining and specialization approach.

#### 3.1. World-Event Decomposition Formalization

The core conceptual shift defines any video as a "persistent world" (W) and a "time-varying event stream" (e).
- **World (W):** This represents the stable context over a video clip or interaction. It is formalized as a flexible structured record `W = {w_j}_j∈S(W)`, where the schema `S(W)` adapts to the clip or task. Common fields include visual context (e.g., medium, style, environment, layout), acoustic context (ambient sound, voice characteristics), and character records (identity, appearance, persona). This information is tokenized and prefilled once before streaming begins.
- **Event (e_k):** This encompasses all changes occurring over time within the defined world. An event is a time-localized record `e_k = (τ_k, c_k, d_k)`, where `τ_k` is its active interval, `d_k` is a free-form description of the change, and `c_k` links the event to a specific character or marks it as character-independent. Descriptions can cover subject behavior, camera motion, environmental changes, speech, or physical sounds.

This decomposition is not a new architectural component but rather a reinterpretation of the interleaved causal sequence already present in v0.1/v0.2, explicitly naming durable conditioning as `W` and time-localized change as `e_k`.

#### 3.2. Streaming World-Event Factorization

The model utilizes an autoregressive factorization, inherited from v0.1, to predict the next response unit. This is expressed as `p_θ(e_1:K | W, x_1:K) = Π_k=1^K p_θ(e_k | W, x_≤k, e_<k)`. Here, `K` is the number of streaming units, `x_≤k` denotes the incoming input driving the stream (observed past for pretraining, user observations for interaction), and `e_k` is the agent's response unit. The key distinction from prior versions is that `W` (the persistent world context) is now an explicit, first-class conditioning object, provided once at inference, guiding the online production of the event stream.

Discrete components of `e_k` (such as language and behavior directives) are optimized via next-token prediction. Continuous audio and video latents are generated using conditional flow matching. This unified approach ensures that speech, motion, appearance, and scene evolution are denoised as a single coupled response, which is then committed back into the causal history for subsequent unit generation.

#### 3.3. Pretraining and Downstream Transfer

- **Pretraining Task:** The defined factorization (Equation 3) forms a general-purpose pretraining task. The model is trained on large-scale, diverse video corpora. For each video clip, an inferred `world` context and time-aligned `events` are extracted (e.g., as shown in Figure 1). The objective is to predict the next event (`e_k`) and its corresponding audio-visual realization based on the `world` and the causal history (`x_≤k, e_<k`). This process teaches the model world-event dynamics – how events plausibly unfold within a given context.
- **Downstream Specialization:** The pretrained world-event competence can be specialized for various tasks by configuring the `world` context and the input `x` that drives the stream.
 - **Instantiation for Real-time Audio-Visual Interaction:** For the specific task of real-time full-duplex audio-visual interaction, the persistent `world` context is instantiated with: scene setting (environment, layout), character setting (identity, appearance, persona), ambient-sound setting, and voice timbre. The user's streaming text, audio, and video serve as the input `x_≤k` that drives the agent's event stream.
 - **Agent Event Stream:** The agent's response (`e_k`) now explicitly interleaves spoken words with "free-form behavior records." This is implemented using a role-play chat format where behavior directives are enclosed in parentheses within the language stream (e.g., "(takes a sip of water) Thanks for the reminder."). These parenthesized directives can describe any behavior expressible in natural language, providing an open-vocabulary action space.
 - **Multimodal Understanding:** Functionally, the model operates in a vision-language-action (VLA)-like manner. Conditioned on the `world`, it continuously maps streaming multimodal user input to language-form speech and behavior actions. These predicted token streams then condition the joint audio-video latent generation. The behavior directives are short language tokens, designed to add minimal overhead to the latency-critical path.

#### 3.4. Operating Point Preservation

The modeling contract and serving topology (Thinker + Ulysses context-parallel performer) are inherited unchanged from v0.2. This allows v0.3 to preserve the established operating point: 640x368 video at 25 FPS, a 160 ms streaming unit, approximately 200 ms model-side response latency, and approximately 550 ms total interaction latency, assuming a 350 ms bidirectional network budget.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Wan-Streamer v0.3 focuses on its ability to expand the agent's expressive capabilities while maintaining the established low-latency, high-resolution performance.

#### 4.1. Latency and Runtime Performance

The paper reports that Wan-Streamer v0.3 successfully preserves the latency and runtime protocol inherited from v0.2.
- **Model-side latency:** Approximately 200 ms. This is measured from the moment a 160 ms user streaming unit becomes available to the 'thinker' module until the corresponding audio-video response unit is decoded and ready for emission.
- **Total interaction latency:** Approximately 550 ms. This figure includes the model-side latency plus an assumed 350 ms bidirectional network budget.
- **Video output:** The model continues to produce video at 640x368 resolution at 25 frames per second (FPS).

These results indicate that the conceptual reframing and the introduction of free-form behavior did not introduce additional computational overhead that would compromise the real-time performance metrics of the previous version.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Behavior Observations

Qualitative observations from generated 640x368 conversations demonstrate the expanded capabilities of Wan-Streamer v0.3.
- **Open-vocabulary behavior:** The agent is observed to perform open-vocabulary behaviors in real-time concurrently with speaking.
- **Behavior realization:** Directives specified in the language stream (e.g., parenthesized descriptions) are realized as coherent visual and acoustic actions. This includes:
 - Facial expressions.
 - Posture changes.
 - Responses to environmental sounds.
 - Interactions with nearby objects within the virtual scene.
- **Synchronization and grounding:** These generated actions remain synchronized with the agent's speech. They are also reported to be grounded in the configured scene and consistent with the agent's identity and appearance, suggesting the model leverages the `world` context effectively.
- **Idle and listening behavior:** When no explicit behavior events are specified, the agent maintains stable idle and listening behaviors, indicating a continuous and natural presence.

The qualitative findings suggest that Wan-Streamer v0.3 successfully expands the agent's expressive range beyond mere speech and implicit listening cues, enabling it to perform a broader spectrum of explicit actions described in natural language, while maintaining the real-time performance characteristics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Wan-Streamer v0.3 carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence.

#### 5.1. Generalization and Transfer Learning

The reframing of video as a "world + event stream" provides a general-purpose pretraining objective. This represents a shift from task-specific training to learning fundamental world dynamics. This approach is significant because it aims to build a broadly competent foundation model capable of understanding how environments and subjects plausibly evolve and respond over time. Such a competence is transferable across a variety of real-time tasks, including embodied manipulation, interactive world modeling, and various forms of human-agent interaction, as opposed to models trained for a singular application. This could accelerate the development of AI systems for diverse domains without requiring extensive re-training for each new task.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Agent Expressiveness and Interaction Fidelity

The introduction of "open-vocabulary free-form behavior" significantly enhances the expressive capabilities of interactive agents. By allowing agents to perform actions described in natural language, the system moves beyond a limited, predefined set of gestures or movements. This enables more nuanced, context-aware, and human-like interactions. Agents can now respond with a broader range of physical behaviors—such as picking up objects, changing posture, or expressing specific emotions—that are synchronized with their speech and grounded in their virtual environment. This can lead to more engaging, immersive, and effective human-agent communication.

#### 5.3. Maintenance of Real-time Performance

A key contribution of Wan-Streamer v0.3 is achieving these expanded capabilities without compromising the critical low-latency and high-resolution performance established in previous versions. Maintaining approximately 200 ms model-side latency for 640x368 video at 25 FPS is crucial for natural, full-duplex interaction. The demonstration that the new conceptual framework and the integration of behavior directives do not introduce new latency-critical paths or increase existing latency validates the efficiency of the underlying architecture and the unified modeling approach. This is important for practical deployment in real-time interactive applications.

#### 5.4. Unified Multimodal Modeling Paradigm

The work reinforces the utility of an end-to-end, native-streaming multimodal Transformer architecture. By integrating perception (vision, language, audio), response generation (speech, behavior actions), and synchronized audio-video synthesis into a single causal timeline, the system learns coherent, coupled behaviors. The "VLA-like" multimodal understanding process, mapping streaming user input to language-form actions that condition joint audio-video generation, exemplifies a unified approach to multimodal intelligence. This paradigm could serve as a model for future developments in complex interactive AI systems.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Future Research

The general "world + event stream" decomposition and the pretraining task lay a foundation for systematic adaptation and evaluation in other real-time domains beyond the current audio-visual interaction specialization. This framework could facilitate the development of more advanced embodied AI agents that learn to navigate, manipulate, and interact within complex virtual or physical environments based on broad world knowledge.
