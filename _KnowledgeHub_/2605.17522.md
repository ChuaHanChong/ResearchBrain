# RoboFlow4D: A Lightweight Flow World Model Toward Real-Time Flow-Guided Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboFlow4D introduces a lightweight, end-to-end flow world model that directly predicts multi-frame 3D flows from RGB images and textual instructions, enabling real-time, resource-efficient robotic manipulation with enhanced success rates.

## Problem

- Traditional 2D flow-based robotic planning methods lack critical 3D spatial information, leading to physically infeasible motions or collisions in 3D environments.
- Existing approaches for generating 3D trajectories often rely on modular pipelines that stack multiple heavy expert models (e.g., video generation, depth estimation), resulting in substantial computational overhead and high latency impractical for real-time robot deployment.
- Robust robotic manipulation requires explicit pre-execution 3D planning, but current methods struggle with balancing spatial awareness and computational efficiency.

## Method

- Developed **RoboFlow4D**, a lightweight, end-to-end model that directly predicts multi-frame 3D flows in 4D spacetime (3D space + time) from historical RGB images, optional 2D query points, and textual task instructions.
- Incorporated a 3D Perceiver module to infuse 3D spatial awareness by encoding 3D geometry from 2D visual tokens and aligning it with features from a frozen 3D foundation model (VGGT).
- Employed a diffusion-based FlowDiT module with spatiotemporal cross-attention to predict future 3D trajectories, conditioned on a multimodal input including image, text, and 3D-aware latent embeddings.
- Implemented a "slow-fast" dual-system closed-loop control architecture where RoboFlow4D provides low-frequency, goal-oriented 4D plans that guide a high-frequency, lightweight action policy.

## Results

- RoboFlow4D improved the average success rates of lightweight policies (DP, DiT) on the LIBERO simulation benchmark by **4.0%** to **8.2%** and achieved average gains of up to **11.0%** on ManiSkill3 tasks.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated that RoboFlow4D increased average success rates by **12.5%** and reduced average task completion times by **1.4** seconds compared to baselines.
- The model achieves real-time 4D motion planning, predicting a complete flow plan within **0.68** seconds and an action chunk within **0.20** seconds, significantly outperforming minute-level latencies of modular video-generation-based approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly predicting multi-frame 3D flows from 2D observations and text in an end-to-end manner can provide robust motion guidance efficiently, bypassing the need for computationally expensive modular pipelines.
> - Infusing 3D spatial awareness into a 2D-input model through a dedicated 3D Perceiver and a 3D alignment loss is crucial for accurate 4D flow prediction.
> - A closed-loop "slow-fast" control system, where a lightweight 4D planner guides a high-frequency action policy, enables real-time, iterative robotic manipulation with adaptive replanning capabilities to correct errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressing toward the development of embodied generalist agents capable of planning and acting in complex 3D environments. Recent learning-based approaches often follow an "observation → action" paradigm, aiming to generate robot actions directly from visual observations and textual instructions. While effective for various skills like grasping and pushing, this paradigm can encounter unexpected failures. Incorporating explicit pre-execution planning is identified as crucial for achieving robust robotic manipulation.

Within this "observation → planning → action" framework, various methods have been explored. Early approaches often focused on 2D flow-based planning, predicting pixel-level trajectories in image space (e.g., Track2Act, RoboTAP). However, guiding robot manipulation in the 3D world with 2D flows is inherently ill-posed, as they lack critical spatial information such as depth and geometry, potentially leading to collisions or physically infeasible motions.

To address the limitations of 2D flows, research has shifted toward 3D spatially-aware action modeling. Some methods augment 2D image trajectories with depth estimations to form pseudo-3D flows (e.g., 3DFlowAction). Others explicitly aim to generate genuine 3D trajectories. The latter often rely on modular pipelines, which typically involve stacking multiple expert modules: synthesizing future videos using video generation experts, estimating depth with video depth experts, and predicting 3D motion through point tracking and grounding modules (e.g., Li et al., 2025a; Dharmarajan et al., 2025). While these modular approaches can be effective, they introduce substantial computational and memory overhead, making them impractical for real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms. Although some prior work like PointWorld (Huang et al., 2026) has explored end-to-end 3D flow prediction, it typically uses RGB-D inputs and predicts fixed temporal-window flows.

RoboFlow4D positions itself within this landscape by addressing the efficiency and real-time performance limitations of existing 3D flow-based planning. It proposes a lightweight, end-to-end flow world model that directly predicts multi-frame 3D flows from standard RGB images and textual instructions, operating in "4D spacetime" (3D space + time). This approach aims to provide robust 3D motion guidance without the computational burden of modular pipelines, fostering real-time and resource-efficient robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RoboFlow4D stems from the identified challenges in current flow-guided robotic manipulation, particularly the computational inefficiency and lack of real-time performance in 3D planning. The paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Lightweight, End-to-End Flow World Model**: The primary objective is to create RoboFlow4D, a unified model that estimates temporal motion in 3D physical space (4D spacetime) directly from visual observations (RGB images) and textual instructions. This aims to overcome the high computational overhead associated with modular pipelines that stack multiple submodels for tasks like video generation, depth estimation, and 3D point tracking.

2. **Enable Real-Time and Resource-Efficient Manipulation**: A central goal is to facilitate practical deployment on robots by reducing planning latency and the overall model scale. By designing RoboFlow4D as an end-to-end framework, the authors aim to achieve significant inference efficiency gains compared to resource-intensive modular setups.

3. **Provide a Plug-and-Play 3D Flow-Guided Planning Interface**: RoboFlow4D is intended to serve as an efficient, lightweight guiding module that can be seamlessly integrated with general action policies. This allows for explicit flow-based planning to modulate and improve action generation without requiring extensive modifications to existing policy architectures.

4. **Implement an Efficient Closed-Loop Robotic Manipulation Framework**: The research aims to establish a robust observation-planning-execution closed loop. This involves a "slow-fast" dual-system architecture where RoboFlow4D acts as a low-frequency, goal-oriented planner that adaptively adjusts its planning horizon, while a lightweight action policy operates as a high-frequency executor. This coordination is designed to reduce overall latency and improve manipulation accuracy.

By achieving these objectives, the authors seek to advance the state-of-the-art in flow-guided planning for embodied intelligence, making 3D trajectory generation more accessible, efficient, and applicable for real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboFlow4D framework is structured into three main components: RoboFlow4D itself, Flow-Conditioned Policy Learning, and Closed-Loop Control.

#### 3.1. RoboFlow4D Module

RoboFlow4D is designed as an end-to-end pipeline for predicting multi-frame 3D flows. It processes a sequence of historical RGB images, optional 2D query points, and a textual task instruction.

- **Multimodal Feature Extraction**:
 - **Vision Encoder**: Utilizes DINO v2 to extract local patch tokens and SigLip for global image tokens from the image sequence. Global tokens are mean-pooled into a single global embedding, while local tokens are aggregated into context tokens using a multi-head self-attention (MHA) module with learnable queries.
 - **Text Encoder**: The text encoder from SigLip encodes the task instruction into a text token.
 - **Point Encoder (Optional)**: If 2D query points are provided, an MLP projects them into point tokens. These are then aggregated into a compact latent embedding using an MHA with a learnable query token.

- **3D Perceiver**: To imbue RoboFlow4D with 3D spatial awareness from 2D observations, a 3D Perceiver module is introduced. This module comprises learnable 3D queries and a Resampler, which employs stacked cross-attention and feed-forward networks to encode 3D geometry from the context tokens. The Resampler's output is projected by an MLP into a 3D-aware condition. 3D knowledge is distilled into this condition by applying an alignment loss between the projected 3D condition and mean-pooled features from a frozen 3D foundation model (VGGT).

- **FlowDiT (Diffusion-based Transformer)**: A diffusion-based FlowDiT module is developed to predict future flows. It consists of N stacked Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks and an MLP Projector. A multimodal condition is constructed by concatenating the global image embedding, 3D-aware condition, point latent embedding, and text token, augmented with timestep information. This multimodal condition, along with noisy flows, is fed into the DiT blocks for conditioned denoising. A spatiotemporal cross-attention mechanism replaces the original MHA within each DiT block to enhance perceptual capabilities. The denoised flows are then passed through the Projector to generate the final output: multi-frame 3D trajectories of keypoints ($F \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{kp} \times K \times 3}$).

#### 3.2. Flow-Conditioned Policy Learning

The predicted multi-frame 3D flow plan from RoboFlow4D serves as an explicit motion guidance signal for a downstream action policy.

- **Flow Encoding**: An MLP first processes the 3D flow plan to extract keypoint features. These features are then aggregated across keypoints and frames using attention pooling to yield a global flow condition.
- **Action Policy Input**: This global flow condition is concatenated with a base condition, which is derived from current robot states (visual-language observations and proprioception), to form the final condition for the action policy.
- **Policy Architectures**: RoboFlow4D is designed to be plug-and-play and is frozen during policy learning. The authors validate its general benefits across different lightweight action policies, specifically Diffusion Policy (DP) and Diffusion Transformer Policy (DiT).

#### 3.3. Closed-Loop Control

The framework implements a closed-loop control system to enable robust and iterative robotic manipulation, mitigating errors inherent in open-loop systems.

- **Slow-Fast Dual-System Architecture**: RoboFlow4D and the action policy operate in a dual-frequency scheme. RoboFlow4D functions as a low-frequency, goal-oriented flow planner. In a single planning step, it predicts a robot trajectory for an entire atomic task, whose time horizon can extend beyond that of a single action chunk. The action policy, as a high-frequency executor, then rolls out multiple action chunks, guided by the single flow plan provided by RoboFlow4D.
- **Iterative Process**: This observation-planning-execution loop continues until the manipulation task is completed. New observations are fed back to RoboFlow4D after each atomic task is finalized, allowing for dynamic replanning and adaptation.

#### 3.4. Data Generation and Training Objective

- **Data Sources**: Training data is collected from both real-world (Droid dataset) and simulated environments (LIBERO, ManiSkill).
- **3D Flow Supervision**: To equip RoboFlow4D with 3D geometric awareness, 3D flow supervision is generated.
 - **Gripper Grounding and Tracking**: Grounded-SAM2 is used to segment the robot gripper in RGB observations. SpatialTrackerV2 then tracks $n_{kp}$ corresponding 3D points on the gripper across the episode. The raw point trajectories undergo a three-stage filtering process to remove static, outlier, or implausibly large displacement points.
 - **Atomic Task Decomposition**: Manipulation videos are segmented into sequential "atomic tasks" based on stable changes in the gripper's open/close signal. This provides physically meaningful interaction phases.
 - **Goal-Oriented Flow Resampling**: For each atomic task segment, $K$ keyframes are non-uniformly resampled using a monotone time-warping rule ($\gamma > 1$). This densifies samples near the atomic task's end-goal, forming the ground-truth 3D flow trajectories ($x_0$) for supervision.
- **Training Objective**: RoboFlow4D is trained as a conditional denoising diffusion model using v-prediction parameterization. The overall objective function is a combination of three losses:
 - **Diffusion Denoising Loss ($L_{diff}$)**: A visibility-weighted mean-squared error term that guides the model to recover plausible trajectories from noisy inputs.
 - **Alignment Loss ($L_{align}$)**: Strengthens 2D-to-3D perception by aligning the 3D-aware condition from RoboFlow4D with features extracted from a frozen VGGT model.
 - **Smooth Policy Loss ($L_{smooth}$)**: A robust Charbonnier penalty that regularizes the denoised flows by penalizing second-order temporal differences, ensuring temporal consistency.
 - **Classifier-Free Conditioning**: Classifier-free conditioning dropout is applied during training, and classifier-free guidance is used during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RoboFlow4D was conducted in both simulation and real-world environments, focusing on manipulation success rates, computational efficiency, and qualitative performance.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

Experiments were performed on two benchmarks: LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023), evaluating spatial, object, and goal generalization, and ManiSkill3 (Tao et al., 2024), focusing on tasks like PushCube, PickCube, and StackCube. RoboFlow4D was integrated with two lightweight policies, Diffusion Policy (DP) and Diffusion Transformer Policy (DiT), and compared against various state-of-the-art Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models and imitation learning baselines.

- **LIBERO Results**: RoboFlow4D consistently improved the success rates of lightweight policies. For DP, improvements of 8.2% on Spatial, 8.0% on Long, and 6.2% on average were observed. When integrated with RoboFlow4D, DP achieved an average success rate of 85.1%. Similarly, DiT saw improvements of 6.0% on Spatial, 6.4% on Long, and 4.0% on average, reaching an average success rate of 87.7%. These results indicate that RoboFlow4D enables efficient controllers to achieve performance comparable to or surpassing many larger VLA baselines, validating the benefits of explicit 4D motion priors.

- **ManiSkill3 Results**: Under the more challenging single third-view setting, RoboFlow4D again demonstrated consistent gains. DP with RoboFlow4D achieved improvements of 10.0% on PushCube, 10.0% on PickCube, and 9.0% on StackCube, leading to an average gain of 9.7% (22.0% average success rate). DiT with RoboFlow4D showed improvements of at least 10.0% across all tasks, resulting in an average gain of 11.0% (23.7% average success rate). These findings support the efficacy of 3D flow plan guidance in scenarios with limited spatial cues.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Modular Ablation**: This study assessed the contribution of different components of RoboFlow4D by measuring 3D $\ell_2$ error on the LIBERO evaluation set. RoboFlow4D achieved the lowest error (0.0142). Removing the context token, query points, or 3D alignment each led to an increase in error (0.0152, 0.0158, and 0.0160, respectively). This indicates that all designed modules contribute to the accuracy of 4D prediction.

- **Dual-System Frequency Ablation**: The stability and robustness of the slow-fast closed-loop control framework were evaluated by varying the update frequency ratio (r) between the fast policy and slow system (r = 4, 2, 1). The success rates for both DP and DiT controllers remained steady across these different ratios, suggesting that the framework is robust to various update schedules.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Real-world validation was conducted using a 6-DoF ROKAE robotic arm with a JODELL gripper, observed by two RealSense D435 cameras. The evaluation included four manipulation tasks: Pick-and-Place, Stack, Assemble, and Drawer, with 50 teleoperated demonstrations per task.

- **Performance and Efficiency**: RoboFlow4D consistently improved success rates and reduced task completion times compared to baselines ($\pi_0$, $\pi_0$-Fast) and standalone policies (DP, DiT). For example, DP with RoboFlow4D achieved a 12.5% higher average success rate and reduced average completion time by 1.4 seconds. DiT with RoboFlow4D yielded an average success rate of 43.8% and an average completion time of 38.3 seconds, outperforming the $\pi_0$ baseline (41.3% success rate, 40.7 seconds completion time). These improvements are attributed to the goal-oriented flow plan guidance providing more efficient and accurate trajectories, reducing unnecessary robot motions caused by real-world uncertainty.

- **Qualitative Evaluation**: Visualizations demonstrated RoboFlow4D's ability to predict goal-oriented multi-frame 3D flow plans for sequential atomic tasks. Furthermore, in the closed-loop system, RoboFlow4D exhibited corrective replanning capabilities; when manipulation errors occurred (e.g., mis-grasping), it re-predicted a corrective flow plan to guide the gripper to recover and successfully complete the task.

- **Latency and Efficiency**: The paper highlights a significant advantage in planning latency. While modular video-generation-based approaches (e.g., Dream2Flow, NovaFlow) report minute-level latencies (3-11 minutes, ~2 minutes) due to their reliance on heavyweight (>1 billion parameter) video generation experts and multi-stage lifting pipelines, RoboFlow4D, with 0.76 billion parameters, directly predicts the 4D motion prior in a single forward pass within 1 second (0.68 seconds for flow plan, 0.20 seconds for policy action chunk). This enables efficient closed-loop manipulation in real-time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboFlow4D introduces a lightweight, end-to-end 4D world model that directly predicts goal-oriented multi-frame 3D flows from 2D observations and textual instructions. This represents a methodological advancement by providing a 3D planning interface that circumvents the high computational costs and latency associated with modular pipelines in prior 3D flow prediction methods.

The research establishes a novel paradigm for low-cost 3D flow-conditioned policy learning, demonstrated by its seamless integration with lightweight action policies like Diffusion Policy and Diffusion Transformer Policy. The proposed observation-planning-execution closed loop, featuring slow-fast collaboration between low-frequency planning and high-frequency control, enhances both efficiency and accuracy in robotic manipulation tasks.

The extensive empirical validation in both simulated environments (LIBERO and ManiSkill3) and real-world settings consistently shows that RoboFlow4D improves manipulation success rates and computational efficiency. The ability to predict 3D motion priors in real-time, coupled with its corrective replanning capabilities, indicates progress toward more robust and adaptable robotic systems.

**Potential Impact (as stated by the authors):**
The primary potential impact of RoboFlow4D is to increase manipulation success rates and reduce the deployment cost of reliable robotic systems across diverse industrial and service scenarios. Its lightweight and efficient design could make advanced robotic manipulation more accessible for real-world applications.

**Potential Risks and Mitigation:**
The authors acknowledge several potential risks associated with the deployment of such systems: misuse in unsafe automation, biased or brittle behaviors under distribution shifts, and harm caused by prediction errors in dynamic environments. To mitigate these risks, they suggest that future deployments should incorporate robust safety constraints, uncertainty-aware planning mechanisms with fallback behaviors, and rigorous evaluation across diverse scenes prior to any real-world operational use.
