# Learning What to Say to Your VLA: Mostly Harmless Vision Language Action Model Steering

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework developed by Carnegie Mellon University enhances the reliability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by learning a language feedback policy that steers frozen VLAs through their language input. This system improves task success rates (e.g., 12.7 percentage points in simulation) and robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios, concurrently controlling the false positive rate of harmful steering interventions.

## Problem

- VLA models exhibit brittle and unpredictable language-to-action mapping, where minor semantic variations in instructions can lead to erroneous robot behaviors.
- Users struggle to intuit how to rephrase instructions to achieve desired behaviors, and certain VLA capabilities may not be consistently elicitable through simple prompting.
- Existing methods for VLA robustness often involve fine-tuning the VLA policy or relying on external feedback, which can be computationally expensive or modify core capabilities.

## Method

- A framework is developed to learn a language feedback policy (LFP) that steers a frozen pre-trained **VLA** purely through its language input, without modifying the base VLA.
- The solution integrates narrated fine-tuning of a smaller VLM to create a prior for plausible language sequences, followed by interactive language search using an LLM to perturb these sequences and identify beneficial steering.
- A "conformalized improvement head" is trained to predict when language steering will be beneficial and, critically, to abstain from intervening when harm is predicted, providing statistical guarantees on the false positive rate of harmful steering.

## Results

- The language feedback policies demonstrated superior success rates (e.g., π_RFT improved performance by **12.7** percentage points in the training environment over the base VLA) and robustness across various in-distribution and out-of-distribution conditions in simulation and hardware.
- Conformalization significantly reduced the false positive rate of harmful steering from **38.92%** to **9.31%** in simulation and from **61.11%** to **2.22%** in hardware, effectively controlling the rate of undesirable interventions.
- The closed-loop language feedback approach proved more sample-efficient than direct VLA fine-tuning (e.g., comparable performance with one-fifth of the data) and enabled dynamic recovery from mid-task environmental perturbations, outperforming open-loop prompt search.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective steering of frozen VLAs can be achieved by learning a closed-loop language feedback policy that adapts to evolving scene states and corrects robot behavior.
> - Leveraging narrated video data to establish a prior for language sequences and then performing interactive search over trajectory-level language perturbations makes the vast language search space tractable.
> - Conformal prediction can be applied to language steering to provide principled statistical guarantees of harmlessness, allowing the system to refuse interventions that are likely to degrade performance, especially in out-of-distribution settings.
