# Unified modality separation: A vision-language framework for unsupervised domain adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> The UniMoS++ framework from UESTC and Tongji University improves unsupervised domain adaptation for Vision-Language Models by explicitly separating vision-associated and language-associated components, processing them in a unified, modality-aware manner. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance across UDA, MSDA, ADA, and SFADA tasks, while exhibiting superior computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face a "modality gap" where vision and text features, despite contrastive pre-training, are not perfectly aligned, hindering optimal transfer learning.
- Existing VLM-based UDA methods primarily focus on transferring modality-invariant knowledge, often neglecting valuable modality-specific information.
- Manually tuning optimal ensemble weights for combining multimodal outputs is a practical limitation in diverse domain adaptation scenarios.

## Method

- UniMoS++ disentangles VLM features into ==Vision-Associated Components (VAC)== and ==Language-Associated Components (LAC)== using dual separation branches and an orthogonal regularization loss.
- A ==Modality Discrepancy Metric (MDI)== categorizes target data at an instance level (Modality-Invariant, Modality-Specific, Uncertain) to guide tailored adaptation strategies and active learning.
- A ==Modality-adaptive Ensemble (MaE)== automatically determines optimal ensemble weights for VAC and LAC outputs at test time, complemented by modality-aware training that leverages deep clustering and a modality discriminator.

## Results

- UniMoS++ achieved new state-of-the-art on DomainNet for UDA (**63.8%** average accuracy) and on DomainNet (**58.6%** avg) and OfficeHome (**78.7%** avg) for MSDA.
- It established new state-of-the-art for Active Domain Adaptation (ADA) on OfficeHome, reaching **82.4%** with **5%** annotation budget (outperforming prior SOTA by over **4%**) and **85.5%** with **10%** budget.
- The framework demonstrated significantly higher computational efficiency, with **9x** faster training, **20x** fewer parameters (**2.65M** vs. **50.71M**), and **1992** throughput compared to previous state-of-the-art methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bypassing, rather than directly bridging or reducing, the modality gap by explicitly separating modality-specific and modality-invariant knowledge can lead to superior VLM-based domain adaptation.
> - Instance-level characterization of data based on cross-modal consistency provides a more effective way to select informative samples for active learning and tailor adaptation strategies.
> - Achieving state-of-the-art performance in VLM-based domain adaptation is possible with highly efficient, lightweight learnable modules and frozen VLM encoders, making these methods practical for resource-constrained environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) is a critical subfield of transfer learning aimed at enabling models trained on a labeled source domain to perform effectively on a related but unlabeled target domain, where data distributions differ. Traditional UDA methods primarily focus on single-modality vision tasks, employing techniques like minimizing distribution discrepancies (e.g., MMD, MDD) or adversarial learning to extract domain-invariant features.

With the advent of large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, the UDA landscape has evolved significantly. VLMs, trained on vast image-text pairs, possess rich semantic knowledge and exhibit impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities, making them highly attractive for mitigating domain shifts. Pioneering VLM-based UDA approaches often involve prompt tuning (adapting text embeddings) or fine-tuning VLM parameters to align visual and textual representations with the target domain.

However, a key challenge in VLM-based UDA is the "modality gap." Despite contrastive pre-training, vision and text features are not perfectly aligned and inherently contain modality-specific information that might not transfer effectively across domains, especially when direct alignment is attempted. Previous research on the modality gap has largely focused on reducing or bridging this gap. This paper, UniMoS++, diverges by proposing to *bypass* the modality gap through explicit modality separation. Instead of forcing alignment, it aims to disentangle modality-specific and modality-invariant components from VLM features, process them separately, and then synergistically combine their strengths. This represents a novel paradigm shift in VLM-based UDA, acknowledging and leveraging, rather than merely combating, the inherent differences between modalities. It also extends beyond UDA to address active domain adaptation (ADA), multi-source DA (MSDA), and source-free ADA (SFADA), demonstrating broad applicability in various domain adaptation settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind UniMoS++ stems from a critical observation: while pre-trained VLMs show promise in UDA by leveraging semantic information and aligning vision-text embeddings, the existing "modality gap" hinders optimal performance. Direct UDA efforts, in the presence of this gap, tend to transfer only modality-invariant knowledge, neglecting valuable modality-specific information.

The key objectives of this research are:

- **Identify and Address the Modality Gap:** To highlight the negative impact of the modality gap on domain adaptation and propose a method that not only acknowledges its existence but also exploits modality-specific information.
- **Unified Modality Separation:** To develop a framework that can disentangle VLM features into distinct vision-associated (VAC) and language-associated (LAC) components, and then process these components in a unified, modality-aware manner during training.
- **Dynamic Modality Integration:** To enable an adaptive mechanism that can automatically determine optimal ensemble weights for combining the strengths of VAC and LAC at test time, maximizing synergy across different components and adapting to various tasks and training phases.
- **Instance-Level Modality Characterization:** To design a metric (Modality Discrepancy Metric, MDI) that quantifies instance-level modality characteristics, allowing for targeted adaptation strategies, including improved active learning for uncertain samples.
- **Broad Applicability and Efficiency:** To create a framework that is compatible with existing prompt-tuning techniques and applicable across a spectrum of domain adaptation settings (UDA, ADA, SFADA, MSDA), while maintaining computational efficiency by keeping VLM encoders frozen.

In essence, the paper aims to overcome the limitations of current VLM-based UDA by transforming the challenge of the modality gap into an opportunity for more comprehensive and fine-grained knowledge transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UniMoS++ framework is built upon frozen CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) encoders and introduces several novel components to achieve unified modality separation and adaptation.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

UniMoS++ conceptualizes vision features (f_v) from a VLM as a composite of Vision-Associated Components (VAC, f_vac) and Language-Associated Components (LAC, f_lac). By disentangling these, the framework can generate modality-specific prediction results (y_v for vision, y_l for language) and combine them via a weighted ensemble (y_ens) at inference time.

#### 3.2. Modality Separation Networks

At the core of UniMoS++ are dual separation branches (Q_v and Q_t) that project the pre-extracted f_v into f_vac and f_lac. An orthogonal regularization loss (L_ortho) is applied to ensure that these disentangled components capture distinct characteristics while maintaining original cross-modal correspondences.
- **Language Modality Output (y_l):** Derived from LAC (f_lac) by computing cosine similarities with text embeddings (μ_i) generated from learnable prompts. An Approximated Controlled Direct Effect (ACDE) method is employed to debias these predictions, mitigating imbalances.
- **Vision Modality Output (y_v):** Generated from VAC (f_vac) through a vision classifier comprising linear layers (Φ).

#### 3.3. Modality Discrepancy Metric (MDI)

The MDI metric is designed to categorize target data at an instance level based on their cross-modal prediction confidences and consistencies. This allows for tailored adaptation strategies.
- **Modality-Invariant (MI) Samples:** Defined as samples where the top-1 predicted class is consistent across both vision (y_v) and language (y_l) modalities. An MI-score (product of top-1 logits minus product of top-2 logits) quantifies confidence and consistency, selecting the most reliable samples (T_C).
- **Modality-Specific (MS) Samples:** Samples exhibiting symmetrical disagreement between modalities, where top-1 of one is top-2 of the other. These samples highlight the presence of modality-specific information.
- **Uncertain (UN) Samples:** Any sample not categorized as MI or MS. These are further divided into UN-annotate (UN-a, for active learning) and UN-ensemble (UN-e). A UN-score (difference between top-1 and top-2 logits) prioritizes UN-a samples for annotation.

#### 3.4. Modality-aware Training and Adaptation

The training process is designed to exploit modality-specific knowledge while promoting cross-modal transfer.
- **LAC Optimization:** For unlabeled target data (T_U), LAC is optimized using knowledge distillation from CLIP's zero-shot similarity scores (y_u). For actively annotated target samples (T_L), handcrafted pseudo-logits (y_l) are used. For labeled source data (D_s), standard cross-entropy loss (L_s_lac) is applied. Strong augmentation ensures semantic consistency.
- **VAC Optimization:** VAC is optimized using a K-means-based deep clustering approach. Centroids (ϕ_k) are calculated from bottleneck features (f_b) and predictions are made by comparing f_b with centroids (y_v). A crucial component is the train-time ensemble output (y_e = w · y_v + (1 − w) · y_l), where 'w' is a learnable weight from a weight generator. This allows the vision branch to leverage text-specific knowledge without forcing destructive cross-modal alignment. Source data (D_s) also uses cross-entropy loss (L_s_vac). An information maximization loss (L_im) is added for regularization.
- **Domain Adaptation via Modality Discriminator:** A modality discriminator (f_d) is introduced to align the distributions of source and target VAC (and LAC separately). Trained initially on source data to differentiate modalities, it then freezes and helps align target VAC/LAC with source ones, facilitating knowledge transfer through a binary cross-entropy loss (L_bce).

#### 3.5. Modality-adaptive Ensemble (MaE)

At test time, MaE automatically determines the optimal ensemble weight (w*) for combining y_v and y_l. It does so by analyzing an "ensemble threshold" (δ) for each sample, which indicates which modality would dominate for a given w*. By approximating the density distributions of "vision-biased" (correctly classified by vision) and "text-biased" (correctly classified by text) samples, MaE identifies w* as the point of maximum deviation in the sorted δ values, ensuring a dynamic and optimal fusion.

#### 3.6. Overall Algorithm

The training algorithm iteratively performs modality separation, calculates modality-aware losses for LAC and VAC, and updates network parameters. For ADA tasks, it integrates active sample selection using MDI to annotate informative uncertain samples. For UDA, T_L is replaced by T_C. The entire training process keeps CLIP encoders frozen and only optimizes lightweight linear layers and prompts, ensuring high computational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniMoS++ was extensively evaluated across various datasets, backbones, baselines, and adaptation settings, consistently demonstrating superior performance and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Datasets and Implementation

Experiments were conducted on five mainstream domain adaptation datasets: OfficeHome, VisDA-2017, DomainNet, Mini-DomainNet, and three image classification tasks from the challenging WILDS benchmark (iWildCam, Camelyon17, FMoW). All experiments were performed on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090Ti GPU, utilizing pre-extracted CLIP features for efficiency.

#### 4.2. Main Results

- **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA):**
 - On **OfficeHome** (ResNet50 and ViT-B backbones), UniMoS++ achieved comparable or better performance than UniMoS with manually tuned optimal weights, and significantly surpassed prior ResNet/CLIP-based methods. For example, on ResNet50, UniMoS++ achieved 78.0% average accuracy, outperforming UniMoS(0.3) (77.9%), PADCLIP(FT) (76.6%), and DAPrompt (74.5%).
 - On **VisDA-2017** (ResNet101), UniMoS++ matched UniMoS with tailored weights (88.1%) and nearly matched the SOTA PADCLIP(FT) (88.5%), which performs full fine-tuning of CLIP parameters. This highlights UniMoS++'s strength despite not fine-tuning CLIP's heavy backbone.
 - On the challenging **DomainNet** (ViT-B), UniMoS++ achieved the best overall average performance of 63.8%, surpassing the SOTA DAMP (63.2%) and showing significant gains on difficult target domains like `qdr` (from 21.3% for DAMP to 25.0% for UniMoS++).
 - The results consistently demonstrate the efficacy of the MaE algorithm in automatically determining optimal ensemble weights, addressing a practical limitation of previous UniMoS versions that required manual or tailored weights.

- **Multi-Source Domain Adaptation (MSDA):**
 - UniMoS++ set new SOTA on both **DomainNet** (58.6% average) and **OfficeHome** (78.7% average), outperforming previous methods like MPA (54.1% on DomainNet) and DAMP (79.2% on OfficeHome, narrowly surpassed). The MaE algorithm proved particularly beneficial in handling the dynamic mixed distributions from multiple source domains.

- **Active Domain Adaptation (ADA) and Source-Free ADA (SFADA):**
 - UniMoS++ is presented as the first method to solve ADA with VLMs. On **OfficeHome** with 5% annotation budget, UniMoS++ achieved 82.4% average accuracy, significantly outperforming prior SOTA methods like LAS (78.2%). With 10% budget, it reached 85.5%.
 - Even surpassing the accuracy of fully supervised training on target data in some settings (e.g., 82.4% vs 82.0% on OfficeHome with 5% budget), UniMoS++ underscores the power of VLM-based ADA.
 - Compatibility with prompt-tuning methods (e.g., CoOp, DenseCLIP/post prp) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning like LoRA was demonstrated, maintaining high performance and flexibility.
 - For **SFADA**, UniMoS++ also established new SOTA, albeit with slightly reduced accuracy compared to ADA due to the absence of source data.
 - On **Mini-DomainNet**, UniMoS++ achieved a remarkable +9.1% accuracy improvement (82.4% vs. 73.3% for DAPM-TT), further highlighting the effectiveness of multimodal knowledge exploitation.

- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization (OOD):**
 - On the challenging **WILDS** benchmarks, UniMoS++ surpassed recent SOTA on CLIP (VDPG) in 2 out of 3 datasets. Notably, on Camelyon17 (medical slides, where CLIP has limited inherent knowledge), it improved accuracy from CLIP's zero-shot 50.1% to 89.5%, demonstrating its ability to adapt to expert domains.

#### 4.3. Analysis

- **Ablation Study:** Confirmed the positive contribution of each proposed component: MaE, MDI, orthogonal loss (L_ortho), information maximization loss (L_im), modality discriminator (L_bce), and learnable train-time weights. The results clearly showed performance degradation when components were removed or replaced (e.g., replacing MDI with random selection dropped accuracy by 2.7%).
- **Feature Visualization (t-SNE):** Demonstrated that modality separation effectively disentangled VAC and LAC features. It also visually confirmed that MDI's active selection strategy targets more informative, less clustered samples compared to energy-based methods, which tended to select already well-classified samples.
- **Computational Analysis:** UniMoS++ exhibited extremely high computational efficiency. With only 2.65M trainable parameters (compared to 50.71M for DAPM-TT), it achieved 1992 throughput (compared to 102 for DAPM-TT) and significantly lower training time (0.06 hours vs. 1.10 hours) and GFLOPs (0.04 vs. 16.53), while achieving higher accuracy. This makes it a highly practical solution for adapting VLMs with limited resources.
- **Parameter Sensitivity:** Analysis of source importance hyperparameters (α, β) showed task-dependent optimal values, e.g., down-weighting source supervision for VisDA (synthetic source data) proved beneficial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniMoS++ framework represents a significant advancement in the field of domain adaptation, particularly for methods leveraging powerful Vision-Language Models. Its key contributions and potential impacts are multifaceted:

1. **Novel Modality Gap Handling:** By proposing to *bypass* rather than reduce or bridge the modality gap, UniMoS++ offers a fresh perspective on a fundamental challenge in multimodal learning. It demonstrates that explicitly separating and then adaptively combining modality-specific information can lead to superior adaptation performance. This could inspire new directions in multimodal foundation model adaptation.
2. **Enhanced VLM Adaptability:** UniMoS++ unlocks the full potential of pre-trained VLMs for UDA by providing a robust and efficient mechanism to adapt them to new domains without costly full fine-tuning. The frozen VLM encoders coupled with lightweight learnable modules make it highly practical for resource-constrained environments.
3. **Unified and Versatile Framework:** The framework's compatibility with various prompt-tuning techniques and its demonstrated success across a broad spectrum of domain adaptation settings—UDA, MSDA, ADA, and SFADA—highlights its versatility and broad applicability. This "one-stop-shop" solution can cater to diverse real-world scenarios.
4. **Advancements in Active Learning:** The Modality Discrepancy Metric (MDI) is a novel contribution that provides instance-level insights into modality characteristics. Its use in active learning for identifying truly "uncertain" and informative samples, especially in multimodal contexts, is a pioneering step and offers a foundation for future VLM-based active learning research.
5. **Computational Efficiency:** The outstanding computational efficiency (e.g., 9 times faster training and significantly fewer parameters than prior SOTA) is a major practical advantage. It democratizes the use of large foundation models for domain adaptation tasks, making advanced AI techniques accessible even with limited computational resources.
6. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** By considering both modality-specific and modality-invariant knowledge and adaptively fusing them, UniMoS++ leads to more robust models that generalize better to unseen target domains, as evidenced by its strong performance in OOD generalization benchmarks.

In conclusion, UniMoS++ offers a paradigm-shifting approach to VLM-based domain adaptation. Its theoretical elegance in addressing the modality gap, coupled with its robust empirical performance and exceptional computational efficiency, positions it as a highly impactful contribution to the fields of computer vision, natural language processing, and machine learning, with significant implications for deploying advanced AI systems in diverse and evolving real-world applications.
