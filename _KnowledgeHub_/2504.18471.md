# Action Flow Matching for Continual Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Indiana University–Bloomington introduced Action Flow Matching (AFM), a generative framework that refines planned robot actions to accelerate continual dynamics model adaptation. AFM enabled a UGV to achieve an 81.4% task success rate, a 34.2% improvement over baselines, and reduced tracking error by 6.6% for a Crazyflie quadrotor in dynamically changing environments.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with continual learning and robustly adapting to unexpected changes in dynamic operating environments.
- Misaligned dynamics models lead to suboptimal or unsafe planned actions and inefficient data collection for model improvement.
- Existing online learning methods often suffer from catastrophic forgetting, outlier sensitivity, and limited generalizability across tasks or robot embodiments.

## Method

- **Action Flow Matching** (AFM) introduces a generative framework that transforms initially planned actions into 'intended' actions, conditioned on the current ==dynamics regime==.
- It learns a ==latent representation of the current dynamics regime== by observing discrepancies between predicted and realized state transitions without requiring a ground-truth model.
- A ==conditional flow matching== model is trained using a ==counterfactual-inspired simulation strategy== to map planned actions to refined actions, which are then executed to collect more informative data.

## Results

- AFM achieved an average success rate of **81.4%** for a UGV in dynamic environments, which is **34.2%** higher than the best-performing baseline, while requiring **15.1%** fewer steps.
- When integrated with an existing online learning approach for a Crazyflie quadrotor, AFM reduced the average tracking Mean Squared Error (MSE) by **6.6%** during mid-flight mass changes.
- Ablation studies confirmed AFM's consistent performance gains, and training with domain randomization further boosted the UGV's success rate to **83.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rather than directly updating complex dynamics models, correcting planned actions acts as an agile 'corrective lens' to improve robot behavior and data collection efficiency.
> - Constraining action transformation to dynamically feasible regions, approximated by the current dynamics model, prevents inefficient exploration of the entire state-action space.
> - By executing more 'informative' actions, the robot accelerates the alignment of its underlying dynamics model, leading to faster and more stable adaptation in changing environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the critical challenge of **continual learning in robotics**, an area focused on enabling robots to constantly adapt to changing environments and tasks, much like humans do. This capability is paramount for long-term robot autonomy in real-world, unpredictable settings where pre-programmed or pre-learned models quickly become outdated.

The core problem tackled is the refinement of **dynamics models**, which are essential for robot planning and control. Traditional approaches in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) often guide exploration using the latest (but potentially misaligned) model, leading to inefficiencies and safety concerns. The existing research landscape highlights several persistent challenges in this domain:
- **Safe Adaptation:** Ensuring that model updates do not lead to unsafe or erratic robot behavior, particularly in dynamic environments.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Preventing the loss of previously acquired knowledge when learning new dynamics, a common issue in continual learning.
- **Outlier Management:** Robustly handling noisy or anomalous data that can compromise model integrity.
- **Data Efficiency:** Learning effectively from limited interaction data, especially in real-world robotic scenarios where data collection can be costly and time-consuming.
- **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** Balancing the need to explore new dynamics for learning with the imperative to exploit current knowledge for task completion, all within operational and resource constraints.

**Existing Approaches and their Limitations:**
The paper positions its contribution against several categories of related work:
- **Continual Robot Dynamics Learning:** Many methods in this space rely on strong inductive biases (e.g., KNODE-MPC, Gaussian processes, sparse representations) that reduce complexity but limit generalizability across tasks or embodiments. They often require replay buffers, architectural changes, or specific knowledge about the system dynamics (e.g., differential equations). These requirements can be memory-intensive, computationally demanding, or impractical for systems with complex, poorly understood dynamics.
- **Online Learning for Robot Adaptation:** These methods often focus on error-based learning or implicit models to adapt specific components (e.g., CPGs for locomotion, fuzzy logic controllers for navigation). While beneficial, they may not address the fundamental dynamics model misalignment or be encapsulated to specific control paradigms, thus lacking broad applicability.
- **Batch-based Learning for Robot Adaptation:** Approaches like Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) or Context-aware Dynamics Models (CaDM) show impressive adaptation. However, they typically involve multi-staged offline training, reliance on privileged information, or the learning of latent representations, making them less suitable for truly *online* and *continual* adaptation without prior knowledge of context switches.

**AFM's Unique Position:**
"Action Flow Matching (AFM)" distinguishes itself by proposing a novel, generative framework that *transforms planned actions* rather than solely focusing on directly updating the dynamics model or exploring with a suboptimal one. The key insight is that instead of trying to fix a complex, misaligned internal dynamics model directly (the "eyes" in their analogy), AFM introduces a "corrective lens" that adjusts the robot's *actions* to better align with its *intended outcomes* (what it would do with a perfect model). This paradigm shift aims to:
1. **Accelerate Learning:** By executing more "informative" or "intended" actions, the robot collects higher-quality data, which in turn speeds up the re-alignment of its internal dynamics model.
2. **Model Agnosticism:** AFM does not require modifications to the underlying dynamics model architecture or training methodology, making it broadly applicable to various existing MBRL systems.
3. **Reduced Dependency:** It can reduce reliance on large replay buffers or managing multiple legacy model snapshots by focusing on efficient online adaptation.

By targeting the action space directly, AFM offers a unique, complementary layer to existing continual learning strategies, promising to enhance the efficiency and robustness of robot adaptation in dynamic, unknown environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that enables robots to *continually and efficiently adapt their dynamics models* in response to unanticipated changes in their operating environment or internal state (e.g., actuator degradation, altered mass). This objective is driven by the desire to endow robots with a human-like ability to adapt and plan effectively even when their internal expectations (dynamics models) do not perfectly match reality.

The key motivations are rooted in overcoming the significant limitations of current robot learning and control systems when faced with model misalignment:
- **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** Robots frequently encounter unforeseen changes (e.g., slippery surfaces, varying payloads) that render pre-learned or fixed dynamics models inaccurate. This necessitates continuous adaptation to maintain safe and effective operation.
- **Shortcomings of Misaligned Models:** When a robot's dynamics model is misaligned with reality, planned actions often lead to suboptimal or even dangerous outcomes. This hinders effective planning and control (e.g., LQR, MPC controllers) which critically depend on accurate predictive models.
- **Inefficient Data Collection:** Operating with a misaligned model can lead to inefficient exploration, where the robot collects low-quality or uninformative data. This significantly slows down the process of model improvement and adaptation.
- **Challenges of Existing Online Learning:** Current online learning methods often struggle with catastrophic forgetting, sensitivity to outliers, and the need for task- or embodiment-specific inductive biases, limiting their general applicability and long-term effectiveness.
- **Bridging the Gap between Plan and Reality:** The motivation is to create a mechanism that "corrects" a robot's planned actions, transforming them into actions that more closely reflect what the robot *would* execute if it possessed a perfectly aligned understanding of its dynamics. This corrective step serves as a "calibration process" to ensure executed actions are more effective and informative for subsequent learning.

In essence, the research seeks to create a framework that allows robots to *learn faster and more safely* in the face of uncertainty and change by intelligently modifying their immediate actions, rather than solely relying on complex, slower, or potentially unstable model updates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, **Action Flow Matching (AFM)**, introduces a generative framework that leverages flow matching to refine planned actions. Rather than directly modifying the dynamics model itself, AFM transforms the actions generated by a potentially misaligned model (`a_0_t`) into "intended" actions (`a_1_t`) that would lead to more desirable outcomes.

The core of AFM is built upon three key insights:

1. **Efficient Dynamics Space Reduction:** To avoid inefficient exploration of the entire state-action space, AFM leverages the robot's initial (or currently available) dynamics model (`f_θ_0`) to approximate the dynamically feasible regions. This constrains the action transformation and learning process to regions of practical relevance for the robot's embodiment.
2. **Representing Dynamics Regime without Ground-Truth Models:** AFM learns a latent representation (`Z_D`) of the current dynamics regime. This representation is derived from the current state (`s_t`), the planned action (`a_0_t`), and the observed discrepancy (`e_t`) between the planned state transition and the realized one. This allows the system to characterize the current environmental dynamics and model reliability without needing access to a perfect "ground-truth" model.
3. **Intent Mapping for Accelerated Learning:** Using a flow matching (FM) model (`g_ϕ`), AFM learns a mapping from planned actions (`a_0_t`) to intended actions (`a_1_t`). This mapping is conditioned on the dynamics regime representation (`Z_D`). The transformed actions `a_1_t` are designed to generate more informative data, thereby accelerating the continual alignment of the robot's primary dynamics model (`f_θ_t`).

**Flow Matching (FM) Integration:**
AFM adopts the conditional flow matching (CFM) framework, which defines a time-continuous probability path between a source distribution (planned actions) and a target distribution (intended actions).
- **Flow Construction:** A neural network, parameterized as a time-dependent velocity field (`u_ϕ_τ`), learns to transform samples from the source to the target distribution by solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE).
- **Probability Path:** The method uses a conditional optimal-transport path where an intermediate sample (`X_τ`) is a linear combination of source (`X_0`) and target (`X_1`) samples.
- **Training Objective:** AFM minimizes the conditional flow matching loss (`L_CFM`), which allows learning the velocity field `u_ϕ_τ` without requiring an explicit ground-truth target velocity field. This is crucial as the "intended" actions (`X_1`) are not directly observed.

**Training the AFM Model (`g_ϕ`):**
A critical challenge is generating the training data for `g_ϕ` (pairs of planned `a_0_t` and intended `a_1_t` actions) without a ground-truth dynamics model. AFM addresses this with a novel "counterfactual-inspired" simulation strategy:
- Given `s_t`, a random `a_0_t` (planned), and a random `a_1_t` (intended), the next state `s_t+1` is *simulated using `a_1_t`* (`s_t+1 = f_θ_0(s_t, a_1_t)`), but the transition is *registered as if it resulted from `a_0_t`*. This generates diverse pairs of (s_t, a_0_t, s_t+1) where `a_0_t` is "misaligned" relative to the observed `s_t+1`.
- The dynamics regime representation (`Z_D`) is learned by an encoder (`E_Z_D`) that takes `s_t`, `a_0_t`, and an error `e_t`. During training, `e_t` is the difference between predicted states using `a_1_t` and `a_0_t` (i.e., `f_θ_0(s_t, a_1_t) - f_θ_0(s_t, a_0_t)`).
- The AFM model (`g_ϕ`) then consists of `E_Z_D`, an action encoder (`E_Z_T`), and a flow network (FM) that predicts the velocity field (`⃗u_τ`). This model is trained to minimize `L_CFM`.

**Continual Learning with AFM (Deployment):**
During deployment, the process unfolds as follows:
1. **Planning:** The robot's existing dynamics model (`f_θ_t`) and planner (e.g., MPPI) generate an initial planned action (`a_0_t`).
2. **Misalignment Detection:** The system continuously monitors the prediction error (`e_t`) between the actual observed next state (`s_real`) and the state predicted by `f_θ_t` using the last *executed* action (`s_pred = f_θ_t(s_t-1, a_0_t-1)`). If `e_t` exceeds a threshold (`δ_M`), a misalignment flag (`M`) is triggered, indicating a dynamics regime change.
3. **Action Transformation (if M is True):** If misalignment is detected, the `E_Z_D` encoder computes `Z_D` based on the current `s_t`, `a_0_t`, and `e_t` (which is now `s_real - s_pred`). This `Z_D` is then fed to the trained AFM model `g_ϕ`, which uses an ODE solver to transform `a_0_t` into the refined action `a_1_t`.
4. **Execution and Model Update:** The robot executes `a_1_t` (or `a_0_t` if no misalignment). The observed transition (`s_t-1`, `a_t-1`, `s_t`) is then used to update the underlying dynamics model `f_θ_t`. This online update typically involves single-step (or small batch) learning to efficiently incorporate new experiences.

The method's design emphasizes agnosticism to the specific dynamics model (`f_θ`) architecture, allowing it to integrate with various model-based control schemes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of AFM was rigorously validated across two distinct robotic platforms and multiple dynamic scenarios: an Unmanned Ground Vehicle (UGV) and a Crazyflie quadrotor, both evaluated in simulation.

**A. Unmanned Ground Vehicle (UGV) Experiments:**
- **Platform:** Jackal UGV, a robust wheeled robot.
- **Task:** Navigating two upscaled ETHZ test tracks, following sparse waypoints. The key challenge involved *sudden, unannounced changes in environmental dynamics* (e.g., altered traction conditions simulated by `v_gain` and `ω_gain` parameters) after 15% and 80% of waypoints were reached.
- **Dynamics Model:** An ensemble of probabilistic neural networks (PE model), which explicitly models epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties, updated online with each new transition.
- **Baselines:**
 - **stream-x PE:** An online adaptation of the PE model using streaming reinforcement learning techniques.
 - **Physics:** A non-adaptive Dubins physics-based model, representing a fixed, pre-calibrated system.
- **Metrics:** Task Success Rate (higher is better) and Average Number of Steps to complete the task (lower is better).
- **Results:**
 - **Superior Success Rate:** AFM achieved an average success rate of **81.4%**, which is **34.2% higher** than the best-performing baseline (stream-x PE, at 47.2%).
 - **Enhanced Efficiency:** AFM required **15.1% fewer steps** on average (3277.95 steps) compared to the best baseline (3865.24 steps).
 - **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** In 15 out of 22 challenging scenarios, baselines exhausted the allotted time. AFM encountered this in only 5 scenarios, showing a **66% improvement** in handling the most difficult dynamic shifts.
 - **Stable Learning:** Ablation studies showed AFM consistently maintained lower loss and faster recovery after dynamics regime changes compared to Online PE, indicating more stable and rapid adaptation.
 - **Trajectory Improvement:** Visualizations confirmed that AFM's action adjustments helped the UGV recover faster and maintain a more stable trajectory after interventions.

**B. Crazyflie Quadrotor Experiments:**
- **Platform:** Crazyflie quadrotor, a lightweight, agile aerial robot with complex dynamics.
- **Task:** Tracking circular trajectories at various speeds, with the quadrotor's *mass dynamically altered mid-flight* (reduced by 50% then increased to 133%).
- **Dynamics Model:** The author-provided implementation of Online-KNODE-MPC, a hybrid model combining ODE-based physics with an adaptive learning component. This choice demonstrated AFM's model-agnostic nature.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Online-KNODE-MPC:** The base model, representing a state-of-the-art online learning approach for quadrotors.
 - **Physics:** A non-adaptive physics-based model.
- **Metrics:** Average Tracking Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the reference trajectory and the robot's state (lower is better).
- **Results:**
 - **Reduced Tracking Error:** AFM, when integrated with Online-KNODE-MPC, reduced the average tracking MSE by **6.6%**. This is a significant improvement, especially considering the already highly optimized and physics-informed nature of the baseline.
 - **Confirmation of AFM's Impact:** This reduction is attributed directly to AFM's ability to refine planned actions, guiding the robot to collect more informative data and accelerate the underlying model's adaptation, particularly in scenarios with rapid dynamics changes.

**C. Ablation Studies and Domain Randomization:**
- **Ablation:** AFM consistently outperformed versions of the base PE model with frozen weights (PE) and online updates (Online PE) in terms of success rate and steps. AFM was superior in 55% more scenarios than Online PE.
- **Domain Randomization (AFM+DR):** Training AFM with an initial dynamics model perturbed by ±10% (simulating real-world inaccuracies) further boosted performance, yielding an **additional 2.2% increase in success rate (to 83.6%)** and requiring **146.7 fewer steps** on average. This suggests that intentionally introducing uncertainty during training can enhance AFM's ability to generalize to unforeseen misalignments.

In summary, AFM demonstrates a robust and efficient approach to continual robot learning, consistently outperforming established baselines across different platforms and dynamic environments. Its ability to accelerate model alignment and improve task success rates without needing ground truth dynamics is a significant finding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Action Flow Matching (AFM) marks a compelling step forward in the field of continual robot learning, with significant implications for the development of truly autonomous and adaptive robotic systems.

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Paradigm Shift in Adaptation:** AFM proposes a novel way to address model misalignment by transforming planned actions rather than solely attempting to update the complex dynamics model directly. This "corrective lens" approach is more agile and less intrusive, making it a powerful complement to existing model learning strategies.
- **Model Agnosticism and Versatility:** A crucial strength of AFM is its independence from the specific architecture or learning methodology of the underlying dynamics model. Demonstrated across a purely data-driven PE model (UGV) and a physics-informed KNODE-MPC model (quadrotor), this versatility dramatically broadens its applicability to diverse robotic platforms and existing control frameworks.
- **Accelerated and Efficient Learning:** By guiding the robot to execute actions that are more "informative" about the true dynamics, AFM accelerates the data collection process. This leads to faster model re-alignment and, consequently, higher task success rates and reduced operational time (fewer steps). This directly addresses the critical challenge of data efficiency in real-world robotics.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Dynamic Changes:** The ability of AFM to detect and respond to unannounced changes in environmental dynamics (e.g., traction, mass) or internal robot state is vital for deployment in unstructured and unpredictable environments. It allows robots to maintain performance even when faced with significant deviations from their expected operational conditions.
- **Principled Exploration-Exploitation Balance:** AFM provides a systematic mechanism to reconcile the trade-off between exploiting current knowledge for task completion and exploring new dynamics for adaptation. By only transforming actions when misalignment is detected, it ensures task productivity while strategically gathering data for long-term improvement.
- **Reduced Resource Dependency:** By fostering efficient data collection and focusing on dynamic transformation, AFM can reduce the dependency on large replay buffers or the need to manage multiple legacy model snapshots, which are often resource-intensive for long-term, lifelong learning systems.

**Potential Impact:**

The successful implementation of AFM paves the way for a new generation of more robust, scalable, and self-sustaining robotic solutions. Its impact could be felt across various domains:
- **Field Robotics:** Robots operating in agriculture, disaster response, or exploration missions will greatly benefit from the ability to adapt to varying terrain, weather conditions, or unexpected system failures.
- **Manufacturing and Logistics:** Robots can quickly adapt to changes in payload, wear and tear of manipulators, or variations in material properties, leading to more flexible and resilient production lines.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots can adapt their physical interactions to varying human physical characteristics or environmental sensitivities, enhancing safety and collaboration.
- **Long-Term Autonomy:** AFM moves closer to the vision of robots that can continuously learn and improve over their entire operational lifespan, reducing the need for manual recalibration or extensive pre-training.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**

Despite its significant advancements, the authors acknowledge certain limitations that present avenues for future research:
- **Generalization of the Dynamics Model:** While AFM enhances the learning process, the underlying dynamics model (`f_θ`) itself still benefits from improvements in its ability to extrapolate beyond previously observed data. This is a general challenge in model learning and not specific to AFM.
- **Data-Intensive Nature of FM Training:** Flow matching models can be data-intensive to train. While AFM cleverly generates high-quality synthetic training data, further algorithmic advancements in the data efficiency of FM models would translate directly to more efficient training for AFM.

In conclusion, AFM represents a notable contribution towards realizing lifelong robot autonomy by offering an elegant and effective solution to the pervasive problem of dynamics model misalignment. Its practical utility, demonstrated versatility, and strong performance position it as an influential method for future advancements in adaptive robotics.
