# LoopSR: Looping Sim-and-Real for Lifelong Policy Adaptation of Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduce LoopSR, a framework enabling lifelong policy adaptation for legged robots by iteratively refining policies. The system uses limited real-world data to reconstruct a 'digital twin' of the environment in simulation, leading to continuous policy improvement, faster traversal times, and higher success rates on diverse challenging terrains.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies trained solely in simulation often suffer from a 'sim-to-real gap', leading to suboptimal performance when deployed in complex real-world environments.
- While domain randomization enhances policy robustness, it can compromise optimal performance for specific real-world conditions, as a policy generalized to many environments may not be optimal for any single one.
- Directly training RL policies in the real world is challenging due to high data collection costs, slow learning, the absence of privileged knowledge (e.g., accurate heightmaps), and noise in observations.

## Method

- **LoopSR** employs a ==two-stage pipeline==: an initial pretraining phase in simulation, followed by a ==lifelong adaptation loop== that refines policies post-deployment.
- It uses limited real-world trajectory data to generate a ==latent representation==, which in turn estimates and updates simulation environment parameters, effectively creating a ==digital twin==.
- Policies are then continually trained within this dynamically updated simulated environment, leveraging simulation benefits (data abundance, privileged information) while minimizing costly real-world interaction.

## Results

- **LoopSR** policies demonstrated performance closely matching or exceeding an 'Expert' policy trained directly on specific test environments in sim-to-sim experiments, significantly outperforming zero-shot sim-to-real methods.
- Real-world evaluations on a Unitree A1 robot showed improved traversal times (e.g., **4.33s** for Stair Up vs. **5.01s** for Origin) and higher success rates (e.g., **100%** on Stair Up vs. **70%** for Origin) across various challenging terrains.
- The framework produced smoother, more stable gaits and motions in real-world scenarios, enhancing operational safety and demonstrating robust adaptation to terrain variations, unlike the jerky and unstable actions observed with baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative reconstruction of the real-world environment within a simulator, using only robot trajectory data, provides an effective mechanism to continuously bridge the sim-to-real gap for lifelong policy adaptation.
> - A hybrid approach for simulation parameter estimation, combining retrieval-based methods for robustness and learning-based decoders for accuracy, improves the fidelity of the digital twin.
> - Employing a transformer-based autoencoder with a contrastive loss constructs an interpretable and discriminative latent space for trajectories, crucial for accurate environment parameter inference and stable adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated its capability in achieving generalized legged locomotion through sim-to-real transfer. However, a significant challenge remains in deploying these policies robustly and efficiently in diverse real-world environments. The "sim-to-real gap," which refers to the discrepancy between simulated and real-world dynamics, is a persistent issue.

Current adaptive methods, such as domain randomization, aim to enhance policy robustness across varied environments by training policies on a wide range of simulated parameters. While this approach improves generalization, the "No Free Lunch theorem" suggests a potential trade-off where increasing robustness across many environments may lead to suboptimal performance in any specific environment. This limitation can result in policies that are robust but not optimally efficient for particular real-world deployment scenarios.

Direct training of RL policies in the real world is an intuitive solution but faces substantial obstacles. Real-world data collection is expensive and slow, contrasting with the data-hungry nature of RL algorithms. Furthermore, real-world settings often lack "privileged knowledge" such as accurate heightfields, contact forces, or specific robot parameters (mass, friction), which are readily available in simulation and crucial for tasks like navigating stairs or complex terrains. Noisy observations in real environments also contribute to training instability. Previous research has explored real-world learning through methods like reward reshaping, sample-efficient algorithms, or model-based RL. However, these approaches often require careful design of action space constraints and reward functions, and have not consistently outperformed zero-shot sim-to-real methods in terms of policy effectiveness or training stability.

LoopSR addresses these challenges by proposing an iterative "sim-and-real" approach that seeks to continuously refine policies post-deployment. Instead of solely training in the real world or relying on a static sim-to-real transfer, the work leverages real-world data to reconstruct and refine the simulation environment, allowing for continuous policy improvement within the more controllable and data-abundant simulation setting. This approach aims to circumvent the limitations of direct real-world training while overcoming the performance compromises inherent in broad domain randomization. It fits into the landscape by offering a dynamic, lifelong adaptation mechanism that bridges the gap between static sim-to-real transfer and challenging direct real-world learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of LoopSR is to enable lifelong policy adaptation for legged robots, continuously refining RL policies after their initial deployment in real-world environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in current sim-to-real transfer paradigms:

1. **Addressing the Performance Compromise of Domain Randomization:** While domain randomization increases policy robustness, it can lead to suboptimal performance in specific real-world environments due as suggested by the No Free Lunch theorem. The motivation is to overcome this trade-off by adapting a robust initial policy to achieve peak performance in a target real-world setting.
2. **Overcoming Real-World Data Scarcity and Training Difficulties:** Directly training RL policies in the real world is severely hampered by the high cost and slow pace of data collection, the immense data requirements of RL algorithms, and the absence of privileged information (e.g., precise terrain height, robot parameters) that is available in simulation. Furthermore, real-world data often contains noise, leading to training instability. The motivation is to develop a data-efficient method that minimizes real-world interaction while maximizing policy improvement.
3. **Leveraging Simulation for Continuous Refinement:** The paper notes the advantages of simulation environments, including readily available privileged information, absence of observation noise, and the ability to conduct extensive, parallel training. The motivation is to establish a feedback loop where limited real-world experience can inform and refine a digital twin of the environment in simulation, thus harnessing the benefits of simulation for ongoing policy optimization without being "trapped in the capricious and noisy real world."
4. **Developing a Robust and Generalizable Environment Reconstruction Method:** A core technical objective is to accurately reconstruct the real-world environment within a simulation using only real-world trajectory data. This involves learning a representation of real-world dynamics from unlabeled trajectories and using this representation to infer corresponding simulation parameters. The motivation is to ensure that the simulated training environment accurately reflects the current real-world conditions, thereby making the continual training effective.

In essence, the motivation is to create a practical, safe, and efficient pipeline for robot policy deployment that allows for continuous improvement and adaptation throughout the robot's operational lifespan, bridging the gap between theoretical RL advancements and real-world application challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LoopSR is structured as a two-stage pipeline: pretraining and lifelong adaptation, designed to facilitate continuous policy improvement for legged robots.

**A. Preliminaries and Overview**
The training process in the simulator is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The simulation environment is characterized by privileged environment parameters `c = [c_e, c_r]`, encompassing terrain distribution (`c_e`) and robot-specific parameters (`c_r`). The real-world adaptation occurs in a POMDP `M_R` where reward `r_R` is implicit. The goal is to adapt a pretrained policy to optimality in `M_R` using minimal real-world data.

**B. Looping Sim and Real Pipeline**

1. **Pretraining Stage:**
 - **Policy Training:** A deployable policy is initially trained in the IsaacGym simulation environment using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This policy builds upon architectures like DreamWaQ and AMP, utilizing an Asymmetric Actor-Critic design with expert motion data and extensive domain randomizations to enhance robustness for sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Latent Space Construction:** Concurrently, an encoder-decoder network is trained to establish an informative latent trajectory space. This network, consisting of a transformer-based encoder (LoopSR-enc) and a multi-head decoder, learns to map simulation trajectories to a latent representation.
 - **Reference Dataset Collection:** Rollouts from the simulation pretraining, along with their corresponding privileged information (`c_e`, `c_r`), are stored to form a reference dataset for subsequent adaptation.

2. **Lifelong Adaptation Stage:**
 - **Real-World Data Collection:** A small batch of trajectories is collected from the real-world testing environment using the current robot policy.
 - **Latent Encoding:** The collected real-world trajectories are fed into the pretrained LoopSR-enc to generate a latent variable `z_R`.
 - **Simulation Parameter Estimation:** The estimated simulation parameters (`ˆc`) are derived through a combination of two methods:
 - **Retrieval-based (`ˆc_1`):** `N` nearest neighbors to `z_R` are retrieved from the pre-collected reference dataset (`D_z`). The corresponding privileged parameters (`c_1..., c_N`) are averaged to produce `ˆc_1`. This method aims to provide robustness against out-of-distribution scenarios.
 - **Learning-based (`ˆc_2`):** The multi-head decoders (`p_e` and `p_r`), trained during the pretraining phase, directly decode `z_R` into estimated terrain distribution (`p_e(z)`) and robot parameters (`p_r(z)`), yielding `ˆc_2`.
 - **Parameter Fusion and Soft Update:** The two estimated parameter sets are fused using a weighted average: `ˆc = αˆc_1 + (1 − α)ˆc_2` (with `α = 0.8`). To ensure training stability and gradual adaptation, a soft update mechanism is applied to the current simulation parameters (`c_curr`): `c_curr = τc_curr + (1 − τ)ˆc` (with `τ = 0.7`). This prevents abrupt changes in the simulated environment.
 - **Continual Policy Training:** A new simulation environment is configured using the `c_curr` parameters. The robot policy is then continually trained in this updated simulation, leveraging the efficiency and data abundance of the simulator.
 - **Iteration:** This adaptation loop repeats, allowing the policy to continuously refine itself based on new real-world data.

**C. Network Architecture**

- **Transformer-based Encoder (LoopSR-enc `q_θ`):** Based on the Decision Transformer architecture, it takes a full trajectory (`o_1:t, a_1:t, o_2:t+1`) as input. The output latent variable `z ∈ R^32` is derived from the average pooling of latent features at each timestep and is projected onto an L2 sphere to bound the latent space.
- **Autoencoder Architecture:** A decoder (`q_ψ`) is used to reconstruct the next observation (`o_n+1`) from the latent variable `z`, current observation (`o_n`), and action (`a_n`). The training uses an L2 reconstruction loss (`L_AE`) to ensure `z` captures sufficient information from the original trajectory.
- **Contrastive Loss (`L_c`):** A supervised InfoNCE loss is applied to encourage `z` to discriminate between different terrain types. It maximizes the inner product of latent variables from trajectories belonging to the same terrain type, enhancing the interpretability and organization of the latent space.
- **Multi-head Decoder (`p_e, p_r`):** Two separate decoders are designed to disentangle different environment characteristics. `p_e` predicts terrain distribution (`c_e`) with a KL divergence loss (`L_e`), and `p_r` predicts robot proprioceptive parameters (`c_r`) with a normalized L1 loss (`L_r`). These decoders are trained only on simulation data.

LoopSR aims to mitigate this by iteratively building a digital twin of the real world and training on similar environments, thereby reducing `ϵ_ρ` and optimizing policy performance for the specific real-world context.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LoopSR was conducted through both sim-to-sim experiments in IsaacGym and real-world evaluations on a Unitree A1 robot.

**A. Sim-to-Sim Experiment Results**

- **Performance Enhancement:** LoopSR demonstrated a prominent enhancement in policy performance, closely matching or approaching the performance of an "Expert" policy (fully trained on the specific testing environment) across various challenging terrains (Stairs, Slope, Discrete). For instance, on Stairs (0.23 difficulty), LoopSR achieved a reward of 1.1330 (±0.00813) compared to the Expert's 1.2414 (±0.0354) and the Origin baseline's 0.7553 (±0.12505).
- **Outperformance over Baselines:** LoopSR consistently outperformed zero-shot sim-to-real methods such as "Origin (DreamWaQ)" and "RMA" across all tested terrain types and difficulty levels. This indicates the effectiveness of LoopSR's continuous optimization in overcoming the limitations of static sim-to-real transfer.
- **Superiority to Direct Real-World Training Approaches:** Baselines representing direct real-world training, such as "SAC" and "World Model (Dreamer-based)," largely failed to produce effective policies, typically exhibiting significantly lower rewards. This outcome supports the hypothesis that these methods struggle with fragility to shifts in state occupancy distribution, noisy inputs, or lack of privileged knowledge in real-world scenarios.
- **Continual Improvement:** The reward curve over sim-to-real loops (Fig. 3) illustrated that LoopSR policies consistently improved, surpassing the performance bottleneck observed in the "Origin" baseline. This demonstrates the framework's ability to achieve constant improvement during real-world deployments.

**B. Sim-to-Real Evaluation Results**

- **Improved Traversal Time:** On a Unitree A1 robot navigating diverse real-world terrains, LoopSR achieved faster traversal times compared to both "Origin" and "RMA" baselines. For example, traversing "Stair Up" took 4.33 (±0.11) seconds with LoopSR, compared to 5.01 (±0.18) seconds for Origin and 6.97 (±0.17) seconds for RMA. Similar improvements were observed across "Stair Down," "Grass," "Slope Down," and "Slope Up."
- **Higher Success Rate:** In challenging terrains, LoopSR exhibited a higher success rate. On "Stair Up," LoopSR achieved a 100% success rate, while Origin managed 70% and RMA 60%. For "Pit" terrain, LoopSR recorded a 90% success rate, exceeding Origin's 80% and RMA's 60%. These results indicate enhanced locomotion policy capacity and operational safety.

**C. Empirical Study on Gaits**

- **Simulated Gaits:** Visualization of robot contact phases in simulation showed that LoopSR gradually developed periodical gaits similar to the expert policy. In contrast, the baseline without refinement frequently exhibited missteps.
- **Real-World Motions:** Snapshots from real-world experiments illustrated that the "Origin" baseline produced jerky actions, unstable motions, and instances where the robot was raised into the air, triggering safety modules. The refined LoopSR policy, however, demonstrated smoother adaptation to terrain variations, ensuring operational safety. This highlights the importance of privileged knowledge (inferred by LoopSR) for navigating height-sensitive and challenging terrains.

**D. Ablation Study Results**

- **Loss Component Importance:**
 - **LSR-w/o-con (without contrastive loss):** Showed a significant drop in terrain prediction accuracy (e.g., from 92.1% to 66.5% for Stair terrain), indicating the importance of contrastive loss in building an interpretable latent space for terrain discrimination.
 - **LSR-w/o-AE (without autoencoder reconstruction loss):** Also resulted in reduced terrain accuracy and higher parameter MSE (e.g., mass MSE increased from 24.37 to 24.71), demonstrating the autoencoder structure's benefit in extracting sufficient information from trajectories.
- **Soft Update Necessity:**
 - **LSR-w/o-su (without soft update):** Drastically underperformed on demanding terrains (e.g., reward of 0.5084 on Stair compared to LoopSR's 1.2522). This outcome underscores the necessity of the soft update mechanism for stable and effective adaptation to environment parameter changes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LoopSR framework introduces a novel approach to lifelong policy adaptation for legged robots, offering several significant implications for the field of robot learning and deployment:

1. **Enhanced Real-World Performance and Robustness:** By continuously refining policies post-deployment, LoopSR addresses the trade-off inherent in domain randomization, where generalized robustness can lead to suboptimal performance in specific environments. The ability to adapt to current real-world dynamics allows robots to achieve near-optimal performance while maintaining a high degree of robustness, which is critical for practical applications.
2. **Data Efficiency in Real-World Learning:** LoopSR significantly reduces the reliance on extensive and costly real-world data collection. By using limited real-world trajectories to reconstruct a digital twin and then performing intensive policy refinement in simulation, the framework offers a more scalable and economical approach to robot learning and adaptation compared to purely real-world or model-based methods.
3. **Improved Safety and Stability:** The empirical studies and real-world evaluations demonstrate that LoopSR policies lead to smoother, more stable motions and higher success rates in challenging terrains. This directly translates to improved safety during robot operation, mitigating risks of hardware damage and undesirable behaviors, which is a paramount concern for real-world robotic systems.
4. **Lifelong Learning Capability:** The continuous looping of sim-and-real data collection and training enables robots to adapt to gradual environmental changes or concept drift over time. This lifelong learning capability allows policies to remain effective and efficient throughout a robot's operational lifespan without requiring periodic, costly retraining from scratch.
5. **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** LoopSR contributes to bridging the sim-to-real gap by introducing a dynamic, iterative mechanism. Instead of a one-shot transfer, it provides a feedback loop that constantly minimizes the discrepancy between the real and simulated environments, thereby making sim-to-real methods more adaptable and robust for diverse, evolving real-world conditions.
6. **Development of Interpretable Latent Representations:** The proposed representation learning approach, utilizing a transformer-based autoencoder with contrastive loss, effectively constructs an interpretable latent space for real-world trajectories. This method for encoding and decoding environmental parameters has potential applicability beyond legged locomotion, potentially informing task-specific or domain-specific representations in other robot learning contexts.

In summary, LoopSR represents a step towards more autonomous and adaptive robotic systems that can continuously learn and improve their locomotion capabilities in complex and changing real-world environments, contributing to the broader goal of deploying intelligent robots beyond controlled laboratory settings.
