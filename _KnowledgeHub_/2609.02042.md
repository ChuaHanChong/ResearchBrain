# Act More, Decide Less: Skill-Guided Adaptive Action Chunking for Long-Horizon LLM Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> SPACE (Skill-guided Policy with Adaptive Chunk Execution) trains an LLM agent to emit variable-length action chunks instead of one action per round. It induces two-level programmatic skills from successful trajectories, whose subskill boundaries become chunk-boundary supervision, then distills them via hybrid on-/off-policy optimization with chunk-aware advantages. On ALFWorld and ScienceWorld it lifts success 7.0-31.3% while cutting LLM decision rounds up to 78.9%.

## Problem

- ReAct-style agents issue one primitive action per LLM round, so long-horizon tasks burn many rounds on routine sequences and amplify compounding errors and repetitive loops.
- Naively widening the action space and training with standard GRPO fails: policies either collapse to single-action behavior or over-commit to excessively long chunks that harm success.
- Sparse terminal rewards carry no information about where chunk boundaries belong, and fixed-length chunking from continuous control is inadequate when granularity varies with textual context.

## Method

- ==Two-level programmatic skill induction==: successful trajectories are segmented into composite skills of ordered subskill calls, filtered by syntax checks and ==AST deduplication==, so subskill boundaries mark chunk boundaries.
- ==Hybrid rollouts== plus ==skill-to-chunk expansion==: a fraction of rollouts emit primitive chunks directly, the rest may call retrieved skills, which are unrolled into chunk-level off-policy supervision targets.
- ==Chunk-aware policy optimization==: ==GiGPO==-style trajectory advantage plus anchor-grouped step advantage drive a clipped on-policy loss and a ==self-imitation off-policy distillation loss==, combined additively.

## Results

- ALFWorld with Qwen3-4B reaches **99.2%** seen and **96.9%** unseen success, up **14.0** and **15.6 points** over the strongest baseline, with rounds falling to **3.7** and **4.4**.
- ScienceWorld with Llama-3.1-8B hits **67.2%** seen and **61.7%** unseen, roughly doubling GiGPO, while halving rounds to **5.2** and **5.8**; entropy stays higher than multi-action GRPO.
- Ablations: dropping skills lowers unseen success to **88.3%**, dropping chunk-aware advantage to **89.1%**; chunk-level Best-of-N gains **8.3 points** at **48.3 calls** versus **74.9** for primitive search.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enabling multi-action output is not the hard part; learning where chunks end is, and sparse terminal rewards alone cannot supply that boundary signal.
> - Structured skills can act purely as training-time teachers and be distilled away, so deployment needs no skill library, retrieval, or human boundary annotation.
> - Coarser decision granularity makes test-time search cheaper and more effective per call, but less frequent replanning is risky in stochastic or safety-critical environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses challenges within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Model (LLM) agents, particularly their application to long-horizon interactive tasks. Conventionally, LLM agents often employ a ReAct-style protocol, where the agent performs a single primitive action, observes the environment, and then engages in a new reasoning step to select the next action. While this step-by-step approach facilitates frequent replanning and has been refined by recent reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods (e.g., RLOO, GRPO, GiGPO), it presents limitations in long-horizon scenarios. These limitations include inefficiency due to numerous LLM decision rounds spent on routine action sequences, potential for short-sighted behavior, amplification of compounding errors, and susceptibility to repetitive loops.

The concept of generating variable-length action sequences, known as temporal abstraction or action chunking, has precedents in robotics and reinforcement learning (e.g., options framework, ACT, Q-chunking, SEAR). However, existing action chunking approaches often pertain to continuous control domains or rely on fixed-length chunks. Such methods are not directly applicable to LLM agents operating with discrete textual primitive actions, where the appropriate chunk granularity can vary dynamically with context. The paper identifies a specific failure mode in directly applying standard reinforcement learning to train LLM policies for variable-length action chunking: agents tend to either collapse into single-action behavior or over-commit to excessively long sequences without achieving task success. This failure is attributed to the sparse nature of terminal rewards, which lack the necessary signal to delineate meaningful chunk boundaries.

Furthermore, the work relates to the development of skill abstractions and skill-augmented learning in LLM agents. Previous research has explored using textual or programmatic skills to guide agents, with programmatic skills (e.g., CodeAct, Voyager, ASI) being noted for their structured, parameterizable, and verifiable nature. This paper extends this direction by leveraging programmatic skills not merely as direct action interfaces or fixed libraries, but as a training-time scaffolding mechanism to induce the temporal structure required for adaptive action chunking. The distinct contribution lies in distilling this high-level skill structure into a low-level, primitive-chunk policy, thereby enabling the agent to learn and execute variable-length action chunks without needing access to the skill library at deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and capability of LLM agents in long-horizon interactive tasks by moving beyond the conventional single-action-per-round execution paradigm. The authors identify a critical bottleneck in the standard ReAct-style protocol: while frequent replanning is possible, it is computationally inefficient and can hinder task completion in complex, multi-step scenarios.

The core motivation stems from the observation that a direct extension of standard reinforcement learning (RL) to train policies for variable-length action sequences typically fails. As illustrated in preliminary experiments, such "naive multi-action RL" policies exhibit two distinct failure modes:
1. **Single-action collapse:** The policy reverts to generating only one primitive action per LLM decision round, negating the efficiency benefits of chunking.
2. **Over-commitment to excessively long sequences:** The policy generates long action chunks that are often detrimental to task success, indicating a lack of understanding of appropriate chunk boundaries.

These failures are attributed to a common underlying problem: the sparse terminal rewards characteristic of many interactive tasks do not provide sufficient information about where meaningful action chunk boundaries should be placed. Without explicit supervision for temporal segmentation, the LLM agent cannot reliably determine which actions should be grouped, how long an open-loop execution should proceed, or when it is optimal to pause for a new observation and replan.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
- **Identify and formalize the problem** of learning effective, variable-length action chunk boundaries for LLM agents.
- **Develop a novel methodology** that provides explicit chunk-boundary supervision to an LLM policy, overcoming the limitations of sparse rewards.
- **Distill this temporal structure** into a primitive-chunk policy that can directly emit variable-length action sequences at deployment, without relying on external skill libraries or human intervention.
- **Demonstrate significant improvements** in both task success rates and decision-making efficiency (reduced LLM decision rounds) compared to existing prompting-based and RL fine-tuning baselines.
- **Analyze the learned chunking behavior** to confirm that the policy generates non-degenerate and task-appropriate action chunks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to contribute a framework for building more efficient and capable LLM agents for complex, long-horizon interactive tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named **SPACE (Skill-guided Policy with Adaptive Chunk Execution)**, operates in four main steps designed to distill temporal structure from successful trajectories into a primitive-chunk policy.

### 4. Problem Formulation

The paper frames interactive text-grounded environments as partially observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs). Traditional LLM agents sample a single primitive action `a_t` from a policy `π_θ(· | h_t)` per LLM round, where `h_t` is the interaction history. SPACE extends this by allowing the policy to generate an action chunk `u_i = (a_{i,1}, ..., a_{i,L_i})` of variable length `L_i` (between 1 and a maximum `K`) per LLM decision round `i`. This induces a semi-Markov decision process where `ℓ_i` actions are executed sequentially before the next LLM decision.

### 5. Trajectory-Induced Programmatic Skills

Recognizing that sparse terminal rewards do not adequately supervise chunk boundaries, SPACE utilizes a library `B` of programmatic skills. These skills are represented as executable code, offering structured, parameterizable, and verifiable abstractions.
- **Two-level Skill Structure:**
 - **Subskills (b_S):** Local routines `⟨d_S, ξ_S, f_S⟩` (description, arguments, function) that emit a single action chunk.
 - **Composite Skills (b_C):** Task-pattern-level programs `⟨d_C, ξ_C, f_C, c⟩` that decompose into an ordered sequence of subskill calls `c = (c_1, ..., c_M)`. The boundaries between these subskill calls serve as explicit chunk-boundary supervision.
- **Skill Induction:** Skills are generated from successful trajectories. For a successful trajectory `τ`, a language model is prompted to segment it into subtask phases and produce a composite skill along with its constituent subskills.
- **Skill Validation and Maintenance:** Generated skills undergo static checks (syntax, compilability, signature consistency) and are deduplicated via abstract syntax tree (AST) canonicalization before being added to the skill library `B`. The skill library is initialized with a small set of manually curated "cold-start" skills and updated periodically.

### 6. Hybrid Rollouts and Skill-to-Chunk Expansion

During training, SPACE alternates between two rollout modes:
- **Primitive-chunk Mode (ρ_prim fraction):** The policy `π_θ` directly emits an action chunk `u_i` in the deployment-time action space `U_K`. These trajectories form the on-policy dataset `D_on`.
- **Skill-augmented Mode (1-ρ_prim fraction):** The policy conditions on retrieved skills `B_i` and can either emit a primitive action chunk or call a composite/subskill `y_i`.
- **Skill-to-Chunk Expansion (Expand(h_i, y_i)):** Outputs from skill-augmented rollouts are converted into primitive action chunk examples for the off-policy dataset `D_off`.
 - If `y_i` is already a primitive chunk, it is retained.
 - If `y_i` calls a subskill, the subskill's emitted chunk is used.
 - If `y_i` calls a composite skill, it is unrolled into a sequence of history-chunk pairs `{(h_{i,1}, u_{i,1}), ..., (h_{i,M}, u_{i,M})}` corresponding to its subskill calls.
This expansion ensures that all training data is in the primitive chunk format, removes deployment-time dependence on the skill library, and, crucially, converts skill calls into chunk-level supervision targets that inherit the temporal structure from the programmatic skills.

### 7. Chunk-Aware Policy Optimization

Both `D_on` and `D_off` data are used for optimization.
- **Chunk-Aware Two-Level Advantages:**
 - **Trajectory-level advantage (A_traj):** Computed from the terminal reward `r(τ)`, normalized within a rollout group `G` of trajectories sharing the same task and initial environment.
 - **Step-level advantage (A_step):** A discounted return `G_{τ,i}` is assigned to each LLM decision round `i` and broadcast to all primitive actions `a_{τ,i,j}` within its chunk `u_i`. This is then normalized within groups of actions sharing the same "anchor observation" `z_{τ,i,j}`.
 - **Combined advantage (A_{τ,i,j}):** `A_traj_τ + λ_step A_step_{τ,i,j}`.
- **On-policy Learning:** A clipped surrogate objective is applied to `D_on` using the combined advantages, similar to PPO.
- **Off-policy Distillation:** A self-imitation learning-style objective is applied to `D_off`, weighting the log-likelihood of actions by their combined advantages. This is where the skill-derived chunk-boundary information is distilled.
- **Overall Objective:** The total loss `L(θ) = L_on(θ) + λ_off L_off(θ)` combines both objectives.

At deployment, the trained policy `π_θ` generates variable-length action chunks directly, without access to the skill library.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on two long-horizon interactive text-based environments: ALFWorld and ScienceWorld, using Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as backbone LLMs.

- **Superior Success-Efficiency Trade-offs:** SPACE consistently achieved higher task success rates while significantly reducing the average number of LLM decision rounds per episode across all benchmarks, splits (seen and unseen), and LLM backbones.
 - On ALFWorld, SPACE improved success by 7.0%–15.6% over the strongest baselines, reducing LLM rounds to 3.7–5.2 per episode.
 - On ScienceWorld, SPACE nearly doubled the strongest baseline's success rate (27.3% to 31.3% improvement) while roughly halving the decision rounds.
- **Failure Modes of Naive Multi-action RL:** A direct "Multi-action GRPO" baseline demonstrated the identified failure modes. With Qwen3-4B, it collapsed to single-action behavior (average ~1 action/round). With Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, it over-committed to longer chunks (~5-6 actions/round) but with substantially lower success rates. In contrast, SPACE learned a non-degenerate, balanced chunking granularity of 3-4 actions per LLM round, leading to higher success.
- **Accelerated and Stabilized Training:** SPACE exhibited faster convergence and greater training stability compared to multi-action PPO and GRPO. It achieved the final performance level of multi-action GRPO at approximately 26.6% of the training steps, continuing to improve thereafter to a higher overall success rate.
- **Enhanced Exploration:** Analysis of policy entropy revealed that SPACE maintained higher entropy during early training phases, suggesting broader exploration of action space, which then decreased as the policy became more refined. This contrasts with multi-action GRPO, which showed a quicker collapse to a narrower policy.
- **Improved Rollout Efficiency:** SPACE achieved stronger performance with fewer LLM-mediated training decisions (rollout rounds). For instance, it required only 43.60K rollout rounds to match the final performance of GRPO on ALFWorld with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing either the trajectory-induced skills component or the chunk-aware advantage estimation led to a degradation in both task success and decision efficiency, confirming the complementary importance of both elements in SPACE.
 - The ratio of primitive-chunk to skill-augmented rollouts (`ρ_prim`) was found to influence performance, with an optimal balance (e.g., 0.75 for ScienceWorld) indicating that skill guidance is beneficial but should not entirely dominate training.
- **Chunk-level Test-Time Scaling (TTS):** When N-best search was applied, chunk policies (SPACE) yielded nearly double the success rate gain compared to primitive-action policies. Moreover, despite both policy types experiencing a similar multiplicative increase in LLM calls under TTS, the chunk policy maintained a structurally lower absolute TTS cost (48.3 vs. 74.9 calls per episode), demonstrating its inherent efficiency.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SPACE represents a notable advancement in the field of LLM agents for long-horizon interactive tasks. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Increased Efficiency for LLM Agents:** The most direct impact is the substantial reduction in the number of LLM decision rounds required to complete complex tasks. By enabling agents to "act more and decide less," SPACE directly addresses the computational and latency costs associated with frequent LLM invocations. This efficiency is critical for scaling LLM agents to more challenging, real-world applications where inference costs and speed are major considerations.
- **Improved Task Success and Robustness:** Beyond efficiency, the framework consistently demonstrates higher task success rates compared to existing baselines. This suggests that learning meaningful action chunks leads to more coherent, goal-oriented behavior, mitigating issues like short-sightedness, compounding errors, and repetitive loops often observed in single-action agents.
- **Effective Learning of Temporal Abstractions:** The paper provides a principled approach to learning temporal abstractions (action chunks) without requiring explicit manual annotation of chunk boundaries. By distilling knowledge from automatically induced programmatic skills, SPACE offers a robust mechanism for LLM agents to acquire hierarchical behaviors from successful trajectories. This approach could be generalized to other domains where expert demonstrations or successful trajectories are available.
- **Bridging Reinforcement Learning and Structured Knowledge:** SPACE effectively integrates structured knowledge (programmatic skills) as a teaching signal within an RL framework. This hybrid approach overcomes limitations of purely reward-driven learning in sparse-reward settings, demonstrating how explicit temporal structure can stabilize and accelerate the training of RL policies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work identifies several avenues for future exploration. These include validating the approach in broader interactive scenarios beyond text-based environments, developing more sophisticated mechanisms for skill exploration, retrieval, and validation, and investigating the trade-offs between reduced replanning frequency and performance in highly stochastic or safety-critical environments. The insights gained from SPACE could inform the design of more sophisticated hierarchical agents and policy optimization techniques.
