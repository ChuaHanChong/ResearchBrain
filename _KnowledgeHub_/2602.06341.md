# HiWET: Hierarchical World-Frame End-Effector Tracking for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The HiWET framework enables humanoid robots to perform precise long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks by tracking end-effector movements in the world frame. It achieves a 12.4 mm end-effector position error in simulation and 12-15 mm in real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid, coordinating locomotion with manipulation to compensate for base disturbances.

## Problem

- Most existing command-driven methods use robot-centric control, leading to cumulative world-frame drift and high-frequency oscillations in end-effector precision during humanoid locomotion.
- Whole-body motion imitation approaches often lack guarantees for accurate task-space precision and require costly, dense reference trajectories.
- The inherent coupling between upper-body manipulation and lower-body locomotion in humanoids makes aggressive arm movements destabilize gait and necessitates active base transport for tasks beyond the robot's static reachable workspace.

## Method

![[2602.06341-method.png]]
*HiWET two-stage architecture: Stage 1 Tracker follows base-relative EEF commands using a pretrained KMP kinematic prior, residual Actor, History Encoder and State Estimator; Stage 2 Commander maps world-frame EEF and base-pose targets into base-frame subgoals.*

- A ==hierarchical reinforcement learning== framework is developed, comprising a high-level world-frame command policy (**Commander**) and a low-level whole-body tracking policy (**Tracker**) to decouple global reasoning from dynamic execution.
- A ==Kinematic Manifold Prior (KMP)==, a pre-trained neural network, is integrated to provide efficient, kinematically consistent upper-body joint configurations, accelerating learning for precise manipulation.
- Policies are trained using ==PPO== in GPU-accelerated simulation (==Isaac Lab==) with a ==spatial curriculum==, then deployed via ==zero-shot sim-to-real== transfer on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Results

- **HiWET** achieved a **12.4 mm** end-effector position error in simulation and **12–15 mm** RMSE in real-world tracking, outperforming SOTA baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the Kinematic Manifold Prior is critical for precise Cartesian tasks (**halving** hand error), and accurate state estimation is necessary to compensate for locomotion-induced oscillations.
- The framework demonstrated stable and precise world-frame navigation across diverse trajectories, actively modulating base position and waist posture (via the learned calpha parameter) to optimize workspace and maintain reachability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly formulating the control problem as world-frame end-effector tracking, rather than body-centric control, is essential for humanoids to achieve high precision over long operational horizons while actively compensating for base disturbances.
> - A hierarchical architecture that separates high-level global spatial reasoning (Commander) from low-level dynamic whole-body execution (Tracker) simplifies the learning problem and improves scalability for complex loco-manipulation tasks.
> - Integrating a learned Kinematic Manifold Prior into the action space provides kinematically consistent references, significantly accelerating reinforcement learning by reducing exploration dimensionality and preventing invalid behaviors, while maintaining real-time inference speed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning techniques. Earlier works like DeepMimic demonstrated the potential of RL for physics-based character skills, leading to benchmarks such as Humanoid-Gym. Motion retargeting methods, including H2O and OmniH2O, have enabled real-time human-to-humanoid teleoperation, allowing robots to mimic human movements while maintaining dynamic balance. More recently, generative approaches leveraging diffusion models and large-scale datasets, such as BeyondMimic and MaskedManipulator, have expanded the versatility of whole-body control, enabling synthesis of complex motions from sparse goals.

Command-driven frameworks, like AMO and HOVER, represent a step towards task-level control, providing abstract interfaces for operators to specify end-effector poses or joint targets. Hierarchical architectures, exemplified by R2S2 and WholeBodyVLA, further refine this by decoupling high-level planning from low-level motor control, often using latent skill interfaces or explicit trajectory-level commands.

Despite these significant strides, the paper highlights several critical challenges that HiWET aims to address:
- **Body-centric vs. World-frame Control:** Most existing command-driven methods operate in robot-centric frames (e.g., referencing commands to the robot base). While effective for mobile manipulators with stable bases (e.g., Mobile ALOHA), this approach leads to cumulative world-frame drift and high-frequency oscillations in legged humanoids. Such drift severely degrades end-effector precision in absolute (world) coordinates, which is essential for many real-world tasks.
- **Lack of Task-Space Precision Guarantees:** Whole-body motion imitation often optimizes joint-space errors, lacking theoretical guarantees for accurate task-space (end-effector) precision. Furthermore, these methods frequently require costly and dense reference trajectories.
- **Coupling of Loco-Manipulation:** The tight coupling between upper-body manipulation and lower-body locomotion in humanoids presents a persistent challenge. Aggressive arm movements can destabilize gait, and locomotion-induced impacts propagate to the end-effectors. When tasks extend beyond the robot's static reachable workspace, active base transport (locomotion) is required to maintain reachability, a coordination explicitly not addressed by body-centric formulations.

HiWET directly tackles these limitations by reformulating the problem as *world-frame end-effector tracking*. It explicitly addresses the geometric and dynamic coupling by proposing a hierarchical framework that decouples global, world-frame reasoning from local, dynamic execution. This "world-to-body" approach allows the robot to actively compensate for base disturbances and reshape its operational workspace through coordinated locomotion and manipulation, setting it apart from existing body-centric or purely imitation-based methods. The introduction of a Kinematic Manifold Prior further distinguishes HiWET by embedding kinematic intelligence directly into the action space, enhancing learning efficiency and kinematic consistency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for HiWET stems from the recognized gap in current humanoid loco-manipulation capabilities: the inability to execute precise manipulation tasks in an absolute world frame while dynamically stable, especially over long operational horizons. Current methods predominantly fall short in inherently correcting cumulative world-frame drift, coordinating whole-body actions effectively, and efficiently adapting to changes in reachable workspace.

The key objectives of the HiWET framework are:

1. **Achieve Precise World-Frame End-Effector Tracking:** To enable humanoids to perform manipulation tasks with high accuracy in global coordinates, overcoming the limitations of body-centric control that suffer from cumulative base drift and oscillations during locomotion.
2. **Decouple Global Reasoning from Dynamic Execution:** To develop a hierarchical reinforcement learning architecture that separates the strategic "where to go" (global planning) from the tactical "how to move" (dynamic execution). This decomposition is intended to simplify learning for both levels and improve scalability for long-horizon tasks.
3. **Facilitate Active Upper-Lower Body Coordination:** To design a control scheme that explicitly coordinates locomotion (base transport and height adjustment) with manipulation, allowing the robot to actively reshape its operational workspace and maintain reachability for targets beyond its static arm span.
4. **Enhance Learning Efficiency and Kinematic Consistency in Manipulation:** To introduce a Kinematic Manifold Prior (KMP) that provides kinematically consistent references for upper-body control. This prior embeds the manipulation manifold into the action space, reducing exploration dimensionality and mitigating kinematically invalid behaviors, thereby accelerating learning and improving tracking precision.
5. **Demonstrate Robustness and Real-World Applicability:** To validate the framework through extensive simulations and demonstrate its ability for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical humanoid platform, showcasing stable locomotion under diverse manipulation commands and accurate world-frame end-effector tracking.

In essence, HiWET aims to provide a comprehensive, robust, and scalable solution for humanoids to perform complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in dynamic and unstructured world environments by integrating intelligent planning with stable, precise execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HiWET employs a **hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL)** framework that distinctly separates global spatial reasoning from dynamic whole-body execution. This two-stage approach involves a high-level **world-frame command policy** (Commander) and a low-level **whole-body tracking policy** (Tracker).

**A. Hierarchical Architecture:**
- **World-Frame Command Policy ($\pi_H$)**: This high-level policy acts as a global task planner. It operates at a lower frequency (every K steps) and receives world-frame end-effector targets ($^wT^*_{\{.\}}$) and the robot's base pose in the world frame ($^wT_t^b$). Its output is a structured subgoal command ($u_t$) for the low-level policy, comprising desired base velocity ($v_{des}^b$), target body height ($h_{des}$), base-relative hand poses ($^bT_{des\{.\}}$), and a waist regularization weight ($\alpha_t$). The policy is trained using PPO and a **spatial curriculum strategy** that progressively expands the world-frame command range as tracking performance improves, starting with local neighborhoods and gradually moving to distant targets.
- **Whole-Body Tracking Policy ($\pi_L$)**: This low-level policy operates at a higher frequency (every control step) and translates the subgoals ($u_t$) from the high-level policy into joint-space targets for the robot's PD controllers. Its primary roles are to ensure dynamic stability, maintain balance, and accurately execute the commanded motions while respecting physical constraints. This policy is trained independently in the first stage and then frozen for high-level policy training.

**B. Low-Level Whole-Body Tracking Policy Details:**
- **State and Action Representation**: The policy observes the robot's proprioceptive state ($s_t$) and the subgoal command ($u_t$). It uses a **hybrid action space**:
 - For the **upper body** (arms and waist), it predicts a *residual correction* ($\Delta q_{t,up}$) that is added to a reference provided by the Kinematic Manifold Prior (KMP). This allows for dynamic adjustments while staying kinematically valid.
 - For the **lower body** (legs), it outputs *absolute joint position targets* ($q_{des,low}$) to maintain direct control over locomotion.
- **Network Architecture**: Modular design for robustness under partial observability and kinematic consistency:
 - **History Encoder**: A CNN that processes a buffer of past states and commands to capture temporal dependencies.
 - **State Estimator**: Another CNN that infers privileged information (base linear velocity, end-effector poses) not directly observable from onboard sensors, trained with an auxiliary estimation loss. This enhances the actor's observability during deployment.
 - **Actor and Critic**: Both are optimized using PPO. The actor uses the raw observation, command, privileged estimates, history embedding, and KMP reference. The critic has access to full privileged information (true base/EE poses, terrain heightmap) for stable value estimation.
- **Kinematic Manifold Prior (KMP)**: A crucial component for upper-body control. It is a **pretrained neural network** (ResNet backbone) that efficiently computes a kinematically consistent upper-body joint configuration ($\hat{q}_{t,up}$) given desired base-relative end-effector poses ($^bT_{des\{.\}}$) and a waist regularization weight ($\alpha_t$).
 - **Training Data Generation**: Millions of samples are generated by solving constrained optimization-based inverse kinematics (IK) problems using PyRoki. The $\alpha_t$ parameter, sampled from [0.1, 10], encourages the KMP to capture configurations ranging from rigid-torso reaching to whole-body lunges, modulating torso engagement.
 - **Importance Sampling**: The KMP training and policy learning processes use an **importance-sampled dataset** to avoid kinematically infeasible or ill-conditioned end-effector targets. This dataset is curated by pruning unreachable poses (based on IK error) and favoring high-manipulability configurations (based on manipulability index). A mixture distribution combines this prioritized sampling with uniform sampling to ensure full workspace coverage.
- **Reward Formulation**: A comprehensive reward function encourages:
 - Accurate command tracking (base velocity, angular velocity, body height, end-effector poses).
 - **KMP Reference Tracking**: A term that penalizes deviations from the KMP's recommended upper-body joint configuration, reinforcing kinematic consistency.
 - **Knee-Aware Base Height Tracking**: An asymmetric reward term that weights height errors based on knee joint margins, activated during zero-velocity to stabilize standing/squatting.
 - Regularization terms for joint velocity, action rate, and torque variation for smooth, hardware-friendly motions.

**C. World-Frame Command Policy Details:**
- **Action Space**: The high-level policy's action *is* the subgoal command ($u_t$) for the low-level policy, including the waist regularization weight ($\alpha_t$), which allows the high-level policy to actively control the robot's posture and CoM stability based on the global task.
- **Reward Formulation**: Designed to optimize global spatial consistency and base positioning:
 - **Workspace Optimization**: Penalizes planar distance between the robot base and active end-effector targets, and rewards alignment of base heading and desired velocity with the target center, encouraging efficient base transport.
 - **Precise End-Effector Tracking**: Direct rewards for minimizing world-frame pose error between current and target end-effectors.
 - **Stability and Regularization**: Penalizes action rates, high-frequency end-effector jitter, and deviations of inactive arms from neutral poses.

**D. Simulation and Real-World Deployment:**
- Policies are trained in Isaac Lab (a GPU-accelerated simulation framework).
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer is performed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot (29 DoF).
- For real-world world-frame tracking, global pose estimation relies on a head-mounted Livox Mid-360 LiDAR and IMU using Fast-LIO2, with base position updated via forward kinematics.

This multi-faceted approach, combining hierarchical control, learned kinematic priors, and intelligent reward shaping, enables HiWET to tackle the complexities of long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of HiWET, encompassing simulation studies, ablation analysis, KMP benchmarking, and zero-shot real-world deployment, yielded several key findings:

**1. Low-Level Tracking Performance and Ablation Studies (Table I):**
- **Superior Performance:** HiWET significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art HOMIE baseline, achieving a 20% reduction in base linear velocity RMSE and demonstrating superior body height consistency. HiWET achieves a precise 12.4 mm end-effector position error in simulation.
- **Criticality of KMP:** Ablating the Kinematic Manifold Prior (HiWET w/o KMP) results in the most significant performance degradation, doubling the hand error to 25.2 mm with 5 times higher variance. This powerfully demonstrates that learning precise Cartesian tasks is substantially harder without explicit kinematic guidance.
- **Importance of State Estimation:** Removing the State Estimator (HiWET w/o State Est.) leads to a nearly 10 mm increase in tracking error, confirming the necessity of accurate end-effector feedback for compensating locomotion-induced oscillations and maintaining precision.
- **Benefits of Importance Sampling:** Training without importance sampling (HiWET w/o IS) results in degraded and less consistent bimanual coordination, highlighting that focusing training on functionally relevant and manipulable workspace regions is crucial for robust learning.
- The full HiWET framework achieves the highest precision and robustness (lowest variance) as a general-purpose loco-manipulation tracker.

**2. World-Frame Navigation and Long-Horizon Tracking (Figures 3, 4, 5):**
- **High Precision Trajectory Tracking:** HiWET demonstrates high success rates and remarkably low tracking errors across diverse and complex geometric trajectories (star, heart, circle, spiral, rectangle) in the world frame. Qualitative results (Figure 4) show errors consistently below 5 mm for HiWET, while ablated variants exhibit significant distortions and oscillations.
- **Adaptive Waist Modulation (alpha):** The ability of the high-level policy to modulate the waist regularization weight ($\alpha$) is crucial. The `HiWET w/ Fixed α` variant shows degraded performance in tasks requiring extensive reaching, as it cannot dynamically trade off between reachability and CoM stability by exploiting waist redundancy.
- **Active Base Mobility and Positioning:** HiWET exhibits the most direct and stable base repositioning paths in 8-directional navigation tasks (Figure 5). It achieves the lowest mean final base positioning error (0.101 m) with significantly less variance. This confirms that HiWET's strategy treats the base as an active degree of freedom for workspace optimization, enabling effective loco-manipulation coordination.

**3. Kinematic Manifold Prior Benchmarking (Figure 6):**
- **Exceptional Inference Efficiency:** The KMP variants (KMP-S, KMP-L) achieve orders of magnitude speedup in inference latency compared to optimization-based Inverse Kinematics (PyRoki). KMP-L provides over 5x speedup at single-sample and maintains millisecond-level latency even at large batch sizes, which is critical for real-time hierarchical control loops.
- **Sufficient Reconstruction Accuracy:** KMP-L achieves a median position error below 15 mm and orientation error below 5 degrees, which is sufficient for accurate whole-body control, especially when refined by residual learning. Importance sampling significantly boosts KMP accuracy over uniform training, demonstrating its effectiveness in capturing functional manipulation manifolds.

**4. Real-World Hardware Experiments (Figures 1, 7, Table II):**
- **Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The low-level tracking policy successfully transfers from simulation to the physical Unitree G1 robot without any additional fine-tuning, demonstrating its robustness and generalizability.
- **Stable Dynamic Loco-Manipulation:** The robot maintains stable locomotion even under demanding conditions with varying and asymmetric arm configurations, including dynamic circular trajectories, effectively rejecting disturbances caused by rapid limb movements (Figure 7).
- **Accurate World-Frame Real-World Tracking:** HiWET achieves the lowest real-world tracking errors for circular (12 mm RMSE) and square (15 mm RMSE) trajectories (Table II), outperforming its ablated counterparts. This underscores the effectiveness of explicit world-frame reasoning and the robust low-level execution in physical hardware.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that HiWET provides a highly effective and robust framework for precise, long-horizon world-frame end-effector tracking, successfully integrating advanced reinforcement learning techniques with kinematic priors for humanoids.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HiWET represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid loco-manipulation, addressing fundamental challenges that have long limited the practical deployment of humanoids in complex, unstructured environments.

**1. Enabling Truly World-Frame Task Execution:** The most profound impact of HiWET is its explicit formulation of the control problem as *world-frame end-effector tracking*. By inherently compensating for cumulative world-frame drift and actively coordinating locomotion with manipulation, HiWET moves beyond the limitations of body-centric control. This enables humanoids to perform tasks requiring absolute precision over large operational areas, tasks that were previously intractable due to the instability and imprecision introduced by base motion.

**2. Robust and Scalable Hierarchical Control:** The hierarchical reinforcement learning framework effectively decouples global planning from dynamic execution. This architectural clarity simplifies the learning problem for both levels, leading to more robust policies and improved scalability for long-horizon tasks. This paradigm can be adopted for other complex robotic systems where planning and execution occur at different timescales and abstraction levels.

**3. Innovation with Kinematic Manifold Prior (KMP):** The introduction of an efficient, learned Kinematic Manifold Prior in a residual action space is a notable methodological contribution. KMP significantly accelerates learning by providing kinematically consistent references, reducing exploration dimensionality, and preventing kinematically invalid behaviors. Its high inference speed makes it suitable for real-time control, showcasing the power of integrating learned models with traditional kinematics for improved efficiency and performance.

**4. Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of the low-level tracking policy to a physical Unitree G1 robot is a crucial validation. This demonstrates the robustness of the trained policies to real-world dynamics and hardware characteristics, reducing the costly and time-consuming need for real-world data collection and fine-tuning. This success paves the way for faster iteration and deployment of complex humanoid skills.

**5. Broader Applications for Humanoid Robots:** By enabling precise and stable loco-manipulation in the world frame, HiWET significantly expands the potential applications for humanoid robots. These include:
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** Precise grasping and placement of objects on shelves or conveyers while navigating dynamic environments.
 - **Healthcare:** Assisting patients or performing intricate tasks in hospital settings.
 - **Disaster Response:** Operating tools and manipulating debris in hazardous or inaccessible areas.
 - **Construction:** Performing assembly or repair tasks that require coordinated movement and manipulation over extended spaces.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors candidly acknowledge limitations, including reliance on LiDAR-based localization accuracy, evaluation on relatively small-scale trajectories, single-arm high-level experiments, and the absence of contact-rich manipulation. These point to clear and promising avenues for future research, such as integrating visual perception and force control for dexterous, contact-aware, and bilateral manipulation. This iterative process of identifying limitations and proposing solutions underscores the vibrant and evolving nature of humanoid robotics research.

In conclusion, HiWET offers a principled and effective framework that pushes the boundaries of humanoid capabilities, bringing them closer to versatile, autonomous operation in human-centric environments. Its contributions in world-frame reasoning, hierarchical control, and learned kinematic priors are poised to influence future research and development in advanced robotics.
