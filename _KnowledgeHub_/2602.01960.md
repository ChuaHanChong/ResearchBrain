# Grounding Generated Videos in Feasible Plans via World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces Grounding Video Plans with World Models (GVP-WM), a method for converting physically inconsistent video-generated plans into dynamically feasible action sequences for embodied agents. It accomplishes this by performing latent-space trajectory optimization within a pre-trained action-conditioned world model during inference, improving execution success on manipulation and navigation tasks.

## Problem

- Large-scale video generative models often produce visually compelling plans that contain physical inconsistencies (e.g., object teleportation, motion blur), making them difficult to execute by robotic systems.
- Directly mapping these flawed video plans to actions using inverse dynamics models frequently results in execution failures due to the models' inability to correct physical violations.
- Existing methods for grounding video plans either assume physical feasibility or require extensive training and environment interaction, lacking an efficient test-time solution for general applicability.

## Method

- **GVP-WM** uses a ==conditional video generative model== to produce a high-level visual plan, which can be optionally fine-tuned for domain adaptation.
- It employs a pre-trained ==action-conditioned world model== to learn and represent the underlying dynamics of the environment in a compact ==latent space==.
- The method formulates grounding as a ==video-guided latent collocation== problem, optimizing both ==latent states== and actions to align with the video plan while adhering to the world model's dynamic constraints using an ==Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM)==.

## Results

- GVP-WM achieved superior performance over direct inverse dynamics models (UniPi) and unguided Model Predictive Control (MPC) baselines, with a success rate of **0.80** on Push-T with domain-adapted video plans where UniPi failed.
- The method demonstrated robustness to physically inconsistent video guidance, maintaining high success rates (e.g., **0.82** with MB-10 blur) even when temporal inconsistencies like motion blur were introduced, unlike UniPi which saw performance drop from **0.52** to **0.03**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of video-based initialization and the scale-invariant alignment loss, and showed that the latent collocation approach is critical for recovering dynamically feasible trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding video-generated plans requires jointly optimizing both latent states and actions, as directly fixing latent states to the video plan leads to performance collapse, indicating the original video is not dynamically consistent.
> - A scale-invariant video alignment loss is crucial for robustly guiding the latent-space optimization by focusing on semantic similarity and mitigating distribution shifts between generated video frames and world model latents.
> - Initializing the latent trajectory from the encoded video plan provides a strong semantic prior, significantly enhancing the efficiency and success of the optimization process, especially with higher-quality video guidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI has seen significant advancements, particularly with large-scale video generative models capable of synthesizing realistic and temporally coherent videos. These models have demonstrated emergent capabilities in visual reasoning and can generate "expert-like" video plans for complex tasks, leveraging their ability to follow prompts, maintain object permanence, and infer physical consistency. This development suggests their potential as zero-shot visual planners for applications such as robotics.

However, a critical challenge arises when attempting to translate these video-generated plans into executable actions in real-world or simulated environments. A common limitation is that generated videos frequently contain physical inconsistencies, such as object teleportation, motion blur, or violations of rigid-body dynamics. These inconsistencies often lead to failures when actions are directly inferred from the video plans, particularly under out-of-distribution conditions where the generative model's training data does not fully reflect the target environment's dynamics.

Prior work has explored various approaches to bridge this gap. Some methods, such as those leveraging inverse dynamics models (e.g., UniPi), attempt to directly map video trajectories to actions. However, these methods are sensitive to the physical feasibility of the input video plan. Hierarchical approaches have used video-generated plans as high-level guidance or subgoals for model-predictive control (MPC) or hierarchical reinforcement learning. These typically assume the visual subgoals are feasible during execution, without explicit mechanisms to account for or correct inaccuracies in the underlying video plans. More recently, efforts have been made to ground video-generated plans during the *policy learning* phase, using goal-conditioned exploration to ensure feasibility. This, however, necessitates additional training and interaction with the environment.

This research aims to address the aforementioned gap by proposing a method that grounds physically inconsistent video-generated plans into feasible action sequences *at test-time*, without requiring further environment interaction or policy training. It builds upon the capabilities of pre-trained action-conditioned world models, which learn compact latent representations of environment dynamics, making planning in pixel space more tractable. The work positions itself as a critical step toward enabling robust and practical utilization of generative video models for autonomous planning and control by focusing on ensuring dynamic feasibility through learned world-model dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a planning method, named Grounding Video Plans with World Models (GVP-WM), which can translate visually rich but potentially physically infeasible video-generated plans into dynamically feasible action sequences. This translation is achieved by leveraging a pre-trained action-conditioned world model.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations within the current landscape of generative AI and robotics:

1. **Inherent Limitations of Video Generative Models for Physical Planning:** Despite their advancements in generating realistic and temporally coherent visual sequences, large-scale video models often fail to capture the precise, low-level physical dynamics of specific environments, especially when operating out-of-distribution from their training data. This leads to generated video plans that may depict object teleportation, incorrect physics, or motion inconsistencies (e.g., motion blur), rendering them unexecutable in real-world systems.
2. **Failure of Direct Video-to-Action Mapping:** Directly inferring actions from such flawed video plans using inverse dynamics models frequently results in execution failures. These models are not inherently equipped to correct physical violations present in their visual input.
3. **Need for Test-Time Feasibility Guarantees:** Existing hierarchical planning methods that use video as guidance or subgoals often assume the visual information is physically feasible. While some methods can account for feasibility during policy learning, this requires extensive additional training and interaction with the target environment. There is a clear need for a method that can perform this grounding *at test-time*, thus offering a more efficient and adaptable solution for leveraging pre-trained models.
4. **Leveraging Action-Conditioned World Models:** Action-conditioned world models have demonstrated success in learning the underlying dynamics of environments in a compact latent space. These models provide a robust mechanism for simulating and predicting future states given actions, making them suitable for enforcing physical constraints. The motivation is to combine the high-level semantic guidance from video models with the low-level dynamic feasibility provided by world models.
5. **Improved Robustness and Performance:** By explicitly projecting video guidance onto the manifold of dynamically feasible latent trajectories, the objective is to create a planning system that is more robust to imperfect video inputs and capable of recovering executable plans for complex, long-horizon tasks where traditional unguided planners might struggle or be computationally expensive.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of large-scale video generative models as versatile planners for embodied agents by systematically addressing and correcting the physical inconsistencies inherent in their outputs through principled optimization within a learned dynamic model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GVP-WM (Grounding Video Plans with World Models) method operates in two main stages: first, generating a video plan, and second, grounding this plan into a feasible action sequence using latent-space trajectory optimization under a pre-trained action-conditioned world model. The overall process is designed to be executed at test-time.

#### 3.1. Video Plan Generation

The process begins by generating a video plan ($\tau_{vid}$) from an initial visual observation ($o_0$) and a goal observation ($o_g$). This is performed using a conditional video generative model, such as an image-to-video (I2V) diffusion model (e.g., Wan2.1-FLF2V-14B). These models can synthesize temporally coherent visual transitions between the start and goal frames. The video generation can optionally be fine-tuned on a small dataset of domain-specific demonstrations ($D_{demo}$) using techniques like LoRA to improve physical consistency within the target environment. If the video model and world model operate at different temporal resolutions, temporal subsampling is applied to align the video plan with the world model's frame skip.

#### 3.2. World Model

GVP-WM relies on a pre-trained action-conditioned latent world model (e.g., DINO-WM). This world model comprises:
- An **encoder ($E_\phi$)**: Maps high-dimensional visual observations to compact latent states ($z_t = E_\phi(s_t)$). The visual encoder is typically frozen and pre-trained (e.g., DINOv2-ViT-S/14) to improve robustness to distribution shifts between video-generated frames and in-distribution world model data.
- A **transition function ($f_\psi$)**: Predicts future latent states conditioned on a history of latent states and actions ($z_{t+1} = f_\psi(z_{t-H:t}, a_{t-H:t})$).
- A **decoder**: Used for visualization purposes only, not during planning.

#### 3.3. Video-Guided Latent Collocation

The core of GVP-WM is its formulation of grounding as a constrained trajectory optimization problem in the latent space of the world model. This approach is termed "video-guided latent collocation." Unlike methods that implicitly determine intermediate states via forward simulation, latent collocation treats both latent states ($z_{0:T}$) and actions ($a_{0:T-1}$) as explicit decision variables to be optimized.

The objective is to find an optimal latent trajectory ($Z^*, A^*$) that minimizes a combined cost function subject to the world model's dynamics:
- **Video Alignment Loss ($L_{vid}$):** Measures the semantic similarity between the optimized latent states ($z_t$) and the latent representations of the video plan ($z_{vid_t}$). This is defined as the squared L2 norm between their L2-normalized versions ($\phi(z) = z/||z||_2$), effectively penalizing angular deviation while being invariant to magnitude changes (which can occur due to distribution shift from generated videos).
- **Terminal Goal Loss ($L_{goal}$):** Measures the squared error between the final latent state ($z_T$) and the latent representation of the visual goal ($z_g$).
- **Action Regularization:** A term (e.g., $||a_t||^2_2$) to regularize the magnitude of actions.

These objectives are combined with hyperparameters ($\lambda_v, \lambda_g, \lambda_r$). The optimization is subject to:
- **World Model Dynamics Constraints:** $z_{t+1} = f_\psi(z_{t-H:t}, a_{t-H:t})$, which enforce that the optimized trajectory must follow the learned physics of the environment.
- **Action Space Constraints:** $a_{min} \preceq a_t \preceq a_{max}$, ensuring actions are within feasible bounds.

Video guidance is integrated through:
1. **Initialization:** The latent trajectory for optimization is initialized directly from the encoded video plan ($z_{vid_{0:T}}$). This provides a strong semantic prior.
2. **Loss Term:** The $L_{vid}$ term continuously guides the optimized trajectory towards the video plan's semantics.

#### 3.4. Optimization via Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM)

To solve this non-linear constrained optimization problem, GVP-WM employs the Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM). ALM constructs an augmented Lagrangian objective function ($L_\rho$) that combines the original cost terms with penalty terms for violating dynamic constraints, scaled by Lagrange multipliers ($\Lambda$) and a penalty parameter ($\rho$). The optimization alternates between:
- **Primal Updates:** Gradient-based updates (e.g., Adam) for the latent states (Z) and actions (A) to minimize $L_\rho$.
- **Dual Updates:** Updates for the Lagrange multipliers ($\Lambda$).
A geometric continuation schedule for $\rho$ is used, progressively increasing the penalty over outer iterations. This strategy prioritizes semantic alignment with the video plan in early iterations and gradually enforces stricter adherence to world-model dynamics. Smooth action reparameterization is used to handle bounded action constraints.

#### 3.5. Visual Model Predictive Control (MPC)

The optimized action sequence is executed using a receding-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) strategy. At each timestep, GVP-WM solves the video-guided latent collocation problem over a finite planning horizon (T), generating an optimal latent trajectory and action sequence ($Z^*, A^*$). The first K actions from $A^*$ are then executed in the environment. This receding-horizon approach, by re-optimizing at each step based on the current state, helps mitigate error accumulation over long horizons. Optionally, a sampling-based refinement step can be applied to the optimal action sequence to further improve performance by evaluating candidate action sequences via open-loop rollouts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of GVP-WM was conducted across two distinct control environments, Push-T (a contact-rich 2D manipulation task) and Wall (a 2D visual navigation task), and under various conditions of video plan quality and planning horizons.

#### 4.1. Grounding I2V-Generated Video Plans

- **Comparison with Baselines:** GVP-WM demonstrated superior performance compared to both direct video-to-action inverse dynamics models (UniPi) and MPC-based planners without video guidance (MPC-CEM, MPC-GD).
 - **Versus UniPi:** GVP-WM significantly outperformed UniPi, particularly under zero-shot (WAN-0S) and domain-adapted (WAN-FT) video guidance. For instance, UniPi often failed completely on Push-T with generated video guidance, while GVP-WM achieved success rates of 0.80 for T=25 with WAN-FT. This highlights GVP-WM's ability to recover feasible actions from physically inconsistent video plans, a task where direct execution models fail.
 - **Versus Unguided MPC:** When provided with domain-adapted video guidance (WAN-FT), GVP-WM generally surpassed MPC-CEM and MPC-GD across most settings. This indicates the benefit of semantic guidance when available. Under zero-shot guidance, where video quality was poor, MPC-CEM sometimes performed better than GVP-WM, suggesting that highly unreliable guidance can hinder performance relative to unguided methods.
- **Efficiency:** GVP-WM required substantially less planning time per episode compared to MPC-CEM (e.g., 86s vs 277s for Push-T T=25), demonstrating a computational advantage despite its iterative optimization.
- **Potential with Oracle Guidance:** With in-distribution expert video plans (ORACLE), GVP-WM achieved near-perfect success rates (e.g., 0.98 for Push-T T=25, 1.00 for Wall T=25/50), underscoring its potential when supplied with high-quality, physically consistent guidance.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Motion Blur

- GVP-WM exhibited significant robustness to temporally inconsistent video guidance induced by synthetic motion blur. While UniPi's performance severely degraded with increasing blur (e.g., success for Push-T T=25 dropped from 0.52 to 0.03 with MB-10 blur), GVP-WM maintained high success rates (e.g., 0.98 to 0.82 under similar conditions). This result indicates GVP-WM's capacity to correct physical constraint violations present in degraded video inputs.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

- Qualitative examples demonstrated that zero-shot I2V-generated plans frequently contained physical inconsistencies, such as morphological drift of objects, violations of rigid-body physics, and spatial bilocation of the agent.
- GVP-WM was observed to successfully recover feasible execution trajectories in some cases despite these flaws (e.g., for certain instances of morphological drift and spatial bilocation), by imposing the world model's dynamics. However, severe semantic drift or fundamental physics violations could still lead to failures.
- Domain-adapted video guidance, obtained by fine-tuning the generative model on a small dataset, produced more physically consistent plans, which GVP-WM could reliably follow to achieve task success.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Video Guidance's Role:**
 - Initializing the latent trajectory from the video plan was critical, especially for domain-adapted and oracle guidance. Removing this initialization led to substantial performance drops.
 - The scale-invariant video alignment loss consistently improved performance across all video sources, with greater benefits when video guidance was more reliable.
 - Completely removing video guidance (random initialization, no video loss) could sometimes improve performance with very poor zero-shot guidance but harmed performance with higher-quality guidance.
- **Latent Collocation Necessity:** Fixing latent states directly to the video plan (i.e., optimizing only actions, without allowing states to deviate) caused performance to collapse. This finding strongly supports the necessity of jointly optimizing both latent states and actions to ensure dynamic feasibility, as raw video-generated latent trajectories are often not dynamically consistent.
- **Optimization Details:** The use of smooth action reparameterization in the ALM solver was found to be more effective than projected gradient descent. Receding-horizon MPC and local refinement steps contributed positively to performance by mitigating model mismatch and refining trajectories.
- **Scale-Invariance:** Replacing the scale-invariant alignment loss with a standard MSE loss significantly reduced performance, confirming the importance of handling magnitude drift in latent representations derived from video models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Grounding Generated Videos in Feasible Plans via World Models" holds several significant implications and potential impacts for the fields of robotics, autonomous systems, and generative AI.

1. **Bridging Semantic and Physical Gaps:** This work addresses a critical challenge in leveraging large-scale video generative models for real-world tasks: the discrepancy between high-level semantic video generation and low-level physical feasibility. By providing a principled method to ground visually guided plans in physically consistent actions, GVP-WM enhances the practical applicability of video models in domains requiring adherence to physical laws.
2. **Enabling Robust Zero-Shot Planning:** GVP-WM demonstrates that even physically inconsistent or temporally degraded video plans, which are common outputs of zero-shot generative models operating out-of-distribution, can be transformed into executable action sequences. This robustness to imperfections in video guidance is crucial for deploying generative models in novel or diverse environments without extensive retraining.
3. **Test-Time Adaptability:** A key advantage of GVP-WM is its ability to perform grounding at inference time. Unlike methods that require policy learning or extensive environment interaction during training, GVP-WM leverages pre-trained world models and video generative models. This test-time adaptation capability makes it more flexible and efficient for diverse applications, allowing for rapid deployment of visual planning strategies.
4. **Improved Efficiency for Guided Planning:** By integrating video guidance as a semantic prior for latent trajectory initialization and alignment, GVP-WM effectively reduces the search space for feasible plans. This contributes to faster convergence and better performance compared to unguided model-predictive control methods, particularly in long-horizon planning scenarios.
5. **Enhanced Robot Control and Manipulation:** The demonstrated success in tasks like Push-T (contact-rich manipulation) and Wall (visual navigation) suggests a direct impact on robot control. GVP-WM could enable robots to interpret human-generated video demonstrations or high-level visual goals more effectively, translating them into precise, physically compliant actions. This has implications for increasing autonomy and versatility in robotics.
6. **Future Directions for Generative Models:** The findings encourage further development of both video generative models and world models. As these foundational models continue to improve in their fidelity and understanding of physics, GVP-WM provides a robust framework for their integration, suggesting a synergistic path towards more capable embodied AI systems. For instance, better domain-adapted video models could further enhance GVP-WM's performance, while more accurate world models could improve its grounding capabilities.
7. **Contribution to Model-Based Reinforcement Learning and Control:** The method's reliance on latent-space trajectory optimization within world models further validates and expands the utility of model-based approaches. It offers a novel way to combine explicit semantic guidance with implicit learned dynamics, potentially inspiring new hybrid control strategies.

While GVP-WM has limitations, such as its reliance on the accuracy of learned world-model dynamics and the computational cost of iterative test-time optimization, its contributions are significant. It offers a tangible solution for converting high-level visual intent into low-level, physically consistent actions, propelling the integration of generative AI with embodied intelligence towards more robust and capable systems.
