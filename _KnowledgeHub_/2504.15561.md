# SPECI: Skill Prompts based Hierarchical Continual Imitation Learning for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences developed SPECI, a hierarchical continual imitation learning framework for robot manipulation that learns and reuses skills across tasks. The approach consistently achieved superior performance on the LIBERO benchmark, notably yielding negative Negative Backward Transfer, indicating that learning new tasks enhanced performance on previous ones.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning for robot manipulation suffers from catastrophic forgetting when learning new tasks sequentially, limiting lifelong adaptation.
- Existing continual imitation learning (CIL) methods often neglect the hierarchical and compositional nature of robot manipulation skills or rely on rigid, manually defined skills, hindering effective knowledge transfer.
- Achieving strong bidirectional knowledge transfer (both forward transfer to new tasks and backward transfer to old tasks) in complex, long-horizon robot manipulation remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **SPECI** proposes a hierarchical continual imitation learning architecture with ==Multimodal Perception==, High-level Skill Inference, and Low-level Action Execution modules.
- It incorporates a dynamic, expandable skill codebook that autonomously acquires and freezes skill vectors, coupled with an attention-driven mechanism for selecting and combining top-C relevant skills.
- A mode approximation mechanism, using CP decomposition, is applied to transformer attention parameters to facilitate efficient task-specific adaptation while preserving and sharing global knowledge.

## Results

- SPECI achieved the highest AUC across all four LIBERO task suites, outperforming competing methods like LOTUS by **9%** to **14%** under the ER paradigm and showing up to **27%** superiority on complex tasks under PACKNET.
- The framework consistently yielded negative Negative Backward Transfer (NBT) scores across all task suites under the PACKNET paradigm, indicating that learning new tasks enhanced performance on previously learned ones.
- SPECI demonstrated strong Forward Transfer (FWT), outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by average margins of 9-**10%** on short-horizon tasks and a significant **25%** on long-horizon tasks, effectively reusing learned skills and knowledge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot manipulation benefits from a hierarchical policy structure that explicitly models skill-level and task-level knowledge, enabling more efficient and robust learning.
> - Autonomously learned and dynamically expandable skill codebooks, combined with attention-driven selection, allow for flexible skill acquisition and reuse, reducing reliance on manual definitions.
> - Mode approximation techniques, like CP decomposition, effectively balance task-specific learning with shared knowledge, leading to positive backward transfer where new learning enhances prior task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is striving towards creating general robotic agents capable of performing diverse tasks in dynamic, unstructured environments. This ambitious goal necessitates robots that can continually adapt and learn throughout their operational lifespan. This paper specifically addresses the challenges within **Continual Imitation Learning (CIL)** for robot manipulation, positioning itself at the intersection of imitation learning, continual learning, and hierarchical control.

Traditional imitation learning (IL), while effective for learning from human demonstrations, typically relies on static training datasets. This approach is fundamentally limited by **catastrophic forgetting**, a pervasive issue in deep neural networks where learning new tasks overwrites knowledge of previously learned ones. CIL emerges as a crucial paradigm to overcome this by enabling incremental task adaptation while preserving learned knowledge.

However, existing CIL methods, often adapted from image classification or natural language processing, frequently fall short in robot manipulation. The authors highlight two key limitations:
1. **Neglect of Intrinsic Skill Characteristics:** Many CIL methods overlook the hierarchical and compositional nature of robot manipulation, treating tasks as monolithic units rather than sequences of reusable skills.
2. **Reliance on Rigid or Manually Defined Skills:** Current skill-based imitation learning approaches often depend on pre-defined skill libraries or require extensive manual annotation, limiting flexibility and autonomous adaptation. This leads to suboptimal cross-task knowledge transfer and a trade-off between skill granularity and learning complexity.

SPECI directly addresses these gaps. It proposes an end-to-end hierarchical CIL policy architecture that explicitly models and leverages **skill-level knowledge transfer** alongside task-level knowledge. By introducing an **expandable, autonomously learned skill codebook** and an **attention-driven skill selection mechanism**, SPECI moves beyond rigid skill definitions, enabling implicit skill acquisition and reuse. This contrasts with prior works like BUDS, which require prior skill dataset partitioning, or ACT, which follows a two-stage paradigm.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the broader challenge of **knowledge transfer** (both forward and backward) in continual learning. Forward transfer refers to how effectively prior knowledge aids in learning new tasks, while backward transfer measures the retention of previously learned knowledge. By incorporating a **mode approximation mechanism** inspired by parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), SPECI aims to enhance task-level knowledge sharing and isolation, mitigating catastrophic forgetting more effectively than general CL techniques (e.g., regularization, replay, parameter isolation) or even existing CIL methods like LOTUS or those based on LoRA (TAIL), which may suffer from plasticity degradation.

In essence, SPECI's contribution lies in unifying multimodal perception, hierarchical skill reasoning, and robust continual learning mechanisms. It pushes the boundaries of robot learning by offering a comprehensive framework for lifelong adaptation that dynamically acquires and reuses skills, leading to more generalizable and adaptable robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptable robotic agent capable of performing a wide range of manipulation tasks in dynamic, unstructured environments throughout its operational lifespan. To achieve this, the authors identify several key motivations and specific objectives:

- **Addressing the Limitations of Traditional Imitation Learning (IL):** Traditional IL methods, relying on static training, are ill-suited for real-world scenarios where robots must continually adapt to evolving objects, scenes, and tasks. They suffer from accumulated errors in long-horizon tasks and are prone to catastrophic forgetting when learning sequentially.
- **Overcoming Shortcomings of Existing Continual Imitation Learning (CIL):** While CIL addresses incremental adaptation, current methods often overlook the inherent "skill" characteristics of robot manipulation or depend on manually defined and rigid skills. This leads to inefficient and incomplete knowledge transfer between tasks. The goal is to move beyond these limitations to enable more effective cross-task knowledge transfer.
- **Enabling Hierarchical Reasoning for Robot Manipulation:** Robots need hierarchical capabilities: understanding environments at the task level, planning motions at the skill level, and executing actions precisely. The authors aim to design an end-to-end hierarchical CIL policy architecture that naturally decomposes robot motion into instructive skills and concrete actions, thereby improving performance in complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Facilitating Efficient Skill-Level Knowledge Transfer:** A core motivation is to allow for **implicit continual skill acquisition and reuse**. This means the robot should be able to automatically discover, learn, and reuse component skills without manual definition or rigid abstraction rules. This will enhance efficiency and flexibility, particularly for complex tasks involving combinations of actions.
- **Enhancing Task-Level Knowledge Transfer (Declarative Knowledge):** Beyond skills (procedural knowledge), the model needs to effectively leverage and transfer declarative knowledge (e.g., about objects, spatial relationships). The objective is to incorporate mechanisms that balance task-specific learning with task-sharing knowledge, mitigating catastrophic forgetting and promoting positive backward transfer.
- **Achieving Superior Bidirectional Knowledge Transfer:** The ultimate goal is a system that excels in both **forward transfer** (improving new task learning using prior knowledge) and **backward transfer** (retaining or even enhancing performance on previous tasks after learning new ones). This holistic measure of lifelong learning ability is critical for truly versatile robots.

In summary, the paper proposes SPECI to create a more intelligent, adaptable, and robust robot manipulation system by integrating a novel hierarchical architecture with dynamic skill learning and parameter-efficient knowledge management within a continual learning framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SPECI (Skill Prompts-based HiErarchical Continual Imitation Learning) is an end-to-end hierarchical CIL policy architecture designed to enable lifelong adaptation for robot manipulation. It decomposes the robot control problem into three tightly-coupled, hierarchical modules: Multimodal Perception and Fusion, High-level Skill Inference, and Low-level Action Execution. Furthermore, it incorporates a novel mode approximation mechanism to enhance knowledge transfer.

**A. Hierarchical Policy Architecture**
The policy is formulated as $\pi = \pi_H \cdot \pi_L$, separating high-level skill selection ($\pi_H$) from low-level motor control ($\pi_L$) through a latent skill variable $z_t$. This contrasts with traditional flat policies, aiming to reduce complexity in the action space and improve knowledge transfer.

**B. Multimodal Perception and Fusion Module**
This module is responsible for processing heterogeneous sensory inputs to create a unified state representation:
- **Linguistic Information:** Natural language task descriptions (e.g., "put the yellow and white mug in the microwave and close it") are encoded using a frozen **CLIP** pretrained text encoder. The output is converted into tokens via an MLP.
- **Visual Information:** Both **wrist-view** (relative end-effector to object) and **workspace-view** (overall scene) images are processed by a **pretrained ResNet-18 encoder**. Unlike the text encoder, the visual encoder is fine-tuned during policy learning to adapt to the robot manipulation scene.
- **Proprioceptive State:** Joint angles and gripper status are processed by a trained MLP.
- **Fusion:** Language tokens are integrated into the ResNet features using **FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation)**, guiding image embedding extraction based on task goals.
- **Temporal Encoding:** All fused multimodal features are aggregated into a temporal state embedding ($s^{k,e}_t \in R^{B \times L \times d}$) and fed into a temporal transformer, capturing sequential context.

**C. High-level Skill Inference Module**
This module is central to SPECI's skill-based continual learning:
- **Dynamic Skill Codebook:** Unlike fixed skill libraries, SPECI employs an **expandable skill codebook**. For each new task $k$, a new subset of $M$ skill vectors is initialized, while previously acquired skill vectors from tasks $j < k$ are frozen. This linear expansion prevents catastrophic forgetting at the skill level. The codebook allows for autonomous skill acquisition without manual definition, interpreting demonstrations to capture diverse and flexible skill representations.
- **Attention-Driven Skill Selection:** Instead of selecting a single skill, SPECI dynamically selects the **top-C relevant skill vectors** from the codebook based on the current state embeddings ($s^e_t$). This selection is performed via an attention mechanism that computes cosine similarity between an "attended query" (state embedding Hadamard product with an attention vector $A$) and skill "keys" ($K$). A Softmax operation on the top-C similarities yields weighting coefficients ($\alpha_c$).
- **Synthesized Latent Skill:** The selected $C$ skill vectors are combined via a **weighted summation** to form a synthesized latent skill ($\tilde{p}$). This allows for flexible and smooth motion by accurately combining skills required by the current state.
- **Skill Injection:** The synthesized latent skill $\tilde{p}$ is factorized into parameter pairs ($p_K, p_V$) and injected into all multi-head cross-attention (MCA) layers of a temporal transformer decoder via **prefix-tuning (P-T)**. This guides the decoder to generate the latent skill variable $z_t$, effectively transferring skill knowledge.
- **Orthogonalization:** Schmidt orthogonalization is applied to skill codebook components (keys and attention vectors) before learning new tasks to prevent interference between skills acquired across different tasks.

**D. Low-level Action Execution Module**
- **Temporal Transformer Decoder:** The sequence of latent skill variables ($z_t$) from the high-level module is passed through another temporal transformer decoder.
- **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) Policy Head:** Instead of simple regression, an MLP-based GMM policy head samples actions. This captures the multimodal distribution of expert demonstrations, generating diverse and precise action sequences (target position, orientation, and gripper commands). The training objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood loss for this GMM.

**E. Mode Approximation**
To enhance declarative knowledge transfer at the task level and facilitate a balance between task-specific and task-sharing knowledge, SPECI introduces a **mode approximation** mechanism based on Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):
- **CP Decomposition:** The attention-based parameters ($W_o$) within the transformer blocks of the Skill Inference and Action Execution modules are treated as 3D tensors. **CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP) decomposition** is applied to factorize a task-specific weight tensor ($W_k$) into a sum of $R$ rank-one components.
- **Task-Specific & Task-Sharing Factors:** This decomposition yields global factors ($U, V$) that capture shared knowledge across tasks, and task-specific factors ($Q_k, \lambda_k$) that capture discriminative features for each task. $Q_k$ and $\lambda_k$ are directly associated with task embeddings.
- **Augmentation:** In forward propagation, the decomposed factors are used to augment the standard transformer weight matrices, allowing for efficient task adaptation while preserving shared knowledge. This mechanism isolates task-specific parameters while enabling beneficial cross-task sharing.

In summary, SPECI combines a modular, hierarchical design with innovative skill management and parameter-efficient learning strategies, all integrated within an end-to-end continual imitation learning pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SPECI's performance was rigorously evaluated on the **LIBERO dataset**, a benchmark for lifelong robot learning comprising four diverse task suites: LIBERO-OBJECT (declarative knowledge, different objects), LIBERO-GOAL (procedural knowledge, different goals), LIBERO-SPATIAL (declarative knowledge, spatial relationships), and LIBERO-LONG (complex, long-horizon tasks with mixed knowledge).

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Forward Transfer (FWT):** Quantifies adaptation speed to new tasks. Higher FWT indicates better prior knowledge integration.
- **Negative Backward Transfer (NBT):** Measures forgetting of previous tasks. Lower NBT (ideally negative) signifies stronger knowledge retention and positive backward transfer.
- **Area Under the Success Rate Curve (AUC):** Provides a holistic measure of average success rates, reflecting overall performance considering both FWT and NBT. Higher AUC is better.

**Comparison Methods:**
SPECI was compared against three flat vision-language policy architectures (RESNET-RNN, RESNET-T, VIT-T), two skill-based hierarchical models (BUDS, LOTUS), and standard continual learning baselines (SEQUENTIAL, MULTITASK, ER, PACKNET).

**Key Findings:**

1. **Superior Overall Performance (AUC):**
 - SPECI consistently achieved the highest AUC across all four LIBERO task suites under the **ER (Experience Replay)** paradigm. It showed improvements of 13% (LIBERO-OBJECT), 9% (LIBERO-GOAL), 10% (LIBERO-SPATIAL), and 14% (LIBERO-LONG) over the best competing methods (LOTUS).
 - Under the **PACKNET** paradigm, SPECI again demonstrated significant AUC improvements, particularly on LIBERO-LONG (27% superiority), highlighting its strong performance in complex, long-horizon scenarios.

2. **Exceptional Backward Knowledge Transfer (NBT):**
 - One of the most remarkable findings is SPECI's ability to consistently yield **negative NBT scores** across all task suites under the PACKNET paradigm. This indicates that learning new tasks systematically *enhances* performance on previously learned tasks, rather than causing forgetting. For example, on LIBERO-SPATIAL, SPECI achieved -0.04±0.01 NBT, compared to 0.07±0.02 for RESNET-T w. PACKNET.
 - This "positive backward transfer" is a strong validation of SPECI's skill reuse mechanisms and knowledge consolidation strategies, demonstrating genuine lifelong learning where the agent gets "smarter" over time.

3. **Strong Forward Knowledge Transfer (FWT):**
 - SPECI showed excellent FWT, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by average margins of 9-10% on short-horizon tasks (LIBERO-OBJECT, -GOAL, -SPATIAL) and a significant 25% on LIBERO-LONG tasks under the PACKNET paradigm. This demonstrates effective utilization of previously acquired skills and task-sharing knowledge for rapid adaptation to novel tasks.
 - Visualizations (Fig. 4) explicitly showed cross-task skill reutilization. For instance, skills learned for "open drawer" (Task 1) were reused for "put bowl inside drawer" (Task 4), confirming the efficacy of the dynamic skill codebook and attention-guided skill sharing.

4. **Ablation Study Validation:**
 - The ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of SPECI's core components:
 - **Mode Approximation (M.A.):** Substantially improved FWT and reduced NBT, demonstrating its effectiveness in preserving task-sharing parameters and enabling task-specific adaptation.
 - **Dynamic Skill Codebook:** Delivered balanced improvements across FWT, NBT, and AUC, showcasing the benefits of structured skill acquisition and preservation for bidirectional knowledge transfer.
 - **Hierarchical Architecture:** Improved overall performance through planning-execution decoupling and expanded model capacity.
 - The full SPECI framework, integrating all components, achieved optimal performance with negative NBT, proving the synergistic effect of its design choices.

5. **Proximity to Upper Bounds:**
 - SPECI maintained minimal deviation from the theoretical upper bounds (MULTITASK) in both FWT and AUC, especially on the challenging LIBERO-LONG benchmark. This indicates that SPECI is highly efficient in learning within a continual setting, closely approaching the performance of an oracle model with full data access.

In essence, the results conclusively demonstrate SPECI's superior performance across diverse robot manipulation tasks under stringent continual learning protocols, establishing a new state-of-the-art in bidirectional knowledge transfer within this domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed SPECI framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly in addressing the critical challenges of lifelong adaptation and knowledge transfer in robot manipulation. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enabling True Lifelong Robot Learning:** By effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting and promoting positive backward transfer, SPECI moves closer to realizing the ultimate goal of general robotic agents. Robots equipped with SPECI can continuously acquire new skills and adapt to evolving environments without compromising previously learned competencies, leading to more robust and versatile autonomous systems. This is crucial for deployment in dynamic real-world settings where tasks and objects are not fully predictable at initial deployment.

2. **Autonomous and Flexible Skill Acquisition:** SPECI's dynamic skill codebook eliminates the need for manual skill definition or pre-segmentation of demonstrations. This self-organizing mechanism allows the robot to autonomously discover and abstract skills from diverse demonstrations, significantly reducing the human engineering overhead typically associated with hierarchical robot control. The ability to implicitly learn and combine skills ("skill prompts") provides unprecedented flexibility and adaptability to novel task compositions.

3. **Enhanced Bidirectional Knowledge Transfer:** The consistent achievement of negative NBT is a particularly impactful finding. It implies that future learning not only avoids degrading past performance but actively refines it through improved understanding and shared representations. This bidirectional transfer is a hallmark of intelligent learning systems and sets SPECI apart from most continual learning methods that primarily focus on minimizing forgetting. This could lead to robots that become progressively more proficient and generalize better over their operational lifespan.

4. **Addressing Complex Long-Horizon Tasks:** The strong performance on LIBERO-LONG tasks demonstrates SPECI's capability in handling complex sequential motions that combine multiple sub-goals. The hierarchical decomposition, coupled with dynamic skill composition, provides a robust framework for managing the increased decision space and accumulation of errors in such tasks, which often plague flat policy architectures.

5. **Multimodal Integration and Robustness:** The fusion of visual, proprioceptive, and linguistic inputs, guided by task-specific language cues, enhances the robot's environmental understanding and task relevance. This multimodal approach makes the policy more robust and versatile, crucial for operating in diverse and unstructured environments.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Industrial Automation:** More adaptable robots that can learn new assembly, handling, or inspection tasks on the fly, reducing reprogramming time and increasing manufacturing flexibility.
- **Service Robotics:** Development of household or care robots that can continually learn new chores, interact with novel objects, and adapt to changing home layouts, improving their utility and user experience.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Robots that can acquire new manipulation skills in hazardous or unknown environments, enabling them to handle unforeseen obstacles or manipulate unfamiliar tools effectively.
- **Foundation for Future AI:** SPECI's approach to implicit skill learning and knowledge transfer could inspire similar hierarchical designs in other complex AI domains beyond robotics, especially those requiring sequential decision-making and continuous adaptation.

While SPECI currently relies on paradigms like ER or PACKNET, which require explicit data replay or task identifiers, the authors' proposed future work on integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for rehearsal-free learning and more granular task decomposition layers promises to further enhance autonomy and generalization in open-world scenarios. This research lays a strong foundation for building truly intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous robotic agents for the future.
