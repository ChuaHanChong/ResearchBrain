# SPECI: Skill Prompts based Hierarchical Continual Imitation Learning for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences developed SPECI, a hierarchical continual imitation learning framework for robot manipulation that learns and reuses skills across tasks. The approach consistently achieved superior performance on the LIBERO benchmark, notably yielding negative Negative Backward Transfer, indicating that learning new tasks enhanced performance on previous ones.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning for robot manipulation suffers from catastrophic forgetting when learning new tasks sequentially, limiting lifelong adaptation.
- Existing continual imitation learning (CIL) methods often neglect the hierarchical and compositional nature of robot manipulation skills or rely on rigid, manually defined skills, hindering effective knowledge transfer.
- Achieving strong bidirectional knowledge transfer (both forward transfer to new tasks and backward transfer to old tasks) in complex, long-horizon robot manipulation remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **SPECI** proposes a hierarchical continual imitation learning architecture with ==Multimodal Perception==, High-level Skill Inference, and Low-level Action Execution modules.
- It incorporates a dynamic, expandable skill codebook that autonomously acquires and freezes skill vectors, coupled with an attention-driven mechanism for selecting and combining top-C relevant skills.
- A mode approximation mechanism, using CP decomposition, is applied to transformer attention parameters to facilitate efficient task-specific adaptation while preserving and sharing global knowledge.

## Results

- SPECI achieved the highest AUC across all four LIBERO task suites, outperforming competing methods like LOTUS by **9%** to **14%** under the ER paradigm and showing up to **27%** superiority on complex tasks under PACKNET.
- The framework consistently yielded negative Negative Backward Transfer (NBT) scores across all task suites under the PACKNET paradigm, indicating that learning new tasks enhanced performance on previously learned ones.
- SPECI demonstrated strong Forward Transfer (FWT), outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by average margins of 9-**10%** on short-horizon tasks and a significant **25%** on long-horizon tasks, effectively reusing learned skills and knowledge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot manipulation benefits from a hierarchical policy structure that explicitly models skill-level and task-level knowledge, enabling more efficient and robust learning.
> - Autonomously learned and dynamically expandable skill codebooks, combined with attention-driven selection, allow for flexible skill acquisition and reuse, reducing reliance on manual definitions.
> - Mode approximation techniques, like CP decomposition, effectively balance task-specific learning with shared knowledge, leading to positive backward transfer where new learning enhances prior task performance.
