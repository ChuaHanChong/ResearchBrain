# CogVideoX: Text-to-Video Diffusion Models with An Expert Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> CogVideoX, developed by Tsinghua University and Zhipu AI, introduces a text-to-video diffusion model capable of generating 10-second continuous videos at 768x1360 resolution that align with complex text prompts. The model achieves this through innovations like a 3D Causal VAE for efficient compression, an Expert Transformer with 3D Full Attention for deep multimodal fusion, and a robust data pipeline, demonstrating leading performance among open-source models and strong competitiveness against closed-source systems.

## Problem

- Previous text-to-video models struggled to generate long-duration videos (e.g., 10 seconds) with dynamic, coherent motion and narrative consistency.
- Existing models often produced videos with limited motion, flicker, or lacked a deep understanding of complex text prompts.
- High-quality, accurately captioned video data is scarce, hindering the training of advanced generative models.

## Method

- A ==3D Causal Variational Autoencoder (VAE)== compresses video spatio-temporally to enhance quality, prevent flicker, and improve compression ratios.
- An ==Expert Transformer== integrates ==Expert Adaptive Layernorm (Expert AdaLN)== for deep multimodal fusion and ==3D Full Attention== to capture complex, large-scale motions, ensuring better text-video alignment and temporal consistency.
- A two-part data pipeline employs sophisticated ==video filtering== to curate high-quality videos and ==dense video captioning==, using models like CogVLM and GPT-4, to generate rich textual descriptions for training.

## Results

- CogVideoX-5B generates **10-second** videos at **768x1360** resolution with high coherence, outperforming openly-accessible models like T2V-Turbo, AnimateDiff, and OpenSora V1.2 in **five out of seven** automated metrics (Human Action, Scene, Dynamic Degree, Multiple Objects, GPT4o-MT Score).
- Human evaluation shows CogVideoX-5B surpasses Kling (a strong closed-source model) in Sensory Quality, Instruction Following, Physics Simulation, and Cover Quality, achieving a total score of **2.74** vs. Kling's **2.17**.
- Ablation studies confirm 3D RoPE, Expert AdaLN, and 3D Full Attention significantly improve convergence, feature alignment, and temporal consistency, respectively, while the custom 3D VAE achieves superior reconstruction quality and less flicker than other open-source 3D VAEs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D Causal VAEs significantly improve spatio-temporal compression and reduce flicker in video generation.
> - Expert Adaptive Layernorm (Expert AdaLN) and unified 3D Full Attention in a Diffusion Transformer effectively enhance text-video alignment and capture large-scale motions while maintaining architectural simplicity.
> - A robust data pipeline, including video filtering and dense video captioning using a cascade of vision-language models, is crucial for training high-quality text-to-video models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video generation has undergone rapid advancements, primarily driven by innovations in Transformer architectures (Vaswani et al., 2017) and Diffusion Models (Ho et al., 2020). Early pioneers in this domain, such as CogVideo (Hong et al., 2022) and Phenaki (Villegas et al., 2022), demonstrated the promise of extending Transformer-based sequence generation to video. Concurrently, diffusion models revolutionized image generation (Rombach et al., 2022) and swiftly transitioned to video synthesis (Singer et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2022).

The integration of Transformers as the backbone of diffusion models, leading to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) (Peebles & Xie, 2023), marked a significant milestone, exemplified by OpenAI's impressive Sora showcases (OpenAI, 2024). Sora's ability to generate long, high-fidelity, and coherent videos with complex scene dynamics set a new benchmark, albeit as a closed-source model.

CogVideoX positions itself directly within this landscape, aiming to push the boundaries of *openly-accessible* text-to-video generation models. While models like Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) (Blattmann et al., 2023), AnimateDiff (Guo et al., 2023), VideoCrafter-2.0 (Chen et al., 2024a), OpenSora (Zheng et al., 2024b), Show-1 (Zhang et al., 2023a), Gen-2 (runway, 2023), Pika (pik, 2023), and LaVie-2 (Wang et al., 2023b) have made substantial progress, they often face limitations in generating long-duration videos with complex, coherent motion and narrative structures. These limitations include issues like limited motion, short durations, flicker, and difficulties in maintaining consistency over time, especially for prompts involving sequential actions or significant object transformations.

CogVideoX directly addresses these challenges, building upon the DiT paradigm. Its key contributions, such as the 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for improved spatial-temporal compression, the Expert Transformer for deep multimodal fusion, and advanced training techniques, represent a concerted effort to overcome the aforementioned limitations. Furthermore, the emphasis on a robust data pipeline, including sophisticated video filtering and dense video captioning, tackles a critical bottleneck in training high-quality video generation models: the scarcity of accurately described, high-quality video data. By publicly releasing its models and code, CogVideoX aims to democratize access to advanced video generation capabilities and foster further research in the open-source community, directly challenging the "black box" nature of some leading models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of CogVideoX is to develop a large-scale text-to-video generation model capable of producing **10-second continuous videos at 16 frames per second (fps) with a resolution of 768×1360 pixels**, which seamlessly align with complex text prompts. The authors explicitly state their motivation arises from several persistent challenges in previous video generation models:
- **Limited Motion and Short Durations**: Many existing models struggled to generate videos longer than a few seconds or to depict significant, dynamic movements.
- **Lack of Coherent Narratives**: It was particularly difficult for previous models to create videos that follow a coherent story or sequence of actions described in a text prompt, often failing on complex scenarios like "a bolt of lightning splits a rock, and a person jumps out from inside the rock." This points to a deeper issue of temporal consistency and semantic understanding over an extended duration.
- **Technical Unclarity in DiTs**: Despite the promise of Diffusion Transformers, the specific architectural and training strategies required for achieving long-term consistency and dynamic plots remained underexplored.
- **Data Quality and Availability**: High-quality, accurately captioned video data is scarce. Many online videos contain noise (e.g., editing artifacts, low quality, minimal motion) or lack detailed textual descriptions, hindering effective model training.

To address these, CogVideoX aims to:
- Enhance **compression efficiency and video fidelity** by compressing videos across both spatial and temporal dimensions.
- Improve **text-video alignment and deep fusion** between modalities.
- Achieve **coherent, long-duration videos with diverse shapes and dynamic movements**.
- Develop an **effective pipeline for data pre-processing**, including advanced video filtering and sophisticated video captioning, to ensure high-quality training data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CogVideoX's methodology revolves around several innovative designs integrated into a Diffusion Transformer framework:

#### 3.1. 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE)

To handle the large volume of video data, CogVideoX introduces a **3D Causal VAE**.
- **Purpose**: Compresses video along both spatial and temporal dimensions, reducing sequence length and training compute while enhancing quality and preventing flicker. It aims for higher compression ratios (e.g., 8x8x4) compared to previous 2D VAE fine-tuning approaches.
- **Architecture**: Comprises an encoder, decoder, and a KL regularizer. It uses 3D convolutions for downsampling/upsampling across stages, some performing full 3D operations, others 2D.
- **Causal Convolution**: Employs temporally causal convolutions to ensure future information does not influence present/past predictions, crucial for sequence generation.
- **Training**: Trained in two stages: initially on 256x256 resolution, 17-frame videos, then fine-tuned with context parallel on 161-frame videos. It combines L1 reconstruction loss, LPIPS perceptual loss, and KL loss, with GAN loss from a 3D discriminator added later.
- **Memory Efficiency**: Utilizes context parallelization along the temporal dimension for 3D convolution to distribute computation, reducing GPU memory usage for long videos.

#### 3.2. Expert Transformer

This component focuses on improving the text-video alignment and handling multimodal data within the DiT.
- **Patchify**: Transforms the 3D VAE latent (T×H×W×C) into a long sequence (`z_vision`) by patchifying.
- **3D-RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding)**: Extends standard RoPE to 3D coordinates (x, y, t) for video, applying 1D-RoPE independently to each dimension and concatenating the embeddings. This helps model long sequences and relative positions effectively.
- **Expert Adaptive Layernorm (Expert AdaLN)**: Concatenates text and video embeddings at the input stage. Expert AdaLN applies modality-specific modulation (derived from the diffusion timestep `t`) to vision hidden states and text hidden states separately. This promotes feature space alignment while minimizing additional parameters, simplifying the architecture compared to approaches like MMDiT.
- **3D Full Attention**: Instead of separated spatial and temporal attention, CogVideoX employs a unified 3D text-video hybrid attention mechanism. This is chosen to better capture large-scale motions and maintain consistency, as separated attention can lead to complex implicit information transmission and inconsistency for large movements. Leveraging FlashAttention's efficiency makes this feasible.

#### 3.3. Training CogVideoX

- **Mixed-Duration Training with Multi-Resolution Frame Pack**: Addresses data utilization and generalization by training with videos of varying durations and resolutions in the same batch. Inspired by Patch'n Pack, it packs videos into a consistent batch shape. 3D-RoPE is adapted via extrapolation to handle different resolutions and durations.
- **Progressive Training**: A multi-stage resolution training approach:
 1. Initial training on 256px videos to learn semantics and low-frequency knowledge.
 2. Gradual increase to 512px, then 768px, to learn high-frequency details.
 3. A final "high-quality fine-tuning" stage on a curated subset of 20% high-quality data to remove artifacts (subtitles, watermarks) and improve visual quality.
- **Explicit Uniform Sampling**: To stabilize training loss and accelerate convergence, timesteps `t` are sampled uniformly across data parallel ranks. Each rank samples within a specific sub-interval of the total timestep range (1 to T), ensuring a more uniform distribution of timesteps across the entire batch compared to purely random sampling on each rank.
- **Image-to-Video Adaptation**: A separate image-to-video model is fine-tuned from the text-to-video model by adding an image as an additional condition and applying noise to the image condition during training to enhance robustness.

#### 3.4. Data Pipeline

Recognizing the criticality of data quality, CogVideoX employs a sophisticated two-part pipeline:
- **Video Filtering**: Aims to filter out low-quality and non-conducive videos for model training. This includes:
 - Defining negative labels (Editing, Lack of Motion Connectivity, Low Quality, Lecture Type, Text Dominated, Noisy Screenshots).
 - Training 6 video filters based on Video-LLaMA using manually labeled data (20,000 videos).
 - Calculating and dynamically adjusting optical flow scores (for dynamism) and image aesthetic scores to ensure quality.
- **Dense Video Captioning**: Addresses the scarcity of descriptive video-text pairs.
 - **Pipeline**: Uses a multi-step process:
 1. Short video captions generated by Panda70M.
 2. Dense image captions for individual frames generated by CogVLM.
 3. GPT-4 summarizes these image captions into detailed video captions.
 4. A LLaMA2 model is fine-tuned on GPT-4 summaries for large-scale caption generation.
 - **CogVLM2-Caption**: An end-to-end video understanding model, fine-tuned on the generated dense caption data, for even faster and more detailed video recaptioning. This also enables video-to-video generation by using the model to caption an input video and then feeding that caption to CogVideoX.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CogVideoX demonstrates state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across various evaluations, validating its architectural and training innovations.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies

- **Position Embedding**: 3D RoPE significantly accelerates loss convergence compared to sinusoidal absolute position embedding, consistent with findings in large language models. The authors chose extrapolation for RoPE interpolation when scaling resolutions to maintain relative positional clarity.
- **Expert Adaptive Layernorm**: Expert AdaLN (CogVideoX's design) significantly outperforms both a model without Expert AdaLN and MMDiT (Esser et al., 2024) in terms of FVD and CLIP4Clip scores, especially with the same number of parameters. This suggests that Expert AdaLN effectively alleviates feature space differences between modalities without requiring two independent Transformers, leading to a more simplified and scalable design.
- **3D Full Attention**: Replacing 3D full attention with separated 2D+1D attention leads to much higher FVD in early steps and unstable training prone to collapse, especially for larger models (5B). This validates the choice of 3D full attention for handling large motions and ensuring consistency.
- **Explicit Uniform Sampling**: This method results in a noticeably more stable decrease in training loss and better final performance. Validation loss at various diffusion timesteps is consistently lower, indicating accelerated convergence across all timesteps due to reduced randomness from uneven sampling.

#### 4.2. VAE Reconstruction Effect

- The custom 3D VAE in CogVideoX achieves superior reconstruction quality compared to other open-source 3D VAEs (Open-Sora, Open-Sora-Plan) on 256x256, 17-frame videos. It exhibits the least flicker (lower L1 difference between adjacent frames) and highest PSNR, demonstrating its effectiveness in spatio-temporal compression and maintaining continuity.

#### 4.3. Automated Metric Evaluation

CogVideoX was evaluated using metrics from Vbench (Huang et al., 2024) and additional dynamic metrics.
- **Metrics Used**: Human Action, Scene, Dynamic Degree, Multiple Objects, Appearance Style (from Vbench), and Dynamic Quality (Liao et al., 2024), GPT4o-MT Score (Yuan et al., 2024). Metrics like 'color' were excluded due to biases towards simple, static videos.
- **Performance**: CogVideoX-5B achieves the best performance in five out of seven metrics (Human Action, Scene, Dynamic Degree, Multiple Objects, GPT4o-MT Score) and shows competitive results in the remaining two (Appearance Style, Dynamic Quality), compared to openly-accessible models like T2V-Turbo, AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter-2.0, OpenSora V1.2, Show-1, Gen-2, Pika, and LaVie-2. CogVideoX-2B also demonstrates highly competitive performance. This indicates strong video generation quality, semantic alignment, and the ability to handle complex dynamic scenes. The radar chart (Figure 2) visually reinforces CogVideoX's comprehensive advantages.

#### 4.4. Human Evaluation

A comprehensive human evaluation framework was established, scoring videos on Sensory Quality, Instruction Following, Physics Simulation, and Cover Quality (each 0, 0.5, or 1, total max 5).
- **Comparison**: CogVideoX-5B was compared against Kling (a strong closed-source model).
- **Results**: CogVideoX-5B consistently wins human preference across all four aspects (Sensory Quality, Instruction Following, Physics Simulation, Cover Quality), achieving a total score of 2.74 compared to Kling's 2.17. This is a significant finding, indicating that CogVideoX rivals or surpasses even top closed-source models in human-perceived quality and adherence to physical laws and instructions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CogVideoX represents a substantial leap forward in the field of text-to-video generation, particularly for open-source models, with several significant implications:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance in Open-Source Domain**: By outperforming established open-source models in automated and human evaluations and even surpassing a strong closed-source contender like Kling, CogVideoX sets a new benchmark for accessible video generation technologies. This democratizes access to high-quality video creation tools, empowering a broader range of researchers, developers, and creators.
- **Long-Duration and High-Coherence Video Generation**: The ability to generate 10-second videos at high resolution (768x1360) with consistent motion and narrative coherence directly addresses major limitations of previous models. This unlocks possibilities for more complex storytelling, animated content creation, and detailed scene visualizations from simple text prompts.
- **Robust Architectural Innovations**:
 - The **3D VAE** significantly improves spatio-temporal compression efficiency and fidelity, mitigating issues like flicker that plagued earlier video models. Its causal nature is crucial for temporal consistency.
 - The **Expert Transformer** with Expert Adaptive LayerNorm and 3D Full Attention offers an efficient and effective solution for deep multimodal fusion and capturing complex, large-scale motions, paving the way for more adaptable and scalable architectures.
- **Sophisticated Data Engineering**: The detailed video filtering and dense video captioning pipelines are critical contributions. They highlight the importance of high-quality, accurately labeled data for training advanced generative models. The method of generating dense captions using a cascade of vision-language models (CogVLM, GPT-4, LLaMA2, CogVLM2-Caption) is itself a significant innovation that can benefit future multimodal research. The release of the CogVLM2-Caption model, which can enable video-to-video generation via prompt extraction, further expands the utility of their data pipeline.
- **Open-Source Release and Future Research**: The public release of CogVideoX (5B and 2B models), including the VAE and video captioning model, is a major contribution to the AI community. It provides a robust foundation for future research, enabling others to build upon their work, explore scaling laws, and push the boundaries of video generation further. This fosters transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative innovation in a field that has seen increasing proprietary development.
- **Commercial-Grade Potential**: The description of CogVideoX as "the first commercial-grade open-source video generation models" underscores its potential for real-world applications in content creation, advertising, education, and entertainment, reducing the barrier to entry for high-quality video production.

In conclusion, CogVideoX represents a comprehensive and impactful research effort that addresses fundamental challenges in text-to-video generation through innovative architecture, training, and data strategies. Its open-source release is poised to accelerate progress and foster a more accessible and collaborative future for generative AI in the video domain.
