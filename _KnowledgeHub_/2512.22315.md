# VideoZoomer: Reinforcement-Learned Temporal Focusing for Long Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VIDEOZOOMER introduces an agentic framework that enables Multimodal Large Language Models to dynamically focus on relevant temporal segments within long videos, improving reasoning capabilities. The system achieves a 10.3 percentage point gain over its base model on the MLVU test set and outperforms proprietary models like GPT-4o on LongVideoReason-eval with an 80.3 score.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are limited by fixed context windows, hindering their ability to effectively process and reason over long video streams.
- Traditional methods like uniform frame sampling are inefficient and risk overlooking critical, often brief, events in lengthy videos.
- Static adaptive frame selection approaches are non-interactive, preventing MLLMs from correcting initial errors or dynamically seeking additional evidence for complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- VIDEOZOOMER proposes an agentic framework that allows an MLLM to act as an ==active agent==, dynamically requesting high-frame-rate clips ('temporal zoom-in') from specific time segments.
- A 'first glance, then zoom' strategy provides an initial low-frame-rate overview, followed by iterative, multi-turn tool interactions for detailed evidence gathering.
- The model is trained using a two-stage process: cold-start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with expert trajectories and reflection data, followed by Multi-Turn Tool-Integrated Reinforcement Learning (RL) using GRPO.

## Results

- VIDEOZOOMER (**7B**) achieved a 55.8 score on the MLVU test set, representing a 10.3 percentage point gain over its Qwen2.5-VL base model and establishing it as the top-performing open-source solution.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance on complex reasoning, scoring 80.3 on LongVideoReason-eval and surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4o (60.7) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (67.3).
- Ablation studies confirmed that reinforcement learning, cold-start initialization, reflection data, and the conditional tool-use bonus are all critical components for the agent's effective tool interaction and reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training an MLLM as an active agent capable of dynamic temporal focusing, rather than passively receiving pre-sampled frames, substantially enhances long video understanding and reasoning.
> - A two-stage training approach, incorporating SFT with expert-generated reflection data, is crucial for effectively initializing complex tool-use policies and teaching error recovery in MLLM agents.
> - Efficient resource allocation through conditional 'zoom-in' operations, guided by reinforcement learning, allows for higher accuracy on long video tasks while significantly reducing the total number of processed frames.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have revolutionized tasks involving vision and language, particularly excelling in image and short-video understanding. However, the paper identifies a critical bottleneck: the **limited context window** of current MLLMs. This limitation severely hinders their ability to process and reason effectively over long video streams, which often span minutes or even hours and contain vast amounts of temporal information.

Historically, approaches to long video understanding with MLLMs have largely fallen into two categories:
1. **Uniform Frame Sampling:** This prevalent method selects frames at fixed intervals to reduce the input size, allowing the video to fit within the MLLM's context window. However, as the authors correctly point out, this strategy is inherently inefficient and prone to missing critical, often fleeting, events. It assumes all moments are equally important, wasting context on redundant segments and risking the oversight of salient details.
2. **Static Adaptive Frame Selection:** More advanced methods employ a lightweight selector module to pre-identify salient frames based on a text query. While an improvement over uniform sampling, these approaches remain "static" and "non-interactive." The initial selection is fixed; if key information is missed or the initial choice is suboptimal, the model lacks a mechanism to correct its error or dynamically revisit the video for more evidence. This rigidity limits performance on complex reasoning tasks requiring iterative information gathering.

VideoZoomer addresses these fundamental limitations by proposing an **agentic framework**. This paradigm shift transforms the MLLM from a passive recipient of pre-selected frames into an **active agent** that dynamically controls its visual focus during the reasoning process. This approach places VideoZoomer within an emerging trend in AI research that emphasizes agentic capabilities, where models learn to interact with their environment and tools to achieve goals, rather than merely processing static inputs.

Related works in this emerging agentic space often fall into:
- **RL for MLLMs:** Methods like MM-Eureka and VL-Rethinker have adapted reinforcement learning to enhance vision-language reasoning, and Video-R1 specifically validated this in the video domain.
- **MLLMs with External Tools:** Recent efforts have extended MLLMs with tools for image cropping, web search, or segmentation, primarily focusing on image tasks and often single-turn interactions.
- **Zero-shot Agentic Models:** Frameworks like VideoDeepResearch and Deep Video Discovery leverage powerful proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o, Deepseek-R1) with prompting techniques for iterative video exploration. While demonstrating potential, these rely on inaccessible, resource-intensive, closed-source models, limiting reproducibility and optimization.

VideoZoomer distinguishes itself by explicitly **training a relatively smaller 7B open-source MLLM** to learn an efficient, multi-turn agentic policy for long video comprehension. It innovatively combines multi-turn tool interaction with reinforcement learning to achieve dynamic temporal focusing, offering a more reproducible, optimizable, and accessible solution compared to proprietary model-based agentic systems. By enabling "temporal zoom-in" capabilities, it directly tackles the "long video sampling dilemma" and the "limited context window" problem in a highly efficient and performant manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the VideoZoomer research are to:

1. **Overcome the Limited Context Window of MLLMs for Long Videos:** The core goal is to enable MLLMs to effectively process and reason about videos that exceed their fixed context window length, moving beyond the restrictive and inefficient methods of uniform sampling or static frame selection.
2. **Enable Dynamic and Adaptive Visual Focusing:** The paper aims to develop a framework where an MLLM can autonomously control its visual attention by dynamically requesting high-frame-rate clips from specific moments in a long video, much like a human would focus on salient events.
3. **Facilitate Multi-Turn Interactive Reasoning:** A key objective is to allow the MLLM to engage in an iterative process of evidence gathering, where it can make an initial assessment, request more detailed visual information (zoom in), analyze the new evidence, and potentially refine its understanding or request further details, until a confident answer can be formed.
4. **Achieve Superior Efficiency and Performance:** The framework seeks to deliver higher accuracy on challenging long video reasoning tasks while simultaneously optimizing resource usage by consuming a significant context budget only when finer-grained details are genuinely needed.
5. **Develop a Robust Training Strategy for Agentic MLLMs:** The work aims to provide a stable and effective method for training MLLMs to act as agents, especially considering the challenges of reinforcement learning with complex action spaces and the need for diverse reasoning patterns.

The motivations driving these objectives stem from the inherent limitations of existing MLLM approaches for long video understanding:

- **Information Loss and Suboptimal Performance:** Uniform sampling risks overlooking crucial, short-duration events, while static selection methods cannot recover from initial errors or explore deeper for complex reasoning, leading to an upper bound on performance.
- **Inefficiency and Wasted Resources:** Processing entire long videos, even with sparse sampling, can be computationally expensive and may allocate context to redundant information, especially if the answer lies in only a few critical moments.
- **Lack of Human-like Cognitive Ability:** Humans naturally navigate complex visual streams by dynamically allocating attention based on their task. Current MLLMs lack this goal-directed ability, hindering their capacity for effective and efficient visual reasoning.
- **Advancing Agentic AI:** The development of more capable and adaptive AI agents that can interact with their environment and tools is a significant frontier in AI. VideoZoomer contributes to this by demonstrating a reinforcement-learned agent that actively seeks visual information.
- **Reproducibility and Accessibility:** By focusing on an open-source MLLM and providing detailed methodologies, the authors aim to make advanced long video reasoning capabilities more accessible to the broader research community, contrasting with proprietary, black-box solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoZoomer proposes a novel agentic framework that empowers an MLLM to actively control its visual focus during reasoning, adopting a "first glance, then zoom" strategy combined with a two-stage training process.

#### 3.1. Overview: "First Glance, Then Zoom" Agentic Framework

The core idea is to train an agent that learns an optimal policy for when and where to request high-frame-rate video clips, termed "temporal zoom-in."
1. **Glance Stage (Initial Overview):** The model first receives a query (Q) and a low-frame-rate version of the entire video (V_low), uniformly sampled at a default rate (f_low). This provides a coarse, computationally inexpensive overview.
2. **Zoom Stage (Iterative Refinement):** If the initial overview is insufficient for a precise answer, the MLLM, acting as an agent, can invoke a `<video zoom>` tool. This tool allows the model to request a specific time segment [`t_start`, `t_end`] from the original video at a higher frame rate (`f_high`).
3. **Multi-Turn Interaction:** Upon a valid tool invocation, the environment returns a high-resolution clip (`V_clip`). The agent integrates this new evidence into its reasoning process and can continue to interact by iteratively calling the tool, refining its understanding, or correcting previous oversights. This process continues until the agent determines it has sufficient information to produce a final answer or reaches a maximum turn limit.
4. **Frame Budget Constraint:** Each tool call and the total number of frames from high-resolution clips are constrained by a frame budget, promoting efficient resource allocation.

This agentic approach enables diverse reasoning strategies such as Direct-hit Reasoning (finding immediate evidence), Progressive Reasoning (aggregating evidence step-by-step), and Self-refine Reasoning (correcting initial errors).

#### 3.2. Cold-Start Initialization (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)

To address the challenges of reinforcement learning from scratch on a complex, high-dimensional action space (generating structured tool calls), VideoZoomer employs a two-stage training strategy, starting with a cold-start SFT phase.

1. **Distillation of Exemplar Trajectories:**
 - **Objective:** To teach the model the basics of tool usage and a baseline reasoning capability.
 - **Process:** State-of-the-art proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-pro) are leveraged as "expert demonstrators." For each video-question pair, the expert model is prompted with the low-frame-rate video. It then performs multi-turn interactions, iteratively calling the `<video zoom>` tool and providing a final answer.
 - **Output:** A collection of "golden" trajectories, each containing the initial prompt, a sequence of tool calls, corresponding clip observations, and the final answer.

2. **Augmentation with Reflection Data:**
 - **Objective:** To expose the model to diverse and complex reasoning patterns, prevent overfitting to a single expert pattern, and teach error recovery.
 - **Problem with Pure Exemplar SFT:** Models trained solely on exemplar data tend to adopt shallow policies, often making only one tool call before guessing an answer, regardless of the clip's utility.
 - **Process:** The initial model (trained on exemplar data) is used to generate its own rollouts. Trajectories where the model fails are identified. These incorrect rollouts are then fed back to the expert model, which is prompted to "reflect" on the flawed reasoning, identify the mistake, and generate a corrected, more robust reasoning path. This corrected path might involve additional tool calls or a different reasoning chain.
 - **Output:** Reflection data that explicitly teaches the model how to recover from errors, critically evaluate tool responses, and persist with further investigation.

The final cold-start dataset is a curated combination of approximately 11,000 verified exemplar and reflection trajectories, providing a rich foundation for tool-use and reasoning.

#### 3.3. Multi-Turn Tool-Integrated Reinforcement Learning (RL)

With the foundational skills established through SFT, the second stage optimizes the model's tool interaction policy and reasoning capability.

1. **RL Algorithm:** GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization), adapted for multi-turn tool calling contexts, is employed. A token-level loss mask is applied to the tool call trajectory, ignoring text and image tokens not generated by the model.
2. **Reward Design:** A composite reward function guides the agent:
 - `R_acc(x, y)`: Primary task-oriented reward, positive if the final answer is correct.
 - `R_format(y)`: Validates the structure of the agent's response at each turn, positive if it adheres to predefined formatting (e.g., `<think></think>`, `<video zoom></video zoom>`, `<answer></answer>`).
 - `R_tool(y)`: A conditional bonus for using the tool, awarded *only if the final answer is correct*. This encourages exploration and tool utility discovery without promoting redundant or unhelpful calls.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, chosen for strong foundational capabilities and RL amenability.
- **Training Frameworks:** LLaMA-Factory for SFT, verl (extended for multi-turn tool-calling) for RL.
- **Training Data:** LongVideoReason dataset (52K QA pairs).
- **Frame Budget:** Initialized with 64 uniformly sampled frames. Up to 4 subsequent tool calls, each retrieving up to 16 frames (max total 128 frames for evaluation comparisons).
- **RL Enhancements:** Clip-higher and dynamic sampling from DAPO are adapted for stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoZoomer demonstrates significant advancements in long video understanding and reasoning, consistently outperforming open-source baselines and rivaling proprietary models.

#### 4.1. Performance on Long Video Understanding Benchmarks

- **Overall Superiority:** VideoZoomer (7B) achieves strong performance across MLVU, LongVideoBench, VideoMME, and LVBench (Table 1).
- **Significant Gains over Base Model:** On MLVU, VideoZoomer achieves 68.8 (dev) and 55.8 (test), showing substantial gains of +10.5 and +10.3 points over its base model, Qwen2.5-VL. This also makes it the top-performing open-source model on these benchmarks.
- **Effectiveness Across Video Lengths:** The method provides a clear performance boost even on benchmarks not exclusively focused on extremely long durations (e.g., VideoMME: 65.2 overall, 55.8 on long-set), indicating the learned dynamic "zoom" policy benefits various video lengths.
- **Detailed Perception Tasks:** On MLVU (Table 2), improvements are most pronounced in tasks requiring detailed perception, such as Ego Reasoning (ER, +19.1 dev, +26.4 test), Needle QA (NQA, +15.2 dev, +10.0 test), Plot QA (PQA, +7.0 dev), and especially Action Count (AC, +36.9 dev, +13.3 test), which directly benefits from high-frame-rate re-sampling of critical moments.

#### 4.2. Performance on Long Video Reasoning Benchmarks

- **Top Open-Source Performer:** VideoZoomer achieves the highest scores among all open-source models on VideoMMLU (67.9) and VideoMMMU (52.2).
- **Outperforming Proprietary Models:** Notably, on LongVideoReason-eval, VideoZoomer achieves 80.3, surpassing powerful proprietary models like GPT-4o (60.7) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (67.3), as well as LongVILA-R1 (67.9) which uses a larger frame budget. This highlights the efficiency and robust reasoning capabilities of the agentic strategy.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation studies (Table 3, Figure 5) confirm the critical contribution of each component:
- **Reinforcement Learning (w/o RL):** Removing RL leads to a catastrophic performance drop (e.g., -17.0 on LongVideoReason), confirming its necessity for an effective tool-use policy.
- **Cold-Start Initialization (w/o cold-start):** Skipping the SFT stage prevents the model from converging to a meaningful policy.
- **Reflection Data (w/o reflection):** Without reflection data, the model adopts a shallow strategy, making fewer tool calls (average ~1.0) and limiting its ability to solve complex problems. The full model learns to make nearly two calls on average, enabling deeper investigation.
- **Conditional Tool-Use Bonus (w/o R_tool):** Removing this bonus causes "policy collapse," where the agent's tool usage trends to zero, failing to discover the tool's utility.

#### 4.4. Performance Across Various Frame Budgets

- **Superior Efficiency:** VideoZoomer consistently achieves higher accuracy with significantly fewer frames compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (Figure 6). For instance, on MLVU, it achieves 0.64 accuracy with 48 frames on average, outperforming the baseline's 0.581 at 128 frames. This trend is maintained on LVBench and LongVideoReason.
- **Optimal Frame Window:** For complex reasoning tasks like LongVideoReason, both models peak around 64 frames, suggesting that excessive visual information can introduce noise. Within this optimal range, VideoZoomer's peak accuracy (0.803 vs. 0.718) is substantially higher.

#### 4.5. Combining with a Frame Selector

Replacing initial uniform sampling with output from a sophisticated frame selector (TSPO-0.4B) further boosts VideoZoomer's performance (e.g., +2.0 on MLVU, +3.0 on LongVideoBench, Table 4). This demonstrates the flexibility and transferability of the agentic approach, showing it can leverage improved starting points for even more efficient investigation.

#### 4.6. OOD Tasks and SFT Data Quantity

- **Generalizability (OOD):** VideoZoomer not only maintains but significantly improves performance on short video captioning benchmarks (TemporalBench, TempCompass, VDC), and retains comparable performance on logical reasoning tasks (CLEVRER) (Tables 7, 8). This indicates that the training process enhances long-video capabilities without degrading foundational abilities.
- **SFT Data Quantity:** Doubling the SFT data quantity did not yield better overall performance (Table 9), suggesting that the quality and diversity of reasoning patterns in the curated 11k trajectory dataset are more crucial than sheer volume.

#### 4.7. Analysis of Chosen FPS and Max Tool Calls

- **Learned FPS Distribution:** The model intelligently chooses moderate FPS values (66.2% in (1, 2] range) for "zoom-in" clips, avoiding excessively high rates unless necessary, demonstrating an efficient resource allocation policy (Table 10).
- **Impact of Multi-Step Reasoning:** Allowing even one tool call leads to a significant performance jump. The benefits continue with two calls, indicating the importance of multi-turn interaction. Beyond two calls, gains show diminishing returns, with the model using the extra budget sparingly for the hardest problems (Table 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoZoomer represents a substantial step forward in enhancing the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for long video understanding and reasoning. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Breaking MLLM Context Window Limitations:** The most direct impact is overcoming the critical "limited context window" problem that has plagued MLLMs in processing long sequential data. By enabling dynamic, on-demand "temporal zooming," VideoZoomer allows MLLMs to engage with videos of arbitrary length efficiently, without being overwhelmed by irrelevant information or missing crucial details. This unlocks a vast new domain of MLLM applications previously constrained by input size.

2. **Advancement in Agentic AI and Tool Use:** VideoZoomer contributes significantly to the burgeoning field of agentic AI. It demonstrates how MLLMs can be trained as active agents capable of intelligently interacting with external tools (the `<video zoom>` tool) and their environment in a multi-turn, iterative manner. This paradigm shift from passive recipient to active investigator is a fundamental step towards more autonomous and intelligent AI systems. The ability to "self-reflect" and "self-correct" during reasoning, facilitated by reflection data, further enhances this agentic capability, making the model more robust and adaptive.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** The framework's "first glance, then zoom" strategy leads to superior efficiency. By only consuming a high-resolution context budget when truly necessary, VideoZoomer achieves higher accuracy with significantly fewer frames processed compared to conventional methods. This efficiency is critical for deploying MLLMs in real-world applications where computational resources are limited or real-time performance is required, making advanced video reasoning more practical and sustainable.

4. **Improved Reasoning and Perception for Complex Tasks:** The experimental results, particularly on long video reasoning benchmarks where VideoZoomer outperforms even powerful proprietary models, highlight a significant improvement in the MLLM's integrated perception and reasoning capabilities. The ability to dynamically gather fine-grained evidence allows the model to build more robust and accurate reasoning chains, crucial for complex question-answering, event detection, and anomaly recognition in long videos.

5. **Open-Source Contribution and Reproducibility:** By building upon an open-source MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) and making the code available, VideoZoomer promotes accessibility and reproducibility within the research community. This contrasts with proprietary model-based agentic approaches, fostering further research and development in this promising direction. The detailed two-stage training strategy, especially the novel cold-start data curation with reflection, offers valuable insights for others working on similar reinforcement learning for LLM agent development.

6. **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The capabilities demonstrated by VideoZoomer have broad implications for various real-world scenarios:
 - **Video Surveillance and Security:** Efficiently identifying critical events or anomalies in hours-long footage.
 - **Content Moderation:** Automatically detecting policy violations in long user-generated videos.
 - **Educational Platforms:** Summarizing lengthy lectures, identifying key explanations, or pinpointing specific instructional moments.
 - **Sports Analytics:** Analyzing complex plays or specific actions within entire games.
 - **Healthcare:** Reviewing long medical procedure recordings for specific events or anomalies.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Better understanding long-term situations and subtle cues in continuous sensor streams.

In conclusion, VideoZoomer addresses a fundamental challenge in MLLM development with an elegant, effective, and efficient agentic solution. Its contributions to dynamic temporal focusing, multi-turn reasoning, and robust training strategies will likely inspire future research in making AI systems more adaptive, intelligent, and capable of understanding the complexities of the real world's continuous visual information.
