# OccluDex: Hierarchical 3D Visuo-Tactile Representation Learning for Egocentric Dexterous Manipulation under Self-Occlusion

> [!summary] Summary
> OccluDex is a hierarchical 3D visuo-tactile representation learner for egocentric dexterous manipulation when the hand occludes the object. Multi-scale masked autoencoding over partial point clouds is fused with 19 binary tactile contact tokens via cross-modal attention, pretrained on human glove-camera demonstrations, then frozen as a PPO backbone. It averages 86.2% success across faucet rotation and object reorientation and transfers zero-shot to a real Shadow Hand.

## Problem

- Close-range egocentric sensing suffers hand-object self-occlusion around contact regions, and existing fixes rely on external viewpoints or explicit 3D reconstruction that still need extra visual evidence.
- Geometric completion recovers missing shape but provides no instantaneous contact information, while tactile signals are local and sparse and reveal little about global object geometry.
- Prior visuo-tactile representation learning is mostly image-based with only implicit spatial structure, and existing 3D visuo-tactile work targets parallel-jaw grippers rather than multi-fingered dexterous hands.

## Method

- **Hierarchical multi-scale point-cloud encoder**: ==FPS== plus ==k-NN grouping== at three scales, with ==progressive masking== that samples visibility at the coarsest scale and propagates it consistently to finer scales.
- **Tactile token embedding with late cross-modal fusion**: 19 binarized contact channels become position-embedded tokens, concatenated with geometric tokens only at the highest encoder stage under a ==block-wise attention mask==.
- **Joint masked pretraining** with a ==hierarchical decoder== that reconstructs masked point patches via ==Chamfer loss== and masked tactile tokens via ==MSE==, using 409K synchronized human frames, then frozen for ==PPO==.

## Results

- Faucet Rotation: **99.9%** seen and **86.6%** unseen-scale success versus **86.3%**/**63.0%** for VTDexManip and **69.7%**/**48.8%** for VTT-3D, with much lower cross-seed variance.
- Tabletop Object Reorientation: **75.1%** seen and **83.3%** held-out YCB success; overall average **86.2%** versus **75.8%** VTDexManip, **73.0%** proprioception-only, and **65.6%** VTT-3D.
- Removing tactile drops Faucet success to **54.3%**/**35.7%**; joint end-to-end training reaches **0%**; real Shadow Hand succeeds **17/20** faucet and **14/20** reorientation trials on unseen objects zero-shot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Under self-occlusion, touch carries more control-relevant signal than geometry: removing tactile hurt far more than removing point clouds, so contact sensing deserves first-class treatment.
> - Fuse sparse local modalities with dense geometry late, after hierarchical aggregation provides a stable global spatial reference, since early fusion mismatches the spatial support of the two modalities.
> - Pretraining a frozen perception encoder on human demonstrations makes heavy 3D encoders tractable for RL, where joint perception-and-control learning at scale can collapse entirely.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot hand turning a faucet handle or reorienting an object on a table often blocks its own view. From a camera near the hand, the hand itself hides the object surfaces and contact regions the controller needs to act on. OccluDex does not try to reconstruct a complete object from that missing evidence. It combines partial 3D geometry, which supplies broader shape and spatial context, with touch, which reports local contact. Touch alone is too local to describe the whole object, and partial vision alone can miss the interaction that guides the next motion. In simulation, this pairing improves success over prior visuo-tactile representations. The learned system was also tested on a physical hand.

Simulation examples (left) and real Shadow Hand task scenes (right), with faucet rotation above and tabletop object reorientation below. The hand occupies much of the interaction view.

### 2. A shared map for shape and contact

OccluDex groups the input point cloud into local neighborhoods and progressively merges them into coarser geometric tokens. During training, parts of the input are masked across scales. Visibility is propagated between levels so that the same geometric regions remain aligned. This mask is a self-supervised learning device, not a simulation of the hand's physical self-occlusion.

The 19 tactile channels are binarized and embedded as location-specific contact tokens. Cross-modal attention joins these tactile tokens with geometry only at the highest semantic stage, where geometric features have accumulated broader context. During pretraining, a decoder reconstructs the masked point patches and tactile observations. Afterward, the decoder is discarded. The encoder is then frozen and serves as the perceptual backbone for a controller trained with PPO, a reinforcement-learning method for optimizing a policy.

Proposed information flow. A masked 3D point cloud passes through hierarchical geometric encoder stages, and binary tactile measurements become contact tokens. The two streams meet in cross-modal attention at the highest semantic stage. A decoder reconstructs both inputs during pretraining. Downstream, the frozen encoder and proprioception feed a PPO actor-critic policy.

### 3. Does the representation help when the view is incomplete?

The simulation metric is task success rate: the proportion of evaluation episodes that meet the task's success condition. Each seed is evaluated over 100 episodes, and results are reported as mean and standard deviation across three seeds.

The faucet task trains on three geometries and tests unseen scales of 0.9 and 1.1. The tabletop task trains on ten YCB objects and tests six held-out YCB objects.

The paper reports an average success rate of 86.2% for OccluDex versus 75.8% for VTDexManip, the strongest aggregate visuo-tactile baseline reported. This figure averages seen and unseen results within each task, then gives the two tasks equal weight. On unseen faucet scales, OccluDex reaches 86.6% (±2.9) versus 63.0% (±25.6) for VTDexManip. The margins vary by setting: on unseen tabletop objects, OccluDex scores 83.3% versus VTDexManip's 81.8%.

The ablations test what each input contributes. Removing tactile input lowers success more than removing point-cloud input in all four task/settings. Removing geometry also lowers performance. These comparisons test input contributions; they do not establish that either modality is the only cause of performance.

Ablation success rates, given as seen / unseen. Faucet Rotation: full 100.0 / 83.7; without point cloud 75.7 / 77.0; without tactile 54.3 / 35.7. Tabletop Object Reorientation: full 77.3 / 81.3; without point cloud 67.7 / 76.7; without tactile 61.7 / 64.0. The "From scratch (w/o hierarchy)" row changes the architecture as well as pretraining status.

The from-scratch row does not isolate the effect of pretraining. Training the full encoder jointly with PPO was impractical under the authors' hardware constraints, and the feasible reference also removes the hierarchy.

### 4. Closing

The hand's occlusion leaves vision incomplete. In OccluDex, touch supplies local interaction information while 3D geometry supplies broader structure.

The authors also ran zero-shot transfer to a Shadow Dexterous Hand, with no real-world updates. They used two previously unseen physical objects per task and ten trials per object. The hand succeeded in 17/20 faucet trials (85%) and 14/20 tabletop reorientation trials (70%). Several conditions limit this result. Simulation did not randomize dynamics or perturb point-cloud observations, and the real camera placement was matched to simulation. The transfer was demonstrated under those conditions; it does not show generalization to arbitrary robots, viewpoints, or objects.
