# Video-Based Optimal Transport for Feedback-Efficient Offline Preference-Based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Video-based Optimal Transport Preference (VOTP) framework from KAIST utilizes Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) and Optimal Transport to generate pseudo-preference labels for unlabeled trajectory data, significantly enhancing feedback efficiency in offline preference-based reinforcement learning. This method enables effective reward function learning with substantially fewer human preference queries and shows strong performance across simulated locomotion and manipulation tasks, as well as on real robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Designing dense and informative reward functions for real-world Reinforcement Learning (RL) tasks is challenging and often leads to reward misspecification.
- Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) addresses reward specification but incurs high human feedback costs, typically requiring thousands of pairwise comparisons.
- Existing PbRL methods under-utilize rich visual perception, which underlies human preference judgments, especially when abundant unlabeled video data is available in offline settings.

## Method

- The Video-based Optimal Transport Preference (**VOTP**) framework uses pre-trained Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) to extract semantically rich latent representations for trajectory segments.
- ==Optimal Transport (OT)== is employed to establish robust correspondences between a small set of human-labeled segments and a large pool of unlabeled segments.
- These correspondences are then leveraged to infer high-fidelity pseudo-preference labels for the unlabeled data, which are combined with human labels to train a reward function and subsequently an offline RL policy.

## Results

- VOTP demonstrated superior feedback efficiency on D4RL and MetaWorld tasks, often matching or exceeding the performance of policies trained with ground-truth rewards using significantly fewer human preference queries (e.g., 10 labeled pairs vs. 50-1000 for baselines).
- Ablation studies confirmed that ViFMs (especially S3D) outperformed Image Foundation Models and that the OT-based pseudo-label inference was more effective and stable than similarity-based approaches, achieving pseudo-label accuracy between **67.0%** and **98.8%**.
- The framework maintained strong performance and robustness across MetaWorld environments with visual distractors and achieved higher success rates (**80%** for Lift Banana, **70%** for Drawer Open) on real robotic manipulation tasks compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ViFMs provide robust, actor-agnostic embeddings that effectively capture both spatial and temporal dynamics in video trajectories, which are crucial for distinguishing subtle behavioral differences in human preferences.
> - Optimal Transport offers a principled way to propagate preference information from a few human-labeled examples to a large unlabeled dataset, by optimally aligning the distributions of labeled and unlabeled trajectory segments.
> - The combination of powerful visual representations from ViFMs and the robust alignment capabilities of Optimal Transport significantly reduces the human feedback burden, enabling effective reward learning with minimal labeled data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved success in various decision-making tasks, provided an appropriate reward function is available. However, in many real-world applications, designing dense and informative reward functions is challenging, often requiring extensive instrumentation or leading to reward misspecification where agents exploit unintended shortcuts.

Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) addresses this challenge by learning reward functions directly from human feedback, typically in the form of pairwise comparisons of trajectory segments. While PbRL allows for learning complex objectives aligned with human intent, its primary limitation is the high cost of human feedback, often necessitating thousands of queries to learn effective reward functions.

Prior research has attempted to mitigate this feedback burden through various strategies, including:
- **Semi-supervised learning**: Leveraging unlabeled data alongside a small set of labeled preferences (e.g., Park et al., 2022).
- **Meta-learning**: Enabling faster adaptation to new tasks with limited feedback.
- **Active learning**: Strategically selecting the most informative queries for human annotation.
- **Preference ranking**: Exploiting higher-order information beyond simple pairwise comparisons.

This paper identifies a gap in existing PbRL methods: the under-utilization of visual perception, which underlies human preference judgments. It proposes to leverage the rich, structured representation space of Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) to infer preferences for new behaviors by comparing them with known preferred examples.

This work integrates three key areas of machine learning:
1. **Preference-based RL**: Enhancing its feedback efficiency, particularly in offline settings where vast amounts of unlabeled data may be available.
2. **Vision Foundation Models (ViFMs)**: Utilizing their robust, general-purpose video representations, pretrained on large-scale datasets, to capture behavioral nuances in trajectories. Existing work with vision models in reward learning often uses Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for direct reward signals or feedback, which can be noisy or require careful prompting. This paper differentiates itself by using ViFMs to generate *pseudo-preference labels*.
3. **Optimal Transport (OT)**: Applying OT, a method for finding the minimal cost to transform one probability distribution into another, to establish correspondences between labeled and unlabeled trajectory segments. While OT has been used in other RL contexts, such as imitation learning for minimizing Wasserstein distance between expert and learner trajectories, and for cross-domain preference transfer, VOTP applies it for *pseudo-labeling within the same domain* and for high-dimensional visual inputs, aiming for more stable and reliable reward learning.

Thus, the work positions itself as a semi-supervised approach that uniquely combines the representational power of ViFMs with the alignment capabilities of Optimal Transport to address the core problem of feedback efficiency in offline PbRL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the significant labeling costs associated with Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL). While PbRL offers a promising avenue for conveying complex objectives to RL agents by learning reward functions from human feedback, the need for extensive human supervision hinders its scalability and practical deployment. Effective reward functions often require adequate coverage of both the state and action spaces, which traditionally demands hundreds to thousands of human preference queries. This annotation burden is a primary bottleneck.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **To develop a feedback-efficient semi-supervised framework for offline PbRL**: The primary objective is to substantially reduce the reliance on human-provided preference labels for learning effective reward functions.
2. **To leverage advanced video representations for pseudo-label generation**: The research aims to exploit the rich and structured latent space provided by Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) to capture the subtle visual distinctions in agent behaviors that inform human preferences.
3. **To employ Optimal Transport (OT) for robust pseudo-label inference**: A core objective is to utilize OT to establish meaningful correspondences between a small set of human-labeled trajectory segments and a large pool of unlabeled segments. This alignment process is intended to enable the inference of high-fidelity pseudo-preference labels for the unlabeled data.
4. **To demonstrate the practical utility and robustness of the proposed framework**: The work seeks to validate the effectiveness of the method in learning performant policies across various simulated locomotion and manipulation tasks, under limited feedback budgets, in the presence of visual distractors, and on real robotic platforms.

In essence, the motivation is to make PbRL more scalable and accessible by automating a significant portion of the preference labeling process, thereby reducing the human annotation effort while maintaining or improving reward function quality and downstream policy performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Video-based Optimal Transport Preference (VOTP), is a semi-supervised framework designed to generate pseudo-preference labels for unlabeled trajectory segments. This framework integrates advanced video foundation models (ViFMs) for trajectory representation with Optimal Transport (OT) for preference propagation.

The VOTP framework operates in two main stages: trajectory representation and pseudo-preference label generation, followed by standard reward learning and policy optimization.

#### 3.1. Trajectory Representation

1. **Segment Definition**: Each trajectory segment, $\sigma$, is treated as a short video clip, represented as a sequence of observations $\{o_1, \ldots, o_H\}$, where $H$ is the segment length. These observations are visual, typically images.
2. **Encoder Selection**: To obtain robust and semantically rich embeddings, the framework utilizes off-the-shelf Video Foundation Models (ViFMs), which are pretrained on large-scale video corpora (e.g., human activity videos). A trajectory encoder, $f_{\phi}$, maps a video segment $\sigma$ to a latent representation $z = f_{\phi}(o_{1:H})$.
3. **Rationale**: ViFMs are chosen for their ability to capture both spatial details within frames and temporal dynamics across the segment, which are considered crucial for distinguishing behavioral differences relevant to human preferences. Their large-scale pre-training is expected to produce actor-agnostic embeddings robust to various visual nuisances and generalizable to robotic environments. The S3D model (Xie et al., 2018; Miech et al., 2020) is adopted in the experiments due to its balance of performance and parameter efficiency.

#### 3.2. Pseudo-Preference Label Generation

VOTP aims to infer preference labels for a large unlabeled dataset, $D_u = \{(\bar{\sigma}_0, \bar{\sigma}_1)^{(i)}\}_{N_u}^{i=1}$, using a small labeled dataset, $D_l = \{(\sigma_0, \sigma_1, \tilde{y})^{(i)}\}_{N_l}^{i=1}$.

1. **Labeled and Unlabeled Segment Sets**:
 - A "labeled set" $L = \{\sigma_i\}_{i=1}^N$ is formed by all individual segments from $D_l$. A preference matrix $R \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^{N \times N}$ encodes the ground-truth preference relationships between these labeled segments (e.g., $R_{ij}=1$ if $\sigma_j \succ \sigma_i$).
 - An "unlabeled set" $U = \{\bar{\sigma}_{i'}\}_{i'=1}^M$ is formed by segments sampled from $D_u$.
2. **Empirical Measures**: Uniform probability measures $\mu_L$ and $\mu_U$ are defined over the segments in $L$ and $U$, respectively, reflecting that segments are typically sampled uniformly from offline datasets.
3. **Optimal Transport Plan Computation**:
 - An optimal transport (OT) plan, $\mu^*$, is computed to align the empirical measures $\mu_L$ and $\mu_U$. This involves solving an optimization problem that minimizes the sum of costs for moving "mass" from each labeled segment to each unlabeled segment:
 $\mu^* = \arg \min_{\mu \in \mathcal{M}} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{i'=1}^M c(\sigma_i, \bar{\sigma}_{i'}) \mu_{ii'}$
 where $\mathcal{M}$ is the set of feasible transport plans satisfying marginal constraints, and $c(\sigma_i, \bar{\sigma}_{i'})$ is the cost function.
 - The cost function is defined as the distance between the latent representations of the segments: $c(\sigma_i, \bar{\sigma}_{i'}) = d(f_{\phi}(\sigma_i), f_{\phi}(\bar{\sigma}_{i'}))$. The Euclidean distance is used in experiments.
 - In practice, the entropy-regularized OT problem is solved using Sinkhorn’s algorithm for computational efficiency and numerical stability.
4. **Preference Score Inference**: The OT plan $\mu^*$ quantifies the correspondence between labeled segments $\sigma_i$ and unlabeled segments $\bar{\sigma}_{i'}$. This plan is leveraged to infer a preference score $S(\bar{\sigma}_{i'}, \bar{\sigma}_{j'})$ for an unlabeled pair $(\bar{\sigma}_{i'}, \bar{\sigma}_{j'})$:
 $S(\bar{\sigma}_{i'}, \bar{\sigma}_{j'}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N R_{ij} (\mu_{ii'} \mu_{jj'} - \mu_{ij'} \mu_{ji'})$
 This formula aggregates preferences from all labeled pairs, weighting their contributions based on how well the unlabeled pair aligns with the labeled pair $(\sigma_i, \sigma_j)$ versus its reverse $(\sigma_j, \sigma_i)$ via the transport plan entries.
5. **Pseudo-label Assignment**: The preference score $S(\bar{\sigma}_{i'}, \bar{\sigma}_{j'})$ is normalized and then compared against a preference threshold $\tau_P$. If the absolute normalized score is above $\tau_P$, a pseudo-label ($\tilde{y} \in \{0, 1\}$) is assigned based on the sign of the score. Otherwise, the pair is considered equally preferable ($\tilde{y}=0.5$). Only pseudo-labels exceeding the threshold are retained for subsequent reward learning.

#### 3.3. Reward Learning and Policy Optimization

1. **Combined Dataset**: The original labeled dataset $D_l$ is augmented with the high-fidelity pseudo-labeled dataset $D_u$.
2. **Reward Function Training**: A reward function $r_{\psi}$ is trained by minimizing the cross-entropy loss between predicted preferences (modeled by the Bradley-Terry model) and the annotated labels (both human-provided and pseudo-labels).
3. **Offline RL Policy Training**: The trained reward function $r_{\psi}$ is used to relabel all state-action pairs in the original offline dataset. Subsequently, an offline RL algorithm, such as Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) (Kostrikov et al., 2022), is employed to train the final policy.

The overall approach prioritizes feedback efficiency by exploiting visual representations and optimal transport to reduce the need for human annotation, making PbRL more practical for real-world scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted across D4RL locomotion tasks (Hopper, Walker2d) and MetaWorld manipulation tasks (Door Open, Drawer Open, Plate Slide, Sweep Into), as well as on real robotic tasks. The evaluation addressed feedback efficiency, component contributions, hyperparameter influence, robustness to visual distractors, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Offline PbRL Benchmarks

- **Feedback Efficiency**: VOTP demonstrated substantial performance gains compared to state-of-the-art offline PbRL baselines, especially under limited feedback budgets (e.g., 10 labeled pairs).
 - VOTP consistently outperformed P-IQL (a preference-based IQL variant trained only on labeled data) across all D4RL and MetaWorld tasks.
 - VOTP effectively matched or even exceeded the performance of the Oracle baseline (IQL trained with synthetic ground-truth pseudo-labels for unlabeled data) on D4RL tasks.
 - In some tasks, like `door-open-v2` and `hopper-medium-replay-v2`, policies trained with VOTP's preference-based rewards achieved higher performance than those trained with ground-truth (GT) rewards.
 - Other semi-supervised methods like SURF showed mixed results, improving performance in some tasks (MetaWorld, `walker2d`) but degrading in others (`hopper-*`), attributed to potentially inaccurate pseudo-labels from reward models under limited supervision.
 - FTB, another method leveraging unlabeled data, showed performance gains but incurred significantly higher computational overhead (2 days vs. <2 hours for VOTP).

- **Varying Number of Queries**: VOTP demonstrated superior feedback efficiency.
 - In D4RL, P-IQL typically required 50-100 labels to match task-reward performance, while in MetaWorld, it needed around 1000 labels.
 - VOTP generally required fewer labels than baselines to achieve comparable or superior performance, highlighting its effectiveness in low-data regimes. For instance, in `door-open-v2`, VOTP with only 10 labels outperformed policies trained with ground-truth rewards.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Video Foundation Models (ViFMs)**:
 - ViFMs (S3D, VideoCLIP, InternVideo) generally yielded better performance than Image Foundation Models (IFMs) (R3M, CLIP) as trajectory encoders, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained temporal understanding like `walker2d` and `door-open`.
 - This indicates the importance of capturing temporal dynamics and subtle motion cues for distinguishing behavioral preferences. S3D was selected for its robust performance and lower parameter count compared to other ViFMs.

- **Effect of Optimal Transport**:
 - VOTP's OT-based pseudo-label inference method significantly outperformed similarity-based baselines (SIM-individual, SIM-mean, SIM-weighted).
 - SIM-mean performed worse than SIM-individual, suggesting that averaging group features can discard crucial fine-grained distinctions. SIM-weighted improved over SIM-individual but remained less stable and performant than VOTP. This validates the effectiveness of the OT formulation in generating robust pseudo-labels by weighting contributions based on relative alignment strengths.

- **Impact of the Preference Threshold ($\tau_P$)**:
 - Performance generally improved as $\tau_P$ (the threshold for accepting pseudo-labels) increased, indicating that higher quality pseudo-labels are beneficial.
 - However, performance could slightly degrade with very high $\tau_P$ values, as this reduces the quantity of pseudo-labels used for training, impacting the quality-quantity trade-off.

- **Comparison of Estimated Rewards with GT Rewards**:
 - Scatter plots and Pearson correlation analysis showed that reward estimates produced by VOTP had a significantly stronger correlation with ground-truth rewards compared to P-IQL. This suggests that leveraging pseudo-labels effectively improves the coverage of the state and action spaces for reward learning, leading to improved policy performance.

- **Cost Metric**: VOTP demonstrated robust performance with both Euclidean and Cosine distances as the cost function in the optimal transport plan, with minimal differences in average scores.

- **Pseudo-label Accuracy**: VOTP achieved high accuracy for generated pseudo-labels when compared against ground-truth scripted preferences (ranging from 67.0% to 98.8% across tasks), confirming the quality of the pseudo-labeling process.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Nuisance Variation

- VOTP maintained strong performance across MetaWorld environments with various visual distractions, including changes in lighting (position, direction, ambient, diffuse), and dynamic video backgrounds.
- In some cases, slight improvements were observed under specific lighting changes, suggesting robustness to a range of nuisance variations due to the generalization capabilities of ViFMs.

#### 4.4. Real Robot Evaluation

- On real-world robotic manipulation tasks (Lift Banana, Drawer Open), VOTP achieved higher success rates (80% for Lift Banana, 70% for Drawer Open) compared to Behavior Cloning (BC) and P-IQL.
- Qualitative analysis showed that VOTP's learned reward function better distinguished between successful and failed trajectories, unlike P-IQL which sometimes assigned high rewards to failed behaviors. This demonstrated the method's utility in learning meaningful rewards with minimal human input in physical robot settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in addressing a critical bottleneck in Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL): the high cost and scalability issues associated with human feedback. By introducing Video-based Optimal Transport Preference (VOTP), the work offers a semi-supervised framework that leverages the strengths of Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) and Optimal Transport (OT) to significantly enhance feedback efficiency.

The key significance of VOTP lies in its ability to learn effective reward functions from a minimal number of human preference labels. This capability directly tackles the primary limitation of traditional PbRL, making it more practical for real-world applications where extensive human annotation is infeasible or expensive. The demonstrated performance gains over state-of-the-art offline PbRL methods, often matching or exceeding "Oracle" performance (trained with ground-truth pseudo-labels), underscore its effectiveness.

The integration of ViFMs for trajectory representation is a crucial aspect, as these models provide robust, semantically rich, and generalizable embeddings that capture the subtle temporal and spatial nuances of behaviors. This allows VOTP to infer high-fidelity pseudo-labels even in visually complex and varied environments. The optimal transport formulation further refines this process by establishing meaningful correspondences between labeled and unlabeled data, leading to more reliable pseudo-label generation compared to simpler similarity-based approaches.

The broad range of experimental validation—spanning simulated locomotion and manipulation tasks, robustness tests against visual distractors, and successful deployment on real robotic platforms—confirms the versatility and practical utility of VOTP. Its robustness to nuisance variations implies a potential for deployment in diverse and unstructured environments without requiring extensive domain-specific data collection for reward learning. The improvements observed in real-world robotics, where VOTP enabled agents to achieve higher success rates with minimal human input and produced reward functions that better differentiated successful from failed trajectories, highlight its potential for immediate application.

Potential impact areas include:
- **Robotics**: Enabling faster and more accessible deployment of RL in complex manipulation or navigation tasks by reducing the human effort required to specify objectives. This could accelerate research and application in areas such as assistive robotics, industrial automation, and exploration.
- **Human-Robot Interaction**: Developing more aligned and intuitive robotic behaviors by leveraging human preferences efficiently, fostering safer and more agreeable interactions.
- **Sequential Decision-Making**: The underlying principle of optimal-transport-based preference propagation could generalize to other domains characterized by scarce annotations and structured sequential data, such as healthcare diagnostics or complex system control.

Despite its advantages, the paper acknowledges limitations, including the potential for inherent biases in pretrained ViFMs to be reflected in learned rewards, necessitating careful evaluation in safety-critical applications. Additionally, the computational cost of OT scales with the dataset size, pointing towards future work on approximate or hierarchical transport methods for even greater scalability. Nevertheless, VOTP represents a significant step towards more scalable and feedback-efficient reinforcement learning from human preferences.
