# Video-Based Optimal Transport for Feedback-Efficient Offline Preference-Based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Video-based Optimal Transport Preference (VOTP) framework from KAIST utilizes Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) and Optimal Transport to generate pseudo-preference labels for unlabeled trajectory data, significantly enhancing feedback efficiency in offline preference-based reinforcement learning. This method enables effective reward function learning with substantially fewer human preference queries and shows strong performance across simulated locomotion and manipulation tasks, as well as on real robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Designing dense and informative reward functions for real-world Reinforcement Learning (RL) tasks is challenging and often leads to reward misspecification.
- Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) addresses reward specification but incurs high human feedback costs, typically requiring thousands of pairwise comparisons.
- Existing PbRL methods under-utilize rich visual perception, which underlies human preference judgments, especially when abundant unlabeled video data is available in offline settings.

## Method

- The Video-based Optimal Transport Preference (**VOTP**) framework uses pre-trained Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) to extract semantically rich latent representations for trajectory segments.
- ==Optimal Transport (OT)== is employed to establish robust correspondences between a small set of human-labeled segments and a large pool of unlabeled segments.
- These correspondences are then leveraged to infer high-fidelity pseudo-preference labels for the unlabeled data, which are combined with human labels to train a reward function and subsequently an offline RL policy.

## Results

- VOTP demonstrated superior feedback efficiency on D4RL and MetaWorld tasks, often matching or exceeding the performance of policies trained with ground-truth rewards using significantly fewer human preference queries (e.g., 10 labeled pairs vs. 50-1000 for baselines).
- Ablation studies confirmed that ViFMs (especially S3D) outperformed Image Foundation Models and that the OT-based pseudo-label inference was more effective and stable than similarity-based approaches, achieving pseudo-label accuracy between **67.0%** and **98.8%**.
- The framework maintained strong performance and robustness across MetaWorld environments with visual distractors and achieved higher success rates (**80%** for Lift Banana, **70%** for Drawer Open) on real robotic manipulation tasks compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ViFMs provide robust, actor-agnostic embeddings that effectively capture both spatial and temporal dynamics in video trajectories, which are crucial for distinguishing subtle behavioral differences in human preferences.
> - Optimal Transport offers a principled way to propagate preference information from a few human-labeled examples to a large unlabeled dataset, by optimally aligning the distributions of labeled and unlabeled trajectory segments.
> - The combination of powerful visual representations from ViFMs and the robust alignment capabilities of Optimal Transport significantly reduces the human feedback burden, enabling effective reward learning with minimal labeled data.
