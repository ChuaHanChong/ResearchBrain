# Revisiting the Power of Prompt for Visual Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Prompt Tuning (SPT) is introduced to enhance Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) by initializing prompts with contextually relevant "downstream inferred token prototypes." This method achieved accuracy improvements of 10-30% over existing VPT methods on self-supervised pre-trained models, outperforming full fine-tuning in 19 out of 24 cases on VTAB and FGVC benchmarks while using less than 0.4% of trainable parameters.

## Problem

- Full fine-tuning of large pre-trained vision models is computationally expensive and requires significant storage for each downstream task.
- Existing Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) methods suffer from high sensitivity to prompt initialization strategies and the chosen prompt length.
- VPT exhibits subpar performance when applied to models pre-trained with self-supervised learning (SSL), limiting its broad applicability.

## Method

- Developed **Self-Prompt Tuning (SPT)**, which initializes learnable prompt tokens using "downstream inferred token prototypes" generated by clustering patch embeddings from the target task.
- Introduced efficient token construction strategies (==mean pooling==, ==max pooling==, ==random sampling==) to dramatically reduce the prompt initialization time from days to seconds.
- Applied SPT in both shallow (**SPT-Shallow**) and deep (**SPT-Deep**) configurations, inserting contextually initialized prompts into specific or all transformer blocks.

## Results

- SPT improved average accuracy by **10-30%** over baseline VPT on self-supervised pre-trained backbones, surpassing full fine-tuning in **19 out of 24** cases while using less than **0.4%** of backbone parameters.
- The method demonstrated increased robustness to varying prompt lengths and exhibited superior scaling behavior with larger model capacities (e.g., up to **15.5%** better than VPT on ViT-H).
- Optimized prompt construction strategies achieved comparable performance to K-means clustering, reducing initialization time from days to mere seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective prompt tuning involves prompt tokens gradually increasing their Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) with input patch tokens during training.
> - Initializing prompts with high NMI via contextually relevant token prototypes from the outset leads to more stable and superior tuning performance.
> - The effectiveness of constructed prompts is highly dependent on using data specific to the downstream task, indicating a crucial distributional alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has largely adopted a paradigm of pre-training large models on extensive datasets, followed by fine-tuning these models for specific downstream tasks. While "full fine-tuning," which involves updating all model parameters, can yield high performance, it presents significant computational and storage challenges. Modern vision models, such as ViT-H and ViT-22B, possess hundreds of millions or even billions of parameters, making full fine-tuning impractical in many real-world scenarios due to the need to store a distinct copy of the fine-tuned model for each task.

To address these limitations, "parameter-efficient fine-tuning" (PEFT) methods have emerged. These approaches aim to achieve competitive performance by tuning only a small subset of model parameters or by introducing a few new learnable modules. Examples of PEFT include methods that selectively fine-tune layers (e.g., SpotTune), update only bias terms (e.g., Bitfit), scale and shift deep features (e.g., SSF), or insert adapters into the network.

Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) is a specific category of PEFT that has gained traction. Inspired by prompt tuning in Natural Language Processing (NLP), VPT introduces a set of learnable prompt tokens into the input space of a pre-trained vision transformer, optimizing these tokens and a task-specific head while keeping the main backbone model frozen. This approach significantly reduces the number of learnable parameters and storage requirements.

However, existing VPT methods and their variants encounter several challenges, as identified by the authors of this paper:
- **Prompt Initialization:** The performance of VPT is sensitive to how prompt tokens are initially set (e.g., random initialization can lead to inconsistent results).
- **Prompt Length:** The number of inserted prompt tokens is a critical hyperparameter, and VPT's performance can be sensitive to its specific value.
- **Subpar Performance with Self-Supervised Pretraining:** VPT has been observed to perform significantly worse when applied to models pre-trained via self-supervised learning (SSL) compared to those pre-trained with supervised learning. This limits its applicability in scenarios leveraging large amounts of unlabeled data, which is a major advantage of SSL.

This research aims to address these identified challenges, positioning itself as a refinement and "substantial complement" to the existing VPT framework. By proposing a novel prompt initialization strategy and optimizing its construction, the work seeks to enhance the robustness, efficiency, and performance of visual prompt tuning, particularly for self-supervised pre-trained models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from the observed limitations of existing Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) methods, particularly concerning prompt initialization, sensitivity to prompt length, and suboptimal performance when applied to self-supervised pre-trained models. These issues hinder VPT's potential to effectively and robustly adapt large pre-trained vision models to diverse downstream tasks.

The authors initiated their investigation by conducting exploratory experiments to understand the dynamics between prompt tokens and patch tokens during proficient VPT training. They observed that as training progresses and the model adapts effectively, the Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) between prompt tokens and patch tokens consistently increases. This finding suggests a gradual convergence of the prompt token distribution towards the distribution of the input patch tokens from the image. This observation formed the core hypothesis: if prompt tokens already share substantial information with patch tokens at the initial stage of tuning (i.e., have high initial NMI), the adaptation process might converge more rapidly and achieve superior results on the target task.

Based on this foundational insight, the key objectives of this study were formulated as follows:

1. **Develop an improved prompt initialization strategy:** The central objective was to propose a method for initializing learnable prompt tokens that inherently possess high mutual information with the target downstream task's patch tokens. This was hypothesized to foster more stable and effective tuning.
2. **Optimize prompt construction efficiency:** While directly leveraging token prototypes via clustering could achieve high mutual information, the computational cost of such an approach (e.g., K-means clustering) was identified as prohibitive. A crucial objective was therefore to devise streamlined and computationally efficient methods for constructing these contextually relevant prompt initializations, making the approach practical for real-world application.
3. **Enhance robustness to prompt length:** Address the observed sensitivity of existing VPT methods to the number of inserted prompt tokens, aiming for a method that performs consistently well across a range of prompt lengths.
4. **Improve performance with self-supervised pre-training:** Specifically target the subpar performance of VPT with models pre-trained using self-supervised learning, aiming to significantly boost accuracy and make VPT a more viable solution in scenarios where vast unlabeled data is leveraged.
5. **Demonstrate scalability with model capacity and data size:** Evaluate whether the proposed method maintains its performance advantages and efficiency when applied to larger model architectures and varying amounts of training data, thereby confirming its general applicability.
6. **Benchmark against state-of-the-art methods:** Conduct extensive experiments to compare the proposed approach (Self-Prompt Tuning, SPT) against existing VPT variants, other parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, and full fine-tuning, quantifying its performance gains and parameter efficiency.

In essence, the motivation was to "revisit the power of prompt" by fundamentally improving how prompts are initialized, transforming them from randomly set learnable parameters into contextually relevant starting points, thereby unlocking greater adaptation capabilities and efficiency for pre-trained vision models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology outlined in this paper begins by reviewing the standard Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) framework and then introduces a novel approach, Self-Prompt Tuning (SPT), driven by an empirical observation regarding the relationship between prompt and patch tokens.

#### 3.1. Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) Overview

The authors first detail the architecture of Vision Transformers (ViT), which typically comprises a patch embedding layer, a stack of *L* transformer blocks, and a classification head. An input image is converted into a sequence of patch embeddings, augmented with positional information. These embeddings, along with a learnable class token, are fed through the transformer blocks.

VPT modifies this by introducing a set of *N*p learnable prompt tokens.
- **VPT-Shallow:** Prompts are inserted only into the first transformer block.
- **VPT-Deep:** Prompts are inserted into all *L* transformer blocks.
During the tuning phase, only these prompt tokens and the task-specific classification head are updated, while the parameters of the pre-trained ViT backbone remain frozen. This design aims to achieve parameter efficiency. The paper acknowledges existing challenges with VPT, including prompt initialization, prompt length sensitivity, and performance issues with self-supervised pre-training.

#### 3.2. Observation: High Normalized Mutual Information Matters

A core aspect of the methodology is an exploratory experiment investigating the relationship between prompt tokens and patch tokens during VPT training. The authors utilize Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) as a metric to quantify this relationship. NMI is computed based on the approximate joint distribution of prompt and patch tokens, derived from sigmoid-normalized cross-attention scores.

The key discovery from this experiment is that as VPT training progresses, the NMI between prompt tokens and patch tokens consistently increases. This indicates that the prompt tokens, designed for downstream contextualization, gradually converge towards the distribution of the patch tokens. This observation led to the central hypothesis of this work: if prompt tokens can be initialized with a high degree of shared information (high NMI) with patch tokens from the outset, the tuning procedure may benefit from faster convergence and achieve superior results.

#### 3.3. Proposed Method: Self-Prompt Tuning (SPT) based on Token Prototypes

Inspired by the NMI observation, the authors propose Self-Prompt Tuning (SPT), which initializes prompt tokens using "downstream inferred token prototypes." The process involves:

1. **Token Generation:** A batch of training images from the target downstream task is fed into the *frozen pre-trained backbone*. This forward pass generates a collection of patch embeddings (inferred tokens) at specific layers.
2. **Prototype Clustering:** These collected inferred tokens are then clustered using the K-means algorithm. The number of clusters (*N*p) is set to the desired prompt length. The centroids of these *N*p clusters are then designated as the "token prototypes."
3. **Prompt Initialization:** The learnable prompt tokens for the downstream task are initialized directly with these generated token prototypes.

Similar to VPT, SPT defines two variants:
- **SPT-Shallow:** Prompts are initialized with prototypes derived from the first layer's patch embeddings and inserted into the first transformer block.
- **SPT-Deep:** Prompts for each layer are initialized with prototypes derived from the corresponding layer's patch embeddings and inserted into that layer's transformer block.

During the subsequent adaptation training, only these initialized prompt tokens and the task-specific head are updated, while the pre-trained ViT backbone remains frozen. Experiments showed that even if the prompts are frozen *after* this initialization (meaning only the task head is learnable), SPT still achieves satisfactory results, suggesting the effectiveness of the initial context provided by the prototypes.

#### 3.4. Optimization for Token Construction

A practical limitation of the K-means clustering approach for prototype generation is its significant computational cost, especially for large datasets. To make SPT more practical, the authors propose three streamlined, computationally efficient alternatives for constructing initial tokens:

1. **Mean Pooling:** Inferred patch embeddings from a single, randomly selected batch of data are mean-pooled into *N*p tokens.
2. **Max Pooling:** Similar to mean pooling, but max-pooling is applied to the inferred patch embeddings from a random batch.
3. **Random Sample:** *N*p tokens are uniformly randomly sampled from the inferred patch embeddings within a single, randomly selected batch.

These optimized strategies dramatically reduce the time cost of prompt construction from days to seconds, while still aiming to provide contextually relevant initializations.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on two sets of image classification benchmarks:
 - **FGVC (Fine-Grained Visual Classification):** Five datasets (CUB-200-2011, NABirds, Oxford Flowers, Stanford Dogs, Stanford Cars) with custom train/val splits.
 - **VTAB-1K (Visual Task Adaptation Benchmark):** Nineteen tasks categorized into Natural, Specialized, and Structured images, each with 1000 training examples (800 train / 200 val split).
- **Pre-trained Backbones:** Plain Vision Transformers (ViT-Base, ViT-Large, ViT-Huge) pre-trained with:
 - Supervised learning on ImageNet-21K.
 - Self-supervised learning (MAE and MoCo-v3) on ImageNet-1K.
- **Implementation Details:** Images were resized to 224x224. Data augmentation included random resize crop and horizontal flip for FGVC, but only resizing for VTAB-1K. The AdamW optimizer was used with a mini-batch size of 32, a linear warm-up for 10 epochs, and a cosine learning rate schedule over 100 epochs. Results were averaged over three runs.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Test accuracy.

The methodology emphasizes a data-driven approach, starting from an empirical observation, developing a theoretically motivated solution, and then pragmatically optimizing it for computational efficiency, all while rigorously evaluating its performance across diverse settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that Self-Prompt Tuning (SPT) addresses several limitations of existing Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) methods, achieving improved performance and robustness.

#### 4.1. Performance Gains with Self-Supervised Pre-trained Backbones

- **Significant Improvement over VPT:** SPT exhibits substantial performance gains over conventional VPT and GateVPT when using self-supervised learning (SSL) pre-trained backbones (MAE and MoCo-v3 on ImageNet-1K).
 - Under MAE pre-training, SPT improves average accuracy by 10% to 30% compared to VPT.
 - Notably, SPT-Deep with MAE pre-training outperforms full fine-tuning in 19 out of 24 evaluated cases while utilizing less than 0.4% of the backbone's learnable parameters. On certain challenging tasks (e.g., CUB-200-2011, NABirds), SPT's performance is comparable to full fine-tuning.
 - With MoCo-v3 pre-training, SPT also achieves considerable gains, such as a +2.88% increase in FGVC Mean Accuracy and a +15.98% increase in the VTAB Structured group compared to VPT.
- **MoCo-v3 vs. MAE:** The results indicate that MoCo-v3 pre-training generally yields better adaptability for downstream tasks compared to MAE pre-training.

#### 4.2. Competitive Performance with Supervised Pre-trained Backbones

- When using a ViT-B backbone pre-trained on supervised ImageNet-21K, SPT-Deep achieves a mean accuracy of 91.40% on FGVC benchmarks, which is competitive with, and in some cases surpasses, other leading parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like SSF and SNF, while maintaining a low number of trainable parameters.

#### 4.3. Impact of Different Prompt Sampling Strategies (Ablation Study)

- **Superiority of Contextual Initialization:** All proposed prompt sampling strategies (K-means, Mean pooling, Max pooling, Random sample) for constructing prompts significantly outperform the random initialization strategy used in baseline VPT.
- **Effectiveness of Frozen Prompts:** Even when the prompts are initialized using SPT and then *frozen* (meaning only the task head is learnable during fine-tuning), SPT-Deep still achieves notable performance improvements compared to randomly initialized and frozen prompts. This suggests that the SPT initialization provides a highly effective starting point. For instance, on CUB-200 with MAE pre-training, frozen SPT prompts yield a +13.8% accuracy gain over frozen random prompts.
- **Efficiency of Optimized Strategies:** The optimized strategies (mean/max pooling, random sampling) provide performance comparable to K-means clustering but reduce the prompt construction time from days to mere seconds, making the approach practical.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Prompt Length

- In contrast to VPT, which often exhibits fluctuating performance and sensitivity to the number of inserted prompts, SPT demonstrates robustness to variations in prompt length. As prompt length increases, SPT generally shows consistent or slightly improved performance. This stability is attributed to the more appropriate initialization of prompts.

#### 4.5. "Right Tool for the Right Job": Specificity of Prompt Construction Data

- Experiments investigating cross-task prompt construction reveal that prompts are most effective when constructed using data specifically from the *downstream task itself*. Using data from other tasks results in suboptimal performance, suggesting that distributional alignment between prompt features and target task data features is important.

#### 4.6. Scalability with Model Capacity

- SPT exhibits superior scaling behavior compared to VPT as the pre-trained backbone size increases (from ViT-B to ViT-H).
- The performance advantages of SPT over VPT persist and even become more pronounced with larger models (e.g., SPT with ViT-H outperforms VPT with ViT-H by up to 15.5%). VPT, conversely, showed signs of degradation for larger models in some comparative settings.

#### 4.7. Insights on Downstream Tuning with Varying Data Sizes

- With limited downstream task data (e.g., less than 10% of ImageNet-1K), both SPT and VPT outperform full fine-tuning, particularly for MAE pre-trained models. This indicates that parameter-efficient methods are beneficial when data is scarce, likely due to reduced overfitting.
- However, when ample task data is available, full fine-tuning tends to surpass SPT and VPT. This suggests that the limited number of learnable parameters in prompt tuning methods may restrict their ability to fully capture information from very large datasets.
- Supervised pre-training is observed to be a preferable choice when task data is minimal (e.g., 1%), as the supervised backbone already possesses strong recognition capabilities, reducing reliance on extensive downstream task data.

#### 4.8. Representation Similarity Analysis (Appendix)

- Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) analysis reveals that SPT consistently achieves higher similarity between prompts and image embeddings within the *same layer* across both supervised and self-supervised models.
- In contrast, VPT, especially with self-supervised pre-training, exhibits a more "disordered" similarity, where prompts from one layer might show higher similarity to image embeddings from *other* layers rather than their corresponding layer. This misalignment is posited as a contributing factor to VPT's weaker performance with SSL pre-training.

In summary, the findings indicate that SPT significantly enhances the effectiveness and robustness of visual prompt tuning, particularly for self-supervised pre-trained models. It achieves this through a principled prompt initialization strategy, which also offers computational efficiency, scalability, and stability across different experimental conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Revisiting the Power of Prompt for Visual Tuning" offers several significant contributions and has considerable potential impact on the field of computer vision, particularly in the domain of efficient model adaptation.

- **Advancement in Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):** The primary significance lies in substantially enhancing the Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) paradigm. By addressing critical weaknesses like prompt initialization, prompt length sensitivity, and performance with self-supervised pre-training, SPT makes PEFT a more robust, reliable, and powerful alternative to full fine-tuning. This could accelerate the adoption of PEFT methods for practical applications.

- **Unlocking the Potential of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** One of the most impactful findings is SPT's ability to dramatically improve VPT's performance on models pre-trained with self-supervised learning (e.g., MAE, MoCo-v3). Previously, VPT showed suboptimal results in this domain, which was a barrier to leveraging the vast quantities of unlabeled data available for SSL. SPT's superior performance with SSL backbones makes it a crucial tool for adapting foundation models trained without labels, thus broadening the applicability of SSL in various domains, including those with limited labeled data.

- **Practicality and Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Reduced Resource Usage:** SPT maintains the core advantage of prompt tuning by requiring a minimal number of learnable parameters (less than 0.4% of backbone parameters), which translates to significantly lower computational costs during training and reduced storage overhead compared to full fine-tuning. This is critical for deploying large models in resource-constrained environments or for multi-task learning where storing distinct full models for each task is unfeasible.
 - **Efficient Initialization:** The proposed optimized strategies for prompt construction (mean/max pooling, random sampling) effectively resolve the high computational cost associated with direct K-means clustering. By reducing initialization time from days to seconds, SPT becomes a highly practical solution for rapid model adaptation.

- **Improved Robustness and Scalability:** SPT's demonstrated robustness to prompt length variations and its superior scaling behavior with increasing model capacity (from ViT-B to ViT-H) imply greater stability and generalizability. This characteristic is essential for developing and deploying adaptable models that can perform consistently across different task complexities and model sizes.

- **Novel Insights into Prompt Learning:** The observation regarding the evolving Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) between prompts and patch tokens provides a novel conceptual understanding of how prompts learn to contextualize. This insight suggests that effective prompts emulate the distribution of input features. This mechanistic understanding could inspire future research into more theoretically grounded prompt design and optimization.

- **Guidance for Model Adaptation Strategy:** The study's exploration of varying training data sizes provides practical guidance for practitioners. It highlights that for tasks with limited data, parameter-efficient methods like SPT (and to some extent VPT) are advantageous, while full fine-tuning may still be preferred for very large downstream datasets. The finding that supervised pre-training offers stronger initial recognition capabilities for minimal task data also informs choices in the pre-training-to-fine-tuning pipeline.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for further investigation, including the theoretical guarantees for the proposed initialization strategy, more quantitative analysis of model transferability across datasets based on prompt construction data, and exploration of dynamic prompt lengths tailored to specific tasks or model states.

In conclusion, SPT represents a significant step forward in making visual prompt tuning more effective, efficient, and broadly applicable. Its contributions have the potential to democratize access to the power of large pre-trained vision models by making their adaptation more accessible and sustainable across a wider range of tasks and computational budgets.
