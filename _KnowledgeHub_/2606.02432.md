# NDPP-Grasp: Non-Differentiable Physical Plausibility Constraint-Guided Task-Oriented Dexterous Grasp Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Lancaster University developed NDPP-Grasp, a framework that integrates non-differentiable physical plausibility constraints directly into the denoising steps of task-aligned diffusion models for dexterous grasp generation. This method achieves superior physical quality, task alignment, and diversity with significantly reduced inference times, by using a gradient-free optimal control law and an amortized lookahead strategy.

## Problem

- Generating dexterous grasps requires simultaneously satisfying task alignment and inherently non-differentiable physical plausibility criteria (e.g., non-penetration, self-collision).
- Existing joint training approaches struggle with the heterogeneity of objectives, often relying on differentiable surrogate losses that can misrepresent true physical constraints.
- Decoupled post-generation refinement methods apply physical constraints too late, making it difficult to correct grasps that have already converged to physically implausible configurations.

## Method

- **NDPP-Grasp** injects non-differentiable physical plausibility guidance directly into the denoising process of a task-aligned ==diffusion== model during inference.
- It formulates this guidance as a stochastic optimal control problem, deriving a gradient-free optimal control law that only requires forward evaluations of the non-differentiable constraints.
- A practical amortized lookahead strategy is introduced to efficiently approximate the intractable optimal control law, reusing future trajectory states with a mathematical correction for real-time computation.

## Results

- NDPP-Grasp consistently improved physical plausibility metrics (e.g., increased success rate, decreased penetration depth) and overall performance across various base diffusion models and benchmarks.
- It achieved superior performance in physical quality, task alignment, and diversity compared to baseline methods while significantly reducing average per-grasp inference time (e.g., from 395.8 ms to 17.7 ms on DexTOG-80K).
- The gradient-free optimal control law proved more effective than methods using surrogate gradients, and the amortized lookahead strategy maintained performance while reducing inference time by over an order of magnitude.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-differentiable physical constraints can be effectively integrated into diffusion model denoising by reframing the problem as stochastic optimal control, even without gradient information.
> - A carefully designed amortized lookahead mechanism drastically reduces the computational cost of direct non-differentiable guidance, making it feasible for real-time robotic applications.
> - Continuously guiding grasp generation with true physical constraints throughout the denoising process yields more robustly plausible and task-aligned grasps than post-hoc refinement or surrogate loss-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of task-oriented dexterous grasp generation, a fundamental problem within the field of dexterous robotic manipulation. This area focuses on synthesizing dexterous hand poses for a given object and a specified manipulation task, with the aim of ensuring both physical plausibility and functional suitability. Successful grasp generation is critical for the subsequent execution of manipulation tasks across various applications, including industrial manufacturing, household services, and medical rehabilitation.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion models, have emerged as a promising direction for this problem. However, current methods face a persistent challenge: simultaneously satisfying two heterogeneous requirements—task alignment and physical plausibility. Task alignment typically involves aligning the grasp with manipulation intents specified by a task (e.g., through natural language), which can often be supervised differentiably. In contrast, physical plausibility—such as hand-object non-penetration, self-collision avoidance, and joint-limit adherence—often involves inherently non-differentiable criteria.

Existing approaches to handle these challenges generally fall into two categories:
1. **Joint Training:** Attempting to train a single generative model (e.g., a diffusion model) to satisfy both task alignment and physical plausibility simultaneously. This strategy can lead to complex optimization problems due to the heterogeneity of the objectives. Moreover, the non-differentiable nature of many physical constraints often necessitates the use of differentiable surrogate losses, which can introduce a gap between the optimization target and the true physical plausibility criteria, limiting effectiveness.
2. **Decoupled Two-Stage Approach:** Training a generative model primarily for task alignment in a first stage, followed by a separate post-generation refinement process to improve physical plausibility. While this allows the generative model to focus on task alignment, it has limitations. Physical constraints are applied only after a grasp has been fully generated, which means the grasp may have already converged to physically implausible regions, making recovery difficult for the refinement stage. Similar to joint training, these refinement stages often still rely on differentiable surrogates for physical constraints.

This work positions itself to overcome the limitations of these existing strategies. It proposes a novel framework that, instead of decoupling the requirements into sequential stages or attempting to optimize them jointly during training, integrates physical plausibility guidance directly into the *denoising process* of a task-aligned diffusion model. This aims to continuously shape grasp generation with physical constraints throughout its formation, without relying on differentiable surrogates for those constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are motivated by the inherent challenges in generating high-quality, practically useful dexterous grasps for robotic manipulation.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **To develop a framework that directly injects physical plausibility guidance into the denoising process of a task-aligned grasp diffusion model.** The aim is to ensure physical constraints influence grasp generation throughout its trajectory, rather than as a post-hoc correction.
2. **To enable this guidance even when physical plausibility constraints are non-differentiable.** This is a critical objective, as many essential physical constraints (e.g., hand-object non-penetration, self-collision) are binary and lack analytical gradients, making them difficult to integrate into gradient-based optimization schemes.
3. **To achieve this non-differentiable guidance without relying on differentiable surrogate losses.** The reliance on surrogate losses in existing methods is identified as a source of performance limitation due to the potential mismatch between the surrogate and the true constraint.
4. **To ensure the proposed guidance mechanism is computationally practical for real-time robotic applications.** Theoretical solutions, if computationally intractable, may not be deployable. Therefore, a practical implementation strategy is a core objective.
5. **To preserve task alignment during the guided generation process.** While improving physical plausibility, the method should ensure the generated grasps remain functionally suitable for the specified manipulation task, leveraging the capabilities of the pre-trained task-aligned diffusion model.

**Motivation:**
The motivation for these objectives stems from the identified shortcomings of current methodologies:
- **Compromised Training in Joint Approaches:** Incorporating heterogeneous task alignment and physical plausibility objectives into a single training framework complicates optimization and can lead to suboptimal performance in both aspects. Non-differentiable constraints further exacerbate this by requiring approximations.
- **Late Intervention in Decoupled Approaches:** Applying physical plausibility constraints only after a grasp has been fully generated means the intermediate denoising steps are unconstrained. This allows the grasp to potentially drift into physically impossible configurations, which can be challenging or impossible to correct in a refinement stage. The limited effectiveness of differentiable surrogates persists here as well.
- **Mismatch with True Physicality:** The use of differentiable surrogate losses to approximate non-differentiable physical constraints introduces a gap. This gap can lead to generated grasps that appear plausible under the surrogate but still violate true physical conditions in real-world environments.
- **Need for Progressive Guidance:** The researchers hypothesize that continuous guidance throughout the denoising process will inherently lead to more robustly plausible grasps, as deviations from physical constraints can be corrected incrementally.

By addressing these points, the research aims to provide a more effective and principled solution for generating dexterous grasps that are both functionally appropriate and reliably physically plausible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, NDPP-Grasp, is designed to address the challenge of incorporating non-differentiable physical plausibility constraints directly into the denoising process of a task-aligned diffusion model. The methodology consists of two primary components: a theoretically grounded non-differentiable guidance mechanism and a practical instantiation strategy using amortized lookahead.

**A. Non-Differentiable Physical Plausibility Guidance for Grasp Denoising**

The approach begins by training a grasp diffusion model solely for task alignment. During inference, physical plausibility guidance is injected into this model's denoising process.

1. **Target Objective Formulation:**
 - A high-quality guided grasp must maintain task alignment and be physically plausible.
 - Task alignment is represented by the conditional distribution `p(y_0 | O, L)` learned by the pre-trained diffusion model, where `y_0` is the final grasp representation, `O` is the object, and `L` is the task specification.
 - Physical plausibility `c` is measured by a non-differentiable constraint evaluator `f_c(y_0)`, where lower values indicate better satisfaction. This is modeled as an energy-based likelihood `p(c | y_0) ∝ exp(-f_c(y_0))`.
 - The combined target objective is to sample `y_0` from the posterior `p(y_0 | O, L, c) ∝ p(y_0 | O, L) exp(-f_c(y_0))`.

2. **Connection to Stochastic Optimal Control:**
 - Existing differentiable guidance methods typically add a guidance term `η_s(y_s, s)` to the reverse-time Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) (Eq. 1), leading to a guided SDE (Eq. 3). For differentiable constraints, `η_s` is often derived from the constraint's gradient.
 - The core problem is to construct `η_s` for *non-differentiable* constraints without relying on gradients.
 - The sequential nature of denoising, where `η_s` guides the sample at each step towards a desired final state, draws a connection to stochastic optimal control, which studies how to make decisions over time to drive a stochastic dynamical system toward goals.

3. **Optimization Problem Formulation:**
 - The reverse-time SDE is transformed into a forward-time control-affine stochastic dynamical system (Eq. 4), where `u_τ` is a control signal induced by `η_s`.
 - The construction of `u_τ` is formulated as a stochastic optimal control problem (Eq. 5). This objective aims to minimize the expectation of a sum of costs: a running cost `(1/2) ||u_ν||^2` (penalizing the control signal's magnitude to stay close to the original unguided dynamics) and a terminal cost `f_c(g_T)` (penalizing physical plausibility violations of the final grasp `g_T`).

4. **Derivation of Gradient-Free Optimal Control Law:**
 - By leveraging the intrinsic properties of grasp diffusion denoising, such as the Markov structure of trajectories and Gaussian noise, an analytical solution for the optimal control law `u*_τ` is derived (Eq. 6).
 - Crucially, this solution `u*_τ` only requires *forward evaluations* of the non-differentiable constraint evaluator `f_c` along uncontrolled denoising trajectories, without needing its gradients. This enables direct non-differentiable guidance.

**B. Practical Instantiation via Amortized Lookahead**

While the derived control law (Eq. 6) is theoretically sound, its direct computation is intractable due to an expectation under the uncontrolled denoising dynamics.

1. **Natural Finite-Lookahead Instantiation (Monte Carlo):**
 - A straightforward approach is to use Monte Carlo sampling. At each denoising step `τ`, `M` uncontrolled future denoising trajectories are sampled. For each trajectory, its terminal grasp `g^(m)_T` is predicted (using Tweedie's formula `T_τ+H(g^(m)_τ+H)` from `H`-step lookahead endpoints `g^(m)_τ+H`), and its physical plausibility violation `f_c(g^(m)_T)` is evaluated. These values are then used to approximate the expectation in Eq. 6.
 - This requires simulating `H` future denoising steps for `M` trajectories at *every* denoising step.

2. **Limitation of Natural Solution:**
 - This approach faces a trade-off between effectiveness and practicality. A larger lookahead horizon `H` improves the reliability of terminal grasp prediction but significantly increases computational cost, making it unsuitable for real-time applications. A smaller `H` reduces computation but can lead to less effective guidance.

3. **Amortized Lookahead Strategy:**
 - To overcome this limitation, the authors propose an amortized lookahead strategy that exploits the overlapping nature of future time ranges in consecutive `H`-step lookahead instantiations.
 - **Key Idea:** Instead of re-simulating `H` steps from scratch for `M` trajectories at each denoising step, the strategy reuses previously generated future states and only simulates one new transition per trajectory at each subsequent step.
 - **Correction for Mismatch:** A mathematical correction is introduced to account for the fact that reused states were generated from a *sampled* previous state, not the *actual* current state. This correction is achieved through a closed-form weight `ρ^(m)_τ+1` derived from the Markov property and Gaussian transition dynamics (Eq. 7).
 - **Five-Operation Procedure:**
 1. **Initialization:** At the first denoising step, perform a full `H`-step lookahead for `M` trajectories and store them.
 2. **Reuse with Correction:** In subsequent steps, reuse the stored future states from `τ+2` to `τ+H` and apply the correction weight `ρ^(m)_τ+1`.
 3. **Extension:** Append one new uncontrolled transition (from `g^(m)_τ+H` to `g^(m)_τ+H+1`) to maintain the `H`-step horizon.
 4. **Monte Carlo Computation:** Use the corrected and extended future segments to perform the Monte Carlo computation of the guidance term.
 5. **Storage:** Store the newly extended future trajectories for reuse in the next denoising step.

This amortized approach maintains the effectiveness of a sufficiently large lookahead horizon `H` while making the per-step computation practical, as only one new transition is simulated per trajectory after initialization.

**Implementation Details:**
- NDPP-Grasp was applied to grasp diffusion models trained following procedures from existing works: Text2Grasp, DexTOG, and DexGYS.
- Three non-differentiable physical plausibility constraints were considered: hand-object non-penetration, self-collision avoidance, and joint-limit adherence.
- Hyperparameters were set to `M = 8` (number of Monte Carlo trajectories) and `H = 20` (lookahead horizon).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and practicality of NDPP-Grasp were evaluated through extensive experiments on two widely used datasets: DexGYSNet and DexTOG-80K. Performance was assessed using a range of metrics covering physical quality, task alignment, diversity, and computational efficiency.

**A. Overall Performance on Benchmarks (Tables 1 & 2)**
- **Physical Plausibility Improvements:** When applied to diffusion models trained following existing methods (Text2Grasp, DexTOG, DexGYS), NDPP-Grasp consistently demonstrated substantial improvements in physical plausibility metrics. For instance, on DexGYSNet, the success rate (`Succ.`) increased, maximal penetration depth (`Pen.`) decreased, and on DexTOG-80K, the collision-free rate (`η_f`) improved, across all tested base models. This indicates that injecting non-differentiable physical guidance during denoising is effective in producing physically sound grasps.
- **Superior Overall Performance:** NDPP-Grasp achieved superior overall performance compared to baseline task-oriented dexterous grasp generation methods across both DexGYSNet (standard and open-set splits) and DexTOG-80K. This suggests that the method not only improves physical quality but also maintains or enhances task alignment and grasp diversity.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A notable result is the significant reduction in average per-grasp inference time (`Time`) achieved by NDPP-Grasp compared to many baseline methods. For example, when training following DexGYS, NDPP-Grasp reduced inference time from 23.2 ms to 11.6 ms on DexGYSNet, and from 395.8 ms (DexTOG baseline) to 17.7 ms when training following DexTOG on DexTOG-80K. This indicates the practical viability of the proposed approach.

**B. Impact of Gradient-Free Optimal Control Law (Table 3)**
An ablation study on DexGYSNet (using a diffusion model trained following DexGYS) investigated the contribution of the proposed gradient-free optimal control law. NDPP-Grasp was compared against four variants:
- **Variant A:** Uses differentiable surrogate losses for physical plausibility during training.
- **Variant B:** Uses gradients of these surrogates for denoising guidance during inference.
- **Variant C:** Approximates gradients of non-differentiable constraints using zeroth-order finite differences.
- **Variant D:** Uses reinforcement learning to approximate gradient guidance for non-differentiable constraints.
NDPP-Grasp outperformed all four variants across physical quality, task alignment, and diversity metrics. Specifically, it showed higher success rates and lower penetration depths, demonstrating that the derived gradient-free optimal control law is more effective than methods relying on surrogate gradients or their approximations for non-differentiable constraints.

**C. Impact of the Amortized Lookahead Strategy (Table 4)**
Another ablation study evaluated the efficiency of the amortized lookahead strategy:
- **Complete-trajectory sampling:** At each denoising step, full trajectories are sampled until the terminal step for optimal control signal estimation.
- **H-step trajectory resampling:** At each denoising step, `H`-step trajectories are sampled from scratch, and Tweedie's formula is applied for terminal grasp estimation.
NDPP-Grasp, utilizing its amortized lookahead, achieved comparable performance in physical quality, task alignment, and diversity metrics to both "Complete-trajectory sampling" and "H-step trajectory resampling." However, it significantly reduced the computational cost, lowering the inference time from 261.4 ms (Complete-trajectory sampling) and 109.8 ms (H-step trajectory resampling) to 11.6 ms. This result validates the practical effectiveness of the amortized lookahead strategy in maintaining performance while enabling real-time inference.

**D. Qualitative Results (Figure 2)**
Qualitative comparisons demonstrated that NDPP-Grasp produced grasps that satisfied physical constraints and aligned with task specifications. In contrast, a comparison with DexGYS showed instances where the latter generated physically implausible grasps with object penetration, further illustrating the efficacy of NDPP-Grasp in producing robustly plausible grasps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of NDPP-Grasp represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic dexterous manipulation and diffusion-based generative models. Its significance and potential impact stem from its novel approach to integrating non-differentiable constraints.

1. **Enabling Direct Non-Differentiable Guidance:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration of a principled framework that allows direct, denoising-time guidance from non-differentiable physical plausibility constraints. This bypasses the limitations of differentiable surrogate losses or post-generation refinement, addressing a fundamental challenge in generating physically plausible robot behaviors.
2. **Improved Robustness and Quality of Grasps:** By continuously shaping the grasp generation process with genuine physical constraints, NDPP-Grasp produces grasps that are more robustly physically plausible. This translates to higher success rates in robotic tasks and reduced issues like object penetration or self-collisions, which are critical for real-world deployment.
3. **Theoretically Grounded and Computationally Efficient:** The methodology is rooted in stochastic optimal control theory, providing a principled basis for the guidance mechanism. Concurrently, the amortized lookahead strategy transforms a theoretically sound but computationally intensive solution into a practical algorithm suitable for real-time robotic control, balancing efficacy with efficiency.
4. **Advancement in Diffusion Model Applications:** This work extends the applicability of diffusion models beyond scenarios with purely differentiable objectives. It provides a blueprint for incorporating hard, non-differentiable constraints, which are common in real-world engineering and robotics problems, into powerful generative frameworks.
5. **Broader Impact on Constrained Generation:** The principles behind NDPP-Grasp could potentially be generalized to other domains requiring constrained generative processes, such as robot motion planning, inverse design problems with discrete constraints, or even creative generation tasks where specific non-differentiable rules must be followed. This could open new avenues for research in constrained artificial intelligence and machine learning.
6. **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation Capabilities:** Ultimately, the ability to reliably generate physically plausible and task-aligned dexterous grasps is a key enabler for more advanced and versatile robotic manipulation. NDPP-Grasp contributes to this by providing a method that produces high-quality grasps efficiently, thereby facilitating the development of robots that can operate more effectively in complex, unstructured environments.
