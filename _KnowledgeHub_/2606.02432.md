# NDPP-Grasp: Non-Differentiable Physical Plausibility Constraint-Guided Task-Oriented Dexterous Grasp Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Lancaster University developed NDPP-Grasp, a framework that integrates non-differentiable physical plausibility constraints directly into the denoising steps of task-aligned diffusion models for dexterous grasp generation. This method achieves superior physical quality, task alignment, and diversity with significantly reduced inference times, by using a gradient-free optimal control law and an amortized lookahead strategy.

## Problem

- Generating dexterous grasps requires simultaneously satisfying task alignment and inherently non-differentiable physical plausibility criteria (e.g., non-penetration, self-collision).
- Existing joint training approaches struggle with the heterogeneity of objectives, often relying on differentiable surrogate losses that can misrepresent true physical constraints.
- Decoupled post-generation refinement methods apply physical constraints too late, making it difficult to correct grasps that have already converged to physically implausible configurations.

## Method

- **NDPP-Grasp** injects non-differentiable physical plausibility guidance directly into the denoising process of a task-aligned ==diffusion== model during inference.
- It formulates this guidance as a stochastic optimal control problem, deriving a gradient-free optimal control law that only requires forward evaluations of the non-differentiable constraints.
- A practical amortized lookahead strategy is introduced to efficiently approximate the intractable optimal control law, reusing future trajectory states with a mathematical correction for real-time computation.

## Results

- NDPP-Grasp consistently improved physical plausibility metrics (e.g., increased success rate, decreased penetration depth) and overall performance across various base diffusion models and benchmarks.
- It achieved superior performance in physical quality, task alignment, and diversity compared to baseline methods while significantly reducing average per-grasp inference time (e.g., from 395.8 ms to 17.7 ms on DexTOG-80K).
- The gradient-free optimal control law proved more effective than methods using surrogate gradients, and the amortized lookahead strategy maintained performance while reducing inference time by over an order of magnitude.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-differentiable physical constraints can be effectively integrated into diffusion model denoising by reframing the problem as stochastic optimal control, even without gradient information.
> - A carefully designed amortized lookahead mechanism drastically reduces the computational cost of direct non-differentiable guidance, making it feasible for real-time robotic applications.
> - Continuously guiding grasp generation with true physical constraints throughout the denoising process yields more robustly plausible and task-aligned grasps than post-hoc refinement or surrogate loss-based methods.
