# ActivePusher: Active Learning and Planning with Residual Physics for Nonprehensile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ACTIVEPUSHER combines residual-physics dynamics models with NTK-based epistemic uncertainty for nonprehensile pushing: uncertainty drives both active-learning data acquisition (BAIT) and active kinodynamic planning (SST), biasing control sampling toward reliable actions. It cuts training data to 55% of baseline while raising planning success, validated in simulation and real UR10 experiments.

## Problem

- Learning-based dynamics models for nonprehensile manipulation need large amounts of costly, time-consuming real-robot interaction data, since collection typically relies on random sampling.
- Learned models exhibit high uncertainty in underexplored regions of the skill space, so predictions there are unreliable and undermine long-horizon kinodynamic planning.
- Prior active skill-learning methods target only binary success classification via Gaussian Processes, giving little attention to regression-based neural skill models usable in kinodynamic planners.

## Method

- ==Residual-physics network==: an MLP predicts residual corrections to an analytical quasi-static pushing model, combining physics priors with learned SE(2) error correction under low-data conditions.
- ==NTK-based uncertainty quantification==: treats the trained network as a ==Gaussian Process== with the ==Neural Tangent Kernel== to derive posterior predictive covariance as an epistemic uncertainty estimate.
- Active learning via ==BAIT== and active kinodynamic planning: a ==Fisher-information== batch acquisition strategy selects informative pushes for training, while an uncertainty-biased sampler steers the ==SST planner== toward low-uncertainty, reliable actions.

## Results

- Residual BAIT matches baseline MLP Random's final SE2 prediction accuracy while using only **55%** of the training data on average, across four simulated and two real-world objects.
- Active planning consistently beats regular planning in task success rate and tracking accuracy, at the cost of roughly **9-13%** longer average path length.
- In closed-loop execution ACTIVEPUSHER reaches **100%** success on Push-to-Region and outperforms HACMan on Push-to-Edge, using far fewer training interactions and generalizing to novel obstacle placements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - NTK-derived posterior covariance gives a lightweight, principled epistemic-uncertainty signal usable simultaneously for data acquisition and for planner action selection, at negligible inference overhead (8.1ms for 10,000 candidates).
> - Combining a coarse analytical physics prior with residual learning is most valuable precisely in the low-data regime that active learning targets, since pure data-driven models need far more samples to match it.
> - Biasing sampling-based planners toward model-confident regions preserves probabilistic completeness while trading modest path-length increases for materially higher execution reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on model-based planning for nonprehensile tasks such as pushing and rolling. These tasks are characterized by rich contact dynamics, which pose significant challenges for accurate modeling.

Traditional analytical physics-based models for nonprehensile manipulation often rely on simplified assumptions regarding friction, contact geometry, and mass distribution. These simplifications can render the models brittle and inaccurate in real-world scenarios, leading to accumulated errors and task failures during execution, particularly in long-horizon planning.

As an alternative, data-driven approaches offer the potential to learn complex dynamics directly from interaction data. However, these methods typically face two primary limitations. Firstly, they are often sample-inefficient, requiring substantial amounts of costly and time-consuming real-world interaction data for training. Secondly, learned models tend to exhibit high uncertainty in underexplored regions of the skill space. This lack of reliability in sparsely sampled areas can compromise the effectiveness of planning algorithms, especially for tasks requiring sequential actions.

Prior research has attempted to address these limitations through various strategies:
- **Residual Model Learning:** This paradigm combines the strengths of analytical and data-driven methods by training a neural network to predict corrections or "residuals" to an approximate physics model. This approach aims to improve data efficiency and real-world performance by modeling only the deviations from an idealized physical behavior, thus preserving physical plausibility. However, it does not fully resolve the issues of sample inefficiency or model uncertainty in underexplored regions.
- **Active Learning:** A well-established machine learning technique, active learning seeks to improve data efficiency by autonomously selecting the most informative data points for labeling. In robotics, active learning has been applied to accelerate skill learning, often focusing on binary outcomes (e.g., success or failure) using Gaussian Processes. However, its application to regression-based skill models implemented with neural networks, particularly in conjunction with kinodynamic planning, has been less explored.
- **Nonprehensile Manipulation Planning:** Existing methods include Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). MPC provides robust execution but requires high-frequency state observations and control, and can be prone to local minima. RL can acquire complex behaviors but demands extensive interaction data and often struggles with distribution shifts and sim-to-real transfer. Kinodynamic motion planning offers global reasoning and generalizability but frequently relies on simplified analytical models and handles execution errors through online replanning, which may not always guarantee recovery from failure.

The presented work, ACTIVEPUSHER, aims to advance the state of the art by tightly integrating residual physics modeling with uncertainty-based active learning and active kinodynamic planning. This framework seeks to overcome the sample inefficiency of data collection and enhance the reliability of learned models by systematically addressing model uncertainty during both the learning and planning phases. The authors position their work as operating without reliance on high-fidelity simulations, large offline datasets, or human demonstrations, thus offering a practical solution for real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and data-efficient framework, termed ACTIVEPUSHER, for versatile real-world nonprehensile manipulation, specifically focusing on planar pushing tasks. This overarching goal is motivated by several challenges inherent in current approaches to learning and planning with dynamics models.

The specific key objectives include:

1. **Enhancing Data Efficiency in Dynamics Model Learning:** A significant challenge in learning-based robotic manipulation is the costly and time-consuming process of collecting interaction data from physical systems. The paper aims to develop a method that can learn accurate dynamics models with substantially fewer real-world interactions. This is critical for practical deployment in environments where extensive data collection is prohibitive.
2. **Improving Model Reliability in Underexplored Skill Space Regions:** Learned dynamics models often exhibit high prediction uncertainty in areas of the skill space that have been sparsely explored during training. This uncertainty undermines the reliability of long-horizon planning, as accumulated prediction errors can lead to task failure. An objective is to quantify and leverage this uncertainty to improve model accuracy and planning robustness.
3. **Seamless Integration of Learning and Planning:** The research seeks to create a unified framework where the learning process actively informs and benefits the planning process, and vice-versa. This involves using model uncertainty not only to guide data acquisition for learning but also to bias control selection during planning towards more reliable actions.

The motivation for pursuing these objectives stems from several recognized limitations in the broader research landscape:

- **Inadequacy of Analytical Models:** For contact-rich nonprehensile tasks, deriving accurate analytical physics models is inherently difficult due due to complexities like friction and contact geometry. Simplified analytical models often prove brittle in practice, leading to significant deviations between predicted and actual outcomes.
- **Limitations of Purely Data-Driven Methods:** While data-driven models can capture complex behaviors, their reliance on large datasets makes them impractical for real-world robotics where data collection is expensive. Furthermore, their performance in novel or underexplored scenarios can be poor, leading to unreliable predictions.
- **Gap in Active Learning Applications:** While active learning has been successfully applied in various machine learning contexts and some robotic learning settings, prior methods in robotic skill learning often concentrate on binary success classification. There is a need for active learning strategies that can efficiently train regression-based neural network models that predict continuous dynamics outcomes and integrate with model-based kinodynamic planners.
- **Vulnerability of Planning to Model Inaccuracies:** The effectiveness of model-based kinodynamic planning critically depends on the accuracy of the underlying forward dynamics model. Inaccuracies can lead to cascading errors and task failures, particularly in open-loop execution or when online replanning cannot fully recover from significant deviations. The ability to generate robust initial plans is crucial.
- **Desire for Autonomous Operation:** The authors explicitly aim for a system that operates effectively without requiring high-fidelity simulations for pre-training, large offline datasets, or human demonstrations, thereby promoting autonomous robot learning and operation in real-world conditions.

By addressing these motivations, ACTIVEPUSHER seeks to enable more efficient, reliable, and autonomous robotic manipulation in complex, contact-rich scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ACTIVEPUSHER formulates nonprehensile pushing as a kinodynamic planning problem, discretized into a discrete-time formulation. Each control action corresponds to a parameterized push skill executed over a fixed duration (τ = 2s), with observations occurring at the terminal state of the object. The object's state `x` is represented as its SE(2) pose `T`, and the effect of a push `f*(u)` is an SE(2) transformation (∆T). Push controls `u` are parameterized by three variables: `s` (side of the bounding box), `o` (lateral offset), and `d` (total push distance), with a sinusoidal velocity profile.

#### 3.1. A. Residual Physics

The framework employs a residual physics approach to model the object's dynamics, `f(u)`, by combining an analytical physics-based model with a neural network. This hybrid model is designed to leverage the structural knowledge from physics while allowing the neural network to learn the deviations or "residual errors" between the idealized physics and observed reality.

The analytical model is adapted from existing work, which describes rigid rectangular objects pushed under quasi-static assumptions with Coulomb friction. For practical implementation and compatibility with the neural network, simplifications are introduced: a fixed `c_ratio` (0.0187) is used, and perfect sticking contact is assumed, where the contact point is fixed and the force is perpendicular to it throughout the push.

The neural network component takes both the skill parameters `u` and the output of the analytical physics model as input. This architecture allows the network to account for object-specific and contact-specific variations not captured by the simplified physics model. The network outputs the residual, which is then added to the physics-based prediction to yield the final model estimate. The combined model is trained by minimizing the SE(2) distance between prediction and observation, as defined in OMPL, with a rotational component weight `w_o` set to 0.2 to prioritize positional accuracy.

#### 3.2. B. Uncertainty Quantification

ACTIVEPUSHER quantifies the epistemic uncertainty of the learned neural network model using the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK). This approach leverages the theoretical correspondence between infinitely wide neural networks and Gaussian Processes (GP) where the NTK serves as the kernel.

The NTK, defined as the inner product of the gradients of the network output with respect to its parameters (Eq. 8), provides a measure of similarity between two inputs from the perspective of the neural network. This gradient similarity is directly related to epistemic uncertainty: regions where inputs have aligned gradients are considered more certain by the model, as parameter updates for one input are likely to improve predictions for similar inputs.

For a finite-width neural network, the empirical NTK after training convergence is used to approximate the predictive covariance of a GP (Eq. 10). This posterior predictive covariance, conditioned on observed training data `S_train`, `L_train`, and a pre-defined inherent data noise `σ_d` (set to 0.005), reflects the model's epistemic uncertainty over an unlabeled data pool `S_pool`. The per-sample model uncertainty is extracted from the diagonal elements of this covariance matrix (Eq. 11).

#### 3.3. C. Active Learning

The framework employs an active learning strategy to efficiently acquire informative training data. Instead of random sampling, ACTIVEPUSHER iteratively selects batches of samples that are predicted to maximize information gain.

The chosen acquisition strategy is Batch Active Learning via Information maTriCes (BAIT), which is based on Fisher information. Fisher information quantifies how sensitive a model's predictions are to changes in its parameters. By selecting a batch of samples that collectively cover the Fisher space, the algorithm ensures that the chosen data points are those where the model can learn the most significant improvements.

Specifically, the acquisition strategy (Eq. 15) aims to minimize the trace of the inverse Fisher information matrix of the selected batch, pre-multiplied by the Fisher information of the entire unlabeled pool. This is shown to be equivalent to minimizing the sum of the NTK-conditioned variances over the combined training and pool sets. The authors modify the original BAIT by using the full gradient NTK and by incorporating the current training set `S_train` into the objective function, ensuring that acquisition decisions consider both new information and existing knowledge. Due to the intractability of exact optimization, a greedy forward-backward selection algorithm is used to select the batch `S_sel`.

#### 3.4. D. Active Planning

ACTIVEPUSHER integrates the learned dynamics model and its uncertainty estimates into an asymptotically near-optimal kinodynamic planner. The object's SE(2) state space is explored using the Sampling-based motion planning with Trajectories (SST) algorithm from the Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL), with a path-length optimization objective.

To improve robustness against accumulated prediction errors, uncertainty estimates are incorporated into the control sampling step. During each planning iteration, a batch of `b` candidate pushing controls is sampled. The epistemic uncertainty of the learned model is evaluated for each control using Eq. 11. Instead of randomly selecting a control, the planner chooses the control with the lowest predicted uncertainty (Algorithm 2). This strategy biases the planner towards actions where the model is more confident and thus more accurate, without sacrificing the probabilistic completeness and asymptotic optimality guarantees of SST, as all actions still retain a non-zero probability of being sampled. This effectively guides the planner to prioritize reliable regions of the skill space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ACTIVEPUSHER was evaluated through experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, covering skill learning, open-loop kinodynamic planning, and closed-loop execution.

The experimental setup utilized a 6-DOF UR10 robot, an Intel RealSense D435 depth camera (mounted on the end-effector for pose estimation using FoundationPose), and a workstation with an NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti Super GPU. A simple reset algorithm facilitated autonomous, continuous real-world data collection. Simulations were conducted using MuJoCo, replicating the real-world scene. Objects from the YCB dataset, with diverse shapes and physical characteristics, were used.

#### 4.1. A. Skill Learning Performance

Skill learning performance was assessed using a pool-based evaluation setup. In simulation, a pool of 9,000 push actions per object was created, while 1,000 actions were used in the real world. During training, batches of 10 samples were acquired over 10 stages (totaling 100 data points), expanding the training set and updating the model. Independent test sets of 1,000 samples were used for evaluation.

Five methods were compared: Pure Physics (analytical model), MLP Random (neural network with random data), Residual Random (hybrid model with random data), MLP BAIT (neural network with active learning), and Residual BAIT (hybrid model with active learning, the proposed method).

The results (Fig. 5) demonstrated the following:
- **Residual Physics Benefit:** Models incorporating residual physics (Residual Random, Residual BAIT) showed clear improvements in prediction accuracy, particularly in low-data regimes, compared to purely data-driven MLP models.
- **Active Learning Efficacy:** Active learning approaches (MLP BAIT, Residual BAIT) consistently outperformed their random-sampling counterparts across all objects. They either achieved the same level of accuracy with fewer training samples or attained higher accuracy given the same amount of data.
- **Combined Advantage:** The proposed Residual BAIT method, leveraging both residual physics and active learning, achieved prediction accuracy comparable to the final performance of MLP Random while requiring, on average, only 55% of the training data. This indicates substantial data efficiency improvements.

#### 4.2. B. Kinodynamic Planning Performance

Kinodynamic planning performance was evaluated on two tasks (Fig. 6): "Push to Region" (simpler, moving a mustard bottle to a target region) and "Push to Edge" (more challenging, pushing a cracker box to the table edge around obstacles). The SST planner in OMPL was used with a path-length optimization objective. Plans were executed in an open-loop manner, with performance measured by task success rate and path-tracking error (average SE(2) deviation).

Comparisons were made between "Regular Planning" (uniform sampling) and "Active Planning" (our proposed uncertainty-biased sampling), using both the baseline MLP Random model and the proposed Residual BAIT model at different data sizes.

The results (Fig. 7) indicated:
- **Model Accuracy Impact:** Planning performance was directly correlated with the accuracy of the underlying dynamics model. More accurate models generally led to higher task success rates and lower execution errors.
- **Active Planning Advantage:** Active planning consistently outperformed regular planning in both success rate and tracking accuracy across all tasks and data sizes. By biasing control sampling towards actions with lower predicted model uncertainty, active planning selected more reliable actions, leading to more robust plans.
- **Path Length vs. Robustness:** Active planning resulted in slightly longer paths (approximately 9–13% increase) due to its preference for low-uncertainty regions, but this trade-off yielded improved task success and execution accuracy.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The computational overhead for uncertainty estimation was found to be negligible, averaging 8.1 milliseconds for 10,000 candidate actions in a batched implementation.

#### 4.3. C. Closed-loop Execution

To assess robustness in practical scenarios, closed-loop execution experiments were conducted, allowing for replanning within 1 second for recoverable failures. The proposed ACTIVEPUSHER (Active Planning with Residual BAIT) was compared against HACMan, a state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) method that also operates in closed-loop.

The results (Fig. 8) showed:
- **Push to Region:** ACTIVEPUSHER achieved 100% success with replanning. HACMan also reached 100% success but required significantly more data (orders of magnitude greater).
- **Push to Edge:** On the more complex "Push to Edge" task, HACMan struggled with distribution shifts (randomized obstacle placement during evaluation differing from training). ACTIVEPUSHER, in contrast, demonstrated effective transfer to novel obstacles and goal constraints with substantially less data.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The paper notes that RL-based approaches like HACMan faced challenges in sim-to-real transfer (reported 70% success in simple planar pushes without obstacles). ACTIVEPUSHER, being trainable directly and efficiently with limited real-world data, achieved robust performance without additional adaptation even with obstacles.
- **Importance of Initial Plans:** Despite closed-loop replanning, some failures occurred in the "Push to Edge" task due to initial plans leading to irrecoverable states. This emphasizes the importance of robust initial plan generation, which active planning contributes to.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ACTIVEPUSHER framework represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for nonprehensile tasks where accurate dynamics modeling is challenging. Its significance and potential impact stem from its novel integration of residual physics, uncertainty-based active learning, and active kinodynamic planning.

**Key Contributions and Innovations:**
- **Data-Efficient Learning:** The framework demonstrates a principled approach to data-efficient skill learning by actively selecting informative data points. By leveraging the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) for uncertainty quantification and the BAIT strategy for batch selection, ACTIVEPUSHER significantly reduces the amount of real-world interaction data required to train accurate dynamics models. This addresses a critical bottleneck in deploying learning-based robotic systems.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Planning:** The integration of epistemic uncertainty estimates into kinodynamic planning is a key innovation. By biasing action sampling towards regions of lower model uncertainty, the planner generates more reliable and robust trajectories, leading to higher task success rates and reduced execution errors. This enables robots to make more informed decisions when faced with model inaccuracies.
- **Unified Learning and Planning:** ACTIVEPUSHER provides a tightly integrated system where model uncertainty guides both the data acquisition process (for learning) and the action selection process (for planning). This holistic approach ensures that the learning efforts directly contribute to more reliable planning outcomes.
- **Practical Applicability:** A significant aspect of this work is its capability to operate without relying on high-fidelity simulations, extensive offline datasets, or human demonstrations. This characteristic makes the framework highly relevant for real-world robotic applications where these resources are often scarce or impractical to obtain.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** By enabling robots to learn skills more efficiently and plan more robustly in uncertain environments, ACTIVEPUSHER contributes to the development of more autonomous robotic systems capable of performing complex manipulation tasks.
- **Broader Real-World Deployment:** The data-efficient nature and robust performance, particularly in real-world experiments with novel obstacles and limited data, suggest that this framework could facilitate the wider deployment of robots in unstructured and dynamic environments outside of controlled laboratory settings. This is especially relevant for logistics, manufacturing, and domestic robotics where nonprehensile manipulation is common.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The core concept of using uncertainty estimation for both active learning and active planning is largely independent of the specific problem modeling. This indicates that the methodology could be extended to richer contact dynamics, more diverse object geometries, and SE(3) nonprehensile skills (beyond planar pushing). Future work could also explore jointly modeling epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty for a more comprehensive understanding of action-dependent noise.
- **Alternative to Data-Intensive Methods:** The strong performance of ACTIVEPUSHER compared to data-intensive reinforcement learning methods, especially in tasks involving distribution shifts and novel obstacles, positions it as a promising alternative for scenarios where obtaining vast amounts of training data is not feasible.

In conclusion, ACTIVEPUSHER offers a promising pathway towards developing data-efficient and reliable nonprehensile manipulation capabilities for robotic systems, with a clear potential for significant impact on both academic research and practical applications.
