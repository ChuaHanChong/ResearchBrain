# Robust Humanoid Walking on Compliant and Uneven Terrain with Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Deep reinforcement learning enables robust bipedal locomotion on the HRP-5P humanoid robot across compliant and uneven terrains through domain randomization and a novel clock-control policy, achieving successful real-world deployment on surfaces ranging from soft cushions to grass lawns without requiring explicit terrain sensing.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based humanoid controllers struggle with uncertain terrain properties and require extensive manual tuning
- Transferring learned locomotion policies from simulation to real humanoid robots is challenging due to the reality gap and complex dynamics

## Method

- Two-phase curriculum training combining domain randomization and current feedback for sim-to-real transfer
- Clock-control policy architecture that enables aperiodic gait generation by modulating step timing
- Reward function designed to encourage forward motion while maintaining stability

## Results

- Successful deployment on real HRP-5P robot across multiple challenging terrains including soft cushions and uneven blocks
- Clock-control policy demonstrates varied swing/support durations and learns to optimize gait cycle timing
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of terrain randomization for real-world robustness
- Policy achieves stable walking without requiring explicit sensing of terrain properties

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Randomizing terrain properties during training is crucial for achieving robustness on varied surfaces
> - Including motor current measurements in the policy observation space helps bridge the sim-to-real gap
> - Aperiodic gaits can improve stability on uneven terrain by adapting step timing

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Conventional Model-Based Approaches:** The paper acknowledges and contrasts its approach with traditional model-based control methods, like the Linear Inverted Pendulum Model and Hybrid Zero Dynamics. These methods rely on accurate models of robot dynamics and environmental interactions. While effective, they often struggle with complex, unstructured environments requiring substantial manual tuning.
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning for Legged Locomotion:** The work falls squarely within the emerging field of deep reinforcement learning (RL) for legged robot locomotion. This field has seen rapid progress in recent years, particularly with quadrupedal robots. However, applying deep RL to bipedal robots, especially full-sized humanoids like the HRP-5P, presents unique challenges due to their higher complexity and potential for instability.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A key aspect of the research is the transfer of learned policies from simulation to the real world (sim-to-real). This is a major challenge in robotics due to the discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics. Techniques like domain randomization are used to bridge this gap.
- **Compliant and Uneven Terrains:** The paper specifically addresses the problem of locomotion on compliant and uneven terrains, a relatively less explored area in learning-based control for humanoids. This is a critical capability for real-world deployment, as robots must be able to navigate diverse and unpredictable environments.
- **Aperiodic Gaits:** The introduction of a control policy that can predict clock signal modulations to achieve aperiodic gaits represents a novel contribution. This challenges the traditional assumption of fixed, periodic stepping patterns and potentially offers increased robustness in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Primary Objective:** To develop a robust locomotion controller for a full-sized humanoid robot (HRP-5P) capable of walking on compliant and uneven terrains using deep reinforcement learning.
- **Motivation:**
 - **Real-World Deployment:** To enable the use of humanoid robots in real-world applications, which requires reliable locomotion in challenging, unstructured environments.
 - **Limitations of Model-Based Control:** To overcome the limitations of traditional model-based approaches, which struggle with uncertainties in terrain properties and require manual tuning.
 - **Advancements in Deep RL:** To leverage the recent advances in deep reinforcement learning for legged locomotion and apply them to the specific challenges of bipedal humanoids.
 - **Blind Locomotion:** To develop a controller that can function without relying on explicit exteroceptive sensing of the terrain, estimating properties from proprioceptive feedback.
 - **Adaptive Gait Generation:** To allow the robot to adapt its gait patterns to different terrain types, moving away from fixed, pre-defined motions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Deep Reinforcement Learning:** The core of the approach is model-free deep reinforcement learning. A neural network policy is trained to map robot state (sensor readings) to actions (joint torques) through trial and error in a simulated environment.
- **Simulation Environment:** The MuJoCo physics engine is used to simulate the robot and its environment. The simulation is modified to incorporate compliant terrains (using soft contact models) and unevenness (using height fields).
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer Techniques:**
 - **Domain Randomization:** The simulation environment is randomized during training by varying dynamics parameters (joint friction, link mass, etc.) and terrain properties (compliance, unevenness). This forces the policy to learn robust behaviors that generalize to the real world.
 - **Current Feedback:** Motor current measurements are included in the policy's observation space. This helps to compensate for unmodeled dynamics and reduces the sim-to-real gap.
- **Curriculum Learning:** A two-phase training curriculum is used:
 - **Phase 1:** Train a base policy on a flat, rigid floor for basic walking modes (standing, in-place, forward).
 - **Phase 2:** Finetune the base policy on randomized compliant and uneven terrains.
- **Aperiodic Gait Generation:** A new policy architecture is proposed that augments the action space to include clock signal modulations. This allows the policy to adjust the timing of steps and potentially generate aperiodic gaits.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function is designed to encourage forward motion, tracking of command velocities, and realistic, safe behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Robust Locomotion on HRP-5P:** The trained policy achieved successful locomotion on the real HRP-5P humanoid robot over a variety of challenging terrains, including flat floors, soft cushions, uneven blocks, paved streets, and grass lawns.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The sim-to-real transfer was successful, demonstrating the effectiveness of domain randomization and current feedback.
- **Importance of Terrain Randomization:** Ablation studies showed that randomizing terrain properties during training is crucial for achieving robustness on compliant and uneven surfaces.
- **Potential of Aperiodic Gaits:** Simulation experiments suggested that aperiodic gaits, generated by the clock-control policy, can improve robustness on uneven terrains by allowing the robot to adapt its step timing.
- **Clock-control policy:** Achieves a higher reward during training, demonstrates varied swing and support durations, learns to speed up the clock during walking (shorter gait cycle).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Humanoid Robotics:** This work demonstrates the feasibility of using deep reinforcement learning to achieve robust locomotion on full-sized humanoid robots in challenging environments.
- **Enabling Real-World Applications:** The ability to navigate diverse terrains is a key requirement for deploying humanoid robots in real-world scenarios, such as search and rescue, construction, and elder care.
- **Contribution to Deep RL for Robotics:** The research contributes to the growing body of knowledge on applying deep RL to legged locomotion, addressing the specific challenges of bipedal humanoids and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The introduction of aperiodic gait generation opens up new avenues for research in adaptive locomotion and control.
- **Potential for Further Improvement:** The paper acknowledges limitations and suggests future work, such as improving sim-to-real transfer for the clock-control policy and addressing state estimation errors, which could lead to even more robust and versatile humanoid robots.
