# BagelVLA: Enhancing Long-Horizon Manipulation via Interleaved Vision-Language-Action Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> BagelVLA, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University and ByteDance Seed, introduces a unified Vision-Language-Action framework that integrates linguistic planning, visual forecasting, and action generation within a single architecture. The model enhances performance in complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks, achieving state-of-the-art results across various simulated and real-world benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often fragment capabilities, focusing on high-level planning or visual prediction in isolation, limiting performance in complex, multi-stage tasks.
- Current methods lack seamless, interactive integration of linguistic reasoning and visual prediction to directly guide low-level control for long-horizon manipulation.
- High latency associated with explicit visual generation in the action loop makes real-time visual foresight impractical for sophisticated robotic control.

## Method

- Proposes **'Interleaved Planning'** which factorizes task execution into sequential ==linguistic planning== (subtask generation), ==visual forecasting== (keyframe prediction), and ==action generation== steps within a unified ==Mixture-of-Transformers== architecture.
- Introduces ==Residual Flow Guidance (RFG)== for efficient visual forecasting, which initializes image noise with the current frame's prior to predict residual changes, significantly reducing latency during action generation.
- Employs a ==two-stage training strategy== leveraging a hybrid dataset of robotic and general multimodal data to build and adapt multimodal reasoning capabilities for embodied control.

## Results

- Achieved an average task completion length of **4.405** on the Calvin (ABC-D split) benchmark, outperforming baselines like π0 (**3.648**) and UP-VLA (**4.078**).
- Attained **75.26%** success in the Robotwin Clean setting and **20.87%** in the Randomized setting, surpassing π0 (**46.42%** Clean, **16.34%** Random) and UP-VLA (**52.92%** Clean, **15.16%** Random).
- Demonstrated **73.3%** average success for 'Stack Cubes' and **63.3%** for 'Calculate and Place Symbol Blocks' on a real-world bimanual robot, showing higher success and planning accuracy compared to π0 and VPP.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly interleaving linguistic planning, visual forecasting, and action generation within a unified framework provides substantial benefits for handling complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
> - The Residual Flow Guidance (RFG) mechanism allows for efficient and low-latency visual foresight by focusing on dynamic scene changes, making sophisticated visual prediction practical for real-time robotic control.
> - Leveraging large-scale, pre-trained unified understanding and generation models (like Bagel) provides a strong foundation for robust semantic generalization and multimodal reasoning in embodied AI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards creating generalist embodied agents capable of performing complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in unstructured environments. This pursuit necessitates combining understanding, prediction, and action execution seamlessly.

Historically, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a prominent paradigm, leveraging large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or visual generation models. Existing VLA approaches can generally be categorized:
- **Direct Mapping VLAs:** Models like RT-2 and OpenVLA directly map vision-language representations to discrete action tokens, offering generalization but sometimes limited expressiveness for continuous control. Others like Octo and π0 use continuous action representations, often via diffusion models, for finer-grained control. However, these methods often suffer from an "alignment gap" between general VLM pre-training and VLA fine-tuning, potentially degrading their original vision-language capabilities during adaptation.
- **Visual Prediction as Bridge:** To mitigate the alignment gap, approaches such as VPP and Cosmos Policy introduce auxiliary visual prediction tasks (e.g., video prediction) to map vision-language signals to action signals. While effective for pixel prediction and alignment, these methods often lack a dedicated VLM backbone, leading to suboptimal instruction-following, especially for tasks requiring complex logical reasoning.
- **Unified Multimodal Models:** Concurrently, multimodal learning has seen the rise of "unified understanding and generation models" like Bagel, Chameleon, LMFusion, and Show-o. These architectures, trained on diverse interleaved datasets, exhibit emergent multimodal reasoning capabilities by processing and generating text and images within a single transformer backbone. Some VLA works have attempted to transfer these capabilities by adding an "action expert," but they often fall short in implementing "step-by-step multimodal chain-of-thought reasoning" explicitly.

BagelVLA directly addresses a critical gap within this landscape: the lack of a unified framework that *seamlessly and interactively integrates linguistic planning, visual forecasting, and action generation* to guide low-level control for long-horizon tasks. Current methods tend to treat these capabilities as isolated modules, focusing on either high-level planning without visual forecasting or visual prediction without complex logical reasoning. By proposing an "interleaved" approach within a single transformer, BagelVLA aims to harness the logical reasoning of language models and the predictive power of visual generation concurrently, ensuring that the visual dynamics are richly aligned with the linguistic instruction throughout the action execution loop. This positions BagelVLA as a significant step towards more robust and intelligent general-purpose robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of BagelVLA is to develop a **unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that integrates linguistic planning, visual forecasting, and action generation within a single architecture** to significantly enhance performance in complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

The motivation for this ambitious goal stems from several critical limitations observed in the current state of embodied AI and VLA research:
1. **Fragmented Capabilities in Existing VLAs:** While recent VLA models have made strides by incorporating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or visual generation models, they typically focus on these capabilities in isolation. Some excel at high-level linguistic planning but lack the ability to foresee physical outcomes, while others prioritize visual prediction but struggle with the logical reasoning necessary for multi-stage tasks. This fragmentation leads to suboptimal performance, especially in tasks requiring complex, multi-stage reasoning over extended time horizons.
2. **Lack of Integrated Reasoning and Prediction:** For a general-purpose robot to operate effectively in unstructured environments, it must simultaneously understand instructions, predict future states, and execute precise actions. Existing models rarely integrate linguistic reasoning and visual prediction *simultaneously* to directly guide action generation, creating a critical bottleneck for complex tasks where intricate causal chains and dynamic environments are prevalent.
3. **Untapped Potential of Unified Multimodal Models:** The emergence of powerful unified understanding and generation models (like Bagel, on which BagelVLA is founded) demonstrates impressive emergent abilities in multimodal reasoning. These models can "think" in text and "imagine" in pixels. However, directly applying these general-purpose models to embodied agents is challenging due to the need for domain-specific reasoning and continuous real-time control, and the difficulty in preserving their full multimodal reasoning capabilities during adaptation.
4. **High Latency in Visual Foresight:** Integrating explicit visual generation into the action loop often incurs high computational costs and latency, making real-time control impractical. A solution is needed to efficiently leverage predictive visual features without the overhead of full image synthesis.

BagelVLA's core motivation is to bridge these gaps by creating a coherent, end-to-end system where these crucial capabilities are not merely co-present but are *interleaved* and mutually informative within a unified transformer architecture, enabling more intelligent and robust robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BagelVLA proposes a novel approach to long-horizon manipulation by formulating the problem as **Interleaved Planning**. Instead of a direct black-box mapping from observation to action, the model explicitly reasons through the causal chain of a task by jointly modeling the immediate subtask, the future visual outcome (keyframe), and the necessary action.

#### 3.1. Interleaved Planning for Robot Control

Given a global instruction (L) and current observation (v_t), BagelVLA factorizes the joint distribution of the current subtask (l_t), the future keyframe (v_t+k), and the action (a_t) as:
`p_θ(a_t, v_t+k, l_t | v_t, L) = p_θ(l_t | v_t, L) ⋅ p_θ(v_t+k | v_t, L, l_t) ⋅ p_θ(a_t | v_t, L, l_t, v_t+k)`
This factorization corresponds to a three-step process:
1. **Linguistic Planning:** The model first generates a textual plan (l_t) to identify the immediate objective.
2. **Visual Forecasting:** Conditioned on the subtask, it predicts the future visual state (v_t+k).
3. **Action Generation:** Finally, the action (a_t) is generated, grounded in both the textual plan and the visual forecast.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

BagelVLA employs a **Mixture of Transformers (MoT)** architecture, comprising three specialized experts:
- **LLM Expert:** Responsible for linguistic planning, using a Qwen2.5-LLM-7B architecture initialized from Bagel. It processes visual inputs (encoded by SigLIP2) and text instructions to autoregressively generate subtasks (l_t) using a Cross-Entropy loss.
- **Generation Expert:** Responsible for visual forecasting (keyframe prediction), also based on Qwen2.5-LLM-7B and initialized from Bagel. It generates keyframes by iteratively denoising VAE-encoded images using Flow Matching, conditioned on current observations, global instruction, and the generated subtask.
- **Action Expert:** A smaller (2B parameter) transformer, connected via the MoT framework, specifically for processing proprioceptive and action modalities. It learns action chunks using Flow Matching, conditioned on current observations, global instruction, generated subtask, and importantly, the intermediate latent states of the image being generated.

#### 3.3. Conditioning Schemes in Dual Flow-Matching

To efficiently couple visual forecasting and action generation, BagelVLA explores three schemes for how the image and action denoising processes interact:
- **Complete Denoise:** Image is fully denoised, then the generated image is used for action generation. High latency (N1 + N2 steps) and prone to visual error accumulation.
- **Joint Denoise:** Image and action denoising are synchronized and performed simultaneously for N steps. Reduces latency but still generates a full image.
- **Single-step Denoise (BagelVLA's default, with RFG):** The most efficient scheme. Action generation conditions only on the KV-cache from the *initial* denoising step of the keyframe. This significantly reduces latency.
 - **Residual Flow Guidance (RFG):** A key innovation here. Instead of initializing image noise from a pure Gaussian distribution (Naive Single-step Denoise), RFG initializes the noise by sampling from N(v_t, I), injecting the current frame (v_t) as a strong structural prior. This allows the model to focus on predicting residual *changes* in the scene related to manipulation, rather than reconstructing static backgrounds, leading to faster action learning convergence and high-quality future frame prediction with very few denoising steps.

#### 3.4. Data Engine

A massive hybrid dataset is constructed using a two-stage process:
- **Robotic Data:** Self-collected and public robotic datasets are manually or automatically annotated (using Seed-1.5-VL-thinking) for subtasks and keyframes, transferring embodied planning and prediction capabilities.
- **General Data:** Egocentric human videos and large-scale image-text VQA data (e.g., LLaVA, FineVision, AgibotWorld, GR) preserve the base model's original understanding and generation abilities.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference Strategy

A **two-stage training strategy** is employed:
1. **Stage 1 (Pretraining):** Finetunes the understanding and generation experts on general VQA, human-hand data, and open-source/self-collected robotic data (without action labels). This stage builds linguistic planning and visual dynamics capabilities while preserving general multimodal proficiency.
2. **Stage 2 (Finetuning):** Introduces downstream robot data with action labels. The entire model (including the action expert) is fine-tuned on all three planning tasks simultaneously, coupling language, predicted visual dynamics, and control for specific scenarios (Calvin, Robotwin, ALOHA).

During inference, the model generates textual plans, keyframes (via single-step RFG), and actions interleaved. The single-step denoising with RFG significantly reduces latency (1.2 seconds per chunk on RTX 5090). **Asynchronous Execution** further boosts inference speed by updating understanding and generation expert KV contexts less frequently, enabling an execution frequency of 72Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BagelVLA's extensive experiments across simulation and real-world environments demonstrate its superior performance, particularly in long-horizon and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, validating the effectiveness of its interleaved planning paradigm and novel architectural components.

#### 4.1. Evaluation in Simulation Environment

- **Calvin (ABC-D split):** BagelVLA achieved an average completion length of **4.405**, outperforming baselines like π0 (3.648), UP-VLA (4.078), and VPP (4.329). This highlights its ability to generalize to OOD scenarios (background/color variations) when relying on visual prediction as an auxiliary task.
- **Robotwin (Clean and Randomized settings):** BagelVLA achieved **75.26% success in Clean** and **20.87% in Randomized** settings, significantly surpassing π0 (46.42% Clean, 16.34% Random) and UP-VLA (52.92% Clean, 15.16% Random). The results showed that even without textual planning, BagelVLA performed comparably to UP-VLA, indicating the consistent gains from the visual prediction component. Crucially, the addition of textual planning propelled BagelVLA to state-of-the-art performance in both in-domain and OOD Robotwin tasks, proving the substantial effectiveness of interleaved planning.

#### 4.2. Real-world Experiments (Aloha-AgileX Bimanual Robot)

- **Basic Tasks (without subtask planning):** BagelVLA achieved an average success rate of **75.5%** across 9 diverse skills, outperforming π0 (65.0%) and VPP (59.5%). Notably, in "Pick & Place Unseen" tasks (OOD objects), BagelVLA achieved **85% success**, significantly higher than π0 (55%) and VPP (45%). This demonstrates its strong multi-task learning capabilities and excellent semantic generalization due to preserved features from pre-training.
- **Long-Horizon Planning Tasks (with subtask planning):**
 - **Stack Cubes in Requested Order:** BagelVLA achieved **73.3% average success** (95% Easy, 65% Middle, 60% Hard) and **95% planning accuracy**. This vastly exceeded π0 (40.0% success, 55% accuracy) and VPP (25.0% success, 45% accuracy).
 - **Calculate and Place Symbol Blocks:** BagelVLA reached **63.3% average success** (80% Easy, 65% Middle, 45% Hard) and **85% planning accuracy**. This was much higher than π0 (31.7% success, 40% accuracy) and VPP (23.3% success, 30% accuracy).
 These results conclusively show that explicit interleaved planning provides a significant advantage for tasks requiring complex reasoning and long sequences of actions, even when all models are trained on the same action data. The high planning accuracy indicates robust semantic understanding.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Comparison of Different Conditioning Schemes (Dual Flow-Matching):** In Calvin, "Single-step Denoise (RFG)" achieved the highest average task completion length (**3.600**) with the lowest latency (**1.23s** per chunk). This significantly outperformed "Complete Denoise" (2.480, 6.04s) and "Joint Denoise" (2.038, 2.90s). This is attributed to avoiding OOD intermediate states during full image denoising in novel environments, confirming RFG's efficiency and robustness.
- **Advantages of RFG over Naive Single-step Denoising:** RFG (initializing noise with current frame prior) demonstrated superior performance in real-world basic tasks and faster action learning convergence than naive single-step denoising (pure Gaussian noise). RFG also enabled high-quality keyframe prediction with very few denoising steps, suggesting it focuses on dynamic changes rather than static backgrounds.
- **Effectiveness of Large-Scale Pre-training:** Pre-training solely on linguistic planning and visual forecasting significantly enhanced semantic generalization, especially in OOD pick&place tasks and medium-horizon tasks (sweep rubbish, pour fries, stack cubes), where pre-trained models showed higher accuracy, even without explicit interleaved planning during inference.
- **Effectiveness of Visual and Language Modalities:**
 - **Linguistic Planning:** Omitting textual planning ("w/o Textual-planning") substantially reduced success rates in Robotwin (from 75.26% to 54.00%) and long-horizon real-world tasks (e.g., Stack Cubes success dropped from 73.3% to 43.3%). This confirms linguistic planning's critical role for long-horizon tasks.
 - **Visual Forecasting:** Omitting keyframe forecasting ("w/o Keyframe-forecasting") also degraded performance in Robotwin (from 75.26% to 56.72%) and long-horizon real-world tasks (e.g., Stack Cubes success dropped from 73.3% to 53.3%). This underscores the importance of visual prediction for accurate action planning.

The ablation studies conclusively confirm that all proposed components—interleaved linguistic planning, visual forecasting, the RFG mechanism, and the two-stage pre-training—contribute positively and synergistically to BagelVLA's overall superior performance and generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BagelVLA represents a significant advancement in the field of general-purpose robotic manipulation, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating the profound benefits of **explicitly and interactively interleaving linguistic planning, visual forecasting, and action generation within a unified framework.**

The potential impact of BagelVLA is substantial:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By bridging the gap between high-level linguistic reasoning and low-level physical interaction through a tightly coupled multimodal planning process, BagelVLA significantly improves a robot's ability to understand novel instructions, adapt to unseen environments (OOD scenarios), and perform tasks robustly even with randomized initial conditions or distractor objects. This is critical for deploying robots in real-world, dynamic settings.
2. **Addressing Long-Horizon Tasks:** The explicit factorization and sequential generation of subtasks, keyframes, and actions allow BagelVLA to tackle multi-stage, long-horizon tasks that demand sequential reasoning and precise execution, where traditional VLA models often struggle due to their "black-box" nature or isolated capabilities. The ability to "think" (linguistic plan) and "imagine" (visual forecast) before acting makes complex causal chains manageable.
3. **Efficient Visual Foresight with RFG:** The introduction of Residual Flow Guidance (RFG) is a crucial technical innovation. It tackles the practical challenge of high latency associated with visual generation, making sophisticated visual forecasting computationally feasible for real-time robotic control. By leveraging current observations as a strong prior, RFG efficiently extracts predictive visual features, allowing the model to focus on task-relevant dynamics and reduce foresight costs without sacrificing performance. This is a vital step toward practical deployment of vision-based predictive policies.
4. **Leveraging Unified Multimodal Priors:** BagelVLA effectively translates the emergent multimodal reasoning capabilities of foundation models like Bagel into actionable robotic policies. This demonstrates a successful strategy for adapting general-purpose AI models to specialized embodied tasks, showcasing how rich, pre-trained multimodal representations can be effectively harnessed for robot control.
5. **Blueprint for Future Generalist Robots:** The unified transformer architecture and interleaved planning paradigm offer a compelling blueprint for developing more intelligent and autonomous generalist robots. It suggests that integrating cognitive-like functions (planning, imagination) directly into the action loop, rather than as separate pre/post-processing modules, is a powerful direction for future research in embodied AI.

In conclusion, BagelVLA not only achieves state-of-the-art performance in complex manipulation benchmarks but also provides a novel architectural and methodological framework that addresses key limitations of existing VLA models. Its success underscores the critical importance of a holistic, interleaved approach to vision, language, and action, paving the way for more capable and adaptive robotic systems.
