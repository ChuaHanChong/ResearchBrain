# DyGRO-VLA: Cross-Task Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models via Dynamic Grouped Residual Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DyGRO-VLA presents a dynamic grouped residual optimization framework to scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for multi-task robotic manipulation, addressing performance degradation and catastrophic forgetting during reinforcement learning fine-tuning. The framework consistently improved multi-task performance on benchmarks like LIBERO and RoboTwin2.0, demonstrating enhanced robustness for complex tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) optimization for VLA models often leads to task-specific policies, overfitting to a narrow set of tasks and losing cross-task scalability.
- Performance substantially degrades and becomes unstable as the number of tasks for RL optimization increases, sometimes negatively impacting the VLA model initially pre-trained on offline data.
- RL fine-tuning distorts shared feature representations, causing catastrophic forgetting of unrelated tasks and reducing cross-task knowledge sharing.

## Method

- An offline pre-training stage learns a task-sharing latent representation using the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle to extract action-relevant features while discarding nuisance factors.
- An online fine-tuning stage employs a Mixture-of-RL-Residuals (MoRR) policy that predicts action refinements (residuals) to the base model's actions without compromising the learned shared representations.
- MoRR dynamically routes and combines outputs from specialized experts, conditioned on learned task embeddings, to provide targeted corrections and mitigate adverse interference during optimization.

## Results

- Achieved leading or comparable performance across all LIBERO suites, improving average success rate to **97.1%** (**+4.4%** over base model) and significantly boosting long-horizon task performance by **+9.8%** (from **85.2%** to **95.0%**).
- Mitigated inter-task interference by dynamically grouping residual experts, which reduced negative gradient correlations between contrasting tasks while preserving positive alignment within similar task groups.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot Sim2Real transfer on the RoboTwin2.0 benchmark, achieving **79.2%** success in simulation and matching or surpassing RFT baselines in real-world evaluations without additional fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard RL optimizers for VLA models tend to distort pre-trained shared latent representations, causing inter-task interference and catastrophic forgetting rather than improving generalizability.
> - Decomposing policy optimization into a shared base policy and task-specific residual refinements, dynamically grouped via a Mixture-of-Experts, effectively balances task-specific dexterity with cross-task generalization.
> - Information-theoretic regularization (Information Bottleneck) and contrastive learning for task embeddings are crucial for learning stable, shared, and discriminative representations that enable robust multi-task VLA fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the growing field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for generalist robot manipulation. VLA models represent a paradigm shift from lightweight, task-specific policies towards unifying perception, language understanding, and action control. They are characterized by stronger semantic grounding, higher representational capacity, and the ability to reuse shared structures across diverse tasks, positioning them as a step towards general-purpose robotic systems.

A challenge in training these models arises from the scarcity and limited diversity of robotic datasets, especially compared to those used for language modeling and visual understanding. To address this, recent studies have incorporated online Reinforcement Learning (RL) for post-training optimization of VLAs. While RL-based optimization has demonstrated success in improving dexterity and success rates on individual manipulation tasks, a noted limitation is its tendency to lead to task-specific policies that may overfit to a narrow set of tasks. This overfitting can result in a loss of cross-task scalability, degradation of performance as the number of tasks increases, and in some cases, catastrophic forgetting of previously learned capabilities, even harming the original VLA model pre-trained on offline data.

The paper identifies a fundamental tension: the trade-off between achieving high task-specific dexterity and maintaining cross-task generalizability. Prior work in RL fine-tuning for VLAs has primarily focused on improving individual task performance or adapting to real-world scenarios. This study differentiates itself by specifically emphasizing the preservation and enhancement of the intrinsic generalization ability of large VLA models during the post-training phase, aiming to prevent the degeneration of generalist controllers into specialized policies. It positions itself as a solution to this scalability challenge, building upon existing multi-task learning techniques and RL advancements while addressing their limitations in the context of VLA model generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observed challenge in optimizing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using Reinforcement Learning (RL) for multi-task robotic control. While RL techniques have improved VLA performance on individual dexterous manipulation tasks, this often comes at the expense of cross-task scalability and generalizability.

The authors identify several specific issues:
- **Task Specificity and Overfitting**: Standard RL optimizers tend to specialize, leading VLA models to become highly task-specific and overfit to a narrow set of tasks.
- **Performance Degradation with Task Scaling**: Empirical analysis demonstrates that as the number of tasks for RL optimization increases, the performance of VLA models substantially degrades and becomes unstable. In some instances, it can even negatively impact the performance of the VLA model initially pre-trained on offline data.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting and Representation Distortion**: An in-depth study revealed that this degradation is linked to the RL optimizer distorting the shared feature representations learned during pre-training. This distortion isolates task-specific experiences, reducing cross-task knowledge sharing, and leading to catastrophic forgetting of unrelated tasks. Visual evidence from t-SNE embeddings shows task representations forming isolated clusters after single-task RL fine-tuning, drifting away from a shared feature space.
- **Balancing Dexterity and Generalizability**: The core problem is framed as a significant open challenge: how to balance the trade-off between achieving high task success rates (dexterity) and maintaining the capacity for generalization across diverse tasks (scalability) during VLA post-training.

Motivated by these observations, the key objective of this work is to introduce a novel RL optimization framework, DyGRO-VLA, designed to enable VLA models to scale effectively across multiple tasks while preserving their generalization capabilities. The framework pursues two main objectives:
1. **Learning Shared Representations Across Tasks**: To develop a robust foundation for multi-task learning, the first objective is to effectively capture cross-task latent representations. This involves employing information-theoretic principles, specifically the Information Bottleneck (IB), to extract action-relevant features from visual observations and robot states. The goal is to retain information crucial for robot motion while discarding nuisance factors, thereby generating features that can be more effectively shared among diverse tasks.
2. **Dynamically Refining Policy Optimization via Mixture-of-RL-Residuals (MoRR)**: The second objective is to fine-tune the action prediction model without disrupting the learned shared cross-task representation. DyGRO-VLA achieves this by designing the RL policy to predict a residual—a refinement—to the actions proposed by the base model. This refinement mechanism is further enhanced by a mixture of RL policies, where a dynamic routing mechanism adaptively selects and combines the outputs of specialized experts. This approach aims to leverage task-relevant latent information while strategically mitigating adverse interference during the optimization process, ensuring both dexterity and generalizability.

Ultimately, the motivation is to develop a scalable and robust solution for efficient multi-task reinforcement fine-tuning and real-world robotic manipulation, addressing the limitations of existing RL post-training methods for VLAs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DyGRO-VLA operates as a two-stage optimization framework: an offline pre-training stage to establish cross-task representations, followed by an online fine-tuning stage using a Mixture-of-RL-Residuals (MoRR).

#### 3.1. Offline Pre-Training for Cross-Task Representation

The initial stage aims to learn a latent representation, denoted `p(z | o)`, that is effective for action prediction across various tasks.

- **Latent Feature Extraction**: The system uses a VLM backbone combined with a fusion module. Visual observations from wrist and third-view cameras are processed by DINOv2 and SigLIP. These visual features are then tokenized and fused with natural language instructions, encoded by a Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM. The resulting hidden states are partitioned into conditioning tokens (`h_cond`) and action tokens (`h_act`). A fusion module then integrates these tokens with robot proprioceptive input (`p_t`) and learnable action-query tokens (`q_a_t`) to produce fused latent features (`h_fuse`).

- **Learning Task-Sharing Representation via Information Bottleneck (IB)**: To ensure the extracted features (`~z_T` from `h_fuse`) are effectively shared across tasks, the learning objective is based on the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle. The objective is to maximize the mutual information between the latent representation `Z` and the actions `A` (`I(Z; A)`) while minimizing the mutual information between the observations `O` and `Z` (`I(Z; O)`), weighted by `λ_IB`. This mechanism promotes the retention of action-relevant information (e.g., object features) and the discarding of nuisance factors (e.g., background clutter). Due to the intractability of direct mutual information computation, a variational approximation is employed, resulting in the `L_base` loss function. This loss includes a maximum likelihood term for action prediction (`-log π_θ(a|z)`) and an IB regularization term approximated using a neural critic `T_ψ`. The joint distribution is estimated with paired samples, while the product of marginals is approximated by permuting `z` within mini-batches. The base policy `π_base_θ(a | z)` is parameterized using an action head that maps the fused latent representation `z` to continuous action chunks of horizon `h`. This offline pre-training yields a shared base policy possessing general multi-task competence.

#### 3.2. Online Finetuning via Mixture of RL Residuals (MoRR)

The second stage focuses on optimizing the action prediction model `π_base_θ(a | z)` through RL without compromising the learned task-sharing representation.

- **Learning Task Embeddings**: DyGRO-VLA learns distinct task embeddings, `~z_T`, designed to capture task-identification patterns. These are different from the latent representations `z`, which focus on decision-relevant patterns. A contrastive learning objective `L_CL` is used to train these embeddings. This objective maximizes the similarity between `~z_T` and its corresponding learnable task prototype `e_ζ`, while minimizing similarity with other task prototypes. Task IDs are used for supervision during training, but the MoE router is conditioned on `~z_T` directly, promoting semantic task understanding.

- **Mixture of Residuals with Dynamic Routing**: A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module is employed to refine action chunks across tasks. Given a task embedding `~z_T`, a router `h(·)` generates gating logits for `N` residual experts (`{π_∆_i}`). Only the top-`m` experts are activated, and their outputs are combined using gating weights (`ω_i(~z_T)`). Each expert `π_∆_i` predicts a residual action chunk `~a_i`, conditioned on the latent features `z` and the base action chunk `a_base` from the pre-trained policy. This residual parameterization allows experts to provide targeted corrections while preserving the base policy as a strong prior.

- **Learning the Residual RL Policies**: The MoE is integrated into the base architecture, with experts sharing the same VLM latent representation `z`. The RL loss `L_RL` for the residual policy combines the predicted action refinement `∆a` with actions from the base policy `π_base_θ`. It employs a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)-style objective with an ensemble of `K` critics. To ensure stable routing and prevent expert collapse, a load-balancing regularizer `L_LB` is added, maximizing the entropy of the routing probabilities. The overall training objective for the MoRR policy combines `L_RL`, `L_CL`, and `L_LB`. Critics are optimized with an h-step Bellman regression term and a Cal-QL calibration regularizer to facilitate a smooth transition from offline data to online rollouts. The residual action space is bounded using a tanh function and a hyperparameter `α`.

- **Difficulty-Aware Sampling**: To improve learning efficiency, tasks are sampled with a bias towards those that are under-solved. This strategy increases the sampling probability for tasks with success rates below a target threshold, ensuring that training focuses on challenging but solvable tasks while limiting exposure to already mastered ones.

- **Practical Implementation**: The system maintains two replay buffers: an offline buffer (`D_offline`) and an online buffer (`D_online`). Training samples are drawn from both buffers using an RLPD-style uniform mix. To stabilize online residual RL, critics and policies are warmed up prior to active environment interaction, consistent with Cal-QL practices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of DyGRO-VLA was evaluated on two benchmarks: LIBERO (a lifelong learning benchmark with multiple suites) and RoboTwin2.0 (a dual-arm manipulation benchmark for real-world transfer), and further validated on real-world robots.

#### 4.1. Performance on LIBERO Benchmark (Multi-task Co-training)

- **Overall Performance**: DyGRO-VLA demonstrated performance that was either leading or comparable across all LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).
- **Improvement Over Baseline**: Compared to its offline Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) base model, DyGRO-VLA consistently improved performance across Spatial, Object, and Goal suites. It achieved an average success rate of 97.1%, representing an absolute gain of +4.4% over its offline base model (92.7%).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks**: The most significant improvement was observed on the LIBERO-Long suite, which involves challenging long-horizon tasks, with success rates increasing from 85.2% to 95.0% (a +9.8% gain). This indicates enhanced robustness for complex sequences of actions.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: DyGRO-VLA remained competitive with state-of-the-art multi-task VLA models, including both generalist large VLA models (e.g., Octo, OpenVLA, π0) and lightweight multi-task policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, MT-ACT).

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) Baseline Comparisons

- **LIBERO-Long Suite**: When compared against other representative RFT baselines on the LIBERO-Long suite, DyGRO-VLA achieved the best performance with a success rate of 95.2%. This comparison was conducted using a suite-wise evaluation protocol to account for differing training assumptions of prior RFT methods.

#### 4.3. Conflict Mitigation via Dynamic Grouping

- **Gradient Conflict Analysis**: An analysis using pairwise cosine similarity of per-task gradients demonstrated DyGRO-VLA's ability to mitigate inter-task interference. For tasks with contrasting interaction semantics (e.g., "Open" vs. "Close"), a single MLP residual head exhibited negative correlations across these contrasting task gradients, indicating strong interference. DyGRO-VLA's Mixture-of-RL-Residuals (MoRR) approach reduced these negative similarities while preserving positive alignment within similar task groups, suggesting that dynamic grouping effectively mitigates cross-task conflicts.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Expert Number Ablation**: Increasing the number of experts in the MoRR module correlated with improved multi-task performance. A single expert achieved 93.4% average success, which improved to 97.0% with 4 experts, and further to 97.1% with 8 experts. The most notable gains were observed on long-horizon tasks, suggesting that more experts enable better specialization and interference mitigation.
- **Component Ablation**:
 - Removing the Information Bottleneck (IB) objective led to a consistent performance drop (97.1% to 96.8% average), indicating its regularization benefits.
 - Removing the InfoNCE-based contrastive learning (CL) objective caused a more significant degradation across suites (97.1% to 94.3% average), particularly on Long tasks (95.0% to 90.4%). This highlights the role of discriminative task embeddings for stable routing and multi-task generalization.
 - Disabling difficulty-aware sampling also reduced overall performance (97.1% to 96.4% average).
 - The full DyGRO-VLA model consistently outperformed ablated versions, demonstrating complementary contributions from each component.
- **Online Training Strategy Ablation**: DyGRO-VLA exhibited the highest success rate and most stable improvement during online fine-tuning iterations. In contrast, standard Residual RL showed large oscillations and lower final performance. Freezing the VLM backbone led to stable but modest gains, while directly updating the VLM backbone with LoRA resulted in severe performance collapse, indicating the instability of large-scale multi-task online RL when modifying the backbone.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments

- **RoboTwin2.0 Benchmark**: DyGRO-VLA achieved the best overall performance in simulation (79.2% success rate) on the RoboTwin2.0 benchmark.
- **Sim2Real Transfer**: In real-world evaluations using a Sim2Real pipeline, DyGRO-VLA matched or surpassed RFT baselines. It demonstrated improved real-world success on tasks such as Pick Dual Bottles and Place Empty Cup, validating its practical applicability for deploying simulation-trained VLA policies in physical environments without additional real-world fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a challenge in the scalability and generalization of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models undergoing Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training. The DyGRO-VLA framework introduces a method to mitigate issues such as catastrophic forgetting and cross-task interference, which have historically limited the effectiveness of RL in complex multi-task robotic environments.

The significance of DyGRO-VLA lies in its demonstrated ability to consistently improve multi-task performance and robustness across diverse manipulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin2) and in real-world settings. Key aspects contributing to this significance include:
- **Enhanced Generalizability**: The framework allows VLA models to maintain and improve their multi-task capabilities, particularly showing notable gains on challenging long-horizon tasks, which are often prone to performance degradation in standard RL approaches.
- **Stable Optimization**: By learning task-sharing representations through an Information Bottleneck principle and employing a dynamically routed Mixture-of-RL-Residuals (MoRR), DyGRO-VLA enables RL optimization to leverage task-relevant information while strategically preventing adverse interference with foundational learned representations. This contrasts with approaches that may distort shared knowledge during task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Modular and Scalable Design**: The modular nature of the MoRR with dynamic routing allows for specialized experts to address subsets of tasks, leading to better overall performance and scalability as the number and diversity of tasks increase. The ablation studies confirm the complementary benefits of its core components, including the IB objective, contrastive learning for task embeddings, and difficulty-aware sampling.
- **Real-World Applicability**: The successful zero-shot Sim2Real transfer demonstrated on the RoboTwin2.0 benchmark suggests practical applicability for deploying simulation-trained VLA policies on physical robots, potentially reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection and fine-tuning.

The research outlines a direction for future work that involves extending DyGRO-VLA to mobile manipulation tasks, encompassing both locomotion and object manipulation, which could broaden the scope of its applicability.

From a broader perspective, the advancements presented could accelerate progress toward generalist robotic systems. Such systems would be capable of performing a wider array of real-world tasks with improved adaptability and data efficiency. Potential positive societal impacts include increased accessibility to automation in various industries, improvements in human-robot collaboration safety, and a reduction in the costs associated with training robotic systems. However, the authors acknowledge ethical considerations inherent in the development of advanced autonomous systems, such as the potential for misuse, labor displacement in automation-sensitive sectors, and biases originating from training data. They encourage continued research into transparent data practices, fairness-aware policy learning, and human-centered deployment standards to address these concerns.
