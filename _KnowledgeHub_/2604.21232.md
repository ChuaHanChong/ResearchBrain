# ReCAPA: Hierarchical Predictive Correction to Mitigate Cascading Failures

> [!summary] Summary
> RECAPA enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems by implementing a hierarchical predictive correction framework and multi-level semantic alignment to address cascading failures in long-horizon tasks. This approach leads to improved performance on embodied agent benchmarks and a reduction in error propagation dynamics.

## Problem

- Long-horizon, multi-step VLA tasks suffer from "cascading failures," where early errors propagate and accumulate, leading to significant performance degradation.
- Current VLA systems lack structured visual-language grounding, causing semantic drift and difficulty in consistently aligning actions with overall task intent across different levels of abstraction.
- Existing error correction mechanisms are often single-level or rely on static task decompositions, struggling to adapt to dynamic environments or prevent the unchecked propagation of errors.

## Method

- Introduction of RECAPA, a framework employing ==Hierarchical Predictive Correction== (HPCC) that predicts higher-level semantics from lower-level steps (actions, subgoals, trajectories) to generate early corrective signals.
- Integration of multi-level Prompt-Trajectory Alignment using both global Sinkhorn-based optimal transport and localized Score-field alignment to ensure consistent semantic grounding with the task prompt.
- A three-tier correction mechanism during inference refines trajectories by resampling actions and adjusting subgoals based on real-time alignment scores, providing dynamic adaptation to deviations.

## Results

- RECAPA achieved superior performance across Vision-Language-Action benchmarks, including VisualAgentBench (58.65 average score), AI2-THOR (**0.75** SR), and MineDojo (leading in 8 out of 10 tasks), surpassing existing large multimodal models.
- The framework demonstrated reduced error propagation, exhibiting the lowest Error Propagation Rate (EPR_k) and highest Propagation Attenuation Coefficient (PAC), indicating more rapid error recovery and prevention of error compounding.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both hierarchical predictive correction and multi-level prompt-trajectory alignment are crucial, with their removal or decoupling leading to significant performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anticipatory correction through hierarchical prediction, operating across action, subgoal, and trajectory levels, is effective in preventing the compounding of errors in long-horizon tasks.
> - Comprehensive multi-level semantic alignment, integrating global distributional matching with localized gradient-based corrections, ensures robust consistency between an agent's execution and the overall task prompt.
> - Jointly trained predictive and alignment modules within a hierarchical structure are necessary to achieve strong performance in VLA systems, empirically outperforming models lacking these integrated mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision–Language–Action (VLA) systems, often powered by Large Language Models (LLMs), are increasingly applied to long-horizon, multi-step tasks in complex embodied environments such as household manipulation, indoor navigation, and human-robot interaction. These tasks necessitate capabilities in perception, planning, and grounded execution under natural language instructions.

Despite advancements, many current VLA systems encounter challenges in generalizing across diverse multi-step environments. Key limitations include a lack of structured visual–language grounding, which can lead to semantic drift where an agent's understanding or actions diverge from the task's true intent. A significant problem is the propagation and accumulation of local errors, termed "cascading failures," where a mis-specified intermediate step can degrade the performance of subsequent steps, sometimes by over 60% in benchmarks like VirtualHome and AI2-THOR.

Prior research has explored various strategies to address these issues:
- **Decomposition Approaches:** Methods such as Re-ReST, LLaMAR, and CityNavAgent decompose complex tasks into subplans. While this improves execution coherence, these approaches often rely on pre-defined or static task decompositions, which struggle to adapt when the initial plan is flawed or the environment changes dynamically.
- **Error-Correction Mechanisms:** Approaches like ReAct, Reflexion, AdaPlanner, and R3V incorporate feedback-based corrections. These mechanisms enable dynamic adjustments to subplans or actions based on environmental feedback or internal reflections. However, many operate at a single level of abstraction (e.g., step-level or episodic updates), potentially leaving unchecked propagation patterns at other hierarchical levels and struggling to maintain consistent alignment across different stages of task execution.
- **Integration Attempts:** Recent efforts, including TrajPrompt, HiP, and VistaWise, integrate decomposition with semantic alignment, aiming to maintain consistency. These methods align substeps or subgoals with visual and action semantics. However, they primarily focus on local alignment, which can result in correct subgoals but failed actions, making it difficult to maintain consistent alignment between the overall task intent and fine-grained operations.

The current work, ReCAPA, aims to address the limitations of existing methods by proposing a hierarchical predictive correction framework that operates across multiple levels of abstraction. It seeks to anticipate and correct deviations early, moving beyond post-hoc corrections and static execution schemes, and to enforce semantic alignment across action, subgoal, and trajectory levels to mitigate execution–goal divergence and cumulative rollout errors. This positions ReCAPA as an attempt to integrate and improve upon multi-level control, predictive capabilities, and comprehensive alignment within the VLA landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to mitigate cascading failures in Vision–Language–Action (VLA) systems executing long-horizon, multi-step tasks in multimodal environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Address Semantic Drift and Error Propagation:** The paper highlights that semantic drift and the propagation of errors are significant bottlenecks in long-horizon reasoning. Existing systems often struggle to flexibly adjust actions when execution deviates from the intended plan, and reliance on local alignment can lead to individual steps being optimized in isolation, causing a drift from the overall task intent. ReCAPA aims to directly tackle this by introducing mechanisms for early detection and correction.

2. **Enable Flexible, Multi-Level Adjustment:** Current VLA approaches often employ post-hoc correction mechanisms or operate under fixed task decompositions. Once an intermediate step is mis-specified, local errors tend to propagate. ReCAPA is motivated by the need to develop a system that can adjust deviations across multiple levels—actions, subgoals, and trajectories—during training and inference, rather than reacting only after failures have occurred. This predictive capability is intended to prevent errors from compounding.

3. **Enforce Hierarchical Semantic Alignment:** A core motivation is to ensure consistent alignment between the agent's actions and the overall task intent across different granularities of planning. Traditional methods may align substeps, but this does not guarantee coherence with the global goal. ReCAPA aims to enforce semantic alignment at all three levels (actions, subgoals, and trajectories) using specialized modules, ensuring that fine-grained steps remain aligned with the broader task objective.

4. **Jointly Update Action Generation:** The framework seeks to integrate predictive correction and alignment signals into the training phase, allowing the action-generator to be continuously updated. This enables the agent to adjust its fine-grained steps proactively to maintain alignment with the overall intent, rather than simply generating sequences of actions without a global corrective loop.

5. **Develop Diagnostic Metrics for Error Dynamics:** The paper identifies a gap in existing evaluation metrics, such as success rate (SR), which only capture terminal outcomes but do not fully characterize how errors propagate, accumulate, or dissipate throughout a long execution. A key objective is to introduce new diagnostic metrics—Error Propagation Rate (EPR) and Propagation Attenuation Coefficient (PAC)—to quantify these dynamics, providing a more comprehensive understanding of agent robustness and recovery processes.

In summary, ReCAPA is motivated by the need for more robust and adaptive VLA agents that can handle the complexities of long-horizon tasks by anticipating and correcting errors hierarchically, maintaining semantic consistency across planning levels, and providing finer-grained insights into error dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReCAPA (Predictive Alignment and Planning Architecture) is a framework designed to mitigate cascading failures in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems through hierarchical predictive correction and multi-level semantic alignment.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

ReCAPA addresses the limitations of prior methods, which often rely on pre-defined segmentation or post-hoc correction. Instead, it introduces prediction to expose deviations early and enforces multi-level alignment to provide global feedback. The framework separates trajectories into three hierarchical levels: actions (fine-grained steps), subgoals (short-term objectives), and trajectories (overall task intent).

In the **training phase**, ReCAPA takes trajectory segments, prompt embeddings, and visual observations. A Hierarchical Predictive Correction (HPCC) module represents trajectories at the three levels. Prompt–trajectory alignment is computed at each level by comparing trajectory representations with prompt embeddings, generating cross-level consistency signals. These signals define the training losses, which are backpropagated to update the action-generator, including action and object selection.

In the **inference phase**, the system uses environmental observations, the prompt, and historical trajectories. An execution network generates actions, while an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) provides task decompositions and completion markers. A three-tier correction mechanism refines the trajectory by resampling actions, adjusting subtasks, and utilizing Sinkhorn-based prompt alignment.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Predictive Correction (HPCC)

HPCC is central to ReCAPA, predicting higher-level semantics from lower-level steps and reflecting corrective signals to maintain consistent task representations.
- **Levels of Representation:**
 - **Action-level:** Captures how fine-grained steps (e.g., [GRAB], [WALK]) compose into short-term subgoals.
 - **Subgoal-level:** Forecasts trajectory outcomes and enforces causal order (e.g., washing before drying).
 - **Trajectory-level:** Encodes the overall task intent and outcome.
- **Predictive Mechanism:** For each level `l` (action, subgoal), the model predicts the representation at level `l+1` (`ˆz^(l+1)`) based on an encoded segment collection `T^l` (optionally with visual embeddings) `z^l`. A Transformer-based predictor performs this task.
- **Cross-Level Alignment Loss (`L_l^pred`):** The discrepancy between the predicted `ˆz^(l+1)` and the target `z^(l+1)` is quantified by an InfoNCE objective. This loss encourages `ˆz^(l+1)` to be close to `z^(l+1)` while distinguishing it from negative samples. Negative samples are constructed from alternative trajectory segments generated by an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) that are plausible but semantically misaligned. During optimization, gradients propagate through the predictor and level-`l` encoder, while the target `z^(l+1)` is detached to prevent its update. The regularized `z^l` then serves as input to the action-generator, which outputs discrete action logits via an MLP head.

#### 3.3. Prompt-Trajectory Alignment

Two modules provide complementary prompt–trajectory alignment:
- **Sinkhorn-based Alignment:**
 - Employs Optimal Transport (OT) to achieve distributional alignment between the trajectory distribution (μ) and the prompt distribution (ν). This operates at a global level, ensuring consistency across the entire trajectory rather than requiring exact token-by-token matching.
 - The loss `L_sinkhorn(μ, ν)` uses an entropy-regularized OT distance (Sinkhorn divergence), promoting semantic alignment in the latent space.
- **Score-field Alignment:**
 - Provides localized, step-wise corrective gradients `s_ψ(z^l, p)`, complementing the global Sinkhorn alignment.
 - The score network (an MLP) is trained to model a vector field that points toward high-density regions of the prompt-defined distribution. It learns to predict the denoising score of perturbed state embeddings `z^l` (with Gaussian noise).
 - The loss `L_score` quantifies the accuracy of this prediction. Any trajectory state `z^l` in a low-density region (indicating deviation) receives a corrective gradient, which acts as an auxiliary regularization term, shifting the state toward prompt consistency.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference Details

- **Training:**
 1. **Pre-training:** Transformer encoders are pre-trained with a contrastive objective (InfoNCE) on sliding windows of state-action trajectory segments. Positives are from the same episode, negatives are LLM-generated. This initializes a structured embedding space.
 2. **Fine-tuning:** Hierarchical encoders, predictors, and alignment modules are jointly fine-tuned using a total objective `L_total`, which is a weighted sum of `L_l^pred` (for action and subgoal levels), `L_l^score` (for action and subgoal levels), and `L_sinkhorn`. Specific weights (`λ` values) are used. The higher-level prediction target `z^(l+1)` is detached. Training employs AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate schedule.
- **Inference:**
 - An LLM (GPT-4o-mini) provides subgoals and completion criteria.
 - **Action-level:** Top-K candidate actions are selected. Their alignment with the current subgoal is computed, and a candidate is accepted if its similarity exceeds a threshold. The threshold is progressively relaxed if no candidate is accepted, with a fallback to the highest-logit action if all retries fail.
 - **Subgoal-level:** A sliding window encodes recent state-action sequences. A switch to the next subgoal is triggered when alignment with the current subgoal falls below a threshold and alignment with the next subgoal exceeds it by a margin.
 - **Trajectory-level:** Each candidate action is re-evaluated by temporarily appending it to the current trajectory buffer and computing prompt–trajectory consistency using Sinkhorn-based alignment. The candidate with the highest trajectory score is selected, or the action-level top choice is used if the highest trajectory score is below a threshold.

#### 3.5. Error Propagation Metrics

To provide a more granular evaluation of error dynamics beyond standard success rates, two new metrics are introduced:
- **Error Propagation Rate (EPR_k):** Quantifies how mistakes compound across `k` subsequent steps. It is defined as the difference in probability of an error occurring `k` steps after an initial error, compared to `k` steps after no initial error. EPR_k = `Pr(e_{t_0+k} = 1 | e_{t_0} = 1) - Pr(e_{t_0+k} = 1 | e_{t_0} = 0)`.
- **Propagation Attenuation Coefficient (PAC):** Measures how errors attenuate or dissipate over time. It is defined as the negative slope obtained by fitting a least-squares linear regression of the natural logarithm of the conditional error probability (`ln Pr(e_{t_0+Δ} = 1 | e_{t_0} = 1)`) on the lag `Δ`. A larger PAC value indicates quicker error recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReCAPA was evaluated across three embodied agent benchmarks: VisualAgentBench, MineDojo, and AI2-THOR, using both standard success metrics and the newly introduced Error Propagation Rate (EPR) and Propagation Attenuation Coefficient (PAC).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Across Benchmarks

- **VisualAgentBench:** ReCAPA achieved an average score of 58.65, demonstrating performance in manipulation and crafting tasks. It scored 50.6 on OmniGibson and 66.7 on Minecraft, outperforming both open-source and proprietary Large Multimodal Model (LMM) baselines, including GPT-4o-mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash.
- **AI2-THOR:** ReCAPA surpassed baselines with a Success Rate (SR) of 0.75, a Transport Rate (TR) of 0.93, and a Balance score of 0.93. While its Coverage metric was slightly lower than GPT-4V, it exhibited strong performance in long-horizon planning and balanced manipulation across diverse scenes.
- **MineDojo:** ReCAPA outperformed prior LLM agents, leading in 8 out of 10 long-horizon tasks with higher success rates.

#### 4.2. Error Propagation and Recovery Dynamics

- ReCAPA exhibited the lowest Error Propagation Rate (EPR_k) across benchmarks. For instance, at k=10 on OmniGibson, ReCAPA's EPR_10 was 0.082, significantly lower than values around 0.3 for GPT-4o-mini and over 0.453 for Claude-4-sonnet. This indicates that the impact of early mistakes in ReCAPA dissipates more quickly compared to other LMMs.
- ReCAPA maintained the highest Propagation Attenuation Coefficient (PAC) trajectory. This suggests that errors dissipate more rapidly in ReCAPA, allowing for quicker recovery in longer tasks rather than experiencing compounding drift. These metrics collectively indicate ReCAPA's robustness in error prevention (low EPR) and error recovery (high PAC).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contributions of ReCAPA’s core components:
- **Hierarchical Predictive Correction (HPCC):** Removing the entire HPCC module resulted in the largest performance drop. For example, on Behavior, the Success Rate (SR) decreased from 72.2 (HPCC-Full) to 59.3 (w/o HPCC). This confirms the importance of multi-level predictive capabilities. Variants using only two levels of HPCC (HPCC-AT, HPCC-ST) generally outperformed the action+subgoal variant (HPCC-AS), suggesting the critical role of trajectory-level representations. HIRO, a two-level subgoal control baseline, performed better than a flat Reinforcement Learning (RL) baseline (PPO) but still underperformed HPCC variants.
- **Prompt-Trajectory Alignment:** The combination of Sinkhorn-based and Score-field alignment (Alignment-Full) achieved the best overall performance. Sinkhorn alignment alone generally yielded higher scores than Score-field alone. KL+Score-field, replacing Sinkhorn with KL divergence, achieved the highest score on the Minecraft task, suggesting its sensitivity can be beneficial in certain skewed distributions, though it was less stable overall than Alignment-Full.
- **Layer-wise Ablation:** Removing any individual hierarchical layer (e.g., "w/o Subgoal-Level" or "w/o Trajectory-Level") consistently degraded performance across all benchmarks. The "FlatHead" variant, which collapsed the hierarchy into a single decoder, performed the worst, empirically validating the necessity of the hierarchical structure itself for effective semantic reasoning.
- **Coupling Strategy Comparison:** Fully joint training of all three corrective modules (ReCAPA) consistently outperformed all decoupled training strategies (Separate–BottomUp, Separate–TopDown, Separate–Parallel, Frozen Traj-Level). This indicates that the integrated and cross-level optimization in ReCAPA is crucial for achieving high performance.
- **Prediction and Correction Components:** Ablations disentangling prediction and corrective mechanisms within HPCC showed that both components are necessary and complementary. Removing both significantly degraded performance. Retaining only prediction offered anticipatory alignment, reducing short-horizon inconsistencies but lacking reactive correction. Retaining only corrective mechanisms provided recovery but lacked proactive guidance. Full HPCC, combining both, consistently achieved superior performance.

#### 4.4. Performance on Embodied Agent Interface

- On the Behavior task, ReCAPA achieved a Performance (Perf.) of 72.2 and a Goal F1 score of 84.8, demonstrating strong trajectory-level goal reasoning and action sequencing.
- On the VirtualHome task, ReCAPA achieved an Overall Perf. of 70.5 and a Transition Modeling Plan SR of 84.6, showcasing its hierarchical planning and trajectory stability in complex environments.

These results indicate that ReCAPA's hierarchical predictive correction, coupled with multi-level prompt-trajectory alignment, enables the system to maintain multi-step consistency and effectively manage compositional goals, contributing to higher success rates and enhanced robustness in long-horizon VLA tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "RECAPA: HIERARCHICAL PREDICTIVE CORRECTION TO MITIGATE CASCADING FAILURES" offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems and embodied AI.

1. **Mitigation of Cascading Failures:** The primary significance of ReCAPA lies in its demonstrated ability to effectively mitigate cascading failures, a critical challenge in long-horizon embodied tasks. By moving beyond post-hoc corrections and static planning, ReCAPA's hierarchical predictive correction mechanism enables VLA agents to anticipate and correct deviations early, reducing the compounding effect of errors and leading to more robust task completion.

2. **Novel Hierarchical Architecture and Multi-Level Alignment:** The proposed framework introduces a novel approach to hierarchical control by coupling multi-level predictive representations with prompt–trajectory distributional alignment. This architectural design, which enforces semantic consistency at action, subgoal, and trajectory levels, provides a more sophisticated method for maintaining alignment with overall task intent compared to single-level or local alignment strategies prevalent in prior work. This could inspire the development of VLA systems with more inherent structural consistency.

3. **Introduction of Diagnostic Metrics:** The development of Error Propagation Rate (EPR) and Propagation Attenuation Coefficient (PAC) represents a significant contribution to the evaluation of long-horizon reasoning. These metrics offer a more nuanced understanding of agent robustness by quantifying how errors spread and dissipate over time, aspects that traditional success-based metrics often overlook. The adoption of such diagnostic tools could lead to more comprehensive and insightful analyses in future embodied AI research, guiding the design of agents that are not only successful but also resilient.

4. **Advancement in Embodied Agent Performance:** ReCAPA achieves competitive results on established and challenging embodied agent benchmarks such as VisualAgentBench, MineDojo, and AI2-THOR. Its ability to outperform strong proprietary and open-source Large Language Model (LLM) baselines demonstrates a tangible advancement in the state of the art for VLA systems, particularly in complex compositional reasoning and long-horizon planning.

5. **Insights into System Design Trade-offs:** The discussion and ablation studies provide valuable insights into the trade-offs inherent in embodied agent design, such as the balance between broader exploration (for coverage) and consistent execution (for stability). ReCAPA's design, which prioritizes structural consistency, suggests that robust long-horizon reasoning benefits from mechanisms that filter out locally plausible but globally detrimental actions.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations of ReCAPA, specifically the discrete nature of its correction mechanism and its deterministic hierarchical generation module, point towards promising avenues for future research. Exploring continuous intermediate feedback and better uncertainty capture in hierarchical reasoning could lead to even more flexible and adaptive long-horizon decision-making systems, further pushing the capabilities of VLA agents in real-world applications.

Overall, ReCAPA provides a robust framework and diagnostic tools that contribute to the development of more intelligent, reliable, and adaptable embodied agents capable of executing complex tasks in dynamic environments.
