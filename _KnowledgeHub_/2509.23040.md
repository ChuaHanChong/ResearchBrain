# Look Back to Reason Forward: Revisitable Memory for Long-Context LLM Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> ReMemR1 introduces a long-context LLM agent capable of non-linear reasoning by dynamically retrieving information from its memory history. This system, guided by a multi-level reinforcement learning reward, achieved higher accuracy in multi-hop question answering across various context lengths and datasets with minimal inference overhead.

## Problem

- Existing "memorize while reading" LLM agents suffer from premature pruning of latent evidence, overlooking information whose significance emerges later in a multi-hop reasoning process.
- Sequential processing leads to progressive information loss as fixed-length memory buffers overwrite older, potentially vital, context.
- Training memory agents typically relies on sparse, delayed final reward signals, which hinders effective optimization of intermediate memory update decisions.

## Method

- **ReMemR1** augments the agent's state with a "==callback query==," enabling explicit, non-linear retrieval of relevant information from its entire history of past memories.
- It employs a lightweight recall-based heuristic for efficient selection of relevant content from memory history, ensuring minimal computational overhead.
- A multi-level reinforcement learning reward system is utilized, integrating trajectory-level outcome rewards with dense, step-level action rewards (information gain, callback bonus, format reward) for robust optimization.

## Results

- ReMemR1 consistently surpassed baseline memory agents and larger general LLMs, achieving up to **7.6%** higher accuracy on multi-hop QA benchmarks and substantially outperforming a **32B** model at 6400 documents.
- The system demonstrated improved coordination of distant evidence, effectively utilizing interdependent facts scattered across long document spans in challenging reverse reasoning order settings.
- Inference-time overhead for the callback mechanism was minimal, adding less than 2 seconds of latency and under 1MB of memory for 6400 documents, representing less than **0.2%** of total inference time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enabling an LLM agent to actively revisit historical memories during sequential document processing significantly mitigates information loss and facilitates non-linear reasoning over extended contexts.
> - A balanced, multi-level reward structure, combining global task outcomes with fine-grained step-level signals, is essential for effectively training complex memory agent behaviors.
> - Dynamically learning when and what to recall, via an RL-driven callback, is critical for effective long-context reasoning, outperforming static, rule-based retrieval methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in the field of large language models (LLMs): effective reasoning over extensive, multi-document contexts. This capability is critical for applications requiring synthesis of information scattered across millions of tokens, such as legal document analysis or scientific literature review. Existing LLMs frequently face difficulties in maintaining long-range dependencies and integrating disparate evidence due to the quadratic complexity of their attention mechanisms with respect to context length.

Two primary paradigms have emerged to mitigate these limitations:
1. **Full-Text Context Retrieval:** This approach involves a retriever component that fetches relevant document chunks from a large corpus, which are then provided to the LLM. While widely adopted, this method can present LLMs with fragmented information and carries a substantial storage burden for the vector index.
2. **"Memorize While Reading" Paradigm:** This more recent approach processes documents sequentially, employing a memory agent that dynamically updates a memory buffer with information extracted from each chunk. This allows for linear-time processing of arbitrarily long contexts, addressing the scalability issue of full-text attention.

However, the "memorize while reading" paradigm, despite its efficiency, introduces its own set of limitations. This paper identifies these as premature pruning of latent evidence, progressive information loss due to memory overwriting, and sparse/delayed supervision signals during training.

The current work, ReMemR1, is positioned as an advancement within the "memorize while reading" paradigm. It seeks to overcome the identified limitations by integrating explicit memory retrieval capabilities into the memory update process itself, thereby allowing for non-linear reasoning paths and more effective information management over long contexts. It also contributes to the broader field of reinforcement learning in LLMs by proposing a multi-level reward design for more robust optimization of memory agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the ability of LLM agents to perform complex, multi-hop reasoning over very long, multi-document contexts, specifically within the "memorize while reading" paradigm. The motivation stems from three intrinsic limitations identified in existing approaches:

1. **Premature Pruning of Latent Evidence:** In conventional "memorize while reading" agents, the relevance of a current document chunk is assessed based solely on the immediate memory state. This can lead to critical evidence being overlooked and subsequently pruned if its significance is not apparent until later steps in a multi-hop reasoning process. For instance, an early piece of information might only become crucial after a later section provides necessary context, but by then, the early evidence may have been discarded or heavily compressed. The objective is to enable the agent to recognize and retain such latent evidence, even if its immediate utility is not apparent.

2. **Progressive Information Loss in Memory Overwriting:** The necessity of compressing information into a fixed-length memory buffer in sequential processing leads to continuous degradation of memory. As new chunks are processed, older, potentially vital information is overwritten or excessively summarized, making it difficult for the LLM to synthesize evidence spread across distant sections of the document. The motivation here is to prevent this irreversible information loss, allowing the agent to maintain a richer and more complete context over the entire document.

3. **Sparse and Delayed Supervision:** Training memory agents, typically via reinforcement learning, often relies on a single, final reward signal (e.g., correctness of the answer). This sparse reward provides insufficient guidance for the many intermediate memory updates and decisions made throughout the long reasoning process. This leads to inefficient optimization and sub-optimal memory management strategies. The objective is to provide denser, more granular supervision signals to guide the agent's intermediate actions and memory usage effectively.

To address these motivations, the overall goal of ReMemR1 is to develop a memory-augmented LLM agent that can selectively recall historical memories during document navigation, thereby facilitating non-linear reasoning, mitigating information degradation, and improving training efficiency through a refined reward mechanism. This aims to enable robust long-context question answering with negligible computational overhead.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReMemR1 extends the conventional "memorize while reading" paradigm by introducing a history-augmented state and a multi-level reinforcement learning reward system.

#### 3.1. Conventional MDP Memory Agent

The baseline "memorize while reading" paradigm is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). At each step `t`, the agent receives a question `Q`, a document chunk `c_t`, and its current memory `m_t`. The agent's state `s_t` is defined solely by its memory `m_t`. It updates its memory to `m_{t+1} = \pi_{\theta}(Q, c_t, m_t)` via a policy `\pi_{\theta}`. After processing all `T` chunks, the final memory `m_T` is used to generate an answer. This traditional MDP formulation is constrained by its forward-only nature and the fixed-length memory buffer, leading to information loss and an inability to revisit past context.

#### 3.2. Memory Agent with History-Augmented State (ReMemR1 Core)

ReMemR1 addresses the limitations of the conventional MDP by augmenting the agent's state to `s_t = (m_t, q_t)`, where `q_t` is a "callback query." This query enables the agent to explicitly retrieve relevant information from its entire history of past memories, `{m_i\}_{i \le t}}`.

- **Callback Mechanism:** At each step `t`, the agent not only updates its current memory `m_t` based on the new chunk `c_t`, but also generates a callback query `q_{t+1}`. This query is then used to search the agent's accumulated memory history. The retrieved information `E({m_i\}_{i \le t}, q_t)` is integrated into the context for the next state update. The state transition is defined as:
 `s_{t+1} = (m_{t+1}, q_{t+1}) = \pi_{\theta}(Q, c_t, m_t, E(\{m_i\}_{i \le t}, q_t))`
 This mechanism allows the agent to construct non-linear reasoning paths, revisiting crucial facts from earlier stages to connect with new evidence, thereby counteracting progressive information loss and the forward-only constraint.

- **Retrieval Function:** The retrieval function `E` is designed to select relevant content from previous memories based on the callback query. It operates by identifying memories that maximize `recall(b, x)`, where `recall(a, b)` measures the proportion of words in `a` that also appear in `b`. This lightweight recall-based heuristic is chosen for its efficiency, ensuring that the retrieval process does not incur substantial computational overhead. The query component `q_{t+1}` itself evolves alongside the memory, allowing the agent to refine its retrieval strategy iteratively.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning with Multi-Level Reward Shaping

To optimize this architecture, ReMemR1 employs a multi-level reward design for reinforcement learning, tailored to provide dense and effective supervision. The training objective is a variant of the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

- **Trajectory-Level Outcome Rewards:** This component measures the ultimate success of the agent in answering the question correctly. The outcome reward `R^{(g)}_{out}` for a given trajectory `g` is calculated using an exact match metric between the agent's predicted final answer `\hat{y}^{(g)}` and the set of ground-truth answers `Y`. It provides a high-level signal of overall performance.

- **Step-Level Action Rewards for Behavior Shaping:** To address sparse supervision, three types of dense, fine-grained rewards are introduced for each intermediate step `t` of a trajectory `g`:
 - **Information Gain in Memory Updates (`r^{(g)}_{memory,t}`):** This reward quantifies the improvement in memory content. It measures the increase in recall of ground-truth entities within the updated memory `m^{(g)}_t` compared to the previous memory `m^{(g)}_{t-1}`. A positive gain indicates that the memory update successfully incorporated more relevant information.
 - **Bonus for Callback Retrievals (`r^{(g)}_{callback,t}`):** This reward encourages meaningful retrieval by evaluating the additional recall of critical information provided by the retrieved content `E(...)` beyond what was already available in the current memory `m^{(g)}_t` and immediate chunk `c_t`.
 - **Format Reward (`r^{(g)}_{format,t}`):** This reward ensures that the agent adheres to the specified output format, such as correctly using `<callback>` and `<memory>` tags for intermediate states, and `\box{}` for the final answer. This is crucial for parsing and reliable agent operation.

 The total step-level state reward `R^{(g)}_{state,t}` is the sum of these three components.

- **Training Objective:** The overall advantage `\hat{A}^{(g)}_t` for GRPO optimization is a weighted combination of the normalized trajectory-level outcome reward `\hat{A}^{(g)}_{out}` and the normalized step-level state reward `\hat{A}^{(g)}_{state,t}`, controlled by a hyperparameter `\alpha`. This balances global outcome goals with local behavior shaping:
 `\hat{A}^{(g)}_t = \alpha \hat{A}^{(g)}_{out} + (1 - \alpha) \hat{A}^{(g)}_{state,t}`
 This multi-level reward structure aims to provide consistent guidance to the agent, enabling it to learn more effective memory update and retrieval strategies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated ReMemR1 across various dimensions, including quantitative performance, ability to handle distant evidence, computational efficiency, and the efficacy of its architectural components.

#### 4.1. Performance on Long-Context Question Answering (RQ1)

ReMemR1 was benchmarked against general LLMs (Qwen2.5, R1-Distill), long-context LLMs (Qwen2.5-1M, Qwen3, QwenLong-L1), and specialized memory agents (MemAgent) on HotpotQA (in-distribution) and 2WikiMultiHopQA (out-of-distribution).

- **Consistent Superiority:** ReMemR1 consistently achieved the highest accuracy across all model scales (3B, 7B), datasets, and context lengths (from 50 to 6400 documents).
- **Significant Gains:** Compared to MemAgent, ReMemR1 showed up to 7.3% higher accuracy on the 3B model and 7.6% on the 7B model. At extreme context lengths (e.g., 6400 documents on HotpotQA), ReMemR1 (7B) achieved 80.8% accuracy, substantially outperforming even much larger models like QwenLong-L1-32B (38.3%).
- **Mitigation of Information Loss:** As context length increased, general-purpose LLMs and dedicated long-context models showed sharp performance degradation. MemAgent mitigated this with its memory buffer, but ReMemR1 further improved upon this by adaptively recalling information, demonstrating its effectiveness in preventing information loss over extended contexts.
- **Strong Generalization:** The performance gains were more pronounced on the out-of-distribution 2WikiMultiHopQA dataset, suggesting that ReMemR1 acquires robust retrieval and reasoning abilities beyond dataset-specific patterns.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness in Distant Evidence Challenge (RQ2)

A specialized setting was designed where supporting evidence was presented in reverse reasoning order, with large distances between successive pieces of evidence.

- **Improved Long-Range Coordination:** ReMemR1 significantly surpassed MemAgent in this challenging setup, where relying on local context was insufficient. This confirms that ReMemR1's callback mechanism enables it to identify, retrieve, and utilize interdependent evidence scattered across long spans, thereby demonstrating non-linear document utilization.

#### 4.3. Computational Efficiency and Scalability (RQ3)

The computational overhead of ReMemR1 was analyzed during both inference and training.

- **Inference-Time Efficiency:**
 - **Negligible Retrieval Overhead:** The callback operations themselves introduced less than 2 seconds of latency and under 1MB of additional memory, even at the 6400-document setting. This corresponds to less than 0.2% of total inference time and less than 0.001% of total GPU memory.
 - **Favorable Trade-off:** The minimal computational cost translated into substantial accuracy gains (up to 5% absolute improvement or 20% error rate reduction), indicating an effective accuracy-efficiency trade-off.
 - **Query Generation as Primary Overhead:** The primary source of additional latency was identified as the autoregressive generation of the callback query, rather than the retrieval process itself.
- **Training-Time Efficiency:**
 - **Moderate Overhead:** ReMemR1 exhibited higher per-step latency during training compared to MemAgent, but the difference was deemed within a practical range.
 - **Similar GPU Memory Usage:** Peak GPU memory usage remained largely similar between ReMemR1 and MemAgent, as the LLM backbone dominated memory consumption, with intermediate memory storage adding only a small constant overhead.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Multi-Level Reward Design (RQ4):** Experiments with varying `\alpha` values (weighting trajectory-level vs. step-level rewards) demonstrated that `\alpha = 0.8` consistently yielded the best accuracy. Purely outcome-based rewards (`\alpha = 1.0`) led to weaker optimization, while over-emphasizing step-level shaping (`\alpha = 0.2`) caused instability and performance degradation. This indicates that a balanced multi-level reward structure is crucial for robust training.

- **RL-Driven vs. Rule-Based Memory Callback (RQ5):** Comparing the RL-driven callback with a rule-based approach (using the question as a fixed query) showed that the RL-driven method consistently outperformed both the vanilla MemAgent and the rule-based callback. The rule-based approach sometimes caused performance drops (up to 7.9%), suggesting that dynamically learning when and what to recall is critical. The advantage of the RL-driven method increased with document length, highlighting its importance in complex, long-context scenarios.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis (Case Study Summary)

A case study on a multi-hop reasoning problem revealed that MemAgent failed due to its forward-only processing and information loss from overwriting. It could not revisit earlier facts when their relevance became clear later, leading to reliance on irrelevant information and incorrect inferences. In contrast, ReMemR1 successfully identified the correct answer by proactively retaining information with a callback query, bridging distant evidence non-linearly, and mitigating information loss through selective retrieval, demonstrating its ability to synthesize facts across disparate timesteps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work addresses critical limitations in long-context reasoning for LLM agents, offering a significant advancement within the "memorize while reading" paradigm.

**Advancement in LLM Reasoning:** ReMemR1 introduces a novel architectural mechanism that enables LLMs to perform non-linear reasoning by actively revisiting historical memories. This fundamentally moves beyond the restrictive, forward-only processing of previous memory agents, allowing for a more flexible and robust integration of distributed evidence. The multi-level reward system further contributes by providing a more effective training framework for complex agent behaviors, mitigating the challenge of sparse supervision in long-horizon tasks.

**Generalizability and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains observed across both in-distribution (HotpotQA) and out-of-distribution (2WikiMultiHopQA) benchmarks, particularly in challenging distant-evidence scenarios, indicate that ReMemR1 acquires genuine retrieval and reasoning abilities that generalize well beyond specific training data. This suggests increased robustness for LLM agents deployed in diverse real-world domains.

**Computational Efficiency:** A key finding is that the benefits of ReMemR1 are achieved with negligible additional computational overhead during inference. The retrieval mechanism is efficient, adding less than 0.2% to inference time. This favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off makes the proposed method practically viable for deployment, allowing for significant improvements in reasoning accuracy without incurring prohibitive scalability issues.

**Potential Impact:** The methodology developed in ReMemR1 has the potential to enhance the capabilities of LLM-based agents in applications requiring deep understanding and synthesis of very long documents, such as legal analysis, scientific literature review, and complex data querying. By improving the agent's ability to manage and recall information over extended contexts, it could lead to more accurate, reliable, and explainable AI systems.

**Future Directions and Ethical Considerations:** The paper's conclusion suggests that this work opens new avenues for research into robust long-context understanding agents. The ethics statement acknowledges the broader challenges of LLMs, particularly regarding biases and the risks associated with memory mechanisms storing and recalling private or proprietary data. Any downstream application of ReMemR1 would necessitate careful evaluation for fairness, transparency, and potential discriminatory impacts, alongside robust safeguards for data privacy and security.
