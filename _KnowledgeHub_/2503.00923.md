# HWC-Loco: A Hierarchical Whole-Body Control Approach to Robust Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> HWC-Loco introduces a hierarchical whole-body control framework for robust humanoid locomotion, explicitly learning to recover from safety-critical situations while dynamically balancing task performance and safety. It achieves superior success rates on challenging terrains and under disturbances in both simulation and real-world deployment on Unitree H1 and G1 robots.

## Problem

- Achieving robust and adaptable locomotion for humanoid robots in complex, unpredictable real-world environments due to their high degrees of freedom and complex dynamics.
- Bridging the 'Sim-to-Real' gap, where policies trained in simplified simulations often fail to perform robustly when deployed on physical robots.
- Balancing task performance with safety guarantees, as traditional robust optimization methods often lead to overly conservative behaviors that compromise a robot's ability to complete tasks efficiently.

## Method

- A hierarchical whole-body control framework with a Goal-Tracking Policy (π1\pi_1π1​) for efficient task completion, a Safety Recovery Policy (π2\pi_2π2​) for emergency handling, and a High-Level Planning Policy (π0\pi_0π0​) to dynamically select between them.
- Policy learning is formulated as a robust constrained Reinforcement Learning problem, where the goal-tracking policy focuses on task rewards and mimic learning, while the safety recovery policy is trained under an 'extreme-case uncertainty set' using ZMP constraints.
- Utilizes Isaac Gym for massively parallel simulation training, incorporates human expert motion data via Wasserstein-1 distance for naturalness, and deploys lightweight policies on physical Unitree H1 and G1 humanoid robots.

## Results

- HWC-Loco achieved high success rates on challenging terrains in simulation (e.g., 99.98% on stairs at low speeds, 84.34% at high speeds) and demonstrated effective navigation on real-world stairs and slopes.
- It exhibited superior robustness under various simulated disturbances (e.g., 95.88% success under low-frequency external forces, 81.27% under high impulse disturbances) and recovered stability during real-world pushes and kicks.
- The framework demonstrated natural, adaptable locomotion styles, transitioning from stationary to walking (1.0 m/s with arm swings) to running (2.5 m/s with bent elbows), and successfully scaled to a different robot (Unitree G1) and complex expressive motion tracking tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating policy learning as a robust constrained Reinforcement Learning problem allows the system to explicitly learn recovery from safety-critical scenarios under extreme-case uncertainties, enhancing real-world robustness.
> - A hierarchical control structure, composed of goal-tracking, safety recovery, and high-level planning policies, dynamically resolves the trade-off between task performance and stability, adapting behavior based on perceived risk.
> - Integrating human motion imitation via Wasserstein distance and dynamic constraints like Zero Moment Point (ZMP) guidance ensures both natural, human-like movements and inherent stability.
