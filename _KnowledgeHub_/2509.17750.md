# EigenSafe: A Spectral Framework for Learning-Based Probabilistic Safety Assessment

> [!summary] Summary
> EigenSafe introduces an operator-theoretic framework for probabilistic safety assessment in stochastic, learning-enabled systems. It leverages the dominant eigenpair of a linear operator to provide explicitly calibrated safety metrics, demonstrating improved safety-performance trade-offs in reinforcement learning and enhanced safety filtering for imitation learning tasks.

## Problem

- Autonomous robotic systems operating in stochastic real-world environments face inherent uncertainties, making binary safe/unsafe classifications inadequate and necessitating probabilistic safety quantification.
- Existing safety assessment methods often rely on surrogate values (e.g., value functions, barrier scores) that do not directly correspond to true safety probabilities, leading to inconsistent assessments, overly conservative policies, and limited interpretability.
- Accurately quantifying and assuring long-term probabilistic safety in learning-enabled systems, particularly without an explicit system model, remains a significant challenge for existing approaches.

## Method

- A spectral framework, **EigenSafe**, is developed that models stochastic systems as discrete-time Markov Decision Processes and defines a linear operator whose ==dominant eigenpair== directly quantifies global (decay rate) and state-wise (safety value) safety probabilities.
- A data-driven learning algorithm is proposed to approximate the dominant eigenpair (specifically, a ==safety Q function== ψ_π) from system trajectory data, utilizing a ==power iteration==-inspired loss functional amenable to standard gradient descent.
- The learned safety Q function is applied in Safe Reinforcement Learning to formulate safety-constrained policy optimization and in Imitation Learning for test-time safety filtering, enabling policies that balance reward with long-term safety.

## Results

- In diverse Gym environments, EigenSafe policies consistently achieved a better balance between **cumulative task reward** and **time to first safety failure** compared to baseline safe reinforcement learning methods, indicating more effective safety-performance trade-offs.
- For a real-world robotic food preparation task, applying the learned safety Q function for test-time filtering in imitation learning resulted in **higher safety and success rates** compared to naive policies, even when trained on mixed demonstration data.
- Visualizations demonstrated that the learned safety Q function (ψ_π) effectively assigned higher values to safer states and exhibited predictive capability, identifying potentially unsafe states *before* actual physical safety violations occurred.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Probabilistic safety over time can be rigorously understood through the dominant eigenpair of a linear operator, with the dominant eigenvalue quantifying the global asymptotic safety decay rate and the eigenfunction serving as a state-wise safety value.
> - The framework provides safety metrics that are explicitly calibrated to the actual safety probability, overcoming the limitations of uncalibrated surrogate values in previous approaches and enabling more interpretable and less conservative safety assessments.
> - A global safety constraint on the dominant eigenvalue (γ_π ≥ γ_0) can be equivalently transformed into a local state-action condition (A_π ψ_π(x, u) ≥ γ_0 ψ_π(x, u)), facilitating practical integration into gradient-based policy optimization for safe reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Ensuring the safe operation of autonomous robotic systems, particularly those incorporating learning-enabled components, represents a fundamental challenge in robotics. Traditional safety assessment methods often face difficulties when dealing with the inherent stochasticity of real-world environments, which arises from factors such as sensing noise and unpredictable disturbances. Existing approaches, such as Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis and control barrier functions (CBFs), have been successfully applied as safety critics in learning-based control. However, these methods typically learn surrogate values (e.g., value functions or barrier scores) that do not directly correspond to the actual probability of maintaining safety over a given time horizon. This lack of explicit calibration to the true safety probability can lead to inconsistent or overly conservative safety assessments, potentially sacrificing system performance unnecessarily.

The existing body of work on safety-critical control theory includes stochastic extensions of CBFs, which provide time-varying lower bounds on safety probability. These are often derived from martingale inequalities, which can introduce conservatism. Model-free approaches based on reinforcement learning (RL) have also been explored for synthesizing safety critics, particularly for complex or high-dimensional systems where explicit dynamic models are unavailable. Yet, many of these methods primarily rely on optimal control formulations designed for deterministic systems, thereby not fully capturing the stochastic nature of robotic operations. Furthermore, safety critics tied to specific risk measures may serve as surrogates rather than directly quantifying safety probability. While some HJ reachability formulations address safety probability, they often result in infinite-horizon safety probabilities that are zero everywhere unless almost-sure safety is achievable, making relative safety comparisons challenging.

In safe decision-making, safety filters are used to minimally modify potentially unsafe reference policies, and constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs) aim to maximize rewards subject to safety constraints. The effectiveness of these approaches is contingent upon the quality and calibration of the underlying safety critics. Poorly calibrated metrics can lead to overly cautious behaviors.

EigenSafe positions itself within this landscape by introducing an operator-theoretic framework that directly addresses the limitations of uncalibrated safety metrics. It seeks to provide a theoretically grounded safety critic explicitly linked to the actual safety probability, applicable to stochastic, learning-enabled systems. By deriving spectral metrics from a linear operator governing safety probability, the work offers an alternative to optimal control-based methods, aiming to overcome the issues of surrogate values, conservatism, and the challenges in quantifying long-term probabilistic safety.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the critical need for accurate and robust safety assessment in autonomous robotic systems operating in stochastic real-world environments. In such settings, factors like sensor noise, environmental disturbances, and the inherent uncertainties of learning-enabled components mean that a binary classification of safe or unsafe states is often unrealistic. Instead, safety is more appropriately quantified as the probability of avoiding failure over a specified time horizon.

This necessitates the development of a safety critic capable of precisely estimating the closed-loop safety probability and understanding its evolution over extended periods. Existing safety critics, derived from optimal control methods such as Hamilton-Jacobi reachability or learning-based control barrier functions, often learn proxy values (e.g., value functions or barrier scores). These values do not inherently correspond to the true safety probability, leading to several limitations:
- **Inconsistent Safety Assessment:** The outputs of these critics may not be consistently calibrated to the actual safety probability.
- **Overly Conservative Policies:** The lack of explicit calibration can lead to excessively cautious policies that unnecessarily compromise task performance.
- **Limited Interpretability:** Surrogate values do not provide a direct, intuitive measure of safety probability, hindering a precise trade-off between performance and safety.

To address these issues, the core objective of this work is to develop a theoretical framework that directly links a learned safety critic to the probabilistic safety assessment of a system. Specifically, the paper aims to:
1. **Develop a Spectral Safety Assessment Method:** Create a novel approach that provides metrics explicitly connected to the actual safety probability, considering its evolution as a repeated application of a linear operator. This approach is intended to offer a rigorous and unified assessment of safety at both local (state-action pairs) and global (closed-loop system) levels.
2. **Propose a Scalable Learning Algorithm:** Design a data-driven algorithm capable of approximating the derived spectral quantities (dominant eigenvalue and eigenfunction) from trajectory data without requiring an explicit model of the system dynamics. This algorithm should be amenable to standard gradient descent optimization.
3. **Demonstrate Utility in Safe Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Apply the framework to formulate a safety-constrained policy optimization problem within an RL setting. The objective is to enable policies that effectively balance maximizing task rewards with maintaining long-term safety.
4. **Validate Applicability in Imitation Learning (IL):** Implement the learned spectral safety critic in imitation learning by introducing a test-time safety filtering mechanism. This aims to enhance the safety of learned stochastic policies by selecting actions deemed sufficiently safe from multiple candidates.

By achieving these objectives, EigenSafe seeks to provide a safety assessment methodology that is both theoretically sound and practically applicable, fostering the deployment of safer and more reliable autonomous robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and approach

EigenSafe proposes an operator-theoretic framework for assessing the safety of learning-enabled stochastic systems, modeling them as discrete-time Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) without a reward term, characterized by (S, A, P). The state space S includes a designated termination state K for safety failures, where C is the safe set. The safety probability Z_π(t, x) is defined as the probability of the system remaining in the safe set until time t, given an initial state x under policy π.

**A. Dynamic Programming and Linear Operator Formulation:**
The evolution of safety probability is governed by a dynamic programming principle:
Z_π(t + 1, x) = E_{u~π(·|x), x'~P(·|x,u)} [Z_π(t, x')]
This principle is reformulated using a linear operator T_π acting on the space of bounded and measurable scalar functions on S. The operator is defined as:
T_π β(x) := E_{u~π(·|x), x'~P(·|x,u)} [β(x')]
Its domain D_T is restricted to functions that are zero at the termination state K. The t-step safety probability can then be expressed as Z_π(t, x) = T_π^t 1_C(x), where 1_C(x) is the indicator function of the safe set.

**B. Spectral Theory for Safety Assessment:**
The linearity of T_π allows for the examination of its spectral properties. The dominant eigenpair (γ_π, ϕ_π) of T_π, consisting of the dominant eigenvalue γ_π and its associated eigenfunction ϕ_π, is central to the framework.
- **Properties of T_π:** The operator T_π is shown to be positive (mapping nonnegative functions to nonnegative functions) and non-expansive (spectral radius bounded by 1). This implies that γ_π is a real number between 0 and 1, and ϕ_π can be chosen to be nonnegative. Under assumptions of compactness of C and continuity of the transition density, T_π is a compact operator, and its eigenfunctions for nonzero eigenvalues are continuous.
- **Dominant Eigenpair as Safety Measure:** For sufficiently large t, the safety probability Z_π(t, x) can be approximated as Z_π(t, x) ≈ c · ϕ_π(x) · γ_π^t. This approximation highlights the significance of the dominant eigenpair:
 - γ_π quantifies the global asymptotic decay rate of safety probability, serving as a global safety metric for the closed-loop system or policy. Values closer to 1 indicate safer policies.
 - ϕ_π(x) quantifies the relative safety of a specific state x ∈ C compared to other states, analogous to a safety value function. Higher values indicate greater safety.
- **Safety Q Function:** To assess safety at the state-action level, an operator A_π is introduced for the safety probability H_π(t, x, u) conditioned on the initial state and action. A_π's dominant eigenpair (γ_π, ψ_π) shares the same dominant eigenvalue γ_π as T_π. The dominant eigenfunction ψ_π(x, u) serves as a safety Q function, providing a directional guide for policy improvement by quantifying the safety of a given state-action pair.

**C. EigenSafe: Learning the Eigenpair:**
A data-driven learning algorithm is proposed to approximate (γ_π, ψ_π) from transition data (x, u, x').
- **Loss Functional:** The algorithm parametrizes γ as a scalar and ψ using a function approximator (e.g., neural network). These parameters are optimized by minimizing a loss functional J_eig, inspired by the power iteration algorithm:
 J_eig[γ, ψ] := W_eig Σ_{(x,u,x')∈D} (1[x∈C ∧ x'∈C] · ψ(x', u') - γ · 1[x∈C] · ψ(x, u))^2 + W_n · (max_{(x,u,·)∈D} ψ(x, u) - 1)^2
 The first term enforces the eigenvalue equation A_π ψ = γψ, while the second term normalizes ψ to prevent trivial solutions.
- **Convergence:** The convergence of this approach is grounded in power iteration principles, where non-dominant modes decay over time, isolating the dominant eigenpair. This avoids the need for artificial discount factors common in RL, which can undermine theoretical guarantees.
- **Implementation Remarks:** An optional auxiliary loss term J_+ is introduced to penalize negative values, biasing optimization towards the nonnegative dominant eigenfunction and accelerating convergence. A semi-gradient approach is used for stability by treating ψ(x', u') as a fixed regression target.

**D. Applications:**
- **Safe Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The learned ψ_π is used as a safety Q function in an online safe RL framework based on Soft Actor-Critic (SAC). The global safety constraint γ_π ≥ γ_0 is transformed into an equivalent local condition A_π ψ_π(x, u) ≥ γ_0 ψ_π(x, u). This constrained optimization problem is solved using Lagrange multipliers, enabling a policy that maximizes rewards while adhering to safety constraints.
- **Safety-Filtered Inference for Imitation Learning (IL):** ψ_π is applied for test-time safety filtering of a learned stochastic policy (e.g., from behavior cloning). Given a state, multiple action candidates are sampled from the policy, their safety scores ψ_π(s_t, u^(i)) are evaluated, and the action corresponding to the k-th largest safety score is selected. This biases action sampling towards safer transitions without modifying policy weights.

The theoretical guarantees, including compactness of the operator, nonnegativity, uniqueness, and spectral gap of the dominant eigenpair, are detailed in Appendix A, establishing the mathematical foundations for EigenSafe.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several key theoretical findings and demonstrates the practical effectiveness of the EigenSafe framework through empirical evaluations in both safe Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) contexts.

**A. Theoretical Findings:**
- **Operator Formulation for Safety Probability:** The paper formally establishes a linear operator T_π that governs the dynamic programming principle for safety probability. This operator provides a spectral lens through which to analyze the long-term safety behavior of stochastic systems.
- **Dominant Eigenpair as Calibrated Safety Measure:** It is shown that the dominant eigenpair (γ_π, ϕ_π) of T_π inherently captures critical safety information. The dominant eigenvalue γ_π quantifies the global asymptotic decay rate of the system's safety probability, serving as a comprehensive metric for the overall closed-loop system or policy. The dominant eigenfunction ϕ_π provides state-wise safety assessment, analogous to a safety value function.
- **Safety Q Function Derivation:** The framework extends to define a safety Q function ψ_π, the dominant eigenfunction of an augmented operator A_π defined over state-action pairs. This ψ_π quantifies the safety level of specific state-action transitions, offering directional guidance for policy improvement.
- **Mathematical Guarantees:** The paper provides formal proofs (detailed in Appendix A) for the properties of these operators and their eigenpairs, under reasonable assumptions:
 - **Compactness of T_π:** Ensuring that the operator behaves similarly to a finite matrix, with discrete eigenvalues.
 - **Nonnegativity and Strict Positivity:** The dominant eigenpair (γ_π, ϕ_π) is guaranteed to be nonnegative, with ϕ_π being strictly positive on the safe set, aligning with the nature of safety probabilities.
 - **Uniqueness and Nonzero Spectral Gap:** The dominant eigenfunction is unique up to scaling, and a strict separation exists between the dominant eigenvalue and other eigenvalues, ensuring the long-term prominence of the dominant mode.
 - **Asymptotic Approximation:** The theoretical justification for approximating long-term safety probability Z_π(t, x) as c · ϕ_π(x) · γ_π^t is provided, validating the use of the eigenpair as safety metrics.
 - **Equivalence of Constraints:** The global safety constraint (γ_π ≥ γ_0) is proven to be equivalent to a local state-action condition (A_π ψ_π(x, u) ≥ γ_0 ψ_π(x, u)), which is crucial for practical implementation in gradient-based RL.

**B. Empirical Results:**
- **Safe Reinforcement Learning:** EigenSafe was applied in four Gym environments (CheetahLow, HopperHigh, LunarLanderHard, AntBall) to train policies that balance reward maximization and safety.
 - **Improved Safety-Performance Trade-off:** Compared to baseline methods (Vanilla SAC, RESPO, EFPPO), EigenSafe consistently achieved a better balance between cumulative task reward and time steps until the first safety failure. This suggests that EigenSafe's safety assessment is more accurate and less conservative, leading to policies that are both safer and more performant.
 - **Stable Learning:** The learning curves demonstrated stable convergence of the eigenfunction loss and policy objectives, with eigenvalue estimates gradually increasing towards 1, indicating enhanced policy safety over training.
- **Safety-Filtered Inference for Imitation Learning:** The learned eigenfunction ψ_π was used as a safety Q function for test-time safety filtering of a stochastic behavior cloning (BC) policy in a real-world food preparation task using a UR3 robotic arm (picking up and pouring almonds).
 - **Enhanced Safety and Success Rates:** The safety-filtered approach, which selects the k-th safest action from multiple candidates, yielded higher safety and success rates compared to naive BC policies and even BC policies trained exclusively on successful demonstrations. This demonstrates the framework's ability to improve safety without relying on exclusively safe expert data.
 - **Predictive Capability of ψ_π:** Visualizations of ψ_π values over time showed that the eigenfunction effectively assigns higher values to safer states and exhibits predictive capabilities, with values dropping significantly *before* physical safety violations (e.g., precarious grasp anticipating spillage).

These findings collectively support that EigenSafe provides a theoretically sound and empirically effective framework for probabilistic safety assessment in stochastic, learning-enabled robotic systems, addressing limitations of prior uncalibrated safety critics.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

EigenSafe presents a significant contribution to the field of safe autonomous systems by introducing a novel operator-theoretic framework for probabilistic safety assessment. Its core significance lies in providing a safety critic that is explicitly and rigorously tied to the actual safety probability, rather than relying on surrogate metrics. This direct calibration addresses a long-standing challenge in safety-critical control and learning, where uncalibrated measures often lead to overly conservative or inconsistent safety guarantees.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By offering a more accurate and interpretable quantification of safety probability, EigenSafe can contribute to the development of more reliable autonomous systems. This increased trustworthiness is crucial for the broader deployment of robots in real-world applications where human safety and operational integrity are paramount.
- **Improved Safety-Performance Trade-offs:** The framework enables a principled balance between maximizing task performance and ensuring long-term safety. By mitigating the over-conservatism often associated with prior methods, EigenSafe allows systems to operate closer to their performance limits while maintaining quantified safety levels. This can lead to more efficient and capable robotic operations.
- **Versatile Applicability Across Learning Paradigms:** The demonstrated utility in both online Reinforcement Learning (RL) for policy optimization and test-time filtering in Imitation Learning (IL) highlights the versatility of the framework. This broad applicability suggests that EigenSafe can be integrated into various learning-based control pipelines, extending safety guarantees to a wider range of robotic tasks and learning methodologies.
- **Theoretical Foundation for Probabilistic Safety:** The rigorous mathematical derivation of the linear operator and its spectral properties provides a robust theoretical foundation for analyzing long-term probabilistic safety. This theoretical depth can inspire further research into operator theory for control and verification, potentially leading to new insights and methodologies.
- **Predictive Safety Assessment:** The ability of the learned eigenfunction (safety Q function) to predict potential failures proactively, as observed in the robot manipulation experiments, is a valuable capability. Early detection of unsafe states can allow for timely intervention or adaptation, preventing actual safety violations and improving overall system resilience.
- **Reduced Reliance on Expert Data for Safety:** In imitation learning, the safety filtering mechanism allows for enhancing the safety of policies even when trained on mixed datasets (including failures), without requiring exclusively safe expert demonstrations. This can alleviate data collection burdens and expand the utility of less-than-perfect demonstration data.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as potential overestimation bias in online learning and dependency on failure data, the foundational approach of directly linking spectral properties to safety probability offers a promising direction for future research. This includes exploring techniques to mitigate bias, integrate out-of-distribution avoidance, and generalize the framework to more complex risk metrics, further solidifying its impact on the development of safe and intelligent autonomous systems.
