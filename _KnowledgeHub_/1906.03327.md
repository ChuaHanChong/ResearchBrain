# HowTo100M: Learning a Text-Video Embedding by Watching Hundred Million Narrated Video Clips

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ENS and Inria introduced HowTo100M, a dataset of 136 million video clips derived from narrated instructional videos, alongside a powerful text-video embedding model. The model, trained on this weakly supervised data, achieved state-of-the-art results in text-to-video retrieval and action localization across various benchmarks, demonstrating robust transferability and significantly reducing the need for manual annotations by achieving SOTA with only 20% of MSR-VTT data.

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, manually annotated datasets hindered the development of robust joint representations for video and language.
- Manual annotation of video-text pairs proved to be expensive, time-consuming, and prone to subjectivity, limiting dataset scale and consistency.
- Existing video-language datasets were too small to train truly generalizable models capable of capturing the vast diversity of real-world human activities.

## Method

- Developed HowTo100M, a large-scale dataset of 136 million video clips automatically paired with narrations from 1.22 million instructional YouTube videos.
- Designed a text-video joint embedding model using non-linear embedding functions, context gating, and pre-extracted Resnet/ResNeXt features for video, and word2vec/1D-CNN for text.
- Employed a sophisticated max-margin ranking loss with a crucial "intra-video negative sampling" strategy to encourage learning fine-grained semantic distinctions.

## Results

- The model consistently improved with increased training data from HowTo100M across all tasks, validating the impact of scale on learning robust multimodal representations.
- The HowTo100M-trained embedding established new state-of-the-art performance on instructional video tasks, including outperforming fully-supervised methods on CrossTask's action step localization.
- Fine-tuning the pre-trained embedding enabled state-of-the-art results on MSR-VTT and substantial improvements on LSMDC, achieving SOTA on MSR-VTT with only 20% of the target dataset's annotations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Weakly supervised learning from the vast amount of existing narrated "how-to" videos on platforms like YouTube offers a scalable alternative to costly manual annotation.
> - The scale of data, specifically 136 million video-text pairs, is paramount for learning highly generalizable and robust text-video embeddings, showing no saturation with increased data.
> - Employing an intra-video negative sampling strategy is essential for forcing the model to distinguish fine-grained actions and objects within the same video context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "HowTo100M" addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of multimodal artificial intelligence: the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, manually annotated datasets for learning joint representations of video and language. Historically, progress in tasks like text-to-video retrieval, action localization, video captioning, and visual question answering has been heavily reliant on datasets where human annotators painstakingly create descriptive captions for video clips (e.g., MSR-VTT, DiDeMo, EPIC-KITCHENS).

This approach faces several inherent limitations:
1. **Cost and Time:** Manual annotation is incredibly expensive and time-consuming, making it difficult to scale to the millions of pairs needed for truly generic and robust models.
2. **Subjectivity and Consistency:** Video annotation can be subjective, leading to inconsistencies among annotators and potentially ill-defined ground truth.
3. **Scope:** The resulting datasets, even the largest ones, typically contain only tens or hundreds of thousands of video-text pairs, which is insufficient to capture the vast variability of human activities and descriptions in the real world.

The "HowTo100M" paper positions itself within an emerging paradigm shift in AI research, inspired by the successes seen in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and image classification. In NLP, models like BERT, GPT, and GPT-2 have achieved unprecedented performance by pre-training on massive amounts of unlabeled text data (e.g., WebText). Similarly, in computer vision, pre-training on ImageNet or using weakly supervised methods with image metadata (e.g., Instagram hashtags) has been instrumental in advancing image understanding.

This paper extends this philosophy to the video-language domain. It recognizes the immense potential of readily available, "found" data on the web, specifically narrated instructional videos. The core insight is that the narration in such videos often explicitly describes the visual content on screen, providing a natural form of weak supervision without requiring any additional manual annotation. This concept builds on prior efforts to utilize speech or subtitles for vision tasks (e.g., Chen et al. (2017) using subtitles for object detection, Malmaud et al. (2015) using speech for cooking video interpretation), but critically, it aims for a much larger scale and a more general text-video embedding that can transfer across domains, rather than focusing on domain-specific labels or tasks.

By introducing HowTo100M, the authors contribute a dataset that is orders of magnitude larger than any previous video-text dataset, effectively bypassing the manual annotation bottleneck. This moves the field towards developing "foundation models" for video-language, where a broadly applicable embedding can be learned from vast amounts of weakly supervised data and then fine-tuned for specific downstream tasks with minimal additional annotation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the recognized limitation of existing video-language datasets: their small scale, high cost of creation, and subjective nature of manual annotation. This scarcity of data hinders the development of generalizable and powerful text-video embeddings, which are foundational for advanced multimodal AI applications.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the paper are three-fold:

1. **To overcome the data scarcity problem by introducing a novel, large-scale dataset of video clips paired with natural language annotations.** The authors hypothesized that narrated instructional videos on platforms like YouTube offer an abundant and readily available source of weakly supervised video-language pairs. The narration in such "how-to" videos often directly describes the visual actions and objects, making it a natural source of grounding information. This led to the creation of **HowTo100M**, a dataset designed to be orders of magnitude larger than any predecessor, enabling learning on an unprecedented scale.

2. **To demonstrate that a text-video embedding model trained on this large, weakly supervised dataset can achieve state-of-the-art performance on existing benchmarks for text-to-video retrieval and action localization.** The objective here was to validate the utility and effectiveness of the HowTo100M dataset and the proposed learning approach. Specifically, the authors aimed to show that leveraging automatically transcribed narrations from millions of instructional videos could lead to a powerful, off-the-shelf embedding capable of outperforming models trained on much smaller, manually annotated instructional video datasets (like YouCook2 and CrossTask).

3. **To prove the generalizability and transferability of the learned embedding across different video domains.** A crucial objective was to assess whether an embedding trained predominantly on instructional videos could effectively adapt to other types of video content, such as generic YouTube videos (MSR-VTT) and movies (LSMDC). The authors aimed to demonstrate that pre-training on HowTo100M followed by fine-tuning on smaller, domain-specific datasets could significantly boost performance, even surpassing models trained exclusively on these target datasets. This objective underscores the goal of creating a broadly applicable foundation model for video-language understanding.

In essence, the motivation was to harness the vast, untapped potential of web-scale narrated videos to create a scalable, cost-effective solution for learning robust multimodal representations, thereby pushing the capabilities of video-language AI beyond the limitations imposed by expensive manual annotation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research can be broadly divided into two main components: the creation of the HowTo100M dataset and the design and training of the text-video joint embedding model.

#### 3.1. HowTo100M Dataset Creation

The core innovation lies in the automated and scalable collection of a massive multimodal dataset:
- **Source of Visual Tasks:** The process began by leveraging WikiHow, an online encyclopedia of "how-to" articles. From WikiHow's hierarchy, 23,611 "visual tasks" were identified across 12 diverse categories (e.g., Food and Entertaining, Home and Garden, Hobbies and Crafts). Tasks were filtered semi-automatically to focus on physical actions (e.g., "make," "build," "change") and discard abstract ones.
- **Video Acquisition:** For each identified task, YouTube was queried using "how to [task name]" (e.g., "how to paint furniture"). The system focused on videos with English subtitles, which could be manually uploaded, automatically generated by YouTube's Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), or automatically translated.
- **Quality Control and Filtering:** To ensure a reasonable quality of videos and relevance, several criteria were applied:
 - Only the top 200 search results were considered.
 - Videos with fewer than 100 views were removed (often low quality/amateurish).
 - Videos with less than 100 words in subtitles were excluded (insufficient text for learning).
 - Videos longer than 2,000 seconds were removed.
 - YouTube IDs were used for deduplication, though minor duplicates from re-uploads were considered acceptable at this scale.
- **Clip-Caption Pairing:** Each line of the acquired subtitles, along with its associated time interval in the video, was treated as a "caption" and paired with the corresponding "video clip." This process generates a vast number of weakly paired video-text examples. The authors acknowledge the inherent noise: narrations might be incomplete, lack punctuation, grammatically incorrect, or occasionally describe content not perfectly aligned with the visual at that exact moment. Manual inspection revealed that 51% of pairs had at least one visually observable object or action mentioned in the caption.
- **Scale:** The resulting HowTo100M dataset comprises 136 million video clips, sourced from 1.22 million distinct YouTube videos, amounting to an unprecedented 134,472 hours (approximately 15 years) of video content.

#### 3.2. Text-Video Joint Embedding Model

The paper proposes learning a joint embedding space where semantically similar video clips and text captions are represented close to each other.
- **Embedding Functions:** The model uses non-linear embedding functions, similar to those in Miech et al. (2018), for both video and text. These functions map input features (4096-dim) to a common 4096-dimensional embedding space. The architecture incorporates a context gating mechanism, which modulates the output of a linear layer, allowing for non-linear multiplicative interactions and proving effective in text-video embedding.
- **Loss Function:** A standard max-margin ranking loss is employed. This loss aims to ensure that the similarity score between a positive video-caption pair (V_i, C_i) is higher than the score between a positive video and a negative caption (V_i, C_j) or a negative video and a positive caption (V_j, C_i), by a predefined margin (δ=0.1).
- **Negative Sampling Strategy (Critical for Performance):** A key methodological aspect is the negative sampling strategy:
 - **Intra-video negative sampling:** Half of the negative pairs are sampled such that the video clip and caption come from *different segments of the same original YouTube video*. This forces the model to learn fine-grained distinctions and focus on relevant visual aspects rather than just generic background content (e.g., distinguishing "kneading dough" from "washing hands" within a cooking video).
 - The other half are sampled from other YouTube videos (inter-video negatives).
 - A re-weighting scheme ensures a fixed ratio between intra-video and inter-video negative examples, regardless of mini-batch composition.
- **Feature Extraction:**
 - **Video Features:** Extracted using a combination of pre-trained 2D CNN (Resnet-152, ImageNet pre-trained) for frame-level features and 3D CNN (ResNeXt-101 16-frames, Kinetics pre-trained) for spatio-temporal features. These are temporally max-pooled over the clip duration and concatenated to form a 4096-dimensional video clip vector.
 - **Text Features:** Word representations are obtained using GoogleNews pre-trained word2vec embeddings. A shallow 1D-CNN processes these word embeddings to generate the caption feature. Narrations are preprocessed by discarding common English stop-words.
- **Training:** The model is trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 10^-4. Despite the dataset's enormous size, training is relatively efficient, taking less than three days on a single Tesla P100 GPU after feature extraction. The authors also investigated a positive pair sampling strategy to deal with noise but found that using all positive pairs (r=1.0) yielded the best results, suggesting robustness to the inherent noise in the auto-transcribed narrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness and significance of the HowTo100M dataset and the learned text-video embedding across various tasks and domains.

1. **The Importance of Scale:** A crucial finding is that increasing the training data size from HowTo100M consistently leads to improved performance across all evaluated tasks (CrossTask, YouCook2, MSR-VTT, LSMDC). The authors observed no saturation, suggesting that even larger datasets could yield further gains. This empirically validates the core hypothesis that "scale matters" for learning robust multimodal representations.

2. **Impact of Intra-Video Negative Sampling:** The study on negative pair sampling confirmed that using intra-video negative pairs (negative examples from the same original video) is highly beneficial. This strategy led to significant improvements, particularly on fine-grained instructional video datasets like YouCook2 and CrossTask, where the model needs to distinguish subtle differences in actions or objects within a continuous activity. This highlights the importance of contextually relevant negative examples for learning discriminative embeddings.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance on Instructional Videos (Off-the-Shelf):**
 - **CrossTask (Action Step Localization):** The model trained solely on HowTo100M significantly outperformed existing state-of-the-art weakly supervised methods for action step localization on the CrossTask dataset. Remarkably, it even surpassed the *fully-supervised upper bound* on average, demonstrating that a massive amount of weakly supervised data can be more effective than smaller, carefully annotated datasets for certain tasks.
 - **YouCook2 (Text-to-Video Retrieval):** The off-the-shelf HowTo100M-trained model delivered superior text-to-video retrieval performance compared to models trained directly on YouCook2 or other prior state-of-the-art methods. Fine-tuning the HowTo100M pre-trained model on YouCook2 further boosted performance, achieving a substantial 13.7% improvement in R@10 over the baseline.

4. **Strong Transferability and Fine-tuning Capabilities:**
 - **MSR-VTT (Generic YouTube Videos):** The HowTo100M-trained embedding, even without fine-tuning, showed competitive performance against several models trained directly on MSR-VTT. Critically, fine-tuning the pre-trained HowTo100M model on MSR-VTT led to a new state-of-the-art, outperforming the previous best (JSFusion). Furthermore, the study revealed that state-of-the-art performance could be achieved with only **20% of the MSR-VTT annotated samples** when pre-trained on HowTo100M, highlighting significant practical implications for reducing annotation effort.
 - **LSMDC (Movies):** Despite a substantial domain gap between instructional videos and movie clips, fine-tuning the HowTo100M pre-trained model on LSMDC still provided improvements over models trained solely on LSMDC. This underscores the robustness and broad applicability of the learned representations.

5. **Cross-Dataset Pre-training Superiority:** Comparative analysis showed that pre-training on HowTo100M consistently yielded the best results when subsequently fine-tuned on YouCook2, MSR-VTT, and LSMDC, outperforming pre-training on other smaller datasets. This establishes HowTo100M as a superior resource for general-purpose video-language pre-training.

6. **Qualitative Results:** Examples of video-clip retrieval from HowTo100M using text queries demonstrated the model's ability to semantically distinguish between similar actions (e.g., "Cut paper" vs. "Cut wood"), showcasing the fine-grained understanding learned by the embedding.

In summary, the results conclusively show that learning from a massive, weakly supervised dataset of narrated instructional videos (HowTo100M) yields a highly effective and transferable text-video embedding. This embedding not only achieves new state-of-the-art performance on instructional video tasks but also generalizes well to other domains, significantly reducing the reliance on costly manual annotations for high-performance multimodal AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "HowTo100M" paper introduces a groundbreaking approach that has profound significance and a wide-ranging potential impact on the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in multimodal understanding:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Multimodal Dataset Creation:** The most immediate impact is the demonstration of a highly scalable, automated, and cost-effective method for generating massive video-language datasets. By leveraging existing web resources (narrated YouTube videos) and their inherent linguistic descriptions, the authors bypass the long-standing bottleneck of expensive and time-consuming manual annotation. This paradigm shift can inspire similar automated data collection strategies for other modalities and tasks, accelerating research in areas previously limited by data scarcity.

2. **Enabling Foundational Models for Video-Language:** HowTo100M provides a critical resource for pre-training large-scale, general-purpose video-language models. Similar to how ImageNet revolutionized image classification or large text corpora enabled powerful NLP models like BERT and GPT, HowTo100M offers the scale necessary to learn robust, broadly applicable visual-linguistic representations. These "foundation models" can then be adapted to a multitude of downstream tasks with minimal fine-tuning, significantly reducing the development effort for new applications.

3. **Advancing Generalizable Video Understanding:** The superior performance of the HowTo100M-trained embedding, both off-the-shelf and with fine-tuning, across diverse tasks (retrieval, localization) and domains (instructional, generic YouTube, movies) signifies a major leap towards more generalizable and robust video understanding systems. It demonstrates that models can learn rich semantic correspondences between visual and textual content from noisy, large-scale data, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

4. **Reduced Reliance on Costly Annotation:** The finding that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved with only a fraction of target dataset annotations (e.g., 20% of MSR-VTT) when pre-trained on HowTo100M is immensely practical. It implies that building high-performance multimodal AI systems can become more accessible, less resource-intensive, and faster, democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The public release of the HowTo100M dataset, pre-trained models, and code (as stated in the paper) is a significant contribution to open science. This generosity will undoubtedly foster new research directions, enable fair comparisons, and accelerate innovation in video-language understanding across the academic and industrial communities. Researchers can now build upon this foundation, exploring more complex architectures, novel learning objectives, and pushing the boundaries of what's achievable with massive-scale multimodal data.

6. **Broadening Real-World Applications:** The enhanced ability to bridge video and language has numerous practical implications:
 - **Improved Video Search and Recommendation:** More accurate and semantically rich text-to-video retrieval can revolutionize how users search and discover video content online.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots could better understand and act upon natural language instructions by grounding them in visual observations, especially in instructional or task-oriented scenarios.
 - **Accessibility:** Automated generation of descriptive video services or advanced summarization tools for visually impaired individuals.
 - **Content Creation and Editing:** Assisting creators with automated scene labeling, tagging, and contextual editing suggestions.

In conclusion, "HowTo100M" marks a pivotal moment in multimodal AI research. It not only solves a critical data acquisition challenge but also provides a powerful benchmark and a foundational resource that will shape the development of video-language understanding for years to come.
