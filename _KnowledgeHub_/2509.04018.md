# FPC-VLA: A Vision-Language-Action Framework with a Supervisor for Failure Prediction and Correction

> [!summary] Summary
> FPC-VLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action framework that integrates a VLM-based supervisor for predicting and correcting robotic manipulation failures. This framework, supported by an automated dataset generation method and a dual-stream action fusion module, achieved an average task success rate of 64.6% on WidowX tasks in SIMPLER and 86.0% in real-world experiments, outperforming existing VLA models.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, trained on successful demonstrations, lack autonomous capabilities for detecting and recovering from deviations or potential failures.
- There is a lack of large-scale datasets specifically designed for training failure prediction and correction mechanisms in robotic manipulation systems.
- Existing VLA methods often process actions in discrete chunks, leading to suboptimal or erratic movements and failing to leverage temporal consistency in action trajectories.

## Method

- Developed **FPC-VLA**, a ==dual-model framework== integrating a VLA model with a ==VLM-based supervisor== to proactively predict potential gripper-related failures and generate natural language-based corrections.
- Introduced an automated methodology to generate a large-scale ==failure prediction and correction dataset== by systematically extracting and synthesizing corrective action scenarios from existing successful robotic data.
- Proposed a ==dual-stream action fusion module== that aggregates historical action predictions, using ==cosine similarity== for end-effector pose and ==temporal decay== for gripper state, to generate smoother and more reliable action sequences.

## Results

- **FPC-VLA** achieved superior zero-shot generalization performance, reaching an average task success rate of **64.6%** on WidowX tasks in SIMPLER and **86.0%** in real-world experiments, surpassing baseline VLA models.
- The fine-tuned VLM-based supervisor demonstrated cross-platform generalization by maintaining competitive success rates across different robot morphologies, even when trained on data from a single platform.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the supervisor and the ==dual-stream action fusion== module were critical for performance, with the supervisor also improving robustness against disturbances by reducing performance drop from **31.3%** to **16%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proactive failure prediction and correction using a VLM-based supervisor can substantially improve the reliability and autonomy of robotic manipulation systems in open-ended tasks.
> - Large-scale datasets for failure prediction and correction can be effectively generated automatically from existing successful demonstration data, mitigating a major data scarcity bottleneck.
> - Temporally fusing historical action predictions with separate strategies for pose and gripper state enhances the robustness and consistency of robot movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a domain that utilizes automation in various sectors. Historically, robotic systems have employed a "perception-planning" pipeline, which involves two sequential stages: perception (e.g., object pose estimation, grasp point identification) and planning (e.g., motion trajectory generation). While effective in structured environments with known objects, this approach exhibits limitations in open-ended or dynamically changing tasks due to its reliance on explicit object recognition, accurate geometric modeling, and susceptibility to challenging conditions like occlusion or novel objects. Failures in the perception module can propagate, leading to unreliable robotic actions.

In response to these limitations, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged. These models integrate perception, task reasoning, and motion synthesis into a single, end-to-end architecture, mapping visual observations and natural language instructions directly to low-level robot actions. VLAs leverage large-scale pre-training on diverse vision-language datasets, acquiring a robust understanding of scenes and instructions and demonstrating generalization across various objects. However, existing VLA models, often trained on successful execution trajectories through imitation learning, exhibit a weakness: they struggle to recover from deviations or failures autonomously. They frequently require external supervision for failure prediction and corrective guidance. Furthermore, generating large-scale datasets that capture both failure prediction and correction remains a challenge, as most existing datasets annotate failures post hoc and lack mechanisms for early prediction or proactive intervention. The methods often segment actions but discard subsequent predictions, overlooking the multimodal nature of action trajectories where multiple plausible actions could achieve a goal.

Related research in robot failure detection and correction has explored using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as auxiliary agents. Some work focuses on identifying failure reasons or implementing corrections based on human input or post-failure analysis. However, many of these approaches lack the ability to anticipate failures beforehand or translate corrected task instructions into direct action changes.

FPC-VLA addresses these specific limitations by integrating a VLM-based supervisor for proactive failure prediction and correction into a VLA framework. It also introduces an automated method for generating relevant datasets and a mechanism for fusing historical action predictions, thereby attempting to enhance the reliability and adaptability of robotic manipulation systems in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing FPC-VLA stems from the identified challenges in current robotic manipulation systems, particularly the limitations of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The research aims to enhance the reliability and robustness of robotic systems in performing complex, open-ended tasks.

The key objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing the Lack of Failure Anticipation and Correction in VLAs**: Current VLA models are predominantly trained on datasets of successful demonstrations, leading to a deficiency in autonomously detecting and recovering from deviations or potential failures. These models typically execute predicted actions without a verification mechanism, requiring external intervention when errors occur. FPC-VLA proposes to overcome this by introducing a VLM-based supervisor that actively evaluates the feasibility and safety of proposed actions. This supervisor is designed to predict potential failures, particularly related to gripper actions, and generate structured, natural language-based corrections to refine the original actions before execution. This proactive approach aims to improve task completion rates and reduce the need for human intervention.

2. **Overcoming Dataset Scarcity for Failure Prediction and Correction**: A significant bottleneck in developing robust robotic systems is the lack of large-scale datasets specifically designed for failure prediction and correction. Existing datasets often annotate failures retrospectively or are limited in scale, preventing effective training for proactive intervention. The second objective is to introduce an automated methodology for generating a large-scale failure prediction and correction dataset. This method leverages existing successful robotic data in RLDS format and systematically extracts or synthesizes scenarios where corrective actions would be necessary, converting continuous action adjustments into discrete natural language signals that VLMs can process. This approach aims to reduce the reliance on manual labeling and enable more scalable training of failure-aware models.

3. **Enhancing Action Robustness and Temporal Consistency**: Many existing VLA methods process actions in chunks and often discard parts of the predicted sequence, potentially leading to suboptimal or erratic movements. Additionally, they may not fully account for the multimodal nature of action trajectories, where different components (e.g., end-effector pose, gripper state) have distinct semantic behaviors. The third objective is to propose a dual-stream action fusion module. This module aggregates historical action predictions, intelligently weighting them based on cosine similarity to the current prediction for pose and applying a temporal decay to prioritize recent inputs. By decoupling the fusion of pose and gripper states, the module aims to generate smoother, more reliable action sequences that are less susceptible to sudden changes and better leverage the entire sequence of predictions.

In summary, FPC-VLA is motivated by the need for robotic systems that can operate more autonomously and reliably in dynamic, unstructured environments by proactively identifying and correcting potential errors, facilitated by improved data generation and action synthesis mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FPC-VLA is a dual-model framework that integrates a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model with a VLM-based supervisor for failure prediction and correction. The system takes an RGB image (I_t) and a natural language instruction (l) at timestep *t* and outputs a refined action (a'_t). The framework consists of three main modules: a VLA model, a dual-stream action fusion module, and a supervisor for robot failure prediction and correction, supported by an automated dataset generation method.

#### 3.1. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model

The core VLA model is responsible for generating initial action predictions. It comprises a vision module, a language module, and an action module.

- **Vision Module**: This module processes the input image I_t. It employs a dual-encoder architecture utilizing both DINO and SigLIP vision backbones. Features extracted from these backbones are concatenated and then projected through a linear layer (W_p) and bias (b_p) into the embedding space of the language model, producing vision tokens V.
- **Language Module**: Built upon Llama2, a transformer-based autoregressive language model, this module tokenizes the language instruction *l* into language tokens L. For multimodal inputs, the language embeddings L are concatenated with the visual embeddings V, preceded by a Beginning-Of-Sequence (BOS) token, forming E_multi. This combined embedding is processed by Llama2 to yield a cognition token Z.
- **Action Module**: This module generates action sequences conditioned on Z using a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). The DiT models action sequences by progressively denoising a noisy input over *T* timesteps. During training, it learns to predict the noise component (ϵ) added to a ground-truth action sequence (x_0). The timestep *t* and cognition variable Z are incorporated into the DiT via Adaptive Layer Norm (AdaLN). The DiT's transformer backbone (Multi-Head Self-Attention and Feed-Forward Network) captures long-range dependencies for coherent multi-step action generation. During inference, it starts from a random noise vector and iteratively denoises it to produce the action sequence x_0.

#### 3.2. Dual-Stream Action Fusion Module

To achieve robust and temporally consistent action outputs, this module fuses a set of historical action predictions.
- **Prediction Collection**: At each timestep *t*, the module collects *N* past action predictions, a^(k)_t, where *k* ranges from 0 (current) to *N*-1 (past). Each a^(k)_t is decomposed into a 6-DOF end-effector pose (p^(k)_t) and a scalar gripper state (g^(k)_t).
- **Pose Fusion**: The module calculates the cosine similarity (sim^(k)) between each historical pose p^(k)_t and the most recent pose p^(0)_t. A sigmoid transformation (s^(k)) with scaling factor α is applied to smooth these similarities. To prioritize recent predictions, an exponential time decay (d^(k)) with coefficient λ is also applied. The final weight (w^(k)) for each pose is the product of the smoothed similarity and time decay, normalized across all *N* predictions, with a regularization factor β. The fused pose (p_hat_t) is then computed as a weighted average of historical poses. The gripper state is explicitly excluded from cosine similarity computation due to its distinct semantic behavior.
- **Gripper State Fusion**: For the scalar gripper state, only temporal decay (d^(k)) is used to compute normalized weights (d_tilde^(k)). The fused gripper state (g_hat_t) is a weighted average of historical gripper states based on these temporal decay weights.
- **Output**: The final fused action (a_hat_t) concatenates the fused pose (p_hat_t) and gripper state (g_hat_t).

#### 3.3. Robot Failure Prediction and Correction Dataset Generation

This module provides an automated method to generate a large-scale failure correction dataset from existing RLDS-format data, addressing the scarcity of such datasets.
- **Grasp Event Identification**: The process identifies critical grasp and release events within demonstration episodes by detecting significant changes in the gripper state (threshold δ_g = 0.5).
- **Contextual Frame Retention**: For each identified gripper change event, a window of preceding frames (k=3) is retained to provide sufficient context for the VLM.
- **Corrective Action Vector Generation**: For every retained frame, the module determines the next gripper change event (c*) and calculates a corrective action vector (∆a = a_c* - a_t) representing the necessary pose adjustment.
- **Natural Language Correction Mapping**: The continuous ∆a vector is mapped into discrete natural language descriptions. This involves decomposing ∆a into translational (∆x, ∆y, ∆z) and z-axis rotational (∆r_z) components. Based on predefined small (δ_small = 0.01) and large (δ_large = 0.1) thresholds, these continuous values are converted into directional and magnitude phrases (e.g., "Move down. Large.", "Rotate counterclockwise. Small."). Rotations around x and y axes are not considered due to VLM's difficulty in interpreting them linguistically.
- **Structured Prompt Creation**: A standard prompt (P_t) for the VLM is constructed for each retained frame, including task requirements, answer limitations, and example samples to guide the VLM's response format. The directions are defined relative to the robot’s base frame.

#### 3.4. VLM-Based Supervisor for Robot Failure Prediction and Correction

This supervisor actively evaluates actions before execution, triggered at critical moments.
- **Trigger Condition**: The supervisor is activated when the gripper state component (g_t) of the predicted action (a_hat_t) indicates a significant change from the previous gripper state (g_{t-1}), exceeding a threshold (δ_g).
- **VLM Query**: When triggered, the supervisor inputs the current image (I_t) and the structured prompt (P_t) into a fine-tuned Vision-Language Model (M), specifically Qwen2.5-vl 7B.
- **Decision and Correction**:
 - If the VLM's response (R_t) begins with "Yes.", the original fused action (a_hat_t) is executed directly, indicating approval of the gripper action.
 - If R_t begins with "No.", the supervisor extracts translation corrections (∆p = [∆x, ∆y, ∆z]) and rotation corrections (∆r_z) from the natural language response. These discrete magnitudes ("small" or "large") are mapped back to scalar step sizes.
 - The original action (a_hat_t) is then refined (a'_t = a_hat_t + [∆x, ∆y, ∆z, 0, 0, ∆r_z, 0]), applying corrections only to specified components, and the refined action (a'_t) is executed.
- **Implementation Details**: The VLA model is pretrained on a mixture of OXE and LIBERO datasets. The supervisor VLM is fine-tuned on synthetic failure datasets (100k entries from BridgeV2, RT-1, LIBERO) and 10k annotated entries from MuJoCo and real robot teleoperation. Inference for non-keyframes takes 0.176s, while keyframes (with supervisor intervention) take 1.766s.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated FPC-VLA across multiple simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic platforms, assessing its performance, generalization capabilities, and the effectiveness of its individual components through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Simulation Benchmarks

- **SIMPLER with WidowX**: FPC-VLA demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on WidowX robot tasks (grasping, stacking, placing). It achieved an average task success rate of 64.6%, outperforming all compared baselines, including fine-tuned models like SpatialVLA (42.7%) and ThinkAct (43.8%), and zero-shot models like CogACT (51.8%). RT-1-X and OpenVLA showed considerably lower performance (1.1% and 1.0% respectively).
- **SIMPLER with Google Robot**: FPC-VLA achieved average success rates of 78.0% in Visual Matching tasks and 65.8% in Variant Aggregation tasks, ranking highest among all evaluated methods in a zero-shot setting. This performance exceeded even methods fine-tuned on in-domain data. For instance, on the "Open Top Drawer and Place Apple" task, FPC-VLA's success rate of 54.5% (Variant Aggregation) was notable.
- **LIBERO with Franka**: On the LIBERO platform, FPC-VLA achieved an average performance of 86.9%, surpassing all other baselines without in-domain fine-tuning. A significant finding was its performance on long-horizon tasks, where it scored 82.2%, considerably higher than the second-best ThinkAct (70.9%). This suggests the supervisor's efficacy in maintaining task progress over extended durations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- FPC-VLA was deployed on Xiaomi Robot and ALOHA platforms for five challenging tasks, including stacking blocks, placing objects, and dual-arm handover.
- It consistently outperformed all baseline approaches, achieving the highest average grasp success rate (92.8%) and task completion success rate (86.0%). This surpassed the next best method, CogACT (86.0% grasp, 78.4% task). The VLM-based supervisor was observed to effectively predict and correct potential grasping or placement failures, contributing to the high success rates.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Cross-Platform Generalization Capability (Supervisor)

- The supervisor, when fine-tuned exclusively on data from the Google Robot dataset, demonstrated cross-platform generalization by achieving competitive success rates on the WidowX robot in SIMPLER. This indicates that the supervisor's reasoning over high-level, natural language queries and its use of a standardized, egocentric vocabulary for corrections allowed it to transfer failure prediction and correction policies across different robot morphologies without additional fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Evaluation of Failure Prediction and Correction Accuracy

- A comparative study showed that the fine-tuned FPC-VLA supervisor consistently outperformed general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as DeepSeek-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B, LLaVA-1.5-7B, and its base architecture Qwen2.5-VL-7B, across WidowX, Google Robot, and Franka robots. This difference in accuracy highlights the effectiveness of the proposed automated dataset generation pipeline and the targeted fine-tuning on semantically structured, robot-relevant QA pairs.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Supervisor Contribution**: Removing the supervisor module resulted in a significant degradation of performance. Disabling the correction mechanism, while retaining failure prediction, also lowered success rates. The inclusion of in-context examples in the supervisor's prompts further improved performance.
- **Action Fusion Module Contribution**: Using only the latest action prediction or directly averaging historical predictions without similarity-guided weighting led to substantially lower success rates compared to the dual-stream action fusion module. The time decay strategy in fusion was also found to be beneficial, as its removal slightly reduced performance.
- **Robustness to Disturbances**: In an experiment with induced motion disturbances (random end-effector pose perturbations), FPC-VLA's performance drop was 16%, whereas without the supervisor, the drop was 31.3%. This indicates that the supervisor contributes to the framework's robustness against external disturbances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FPC-VLA represents an advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The research addresses a fundamental limitation of current VLA systems by introducing a mechanism for proactive failure prediction and correction, which has several implications.

First, the integration of a VLM-based supervisor that evaluates action viability and generates corrective guidance marks a shift from reactive error handling to proactive failure mitigation. This capability is significant for improving the reliability and robustness of robots, allowing them to operate more autonomously in complex and unstructured environments where unforeseen events or deviations from ideal trajectories are common. By enabling robots to self-correct before executing potentially erroneous actions, FPC-VLA contributes to safer and more dependable robotic deployments.

Second, the proposed automated method for generating large-scale failure prediction and correction datasets is a practical contribution to the field of robot learning. The manual annotation of failure scenarios is labor-intensive and challenging to scale. An automated pipeline that converts continuous action differences into structured natural language corrections from existing robotic data mitigates a major bottleneck in dataset creation. This approach can facilitate the training of more sophisticated and failure-aware robotic policies without prohibitive data labeling costs.

Third, the dual-stream action fusion module enhances the temporal consistency and smoothness of robot movements. By aggregating historical predictions and weighting them based on similarity and temporal relevance, while decoupling pose and gripper state fusion, the system can produce more refined and stable actions. This contributes to the overall precision and efficacy of robotic manipulation, making robot operations less erratic and more reliable over time.

The comprehensive evaluation across diverse simulation platforms (SIMPLER, LIBERO) and real-world robots (WidowX, Google Robot, Franka, Xiaomi Robot, ALOHA) confirms FPC-VLA's generalized applicability. Its demonstrated superior performance in zero-shot settings, even compared to fine-tuned baselines, highlights its strong generalization capabilities across different robot embodiments and tasks. The specific advantage in long-horizon tasks also indicates its potential for more complex, multi-step operations.

Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges certain limitations. The supervisor's reliance on gripper state changes means it cannot address post-grasp failures (e.g., object slippage), which often require force/torque sensing beyond current visual-only VLA capabilities. Additionally, while tested on long-horizon tasks, the complexity and duration of these tasks remain constrained compared to real-world domestic service demands.

In conclusion, FPC-VLA's contributions in proactive failure prediction and correction, scalable dataset generation, and robust action fusion represent a step towards more reliable and autonomous robotic systems. The framework has the potential to impact various applications requiring robust robot manipulation, such as manufacturing, logistics, and healthcare, by fostering the development of robots capable of navigating and performing tasks with greater independence and resilience. Future work could focus on integrating additional sensory modalities like force sensing and scaling the framework to address even more extended and intricate real-world scenarios.
