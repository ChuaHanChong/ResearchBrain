# FPC-VLA: A Vision-Language-Action Framework with a Supervisor for Failure Prediction and Correction

> [!summary] Summary
> FPC-VLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action framework that integrates a VLM-based supervisor for predicting and correcting robotic manipulation failures. This framework, supported by an automated dataset generation method and a dual-stream action fusion module, achieved an average task success rate of 64.6% on WidowX tasks in SIMPLER and 86.0% in real-world experiments, outperforming existing VLA models.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, trained on successful demonstrations, lack autonomous capabilities for detecting and recovering from deviations or potential failures.
- There is a lack of large-scale datasets specifically designed for training failure prediction and correction mechanisms in robotic manipulation systems.
- Existing VLA methods often process actions in discrete chunks, leading to suboptimal or erratic movements and failing to leverage temporal consistency in action trajectories.

## Method

- Developed **FPC-VLA**, a ==dual-model framework== integrating a VLA model with a ==VLM-based supervisor== to proactively predict potential gripper-related failures and generate natural language-based corrections.
- Introduced an automated methodology to generate a large-scale ==failure prediction and correction dataset== by systematically extracting and synthesizing corrective action scenarios from existing successful robotic data.
- Proposed a ==dual-stream action fusion module== that aggregates historical action predictions, using ==cosine similarity== for end-effector pose and ==temporal decay== for gripper state, to generate smoother and more reliable action sequences.

## Results

- **FPC-VLA** achieved superior zero-shot generalization performance, reaching an average task success rate of **64.6%** on WidowX tasks in SIMPLER and **86.0%** in real-world experiments, surpassing baseline VLA models.
- The fine-tuned VLM-based supervisor demonstrated cross-platform generalization by maintaining competitive success rates across different robot morphologies, even when trained on data from a single platform.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the supervisor and the ==dual-stream action fusion== module were critical for performance, with the supervisor also improving robustness against disturbances by reducing performance drop from **31.3%** to **16%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proactive failure prediction and correction using a VLM-based supervisor can substantially improve the reliability and autonomy of robotic manipulation systems in open-ended tasks.
> - Large-scale datasets for failure prediction and correction can be effectively generated automatically from existing successful demonstration data, mitigating a major data scarcity bottleneck.
> - Temporally fusing historical action predictions with separate strategies for pose and gripper state enhances the robustness and consistency of robot movements.
