# MosaicMem: Hybrid Spatial Memory for Controllable Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MosaicMem introduces a hybrid spatial memory system for video diffusion models that combines explicit 3D geometry with implicit attention-based learning. The model enables long-horizon, camera-controlled video generation with superior consistency, dynamic object modeling, and manipulability, alongside facilitating real-time autoregressive generation.

## Problem

- Developing consistent, explorable "world simulators" in video diffusion models that maintain object permanence and viewpoint consistency over long durations.
- Existing explicit spatial memory methods struggle with dynamic scenes and limit generative flexibility by relying on static 3D geometric caches.
- Current implicit spatial memory methods suffer from inaccurate egomotion (drift), redundancy, inefficiency in context usage, and difficulty in manipulating latent scene representations.

## Method

- Proposes **MosaicMem**, a hybrid spatial memory approach that lifts 2D patches from observations into 3D space using off-the-shelf 3D estimators, then uses these 3D-localized patches as contextual conditioning for a ==Diffusion== ==Transformer== (DiT).
- Introduces Warped RoPE and Warped Latent mechanisms to align memory patches across time and camera motion in latent space, ensuring geometry-consistent correspondence and addressing coordinate resolution issues.
- Integrates Projective Positional Encoding (PRoPE) as a principled camera conditioning interface within the DiT to achieve precise and controllable camera motion adherence.

## Results

- MosaicMem achieved superior camera motion accuracy (e.g., RotErr 0.51°) compared to explicit (SEVA 1.42°) and implicit (CaM 4.65°) baselines.
- Demonstrated enhanced dynamic object modeling (Dynamic Score 2.58) and significantly higher memory retrieval consistency (SSIM 0.75, PSNR 23.57) over existing methods.
- Enabled minute-level coherent video generation, memory-based scene editing (object manipulation, scene stitching), and an autoregressive variant ("Mosaic Forcing") for real-time generation at **16** FPS with improved VBench metrics over other real-time systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid spatial memory, unifying explicit 3D localization with implicit attention, effectively combines precise geometric consistency with the flexibility to model dynamics and text-driven content.
> - Targeted, patch-level 3D memory retrieval significantly reduces context redundancy compared to full-frame memory, enabling more efficient and persistent long-horizon generation.
> - Advanced warping mechanisms are crucial for maintaining spatiotemporal alignment and high-resolution coordinate information during feature compression and re-projection across views.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical challenge in the development of advanced video diffusion models: the creation of consistent and explorable "world simulators." Recent progress in video diffusion models has enabled the generation of high-fidelity, controllable video clips. The broader research goal is to move beyond short, plausible clips towards systems that can simulate environments with object permanence, viewpoint consistency, and stable cause-and-effect relationships under agent intervention. Such simulators are envisioned to empower agents to anticipate outcomes and inform decision-making in fields like reinforcement learning.

The primary bottleneck for achieving long-duration, physically consistent interaction in these systems is effective spatial memory. Current approaches typically fall into two categories:
- **Explicit Spatial Memory**: These methods rely on external 3D estimation (e.g., point clouds, 3D Gaussians) to build a geometric cache. This approach offers advantages in view consistency and metric faithfulness due to grounded 3D inference. However, it struggles to model dynamic scenes, particularly those with multiple independently moving objects, thereby limiting its generality.
- **Implicit Spatial Memory**: This paradigm stores world state within the model’s latent representation, often by feeding back posed frames. It is flexible for handling dynamics and appearance changes but often produces inaccurate egomotion, leading to noticeable drift over revisits, even when precise camera poses are provided. Furthermore, frame-based representations can be highly redundant and inefficient for long-term persistence, and the implicit state is difficult to interpret or manipulate for scene editing.

This research aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms by proposing a hybrid spatial memory system that unifies their complementary strengths, addressing their respective limitations in consistency, dynamic modeling, efficiency, and manipulability. The objective is to facilitate the development of persistent, explorable video world simulators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust spatial memory mechanism, termed Mosaic Memory (MosaicMem), that can enable long-horizon, camera-controlled video generation with improved consistency, dynamic object modeling, and manipulability. This overarching goal is motivated by the specific limitations observed in existing explicit and implicit spatial memory approaches:

**Motivation from Explicit Memory Limitations:**
- **Inflexibility with Dynamics:** Explicit memory methods, which construct global 3D geometric caches, are primarily suited for static or largely static scenes. They face significant challenges in maintaining and updating a coherent 3D structure in the presence of multiple independently moving objects. This limits their applicability in dynamic environments.
- **Limited Generative Flexibility:** By anchoring generation to projected geometric evidence, explicit methods can restrict the model's ability to produce rich, text-driven dynamics and novel content.
- **Accumulation of Misalignments:** The reliance on global 3D reconstruction can lead to the accumulation of small cross-view misalignments over time, making long-horizon memory updates fragile and prone to artifacts.

**Motivation from Implicit Memory Limitations:**
- **Inaccurate Egomotion:** Even when provided with precise camera poses, implicit memory often results in inaccurate egomotion in generated videos, leading to spatial drift during revisits. This undermines viewpoint consistency over time.
- **Redundancy and Inefficiency:** Storing memory as posed frames or frame-derived features is highly redundant. This redundancy consumes significant context resources, impacting generation speed and capping persistence within finite context windows. Efforts to compress these tokens often degrade retrieval fidelity and long-horizon consistency.
- **Lack of Manipulability:** The implicit nature of memory storage in latent representations makes it difficult to interpret or directly manipulate for intricate spatial editing operations.

**Specific Objectives of MosaicMem:**
Based on these motivations, the key objectives are to:
1. **Unify Strengths**: Combine the precise localization and geometric consistency benefits of explicit memory with the flexibility and prompt-following generative capabilities of implicit memory.
2. **Targeted Retrieval and Efficiency**: Leverage 3D estimation to lift 2D patches into 3D, enabling reliable patch-level localization and targeted retrieval. This aims to substantially reduce the effective context required for long-term persistence compared to full-frame implicit methods.
3. **Preserve Dynamic Generation**: Condition generation through the model's native attention mechanisms, allowing it to naturally accommodate dynamic objects, non-rigid changes, and text-driven content evolution.
4. **Enhance Viewpoint Controllability**: Integrate a principled camera conditioning interface, such as Projective Positional Encoding (PRoPE), to achieve more accurate and controllable camera motion adherence.
5. **Improve Memory Alignment**: Address issues of spatiotemporal ambiguity and coordinate resolution reduction through novel warping mechanisms (Warped RoPE and Warped Latent) to ensure geometry-consistent correspondence between retrieved memory patches and the current view.
6. **Enable Advanced Capabilities**: Facilitate capabilities such as minute-level navigation, memory-based scene editing through direct patch manipulation, and efficient autoregressive video generation.
7. **New Benchmark**: Create a dedicated benchmark, MosaicMem-World, to rigorously test spatial memory retrieval under complex camera motions and dynamic object scenarios, which are underserved by existing datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MosaicMem methodology integrates explicit 3D understanding with implicit, attention-based conditioning within a video diffusion model framework.

**Core Design: Mosaic Memory (MosaicMem)**
MosaicMem proposes a hybrid spatial memory that utilizes patches as the fundamental unit of memory, combining elements from both explicit and implicit paradigms.
1. **Geometric Lifting**: Similar to the initial stages of explicit memory pipelines, each 2D patch from an observation is lifted into 3D space. This is achieved by employing an off-the-shelf 3D estimator to infer depth and associated camera intrinsics/extrinsics for the patch. This step provides precise, patch-level 3D localization.
2. **Conditioning and Composition**: When a new viewpoint is queried, relevant 3D-localized memory patches are retrieved. These retrieved patches are then provided as contextual conditioning to the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model. The system conceptually "composes" these spatially aligned patches onto the target frame, similar to assembling a mosaic. This structured "patch-and-compose" interface ensures that persistent scene elements are maintained while allowing the generative model to inpaint and update dynamic or novel content consistent with text prompts.

MosaicMem is integrated into text+image-to-video (TI2V) models by learning a neural vector field that generates video rollouts conditioned on an input image, text prompts, camera poses, and the spatial memory (`M`). Initial validation demonstrated that even without fine-tuning, a vanilla DiT model could project injected MosaicMem conditions to correct spatiotemporal locations, indicating the pipeline's basic functionality.

**Memory Alignment Through Warping**
To overcome spatiotemporal ambiguity and reduced RoPE coordinate resolution caused by the 3D VAE's compression, two warping mechanisms are introduced:
1. **Warped RoPE**: This mechanism aligns patches across time and camera motion in latent space. For a retrieved memory patch, its original 2D RoPE coordinates and depth are back-projected into 3D world space. These 3D points are then re-projected into the target camera view, yielding new 2D coordinates (u', v'). The fractional part of these reprojected coordinates is preserved, and RoPE is sampled at a higher resolution to maintain accuracy, defining a 3D RoPE coordinate.
2. **Warped Latent**: Complementary to Warped RoPE, this method directly transforms retrieved memory patches in the feature space. It uses differentiable bilinear grid sampling on the original latent features, guided by the dense geometric correspondences established by the reprojected (u', v') coordinates from Warped RoPE.
The research indicates that training with a mixture of both warping strategies yields the most robust performance.

**PRoPE for Camera Control**
While MosaicMem provides visual cues, explicit camera control is crucial for reliable trajectory adherence. The paper adopts Projective Positional Encoding (PRoPE) as a camera conditioning interface for DiT-based video generation.
- **Mechanism**: PRoPE encodes the relative geometry between two camera views using their projection matrices. This relative information is injected into the self-attention mechanism through a transformed attention formulation.
- **Adaptation for Video**: For spatio-temporal tokens compressed by a factor of 4 in the VAE, PRoPE is adapted to "unfold" an extra sub-index. This ensures that each temporally compressed latent frame is conditioned by the correct per-frame projective information from the four original frames it represents.
- **Rationale**: PRoPE addresses the need for precise motion signals under large camera movements or sparse memory, compensates for fine-grained inter-frame motion not captured by the VAE's temporal compression, and enables the reuse of previously generated latents for efficiency.

**Data Curation: MosaicMem-World Benchmark**
A new benchmark dataset, MosaicMem-World, was created to stress-test spatial memory under viewpoint changes and revisits, a gap in existing datasets dominated by forward navigation.
- **Data Sources**: It aggregates tens of hours of video from four types of sources: curated Unreal Engine 5 scenes (with explicit revisits), commercial game environments (e.g., Cyberpunk 2077 for dynamics), real-world first-person captures, and selected high-revisit frequency sequences from existing datasets (e.g., Sekai).
- **Preprocessing and Annotation**: A unified pipeline reconstructs depth and camera motion (using Depth Anything V3 or VIPE). Videos are segmented into 32-frame clips, and each segment is annotated with two text descriptions via Gemini 3: one for static scene content (first frame) and one for temporal dynamics (remaining frames). This factorized labeling supports compositional training. Videos with inaccurate 3D estimates or motion blur are filtered.

**Training Details**
The system fine-tunes a pre-trained Wan 2.2 5B text-to-image-to-video (TI2V) DiT model. Training is performed using the AdamW optimizer for 250k steps with an effective batch size of 64 on 8 H100 GPUs. Inference uses 50 denoising steps with Wan's default sampler.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MosaicMem involved a comprehensive set of quantitative and qualitative comparisons against explicit and implicit memory baselines, as well as ablation studies on its components.

**Quantitative Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Video Quality**: FID (Fréchet Inception Distance), FVD (Fréchet Video Distance).
- **Camera Control**: RotErr (Rotation Error), TransErr (Translation Error), estimated by Depth Anything V3.
- **Motion Dynamics**: Dynamic Score (Average Optical Flow Magnitude), measured on trajectories with minimal camera motion to isolate object dynamics.
- **Memory Retrieval Consistency**: Consistency Score (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS), calculated within annotated corresponding regions between input and future frames.

**Comparison with Explicit Memory Baselines (GEN3C, SEVA, VMem, VWM):**
- **Camera Control**: Explicit memory baselines generally achieve lower RotErr/TransErr than implicit methods, but MosaicMem achieves even better accuracy (e.g., MosaicMem RotErr 0.51° vs. SEVA 1.42°).
- **Dynamic Modeling**: MosaicMem significantly outperforms explicit memory baselines in Dynamic Score (2.58 vs. 1.18-1.41), demonstrating its ability to generate rich, text-driven dynamics, including novel moving objects and animation. Explicit methods tend to produce static scenes.
- **Visual Quality and Consistency**: MosaicMem achieves superior FID and FVD, indicating higher overall generation quality, and significantly better Consistency Scores (e.g., SSIM 0.75, PSNR 23.57, LPIPS 0.11) than explicit baselines.

**Comparison with Implicit Memory Baselines (WorldMem, Context-as-Memory (CaM)):**
- **Camera Control**: MosaicMem dramatically surpasses implicit memory methods in camera motion accuracy (e.g., MosaicMem RotErr 0.51° vs. CaM 4.65°). Implicit methods exhibit substantial egomotion drift.
- **Memory Retrieval Consistency**: MosaicMem shows significantly higher Consistency Scores, indicating more faithful reconstruction of previously observed scenes.
- **Visual Quality**: MosaicMem achieves better FID and FVD, attributed to more accurate camera motion modeling.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Camera Control Module**: Comparing ControlMLP, PRoPE alone, and MosaicMem alone showed progressive improvements. PRoPE outperformed ControlMLP, and MosaicMem alone improved over PRoPE. However, MosaicMem alone struggled with large camera movements and sparse memory, leading to significant camera errors and less effective guidance without explicit camera conditioning.
- **Warping Mechanisms**:
 - **Warped Latent**: Demonstrated better camera motion accuracy.
 - **Warped RoPE**: Achieved superior visual quality and memory retrieval performance.
 - **Full MosaicMem (mixture of both)**: Training with a mixture of both warping strategies resulted in the best overall performance and robustness, resolving an issue where Warped RoPE alone might repeatedly generate new objects near image boundaries in slow camera motion scenarios.

**Long-horizon Video Generation:**
- MosaicMem successfully generated minute-level navigation videos (80-frame segments, iteratively updating memory).
- It demonstrated robust consistency, accurately recalling previously observed regions and rendering moving objects, even after long-duration and large-scale camera motions.
- In contrast, CaM (a strong implicit baseline) accumulated artifacts and eventually collapsed in extended rollouts.

**Memory Manipulation:**
- The patch-based 3D memory structure of MosaicMem allows for direct manipulation of object placement (deletion, duplication, relocation).
- The paper demonstrated "scene stitching," where videos or images from different scenes were registered into MosaicMem and spatially connected. This enabled the generation of unified, geometrically continuous scenes, allowing seamless transitions between diverse environments (e.g., medieval to modern street) or creating surreal compositions (e.g., a street scene registered in the sky).

**Mosaic Forcing (Autoregressive Generation):**
- The bidirectional MosaicMem model was distilled into an autoregressive (AR) variant, "Mosaic Forcing," using Causal Forcing and Rolling Forcing.
- This AR model achieved real-time generation at 16 FPS (640x360 resolution).
- Compared to existing real-time AR video generation systems (RELIC, Matrix-Game 2.0), Mosaic Forcing significantly outperformed them across a suite of VBench metrics (Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering, Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality) and camera accuracy. This highlights its ability to maintain visual quality and temporal consistency even with few-step inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MosaicMem represents a notable advancement in the field of controllable video generation and world modeling, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging Paradigms**: The core significance of MosaicMem lies in its novel hybrid spatial memory design, which effectively integrates the strengths of explicit 3D geometry with the flexibility of implicit, attention-based learning. This approach addresses critical limitations of prior work, such as the struggle of explicit methods with dynamics and the consistency issues of implicit methods.

2. **Enhanced Consistency and Control**: The system demonstrates superior camera pose adherence and long-term consistency, even over minute-level video rollouts. This is crucial for creating believable and explorable virtual environments. The integration of PRoPE further provides precise camera control, which is essential for agent interaction and realistic navigation within simulated worlds.

3. **Robust Dynamic Modeling**: Unlike explicit memory systems that often produce static scenes, MosaicMem retains the ability to generate rich, text-driven dynamics and animate moving objects with temporal consistency. This capability expands the realism and versatility of generated content, moving closer to dynamic world simulation.

4. **Practical Efficiency and Scalability**: By lifting patches into 3D for targeted retrieval, MosaicMem reduces the redundancy inherent in frame-based implicit memory. This allows for more efficient use of context windows, enabling longer-horizon persistence without sacrificing detail or consistency.

5. **New Avenues for Content Creation**: The explicit-like structure of MosaicMem's patch memory facilitates direct manipulation for scene editing, such as object relocation or deletion, and enables the seamless stitching of diverse scenes. These capabilities open new creative possibilities for artists, designers, and developers in generating complex, interactive virtual environments.

6. **Enabling Advanced World Simulators**: MosaicMem directly contributes to the realization of "world simulators" that can empower AI agents to visualize plausible futures and learn from simulated experiences. Its ability to generate long, consistent, and dynamic videos with precise camera control makes it a foundational component for reinforcement learning in complex virtual environments.

7. **Real-time Interactive Experiences**: The development of "Mosaic Forcing," an autoregressive variant of MosaicMem, demonstrates the potential for real-time video generation. This could pave the way for interactive virtual worlds and gaming experiences where the environment dynamically responds to user input with high visual fidelity and temporal consistency.

8. **New Benchmark for Research**: The introduction of the MosaicMem-World benchmark provides a valuable resource for the research community, specifically designed to stress-test spatial memory under revisits and dynamic changes. This will help drive future advancements in the field by enabling more rigorous evaluation.

In conclusion, MosaicMem represents a substantial step towards building more capable and versatile video world models. Its hybrid memory design, coupled with advanced camera control and memory alignment mechanisms, addresses key limitations of existing approaches and unlocks new possibilities for long-horizon, controllable, and interactive video generation.
