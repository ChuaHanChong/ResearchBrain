# Training Data Efficiency in Multimodal Process Reward Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Balanced-Information Score (BIS) was introduced to improve training data efficiency for Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs). This method allows models to achieve full-data performance using significantly smaller subsets of existing Monte Carlo-annotated corpora, such as matching full-data performance with 95.5% less computational cost using just 10% of the data.

## Problem

- Training Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs) necessitates large-scale Monte Carlo (MC)-annotated datasets, incurring high computational costs and energy consumption.
- The extensive data requirements create bottlenecks in the practical deployment and scaling of MLLMs for complex visual reasoning tasks.
- Existing data efficiency approaches for reward models often involve costly re-annotation, additional model calls, or architectural changes, adding complexity.

## Method

- Empirical and theoretical analysis identified significant data redundancy and the specific characteristics of informative rollouts (mixed but reliable labels) within current datasets.
- A theoretical framework was developed to explain observed performance saturation and the impact of label noise on the effective gradient signal during training.
- The ==Balanced-Information Score (BIS)== was proposed, which filters existing Monte Carlo-annotated training data based on ==rollout label mixture and reliability== to select informative subsets without additional cost.

## Results

- BIS-selected **10%** of the training data enabled an InternVL2.5-8B MPRM to match full-data performance, achieving a **95.5%** reduction in computational cost.
- For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, BIS-**5%** improved over random selection by **+10.9** points, with BIS-**25%** matching full-data performance while significantly reducing training data volume.
- MPRMs trained on BIS-selected data consistently achieved superior best-of-N reranking performance across four diverse multimodal benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced model effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Monte Carlo-annotated datasets exhibit significant redundancy, as MPRM performance quickly plateaus even with random subsampling, indicating a noise-limited training regime.
> - The most informative training data consists of rollouts with a high mixture of positive and negative steps, combined with reliable positive labels (high average MC scores).
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrated that label mixture (uncertainty) and reliability (low noise) multiplicatively influence the effective gradient signal, making their balanced combination crucial for data selection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly applied to complex visual reasoning tasks. A crucial component in enhancing their performance, particularly in step-level supervision, is the use of Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs). MPRMs provide granular feedback by scoring intermediate reasoning steps, in contrast to outcome-based reward models that only evaluate the final answer. This step-level supervision is utilized both during training (e.g., for reinforcement learning fine-tuning) and at inference time (e.g., for trajectory ranking).

A significant challenge in the deployment and scaling of MPRMs is their reliance on large-scale Monte Carlo (MC)-annotated corpora, such as VisualPRM400K-v1.1, which contain millions of annotated steps. The process of generating and training on these extensive datasets is computationally expensive, leading to practical bottlenecks in computational cost and energy consumption.

The current research is situated within the broader effort to improve the data efficiency of process supervision for reward models. This landscape includes approaches that:
1. **Optimize the annotation pipeline**: Methods that refine MC or search-based annotators to yield higher-quality step labels from fewer or cheaper model calls.
2. **Learn robust supervision from weak signals**: Techniques designed for PRMs to learn effectively from imperfect MC labels or even outcome-only signals, thereby reducing the need for costly, high-quality process annotations.
3. **Data selection and supervision allocation**: Strategies that actively select the most informative samples for training or determine how supervision should be allocated across examples.

This work specifically falls into the third category, focusing on post-hoc data selection. Unlike approaches that require additional model calls, relabeling, or modifications to the MPRM architecture or training objective, this research proposes a method for selecting informative rollouts based solely on existing MC statistics within the dataset, without incurring additional annotation or model evaluation costs. The objective is to identify and leverage data subsets that are more informative, thereby reducing the overall training data volume and computational expenditure without compromising model performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the data efficiency bottleneck in training Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs). Training MPRMs typically necessitates large-scale Monte Carlo (MC)-annotated corpora, a practice that incurs substantial computational costs. The authors aim to investigate how MPRM performance scales with the available data budget and, critically, to develop a principled method for selecting informative data subsets that can preserve, or even surpass, the performance achieved with the full dataset.

The motivation for this study is rooted in several preliminary observations and insights:

1. **Evidence of Data Redundancy**: Initial experiments involving random subsampling of existing MC-annotated training data revealed that MPRM performance quickly saturates. This empirical finding suggests that current large-scale MC-annotated corpora contain a significant degree of redundant information. A substantial fraction of rollouts can be discarded with only a modest degradation in performance, indicating an opportunity for more efficient data utilization.
2. **Characterizing Informative Rollouts**: Further preliminary studies involved constructing heuristic subsets (e.g., randomly sampled, low-MC score, and mixed positive/negative steps). These experiments indicated that training on subsets containing a mixture of correct and incorrect steps yielded better performance than purely random selection. Conversely, rollouts characterized by very low average MC scores, often implying noisy pseudo-positive labels, tended to degrade performance. These observations suggested that effective supervision derives from rollouts that are "mixed" (containing both positive and negative steps) but also "reliable" (where positive labels are trustworthy).
3. **Need for a Principled Selection Mechanism**: The empirical findings pointed to the need for a systematic approach to identify and prioritize informative rollouts. The observed redundancy and the impact of label mixture and reliability motivated the development of a theoretically grounded criterion for data selection.
4. **Addressing Gradient Noise vs. Data Scarcity**: The authors hypothesized that MPRM training might be more limited by gradient noise (stemming from noisy MC labels) than by a sheer lack of data. A theoretical framework was sought to formalize this interplay between gradient signal, label noise, and data redundancy, and to guide the design of an effective data selection score.

In summary, the core motivation is to enhance the computational efficiency of MPRM training by reducing the required data volume without sacrificing model quality, driven by empirical evidence of data redundancy and the differential informativeness of various rollout characteristics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved a combination of empirical preliminary studies, a theoretical analysis to formalize observed phenomena, and the development of a novel data selection score.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Study (Section 2)

- **Background and General Setup**:
 - **MPRM Training**: Standard setup adopted from prior work, using VisualPRM400K-v1.1 dataset. This dataset provides reasoning steps annotated with Monte Carlo (MC) estimated success rates (from $N=16$ sampled continuations).
 - **Label Binarization**: Step labels ($y_t$) are binarized: $y_t=1$ if the MC score is $>0$ (at least one continuation is successful), and $y_t=0$ otherwise.
 - **Loss Function**: Cross-entropy loss ($L = - \sum_{t=1}^T (y_t \log p_t + (1-y_t) \log(1-p_t))$), where $p_t$ is the probability of predicting "Yes" for step $t$.
 - **Backbone Models**: InternVL2.5-8B (default for preliminary studies) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B for main experiments.
 - **Evaluation**: MPRM performance assessed on VisualProcessBench, a human-annotated step-level benchmark. Metrics reported are per-source macro-F1 and overall micro-F1.
 - **Training Protocol**: Single-pass fine-tuning (each rollout seen once) to study data efficiency.

- **Random Sub-Sampling**:
 - To quantify data redundancy, the training data was randomly subsampled at various fractions ($\rho \in \{5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 25\%, 35\%, 50\%\}$). `Random-ρ` subsets were constructed by retaining $\rho$ fraction of rollouts from each source, preserving their original composition.
 - Performance saturation was observed, with diminishing returns for larger $\rho$, indicating redundancy.
 - A comparison between `Full-Data` and `Random-25%` with matched training steps confirmed a moderate performance gap, reinforcing the redundancy hypothesis.

- **Characterizing Informative Rollouts**:
 - Three $25\%$ subsets were created to investigate the impact of different data characteristics:
 - `Random-25%`: A baseline.
 - `Low-MC-25%`: Rollouts with the lowest $25\%$ average MC scores per step, leading to a higher incorrect-step ratio and lower average MC score. This subset was expected to contain many pseudo-positive (noisy) labels.
 - `Mixed-25%`: Rollouts prioritized for containing both positive and negative steps. If a source had less than $25\%$ mixed rollouts, the remainder was randomly sampled. This aimed for high label mixture while maintaining reasonable average MC scores.
 - Empirical observation: `Mixed-25%` consistently yielded the strongest performance, followed by `Low-MC-25%`, and then `Random-25%`. This highlighted the benefit of exposing the model to more incorrect steps and suggested that a balance between mixture and reliability was crucial, avoiding excessively noisy low-MC steps.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis (Section 3)

- **Teacher-Student Abstraction**: A linear teacher-student framework was formalized, where an ideal teacher model knows true step-level correctness, and a student model learns from noisy MC-annotated labels (modeled as logistic regression).
- **Understanding the Plateau of Random Subsets**:
 - The excess risk for SGD was decomposed into a data complexity term ($C_{data} N_{eff}^{-1/2}$) and an optimization error term ($C_{opt} T^{-1/2}$).
 - The argument was made that MC label noise increases the effective constant $C_{opt}$, making the optimization error dominant. In this noise-limited regime, increasing the effective sample size ($N_{eff}$) yields only marginal gains, explaining the observed performance saturation.
- **Why Mixed but Reliable Rollouts Are Informative**:
 - **Step-level Information**: The theoretical framework showed that the expected per-step learning signal (second moment of the gradient) is proportional to $q^*(\phi)(1-q^*(\phi))||\phi||^2$, where $q^*(\phi)$ is the teacher's probability of correctness. This implies maximum information from steps where the teacher is most uncertain ($q^*(\phi) \approx 1/2$).
 - **Effect of Label Noise**: A symmetric label flip model revealed that label noise ($\eta$) reduces the useful signal term and adds a noise-dominated term. Specifically, low-MC positive steps behave like label-reversed samples, increasing effective noise and harming training.
 - **Rollout Label Mixture as Uncertainty Estimator**: The empirical positive-label fraction's variance, $\hat{p}_x(1-\hat{p}_x)$, was shown to be an approximately unbiased (with $O(1/n)$ bias) estimator of the teacher-level uncertainty, $\bar{q}_x(1-\bar{q}_x)$. Rollouts with mixed labels thus indicate higher teacher uncertainty.
 - **MC Scores as Reliability Indicators**: MC scores ($s_{x,j} = K_{x,j}/N$) estimate the one-shot success probability ($r_{x,j}$). The effective noise level for positive steps (probability of being pseudo-positive) was proven to be strictly decreasing with the number of successful continuations ($K_{x,j}$). Higher MC scores directly imply higher label reliability.
 - **Multiplicative Coupling**: The theoretical analysis concluded that informative rollouts are those where both label mixture (uncertainty) and reliability (low noise in positive labels) are high, as these factors multiply to shape the effective gradient signal.

#### 3.3. Balanced-Information Score (BIS) (Section 4)

- **Definition**: Based on theoretical insights, the Balanced-Information Score (BIS) for a rollout $x$ was proposed:
 $BIS(x) = (p_{pos}(x) \cdot (1 - p_{pos}(x)) + \alpha) \cdot R(x)$
 - `p_pos(x)`: Positive-step ratio, quantifying label mixture within rollout $x$.
 - `R(x)`: Average MC score over all positive steps in rollout $x$. If no positive steps, $R(x)=1$. This quantifies reliability.
 - `α`: A small smoothing constant (set to $0.05$ based on ablation) to ensure low-mixture but reliable rollouts receive a non-zero base weight.
- **Subset Construction**: For a given `keep-ratio ρ`, rollouts within each source dataset are ranked by their BIS in descending order, and the top $\rho$ fraction is selected. This process utilizes only existing MC signals, requiring no additional cost.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the Balanced-Information Score (BIS) method against random subsampling and other heuristic strategies across two MLLM backbones (InternVL2.5-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B) on the VisualProcessBench benchmark.

- **BIS Recovers and Surpasses Full-Data Performance at Small Ratios**:
 - Across both backbones, BIS consistently outperformed random subsampling, with the most significant gains observed in low-budget regimes.
 - For InternVL2.5-8B, a BIS-selected 10% subset (BIS-10%) achieved an overall micro-F1 of 65.46%, effectively matching the full-data performance (65.12%). This represented a +2.6 point improvement over the `Random-10%` baseline, utilizing only one-tenth of the rollouts and training steps, leading to a 95.5% reduction in computational cost.
 - For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, BIS demonstrated even greater advantages in extremely low-budget settings. `BIS-5%` improved over `Random-5%` by +10.9 points, and `BIS-15%` showed a +5.5 point gain. `BIS-25%` for Qwen2.5-VL-7B matched the full-data performance.
 - The performance of BIS-selected subsets generally improved rapidly at small budgets, peaked at a moderate keep ratio, and could slightly decrease thereafter, suggesting that increasing the $\rho$ value beyond an optimal point mainly introduces less informative rollouts.

- **Impact of Labeling Scheme**:
 - Training with continuous soft MC scores as targets was found to be inferior to using binarized hard labels, indicating that soft labels are prone to fitting sampling noise.
 - Increasing the binarization threshold for hard labels (e.g., from `τ=0` to `τ=1/N` or `τ=2/N`) consistently degraded performance. This suggests that low-MC labels represent an ambiguous regime where genuinely hard but correct steps are conflated with noisy pseudo-positives, and stricter thresholding can mislabel hard cases as negatives. BIS implicitly addresses this by down-weighting low-reliability positive steps.

- **Improved Best-of-N Reranking**:
 - In a practical application of MPRMs for best-of-N reranking (N=16 candidates) on four diverse benchmarks (MM-K12, OlympiadBench, MathVerse, MathVista), MPRMs trained on BIS-selected subsets (BIS-25%) consistently achieved the strongest reranking performance. This indicated that BIS yields robust improvements in MPRM effectiveness beyond simple benchmark metrics.

- **Analysis of BIS Component Distributions**:
 - Distributional analysis of the reliability term `R(x)` and mixture term `p_pos(x)(1-p_pos(x))` for all rollouts vs. BIS-selected subsets revealed consistent patterns. BIS strongly suppressed low-reliability rollouts.
 - BIS also favored rollouts with higher label mixture. Notably, coverage often peaked at moderate `R(x)` values (around 0.2–0.6) rather than maximizing `R(x)` towards 1. This suggests that BIS effectively balances both reliability and mixture; very high `R(x)` rollouts might have low mixture, leading to a lower overall BIS score.

- **Ablation Study of BIS Components**:
 - An ablation study with a 25% rollout budget confirmed that both mixture and reliability are crucial for optimal performance:
 - `Mixed-25%` (using only mixture) was competitive but consistently weaker than BIS.
 - `Reliable-25%` (using only reliability) was insufficient, significantly lagging behind BIS on most benchmarks.
 - `Low-MC-25%` also performed worse than BIS.
 - These results corroborate the theoretical finding that the combination of mixture (providing contrast between labels) and reliability (avoiding noisy pseudo-positives) is essential for effective data selection.

- **Robustness to Smoothing Constant (α)**:
 - The performance of BIS was found to be generally stable across different values of the smoothing constant $\alpha$, with $\alpha=0.05$ providing a consistent balance. This suggests that BIS is not overly sensitive to this hyperparameter.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal reasoning and the training of large language models, particularly by addressing the computational efficiency of Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs).

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

1. **Reduced Computational Cost and Energy Consumption**: The most direct impact is the demonstration that MPRMs can achieve equivalent or superior performance with substantially less training data and fewer optimization steps. For instance, BIS-10% matching full-data performance with a 95.5% reduction in computational cost translates directly into substantial savings in GPU time, electricity consumption, and carbon footprint associated with training large models. This is particularly relevant in an era where the scale and energy demands of AI models are a growing concern.

2. **Practical Data Curation Strategy**: The Balanced-Information Score (BIS) provides a practical, model-agnostic, and cost-free method for curating high-quality training subsets from existing MC-annotated corpora. Because it relies solely on readily available MC signals within the dataset, it can be easily integrated into existing MPRM training pipelines without requiring additional annotation effort, model calls, or architectural changes. This makes it a readily applicable "recipe" for researchers and practitioners.

3. **Enhanced Model Quality and Robustness**: Beyond merely matching full-data performance, MPRMs trained with BIS-selected subsets sometimes surpassed the performance of models trained on the entire dataset. This, coupled with improved performance in best-of-N reranking tasks across various benchmarks, suggests that BIS not only makes training more efficient but can also lead to more effective and robust MPRMs by exposing them to higher-quality gradient signals. By filtering out redundant or noisy data, BIS enables the model to learn more efficiently from genuinely informative examples.

4. **Theoretical Grounding for Data Efficiency**: The formal theoretical framework developed in this paper provides a principled explanation for the empirical observations regarding data redundancy and the informativeness of different rollout characteristics. By elucidating how gradient signal, label noise, and data redundancy interact, the theory substantiates the "mixed but reliable" principle underlying BIS. This analytical insight advances the fundamental understanding of data efficiency in process reward modeling.

5. **Guidance for Future Corpus Design**: The findings offer valuable guidance for the design and annotation of future MPRM corpora. Rather than simply collecting vast quantities of data, the research emphasizes the importance of data quality, specifically focusing on a balanced mixture of positive and negative steps with high label reliability. This data-centric principle can inform more efficient and effective data collection strategies, moving beyond mere volume towards informative density.

In conclusion, this work offers both a theoretically grounded explanation and a practical, efficient solution to a critical bottleneck in MPRM training. Its impact is poised to be felt across the development and deployment of advanced multimodal reasoning systems, promoting more sustainable and effective AI research.
