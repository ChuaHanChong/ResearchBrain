# Training Data Efficiency in Multimodal Process Reward Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Balanced-Information Score (BIS) was introduced to improve training data efficiency for Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs). This method allows models to achieve full-data performance using significantly smaller subsets of existing Monte Carlo-annotated corpora, such as matching full-data performance with 95.5% less computational cost using just 10% of the data.

## Problem

- Training Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs) necessitates large-scale Monte Carlo (MC)-annotated datasets, incurring high computational costs and energy consumption.
- The extensive data requirements create bottlenecks in the practical deployment and scaling of MLLMs for complex visual reasoning tasks.
- Existing data efficiency approaches for reward models often involve costly re-annotation, additional model calls, or architectural changes, adding complexity.

## Method

- Empirical and theoretical analysis identified significant data redundancy and the specific characteristics of informative rollouts (mixed but reliable labels) within current datasets.
- A theoretical framework was developed to explain observed performance saturation and the impact of label noise on the effective gradient signal during training.
- The ==Balanced-Information Score (BIS)== was proposed, which filters existing Monte Carlo-annotated training data based on ==rollout label mixture and reliability== to select informative subsets without additional cost.

## Results

- BIS-selected **10%** of the training data enabled an InternVL2.5-8B MPRM to match full-data performance, achieving a **95.5%** reduction in computational cost.
- For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, BIS-**5%** improved over random selection by **+10.9** points, with BIS-**25%** matching full-data performance while significantly reducing training data volume.
- MPRMs trained on BIS-selected data consistently achieved superior best-of-N reranking performance across four diverse multimodal benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced model effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Monte Carlo-annotated datasets exhibit significant redundancy, as MPRM performance quickly plateaus even with random subsampling, indicating a noise-limited training regime.
> - The most informative training data consists of rollouts with a high mixture of positive and negative steps, combined with reliable positive labels (high average MC scores).
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrated that label mixture (uncertainty) and reliability (low noise) multiplicatively influence the effective gradient signal, making their balanced combination crucial for data selection.
