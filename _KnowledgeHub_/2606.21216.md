# A scalar per patch from pre-trained ViTs enables fast moving navigation in the real world

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NaverLabs Europe evaluated various pre-trained Vision Transformers (ViTs) and projection strategies for enabling fast-moving robotic navigation in real-world indoor environments using only RGB input. They demonstrated that highly bottlenecked visual information, such as one scalar per image patch, can lead to effective navigation and the emergence of interpretable spatial "affordance maps."

## Problem

- Integrating pre-trained computer vision components effectively into real-world robotic navigation systems for fast-moving agents using only RGB input.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap to ensure policies trained in simulation perform robustly on physical robots.
- Identifying the minimal yet sufficient visual information from high-dimensional encoder outputs required for reliable and efficient navigation.

## Method

- Trained a recurrent neural network-based agent using ==Reinforcement Learning== (PPO) in an augmented Habitat simulator, incorporating a second-order dynamical motion model identified from real robot trajectories.
- Evaluated a diverse set of frozen, pre-trained Vision Transformers (e.g., DinoV2, VC1, AM-Radio, Dune) as visual encoders, comparing generalist, domain-specific, and multi-teacher distilled models.
- Developed and tested novel projection mechanisms, including Pure Attention Projection (PAP), to bottleneck the ViT output features into spatially structured and interpretable representations for the downstream agent.

## Results

- The combination of robust pre-trained vision encoders and realistic motion modeling during simulation training effectively minimized the sim-to-real gap for fast-moving, RGB-only agents.
- Distilled vision encoders (e.g., Dune, AM-Radio) and those pre-trained on egocentric robotics data (VC-1) generally outperformed generalist ViTs (DinoV2/V3) in real-world navigation metrics, with AM-Radio achieving **100%** success rate in real tests.
- Policies initialized with privileged 3D information and then fine-tuned on RGB showed improved performance across most vision encoders, indicating effective transfer of spatial reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning visual perception for robotic navigation from scratch with only downstream RL losses leads to poor performance and difficult real-world transfer, necessitating pre-trained visual encoders.
> - Interpretable spatial "affordance maps" can emerge from spatially preserving projection mechanisms (e.g., PAP) from pre-trained ViTs, without explicit supervision for affordances, and are robust to the sim-to-real gap.
> - Robust real-world navigation can be achieved even when visual information is severely bottlenecked, with as little as one scalar per image patch, demonstrating potential for computational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the intersection of computer vision (CV) and robotics, specifically focusing on how visual perception components contribute to real-world robotic navigation. Historically, computer vision and robotics have been treated as distinct disciplines: computer vision focused on understanding visual information (e.g., 3D reconstruction, semantic segmentation, object detection), while robotics focused on mapping these perceptions to motor commands and control.

Recent discussions within the field, often referred to as the "Bitter Lesson" of computer vision, suggest a shift towards end-to-end perception-action loops where the utility of visual representations is primarily measured by their contribution to intelligent action rather than as independent intermediate mappings. While acknowledging this long-term trend, the authors highlight that current robotics, even with the emergence of generalist agents and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, heavily relies on pre-trained vision encoders. These encoders are typically not trained from scratch on robotics-specific downstream losses but instead leverage pre-training with auxiliary losses on large-scale computer vision datasets. The reliance on pre-trained encoders is attributed to the inherent sparsity and difficulty of acquiring large volumes of robotics data.

Within this context, the paper positions itself as a large-scale empirical study that systematically evaluates state-of-the-art visual encoders under realistic conditions for real-world robotic navigation. It specifically scrutinizes several categories of visual encoders, including those trained with masked image modeling (e.g., VC1 based on MAE), self-distilled and iBOT-based models (e.g., DinoV2, DinoV3), and those derived from multi-teacher distillation (e.g., AM-Radio, Dune). These distilled models, which aggregate capabilities from diverse sources like 3D reconstruction, human mesh recovery, and general vision features, represent an advanced direction in creating versatile visual representations.

The research differentiates itself from prior work by conducting extensive real-world validation, in contrast to studies that primarily rely on simulation or handcrafted features. It also investigates novel methods for bottlenecking and interpreting visual features, aiming to identify the minimal yet effective information required for robust navigation. By focusing on fast-moving agents using only RGB input and realistic motion dynamics, the study addresses practical challenges in deploying autonomous robots, offering insights into bridging the sim-to-real gap that is crucial for practical robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a detailed, large-scale analysis of how various visual pre-training strategies influence the navigational performance and reliability of fast-moving robots operating in complex, real-world environments. The study specifically investigates the role of visual perception in navigation from several critical angles, motivated by the current reliance of robotics on pre-trained computer vision components and the practical challenges of deploying agents in physical spaces.

The key motivations and specific questions addressed are:

1. **Learning Perception from Actions Alone:** The authors aim to determine if visual perception for robotics can be effectively learned solely through downstream robotic actions. They hypothesize that direct training of visual encoders from scratch using only reinforcement learning (RL) signals leads to poor performance and difficult transfer to real robots, primarily due to overfitting to visual artifacts and spurious correlations. Conversely, they propose that pre-trained vision encoders, when combined with realistic motion modeling, are instrumental in minimizing the sim-to-real gap.

2. **Suitability of Pre-training Strategies:** The research seeks to identify which pre-training strategies for visual encoders are most effective for downstream navigation tasks. This involves comparing approaches such as Masked Autoencoders (MAE, exemplified by VC1) and advanced self-distillation and iBOT-based methods (e.g., DinoV2 and DinoV3), to ascertain their respective benefits in real-world scenarios.

3. **Benefits of Heterogeneous Multi-teacher Distillation:** A significant motivation is to explore whether robotic/navigation tasks can benefit from visual encoders that possess complementary and diverse capabilities, acquired through distillation from multiple high-level tasks. The study evaluates encoders like AM-Radio and Dune, which are distilled from heterogeneous sources including 3D reconstruction (MASt3R), human mesh recovery (MHMR), and general vision features (DinoV2). The underlying motivation is to assess if a more versatile, aggregated feature set can enhance navigation.

4. **Information Necessity and Interpretability:** The authors investigate the minimum amount of information from visual encoders that is necessary for robust navigation. This involves bottlenecking the high-dimensional encoder outputs in a structured and "spatially useful" manner. A core motivation here is to determine if highly compressed representations can maintain performance, and whether this bottlenecking can lead to the emergence of interpretable features, such as "affordances," without explicit supervision.

5. **Impact of Privileged Sensor Information during Training:** The study examines whether policies pre-trained with privileged information (e.g., Lidar-like input providing 360° 3D scene structure) can yield performance gains when subsequently fine-tuned and deployed with only forward-facing RGB input. This aims to understand the transferability of spatial reasoning learned from richer modalities to a more constrained visual-only input.

In summary, the research is motivated by a desire to provide comprehensive, empirically-backed insights into the optimal integration of computer vision components into real-world robotic navigation systems, particularly for fast-moving agents operating with visual-only input.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic experimental methodology involving a recurrent neural network-based agent, diverse visual encoders, novel projection mechanisms, and evaluation in both augmented simulation and real-world environments. The goal is to isolate and analyze the impact of visual perception on robotic navigation.

**Agent Architecture and Task:**
The study builds upon an existing agent architecture designed for real-world, fast-moving robots, which has been optimized for minimizing the sim-to-real gap. The chosen task is static point-goal navigation in real indoor environments, where the agent's objective is to reach a target defined by constant polar coordinates relative to its starting pose. The agent operates without an external map, relying on onboard ego-motion estimation from wheel encoders and IMUs for localization.
The agent's decision-making loop operates at 3 Hz, processing sensor readings (primarily an RGB image $I_t$) and outputting actions from a discretized space of 28 linear and angular velocity pairs. Crucially, the agent in this study relies solely on RGB input, without access to Lidar or depth sensors.
The agent's internal state is managed by a two-layer Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU). Visual input is processed by a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) $v$, extracting features $F_t$. This $F_t$, along with the previous hidden state $h_{t-1}$, the fixed goal $g_0$, odometry $\hat{p}_t$, and previous action $a_{t-1}$, is fed into the GRU $d$ to update the recurrent hidden state $h_t$. A linear head $\pi$ then derives the current action $a_t$. An auxiliary linear head $l$ predicts the instantaneous relative goal $\hat{g}_t$, supervised by ground truth during simulation training to aid spatial reasoning.

**Training Regimen:**
The agent is trained using Reinforcement Learning, specifically the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. The reward function incentivizes success and efficiency while penalizing geodesic distance to the goal, slack time, and collisions.
A critical aspect of the training methodology is the use of a Habitat simulator augmented with a second-order dynamical motion model. This model, identified from real-world robot trajectories, accurately reflects authentic inertial effects, allowing the simulated robot to accelerate, brake, and turn like its physical counterpart. This realistic motion modeling is highlighted as essential for training fast-moving agents that require precise trajectory anticipation.

**Experimental Protocol and Metrics:**
Models are trained on the HM3D training set, comprising 800 3D-scanned scenes. Evaluation is conducted in two domains:
1. **Physical Deployment ("Real"):** Tests are performed on a "Rookie" robot in a designated office environment. Results are averaged over 3 runs, each consisting of 14 episodes, totaling 42 real navigation episodes per configuration to account for environmental variability. Safety mechanisms (e.g., Lidar-based obstacle detection) are integrated into the real robot but not directly provided as input to the trained agent, though their effects are simulated during training.
2. **Augmented Simulation ("Sim"):** Evaluations are carried out on the HM3D validation set (2,500 episodes) within the simulator equipped with the identified dynamics model, providing results predictive of real-world behavior.
Performance is quantified using standard navigation metrics: Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), and Success weighted by Completion Time (SCT). Success thresholds differ between simulation (0.2 m from goal) and real-world (1.0 m, accounting for noisy onboard sensors and AMCL).

**Lineup of Vision Models:**
All visual encoders, except for a baseline ResNet trained from scratch, are pre-trained ViT-Base architectures frozen during RL training. These include:
- **DinoV2 & DinoV3:** Self-distillation and iBOT-based models, trained on vast curated datasets.
- **VC1:** MAE-trained model specifically targeting robotics with egocentric video data.
- **AM-RADIO v2.5 & DUNE:** Distilled models combining features from heterogeneous sources (e.g., CLIP, DinoV2, SAM, MASt3R, MHMR) to achieve versatile capabilities.
- **ResNet from scratch:** A non-pretrained ResNet-18 as a baseline, trained entirely with the downstream RL loss.

**Lineup of Projection Heads:**
A trainable projection layer is inserted between the frozen ViT output ($F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times E}$, where P is number of patches, E is embedding dimension) and the agent's recurrent memory. Several projection mechanisms are explored:
- **Cross-attention pooling (CAP):** A standard method where $K$ learned query embeddings cross-attend into the ViT feature tokens using multi-head attention.
- **Pure Attention Projection (PAP):** A novel variant of CAP that discards value projections. The attention distributions themselves (representing $K$ interpretable heatmaps) constitute the entire visual information passed to the downstream agent.
- **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):** Projects each patch feature onto $C$ principal components, learned offline, with no learnable parameters in the projection beyond a final linear layer.
- **Linear Patchwise Reduction (LPR):** Projects each patch feature vector $F_{t,p}$ independently down to a low-dimensional vector of size $D$ using a weight matrix $W$.

**Privileged Information Pre-training:**
An additional approach involves pre-training an agent using a 2D 360° Lidar-like scan as input for an extended period, which directly provides 3D scene structure. Subsequently, this agent is fine-tuned for a shorter duration using only RGB input and the chosen vision encoders, to assess the benefits of transferring spatial reasoning from a privileged modality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings concerning the efficacy and characteristics of visual encoders and projection strategies for real-world robotic navigation:

1. **Failure of Learning Perception from Scratch:** Policies trained with a visual encoder (ResNet-18) initialized from scratch and learned solely through the downstream RL loss exhibited poor performance, with a Success Rate (SR) of 53.5%. This supports the hypothesis that the weak learning signal from RL combined with the complexity of training with realistic motion dynamics makes it challenging to learn robust visual representations from scratch for real-world transfer.

2. **Closure of the Sim2Real Gap with Pre-trained Encoders:** The use of pre-trained Vision Transformers (ViTs) with a simple Cross-attention pooling (CAP) mechanism demonstrated high and comparable performance between augmented simulation and real-world environments. This suggests that the combination of robust pre-trained vision encoders and realistic motion modeling during simulation training has significantly minimized the sim-to-real gap, enabling effective policy training purely in simulation for fast-moving agents.

3. **Performance of Vision Encoders:**
 - **Distilled Models Excel:** Encoders distilled from heterogeneous teachers, such as Dune (distilled from DinoV2, MASt3R, MHMR) and AM-Radio (distilled from CLIP, DinoV2, SAM), generally outperformed generalist ViTs like DinoV2 and DinoV3, particularly in real-world experiments. This indicates that integrating diverse, complementary capabilities into a single encoder can be beneficial for complex navigation tasks. AM-Radio, for instance, achieved 100% SR in real-world tests when trained from scratch.
 - **VC-1's Strong Performance:** VC-1, pre-trained with MAE on a dataset specifically including egocentric robotics videos, also showed strong performance, comparable to the distilled models. This suggests that domain-specific pre-training data can significantly contribute to effective visual features for robotics.
 - **DinoV2/DinoV3 Underperformance (Initial):** Initially, DinoV2 and especially DinoV3, despite their general computer vision capabilities, showed lower performance compared to VC-1, Dune, and AM-Radio when trained from scratch with CAP.

4. **Benefits of Privileged Information Pre-training:** Agents initialized from a model pre-trained with a Lidar-like 360° 3D input, and then fine-tuned on RGB input, showed a general boost in navigation metrics across most vision encoders. This indicates that spatial reasoning learned from privileged 3D information can transfer effectively to vision-only agents, even when the visual encoders themselves remain frozen, suggesting a more robust initialization for the recurrent policy. This approach also narrowed the performance differences between various visual encoders.

5. **Emergence of Interpretable Spatial Maps via Pure Attention Projection (PAP):** The introduction of the Pure Attention Projection (PAP) layer demonstrated that interpretable spatial maps, akin to "affordance maps," can emerge. These maps, which represent the only visual information passed to the downstream agent (1 scalar per image patch), focus attention on navigable areas or potential movement directions. This phenomenon was observed consistently across different pre-trained ViTs without explicit supervision for affordances. The generated attention maps were also robust to the sim-to-real gap, showing similar distributions for corresponding regions in simulated and real images.

6. **Efficiency of Bottlenecked Visual Information:** Experiments with different projection mechanisms (CAP, PAP, PCA, LPR) revealed that excellent navigation performance can be achieved even when visual information is severely bottlenecked.
 - PAP and Linear Patchwise Reduction (LPR), which preserve spatial information, performed comparably to or better than CAP.
 - Principal Component Analysis (PCA) consistently underperformed.
 - A sensitivity analysis on the number of read-out tokens for CAP and PAP showed that as few as **one scalar per image patch** (e.g., K=1 for PAP) can enable effective navigation in real-world scenarios. While simulation performance sometimes benefited from more tokens, this did not always translate to improved real-world performance, suggesting that bottlenecking can positively impact sim-to-real transfer.

7. **Special Case of DinoV3:** DinoV3 exhibited extremely poor performance with standard Cross-attention pooling (CAP) and even Self-Attention Pooling (SAP), failing to navigate effectively. However, it achieved reasonable performance when combined with the less complex and spatially preserving projectors, PAP and LPR. This finding suggests that for certain complex vision encoders like DinoV3, the choice of projection mechanism and its expressivity can be critical, with simpler, spatially-aware methods proving more effective when paired with a weak RL learning signal. This aligns with other studies suggesting DinoV3 relies on global, low-frequency structures rather than fine-grained details.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides substantial contributions to the fields of embodied AI and robotic navigation, with several potential impacts:

1. **Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The study empirically demonstrates that the combination of powerful pre-trained vision encoders and realistic motion dynamics in simulation can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap for fast-moving agents operating with RGB-only input. This is highly significant as it suggests that extensive, costly, and time-consuming real-world data collection for training robotic policies might be reduced, allowing for more rapid development and deployment of autonomous systems.

2. **Guidance for Vision Encoder Selection:** The comparative analysis of various state-of-the-art vision encoders (DinoV2/V3, VC1, AM-Radio, Dune) offers practical guidance for researchers and practitioners in robotics. The finding that distilled models from heterogeneous teachers and domain-specific pre-training (VC1) can outperform generalist models underscores the importance of tailored or multi-task representations for specific robotic applications.

3. **Enhanced Interpretability and Trust:** The introduction of Pure Attention Projection (PAP), which creates interpretable "affordance maps" directly from visual encoders without explicit supervision, represents a significant step towards more transparent embodied AI. By making the agent's perception of "what to do where" explicit and visual, this method can increase human understanding, trust, and facilitate debugging of autonomous systems, moving beyond black-box decision-making.

4. **Computational Efficiency for Edge Devices:** The demonstration that robust navigation can be achieved with highly bottlenecked visual information (as little as one scalar per image patch) has major implications for deploying intelligent agents on resource-constrained platforms, such as small robots or drones. This allows for more computationally efficient perception pipelines, conserving processing power and battery life without sacrificing performance.

5. **Reconsideration of Encoder-Projector Design:** The unexpected challenges with DinoV3, and its success only with simpler, spatially-preserving projection layers (PAP, LPR), highlights that a more complex or general visual encoder is not always universally superior for all downstream tasks. It underscores the importance of matching the expressivity and spatial properties of the projection mechanism to both the encoder features and the learning signal of the downstream task, particularly in reinforcement learning settings where rewards can be sparse. This insight may prompt further research into designing more specialized or adaptive projection interfaces.

6. **Advancement of Foundation Models in Robotics:** The work reinforces the critical role of large-scale pre-training and distillation in computer vision for enabling advanced robotic capabilities. It contributes to the ongoing discussion about how to best leverage foundation models, originally developed for general vision tasks, to build increasingly capable and generalist robotic agents, influencing future research directions in VLA and embodied AI models.
