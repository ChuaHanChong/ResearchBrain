# A scalar per patch from pre-trained ViTs enables fast moving navigation in the real world

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NaverLabs Europe evaluated various pre-trained Vision Transformers (ViTs) and projection strategies for enabling fast-moving robotic navigation in real-world indoor environments using only RGB input. They demonstrated that highly bottlenecked visual information, such as one scalar per image patch, can lead to effective navigation and the emergence of interpretable spatial "affordance maps."

## Problem

- Integrating pre-trained computer vision components effectively into real-world robotic navigation systems for fast-moving agents using only RGB input.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap to ensure policies trained in simulation perform robustly on physical robots.
- Identifying the minimal yet sufficient visual information from high-dimensional encoder outputs required for reliable and efficient navigation.

## Method

- Trained a recurrent neural network-based agent using ==Reinforcement Learning== (PPO) in an augmented Habitat simulator, incorporating a second-order dynamical motion model identified from real robot trajectories.
- Evaluated a diverse set of frozen, pre-trained Vision Transformers (e.g., DinoV2, VC1, AM-Radio, Dune) as visual encoders, comparing generalist, domain-specific, and multi-teacher distilled models.
- Developed and tested novel projection mechanisms, including Pure Attention Projection (PAP), to bottleneck the ViT output features into spatially structured and interpretable representations for the downstream agent.

## Results

- The combination of robust pre-trained vision encoders and realistic motion modeling during simulation training effectively minimized the sim-to-real gap for fast-moving, RGB-only agents.
- Distilled vision encoders (e.g., Dune, AM-Radio) and those pre-trained on egocentric robotics data (VC-1) generally outperformed generalist ViTs (DinoV2/V3) in real-world navigation metrics, with AM-Radio achieving **100%** success rate in real tests.
- Policies initialized with privileged 3D information and then fine-tuned on RGB showed improved performance across most vision encoders, indicating effective transfer of spatial reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning visual perception for robotic navigation from scratch with only downstream RL losses leads to poor performance and difficult real-world transfer, necessitating pre-trained visual encoders.
> - Interpretable spatial "affordance maps" can emerge from spatially preserving projection mechanisms (e.g., PAP) from pre-trained ViTs, without explicit supervision for affordances, and are robust to the sim-to-real gap.
> - Robust real-world navigation can be achieved even when visual information is severely bottlenecked, with as little as one scalar per image patch, demonstrating potential for computational efficiency.
