# EXPO: Stable Reinforcement Learning with Expressive Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A new online reinforcement learning framework, EXPO, enables stable fine-tuning of expressive policies like diffusion models by combining an imitation-learned base policy with a separate, value-optimizing edit policy for on-the-fly action selection. The method achieves superior sample efficiency and robust performance in both leveraging offline data and fine-tuning pre-trained policies across challenging sparse-reward tasks.

## Problem

- Expressive policies, useful for complex behaviors, are difficult to stably optimize with online Reinforcement Learning (RL) due to their extended denoising chains and complex parameterization.
- Traditional online RL methods are designed for simpler Gaussian policies, leading to unstable gradient propagation and hindered fine-tuning when applied to expressive policies.
- Bridging the gap between pre-trained expressive policies and online RL for continuous self-improvement often results in performance drops or insufficient leveraging of prior offline data.

## Method

- **EXPO** introduces an '==on-the-fly==' (OTF) RL policy for value maximization by combining a stable, imitation-learned base expressive policy with a light-weight Gaussian edit policy.
- The base expressive policy is never explicitly trained against Q-function gradients; instead, the edit policy locally refines actions from the base policy, trained with an entropy-regularized policy loss and an edit distance constraint.
- For both action sampling and TD backup, the OTF policy selects the highest Q-value action from a set of candidates generated by both the base and edited policies.

## Results

- EXPO significantly outperformed comparison methods in sample efficiency across **12** sparse-reward continuous control tasks, both when leveraging offline data from scratch and when fine-tuning pre-trained policies.
- The method demonstrated robust performance during the offline-to-online fine-tuning transition, successfully avoiding the common performance drops observed in many other offline-to-online RL algorithms.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of both the on-the-fly policy extraction in the TD backup and the action editing mechanism, with an optional entropy backup further compensating for poor offline data quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the expressive base policy's training from direct Q-value optimization ensures stability while still leveraging its generative power for action distributions.
> - A small, locally-optimizing edit policy provides the necessary mechanism for Q-value maximization and controlled exploration without destabilizing the complex underlying expressive policy.
> - Constructing an 'on-the-fly' policy for value-maximizing action selection in the TD backup provides a stable and sample-efficient way to integrate expressive policies into online RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robotics have demonstrated the utility of expressive policies, such as diffusion and flow-matching models, trained on large datasets through imitation learning for various real-world tasks. While imitation learning has shown promising results in generating complex behaviors, it often falls short in achieving the high reliability and optimal performance required for practical applications. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a mechanism for online self-improvement that can address these limitations.

However, integrating expressive policy classes with online RL presents distinct challenges. Traditional online RL methods, exemplified by algorithms like PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018), are predominantly designed for simpler Gaussian policies. Expressive policies, characterized by their parameterization through extended denoising chains, complicate stable gradient propagation from actions to policy parameters when optimizing against a value function. This instability hinders their effective fine-tuning in online RL settings (Ding & Jin, 2024; Park et al., 2025). Previous work in purely offline or purely online contexts has attempted to mitigate this by incorporating losses at intermediate denoising steps to guide the process towards high-value actions (Psenka et al., 2023; Fang et al., 2024), but a robust solution for stable value maximization in efficient online fine-tuning remains an area of active research.

This paper contributes to the area of bridging the gap between expressive policies and online reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of leveraging prior offline data. Existing research on using prior data to accelerate online learning often involves initializing replay buffers (Vecerik et al., 2018; Nair et al., 2018; Hansen et al., 2022; Ball et al., 2023), pretraining value functions or policies with offline RL techniques (Hester et al., 2017; Lee et al., 2021; Nair et al., 2021; Song et al., 2023; Nakamoto et al., 2024), or maintaining separate policies for offline and online phases (Yang et al., 2023a; Zhang ets al., 2023; Mark et al., 2023). However, these methods typically employ simpler policy classes.

Within the domain of expressive policies for RL, approaches include weighted behavior cloning to align with Q-values (Lu et al., 2023; Kang et al., 2023; Ding et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025), or training a residual policy online after pretraining an expressive policy offline (Yuan et al., 2024; Ankile et al., 2024). Some methods directly backpropagate Q-value gradients into diffusion policies, which can be computationally intensive and unstable (Wang et al., 2022), leading to distillation into simpler policies (Ding & Jin, 2024; Park et al., 2025) or action gradient-based guidance at intermediate steps (Psenka et al., 2023; Fang et al., 2024). Sampling-based Q-value maximization has also been explored, often for exploration (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023; He et al., 2024) rather than for temporal-difference (TD) backups or with less expressive models (Ghasemipour et al., 2021).

EXPO distinguishes itself by focusing on the online fine-tuning of the expressive policy itself, rather than distilling it or learning a separate residual policy. It addresses the stability issue by avoiding direct Q-value optimization of the expressive policy, instead employing a separate, light-weight edit policy and an on-the-fly selection mechanism. This approach aims to provide a stable and sample-efficient method for leveraging the capabilities of expressive policies within an online RL framework, particularly when initial offline datasets are available.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the capabilities of expressive policies in generating complex behaviors via imitation learning and the limitations of current online reinforcement learning methods in stably optimizing these policies for improved performance. While expressive policies (e.g., diffusion, flow-matching) have shown promise in robotics tasks when trained with large datasets through imitation, pure imitation learning often lacks the precision and robustness needed for real-world deployment. Fine-tuning these policies with online RL is a logical step for self-improvement and achieving higher performance and reliability.

However, the primary challenge lies in the inherent structural complexity of expressive policies. Unlike simpler Gaussian policies, expressive policies often involve long chains of denoising steps. This complex parameterization makes it difficult to propagate stable gradients from the action output back to the policy parameters when attempting to directly optimize them against a value function. Such instability can hinder the learning process, leading to suboptimal or unstable policy updates during online fine-tuning.

Therefore, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To develop a stable online reinforcement learning algorithm for training and fine-tuning expressive policies.** This addresses the core problem of unstable gradient propagation inherent in directly optimizing expressive policies with value functions.
2. **To enable efficient online fine-tuning of pre-trained expressive policies given an offline dataset.** The goal is to effectively leverage the rich behavioral information present in offline datasets and the learned capabilities of pre-trained expressive policies to accelerate online learning.
3. **To achieve sample efficiency in both the online RL setting (training with offline data from scratch) and the offline-to-online RL setting (fine-tuning a pre-trained policy).** This aims to reduce the amount of interaction needed with the environment for policy improvement.
4. **To propose a method that is agnostic to the specific parameterization of the expressive policy.** This objective ensures broader applicability, allowing the method to fine-tune various types of pre-trained expressive policies (e.g., diffusion, flow-matching).

The motivation is to overcome the technical barriers that prevent the full integration of high-capacity expressive policies with the performance-enhancing capabilities of online RL, thereby enabling robots and other agents to achieve greater reliability and performance in complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, EXpressive Policy Optimization (EXPO), is a sample-efficient online RL algorithm designed for stable fine-tuning of expressive policies, particularly when an offline dataset is available. The core insight of EXPO is to avoid direct value optimization of the expressive policy, which often leads to instability due to its long denoising chains. Instead, EXPO constructs an "on-the-fly" (OTF) RL policy for value maximization using two distinct parameterized policies: a base expressive policy and a light-weight Gaussian edit policy.

#### 3.1. Q-Value Maximization and Exploration Through Action Edits

1. **Base Expressive Policy (π_base)**: This policy is typically initialized from offline pre-training using an imitation learning objective. For instance, in the experiments, a diffusion policy trained with the DDPM objective is used. Crucially, the base policy is *never explicitly trained to maximize value* through Q-function gradients. Its training focuses on generating actions consistent with a stable supervised learning objective.

2. **Gaussian Edit Policy (π_edit)**: To enable value maximization and exploration, EXPO introduces a small, light-weight Gaussian edit policy, `π_edit(ˆa|s, a)`. This policy refines actions sampled from the base policy (`a ∼ π_base(·|s)`) by adding an edit term: `˜a ← a + ˆa`.
 - **Training Objective**: The edit policy is trained with a standard entropy-regularized policy loss: `L(π_edit) = −E[(Q_ϕ(s, a + ˆa) − α log π_edit(ˆa|s, a))]`, where `Q_ϕ(s, a)` is the critic value function. This objective encourages the edit policy to locally optimize the Q-function and promotes action diversity through entropy maximization.
 - **Edit Distance Constraint**: To prevent the edit policy from deviating excessively from the base policy's behavior distribution, the action edits `ˆa` are scaled within a range `[−β, β]`, controlled by a hyperparameter `β`. This localizes the optimization problem for `π_edit`, ensuring stability and efficient learning. The edits can be interpreted as refining actions within existing modes of the base policy's distribution.

#### 3.2. On-the-Fly Parameterization of the RL Policy

EXPO utilizes an on-the-fly (OTF) policy, `π_OTF`, to implicitly perform value maximization. This policy is not directly parameterized but is constructed in two steps for both action sampling during environment interaction and for computing the TD backup target:

1. **Action Candidate Generation**: For a given state `s`, `N` actions `a_i` are sampled from the base policy `π_base(·|s)`. Each `a_i` is then refined by the edit policy to produce `˜a_i = a_i + ˆa_i`, where `ˆa_i ∼ π_edit(·|s, a_i)`. This generates a set of `N` base actions and `N` edited actions.
2. **Value-Maximizing Action Selection**: From the combined set of `2N` action candidates (`{a_1..., a_N, ˜a_1..., ˜a_N}`), the action `a*` with the highest Q-value is selected: `a* = argmax_a Q_ϕ(s, a)`. This `a*` is the action executed in the environment and used in the TD target.

- **Q-function Objective**: The Q-function (`Q_ϕ`) is updated using a standard Q-learning loss:
 `min_ϕ E_{(s_t,a_t,s_{t+1})∼D} [(r_t + γQ_ϕ′(s_{t+1}, ˜a*_{t+1}) − Q_ϕ(s_t, a_t))^2]`, where `˜a*_{t+1}` is the value-maximizing action chosen by `π_OTF(·|s_{t+1})` using the target Q-network `Q_ϕ′`. This formulation ensures that the Q-function learns from actions that are implicitly chosen to maximize value.

#### 3.3. Entropy Backup for Data-Limited Regimes (Optional)

For scenarios with limited or narrow offline datasets, EXPO offers an optional entropy-regularized variant to encourage broader exploration.
- **Soft Sampling Distribution (π_sampling)**: Instead of a hard `argmax` selection, this variant uses a soft sampling distribution. After generating `2N` action candidates (N from base, N edited), the probability of selecting an action `a_k` is `π_sampling(a_k|s) = exp(β_soft Q(s,a_k)) / Σ_j exp(β_soft Q(s,a_j))`.
- **Entropy-Regularized TD Target**: This soft sampling allows for a closed-form entropy term `log π_sampling(˜a*_{t+1}|s_{t+1})` to be included in the TD target:
 `y = r_t + γ[Q_ϕ′(s_{t+1}, ˜a*_{t+1}) − α log π_OTF(˜a*_{t+1}|s_{t+1})]`. This incentivizes exploration beyond the immediate highest-Q action.

#### 3.4. Practical Implementations

- The base expressive policy is instantiated as a diffusion model trained via the DDPM objective.
- The edit policy is a simple Gaussian policy, and its training incorporates entropy regularization similar to SAC.
- Hyperparameters such as the number of action samples `N` for the OTF policy and the edit scale `β` are tunable to specific tasks.

The overall EXPO algorithm iteratively collects data using the OTF policy, updates the Q-function, and then updates both the base policy (via imitation learning) and the edit policy (via entropy-regularized Q-value maximization). This decoupling allows for stable online fine-tuning by leveraging the expressive power of the base policy and the local Q-maximization capabilities of the edit policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on 12 challenging sparse-reward continuous control tasks across four domains: Antmaze, Adroit, Robomimic, and MimicGen. The evaluation addressed three primary research questions.

**Q1: Can EXPO effectively leverage offline data for online sample-efficient RL?**
The results indicate that EXPO significantly surpasses comparison methods in terms of sample efficiency across most tasks when leveraging offline data in an online setting, even without explicit pre-training.
- **Comparison with RLPD**: EXPO consistently demonstrates improved sample efficiency compared to RLPD, a method known for fast learning with prior data, with one exception (`relocate-binary-v0`) where the dataset was particularly narrow. EXPO's ability to quickly leverage imitation learning signals from the offline data through its base policy, followed by refinement via the edit policy, contributes to this efficiency.
- **Comparison with IDQL, DIPO, QSM**: These methods, which also utilize expressive policy classes (e.g., diffusion), often struggled to learn effectively. IDQL's policy-constrained objective and limited exploration capabilities, and QSM's training instabilities, presented challenges. EXPO's stable value maximization mechanism enabled effective utilization of expressive policies, leading to stronger performance.

**Q2: How sample efficient is EXPO in fine-tuning pretrained policies compared to prior methods?**
In the offline-to-online fine-tuning setting, EXPO achieved superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. A notable finding is that EXPO generally avoided a significant performance drop during the transition from offline pre-training to online fine-tuning, a common issue for many traditional offline-to-online RL methods (e.g., DAC, Cal-QL on harder tasks). This stability is attributed to EXPO's design, which keeps the base expressive policy's actions close to the behavior distribution, while the edit policy refines these actions locally.
- **Comparison with IDQL (with pre-training)**: IDQL often showed limited online improvement after pre-training, likely due to excessive constraints on the policy and insufficient exploration.
- **Comparison with Cal-QL**: While Cal-QL performed strongly on simpler Antmaze tasks, its sample efficiency was lower on more complex tasks, indicating less effective leveraging of offline data for continuous policy improvement.
- **Comparison with DAC**: DAC achieved strong pre-training performance but exhibited rapid performance collapse during online fine-tuning, rendering it unsuitable for this setting.
- **Comparison on manipulation tasks**: On Robomimic and MimicGen tasks, many baselines experienced performance degradation when transitioning from pre-training to fine-tuning. In contrast, EXPO consistently demonstrated significant improvement on these tasks with high sample efficiency, by maintaining actions close to the learned behavior distribution while stably refining them.

**Q3: What components of EXPO are most important for performance? (Ablation Studies)**
Ablation studies investigated the contributions of three key components:

1. **On-the-fly policy extraction in the TD backup**: Ablations demonstrated that using value-maximizing actions chosen by the on-the-fly policy in the TD backup is crucial for high performance and sample efficiency. Without this, the policy action used in the TD backup does not inherently maximize the Q-function, resulting in a slower, SARSA-like learning objective.
2. **Action edits**: Comparisons showed that action edits are essential for achieving robust performance. For tasks requiring significant exploration (e.g., `pen-binary-v0`), removing action edits led to suboptimal convergence, as the imitation-trained base policy lacked effective exploration mechanisms. Even for tasks where the offline dataset provides a good initial behavior policy (e.g., `Square`), action edits were vital for continuous refinement and improvement.
3. **Offline dataset quality/size and entropy backup**: A correlation was observed between the quality of the offline dataset (measured by the performance of an imitation learning policy trained on it) and EXPO's fine-tuning performance *without entropy backup*. This indicates that EXPO relies on a sufficient prior for effective learning. However, the introduction of the *entropy backup* (using a soft sampling distribution) demonstrated the ability to compensate for poor offline data. Even with an offline dataset that yielded low imitation learning performance (<10%), EXPO with entropy backup achieved near-perfect policies by incentivizing broader exploration.

In summary, EXPO consistently achieved strong performance and superior sample efficiency across various challenging tasks, both for leveraging offline data and fine-tuning pre-trained policies. Its design choices, particularly the on-the-fly value maximization and the action editing mechanism, address the stability challenges of training expressive policies in online RL, and the entropy backup further enhances its robustness to data limitations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EXPO framework represents a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning, specifically in its ability to stably and efficiently integrate expressive policy classes with online self-improvement mechanisms. The core insight of decoupling the expressive policy's training from direct value optimization, and instead relying on an "on-the-fly" policy constructed from a stable imitation-learned base policy and a value-maximizing edit policy, addresses a fundamental stability challenge.

#### 5.1. Key Contributions

- **Addresses a Core Challenge**: EXPO tackles the problem of unstable gradient propagation inherent in directly optimizing expressive policies (like diffusion models) against Q-functions in online RL. This has been a barrier to fully leveraging the representational power of such policies.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency**: The method demonstrates notable improvements in sample efficiency across a range of complex, sparse-reward tasks, both when training from scratch with offline data and when fine-tuning pre-trained policies. This is critical for practical applications where real-world data collection can be expensive and time-consuming.
- **Effective Fine-Tuning of Expressive Policies**: EXPO provides a robust mechanism to fine-tune pre-trained expressive policies, preventing the performance degradation often observed in other offline-to-online methods. This allows for leveraging the extensive prior knowledge encoded in policies trained via large-scale imitation learning.
- **Generality**: The approach is agnostic to the specific parameterization of the expressive policy (e.g., diffusion, flow-matching), making it broadly applicable to various advanced policy architectures.
- **Enhanced Exploration**: The inclusion of a light-weight edit policy with entropy regularization, and an optional entropy backup mechanism, provides a controlled way to encourage exploration, which is often difficult with expressive policies alone. This makes EXPO more adaptable to scenarios with narrow or suboptimal offline datasets.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancements in Robotics and Control**: The ability to stably fine-tune expressive policies in an online setting can lead to more reliable and higher-performing robotic systems. Policies initially learned from demonstrations can be continuously improved through interaction, achieving performance levels beyond what imitation learning alone can provide. This can accelerate the deployment of autonomous agents in complex real-world environments.
- **Broader Adoption of Expressive Models**: By making it feasible to train and fine-tune complex generative models (like diffusion policies) within RL frameworks, EXPO could encourage their wider adoption in diverse RL applications beyond robotics, such as in game playing, industrial automation, or computational design, where rich action distributions are beneficial.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The principles introduced by EXPO, particularly the idea of constructing an on-the-fly policy and using localized edits for value maximization, could inspire new directions in algorithm design for complex policy spaces.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
1. **Computational Expense**: Sampling multiple actions for the TD backup at each training step can be computationally expensive, especially for large batch sizes. Future work could focus on optimizing this aspect to improve computational efficiency.
2. **Reliance on a Prior**: The method assumes the availability of a "reasonable prior" either through an offline dataset or a pre-trained policy. While this assumption holds in many practical robotics settings, exploring the framework's applicability to scenarios with a completely uninformed prior remains an interesting avenue for future research.

Overall, EXPO presents a practical and effective solution to a challenging problem in modern reinforcement learning, bridging the gap between expressive policy representation and robust online optimization, with significant implications for real-world autonomous systems.
