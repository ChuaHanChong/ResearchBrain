# Prior-Guided Diffusion Planning for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Korea University researchers developed Prior Guidance (PG), a novel guided sampling framework for diffusion-based offline reinforcement learning planners. PG efficiently generates high-value trajectories by learning a latent space prior and utilizing a latent value function, achieving state-of-the-art performance on long-horizon D4RL tasks while significantly reducing inference and training computational overhead.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion planners, particularly Monte Carlo Sample Selection (MCSS), suffer from high inference-time computational costs due to multi-trajectory sampling requirements.
- Other guidance methods like Classifier Guidance often produce suboptimal plans due to multimodal optimal policies, while Classifier-Free Guidance struggles with distributional shift.
- Directly optimizing diffusion models for value maximization with behavior regularization is computationally expensive due to backpropagation through iterative denoising and intractable density evaluation.

## Method

- Trains a learnable, ==state-conditioned Gaussian prior== distribution in the latent space of a pre-trained behavior-cloned diffusion planner.
- Reformulates the behavior-regularized objective into the latent space, leveraging DDIM's approximate bijectivity for tractable f-divergence computation between prior distributions.
- Introduces a separate latent value function to predict trajectory values directly from latent noise, eliminating costly backpropagation through the diffusion model during prior optimization.

## Results

- PG achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging long-horizon offline RL benchmarks, including Kitchen, Antmaze, and Maze2D.
- It significantly reduces inference-time computational overhead by generating a single trajectory per step, outperforming MCSS which requires sampling multiple candidates.
- Training time is substantially decreased by several times (e.g., for Mujoco and Maze2D) by avoiding backpropagation through the full denoising process during prior optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The latent space of diffusion models can be effectively exploited by a learnable prior to concentrate samples on high-value trajectories, improving planning efficiency.
> - DDIM sampling's approximate bijectivity allows for a principled reformulation of complex behavior regularization into a tractable objective over simpler latent priors.
> - Decoupling value estimation from the iterative denoising process through a latent value function dramatically reduces the computational burden for training diffusion planners.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Offline Reinforcement Learning, Diffusion Models, and AI Planning. Its primary contribution is to advance the state-of-the-art in **diffusion-based planners for offline reinforcement learning**.

**Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL)** is a crucial paradigm where agents learn policies solely from fixed, pre-collected datasets without any further interaction with the environment. This is particularly valuable in real-world applications where online data collection is expensive, unsafe, or impractical (e.g., robotics, healthcare, autonomous driving). A core challenge in offline RL is **distributional shift**, where policies trained on static data might attempt to take actions not present in the dataset (out-of-distribution, or OOD actions). This often leads to overestimation of value functions for OOD actions and ultimately suboptimal performance. Many existing offline RL methods address this through various forms of **behavior regularization**, aiming to keep the learned policy close to the data-generating behavior policy.

**Diffusion Models** have recently emerged as powerful generative models, demonstrating impressive capabilities in learning complex data distributions and generating high-quality samples across various domains, including image synthesis, text generation, and video generation. Their ability to model long-range temporal dependencies and condition on auxiliary information makes them particularly attractive for sequence modeling tasks in decision-making. In the context of RL, diffusion models are increasingly being used in two main capacities:
1. **Diffusion Policies**: These models directly generate actions (or distributions over actions) conditioned on the current state. They serve as expressive policy classes, enhancing regularization and performance in offline RL settings (e.g., Diffusion-QL, IDQL).
2. **Diffusion Planners**: These models generate entire sequences of states and actions (trajectories) conditioned on the current state and a desired outcome (e.g., high return). They are well-suited for long-horizon decision-making tasks where foresight and temporal consistency are critical (e.g., Diffuser, AdaptDiffuser, Diffusion Veteran).

The paper specifically focuses on **diffusion planners**. Prior work in this area has utilized **guided sampling strategies** to steer the trajectory generation process towards high-return outcomes. Common guidance methods include:
- **Classifier Guidance (CG)**: Uses a learned critic to directly influence the denoising steps. However, it can struggle with multimodal optimal policies.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**: Interpolates between conditional and unconditional diffusion models. Its limitation lies in generalizing when conditioning signals deviate from training data, leading to unreliable plans.
- **Monte Carlo Sample Selection (MCSS)**: Generates multiple candidate trajectories and selects the one with the highest estimated value from a separate critic. While empirically successful, MCSS suffers from significant computational overhead during inference, as it requires sampling and evaluating many trajectories. It also risks selecting OOD trajectories with spuriously high value estimates, especially when the number of candidates is large.

An alternative approach involves directly training the diffusion model to generate high-value behaviors, often by maximizing a learned critic under behavior regularization. However, this is typically computationally expensive due to the need for backpropagation through the entire iterative denoising process, limiting its application primarily to diffusion policies rather than more complex diffusion planners. Furthermore, the intractable density evaluation of diffusion models makes sophisticated behavior regularization difficult.

This work, "Prior-Guided Diffusion Planning," fits into this landscape by offering a novel guided sampling framework that directly addresses the aforementioned limitations of existing diffusion planners. It seeks to combine the benefits of efficient, high-value trajectory generation with robust behavior regularization, without incurring the prohibitive computational costs of direct diffusion model optimization or the inference overhead of MCSS. By focusing on learning a *prior distribution* in the latent space, it provides a unique and principled solution to these challenges, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with diffusion models in complex, long-horizon offline RL tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "Prior-Guided Diffusion Planning (PG)" is to develop a novel and efficient guided sampling framework for diffusion-based offline RL planners that overcomes the practical limitations of existing methods, particularly for long-horizon decision-making.

The authors are motivated by several critical shortcomings observed in current diffusion-based planning strategies:

1. **Computational Overhead of Monte Carlo Sample Selection (MCSS)**: MCSS, a prevalent and often high-performing technique, requires generating a large number (N) of candidate trajectories at each environment step and then evaluating them with a learned critic to select the best one. This multi-sampling process is inherently expensive due to the iterative nature of diffusion model sampling, leading to significant inference-time computational costs. For complex, high-dimensional, or long-horizon tasks, this overhead can be prohibitive, hindering real-world deployment.
2. **Suboptimal and Unreliable Guidance from Other Methods**:
 - **Classifier Guidance (CG)** can produce suboptimal actions when the optimal policy is multi-modal, failing to capture the full spectrum of high-value behaviors.
 - **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)** often struggles with distributional shift, leading to unreliable plans when the conditioning signal diverges from the training distribution.
3. **Challenges with Direct Diffusion Model Optimization**: Directly training a diffusion model to maximize returns under behavior regularization is conceptually appealing but faces major practical hurdles:
 - **Expensive Backpropagation**: Optimizing such an objective necessitates backpropagation through the entire iterative denoising process, which is computationally intensive, especially for diffusion planners that generate long trajectories. This often restricts such approaches to simpler diffusion policies.
 - **Intractable Density Estimation**: Diffusion models do not offer tractable density evaluation, making it difficult to apply sophisticated behavior regularization functions (e.g., f-divergences) that require comparing policy densities. Prior works have resorted to simpler surrogates like auxiliary behavior cloning losses.
4. **Balancing Performance and Safety**: Existing methods often present a trade-off between maximizing performance (generating high-value trajectories) and mitigating the risk of selecting out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, which can lead to instability or unsafe behavior due to overestimated Q-values. MCSS, for example, struggles to guarantee high-quality, in-distribution samples while avoiding OOD selections, especially when N is large.

In light of these challenges, Prior Guidance aims to provide a solution that:
- Generates high-value trajectories efficiently with a single forward pass, eliminating MCSS's inference-time overhead.
- Enables stable and analytically tractable behavior regularization to prevent OOD actions, directly addressing distributional shift.
- Avoids the costly backpropagation through the full denoising process, making it computationally feasible for diffusion planners.
- Ultimately, achieve state-of-the-art performance on long-horizon offline RL benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Prior Guidance (PG) introduces a novel guided sampling framework that leverages a learnable prior distribution in the latent space of a pre-trained behavior-cloned diffusion model. The core idea is to shift the optimization burden from the complex, iterative denoising process to a simpler, parameterized prior.

The methodology proceeds in several key steps:

1. **Behavior-Cloned Diffusion Planner**: First, a standard diffusion planner, denoted as $\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}$, is trained via behavior cloning on the offline dataset $D$. This planner learns to generate high-quality state-action trajectories ($x_0$) from a standard Gaussian noise vector ($x_T$) conditioned on the current state ($s$). The denoising process $g_s$ is learned during this phase and then kept fixed.
2. **Learnable Prior Distribution**: Instead of sampling the initial latent noise $x_T$ from a fixed standard Gaussian distribution $p(x_T) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, PG proposes to learn a parameterized Gaussian prior $p_{\psi}(x_T|s)$. This learnable prior is conditioned on the current state $s$ and aims to concentrate its samples $x_T$ in regions of the latent space that, when denoised by $g_s$, yield high-value trajectories $x_0$. The prior network is parameterized using a GRU to capture temporal dependencies, outputting mean and log standard deviation vectors for the Gaussian distribution.
3. **Behavior-Regularized Objective**: The optimization of the learnable prior $p_{\psi}(x_T|s)$ is formulated as a behavior-regularized objective (Equation 2 in the paper). The goal is to maximize the expected value $V(x_0)$ of the generated trajectories $x_0 = g_s(x_T)$ while penalizing deviations from the behavior distribution $\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}(x_0|s)$. This regularization term is expressed as $\alpha f\left(\frac{\pi_{\psi}(x_0|s)}{\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}(x_0|s)}\right)$, where $f(\cdot)$ is an f-divergence function (e.g., KL-divergence, chosen for practical implementation).
4. **Tractable Reformulation using Bijectivity (Proposition 1)**: A critical innovation of PG is addressing the intractability of evaluating $\pi_{\psi}(x_0|s)$ and $\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}(x_0|s)$ directly. The authors leverage the properties of DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) sampling, which, with a sufficiently large number of discretization steps, approximates a bijective mapping between the latent noise $x_T$ and the generated trajectory $x_0$. Under this bijectivity assumption ($x_0 = g_s(x_T)$), the original behavior-regularized objective (Equation 2) can be elegantly reformulated into an equivalent objective (Equation 3) that operates directly on the latent prior distributions:
 $$ \max_{\psi} E_{s \sim D, x_T \sim p_{\psi}(\cdot|s)} \left[ V(g_s(x_T)) - \alpha f\left(\frac{p_{\psi}(x_T|s)}{p(x_T)}\right) \right] $$
 This reformulation is significant because it allows the behavior regularization term to be computed between two Gaussian distributions ($p_{\psi}$ and $p(x_T)$), which admits a closed-form solution for many f-divergences. This resolves the density estimation challenge.
5. **Avoiding Backpropagation through Denoising**: Even with the reformulated objective, direct optimization would still require backpropagating gradients through $g_s$ to compute $V(g_s(x_T))$. To eliminate this computational bottleneck, PG introduces a **latent value function $\bar{V}_{\phi}(x_T)$**. This separate neural network is trained to predict the value of a trajectory directly from its corresponding latent noise $x_T$, minimizing the squared error between $\bar{V}_{\phi}(x_T)$ and $V(g_s(x_T))$ (Equation 4). Once trained, $\bar{V}_{\phi}(x_T)$ replaces $V(g_s(x_T))$ in the prior optimization objective (Equation 5):
 $$ \max_{\psi} E_{s \sim D, x_T \sim p_{\psi}(\cdot|s)} \left[ \bar{V}_{\phi}(x_T) - \alpha f\left(\frac{p_{\psi}(x_T|s)}{p(x_T)}\right) \right] $$
 This design allows the prior $p_{\psi}$ to be optimized without any gradient flow through the fixed denoising operator $g_s$, making the training process significantly more efficient. The overall training alternates between updating $\bar{V}_{\phi}$ and optimizing $p_{\psi}$.
6. **Action Generation**: Once a high-value state sequence $x_0$ is generated by sampling from the learned prior $p_{\psi}(x_T|s)$ and denoising, a separately trained inverse dynamics model $\epsilon_{\omega}$ is used to recover the executable action $a^{(\tau)}$ for the current environment step $\tau$.

By combining these elements, PG provides a principled, computationally efficient, and robust approach to guided sampling for diffusion planners in offline RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Prior Guidance (PG) across diverse D4RL benchmarks demonstrates its effectiveness and addresses the limitations of prior methods.

1. **Reduced Computational Cost (Training and Inference)**:
 - **Inference**: PG generates a single trajectory at inference time, unlike MCSS which requires sampling and scoring multiple (N) candidates. This significantly reduces inference-time overhead, as shown in Figure 4 (Right). For instance, PG achieves substantially lower inference times than MCSS, especially for large N (e.g., N=500 or 5000), making it more practical for real-world applications.
 - **Training**: The proposed approach of training a latent-space value function ($\bar{V}_{\phi}$) to avoid backpropagation through the denoising process ("PG w/o Bp") dramatically reduces training time compared to directly optimizing the diffusion model ("PG w/ Bp" or "DQL (planner)"). Figure 4 (Left) illustrates that PG w/o Bp is several times faster to train across various environments (e.g., 29 minutes vs. 344 minutes for Mujoco, 127 minutes vs. 582 minutes for Maze2D).

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance, Especially for Long-Horizon Tasks**:
 - Table 1 showcases PG's competitive performance, achieving state-of-the-art results on tasks requiring long-horizon decision-making (e.g., Kitchen, Antmaze, Maze2D). PG substantially outperforms all baselines, including other diffusion planners like DV*, across most tasks in these challenging domains.
 - While diffusion planners, including PG, generally underperform diffusion policies in simpler MuJoCo tasks (which do not require long-horizon planning), PG still achieves the highest scores among diffusion planners in these environments. An additional experiment (Table 16 in Appendix I) demonstrates that when PG is applied to a diffusion *policy* instead of a *planner*, it surpasses baseline diffusion policies like DQL in MuJoCo tasks, indicating that the PG framework itself is effective across task types and the observed lower performance in MuJoCo for the *planner* version is due to the inherent nature of the task not requiring complex planning.

3. **Addresses MCSS Limitations**:
 - Figure 5 empirically validates that PG effectively resolves the issues with MCSS. It shows that PG consistently matches or outperforms MCSS (even with MCSS's best-performing N) across different D4RL tasks. This is attributed to PG's ability to generate high-quality trajectories without the need for multiple candidates and its stable behavior regularization which mitigates OOD selection risks that MCSS faces with large N (as seen in Figure 3).

4. **Effective Latent Space Guidance and Value Learning**:
 - **Prior Visualization**: Visualizations of latent noise and corresponding trajectories (Figure 6) confirm that the learned prior $p_{\psi}$ in PG successfully focuses on high-value regions in the latent space $x_T$, while the standard Gaussian prior of the behavior diffusion model spans a wider range of values. This demonstrates that the latent space of the diffusion model is well-structured to allow for such targeted sampling.
 - **Latent Value Function Efficacy**: The latent value function $\bar{V}_{\phi}$ shows a strong positive correlation with the ground-truth trajectory values $V(x_0)$ (Figure 7a), indicating its ability to accurately estimate values directly in the latent space. Crucially, the observed approximation errors do not lead to performance degradation, as evidenced by nearly identical scores for PG with and without backpropagation through the denoising process (Figure 7b).

5. **Flexibility of Prior Representation**:
 - Exploring a more expressive multi-modal Gaussian prior (Table 2) showed marginal improvements in complex tasks like AntMaze, while a uni-modal prior was sufficient for MuJoCo. This suggests that the latent spaces of diffusion models can be rich enough to capture complex distributions with simpler priors in some domains, but benefits from increased expressiveness in others.

6. **Robustness to Regularization Choices**:
 - Ablation studies on different f-divergence regularization functions (KL, Reverse KL, Pearson $\chi^2$) and the regularization coefficient $\alpha$ (Appendix H) demonstrate that PG is robust to these choices, with KL-divergence being the default and performing well across tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Prior Guidance (PG) marks a significant advancement in the field of offline reinforcement learning, particularly for problems requiring long-horizon planning using diffusion models. Its contributions offer both practical and theoretical implications that could impact future research and applications.

1. **Advancing Diffusion Planners in Offline RL**: PG pushes the boundaries of diffusion-based planning by overcoming key limitations of existing methods. It establishes a new state-of-the-art for long-horizon tasks in offline RL, demonstrating that powerful generative models can be guided effectively and efficiently to synthesize complex, high-value behaviors from static datasets. This success can accelerate the adoption of diffusion planners in domains like robotics, where learning from diverse, pre-recorded demonstrations is crucial.

2. **Enhanced Computational Efficiency**: By replacing Monte Carlo Sample Selection (MCSS) with a single-pass generation process, PG drastically reduces inference-time computational overhead. This improvement is critical for deploying RL agents in real-time or resource-constrained environments where quick decision-making is necessary. Furthermore, the novel strategy of learning a latent-space value function to avoid costly backpropagation through the denoising process significantly optimizes training efficiency, making complex diffusion planners more feasible to develop and iterate upon.

3. **Principled and Tractable Behavior Regularization**: The paper introduces a principled method for analytically tractable behavior regularization in diffusion models, a problem previously complicated by the intractability of density evaluation. By reformulating the objective in the latent space and applying f-divergences between simple Gaussian priors, PG offers a robust mechanism to prevent the generation of out-of-distribution (OOD) actions. This leads to more stable and reliable policy learning, mitigating the risk of performance degradation due to spurious value estimates, a common challenge in offline RL.

4. **Broader Applicability and Generalizability**: The core concept of learning an informed prior distribution in the latent space of a generative model, decoupled from the main generation process, has potential implications beyond diffusion models and reinforcement learning. Similar strategies could be explored in other generative modeling tasks where efficient, controlled, and condition-aware sampling is desired. The proof of bijectivity for DDIM sampling, which enables the objective reformulation, also provides a theoretical foundation that could inspire further research into the mathematical properties of diffusion models and their latent spaces.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The paper explicitly outlines several limitations, which serve as clear directions for future work. These include deeper investigations into prior network architectures, leveraging more advanced generative techniques like flow-matching to achieve exact bijectivity, and extending the framework to high-dimensional observations (e.g., images) or online RL settings. This forward-looking perspective demonstrates the foundational nature of PG, inviting further exploration and refinement of prior-guided generative methods in RL.

In summary, Prior Guidance represents a significant step forward in making diffusion-based offline RL planners more practical, efficient, and robust, particularly for long-horizon and complex tasks. Its methodical approach to addressing computational and theoretical challenges lays a strong foundation for future research in generative models for decision-making.
