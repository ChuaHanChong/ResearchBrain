# Prior-Guided Diffusion Planning for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Korea University researchers developed Prior Guidance (PG), a novel guided sampling framework for diffusion-based offline reinforcement learning planners. PG efficiently generates high-value trajectories by learning a latent space prior and utilizing a latent value function, achieving state-of-the-art performance on long-horizon D4RL tasks while significantly reducing inference and training computational overhead.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion planners, particularly Monte Carlo Sample Selection (MCSS), suffer from high inference-time computational costs due to multi-trajectory sampling requirements.
- Other guidance methods like Classifier Guidance often produce suboptimal plans due to multimodal optimal policies, while Classifier-Free Guidance struggles with distributional shift.
- Directly optimizing diffusion models for value maximization with behavior regularization is computationally expensive due to backpropagation through iterative denoising and intractable density evaluation.

## Method

- Trains a learnable, ==state-conditioned Gaussian prior== distribution in the latent space of a pre-trained behavior-cloned diffusion planner.
- Reformulates the behavior-regularized objective into the latent space, leveraging DDIM's approximate bijectivity for tractable f-divergence computation between prior distributions.
- Introduces a separate latent value function to predict trajectory values directly from latent noise, eliminating costly backpropagation through the diffusion model during prior optimization.

## Results

- PG achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging long-horizon offline RL benchmarks, including Kitchen, Antmaze, and Maze2D.
- It significantly reduces inference-time computational overhead by generating a single trajectory per step, outperforming MCSS which requires sampling multiple candidates.
- Training time is substantially decreased by several times (e.g., for Mujoco and Maze2D) by avoiding backpropagation through the full denoising process during prior optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The latent space of diffusion models can be effectively exploited by a learnable prior to concentrate samples on high-value trajectories, improving planning efficiency.
> - DDIM sampling's approximate bijectivity allows for a principled reformulation of complex behavior regularization into a tractable objective over simpler latent priors.
> - Decoupling value estimation from the iterative denoising process through a latent value function dramatically reduces the computational burden for training diffusion planners.
