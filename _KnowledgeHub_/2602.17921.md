# Latent Diffeomorphic Co-Design of End-Effectors for Deformable and Fragile Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KTH Royal Institute of Technology developed a co-design framework that jointly optimizes robotic end-effector morphology and control strategies for manipulating deformable and fragile objects. This approach, using latent diffeomorphic shape parameterization and a stress-aware bi-level optimization, demonstrated up to a 33% higher success rate and 36% reduced object stress in simulation, alongside effective zero-shot transfer to real robots.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable and fragile objects is challenging due to high-dimensional states, complex non-linear contact dynamics, and diverse material properties.
- Existing methods optimize end-effector hardware design or control strategies in isolation, leading to suboptimal performance and limiting adaptability.
- Ensuring object integrity (preventing damage or breakage) demands extremely gentle and precise robotic control, which traditional approaches struggle to provide.

## Method

- A bi-level co-design framework jointly optimizes end-effector morphology using Latent Diffeomorphic Co-Design (LDM) and an adaptive control strategy.
- LDM represents shapes via diffeomorphic deformations parameterized by Radial Basis Functions (RBFs), then uses PCA to create a compact, low-dimensional latent space for optimization.
- A stress-aware control policy is optimized in simulation using privileged feedback, then distilled into a 3D diffusion policy for zero-shot real-world deployment on point cloud observations.

## Results

- In simulation, the co-designed system achieved a 33% higher success rate and 36% reduced induced stress for large jelly cube grasping compared to a parallel-jaw gripper.
- Optimized end-effectors showed specialized morphologies, such as concave surfaces for robust pushing and extended shapes for scooping, outperforming baseline designs.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was successfully demonstrated on a real robot, achieving high success rates (e.g., 9/9 grasping, 10/12 pushing) with minimal object damage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-design, integrating hardware and control optimization, effectively addresses the unique challenges of deformable and fragile object manipulation, surpassing isolated approaches.
> - Latent Diffeomorphic Co-Design (LDM) provides an expressive yet tractable method for exploring complex end-effector geometries while ensuring physical validity.
> - Privileged simulation feedback, particularly internal stress signals, is crucial for optimizing object integrity, and can be effectively transferred to real-world reactive controllers via policy distillation.
