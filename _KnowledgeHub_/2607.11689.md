# From World Action Models to Embodied Brains: A Roadmap for Open-World Physical Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers propose a comprehensive co-evolution roadmap for open-world physical intelligence, aiming to address barriers in embodied AI by decoupling general physical reasoning from embodiment-specific execution through standardized interfaces and a closed-loop learning framework. This approach seeks to enable cumulative progress and the development of adaptive, self-improving agents capable of operating effectively in the physical world.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI systems often lack a persistent and actionable representation of physical state, hindering the development of reusable physical reasoning capabilities.
- Diverse and often incompatible evaluation methods, data collection practices, and task definitions prevent cumulative progress and comparable supervision across different research efforts.
- Physical agents currently suffer from project-specific runtimes, tightly coupling perception, reasoning, and control, which limits component substitution and the reuse of interaction traces.

## Method

- A ==functional decomposition== of physical agents into an **Embodied Brain** for general reasoning and a **Physical Harness** for embodiment-specific execution, mediated by explicit contracts.
- Standardized **WAM Prediction Contract** for ==World Action Models (WAMs)== to expose decision-relevant consequences, along with **Embodiment, Task, and Trace Cards** for consistent data documentation and evaluation.
- A ==closed-loop learning== process integrates verified ==interaction traces== from the harness back into model and component refinement, supported by layered evaluation protocols.

## Results

- Identified **three** core "coupled gaps" (model and representation, objective and standardization, ecosystem and systems) as the primary barriers to cumulative progress in embodied AI.
- Proposed the "WAM Prediction Contract" and the "Embodied Brain" concept to clarify the roles and interfaces of physical reasoning models, focusing on decision-relevant consequences.
- Introduced the "Physical Harness" for intent grounding, capability resolution, verification, and trace recording, alongside "Embodiment, Task, and Trace Cards" to standardize experience and enable layered evaluation protocols.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving scalable physical intelligence necessitates a clear separation between abstract, general physical reasoning and the concrete, embodiment-specific execution mechanisms.
> - Standardizing interfaces, data records, and evaluation protocols is crucial for transforming disparate research efforts into a cohesive, cumulatively progressing field.
> - A 'physical harness' acting as an intermediary between a brain model's intent and physical reality is essential for modularity, inspectability, and robust operation in diverse environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the broader ambition of developing artificial general intelligence (AGI) that can operate effectively in the physical world. The authors note that while large language models (LLMs) and multimodal foundation models have demonstrated significant progress in digital domains, human intelligence is fundamentally grounded in physical interaction—involving space, time, objects, forces, and consequences. Physical intelligence is therefore considered a crucial test for general-purpose agency, requiring systems to integrate perception, prediction, decision-making, and action under real-world constraints.

The paper draws upon several influential perspectives in AI. It references "the bitter lesson" by Sutton (2019), which advocates for general methods benefiting from computation and data. It also aligns with discussions on an "era of experience" (Silver & Sutton, 2025), suggesting that future agents should learn from their own interaction traces and consequences, not solely from static corpora. Additionally, it incorporates insights from world model and spatial intelligence perspectives (LeCun, 2022; Li, 2025), which argue for internal world representations to support prediction, planning, and control. Collectively, these perspectives emphasize the need for AI systems to operate in closed loops of perception, intervention, feedback, and self-improvement.

In the context of embodied AI and robot learning, the paper acknowledges advancements in systems such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies (e.g., Ahn et al., 2022; Driess et al., 2023; Brohan et al., 2022, 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Team et al., 2024; Black et al., 2024), which map multimodal context to robot actions. While these systems expand task and embodiment coverage, the authors identify a limitation: they primarily focus on observation-to-action prediction and often do not explicitly expose anticipated changes, compare interventions, estimate feasibility or uncertainty, or manage execution delegation. This aligns with classical motor control and affordance theory, where perception is organized around action and its consequences (Wolpert & Kawato, 1998; Gibson, 1979).

The paper specifically introduces World Action Models (WAMs) as a promising development. WAMs are defined as action-conditioned predictive models that explicitly estimate intervention outcomes for purposes such as planning, verification, control, or learning (Wang et al., 2026). Existing WAM-oriented systems explore this capability through various means, including joint image-action prediction, unified video-action modeling, 3D scene prediction, and world-language-action modeling (Wu et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025a; Lu et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026; Kairos Team et al., 2026). The current work surveys the evolution of these models, noting a convergence of three trajectories: learning executable robot actions (e.g., Behavior Transformers, Diffusion Policy), grounding action in language and multimodal context (e.g., Decision Transformer, PaLM-E), and predicting world changes under intervention (e.g., DreamerV3, visual foresight). WAMs represent the point where these trajectories intersect, focusing on the question of "what will happen if this embodied system takes this action."

However, the authors observe that despite these advancements, progress does not accumulate readily. Action-centric systems bind outputs to specific action spaces. Predictive systems use diverse variables and incompatible conventions for datasets, tasks, and runtimes. While efforts like Open X-Embodiment (Open X-Embodiment Collaboration et al., 2023) and Octo (Team et al., 2024) have made strides in cross-embodiment learning, they highlight that reuse depends on explicit mappings between shared model representations and local control, a mapping that often remains implicit. The paper aims to systematize these observations by diagnosing current barriers and proposing a roadmap for more scalable and cumulative progress.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to propose a co-evolution roadmap for scalable physical intelligence, addressing limitations that currently hinder cumulative progress in embodied AI. The authors are motivated by the recognition that achieving artificial general intelligence necessitates agents that can learn, reason, and act effectively in the physical world, moving beyond purely digital forms of intelligence.

The core motivation stems from three identified and coupled gaps in current research:

1. **Model and Representation Gap:** Current systems, which combine various AI components like language models, vision-language models, and video generators, often lack a persistent and actionable representation of physical state. Challenges include effective spatial structure, contact-sensitive reasoning, calibrated uncertainty, and recovery mechanisms. There is no consensus on the central model's role—specifically, which physical capabilities it should learn, which consequences it should expose, and which responsibilities should remain downstream. This ambiguity impedes the development of reusable physical reasoning.

2. **Objective and Standardization Gap:** Existing evaluations, data collection methods, task definitions, and supervision protocols are diverse and often incompatible. Success in an end-task may not reveal whether a system benefited from genuine physical foresight, exploited a benchmark regularity, or relied on a robust controller. Without shared semantics for interventions, predicted changes, and outcomes, data and evaluation metrics are not fully comparable, preventing the accumulation of reusable physical experience.

3. **Ecosystem and Systems Gap:** Physical agents currently lack a mature interface for orchestrating components like spatial relations, coordinate frames, intended state transitions, uncertainty, and controller constraints, unlike digital agents that utilize harnesses for tool calls and multi-step workflows. This leads to project-specific runtimes where perception, reasoning, and control are tightly coupled, limiting component substitution and the reuse of interaction traces.

To address these gaps, the paper proposes several key objectives:

- **Define a WAM Prediction Contract:** To clarify the role of WAMs, the objective is to establish a contract where, given physical context and a candidate intervention, a WAM exposes a decision-relevant consequence, along with its horizon, reference frame, uncertainty, and validity conditions. This aims to standardize the output semantics of WAMs, regardless of their specific modality (e.g., video, geometry, structured state).
- **Establish the Embodied Brain as a Long-Term Target:** The paper aims to define the "embodied brain" as a central model that integrates multimodal context, reasons over possible interventions, and communicates an "intermediate intent representation" (an intended state transition or capability request) rather than low-level actuator commands. This separates general reasoning from embodiment-specific execution.
- **Develop a Physical Harness:** A key objective is to outline the design and responsibilities of a "physical harness" that grounds the embodied brain's output, resolves tools and controllers, verifies execution, and records interaction traces. This component is crucial for mediating between abstract intent and physical reality.
- **Introduce Shared Contracts for Experience and Evaluation:** The roadmap seeks to introduce standardized records—Embodiment Cards, Task Cards, and Trace Cards—to make heterogeneous data, tasks, and components comparable while preserving necessary embodiment details. This includes developing layered evaluation protocols to assess different aspects of physical intelligence.
- **Enable Closed-Loop Post-Training:** The objective is to facilitate a process where verified interaction traces are systematically used to improve brain models, harness adapters, and tool models, thus enabling adaptive and self-improving agents.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create an "actionable path" toward physical intelligence by decoupling general physical reasoning from local execution, thereby making components independently testable, mutually compatible, and jointly improvable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper's methodology involves a multi-faceted approach, beginning with a comprehensive survey and diagnosis of existing embodied AI research, followed by the proposition of a conceptual framework and roadmap.

#### 3.1. Survey and Diagnosis

The authors initiated their work by surveying the evolution of embodied models, categorizing them into:
- **Action Prediction:** Systems that convert sensory input into robot actions (e.g., Behavior Transformers, Diffusion Policy, QT-Opt).
- **Sequence Modeling and Semantic Grounding:** Approaches that apply sequence modeling to states, actions, and rewards (e.g., Decision Transformer, Gato), and those that ground language and multimodal prompts in robotic affordances (e.g., CLIPort, PaLM-E).
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Policies:** Models that scale large-scale vision-language priors to executable robot action spaces (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Octo).
- **World Models:** Systems focused on predicting future observations or states under candidate actions, often for model-predictive control (e.g., DreamerV3, UniPi).
- **WAM-Oriented Models:** Recent systems that explicitly couple prediction and control through diverse interfaces (e.g., GR-1, WLA, GWM, UWM).
This survey identified three main categories of predictive interfaces: explicit observation/geometric forecasts, predictive latent representations, and latent transition/action abstractions. The analysis also reviewed the landscape of data sources (human video, robot trajectories, autonomous interaction, simulation) and evaluation strategies (predictive fidelity, interface grounding, task utility, executability).

From this comprehensive review, the authors diagnosed three interconnected limitations, termed "gaps":
- **Model and Representation Gap:** Lack of clear physical state representation and an unsettled role for a central physical reasoning model.
- **Objective and Standardization Gap:** Incompatible data, task definitions, and evaluation metrics hinder comparability and accumulation of experience.
- **Ecosystem and Systems Gap:** Project-specific runtimes prevent modular component substitution and trace reuse.

#### 3.2. Roadmap Development and Conceptual Framework

The core of the methodology is the construction of a co-evolution roadmap that addresses these diagnosed gaps through explicit contracts and defined roles.

- **Defining the Physical-Intelligence Stack:** The paper proposes a functional decomposition of a physical agent system into distinct but interacting components:
 - **Embodied Brain:** This is defined as the long-term model target for general physical reasoning. Its approach is to integrate multimodal observations and task context, compare possible interventions, and emit an *intermediate intent representation*. This intent is a proposed state transition or capability request, not direct actuator commands, allowing the brain to be independent of specific robot morphologies or controllers.
 - **WAM Prediction Contract:** For current WAM research, the paper proposes a specific contract: given physical context and a candidate intervention, a WAM must expose a *decision-relevant consequence* along with its horizon, reference frame, uncertainty, and validity conditions. This allows for diverse output modalities while standardizing the information communicated for decision-making.
 - **Physical Harness:** This component acts as the intermediary between the embodied brain and the physical world. Its approach involves:
 - **Intent Grounding:** Translating the abstract brain intent into concrete, embodiment-specific actions.
 - **Capability Resolution:** Identifying and invoking appropriate "tool models" (learned or algorithmic modules for tasks like grasping, navigation, or simulation) and "controllers" (for low-level actuation). This relies on a "capability contract" that defines inputs, outputs, timing, and failure modes for each tool.
 - **Verification and Monitoring:** Implementing verifier gates to check preconditions, ensure safety, and monitor execution.
 - **Trace Recording:** Documenting the entire decision and execution process in a "Trace Card" for later analysis and learning.
- **Standardization Contracts (Cards):** To address the objective and standardization gap, the paper introduces three lightweight, semantic records:
 - **Embodiment Card:** Describes robot morphology, sensors, control parameters, and safety limits.
 - **Task Card:** Defines task goals, scene context, constraints, and success/failure criteria.
 - **Trace Card:** A comprehensive record of an interaction, including brain requests, chosen tool chains, verifier outputs, controller states, execution outcomes, and data quality flags. These cards aim to make diverse physical experiences comparable and replayable.
- **Closed-Loop Learning and Layered Evaluation:** The methodology includes integrating these contracts into a closed-loop learning process. Verified interaction traces from the harness are fed back for "harness-in-the-loop physical learning," which can train or refine various components (e.g., physical representation learning, predictive physical reasoning, brain-harness interface alignment, tool-chain supervision, verifier alignment). This learning process is governed by a *layered evaluation protocol* that assesses distinct aspects of performance (e.g., predictive fidelity, interface grounding, tool orchestration, safety, traceability, self-improvement) rather than just end-task success, facilitating granular diagnosis and improvement.
- **Ecosystem Coordination:** The roadmap emphasizes the importance of community-wide adoption of these operational agreements to foster cumulative progress by making independent contributions compatible.

This approach is fundamentally conceptual and aims to define the architecture, interfaces, and evaluation mechanisms necessary for scaling physical intelligence. It frames existing WAM research as prototypes for capabilities required by the "embodied brain" and explicitly details how modularity, traceability, and structured feedback can lead to more robust, adaptive, and self-improving physical agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

This paper presents a conceptual roadmap rather than empirical findings from experiments. Its "results" are the diagnoses of current challenges in embodied AI and the proposed solutions encapsulated in its framework.

#### 4.1. Diagnosis of Current Barriers (The Three Coupled Gaps)

The primary finding is a comprehensive diagnosis of why progress in physical intelligence, despite advancements in component technologies like VLA models and WAMs, has not been as cumulatively effective as desired. These limitations are articulated as three interconnected "gaps":

- **Model and Representation Gap:**
 - **Unclear WAM role:** There is no universal agreement on what capabilities a general physical reasoning model (like a WAM) should learn, which consequences it should explicitly expose, or where its responsibilities end.
 - **Inconsistent targets:** Model outputs vary widely (e.g., raw actions, video, latent states, structured geometry), making it difficult to integrate different predictive systems or to evaluate physical understanding independently of output modality.
 - **Implicit physical state:** Many models do not provide a persistent, actionable account of physical state, struggling with detailed spatial structure, contact reasoning, calibrated uncertainty, or recovery conditions.
- **Objective and Standardization Gap:**
 - **Mixed objectives:** Training objectives (e.g., imitation, prediction, reward maximization) and evaluation metrics are diverse and often unaligned. End-task success alone is an insufficient measure, as it does not attribute performance to specific reasoning capabilities or identify failure modes.
 - **Incompatible protocols:** Data collection, task definitions, supervision schemas, and evaluation protocols differ significantly across projects and datasets. This heterogeneity prevents comparable supervision and the accumulation of reusable physical experience.
 - **Weak attribution:** It is often unclear whether reported performance stems from physical foresight, benchmark regularities, or strong controllers, hindering targeted improvements.
- **Ecosystem and Systems Gap:**
 - **Hidden harness:** The mechanisms that translate model outputs, resolve tools, verify actions, and manage execution (referred to as the "physical harness") are typically project-specific and often implicit.
 - **Isolated platforms:** This lack of standardized interfaces leads to isolated platforms and benchmarks, making it difficult to substitute components (e.g., a controller, a gripper) without requiring extensive retraining or redesign of the entire system.
 - **Limited component reuse:** The entanglement of reasoning, control, and runtime logic prevents components from being independently developed, tested, and reused across different systems.

#### 4.2. Proposed Solutions (The Co-evolution Roadmap Contributions)

To systematically address these diagnosed issues, the paper proposes a detailed roadmap with specific conceptual contributions:

- **WAM Prediction Contract:** This contract specifies that a WAM should expose a "decision-relevant consequence" for a given intervention, including its horizon, reference frame, uncertainty, and validity conditions. This shifts the focus from a mandatory output modality (e.g., video) to the utility of the prediction for downstream decision-making, verification, and planning.
- **Embodied Brain System Model:** The paper defines the "embodied brain" as a long-term target for general physical reasoning. Its key function is to integrate multimodal context, compare interventions, and communicate an *intermediate intent representation* (an intended state transition or capability request, *not* low-level actuator commands). This separates general intelligence from embodiment-specific execution.
- **Physical Harness:** A critical component that mediates between the embodied brain and the physical world. It is responsible for:
 - Grounding the brain's intent in physical reality.
 - Resolving requested capabilities to specific tool models and controllers.
 - Verifying execution preconditions and monitoring outcomes.
 - Recording a detailed "Trace Card" of interactions.
 This explicit separation aims to make execution inspectable and allow for modular component substitution.
- **Experience and Evaluation Contracts:**
 - **Embodiment Card, Task Card, and Trace Card:** These semantic records are proposed to standardize the documentation of robot capabilities, task definitions, and interaction histories. The Trace Card, in particular, captures the full decision context, execution details, and outcomes, enabling replayable and attributable experience.
 - **Layered Evaluation:** A protocol is introduced to evaluate different aspects of physical intelligence (e.g., predictive quality, brain-harness grounding, tool orchestration, safety, traceability) independently. This facilitates precise diagnosis of strengths and weaknesses, moving beyond aggregate end-task scores.
- **Co-evolution Roadmap:** The framework proposes a structured path for connecting brain capabilities, representations, harnesses, tools, data, tasks, and closed-loop learning. It emphasizes that verified interaction traces, filtered through evaluation gates, should provide feedback for continuous improvement of both models and system components.

These contributions collectively define a theoretical and practical framework intended to enable cumulative progress in physical intelligence by promoting modularity, standardization, and rigorous evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this roadmap paper are rooted in its attempt to systematize and provide direction for the complex field of embodied AI and physical intelligence.

#### 5.1. Enabling Cumulative Progress

The most significant impact is its potential to foster cumulative progress in physical intelligence research. By explicitly diagnosing the "three coupled gaps" (model and representation, objective and standardization, ecosystem and systems), the paper articulates specific obstacles to progress. Its proposed solutions, particularly the emphasis on explicit interfaces and contracts (WAM prediction contract, embodied brain intent interface, Embodiment/Task/Trace Cards), aim to create a shared foundation. This could allow individual research contributions (e.g., a new predictive model, a more efficient controller, a robust safety verifier) to be directly comparable, reusable, and composable across different projects and platforms, rather than remaining isolated within specific system stacks.

#### 5.2. Promoting Modularity and Independent Development

The clear functional separation between the "embodied brain" (general reasoning) and the "physical harness" (embodiment-specific execution) is designed to promote modularity. This could enable different research communities to specialize and advance their respective components (e.g., developing better physical reasoning models, more adaptive harness architectures, or robust tool models) independently. A new gripper or controller, for example, could theoretically be integrated by updating the harness and its capability contracts, without requiring a complete retraining or redesign of the central brain model. This modularity is posited to accelerate innovation by reducing interdependencies.

#### 5.3. Improving Debugging, Diagnosis, and Safety

The introduction of Trace Cards and a layered evaluation protocol has the potential to significantly improve the diagnostic capabilities within embodied AI. By recording the full decision context, brain requests, harness translations, verifier decisions, and execution outcomes, researchers could more accurately attribute successes and failures to specific components. This granular attribution is crucial for targeted debugging, understanding the limitations of models, and developing more robust and safer agents. For instance, failures could be localized to a prediction error in the brain, a faulty translation in the harness, an incorrect tool selection, or a controller malfunction, rather than being obscured by a single end-to-end task failure. This increased transparency is particularly important for safety-critical applications.

#### 5.4. Facilitating Adaptive and Self-Improving Agents

The proposed closed-loop learning framework, which feeds verified interaction traces back into model and harness refinement, lays a pathway for truly adaptive and self-improving physical agents. By systematically learning from their own experiences—including successes, near-misses, and failures—agents could continually refine their understanding of the physical world, improve their predictive capabilities, and enhance their ability to ground intent and orchestrate tools. The emphasis on "update gates" and regression tests aims to ensure that such self-improvement maintains or enhances overall capability and safety.

#### 5.5. Guiding Future Research in WAMs and Beyond

The paper redefines WAMs as "promising prototypes for predictive capabilities" needed by a future embodied brain, rather than a final architectural solution. This perspective provides a clear experimental direction for current WAM research, focusing it on generating decision-relevant consequences that can inform downstream reasoning and control. By setting near-term milestones for decision-relevant WAM prediction contracts, grounded brain-harness representations, and adaptive physical harnesses, the roadmap offers concrete objectives for the research community, potentially aligning disparate efforts toward a common vision for general physical intelligence.

In summary, this roadmap's significance lies in its comprehensive diagnosis of systemic issues and its provision of a structured, actionable framework designed to transition embodied AI from disparate, project-specific advances to a cohesive, cumulatively progressing field capable of building adaptive, self-improving agents grounded in real-world physical interaction.
