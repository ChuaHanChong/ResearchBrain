# Multiscale Vision Transformers meet Bipartite Matching for efficient single-stage Action Localization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Queen Mary University of London and Samsung AI Center Cambridge developed BMViT, an encoder-only Multiscale Vision Transformer architecture that applies bipartite matching directly to output tokens for efficient single-stage action localization. The method achieves 30.0 mAP on AVA 2.2, outperforming two-stage MViTv2-S (26.8 mAP) while requiring fewer computational resources, and demonstrates competitive performance against other single-stage approaches.

## Problem

- Existing two-stage action localization methods are computationally inefficient and slow, making them unsuitable for real-time applications due to separate detection and classification stages.
- Current single-stage methods, often based on DETR, introduce significant architectural complexity with encoder-decoder designs and learnable queries, making them challenging to train.
- The task of spatio-temporal action localization itself is complex, requiring sophisticated reasoning beyond static object detection to simultaneously detect bounding boxes and classify actions.

## Method

- The ==BMViT== framework employs a standard ==Multi-scale Vision Transformer (MViTv2-S)== as an encoder-only backbone, directly utilizing its output tokens as prediction slots.
- It eliminates the need for external person detectors or complex encoder-decoder structures by feeding output tokens into simple MLP heads for bounding box, actor likelihood, and action class predictions.
- Training involves a ==direct bipartite matching loss (Hungarian Algorithm)== applied to these output tokens, enabling end-to-end optimization for simultaneous detection and classification.

## Results

- BMViT achieved **30.0 mAP** on AVA 2.2 using MViTv2-S, surpassing the two-stage MViTv2-S (**26.8 mAP**) while requiring significantly fewer FLOPs (**121.2 GFLOPs** vs. **311 GFLOPs** including external detector).
- The method demonstrated competitive or superior performance against leading single-stage methods on AVA 2.2, such as outperforming TubeR (**29.2 mAP**) and STMixer (**27.8 mAP**) with its simpler architecture.
- BMViT generalized well across datasets, achieving **85.6 mAP** on UCF101-24 and **80.7 mAP** on JHMDB51-21 with MViTv2-S, with further improvements when scaling to a ViT-B backbone (**90.7 mAP** on UCF101-24).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A streamlined, encoder-only Vision Transformer architecture, combined with direct bipartite matching, can effectively perform single-stage action localization, challenging the need for complex decoder structures or external detectors.
> - Increasing the spatial resolution of the Vision Transformer's output tokens and employing a strategic selection method for detection and classification tasks significantly improves performance.
> - Training with fixed input aspect ratios enhances model stability and performance by providing a consistent structure for output tokens, which are directly matched to ground truth.
