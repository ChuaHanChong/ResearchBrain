# The Alignment Waltz: Jointly Training Agents to Collaborate for Safety

> [!summary] Summary
> WaltzRL introduces a collaborative multi-agent reinforcement learning framework that jointly trains a conversation agent and a feedback agent to enhance LLM safety. This approach reduces both unsafe content generation and overrefusals, achieving an Attack Success Rate of 4.6% and an Over-Refuse Rate of 9.9% on respective benchmarks while preserving general capabilities.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) face a trade-off where aggressive safety measures to prevent harmful outputs often lead to an increase in overrefusals, declining to answer benign but sensitive prompts.
- Traditional standalone safeguard models (e.g., Llama Guard) act as external filters, often blocking entire responses for dual-use prompts without providing nuanced guidance or a path for improvement.
- Existing multi-agent AI safety approaches frequently adopt competitive dynamics or deploy only a single 'defender' model at inference, limiting collaborative and adaptive content moderation.

## Method

- **WaltzRL**, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework, models safety alignment as a ==collaborative==, positive-sum game between a conversation agent and a feedback agent.
- An iterative feedback protocol enables the feedback agent to provide nuanced, constructive textual suggestions in JSON format, guiding the conversation agent to revise initial unsafe or overrefusing responses.
- A novel Dynamic Improvement Reward (DIR) incentivizes the feedback agent to generate feedback that genuinely improves the conversation agent's subsequent response, conditioned on the feedback agent's accurate identification of issues.

## Results

- WaltzRL reduced the Attack Success Rate (ASR) from **39.0%** to **4.6%** on the WildJailbreak dataset and the Over-Refuse Rate (ORR) from **45.3%** to **9.9%** on the OR-Bench dataset.
- The framework maintained the general capabilities of the conversation agent across benchmarks like AlpacaEval 2.0, IF-Eval, GPQA, MMLU, and TruthfulQA, demonstrating no degradation despite safety-focused RL training.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Dynamic Improvement Reward (DIR) and the multi-agent co-evolution process in achieving superior safety and helpfulness compared to baselines and alternative training strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning LLMs for safety can be achieved through a collaborative, positive-sum multi-agent framework, simultaneously improving both harmlessness and helpfulness rather than presenting a trade-off.
> - Dynamic and specific textual feedback from a specialized agent is more effective at steering models away from overrefusals and towards helpful responses compared to binary classification or template-based guidance.
> - A two-stage training approach, initially focusing on the feedback agent's label accuracy and then enabling joint co-adaptation with a conditioned improvement reward, is crucial for stable and effective collaborative learning.
