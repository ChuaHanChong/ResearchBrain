# Grounding Video Reasoning in Physical Signals

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Queen Mary University of London introduced a diagnostic benchmark for evaluating video reasoning models on physical events, specifically probing whether models truly ground events in time and space rather than just providing correct textual answers. The evaluation framework revealed a persistent weakness in spatial grounding across all tested models and domains, and demonstrated that model performance is highly sensitive to prompt phrasing and visual input perturbations.

## Problem

- Large Vision and Language Models (Video-LLMs) show progress on video understanding but current benchmarks primarily evaluate final answer accuracy, overlooking visual grounding in time and space.
- Existing physical reasoning datasets rely on categorical outputs, allowing models to infer answers from linguistic cues or dataset regularities without localizing physical interactions.
- A correct answer to a video question, especially for physical events, does not necessarily imply visually grounded understanding, which is crucial for real-world applications like robotics.

## Method

- A physics-focused benchmark requiring models to provide a structured ==what–when–where== prediction (event description, temporal interval, spatial bounding box) for physical interactions.
- Three ==prompt families== (`physics`, `vstar like`, `neutral rstr`) evaluate robustness to linguistic variations, and four ==input perturbation conditions== (Original, Shuffled, Ablated, Frame-Masked) diagnose model sensitivity to temporal order, fine appearance, and visual evidence.
- Performance measured via ==Logarithmic Geometric Mean (LGM)== of accuracy, temporal IoU, and spatial IoU, with diagnostic indices (SBI, PRI, SPI).

## Results

- Confirmed persistent spatial grounding weakness (V-STaR failure pattern): no model exceeded mean spatial IoU **0.073**, and though domain leadership varied (**VideoLLaMA3** in Fluids, **Qwen2.5-VL** in Collisions), this weakness held across all six physics domains.
- The `physics` prompt family yielded mean LGM **1.779**, outperforming `vstar like` (**1.139**) and `neutral rstr` (**0.547**).
- Performance "gains" under degraded visual input correlated with weaker original-condition baselines (e.g., **Qwen3-VL** often improved under ablation).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'grounded failure pattern' persists in physical video understanding, where models achieve varying semantic accuracy but consistently struggle with precise temporal and especially spatial localization of events; indeed, across all six physics domains (Gravity, Fluids, Collisions, Deformation, Friction, State Changes) no single model emerged as a universal leader, with spatial grounding remaining a pervasive weakness.
> - Model performance is significantly sensitive to linguistic formulation, with physics-specific prompts generally yielding stronger results than `vstar like` or `neutral rstr` prompts, indicating a lack of robust transfer across semantic framings.
> - Performance 'gains' or robustness under visual perturbations often correlate with weaker original-condition baselines, suggesting that simplified inputs might reduce distractors or reveal specific model limitations rather than universal robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses a persistent challenge in video understanding, particularly concerning large vision and language models (Video-LLMs): the discrepancy between a model's ability to provide a correct textual answer to a question and its actual visual grounding of the event in time and space. While Video-LLMs have demonstrated considerable progress on general video understanding benchmarks such as Video-MME, MVBench, and LongVideoBench, these evaluations primarily focus on the final answer's accuracy rather than the underlying process of visual grounding.

Existing literature on physical reasoning, exemplified by datasets like CLEVRER, IntPhys, and PhysBench, primarily relies on categorical or multiple-choice outputs. This design allows models to perform well by inferring answers from linguistic cues or dataset regularities, potentially without localizing the physical interaction in the video. Concurrently, grounded video understanding benchmarks such as TALL, Charades-STA, ActivityNet Captions, VidSTG, TVQA+, and NExT-GQA emphasize temporal or spatio-temporal localization but are not specifically structured around the complexities of physical events.

A critical precursor to this work is V-STaR, which introduced a diagnostic `what–when–where` evaluation structure. V-STaR demonstrated that high performance on event recognition (`what`) can coexist with poor performance on temporal (`when`) and spatial (`where`) localization. This paper builds directly upon V-STaR's diagnostic approach by extending it to the domain of physical video understanding. It integrates physics-specific event structures, introduces multiple prompt families to test robustness to linguistic variation, and incorporates controlled input perturbations to assess model sensitivity to visual evidence degradation.

This work differentiates itself by combining the strengths of these disparate areas while addressing their limitations. It provides a benchmark that:
1. Moves beyond categorical answers by explicitly requiring spatio-temporal grounding for physical events.
2. Organizes events around six distinct physics domains, enabling more granular analysis of model capabilities.
3. Evaluates models under varying prompt formulations derived from a shared ground truth, diagnosing reliance on prompt-specific cues.
4. Introduces a perturbation analysis framework to assess robustness to degraded visual input, revealing dependencies on temporal order, fine appearance, and continuous evidence.

By doing so, the research aims to provide a more comprehensive and diagnostic evaluation framework for physical video reasoning, thereby enhancing the understanding of whether models truly "see" and localize physical interactions or merely exploit superficial textual or visual patterns.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that a correct answer in video question answering, particularly for physical events, does not necessarily imply visually grounded understanding. Models can accurately name an event like "pouring" or "collision" by leveraging linguistic patterns in the question, without actually identifying the precise temporal span or spatial location of the event within the video. Standard answer-only benchmarks fail to distinguish between these superficial successes and genuinely grounded comprehension.

This distinction is crucial in applications requiring precise interaction with the physical world, such as robotics, video moment retrieval, and embodied decision-making, where merely knowing *what* happened is insufficient without knowing *when* and *where* it occurred. For physical phenomena like motion, contact, force application, and state changes, spatio-temporal localization is fundamental to understanding.

To address this, the authors establish a physics-focused benchmark designed to probe the depth of model understanding. The key objectives are formulated as four research questions (RQs):

- **RQ1: Does the grounded failure pattern identified by V-STaR persist in physical video understanding?** This question investigates whether models still exhibit strong performance on textual event recognition (`what`) while struggling with temporal (`when`) and spatial (`where`) grounding when the focus is on physical interactions.
- **RQ2: How stable is grounded performance across `physics`, `vstar like`, and `neutral rstr` prompt families when the underlying event record is fixed?** This aims to determine if models' ability to ground events is robust to different linguistic formulations of the same underlying physical event, or if performance is highly sensitive to prompt wording.
- **RQ3: What do perturbation gains and losses reveal beyond original-condition performance?** This seeks to understand how degrading visual evidence (by shuffling frames, ablating visual details, or masking frames) affects model performance and what these changes diagnose about a model's reliance on specific visual cues (e.g., temporal order, fine textures).
- **RQ4: Which weaknesses remain even for strong models and favorable prompt families?** This focuses on identifying persistent limitations in current Video-LLMs, even under optimal conditions, to highlight areas for future research and development.

The authors hypothesize that the V-STaR failure pattern will persist, with spatial grounding remaining the weakest component. They also anticipate that the `physics` prompt family will yield the strongest overall performance, `vstar like` will serve as the primary non-physics comparison, and `neutral rstr` will function as a harder control. Furthermore, they expect perturbation gains to primarily occur in cases with weak or moderate original-condition performance, serving as diagnostic indicators of evidence sensitivity rather than universal robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a grounded benchmark for physical video understanding, incorporating a `what–when–where` evaluation structure, multiple prompt families, and perturbation conditions.

#### 3.1. Task Format and Prompt Families

Models are required to produce a single structured prediction comprising:
- `a what`: a short text description of the physical event.
- `a when`: a temporal interval [start_sec, end_sec].
- `a where`: a normalized bounding box [x, y, w, h].
This design ensures that models are forced to provide a coherent, multi-component account, making it more difficult to succeed through partial understanding.

The benchmark evaluates the same underlying grounded event record using three distinct prompt families:
- **`physics`**: Formulated to emphasize physical dynamics and event descriptions. This is the main benchmark regime.
- **`vstar like`**: Designed to mimic the style of V-STaR prompts, serving as a semantic comparison but not an exact reproduction of V-STaR.
- **`neutral rstr`**: A neutrally worded control prompt, functioning as a templated ablation by removing physics-specific phrasing while preserving the grounded event and output schema.

A crucial design choice is that all three prompt families are derived from a single, shared grounded event record, which fixes the event identity, temporal span, and spatial reference. Consequently, `a when` and `a where` are scored against identical targets across all families. For `a what`, the `physics` family uses the full reference event description, while `neutral rstr` and `vstar like` use shorter, family-appropriate semantic targets derived deterministically from the same record.

#### 3.2. Perturbation Conditions

Each base video clip is evaluated under four input conditions to diagnose different aspects of visual evidence reliance:
- **Original**: Unmodified RGB video, establishing baseline performance.
- **Shuffled**: Frames are randomly permuted, maintaining the set of frames but removing temporal order to probe temporal order sensitivity.
- **Ablated**: Each frame is converted to grayscale and blurred, suppressing color and fine texture while retaining coarse spatial structure, to assess dependence on appearance details.
- **Frame-Masked**: Half of the frames are replaced by black frames, removing intermittent evidence while preserving video length and frame rate, to test robustness to missing visual information.
The grounded targets (temporal span and bounding box) remain fixed across all perturbation conditions.

#### 3.3. Metrics and Reporting Conventions

Performance is measured using:
- **Acc**: Text accuracy for `a what`.
- **tIoU**: Temporal Intersection-over-Union for `a when`.
- **sIoU**: Spatial Intersection-over-Union for `a where`.
These three components are combined using the **Logarithmic Geometric Mean (LGM)**, which heavily penalizes poor performance in any single component. The LGM formula is given by $LGM = - \frac{1}{3} \left[ \log(1 - Acc + \epsilon) + \log(1 - tIoU + \epsilon) + \log(1 - sIoU + \epsilon) \right]$, where $\epsilon$ is a small constant. Unlike V-STaR, this study reports LGM directly without additional linear readability scaling. Model outputs that are empty, malformed, or refusal-like are scored as zero and included in the denominator to avoid artificially inflated scores under challenging conditions.

#### 3.4. Diagnostic Indices

Three diagnostic indices are derived from the LGM scores under different conditions:
- **SBI (Shuffled Baseline Index)**: $1 - (LGM_{orig} - LGM_{shuf})$. Measures robustness to temporal shuffling. A value of 1 indicates no change.
- **PRI (Perturbation Retention Index - Ablated)**: $LGM_{abl} / LGM_{orig}$. Measures retention of performance under ablation. A value of 1 indicates performance matches the original.
- **SPI (Perturbation Retention Index - Masked)**: $LGM_{mask} / LGM_{orig}$. Measures retention of performance under frame masking. A value of 1 indicates performance matches the original.
Higher index values generally indicate greater robustness to the respective perturbation, but interpretation is performed alongside component deltas and baseline strata.

#### 3.5. Dataset Construction

The benchmark comprises 1,560 base video clips, which expand to 6,240 scored video-condition pairs. These clips are sourced from four diverse datasets:
- **SSV2 (600 clips)**: Short object-manipulation clips.
- **YouCook2 (600 clips)**: Longer procedural clips with state changes and fluid events.
- **HoloAssist (275 clips)**: Egocentric manipulation scenes, challenging due to camera motion and object overlap.
- **Roundabout-TAU (85 clips)**: Overhead traffic footage, emphasizing motion structure over texture. A filtering step retains only clips with localized physical interactions (e.g., collision-like behavior).

Every clip is assigned to one of six physics domains: Gravity, Fluids, Collisions, Deformation, Friction, and State Changes. The domain distribution is relatively balanced, supporting per-domain analysis.

The annotation pipeline generates a common grounded record for each base clip, containing a reference event description, temporal span, bounding box, and physics-domain label. This record is prompt-family-agnostic. Text annotations are generated using a Qwen3.5-based generator, rewriting source metadata. Temporal spans are from source adapters and event windows. Spatial annotations are generated once on the original clip using GroundingDINO and then reused across all perturbation conditions to ensure a fixed target object reference.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

Ten diverse models are evaluated: Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, Qwen3-VL-Thinking, VideoLLaMA3, InternVideo2.5, InternVL3.5, MiniCPM-o, Qwen3.5, Gemma4, and Molmo2. These include general-purpose VLMs, video-native models, image-centric VLMs, compact multimodal models, and open multimodal LLMs. All models are configured to return a JSON object with the three required fields. Frame budgets follow native model presets, allowing for evaluation in their typical operating modes. Evaluation involves per-sample comparisons, condition-level means, and in-depth analysis of perturbation behavior using confidence intervals, sign tests, baseline-stratified summaries, and qualitative examples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Main Physics Benchmark

The evaluation on the `physics` prompt family under original conditions confirms the V-STaR failure pattern: models achieve varying semantic accuracy (`Acc`) but consistently struggle with temporal (`tIoU`) and especially spatial (`sIoU`) grounding.
- **LGM Performance:** VideoLLaMA3 achieved the highest LGM score (2.634), followed by Qwen3-VL (2.551) and Molmo2 (2.515).
- **Component-wise Performance:** Qwen3-VL showed the highest `Acc` (0.687), while Qwen2.5-VL led in both `tIoU` (0.560) and `sIoU` (0.073).
- **Spatial Grounding Weakness:** Across all models, spatial grounding (`sIoU`) remained the clearest weakness, with no model exceeding a mean `sIoU` of 0.073. This indicates that even models with strong LGM scores struggle to precisely localize physical events in space.

#### 4.2. Prompt-Family Changes and V-STaR-style Prompting

Performance varies significantly across the three prompt families, demonstrating sensitivity to linguistic formulation.
- **Overall Trend:** The `physics` family consistently yielded the strongest overall LGM (mean 1.779), followed by `vstar like` (mean 1.139), and then `neutral rstr` (mean 0.547), confirming the expected ordering of difficulty.
- **Model-Specific Robustness:** Cross-family shifts were model-dependent. VideoLLaMA3 maintained strong performance under `vstar like`. MiniCPM-o 2.6 showed a notable positive shift, with its `vstar like` LGM exceeding its `physics` LGM. Molmo2 also showed meaningful recovery under `vstar like`. In contrast, Qwen3-VL and Qwen3-VL-Thinking exhibited sharp declines in LGM outside the `physics` framing. This suggests that high performance in physics-framed queries does not universally transfer to alternative semantic formulations.

#### 4.3. Interpreting Perturbation Indices

The diagnostic indices (SBI, PRI, SPI) revealed varied responses to visual perturbations.
- **Gains in Weak Baselines:** Values greater than 1 for PRI and SPI, or close to 1 for SBI, indicate that the perturbed input either matched or outscored the original condition. However, these "gains" were more concentrated in models or cases with lower or mid-range original-condition LGM scores. For instance, Qwen3-VL often improved under ablation, while VideoLLaMA3's performance was flatter or declined under shuffled and ablated inputs, despite similar original LGM values.
- **Diagnostic Insights:** This suggests that improvements under perturbation are not always indicative of general robustness but can reflect specific conditions such as reduced distractors, a cleaner temporal signal in certain cases, or models struggling less with simplified inputs when their original baseline performance is weak.

#### 4.4. Domain-Level Findings

Analysis across the six physics domains showed that no single model emerged as a universal leader.
- **Varying Leadership:** Qwen3-VL led in Gravity and Friction, VideoLLaMA3 in Fluids, and Qwen2.5-VL in Collisions, Deformation, and State Changes. This indicates that different models possess varying strengths across physical event types.
- **Persistent Spatial Weakness:** Despite varying domain leadership and model strengths, spatial grounding remained a consistent and significant weakness across all domains and models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a valuable diagnostic benchmark that advances the field of video understanding by shifting the focus from mere answer correctness to truly grounded comprehension of physical events. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Diagnostic Capabilities:** The benchmark's `what–when–where` structure, combined with multiple prompt families and perturbation conditions, offers a more granular and diagnostic evaluation than previous benchmarks. It provides deeper insights into where and why Video-LLMs succeed or fail, moving beyond aggregate accuracy scores to reveal specific vulnerabilities in spatio-temporal grounding and robustness to input variations.

2. **Guiding Model Development:** By clearly identifying spatial grounding as a persistent weakness across diverse models and physical domains, the benchmark provides concrete direction for future model development. Researchers can focus on designing architectures and training methodologies that explicitly improve precise localization of objects and interactions within physical scenes. The observed sensitivity to prompt phrasing and visual perturbations also encourages the development of more robust and generalizable video reasoning models.

3. **Challenging Assumptions about Understanding:** The findings reinforce the critical distinction between linguistic inference and genuine visual grounding. It demonstrates that models can often infer correct event labels from textual cues or statistical regularities without truly understanding the event's spatio-temporal manifestation. This challenges the assumption that high performance on `what` necessarily equates to comprehensive video understanding, particularly in physical contexts.

4. **Implications for Real-World Applications:** For practical applications such as robotics, autonomous systems, and advanced human-computer interaction, precise spatio-temporal grounding of physical events is paramount. A robotic arm needs to know exactly *where* to grasp an object involved in a "pouring" action, and *when* to execute the grasp. This benchmark contributes to developing models that can meet these stringent requirements, bridging the gap between abstract event recognition and actionable physical understanding.

5. **Framework for Robustness Analysis:** The perturbation analysis framework is significant for evaluating model robustness. By systematically degrading visual input, it helps diagnose a model's reliance on specific visual cues like temporal order, texture, or continuous evidence. This provides a methodology for developing and assessing models that can perform reliably even under less-than-ideal real-world conditions (e.g., noisy video feeds, occlusions, complex scenes).

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The benchmark serves as a robust platform for further research into advanced physical reasoning, including causal inference, counterfactual physics, and long-horizon planning. While the current scope focuses on event understanding, the grounded nature of the benchmark provides a solid foundation for extending these capabilities. It also encourages the development of better LLM-based annotation strategies and methods for handling inherent noise and biases in automatically generated data.
