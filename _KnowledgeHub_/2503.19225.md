# CoinFT: A Coin-Sized, Capacitive 6-Axis Force Torque Sensor for Robotic Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> CoinFT is a compact (20 mm diameter, 2g), low-cost (~$11) 6-axis force/torque sensor, developed at Stanford University, that offers mechanical robustness and a 97 Hz mechanical bandwidth. It achieves an average RMSE of 0.16 N for force and 1.08 mNm for moment measurements, enabling delicate contact-rich interactions for platforms such as drones and robotic hands where larger, more fragile, or expensive sensors are impractical.

## Problem

- Commercial 6-axis F/T sensors are often fragile and prone to damage from impacts in dynamic or unstructured robotic environments.
- Existing F/T sensors are typically too bulky, heavy, and expensive for integration into compact platforms like small drones, multi-finger robot hands, or for applications requiring multiple sensing points.
- Alternatives often compromise on robustness, form factor, or ease of fabrication, limiting their widespread practical utility in diverse robotic embodiments.

## Method

- Developed **CoinFT**, a 20 mm diameter, 2 mm thick, 2-gram capacitive 6-axis F/T sensor with a material cost of approximately $11.
- The sensor's core comprises two rigid PCBs connected by an array of silicone rubber pillars, allowing for sensitive measurement of both normal and shear deformations.
- It utilizes a ==Cypress PSoC== microcontroller with a dual-mode electrode-switching strategy to reconfigure electrodes for normal and shear force/torque sensing from 12 raw capacitance signals.

## Results

- CoinFT demonstrated an average RMSE of **0.16** N for force and **1.08** mNm for moment (R² up to **0.998**) when calibrated over ranges up to 14 N normal and 5 N shear.
- The sensor exhibited high robustness, maintaining accuracy for small loads after a 180 N hammer impact and showing consistent calibration after ten **3.3** J drop tests.
- Achieved a mechanical bandwidth of 97 Hz and demonstrated effective thermal compensation using an on-chip sensor, reducing error to below **0.4** N force and **1.5** mN·m torque.
- Successfully applied on a drone for contact-rich tasks, enabling autonomous surface finding and force modulation with an RMS error of **0.1779** N in tracking sinusoidal force commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-mode electrode configuration on a single PCB layer, dynamically reconfigured via a microcontroller, can effectively measure both normal and shear F/T without increasing sensor bulk.
> - Tuning the mechanical properties of the elastomer pillars (e.g., diameter, material) allows for tailoring the sensor's sensitivity and dynamic range to specific application requirements.
> - Combining a robust silicone pillar structure with rigid PCBs provides a mechanically resilient and compact F/T sensor, addressing the fragility common in other precise F/T sensing technologies.
