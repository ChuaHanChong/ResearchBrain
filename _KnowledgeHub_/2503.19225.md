# CoinFT: A Coin-Sized, Capacitive 6-Axis Force Torque Sensor for Robotic Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> CoinFT is a compact (20 mm diameter, 2g), low-cost (~$11) 6-axis force/torque sensor, developed at Stanford University, that offers mechanical robustness and a 97 Hz mechanical bandwidth. It achieves an average RMSE of 0.16 N for force and 1.08 mNm for moment measurements, enabling delicate contact-rich interactions for platforms such as drones and robotic hands where larger, more fragile, or expensive sensors are impractical.

## Problem

- Commercial 6-axis F/T sensors are often fragile and prone to damage from impacts in dynamic or unstructured robotic environments.
- Existing F/T sensors are typically too bulky, heavy, and expensive for integration into compact platforms like small drones, multi-finger robot hands, or for applications requiring multiple sensing points.
- Alternatives often compromise on robustness, form factor, or ease of fabrication, limiting their widespread practical utility in diverse robotic embodiments.

## Method

- Developed **CoinFT**, a 20 mm diameter, 2 mm thick, 2-gram capacitive 6-axis F/T sensor with a material cost of approximately $11.
- The sensor's core comprises two rigid PCBs connected by an array of silicone rubber pillars, allowing for sensitive measurement of both normal and shear deformations.
- It utilizes a ==Cypress PSoC== microcontroller with a dual-mode electrode-switching strategy to reconfigure electrodes for normal and shear force/torque sensing from 12 raw capacitance signals.

## Results

- CoinFT demonstrated an average RMSE of **0.16** N force and **1.08** mNm moment (R² up to **0.998**), a **97 Hz** mechanical bandwidth, and thermal compensation reducing error below **0.4** N force and **1.5** mN·m torque.
- The sensor exhibited high robustness, maintaining accuracy for small loads after a 180 N hammer impact and showing consistent calibration after ten **3.3** J drop tests.
- Successfully applied on a drone for contact-rich tasks, enabling autonomous surface finding and force modulation with an RMS error of **0.1779** N in tracking sinusoidal force commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-mode electrode configuration on a single PCB layer, dynamically reconfigured via a microcontroller, can effectively measure both normal and shear F/T without increasing sensor bulk.
> - Tuning the mechanical properties of the elastomer pillars (e.g., diameter, material) allows for tailoring the sensor's sensitivity and dynamic range to specific application requirements.
> - Combining a robust silicone pillar structure with rigid PCBs provides a mechanically resilient and compact F/T sensor, addressing the fragility common in other precise F/T sensing technologies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate force and torque (F/T) measurement is a fundamental requirement for robots to perform contact-rich tasks safely and effectively across various applications, including assembly, manipulation, and delicate interactions. The broader research landscape for 6-axis F/T sensors encompasses diverse transduction methods such as piezoresistive, capacitive, optical, magnetic, and pneumatic approaches.

Commercially available 6-axis F/T sensors, such as those from ATI Industrial Automation, MinebeaMitsumi, or ReSense, offer precise measurement capabilities, often leveraging piezoresistive technology for high signal-to-noise ratios. However, these commercial solutions typically present several challenges that limit their widespread adoption across all robotic platforms:
- **Fragility:** Many commercial sensors are susceptible to damage from impacts, which can occur frequently in dynamic or unstructured environments (e.g., drone crashes, incidental contact for robot end-effectors).
- **Size and Form Factor:** Their thickness and overall bulk can add unnecessary mass and volume, making them unsuitable for compact platforms like small drones, multi-finger anthropomorphic robot hands, or slim wearable haptic devices.
- **Cost:** The high cost of these sensors often prohibits their use in applications requiring multiple sensing points, such as an anthropomorphic hand equipped with sensors on each fingertip.

Research efforts have explored alternatives. Piezoresistive sensors, while precise, are often noted for their fragility. Capacitive sensing has gained attention for its potential to achieve compact form factors and high sensitivity, typically through single-layer designs (which can be complex to fabricate) or multi-layer electrode stacks (which can be bulky). Optical methods, including light intensity, Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG), and vision-based techniques, offer robust options but may involve complex setups or larger form factors. Other methods like piezoelectric (limited for static loads) and magnetic sensing (vulnerable to external noise) also have specific limitations.

This work, introducing CoinFT, is positioned to address these gaps by developing a 6-axis F/T sensor that simultaneously offers compactness, low cost, mechanical robustness, and reliable performance. By combining these features, CoinFT aims to enable advanced contact-rich interactions in applications where existing solutions are inadequate due to fragility, bulk, or cost, thereby expanding the practical utility of 6-axis F/T sensing in diverse robotic embodiments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a 6-axis force/torque (F/T) sensor, named CoinFT, that overcomes significant limitations of existing commercial and research-grade sensors, thereby facilitating more widespread and practical deployment in robotics.

The motivation for developing CoinFT stems from several unaddressed needs in the robotics community:

1. **Enabling Contact-Rich Tasks on Diverse Platforms:** Precise F/T measurement is critical for robots to safely and effectively perform tasks requiring interaction with the environment. However, many robotic platforms, such as small drones, multi-finger robot hands, and wearable haptic devices, cannot readily integrate existing F/T sensors due to their form factor or other characteristics.
2. **Addressing Size and Weight Constraints:** Commercial 6-axis F/T sensors are often too bulky and heavy for integration into compact and lightweight robotic systems. For instance, small drones require extremely light components to maximize flight time and payload capacity. Robot fingertips and haptic devices demand slim designs to avoid altering geometry or becoming cumbersome. CoinFT aims to provide a solution that is comparable in size to a US quarter-dollar coin and weighs only 2 grams.
3. **Improving Robustness for Unstructured Environments:** Many robotic applications involve incidental impacts or unpredictable contacts. Commercial sensors, despite their precision, are frequently fragile and prone to damage (e.g., plastic deformation or failure) from such events. This fragility limits their deployment in unstructured, real-world environments where robustness against impacts (like drone crashes or unexpected contact on an end-effector) is crucial. CoinFT is motivated by the need for a sensor that can reliably withstand these conditions.
4. **Reducing Cost for Scalable Deployment:** The high cost of commercial 6-axis F/T sensors (often exceeding $1000) makes it economically prohibitive to sensorize multiple points on a robot, such as individual fingers of an anthropomorphic robot hand. A low-cost solution is necessary to enable widespread research and application of multi-point force sensing. CoinFT aims for a material cost of less than $11 per unit.
5. **Achieving a Balance of Desirable Features:** The overarching motivation is to develop a sensor that combines all these desirable attributes—compactness, lightness, low cost, and robustness—with sufficient accuracy for 6-axis F/T measurement within a practical dynamic range. Existing solutions typically excel in one or two of these areas but compromise on others.

To validate these objectives, the research sets out to:
- Design, fabricate, and comprehensively characterize CoinFT's performance, including its accuracy, dynamic range, bandwidth, robustness, and thermal stability.
- Demonstrate CoinFT's utility through representative applications:
 - A multi-axial contact-probing experiment to showcase its accuracy in scenarios relevant to fine manipulation with robot fingertips.
 - An attitude-based force-control task on a drone to illustrate its suitability for contact-rich aerial interactions.
- Open-source the design, fabrication details, and firmware of CoinFT to encourage broader adoption and further research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the design, fabrication, characterization, and application of CoinFT, a capacitive 6-axis F/T sensor.

**Sensor Design (CoinFT):**
CoinFT features a circular sensing area, 20 mm in diameter and approximately 2 mm thick, weighing 2 grams. Its core structure comprises two 0.8 mm thick rigid Printed Circuit Boards (PCBs) connected by an array of 127 μm tall cylindrical pillars. These pillars are cast from TAP® Silicone RTV Mold-Making System, selected for its mechanical properties, providing sensitivity in both normal and shear directions without compromising bandwidth. The radial density of the pillars increases towards the PCB edges to enhance delamination resistance.

The electrical architecture involves specialized electrodes on each PCB. The upper sensing layer contains eight comb-shaped electrodes, while the lower sensing layer has two electrodes. Passive shielding is provided by a ground plane on the reverse side of the PCBs and a 127 μm thick flexible PCB (fPCB) top shield layer. An electronics tab (10 mm × 11 mm) houses a Cypress PSoC™ 4100S microcontroller, which uses its native capacitance-to-digital conversion (CDC) module to measure capacitance at 360 Hz. Data can be acquired via serial communication or a USB port through a converter.

**Working Principle:**
CoinFT measures 6-axis F/T by monitoring capacitance changes between the upper and lower sensing layers. Capacitance ($C = \epsilon A/d$) is influenced by the dielectric constant ($\epsilon$), electrode overlapping area ($A$), and distance ($d$) between electrodes. The microcontroller dynamically reconfigures the electrode connections between two modes:
- **Normal Mode:** The eight electrodes in the upper sensing layer are internally connected to form four distinct electrodes (Z1, Z2, Z3, Z4), while the two lower electrodes are grounded. This configuration is sensitive to changes in $d$, responding to normal force (Fz) (uniform signal increase) and moments (Mx, My) (differential signal patterns).
- **Shear Mode:** The upper sensing layer electrodes function independently (X1-X4, Y1-Y4), while the lower layer electrodes alternate between ground and an active shield. This mode is primarily sensitive to changes in $A$ caused by lateral shifts, thus measuring shear forces (Fx, Fy) and torque (Mz). Comb-shaped electrodes are arranged for directional sensitivity (X-direction in quadrants 1 and 3, Y-direction in quadrants 2 and 4). Active shielding mitigates stray capacitance.

**Sensor Fabrication:**
The fabrication process involves several steps (Fig. 3):
1. A UV laser-cut polyimide film mask defines pillar locations on an acrylic plate.
2. Uncured silicone rubber is spread into the mask cavities.
3. A primed lower PCB is placed on the mask and compressed with a 3.1 kg weight for 3 minutes to minimize pillar base thickness.
4. The assembly is cured in a pressure chamber at approximately 414 kPa for at least 12 hours with a 100 g mass to remove micro-bubbles.
5. The upper PCB is hand-soldered with components, primed, and then coated with spin-coated uncured silicone rubber (5000 rpm for 75 seconds).
6. The pillar layer is assembled with the upper PCB using a 203 μm polyimide film spacer to control distance, and cured in a 65°C oven for 72 hours with a 100 g mass.
7. Finally, the fPCB top shield layer is adhered to the upper PCB. The total material cost is approximately $11.

**Sensor Characterization:**
- **Finite Element Analysis (FEA):** ANSYS was used for structural analysis of pillar deformation, and COMSOL for electrostatics analysis of capacitance change. A linear elastic model with the Gent model for Young's modulus (derived from Shore hardness) and an incompressible disk model for effective Young's modulus were used. FEA results were validated against real sample responses to normal force.
- **Calibration:** CoinFT was mounted on a commercial ATI Gamma sensor (ground truth). Human-applied forces and torques provided diverse loading conditions. A second-order least-squares regression model was trained on ten 35-second trials and tested on one additional trial to map 12 raw capacitance signals to 6 F/T values.
- **Sensitivity and Dynamic Range Tuning:** The effect of pillar diameter (100, 150, 200 μm) on sensor response was investigated via FEA and experiments, demonstrating that larger diameters decrease sensitivity but increase dynamic range. Other parameters like pillar number, material, and arrangement can also be adjusted.
- **Dynamic Response:** A dual-mode muscle lever (Aurora Scientific Inc., 309C) applied sinusoidal forces (0-120 Hz, 0-2 N normal, 0.1-1.1 N shear) to determine mechanical bandwidth.
- **Robustness:**
 - **Hammer Impact Test:** CoinFT's ability to measure small loads (1-3 N) was tested after a 180 N impact.
 - **Drop Test:** A 0.53 kg chrome steel ball was dropped from 0.63 m ten times, and calibration consistency was evaluated before and after.
 - **Tensile Strength Test:** Five samples were tested for ultimate tensile strength.
- **Thermal Effects:** Baseline capacitance drift (1-3% per 10°C) was characterized in a temperature-controlled chamber. A second-order polynomial fit, incorporating the PSoC's on-chip temperature sensor, was used for compensation during inference.

**Demonstrations:**
- **Multi-axial Sensing for Robot Manipulators:** CoinFT, mounted beneath a hemispherical fingertip, measured F/T applied by a 7-DoF robotic arm (Flexiv Rizon 4), with an ATI Gamma sensor providing ground truth. A CoinFT with 300 μm pillars was used for a larger input range.
- **Contact Force Modulation of Aerial Vehicles:**
 - **Drone Setup:** A 127 mm frame quadrotor equipped with an UpBoard companion computer, PixRacer flight controller, and CoinFT on a custom compliant mount with a telescoping mechanism and rubber bands. Motion capture provided accurate pose/orientation.
 - **End-Effectors:** A general contact tip and an electronics package (95g) with pressure-sensitive adhesives.
 - **Attitude Controller:** A switching, cascaded PID controller ran on the companion computer at 20 Hz, operating at the attitude control level (commanded normalized thrust and orientation). It used position/velocity errors to compute a desired force vector, which was then projected onto the drone's observed Z-axis to derive a desired thrust. A state machine (FREE, SEARCH, HOLD) dynamically switched gains and regulated thrust based on CoinFT feedback, allowing for autonomous surface finding and force tracking.
 - **Force Controlled Deployment:** The drone performed a task of attaching an electronics package to a horizontal surface by autonomously detecting contact, ramping up to a commanded force, confirming adhesion failure/success via CoinFT, and activating the package via WiFi.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research successfully developed and characterized CoinFT, a capacitive 6-axis F/T sensor, demonstrating its performance across various metrics and applications.

- **Physical Characteristics:** CoinFT is notably compact, with a 20 mm diameter and approximately 2 mm thickness. It weighs 2 grams, increasing to 6.7 grams including wiring and electronics for integration. The material cost of CoinFT is approximately $11 per unit.
- **Calibration Accuracy:**
 - When calibrated over an input range of 0-14 N normal force and 0-5 N shear force, CoinFT achieved an average root-mean-squared error (RMSE) of 0.16 N for force measurements and 1.08 mNm for moment measurements.
 - The coefficient of determination (R²) values were consistently high, ranging from 0.983 (Fx, Fy) to 0.998 (Fz) for forces, and from 0.986 (Mz) to 0.989 (Mx) for moments, indicating a strong correlation with ground truth data (Table III).
 - The minimum detectable force was determined to be 20 mN.
 - The inclusion of normal mode electrodes in the calibration process significantly improved accuracy, particularly for Fz, Mx, and My.
- **Sensitivity and Dynamic Range:** The study demonstrated that the sensor's sensitivity and dynamic range can be adjusted by modifying the pillar diameter. Larger pillar diameters decrease sensitivity but increase the maximum measurable force and moment, as shown by both FEA and experimental results. This adaptability allows CoinFT to be tuned for different application requirements.
- **Dynamic Response (Bandwidth):**
 - The mechanical bandwidth of CoinFT was determined to be approximately 97 Hz.
 - In the normal direction, the magnitude response remained above 70.7% (-3dB) of the input signal across the entire 0-120 Hz swept frequency range, with a phase lag of -34.6° at 120 Hz.
 - In the shear direction, the magnitude response reached 70.7% (-3dB) at 97 Hz, with a phase lag of -94.2°.
- **Robustness:**
 - CoinFT demonstrated reliable robustness against impacts. It maintained accurate readings of small loads (1-3 N) even after sustaining a 180 N impact from a hammer.
 - A controlled drop test, involving ten impacts from a 0.53 kg steel ball (3.3 J impact energy), showed that the sensor's calibration performance remained consistent, with an MSE of 0.055 N and R² of 0.982 compared to reference forces.
 - The average ultimate tensile strength was 2.84 MPa (143 N), with a standard deviation of 1.21 MPa (61 N).
- **Thermal Stability:** Without compensation, raw capacitance signals exhibited a baseline drift of 1-3% per 10°C. Implementing a second-order polynomial compensation method using the PSoC's on-chip temperature sensor successfully reduced residual force error to below 0.4 N and torque error to below 1.5 mN·m across varying temperatures.
- **Demonstration in Robot Manipulators:**
 - During multi-axial contact-probing experiments with a hemispherical fingertip mounted on a 7-DoF robotic arm, CoinFT accurately measured applied forces and torques.
 - For an input range of 0-30 N normal, 0-7.5 N shear, and 0-200 mN·m moment, Fz achieved an RMSE of 0.378 N (R² 0.999), while Mx and My showed RMSEs of 5.669 mNm (R² 0.993) and 3.874 mNm (R² 0.996) respectively (Table IV).
- **Demonstration in Aerial Vehicles:**
 - A drone equipped with CoinFT (6.7g total) successfully performed contact-rich tasks using an attitude-based switching cascaded PID controller.
 - The controller enabled autonomous surface finding (FREE and SEARCH states) and accurate force modulation (HOLD state), achieving an RMS error of 0.1779 N when tracking a sinusoidal force command.
 - The drone successfully demonstrated attaching a 95g electronics package to a surface by applying controlled forces. It autonomously detected insufficient adhesion and reapplied a higher force, confirming successful deployment via CoinFT feedback and signaling package activation.
 - CoinFT's performance remained stable on the drone, with no significant increase in noise or signal degradation despite potential electromagnetic interference from motors.
- **Open-Source Availability:** The design, fabrication instructions, and firmware of CoinFT have been open-sourced, facilitating reproducibility and further development by the robotics community.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of CoinFT is significant as it addresses several practical limitations inherent in existing 6-axis F/T sensors, thereby enabling a broader range of robotic applications and potentially democratizing access to multi-axial force sensing technology.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

1. **Bridging the Gap in Sensor Capabilities:** CoinFT provides a unique combination of attributes—compact size (coin-sized, 2 mm thick), light weight (2g), low cost (under $11 BOM), mechanical robustness, and 6-axis F/T sensing capability. This blend of features distinguishes it from most commercial sensors (which are often expensive and fragile) and many research prototypes (which may lack robustness or broader applicability).
2. **Enabling Novel Robotic Applications:**
 - **Drones:** Its lightweight and robust design makes CoinFT highly suitable for drones, which typically cannot carry heavy or fragile sensors. This enables drones to perform contact-rich tasks like environmental sampling, surface cleaning, and precise object deployment in unstructured and potentially hazardous environments, where impacts or crashes are common.
 - **Robot Manipulators and Dexterous Hands:** The compact and slim form factor allows for integration into robot fingertips and anthropomorphic hands without significantly altering their geometry or increasing bulk. This facilitates fine-grained manipulation, force-informed actions from demonstrations, and intrinsic tactile sensing for object interaction. Its low cost makes it feasible to instrument multiple fingers.
 - **Wearable Haptic Devices:** CoinFT's small size and sensitivity are beneficial for wearable haptic interfaces, enabling precise force and torque feedback for user studies and personalized haptic experiences, which can account for individual variations in body shape and contact mechanics.
3. **Enhanced System Robustness and Reduced Operational Cost:** The demonstrated resilience of CoinFT against significant impacts (e.g., hammer blows, drop tests) is critical for real-world robotic deployments where accidental collisions are inevitable. This robustness reduces the risk of sensor damage, thereby lowering maintenance costs and increasing operational reliability compared to more fragile commercial alternatives. The low unit cost further facilitates replacement if damage does occur.
4. **Advancing Capacitive Sensing Methods:** The dual-mode electrode-switching strategy for multi-axial sensing, which efficiently utilizes a single pair of sensing electrodes through programmable reconfiguration, presents a methodological advancement in capacitive sensor design. This approach can inspire future designs for compact and versatile tactile sensors, particularly with the increasing availability of programmable capacitance sensing microcontrollers.
5. **Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to open-source CoinFT's design, fabrication instructions, and firmware significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers. This promotes wider adoption, encourages further innovation, and facilitates the integration of 6-axis F/T sensing into diverse research and development projects without the constraints of proprietary or expensive hardware.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
While offering considerable versatility, CoinFT is not intended as a direct replacement for high-end commercial F/T sensors that offer significantly higher dynamic range, finer force resolution, and higher mechanical bandwidth (e.g., the ATI Gamma, which has a 1 kHz bandwidth compared to CoinFT's 97 Hz). The paper acknowledges some challenges, such as potential minor elastomer creep over prolonged large loads, incomplete shielding due to exposed surface-mounted electrodes, and possible out-of-plane deformation of thin PCBs under local pressure. However, these are presented as areas for future refinement.

The potential impact of CoinFT lies in its ability to make robust, multi-axial force sensing accessible and practical for a broad spectrum of robotic platforms and applications where cost, size, weight, and robustness are primary considerations, even if sacrificing some peak performance metrics. It serves as a valuable tool for expanding the capabilities of robots in contact-rich, real-world interactions.
