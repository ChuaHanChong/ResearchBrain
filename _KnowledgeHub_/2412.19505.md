# DrivingWorld: Constructing World Model for Autonomous Driving via Video GPT

> [!summary] Summary
> DrivingWorld is a GPT-style autoregressive world model for autonomous driving that decouples temporal and spatial-modality attention, uses a temporal-aware VQ tokenizer plus next-state then next-token prediction, and adds masking/reweighting for long-horizon stability. It generates coherent driving videos beyond 40 seconds, 2x longer than prior state-of-the-art, with more accurate controllable prediction.

## Problem

- Classic GPT frameworks handle 1D contextual data like text and lack inherent capacity to model the spatial-temporal dynamics needed for coherent video generation.
- GAIA-1 extends autoregressive next-token prediction to driving video but produces low-fidelity outputs with visible artifacts and large parameter/compute growth as condition frames increase.
- Diffusion-based driving world models struggle to enforce precise controllable generation, while GPT-based methods scale quadratically in compute with sequence length.

## Method

- ==Temporal-aware vector quantized tokenizer== inserts ==temporal self-attention== before and after ==VQGAN== quantization so image tokens stay consistent across frames rather than being encoded independently.
- ==Temporal-multimodal fusion module== decouples a ==causal temporal-attention layer== from a ==bidirectional multimodal fusion layer==, then an ==internal-state autoregressive module== predicts next-state tokens (==next-state then next-token prediction==).
- Long-term controllable strategies: ==random masking strategy== (token dropout during training) fights drift, and ==balanced attention== upweights sparse pose tokens against dense image tokens for precise trajectory control. Together these form **DrivingWorld**.

## Results

- Generates up to **640** frames at 10Hz (**64** seconds) conditioned on just **15** frames, over **2x** longer than prior state-of-the-art driving world models (max **15s**/**150** frames for Vista).
- On NuScenes zero-shot evaluation achieves FID **7.4** and FVD **90.9**, comparable to SVD-based Vista (FID **6.9**, FVD **89.4**) despite training the GPT framework from scratch versus billions of pretraining images.
- Ablations show the temporal-aware tokenizer beats Llama-Gen finetuned (FVD **14.66** vs **20.33**), and the decoupled structure cuts GPT-2 FVD40 from **4017.15** to **637.60** while using far less GPU memory (**34.3GB** vs OOM at 15 frames).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling temporal causal attention from spatial multimodal fusion lets an autoregressive video model scale to long horizons without the quadratic memory blowup of flattening all tokens into one 1D GPT sequence.
> - Random token dropout during training, simulating inference-time prediction errors, is a cheap general fix for autoregressive drift in long-horizon generative rollouts.
> - Deliberately imbalancing attention weights toward low-cardinality control signals (pose tokens) prevents them from being drowned out by high-cardinality perceptual tokens (image patches) in joint multimodal sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The "DrivingWorld" paper situates itself at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: autonomous driving, generative AI (specifically autoregressive models like GPT), and vision-based world models.

**Autonomous Driving and Prediction:** A critical challenge in autonomous driving is the ability to accurately predict future events and scenarios. Traditional prediction models often rely on vast amounts of labeled data, making them vulnerable to out-of-distribution events, long-tail scenarios, and rare, safety-critical cases (like accidents) where data is scarce. Unsupervised learning through world models offers a promising alternative by learning comprehensive environmental dynamics from unlabeled data, thereby enhancing robustness, generalization, and safety.

**World Models:** The concept of a "world model," which captures a representation of the environment and forecasts future states, has been extensively explored in reinforcement learning, particularly in game environments (e.g., Dreamer series) and more recently in robotics (e.g., DayDreamer). Extending these to complex, real-world driving scenarios presents unique challenges due to the high dimensionality of visual data, intricate spatial-temporal dynamics, and the need for precise control. Prior driving world models (e.g., Drive-WM, WoVoGen, ADriver-I, GenAD, Vista) have used various approaches, including diffusion models, but often face limitations in the duration, fidelity, or controllability of generated predictions.

**Generative AI and GPT-style Models:** The extraordinary success of autoregressive (AR) models like the GPT series in natural language processing (NLP) has inspired efforts to replicate this success in visual domains. The idea is to transform visual data into sequences of tokens and apply AR generation. GAIA-1 was a pioneering work in extending the GPT framework to video for autonomous driving, aiming to build a video-based world model. However, a significant gap identified by the "DrivingWorld" authors is that the classic GPT framework, designed for 1D text sequences, inherently struggles to model the complex spatial and temporal dynamics essential for high-fidelity video generation. This leads to issues like low visual quality and noticeable artifacts in prior attempts.

**Video Generation:** The paper also contributes to the broader field of video generation. It compares its GPT-based approach with GAN-based (prone to mode collapse, training instability) and diffusion-based methods (lack precise control due to stochasticity). The authors acknowledge that traditional GPT-based methods for video can be computationally expensive due to quadratic scaling with sequence length.

"DrivingWorld" directly addresses the limitations of existing GPT-style world models for driving by introducing novel mechanisms to effectively model spatio-temporal dynamics and enhance temporal coherence. It aims to overcome the "1D context" limitation of vanilla GPT, achieve significantly longer generation durations, and provide more precise control, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in autonomous driving world models and controllable video synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "DrivingWorld" stems from the shortcomings of existing autoregressive (AR) generative models when applied to complex visual tasks like autonomous driving video prediction. While AR models have revolutionized natural language processing, their direct application to video (as seen in models like GAIA-1) has yielded unsatisfactory results due to their inherent design for 1D contextual information, failing to adequately capture the critical spatial and temporal dynamics of video.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of "DrivingWorld" are:

1. **Enhance Spatial and Temporal Dynamics Modeling:** To overcome the limitations of 1D GPT frameworks, the primary objective is to develop a GPT-style world model specifically designed with spatial-temporal fusion mechanisms that can effectively model both the spatial information within each video frame and the temporal coherence across consecutive frames.
2. **Achieve High-Fidelity, Long-Duration Video Generation:** The authors aim to significantly extend the capability of driving world models to generate realistic and consistent future video sequences for much longer durations. Specifically, they target generating videos exceeding 40 seconds, a substantial improvement over prior state-of-the-art models that are often limited to much shorter clips.
3. **Enable Precise Controllable Future Scenario Generation:** A crucial requirement for autonomous driving is the ability to generate future scenarios conditioned on specific ego-vehicle actions (e.g., different trajectory paths). The model intends to achieve accurate and precise control over the generated driving scenarios, allowing for the manipulation of vehicle location and orientation.
4. **Mitigate Long-Term Drifting Issues and Enhance Robustness:** Autoregressive generation, especially over long sequences, is prone to error accumulation, leading to content drifting and degradation over time. A key objective is to introduce strategies that enhance the model's generalization ability and robustness, thereby preventing these drifting issues.
5. **Improve Computational Efficiency:** While leveraging a GPT-style framework, the authors also aim to design a model that is more computationally efficient and scalable than vanilla GPT for long video sequences, addressing the quadratic cost associated with traditional approaches.

By addressing these objectives, "DrivingWorld" seeks to build a more robust, generalizable, and practically applicable world model that can significantly contribute to the development of safer and more intelligent autonomous driving systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"DrivingWorld" introduces a novel GPT-style world model architecture for autonomous driving, built upon several key innovations designed to address the challenges of spatio-temporal modeling and long-duration video generation.

**Overall Pipeline:** The model takes historical states—comprising vehicle orientation ($\theta_t$), location ($(x_t, y_t)$), and a front-view image ($I_t$)—as input. It tokenizes this multimodal information into a unified latent space, uses a GPT-style world model to forecast future states, and then decodes these future tokens back into predicted orientations, locations, and images. This autoregressive process allows for the generation of long video sequences.

#### 3.1. Tokenizer

To convert continuous multimodal data into discrete tokens suitable for a GPT-style model, "DrivingWorld" proposes:
- **Temporal-aware Vector Quantized Tokenizer:** Standard single-image VQ tokenizers struggle with temporal consistency across frames. To overcome this, the authors embed temporal dependencies by inserting self-attention layers both *before and after* VQGAN quantization. These attention layers operate along the temporal dimension, enabling the model to capture long-term relationships between frames and ensure coherent embeddings. This builds upon an open-source VQGAN implementation from LlamaGen and is fine-tuned for driving scenes.
- **Vehicle Pose Tokenizer:** Instead of using absolute global poses, which can lead to normalization difficulties and reduced robustness over long sequences, the model uses *relative* poses between adjacent time steps ($\Delta\theta_t$, $\Delta x_t$, $\Delta y_t$). These relative values are then discretized into categories and bins, respectively, to form discrete tokens.

#### 3.2. World Model (Hybrid Token Prediction Strategy)

The core of "DrivingWorld" is its hybrid prediction strategy, which decouples spatio-temporal processing for efficiency and improved performance, unlike vanilla GPT-2 that treats all tokens as a 1D sequence.
- **Temporal-multimodal Fusion Module:** This module comprises alternating temporal and multimodal layers.
 - **Temporal Layer:** Utilizes a causal attention mask where each token attends only to itself and tokens at the *same sequential position from all previous frames*. This effectively captures long-range temporal dependencies.
 - **Multimodal Layer:** Employs a bidirectional mask *within the same frame*, allowing tokens from different modalities (pose and image) at the same time step to fully interact and integrate internal-state information.
 This decoupled design enhances training and inference speed while reducing GPU memory consumption.
- **Internal-state Autoregressive Module:** After the fusion module generates features for the next frame, this module performs *next-token prediction* within that future state. It takes the output features from the fusion module and sequentially predicts the pose and image tokens for the next time step using causal attention. This autoregressive generation *within* a state is inspired by works showing its effectiveness for high-quality image generation, even outperforming diffusion methods. The training incorporates two teacher-forcing strategies: one at the frame level and another at the internal-state level. The entire world model is trained using a cross-entropy loss.

#### 3.3. Decoder

Once the next-state tokens are predicted by the world model:
- **Vehicle Pose Decoder:** Inverts the discretization process from the tokenizer to recover the continuous relative orientation and location values.
- **Temporal-aware Decoder:** Retrieves corresponding features from the codebook using the predicted image tokens and uses a temporal self-attention layer (consistent with the tokenizer) to reconstruct the high-fidelity, temporally consistent front-view image.

#### 3.4. Long-term Controllable Generation Strategies

To ensure robustness and precise control over long durations:
- **Token Dropout for Drifting-free Autoregression (Random Masking Strategy - RMS):** To combat the "content drifting" problem (where models trained on perfect ground truth struggle when conditioned on imperfect generated tokens during inference), a random masking strategy is introduced. During training, some ground-truth tokens are randomly replaced with other random tokens (50% chance per token, applied to the entire conditioning sequence with 30% probability). This forces the model to learn to handle noisy inputs, significantly mitigating drifting.
- **Balanced Attention for Precise Control:** Given the imbalance between numerous image tokens (512) and few pose tokens (2), the model could overlook crucial ego-state signals. To address this, the authors manually increase the attention weights of the orientation (0.4) and location (0.2) tokens (before the softmax layer). This prioritizes ego-state information, enabling more precise control over the generated trajectory. QK-norm and 2D-rotary positional encoding are also incorporated for training stability and performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate "DrivingWorld"'s superior performance in long-duration, high-fidelity, and controllable video generation for autonomous driving.

**Long-time Video Generation:**
- A key achievement is the ability to generate videos over **40 seconds (400 frames at 10 Hz)**, and even up to 64 seconds (640 frames) from just 15 conditioning frames. This is a significant leap compared to state-of-the-art models, which typically generate much shorter clips (e.g., 15 seconds for Vista, 4 seconds for GenAD).
- The generated videos exhibit strong temporal consistency and preserve 3D structural integrity throughout, effectively addressing the drifting and degradation issues commonly seen in long-duration autoregressive video generation.

**Quantitative Comparison of Generated Videos (NuScenes Dataset):**
- "DrivingWorld" achieves **comparable state-of-the-art performance** in metrics like Frechet Video Distance (FVD) and Frechet Inception Distance (FID) on the NuScenes validation set (FID: 7.4; FVD: 90.9). Notably, NuScenes is a *zero-shot* test set for "DrivingWorld," while many compared methods were trained on it.
- The model significantly surpasses competitors in terms of maximum generation duration (40s/400 frames versus 15s/150 for Vista, 4s/48 for DriveDreamer).
- Even without access to private training data ("DrivingWorld (w/o P)"), the model shows competitive performance (FID: 16.4; FVD: 174.4), underscoring the effectiveness of its architecture.

**Qualitative Comparison of Generated Videos:**
- Visually, "DrivingWorld" produces videos with superior temporal consistency compared to methods like Stable Video Diffusion (SVD). It maintains finer details, such as street lanes and the identity of other vehicles, more effectively over extended sequences.

**Quantitative Comparison of Image Tokenizers (NuPlan Dataset):**
- The proposed **temporal-aware VQVAE tokenizer** demonstrates superior performance across all evaluation metrics (FVD, FID, PSNR, LPIPS) when compared to other VQVAE methods like VAR, VQGAN, and even fine-tuned Llama-Gen. This highlights its effectiveness in capturing temporal consistency for driving scenes.

**Ablation Studies (Smaller NuPlan Dataset):**
- **Random Masking Strategy (RMS):** Crucially, disabling the RMS during training ("w/o Masking") leads to a substantial performance degradation, with FVD scores increasing by 4% to 32% across different video lengths. This confirms that RMS effectively mitigates the content drifting problem in long-term autoregressive generation, enhancing robustness.
- **Comparison with Vanilla GPT-2:**
 - **Memory Usage:** "DrivingWorld" is significantly more memory-efficient than vanilla GPT-2. GPT-2's memory consumption grows quadratically with sequence length, leading to "Out of Memory" (OOM) errors for sequences of 15 frames, while "DrivingWorld" maintains stable and much lower memory usage (45.873 GB for 15 frames).
 - **Performance:** Our method dramatically outperforms GPT-2 in FVD scores, indicating superior video generation quality and coherence (e.g., FVD$_{40}$: 637.60 for Ours vs. 4017.15 for GPT-2).
- **Number of Condition Frames:** Experiments show a consistent improvement in FVD scores as the number of conditioning frames increases (e.g., from 5 to 25 frames), indicating that more temporal context helps the model generate more precise outputs.
- **Internal-state Autoregressive Module:** Removing this module leads to a noticeable decline in performance, with FVD scores increasing by 18% to 71%. This confirms its critical role in refining internal-state predictions and improving long-term generation quality.
- **Scaling Law:** The model exhibits a clear scaling law, where increasing the number of parameters (from 10M to 1B) consistently leads to improved FVD scores, especially for longer video durations.

These findings collectively affirm that "DrivingWorld" successfully addresses the core challenges of spatio-temporal modeling in GPT-style world models, delivering state-of-the-art performance in long-duration, high-fidelity, and controllable autonomous driving scenario generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "DrivingWorld" paper represents a significant advancement in the development of world models for autonomous driving and the broader field of controllable video generation using autoregressive frameworks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancing Autonomous Driving Systems:**
 - **Enhanced Safety and Robustness:** By enabling the generation of high-fidelity, long-duration future driving scenarios, "DrivingWorld" provides a powerful tool for developing and testing autonomous driving agents. This capability is crucial for anticipating complex and rare "long-tail" events or out-of-distribution scenarios (like accidents) that are difficult to capture in sufficient quantities with labeled real-world data. Learning from unlabeled video data through world models allows for more robust decision-making and a reduction in catastrophic errors.
 - **Improved Planning and Decision-Making:** The model's ability to support controllable simulation of future scenarios, conditioned on specific ego-vehicle trajectories, offers a direct pathway to more effective planning and policy learning for autonomous vehicles. This allows AD systems to "imagine" the consequences of different actions before execution.
 - **Reduced Data Dependency for Prediction:** World models, by learning from massive amounts of unlabeled video, can potentially reduce the heavy reliance on labor-intensive labeled data for traditional prediction models, making development more scalable and adaptable to new environments.

2. **Breakthrough in GPT-style Video Generation:**
 - **Overcoming 1D Limitations:** The paper successfully addresses a fundamental limitation of applying vanilla GPT frameworks to visual data, specifically their inadequacy in modeling complex spatial and temporal dynamics. The proposed hybrid prediction strategy and spatio-temporal fusion mechanisms provide a blueprint for building more effective AR visual models.
 - **Unprecedented Long-Duration Generation:** The ability to generate temporally consistent and high-fidelity videos for over 40-60 seconds marks a significant milestone in controllable video synthesis. This duration is critical for practical applications in autonomous driving, which require long-term predictive horizons.
 - **Controllability and Fidelity:** "DrivingWorld" demonstrates a superior balance of controllability (via pose token manipulation) and visual fidelity compared to existing methods, including diffusion models, which often struggle with precise control. The proposed masking and balanced attention strategies are key to this achievement.

3. **Efficiency and Scalability:** The decoupled spatio-temporal architecture significantly improves computational efficiency and memory usage compared to vanilla GPT, making long-sequence video generation feasible on practical hardware, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

4. **Industry-Academia Collaboration Model:** The successful collaboration between HKUST and Horizon Robotics exemplifies how deep academic research, combined with industrial resources and real-world application focus, can yield high-impact results in challenging fields like autonomous driving. The access to extensive private driving data from Horizon Robotics further strengthens the practical relevance and scalability of this work.

Looking ahead, the authors propose incorporating more multimodal information (e.g., radar, lidar) and integrating multiple camera views. These future directions promise to further enhance the model's understanding of complex driving environments, leading to even more accurate action control and video generation, and ultimately, more capable and safer autonomous driving systems.
