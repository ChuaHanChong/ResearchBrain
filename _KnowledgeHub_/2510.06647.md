# Q-Learning with Fine-Grained Gap-Dependent Regret

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Pennsylvania State University established the first rigorous fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bounds for model-free Q-learning algorithms, introducing a novel analytical framework that differentiates optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs. This framework enabled UCB-Hoeffding and a new ULCB-Hoeffding algorithm to achieve such bounds, and they also identified and corrected fundamental flaws in the existing AMB algorithm, leading to a "Refined AMB" with robust theoretical guarantees.

## Problem

- Model-free RL algorithms lacked fine-grained gap-dependent regret bounds, typically relying on a global minimum suboptimality gap ("Δmin""Δmin") rather than individual state-action-step gaps ("Δh(s,a)""Δh(s,a)").
- UCB-based algorithms faced analytical challenges in achieving fine-grained bounds due to difficulties in bounding cumulative weighted estimation errors, especially when treating optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs uniformly.
- The Adaptive Multi-step Bootstrap (AMB) algorithm, the only prior non-UCB-based attempt claiming fine-grained bounds, contained fundamental algorithmic and analytical flaws that invalidated its theoretical guarantees.

## Method

- A novel analytical framework was developed for ==UCB-based== algorithms that explicitly distinguishes and separately analyzes the visitation patterns of optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs.
- The existing UCB-Hoeffding algorithm was re-analyzed, and a new UCB-based algorithm, ULCB-Hoeffding, was introduced, both leveraging the new framework to achieve fine-grained regret bounds.
- Algorithmic and analytical issues in the AMB algorithm were identified and corrected, leading to a "Refined AMB" with revised update rules, a rigorous proof of unbiased multi-step bootstrapping, and correct application of concentration inequalities.

## Results

- The first fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bound was established for UCB-Hoeffding, showing improved dependence on the minimum gap from O(H6SA/Δmin)O(H^6SA/\Delta_{min})O(H6SA/Δmin) to O((H5+H3S)/Δmin)O((H^5 + H^3S)/\Delta_{min})O((H5+H3S)/Δmin).
- ULCB-Hoeffding achieved the same fine-grained gap-dependent regret bound as UCB-Hoeffding, demonstrating the generality of the new analytical framework.
- Refined AMB provided the first rigorous fine-grained gap-dependent regret bound for a non-UCB-based algorithm, bounded by O(∑h=1H∑Δh(s,a)>0H5log⁡(SAT)Δh(s,a)+H6∣Zmul∣log⁡(SAT)Δmin+SAH2)O(\sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{\Delta_h(s,a)>0} (H^5 \log(SAT))/(\Delta_h(s,a)) + \frac{H^6|Z_{mul}| \log(SAT)}{\Delta_{min}} + SAH^2)O(∑h=1H∑Δh(s,a)>0Δh(s,a)H5log(SAT)+ΔminH6∣Zmul∣log(SAT)+SAH2).
- Numerical experiments demonstrated UCB-Hoeffding, ULCB-Hoeffding, and Refined AMB empirically outperformed the original AMB, with regret growth consistent with logarithmic theoretical guarantees.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly differentiating the analysis of optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs is crucial for establishing tighter, fine-grained gap-dependent regret bounds in model-free RL.
> - The precise design of Q-update rules, especially regarding truncation, is critical to preserve the recursive structure required for theoretical properties like optimism and pessimism in multi-step bootstrapping algorithms.
> - Ensuring the martingale difference condition is met for concentration inequalities is paramount for the validity of theoretical guarantees in reinforcement learning algorithms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) involves an agent learning to maximize cumulative rewards through interaction with an environment. RL algorithms are broadly categorized into model-based and model-free approaches. Model-free methods, which directly learn value functions without explicitly modeling the environment's dynamics, are widely used due to their comparative simplicity of implementation and lower memory requirements, scaling linearly with the number of states. This contrasts with model-based methods, which typically incur quadratic memory costs.

This paper focuses on model-free RL in the context of episodic tabular Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with inhomogeneous transition kernels. For such MDPs, the established minimax regret lower bound over $K$ episodes is $\Omega(\sqrt{H^2SAT})$, where $T = KH$ is the total number of steps. A number of model-free algorithms have achieved $\sqrt{T}$-type regret bounds, with some matching the minimax bound up to logarithmic factors. These algorithms primarily rely on the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) approach for exploration, except for the Adaptive Multi-step Bootstrap (AMB) algorithm (Xu et al., 2021), which employs a multi-step bootstrapping technique.

While worst-case regret bounds provide guarantees, practical performance of RL algorithms often exceeds these bounds when a positive suboptimality gap exists. This motivates research into "gap-dependent" regret bounds, which incorporate the specific structure of an MDP instance. In the model-free setting, initial UCB-based gap-dependent regret bounds for algorithms like UCB-Hoeffding (Yang et al., 2021) were of order $\tilde{O}(H^6SA/\Delta_{min})$, where $\Delta_{min}$ is the smallest positive suboptimality gap across all state-action-step triples. Subsequent work improved the dependence on $H$. However, these bounds are considered "coarse-grained" as they depend on a global $\Delta_{min}$ term rather than individual suboptimality gaps $\Delta_h(s,a)$, thus failing to fully capture the problem structure.

In contrast, model-based RL literature has achieved "fine-grained" gap-dependent regret bounds. These bounds incorporate individual suboptimality gaps $\Delta_h(s,a)$ and exhibit reduced reliance on the global $\Delta_{min}$ factor, often taking a form such as $\tilde{O}\left(\left(\sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{\Delta_h(s,a)>0} \frac{1}{\Delta_h(s,a)} + \frac{|Z_{opt}|}{\Delta_{min}} + SA\right) \text{poly}(H)\right)$, where $|Z_{opt}|$ is the number of optimal state-action-step triples.

The AMB algorithm (Xu et al., 2021) was the only prior model-free, non-UCB-based attempt to achieve a fine-grained regret upper bound. However, the authors of the current paper identify significant issues in both the algorithmic design and theoretical analysis of AMB. These issues include improper truncation in Q-updates, which breaks the link between Q-estimates and historical V-estimates, and a violation of the martingale difference condition in its concentration arguments, casting doubt on its claimed fine-grained guarantees. This landscape highlights a notable open question: whether fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bounds, incorporating individual suboptimality gaps $\Delta_h(s,a)$ and improved dependence on $1/\Delta_{min}$, can be established for model-free RL algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to affirmatively answer the open question regarding the establishment of fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bounds for model-free Reinforcement Learning. The paper aims to achieve this for both UCB-based and non-UCB-based algorithms in episodic tabular MDPs.

The motivation stems from several identified limitations and challenges in the existing model-free RL literature:
1. **Coarse-grained gap dependence:** Prior model-free algorithms with gap-dependent bounds (e.g., UCB-Hoeffding by Yang et al., 2021) rely on the minimum global suboptimality gap $\Delta_{min}$. This approach does not leverage the full structure of the problem when many actions have larger gaps, leading to looser bounds and potentially overestimating the necessary exploration. The goal is to develop bounds that explicitly incorporate individual suboptimality gaps $\Delta_h(s,a)$.
2. **Analytical challenges for UCB-based algorithms:** Achieving fine-grained gap-dependent regret for UCB-based algorithms is analytically difficult. Specifically, bounding the cumulative weighted estimation error of Q-estimates is challenging. Existing analyses often treat all state-action pairs uniformly. However, optimal and suboptimal pairs exhibit significantly different visitation patterns (suboptimal pairs are typically visited only $\hat{O}(\log T)$ times). Ignoring this imbalance leads to overly conservative dependence on $1/\Delta_{min}$ and loose bounds. The objective is to devise a framework that distinguishes these visitation patterns.
3. **Issues with existing non-UCB-based fine-grained attempts:** The only prior non-UCB-based algorithm, AMB, claimed fine-grained regret bounds but contains fundamental algorithmic and analytical flaws. These include improper truncation in Q-updates, which breaks a critical recursive structure necessary for theoretical guarantees, and incorrect application of concentration inequalities due to a violation of the martingale difference condition. A key motivation is to revisit and rectify these issues, providing a rigorously sound non-UCB-based algorithm with fine-grained guarantees.
4. **Bridging the gap with model-based methods:** Model-based RL algorithms have already demonstrated fine-grained gap-dependent regret bounds. The motivation is to bring model-free RL algorithms to a comparable theoretical standing in problem-dependent performance, while retaining their practical advantages of simplicity and memory efficiency.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- Develop a novel analytical framework for UCB-based algorithms that explicitly separates the analysis of optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs.
- Establish the first fine-grained, gap-dependent regret upper bound for a prominent UCB-based algorithm, UCB-Hoeffding.
- Introduce a new UCB-based algorithm, ULCB-Hoeffding, inspired by AMB's structure but simplified, and demonstrate its fine-grained regret guarantees using the new framework.
- Identify and thoroughly analyze the algorithmic and analytical issues in the original AMB algorithm.
- Propose a refined version of AMB, addressing all identified issues, and establish the first rigorous fine-grained gap-dependent regret bound for a non-UCB-based algorithm.
- Empirically validate the improved performance of the proposed and refined algorithms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs distinct methodological approaches for UCB-based and non-UCB-based algorithms to achieve fine-grained gap-dependent regret bounds.

#### 3.1. For UCB-Based Algorithms (UCB-Hoeffding and ULCB-Hoeffding)

The core of the methodology for UCB-based algorithms is a novel analytical framework that explicitly differentiates the treatment of optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs. This distinction is crucial because optimal and suboptimal pairs exhibit differing visitation frequencies during exploration, which significantly impacts the tightness of regret bounds. Suboptimal pairs are generally visited fewer times (typically logarithmic in the total number of steps, $\hat{O}(\log T)$) compared to optimal pairs.

- **UCB-Hoeffding:** The paper re-analyzes UCB-Hoeffding, an existing and popular UCB-based algorithm. This algorithm maintains an upper bound $Q_h^k(s,a)$ on the optimal Q-value function $Q_h^*(s,a)$ for each state-action-step triple $(s,a,h)$ at the start of episode $k$. Actions are selected greedily based on these upper bounds. The Q-estimates are updated using a standard Bellman update rule with a decaying step size $\eta_t$ and a Hoeffding bonus $b_t$. The new analytical framework allows for a tighter analysis of this algorithm by accounting for the sparse visitation of suboptimal actions, leading to fine-grained bounds.

- **ULCB-Hoeffding:** To demonstrate the generality of the new analytical framework, the authors introduce a new UCB-based algorithm named ULCB-Hoeffding. This algorithm is conceptually inspired by AMB but features a simplified structure, particularly in its Q-updates. ULCB-Hoeffding maintains both upper ($\bar{Q}_h^k(s,a)$) and lower ($\underline{Q}_h^k(s,a)$) bounds on the optimal Q-value function. It constructs a candidate action set $A_h^k(s)$ by eliminating actions whose upper bound is below the lower bound of another action, effectively removing actions deemed suboptimal. The policy selection aims to maximize the width of the confidence interval $(\bar{Q}_h^k - \underline{Q}_h^k)(s,a)$, promoting exploration of uncertain, potentially optimal actions. Crucially, ULCB-Hoeffding utilizes standard Bellman updates for its Q-functions, similar to UCB-Hoeffding, rather than the multi-step bootstrapping employed by AMB. This design choice is highlighted as essential for establishing fine-grained regret guarantees within the UCB framework.

#### 3.2. For Non-UCB-Based Algorithms (Refined AMB)

The approach for non-UCB-based algorithms centers on a rigorous re-evaluation and refinement of the existing AMB algorithm, which previously claimed fine-grained bounds. The authors identify and address two main categories of issues in the original AMB:

- **Identified Issues in Original AMB:**
 1. **Improper Truncation in Algorithm Design:** The original AMB algorithm applies truncations at $H$ and $0$ (the maximum and minimum possible Q-values) directly to the Q-estimates during multi-step bootstrapping updates. The authors argue that this truncation breaks the essential recursive structure linking Q-estimates to historical V-estimates, which is critical for proving the optimism and pessimism properties of the Q-estimates. This invalidates certain foundational equalities used in AMB's theoretical analysis.
 2. **Violation of Martingale Difference Conditions:** AMB decomposes the Q-function into rewards accumulated within "decided" states and values from "undecided" states, using multi-step bootstrapping. When applying concentration inequalities (e.g., Azuma-Hoeffding) to bound deviations, the original AMB incorrectly centers estimators on their expectations rather than their conditional expectations. This violates the martingale difference condition, rendering the application of these inequalities unsound. Furthermore, the paper notes that the unbiasedness of the multi-step bootstrapping estimators themselves was not rigorously established in AMB.

- **Refinements in Refined AMB:** To rectify these issues and establish rigorous fine-grained guarantees, the authors propose "Refined AMB" with the following modifications:
 1. **Revised Update Rules:** The truncations at $H$ and $0$ are moved from the Q-estimates to the corresponding V-estimates. This preserves the recursive structure of the Q-updates, allowing the recovery of crucial equalities necessary for theoretical analysis.
 2. **Established Unbiasedness of Multi-Step Bootstrapping:** A rigorous proof is provided to confirm that the estimators derived from multi-step bootstrapping (sum of empirical rewards from decided states plus the bootstrapped value from the first undecided state) indeed form an unbiased estimate of the optimal Q-value function. This is a foundational step for applying concentration inequalities correctly.
 3. **Ensured Martingale Difference Condition:** The application of concentration inequalities, specifically Azuma-Hoeffding, is performed correctly. The estimators used in multi-step bootstrapping are now centered around their conditional expectations, satisfying the martingale difference condition required for the validity of these inequalities.
 4. **Tightened Confidence Bounds:** Through a joint analysis of the concentration properties of both estimators involved in multi-step bootstrapping, the confidence bonus term is reduced by half compared to the original AMB. This refinement aims to improve empirical performance.

The theoretical guarantees for Refined AMB are then derived based on these validated properties, following the general analytical pathway outlined in the original AMB but with corrections for the identified flaws.

#### 3.3. Numerical Experiments

The methodology also includes numerical experiments conducted in synthetic MDP environments. These experiments evaluate the performance of the original AMB, Refined AMB, UCB-Hoeffding, and ULCB-Hoeffding across various scales of $(H, S, A, K)$. Rewards and transition kernels are sampled uniformly, and initial states are chosen randomly. The primary metric reported is Regret(T)/log(K+1) plotted against the number of episodes K, with median regret and 10th-90th percentile intervals to capture uncertainty. The bonus coefficients are adjusted for AMB to reflect the identified issues (c=2 for AMB vs. c=1 for others).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings and results, encompassing theoretical guarantees for both UCB-based and non-UCB-based algorithms, as well as empirical validations.

#### 4.1. Fine-Grained Regret Bounds for UCB-Based Algorithms

- **UCB-Hoeffding (Algorithm 1):** The paper establishes the first fine-grained, gap-dependent regret upper bound for UCB-Hoeffding. The expected regret $E[\text{Regret}(T)]$ is bounded by:
 $O\left(\sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{\Delta_h(s,a)>0} \frac{H^5 \log(SAT)}{\Delta_h(s,a)} + \sum_{h=1}^H H^3 \sqrt{|Z_{opt,h}| \cdot 2\log(SAT)} \frac{1}{\Delta_{min,h}} + SAH^3\right)$.
 Here, $Z_{opt,h} = \{(s,a) \in S \times A | \Delta_h(s,a)=0\}$ represents optimal state-action pairs at step $h$, and $\Delta_{min,h}$ is the minimum positive gap at step $h$.
 - This bound demonstrates an improved dependence on the minimum gap compared to previous works. For instance, in an ideal scenario where only one suboptimality gap equals $\Delta_{min}$ at $h=H$ and $|Z_{opt,H}|=S$, the result is $\tilde{O}((H^5 + H^3S)/\Delta_{min})$, which is a notable improvement over $\tilde{O}(H^6SA/\Delta_{min})$ from Yang et al. (2021). The paper states that its result outperforms Yang et al. (2021) in all cases for UCB-Hoeffding.
 - The result is shown to be consistent with the lower bound established by Simchowitz & Jamieson (2019) for UCB-based algorithms, matching it up to polynomial factors in $H$. The dependence on $|Z_{opt}|/\Delta_{min}$ also aligns with the lower bound in Xu et al. (2021), indicating tightness.

- **ULCB-Hoeffding (Algorithm 2):**
 - **Worst-Case Regret:** ULCB-Hoeffding achieves a worst-case regret upper bound of $O(\sqrt{H^4SAT\iota})$, which matches the $\sqrt{T}$-type performance of UCB-Hoeffding.
 - **Fine-Grained Regret:** Critically, ULCB-Hoeffding achieves the *same* fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bound as UCB-Hoeffding, as specified above. This demonstrates the generality and flexibility of the novel analytical framework developed in the paper.

#### 4.2. Fine-Grained Regret Bounds for Non-UCB-Based Algorithms (Refined AMB)

The paper identifies and addresses significant algorithmic and analytical issues within the original AMB algorithm (Xu et al., 2021).
- **Identified Issues:**
 - **Improper Truncation:** Truncations applied directly to Q-estimates in AMB's multi-step bootstrapping updates (lines 13-14 in Algorithm 3 of the Appendix) break the recursive structure, invalidating the crucial link between Q-estimates and historical V-estimates required for establishing optimism and pessimism.
 - **Martingale Difference Condition Violation:** AMB incorrectly applies concentration inequalities by centering estimators on their expectations rather than conditional expectations, violating the martingale difference condition. This compromised the theoretical guarantees on the optimism and pessimism of Q-estimates.
- **Refined AMB (Algorithm 4 & 5):** The proposed Refined AMB algorithm addresses these issues.
 - It shifts truncations from Q-estimates to V-estimates, preserving the recursive structure.
 - It provides a rigorous proof that its multi-step bootstrapping estimators form an unbiased estimate of the optimal Q-function.
 - It ensures the martingale difference condition is met for correct application of concentration inequalities.
 - It also achieves tighter confidence bounds by jointly analyzing estimator concentrations, halving the bonus coefficient.
- **Theoretical Guarantees for Refined AMB:** These refinements enable the establishment of the first rigorous fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bound for a non-UCB-based algorithm. The expected regret is bounded by:
 $O\left(\sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{\Delta_h(s,a)>0} \frac{H^5 \log(SAT)}{\Delta_h(s,a)} + \frac{H^6|Z_{mul}| \log(SAT)}{\Delta_{min}} + SAH^2\right)$.
 Here, $Z_{mul} = \{(s,a,h) \in S \times A \times [H] | \Delta_h(s,a)=0, |Z_{opt,h}(s)| > 1\}$, where $|Z_{opt,h}(s)|$ is the number of optimal actions for state $s$ at step $h$.

#### 4.3. Numerical Experiments

Experiments conducted across four synthetic MDP environments with increasing scale demonstrate the practical implications of the theoretical work.
- **Performance Comparison:** ULCB-Hoeffding and Refined AMB exhibit comparable performance, both empirically outperforming the original AMB algorithm across all tested scales.
- **Overall Best Performance:** UCB-Hoeffding consistently demonstrates the best empirical performance among the evaluated algorithms.
- **Logarithmic Regret Growth:** For UCB-Hoeffding, ULCB-Hoeffding, and Refined AMB, the regret curves, normalized by $\log(K+1)$, flatten as the number of episodes $K$ increases. This trend is consistent with the derived fine-grained theoretical guarantees, which imply a logarithmic growth in regret. The original AMB's regret curve does not flatten as consistently, further supporting the identified theoretical issues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Reinforcement Learning by addressing a fundamental open question regarding fine-grained gap-dependent regret bounds for model-free algorithms. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Theoretical Understanding of Model-Free RL:** The paper provides the first rigorous fine-grained gap-dependent regret bounds for model-free RL algorithms, effectively elevating their theoretical understanding to a level previously achieved predominantly by model-based methods. This advancement enhances the theoretical foundation for model-free exploration-exploitation strategies.

2. **Novel Analytical Framework:** The development of an analytical framework that explicitly distinguishes optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs is a methodological contribution. This approach addresses a critical challenge in bounding cumulative estimation errors more precisely, paving the way for tighter analyses in future model-free RL research.

3. **Correction and Refinement of Existing Algorithms:** The detailed identification and correction of algorithmic and analytical flaws in the prominent AMB algorithm are impactful. This work provides a rigorously sound version, Refined AMB, thereby ensuring the validity of fine-grained gap-dependent regret guarantees for a non-UCB-based approach. Such corrections are essential for the reliability and trustworthiness of theoretical results in the field.

4. **Introduction of New, Empirically Validated Algorithms:** The introduction of ULCB-Hoeffding, a new UCB-based algorithm, demonstrates the versatility of the proposed analytical framework. Its performance, both theoretically and empirically, positions it as a competitive method in the model-free RL landscape. The empirical results, showing UCB-Hoeffding, ULCB-Hoeffding, and Refined AMB outperforming the original AMB, reinforce the practical relevance of these theoretical advancements.

5. **Potential for More Efficient RL Applications:** Algorithms with fine-grained gap-dependent regret bounds are designed to be more efficient in environments where significant suboptimality gaps exist. By reducing over-exploration of suboptimal actions, these algorithms can learn optimal policies faster. This could lead to more practical and efficient RL agents in real-world scenarios where computational resources or interaction time are limited, particularly in problems characterized by distinct performance differences between actions.

6. **Bridging the Gap between Model-Based and Model-Free Approaches:** By achieving comparable fine-grained theoretical guarantees, this work helps bridge the performance gap between model-based and model-free RL algorithms in problem-dependent settings. This development encourages further exploration of the strengths of model-free methods while mitigating their historical theoretical limitations.

In conclusion, this research significantly pushes the boundary of theoretical understanding in model-free Reinforcement Learning by providing fine-grained performance guarantees. It offers both novel algorithms and crucial corrections, contributing to the development of more robust and efficient RL agents.
