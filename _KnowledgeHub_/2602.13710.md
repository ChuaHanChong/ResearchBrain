# HBVLA: Pushing 1-Bit Post-Training Quantization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HBVLA introduces a 1-bit post-training quantization framework for Vision-Language-Action models, enabling their robust deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms. It achieves this by intelligently partitioning and spectrally quantizing weights, maintaining near full-precision performance with marginal degradation in simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Large parameter counts and high memory requirements of VLA models prevent their deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- Direct application of 1-bit binarization methods designed for LLMs or VLMs to VLA models leads to severe policy degradation due to amplified quantization errors in continuous action spaces.
- Identifying critical weights in VLAs for aggressive quantization is challenging due to issues like the "dual dominance problem" in saliency estimation and modality mixing in weight distributions.

## Method

- A ==policy-aware weight partitioning strategy== uses a ==rectified Hessian==, derived from a block-wise gradient backpropagation, to identify and protect weights critical for action generation.
- Non-salient weights undergo a ==sparse orthogonal transform (permutation)== to reorder columns, minimizing high-frequency noise before being quantized in the ==Haar domain== using ==group-wise 1-bit binarization== with a shared mean.
- Salient weights are quantized on the residual, using a ==column-wise Haar transform== and group-wise 1-bit binarization, then combined with the non-salient reconstruction.

## Results

- **HBVLA** consistently achieved higher success rates on the LIBERO (**70.0%** for OpenVLA, **90.3%** for OpenVLA-OFT) and SIMPLER (**70.0%** for CogACT) benchmarks, showing significantly smaller performance degradation (e.g., **6.5%** on OpenVLA) compared to other 1-bit PTQ methods.
- Real-world evaluation on Mobile ALOHA with a dual-arm cobot demonstrated only marginal success rate decreases for HBVLA-quantized models, maintaining strong performance (e.g., **83.3%** for Pick and Place) where other baselines failed due to oscillations.
- The proposed policy-aware Hessian effectively identified task-critical weights, reducing quantization error and improving success rates (e.g., **10.3%** degradation vs. **12.5%** for standard Hessian) compared to generic saliency estimation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA model components show differential sensitivity to quantization, with action heads and projectors being the most sensitive, while vision encoders are more robust.
> - Standard Hessian-based saliency identification methods are inadequate for VLAs due to the "dual dominance problem," requiring a policy-aware rectified Hessian to prioritize action-critical signals.
> - Applying a sparse orthogonal transform to VLA weights before Haar decomposition helps narrow the distribution gap for 1-bit quantization by reducing high-frequency energy caused by intermingled modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an advancement in embodied artificial intelligence, facilitating instruction-following robotic control by integrating visual perception, language comprehension, and action generation capabilities. Models such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and CogACT have demonstrated performance on manipulation tasks, including long-horizon instruction execution and generalization. These models typically rely on a large number of parameters and extensive datasets, leading to high computational and memory requirements.

To mitigate these demands, Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a method for reducing model footprints. PTQ involves quantizing already trained models using a small calibration dataset, without requiring full retraining as in quantization-aware training (QAT). While PTQ has shown results in reducing bitwidths to 8-bit or 4-bit for various models, the deployment constraints of contemporary VLAs on resource-limited robots and edge devices often necessitate more aggressive compression, such as binarization (reducing weights to 1-bit precision).

However, direct application of binarization methods developed for Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to VLAs has been found to be ineffective. Unlike LLMs or VLMs, which are evaluated on metrics like perplexity or feature fidelity, VLA policies generate continuous actions in a closed-loop physical environment. Minor quantization-induced action deviations can be amplified by contact dynamics and accumulate over long-horizon tasks, leading to policy degradation and potential failures. This work addresses the specific challenges of applying ultra-low-bit PTQ to VLAs, aiming to preserve policy behavior in the presence of aggressive compression.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an ultra-low-bit post-training quantization framework, termed HBVLA, specifically designed for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to enable their robust deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms. The motivation for this research stems from several identified limitations and challenges:

1. **Computational and Memory Footprint of VLAs:** Modern VLA models, while capable, possess large parameter counts and high memory requirements. These characteristics hinder their deployment on hardware-limited robots and edge devices that require real-time, closed-loop control. Existing 8-bit and 4-bit quantization methods for VLAs do not provide sufficient compression for these stringent deployment scenarios.
2. **Inadequacy of Existing Binarization Methods for VLAs:** While 1-bit neural network binarization offers substantial efficiency gains, current methods, primarily developed for LLMs and VLMs, do not transfer effectively to VLAs. The critical difference lies in the nature of VLA outputs: continuous actions in a closed-loop physical system. Even small quantization errors can propagate and amplify, leading to significant action degradation and task failure, a problem less pronounced in open-loop perception or language generation tasks.
3. **Distribution Gap and Quantization Error Accumulation:** Existing binarization techniques struggle to narrow the distribution gap between binarized and full-precision weights. This issue causes quantization errors to accumulate, particularly under long-horizon closed-loop execution characteristic of robotic tasks, severely degrading action generation.
4. **Differential Sensitivity of VLA Components:** The authors observe that different components within a VLA model exhibit varying sensitivities to quantization. For instance, the vision encoder is found to be robust, the language model less sensitive, while the projector and action model show considerable sensitivity. Generic quantization approaches may not account for this differential impact.
5. **Inaccurate Saliency Identification:** Standard Hessian-based methods (e.g., H = XXᵀ) for identifying critical weights are problematic in VLAs. VLA activation maps can suffer from a "dual dominance problem," where statistical outliers from background elements or numerical imbalances among visual tokens disproportionately influence saliency metrics. This leads to inaccurate identification of weights truly critical for action generation, causing essential policy information to be incorrectly quantized.
6. **Challenges with Standard Transforms for Mixed Modalities:** Standard transforms, such as the Haar transform, are observed to be ineffective when applied directly to VLA weights because these weights often mix different modalities. Pairing columns from disparate modalities can create large value jumps, introducing outliers and noise that compromise the accuracy of 1-bit quantization.

To address these motivations, HBVLA aims to:
- Identify and protect weights critical for action generation using a policy-aware enhanced Hessian.
- Induce a low-entropy intermediate state for non-salient weights through a sparse orthogonal transform to optimize for Haar domain quantization.
- Quantize both salient and non-salient weights in the Haar domain using group-wise 1-bit quantization, with a shared-mean strategy for non-salient weights to balance efficiency and accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HBVLA framework is a two-step post-training binarization strategy designed for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The core idea is to identify and protect functionally critical weights while efficiently quantizing the remaining weights using spectral transformation.

#### 3.1. Policy-Aware Weight Partitioning

The first step involves identifying salient weights that are crucial for preserving the continuous action policy, as quantization errors in these weights can severely degrade performance.

- **Problem with Standard Hessian:** Conventional Hessian-based saliency metrics (H = XXᵀ) are deemed inadequate for VLAs due to a "dual dominance problem." This means the metric can be overwhelmed by high-magnitude background outliers or numerical imbalances from visual tokens, obscuring the sparse, instruction-conditioned signals vital for robot control.
- **Rectified Hessian:** To overcome this, HBVLA proposes a policy-aware rectified Hessian, $\tilde{H} \triangleq XSX^\top = \sum_{t=1}^N s_t x_t x_t^\top$, where $S$ is a token-level importance matrix. This reweights each token's contribution based on its influence on action generation, reducing the impact of redundant visual tokens and outliers while emphasizing task-critical ones.
- **Token Importance Matrix (S) Acquisition:** The token importance matrix $S$ is obtained via a block-wise gradient backpropagation method:
 1. **Forward Pass:** For a specific attention block $\Phi(X)$ in the action pathway, the deviation $L_{blk} = \lVert Z - \hat{Z} \rVert_F^2$ is measured between the full-precision output $Z$ and the binarized output $\hat{Z}$ for a given input $X$. This local alignment loss isolates binarization-induced distortion.
 2. **Backward Pass:** A local backpropagation on $L_{blk}$ computes gradients $G^{(p)} \triangleq \frac{\partial L_{blk}}{\partial Y^{(p)}}$ for attention projection outputs $Y^{(p)} \in \{Q, K, V, O\}$. A larger norm $\lVert G^{(p)}_{:,t} \rVert$ indicates higher sensitivity of token $t$ at projection $p$ to binarization.
 3. **Process Gradient:** Token-wise importance $a^{(p)}_t \triangleq \frac{1}{d_p} \lVert G^{(p)}_{:,t} \rVert_2^2$ is computed per projection. These scores form a diagonal importance matrix $S^{(p)}_{attn} \triangleq \text{Diag}(a^{(p)}_1, \dots, a^{(p)}_N)$, which is then used to construct the rectified Hessian proxy $\tilde{H}^{(p)}_{attn} \triangleq XS^{(p)}_{attn}X^\top$. This process filters token importance, prioritizing features critical for instruction-grounded semantics and mitigating attention drift.
- **Weight Partitioning:** Finally, column saliency is computed from the rectified Hessian $\tilde{H}$, partitioning each layer into salient ($I_{sal}$) and non-salient ($I_{non-sal}$) column sets. The number of salient columns is determined by minimizing a local reconstruction error.

#### 3.2. Saliency-Aware Hybrid Binarization with Haar Transform

HBVLA employs a hybrid binarization approach, treating salient and non-salient weights differently within a shared two-step quantization primitive based on the Haar transform.

- **Haar Transform Primitive:** For a weight vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times m}$, the one-level Haar transform $H(w) \triangleq wH_m = [w_{lo}, w_{hi}]$ decomposes it into low-pass ($w_{lo}$) and high-pass ($w_{hi}$) subbands. This is implemented via two fixed 1D convolutions (kernels $h_{lo}=[0.5, 0.5]$ and $h_{hi}=[0.5, -0.5]$) with stride 2.
- **Group-wise 1-bit Quantization:** After the Haar transform, coefficients within each frequency band are adaptively split into groups. Each group is then binarized using $Q(u) = \alpha_g \cdot \text{sign}(u - \mu_g)$, where $\mu_g$ and $\alpha_g$ are group-specific mean and scale. For non-salient weights, a single shared mean $\mu$ is enforced across groups within the same row and frequency band to optimize storage and metadata.

#### 3.2.1. Non-salient Weights Binarization

- **Pre-processing:** Missing values in salient columns are filled using adjacent averages to obtain $W_{l,filled}$.
- **Sparse Orthogonal Transform (Permutation P):** To address the challenge of intermingled modalities in non-salient weights, which leads to large step-change outliers in the Haar domain, a sparse orthogonal transform (permutation matrix $P$) is applied prior to the Haar transform. This reorders weight columns to minimize high-frequency energy generated during decomposition, which is proportional to the sum of squared discrepancies between paired columns.
 - An identity is proven showing that $\lVert W_{l,non-sal}PH_{hi} \rVert_F^2 = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor m/2 \rfloor} \lVert W_{l,non-sal}(:, \pi(2k-1)) - W_{l,non-sal}(:, \pi(2k)) \rVert_2^2$.
 - A greedy pairing-and-chaining heuristic (Algorithm 1) is used to efficiently generate the permutation $\pi$ by minimizing these within-pair distances and then ordering the pairs.
- **Haar Transform and Quantization:** The permuted non-salient weights are transformed into the Haar domain: $U = W_{l,non-sal}PH$. The binarized coefficients $U_B = Q(U)$ are then transformed back to obtain the approximated non-salient weights $\hat{W}_{l,non-sal} = U_B H^\top P^\top$.

#### 3.2.2. Salient Weights Binarization

- **Residual Quantization:** Salient columns are quantized on the residual $R_l = W_l - \hat{W}_{l,non-sal}$ to prevent interference from the non-salient reconstruction.
- **Column-wise Haar Transform:** The salient residual $R_{l}(:, I_{sal})$ undergoes a column-wise Haar transform: $R^c_{l,sal} = H_n R_l(:, I_{sal})$.
- **Quantization and Reconstruction:** The Haar-domain coefficients are then quantized using the primitive $Q(R^c_{l,sal})$ and inverse-transformed to yield $\hat{W}_{l,sal} = H_n^{-1} Q(R^c_{l,sal})$.
- **Final Reconstruction:** The full quantized layer weights are reconstructed by summing the two components: $\hat{W}_l = \hat{W}_{l,non-sal} + \hat{W}_{l,sal}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HBVLA framework was evaluated across simulated benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating performance compared to existing 1-bit Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) baselines.

#### 4.1. Performance on LIBERO Benchmark

Evaluations on the LIBERO benchmark using OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT models (both with 1.08-bit weights) showed:
- **OpenVLA:** HBVLA achieved an average success rate of 70.0%, outperforming HBLLM (58.6%), BiVLM (47.9%), and BiLLM (43.9%). This represented a 6.5% degradation from the full-precision (FP) OpenVLA model (76.5%), which was substantially smaller than other baselines (17.9% to 32.6% degradation).
- **OpenVLA-OFT:** HBVLA maintained 90.3% average success, closer to the FP OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) than any other method. HBLLM, BiVLM, and BiLLM showed significantly larger performance drops (e.g., BiLLM at 57.7% for a 39.4% degradation).
- HBVLA exhibited robustness on challenging long-horizon tasks within LIBERO, suggesting better preservation of action-critical representations.

#### 4.2. Performance on SIMPLER Benchmark

Experiments on SIMPLER using the CogACT model (1.08-bit weights) under two evaluation settings yielded:
- **Visual Matching:** HBVLA achieved an average success rate of 70.0%, superior to HBLLM (62.3%), BiVLM (57.1%), and BiLLM (28.8%). This was a 4.8% relative degradation from the FP model (74.8%).
- **Variant Aggregation:** HBVLA attained an average success rate of 51.0%. While slightly lower on individual tasks (e.g., "O/C Drawer") compared to HBLLM and BiVLM, it delivered the highest overall average success rate in this more challenging setting, with a 10.3% degradation from FP (61.3%).
- These results indicate HBVLA's ability to balance compression and accuracy, maintaining near-full-precision fidelity and improved robustness.

#### 4.3. Real-world Evaluation Suite (Mobile ALOHA)

Real-world adaptability was assessed using a stationary Magic dual-arm cobot on three tasks: Pick and Place, Sequenced Instruction, and Flexible Folding.
- The FP OpenVLA-OFT model achieved high success rates across all tasks (e.g., 90% for Pick and Place, 87.5% for Sequenced Instruction, 83.3% for Flexible Folding).
- HBVLA demonstrated only marginal decreases in success rate compared to the FP model (e.g., 83.3% for Pick and Place, 79.2% for Sequenced Instruction, 75% for Flexible Folding).
- Other baselines, particularly BiLLM, exhibited substantially poorer performance, with the robotic arm observed to suffer from persistent oscillations leading to failures.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis

- **Sensitivity Analysis:** Quantizing different components of CogACT in SimpleRenv showed that the vision encoder was robust to quantization, while the language model exhibited considerable sensitivity. The projector and action head demonstrated the highest sensitivity, with minor precision loss leading to severe degradation. This guided the selective quantization strategy.
- **Column-Norm Criterion for Permutation:** A study on the permutation criterion for non-salient columns found that using the $\ell_2$-norm for column similarity in the greedy pairing-and-chaining algorithm yielded lower quantization error and superior downstream performance compared to the $\ell_1$-norm, indicating better capture of energy distribution.
- **Effectiveness of Policy-Aware Hessian:** Comparison with a standard Hessian formulation for saliency estimation demonstrated that the proposed policy-aware Hessian was more effective in filtering quantization noise and accurately identifying task-critical weights, resulting in significant improvements in success rate (e.g., 10.3% degradation vs. 12.5% for standard Hessian in Visual Matching).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HBVLA framework presents a method for applying 1-bit post-training quantization to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a domain where such aggressive compression has historically led to significant performance degradation.

The work's significance lies in its tailored approach to address the unique challenges of VLA binarization. By introducing a policy-aware weight partitioning strategy, it aims to preserve the functional integrity of VLA policies, which is critical for closed-loop robotic control. The rectified Hessian mechanism, designed to filter out irrelevant visual noise and prioritize action-critical signals, represents an advancement in saliency estimation for multimodal, action-generating models. Furthermore, the sparse orthogonal transform, combined with Haar domain quantization, offers a structured approach to manage modality heterogeneity and reduce high-frequency noise, which is pertinent for the complex weight distributions in VLAs.

The consistent outperformance of HBVLA over existing state-of-the-art binary PTQ methods across diverse benchmarks (LIBERO, SIMPLER) and real-world robotic tasks (Mobile ALOHA) suggests its practical applicability. The reported marginal success-rate degradation compared to full-precision models, particularly in real-world scenarios, indicates that HBVLA could enable the deployment of large-scale VLA models on resource-constrained robotic platforms. This could facilitate broader adoption of sophisticated embodied AI systems in applications such as manufacturing, service robotics, and exploration, where on-device processing and limited memory are common constraints.

The findings also contribute to the understanding of VLA model components' sensitivity to quantization, providing insights that could guide future compression strategies. The proposed methodologies, such as the policy-aware Hessian and the sparse orthogonal transform for spectral domain quantization, may be adaptable to other domains where robust ultra-low-bit quantization of complex, multimodal models is required.
