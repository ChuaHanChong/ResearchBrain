# VOTE: Vision-Language-Action Optimization with Trajectory Ensemble Voting

> [!summary] Summary
> VOTE introduces a Vision-Language-Action framework that employs a special token for efficient action generation and a trajectory ensemble voting mechanism for robust action selection. This approach significantly reduces inference latency by up to 48.8x on A6000 GPUs and 38.6x on edge platforms, while achieving higher task success rates, such as 98.0% on the LIBERO benchmark.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from high inference latency and computational costs due to extensive token generation for actions.
- Many VLA models inefficiently utilize generated action sequences, often discarding valuable historical predictions and leading to less stable robot trajectories.
- Existing action aggregation methods struggle to effectively combine predictions from different timesteps without introducing inconsistencies or erroneous modes.

## Method

- A training framework employs a special `` token to represent entire action chunks, significantly reducing the number of generated tokens for action prediction.
- An MLP Action Head with a bottleneck architecture directly maps the `` token's hidden state to continuous, multi-step action predictions.
- An ensemble voting inference strategy aggregates current and historical action predictions by identifying consistent modes based on cosine similarity to the newest prediction.

## Results

- VOTE achieved significant inference speedups of up to 48.8x on A6000 GPUs and 38.6x on NVIDIA Jetson Orin, with minimal memory overhead compared to existing models.
- The framework demonstrated higher task success rates, notably an average of 98.0% on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing leading VLA models like CogACT and SpatialVLA.
- Training efficiency was improved, requiring less than 20% of training samples for most tasks compared to OpenVLA-OFT and 15.6% of epochs relative to SpatialVLA for comparable or better performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing a multi-step, multi-dimensional action sequence with a single special token can drastically reduce computational overhead without sacrificing performance.
> - An intelligent, similarity-based ensemble voting mechanism can effectively leverage historical action predictions to improve trajectory stability and filter out inconsistent actions.
> - A compact bottleneck architecture for the action head can be more efficient and performant than larger, isotropic designs for VLA tasks.
