# WebSailor-V2: Bridging the Chasm to Proprietary Agents via Synthetic Data and Scalable Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> WebSailor-V2 introduces an open-source web agent that significantly reduces the performance gap between open-source and proprietary systems, achieving state-of-the-art results on six challenging benchmarks including BrowseComp-EN (35.3) and Humanity’s Last Exam (30.6), often outperforming larger proprietary models. This advancement is built on innovations in synthetic data generation and a scalable reinforcement learning framework from Tongyi Lab, Alibaba Group.

## Problem

- A significant performance disparity existed between open-source web agents and powerful proprietary systems, hindering broader accessibility to advanced "Deep Research" capabilities.
- Current training data for web agents lacked sufficient complexity and diversity in logical structures and uncertainty types, limiting generalization to intricate real-world problems.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) for web agents faced challenges from the high cost, latency, and inconsistency of real-world web interactions and external API calls, impeding scalable and robust training.

## Method

- Developed a comprehensive ==post-training pipeline== for open-source web agents, integrating advanced synthetic data generation, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- Introduced SailorFog-QA-V2, a novel data construction method that creates dense knowledge graphs and incorporates a wide array of uncertainty definitions to foster complex reasoning.
- Implemented a dual-environment RL framework, combining a high-fidelity simulator for efficient experimentation with a robust real-world tool execution interface to ensure stable and scalable training despite real-world web complexities.

## Results

- WebSailor-V2-**30B**-A3B achieved state-of-the-art performance on six challenging benchmarks, notably scoring 35.3 on BrowseComp-EN and 30.6 on Humanity’s Last Exam, surpassing the **671B** DeepSeek-V3.1.
- The agent demonstrated strong competitiveness with top proprietary systems, including a score of 48.9 on DeepResearch Bench, placing it second only to Gemini-2.5-pro-DeepResearch.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) alone provided formidable baseline capabilities, scoring 24.4 on BrowseComp-EN and 23.9 on HLE, establishing a critical foundation for subsequent Reinforcement Learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality and diversity of training data, alongside the stability of the training environment, are more critical for successful agentic Reinforcement Learning than the specific RL algorithm.
> - An initial strong Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) policy is essential to provide a sufficiently dense reward signal, enabling effective exploration in the subsequent sparse-reward RL phase.
> - Generating dense knowledge graphs with varied uncertainty definitions in synthetic data (SailorFog-QA-V2) can significantly enhance an agent's ability to perform complex information retrieval and synthesis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous AI agents capable of leveraging external tools like search engines and web browsers for "Deep Research" is widely recognized as a critical milestone in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The research landscape for web agents is rapidly evolving, with advancements stemming from both data construction and training methodologies. However, a significant performance disparity has persisted between open-source solutions and powerful proprietary systems, such as OpenAI's DeepResearch or DeepSeek-V3.1, creating a bottleneck in the widespread accessibility of advanced research capabilities.

WebSailor-V2 directly addresses this chasm by presenting an open-source solution that aims to match or surpass the performance of leading proprietary agents. The paper situates itself within a lineage of recent advancements, acknowledging the contributions of existing open-source data construction methods like the graph-based SailorFog-QA (WebSailor’s predecessor) and "easy-to-hard" paradigms employed by WebShaper, ASearcher, and WebExplorer. These prior works have introduced structured information retrieval, integrated live web tools, and incorporated uncertainty (often via obfuscation) into data generation. WebSailor-V2 builds upon this by pushing the boundaries of data quality, particularly in generating complex logical structures and diverse uncertainty types, aiming for a more realistic reflection of real-world knowledge.

On the training front, the paper notes the prevalence of a two-stage training pipeline—Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for a "cold start" followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) for policy refinement, often utilizing variants of GRPO. While acknowledging algorithmic nuances, the authors contend that at this stage of agentic RL, the quality and distribution of training data and the stability of the training environment are more critical determinants of success than the specific RL algorithm itself. This perspective aligns with "The Bitter Lesson" (Sutton, 2019), which posits that scalable general methods tend to outperform complex, human-engineered designs in the long run. By choosing the foundational ReAct framework, WebSailor-V2 deliberately isolates the impact of its data and training innovations, aiming to evaluate the model's intrinsic capabilities rather than the efficacy of intricate prompt engineering or inference paradigms. This work, therefore, represents a focused and comprehensive effort to elevate open-source web agents to a new echelon of performance, thereby democratizing access to capabilities previously exclusive to large, proprietary systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WebSailor-V2 is to significantly advance the capabilities of open-source web agents, specifically aiming to bridge the considerable performance gap that currently exists between these solutions and leading proprietary systems. This overarching goal is driven by a clear motivation to democratize powerful "Deep Research" capabilities, making them accessible to a broader community.

The authors identify two fundamental challenges underpinning the existing performance disparity, which directly inform their key motivations:

1. **Insufficient Data Diversity and Monolithic Uncertainty Definitions:** Existing training data often lacks the complexity and variety of logical structures necessary for agents to generalize to novel and intricate problems. Current methodologies frequently rely on a narrow set of uncertainty definitions, primarily obfuscation. The motivation here is to develop a dataset that fosters more sophisticated reasoning by introducing a wider array of uncertainty types, compelling models to engage in more dynamic and nuanced information-seeking behaviors. The goal is to move beyond simple keyword matching to contextual inference, hypothesis generation, and evidence synthesis, better preparing agents for the inherent ambiguity of real-world research.

2. **Lack of Scalable and Robust Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training Environments:** Developing and iterating on agentic RL systems is profoundly challenging due to the massive rollouts required and the high cost, latency, and inconsistency associated with external API calls (e.g., search engines, web parsers). These practical issues contaminate training data, degrade learned policies, and severely impede rapid algorithmic iteration. The motivation is to engineer a scalable and robust RL framework that allows for efficient algorithm development and stable policy training, insulating the learning process from the inherent stochasticity and engineering complexities of real-world web interactions.

In essence, WebSailor-V2 aims to establish a new state-of-the-art for open-source web agents by innovating across the entire post-training pipeline—from data construction to SFT and RL—with a strong belief that high-quality data and a stable training environment are paramount for success.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WebSailor-V2 introduces a comprehensive post-training pipeline encompassing data construction, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Reinforcement Learning (RL), built upon the Qwen3-30B-A3B foundational model with an expanded context length of 128k. The agent operates within the ReAct framework due to its simplicity and universality, intentionally avoiding complex prompt engineering to isolate the model's intrinsic capabilities. The action space includes `search`, `visit`, `google scholar`, `Python interpreter`, and `final answer`.

#### 3.1. SailorFog-QA-V2: Data Construction

The cornerstone of WebSailor-V2's data strategy is SailorFog-QA-V2, an advancement of the previous SailorFog-QA.

- **Graph Construction:** Departing from methods that tend to produce tree-like structures, SailorFog-QA-V2 emphasizes the construction of a *dense knowledge graph*. It starts with seed entities and leverages web tools to discover related entities and facts, but critically, it actively seeks to establish *dense connections and cyclic structures*. This ensures the graph more accurately reflects the complex, non-linear interdependencies prevalent in real-world knowledge. Furthermore, it preserves complete procedural information (e.g., specific search queries and source URLs) and computes statistical features for each entity, enhancing the subsequent QA generation.
- **Subgraph Extraction:** To efficiently handle the combinatorial explosion resulting from denser graphs, a *random-walk based approach* is employed for subgraph extraction. This method enables the efficient gathering of a sufficient quantity of non-isomorphic (verified by Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm), connected subgraphs, which collectively represent a full spectrum of structural complexities.
- **QA Generation:** Rather than end-to-end LLM generation, the process first analyzes non-isomorphic nodes to distribute QA focus evenly across different structural roles. A key innovation is the introduction of a *wider array of uncertainty definitions beyond simple obfuscation*. This aims to elicit a more diverse and comprehensive suite of advanced reasoning abilities, such as contextual inference, hypothesis generation, and evidence synthesis, preparing the model for real-world ambiguity.

#### 3.2. Agentic Post-training

The training pipeline consists of an SFT cold start, followed by a dual-environment RL framework.

- **SFT Cold Start:** This initial phase equips the base model with a robust policy. The SFT dataset is composed entirely of synthetic data generated from SailorFog-QA-V2, with trajectories constructed by open-source models using rejection sampling. This strong initial policy is crucial for providing a sufficiently dense reward signal, enabling effective exploration in the subsequent RL phase where rewards are often sparse.
- **Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Dual-Environment RL Framework:**
 - **Simulated Environment:** A dedicated, high-fidelity simulator built from the ground up using a large-scale offline Wikipedia knowledge base. This environment is low-cost, exceptionally fast, and fully controllable, making it ideal for high-frequency algorithmic experimentation and data curation. SailorFog-QA-V2's generation pipeline is adapted to create simulation-tailored tasks, ensuring realistic interaction dynamics and reward mechanisms.
 - **Real Environment:** To tackle the engineering complexities of training with real-world web APIs, a *unified tool execution interface* is architected. This interface features a scheduling and management layer with robust concurrency handling and fault-tolerance strategies (e.g., QPS constraints, caching, timeout-and-retry protocols, service degradation, backup data sources). This multi-layered design ensures that the agent's interaction with external tools is deterministic and stable, insulating the training loop from real-world stochasticity and reducing operational costs.
 - **Data Curation:** A *symbiotic data-policy feedback loop* integrates data construction and RL training. This automated data synthesis and filtering pipeline dynamically curates the training set in real-time based on training dynamics, ensuring training stability and delivering substantial performance gains by continuously refining policies from relevant information.
 - **RL Algorithm:** The algorithm is a tailored adaptation of GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization). It employs a strictly on-policy training regimen, continuously sampling trajectories with the most up-to-date policy. Key enhancements include a token-level policy gradient loss, a leave-one-out strategy to reduce advantage estimation variance, and a conservative approach to negative samples (selectively excluding those that don't yield a final answer) to prevent "format collapse" and maintain training stability. Large batch and group sizes are used to maintain smaller variance and provide adequate supervision. The authors explicitly state their belief that data quality and environment stability are more critical than the specific RL algorithm for agentic RL success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WebSailor-V2-30B-A3B demonstrates a breakthrough in open-source web agent capabilities, achieving state-of-the-art results that are highly competitive with leading proprietary systems, despite being built on a moderately sized 30B Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance

The agent was evaluated on six challenging benchmarks, consistently outperforming all existing open-source agents and often surpassing much larger proprietary models:

- **BrowseComp-EN:** Achieved 35.3, significantly outperforming all open-source agents and notably surpassing the 671B DeepSeek-V3.1 (30.0).
- **BrowseComp-ZH:** Scored 44.1, leading the open-source field.
- **Humanity’s Last Exam (HLE):** Achieved 30.6, establishing a new state-of-the-art. This score notably exceeded the 671B DeepSeek-V3.1 (29.8) and even OpenAI-o3 (20.2), validating the hypothesis that strong information retrieval and synthesis can enhance logical reasoning beyond intrinsic model scale.
- **xbench-DeepSearch:** Scored 73.7, not only leading open-source but also surpassing some proprietary systems.
- **GAIA:** Achieved 74.1, similarly demonstrating superior performance among open-source and strong competitiveness with proprietary agents.
- **DeepResearch Bench:** WebSailor-V2 scored 48.9, placing it second only to the state-of-the-art Gemini-2.5-pro-DeepResearch (49.7). The marginal gap is attributed to the training focus on information retrieval and synthesis rather than stylistic report generation.

#### 4.2. Role of SFT Cold Start

The Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase proved indispensable. Even after SFT alone, WebSailor-V2 exhibited formidable capabilities, scoring 24.4 on BrowseComp-EN and 23.9 on HLE, surpassing many fully-trained open-source agents. This strong initial policy is identified as a critical prerequisite for the success of reinforcement learning, as it enables meaningful exploration and provides a sufficiently dense reward signal in tasks with sparse rewards.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics

The RL training dynamics showed a clear and significant upward trend in training reward, successfully translating to performance improvements on validation benchmarks.

- **Difficult vs. Simpler Benchmarks:** On challenging benchmarks like BrowseComp, both `pass@1` and `pass@3` scores showed concurrent increases, indicating a genuine expansion of the model's fundamental problem-solving capabilities. For simpler benchmarks (e.g., xbench-DeepSearch, GAIA), `pass@1` improved significantly while `pass@3` gains were marginal, suggesting RL primarily enhanced sampling efficiency (i.e., selecting the optimal path on the first attempt) for tasks already within the model's base capabilities.
- **Entropy Dynamics:** Policy entropy remained consistently high throughout training, oscillating without a clear downward trend. This indicates sustained exploration and adaptability, preventing premature convergence to a deterministic policy. The authors hypothesize this is a consequence of the web environment's non-stationary nature, fostering a more robust and adaptive policy.

#### 4.4. Context Scaling

Increasing the context length from 32k to 128k (and raising the maximum ReAct iterations to 100) positively correlated with accuracy. Approximately 90% of correctly solved instances were completed within a 64k context. Notably, WebSailor-V2 (30B MoE) achieved around 16% accuracy on BrowseComp-EN at a 32k context limit, a significant improvement over its predecessor, WebSailor-V1, which was built on a larger 72B dense model. This highlights the profound impact of the improved data and training pipeline on the agent's fundamental reasoning and tool-use capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WebSailor-V2 represents a pivotal advancement in the field of autonomous web agents, with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Open-Source/Proprietary Performance Gap:** This work demonstrates, for the first time, that a meticulously trained agent built on a moderately-sized open-source model (Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE) can achieve performance highly competitive with, and in some cases superior to, significantly larger and more powerful proprietary Deep Research agents. This achievement is crucial for democratizing access to advanced AI research capabilities, reducing the entry barrier for researchers and developers outside of well-resourced corporate labs.

2. **Validation of Data-Centric AI and Stable Environments:** The paper strongly reinforces the argument that high-quality data and a stable training environment are more critical determinants of success in agentic Reinforcement Learning than the specific algorithmic nuances. By demonstrating superior performance through innovations in data construction and environment engineering, WebSailor-V2 provides compelling evidence for prioritizing these foundational aspects, which can guide future research and development efforts.

3. **Novel Data Construction Methodology:** The introduction of SailorFog-QA-V2, with its focus on generating dense knowledge graphs and incorporating a wider array of uncertainty definitions beyond simple obfuscation, offers a robust framework for creating synthetic data that elicits sophisticated reasoning. This methodology can inspire and inform the development of next-generation training datasets for agents requiring complex information retrieval and synthesis skills.

4. **Scalable and Robust RL Training Framework:** The dual-environment RL framework, combining a high-fidelity simulator for rapid iteration with a robust real-world environment management system, provides a practical and effective blueprint for training agents in complex, noisy real-world settings. Its engineering solutions for fault-tolerance, concurrency, and API stability are invaluable for addressing the pervasive challenges of real-world tool use.

5. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving state-of-the-art results with a 30B MoE model that outperforms models of 671B parameters underscores the efficiency of WebSailor-V2's pipeline. This efficiency makes advanced agent capabilities more accessible, as it reduces the immense computational resources typically required for training ultra-large models, potentially accelerating innovation within the broader AI community.

6. **Implications for AGI and Logical Reasoning:** The demonstrated ability of WebSailor-V2 to significantly enhance logical reasoning by equipping the model with exceptionally strong information retrieval and synthesis capabilities suggests a powerful paradigm for overcoming the inherent scale limitations of LLMs. This agentic approach, allowing models to "reason over" externally acquired knowledge, offers a promising pathway toward Artificial General Intelligence, where problem-solving extends beyond parametric knowledge to dynamic, adaptive interaction with the world.

In conclusion, WebSailor-V2 represents a significant leap forward for open-source AI, offering valuable insights and a robust foundation that is poised to inspire and accelerate future endeavors in the development of intelligent, autonomous web agents.
