# Geometry-Aware Rotary Position Embedding for Consistent Video World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A geometry-aware rotary position embedding called ViewRope is introduced to enhance consistency in pose-conditioned video world models by embedding per-patch camera ray directions directly into the attention mechanism. This method achieves superior loop closure performance, reducing Loop Closure Error by 4-9% compared to baselines, and enables efficient long-sequence generation.

## Problem

- Existing video world models struggle to maintain long-term geometric consistency and spatial persistence, leading to "geometric drift" and hallucination when cameras revisit locations.
- Current screen-space positional embeddings in video transformers are misaligned with 3D projective geometry, hindering consistent content identification across different viewpoints.
- Prior solutions for 3D consistency either sacrifice open-domain generative flexibility or rely on computationally expensive and brittle external memory mechanisms.

## Method

- ViewRope integrates per-patch 3D viewing directions into the attention mechanism by rotating query/key feature subvectors, creating an intrinsic geometric bias for 3D consistency.
- Geometry-Aware Frame Sparse Attention combines ViewRope with a sparse attention scheme that selects top-k geometrically relevant historical frames to efficiently process long video sequences.
- A four-stage progressive training pipeline stabilizes learning, and a new benchmark, ViewBench, featuring loop-closure trajectories and Loop Closure Error (LCE) metrics, is introduced for evaluation.

## Results

- ViewRope reduces Loop Closure Error (LCE) by 4% compared to the GTA baseline and 9% compared to 3D RoPE, demonstrating superior long-term geometric consistency.
- The approach consistently outperforms leading interactive world models like HY-WorldPlay for camera rotations between 30° and 75°, with LCE reductions up to 11.4%.
- Geometry-Aware Frame-Sparse Attention reduces training time by approximately 25% for 201-frame sequences while maintaining or improving visual quality and LCE compared to other sparse methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating "view as position" by encoding per-patch camera ray directions provides a model-native inductive bias, allowing transformers to inherently correlate content across varying viewpoints for 3D consistency.
> - Encoding relative geometric relationships between viewing rays is more effective for view-consistent generation than using absolute 3D coordinates.
> - Geometry-guided sparse attention significantly improves efficiency for long sequences and offers better training stability compared to other sparse attention methods by intelligently selecting causally important historical frames.
