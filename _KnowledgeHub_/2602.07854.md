# Geometry-Aware Rotary Position Embedding for Consistent Video World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A geometry-aware rotary position embedding called ViewRope is introduced to enhance consistency in pose-conditioned video world models by embedding per-patch camera ray directions directly into the attention mechanism. This method achieves superior loop closure performance, reducing Loop Closure Error by 4-9% compared to baselines, and enables efficient long-sequence generation.

## Problem

- Existing video world models struggle to maintain long-term geometric consistency and spatial persistence, leading to "geometric drift" and hallucination when cameras revisit locations.
- Current screen-space positional embeddings in video transformers are misaligned with 3D projective geometry, hindering consistent content identification across different viewpoints.
- Prior solutions for 3D consistency either sacrifice open-domain generative flexibility or rely on computationally expensive and brittle external memory mechanisms.

## Method

- ViewRope integrates per-patch 3D viewing directions into the attention mechanism by rotating query/key feature subvectors, creating an intrinsic geometric bias for 3D consistency.
- Geometry-Aware Frame Sparse Attention combines ViewRope with a sparse attention scheme that selects top-k geometrically relevant historical frames to efficiently process long video sequences.
- A four-stage progressive training pipeline stabilizes learning, and a new benchmark, ViewBench, featuring loop-closure trajectories and Loop Closure Error (LCE) metrics, is introduced for evaluation.

## Results

- ViewRope reduces Loop Closure Error (LCE) by 4% compared to the GTA baseline and 9% compared to 3D RoPE, demonstrating superior long-term geometric consistency.
- The approach consistently outperforms leading interactive world models like HY-WorldPlay for camera rotations between 30° and 75°, with LCE reductions up to 11.4%.
- Geometry-Aware Frame-Sparse Attention reduces training time by approximately 25% for 201-frame sequences while maintaining or improving visual quality and LCE compared to other sparse methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating "view as position" by encoding per-patch camera ray directions provides a model-native inductive bias, allowing transformers to inherently correlate content across varying viewpoints for 3D consistency.
> - Encoding relative geometric relationships between viewing rays is more effective for view-consistent generation than using absolute 3D coordinates.
> - Geometry-guided sparse attention significantly improves efficiency for long sequences and offers better training stability compared to other sparse attention methods by intelligently selecting causally important historical frames.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in the rapidly evolving field of **interactive AI and visual world models**: achieving long-term geometric consistency in generated videos under explicit camera control. Predictive simulators that can generate future observations based on a specified viewpoint trajectory are crucial for applications ranging from robotics and autonomous navigation to virtual reality and game development.

The existing landscape of video generation models, particularly those based on open-domain video diffusion, has made remarkable progress in producing high-quality and diverse video content. However, as the paper points out, these systems largely suffer from a critical limitation: **lack of spatial persistence**. When a camera revisits a previously observed location after traversing a long trajectory (a "loop-closure" scenario), current models frequently "hallucinate" new details or exhibit "geometric drift," failing to reconstruct identical or consistent scene structures and appearances.

The paper identifies two main strategies in prior work to tackle this issue and highlights their shortcomings:
1. **External Context/Memory Mechanisms:** Approaches like Context-as-Memory (Yu et al., 2025a) or Memory Forcing (Huang et al., 2025a) attempt to retrieve historical frames or maintain explicit 3D spatial structures. While these improve consistency, they often rely on pixel-level concatenation or external data structures, incurring substantial computational costs and becoming brittle with long or complex histories. They are not *model-native* solutions.
2. **Geometry-First Pipelines:** Methods such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) or specialized novel-view synthesis transformers (Jin et al., 2024) enforce strict 3D consistency by building explicit 3D representations. However, this often comes at the cost of **sacrificing open-domain generative flexibility**, limiting their applicability to more constrained environments or specific rendering tasks.

The authors pinpoint the core of the problem to a "bottleneck in positional modeling." Current video transformers typically use screen-space (x, y, t) positional embeddings. This approach conflicts with the underlying projective geometry of 3D scenes, where a single 3D point can project to widely different 2D coordinates across frames due to camera motion. This misalignment prevents the model from consistently identifying and reusing the same physical content across temporally distant tokens.

ViewRope distinguishes itself by proposing a *model-native inductive bias* for 3D consistency. Instead of external memory or rigid 3D reconstruction, it integrates geometric priors *directly into the attention mechanism*. By encoding per-patch camera-ray directions into the attention layer, it allows the transformer to intrinsically understand and exploit the angular correspondence of viewing directions, which is key to long-horizon view consistency. This bridges the gap between geometry-rigid 3D pipelines and open-domain diffusion generators, offering a lightweight and complementary solution to the problem of geometric drift.

Furthermore, the paper contributes to the broader field of **efficient transformer architectures** by proposing Geometry-Aware Frame-Sparse Attention. This mechanism addresses the quadratic complexity of dense attention, a significant hurdle for long-sequence generation, by leveraging the geometric cues introduced by ViewRope to intelligently select relevant historical frames. This innovation fits into the ongoing research efforts to develop sparse attention mechanisms (e.g., SampleAttention, Sliding Window Attention) that enable processing of longer contexts while maintaining computational efficiency.

Finally, the introduction of **ViewBench** contributes to the benchmarking aspect of the field. Many existing video generation benchmarks focus on perceptual quality or action controllability but lack specific metrics for evaluating long-term geometric consistency and loop-closure fidelity. ViewBench fills this gap, providing a much-needed diagnostic tool for systematically assessing progress in this crucial area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a pose-conditioned visual world model capable of generating videos with long-term spatial persistence and geometric consistency, particularly in scenarios where the camera revisits previously observed viewpoints.**

The core motivations stemming from the limitations of current systems are:

1. **Addressing Geometric Drift and Hallucination:** Existing video world models struggle to maintain stable scene structures over long camera trajectories. When a camera returns to a prior location, they frequently "hallucinate" new details or exhibit significant visual inconsistencies, fundamentally failing to remember and reconstruct the original scene. This "geometric drift" is a major barrier to reliable interactive AI applications.
2. **Overcoming Screen-Space Positional Embedding Limitations:** The authors identify that the prevalent use of screen-space (x, y, t) positional embeddings in video transformers is fundamentally misaligned with the projective geometry of 3D scenes. This misalignment prevents the model from accurately correlating content from different viewpoints over time, leading to inconsistency.
3. **Achieving Model-Native 3D Consistency without Sacrificing Generative Flexibility:** Current solutions either impose strict 3D consistency at the cost of open-domain generative capabilities (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) or rely on external memory mechanisms that add computational overhead and can be brittle. The motivation is to find a middle ground: an intrinsic mechanism within the generative model itself that provides a strong inductive bias for 3D consistency without compromising the flexibility of diffusion models.
4. **Enabling Efficient Long-Context Video Generation:** Autoregressive video generation for long sequences faces a computational bottleneck due to the quadratic complexity of dense self-attention. A key motivation is to develop an efficient attention mechanism that can leverage geometric information to selectively attend to relevant historical frames, making long-horizon consistent video generation computationally feasible.
5. **Establishing a Robust Evaluation Benchmark:** The lack of a specialized benchmark for systematically evaluating loop-closure fidelity and geometric drift in camera-conditioned video generation models hinders progress. A motivation is to create such a diagnostic suite (ViewBench) to provide clear and targeted metrics for future research.

In essence, the authors aim to "equip transformers with a mechanism to identify and reuse the same physical content across temporally distant tokens whose image-plane coordinates are decorrelated by camera motion—without resorting to explicit 3D scene reconstruction or compromising open-domain generative flexibility."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ViewRope methodology revolves around injecting geometry-aware information directly into the self-attention mechanism of a video transformer, combined with an efficient sparse attention scheme and a specialized training/evaluation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The paper formulates pose-conditioned video generation as learning a conditional distribution `pθ(x1:T | x≤0, C1:T, Y)`, where `x` is the video, `C` is the camera trajectory (rotation, translation, intrinsics), and `Y` is additional conditioning (text/action). It highlights that standard temporal coherence objectives are insufficient, formally introducing a **loop-closure loss** (`L_lc`) that enforces photometric consistency when the camera revisits a prior viewpoint (`wt,k = 1`). This loss couples frames across arbitrary temporal gaps, necessitating a mechanism for retrieving geometrically corresponding content.

#### 3.2. ViewRope: View-Centric Positional Encoding in Attention

The core innovation, ViewRope, integrates per-patch 3D viewing directions into the attention mechanism.
- **Per-patch Ray Construction:** For each patch in an image, its normalized viewing ray `ri,u,v` (a unit vector) is computed in the camera coordinate system using the camera intrinsics `Ki`. This ray is then transformed into a world-aligned view rotation `Ri,u,v` by combining a local rotation (mapping the canonical optical axis to `ri,u,v`) with the camera's extrinsic rotation `Rcam_i`. This provides a unique 3D orientation for each patch.
- **Rotating Query/Key Feature Subvectors:** Instead of directly concatenating ray information, ViewRope rotates a reserved subset of channels within the query (`q`) and key (`k`) feature vectors. Specifically, these channels are grouped into 3D subvectors, and each subvector is rotated by the corresponding `Ri,u,v`.
- **Geometry-Aware Attention Scores:** The dot product between a rotated query `VR(q, Ri,ui,vi)` and a rotated key `VR(k, Rj,uj,vj)` inherently becomes sensitive to the *relative rotation* `R−1_i,ui,vi Rj,uj,vj` between their viewing rays. This means attention scores are higher for patches whose viewing rays are geometrically aligned, providing a model-native inductive bias for 3D consistency and long-range content retrieval.

#### 3.3. Geometry-Aware Frame Sparse Attention

To handle long video sequences efficiently, ViewRope is combined with a sparse attention mechanism.
- **Frame-Aligned Block Sparsity:** The token sequence is partitioned into blocks, with each block corresponding to a single latent frame. This allows for frame-level selection.
- **Block Relevance Estimation:** Instead of calculating full block-to-block attention, a small set of token indices `Ks` is sampled from each query and key block. A head-averaged affinity `˜Sij` is estimated based on the dot product of these sampled tokens. A causal constraint is applied, only considering past blocks.
- **Top-k Block Selection:** For each query block (current frame), the top-k key blocks (historical frames) with the highest `˜Sij` (geometric relevance) are selected. The current block is always included.
- **Sparse Attention Computation:** Attention is then computed only between the query block and its selected top-k key blocks, reducing the quadratic complexity to linear with respect to sequence length. The implementation leverages optimized kernels (TileLang, FlashAttention principles).
- **Training and Inference:** The model is trained with teacher forcing, where ground-truth historical latents populate the KV cache. During autoregressive inference, the KV cache is populated by previously generated latents, and the same relevance estimation and top-k selection mechanism is applied to maintain causality and retrieve geometrically relevant history.

#### 3.4. Progressive Training Pipeline

A four-stage progressive training schedule is used to stabilize learning:
1. **Stage I (Short-clip teacher forcing):** Initial training on short video clips (e.g., 17 frames) to align with autoregressive generation and caching.
2. **Stage II (Introduce ViewRope):** ViewRope is enabled on short clips to allow the model to learn view-conditioned correspondence without long-context complexities.
3. **Stage III (Enable Frame Sparse Attention):** Frame-aligned block sparsity is activated, adapting the model to efficient long-context retrieval on moderate sequence lengths.
4. **Stage IV (Scale context length):** Training sequence length is substantially increased under sparse attention to achieve long-horizon generation and loop-closure consistency.

#### 3.5. ViewBench Benchmark

The authors introduce ViewBench, a new diagnostic benchmark designed to specifically evaluate view-consistency under camera motion.
- **Features:** Covers complete 3-axis rotation (yaw, pitch, roll) with systematic angle sampling, includes "rotate-away–rotate-back" loop-closure trajectories, and utilizes 10 photorealistic UE5 environments. This addresses limitations of existing datasets (e.g., CaM, GF-Minecraft) that lack general 3D rotations or loop-closure scenarios.
- **Metrics:** In addition to standard visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), ViewBench introduces **Loop Closure Error (LCE)**, measured as the LPIPS between the starting frame and the generated frame upon returning to the start pose, directly quantifying persistent spatial memory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of ViewRope and Geometry-Aware Frame-Sparse Attention in improving consistency and efficiency.

#### 4.1. View Consistency Comparison (Table 2)

- **Superior Loop Closure:** ViewRope achieves the best loop closure performance, reducing LCE by 4% compared to GTA (a strong baseline encoding relative SE(3) pose) and 9% compared to 3D RoPE (a no-geometry baseline). This highlights the precision of per-patch ray encoding over per-camera projective encoding for maintaining geometric alignment.
- **Geometry-Aware vs. Absolute Encoding:** Both ViewRope and GTA significantly outperform 3D RoPE, confirming that encoding relative geometric relationships is more effective than absolute coordinates for view-consistent generation.
- **Competitive Visual Quality:** ViewRope maintains comparable or even better visual quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) than baselines, indicating that the geometric inductive bias does not compromise overall generation quality.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Interactive World Models

- **Outperformance on Small-to-Medium Angles:** ViewRope consistently outperforms leading interactive world models like Matrix-Game-2 and HY-WorldPlay across rotation magnitudes from 30° to 75°. The improvement is particularly noticeable in LCE, with reductions of 6.5% (30°), 7.9% (45°), and 11.4% (75°) compared to HY-WorldPlay. This suggests that ViewRope's per-patch geometric encoding provides stronger spatial memory than action-conditioned approaches, especially as camera excursions increase.
- **Insights into Large-Angle Limitations:** For larger angles (90°-180°), ViewRope's performance relative to HY-WorldPlay slightly declines. The authors attribute this not to a failure of ViewRope's core mechanism, but to system-level limitations: (1) evaluation frame-rate mismatch, where large angular steps exceed training velocities, and (2) error accumulation in teacher-forcing models over very long sequences. These are identified as areas for future improvement through advanced training strategies.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Visual comparisons (Figures 5-7 in Appendix C.7) clearly show ViewRope's superiority in maintaining structural and lighting consistency, preventing brightness collapse, hallucination, and severe geometric drift observed in baselines during loop-closure scenarios.

#### 4.3. Efficiency of Geometry-Aware Sparse Attention (Table 3)

- **Best-in-Class Sparse Performance:** ViewRope with Geometry-Aware Frame-Sparse Attention consistently outperforms other sparse methods, including sliding window attention, reducing LCE by 16%. This validates the effectiveness of geometry-aware frame selection for long-sequence view synthesis.
- **Improved Training Stability:** Crucially, ViewRope's ray-based rotations provide a geometrically meaningful structure to Q/K dot products, enabling stable convergence during sparse training. Both naive sparse attention and GTA with sparse attention suffered from loss divergence.
- **Causal Importance Validation:** Counterfactual experiments (Table 4) confirm that ViewRope's geometry-aware selection identifies causally important frames. Random selection degrades LCE by 25.2%, but explicitly excluding ViewRope's top-k selected frames causes an even larger 38.1% degradation, proving their necessity for consistency.
- **Attention Map Visualization:** Figure 3 visually demonstrates that specialized attention heads emerge to capture long-range spatial overlap (antidiagonal activation for loop closure), guiding the sparse block selection mechanism.
- **Computational Acceleration:** Sparse attention (with top-k=5) reduces training time by approximately 25% (from 27.66 s/iter to 22.01 s/iter) for 201-frame sequences.
- **Qualitative Benefit:** Case studies (Figure 4) highlight ViewRope's ability to preserve fine details and avoid object disappearance or blurring, which occurred with sliding window attention.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Table 5 & Table 9 in Appendix D.1)

- **Channel Allocation for ViewRope:** Embedding ViewRope into the lowest frequency bands of the temporal (T) dimension yielded the best training loss (0.0859). This suggests the temporal dimension is most suitable for encoding view information without interfering with critical spatial features. Replacing 3D RoPE or distributing ViewRope across all dimensions led to degraded performance.
- **Number of Retrieved Frames (k):** While increasing `k` generally improved visual quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) by providing more texture details, LCE peaked at `k=5` (the training setting). This indicates a potential trade-off between texture richness and geometric precision, where too much context might distract the model from optimal geometric consistency given its training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Geometry-Aware Rotary Position Embedding for Consistent Video World Model" paper introduces a highly significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for interactive and simulation-based applications.

#### 5.1. Core Significance

- **Addressing a Grand Challenge:** The paper tackles one of the most persistent and critical challenges in video generation: maintaining long-term geometric consistency and spatial memory, especially during camera revisits. This problem, often referred to as "geometric drift" or "hallucination," has limited the deployment of generative world models in real-world interactive systems. ViewRope offers a powerful and elegant solution.
- **Novel Model-Native Inductive Bias:** The central contribution, ViewRope, is a novel approach that injects geometric priors directly into the attention mechanism. By treating "view as position" and encoding per-patch camera ray directions, it provides a *model-native inductive bias* for 3D consistency. This is a significant conceptual shift from previous methods that relied on external memory or rigid 3D reconstruction, offering a more integrated and flexible solution within the transformer architecture itself.
- **Bridging the Gap:** ViewRope successfully bridges the gap between geometry-rigid 3D pipelines (which offer high consistency but low generative flexibility) and open-domain diffusion models (which offer high flexibility but low consistency). It enables consistent generative modeling without sacrificing the rich, open-domain capabilities of diffusion models.
- **Efficiency for Long Sequences:** The proposed Geometry-Aware Frame-Sparse Attention effectively addresses the quadratic complexity bottleneck of transformers for long video sequences. By intelligently selecting geometrically relevant historical frames, it makes long-horizon video generation computationally feasible and efficient, a crucial step for real-time interactive applications.
- **Advancing Evaluation Standards:** The introduction of ViewBench is a valuable contribution to the research community. By providing a targeted diagnostic benchmark that specifically measures loop-closure fidelity and geometric drift across diverse camera motions and environments, it enables more precise and meaningful evaluation of future world models.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The advancements presented in this paper have far-reaching potential impacts across various domains:
- **Interactive AI Systems:** ViewRope can significantly enhance the realism and stability of interactive AI applications, such as AI agents navigating and interacting within virtual environments (e.g., games, simulations), providing them with a more consistent understanding of their surroundings.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** For VR/AR content creation, the ability to generate consistent virtual worlds that adapt fluidly to user movement and revisit previously seen areas without visual glitches is paramount. ViewRope could enable more immersive and stable VR/AR experiences.
- **Entertainment and Media:** High-quality, consistent video generation under camera control opens new avenues for automated content creation in film, animation, and video games, reducing manual effort and increasing creative possibilities.
- **Training and Simulation:** In fields requiring realistic simulations (e.g., military, medical, industrial training), ViewRope could power more stable and immersive training environments where consistent spatial memory is crucial for effective learning.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** While not directly providing control, better visual world models contribute to the underlying perception and prediction capabilities needed for robust robotic navigation and interaction in complex environments.

#### 5.3. Future Work and Ethical Considerations

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as potential struggles with drastic scene transitions (e.g., moving between rooms) where geometric correspondences might be weak. They propose future directions including:
- **Integration with Explicit 3D Modeling:** Combining ViewRope with explicit 3D representations (e.g., Neural Radiance Fields or 3D Gaussian Splatting) to leverage the strengths of both implicit and explicit modeling.
- **Distillation and Reinforcement Learning:** Further post-training with techniques like distillation and reinforcement learning could improve performance in dynamic scenarios and enhance robustness to system-level limitations, such as those observed in large-angle trajectories.

The paper also includes an important impact statement, acknowledging the potential for misuse, such as deepfake generation, and emphasizing the need for responsible development regarding copyright and privacy protection in virtual content creation. This highlights a growing awareness of the ethical implications of advanced generative AI technologies.
