# Simulating the Visual World with Artificial Intelligence: A Roadmap

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University, Nanyang Technological University, and Kuaishou Technology present a conceptual roadmap for evolving video generation models into comprehensive artificial intelligence world models. This work introduces a four-generation taxonomy, defines key concepts like "physical world models" and "navigation modes," and outlines a structured progression towards physically plausible, highly interactive, and planning-capable simulations.

## Problem

- The term "world model" in AI research lacks a precise and unified definition, leading to ambiguity in evaluating progress and aligning research efforts.
- The rapidly advancing field of video generation, despite its potential for world models, lacks a structured taxonomy to categorize model capabilities and delineate future research trajectories.
- Current video generation models face limitations such as restricted video length, challenges in maintaining 3D consistency, and insufficient real-time interaction, hindering their development into comprehensive world simulators.

## Method

- A ==four-generation taxonomy== (==Faithfulness==, ==Interactiveness==, ==Planning==, ==Stochasticity==) is proposed to systematically categorize the evolutionary stages of video generation models transitioning into world models.
- A formal definition for a ==physical world model== is established as a digital simulation engine for ==next-scene video prediction==, distinct from 'mental world models', alongside the introduction of ==navigation modes== to characterize interactive control signals.
- Existing video generation methods are analyzed and mapped across various application domains (robotics, autonomous driving, gaming) within this new taxonomic framework to illustrate progress and identify gaps.

## Results

- The paper categorizes current high-profile video generation models, such as Sora and Kling 1.0/2.1 Master, within the first two generations (Faithfulness and Interactiveness), highlighting their advancements in visual realism, consistency, and basic control.
- It identifies the third generation (Planning) as an emerging stage, with models like NWM and Genie 3 demonstrating complex task planning, self-evolving scenarios, and physically intrinsic faithfulness.
- The fourth generation (Stochasticity) is presented as a largely underexplored frontier, emphasizing the need for future research into simulating low-probability events and multi-scale spatiotemporal dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern video generation models implicitly function as world models, inherently capable of simulating physical dynamics, temporal causality, and agent-environment interactions.
> - The development of world models follows a progressive evolutionary ladder, building capabilities in faithfulness, interactiveness, and planning across distinct generations.
> - "Navigation modes," defined by temporality, content independence, and spatial reasoning, are critical for discerning true interactive controls from static spatial conditions in generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work positions itself within the intersection of artificial intelligence, particularly generative AI, and the long-standing challenge of building comprehensive world models. Historically, world models have been conceptualized as systems capable of simulating real-world dynamics to support applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and gaming. Various approaches, including 3D/4D scene generation and video generation, have contributed to aspects of world modeling such as motion dynamics, interaction, controllability, visual quality, and 3D consistency.

The authors observe a shift in video generation, moving beyond merely producing visually appealing clips to creating virtual environments that exhibit physical plausibility and support interaction. This evolution, they argue, highlights the emergence of video foundation models as implicit world models. These models are not just visual generators but latent simulation engines that encode physical dynamics, agent-environment interactions, and task planning.

The paper differentiates itself from existing surveys and benchmarks by offering a systematic elucidation and comprehensive taxonomy of this specific evolution from video generation to world models. While prior works summarize methodologies and applications, this survey explicitly clarifies addressed areas and underexplored aspects, such as real-time integration, controllable video-to-world pipelines, and comprehensive evaluation metrics. The authors also highlight a perceived interdependence where rapid advancements in video generation, particularly starting in 2024, are acting as key enablers for renewed progress in world models. Furthermore, the paper introduces a conceptual distinction between a "physical world model" (focused on objective, external dynamics) and a "mental world model" (focused on an agent's internal states and intentions), framing the physical world model as the primary focus of this survey.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the growing recognition that modern video generation models are increasingly functioning as implicit world models, capable of simulating complex real-world dynamics. Despite this trend, the field lacks a systematic framework to define, categorize, and evaluate progress in this integrated domain.

The specific motivations are:
- **Addressing Ambiguity:** The concept of a "world model" remains ambiguous, hindering unified perspectives and progress evaluation. There is a need for a clear definition, especially in the context of video generation.
- **Lacking Taxonomy:** The field lacks a structured taxonomy to organize modeling capabilities, developmental stages, and potential research trajectories from video generation to comprehensive world modeling.
- **Cognitive Science Perspective:** Vision is identified as the dominant sensory modality for humans and embodied agents to perceive and reason about the world. Visual streams convey spatial layout, object properties, temporal dynamics, and causal relationships. This intrinsic reliance on visual representation makes video generation a natural foundation for world models.
- **Technological Advancement:** Recent breakthroughs in end-to-end video generation, particularly with diffusion models and autoregressive transformers, enable high-quality visual rendering and the incorporation of multimodal conditioning. These advancements reinforce the potential for video generation systems to serve as fundamental components for constructing world models.
- **Emerging Applications:** The integration of world models into interactive, real-time environments is becoming feasible with technologies like virtual reality (VR) and embodied AI, necessitating a clearer roadmap for development.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this survey are:
- **Provide a Global Taxonomy:** To propose a four-generation taxonomy that categorizes the evolution of video generation towards world models, grounded in the core capabilities of faithfulness, interactiveness, and planning.
- **Clarify World Model Definition:** To formally define a physical world model as a digital simulation engine embedded with comprehensive world knowledge, capable of predicting the next scene based on environmental states and contextual priors. This definition positions the world model as an interactive environment system responding to external interventions.
- **Define Navigation Modes:** To formally define "navigation modes" and distinguish them from "spatial conditions" based on criteria of temporality, content independence, and spatial reasoning, thereby clarifying their roles in interactive control.
- **Outline Future Perspectives:** To discuss key capabilities required for video generation models to evolve into fully-fledged world models, offering insights for future research directions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors' approach involves a systematic conceptualization and categorization of existing and future video generation models as they evolve into world models.

**Core Conceptualization:** The paper conceptualizes modern video foundation models as comprising two core components:
1. **Implicit World Model:** This component encodes structured knowledge (physical laws, interaction dynamics, agent behavior) and functions as a latent simulation engine for coherent visual reasoning, long-term temporal consistency, and goal-driven planning.
2. **Video Renderer:** This component transforms the latent simulation into realistic visual observations, effectively producing videos as a "window" into the simulated world.

**Problem Definition:** A formal definition of the world model is provided: `V_1:T = G(I)`, where `V_1:T` is the sequence of observable video frames, `G` is the video generation model, and `I` represents the multimodal input space `{T, O, Au, N, X}` (text prompts, current observations, audio signals, navigation modes, and spatial conditions). The internal state `S_t` and transition function `F` are considered implicit, mapping external inputs to video outputs. This framework aligns with the concepts of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and Partially Observable MDPs (POMDPs) for training and inference, respectively.

**Taxonomy of Four Generations:** The central methodology is the proposal of a four-generation taxonomy, illustrating the step-by-step advancement of core capabilities (faithfulness, interactiveness, and planning) from video generation to world models. Each generation is characterized by its emphasis on these capabilities:
- **Generation 1 - Faithfulness:** Focuses on "superficial faithfulness" (basic visual quality, short video generation, basic text-video consistency) and "low-level interactiveness" (pixel-level interaction, limited controllability, simple commands). Planning ability is not yet emerged.
- **Generation 2 - Interactiveness:** Advances to "consistent faithfulness" (long videos, perfect temporal and video-text consistency, basic physical world understanding) and "semantic and navigational interactiveness" (flexible control via navigation modes, basic 2D/3D reasoning, subject-centric control). "Simple task planning" emerges for short-horizon, goal-directed behaviors.
- **Generation 3 - Planning:** Emphasizes "complex task planning" (long-term, self-evolving video sequences at mesoscopic spatiotemporal scales, real-time adaptability), "physically intrinsic faithfulness" (arbitrarily long videos, internalization of physical laws, causal dynamics), and "real-time and local interactiveness" (frame-level interaction, high-fidelity subject-centric control).
- **Generation 4 - Stochasticity:** Aims for "stochasticity planning" (modeling low-probability and outlier events, arbitrary spatial and temporal scale planning – macroscopic, mesoscopic, microscopic), maintaining "physically intrinsic faithfulness," and achieving "global and multi-modal interactiveness" (long-term, vision, language, control, multi-entity coordination, dynamic backgrounds).

**Definition of Navigation Mode:** To clarify control signals, "navigation mode" is formally defined by three properties:
- **Temporality (T):** The signal must be a temporally ordered sequence or influence the entire duration.
- **Content Independence (R):** The signal must not explicitly reference video content or spatial characteristics (e.g., semantic maps, layouts).
- **Spatial Reasoning (S):** The mode must support spatial reasoning across the generated sequence, understanding dynamic transformations.
This distinction separates navigation modes (e.g., actions, text commands, trajectories) from spatial conditions (e.g., sketches, depth maps) which are content-dependent.

**Condition Injection Strategies:** The paper briefly outlines five major architectural strategies for integrating control signals into video generation models: ControlNet-based methods, multi-modal Transformers, cross-attention, concatenation, and addition.

**Application Domains:** The survey organizes discussions across general scenes, robotics, autonomous driving, and gaming, demonstrating how models within each generation apply and advance these domains, with a particular focus on Generation 2 where most recent works reside.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper does not present new empirical data but rather synthesizes and categorizes existing research, establishing a conceptual framework and roadmap for the field. The main "findings" are the comprehensive taxonomy and the detailed characterization of each generational stage:

**Generation 1 - Faithfulness: Superficial Simulation of the Real World**
- **Core Capability:** Models acquire basic video generation ability, producing short videos (2-5 seconds) with tolerable visual quality and basic text-video consistency.
- **Interactiveness:** Exhibits "low-level interactiveness," enabling pixel-level interaction and limited controllability through simple, short actions. Condition-to-video consistency is basic, often with subject distortions.
- **Planning:** Planning capability is not yet developed.
- **Methods:** Includes foundational Text-to-Video (T2V) and Image-to-Video (I2V) models, as well as early spatial world models conditioned on geometry (sketches, depth), 3D priors, or physics principles, and navigation world models for local motion or camera control. Limitations include weak 3D dynamics and potential for unrealistic artifacts.

**Generation 2 - Interactiveness: Controllable and Interactive Simulation of the Real World**
- **Core Capability:** Marked improvement in interactiveness, moving towards "semantic and navigational interactiveness." Models offer flexible control, especially with navigation modes (actions, text commands, trajectories), and exhibit basic 2D/3D reasoning.
- **Faithfulness:** Achieves "consistent faithfulness" with long videos, richer content complexity, better visual realism, and perfect temporal/video-text consistency. Basic physical world understanding (projective geometry) emerges.
- **Planning:** "Simple task planning" develops for basic task-oriented behaviors over short horizons (e.g., picking up an object).
- **Methods:** Foundation models (e.g., Sora, MAGI-1, Cosmos series) leverage large-scale pretraining and higher-capacity architectures. In general scenes, this includes fine-grained control via sparse geometric cues (SketchVideo) and advanced camera/instruction navigation (CameraCtrl II, Pandora, AETHER). In robotics, navigation modes like action, text instruction, and image-based goals are prevalent (COMBO, UniPi, UniSim). Autonomous driving sees layout-prior models and emerging multi-modal navigation (GAIA-2, Vista). Gaming shows advanced real-time interactive models with controller/keyboard inputs (Genie series), nearing open-world simulation. Challenges remain in real-time interaction for autonomous driving and full physical consistency in gaming.

**Generation 3 - Planning: Real-Time and Complex Prediction of the Real World**
- **Core Capability:** Planning becomes a central capability, enabling "complex task planning." Models can autonomously generate infinite or arbitrarily long videos for complex tasks based on intrinsic physical knowledge, with real-time controllability.
- **Faithfulness:** Achieves "physically intrinsic faithfulness," where simulations evolve according to intrinsic physical principles, generating arbitrarily long sequences with temporal coherence and learning causal dynamics (rigid-body mechanics, fluid dynamics).
- **Interactiveness:** Demonstrates "real-time and local interactiveness," allowing frame-level interaction without perceptible delay and precise subject-centric manipulation with contextual consistency.
- **Methods:** Few models currently reach this level (e.g., NWM, Meta’s V-JEPA 2, Google's Genie 3). These models can imagine trajectories in unknown environments, predict physical scene evolution, and support real-time interaction with minute-scale visual memory, including promptable world events. Limitations include scalability, action richness, and long-duration temporal consistency.

**Generation 4 - Stochasticity: Modeling Outlier and Low-Probability Events (Envisioned)**
- **Core Capability:** Introduces "stochasticity planning," modeling both high-probability and low-probability events, including rare occurrences (e.g., genetic mutations, accidents, financial crises).
- **Planning:** Achieves arbitrary spatial and temporal scale planning (macroscopic, mesoscopic, microscopic), focusing on salient events over decades or fine-grained phenomena at millisecond precision.
- **Faithfulness:** Maintains the "physically intrinsic faithfulness" achieved in Generation 3.
- **Interactiveness:** Features "global and multi-modal interactiveness," predicting long-term multimodal influences, enabling global control by an internal agent, and supporting multi-entity coordination.
- **Future Vision:** Envisions world models that simulate "everything, everywhere, anytime," yielding diverse plausible outcomes like parallel universes. Also foresees multimodal audiovisual world models and generalization to arbitrary physical laws beyond Earth, enabling cosmic simulations. This generation remains largely underexplored.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper makes several significant contributions and highlights a profound potential impact for the field of AI and beyond:

**Key Contributions:**
- **Global Taxonomy:** Proposes a structured, four-generation taxonomy for the evolution of video generation towards world models, providing a framework for organizing and understanding progress.
- **Clarified Definition:** Offers a formal and unambiguous definition of a "physical world model" and its operational characteristics within the context of video generation.
- **Formalized Navigation Modes:** Introduces a clear, triadic definition of "navigation modes," distinguishing them from spatial conditions to better characterize interactivity and control flexibility.
- **Future Roadmap:** Provides a roadmap for future research directions, outlining the necessary capabilities for video generation models to become comprehensive world models, including stochasticity, multi-scale planning, and multimodal integration.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Transformative Production and Daily Life:** World models are anticipated to profoundly impact human modes of production, daily life, perception of the world, machine intelligence, and research methodologies.
- **Scientific Advancement:** They could serve as powerful scientific instruments across diverse disciplines:
 - **Physics & Chemistry:** Simulating molecular structures, predicting material properties, and validating hypotheses *in silico*.
 - **Robotics & Autonomous Driving:** Generating infinite, diverse real-world interaction data and simulating endless failure cases for enhanced safety and robust policy learning, addressing data scarcity and safety concerns.
 - **Biology & Medicine:** Predicting wildlife habitats, monitoring microbial growth, simulating atmospheric changes, forecasting scenarios for endangered species, drug testing, and protein structure prediction.
 - **Astronomy:** Simulating cosmic formation scenarios and asteroid impacts.
- **Enhanced Machine Intelligence:** By providing virtually unlimited training trajectories and interaction scenarios, world models could resolve debates about algorithms versus data scale in AI development, leading to more robust and generalized intelligent agents.
- **Dual Development Paths:** The authors envision two complementary development directions:
 - **Precision Simulators:** Aiming for maximum fidelity to the real physical world, capable of passing a "Turing Test for reality," acting as scientific instruments for hypothesis validation.
 - **Generative Engines of World Knowledge:** Focused on mastering world knowledge for zero-shot generation of diverse world patterns, enabling the creation of "parallel universes" and empowering individuals to shape virtual worlds.
- **Beyond Earth-Centric AI:** The ultimate vision extends to models capable of generalizing to arbitrary physical laws, enabling simulations of environments beyond Earth and advancing human understanding of the broader universe.

In essence, this work underscores that the evolution of world models represents a frontier in AI, promising to reshape humanity's ability to explore, understand, and intervene in complex systems, both real and imagined.
