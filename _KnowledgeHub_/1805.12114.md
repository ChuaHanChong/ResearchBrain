# Deep Reinforcement Learning in a Handful of Trials using Probabilistic Dynamics Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Probabilistic Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PETS), a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that utilizes uncertainty-aware deep dynamics models to achieve high sample efficiency while matching or exceeding the asymptotic performance of state-of-the-art model-free methods on continuous control tasks. PETS demonstrates that explicitly modeling both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty in deep neural network dynamics is crucial for effective model-based control.

## Problem

- Model-free deep reinforcement learning algorithms require vast amounts of interaction data, severely limiting their application to real-world scenarios like robotics where data collection is expensive.
- Traditional model-based RL methods (e.g., using Gaussian Processes) scale poorly to high-dimensional problems and struggle with complex, non-smooth dynamics.
- Prior deep neural network-based model-based RL approaches often overfit on small datasets and propagate prediction errors, leading to sub-optimal asymptotic performance compared to model-free methods.

## Method

- **PETS** employs an ==ensemble of probabilistic neural networks (PE-NNs)== to model environment dynamics, capturing both ==aleatoric== (inherent system stochasticity) and ==epistemic== (model uncertainty due to limited data) uncertainties.
- It uses ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with a sampling-based uncertainty propagation method called ==Trajectory Sampling== (specifically TS∞) to plan actions over a future horizon.
- The ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)== is used to efficiently optimize action sequences within the MPC framework, selecting the best actions to execute in the environment.

## Results

- PETS achieved asymptotic performance comparable to or exceeding state-of-the-art model-free algorithms (SAC, PPO) on challenging continuous control tasks.
- It significantly reduced sample requirements, needing **8 times fewer samples** than SAC and **125 times fewer** than PPO on Half-cheetah to reach similar performance levels.
- Empirically validated that Probabilistic Ensembles (PE) consistently outperformed other model types (P, DE, D), demonstrating the necessity of robust uncertainty quantification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive uncertainty estimation, particularly modeling both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties with probabilistic ensembles, is critical for deep dynamics models to achieve both high sample efficiency and high asymptotic performance in model-based RL.
> - Probabilistic propagation methods demonstrate greater robustness to long planning horizons and system stochasticity compared to deterministic approaches, preventing compounding errors.
> - The choice of dynamics model (e.g., probabilistic ensembles) has a greater impact on overall performance than the specific uncertainty propagation technique employed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) broadly seeks to enable agents to learn optimal decision-making policies through trial and error in dynamic environments. The RL landscape is largely divided into two major paradigms: **model-free RL** and **model-based RL (MBRL)**.

**Model-free RL** algorithms (e.g., PPO, SAC, DDPG) learn policies directly from experience without explicitly modeling the environment's dynamics. While these methods have achieved remarkable successes, particularly in simulated domains (e.g., mastering complex games), they are notoriously **sample-inefficient**. This means they require vast amounts of interaction data, often hundreds of thousands or millions of steps, to converge to optimal policies. This high sample complexity severely limits their application to real-world scenarios, such as robotics, where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and potentially damaging to equipment.

**Model-based RL**, in contrast, aims to improve sample efficiency by first learning a predictive model of the environment's dynamics (how actions affect states) and then using this model for planning or policy optimization. Traditional MBRL approaches, often employing **Bayesian non-parametric models** like Gaussian Processes (GPs), have demonstrated excellent sample efficiency in low-dimensional problems. However, GPs face significant scalability challenges with increasing state/action dimensionality and struggle to represent complex, high-dimensional, or discontinuous dynamics (e.g., contact forces in robotics) due to inherent assumptions like smoothness.

The rise of deep learning introduced **deep neural networks (NNs)** as powerful, high-capacity function approximators capable of modeling complex, high-dimensional data. Integrating deep NNs into MBRL seemed a natural fit, offering the potential to overcome the limitations of GPs. However, prior attempts at **NN-based MBRL** largely struggled. While NNs could scale to large datasets, they tended to **overfit on the small datasets** available in the early stages of RL training, leading to inaccurate predictions and sub-optimal asymptotic performance. This overfitting compounded over time horizons, making long-term planning unreliable. As a result, many deep MBRL algorithms either lagged behind model-free counterparts in asymptotic performance or were even outperformed by simpler linear models in some cases.

This paper directly addresses this crucial gap: **how to combine the high representational power of deep neural networks with the sample efficiency of model-based RL, while simultaneously achieving the high asymptotic performance of state-of-the-art model-free methods.** The authors observe that the key to bridging this gap lies in **properly incorporating uncertainty** into deep dynamics models, a concept explored in prior work but not yet successfully demonstrated to match model-free asymptotic performance on challenging benchmarks. PETS aims to demonstrate that a purely model-based deep RL algorithm can achieve this elusive balance by meticulously handling uncertainty.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that overcomes the limitations of previous deep NN-based MBRL approaches, specifically by achieving:

1. **High Sample Efficiency:** Significantly reducing the amount of data (environmental interactions) required for learning, thereby making RL more practical for real-world applications like robotics.
2. **State-of-the-Art Asymptotic Performance:** Matching or exceeding the final performance levels achieved by leading model-free deep RL algorithms on challenging continuous control tasks.
3. **Robustness with High-Capacity Models:** Effectively utilizing expressive deep neural networks as dynamics models without suffering from overfitting in low-data regimes or compounding prediction errors over long horizons.

The motivation stems from the contrasting strengths and weaknesses of existing RL paradigms:

- **Model-free RL** offers high asymptotic performance but demands prohibitive amounts of data, limiting its real-world applicability.
- **Traditional MBRL** (e.g., with GPs) provides good sample efficiency but struggles with complex, high-dimensional dynamics and often achieves sub-optimal asymptotic performance.
- **Deep NN-based MBRL**, while promising due to NNs' representational power, has historically failed to achieve both high sample efficiency and high asymptotic performance. NNs tend to overfit on small datasets and propagate errors across predicted trajectories, leading to poor decisions.

The core insight motivating PETS is that the difficulty of using NNs in MBRL can be largely mitigated by **properly quantifying and incorporating uncertainty** in the dynamics model. By accounting for different types of uncertainty, the model can make more informed predictions, especially when data is scarce or the system inherently stochastic, ultimately leading to more robust learning and control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithm, **Probabilistic Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PETS)**, combines uncertainty-aware deep network dynamics models with sampling-based uncertainty propagation for Model Predictive Control (MPC).

#### 3.1. Uncertainty-Aware Neural Network Dynamics Models

PETS uses a novel approach to capture two distinct types of uncertainty in its deep NN dynamics model:

- **Aleatoric Uncertainty (Inherent System Stochasticity):** This uncertainty arises from inherent noise in the system or observations, which cannot be reduced by collecting more data. PETS captures this by using **Probabilistic Neural Networks (P-NNs)**. Instead of outputting a single point prediction for the next state, a P-NN outputs the parameters of a probability distribution (e.g., mean and covariance for a Gaussian distribution). The network is trained to maximize the log-likelihood of observing the actual next state given the predicted distribution.
- **Epistemic Uncertainty (Subjective Uncertainty due to Limited Data):** This uncertainty reflects the model's lack of knowledge about the true dynamics function due to insufficient training data. It is reducible with more data. PETS captures epistemic uncertainty using **Ensembles of NNs**. Multiple P-NN models (e.g., B=5) are trained independently on different bootstrapped versions of the collected dataset (sampling with replacement). The disagreement among the predictions of these ensemble members reflects the epistemic uncertainty.

By combining these, PETS utilizes **Probabilistic Ensemble Neural Networks (PE-NNs)**, where each member of the ensemble is itself a probabilistic neural network. A crucial detail is bounding the variance output of the probabilistic networks to prevent erratic behavior outside the training data distribution.

#### 3.2. Planning and Control with Learned Dynamics (Model Predictive Control)

PETS employs **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** for planning. At each time step:
1. The learned PE dynamics model is used to predict future state trajectories for a set of candidate action sequences over a planning horizon (T).
2. The action sequence that yields the highest expected cumulative reward is selected.
3. Only the *first action* of this optimal sequence is executed in the real environment.
4. The process repeats by re-planning at the next time step with updated state information.

#### 3.3. Sampling-based Uncertainty Propagation (Trajectory Sampling - TS)

To compute the expected reward over state trajectories, PETS uses a particle-based approach for propagating uncertainty through the dynamics model:

- **Trajectory Sampling (TS):** Instead of just propagating the mean state or fitting a simple Gaussian distribution, TS propagates multiple "particles" (hypothetical states).
 - **TS1:** Particles uniformly re-sample a bootstrap model at each time step.
 - **TS∞ (PETS's primary choice):** Each particle is assigned a specific bootstrap model from the ensemble and uses that same model consistently for the entire prediction horizon of a trajectory. This allows for the separation of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties, which could be beneficial for exploration (though not explicitly leveraged for exploration in this paper). The method uses P=20 particles.

#### 3.4. Action Optimization with Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)

To efficiently find optimal action sequences within the MPC framework, PETS uses the **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)**. CEM is an iterative optimization technique that samples candidate action sequences from a distribution, evaluates them, selects the "elite" candidates, and then updates the sampling distribution to generate more promising candidates in subsequent iterations. This is more effective than simple random shooting, especially in high-dimensional action spaces.

#### 3.5. Learning Loop (Algorithm 1 Summary)

1. Initialize data `D` with one trial using a random controller.
2. For `K` trials:
 a. Train the PE dynamics model on the current dataset `D`.
 b. For each time step in the trial:
 i. Use CEM to sample candidate action sequences.
 ii. For each candidate sequence, propagate state particles using TS (specifically TS∞) and the trained PE model.
 iii. Evaluate the expected reward for each sequence.
 iv. Update the CEM distribution.
 c. Execute the first action of the optimal sequence found by CEM.
 d. Record the `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})` tuple and add it to `D`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PETS was rigorously evaluated on four challenging continuous-control benchmark tasks simulated in MuJoCo: Cartpole, 7-DOF Pusher, 7-DOF Reacher, and Half-cheetah. Key findings include:

- **Superior Sample Efficiency and Comparable Asymptotic Performance:**
 - PETS consistently achieved asymptotic performance levels comparable to, or even exceeding, state-of-the-art model-free algorithms like Soft Actor Critic (SAC) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
 - Crucially, PETS accomplished this with *significantly fewer samples*. For instance, on the Half-cheetah task, it required 8 times fewer samples than SAC and 125 times fewer samples than PPO to reach similar performance. This demonstrates a breakthrough in bridging the efficiency-performance gap.
- **Critical Importance of Uncertainty Estimation in Dynamics Models:**
 - The choice of dynamics model was found to be more impactful on performance than the specific uncertainty propagation technique.
 - **Probabilistic Ensembles (PE)**, which capture both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, consistently yielded the best performance across all tasks (except Cartpole, where GP-MM was marginally better but less scalable).
 - The performance ranking of model types was clear: **PE > P > DE > D**. This empirically validates that comprehensively modeling both types of uncertainty (aleatoric with probabilistic NNs, epistemic with ensembles) is essential for successful deep NN-based MBRL.
 - PETS (PE-TS1) substantially outperformed prior deterministic deep MBRL methods (e.g., [Nagabandi et al., 2017]'s D-E variant), underscoring the value of uncertainty estimation.
- **Robustness to MPC Horizon and Stochasticity:**
 - Probabilistic propagation methods (like TS) were more robust to "too long" MPC planning horizons compared to deterministic methods. The inherent uncertainty estimates prevented compounding errors from leading to catastrophic planning failures.
 - Probabilistic models (PE) demonstrated superior and more consistent performance under system stochasticity (e.g., noise added to actions), highlighting their ability to effectively capture and manage aleatoric uncertainty.
- **Effectiveness of Cross-Entropy Method (CEM):**
 - Experiments confirmed that CEM significantly outperformed simple random shooting for action optimization within MPC, particularly in higher-dimensional tasks like Half-cheetah. This highlights the importance of an efficient action selection strategy.
- **Scalability Advantage over Gaussian Processes:**
 - While GP-MM showed competitive performance on the low-dimensional Cartpole task, its cubic time and quadratic state dimensionality scaling made it infeasible for higher-dimensional tasks like Half-cheetah. PETS, being NN-based, maintains scalability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PETS algorithm represents a significant advancement in the field of reinforcement learning, particularly for its implications in real-world applications.

- **Redefining State-of-the-Art in MBRL:** PETS establishes a new benchmark for model-based deep reinforcement learning. It demonstrably shows that purely model-based approaches, using high-capacity parametric models like deep neural networks, can achieve asymptotic performance on par with the best model-free algorithms, a long-standing challenge in the field.
- **Bridging the Efficiency-Performance Gap:** The core contribution of PETS is successfully bridging the gap between the sample efficiency of model-based RL and the high asymptotic performance of model-free RL. This is a crucial step towards making reinforcement learning practical for domains where data collection is expensive, such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and industrial control.
- **Empirical Validation of Uncertainty Importance:** The systematic analysis within the paper provides strong empirical evidence for the critical role of comprehensively estimating both aleatoric (inherent system noise) and epistemic (model uncertainty due to limited data) uncertainties in deep dynamics models. This confirms a long-held theoretical belief and offers clear guidance for future model-based RL research.
- **Enabling Data-Efficient Robotics:** The ability to learn complex control tasks like Half-cheetah, 7-DOF Pusher, and Reacher with a "handful of trials" (significantly fewer interactions) is transformative for robotics. It reduces the time, cost, and wear-and-tear associated with training robots in the physical world, accelerating the deployment of RL-driven robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - The work opens avenues for integrating uncertainty quantification into directed exploration strategies, where epistemic uncertainty could guide the agent to explore areas where the model is least certain, leading to even faster learning.
 - It provides a strong foundation for investigating how policy learning can be combined with uncertainty-aware dynamics models to amortize the computational cost of planning at test-time, potentially addressing challenges like "chaotic policy gradients" identified in initial attempts.
 - The success of PETS encourages further investigation into the intricate design choices within model-based RL, suggesting that careful combination of existing techniques can yield substantial breakthroughs.

In conclusion, PETS offers a compelling solution to a fundamental challenge in deep reinforcement learning, demonstrating that efficient, high-performing RL in data-scarce environments is achievable through principled uncertainty modeling. This work holds immense potential for advancing the practical application of RL across various engineering and scientific domains.
