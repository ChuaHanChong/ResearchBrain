# R2S-EGO: Dual-Proxy Refinement for Sparse-Capture Real-to-Sim

> [!summary] Summary
> R2S-EGO refines a sparse-capture real-to-sim scene along robot ego views by coupling two proxies: a simulator-derived robot proxy that supplies behavior-admissible camera queries and locates support deficits, and a capture-anchored geometry proxy that conditions generation and supplies collision surfaces. Generated views enter the Gaussian asset as lower-weight pseudo-observations, giving 19.062 dB PSNR from six captures and 82.5% real-G1 sitting success.

## Problem

- Dense multi-view capture is costly per environment, while human-convenient sparse capture samples poses that leave behavior-scoped robot ego views visually and structurally unsupported.
- Camera-controlled novel-view generators can fill missing views, but they issue queries no embodied robot can execute and lack structure tied to the captured scene.
- Prior generation-feedback reconstruction stops at rendering quality, never coupling the refined visual asset to collision geometry, embodied control, and downstream policy transfer.

## Method

- **R2S-EGO**'s ==robot proxy==: rolling out a fixed controller in simulation each round maps robot configurations through the camera mount into executable ego-pose queries, ranked by asset deficit and geometry availability into a fixed keyframe budget.
- ==Geometry proxy==: triangulated capture fragments fuse with a ==SAM 3D== shape prior aligned in scale, rotation, and translation, ==NKSR== reconstructing prior-free regions; its renders condition generation and supply the collision mesh.
- Generated ego videos, conditioned on a real reference image, relative camera motion, and proxy renders, are registered by ==VGGT== and assimilated as lower-weight ==pseudo-observations==, with real captures kept as persistent anchors.

## Results

- On **48** frozen G1 ego views across three Replica scenes, six-view R2S-EGO reaches **19.062 dB** PSNR, **0.757** SSIM, and **0.273** LPIPS versus GaussGym's **14.226 dB**.
- Real-G1 sitting succeeds in **33/40** trials (**82.5%±6.8%**) versus GaussGym's **4/40** (**10.0%±10.5%**), despite close in-simulation success of **97.6%** and **87.2%**.
- Ablations drop PSNR to **16.182 dB** without robot-proxy allocation, **16.384 dB** without shape-prior grounding, and **17.508 dB** for one-shot instead of iterative refinement, under matched budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Allocate synthetic views along the consumer's executable trajectory distribution, not uniform reconstruction coverage; who consumes the views decides which support deficits actually matter.
> - Generated views need capture-anchored geometric conditioning: perceptually plausible frames with misplaced occluders corrupt an asset once treated as calibrated simulator observations.
> - In-simulation scores can hide transfer gaps; near-equal simulated success masked an eightfold hardware difference, so validate reconstructed assets downstream on hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. R2S-EGO: Dual-Proxy Refinement for Sparse-Capture Real-to-Sim - Research Report

#### 1.1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work is situated within the field of Real-to-Sim (R2S), which focuses on translating real-world environments into repeatable simulation settings for the purpose of robot training and evaluation. Existing R2S systems commonly reconstruct scenes from calibrated multi-view observations using various representations, including neural radiance fields (NeRF) or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).

The paper specifically addresses the challenge of "per-environment real-image capture-count efficiency". Traditional dense multi-view capture can be resource-intensive, while sparse capture often results in a "capture–consumption support gap." This gap arises because human-convenient capture poses may not adequately cover the specific views, surfaces, or occlusions relevant to a robot's behavior-scoped camera trajectories. Consequently, robot ego views—critical for policy training—may lack sufficient visual and structural support.

To mitigate this, camera-controlled novel-view and video synthesis methods can generate observations beyond the initially captured views. However, for R2S scene refinement, such synthetic observations must satisfy two specific validity criteria:
1. **Behavioral Admissibility**: The requested camera sequences must be physically realizable by the embodied robot within its declared behavior scope. This ensures the generated views are relevant to the robot's operational capabilities.
2. **Capture-Anchored Structural Conditioning**: The generated content must be conditioned on scene structure that is consistent with the captured environment. Generic view synthesis might produce perceptually plausible but structurally inconsistent images, which can be problematic when integrated into a calibrated simulator for training.

The R2S-EGO work distinguishes itself from prior art by focusing on targeted support for behavior-scoped executable robot views, rather than uniform global view completion or generic reconstruction. While methods exist for feeding generated views back into 3DGS or NeRF optimization to improve novel-view rendering, R2S-EGO's output is a refined *simulation scene* that includes both an updated visual asset and a refreshed collision geometry. This integration supports the full robot simulation interface, encompassing dynamics, embodied control, and downstream policy use, a boundary that many scene reconstruction or visual camera exploration methods do not explicitly define or evaluate. Furthermore, unlike active mapping techniques that acquire new real measurements, R2S-EGO allocates synthetic observations to existing support deficits within the simulator, grounding them in capture-anchored structure.

#### 1.2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for refining existing robot simulation scenes, particularly those initialized with sparse real-image captures, to enhance their fidelity and utility for robot learning.

The detailed motivations include:

1. **Overcoming Sparse Capture Limitations**: Real-world environments are often captured using a limited number of images due to practical constraints. This sparse data can lead to incomplete scene representations, especially for specific robot viewpoints (ego views) crucial for task execution and policy learning. The work aims to bridge this "capture–consumption support gap" where behavior-scoped robot views lack sufficient visual and structural information.
2. **Ensuring Validity of Synthetic Data**: While novel-view synthesis can generate additional views, their utility in R2S is contingent on two forms of validity:
 - **Behavioral Relevance**: The synthesized camera trajectories must correspond to physically executable motions of the robot within its defined behavior space. This prevents the generation of unrealistic or irrelevant views that would not be encountered by the robot in its operational context.
 - **Structural Fidelity**: The content of generated images must accurately reflect the underlying geometry, visibility, and occlusion patterns of the captured scene. Without this, synthetic views, even if visually appealing, could introduce inconsistencies that compromise their value as training observations in a calibrated simulator. The motivation is to anchor synthetic content to real-world structural evidence.
3. **Efficient Allocation of Computational Resources**: Given that generating high-quality synthetic observations can be computationally intensive, a critical motivation is to prioritize which missing observations to synthesize. This involves identifying specific under-supported regions within the robot's behavior space for which reliable structural conditioning information is available.
4. **Integrating Refinement into the R2S Pipeline**: The goal is to provide a complete, reusable simulation asset. This means the refinement process must not only improve the visual representation but also provide a consistent and updated collision surface for robot interaction and dynamics. The refined scene should seamlessly integrate with existing robot control stacks and physics engines without requiring changes to robot dynamics or control interfaces.
5. **Improving Sim-to-Real Transfer for Robot Policies**: The ultimate motivation is to create simulation environments that lead to more robust and successful deployment of learned policies on real robots. By producing higher-fidelity and behavior-aligned simulation scenes, the research aims to reduce the sim-to-real gap, thereby improving the transferability and performance of robot policies in the physical world.

#### 1.3. Methodology and approach

R2S-EGO introduces a dual-proxy method designed to refine existing robot simulation scenes from sparse real-image captures. The approach iteratively updates a visual asset and a collision surface by leveraging synthetic observations generated under specific conditions.

**A. Problem Setting and Output**
The system takes as input sparse RGB captures with calibrated poses (C), an initial visual asset (A₀), a simulator (S) defining robot embodiment, dynamics, and camera mount, and a declared behavior scope (B). R2S-EGO iteratively refines the scene over multiple rounds (l). The output is a refined visual asset (A*) and a current collision surface derived from capture-anchored fused geometry.

**B. Dual-Proxy Ego-View Construction**
The core of R2S-EGO involves two coupled proxies: a robot proxy for behavior-admissible queries and a geometry proxy for capture-anchored structural conditioning.

1. **Capture-Anchored Geometry Proxy**:
 - **Purpose**: To supply scene-specific structural conditions for novel view generation, extending context into sparsely observed regions while maintaining consistency with real captures.
 - **Process**:
 - **Fragmentary Geometry (`M_obs`)**: Initial 3D points are triangulated from calibrated multi-view tracks of the sparse RGB captures. This provides observed surfaces and their multi-view support.
 - **Shape Prior (`P_prior`)**: A predefined category vocabulary is used to prompt SAM 3 (Segment Anything Model 3), which, for each detected instance mask, leverages SAM 3D to obtain 3D object priors. These priors initially exist in their own canonical coordinate frames.
 - **Prior Alignment**: Each object prior is aligned in scale, rotation, and translation to the simulator's world frame. This alignment (T*m) is estimated using overlap between the prior points and the real triangulated points (`M_obs`), along with multi-view consistency checks between the projected prior silhouette and image masks. This transform is computed once and remains fixed.
 - **Fusion (`M_scaf`)**: The aligned SAM 3D surfaces are combined with the observed fragments. For regions without an object prior, NKSR (Neural Kernel Surface Reconstruction) reconstructs the geometry using remaining real triangulated points and the current backend geometry (from the visual asset A_l). This fused geometry `M_scaf` serves as the geometry proxy.
 - **Rendering for Conditioning**: Rendering `M_scaf` along a target camera path provides frame-aligned structural conditions (layout, visibility, depth ordering, occlusion cues) for the ego-view generator.

2. **Behavior-Scoped Robot Proxy**:
 - **Purpose**: To represent the ego-camera query domain executable by the robot within its declared behavior scope and identify under-supported views for generation.
 - **Process**:
 - **Executable Ego-Camera Poses (`V_l₀`)**: In each refinement round, a fixed behavior controller (`μ_B`) is executed in the simulator with resampled initial conditions or reference motions. Forward kinematics, combined with the robot's fixed camera mount (`B_T_C`), generate a stream of executable ego-camera poses (`W_T_C,t`). Failed rollouts are discarded and resampled until a target number (e.g., 12) of successful rollouts is obtained.
 - **Fixed-Budget Selection (`V_l`)**: From `V_l₀`, a fixed budget (`K`, e.g., 12) of target views is selected. Each candidate pose `t` is scored based on:
 - **Asset Deficit (`u_l(t)`)**: The fraction of pixels the current visual asset (`A_l`) cannot render at that pose.
 - **Geometry Availability (`g_l(t)`)**: The fraction of pixels the geometry proxy (`M_scaf`) can render at that pose.
 - The score `s_l(t) = u_l(t) * g_l(t)` prioritizes views where visual support is genuinely missing and reliable structural conditioning is available.
 - Non-maximum suppression (NMS) is applied temporally to collapse runs of similar scores into single keyframes. `TopK` selects the `K` highest-scoring surviving poses as the target views (`V_l`).

3. **Joint Ego-View Generation**:
 - **Inputs for Generation**: For each target view `v` ∈ `V_l`:
 - A real reference capture `I_i(v)` is identified (based on maximum capture-anchored overlap). This provides appearance.
 - A camera path `τ_v` is interpolated from the reference camera to the target pose over a fixed sequence length (`N_f`, e.g., 25 frames).
 - The structural condition `z_l_v` is rendered from `M_scaf` along `τ_v`.
 - The relative motion `¯τ_v` (poses relative to the reference camera) is provided.
 - **Generator**: A camera-controlled video diffusion model (ViewCrafter) (`G_θ`) takes `I_i(v)`, `z_l_v`, and `¯τ_v` to generate a sequence of ego-view frames (`X_l_v`).
 - **Pseudo-Observation Formation**: Only the terminal frame of the generated sequence (at the query endpoint) is retained. VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) then estimates its intrinsics and pose (`ˆπ_l_v`) relative to the fixed real reference.
 - **Assimilation**: These registered generated frames (`D_l_ego`) are assimilated into the visual asset as pseudo-observations with a lower weight (w_syn = 0.5) compared to real captures (w_real = 1), which remain persistent anchors.

**C. Scene Refinement and Implementation**
- **Visual Asset Update**: The visual representation (3DGS) is refined jointly using the real captures and the cumulative set of registered generated observations. The visual asset `A_l` is updated to `A_l+1`.
- **Collision Surface Update**: The fused geometry proxy (`M_scaf`) is installed as the scene's collision surface. This surface is refreshed between rounds to support subsequent robot rollouts and downstream simulation.
- **Iterative Process**: The entire process (geometry proxy update, robot proxy sampling and selection, ego-view generation, visual asset update, collision mesh update) is repeated for a fixed number of refinement rounds (L, e.g., 3).
- **Fixed Parameters**: Robot dynamics and control interfaces remain constant throughout the refinement process. All learned components use frozen public checkpoints.

#### 1.4. Main findings and results

The study evaluated R2S-EGO through quantitative and qualitative scene refinement metrics, component ablations, and a downstream real-robot policy validation.

**A. Ego-View Quality and View Efficiency**
- **Quantitative Comparison on Frozen G1 Ego Views**: Across 48 fixed Unitree G1 robot ego views in three Replica scenes (office_2, office_3, room_0), R2S-EGO significantly outperformed baseline methods.
 - R2S-EGO achieved a mean PSNR of **19.062 dB**, compared to 14.226 dB for GaussGym (the strongest reported R2S baseline), 14.059 dB for Difix3D+, 14.012 dB for GenFusion, and 13.239 dB for Vanilla 3DGS.
 - Similarly, R2S-EGO achieved the highest SSIM at **0.757** (vs. 0.567 for GaussGym) and the lowest LPIPS-Alex (indicating better perceptual similarity) at **0.273** (vs. 0.628 for GaussGym).
- **Qualitative Comparison**: Visual inspection of corresponding camera poses revealed that R2S-EGO preserved object boundaries and structural details (e.g., table rim, chair geometry, sofa contour) more accurately than baselines, which often showed visual artifacts like floaters, blur, or structural distortions.
- **Real-Image Capture-Count Efficiency**: In the office_2 scene, R2S-EGO achieved 19.674 dB PSNR using only six real input views. In contrast, baseline methods (Vanilla 3DGS, GaussGym, Difix3D+) required 45 real views to reach PSNRs of 12.424 dB, 13.458 dB, and 13.221 dB, respectively. None of the measured baseline configurations, even with significantly more real input views, matched the performance of R2S-EGO with its sparse (six-view) input.

**B. R2S-EGO Component Ablation**
An ablation study examined the contribution of key R2S-EGO components on the 48 frozen G1 views, reporting mean performance over five paired pipeline seeds.
- **"w/o ego video generation"**: Removing the generation of pseudo-observations reduced the PSNR to 13.417 dB, representing a 5.645 dB drop from the full R2S-EGO (19.062 dB). This indicates the critical role of synthetic views.
- **"w/o robot-proxy allocation"**: Replacing the targeted robot-proxy allocation with random camera-space trajectory sampling reduced PSNR to 16.182 dB (a 2.880 dB drop) and SSIM from 0.757 to 0.661, while LPIPS-Alex increased from 0.273 to 0.406. This supports the efficacy of the behavior-scoped and deficit-aware allocation strategy.
- **"w/o SAM 3D grounding"**: Removing the object-prior branch and relying solely on NKSR for geometry conditioning yielded a PSNR of 16.384 dB, indicating the contribution of capture-anchored structural conditioning.
- **"w/o iterative refinement"**: A budget-matched one-shot update (without intermediate NKSR/collision updates, controller rerollouts, or reranking) achieved 17.508 dB PSNR, lower than the 19.062 dB of the full iterative refinement, demonstrating the value of repeated reassessment and refinement rounds.

**C. Real-Robot Sitting Validation**
A sim-to-real transfer experiment was conducted using the Unitree G1 humanoid robot for a sitting task, with policies trained in either GaussGym or R2S-EGO simulation scenes. Five independently trained policies were evaluated for each visual asset condition.
- **In-Simulation Success**: Policies trained in GaussGym achieved 87.2% ± 5.2% success in simulation, while R2S-EGO-trained policies achieved 97.6% ± 2.2%.
- **Real-Robot Deployment Success**: On the real G1 robot, GaussGym-trained policies achieved a success rate of 10.0% ± 10.5% (4 out of 40 trials), whereas R2S-EGO-trained policies achieved **82.5% ± 6.8%** success (33 out of 40 trials).
- The improved hardware success rate was consistent across all five independently trained policy seeds. This result indicates a strong association between the refined R2S-EGO ego-view asset and improved sim-to-real transfer performance for the evaluated task.

#### 1.5. Significance and potential impact

The R2S-EGO research contributes to the field of robot learning by addressing critical challenges in creating high-fidelity and behavior-relevant simulation environments from sparse real-world data. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer Reliability**: The most direct and impactful finding is the substantial increase in real-robot task success rates demonstrated by policies trained in R2S-EGO-refined scenes (82.5% vs. 10% for the sitting task). This improvement suggests that carefully constructed simulation assets, especially those optimized for robot ego-views, can significantly close the sim-to-real gap, leading to more robust and deployable robot behaviors in the physical world. This is crucial for accelerating the development and deployment of autonomous robots.

2. **Increased Efficiency in R2S Scene Construction**: R2S-EGO demonstrates that high-quality simulation environments can be created from sparse real-image captures. Its ability to achieve superior rendering quality with only six real views compared to baselines using 45 views indicates a considerable improvement in data efficiency. This can reduce the time, cost, and complexity associated with dense real-world data collection, making R2S pipelines more practical for a wider range of applications and environments.

3. **Principled Approach to Synthetic Data Generation**: The dual-proxy methodology provides a structured and principled way to generate synthetic observations. By coupling a robot proxy (ensuring behavioral admissibility and targeting support deficits) with a capture-anchored geometry proxy (ensuring structural fidelity), the method generates synthetic data that is both relevant to robot operation and consistent with the real environment. This goes beyond generic novel-view synthesis, which might produce perceptually plausible but structurally inconsistent views.

4. **Foundation for Advanced Robot Learning**: By providing simulation environments that more accurately reflect robot ego-views and real-world interactions, R2S-EGO can serve as a stronger foundation for training advanced robot policies, especially those relying heavily on visual perception and fine-grained interactions. This could enable the training of more complex behaviors and skills that are difficult to learn in less accurate or less targeted simulated settings.

5. **Iterative Refinement for Continuous Improvement**: The iterative nature of R2S-EGO, where the scene and collision geometry are progressively refined over rounds, allows for continuous improvement of the simulation asset. This adaptive process, informed by current support deficits and geometry availability, ensures that the refinement efforts are consistently targeted where they are most needed.

**Potential Limitations and Future Work Identified by Authors**:
The authors acknowledge several boundaries and areas for future work:
- **Prior-Completed Geometry as Hypothesis**: The geometry completed by priors (e.g., SAM 3D) remains a "structural hypothesis" rather than verified scene truth. The independent quantification of its metric or collision accuracy, and its isolated contribution from visual-asset refinement, were not undertaken.
- **Behavior Scope vs. Learned Policy Occupancy**: The robot proxy covers a declared behavior scope, not necessarily the exact occupancy patterns of a learned policy, which might differ.
- **Error Propagation**: Repeated pseudo-observation updates could potentially propagate errors in generated appearance or prior-completed structure.
- **Scope of Hardware Evaluation**: The current hardware evaluation focused on a specific task (sitting) and variations in robot/chair poses and types. Future work includes evaluating additional behaviors and skills, such as arm-based manipulation and fine-grained hand–object interaction.

Despite these limitations, R2S-EGO represents a significant step towards creating more effective and efficient R2S pipelines for robot learning, demonstrating a clear benefit in sim-to-real transfer performance.
