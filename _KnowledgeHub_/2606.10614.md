# Dexterous Point Policy: Learning Point-based Dexterous Hand Policies from Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> The Dexterous Point Policy (DPP) framework enables learning dexterous robotic manipulation policies exclusively from human video demonstrations, eliminating the need for robot-specific data collection. It achieved an average 75.0% success rate on eight real-world dexterous tasks, demonstrating robust generalization to cluttered and novel object scenarios.

## Problem

- Collecting extensive robot trajectory data for dexterous multi-fingered manipulation is expensive and time-consuming, creating a data scarcity bottleneck.
- A significant 'embodiment gap' exists between human and robot hands, making it difficult to directly transfer policies learned from human videos to robots without substantial robot-specific fine-tuning.
- Prior point-based robot learning methods were limited to simple grippers and lacked mechanisms to incorporate crucial contact or force information for stable dexterous manipulation.

## Method

- A unified ==3D keypoint== representation abstracts both human and robot end-effectors as six points (wrist + five fingertips), bridging the embodiment gap for observation and action spaces.
- An autoregressive transformer policy is pretrained on a large internet-scale human video corpus (VITRA) and then fine-tuned on task-specific human demonstrations with minimal manual contact annotations.
- A contact-point prediction mechanism, learned during fine-tuning, is integrated to infer per-fingertip contact probabilities, which are then used to inject appropriate closing forces during robot execution.

## Results

- DPP achieved an average **75.0%** success rate across eight real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming adapted baselines (Point Policy: **3.7%**, VITRA: **1.0%**).
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization, maintaining **80.0%** success in multi-object cluttered environments and **76.7%** with novel objects, compared to **81.7%** on training objects.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of autoregressive modeling (full model **81.7%** vs. non-AR **37.5%** on pick-and-place) and internet-scale pretraining (+14.2 percentage points benefit).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstracting human and robot hands into a unified 3D keypoint representation effectively bridges the embodiment gap, enabling direct policy transfer from diverse human videos to dexterous robot hands.
> - Pretraining on internet-scale human video datasets significantly enhances the robustness and generalization of learned policies, contributing a +14.2 percentage point improvement over fine-tune-only training.
> - Augmenting point-based representations with even minimal contact information is crucial for stable manipulation, leading to a substantial +71.3 percentage point improvement in task success over a point-only baseline.
