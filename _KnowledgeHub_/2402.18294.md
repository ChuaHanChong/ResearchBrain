# Whole-body Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Human Reference

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and PNDbotics developed "Adam," a cost-effective and maintainable full-size humanoid robot, demonstrating human-like locomotion capabilities. Utilizing an adversarial motion prior framework, the robot exhibited nuanced human gaits, including heel-to-toe transitions and straight-knee movements, and achieved robust locomotion on diverse terrains.

## Problem

- High cost and maintenance complexity of traditional full-size humanoid robots hinder research accessibility.
- Designing effective and comprehensive reward functions for Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in high-dimensional humanoid systems is challenging.
- Bridging the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap remains a persistent challenge for transferring learned policies from virtual environments to physical humanoid robots.

## Method

- Designed and built 'Adam', a full-size biomimetic humanoid robot with cost-effective, maintainable motor-joint-driven QDD actuators and an integrated real-time control system.
- Employed an ==Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)== framework to learn human-like locomotion from diverse human motion capture datasets, circumventing complex reward function design.
- Implemented an end-to-end reinforcement learning setup with a comprehensive reward structure (command, periodic, regularization rewards) and extensive domain randomization for robust Sim2Real transfer.

## Results

- The 'Adam' humanoid robot was successfully developed, featuring a cost-effective, modular, motor-joint-driven design with high-performance actuators.
- Achieved first-time demonstration of nuanced human gait features, such as 'heel-to-toe' transitions and 'straight-knee' movements, in a full-size humanoid robot through imitation learning.
- Demonstrated robust and adaptable locomotion on various unseen terrains and under external disturbances, with successful policy transfer from simulation to the physical 'Adam' robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Humanoid robot hardware design, particularly using motor-joint-driven and modular structures, can significantly reduce cost and maintenance, making advanced research more accessible.
> - Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) combined with rich human motion data and a comprehensive reward structure can enable full-size humanoids to learn and exhibit complex, nuanced human gaits.
> - Robust Sim2Real transfer for humanoids is achievable through a combination of careful hardware design, extensive domain randomization during simulation training, and multi-platform cross-validation.
