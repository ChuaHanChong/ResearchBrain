# Whole-body Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Human Reference

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and PNDbotics developed "Adam," a cost-effective and maintainable full-size humanoid robot, demonstrating human-like locomotion capabilities. Utilizing an adversarial motion prior framework, the robot exhibited nuanced human gaits, including heel-to-toe transitions and straight-knee movements, and achieved robust locomotion on diverse terrains.

## Problem

- High cost and maintenance complexity of traditional full-size humanoid robots hinder research accessibility.
- Designing effective and comprehensive reward functions for Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in high-dimensional humanoid systems is challenging.
- Bridging the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap remains a persistent challenge for transferring learned policies from virtual environments to physical humanoid robots.

## Method

- Designed and built 'Adam', a full-size biomimetic humanoid robot with cost-effective, maintainable motor-joint-driven QDD actuators and an integrated real-time control system.
- Employed an ==Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)== framework to learn human-like locomotion from diverse human motion capture datasets, circumventing complex reward function design.
- Implemented an end-to-end reinforcement learning setup with a comprehensive reward structure (command, periodic, regularization rewards) and extensive domain randomization for robust Sim2Real transfer.

## Results

- The 'Adam' humanoid robot was successfully developed, featuring a cost-effective, modular, motor-joint-driven design with high-performance actuators.
- Achieved first-time demonstration of nuanced human gait features, such as 'heel-to-toe' transitions and 'straight-knee' movements, in a full-size humanoid robot through imitation learning.
- Demonstrated robust and adaptable locomotion on various unseen terrains and under external disturbances, with successful policy transfer from simulation to the physical 'Adam' robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Humanoid robot hardware design, particularly using motor-joint-driven and modular structures, can significantly reduce cost and maintenance, making advanced research more accessible.
> - Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) combined with rich human motion data and a comprehensive reward structure can enable full-size humanoids to learn and exhibit complex, nuanced human gaits.
> - Robust Sim2Real transfer for humanoids is achievable through a combination of careful hardware design, extensive domain randomization during simulation training, and multi-platform cross-validation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Current State of Humanoid Robotics

The field of humanoid robotics is currently experiencing rapid advancements, characterized by significant investment and groundbreaking demonstrations from both academic institutions and industry leaders. The authors position their work within this vibrant landscape, referencing prominent examples such as Boston Dynamics' Atlas (known for parkour-level mobility), Tesla's Optimus and Figure's humanoids (learning complex manipulation from human data), Agility Robotics' Cassie/Digit (bipedal locomotion), Unitree's H1, and Apptronik's Apollo. The entry of general-purpose AI companies like OpenAI (acquiring 1X robot company and proposing embodied intelligence plans) further signifies the field's growing importance as a bridge between digital AI and physical world interaction.

#### 1.2. Challenges in Humanoid Locomotion Control

Historically, traditional robot control algorithms, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), have been effective for legged robots (e.g., Boston Dynamics' Atlas and Spot). However, these methods critically depend on precise mathematical models and predefined motion planning, limiting their robustness and generalizability in unknown, dynamically changing environments. They also require high levels of expertise for model building and maintenance.
More recently, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising alternative, enabling robots to autonomously discover effective strategies through environmental interaction, offering unprecedented adaptability. Despite its successes in legged robots, DRL application to humanoids faces unique challenges:
- **High Cost and Maintenance:** Humanoid robots are expensive to acquire and maintain, posing a barrier for many research institutes.
- **Sim2Real Gap:** The disparity between simulated training environments and real-world physics makes transferring DRL models challenging.
- **Complexity of Reward Functions:** The high dimensionality and intricate dynamics of humanoid robots make designing effective and comprehensive reward functions for DRL incredibly difficult.

#### 1.3. The Role of Imitation Learning and Sim-to-Real Transfer

Learning from human references, particularly through imitation learning (IL), has become a crucial paradigm for endowing robots with complex, human-like motion capabilities. Traditional behavior cloning faced limitations in flexibility. Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL), particularly its variant Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP), has shown promise in allowing physics-based characters to imitate diverse motion styles from unstructured datasets. AMP has been successfully applied to quadrupedal locomotion. However, a significant limitation of prior AMP applications to humanoids is their focus on kinematic motion relationships without robust physics constraints, leading to a substantial Sim2Real gap. This paper aims to bridge this gap by integrating advanced hardware design with a sophisticated imitation learning framework that explicitly addresses real-world applicability and complex humanoid dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are multi-faceted, aiming to overcome the inherent complexities in achieving robust and human-like locomotion in full-size humanoid robots:

1. **Develop a Cost-Effective and Maintainable Humanoid Robot Optimized for Learning:** A core motivation was to create a full-size humanoid robot, "Adam," whose structural design (motor-joint-driven, modular, high-performance actuators) would significantly reduce the cost and complexity of maintenance compared to traditional hydraulic robots, thereby making advanced research more accessible and efficient. This design explicitly aimed to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of the imitation learning process.

2. **Propose a Novel and Generalizable Imitation Learning Framework:** The authors sought to develop an imitation learning framework based on adversarial motion priors (AMP) that is not only applicable to their custom robot, Adam, but also generalizable to other humanoid platforms. The key motivation here was to circumvent the notorious difficulty of designing intricate reward functions for complex humanoid tasks in traditional reinforcement learning.

3. **Achieve Unprecedented Human-like Locomotion:** A central goal was to enable the humanoid robot to exhibit human-comparable performance and characteristics in locomotion tasks, particularly demonstrating nuanced human gaits like "heel-to-toe" transitions and "straight-knee" movements, which had not been achieved before in full-size humanoids using imitation learning.

4. **Mitigate the Sim2Real Gap:** Recognizing the persistent challenge of transferring learned policies from simulation to the real world, the authors aimed to incorporate robust strategies, including extensive domain randomization and multi-platform cross-validation, to ensure the practical applicability and stability of their learned locomotion policies on the physical robot.

5. **Provide a New Research Paradigm:** Ultimately, the work aims to introduce a new methodology and experimental validation for the learning, adaptation, and optimization of humanoid robots, thereby paving a new way for future research and development in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors meticulously developed a comprehensive approach combining novel hardware design, data acquisition, and a sophisticated reinforcement learning framework.

#### 3.1. The Humanoid Robot "Adam"

The research centers around "Adam (Lite)," a custom-designed, full-size humanoid robot. Standing at 1.6 meters tall and weighing 60 kilograms, Adam is equipped with 25 force-controlled, Quasi-Direct Drive (QDD) PND actuators. Its design emphasizes biomimetics, particularly in its hip joint and overall torso configuration, to emulate human skeletal structure and range of motion. Key features contributing to its suitability for learning include:
- **Cost-effectiveness and Maintainability:** Its motor-joint-driven and modular design makes it more affordable and easier to repair than traditional hydraulic systems.
- **High Performance Actuators:** QDD actuators provide high sensitivity, back-drivability, and significant torque (up to 340 N·m in legs), crucial for dynamic and human-like movements.
- **Integrated Control System:** A full-stack, in-house designed PND Network (PDN) ensures real-time communication, managed by an Intel NUC and PND RCU, facilitating large-scale parallel simulations and neural network training.
The focus for this paper was on blind locomotion, so dexterous hands and vision modules were optional and not included.

#### 3.2. Human Motion Data and Re-Targeting

To achieve human-like locomotion, the robot was trained using diverse human motion data. The dataset was compiled from:
- **Public Motion Capture (Mocap) Databases:** Specifically, the SFU mocap dataset and the CMU mocap dataset, which cover a wide range of human activities (everyday actions, sports, dance, combat).
- **Custom High-Precision Mocap Recordings:** Used to capture specific, tailored motion data not available in public datasets, allowing for fine-tuning to experimental needs.
Crucially, the authors did not treat re-targeting as an optimization problem (as in some prior works like HumanMimic). Instead, they **manually calibrated and converted each dataset** to secure high-quality motion data precisely tailored for Adam. This manual, meticulous approach suggests a commitment to minimizing discrepancies between human reference and robot capabilities.

#### 3.3. Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) Framework

The core of the imitation learning approach is built upon the AMP framework. AMP utilizes a discriminator network (D) that differentiates between real human motion trajectories (expert demonstrations) and those generated by the robot's policy.
- **Discriminator Input:** The discriminator's observation includes the velocity and position of each actuated joint, along with the positions of the hands and feet (a 58-dimensional vector), ensuring it captures relevant kinematic and pose information for locomotion style.
- **Loss Functions:** The discriminator is trained with losses (`L_expert`, `L_policy`, `L_GP`) that encourage it to assign scores close to +1 for expert motions and -1 for policy-generated motions.
- **Imitation Reward (`r_I`):** The robot's policy receives an imitation reward `r_I` that incentivizes it to generate motions convincing enough to fool the discriminator, thereby closely mimicking the reference motions.

#### 3.4. End-to-End Reinforcement Learning with Task and Regularization Rewards

Beyond the AMP imitation reward, the policy is trained using a comprehensive reward structure within an end-to-end reinforcement learning setup, crucial for world-coordinate control, natural gaits, and Sim2Real transfer:
- **Command Reward (`r_com`):** Encourages the robot to move according to desired linear (x, y) and angular (yaw) velocities, allowing for explicit control over locomotion direction.
- **Periodic Reward (`r_per`):** Promotes stable and natural gaits by aligning with the periodic nature of walking/running. This reward is based on the swing and stance phases of the feet, modeled using the Von Mises distribution. It includes sub-rewards for foot speed tracking (encouraging higher speed during swing), height difference (maintaining balance), and gait symmetry (for natural movement).
- **Regularization Rewards:** A suite of rewards designed to enhance robustness, safety, and smoothness:
 - **Action Differential:** Encourages smooth control signals.
 - **DoF Limits, Velocity, and Acceleration:** Constrain joint movements within physical limits and encourage smooth motion profiles.
 - **Arm DoF Penalty:** Discourages unwanted large arm movements, keeping them close to the body for balance unless explicitly required.
 - **Orientation Differential and Torso Yaw:** Maintain stable body orientation and balance, particularly important for bipedal robots.
 - **Torques:** Penalizes excessive motor torques, promoting energy efficiency and reducing wear.

#### 3.5. Training and Domain Randomization

The policy was trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm across 4096 parallel Isaac Gym simulation environments.
- **Observations (`o_t`):** A 91-dimensional vector including linear/angular velocities, IMU data (gravity vector orientation), joint positions/velocities, desired command velocities, periodic observations (sine/cosine of cycle time, swing phase ratio), root height, and the previous action.
- **Actions (`a_t`):** A 25-dimensional vector representing desired positional adjustments for each actuated joint, commanded via a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** To bridge the Sim2Real gap and improve robustness, various physical parameters were randomized during training, including mass, center of mass (CoM) position, motor strength, impulse forces (simulated as sudden velocity changes), external forces, and linear velocity noise. This forces the policy to be robust to uncertainties and discrepancies between simulation and reality.

#### 3.6. Cross-Validation and Real-World Deployment

The authors performed comprehensive validation, not only within Isaac Gym but also through cross-validation experiments in Webots, another physics-rich simulation platform. This multi-simulator approach further confirmed the generalizability and robustness of the learned policies. Finally, the ultimate validation came from real-world tests on the physical "Adam" robot, demonstrating its ability to transition seamlessly from standing to running, navigate unseen terrains, and exhibit human-like gait characteristics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings and results, demonstrating the efficacy of their proposed humanoid robot and imitation learning framework:

1. **Successful Development of "Adam" Humanoid Robot:** The paper successfully introduces and details the innovative biomimetic humanoid robot "Adam," highlighting its advantages in terms of low cost, ease of maintenance (due to modular, motor-joint-driven design), and a human-comparable range of motion. This hardware development itself is a key enabling factor for the subsequent results.

2. **Unprecedented Human-like Locomotion:** The proposed whole-body imitation learning framework, utilizing adversarial motion priors (AMP) with human motion data, enabled "Adam" to exhibit a level of human-like characteristics in locomotion tasks previously unseen in full-size humanoid robots.

3. **Human-Comparable Performance in Complex Tasks:** Experimental results demonstrate that "Adam" can achieve performance comparable to humans in complex locomotion tasks. This includes robust walking and running on various terrains and under external disturbances.

4. **First-Time Demonstration of Nuanced Human Gaits:** The paper reports the first instance of a full-size humanoid robot demonstrating specific nuanced human gait features through imitation learning:
 - **"Heel-to-toe" transitions:** This natural walking/running characteristic, where the heel touches down first, followed by the toe, was successfully exhibited.
 - **"Straight-knee" movements:** Adam demonstrated walking with extended, "straight" knees, a hallmark of efficient human gait, distinguishing it from the bent-knee gaits often seen in robots.

5. **Robustness and Adaptability:** The integration of comprehensive regularization rewards and extensive domain randomization during training, coupled with the unique structural design of Adam, contributed to the robot's high robustness. This was demonstrated through its ability to walk and run on unseen and complex terrains, and maintain balance even when subjected to external disturbances.

6. **Effective Sim2Real Transfer:** The cross-validation experiments using both Isaac Gym and Webots, followed by successful deployment on the real "Adam" robot, confirmed the effectiveness of their method in bridging the simulation-to-reality gap. The policy learned in simulation translated seamlessly to the physical robot, showcasing its practical applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial step forward in the field of humanoid robotics, with several significant implications:

1. **Addressing Core Humanoid Robotics Challenges:** By introducing "Adam," a full-size, motor-joint-driven robot that is both cost-effective and easy to maintain, the authors directly tackle two major barriers to widespread humanoid research and development (cost and complexity). This could democratize access to humanoid platforms for more research groups.

2. **Advancing Imitation Learning for Humanoids:** The novel imitation learning framework based on adversarial motion priors, integrated with comprehensive task and regularization rewards, effectively bypasses the notoriously difficult problem of manual reward function design in complex humanoid reinforcement learning. This simplifies the development process for advanced humanoid behaviors.

3. **Achieving Higher Fidelity Human-like Movement:** The successful demonstration of "heel-to-toe" and "straight-knee" gaits for the first time in a full-size humanoid robot highlights a significant improvement in the naturalness and efficiency of robot locomotion. This increased bio-fidelity is crucial for human-robot interaction and deployment in human-centric environments.

4. **Robust Sim2Real Transfer:** The rigorous approach to domain randomization and multi-simulator cross-validation, validated by real-world performance, significantly contributes to the state-of-the-art in Sim2Real transfer for complex humanoid systems. This boosts confidence in applying simulation-trained policies to physical robots, accelerating development cycles.

5. **Paving the Way for Embodied AI:** This work provides a new methodology and experimental validation for learning, adaptation, and optimization of humanoid robots. By enabling humanoids to learn complex, natural motions from human data, it brings closer the vision of general-purpose embodied AI capable of operating fluently and robustly in diverse, unstructured human environments. The future integration of perceptual modules, as mentioned by the authors, will further enhance Adam's ability to learn and adapt, moving beyond blind locomotion to perceptive and interactive tasks.

In summary, this paper not only presents an impressive feat of robotic engineering with the "Adam" robot but also introduces a robust and practical imitation learning framework that pushes the boundaries of human-like locomotion in full-size humanoid robots, laying critical groundwork for their widespread adoption and utility in real-world service scenarios.
