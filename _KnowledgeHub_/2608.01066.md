# OC-VLA++: Monocular Geometry-Guided Cross-View Consistency for Viewpoint-Robust Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> OC-VLA++ enhances viewpoint generalization for robotic manipulation by integrating geometry-guided paired-view supervision and a cross-view action-equivariance objective into observation-centric Vision-Language-Action models. This approach leads to a slower degradation in success rates under increasing camera displacement and improves average success by 4.4 percentage points over OC-VLA in multi-view training simulation.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation exhibit sensitivity to viewpoint variations, with performance degrading when the camera's position or orientation changes from training.
- Policies trained with fixed camera setups often overfit to specific camera-related appearance cues due to insufficient cross-view supervision in the collected demonstration data.
- Existing observation-centric action grounding methods, such as OC-VLA, improve generalization but are still limited by the diversity of viewpoints present during training, especially with restricted data collection.

## Method

- **OC-VLA++** introduces ==geometry-guided paired-view supervision==, generating synthetic geometrically related views from a single original observation offline using a frozen monocular 3D reconstruction model.
- Enforced explicit ==cross-view action equivariance== by training the model with an objective that ensures action predictions from original and synthesized views align consistently in the robot's base frame after transformation.
- Integrated these components as additional training objectives into existing VLA architectures (==Dita-based and Qwen3-VL-2B-based==) without modifying their core network structure.

## Results

- Achieved comparable manipulation performance to OC-VLA at the training viewpoint (e.g., **68.3%** success for Dita-based models), indicating no degradation in in-distribution performance.
- Demonstrated a slower performance degradation under increasing camera displacement on a real robot, outperforming OC-VLA and robot-base-frame baselines (e.g., **48.3%** vs. **40.8%** for Dita at maximum shift).
- Improved average success by **4.4** percentage points over OC-VLA in a multi-view training simulation (from **52.4%** to **56.8%**), confirming the benefits of explicit cross-view action equivariance even with diverse training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly supervising how action predictions should transform across viewpoints is crucial for achieving viewpoint-robustness, complementing implicit learning from diverse image augmentations.
> - Leveraging offline monocular 3D reconstruction to synthesize geometrically consistent paired views from single-camera demonstrations effectively reduces the need for costly multi-view real-world data collection.
> - The proposed geometric supervision and action equivariance objective can be seamlessly integrated into existing observation-centric VLA architectures as training losses, enhancing generalization without requiring architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has increasingly adopted Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which process multimodal inputs such as visual observations, natural language instructions, and robot proprioceptive states to predict executable actions. These models have demonstrated advancements in manipulating objects and executing complex, long-horizon tasks. However, a persistent challenge in deploying VLA models is their sensitivity to viewpoint variations. Policies trained with a fixed camera often experience significant performance degradation when the camera's position or orientation changes, even if the task and scene remain constant. This issue is particularly relevant in practical applications where robot demonstrations are typically collected from a limited number of convenient camera placements.

Previous research has explored several approaches to address viewpoint robustness in robot learning. These include:
- **Explicit camera conditioning:** Methods that incorporate camera parameters or geometric features (e.g., camera rays, calibrated features, robot-centric point representations) to reduce geometric ambiguity between perception and control.
- **Camera-centric action formulations:** Approaches that express actions relative to the observing camera, aligning action representations with visual observations. A notable example is OC-VLA (Observation-Centric Vision-Language-Action), which grounds robot actions in the camera coordinate system to simplify the mapping from visual evidence to control.
- **View synthesis for augmentation:** Techniques that generate alternative viewpoints during training or for test-time adaptation to increase viewpoint diversity.

While observation-centric action grounding, as employed by OC-VLA, improves generalization across camera configurations, its effectiveness can still be limited by the diversity of viewpoints available during training. If demonstrations are collected from only a few fixed cameras, the policy may still overfit to camera-specific appearance cues due to insufficient cross-view supervision.

OC-VLA++ contributes to this landscape by extending OC-VLA. It aims to overcome the limitation of overfitting to specific viewpoints by introducing explicit geometric supervision across views. Unlike methods that solely rely on image-level augmentation or maintain invariant robot-frame targets, OC-VLA++ combines camera-space action grounding with geometry-guided paired-view supervision and a cross-view action-equivariance objective. This approach explicitly supervises how action predictions should transform across viewpoints, seeking to enhance robustness under conditions of limited camera coverage during data collection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the viewpoint generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, especially when the training data is acquired from a restricted set of camera viewpoints.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations:
- **Real-world deployment challenges:** Robotic systems operating in unstructured environments frequently encounter variations in camera placement or perspective. Policies trained in controlled settings with fixed cameras often fail to generalize to these unseen viewpoints, hindering their practical utility.
- **Limitations of current VLA models:** Despite advancements in VLA models, their robustness to camera changes remains an area requiring improvement. The mismatch between the camera-centric nature of visual observations and the robot-centric nature of traditional action targets contributes to this vulnerability.
- **Incompleteness of prior solutions:** OC-VLA addressed the coordinate system mismatch by grounding actions in the camera frame, demonstrating improved generalization. However, even with this observation-centric representation, the policy's robustness still relies on the diversity of viewpoints available during training. If the training data lacks sufficient viewpoint variation, the model can still overfit to specific camera-related visual cues. This indicates that while camera-space grounding is beneficial, it does not fully resolve the problem of insufficient cross-view supervision under limited data.

To address these motivations, OC-VLA++ was developed with the following specific objectives:
- **Augment observation-centric grounding:** To complement OC-VLA's camera-space action representation with additional mechanisms that enhance viewpoint robustness.
- **Introduce geometry-guided paired-view supervision:** To generate synthetic, geometrically related views from a single original observation, effectively creating "paired" training examples. This aims to provide the model with exposure to varied viewpoints without requiring extensive real-world multi-view data collection.
- **Enforce explicit cross-view action equivariance:** To train the model such that action predictions derived from geometrically related paired views (original and synthesized) correspond to the same physical action in the robot's base frame. This objective explicitly teaches the policy how action predictions should transform in response to camera pose changes, moving beyond implicit learning from image augmentations.
- **Demonstrate improved generalization:** To empirically show that these proposed additions lead to substantial improvements in unseen-view generalization performance, particularly under increasing camera displacement, while maintaining performance at the training viewpoint.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OC-VLA++ builds upon the observation-centric action grounding of OC-VLA by introducing two main components: geometry-guided paired-view supervision and a cross-view action-equivariance objective. The overall framework is designed to introduce explicit geometric supervision across viewpoints without modifying the underlying policy architecture.

The methodology involves the following steps:

#### 3.1. Geometry-Guided Paired-View Construction

- **Offline Process:** For each real-world observation (an RGB image) `I_c_t` from camera `c`, a nearby synthesized view `I_c'_t` from a different camera pose `c'` is generated. This process is conducted offline and does not incur inference-time overhead.
- **Monocular 3D Reconstruction:** A frozen monocular geometry estimator (specifically, the MoGe-2 model) is used with the calibrated camera intrinsics `K_c` to reconstruct a dense point map `P_c_t` and a validity mask `M_c_t` from the single input image `I_c_t`.
- **View Synthesis:** A set of `K` local camera transformations `{∆T_c→c_k}` are sampled. These transformations involve small perturbations in translation (e.g., up to 15 cm) and rotation (e.g., up to 10°). For each sampled transformation, the reconstructed 3D points are rigidly transformed into the new camera frame and then reprojected onto the target image plane using the calibrated intrinsics. Depth-based visibility resolution is applied to handle occlusions.
- **View Bank and Filtering:** The synthesized views, along with their validity masks and new camera poses, form an offline view bank. During training, one synthesized view is randomly sampled and paired with the original observation. Views with insufficient valid-pixel coverage are discarded.
- **Action Target Adaptation:** Critically, while the original and synthesized views correspond to the same physical robot-frame action `A_robot_t`, their camera-space action targets `A_c_t` and `A_c'_t` are computed relative to their respective camera frames. This ensures that each view is supervised by an action target correctly expressed in its own coordinate system.
- **Simulation vs. Real-world:** In simulation, paired views are generated by directly re-rendering the same simulator state from different camera poses, providing exact observations without monocular reconstruction errors.

#### 3.2. Cross-View Action Equivariance

This objective enforces consistency between action predictions from the paired views after they are transformed into a shared robot frame.
- **Shared Generative Model:** The original and synthesized views are processed by the same underlying generative model (e.g., a diffusion model or flow-matching model), ensuring parameter sharing.
- **Clean-Action Recovery:** The raw generative outputs of the model (e.g., predicted noise in diffusion models or predicted velocity in flow-matching models) are not directly suitable for action-level consistency. Therefore, a differentiable process is applied to recover a "clean" action estimate (`bA_v_t`) from each branch's output.
- **Robot-Frame Alignment:** The recovered camera-space action estimates (`bA_c_t` and `bA_c'_t`) are first unnormalized and then transformed from their respective camera frames into the shared robot base frame (`bA_robot,c_t` and `bA_robot,c'_t`).
- **Stop-Gradient Mechanism:** A stop-gradient operation is applied to the original-view's robot-frame action prediction (`bA_robot,c_t`) when computing the equivariance loss. This ensures that the loss primarily updates the shared model parameters through the synthesized-view path, making the original-view prediction a stable reference and mitigating potential issues from artifacts in the synthesized observation.
- **Action-Level Equivariance Loss (`L_eq`):** The robot-frame action predictions are decomposed into translational (position), rotational (unit quaternion), and gripper command components. A component-wise loss is calculated:
 - **Translation:** Huber loss on the Euclidean distance between predicted positions, capped at a maximum value (`κ_p`).
 - **Rotation:** Huber loss on the quaternion geodesic angle, also capped (`κ_R`).
 - **Gripper:** Huber loss on the difference in gripper commands.
 - These component-wise discrepancies are weighted (`λ_p`, `λ_R`, `λ_g`) and summed to form the total `L_eq`.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

The overall training objective combines three terms:
- `L = L_c_gen + λ_syn * L_c'_gen + λ_eq * L_eq`
 - `L_c_gen`: The standard generative objective for the original (real) view.
 - `L_c'_gen`: The generative objective for the synthesized view, weighted by `λ_syn`. This supervises the synthesized view with its corresponding camera-space action target.
 - `L_eq`: The cross-view action-equivariance loss, weighted by `λ_eq`. This aligns the recovered action predictions in the shared robot frame.

#### 3.4. Model Architectures

OC-VLA++ is model-agnostic and was evaluated on two distinct architectures:
- **Dita-based model:** A diffusion transformer (Dita) that directly denoises continuous action chunks. It was first pretrained on the DROID dataset and then fine-tuned on target datasets.
- **Qwen3-VL-2B-based action expert model:** This model uses a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), Qwen3-VL-2B, as a backbone to encode multimodal context, coupled with a dedicated action expert that generates continuous actions via flow matching. It was fine-tuned directly on target datasets.

In both implementations, OC-VLA++ does not introduce new network modules; the proposed supervision is applied through additional training objectives using the existing shared model parameters.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Real-Robot Experiments:** Conducted with a Franka Emika Panda robot. Training data consisted of 10 demonstrations per task, collected from a *single fixed camera*. Evaluation involved a movable camera to test robustness under increasing translational and rotational displacement (up to 30.5 cm and 23.4°).
- **Simulation Experiments:** Used ManiSkill2 tasks, providing controlled environments for multi-view training and ablation studies. Paired observations were generated by re-rendering the same simulator state from different camera poses.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer was used with specific learning rate schedules and loss weights (e.g., `λ_syn = 0.5`, `λ_eq = 0.5`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both on real-robot systems and in simulation demonstrated the contributions of OC-VLA++ to viewpoint-robust robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Fixed-View Evaluation (Real-Robot)

- When evaluated with the same fixed camera configuration used for training (in-distribution setting), OC-VLA++ achieved average success rates of 68.3% with the Dita-based architecture and 59.2% with the Qwen3-VL-2B-based architecture.
- These results were nearly identical to those achieved by the corresponding OC-VLA variants (68.3% and 60.0%).
- **Finding:** The additional geometry-guided paired-view supervision and cross-view action equivariance did not degrade manipulation performance at the training viewpoint.

#### 4.2. Camera-Displacement Evaluation (Real-Robot)

- As the observation camera was progressively moved away from the training configuration, the performance of all models decreased.
- **Finding:** OC-VLA++ consistently exhibited a slower degradation in success rate compared to both OC-VLA and robot-base-frame baselines, for both Dita-based and Qwen3-VL-2B-based architectures.
 - At the largest camera shift (30.5 cm translational, 23.4° rotational displacement), OC-VLA++ achieved success rates of 48.3% (Dita) and 43.3% (Qwen-based).
 - In comparison, OC-VLA achieved 40.8% (Dita) and 37.5% (Qwen-based), and robot-base-frame models achieved 31.7% (Dita) and 30.8% (Qwen-based).
- **Finding:** The locally generated paired-view supervision (trained with perturbations up to 15 cm and 10°) effectively generalized to substantially larger physical camera shifts, indicating a broader applicability of the learned equivariance.
- Qualitative results (Figure 5) illustrated that OC-VLA++ consistently produced valid manipulation behaviors across diverse object configurations and viewing conditions, even under significant camera displacement.

#### 4.3. Simulation Experiments (ManiSkill2) - Multi-View Training

- In a multi-view training setting with diverse camera observations already available, OC-VLA++ (56.8%) still improved over OC-VLA (52.4%) by 4.4 percentage points on average.
- **Finding:** This indicates that explicit cross-view action equivariance provides benefits beyond merely exposing the model to additional view diversity, suggesting its unique contribution to learning how actions transform across viewpoints.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Simulation)

- Adding generative supervision on the synthesized view (`L_c'_gen` only) improved the average success rate from 52.4% (OC-VLA) to 53.4%.
- Adding cross-view action equivariance (`L_eq` only) improved the average success rate to 54.2%.
- **Finding:** Both components (synthesized-view supervision and cross-view action equivariance) contributed to the overall improvement, with action equivariance providing a larger individual gain. Combining both components yielded the highest success rate of 56.8%, demonstrating their complementary nature.

#### 4.5. Sensitivity Analysis of Loss Weights

- An analysis of the weights `λ_syn` and `λ_eq` showed that OC-VLA++ performed consistently well across a moderate range of objective weights.
- **Finding:** The model's performance was not highly sensitive to the precise choice of these weights, indicating robustness in hyperparameter selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented on OC-VLA++ offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Enhanced Viewpoint Robustness:** The primary significance lies in providing a method that substantially improves the robustness of robotic manipulation policies to camera viewpoint variations. This is a crucial step towards deploying VLA models in real-world scenarios where camera positions are not always fixed or ideal. The more graceful degradation under increasing camera displacement observed with OC-VLA++ is a key indicator of this enhanced robustness.

- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** OC-VLA++ effectively complements observation-centric action grounding (as in OC-VLA) by addressing its limitations regarding insufficient cross-view supervision. It demonstrates that combining camera-space action representations with explicit geometric consistency constraints across views yields superior generalization.

- **Practicality for Data Collection:** The approach leverages offline monocular geometry estimation to synthesize paired views from single-camera demonstrations. This is highly practical for real-world data collection, where acquiring multi-view demonstrations for diverse camera poses is often costly and time-consuming. The method reduces the dependency on extensive multi-view training data, making VLA models more accessible for deployment.

- **Architectural Flexibility:** The demonstrated effectiveness across two distinct model architectures (diffusion-based Dita and VLM-based Qwen3-VL-2B) suggests that OC-VLA++ is a generalizable approach. It does not require modifying the core policy architecture, integrating as an additional training objective, which eases adoption by various VLA models.

- **Conceptual Advancement in Action Equivariance:** The research establishes action-level cross-view supervision as a vital component for viewpoint-robust manipulation. By explicitly modeling how action predictions should transform across viewpoints, rather than relying solely on image-level augmentations, the policy gains a deeper geometric understanding essential for generalized control. This highlights a critical principle for designing more robust robot learning systems.

- **Potential for Real-World Deployment:** By mitigating the performance drop due to camera changes, OC-VLA++ makes VLA models more reliable and adaptable for real-world robotic tasks. This can facilitate wider adoption of advanced VLA capabilities in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and other domains where robots need to operate effectively despite varied observation conditions.

In conclusion, OC-VLA++ represents an advancement in viewpoint-robust robotic manipulation. Its ability to maintain performance at training viewpoints while exhibiting slower degradation under significant camera displacement, coupled with its practical data requirements and architectural flexibility, positions it as a relevant contribution to the development of more generalizable and deployable robotic agents.
