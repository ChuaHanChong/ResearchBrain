# EquiBot: SIM(3)-Equivariant Diffusion Policy for Generalizable and Data Efficient Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EquiBot, developed by Stanford University, introduces a SIM(3)-equivariant diffusion policy that enables robots to learn manipulation tasks from limited data and generalize robustly to unseen variations in object scale, rotation, and translation. The framework consistently outperformed baselines, demonstrating superior out-of-distribution generalization and data efficiency in both simulation and real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional visuomotor policies require extensive data and struggle to generalize to novel object appearances, scales, or poses in real-world environments.
- Existing equivariant robot manipulation methods are often limited to simpler tasks, lack support for closed-loop policies, or do not fully address SIM(3) symmetries (scale, rotation, translation).
- Deterministic policy architectures often struggle with multi-modal behaviors and maintaining temporal consistency in actions.

## Method

- **EquiBot** integrates SIM(3)-equivariant neural network architectures with diffusion models, modifying the noise prediction network to maintain equivariance at each denoising step.
- It uses a SIM(3)-equivariant PointNet-based encoder to extract latent features, object centroid, and scale, which are then used to canonicalize proprioception and noisy actions.
- The core of the policy is an SO(3)-equivariant conditional U-net that processes canonicalized inputs, with FiLM layers adapted using 'vector neuron' layers to ensure rotation equivariance.

## Results

- EquiBot consistently maintained robust performance and exhibited the least performance degradation across all out-of-distribution (rotation, scale, position) setups in simulation experiments (e.g., Cloth Folding, Box Closing).
- It demonstrated significantly higher data efficiency, maintaining performance with as few as 25 demonstrations where baseline Diffusion Policy performance dropped considerably.
- In real-world mobile manipulation tasks (e.g., Push Chair, Laundry Door Closing), EquiBot achieved significantly higher success rates (e.g., 8/10 for Push Chair vs. 0/10 for DP) and fewer collision/safety issues compared to non-equivariant baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding SIM(3)-equivariance directly into a diffusion policy allows for inherent generalization to variations in object scale, rotation, and translation without extensive data augmentation.
> - Combining the robustness and multi-modality handling of diffusion models with the generalization benefits of equivariance leads to a data-efficient and stable learning framework for complex tasks.
> - Canonicalizing observations and actions based on learned object properties (centroid, scale) before feeding them into an SO(3)-equivariant U-net enables end-to-end SIM(3)-equivariance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous robotic agents for real-world environments is significantly challenged by the inherent unpredictability and variability of these settings. Traditional visuomotor policies, often learned through imitation learning, exhibit proficiency in controlled environments but typically necessitate extensive data collection and demonstrate limited generalization capabilities to novel or unseen scenarios. This constraint hinders their practical deployment in dynamic, real-world applications.

Existing approaches to address these limitations have explored several avenues. Some research focuses on data-efficient imitation learning by employing one-shot or few-shot learning paradigms, which usually depend on the availability of multi-task demonstration data, or by using sampling-based optimization methods, often restricted to small action spaces or open-loop settings. Another common strategy for improving generalization involves data augmentation, where training data is synthetically expanded. While this can enhance robustness to some variations, it frequently increases training time and does not guarantee generalization to visual appearances, object scales, or poses that fall outside the augmented training distribution.

A distinct line of research incorporates equivariant representations into policy learning. Equivariant networks are designed such that their outputs transform predictably (e.g., scale, translate, rotate) in response to corresponding transformations of their inputs. This inherent property can enable generalization to previously unobserved object conditions without explicit training on those specific variations. However, prior work in equivariant robot manipulation has often been restricted to simpler tasks like pick-and-place, lacked support for closed-loop policies, or only addressed specific types of equivariance (e.g., SO(2) or SO(3), but not SIM(3) which includes scaling). For instance, EquivAct, a previous work by some of the current authors, introduced SIM(3)-equivariant visuomotor policy learning for complex objects but could not effectively handle multi-modal training data due to its deterministic architecture, nor could it consistently predict future actions for temporal consistency.

Concurrently, diffusion models have emerged as a robust paradigm for policy representation in visuomotor learning, offering advantages such as handling multi-modality and producing temporally consistent action sequences. However, directly applying diffusion policies often inherits the data-intensive and generalization limitations of other non-equivariant methods, necessitating substantial data or extensive augmentations to cope with scene variability.

EquiBot positions itself at the intersection of these fields by integrating SIM(3)-equivariant neural network architectures with diffusion models. This approach aims to synergistically combine the generalization benefits of equivariance (robustness to scale, rotation, and translation changes) with the robustness, multi-modality, and temporal consistency offered by diffusion-based policy learning. The work seeks to overcome the data inefficiency and poor generalization of conventional imitation learning, the limitations of augmentation-based methods, and the architectural constraints of prior equivariant policies. By doing so, EquiBot endeavors to create a visuomotor policy learning method that is both highly data-efficient and generalizable across diverse manipulation tasks in real-world, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an effective imitation learning method that enables robots to learn from limited data and generalize robustly across diverse real-world environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Address Data Scarcity in Robot Learning:** A primary motivation is to overcome the requirement for substantial data in existing visuomotor policy learning methods. Collecting large, diverse datasets for robot manipulation tasks is resource-intensive and time-consuming, hindering the practical deployment of robots. The authors aim to significantly reduce the data requirements for learning effective policies.

2. **Enhance Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** Current imitation learning policies often perform poorly when faced with environments or object configurations that differ from their training data. This includes variations in object visuals, sizes, placements, and orientations. A key objective is to enable policies to generalize robustly to these "unseen" scenarios, moving beyond the limitations of controlled settings.

3. **Improve Robustness and Handle Multi-modality:** Traditional, deterministic policy architectures can struggle with the inherent complexities of real-world tasks, such as multi-modal behaviors (e.g., multiple valid ways to complete a sub-task) and the need for temporally consistent actions. The research aims to develop a policy that can learn robustly, accommodate diverse action distributions, and generate coherent action sequences over time.

4. **Leverage Inherent Symmetries for Learning Efficiency:** The authors are motivated by the principle that if a robot's action should transform predictably when its observation transforms predictably (e.g., if an object is rotated, the action should also rotate), then embedding this symmetry (equivariance) directly into the network architecture can inherently facilitate generalization and reduce the need to learn these transformations from vast amounts of data. Specifically, SIM(3)-equivariance (to scale, translation, and rotation) is targeted as a key mechanism for achieving data efficiency and generalization.

5. **Overcome Limitations of Prior Equivariant Methods:** Existing equivariant approaches in robot manipulation often have limitations, such as being restricted to simple pick-and-place tasks, lacking support for closed-loop policies, or not covering all relevant symmetries (e.g., scale). The motivation is to build upon and extend these by developing a SIM(3)-equivariant architecture capable of handling complex, closed-loop 3D manipulation tasks with deformable and articulated objects, which was a challenge for prior works like EquivAct due to its deterministic nature.

6. **Integrate Diffusion Models with Equivariance:** A specific architectural objective is to combine the benefits of diffusion models (robustness, multi-modality, temporal consistency) with the generalization advantages of SIM(3)-equivariance. This involves designing an equivariant noise prediction network such that each diffusion step maintains equivariance, leading to an overall equivariant action distribution.

In summary, the motivation is to create a robust, data-efficient, and generalizable robot manipulation learning paradigm capable of performing a wide range of household tasks from minimal human demonstrations, using single-view, object-centric point clouds and robot proprioception to output 6D end-effector velocity commands and gripper signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EquiBot method is designed to combine SIM(3)-equivariant neural network architectures with diffusion models to learn generalizable and data-efficient visuomotor policies. The approach centers on modifying existing diffusion policy architectures to embed equivariance at each step of the denoising process.

**Core Principles:**
The method begins by leveraging the framework of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) to represent the conditional distribution of actions, `p(A_t|O_t)`. The key insight is to ensure that the noise prediction network, `ϵ_θ`, within the diffusion process is SIM(3)-equivariant. This design principle ensures that if inputs (observations) scale, translate, or rotate, the network's internal processing and subsequent denoised actions will transform accordingly. This implicit generalization capability is intended to reduce reliance on extensive data augmentations.

**Formal Argument for Equivariant Distributions:**
The paper presents a proposition stating that if the prior distribution `p(A_K|O_t)` (which is a standard Gaussian and SO(3)-equivariant) and the Markov transition probabilities `p(A_{k-1}|A_k, O_t)` (predicted by the equivariant network) are SO(3)-equivariant, then the final output action distribution `p(A_0|O_t)` will also be SO(3)-equivariant. For SIM(3)-equivariance, translation and scaling are handled via canonicalization steps performed before the diffusion process.

**SIM(3)-Equivariant Diffusion Policy Architecture (EquiBot):**
The architecture systematically modifies components of a CNN-based Diffusion Policy to achieve SIM(3)-equivariance.

1. **Observation and Action Spaces:**
 - **Observations (O_t):** Consist of a scene point cloud (X_t) and robot proprioception (S_t). The proprioception is structured into 3D positions (`S_t^(x)`), normalized directions (`S_t^(d)`), and scalars (`S_t^(s)`). This format is designed to align with equivariant processing.
 - **Actions (A_t):** Represented similarly as 3D offsets/velocities (`A_t^(v)`), normalized directions (`A_t^(d)`), and scalars (`A_t^(s)`).

2. **Encoder:**
 - A PointNet-based encoder, specifically a SIM(3)-equivariant variant as described in, is used.
 - This encoder takes the input point cloud `X` and outputs a latent code `Θ = (Θ_R, Θ_inv, Θ_c, Θ_s)`. `Θ_R` is a rotation-equivariant representation, `Θ_inv` is an invariant representation, `Θ_c` is the computed object centroid, and `Θ_s` is the computed object scale.
 - Unlike prior work, this encoder is learned from scratch without pre-training, removing the need for task-specific simulation environments.

3. **Routing Inputs into a Conditional U-net (Canonicalization):**
 - To achieve SIM(3)-invariance for processing within the core U-net, `Θ_c` and `Θ_s` are used to canonicalize the robot proprioception (`S_t`) and noisy action (`A_t`).
 - **Action Representation (Z_a):** Composed by fusing scaled velocity components (`A_t^(v) / Θ_s`) and directional components (`A_t^(d)`) using a Vector Neuron layer. Scalar action components (`A_t^(s)`) are also included.
 - **Conditioning Information (Z_c):** Formed from invariant features (`Θ_inv`), canonicalized proprioceptive positions `((S_t^(x) - Θ_c) / Θ_s)`, proprioceptive directions (`S_t^(d)`), scalar proprioceptive components (`S_t^(s)`), and positional embeddings of the diffusion iteration `k`. These are separated into vector and scalar conditioning inputs for the FiLM layers.

4. **SO(3)-Equivariant Conditional U-net:**
 - The U-net, which processes `Z_a` and `Z_c`, is adapted to be SO(3)-equivariant.
 - **1D Convolution Layers:** Made SO(3)-equivariant by treating vector channels as batch dimensions and applying standard convolution operations.
 - **Upsampling Layers:** These are inherently SO(3)-equivariant and require no modification.
 - **FiLM Layers:** The standard linear layers within FiLM are replaced with 'vector neuron' layers, ensuring rotation equivariance in the modulation process (`η = f(x)` and `β = h(x)` are predicted by vector neuron layers).

5. **Output:**
 - The SO(3)-equivariant U-net outputs scale- and translation-invariant actions (`A_inv`).
 - The final output action `A_t` is re-scaled using `Θ_s` and `Θ_c` to restore the SIM(3)-equivariant property: `A_t = (A_inv^(v) * Θ_s, A_inv^(d), A_inv^(s))`.

**Training and Implementation Details:**
- The method operates in an imitation learning setup, utilizing a dataset of demonstration trajectories.
- The noise prediction network hyperparameters are inherited from Diffusion Policy.
- DDPM scheduler is used for simulation experiments, while DDIM scheduler with fewer denoising steps (8) is used for real-robot experiments to optimize inference speed.
- A specific normalization scheme is applied: all 3D-vector inputs (observations and actions) are normalized together based on computed mean point cloud and action scales, ensuring values remain within a desirable range (`-1` to `1`). Scalar information is normalized conventionally.
- Point cloud sizes (e.g., 512 or 1024 points) and observation/prediction horizons are consistent with.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EquiBot was conducted through a series of quantitative experiments in both simulation and real-world robot settings, comparing its performance against baseline methods across various metrics including out-of-distribution generalization, data efficiency, and training stability.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

The simulation experiments aimed to assess EquiBot's generalization capabilities to unseen scenarios and its data efficiency.

- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:**
 - **Tasks:** Four simulated tasks were used: Cloth Folding, Object Covering, Box Closing (involving two mobile robots with deformable/articulated objects), and Push T (a 2D T-shape pushing task with multi-modal demonstrations).
 - **Baselines:** Diffusion Policy (DP) with a PointNet++ encoder, Diffusion Policy with Augmentations (DP+Aug), and EquivAct (re-implemented without pre-training). DP+Aug used augmentations including rotation around z-axis, uniform scaling (0.5x–1.5x), and random Gaussian offsets.
 - **Evaluation Setups:** Original (same as demos), OOD(R) (random z-axis rotation), OOD(S) (scene scaling 1x–2x with aspect ratio change), OOD(P) (dramatic position randomization), and OOD(R+S+P) (combined OOD factors).
 - **Results:**
 - DP performed well in the "Original" setup but experienced significant performance drops in all OOD scenarios.
 - DP+Aug showed strong performance in OOD(R) due to aligned augmentations, but its performance substantially decreased in OOD(P) and OOD(R+S+P) scenarios.
 - EquivAct performed well in Cloth Folding but poorly in Object Covering and Box Closing, and failed in Push T, attributed to its deterministic architecture's inability to handle multi-modal data. It also demonstrated unstable training performance.
 - EquiBot consistently maintained robust performance across all four tasks and exhibited the least performance degradation in all OOD setups (R, S, P, R+S+P). This indicates EquiBot's superior out-of-distribution generalization.

- **Data Efficiency:**
 - **Tasks:** Robomimic Can and Square environments.
 - **Setup:** Methods were trained with varying amounts of demonstration data: 100, 50, and 25 demonstrations, and evaluated in-distribution.
 - **Results:** As the number of demonstrations decreased from 100 to 25, the performance of the DP baseline dropped considerably. In contrast, EquiBot maintained a comparatively higher level of performance in low-data regimes. This demonstrates that EquiBot's inherent equivariance allows it to cope with novel initial object poses at test time more effectively, even with limited training data coverage.

- **Ablation Studies (Supplementary Materials):**
 - **Architecture Design:** EquiBot's architecture significantly outperformed a naive extension of EquivAct with a diffusion head, especially in OOD scenarios and when varying action prediction horizons.
 - **Equivariance Components:** Ablations confirmed that removing any specific form of equivariance (translation, rotation, or scaling) from EquiBot resulted in performance degradation when tested in out-of-distribution scenarios corresponding to the removed equivariance. This indicates that each component contributes to the method's overall generalization.
 - **Training Stability:** EquiBot demonstrated more stable training performance across checkpoints compared to EquivAct on the Push T task, which is advantageous for practical deployment as it simplifies checkpoint selection.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Experiments

Real-world experiments evaluated EquiBot's practical applicability and generalization in mobile manipulation tasks.

- **Tasks:** A suite of 6 mobile manipulation tasks in home settings were performed: Push Chair, Luggage Packing, Luggage Closing, Laundry Door Closing, Bimanual Folding, and Bimanual Make Bed. These tasks involved diverse objects (rigid, articulated, deformable).
- **Data Collection:** Policies were learned from just 5 minutes of single-view human demonstration videos (15 videos per task). Human demonstrations were parsed using off-the-shelf hand detection and object segmentation models, with an alignment module for 3D coordinate consistency.
- **Evaluation:** Methods were evaluated on 10 episodes per task, often involving unseen objects, novel object poses, or translated/rotated scene layouts.
- **Baselines:** DP and DP+Aug.
- **Results:**
 - EquiBot demonstrated significantly higher success rates across nearly all real robot tasks and their variations compared to both DP and DP+Aug. For instance, in "Push Chair," EquiBot achieved 8/10 successes, while DP and DP+Aug achieved 0/10 and 1/10 respectively. In "Laundry Door Closing," EquiBot achieved 10/10 successes compared to DP's 3/10.
 - The method successfully generalized to diverse unseen objects and scenes after learning from minimal human demonstrations.
 - Detailed analysis for the "Laundry Door Closing" task further highlighted EquiBot's superiority: it had a higher success rate, more complete closures (indicated by "Close with Click Sound"), a significantly smaller "Total Accumulated Missing Angle," and critically, fewer "Collision or Safety Issue" incidents compared to DP. This suggests not only better task completion but also safer and more reliable robot operation.

In summary, the experimental results consistently demonstrate that EquiBot surpasses conventional diffusion policies and prior equivariant methods in both simulation and real-world scenarios, particularly concerning generalization to out-of-distribution inputs and data efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EquiBot represents an advancement in robot learning by addressing fundamental challenges related to data efficiency and generalization in visuomotor policy learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Generalization to Unseen Environments:** The integration of SIM(3)-equivariance directly into the neural network architecture allows EquiBot to inherently generalize to variations in object scale, rotation, and translation. This capability is crucial for robots operating in unstructured real-world environments where object appearances and placements are highly variable. It reduces the necessity for policies to encounter every possible variation during training, which is often infeasible.

2. **Significant Data Efficiency:** By leveraging equivariance, EquiBot can learn robust policies from substantially smaller datasets than traditional methods. The ability to learn from as little as 5 minutes of human demonstrations in complex real-world mobile manipulation tasks represents a notable reduction in data requirements. This lowers the barrier to entry for teaching robots new tasks, making robot deployment more practical and scalable.

3. **Robustness and Multi-modal Behavior Handling:** The use of diffusion models as the policy representation endows EquiBot with robustness to noise and the capacity to model multi-modal action distributions. This is vital for tasks where multiple valid actions might exist or where actions need to be temporally consistent, preventing issues like unstable training or suboptimal policy execution often seen in deterministic architectures.

4. **Broader Applicability to Complex Manipulation Tasks:** Unlike some prior equivariant methods that were limited to simpler tasks (e.g., pick-and-place), EquiBot demonstrates effectiveness across a diverse suite of complex 3D manipulation tasks involving rigid, deformable, and articulated objects in both simulation and the real world. This expands the range of problems that can be tackled with equivariant learning.

5. **Reduced Reliance on Data Augmentation:** The inherent generalization provided by equivariance minimizes the need for extensive data augmentation, which can be computationally expensive and does not always guarantee generalization to entirely novel distributions. EquiBot offers a more principled approach to handling variations.

6. **Safer and More Reliable Robot Operation:** The detailed analysis in real-world experiments, particularly regarding safety issues and successful task completion (e.g., "Close with Click Sound" in Laundry Door Closing), indicates that EquiBot not only performs tasks more effectively but also with greater reliability and fewer undesirable incidents compared to baselines. This aspect is critical for the safe integration of robots into human environments.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** While the paper notes limitations, such as the current method not inherently handling non-linear object shape changes, variations in relative object positioning, or severe occlusions, the established framework provides a strong foundation. Future work can build upon this by explicitly modeling scene dynamics, individual objects, or integrating more robust 3D representations to address these challenges. Extending to multi-task setups is also a clear direction.

In conclusion, EquiBot contributes a robust, data-efficient, and generalizable solution to long-standing problems in robot learning, bringing practical robot manipulation closer to real-world feasibility in diverse and unpredictable environments.
