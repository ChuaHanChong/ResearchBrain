# Diffusion-VLA: Generalizable and Interpretable Robot Foundation Model via Self-Generated Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffusionVLA (DiVLA), developed by researchers including those from Midea Group, unifies autoregressive reasoning with diffusion-based policies, creating a robot foundation model capable of both understanding instructions and executing precise actions. This approach led to a 63.7% success rate on zero-shot bin picking and significantly faster inference speeds of up to 82Hz, while also providing explicit interpretability through self-generated reasoning.

## Problem

- Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often suffer from imprecise action generation due to discretization and slow inference speeds.
- Diffusion-based policies excel at precise action generation but lack explicit reasoning capabilities, limiting their ability to handle complex or novel tasks.
- Existing robot foundation models struggled to effectively combine the language understanding and reasoning of autoregressive models with the robust and efficient action generation of diffusion models.

## Method

- DiffusionVLA (DiVLA) integrates a pre-trained ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== backbone (Qwen2-VL) for autoregressive reasoning with a diffusion model for robust continuous action generation.
- A novel 'Reasoning Injection Module' directly embeds self-generated reasoning from the VLM into the diffusion-based policy using Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), ensuring reasoning actively influences action.
- The framework is trained using a combined diffusion and next-token prediction loss, leveraging datasets like Droid and OXE, with Droid data enhanced with GPT-4o-generated reasoning for pre-training.

## Results

- Achieved **63.7%** success on a challenging zero-shot bin-picking task involving 102 unseen objects, significantly outperforming baselines like Diffusion Policy (**8.9%**) and OpenVLA (**28.4%**).
- Demonstrated fast inference speeds, with DiVLA-2B running at 82Hz and DiVLA-7B at 42Hz on a single A6000 GPU, considerably faster than OpenVLA-7B (5Hz).
- Provided explicit action interpretability through self-generated natural language rationales, enabling dynamic failure diagnosis and a better understanding of the robot's decision-making process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying autoregressive reasoning with diffusion-based policies addresses the limitations of both paradigms, creating a more comprehensive robot foundation model that can both reason and precisely act.
> - Directly injecting self-generated reasoning into the diffusion policy via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) enables explicit action interpretability and guides robust action generation without iterative computational overhead.
> - The model demonstrates strong scalability, with larger models and increased data volume leading to improved generalization and performance across diverse robotic tasks.
