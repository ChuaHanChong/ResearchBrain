# Diffusion-VLA: Generalizable and Interpretable Robot Foundation Model via Self-Generated Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffusionVLA (DiVLA), developed by researchers including those from Midea Group, unifies autoregressive reasoning with diffusion-based policies, creating a robot foundation model capable of both understanding instructions and executing precise actions. This approach led to a 63.7% success rate on zero-shot bin picking and significantly faster inference speeds of up to 82Hz, while also providing explicit interpretability through self-generated reasoning.

## Problem

- Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often suffer from imprecise action generation due to discretization and slow inference speeds.
- Diffusion-based policies excel at precise action generation but lack explicit reasoning capabilities, limiting their ability to handle complex or novel tasks.
- Existing robot foundation models struggled to effectively combine the language understanding and reasoning of autoregressive models with the robust and efficient action generation of diffusion models.

## Method

- DiffusionVLA (DiVLA) integrates a pre-trained ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== backbone (Qwen2-VL) for autoregressive reasoning with a diffusion model for robust continuous action generation.
- A novel 'Reasoning Injection Module' directly embeds self-generated reasoning from the VLM into the diffusion-based policy using Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), ensuring reasoning actively influences action.
- The framework is trained using a combined diffusion and next-token prediction loss, leveraging datasets like Droid and OXE, with Droid data enhanced with GPT-4o-generated reasoning for pre-training.

## Results

- Achieved **63.7%** success on a challenging zero-shot bin-picking task involving 102 unseen objects, significantly outperforming baselines like Diffusion Policy (**8.9%**) and OpenVLA (**28.4%**).
- Demonstrated fast inference speeds, with DiVLA-2B running at 82Hz and DiVLA-7B at 42Hz on a single A6000 GPU, considerably faster than OpenVLA-7B (5Hz).
- Provided explicit action interpretability through self-generated natural language rationales, enabling dynamic failure diagnosis and a better understanding of the robot's decision-making process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying autoregressive reasoning with diffusion-based policies addresses the limitations of both paradigms, creating a more comprehensive robot foundation model that can both reason and precisely act.
> - Directly injecting self-generated reasoning into the diffusion policy via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) enables explicit action interpretability and guides robust action generation without iterative computational overhead.
> - The model demonstrates strong scalability, with larger models and increased data volume leading to improved generalization and performance across diverse robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models is a rapidly evolving frontier in robotics, aiming to enable robots to understand human instructions in natural language and translate them into complex physical actions. The current landscape is largely dominated by two distinct paradigms:

- **Autoregressive (Next-Token Prediction - NTP) Models:** Pioneered by models like RT-2 (Brohan et al., 2023) and OpenVLA (Kim et al.), these models frame action prediction as a sequential token generation task, akin to Large Language Models (LLMs). Their strength lies in leveraging vast internet-scale vision-language data, enabling strong language understanding and reasoning capabilities. However, they often suffer from limitations such as imprecise action generation due to action discretization and slow inference speeds, making them less suitable for real-time robotic control.
- **Diffusion-based Policies:** Building on the success of diffusion models in generative AI (e.g., image generation), these approaches (e.g., Diffusion Policy by Chi et al., 2023) model action sequence generation as a noise-denoising process. They excel at capturing the multimodal nature of robotic actions, offering more precise and robust continuous action generation, and generally achieving faster inference compared to NTP-based VLAs. However, a significant drawback is their inherent lack of explicit reasoning capabilities, which are crucial for handling complex, multi-step tasks or adapting to novel, unseen scenarios.

The present work, DiffusionVLA (DiVLA), directly addresses this dichotomy. It positions itself as a unified framework that seeks to "bring together the advantages of both parties," combining the powerful reasoning capabilities of autoregressive VLMs with the robust and efficient action generation of diffusion models. This unification is a critical step towards creating truly general-purpose robot foundation models that can not only "act" with high precision but also "think" and "talk" with interpretability. The paper also acknowledges related work in unifying autoregressive models with image generation (e.g., Show-O, Transfusion), but highlights that its focus is on unifying these paradigms specifically for robot policy learning to enhance reasoning and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind DiffusionVLA are:

- **Bridging the Gap:** To overcome the inherent limitations of existing VLA models: the lack of precise and robust action generation in autoregressive models and the absence of reasoning capabilities in diffusion-based policies.
- **Unified Framework:** To develop a single, integrated model that can simultaneously perform language-driven reasoning and robust robotic action generation.
- **Generalizability:** To enable the robot to generalize effectively to novel visual inputs (unseen objects, distractors, new backgrounds), adapt to new robot morphologies (embodiments), and follow novel instructions, including complex sequential commands.
- **Interpretability:** To provide explicit insights into the model's decision-making process, allowing for easier failure diagnosis and a better understanding of why the robot acts in a certain way. This is crucial for building trust and for debugging robotic systems in real-world scenarios.
- **Efficiency:** To achieve fast inference speeds critical for real-time robotic applications, ensuring responsive and seamless deployment across diverse robotic platforms.
- **Data Efficiency and Scalability:** To demonstrate that the proposed framework can achieve strong performance with relatively less pre-training data compared to some large-scale models, and that its performance improves with increased model size, aligning with established scaling laws.

In essence, DiVLA aims to create a "smart" robot that can reason, understand, adapt, and act proficiently and quickly across a wide range of complex tasks and environments, while also being transparent in its operation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffusionVLA's innovative methodology lies in its architectural integration of autoregressive and diffusion components, particularly through its novel reasoning injection module.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

The core architecture of DiVLA is designed as a hybrid system:

- **Visual Encoding:** Given interleaved sequences of images, text, and video, visual inputs from multiple camera views are first encoded into dense visual features using **SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023)**. These encodings are then transformed into a fixed number of visual embeddings via a Transformer, and concatenated if multiple camera views are present.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM) Backbone:** DiVLA leverages a pre-trained **Qwen2-VL (Wang et al., 2024b)** as its VLM backbone, available in sizes from 2B to 72B parameters. This VLM retains its autoregressive capabilities for text-based reasoning (e.g., generating explanations or decomposing tasks). The visual encoder and VLM are typically frozen during fine-tuning, with LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) applied to the VLM for efficiency.
- **Action Generation via Diffusion Model:** Following the VLM's final embedding layer, a fixed number of action tokens are generated. These tokens are then fed into a projection module (two MLP layers with LayerNorm), which bridges the VLM's output to a diffusion model. The diffusion model itself adheres to the standard **Diffusion Policy design (Chi et al., 2023)**, with randomly initialized weights. An MLP layer attached to the action decoder predicts the robot's joint space. For new embodiments, a new MLP layer is initialized, preserving pre-trained knowledge and enabling fast adaptation.

#### 3.2. Reasoning Injection Module

This is the central innovation of DiVLA. Unlike traditional autoregressive VLAs that might require a recursive loop (reasoning output -> new input), DiVLA directly embeds the self-generated reasoning into the policy model.

- **Mechanism:** The final embedding from the tokenized output of the reasoning component (from the VLM) is injected directly into the diffusion-based policy model using **Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)** (Perez et al., 2018).
- **Functionality:** This "injection" technique modulates the policy network's layers based on the reasoning signal. It ensures that reasoning actively influences action generation without dominating the primary action-specific token processing. This avoids the computational and operational overhead of iterative input-output cycles, leading to faster and more seamless reasoning incorporation during policy learning. The reasoning module acts as an auxiliary enhancement, providing contextual depth to guide the diffusion process.

#### 3.3. Model Design Choices and Training Strategy

- **Training Objectives:** The overall training loss combines both diffusion loss ($L_{diff}$) and next-token prediction loss ($L_{ntp}$): $L = L_{diff} + \alpha L_{ntp}$. A hyper-parameter $\alpha$ (typically set to 10) balances the contribution of each component, as $L_{ntp}$ is observed to be significantly smaller in magnitude.
- **Pretraining Data:** DiVLA models are pretrained on the **Droid (Khazatsky et al., 2024)** dataset and, for larger models (DiVLA-72B), also on the **OXE (O’Neill et al., 2023)** dataset. Crucially, to enhance language generalization, the original Droid data (which contained only robotic actions) was automatically transformed using **GPT-4o** to include reasoning, ensuring a consistent network architecture across pre-training and fine-tuning stages.
- **Fine-tuning:** For evaluation tasks, the model is fine-tuned. LoRA is applied to the VLM models, and a fixed learning rate of 2e-5 is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DiVLA demonstrates strong performance across a range of real-world robotic tasks, addressing key limitations of prior approaches.

#### 4.1. Generalization Capabilities

- **Visual Generalization:** DiVLA consistently maintains the highest average success rate in multi-task settings even with significant visual changes (distractors, different backgrounds, colorful lighting), showcasing inherent robustness without specific data augmentation.
- **Instance Generalization (Zero-Shot Bin Picking):** On a challenging zero-shot bin-picking task involving 102 previously unseen objects with diverse properties (dimensions, colors, textures, deformability), DiVLA achieved an impressive 63.7% success rate, significantly outperforming Diffusion Policy (8.9%), Octo (19.6%), TinyVLA (23.5%), and OpenVLA (28.4%). This highlights its ability to generalize to novel instances.
- **Adaptation to New Embodiments:** DiVLA quickly adapts to a bimanual AgileX robot for a table bussing task, achieving 72.9% success on seen objects and 70.8% on mixed seen/unseen objects, far surpassing Diffusion Policy (45.8% and 31.2%) and OpenVLA (0% for both). This demonstrates its flexibility across robot morphologies.
- **Following Novel Instructions:** DiVLA successfully follows novel instructions, including complex sequential commands (e.g., "Pick up watermelon first, then pick up the blue paper trash, finally pick up lemonade"), whereas OpenVLA struggles with complex sequences, often performing random selections. This suggests its reasoning facilitates task decomposition for multi-step instructions.
- **View Shifting Generalization:** DiVLA-2B showed strong robustness to view shifts (60% success rate in factory sorting) where OpenVLA and Diffusion Policy failed entirely, indicating benefits from pretraining on large-scale datasets.

#### 4.2. Action Interpretability

The reasoning injection module enables unprecedented interpretability:

- **Self-Generated Reasoning:** The model can generate natural language rationales alongside its actions, providing insight into its "thought process." For example, if a hex key replaces a toy car in the gripper, the reasoning dynamically shifts from "grabbing the toy car" to "grabbing the hex key," guiding subsequent actions.
- **Failure Case Analysis:** This dynamic reasoning allows for explicit failure diagnosis. By observing the generated reasoning, researchers can understand why a robot might fail (e.g., misidentifying an object or spatial relationship), facilitating debugging and refinement.
- **Analogical Reasoning:** DiVLA demonstrates the ability to analogize to new objects (e.g., categorizing a screwdriver as a "hex key" due to visual similarity), suggesting generalization based on semantic features rather than direct recognition. It also shows strong sensitivity to object color.

#### 4.3. Adaptability and Efficiency

- **Multi-Task Learning:** DiVLA-2B achieved an average success rate of 83.6% across five diverse in-distribution tasks, significantly outperforming baselines like Diffusion Policy (27.9%), TinyVLA (45.5%), Octo (24.3%), and OpenVLA-7B (39.4%), despite using significantly less pre-training data than Octo and OpenVLA.
- **Factory Sorting:** DiVLA achieved a robust 66.2% average success rate across various factory sorting scenarios (seen, mixed, cluttered), maintaining substantial performance even in highly complex settings (60% in cluttered mixed scenarios) where other models like DP sharply declined.
- **Fast Inference Speed:** DiVLA boasts impressive inference speeds: DiVLA-2B runs at 82Hz and DiVLA-7B at 42Hz on a single A6000 GPU. This is dramatically faster than OpenVLA-7B, which runs at 5Hz, highlighting DiVLA's real-time applicability.

#### 4.4. Scalability and Conversational Abilities

- **Model Scaling:** Experiments with DiVLA-2B, DiVLA-7B, and DiVLA-72B demonstrated improved generalization and performance with increased model size and data volume. DiVLA-72B achieved 82.4% success on sorting and 75.9% on bin-picking, showcasing consistent scalability.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** Despite not being explicitly co-trained on VQA data, DiVLA retains conversational ability and performs proficiently in common VQA tasks, recognizing objects (tulips, oranges) and describing spatial relationships. While it may sometimes misidentify specific objects (toy dragon as toy tiger), it shows strong color sensitivity and general scene understanding derived from its pre-trained VLM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiffusionVLA represents a significant step forward in the development of robot foundation models, offering a compelling blend of capabilities that were previously disparate.

- **Towards Generalist Robots:** By effectively integrating reasoning with robust action generation, DiVLA moves closer to the vision of a generalist robot that can understand, adapt, and operate autonomously in diverse, unstructured real-world environments. Its ability to handle unseen objects, novel instructions, and varying visual conditions is crucial for deploying robots beyond highly controlled industrial settings.
- **Enhanced Trust and Debugging:** The explicit interpretability provided by the self-generated reasoning is a game-changer for robotic systems. It transforms the "black box" nature of deep learning models into a more transparent process, allowing human operators to understand the robot's intent, diagnose failures, and refine its behavior more effectively. This fosters greater trust in autonomous systems and accelerates development cycles.
- **Real-World Deployment:** The high inference speed and adaptability to different robot embodiments make DiVLA highly practical for real-time applications. This efficiency could enable a new generation of responsive and versatile robots for logistics, manufacturing, home assistance, and beyond.
- **Foundation Model Advancement:** The work provides a novel architectural blueprint for VLA models, encouraging a re-evaluation of how reasoning capabilities (typically associated with LLMs) can be seamlessly and efficiently integrated into embodied control policies. This unified approach could inspire future research into more holistic AI systems that can both comprehend and physically interact with the world.
- **Data Efficiency:** While leveraging large pre-trained models, DiVLA demonstrates strong performance even with comparatively smaller robot datasets for fine-tuning, hinting at potential for wider adoption where massive proprietary datasets are not available.

In conclusion, DiffusionVLA offers a powerful and well-rounded solution to some of the most pressing challenges in robotics. Its contribution to generalizability, interpretability, and efficiency paves the way for more capable, reliable, and deployable robotic systems in a wide array of applications.
