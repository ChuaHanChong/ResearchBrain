# A Survey of Optimization-based Task and Motion Planning: From Classical To Learning Approaches

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey framing optimization-based task and motion planning (TAMP) as a joint hybrid optimization between symbolic task planning and continuous trajectory optimization, reviewing domain representations, hierarchical and distributed solution structures, and their fusion with learning methods including LLMs and diffusion models; synthesizes 182 references from 19 countries into future research directions.

## Problem

- Prior surveys on TAMP focus on sampling- or constraint-based methods and lack a unified review centered specifically on optimization-based formulations and their algorithmic structures.
- Classical optimization-based TAMP solvers are computationally intractable at scale, trading off task-planning combinatorial complexity against motion-planning numerical complexity with no systematic account of that tradeoff.
- Emerging learning-based components such as feasibility classifiers, learned samplers, and LLM-driven planners are scattered across disconnected papers without a framework connecting them to the classical optimization pipeline they accelerate.

## Method

- Formulates ==TAMP== as a joint ==hybrid optimization== problem unifying symbolic task-planning state transitions with continuous ==trajectory-optimization== constraints across sequential discrete-mode transitions and manifold intersections.
- Organizes domain representation into ==PDDL==-style action languages, ==temporal logics (LTL/STL/MTL)==, and learned operators and state abstractions, contrasted against LLM-native versus LLM-aided planning methods for generating action and goal descriptions.
- Introduces a three-way taxonomy (spatial, temporal, system) for distributed trajectory optimization via ==consensus ADMM==, and a five-category taxonomy of learned components (feasibility classifiers, search guidance, skill policies, error recovery, learned ==MIP==) for integrated TAMP.

## Results

- Synthesizes **182** directly relevant TAMP references from **19** countries, with contributions concentrated **59.9%** in the top-producing country, revealing a geographically skewed research landscape.
- Comparative analysis finds optimization-based TAMP converges to optimal or near-optimal plans and natively handles nonlinear dynamics and constraints, but lacks completeness guarantees and is initialization-sensitive versus sampling-based alternatives.
- Catalogs **11** learning-augmented TAMP works across five learned-component categories, showing feasibility classifiers and learned action samplers are the two most frequently adopted mechanisms for accelerating classical TAMP search.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimization-based TAMP's core tension between task-level combinatorial search and motion-level numerical complexity persists across classical and learned variants; learning shifts where the tradeoff bites rather than removing it.
> - LLMs excel at commonsense and symbolic reasoning but remain weak at numerical optimization, so LLM-native and LLM-aided planning should be read as complements to optimization-based solvers, not replacements for them.
> - The spatial, temporal, and system decompositions used for distributed trajectory optimization are a reusable lens for any large-scale robot optimization problem with separable substructure, beyond TAMP itself.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey positions itself within the highly active and critical field of **Task and Motion Planning (TAMP)**, which is essential for endowing robots with the autonomy to perform complex tasks in dynamic, unstructured environments. As robots move from controlled factory settings to human-centric spaces, the demand for sophisticated planning systems that can handle "long-horizon, dynamic tasks" and "physical interaction" grows exponentially. TAMP is the interdisciplinary bridge addressing this by integrating high-level symbolic task planning (what to do) with low-level continuous motion planning (how to do it).

The broader TAMP landscape is typically divided into three mainstream classes: constraint-based, sampling-based, and optimization-based methods. This survey *specifically focuses* on **optimization-based TAMP**, setting it apart from previous general TAMP surveys. The authors argue that optimization-based TAMP is uniquely suited for problems requiring open-ended goals, precise handling of robot dynamics, and complex physical interactions (e.g., contact-rich locomotion and manipulation). This is because it naturally incorporates objective functions, allowing for the evaluation and comparison of plan quality, beyond mere feasibility.

A crucial aspect that differentiates this survey is its emphasis on the **synergy between classical methods and contemporary learning-based innovations**, including the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs). While classical TAMP methods provide theoretical guarantees and structured problem-solving, they often suffer from scalability issues (combinatorial explosion in task planning, numerical complexity in motion planning) and lack generalizability to novel scenarios. The survey highlights how learning approaches can enhance robustness, efficiency, and adaptability, acting as heuristics, replacing components, or providing domain knowledge.

By providing a comprehensive review that connects the historical foundations of optimization-based TAMP with cutting-edge advancements in robot learning and foundation models, this paper aims to serve as a critical resource. It addresses the ongoing challenge of enabling robots to perform truly intelligent, adaptable, and efficient actions in the real world, thus pushing the boundaries of autonomous robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this survey stems from the increasing need for robots to operate autonomously in complex, unstructured, and human-centric environments. Such environments demand planning systems capable of tackling "long-horizon, dynamic tasks" that involve intricate physical interactions. Traditional planning approaches often fall short in generating plans that are not only feasible but also optimal, efficient, and dynamic.

Specifically, the authors identify several gaps and challenges that motivate their focus on optimization-based TAMP:
1. **Limitations of existing TAMP paradigms:** While constraint- and sampling-based TAMP methods can find feasible solutions, they often lack the mechanisms to evaluate or optimize plan quality, especially for open-ended goals (e.g., "build a stable structure as tall as possible with minimal robot control effort"). Optimization-based TAMP inherently addresses this by defining goals via objective functions.
2. **Handling complex robot dynamics and interaction:** Optimization-based TAMP, particularly through Trajectory Optimization (TO), naturally incorporates complex robot kinematics and dynamics, crucial for contact-rich manipulation and dynamic locomotion, leading to more natural and efficient robot behaviors.
3. **Computational Intractability:** The hybrid nature of TAMP, combining discrete (task) and continuous (motion) planning, often leads to computationally intractable problems. A key motivation is to survey efficient algorithmic structures, especially hierarchical and distributed approaches, that manage this complexity.
4. **Bridging the gap between classical and learning methods:** Classical TAMP provides robust frameworks but often lacks scalability and generalizability. Recent advancements in machine learning, particularly deep learning and LLMs, offer significant potential to address these limitations. The survey aims to systematically explore this synergy.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this survey are articulated through four specific questions:
- **Q1: Why are optimization-based methods important for TAMP? What are the benefits?** This objective aims to delineate the unique advantages of optimization-based TAMP, such as its ability to handle objective functions, complex robot dynamics, and physical interactions.
- **Q2: How will solutions for each individual component of optimization-based methods inform the strategies to solve integrated TAMP?** This objective focuses on understanding how advancements in domain representation, classical task planning, and motion planning contribute to the development of holistic, integrated TAMP algorithms.
- **Q3: What common structures are observed in optimization-based TAMP problems, and which tools and strategies can exploit these structures to efficiently generate long-horizon, dynamic robot plans?** This objective delves into algorithmic efficiency, examining hierarchical, distributed, and decomposition strategies to tackle the combinatorial and numerical complexities.
- **Q4: How to leverage machine learning algorithms to enable robust, and generalizable TAMP frameworks?** This objective specifically addresses the integration of modern AI techniques, including LLMs, to enhance scalability, generalization, and robustness of TAMP.

In essence, the survey is motivated by the desire to push the frontier of robotic autonomy by providing a structured, comprehensive, and forward-looking review of optimization-based TAMP, highlighting its theoretical foundations, practical implementations, and transformative potential through integration with learning-based paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology primarily involves a structured and critical review, synthesis, and categorization of existing literature within the domain of optimization-based Task and Motion Planning (TAMP). The authors adopt a layered approach, dissecting TAMP into its fundamental components and then examining their integration and enhancement through modern learning techniques.

The specific approach includes:

1. **Defining the Scope:** The survey begins by clearly defining "optimization-based TAMP" and distinguishing it from other TAMP paradigms (constraint-based, sampling-based). This precise scope ensures a focused and deep dive into the chosen area. A motivating example of a tabletop manipulation task is introduced early and consistently used throughout the paper to illustrate concepts and algorithms, making the abstract ideas more concrete.
2. **Problem Formulation:** The paper provides a formal mathematical formulation of the optimization-based TAMP problem as a hybrid optimization, explicitly linking symbolic task planning decisions with continuous motion planning trajectories through interconnected objective functions and constraints. This formal groundwork is crucial for understanding the algorithmic challenges and solutions.
3. **Decomposition into Core Components:** The survey systematically breaks down TAMP into its constituent parts, reflecting the logical flow of problem-solving in this domain:
 - **Planning Domain Representation (Section III):** Reviews how task environments, robot capabilities, and goals are formally expressed. This includes classical methods like PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language) and various forms of Temporal Logic (LTL, MTL, STL), as well as emerging learning-based approaches for automatic operator and state abstraction learning, and the use of LLMs for generating domain knowledge from natural language.
 - **Task Planning (Section IV):** Discusses methods for generating sequences of symbolic actions. This covers classical AI planning (state-space search, heuristics) and automata-based temporal logic approaches. Importantly, it also details how learning models and LLMs are being used to guide or even generate task plans, addressing the combinatorial complexity.
 - **Optimization-based Motion Planning (Section V):** Focuses on generating continuous, dynamically feasible robot trajectories. This section reviews core Trajectory Optimization (TO) techniques (direct collocation, DDP) and advanced methods like distributed optimization (ADMM) to handle the numerical complexity and exploit spatial, temporal, and system structures. It also covers learning methods that aid TO by learning objectives, physical models, or end-to-end policies.
4. **Integrated TAMP Approaches (Section VI):** This is a critical section that synthesizes the individual components into complete TAMP solutions. The authors categorize these into two main paradigms:
 - **Logic-Guided TAMP:** Where symbolic planning drives the process, and motion planning refines the plan skeleton (e.g., Logic-Geometric Programming - LGP). It discusses search heuristics, multi-modal motion planning, and receding horizon TAMP.
 - **TO-Guided TAMP:** Formulated as a single, large optimization problem, often Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP), with discrete variables representing task decisions. This includes Branch-and-Bound methods, hierarchical/distributed optimization (e.g., ADMM for MINLP), and smooth approximation methods for temporal logic specifications.
5. **Emphasis on Learning Innovations:** Throughout the structured review, the survey consistently interweaves discussions on learning-based advancements with classical methods. This is not treated as a separate, isolated topic but rather as an integral part of modern TAMP solutions, categorized by their function: learning feasibility classifiers, search guidance, skill policies, error recovery, and methods for accelerating MIPs.
6. **Identification of Future Challenges and Opportunities (Section VII):** The survey concludes by outlining open research questions and promising directions, covering topics like foundation models, diffusion models, multi-modal sensing, policy learning, loco-manipulation, human-robot collaboration, real-world applications, and ethical implications.

By dissecting the problem, reviewing established techniques, highlighting their limitations, and showcasing how emerging AI and machine learning methods can address these limitations, the survey provides a structured and forward-looking roadmap for researchers and practitioners in TAMP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey provides a comprehensive synthesis of the state-of-the-art in optimization-based Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), highlighting both the strengths of classical approaches and the transformative potential of integrating learning-based methods. The main findings and results can be summarized as follows:

1. **Fundamental Importance of Optimization-based TAMP:** The survey firmly establishes that optimization-based TAMP is uniquely positioned to solve complex, contact-rich, and dynamic robot tasks, particularly those with open-ended or quality-sensitive goals. Its ability to incorporate objective functions and detailed robot dynamics (via Trajectory Optimization) provides a significant advantage over constraint- or sampling-based alternatives for generating high-quality, efficient, and natural robot motions.

2. **Classical Foundations are Robust but Limited:**
 - **Domain Representation:** PDDL and temporal logic formalisms (LTL, MTL, STL) offer powerful declarative means to represent tasks and their constraints. However, these are typically hand-specified by experts and face limitations in expressiveness and scalability for real-world complexity.
 - **Task Planning:** Classical AI planning algorithms (e.g., state-space search with heuristics) are effective but prone to combinatorial explosion. Temporal logic-based reactive synthesis methods provide formal guarantees but can be computationally intensive.
 - **Motion Planning (Trajectory Optimization - TO):** Direct collocation and Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP) are robust for generating continuous trajectories while adhering to complex robot dynamics and kinematics. Distributed optimization techniques like ADMM are crucial for scaling TO to high-dimensional systems (multi-robot, multi-contact, hierarchical dynamics) by exploiting spatial, temporal, and system structures.

3. **Integrated TAMP Approaches – A Hybrid Landscape:**
 - **Logic-Guided TAMP:** Methods like Logic-Geometric Programming (LGP) leverage symbolic planners to generate plan skeletons, which are then refined by motion planners. Key challenges involve managing the interaction between symbolic and continuous layers, particularly through heuristics (e.g., IK-based checks, coarse TO) and mechanisms for replanning/backtracking. Receding horizon TAMP is crucial for robustness in dynamic environments.
 - **TO-Guided TAMP:** These approaches formulate TAMP as a single optimization problem, often Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP), especially for temporal logic specifications. While commercial MIP solvers (Branch-and-Bound) exist, directly solving MINLPs (Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programs) is computationally prohibitive. Hierarchical and distributed (ADMM-based) decomposition methods, or smooth approximations of discrete logic, are key strategies to make these problems tractable.

4. **Learning as a Catalyst for Scalability and Generalizability:** The survey extensively demonstrates how learning-based methods are becoming indispensable for overcoming the inherent limitations of classical TAMP:
 - **Accelerating Planning:** Learning is used to predict action feasibility, guide search processes (e.g., learning action samplers, value functions, heuristics), and identify infeasible branches, significantly reducing computational overhead.
 - **Automating Domain Knowledge:** Learning symbolic operators, state abstractions, and skill effect models from data reduces the burden of manual engineering, enhancing adaptability to novel objects and scenarios.
 - **Empowering with Foundation Models:** Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are emerging as powerful tools for generating domain knowledge (PDDL, goal specifications from natural language), acting as high-level planners, and incorporating commonsense reasoning, thereby improving generality and intuitive human interaction.
 - **Skill Acquisition and Reusability:** Learning low-level skill policies (e.g., through hierarchical RL or imitation learning guided by TO) allows for the creation of reusable motion primitives, accelerating motion generation for long-horizon tasks and improving generalizability.
 - **Error Recovery:** Learning robust policies to prevent and recover from execution failures in unstructured environments is crucial, leveraging multimodal sensing and LLMs/VLMs for failure identification and replanning.
 - **Enhancing MIP Solvers:** Machine learning is applied to improve the efficiency of MIP solvers by learning branching heuristics, warm-starts, and combinatorial decision strategies, making TO-guided TAMP more practical.

5. **Identified Future Research Directions:** The paper concludes with a forward-looking perspective, outlining critical open challenges and opportunities, including the full integration and grounding of foundation models, leveraging diffusion models for generative planning, incorporating multi-modal sensing for contact-rich tasks, advancing policy learning for complex tasks, developing unified TAMP for loco-manipulation (e.g., humanoid robots), enabling more sophisticated human-robot collaboration, ensuring robust deployment in real-world applications, and addressing ethical considerations.

In essence, the survey's main finding is that the future of TAMP lies in a sophisticated synergy, where robust mathematical optimization provides guarantees and structure, while advanced machine learning and AI (including foundation models) provide the adaptability, scalability, and generalizability needed for truly autonomous robots in complex, real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the robotics and artificial intelligence communities, with profound potential impacts on both academic research and practical applications:

1. **Consolidation and Clarification of the Field:** By meticulously reviewing and categorizing optimization-based TAMP, the paper consolidates a vast and fragmented body of literature. It provides a clear conceptual framework, distinguishing this paradigm from others and offering a consistent terminology and problem formulation. This is invaluable for new researchers entering the field, providing a structured understanding of its complexities, and for experienced researchers seeking a comprehensive overview and interdisciplinary connections.

2. **Bridging Classical and Modern AI:** One of the most impactful contributions is the explicit focus on the "synergy between classical methods and contemporary learning-based innovations." This bridge is crucial for overcoming the limitations of both pure classical (scalability, generalizability) and pure learning (lack of guarantees, sample inefficiency) approaches. The survey systematically illustrates *how* learning can augment, accelerate, or replace components of classical TAMP, providing a roadmap for hybrid intelligent systems that combine the best of both worlds.

3. **Driving Algorithmic Innovation:** By clearly articulating the inherent combinatorial and numerical complexities of TAMP, and then detailing various decomposition strategies (hierarchical, distributed, smooth approximation) and learning-based accelerators (feasibility classifiers, search guidance), the survey directly inspires further algorithmic development. It encourages researchers to devise more efficient, robust, and scalable solutions for integrated planning.

4. **Enabling Advanced Robot Autonomy:** The core promise of TAMP is to enable robots to operate autonomously in complex, unstructured environments. This survey highlights methods particularly suited for "long-horizon, dynamic tasks" and "contact-rich locomotion and manipulation." These capabilities are fundamental to deploying robots in real-world scenarios such as industrial automation, logistics, home assistance, disaster response, and exploration. The ability to generate optimal and dynamic plans, coupled with learning-driven adaptability, moves robots closer to human-level dexterity and intelligence.

5. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The dedicated section on "Future Challenges and Opportunities" serves as a critical compass for the field. By identifying key open problems in areas like foundation models (LLMs, VLMs), diffusion models, multi-modal sensing, policy learning, unified loco-manipulation, and human-robot collaboration, the survey actively shapes the research agenda for the next generation of roboticists. It points towards interdisciplinary efforts required to tackle these frontier challenges, including considerations for real-world deployment and ethical implications.

6. **Interdisciplinary Catalyst:** The paper inherently promotes interdisciplinary research by drawing from AI planning, optimal control theory, robotics, and machine learning. By showing how concepts and techniques from each domain contribute to the solution of TAMP, it fosters cross-pollination of ideas and encourages collaborations that are essential for advancing complex robotic systems.

In summary, this survey is not just a review; it's a strategic analysis that frames optimization-based TAMP as a central pillar for advanced robot autonomy. It provides the intellectual scaffolding for understanding current capabilities, highlights the critical role of hybrid approaches, and charts a clear course for future innovations that will undoubtedly impact the development of more intelligent, versatile, and capable robots.
