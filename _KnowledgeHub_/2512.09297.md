# One-Shot Real-World Demonstration Synthesis for Scalable Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called BiDemoSyn synthesizes thousands of physically feasible bimanual robot demonstrations from a single real-world example, enabling scalable data collection for imitation learning. This method results in policies with enhanced generalization capabilities and zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer, achieving average out-of-distribution success rates of 66.7% compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world demonstration datasets for complex bimanual robot manipulation is labor-intensive and costly, creating a data scalability bottleneck.
- Simulation-based data generation for robot manipulation often suffers from a sim-to-real gap, leading to performance degradation in real-world deployment.
- Existing data acquisition methods struggle to balance data quantity and physical fidelity, limiting the generalizability and robustness of learned policies for bimanual tasks.

## Method

- **BiDemoSyn** synthesizes contact-rich, physically feasible bimanual robot demonstrations from a single real-world example using a ==three-stage process==.
- A single expert demonstration is decomposed into ==invariant task primitives== and ==variable, object-dependent execution blocks== for adaptive synthesis.
- ==Vision-based initial frame alignment==, ==collision-aware validation==, and ==instance-level motion adaptation== ensure real-world executability and robustness of synthesized trajectories.

## Results

- BiDemoSyn synthesizes demonstrations efficiently, requiring approximately **5 seconds** per sample, enabling the collection of thousands of diverse, artifact-free demonstrations for training.
- Policies trained on BiDemoSyn data achieved superior in-distribution success rates (e.g., EquiBot **86.7%**) and out-of-distribution generalization (e.g., EquiBot **66.7%**) across six bimanual tasks, outperforming baselines.
- The framework enables zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer, allowing policies to deploy directly on different robotic platforms without additional training or fine-tuning, demonstrating stable execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex manipulation tasks can be decomposed into invariant coordination blocks and variable, object-dependent adjustments, enabling selective diversification while preserving task fidelity.
> - Synthesizing demonstrations entirely within the physical domain, rather than simulation, intrinsically addresses the sim-to-real gap by maintaining real-world physical grounding.
> - Employing object-centric observations and 6-DoF end-effector action representations facilitates zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer of learned policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation, particularly for dexterous bimanual tasks, has seen significant advancements driven by data-driven imitation learning. Visuomotor policies, such as those implemented in ACT, DP, and π₀, have demonstrated capabilities in complex, contact-rich scenarios like battery assembly and garment folding. These successes, however, rely heavily on the availability of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality demonstration datasets, typically acquired through expert teleoperation. The collection of such data for complex, long-horizon bimanual tasks presents a scalability bottleneck.

Current data acquisition methods primarily fall into two categories, each with inherent limitations. Human teleoperation (e.g., ALOHA series) provides physically accurate and grounded trajectories, but it is labor-intensive, demands specialized expertise, and is impractical for generating large-scale data for diverse or long-horizon tasks. In contrast, simulation-based methods (e.g., MimicGen, RoboGen, RoboCasa) offer efficiency and scalability by programmatically generating data using 3D assets. However, these methods often suffer from a sim-to-real gap, where discrepancies in contact dynamics and visual rendering hinder real-world deployment. Another emerging approach involves learning from human videos, which are abundant and low-cost. Yet, the significant morphological differences between human and robotic embodiments typically necessitate heuristic translation rules or complex motion retargeting, limiting direct applicability.

This work, BiDemoSyn, aims to address the trade-off between data quality (rooted in physical reality) and data quantity (enabled by automation). It inherits motivation from advancements in single-arm one-shot imitation learning and extends this concept to the more complex domain of bimanual manipulation. Unlike simulation-based methods, BiDemoSyn operates entirely within the physical domain, synthesizing demonstrations directly in the real world. It distinguishes itself from concurrent methods like DemoGen and YOTO, which diversify demonstrations via 3D editing of point clouds but can introduce visual artifacts and perspective ambiguities. BiDemoSyn mitigates these issues by not editing observations, and instead generating physically consistent trajectories. It also differs from approaches like ODIL, which rely on iterative replanning, by providing a more scalable and efficient synthesis framework. By bridging the gap between data efficiency and real-world fidelity, BiDemoSyn positions itself as a method for scaling real-world bimanual demonstration collection while maintaining physical grounding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, BiDemoSyn, that can synthesize contact-rich, physically feasible bimanual robot demonstrations from a single real-world example. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Data Scalability Bottleneck:** Learning robust and generalizable bimanual manipulation policies critically depends on large-scale, high-quality demonstration datasets. Existing paradigms, such as manual teleoperation, are prohibitively labor-intensive and costly when attempting to scale to the diversity and quantity required for complex, long-horizon tasks. This necessitates a more efficient data generation method.
2. **Overcoming Sim-to-Real Gaps:** While simulation-based data synthesis offers scalability, it frequently encounters "sim-to-real" discrepancies in visual appearance and contact dynamics. These discrepancies can lead to policies that perform well in simulation but fail in real-world deployment. A key motivation for BiDemoSyn is to generate data that inherently possesses real-world physical fidelity, thereby avoiding these gaps.
3. **Minimizing Human Effort for Data Collection:** The framework seeks to significantly reduce the human effort involved in acquiring diverse demonstrations. By leveraging a *single* real-world expert demonstration, BiDemoSyn aims to synthesize thousands of new, varied, and feasible demonstrations within a few hours, eliminating the need for extensive, repeated teleoperation.
4. **Enabling Robust Generalization:** The synthesized demonstrations are intended to capture sufficient geometric and kinematic diversity to enable policies to generalize robustly to novel object poses, shapes, and different workspace layouts. This includes tasks requiring precise dual-arm coordination and interaction with diverse objects.
5. **Facilitating Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** A further motivation is to enable policies trained on BiDemoSyn data to exhibit zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer to new robotic platforms. This is pursued by employing object-centric observations and a simplified 6-DoF end-effector action representation, which decouples policy learning from embodiment-specific dynamics.

The core insight motivating BiDemoSyn's design is the recognition that complex manipulation tasks exhibit a duality: they contain both invariant coordination blocks (motion primitives that are consistent across instances, like stable grasp sequences or arm synchronization) and variable, object-dependent adjustments (trajectory segments that adapt to contextual changes, such as object shifts or shape variations). By systematically decomposing a task into these components and then adaptively recomposing them, the framework aims to achieve efficient and reliable demonstration synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BiDemoSyn operates through a three-stage process to synthesize contact-rich, physically feasible bimanual demonstrations from a single real-world example. The methodology integrates task decomposition, vision-guided adaptation, and trajectory optimization.

#### 3.1. A. Deconstruction of One-Shot Teaching

The initial step involves analyzing a single kinesthetic demonstration, denoted as τ = {oₜ, aₜ}ᵀₜ=₀, and decomposing it into a set of bimanual execution blocks {Bᵢ}ⁿᵢ=₁. Each block Bᵢ = (sᴸᵢ, sᴿᵢ) represents a discrete phase of dual-arm interaction, where sᴸᵢ and sᴿᵢ are the left and right arm state sequences (e.g., end-effector 6-DoF poses and gripper status). Blocks are categorized based on the coordination of arm motions:
- **Single-arm motion:** One arm undergoes a significant state transition while the other remains static, defined by state change thresholds δ (minimal motion saliency) and ζ (static tolerance).
- **Dual-arm coordination:** Both arms synchronously or asynchronously adjust their states, with both arms exceeding the minimal motion saliency threshold δ.

1. **From Blocks to Atomic Execution Primitives (AEPs):** Each coarse block is further refined into Atomic Execution Primitives (AEPs), which are minimal motion units where an arm performs a salient state transition (e.g., gripper closure, movement, or pose shift). An AEP for arm A (L or R) is defined by a state transition from sᴬₜ to sᴬₜ+Δt, where the state difference exceeds a distance threshold γ, the duration Δt is within a maximum Tmax, and the path is collision-free. This approach prioritizes task-oriented transitions over temporal granularity and assumes quasi-static motions.

2. **Semantic Categorization for Adaptive Synthesis:** The refined blocks are then semantically categorized into two types:
 - **Invariant blocks:** These encode fundamental task primitives (e.g., screwing, pressing) or general motions (e.g., lifting, transferring) that are structurally consistent across different instances of the task.
 - **Variable blocks:** These blocks are object-centric and adapt to instance-level geometric variations (e.g., object poses, shapes). Adaptation occurs via a visual adapter Φ, which modifies the block Bᵢ into B'ᵢ based on the task goal g and a novel observation o_novel. This decomposition allows for selective diversification while preserving core task fidelity.

#### 3.2. B. Vision-based Initial Frame Alignment

To adapt the identified variable blocks to novel object poses and geometries, a vision-driven adapter Φ(·) is employed. This stage ensures that the initial robotic grasp aligns with the task goal in new scenes and involves three steps:

1. **Object Perception:** The target object in the novel observation o_novel is detected and segmented to obtain a binary mask M. The framework prioritizes vision foundation models (e.g., Florence2 with SAM2) for robust detection, particularly for rare categories.
2. **State Estimation:** Instead of relying on category-level CAD models, the instance-level 6D object pose P_obj ∈ SE(3) is estimated using geometry-aware processing. Classic image moments are utilized to calculate the object mask centroid c and principal axes R (via PCA on 3D points derived from depth values). This method yields P_obj = (R, c), making it robust to arbitrary object states (e.g., fallen, inverted).
3. **Pose Alignment:** The rigid transformation T ∈ SE(3) that maps the object pose from the original demonstration (P_demo) to the estimated novel object pose (P_obj) is computed. This transformation is then applied to the initial grasp pose P_grasp,demo from the variable block, resulting in an adapted grasp pose P_grasp,novel. The robot arm then executes a collision-free motion to reach this adapted pose, enabling goal-conditioned grasping without relying on dense grasp proposals.

#### 3.3. C. Trajectory Modulation and Optimization

After decomposition and vision-based adaptation, the synthesized trajectory undergoes further refinement to ensure executability and robustness in the real world:

1. **Collision-Aware Validation:** Each adapted block is checked for kinematic feasibility. Inverse Kinematics (IK) is used to validate the reachability of target poses (P_grasp,novel) and confirm that robot joint limits are not violated. A motion planner verifies collision-free paths between the start and end states within each block. This simplified two-point constraint approach reduces computational overhead, assuming the original demonstration's trajectory was collision-free. Unreachable or collision-prone samples are discarded.

2. **Instance-Level Motion Adaptation:** To account for geometric variations across different object instances, motion primitives within variable blocks are adjusted based on the 3D bounding box dimensions of the novel object (∆l, ∆w, ∆h). Motion endpoints (e.g., grasp or release positions) are offset by a scaled factor λ. For objects with irregular shapes, depth-aware masks or minimal human input can approximate volumetric differences.

Finally, the validated and adapted blocks are reassembled to form synthetic trajectories τ', populating the diverse and physically feasible demonstration dataset D_syn. The visuomotor policy learning is conducted using adapted diffusion policy models (DP, DP3, EquiBot) that map object-centric observations to dual-arm end-effector 6-DoF actions. These models are trained to denoise action sequences, with a Mahalanobis norm used to prioritize arm interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with BiDemoSyn address five core questions, demonstrating its capabilities in efficiency, scalability, generalization, few-shot integration, and cross-embodiment transfer.

**A1: Efficiency and Usability:**
BiDemoSyn exhibits efficiency in demonstration synthesis, requiring approximately 5 seconds per demonstration. This primarily involves manual object repositioning, visual verification of initial frame alignment, and automated trajectory optimization. This speed is attained while ensuring artifact-free data synthesis, balancing speed and reliability. In comparison, DemoGen generates trajectories faster (under 1 second) but introduces visual artifacts due to perspective distortion. YOTO, which relies on manual alignment and auto-replay, takes 42 seconds per demonstration, while teleoperation demands about 91 seconds for high-quality but labor-intensive collection.

**A2: Scalable Visuomotor Imitation Learning:**
BiDemoSyn facilitated the efficient collection of thousands of demonstrations per task, providing diverse training data. Policies trained on this data (DP, DP3, EquiBot adapted for bimanual tasks) achieved superior in-distribution (ID) success rates across six bimanual manipulation tasks compared to baselines like DemoGen and YOTO. For instance, EquiBot trained on BiDemoSyn data achieved an average ID success rate of 86.7%, outperforming DemoGen (68.9%) and YOTO (71.1%). The policies also demonstrated stronger scaling trends with increased data size, indicating that BiDemoSyn's artifact-free synthesis and efficient data scaling effectively support imitation learning.

**A3: Generalization to Unseen Variations:**
The research demonstrated that policies trained on BiDemoSyn data exhibit improved generalization to novel object instances and spatial variations. In out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, where policies were tested on unseen objects, BiDemoSyn-trained policies consistently achieved higher success rates than baselines. For example, EquiBot on BiDemoSyn data achieved an average OOD success rate of 66.7%, compared to 40.6% for DemoGen and 47.8% for YOTO. Furthermore, analysis of spatial generalization revealed that denser positional coverage (e.g., 10x10 workspace for 'unscrew') and increased orientation diversity (e.g., 7 orientations for 'reorient') in the training data directly translated to higher success rates on unseen configurations. Qualitative results showed effective execution in contact-rich tasks like 'plugpen,' 'inserting,' 'unscrew,' 'pouring,' 'pressing,' and 'reorient,' handling real-world complexities.

**A4: Few-Shot Integration:**
BiDemoSyn can seamlessly incorporate a few additional real demonstrations (5–20 samples) as complementary seeds without altering its core synthesis pipeline. While these few-shot extensions yielded limited performance gains in ID settings (where one-shot synthesis already achieved high performance), they resulted in moderate yet consistent improvements under OOD conditions. These improvements are attributed to expanded coverage of object geometries and intermediate contact states introduced by the new demonstrations, enriching representational diversity.

**A5: Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer:**
Policies trained with BiDemoSyn demonstrated zero-shot cross-embodiment transferability. By utilizing object-centric observations and 6-DoF end-effector action representations, the policies are decoupled from robot-specific kinematics. This enabled direct deployment of policies trained on the primary dual-arm platform to an unseen humanoid-style bimanual robot for tasks like 'plugpen' and 'reorient' without additional training or fine-tuning. The transferred policies maintained stable and consistent execution performance, comparable to those on the original platform, even under external perturbations during the pre-grasp stage.

**Failure Analysis:**
An analysis of failure cases, particularly for EquiBot under OOD evaluations, revealed that orientation estimation errors (32%) and initial grasp failures (28%) were the dominant causes. Visual perception errors accounted for 15%, alignment failures for 18%, and error propagation for 7%. These findings indicate challenges with existing pose estimators for symmetric or textureless objects and limitations in fine-grained contact modeling during gripper-centric path planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BiDemoSyn represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a scalable solution to the long-standing challenge of acquiring high-quality, real-world demonstration data for complex bimanual tasks. Its core significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Scalable Imitation Learning for Bimanual Tasks:** The framework directly addresses the critical bottleneck of data scarcity in bimanual manipulation. By synthesizing thousands of physically feasible demonstrations from a single expert example, BiDemoSyn dramatically lowers the cost and labor associated with data collection, making large-scale real-world imitation learning more practical and accessible.
2. **Ensuring Physical Fidelity and Mitigating Sim-to-Real Gaps:** Unlike simulation-based methods, BiDemoSyn operates entirely within the physical domain. This ensures that the generated demonstrations inherently possess real-world physical grounding, including realistic contact dynamics and visual appearances, thereby overcoming the pervasive sim-to-real gap that often hinders robot deployment.
3. **Enhancing Policy Generalization and Robustness:** Policies trained on BiDemoSyn's diverse and high-quality data demonstrate superior generalization capabilities. They robustly adapt to novel object poses, shapes, and varied workspace layouts, exhibiting higher success rates in out-of-distribution scenarios compared to existing baselines. This improved generalization is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic environments.
4. **Facilitating Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The design choice of utilizing object-centric observations and embodiment-agnostic 6-DoF end-effector action representations allows for zero-shot transfer of trained policies across heterogeneous robot platforms. This capability significantly reduces the need for platform-specific retraining, potentially accelerating the deployment of learned skills on a wider range of robotic systems.
5. **Establishing a New Paradigm for Data Generation:** BiDemoSyn introduces a novel paradigm that effectively bridges the divide between data quantity and physical fidelity. By combining hierarchical task decomposition, vision-guided adaptation, and physics-aware optimization, it offers a principled approach to algorithmically amplify task semantics and generate diverse, real-world data without extensive human teleoperation or reliance on imperfect simulators.
6. **Paving the Way for More Complex Tasks:** While currently focused on static, geometrically varied scenes, BiDemoSyn lays a foundation for future work on more complex scenarios. Its modular nature suggests potential extensions to dynamic environments, manipulation of deformable or articulated objects, and tasks requiring multi-stage interdependent contacts, ultimately contributing to the development of more versatile and capable general-purpose robots.

In conclusion, BiDemoSyn offers a scalable, robust, and physically grounded pathway towards practical imitation learning for complex bimanual manipulation, holding the potential to significantly accelerate progress in real-world robot autonomy.
