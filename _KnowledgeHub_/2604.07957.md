# WorldMAP: Bootstrapping Vision-Language Navigation Trajectory Prediction with Generative World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldMAP introduces a teacher-student framework that leverages generative world models to synthesize structured, planning-derived supervision for vision-language navigation trajectory prediction. The system achieved state-of-the-art performance on the Target-Bench dataset, reducing Average Displacement Error (ADE) by 18.0% and Final Displacement Error (FDE) by 42.1% compared to the best competing VLM baseline, Gemini-3-Pro.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with generating stable, grounded navigation trajectories directly from single egocentric observations, often failing to ensure traversability or accurate stopping.
- Generative world models can produce plausible future views, but their raw outputs lack the structured, planning-derived signals necessary for effective navigation agent training.
- There is a need to convert transient information from imagined futures into persistent semantic and geometric structures that can serve as high-quality supervision for trajectory learning.

## Method

- **WorldMAP** employs a ==teacher-student distillation== framework where a world-model-driven teacher generates structured trajectory pseudo-labels for a lightweight student predictor.
- The teacher pipeline constructs ==3D semantic-spatial memory== from imagined future videos, performs ==task-aware grounding== for targets and obstacles, and executes ==geometric planning== using a ==Fast Marching Method (FMM)== on a ==Bird's-Eye View (BEV) cost map==.
- The student is a compact ==VLM trajectory decoder==, trained with a combination of benchmark ground truth and ==quality-filtered teacher-generated pseudo-labels==, enabling efficient inference from vision-language inputs.

## Results

- WorldMAP achieved an ADE of **42.06** and FDE of **38.87** on the Target-Bench dataset, outperforming all proprietary and open-source VLM baselines, including a **18.0%** ADE reduction and **42.1%** FDE reduction over Gemini-3-Pro.
- Qualitative comparisons show WorldMAP consistently generates trajectories that adhere to traversable geometry and achieve more accurate stopping positions, unlike baselines prone to drift or implausible paths.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining benchmark ground truth with quality-filtered teacher-generated pseudo-labels is critical for optimal student performance, significantly surpassing training with either data source alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative world models are more effectively utilized as 'supervision engines' to synthesize structured training data rather than for direct, real-time action planning during inference.
> - Transforming transient imagined futures into persistent, structured representations (e.g., semantic-spatial memory, grounded targets/obstacles, geometric cost maps) is crucial for reliable trajectory prediction.
> - An amortized 'fast-slow' system design, where a computationally intensive teacher generates supervision for an efficient student, allows smaller VLM backbones to achieve competitive performance against larger proprietary models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in WorldMAP addresses the challenge of reliable navigation from egocentric observations and natural-language instructions, particularly concerning continuous trajectory prediction in previously unseen environments. This problem is fundamental to embodied artificial intelligence (AI).

Historically, navigation systems have relied on classical pipelines involving explicit localization, mapping (e.g., SLAM-style geometric maps), and analytical path planners. These methods offer interpretability and reliable motion planning but often require persistent map construction or repeated scene exploration, which can be cumbersome in novel or dynamic environments. Recent research in language-grounded navigation has highlighted the continued value of explicit spatial representations, such as top-down semantic maps and structured 3D environment representations, even in learned agents.

More recently, two complementary paradigms have emerged:
- **Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** These models are explored as high-level planners, action selectors, or end-to-end embodied policies for navigation. While strong in language understanding, current VLMs often struggle with generating stable, grounded navigation trajectories directly from single egocentric observations, particularly in maintaining traversability, respecting scene geometry, and achieving precise stopping positions. Benchmarks like NaviTrace have indicated substantial errors in spatial grounding and goal localization for VLM-generated navigation traces.
- **Generative World Models:** These models predict future observations or latent scene evolution to support look-ahead planning, imagined rollouts, or mental simulation. While they can synthesize visually plausible future views, the direct application of these generated views as action-ready evidence for navigation has shown mixed results. Studies have indicated that imagination can be beneficial in some contexts, but excessive or ungated imagination may introduce misleading information and degrade performance.

WorldMAP positions itself to bridge a gap between these two approaches. It observes that VLMs are capable of semantic grounding but unstable as direct trajectory predictors, while world models can synthesize futures but do not inherently provide the structured supervision needed for grounded trajectory learning. The paper suggests that the primary value of world models in embodied navigation may not lie in supplying direct imagined evidence for real-time action, but rather in synthesizing structured, persistent supervision that can be used for training robust navigation policies. This approach contrasts with prior work that primarily uses generated futures as transient evidence for test-time planning or reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question driving this research is: "how can generated futures be turned into supervision for grounded trajectory prediction?" This question arises from the identified limitations of existing VLM-only and world-model-augmented navigation systems.

The specific motivations for developing WorldMAP are detailed as follows:
- **Instability of VLM-only Trajectory Prediction:** Current VLMs, despite their advancements, frequently generate unstable and ungrounded navigation trajectories from a single egocentric observation. This instability is evident in challenges related to ensuring traversability, adherence to scene geometry, and accurate stopping at semantically specified targets.
- **Lack of Structured Supervision from World Models:** While generative world models can produce visually plausible future views, these raw imaginations do not inherently provide the structured, planning-derived signals necessary to train a navigation agent effectively. They offer imagined evidence, but not directly actionable supervision for learning.
- **Need for Persistent Semantic-Spatial Structure:** The existing gap indicates a requirement for a mechanism that can convert the transient information from generated futures into a persistent semantic and geometric structure. This structured representation could then serve as high-quality supervision for training a learnable, grounded trajectory predictor.
- **Repositioning the Role of World Models:** The authors propose to redefine the utility of world models in embodied navigation. Instead of being used as direct action policies or for test-time augmentation, WorldMAP aims to leverage world models as "supervision engines" that synthesize structured training data.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the WorldMAP project are:
- **Develop a Novel Teacher-Student Distillation Framework:** To introduce a framework that effectively distills planning-derived trajectory supervision, derived from world-model-generated futures, into a lightweight student predictor. This student would then operate directly on vision-language inputs during inference.
- **Design a World-Model-Driven Engine for Grounded Trajectory Generation:** To construct a pipeline capable of converting generated future videos into several critical components: semantic-spatial memory, task-aware target and obstacle grounding, and explicit geometric planning signals. This engine aims to transform world-model outputs into learnable supervision.
- **Achieve Robust Performance on Navigation Benchmarks:** To demonstrate that this teacher-student approach can lead to improved navigation trajectory prediction performance, specifically on benchmarks that evaluate navigation toward semantic targets in unstructured, real-world environments.
- **Enable Smaller VLMs to Compete with Proprietary Models:** To show that by leveraging the structured supervision generated by the teacher, a smaller, open-source VLM backbone can achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding much larger and proprietary models.

The framework's design is inspired by Yann LeCun's "World-Memory-Action-Perception" decomposition for autonomous machine intelligence, suggesting an organization where complex reasoning (teacher) informs efficient execution (student).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldMAP employs a teacher–student distillation framework, structured around a World-Memory-Action-Perception decomposition. This framework consists of two main components: a world-model-driven teacher and a lightweight student trajectory predictor.

**A. Teacher Pipeline (World-Model-Driven Trajectory Teacher)**
The teacher's role is to convert generated future observations into structured trajectory supervision. This process involves three stages: World Construction, Task-Aware Grounding, and Geometric Planning and Supervision.

1. **World Construction:**
 - **Future Video Generation:** The teacher begins by generating short future videos using multiple streams from a world model. These generated videos provide complementary nearby viewpoints and scene hypotheses.
 - **3D Reconstruction and Memory Construction:** For each generated frame, monocular depth is estimated using Depth Anything 3, which is then backprojected into a shared 3D scene representation. Simultaneously, each frame is encoded into a semantic-spatial memory, comprising its visual embedding, caption, and camera pose. These two representations provide geometric and semantic context.
 - **Navigation Plane:** A scene-level ground plane is estimated and used as a consistent shared navigation plane for subsequent projection, Bird's-Eye View (BEV) construction, and trajectory generation.

2. **Task-Aware Grounding:**
 - **Target Grounding:** Given a language instruction, the teacher retrieves top-ranked memory frames most relevant to the instruction using CLIP-based visual-text similarity. A VLM then summarizes the task into a target description, which is fed to the open-vocabulary segmentation model UniPixel to predict target masks. These masks are fused in 3D and projected into the BEV coordinate system, resulting in a grounded target region.
 - **Obstacle Grounding:** The same semantic-spatial memory is used to identify navigation-relevant obstacles. A VLM, informed by the task and retrieved frame captions, proposes object categories that might block the path. These categories guide UniPixel to segment obstacle masks, which are also projected into the same BEV coordinate system. This step differentiates task-specific target grounding from path-specific obstacle grounding.

3. **Geometric Planning and Supervision:**
 - **Cost BEV Construction:** Grounded multi-view evidence (target and obstacle regions) is projected onto the shared navigation plane and aggregated into a top-down BEV grid. Depth pixels are backprojected into world coordinates, and their RGB values are accumulated on the grid, retaining only points within a ground-level height band to capture traversable surfaces and low obstacles. Colors from multiple frames observing the same cell are fused. The grounded target and obstacle regions are then overlaid onto this metric-preserving RGB BEV.
 - **Cost-Aware Planning:** The BEV representation is converted into a planning cost map. Cells corresponding to obstacle masks are blocked, and unobserved background regions are conservatively treated as non-traversable. A safety margin is applied around blocked cells, assigning higher traversal costs to discourage paths too close to obstacles.
 - **Fast Marching Method (FMM) Planning:** The Fast Marching Method (FMM) is run on the BEV cost map, starting from the current agent position to the grounded target region, to compute a minimum-cost path. This raw grid path is then smoothed and resampled into a sparse waypoint sequence.
 - **Teacher Trajectory Generation:** The resulting BEV waypoints are mapped back onto the navigation plane to form the teacher trajectory. This trajectory serves as the structured pseudo-label supervision for training the student.

**B. Student Trajectory Learning**
The student is a lightweight vision-language trajectory predictor, trained to predict navigation trajectories directly from vision-language inputs at inference time, without requiring the teacher pipeline.

1. **Architecture:** The student utilizes a compact trajectory decoder positioned atop multimodal conditioning features. Text tokens, vision tokens, and their pooled cross-modal summary are fused into a concise representation. This representation is then decoded into K future waypoint hypotheses, each accompanied by a confidence score, to address ambiguity in teacher supervision. The student uses a LoRA adapter on a Qwen3-VL-8B backbone in a two-stage setup.
2. **Training Supervision:** The student is trained using the planning-derived pseudo-labels generated by the teacher. This supervision is enriched with target grounding, obstacle awareness, and explicit geometric planning, going beyond direct image-to-trajectory regression. Benchmark ground truth (GT) trajectories are also incorporated into the training.
3. **Training Objective:** The training objective `L_student` selects the best-matching hypothesis out of K predicted sequences. For each hypothesis, a combined loss `L^(k)` is calculated, comprising a per-waypoint L2 regression term and a segment-direction consistency term (based on cosine similarity of trajectory segments). This objective encourages alignment with the target trajectory while maintaining local shape and flexibility.
4. **Inference:** During evaluation, the student, trained using this supervision, directly takes a first-person observation and a language instruction to predict a single navigation trajectory selected from its K hypotheses.

**C. Quality Assessment of Pseudo-Labels**
A VLM-based quality assessment protocol is employed to curate the teacher-generated pseudo-labels. Trajectories are evaluated against five criteria: task success, stop accuracy, collision safety, trajectory naturalness, and cross-view consistency. Each trajectory is categorized as "usable," "borderline," or "reject," influencing how they are utilized in student training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of WorldMAP was evaluated on Target-Bench, a real-world benchmark for mapless navigation toward semantic targets in unstructured indoor and outdoor environments. Performance was measured using Average Displacement Error (ADE), Final Displacement Error (FDE), and normalized Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), all computed on 2D trajectories projected onto the first-frame image (lower values indicate better performance).

**A. Comparative Performance (Main Results)**
WorldMAP was compared against three groups of methods: proprietary Vision-Language Models (VLMs), open-source VLMs used as direct predictors, and other world-model-augmented methods. The WorldMAP student utilized a Qwen3-VL-8B backbone.

- **Overall Performance:** WorldMAP achieved the best ADE and FDE among all compared methods.
 - **ADE:** WorldMAP reported an ADE of 42.06. Relative to the best competing baseline, Gemini-3-Pro (ADE 51.27), this represents an 18.0% reduction.
 - **FDE:** WorldMAP achieved an FDE of 38.87. Compared to Gemini-3-Pro (FDE 67.19), this is a 42.1% reduction.
 - **DTW:** WorldMAP’s normalized DTW of 31.95 was competitive with Gemini-3-Pro (31.63).
- **Direct VLM Prediction:** Both proprietary (e.g., GPT-5.4, o3, Gemini-3-Pro) and open-source (e.g., Qwen3-VL variants, InternVL3-14B) VLMs, when used as direct trajectory predictors, exhibited higher ADE and FDE values compared to WorldMAP, indicating unreliability in generating precise, grounded trajectories from single egocentric observations. For instance, Qwen3-VL-8B as a direct predictor had an ADE of 183.93 and FDE of 339.58, substantially higher than WorldMAP.
- **World-Model-Augmented Baselines:** MindJourney, another world-model-augmented method, underperformed the direct o3 baseline on all three metrics (e.g., MindJourney ADE 152.41 vs. o3 ADE 112.14). This suggests that merely providing additional imagined views at test time does not automatically translate to improved trajectory prediction, and can even degrade performance.

**B. Qualitative Observations**
Qualitative comparisons illustrated that WorldMAP consistently generated trajectories that followed traversable floor geometry, avoided implausible shortcuts, and achieved more accurate stopping positions relative to the intended target. In contrast, baseline methods often exhibited tendencies to drift, overshoot, stop with large offsets, or follow implausible headings, even when the coarse semantic target was identified.

**C. Ablation Studies**

1. **Effect of Student Backbone Scale:**
 - Comparing WorldMAP students built on Qwen3-VL-4B (ADE 45.07, FDE 42.84) and Qwen3-VL-8B (ADE 42.06, FDE 38.87) revealed that the larger 8B backbone performed better across all metrics. This indicates that increased student capacity remains beneficial even with consistent teacher supervision. However, the improvement was moderate compared to the gains observed from refining the supervision strategy.

2. **Effect of Training Supervision Composition:**
 - This ablation investigated the impact of different combinations of supervision on student training:
 - **Train GT only:** Training solely on benchmark Ground Truth resulted in poor performance (ADE 78.34, FDE 121.75).
 - **WM pseudo-labels only (usable / borderline):** Training exclusively on teacher-generated pseudo-labels (even including "borderline" quality) also performed poorly (ADE 95.98, FDE 141.10).
 - **Train GT + WM pseudo-labels (usable):** The most effective strategy involved combining benchmark GT with only the "usable" quality teacher-generated pseudo-labels, yielding the strongest results (ADE 42.06, FDE 38.87).
 - **Train GT + WM pseudo-labels (usable / borderline):** Including "borderline" pseudo-labels alongside GT and "usable" pseudo-labels resulted in slightly better DTW (31.78 vs. 31.95 for usable-only) but degraded ADE and FDE (42.85 and 40.97 respectively).
 - These results demonstrate that a synergistic combination of benchmark ground truth and quality-filtered teacher-generated pseudo-labels is critical for optimal student performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings from WorldMAP propose a re-evaluation of the role of generative world models in embodied navigation. The primary significance of this work lies in demonstrating that:

- **World Models as Supervision Engines:** WorldMAP suggests that world models may be more valuable as sources for synthesizing structured and aligned supervision for navigation learning rather than serving as direct providers of action-ready imagined evidence at test time. This shifts the paradigm from using imagination for direct planning to using it for curriculum generation.
- **Structured Supervision is Critical:** Reliable navigation trajectory prediction is shown to depend strongly on transforming transient generated futures into persistent, structured representations for downstream supervision. The teacher's pipeline, which involves memory retrieval, semantic grounding, and explicit geometric planning, effectively consolidates world-model evidence into a form conducive to navigation learning.
- **Amortizing Expensive Computation:** WorldMAP embodies a "fast-slow" system design. The teacher represents the "slow" system, performing computationally intensive tasks like world-model imagination, memory retrieval, semantic grounding, and FMM planning to generate high-quality pseudo-labels. The student acts as the "fast" system, amortizing this complex process into a lightweight predictor that generates trajectories efficiently at inference time. This approach offers a path towards efficient deployment of agents trained with sophisticated reasoning.
- **Leveraging Imperfect Generations:** The framework demonstrates the capacity to extract stable navigational structure from potentially inconsistent or imperfect world-model generations. Through robust mechanisms like multi-view grounding and geometric projection, the teacher can convert flawed imaginations into useful, learnable supervision. This is a crucial aspect for practical applications, as world models often produce visually plausible but geometrically inconsistent futures.
- **Empowering Smaller Models:** By providing rich, planning-derived supervision, WorldMAP enables smaller, open-source VLM backbones to achieve performance competitive with or superior to much larger proprietary models. This has implications for democratizing access to advanced navigation capabilities, reducing computational overhead, and fostering further research with open-source tools.

The potential impact of WorldMAP extends to advancing embodied AI research by offering a principled method for incorporating the predictive capabilities of world models into practical navigation systems. It highlights a promising direction for future embodied systems where the focus shifts from real-time consumption of imagined observations to using world models for constructing persistent semantic-spatial representations and aligned supervision that teach robust and grounded navigation behaviors.

However, the current results are primarily established on the Target-Bench dataset. Future work would need to assess transferability to highly dynamic scenes, multi-level environments, or long-horizon exploration to fully understand the generalizability and scalability of this approach.
