# SketchThinker-R1: Towards Efficient Sketch-Style Reasoning in Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SketchThinker-R1, developed by researchers from the University of Macau and Shanghai AI Laboratory, introduces a reinforcement learning framework that enables Large Multimodal Models to perform efficient, human-like sketch-style reasoning. This approach achieved an over 64% reduction in reasoning token cost while maintaining or improving answer accuracy across diverse multimodal benchmarks, enhancing interpretability and inference speed.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) suffer from high computational costs and slow response times due to extensive step-by-step reasoning (Chain-of-Thought).
- Verbose reasoning can lead to 'overthinking,' potentially introducing errors or redundancy that compromise accuracy and efficiency.
- Long and detailed reasoning traces often lack interpretability, making it difficult for humans to understand the model's decision-making process.

## Method

- A three-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework is proposed: ==Sketch-Mode== Cold Start, SketchJudge Reward Model, and Sketch-Thinking Reinforcement Learning.
- The Sketch-Mode Cold Start uses Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on a dataset of detailed reasoning transformed into concise, sketch-style versions by a proprietary LLM.
- A specialized SketchJudge Reward Model is trained to evaluate the 'sketch-style' of reasoning, providing crucial supervision for the subsequent RL stage using a composite reward function.

## Results

- SketchThinker-R1 achieved over **64%** reduction in reasoning token cost across four multimodal benchmarks (MMMU, MathVision, VisuLogic, PhyX) compared to Vanilla-R1.
- The model maintained or slightly improved final answer accuracy (e.g., +**1.8%** on MMMU) while significantly boosting Efficiency of Thinking (EoT = Accuracy / #Token).
- Qualitative and quantitative analyses confirmed enhanced interpretability and significantly faster inference times for SketchThinker-R1's concise reasoning outputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining initial SFT-based 'cold start' with subsequent RL-based refinement is critical for effectively instilling and generalizing sketch-style reasoning in LMMs.
> - Explicitly supervising the 'thinking style' through a dedicated reward model, rather than just output length, effectively guides the LMMs towards concise, human-like reasoning.
> - A binary (0/1) reward for thinking style and dynamic weighting of reward components (accuracy, format, style) proved most effective for stable and efficient RL training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "reasoning" capabilities in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has been a significant trend, heavily influenced by the success of Large Reasoning Language Models (LRLMs) like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1. These models achieve impressive results by performing extensive, step-by-step reasoning, often termed Chain-of-Thought (CoT). This work directly addresses a critical and emerging challenge within this landscape: the **efficiency crisis** associated with such elaborate reasoning.

The broader research landscape for LLMs and LMMs is currently grappling with the trade-offs between enhanced reasoning capabilities and their associated computational costs. While extensive CoT improves accuracy and problem-solving, it leads to:
1. **High Token Costs:** Generating lengthy reasoning chains consumes more tokens, directly increasing API costs for proprietary models and computational resources for open-source ones.
2. **Increased Response Times:** Longer outputs naturally take more time to generate, hindering real-time applications and user experience.
3. **Risk of "Overthinking":** As highlighted by the authors and other related works (Chen et al., 2024b; Cuadron et al., 2025), redundant steps can sometimes introduce misleading information or accumulate errors, potentially compromising correctness.
4. **Reduced Interpretability:** Long, verbose reasoning traces can be difficult for humans to follow and interpret, obscuring the core logic.

SketchThinker-R1 positions itself at the intersection of enhancing reasoning efficiency and maintaining accuracy. It is fundamentally inspired by human cognitive processes, where concise, goal-directed "sketch-style" reasoning often suffices for effective problem-solving. This contrasts with the prevalent "slow thinking" paradigm in LMMs.

The paper extensively references existing research on "Efficient Reasoning," categorizing it into three trajectories:
- **Prompt-based:** Methods that use specific instructions to control reasoning length (e.g., Constrained CoT, Chain-of-Draft). These often compromise accuracy.
- **Training-based:** Methods that directly train models for concise reasoning, typically via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on short reasoning data (e.g., C3oT, VeriThinker). SketchThinker-R1 falls into this broader category but introduces a novel reinforcement learning approach.
- **Output-based:** Methods that dynamically reduce reasoning length during inference or replace explicit CoT with latent representations (e.g., Coconut, CODI).

SketchThinker-R1 differentiates itself from these existing approaches by explicitly supervising the *thinking style* of the model during reinforcement learning using a custom "SketchJudge" reward model. This allows it to foster an inherent "sketch-style thinking ability" rather than merely truncating outputs or relying on prompt constraints. It builds upon the established "R1-style" training (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback, or similar paradigms for "Reasoning 1" models) for multimodal models, exemplified by works like VisualThinker-R1-Zero, MM-Eureka, and Vision-R1, but shifts the focus from purely improving reasoning capability to optimizing its efficiency without degradation in performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind SketchThinker-R1 stems from the observed inefficiencies and potential drawbacks of the extensive, step-by-step reasoning processes commonly employed by modern Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). While these "slow thinking" strategies have dramatically improved LMMs' capabilities in complex tasks, they come at a significant cost.

**Primary Motivations:**
1. **High Computational Overhead:** Lengthy reasoning chains translate directly into substantial computational costs, including higher token consumption (leading to increased monetary costs for API usage) and longer response times, which are detrimental to real-time applications and interactive user experiences.
2. **Risk of "Overthinking":** Counter-intuitively, excessive reasoning steps can sometimes introduce noise, propagate errors, or lead to misdirection, ultimately compromising the accuracy and efficacy of the reasoning process.
3. **Lack of Interpretability:** Verbose and detailed reasoning traces, while comprehensive, can often obscure the core logical steps, making it difficult for human users to understand the model's decision-making process.
4. **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** In contrast to machines, humans often employ a more efficient "sketch-style" reasoning approach, focusing on salient information and critical logical steps to quickly arrive at solutions. This cognitive efficiency serves as a key inspiration for this research.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, the authors set out to achieve the following objectives with SketchThinker-R1:
1. **Incentivize Sketch-Style Reasoning:** Develop a framework that trains LMMs to adopt a concise, goal-directed reasoning style, mirroring human-like sketch thinking.
2. **Improve Reasoning Efficiency:** Significantly reduce the computational cost associated with reasoning, specifically by decreasing the number of tokens generated and thereby lowering response times.
3. **Maintain or Enhance Accuracy:** Achieve the above efficiency gains without compromising the final answer accuracy of the LMMs; ideally, even improving it by avoiding overthinking.
4. **Enhance Interpretability:** Make the reasoning processes more transparent and easier for humans to understand by distilling complex logic into essential, key steps.

In essence, the overarching objective is to make LMMs not just capable reasoners, but *efficient*, *accurate*, and *interpretable* reasoners, bridging the gap between current AI reasoning paradigms and human cognitive efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SketchThinker-R1 proposes a three-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework to instill and generalize efficient sketch-style reasoning in large multimodal models. The approach is designed to leverage existing detailed reasoning data to construct concise versions, train a specialized reward model, and then use this reward model to guide an RL process.

#### 3.1. Sketch-Mode Cold Start

- **Goal:** To provide the base multimodal model with an initial understanding and ability to generate sketch-style reasoning. This stage is crucial as the authors found direct RL for sketch-style reasoning to be slow and inefficient.
- **Data Construction (SketchColdStart-20K):**
 - **Source Data:** Two prominent multimodal reasoning datasets, LLaVA-CoT-100K and Vision-R1-cold, are utilized. These datasets already contain images, questions, *long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning* (`T_Long`), and answers.
 - **Transformation Process:** A "strong proprietary LLM" (later revealed to be GPT-5 in ablation studies) is employed to convert `T_Long` into a concise, sketch-style reasoning (`T_Sketch`). The transformation adheres to strict guidelines:
 - Minimizing content while preserving the core logical flow.
 - Removing irrelevant details, verbose explanations, and specific examples.
 - Formatting the reasoning into a numbered list of key steps.
 - **Scale:** The constructed dataset comprises 20,000 samples (10K from each source).
- **Training:** The base multimodal model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) undergoes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on this SketchColdStart-20K dataset. The SFT objective minimizes the negative log-likelihood of generating the target sketch-style reasoning tokens given the input image and question.

#### 3.2. SketchJudge Reward Model

- **Goal:** To explicitly evaluate the reasoning style of an LMM's output, assigning higher scores to concise, sketch-style reasoning and penalizing verbose explanations. This model will provide the crucial supervisory signal for the subsequent RL stage.
- **Data Construction:** The `T_Long` (normal reasoning) and `T_Sketch` (sketch-style reasoning) data from the cold start stage are used. `T_Long` is assigned a score of 0, and `T_Sketch` is assigned a score of 1.
- **Training:** An open-source LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) is fine-tuned to become the SketchJudge. The model is trained to predict the binary score (0 or 1) based on a given reasoning process.
- **Evaluation Prompt:** During RL, SketchJudge is prompted with "Give a score of 1 for sketch-style thinking and a score of 0 for normal thinking. Normal thinking contains detailed analysis. Sketch-style thinking contains only the key logic flow. Only output the final score. Now, score this thinking process: [THINKING]".
- **Rationale:** Supervised fine-tuning for SketchJudge ensures higher accuracy in style evaluation compared to direct prompting of an off-the-shelf LLM. Focusing solely on *style* rather than strict *length* allows for adaptive reasoning, where challenging questions can still prompt slightly longer but always concise-style reasoning.

#### 3.3. Sketch-Thinking Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **Goal:** To generalize and further refine the sketch-style reasoning ability initialized during the cold start stage, making it robust across diverse tasks and inputs.
- **Algorithm:** The Group Reward Proximal Optimization (GRPO) algorithm (Shao et al., 2024) is adopted for this stage. GRPO involves multiple rollout samplings and optimizes the model's policy to favor responses with higher assigned rewards.
- **RL Training Set (SketchRL-1K):** To ensure generality, the RL training set consists of 1,000 samples (250 each) drawn from four diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks: MMStar, MathVista, LogicVista, and SeePhys.
- **Reward Design (`R_i`):** A composite reward function is used to guide the RL process:
 `R_i = 0.5 × R_accuracy(o_i) + 0.4 × R_format(o_i) + 0.1 × R_thinking-style(o_i)`
 - `R_accuracy(o_i)`: Evaluates the correctness of the final answer derived from the model's output `o_i`.
 - `R_format(o_i)`: Checks if the response adheres to the required output format (e.g., numbered list).
 - `R_thinking-style(o_i)`: This is the crucial component, providing the binary score (0 or 1) from the SketchJudge Reward Model, directly incentivizing sketch-style reasoning.
- **Implementation Details:** The Easy-R1 framework (Zheng et al., 2025) is used. The base model for RL is the cold-started multimodal model. Standard RL hyperparameters such as KL coefficient, learning rate (AdamW), and rollout settings are specified.

This methodical three-stage approach, combining carefully curated SFT with style-guided RL, forms the backbone of SketchThinker-R1's ability to develop efficient and effective sketch-style reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SketchThinker-R1 consistently demonstrates its effectiveness in achieving efficient reasoning without compromising accuracy, and often improving it.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results (Efficiency and Accuracy)

- **Significant Token Cost Reduction:** SketchThinker-R1-7B (using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as backbone) achieved an *over 64% reduction in reasoning token cost* across four diverse multimodal benchmarks (MMMU, MathVision, VisuLogic, PhyX) compared to the Vanilla-R1 baseline. For instance, on MMMU, the token count was reduced from 182.2 to 64.3.
- **Maintained or Improved Accuracy:** Despite the drastic reduction in reasoning length, SketchThinker-R1 maintained and often slightly *improved* final answer accuracy. For example, on MMMU, accuracy increased from 61.0% (Vanilla-R1) to 62.8% (SketchThinker-R1-7B). Similar trends were observed across other benchmarks.
- **Superior Efficiency of Thinking (EoT):** The novel metric, Efficiency of Thinking (EoT = Accuracy / #Token), clearly highlights SketchThinker-R1's superior performance, demonstrating significantly higher values across all benchmarks. On MMMU, EoT jumped from 0.335 to 0.977.
- **Outperformance Against Baselines:** SketchThinker-R1 consistently outperformed various prompt-based (Constrained CoT, Chain-of-Draft), SFT-based (C3oT, VeriThinker), and RL-based (L1, ThinkPrune) efficient thinking methods. These baselines either suffered accuracy degradation due to forced brevity (prompt-based) or exhibited suboptimal generalization (SFT-based) or less effective length control (other RL-based methods).
- **Scalability Across Model Sizes:** The framework's effectiveness was validated on a smaller 3B model (SketchThinker-R1-3B), showing consistent improvements in both accuracy and a reduction of over 50% in thinking cost compared to its Vanilla-R1 counterpart. This suggests the robustness and generalizability of the proposed method across different model scales.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- Qualitative examples (e.g., Figure 3, Figures 5-8 in Appendix C) visually confirm that SketchThinker-R1 generates reasoning processes that are highly concise, structured (often in numbered lists), and directly focus on the "key cues" and logical steps essential for problem-solving. This contrasts with the more verbose and sometimes redundant reasoning of Vanilla-R1, validating the "sketch-style" nature.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Further Analysis

- **Importance of Two Stages:** Ablations showed that combining both Sketch-Mode Cold Start and Sketch-Thinking RL is critical. Neither stage alone yields optimal results, highlighting the synergistic effect of initial SFT-based capability instillation and subsequent RL-based generalization.
- **LLM for Cold Start Data Generation:** GPT-5 proved most effective for generating high-quality sketch-style data, balancing conciseness with logical completeness.
- **Diverse Cold Start Data:** Using multiple data sources (LLaVA-CoT-100K and Vision-R1-cold) for the cold start dataset improved robustness and generalized sketch-thinking ability.
- **Fine-Tuned SketchJudge:** An SFT-trained SketchJudge (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) was crucial for providing reliable reward signals during RL, leading to better overall efficiency.
- **Binary vs. Dense Rewards:** A binary (0/1) sketch-thinking reward was found to be more effective than a dense (floating-point) reward, likely due to its clearer, stricter supervision signal.
- **Dynamic Reward Weighting:** A dynamic weighting scheme for the composite reward function (gradually decreasing sketch-style reward weight and increasing accuracy weight during RL) yielded the best results, suggesting an optimal progression from learning the style to refining its application for accuracy.
- **Scaling Experiments:** Both scaling the RL training set size (up to 5K samples) and the number of training steps demonstrated stable training dynamics and consistent performance improvements, indicating the method's scalability and stability.

#### 4.4. Interpretability and Time Costs

- **Enhanced Interpretability:** Both human studies and large-scale LVLM-based evaluations confirmed that SketchThinker-R1's concise reasoning traces are significantly more interpretable than Vanilla-R1's outputs, making the model's logic easier to follow.
- **Reduced Inference Time:** The reduced token count directly translated to significantly faster inference times for SketchThinker-R1 compared to Vanilla-R1 across all evaluated benchmarks.
- **Reduced Training Time:** SketchThinker-R1 also exhibited approximately a 20% reduction in training time, attributed to faster rollout processes during its reinforcement learning stage.

In summary, SketchThinker-R1 successfully addresses the efficiency concerns of LMM reasoning, delivering substantial reductions in token cost and inference time while maintaining or improving accuracy and boosting interpretability, thus setting a new benchmark for efficient multimodal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SketchThinker-R1 framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), particularly in addressing the critical challenges of computational efficiency, response latency, and interpretability in complex reasoning tasks. Its potential impact extends across various dimensions of AI research and application.

#### 5.1. Revolutionizing LMM Efficiency

The most direct impact is the remarkable improvement in reasoning efficiency. A *64% reduction in reasoning token cost* without compromising accuracy is a profound achievement. This directly translates to:
- **Reduced Operational Costs:** Lower API costs for developers and organizations using large proprietary models.
- **Faster Response Times:** Enabling LMMs for real-time applications, such as interactive agents, conversational AI, and time-sensitive decision-making systems where immediate feedback is crucial.
- **Accessible AI:** Making advanced multimodal reasoning more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources, democratizing access to powerful AI capabilities.

#### 5.2. Bridging AI Reasoning with Human Cognition

The inspiration drawn from human "sketch-style" thinking is a significant aspect. By successfully incentivizing LMMs to adopt a concise, goal-directed reasoning process, SketchThinker-R1 moves AI closer to mimicking efficient human cognitive strategies. This opens new avenues for research into integrating more nuanced aspects of human thought into AI systems, potentially leading to more intuitive and effective AI.

#### 5.3. Enhancing AI Interpretability and Trust

The qualitative and quantitative evidence showing improved interpretability of SketchThinker-R1's reasoning traces is highly impactful. Concise, logical steps are easier for humans to understand, verify, and debug. This enhanced transparency is crucial for:
- **Debugging and Error Analysis:** Researchers can more easily identify where a model's reasoning might have gone wrong.
- **Auditing and Compliance:** In sensitive applications, being able to follow the model's logic is vital for accountability and regulatory compliance.
- **User Trust and Adoption:** Users are more likely to trust and adopt AI systems whose decision-making processes are comprehensible.

#### 5.4. Establishing a New Paradigm for Efficient Reasoning

SketchThinker-R1 sets a new benchmark for efficient multimodal reasoning, surpassing existing prompt-based, SFT-based, and other RL-based methods. The novel combination of Sketch-Mode Cold Start, the specialized SketchJudge Reward Model, and style-guided Reinforcement Learning offers a robust and generalizable framework that can inform future research into optimizing complex AI behaviors. The findings regarding binary vs. dense rewards and dynamic reward weighting also contribute valuable insights for RL practitioners.

#### 5.5. Broader Applicability and Future Directions

- **Generalizability:** Demonstrated effectiveness across different model scales (3B and 7B) and a diverse set of multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, MathVision, VisuLogic, PhyX) underscores the framework's robustness and broad applicability.
- **Pre-training Implications:** As discussed in the paper's appendix, the success of sketch-style reasoning could influence future LMM pre-training strategies. Training on inherently shorter, higher-information-density reasoning data could significantly reduce pre-training costs and potentially improve the foundational reasoning capabilities of models from the outset.
- **Adaptive Reasoning:** The approach of explicitly supervising reasoning *style* rather than strict *length* promotes more adaptive thinking. This means models can still generate slightly longer reasoning for genuinely difficult problems while maintaining conciseness when appropriate, a highly desirable trait for real-world robustness.

In conclusion, SketchThinker-R1 addresses a critical bottleneck in the scaling and deployment of LMMs by making their powerful reasoning abilities dramatically more efficient, interpretable, and cost-effective. This work pushes the boundaries of AI reasoning, offering practical benefits and laying foundational groundwork for more human-aligned and sustainable AI development.
