# BabyVision: Visual Reasoning Beyond Language

> [!summary] Summary
> BabyVision introduces a benchmark evaluating Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on foundational visual reasoning tasks, revealing that even state-of-the-art models significantly underperform human children on basic visual perception, often due to a "verbalization bottleneck" that limits true visual understanding.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) demonstrate an "inverted profile of competence," excelling at complex, knowledge-intensive tasks but failing at basic, pre-linguistic visual perception.
- Current benchmarks primarily evaluate MLLMs on high-level, language-dependent visual reasoning, overlooking foundational visual skills acquired by young children.
- MLLMs frequently rely on linguistic priors or exhibit "language leakage," rather than true visual understanding, during visual reasoning.

## Method

- Developed ==BABYVISION==, a benchmark with **388** questions across **22** subtypes, specifically designed to test MLLMs' foundational visual understanding akin to human children (3-12 years old).
- Introduced ==BABYVISION-GEN==, a generative benchmark (280 questions) where models provide visual annotations as answers, addressing tasks more naturally solved through visual actions.
- Established human performance baselines using children and adults, and implemented an LLM-as-judge evaluation for model outputs, validated by human experts.

## Results

- The best MLLM, Gemini3-Pro-Preview, scored **49.7%** on BABYVISION, demonstrating a **44.4%** absolute performance gap compared to adult humans (**94.1%**) and lagging behind average 6-year-old children.
- MLLMs showed consistent deficits across all four early-vision categories (Fine-grained Discrimination, Visual Tracking, Spatial Perception, Visual Pattern Recognition), with "Count 3D Blocks" (max **20.5%**) being especially challenging.
- Generative models achieved an average accuracy of **18.3%** on BABYVISION-GEN, with visual tracking tasks remaining particularly difficult (**6.7%** for the best model), suggesting limitations in visual reasoning through direct generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "verbalization bottleneck" is a core reason for MLLMs' failures, as encoding visual information into language for reasoning leads to loss of fine-grained detail, spatial coherence, and continuous identity.
> - MLLMs exhibit systemic failures in tasks requiring precise fine-grained discrimination, visual tracking, 3D spatial imagination, and abstract visual pattern induction.
> - Achieving human-level visual intelligence requires moving beyond current vision-language encoders towards natively multimodal architectures that can reason directly in visual space.
