# Learning complete and explainable visual representations from itemized text supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> ItemizedCLIP provides a framework for learning complete and explainable visual representations from itemized text supervision, where each text item describes distinct visual findings. It achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across medical imaging datasets like Brain MRI, Head CT, and Chest CT, demonstrating superior item independence and interpretability.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are primarily designed for 'multi-positive text supervision' where captions provide redundant descriptions of similar visual content.
- Critical visual domains like medical imaging and remote sensing use 'itemized text supervision,' where multiple independent text items describe distinct visual findings within the same image.
- Current VLM methods fail to capture both item independence (differentiating distinct findings) and representation completeness (encoding all findings) from such itemized annotations.

## Method

- ItemizedCLIP introduces a VLM framework with a ==multi-headed cross-attention module== to generate text item-conditioned visual embeddings from visual patch representations.
- It employs a suite of SigLIP-based objectives including ==Itemized Local Alignment (ILA)== with ==Upweighting Worst Positive (UWP)== and Masked Attention for completeness and robustness.
- ==Inter-Item Separation (IIS) loss== is specifically designed to enforce item independence by pushing apart text-conditioned visual representations from different items within the same image.

## Results

- ItemizedCLIP achieved significant gains in zero-shot performance, including a **+6.8%** mean AUC increase on UM220K Brain MRI and a **+9.3%** mean AUC increase on HeadCT240K, often surpassing linear probing of recent foundation models.
- The model demonstrated strong item-level explainability, naturally generating attention maps that accurately localize text descriptions to corresponding visual regions.
- Inter-Item Separation (IIS) loss quantitatively reduced mean Attention Map Similarity between different positive items, improving item differentiability and leading to more focused visual grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formalizing 'itemized text supervision' as a distinct VLM paradigm is crucial for domains where annotations describe independent visual findings rather than redundant ones.
> - Achieving both 'item independence' (differentiating distinct visual features for each item) and 'representation completeness' (encoding all items) is vital for accurate and trustworthy AI in high-stakes fields like medical diagnostics.
> - Tailored loss functions, such as Inter-Item Separation and Upweighting Worst Positive, are effective in learning fine-grained, localized, and comprehensive visual representations without explicit region supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a specific challenge within the field of vision-language modeling, particularly concerning the type of textual supervision available in certain domains. The broader landscape is dominated by models like CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training), which align visual and textual representations using contrastive objectives to achieve general and transferable visual features. Subsequent developments have explored multi-positive text supervision, where an image is paired with multiple captions that describe largely similar or redundant visual content, often to improve robustness and semantic coverage.

However, the authors identify a gap in these existing paradigms when applied to domains characterized by "itemized text supervision." In such settings, an image is not accompanied by redundant or highly overlapping captions but rather by multiple, distinct textual items, each describing an independent finding or region within the same image. Examples include medical imaging (e.g., a radiology report listing separate pathologies in different anatomical locations) and remote sensing. Standard multi-positive approaches, which tend to pull all positive captions for an image close together in the embedding space, are ill-suited for this itemized structure, as they fail to preserve the distinctness and spatial localization of individual text items. Similarly, concatenating all text items into a single caption, as some previous medical imaging models do, discards the compositional structure and fine-grained information inherent in itemized supervision. This research positions itself by proposing a framework specifically designed to leverage this prevalent but underexplored itemized text supervision, aiming to learn visual representations that are both complete (covering all distinct items) and explainable (attributable to specific items and regions).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named ItemizedCLIP, capable of learning complete and explainable visual representations directly from itemized text supervision. This objective is motivated by the observation that many important visual domains, particularly non-object-centric ones such as medical imaging and remote sensing, inherently provide itemized textual annotations rather than the multi-caption supervision typically assumed by existing vision-language models.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Address the limitations of existing models:** Current CLIP-style and multi-positive text supervision approaches are not designed for itemized text. Multi-positive contrastive objectives, for instance, explicitly pull together embeddings of all positive captions, which is contradictory to the need for distinct representations when text items describe independent findings. Methods that concatenate itemized text into a single caption lose the compositional structure and granular information.
- **Satisfy two key properties for effective representations:** Under itemized supervision, visual representations must fulfill two criteria that are critical for accurate interpretation and downstream tasks:
 - **Item independence:** Features associated with different text items should remain distinguishable and localized to their corresponding visual regions. For example, a medical report mentioning a tumor and hydrocephalus in the same brain MRI should lead to distinct visual features for each finding, not a blended representation.
 - **Representation completeness:** The combined visual embedding for an image should encode information from all associated text items. In clinical contexts, omitting any finding, such as a tumor or hemorrhage, could lead to a misdiagnosis.
- **Enable fine-grained interpretability and grounding:** The ability to link specific text items to distinct visual regions is crucial for explainability, especially in high-stakes applications like medical diagnostics. This fine-grained alignment allows for verification of the model's understanding of intricate visual details based on natural language input, moving beyond global explanations.
- **Demonstrate broad applicability:** The research aims to show that the proposed framework is effective across diverse domains where itemized text supervision naturally occurs, including multiple medical imaging modalities (brain MRI, head CT, chest CT) and remote sensing, as well as a synthetic natural image dataset.

By achieving these objectives, ItemizedCLIP seeks to advance vision-language learning in settings where granular, independent textual descriptions are the norm, thereby fostering more trustworthy and semantically grounded visual models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ItemizedCLIP is a framework designed to learn complete and explainable visual representations from itemized text supervision. Its methodology comprises an architectural overview and a set of tailored training objectives.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

The framework includes a visual encoder ($E_V$) and a text encoder ($E_T$), similar to most CLIP-based models. The visual encoder is a vision transformer with a CLS token. An additional multi-headed cross-attention module, `CrossAttn`, is incorporated with linear query, key, and value projection layers.

During the forward pass:
- Each text item $item^{(i)}_j$ from the input $T^{(i)}$ (containing $n_i$ items) is encoded separately by $E_T$ to produce its representation $t^{(i)}_j$.
- The visual encoder $E_V$ processes the visual input $V^{(i)}$ to generate two types of representations: a global visual representation $vg^{(i)}$ (from the CLS token) and patch-level (local visual token) representations $vp^{(i)} = \{vp^{(i)}_1..., vp^{(i)}_m\}$.

#### 3.2. ItemizedCLIP Training Objective

ItemizedCLIP utilizes a family of objectives, each designed to address specific aspects of itemized text supervision, such as completeness, independence, and localization. The overall loss ($L_{all}$) is a weighted sum of four components: Itemized Local Alignment (ILA), Inter-Item Separation (IIS), Multi-Positive SigLIP (MPS), and Key Token Alignment (KTA).

**a) Itemized Local Alignment (ILA)**
ILA is adapted from the Text Conditioned SigLIP (TCS) objective used in FLAIR. It measures the cosine similarity ($CS$) between a text item $t$ and its text-conditioned visual representation obtained from `CrossAttn(t, vp, vp)`. The `CrossAttn` module uses the text item $t$ as a query to focus on relevant visual patches $vp$. The objective maximizes this similarity for positive pairs (text item and its corresponding visual) and minimizes it for negative pairs (text item from a different image and the current visual).

ILA introduces two modifications to TCS:
- **Upweighting Worst Positive (UWP):** To promote representation completeness, the loss for the positive pair with the lowest `TCSim` score for a given visual input is upweighted by a factor $w_{uwp}$. This encourages the model to learn from items it currently struggles to align.
- **Masked Attention:** During training, a randomly generated binary mask (from `Bernoulli(p_mask)`) is applied within the `CrossAttn` calculation. This partial occlusion prevents overfitting and enhances the robustness of visual grounding.

**b) Inter-Item Separation (IIS)**
This objective is designed to enforce item independence. It treats text-conditioned visual representations ($v_{tc_{i,j}} = CrossAttn(t^{(i)}_j, vp^{(i)}, vp^{(i)})$) corresponding to different text items ($t^{(i)}_k$ where $k \neq j$) from the *same* visual input as negative pairs. Conversely, $v_{tc_{i,j}}$ and $t^{(i)}_j$ are positive pairs. This loss pushes apart the visual representations conditioned on distinct items within the same image, compelling the cross-attention module to focus on different regions for different items. The paper notes that a similar objective was explored in FLAIR but found ineffective for multi-positive supervision, but it proves effective for itemized supervision.

**c) Multi-Positive SigLIP (MPS)**
MPS is a standard global alignment objective that computes SigLIP loss between the global visual representation ($vg^{(i)}$) and each text item representation ($t^{(i)}_j$). It aims to align the overall image content with all its associated text items. While this might appear to conflict with item independence by drawing text items closer, the authors empirically found that a lower-weighted MPS loss benefits downstream zero-shot classification by improving the model's awareness of global visual properties.

**d) Key Token Alignment (KTA)**
KTA focuses on strengthening localization by aligning text items with compact, high-attention visual tokens. For a given text item and visual tokens, the attention map from `CrossAttn` is used to identify the top K% visual tokens with the highest attention scores. The TCS loss is then applied using only these "key tokens" (`KT(t, vp)`) instead of all visual patches. This guides the model to associate text items with the most salient and relevant visual information.

**e) Overall Loss**
The final training objective is a weighted sum: $L_{all} = L_{ILA} + \lambda_{IIS}L_{IIS} + \lambda_{MPS}L_{MPS} + \lambda_{KTA}L_{KTA}$. The weights ($\lambda$) are hyperparameters.

#### 3.3. Zero-shot Inference

For zero-shot classification, given an image and a set of candidate text class descriptions, ItemizedCLIP computes the `TCSim` between the encoded class text representation and the image's patch-level visual representation. The class with the highest `TCSim` score is chosen as the prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ItemizedCLIP was evaluated across five diverse domains, demonstrating improvements over baseline models in zero-shot performance, interpretability, item differentiability, and representation completeness.

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Performance Across Domains

ItemizedCLIP consistently outperformed current leading baselines in zero-shot evaluations across all tested domains:
- **Whole-study Brain MRI:** On the UM220K prospective test set (52 diagnostic tasks), ItemizedCLIP achieved a 90.5% mean AUC, representing a 6.8% improvement over the previous leading model, HLIP (83.7%). On Pub-Brain-5 (12 metrics), ItemizedCLIP showed a 7.1% improvement in mean balanced accuracy over previous leading models.
- **Whole-study Head CT:** On HeadCT240K's prospective test set (83 diagnostic tasks), ItemizedCLIP achieved an 85.1% mean AUC, a 9.3% improvement over HLIP (75.8%). On public benchmarks RSNA (5 tasks) and CQ500 (10 tasks), ItemizedCLIP achieved 91.5% and 90.0% mean AUC respectively, outperforming HLIP and even surpassing the linear probing performance of specialized head CT foundation models like FM-HeadCT, Google-CT, and Merlin.
- **Single-sequence Chest CT:** On the CT-Rate test split (16 diagnostic tasks) and an external dataset Rad-ChestCT (14 tasks), ItemizedCLIP achieved 83.2% and 74.7% mean AUC respectively, outperforming all baselines including HLIP.
- **Remote Sensing:** On RSICD (30-way classification), ItemizedCLIP achieved 46.2% Top-1 Accuracy and 78.7% Top-5 Accuracy, surpassing all CLIP-style baselines trained solely on RSICD data.
- **Synthetic Itemized-cc0.3M (Natural Images):** When trained from scratch on this synthetically itemized dataset, ItemizedCLIP outperformed all baselines in zero-shot image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval on MS-COCO and Flickr datasets.

#### 4.2. Item-level Explainability

ItemizedCLIP models inherently provide explainable visual representations through attention scores from the `CrossAttn` module. Visualizations of these attention maps demonstrate strong alignment with human-interpretable findings. For example, in brain MRI, ItemizedCLIP correctly visualizes distinct regions for separate findings like "posterior fossa enhancing tumor," "enlarged ventricles," and "transependymal flow" within the same image (Figure 4a). It can also identify multiple distinct locations of the same pathology (e.g., encephalomalacia in two brain regions, Figure 4b), showcasing fine-grained anatomical awareness without explicit segmentation supervision.

#### 4.3. Region-based Text Item Retrieval

The model demonstrates the capability to retrieve relevant text items given a specific region of an image. By querying a subset of visual tokens corresponding to a region of interest, ItemizedCLIP can identify text items from a corpus that best describe the pathology or feature within that specific visual area (Figure 5a), indicating strong local semantic grounding.

#### 4.4. Improved Item Differentiability via IIS

The Inter-Item Separation (IIS) objective is critical for enhancing item differentiability. This was quantitatively assessed using Mean Attention Map Similarity (mAMS), which measures the average cosine similarity between attention maps generated by different positive text items for the same image. Models trained with IIS exhibited significantly lower mAMS scores compared to those without (e.g., 0.33 vs. 0.50 on brain MRI for FLAIR-based models; 0.32 vs. 0.39 for ILA-based models, Figure 5b), indicating that IIS effectively forces the model to attend to distinct visual regions for different text items. Qualitative examples (Figure 5c, Figure 18) further illustrate this, showing that IIS helps the model focus on the specific information mentioned in each text item, rather than broadly attending to related visual content.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed that each component of ItemizedCLIP (ILA, IIS, MPS, UWP, KTA, and TCS Masking) contributes positively to overall performance across various metrics and domains (Table 9). For example, adding IIS to TCS improved mean balanced accuracy on Pub-Brain-5 by 1.3%. UWP was particularly impactful for representation completeness, and KTA for improving zero-shot classification performance.

#### 4.6. Representation Completeness

Representation completeness was evaluated using the Mean Lowest Logit (MLL) metric, which calculates the average minimum `TCSim` between a visual representation and any of its corresponding text items. ItemizedCLIP consistently achieved the highest MLL scores across brain MRI and head CT datasets (Table 9), indicating that it is more successful at aligning with *all* associated text items. The Upweighting Worst Positive (UWP) component was shown to significantly contribute to improving MLL scores, confirming its role in enhancing completeness.

#### 4.7. Zero-shot Tumor Segmentation

ItemizedCLIP demonstrated the ability to perform zero-shot tumor segmentation on the BraTS2021 dataset by using attention heatmaps generated from tumor-describing text prompts. It achieved an mIoU of 17.5%, and the ablation studies revealed that strong segmentation performance was achieved only when both IIS and MPS objectives were utilized (Table 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research formally defines and addresses the paradigm of itemized text supervision, a common yet underexplored data structure in various real-world applications. By introducing ItemizedCLIP, the authors provide a framework that not only recognizes the distinct nature of such supervision but also leverages it to learn visual representations with critical properties:

- **Enhanced Trustworthiness and Interpretability:** The framework yields semantically grounded and visually interpretable attention maps that align specific text items with distinct visual regions. This fine-grained explainability is particularly significant in high-stakes domains like medical imaging, where understanding *why* a model makes a certain prediction is crucial for clinical acceptance and safety. It allows medical professionals to verify the model's understanding of specific findings.
- **Improved Performance in Specialized Domains:** ItemizedCLIP consistently outperforms leading baseline models in zero-shot classification across diverse medical imaging modalities (Brain MRI, Head CT, Chest CT) and remote sensing. This demonstrates its practical utility and potential to accelerate research and development in these specialized fields, where manually annotated datasets are often scarce and expensive to produce.
- **Dual Guarantees of Item Independence and Representation Completeness:** The core innovation lies in objectives like Inter-Item Separation (IIS) and Upweighting Worst Positive (UWP), which jointly enforce item independence and representation completeness. These properties ensure that the model differentiates between distinct findings within an image while simultaneously capturing all relevant information, preventing critical omissions.
- **Advancement of Vision-Language Learning:** By extending the capabilities of contrastive vision-language models to handle itemized supervision, this work contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of how language can guide visual learning. It highlights the importance of matching model design to the specific characteristics of textual supervision, rather than assuming a universal captioning format.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that the work's exploration is currently bounded by data scale and compute resources, not having yet examined billion-scale pretraining or initialization from very large pretrained vision-language models. The approach's effectiveness is also dependent on the quality and granularity of the itemized text supervision, which can vary across domains. Future work is suggested to explore large-scale fine-tuning, more efficient attention mechanisms, and longitudinal item reasoning for sequential or temporal datasets.

Overall, ItemizedCLIP represents a step towards more robust, interpretable, and context-aware vision-language models, particularly valuable for domains rich in structured, itemized textual descriptions.
