# iManip: Skill-Incremental Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The iManip framework introduces a method for robots to incrementally learn new manipulation skills, leveraging a temporal replay strategy and an extendable PerceiverIO architecture. It effectively addresses catastrophic forgetting, enabling continuous skill acquisition while preserving previously learned capabilities, demonstrating robust performance in simulation and real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robotic agents struggle with incrementally acquiring new manipulation skills while retaining previously learned ones, often experiencing catastrophic forgetting.
- Traditional incremental learning methods, primarily developed for visual classification, are insufficient for robotic manipulation due to its unique temporal dynamics and complex 3D action primitives.
- Existing lifelong learning benchmarks for robots typically focus on variations of similar skills, rather than evaluating the acquisition of truly diverse and novel manipulation capabilities.

## Method

- The **iManip** framework is proposed, comprising a ==Voxel Encoder==, an Extendable PerceiverIO, and a Policy Decoder for sequential skill learning from demonstration data.
- A Temporal Replay Strategy (TRS) is implemented to preserve the temporal integrity and diversity of old skill demonstrations, using keyframes and farthest-distance entropy sampling.
- An Extendable PerceiverIO (EPIO) is designed with skill-specific action prompts and dynamically expanding weight matrices, enabling adaptation to new action primitives while freezing existing knowledge.

## Results

- In simulation, iManip consistently outperformed multi-task policies and traditional incremental learning baselines, achieving an average success rate of **45.5%** in the B5-5N1 setting, demonstrating superior learning and retention.
- Ablation studies confirmed the Temporal Replay Strategy as the most impactful component, improving success rates by **22.4%** in the B5-5N1 setup by effectively preserving temporal data integrity.
- Real-world experiments with a Franka Panda robot validated iManip's effectiveness in maintaining high success rates on previously learned skills while incrementally acquiring new ones, showcasing robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving the temporal integrity and diversity of historical demonstration data is critical for preventing catastrophic forgetting in dynamic robotic manipulation tasks.
> - An extendable PerceiverIO architecture, utilizing skill-specific action prompts and dynamically growing weight matrices, can effectively integrate new action primitives while protecting previously acquired knowledge.
> - Freezing core network components (encoders and much of the PerceiverIO) during incremental learning, combined with training only newly appended elements and the policy decoder, is an efficient strategy to balance learning new skills and retaining old ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic agents capable of acquiring and adapting to multiple manipulation skills incrementally represents a long-standing objective within the robotics community. Prior research in robotic manipulation has predominantly focused on enhancing manipulation performance in specific tasks or leveraging knowledge transfer from pre-trained large language or vision models to learn robotic manipulations. While these areas have seen progress, the challenge of enabling robots to continually learn new manipulation skills based on previously acquired knowledge without requiring retraining of the entire system, a concept termed "skill-incremental learning," has received comparatively less attention.

Existing benchmarks, such as LIBERO, have begun to explore lifelong robot learning by evaluating incremental capabilities related to new objects, goals, or spatial positions. However, these benchmarks often involve tasks that share similar underlying skill characteristics, rather than requiring the agent to learn entirely novel skills. This limits their scope in addressing the complexity of acquiring diverse manipulation capabilities over time.

This paper identifies a gap in the current research landscape where existing incremental learning methods, often derived from visual classification tasks, exhibit limitations when applied to the dynamic and action-complex domain of robotic manipulation. These limitations primarily manifest as catastrophic forgetting of previously learned skills when new skills are introduced. The present work positions itself to address this gap by proposing a framework specifically designed for skill-incremental learning in robotic manipulation, accounting for the unique characteristics of temporal dynamics and action complexity inherent in these tasks. By doing so, it aims to contribute towards the development of more adaptable and generalist robotic agents capable of continuous skill acquisition in varied environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate and address the challenge of skill-incremental learning in robotic manipulation. This involves enabling robots to continuously acquire new manipulation skills by leveraging existing knowledge, rather than initiating training from scratch for each new skill, while simultaneously mitigating the forgetting of previously learned skills.

The motivation for this work stems from the aspiration to develop generalist robotic agents that can adaptively learn a diverse range of manipulation skills. The paper illustrates this with a household robot scenario: a robot with basic functions (e.g., folding clothes, fetching items) should be able to learn new tasks, such as loading a dishwasher, by observing demonstrations, without needing extensive retraining that would compromise its existing abilities.

The authors identify two key challenges that hinder the application of traditional incremental learning methods, largely developed for visual classification, to robotic manipulation:

1. **Temporal Complexity:** Robotic manipulation tasks involve dynamic changes in both the environment and the robot's state over time, where each action influences subsequent actions within a trajectory. Traditional incremental learning methods often focus on selecting representative individual samples per class, which, when directly applied to temporal demonstration data, can lead to temporal imbalance in the replayed trajectories and result in instability during task execution. This neglect of temporal integrity is identified as a significant factor contributing to catastrophic forgetting.

2. **Action Complexity:** Unlike general visual feature learning in classification, robotic manipulation necessitates complex action planning through interactions with a physical environment, integrating visual and language inputs. When a new manipulation skill emerges, the robot must acquire new visual-language interactions and develop new action primitives. Traditional methods, focused on general visual features, often overlook the nuanced requirements of learning and adapting diverse 3D action primitives involving translation, rotation, and gripper control. This oversight further contributes to their susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting of manipulation-specific knowledge.

To address these motivations and challenges, the paper outlines several key objectives:
- To construct a skill-incremental environment based on the RLBench benchmark to facilitate the study of continuous skill acquisition.
- To empirically evaluate the performance of traditional incremental learning methods within this new robotic skill-incremental setting and confirm their susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting.
- To propose a novel framework, termed iManip, specifically designed to alleviate catastrophic forgetting by accounting for the temporal and action complexities of robotic manipulation.
- To design a "temporal replay strategy" to preserve the temporal integrity and diversity of old skill data.
- To propose an "extendable PerceiverIO" architecture that can adapt to new action primitives by utilizing skill-specific action prompts and extendable weights, thereby preserving knowledge of previous skills.
- To conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness, robustness, lightweight nature, and extendability of the proposed iManip framework in both simulation and real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed iManip framework is designed to address the challenges of skill-incremental learning in robotic manipulation, particularly focusing on mitigating catastrophic forgetting caused by temporal and action complexities. The framework's overall pipeline involves sequential learning of manipulation skills from a stream of demonstration data.

**Overall Pipeline:**
The agent learns a sequence of manipulation skills, with each skill represented by demonstration trajectories comprising visual inputs (RGB-D images, proprioception matrix including openness, end-effector position, and timestep) and language instructions. The objective is to generate optimal actions, which include target translation in voxel space, rotation, gripper openness, and collision avoidance.
The iManip framework consists of three main components:
1. **Voxel Encoder:** Processes RGB-D images and projects them into 3D voxels, which are then encoded by a 3D convolutional UNet architecture to learn 3D scene features.
2. **Extendable PerceiverIO:** A latent transformer that integrates multimodal inputs (voxel features, language encodings from CLIP RN50, and proprioception data encoded by an MLP).
3. **Policy Decoder:** Predicts optimal robot actions from the processed features.

**Temporal Replay Strategy (TRS):**
To address the temporal complexity of robotic manipulation, the iManip framework incorporates a temporal replay strategy. Unlike conventional rehearsal-based methods in incremental vision classification that sample representative individual data points, this strategy focuses on maintaining the temporal integrity of entire expert episodes.
- **Keyframes:** The method identifies "keyframes" within demonstration episodes. These are defined as samples where the end-effector's state changes (e.g., gripper closing) or its velocity approaches zero, representing critical temporal landmarks in a trajectory.
- **Farthest-distance Entropy Sampling:** To ensure the replay buffer stores a diverse set of samples for each keyframe, the strategy employs farthest-distance entropy sampling. For each keyframe, it samples a fixed number of demonstrations that exhibit the largest entropy divergence in their action predictions. This approach selects samples that are maximally distinct from each other in terms of action uncertainty, thereby maximizing information content and variety within the replay buffer for each keyframe. Algorithm 1 details this process, ensuring storage of temporally balanced, information-rich samples.

**Extendable PerceiverIO (EPIO):**
To address the action complexity inherent in robotic manipulation, iManip introduces an Extendable PerceiverIO. This component is designed to adapt to new action primitives associated with new skills while preserving knowledge of existing skills.
- **Multimodal Input:** The input to the PerceiverIO is a concatenation of multimodal patches: `X_voxel` (voxel encodings), `X_language` (language encodings), and `X_action` (skill-specific action prompts).
- **Skill-Specific Action Prompts:** `X_action` itself is a concatenation of `X_old_action` and `X_new_action`, representing prompts for previously learned and newly acquired skills, respectively.
- **Extendable Weights:** Within the PerceiverIO's self-attention layers, the weight matrices for query (`W_scale_Q`) and key (`W_scale_K`) are designed to be extendable. When learning a new skill, new weight matrices (`W_new_Q`, `W_new_K`) are appended to the existing ones (`W_old_Q`, `W_old_K`), dynamically expanding the model's capacity to handle new action primitives.
- **Parameter Freezing:** During the acquisition of new skills, the learned parameters of the existing PerceiverIO are frozen. Only the new skill-specific action prompts (`X_new_action`) and the newly appended weight matrices (`W_new_Q`, `W_new_K`) are trained. This selective training allows the agent to quickly adapt to new action requirements without corrupting the representation of previously learned skills, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

**Knowledge Distillation:**
To further preserve knowledge from old skills during the learning of new ones, iManip employs a knowledge distillation mechanism.
- **Distillation Loss (L_dis):** The output probability distributions of the old model (acting as a teacher) are used to guide the training of the new model (the student). An L2 loss is applied between the predicted action probabilities (translation, rotation, openness, collision avoidance) of the old and new agents. This objective encourages the new agent to maintain consistency with the behaviors of the old agent on previously learned tasks.

**Learning Objectives:**
The overall training loss function for iManip combines the action loss and the distillation loss:
`L_total = L_act + λ_dis * L_dis`
- **Action Loss (L_act):** A cross-entropy loss is used for imitation learning, ensuring accurate prediction of translation, rotation, gripper openness, and collision avoidance based on ground truth one-hot encodings from expert demonstrations.
- **Training Strategy:** During incremental steps, the agent is trained on a combination of new skill demonstrations and the replayed samples from the cached memory (`M`). The encoder and the main components of the Extendable PerceiverIO are typically frozen, with only the policy decoder, new action prompts, and newly appended weights being updated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments to evaluate the iManip framework, demonstrating its capabilities across several metrics.

**Simulation Results:**

1. **Performance Comparison with Baselines:**
 - iManip was compared against standard multi-task manipulation policies (PerAct, ManiGaussian) and their adaptations with traditional incremental learning baselines (P-TIB, M-TIB) in the B5-5N1 setting (5 base skills, 5 incremental steps with 1 new skill each).
 - Results showed that iManip consistently and significantly outperformed all baselines in terms of average success rate for both old and all learned skills. For instance, in the B5-5N1 setting, iManip achieved an average success rate of 45.5%, notably higher than M-TIB's 36.1% and PerAct's 5.9%, indicating its effectiveness in learning new skills while mitigating forgetting of previous ones.

2. **Ablation Study:**
 - An ablation study on the B5-1N1 and B5-5N1 setups evaluated the contribution of each proposed component: Temporal Replay Strategy (TRS), Extendable PerceiverIO (EPIO), and Knowledge Distillation (DIS).
 - The TRS demonstrated the most substantial impact, improving success rates by 28.6% (B5-1N1) and 22.4% (B5-5N1) over the baseline without any incremental policy (R1). This highlights the importance of maintaining temporal data integrity.
 - EPIO further improved performance by 4.7% (B5-1N1) and 4.8% (B5-5N1), confirming its role in adapting to new action primitives while preserving old knowledge.
 - Knowledge Distillation provided additional performance gains of 2.7% (B5-1N1) and 3.6% (B5-5N1).
 - The study concluded that the replay policy is critical, as the absence of old data for retraining significantly increases catastrophic forgetting.

3. **Effect of Parameter Freezing:**
 - Experiments on freezing different network components (encoders, Extendable PerceiverIO, policy decoder) revealed that freezing the encoders or the Extendable PerceiverIO helped retain knowledge of old skills without hindering new skill learning.
 - The policy decoder was found to be crucial for learning new skills.
 - The most effective strategy involved freezing both the encoder and the Extendable PerceiverIO (except for the newly appended components) and training only the policy decoder, new action prompts, and new weights. This approach resulted in faster convergence, fewer trainable parameters (e.g., 8M parameters compared to 47M for non-frozen), and superior performance in both old and new skill success rates.

4. **Experiments on Different Skill-Incremental Setups:**
 - iManip maintained higher success rates across various incremental settings (B5-1N5, B2-4N2, B3-2N3) compared to ManiGaussian and M-TIB, demonstrating its generalizability and robustness to different learning sequences.

5. **Exploring Data Replay Methods:**
 - Comparison with classical rehearsal-based methods (Herding, Hardsample, Episode, Random) showed that iManip's temporal replay strategy, leveraging farthest-distance entropy sampling for keyframes, achieved the best success rate. Classic methods that neglect temporal integrity performed poorly on old skills.

6. **Visualization of Skill-Specific Action Prompts:**
 - Grad-CAM visualization of action prompts demonstrated that they learn skill-specific action primitives. Different action prompts were activated (maximized weights) for different actions or skills (e.g., "move down to the target" vs. "rotate the gripper to close the jar"). This supports the design principle of freezing old prompts and learning new ones for adaptability.

**Real-World Experiments:**

- Five manipulation skills were tested with a Franka Panda robotic arm in a B1-4N1 setup (1 base skill, 4 incremental steps with 1 new skill each).
- The baseline without an incremental policy showed rapid forgetting of previously learned skills when new skills were introduced.
- iManip, when integrated, maintained the success rate on old skills effectively. A noted trade-off was that iManip's success rate on *new* skills could be slightly lower than a baseline that was completely fine-tuned and potentially overfitted to only that new skill. However, iManip's overall performance on *all* learned skills was significantly higher, demonstrating its capability for continuous learning.

**Supplementary Experiments:**

- **Hyperparameter Studies:** Detailed experiments on key hyperparameters (replays per keyframe, action prompt length, distillation loss factor, extended PerceiverIO dimension) provided insights for optimal configuration, indicating settings that balance performance and memory efficiency.
- **Effect of Skill Learning Order:** iManip demonstrated balanced average accuracy regardless of the order in which short, medium, or long-horizon skills were learned, highlighting its robustness. Longer-horizon skills were observed to be more effective in acquiring general knowledge, which aids in mitigating forgetting.
- **Plug and Play:** The iManip policy was shown to be adaptable to other robotic multi-task learning pipelines (PerAct, GNFactor, 3DDA), significantly improving their performance in incremental learning settings by incorporating its temporal replay strategy and extendable transformer architecture.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by explicitly addressing the challenging and underexplored problem of skill-incremental learning. The significance of this work lies in its potential to enable robots to acquire and retain a growing repertoire of manipulation skills over their operational lifespan without undergoing repetitive and resource-intensive retraining processes.

The key contributions and potential impacts include:

- **Advancement in Generalist Robotics:** By proposing a framework that successfully mitigates catastrophic forgetting in a skill-incremental learning setting, this work moves towards the realization of more generalist robotic agents. Such robots would be capable of continuously adapting to new tasks and environments, enhancing their utility in dynamic real-world scenarios like household assistance, industrial applications, or service robotics.
- **Novel Skill-Incremental Environment:** The construction of a skill-incremental environment based on the RLBench benchmark is a valuable contribution to the research community. This environment provides a standardized and challenging platform for future research in lifelong robotic learning, fostering further development and comparison of incremental learning algorithms.
- **Addressing Fundamental Challenges:** The iManip framework specifically tackles the identified limitations of applying traditional incremental learning methods to robotics by addressing temporal and action complexities. The proposed temporal replay strategy and extendable PerceiverIO represent architectural and methodological advancements tailored to the unique characteristics of robotic manipulation data and tasks.
- **Demonstrated Effectiveness and Robustness:** The extensive experiments, both in high-fidelity simulation and real-world robot setups, provide empirical evidence of iManip's effectiveness, robustness across different incremental settings, and lightweight design. The ability to generalize and integrate with existing robotic pipelines further underscores its practical applicability.
- **Resource Efficiency:** By enabling continuous learning without retraining from scratch and requiring only lightweight finetuning of specific model components, iManip contributes to more resource-efficient robot deployment and adaptation. This reduces computational costs and the need for large, continuously updated datasets.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the skill-incremental environment and the iManip framework will facilitate broader adoption, replication, and extension of this research by other scientists and developers, accelerating progress in lifelong robotic learning.

Overall, this research represents a step towards building truly autonomous and adaptable robots that can continuously learn and evolve their capabilities, moving beyond static task-specific programming to more flexible and intelligent agents.
