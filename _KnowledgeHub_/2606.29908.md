# Pondering the Way: Spatial-perceiving World Action Model for Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Spatial-perceiving World Action Model (SWAM) is introduced as a latent diffusion model for embodied visual navigation, jointly generating RGBD observation sequences and corresponding action trajectories from start and goal RGB inputs. SWAM achieved up to 75.1% lower Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and significantly higher inference efficiency compared to previous two-stage planning baselines, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization capabilities across diverse environments.

## Problem

- Existing world model-based planners for visual navigation use a "verification-centric" approach, sampling action candidates and then evaluating them, which leads to candidate dependence and high computational cost.
- Current methods often produce geometrically inconsistent trajectories or lack explicit spatial constraints, resulting in visually plausible but physically infeasible paths.
- The decoupling of goal intent from trajectory synthesis in previous approaches hinders the generation of intrinsically goal-aligned and spatially feasible paths for long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- **SWAM**, a ==latent diffusion model==, jointly generates intermediate RGBD observation sequences and corresponding 2D planar action trajectories in a single inference pass, conditioned on start and goal RGB observations.
- It leverages a pretrained ==Diffusion Transformer== (CogVideoX) backbone and integrates ==depth pseudo-labels== during training for explicit spatial grounding, requiring only monocular RGB input at inference time.
- The model incorporates a ==Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR)== module to align actions with generated visual cues and a ==Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR)== loss to stabilize trajectory predictions and mitigate cumulative errors.

## Results

- SWAM achieved substantially lower Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) across diverse datasets, reducing ATE by up to **75.1%** on TartanDrive compared to the strongest two-stage baseline.
- It demonstrated significantly higher inference efficiency (**16.91s/sample** vs. **245.98s/sample** for the best baseline) and improved goal-reaching success rates, particularly at stricter thresholds (**2.1x** higher success on RECON at τ=0.25).
- SWAM exhibited strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, achieving **21.2%** lower ATE on the unseen HuRoN dataset compared to a baseline explicitly trained on it, while also producing state-of-the-art video generation quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly generating visual (RGB-D) and action sequences within a single task-centric framework significantly improves trajectory accuracy and spatial consistency compared to verification-centric, two-stage planning methods.
> - Integrating depth pseudo-labels during training, even with monocular RGB input at inference, provides crucial geometric priors that enhance the physical plausibility and structural integrity of predicted trajectories and observations.
> - Specific regularization techniques like Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR) and the Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR) module are essential for maintaining stable, accurate long-horizon predictions and for aligning generated actions with visual evidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied visual navigation, which tasks an intelligent agent with inferring movement and traversable paths to a visual goal, necessitates the integration of visual perception, spatial reasoning, and decision-making. Existing approaches generally fall into several categories:

**Direct Online Policy Methods:** These methods learn a direct mapping from observations to actions, often using reinforcement learning or imitation learning. While efficient at inference, they typically lack explicit visual horizons and exhibit limitations in error recovery or handling the multi-modality of navigation behaviors, where diverse valid paths can lead to the same goal. Diffusion-based policies, such as NoMaD, have addressed the multi-modality by modeling distributions over action trajectories but generally generate action sequences without explicitly modeling intermediate visual states.

**Action-Conditioned World Models for Planning and Navigation:** This paradigm involves learning predictive models of environment dynamics to enable agents to plan by imagining future observations under candidate actions. Recent advancements in generative modeling, particularly video prediction and video diffusion models, have enhanced the realism of these imagined rollouts. However, these methods often follow a "verification-centric" or "action-first, verify-later" pipeline. This involves first sampling candidate action sequences from an external policy and then evaluating them using the world model to predict future visual trajectories. This modular design can lead to several issues:
- **Candidate dependence:** Planning quality is constrained by the diversity and coverage of the sampled candidates.
- **Computational inefficiency:** Exhaustive sampling and rolling out multiple candidates for evaluation can be computationally expensive, particularly for long-horizon tasks.
- **Geometric inconsistency:** Without explicit spatial constraints guiding action generation, the world model may produce visually plausible but geometrically or physically infeasible trajectories (e.g., abrupt viewpoint changes). The world model acts as a passive evaluator rather than an active participant in trajectory synthesis, decoupling goal intent from spatial reasoning.

**Joint Video-Action Modeling for Embodied Intelligence:** A more recent trend in embodied AI emphasizes the joint modeling of observations and actions to capture the causal dynamics of agent-environment interactions within generative world models. Some navigation-oriented world models, like DINO-WM and WoMaP, leverage pretrained visual features and structured modeling for open-vocabulary localization and zero-shot planning. However, these often still treat the world model as a simulator, separating environment dynamics from the explicit generation of executable action sequences. Other joint video-action generation efforts (e.g., VideoVLA, CoVAR, PAD) primarily focus on short-horizon manipulation or local workspace interactions, frequently lacking the long-horizon spatiotemporal priors and explicit geometric grounding essential for complex navigation. Aether combines geometric reconstruction with generative modeling but does not explicitly evaluate navigation-specific planning metrics or goal-conditioned trajectory generation.

This paper introduces the Spatial-perceiving World Action Model (SWAM) to address the limitations of current approaches. SWAM distinguishes itself by proposing a task-centric joint observation-action generation framework that directly synthesizes control trajectories and corresponding visual (RGB-D) sequences in a single inference pass. This contrasts with verification-centric models by integrating spatial reasoning and goal intent directly into the generation process for long-horizon visual navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing SWAM stems from the identified limitations of existing world model-based planners in visual navigation, which predominantly follow a verification-centric paradigm. The key issues that SWAM aims to address are:

1. **Decoupling of Goal Intent from Trajectory Synthesis:** Traditional methods treat the world model as a passive evaluator, where goal intent is implicitly assessed after trajectories are proposed by an external policy. This separation hinders the generation of intrinsically goal-aligned and spatially feasible paths.
2. **Candidate Dependence:** The quality of planning is directly limited by the range and coverage of candidate action sequences sampled externally. A narrow or insufficient set of candidates can lead to suboptimal or failed navigation.
3. **High Computational Overhead:** The process of sampling numerous candidate trajectories and then rolling out visual predictions for each using the world model is computationally intensive. This renders such approaches inefficient, especially for complex or long-horizon navigation tasks.
4. **Geometric Inconsistency:** The absence of explicit spatial constraints during action generation can result in predicted visual trajectories that are geometrically infeasible or exhibit abrupt, unrealistic viewpoint changes. This reduces the reliability of the simulated evidence used for evaluation.

To mitigate these limitations, SWAM's key objectives are:

- **Joint Observation-Action Generation:** To develop a unified framework that, given start and goal RGB observations, simultaneously generates intermediate RGB-D sequences and the corresponding action trajectories in a single forward pass. This "task-centric" approach promotes inherent goal alignment and temporal consistency between actions and visual outcomes.
- **Improved Spatial Feasibility:** By co-generating visual (including depth) and action sequences, SWAM aims to enhance the spatial plausibility of predicted trajectories. Leveraging depth pseudo-labels during training injects spatial priors, ensuring actions are consistent with navigable space, while only requiring monocular RGB input at inference time.
- **Reduced Inference Cost:** By performing single-pass inference without the need for extensive candidate sampling and iterative evaluation, SWAM intends to significantly decrease computational overhead compared to two-stage planners.
- **Fine-Grained Alignment and Stability:** To introduce specific mechanisms—a Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR) module and a Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR) loss—to enforce detailed alignment between predicted motion and generated visual cues, and to stabilize trajectory predictions across varying distances and horizons, mitigating compounding errors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Spatial-perceiving World Action Model (SWAM) is a latent diffusion model designed for goal-conditioned visual navigation. It jointly generates an RGBD observation sequence and a corresponding action sequence from start and goal RGB observations.

**Problem Formulation:** The model aims to learn the conditional distribution $p_\theta(\{o_n\}_{n=1}^N, \{a_n\}_{n=1}^N | o_0, o_G)$, where $o_0 = (I_0, D_0)$ and $o_G = (I_G, D_G)$ are the start and goal observations (RGB image $I$ and depth $D$), and $\{o_n\}_{n=1}^N$ and $\{a_n\}_{n=1}^N$ represent the horizon-$N$ sequence of future observations and planar actions, respectively. During training, depth data is obtained using DepthAnything v3, a monocular depth estimator, serving as geometric supervision. At inference, only monocular RGB input is required.

**Overall Architecture:** SWAM extends a pretrained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, specifically CogVideoX, to handle joint RGBD observation and action generation.
- **Latent Representation and Tokenization:** For each observation $o_n = (I_n, \hat{D}_n)$, a frozen 3D variational autoencoder (VAE) from CogVideoX encodes the RGB image and estimated depth map into separate latent feature maps ($z^i_n$ for RGB, $z^d_n$ for depth). The action sequence $\{a_n\}_{n=1}^N$, where $a_n = (\Delta x_n, \Delta y_n)$ represents 2D planar motion, is transformed into a token matrix $x_a$ using a learnable MLP, ensuring a unified embedding dimension with visual tokens.
- **Multimodal Sequence Construction:** A single unified token sequence $X_t$ is constructed comprising the clean target variables for diffusion: intermediate RGB tokens ($\{z^i_n\}_{n=1}^N$), intermediate depth tokens ($\{z^d_n\}_{n=1}^N$), and action tokens ($x_a$). The start and goal latents ($c = (z^i_0, z^d_0, z^i_G, z^d_G)$) are treated as conditioning information.
- **Denoising Process:** A DDPM-style forward noising process is applied to the unified sequence. The Diffusion Transformers iteratively denoise the sequence over $T$ steps, predicting the noise residual $\epsilon_\theta(X_t, c, t)$ to recover the clean sequence.
- **Decoding:** After denoising, the RGB and depth latents are decoded back into image space using the VAE decoder. The action tokens are processed by a lightweight network that incorporates the Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR) module before being projected to planar actions.

**Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR) Module:** This lightweight module is introduced to inject generated RGBD evidence into the action tokens just prior to decoding, without altering the joint diffusion backbone. It refines the final-layer action tokens ($X_a$) by cross-attention with the final-layer visual tokens ($X_v$). A gated residual connection, controlled by a sigmoid function, determines the extent to which visual evidence influences the action representation. This helps ensure that the final actions are consistent with the predicted visual and spatial environment.

**Joint-Training Objective:** The model is trained using a composite loss function:
- **Diffusion Loss ($L_{DDPM}$):** This is the standard DDPM denoising objective, which minimizes the squared error between the predicted noise residual and the actual noise added to the latent sequence.
- **Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR) Loss ($L_{TSR}$):** To address cumulative errors in action integration that can lead to trajectory drift, particularly over long horizons, TSR directly supervises the cumulative displacement. It minimizes the L2 distance between the predicted endpoint (obtained by integrating denoised actions) and the ground-truth goal position in the local coordinate frame. A $1/N$ normalization is applied to stabilize the loss across trajectories of varying lengths ($N$).
- **Final Objective:** The total loss is $L = L_{DDPM} + \lambda_{TSR} L_{TSR}$, where $\lambda_{TSR}$ is a weighting factor. This objective encourages both local denoising accuracy and global trajectory consistency.

**Model Initialization and Architectural Adaptation:** To leverage the spatiotemporal priors of the pretrained CogVideoX backbone and prevent distribution shifts, all newly added modules (e.g., action MLP, VGAR) are initialized with zero weights. This ensures that the model initially behaves as the original pretrained generator, producing high-quality RGB predictions. During training, gradients progressively update these new parameters, allowing SWAM to learn the coupling between RGB observations, depth signals, and actions in a data-driven manner, which stabilizes the training process.

**Implementation Details (from Supplementary Material):** For quantitative evaluations, each training sample consists of a fixed-length segment containing 8 observation-action pairs (9 frames). For specific long-range visualization, a separate model variant was trained using variable-length segments (N from {9, 17, 33, 65}) with RoPE interpolation for time-position embeddings. Training utilized bf16 mixed precision, gradient checkpointing, a batch size of 1, Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-4}$, 1,000 warm-up steps, and gradient clipping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SWAM against several baselines across diverse navigation datasets, demonstrating its performance in trajectory accuracy, goal-reaching, video generation quality, and zero-shot generalization.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets:** RECON (large-scale outdoor, long horizons), SCAND (indoor/outdoor, dynamic obstacles), TartanDrive (off-road, irregular terrain), and HuRoN (indoor, dynamic humans, used for zero-shot generalization). Actions are derived from robot poses and normalized.
- **Baselines:** Direct policy methods (GNM, NoMaD), and world-model-based planners (NWM combined with NoMaD for candidate generation, denoted as NWM+NoMaD (×N) with N = 2, 4, 8, 16 candidates). A custom CogVideoX (Joint) baseline was also established, using the same pretrained backbone but without SWAM's specific depth modeling, TSR, or VGAR.
- **Metrics:** Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) for trajectory accuracy (in unit grids); success@($\tau$) for goal-reaching ($\tau \in \{1.0, 0.5, 0.25\}$ grids); PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS for video generation quality. Inference time per episode was also measured.

**Trajectory Accuracy:**
- SWAM achieved substantially lower ATE and RPE values across all tested datasets compared to all baselines. For example, SWAM reduced ATE by 39.2% on RECON, 47.2% on SCAND, and 75.1% on TartanDrive compared to NWM+NoMaD (×16), which is the strongest two-stage baseline.
- This performance was achieved with significantly higher inference efficiency (16.91s/sample for SWAM vs. 245.98s/sample for NWM+NoMaD (×16)), indicating a favorable efficiency-accuracy tradeoff.
- The CogVideoX (Joint) baseline showed improved ATE over NWM-based methods on TartanDrive, suggesting the benefit of joint RGB-action generation. However, its overall performance was notably inferior to SWAM, particularly in RPE, indicating that visual coherence alone is insufficient for precise navigation without explicit spatial and trajectory guidance.

**Goal-reaching Performance:**
- SWAM demonstrated higher success rates across various distance thresholds, particularly at stricter thresholds (e.g., 2.1x higher success at $\tau = 0.25$ on RECON compared to NWM+NoMaD (×16)). This indicates enhanced precision in reaching the final goal position.

**Video Generation Quality:**
- SWAM achieved state-of-the-art results in video generation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all datasets.
- This improvement is attributed to two factors: (1) Spatial grounding through predicted depth, which provides geometric constraints and improves structural consistency, reducing visual artifacts. (2) More accurate and geometrically feasible action predictions, which naturally lead to higher fidelity in corresponding RGBD frames.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- SWAM demonstrated robustness to path length, maintaining accurate trajectory scaling across varying distances, whereas NWM exhibited scale drift that increased with the prediction horizon.
- In diverse scenarios, SWAM accurately captured complex turning trajectories, a challenge for NWM which often showed a straight-motion bias. SWAM preserved both trajectory accuracy and spatiotemporal consistency in generated observations, while NWM sometimes suffered from context loss, mode collapse, or hallucinated frames.
- Longer-sequence visualizations (up to 64 timesteps, from supplementary material) showed that SWAM maintained stable motion dynamics and avoided rapid degradation over extended prediction horizons, preserving temporal consistency.

**Zero-Shot Generalization:**
- When tested on the unseen HuRoN dataset without any training, SWAM achieved an ATE of 2.94 and an RPE of 0.85. This performance was 21.2% lower in ATE than NWM+NoMaD (×16) which was explicitly trained on HuRoN, demonstrating SWAM's strong cross-domain generalization capability across environments with different layouts and visual characteristics.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Depth Modality:** Incorporating depth pseudo-labels during training significantly improved trajectory accuracy (e.g., RECON ATE decreased from 2.09 to 1.70). This indicates that explicit spatial cues enhance geometric understanding and produce more physically consistent trajectories.
- **Effect of Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR) Loss:** TSR alone improved trajectory accuracy over the baseline, particularly for long-horizon prediction (e.g., TartanDrive ATE decreased from 4.90 to 2.63). When combined with depth, TSR further enhanced performance, showing complementary gains.
- **Effect of Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR):** The VGAR module consistently refined predicted trajectories, further improving ATE and RPE across most datasets when integrated with depth and TSR.

**Failure Cases and Limitations:**
- SWAM encounters difficulties in environments with **ambiguous traversable weeds**, often misinterpreting them as solid obstacles, leading to erroneous avoidance and trajectory drift due to a lack of semantic-aware traversability reasoning.
- The model also shows limitations in **sharp-turn scenarios**, where the current planar displacement-based action representation is insufficient to capture rapid heading adjustments. This results in deviations from high-curvature paths due to the absence of explicit orientation dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SWAM represents an advancement in the field of embodied visual navigation by proposing a task-centric, joint observation-action generation framework. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** SWAM directly tackles critical drawbacks of existing verification-centric world model planners, specifically candidate dependence, high computational overhead, and geometric inconsistencies. By jointly generating visual observations (including depth) and action trajectories in a single inference pass, the model inherently promotes goal alignment and spatial feasibility, moving beyond a passive evaluation paradigm.
- **Improved Performance and Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that SWAM achieves superior trajectory accuracy, goal-reaching success rates, and visual fidelity compared to state-of-the-art two-stage baselines. Crucially, these improvements are realized with significantly higher inference efficiency, making SWAM more practical for real-world deployment where computational resources and real-time decision-making are critical.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The strong zero-shot generalization capability of SWAM to unseen environments, as evidenced by its performance on the HuRoN dataset without specific training, indicates its ability to learn transferable spatiotemporal representations. This suggests the potential for more robust and adaptable navigation systems that can operate effectively in diverse, unencountered settings.
- **Spatial Awareness through Depth:** The integration of depth pseudo-labels during training and the subsequent generation of RGB-D sequences, even with monocular RGB input at inference, highlights the value of explicit spatial priors. This approach provides stronger geometric constraints that improve the physical plausibility and structural consistency of both predicted movements and visual rollouts.
- **Robustness to Trajectory Challenges:** The introduction of the Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR) module and Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR) loss contributes to more stable predictions over varying distances and mitigates compounding errors common in long-horizon planning. This enhances the model's reliability for extended navigation tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
SWAM's advancements could lead to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems that can navigate complex, dynamic environments more efficiently and reliably. This has implications for various applications, including:
- **Autonomous Vehicles:** More precise and efficient navigation systems for self-driving cars, especially in challenging urban or off-road conditions.
- **Service Robotics:** Improved capabilities for robots operating in unknown or partially known indoor environments, such as logistics, healthcare, or domestic assistance.
- **Exploration and Inspection:** More robust planning for robots in dangerous or inaccessible areas, where accurate trajectory prediction and environmental understanding are paramount.

**Future Directions:**
Acknowledging its limitations in handling ambiguous traversable surfaces and sharp turns, future research could focus on integrating semantic scene understanding for more nuanced traversability reasoning and developing richer action representations that explicitly model orientation dynamics alongside planar displacement. Extending prediction to even longer action-video sequences also represents a promising avenue for further development.
