# Near-Future Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Near-Future Policy Optimization (NPO) introduces a mixed-policy Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) scheme that leverages verified trajectories from a model's own near-future self to guide current policy training. The automated variant, AutoNPO, achieved an average performance of 63.15% across eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating improved convergence speed and exploration compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Pure on-policy exploration in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) suffers from sparse correct trajectories in early stages and premature convergence in later stages.
- Existing mixed-policy RLVR methods face a fundamental trade-off between the quality of the learning signal (Q) from auxiliary trajectories and the gradient variance (V) incurred when using them.
- Approaches using external teachers offer high Q but incur high V, while replaying past trajectories maintains low V but provides limited Q.

## Method

- **Near-Future Policy Optimization (NPO)** guides the current policy using verified trajectories generated by a ==near-future checkpoint== from the same training run, aiming for an optimal balance between signal quality (**Q**) and variance cost (**V**).
- For prompts where the current policy struggles, a verified correct trajectory from the near-future guide is strategically injected into the rollout group, with optional ==importance sampling correction==.
- **AutoNPO** automates this process by adaptively triggering interventions based on ==training reward and entropy signals==, dynamically selecting the optimal checkpoint distance (Δ) to maximize the effective learning signal **S = Q/V**.

## Results

- **AutoNPO** achieved an average performance of **63.15%** across eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks, surpassing the base model (**57.88%**), pure on-policy **GRPO** (**60.25%**), and other mixed-policy baselines.
- The method demonstrated **accelerated convergence** and maintained **higher policy entropy** during training, leading to higher final validation plateaus compared to standard on-policy RL.
- Ablation studies confirmed NPO variants with and without IS correction performed nearly identically and both significantly outperformed **GRPO**, supporting the dispensability of exact IS correction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An optimal balance between signal quality (Q) and variance cost (V) in mixed-policy RLVR is achieved by leveraging a 'near-future self' checkpoint, empirically demonstrating an interior maximum for the effective learning signal S=Q/V.
> - Importance sampling correction for off-policy trajectories in NPO is largely unnecessary due to the 'near-policy' nature of the guide, simplifying implementation and reducing computational overhead.
> - NPO interventions actively re-expand exploration by preserving higher policy entropy, which prevents premature convergence and enables models to break through performance plateaus.
