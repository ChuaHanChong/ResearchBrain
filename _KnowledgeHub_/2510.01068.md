# Compose Your Policies! Improving Diffusion-based or Flow-based Robot Policies via Test-time Distribution-level Composition

> [!summary] Summary
> General Policy Composition (GPC), a training-free framework, enhances diffusion-based and flow-based robot policies by combining distributional scores from multiple pre-trained models via convex combination and test-time search. The method consistently boosts average success rates by up to 7.55% in diverse simulation tasks and 10% in real-world robotic environments.

## Problem

- High data acquisition costs and representational limits hinder the advancement and scaling of diffusion- and flow-based robot policies.
- Conventional post-training methods like fine-tuning or reinforcement learning incur significant expenses and complexity.
- A principled, training-free method was needed to enhance policy performance without requiring additional data or model retraining.

## Method

- Proposed ==General Policy Composition== (**GPC**), a training-free framework for enhancing robot policy performance.
- GPC combines distributional scores from multiple pre-trained policies using a convex combination, identified via test-time search.
- The approach is supported by a theoretical framework demonstrating that convex score composition reduces single-step estimation errors, leading to systemic performance gains.

## Results

- GPC consistently increased success rates by up to **7.55%** in simulation and **10%** in real-world tasks compared to individual policies.
- The framework successfully composed policies with varied input modalities (e.g., RGB and point cloud) and architectures (e.g., Diffusion-based and Flow-based models), showing adaptability.
- Test-time weight search for GPC is efficient, typically requiring 1-2.5 hours, and adds a modest inference overhead of **0.04** seconds per action chunk.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Convex combinations of score estimators significantly reduce single-step estimation error, which theoretically translates to reduced overall trajectory error.
> - General Policy Composition (GPC) offers a versatile "plug-and-play" mechanism for combining heterogeneous policies across different generative modes, input modalities, and network architectures.
> - Test-time search for optimal policy weights allows for task-dependent composition, enabling synergistic improvements, particularly when combining policies with moderate individual accuracy.
