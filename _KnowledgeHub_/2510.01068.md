# Compose Your Policies! Improving Diffusion-based or Flow-based Robot Policies via Test-time Distribution-level Composition

> [!summary] Summary
> General Policy Composition (GPC), a training-free framework, enhances diffusion-based and flow-based robot policies by combining distributional scores from multiple pre-trained models via convex combination and test-time search. The method consistently boosts average success rates by up to 7.55% in diverse simulation tasks and 10% in real-world robotic environments.

## Problem

- High data acquisition costs and representational limits hinder the advancement and scaling of diffusion- and flow-based robot policies.
- Conventional post-training methods like fine-tuning or reinforcement learning incur significant expenses and complexity.
- A principled, training-free method was needed to enhance policy performance without requiring additional data or model retraining.

## Method

- Proposed ==General Policy Composition== (**GPC**), a training-free framework for enhancing robot policy performance.
- GPC combines distributional scores from multiple pre-trained policies using a convex combination, identified via test-time search.
- The approach is supported by a theoretical framework demonstrating that convex score composition reduces single-step estimation errors, leading to systemic performance gains.

## Results

- GPC consistently increased success rates by up to **7.55%** in simulation and **10%** in real-world tasks compared to individual policies.
- The framework successfully composed policies with varied input modalities (e.g., RGB and point cloud) and architectures (e.g., Diffusion-based and Flow-based models), showing adaptability.
- Test-time weight search for GPC is efficient, typically requiring 1-2.5 hours, and adds a modest inference overhead of **0.04** seconds per action chunk.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Convex combinations of score estimators significantly reduce single-step estimation error, which theoretically translates to reduced overall trajectory error.
> - General Policy Composition (GPC) offers a versatile "plug-and-play" mechanism for combining heterogeneous policies across different generative modes, input modalities, and network architectures.
> - Test-time search for optimal policy weights allows for task-dependent composition, enabling synergistic improvements, particularly when combining policies with moderate individual accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of robot learning, specifically concerning the deployment and enhancement of diffusion-based and flow-based policies for robotic control. These policy types, including vision-language-action (VLA) and vision-action (VA) models, have demonstrated capabilities in representing complex, multi-modal action distributions, particularly for high-dimensional inputs such such as vision and language in manipulation and navigation tasks.

However, the widespread adoption and advancement of these policies face significant constraints. A primary limitation is the substantial cost associated with acquiring large-scale interaction datasets required for training or scaling up model architectures. Performance can reach a plateau due to the inherent representational limits of a specific model, yet increasing model capacity typically necessitates even more extensive and expensive data collection. Traditional post-training strategies, such as supervised fine-tuning, also demand costly data, while reinforcement learning methods introduce complexities related to reward engineering and prolonged online interaction.

Existing research in compositional generative modeling provides a foundation for combining multiple components or distributions. This includes energy-based models (EBMs) for visual generation, which enable composition by summing energies, and methods for language generation that combine outputs from multiple agents. Within the domain of diffusion models in robotics, prior work has explored compositional planning and motion planning. Notably, Policy Composition (PoCo) has investigated constraint-based, task-based, and input modality-based composition.

This research distinguishes itself by proposing an alternative paradigm for policy enhancement that avoids additional model training. While prior work like PoCo has explored static model composition, this paper focuses on dynamic, task-dependent weighting of policies and a more general framework for composing heterogeneous policies at the distribution level. The work aims to fill a gap by providing a theoretical justification for such composition and demonstrating its practical utility in improving policy performance without incurring the typical costs associated with data acquisition or model retraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a method that can enhance the performance of diffusion-based and flow-based robot policies without requiring additional model training or extensive data acquisition. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in the current state of robot learning:

- **High Cost of Data Acquisition:** A fundamental barrier to the advancement of diffusion- and flow-based policies is the significant expense and effort involved in collecting large-scale interaction datasets. These datasets are crucial for training complex models and for scaling existing architectures to achieve higher performance.
- **Representational Limits of Single Models:** Individual policy models, despite their capabilities, can reach intrinsic representational limits, leading to performance plateaus. Scaling model architectures to overcome these limits typically requires even larger, more costly datasets to fully realize the potential benefits.
- **Inefficiencies of Conventional Post-training Methods:** Existing strategies for improving policy performance post-training, such as supervised fine-tuning, are often data-intensive. Reinforcement learning, while powerful, introduces challenges like complex reward engineering and the need for extensive online interaction, which can be prohibitive in real-world robotic systems.
- **Underexplored Potential of Policy Composition:** While the concept of composing policies has been explored, previous efforts have largely focused on static combinations. The potential for dynamically weighting and combining heterogeneous policies at the distribution level, particularly in a task-dependent manner, remained largely unaddressed. The researchers observed that the optimal weighting for policy combination is not universal but varies significantly depending on the specific task.
- **Lack of Theoretical Foundation for Score Composition:** A formal mathematical justification for why combining distributional scores from multiple policies could lead to superior overall performance was absent. Establishing such a foundation would provide a principled basis for developing robust policy composition methods.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve three main contributions:
1. **Theoretical Foundation:** To establish a mathematical framework demonstrating that a convex combination of distributional scores from multiple diffusion models can yield a superior one-step functional objective. This is further extended to show that this single-step improvement can propagate through entire generation trajectories, resulting in systemic performance gains.
2. **Method Proposal:** To introduce General Policy Composition (GPC), a training-free framework that enhances policy performance by combining the distributional scores of multiple pre-trained policies via convex combination and test-time search. GPC is designed to be versatile, supporting the "plug-and-play" composition of heterogeneous policies (e.g., VA and VLA models, diffusion- or flow-matching based, irrespective of visual modalities).
3. **Empirical Validation:** To provide comprehensive experimental evidence in both simulated and real-world robotic environments. These evaluations aim to confirm GPC's consistent ability to improve performance and adaptability across a diverse set of tasks, alongside providing insights into the mechanisms underlying its effectiveness through analysis of alternative composition operators and weighting strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on General Policy Composition (GPC), a training-free framework for enhancing robot policy performance by combining the distributional scores of multiple pre-trained policies. The approach is grounded in a theoretical analysis that justifies the effectiveness of score composition.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis of Convex Score Composition

The paper establishes a two-level theoretical foundation:

- **Functional-level Improvement (Proposition 4.1):** This proposition demonstrates that combining score estimators can yield better approximations of the true score. Given two score estimators, ε₁ and ε₂, each comprising a true score (s*), a deterministic bias (bᵢ), and zero-mean noise (ηᵢ), a convex combination ε(w) = wε₁ + (1-w)ε₂ results in a mean-squared error Q(w) that is a convex quadratic function of the weight w. Importantly, the minimizer w\* of this quadratic function satisfies Q(w\*) ≤ min(Q(0), Q(1)), implying that the optimal convex combination can achieve an error no greater than the better individual estimator, and strictly smaller in most cases where the errors of the two estimators are not perfectly aligned. This suggests that convex score composition can reduce estimation error at each step of the generative process.

- **System-level Stability (Proposition 4.2):** This proposition addresses how single-step score errors translate into deviations in the overall trajectory. It posits that the sampling dynamics, modeled as an ordinary differential equation (ODE) ∂x(t)/∂t = F(t, x(t), s(t, x(t))), are stable. The proposition states that the terminal error between an oracle trajectory (x\*(t)) and an approximate trajectory (x\_hat(s)(t)) is bounded by the cumulative score error. Specifically, the expected difference in trajectories at time T is bounded by an expression that scales with the integral of the squared expected score error. This implies that reducing score error at each step, as suggested by Proposition 4.1, contributes directly to reducing the overall trajectory error.

- **Implications for Policy Composition (Corollary 4.1):** By combining these two propositions, the paper concludes that if a convex combination of scores (s\_comp) results in a strictly lower integrated mean-squared score error over time compared to individual policies, then the theoretical upper bound on the expected sampling error for the composed policy will also be strictly reduced. This provides a direct mathematical justification for the use of convex score composition to achieve system-level performance gains.

#### 3.2. General Policy Composition (GPC) Method

Motivated by this theoretical foundation, GPC is proposed as a practical, training-free framework for policy enhancement:

- **Compositional Diffusion Models (CDM) as a Basis:** GPC draws inspiration from CDMs, which model the distribution of a trajectory conditioned on multiple concepts. These models typically express a joint probability as a product of individual conditional probabilities, leading to a score function that is a sum of individual scores and an unconditional score. GPC generalizes classifier-free guidance (CFG) by allowing arbitrary weights.

- **Convex Score Combination:** At its core, GPC combines the score functions (s\_θ) of multiple conditional diffusion or flow-based policies. The composed score, s\_comp(τ\_t, t, c), is calculated as a convex combination: Σ wᵢ \* s\_θ(τ\_t, t, cᵢ), where wᵢ are the weights and sum to one. This convex combination ensures that the composite score remains within the feasible space defined by the individual policies, contributing to stability. The linear relationship between common parameterizations (noise, data, velocity prediction) and the score function ensures that GPC is flexible across different prediction types without requiring specific conversions.

- **Heterogeneous Policy Integration:** GPC is designed for versatility, enabling the "plug-and-play" composition of policies irrespective of:
 - **Generative Mode:** Supports both diffusion-based and flow-based policies.
 - **Input Modalities:** Can combine policies conditioned on different visual modalities (e.g., vision-action (VA) and vision-language-action (VLA) models, or policies using RGB images vs. point clouds).
 - **Network Architectures:** Compatible with policies built on diverse backbones (e.g., U-Net, Transformer, Mamba).

- **Training-Free and Test-Time Search:** GPC operates entirely at inference time. It does not require any additional training of the base policies. Instead, it employs a test-time search strategy (Algorithm 1) to identify optimal weights (w\*) for the convex combination. This typically involves iterating through a discretized range of weights (e.g., 0.0 to 1.0 in steps of 0.1) and evaluating the success rate for each configuration. An "optimized" search method is suggested, where knowledge of relative policy strengths can bias the search to a narrower range of weights (e.g., w > 0.5 for a stronger policy) to reduce computational time.

- **GPC with Superposition:** Beyond convex averaging, the framework also explores alternative composition operators based on the principle of superposition:
 - **Logical OR:** Implemented by applying a softmax function to weighted log probabilities at each sampling step, effectively sampling from a mixture of distributions.
 - **Logical AND:** Aims to enforce agreement among policies, corresponding to the intersection of their distributions. This is achieved by solving a linear system to ensure consistency in score gradients across policies during sampling.

The overall methodology combines rigorous theoretical underpinnings with a flexible, practical, and computationally efficient approach to enhance robotic control policies by intelligently leveraging existing pre-trained models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation of GPC was conducted through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, addressing three key questions: performance, influence of weight configurations, and underlying mechanisms of GPC's success.

#### 4.1. Simulation and Real-world Performance

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** GPC demonstrated consistent improvements across diverse tasks and benchmarks:
 - **Robomimic & PushT:** Achieved an average increase of up to +7.55% in success rate. For instance, combining Florence-Policy-F (VLA, Flow Matching) and Flow Policy (VA, Flow Matching) yielded an 86.39% average success rate, a 7.55% increase.
 - **RoboTwin:** Showed an average increase of up to +7% in success rate across six bimanual manipulation tasks. The combination of RDT (VLA) and DP\_pcd (VA) resulted in a 0.72 average success rate, a 7% increase over DP\_pcd alone and a 32% increase over RDT.
 - **Real-world Evaluations:** Consistently exhibited stronger performance than single-policy baselines. In the "Clean Table" task, GPC achieved 14/20 successes, surpassing the base policies (12/20 and 7/20). In "Place Bottles," it achieved 13/20 successes compared to 7/20 and 11/20 for individual policies. Overall, GPC yielded an average increase of +10% in real-world tasks.

- **Versatility in Composition:** GPC successfully composed policies across various configurations:
 - **Same Input Modality, Different Architectures:** Combining DP and Mamba Policy (both VA, Diffusion-based) on Robomimic/PushT resulted in a +2.22% average improvement.
 - **Different Modalities, Similar Architectures:** Composing RGB-based DP (DP\_img) and point cloud-based DP (DP\_pcd) on RoboTwin increased the average success rate by +5% (from 0.46/0.65 to 0.70).
 - **Different Modalities, Different Architectures:** Integrating RDT (VLA) with DP\_pcd (VA) on RoboTwin produced consistent improvements, with an average success rate increase of +7%.

#### 4.2. Influence of Weight Configurations

Analysis of GPC performance under different weight configurations yielded three key findings:

- **Finding 1: Synergistic Improvement for Moderate Policies:** When both base policies exhibit moderate accuracy (e.g., >30%), GPC often achieves higher accuracy than either individual policy with appropriate weight tuning. For example, in the "Empty Cup Place" task, where DP\_img and DP\_pcd achieved 0.42 and 0.62 respectively, GPC peaked at 0.86 (+24%) with a weight (w₁) of 0.4, indicating that composition can capture a more generalized distribution.
- **Finding 2: Limitations with Weak Policies:** If one policy has significantly lower accuracy, GPC struggles to surpass the performance of the better-performing base policy. In the "Pick Apple Messy" task, with DP\_pcd at 0.26 and DP\_img at 0.05, GPC peaked at 0.25, suggesting that low-accuracy scores can degrade the joint distribution.
- **Finding 3: Dominance of Stronger Policies:** The maximum improvement from GPC typically occurs when the better-performing base policy is assigned a larger weight. For instance, in "Dual Bottles Pick (Easy)," DP\_img (0.77) combined with DP\_pcd (0.36) achieved 0.85 with w₁=0.8, demonstrating effective leveraging of the stronger policy.

#### 4.3. Time and Computational Efficiency

- **Reduced Search Cost:** GPC's weight search is more efficient than retraining or fine-tuning. A full search (9 weight configurations) takes approximately 2.5 hours, while an optimized search (4 configurations) takes about 1 hour. This is significantly less than days for training from scratch or hours for fine-tuning.
- **Modest Inference Overhead:** GPC introduces a modest increase in inference latency per action chunk, from 0.09s for a single policy to 0.13s for a composed policy. This overhead is purely computational and can be further optimized.

#### 4.4. Comprehensive Analysis of GPC Effectiveness

- **Visualization of Superiority:**
 - **Different Modalities:** Visual analysis (Fig. 4a) showed that GPC smoothly adapts between distinct distributions learned by policies trained on different modalities (e.g., RGB vs. point cloud), leveraging complementary information for a more complete distribution.
 - **Different Architectures:** For policies with similar but biased distributions (e.g., Florence and FlowP), GPC refined their alignment, expanded coverage, and enhanced precision through convex composition (Fig. 4b).
- **Execution-Time Sample Distributions:** GPC produced more coherent and concentrated action distributions during execution compared to the scattered or noisy patterns of baseline policies (Fig. 5). This indicated greater stability and reduced error accumulation over time.
- **GPC with Superposition:** Experiments with logical AND and OR operators showed substantial performance boosts (e.g., +25.73% SR for DP+MP with Logical AND). However, these operators have limitations, such as non-applicability to flow-based models and increased inference costs due to per-step weight recomputation.
- **Heterogeneous Inference Settings:** GPC demonstrated flexibility in combining policies with different inference steps and action chunk lengths, yielding clear gains even in such mismatched configurations (Table 8).
- **Multiple Base Policies:** GPC successfully extended to three base policies, matching or improving upon the best two-policy configuration and consistently outperforming individual policies, indicating its scalability beyond pairwise composition (Table 9).

In summary, the results consistently validate GPC as an effective, versatile, and computationally efficient method for enhancing robot control policies by intelligently combining existing pre-trained models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents General Policy Composition (GPC) as a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, particularly for diffusion-based and flow-based control policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Paradigm for Policy Enhancement:** GPC introduces an alternative approach to improving robot policy performance by leveraging pre-trained models through test-time distribution-level composition. This moves beyond traditional methods that rely on extensive data collection, model retraining, or complex online reinforcement learning, which are often resource-intensive.
- **Strong Theoretical Foundation:** The establishment of a mathematical justification for convex score composition is a key aspect of this work. Proving that convex combinations of scores can reduce estimation error at each step and that this improvement propagates to entire trajectories provides a principled basis for the method's effectiveness. This theoretical backing enhances confidence in the approach and offers a framework for future analyses in compositional policy learning.
- **Versatility and General Applicability:** GPC's plug-and-play nature allows for the flexible combination of heterogeneous policies, irrespective of their generative mode (diffusion or flow), input modalities (VA or VLA, RGB or point cloud), or underlying network architectures. This broad applicability means that GPC can be used with a wide range of existing and future policies, maximizing the utility of pre-trained models.
- **Resource Efficiency:** By being entirely training-free, GPC offers a cost-effective solution for improving policy performance. It circumvents the need for expensive interaction datasets, prolonged training cycles, or complex reward engineering, which are major bottlenecks in real-world robotics development. The modest computational overhead at inference time, coupled with efficient test-time weight searching, makes it a practical alternative for deployment.
- **Consistent Performance Gains:** The extensive empirical validation, encompassing diverse simulated benchmarks (Robomimic, PushT, RoboTwin) and real-world robotic tasks, consistently demonstrates that GPC leads to notable and reliable performance improvements. These gains, often exceeding those of the best individual policies, confirm the practical utility of the method.
- **Insights into Policy Interaction:** The detailed analysis of weight configurations and alternative composition operators (Logical AND/OR) provides valuable insights into how different policies contribute to a composite behavior. Understanding when and why certain combinations are effective, and how task-dependent weighting impacts performance, guides both the application of GPC and future research directions in policy composition.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for further exploration. These include developing more adaptive and automated strategies for weight search, scaling GPC to compose many policies efficiently (e.g., through feature sharing or compact representations), and designing novel composition operators that can further amplify policy performance, potentially extending to non-diffusion-based policy classes and other sequential decision-making domains like vision-language-navigation.

In conclusion, GPC represents a simple yet effective method that advances robotic control by enabling the synergistic combination of existing policies. Its theoretical grounding, practical efficiency, and demonstrated performance gains position it as a valuable tool for enhancing the capabilities of autonomous systems and fostering new research into modular and compositional approaches to robot learning.
