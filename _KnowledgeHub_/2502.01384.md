# Fine-Tuning Discrete Diffusion Models with Policy Gradient Methods

> [!summary] Summary
> Score Entropy Policy Optimization (SEPO) offers an efficient, theoretically grounded algorithm for fine-tuning discrete diffusion models using policy gradient methods, capable of handling non-differentiable rewards. It demonstrated superior generation quality and stability, achieving state-of-the-art results in DNA sequence design and improving response quality in language modeling tasks.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning discrete diffusion models is difficult, particularly when optimizing for non-differentiable rewards encountered in real-world applications like human feedback or scientific discovery.
- Existing fine-tuning methods for discrete diffusion models face scalability issues, computational intractability, or are restricted to specific model architectures or differentiable reward functions.
- Policy gradient estimation for discrete diffusion models suffers from high variance due to non-differentiable discrete sampling and the intractability of marginal distributions in large state spaces.

## Method

- **SEPO** introduces an explicit characterization of policy gradients for discrete diffusion models, leveraging the ==concrete score== framework to handle non-differentiable rewards.
- It employs the Discrete REINFORCE trick combined with Self-Normalized Importance Sampling (SNIS) to reduce variance in gradient estimation for the intractable marginal distribution.
- The algorithm incorporates ideas from Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), using clipped importance sampling to ensure stable and controlled policy updates during fine-tuning.

## Results

- SEPO achieved state-of-the-art performance in DNA sequence generation, yielding a Pred-Activity of 7.55 and ATAC-Acc of **99.5%**, with a Gradient Flow enhanced version reaching 7.64 Pred-Activity and **99.9%** ATAC-Acc.
- Fine-tuned discrete diffusion models (SEDD-SEPO) significantly improved language model response quality compared to the pretrained vanilla model, as evaluated by a GPT-3.5 Turbo judge on diverse prompts.
- The method demonstrated significantly lower run-to-run variance and higher stability across DNA sequence modeling experiments, consistently maintaining high predicted activity scores even in worst-case scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy gradients for discrete diffusion models can be formulated and efficiently estimated for non-differentiable rewards by adapting existing reinforcement learning techniques within the concrete score framework.
> - Self-Normalized Importance Sampling (SNIS) is crucial for stabilizing gradient estimates by effectively addressing the high variance caused by sampling from intractable discrete distributions.
> - Integrating trust region-like mechanisms, such as clipped importance sampling, is vital for achieving stable and effective policy optimization in discrete diffusion models, preventing overshooting during updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on "Fine-Tuning Discrete Diffusion Models with Policy Gradient Methods" is situated at the intersection of generative modeling, discrete data analysis, and reinforcement learning. Diffusion models have revolutionized continuous generative tasks like image and video generation. More recently, there has been a significant push to extend these powerful models to discrete state spaces, enabling their application in areas such as language modeling and biological sequence generation (e.g., DNA, proteins). These discrete diffusion models have shown promise in being competitive with established autoregressive models like GPT and Llama, offering greater flexibility in generation.

However, a critical challenge in advancing discrete diffusion models lies in their effective fine-tuning, especially to align their output with specific, often complex, objectives or human preferences. Traditional fine-tuning strategies, such as classifier guidance or steering, frequently encounter scalability issues or computational intractability. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which optimizes a pre-trained model based on reward signals, is a powerful paradigm for this alignment task. Yet, applying standard policy gradient methods within RL to discrete diffusion models is complicated by two main factors:
1. **Non-differentiability of discrete sampling:** Sampling from categorical distributions, inherent in discrete models, is a non-differentiable operation, making direct gradient-based optimization difficult.
2. **Reward function characteristics:** Real-world reward functions (e.g., human preferences, biological scores) are often non-differentiable with respect to the model's parameters or even the generated output's continuous embeddings.

Previous attempts to address these challenges have had limitations. For instance, methods relying on the Gumbel-Softmax trick (Wang et al., 2025) require differentiable rewards and can be memory-intensive at scale. Other policy gradient approaches, like GLIDE (Cao et al., 2025) and d1 (Zhao et al., 2025), are often tailored to specific setups, such as relying on reward shaping or being restricted to masked diffusion models, thereby limiting their generality (e.g., d1 cannot handle unconditional generation).

SEPO distinguishes itself by offering a **unified, scalable, and theoretically justified policy gradient algorithm** that directly tackles these limitations. It explicitly handles non-differentiable rewards, which is a significant advantage over Gumbel-Softmax-based methods. Furthermore, its design accommodates both conditional and unconditional generation, providing a more general framework compared to prior specialized RL approaches for discrete diffusion. By adapting established policy gradient techniques (like PPO and GRPO) to the discrete diffusion setting, SEPO aims to provide a robust and broadly applicable solution for aligning these generative models with diverse and challenging objectives, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in language and biomolecular design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, broadly applicable, and theoretically justified policy gradient algorithm for fine-tuning discrete diffusion models. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and the need to address key limitations in the current landscape of discrete generative modeling:

1. **Enabling Fine-tuning with Non-differentiable Rewards:** A central motivation is to overcome the challenge of optimizing discrete diffusion models using rewards that are not differentiable with respect to the model's parameters or output embeddings. Many real-world applications, particularly in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or certain scientific discovery tasks (e.g., designing proteins with desired properties evaluated by an oracle), rely on such non-differentiable reward functions. Existing gradient-based methods often fall short in these scenarios.
2. **Addressing Scalability and Efficiency Issues:** Current fine-tuning strategies for discrete diffusion models, including classifier guidance, steering, and some RL-based approaches (e.g., Gumbel-Softmax variants), often face scalability bottlenecks or become computationally intractable when applied to complex discrete structures or large models. The authors are motivated to propose a method that is both efficient and scalable, making it practical for real-world applications.
3. **Providing a Unified and General Framework:** Many existing policy gradient approaches for diffusion models are either designed for continuous state spaces or are highly specialized for particular discrete diffusion architectures or generation types (e.g., only masked diffusion, or only conditional generation). A key motivation is to develop a more general framework, SEPO, that can be applied consistently across different discrete diffusion model architectures and supports both conditional and unconditional generation tasks.
4. **Leveraging the Concrete Score Framework:** The research aims to explicitly characterize policy gradient algorithms within the concrete score framework, which is a recent development for continuous-time discrete diffusion models. This theoretical foundation is crucial for justifying the proposed algorithms and ensuring their robustness.
5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance and Stability:** Ultimately, the motivation is to develop an algorithm that not only addresses the theoretical and practical challenges but also demonstrates superior performance and stability in empirical evaluations across diverse discrete generative tasks, thereby establishing a new benchmark for fine-tuning discrete diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEPO's methodology is built upon a careful adaptation of policy gradient methods to the unique characteristics of discrete diffusion models operating within the concrete score framework. The approach addresses the core challenges of non-differentiable sampling and high-variance gradient estimation.

#### 3.1. Policy Gradients for Concrete Score

The foundation of SEPO lies in deriving an explicit characterization of policy gradients for discrete diffusion models. The paper leverages the "concrete score framework," where the score function `s_θ(x, t)_y` approximates the ratio of probabilities `p_t(y)/p_t(x)`.

- **Discrete REINFORCE Trick (Theorem 3.1):** The first step is to derive a gradient estimator for the loss function `ℓ_t(θ)` (which involves maximizing a reward `R_t(x)` and optionally a KL divergence term). This "Discrete REINFORCE trick" expresses `d∇ℓ_t(θ)` in terms of `q_θ_t(x)R_t(x) Σ_y q_θ_t(y) ∇_θ log s_θ(x, T-t)_y`. A critical aspect here is that this formulation allows for **non-differentiable rewards** `R_t(x)`, which is a major advantage.
- **Challenge with `q_θ_t(y)` Estimation:** A direct computation of `q_θ_t(y)` (the marginal distribution of `y`) within the sum is intractable due to the enormous state space `X`. Naive Monte Carlo sampling for this term leads to extremely high variance because samples `x_i` are unlikely to be neighbors of `y` simultaneously (Fig. 1).
- **Solution: Self-Normalized Importance Sampling (SNIS):** To overcome the high-variance issue, SEPO introduces SNIS for estimating `q_θ_t(y)`. Instead of directly sampling `y` from `q_θ_t`, it samples `M` neighbors `z_i` from `q_θ_t(·|y)` and uses an importance sampling approximation. By leveraging the relationship between `q_θ_t(z_i)/q_θ_t(z_i|y)` and the score, the SNIS estimator for `q_θ_t(y)` simplifies to the harmonic mean of the conditional probabilities: `(1/M * Σ_i 1/q_θ_t(y|z_i))^-1`. This method significantly reduces variance to `O(M^-1)`.
- **Challenge with REINFORCE Variance:** Even with SNIS, the base REINFORCE algorithm can suffer from high variance in the "outer sum" (over `x`) and does not naturally restrict large policy updates.
- **Solution: Importance Sampling of the Outer Sum (TRPO-inspired):** To enhance stability and control policy updates, SEPO incorporates ideas from Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It introduces importance sampling by sampling from an "old" policy `q_θ_old_t` and clips the ratio `q_θ_t(x)/q_θ_old_t(x)` to an interval `[1-ϵ, 1+ϵ]`. This clipping acts as a trust region, analogous to controlling the KL divergence between old and new policies, ensuring more stable training. The final gradient form `d∇ℓ_t(θ)` then involves an expected value over `x~q_θ_old_t` of `R_t(x)h(x,θ)`, where `h(x,θ)` incorporates `q_θ_t(y)` (estimated via SNIS) and the clipped ratio.

#### 3.2. Score Entropy Policy Optimization (SEPO)

The SEPO algorithm (Algorithm 1) is an iterative framework that integrates these components:
1. **Initialization:** Set current and old policy parameters (`θ` and `θ_old`) to the pretrained `θ_pre`.
2. **Sampling:** In each iteration, samples are generated from the "old" policy `π_θ_old` using the continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) `Q_θ_old`. This can be done efficiently in `O(1)` time using techniques like the queuing trick.
3. **Reward and Advantage Computation:** For the generated samples, rewards `R_t(x)` are computed. To further reduce variance and improve learning, an advantage function `A(x)` is calculated. This can be done using a learned value network (as in PPO, where `A(x) = R(x) - V(x)`) or by standardizing rewards within groups (as in GRPO, `A(x) = (R(x) - mean(R(x)))/std(R(x))`).
4. **Optimization:** The model parameters `θ_s` are updated for `K` epochs by optimizing the policy loss function `ℓ_A(θ)`. This loss is constructed from the REINFORCE-like gradient, incorporating the SNIS estimates for `q_θ_t(y)` and the clipped ratios `u_t_x,y` (which define the function `f` in the weight `w_x,y` of Eq. 9). An optional KL regularization term `KL(q_θ_t || q_θ_pre_t)` can also be added.
5. **Policy Update:** After optimization, `θ_old` is updated to `θ_s` for the next iteration.

#### 3.3. Sampling through Gradient Flow (Optional Enhancement)

SEPO also explores an alternative sampling strategy to potentially improve sample quality, interpreting the generation process as a gradient flow.
- **Gradient Flow Interpretation:** Lemma 3.3 shows that sampling from a specific time-homogeneous CTMC (rate matrix `Q_c_t = Q_t + Q_t`, where `Q_t` is the time-reversal of `Q_t`) implements a gradient flow for the KL divergence between the current distribution and the target distribution in a Wasserstein-like metric space.
- **Corrector Steps:** In practice, this means adding "corrector steps" during the denoising process using the combined rate matrix `Q_c_θ_t = Q_t + Q_θ_t`. This is known to improve sample quality in discrete diffusion models, albeit at a higher computational cost.
- **Gradient Computation:** When using this gradient flow interpretation for sampling, the gradient `∇_θ π_θ` is obtained by solving a linear system (Proposition 3.4) using the implicit function theorem. While potentially more accurate, this method is generally more complex computationally, though the inverse of the matrix involved can be explicitly computed using the Sherman–Morrison formula.

#### 3.4. Convergence Bounds

The paper provides theoretical convergence guarantees for Algorithm 1, treating it as a system of coupled continuous-time differential equations. Theorem 3.5 demonstrates that, under mild assumptions, the average objective gradients `(1/S) ∫_0^S ||∇ℓ_A(θ_s)||^2 ds` converge to zero at a rate of `O(1/√S)` as the number of iterations `S` goes to infinity, providing confidence in the algorithm's learning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SEPO was evaluated across two distinct discrete generative tasks: language modeling and DNA sequence modeling, demonstrating its broad applicability and effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Language Modeling

- **Setup:** SEPO was used to fine-tune SEDD Medium Absorb (a discrete diffusion model with 320M parameters) pretrained on OpenWebText. The fine-tuning was performed in an Actor-Critic PPO framework, with a reward model built from GPT-2 architecture and trained on the HH-RLHF dataset. Two variants were trained: SEDD-SEPO-128 (128 denoising steps) and SEDD-SEPO-1024 (1024 denoising steps).
- **Evaluation:** Models were evaluated using pairwise comparisons by GPT-3.5 Turbo as a "Judge LLM" on 153 prompts from the Awesome ChatGPT Prompts dataset.
- **Results:**
 - **Performance Improvement:** Both SEDD-SEPO variants significantly outperformed the pretrained SEDD Vanilla model, indicating the strong benefits of RL fine-tuning.
 - **Impact of Denoising Steps:** Increasing the number of denoising steps from 128 to 1024 generally improved response quality, with SEDD-SEPO-1024 achieving the best overall results. This aligns with prior observations that more denoising steps lead to better generation quality in diffusion models.
 - **Training-Evaluation Alignment:** Models performed best when evaluated with the number of denoising steps they were trained with, suggesting that fine-tuning optimizes for a specific generation fidelity associated with the number of steps.
 - **Qualitative Observations:** While SEPO improved coherence and structure compared to the vanilla model, the RLHF training on conversational data (HH-RLHF) sometimes led to outputs that continued the prompt in a dialogue style rather than directly answering, especially when an explicit Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) stage was skipped.

#### 4.2. DNA Sequence Modeling

- **Setup:** The task involved unconditional generation of 200 bp regulatory DNA sequences, following the setup of Wang et al. (2025). A masked discrete diffusion model, pretrained on the Gosai dataset, was fine-tuned to maximize enhancer activity in HepG2 cell lines using a continuous reward oracle.
- **Baselines:** SEPO was compared against a diverse set of state-of-the-art methods: guidance-based (Classifier Guidance (CG), Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC), Tree-Diffusion-Sampler (TDS), Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)), and other RL-based (DRAKES, GLID2E).
- **Metrics:** Pred-Activity (median predicted enhancer activity from a held-out oracle), ATAC-Acc (binary chromatin accessibility score), 3-mer Corr (correlation of 3-mer frequencies), and Log-Lik (log-likelihood under the pretrained model).
- **Results:**
 - **State-of-the-Art Performance:** SEPO achieved the highest performance across all key functional metrics: Pred-Activity (7.55 vs. previous best GLID2E 7.35) and ATAC-Acc (99.5% vs. previous best DRAKES 92.5%).
 - **Gradient Flow Enhancement:** SEPO with Gradient Flow (GF) achieved even higher Pred-Activity (7.64) and near-perfect ATAC-Acc (99.9%), demonstrating the value of incorporating corrector steps for improved sample quality.
 - **Stability:** SEPO exhibited significantly lower run-to-run variance (smaller standard deviations in Table 2) compared to all baselines, highlighting the stability and reliability of its optimization process and generated sequences. Even in worst-case scenarios over 640 samples, SEPO methods maintained a minimum Pred-Activity score around 6, while other methods dropped to 0-2.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Although SEPO's per-epoch cost is higher due to SNIS (a batched forward pass for transition probabilities), it achieves higher final performance with substantially fewer epochs. This results in competitive total wall-clock runtime, demonstrating that the variance reduction provided by SNIS effectively trades a moderate increase in per-epoch cost for overall efficiency and stability.
 - **Ablation Study (SEPO w/o SNIS):** Removing the Self-Normalized Importance Sampling (SNIS) component led to a significant performance degradation (e.g., Pred-Activity 4.66, ATAC-Acc 32.7%). This strongly validates the necessity of SNIS for achieving stable and effective gradient estimates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Score Entropy Policy Optimization (SEPO) represents a significant advancement in the field of discrete generative modeling, particularly for fine-tuning diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Bridging the Gap for Non-differentiable Rewards:** SEPO's most critical contribution is its ability to efficiently fine-tune discrete diffusion models using non-differentiable rewards. This capability is paramount for real-world applications such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for large language models, where human preferences are inherently non-differentiable, or in scientific domains like biomolecular design, where functional properties are often evaluated by non-differentiable oracle models or experimental assays. By enabling direct optimization with these complex rewards, SEPO broadens the applicability of discrete diffusion models to a wider array of challenging alignment tasks.

2. **Generality and Scalability:** Unlike many specialized policy gradient methods, SEPO offers a unified and scalable framework that supports both conditional and unconditional generation. This generality means it can be applied to diverse tasks, from generating coherent text given a prompt to creating novel biological sequences without any initial conditioning signal. Its demonstrated scalability on language and DNA tasks suggests its potential for even larger and more complex discrete generative problems.

3. **Enhanced Stability and Performance:** The empirical results, particularly in DNA sequence modeling, demonstrate that SEPO achieves state-of-the-art performance, surpassing existing guidance-based and RL-based fine-tuning methods. Crucially, SEPO exhibits significantly lower run-to-run variance, indicating a more stable and reliable optimization process. This stability is invaluable in applications where consistent high-quality generation is a priority, reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning or multiple training runs. The "gradient flow" extension further boosts sample quality, pushing performance even higher.

4. **Strong Theoretical Foundation:** The paper provides a rigorous theoretical justification for SEPO, including an explicit characterization of policy gradients for discrete diffusion models in the concrete score framework and convergence bounds. This theoretical underpinning instills confidence in the algorithm's design and provides a basis for future research.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancing RLHF for Discrete Models:** SEPO could significantly accelerate the development of more aligned and capable large language models and other discrete generative AI, enabling them to better understand and fulfill complex user instructions or preferences.
- **Revolutionizing Biomolecular Design:** In computational biology, SEPO opens new avenues for designing novel DNA sequences, proteins, or other biomolecules with desired functional properties. The ability to directly optimize for non-differentiable metrics (e.g., binding affinity, stability, drug efficacy) could lead to breakthroughs in drug discovery, materials science, and synthetic biology.
- **Broader Applications in Structured Generative Modeling:** Beyond language and biology, SEPO's principles could be extended to other structured discrete data generation tasks, such as code synthesis, graph generation, or symbolic reasoning, wherever non-differentiable evaluation metrics are prevalent.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge a limitation: the inner importance sampling steps (SNIS) introduce additional computational complexity, as resampling is performed for each neighbor of the outer Monte Carlo samples. Future work will likely focus on further refining gradient estimation techniques to mitigate this overhead and exploring a broader range of applications in structured generative modeling. Despite this, SEPO represents a robust and highly impactful contribution to the field, offering a principled solution to a fundamental challenge in discrete generative AI.
