# Fine-Tuning Discrete Diffusion Models with Policy Gradient Methods

> [!summary] Summary
> Score Entropy Policy Optimization (SEPO) offers an efficient, theoretically grounded algorithm for fine-tuning discrete diffusion models using policy gradient methods, capable of handling non-differentiable rewards. It demonstrated superior generation quality and stability, achieving state-of-the-art results in DNA sequence design and improving response quality in language modeling tasks.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning discrete diffusion models is difficult, particularly when optimizing for non-differentiable rewards encountered in real-world applications like human feedback or scientific discovery.
- Existing fine-tuning methods for discrete diffusion models face scalability issues, computational intractability, or are restricted to specific model architectures or differentiable reward functions.
- Policy gradient estimation for discrete diffusion models suffers from high variance due to non-differentiable discrete sampling and the intractability of marginal distributions in large state spaces.

## Method

- **SEPO** introduces an explicit characterization of policy gradients for discrete diffusion models, leveraging the ==concrete score== framework to handle non-differentiable rewards.
- It employs the Discrete REINFORCE trick combined with Self-Normalized Importance Sampling (SNIS) to reduce variance in gradient estimation for the intractable marginal distribution.
- The algorithm incorporates ideas from Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), using clipped importance sampling to ensure stable and controlled policy updates during fine-tuning.

## Results

- SEPO achieved state-of-the-art performance in DNA sequence generation, yielding a Pred-Activity of 7.55 and ATAC-Acc of **99.5%**, with a Gradient Flow enhanced version reaching 7.64 Pred-Activity and **99.9%** ATAC-Acc.
- Fine-tuned discrete diffusion models (SEDD-SEPO) significantly improved language model response quality compared to the pretrained vanilla model, as evaluated by a GPT-3.5 Turbo judge on diverse prompts.
- The method demonstrated significantly lower run-to-run variance and higher stability across DNA sequence modeling experiments, consistently maintaining high predicted activity scores even in worst-case scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy gradients for discrete diffusion models can be formulated and efficiently estimated for non-differentiable rewards by adapting existing reinforcement learning techniques within the concrete score framework.
> - Self-Normalized Importance Sampling (SNIS) is crucial for stabilizing gradient estimates by effectively addressing the high variance caused by sampling from intractable discrete distributions.
> - Integrating trust region-like mechanisms, such as clipped importance sampling, is vital for achieving stable and effective policy optimization in discrete diffusion models, preventing overshooting during updates.
