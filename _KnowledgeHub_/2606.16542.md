# ADAPT: Analytical Disturbance-Aware Policy Training for Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> The ADAPT framework integrates an analytical disturbance observer with reinforcement learning policies to enhance humanoid locomotion, enabling robust and adaptive responses to external perturbations. This approach, developed by researchers at Nanyang Technological University, improved control performance under various disturbances and facilitated emergent adaptive behaviors like postural adjustments for asymmetric loads, while also allowing for reward shaping to achieve 'lighter' locomotion.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to maintain locomotion accuracy and stability when encountering unexpected external disturbances during force-interactive tasks.
- Existing learning-based control methods often lack robustness or generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution forces, or require extensive task-specific designs.
- Inferring whole-body external forces is challenging due to limited on-board force/torque sensors and the noise inherent in direct acceleration measurements.

## Method

![[2606.16542-method.png]]
*The ADAPT framework integrates an analytical disturbance observer with an RL policy. The observer estimates residual forces in real-time, allowing the policy to resist pushes, compensate for unbalanced loads, and adopt lighter gaits.*

- A momentum-based, first-order ==analytical disturbance observer== estimates whole-body residual forces and torques online, using only proprioceptive state, control commands, and nominal dynamics.
- The estimated, effort-scaled disturbances are integrated as a new observation modality into a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) trained whole-body locomotion policy.
- The disturbance estimates are utilized for reward shaping, allowing the policy to learn specific behaviors, such as a 'light-step' reward to reduce foot-ground impact.

## Results

- The disturbance observer accurately tracked known injected disturbances in simulation and reliably captured the onset and relative magnitudes of perturbations on real hardware.
- The ADAPT policy reduced forward velocity tracking error, lateral drift, and yaw drift under torso pulling forces (up to 60 N) and successfully compensated for asymmetric hand loads (up to 4 kg), outperforming a proprioception-only baseline.
- Reward shaping with disturbance estimates led to a consistently lower disturbance residual envelope during walking, indicating reduced foot-ground impact and resulting in an emergent 'tiptoeing gait'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a physically grounded, analytical disturbance observer directly into the observation space of an RL policy significantly enhances its robustness and generalization to diverse external perturbations.
> - Normalizing disturbance observations relative to physical parameters (e.g., robot mass, joint actuation limits) provides a robust and physically interpretable signal for the policy.
> - The analytical disturbance estimates are versatile, serving both as direct policy observations for reactive control and as effective signals for reward shaping to influence specific locomotion styles and contact transitions.
