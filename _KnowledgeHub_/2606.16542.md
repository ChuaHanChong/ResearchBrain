# ADAPT: Analytical Disturbance-Aware Policy Training for Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> The ADAPT framework integrates an analytical disturbance observer with reinforcement learning policies to enhance humanoid locomotion, enabling robust and adaptive responses to external perturbations. This approach, developed by researchers at Nanyang Technological University, improved control performance under various disturbances and facilitated emergent adaptive behaviors like postural adjustments for asymmetric loads, while also allowing for reward shaping to achieve 'lighter' locomotion.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to maintain locomotion accuracy and stability when encountering unexpected external disturbances during force-interactive tasks.
- Existing learning-based control methods often lack robustness or generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution forces, or require extensive task-specific designs.
- Inferring whole-body external forces is challenging due to limited on-board force/torque sensors and the noise inherent in direct acceleration measurements.

## Method

![[2606.16542-method.png]]
*The ADAPT framework integrates an analytical disturbance observer with an RL policy. The observer estimates residual forces in real-time, allowing the policy to resist pushes, compensate for unbalanced loads, and adopt lighter gaits.*

- A momentum-based, first-order ==analytical disturbance observer== estimates whole-body residual forces and torques online, using only proprioceptive state, control commands, and nominal dynamics.
- The estimated, effort-scaled disturbances are integrated as a new observation modality into a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) trained whole-body locomotion policy.
- The disturbance estimates are utilized for reward shaping, allowing the policy to learn specific behaviors, such as a 'light-step' reward to reduce foot-ground impact.

## Results

- The disturbance observer accurately tracked known injected disturbances in simulation and reliably captured the onset and relative magnitudes of perturbations on real hardware.
- The ADAPT policy reduced forward velocity tracking error, lateral drift, and yaw drift under torso pulling forces (up to 60 N) and successfully compensated for asymmetric hand loads (up to 4 kg), outperforming a proprioception-only baseline.
- Reward shaping with disturbance estimates led to a consistently lower disturbance residual envelope during walking, indicating reduced foot-ground impact and resulting in an emergent 'tiptoeing gait'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a physically grounded, analytical disturbance observer directly into the observation space of an RL policy significantly enhances its robustness and generalization to diverse external perturbations.
> - Normalizing disturbance observations relative to physical parameters (e.g., robot mass, joint actuation limits) provides a robust and physically interpretable signal for the policy.
> - The analytical disturbance estimates are versatile, serving both as direct policy observations for reactive control and as effective signals for reward shaping to influence specific locomotion styles and contact transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid locomotion control has experienced advancements through learning-based approaches, enabling robots to navigate diverse environments. However, a significant challenge arises when humanoids engage in force-interactive tasks, such as carrying payloads, pushing objects, or interacting with humans. These interactions introduce unexpected external disturbances that can compromise the robot's locomotion accuracy and stability.

Existing learning-based strategies for managing these contact-rich scenarios generally fall into three categories:
1. **Domain Randomization:** Policies are trained by exposing them to randomized external forces and internal dynamics variations in simulation. While this improves sim-to-real robustness, it can sometimes compromise tracking accuracy.
2. **Task-Specific Force Objectives:** Force perception is explicitly integrated into loco-manipulation training to achieve specific objectives, such as exerting desired forces or producing compliant responses. These methods often require task-specific design and may not generalize broadly.
3. **Learning-Based Force Estimators:** Interaction forces are inferred from proprioceptive histories, and these estimated signals are fed to the controller. However, policies relying solely on learned estimators may exhibit poor generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) disturbances.

This work addresses the limitations of existing methods by proposing a disturbance-aware strategy that is designed for broader generalization and robustness. It builds upon the concept of physics-informed learning, where prior physical knowledge of the robot is leveraged to enhance policy learning. While analytical disturbance estimation has a history in classical robot control, its direct integration into modern Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based humanoid policies as an explicit observation modality has remained an area for further exploration. This paper aims to bridge this gap by incorporating a physically grounded disturbance observer into the RL training pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to equip humanoid locomotion policies with an enhanced capability to handle external disturbances reliably and robustly across diverse, potentially unseen, scenarios. The authors are motivated by the observation that humanoids operating in human-centered environments inevitably encounter unpredictable physical interactions that disrupt their motion.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Improving Robustness to External Disturbances:** Current learning-based methods face challenges in maintaining locomotion accuracy and stability when subjected to unexpected forces. The goal is to develop a framework that allows humanoids to effectively resist, adapt to, and compensate for such disturbances.
- **Enhancing Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Forces:** Existing techniques often struggle when encountering disturbances that significantly deviate from the training distribution. The authors aim for a solution that leverages fundamental physical principles to generalize beyond specific training data, thereby improving performance in novel interactive tasks.
- **Providing a Physically Grounded Disturbance Awareness:** Instead of relying on data-driven inference of external forces solely from motion histories, the research seeks to provide the policy with an explicit, physics-derived "sense" of external force/torque. This is intended to offer a more structured and reliable perception channel for interaction.
- **Minimally Policy-Invasive Strategy:** The objective is to augment existing RL-based control frameworks with disturbance awareness in a manner that requires minimal modification to the core policy architecture, primarily by enhancing its observation space.
- **Enabling Task-Specific Behavior through Reward Shaping:** Beyond direct policy input, the estimated disturbance signal is intended to be versatile enough to enable the formulation of contact-aware reward functions, thereby encouraging emergent behaviors such as lighter, lower-impact locomotion.
- **Addressing the Challenge of Whole-Body Disturbance Estimation without Force/Torque Sensors:** Humanoids often lack extensive whole-body force/torque sensing. The research aims to develop an analytical disturbance observer that can estimate residual force/torque online using only accessible robot dynamics and proprioceptive information.

By addressing these objectives, the authors seek to advance the reliability and adaptability of humanoids for real-world deployment in complex, interactive environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ADAPT (Analytical Disturbance-Aware Policy Training) is a framework designed to integrate a physically grounded disturbance observer into RL-based humanoid control. The methodology consists of two primary modules: an analytical disturbance estimator and an RL-based whole-body controller.

#### 3.1. Whole-body Disturbance Observer

The core of ADAPT is an analytical disturbance observer that estimates residual forces and torques acting on the robot.
- **Problem Formulation:** The whole-body dynamics of a humanoid are expressed by the equation $M(q)\dot{v} + C(q, v)v + g(q) = \tau_m + \tau_e$, where $M(q)$ is the inertia matrix, $C(q, v)v$ represents Coriolis and centrifugal terms, $g(q)$ is gravity, $\tau_m$ are motor torques, and $\tau_e$ is the generalized disturbance. Direct measurement of $\tau_e$ and generalized acceleration $\dot{v}$ is challenging due to sensor limitations and noise.
- **Momentum-Based Observer:** To circumvent the need for noisy acceleration measurements, the observer is derived using the generalized momentum $p = M(q)v$. Differentiating $p$ and substituting the dynamics equation leads to $\tau_e = \dot{p} - \beta(q, v) - \tau_m$, where $\beta(q, v) = C^\top(q, v)v - g(q)$. A first-order observer is then formulated for the estimated disturbance $\hat{\tau}_e$: $\dot{\hat{\tau}}_e = K_o(\tau_e - \hat{\tau}_e)$. By introducing an auxiliary state $z = \hat{\tau}_e - K_o p$, the observer is expressed as:
 $\dot{z} = -K_o(\beta + \tau_m + \hat{\tau}_e)$
 $\hat{\tau}_e = z + K_o p$
 This formulation requires only accessible signals: current proprioceptive state, control command, and nominal dynamics terms.
- **Nominal Dynamics and Approximation:** The nominal inertia matrix $M(q)$ and bias terms are obtained from a synchronized MuJoCo model for efficiency. The term $\beta(q, v)$ is approximated using the negative MuJoCo bias force, $-\text{q}_{\text{bias}}$, where $\text{q}_{\text{bias}} = C(q, v)v + g(q)$. This approximation introduces a velocity-dependent error, which the authors show to be negligible in practice.
- **Observer Gain and Filtering:** A positive-definite diagonal observer gain $K_o$ is used, balancing convergence speed and noise amplification. A low-pass filter is applied to the raw estimate $\hat{\tau}_e$ to enhance robustness, particularly on real hardware.
- **Output Representation:** The estimated residual $\bar{\tau}_e$ is composed of linear and angular residuals for the floating base (in the root frame) and residuals for each joint (e.g., $\bar{\tau}_e = [\bar{\tau}^\text{lin}_e, \bar{\tau}^\text{ang}_e, \bar{\tau}^\text{jnt}_e]$). Gravity bias in the linear floating-base residual is removed, and the residual is rotated into the root frame before being fed to the policy.

#### 3.2. Disturbance-Aware Policy

The RL policy uses a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) as a whole-body controller, trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Actor-Critic Architecture:** An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is employed. The actor receives a 554-dimensional observation, including a five-step history of base velocities, projected gravity, joint positions and velocities, velocity command, the previous action, and crucially, the effort-scaled disturbance estimation $\rho_k$. The critic receives these observations plus additional privileged foot-contact information (foot height, air time, contact state, force) during training for value estimation.
- **Training Pipeline:** A two-stage curriculum is used:
 1. **Stage 1:** Policy acquires basic locomotion in a disturbance-free setting, with the disturbance observation still provided as input.
 2. **Stage 2:** External forces are introduced to selected bodies, and the policy learns to track velocity commands under increasing disturbance levels.
- **Disturbance Normalization:** A unified scaling is applied to the disturbance observation channels across both training stages. Specifically, linear residuals are scaled by $1/(mg)$, angular residuals by $1/(mgl_{\text{ref}})$, and joint residuals by $1/\tau^{\text{max}}_j$. This incorporates joint-dependent actuation capacity by expressing each joint's residual relative to its effort limit.
- **Reward Shaping:** Beyond direct observation feedback, the estimated disturbances can be used for reward shaping. For "lighter locomotion," a light-step reward $r^\text{light}_k$ is introduced, which penalizes high-impact lower-body residual peaks. This is calculated using a "residual envelope" based on the root-mean-square of scaled leg-joint disturbances within a rolling window, with penalties tied to the high-tail average and peak values of this envelope.

#### 3.3. Practical Considerations

- **FAST-LIO Usage:** Accurate root linear velocity is essential for the disturbance observer. Since IMU integration suffers from drift, FAST-LIO (a LiDAR-Inertial Odometry system) is used to obtain robust robot motion estimation during real-world deployment. The LiDAR velocity is transformed from the LiDAR frame to the robot's root frame, and then the relative velocity of the LiDAR with respect to the root is subtracted to yield the root linear velocity.
- **Simulation and Hardware Synchronization:** The MuJoCo model is used as a synchronized nominal dynamics model for the observer during both training and real-world deployment, not for forward simulation during deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ADAPT was evaluated through both simulation and real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid. The experiments focused on disturbance estimation accuracy, control performance under perturbations, and the benefits of disturbance-aware reward shaping.

#### 4.1. Estimation Performance of Disturbance Observer

- **Accuracy:** The disturbance observer demonstrated consistent and reliable estimation of joint-space disturbances.
 - **Simulation:** The observer closely tracked known injected disturbance torques across multiple joints, maintaining clear isolation between different joint channels.
 - **Real-world:** While exhibiting a larger estimation error compared to simulation (attributed to real-world factors like joint friction and motor modeling error), the observer effectively captured the onset and relative magnitude of applied disturbances.
- **Conclusion:** The results confirm that the observer provides a reliable force-aware signal for the policy.

#### 4.2. Control Performance of Disturbance-Aware Policy

ADAPT's policy, augmented with the estimated disturbance, was compared against a proprioception-only baseline.

- **Torso Pulling Test (Simulation):**
 - **Reduced Errors:** Under sagittal torso forces ranging from 0 N to 60 N (training maximum was 40 N), ADAPT achieved lower forward velocity tracking error, lateral drift, and yaw drift, particularly with stronger perturbations and across varying walking speeds (0.5 to 1.1 m/s).
 - **Improved Generalization:** When subjected to torso forces from different horizontal directions, ADAPT consistently outperformed the baseline for both in-distribution (40 N) and out-of-distribution (60 N) magnitudes, indicating enhanced robustness and generalization.
- **Stance Pulling Test (Real Robot):**
 - **Stronger Resistance and Postural Adjustment:** During standing, when external forces were applied to the torso and shoulder, the ADAPT-trained policy exhibited stronger resistance and actively adjusted its whole-body posture to counteract the perturbations, unlike the baseline.
- **Asymmetric Hand Loading Test (Simulation and Real Robot):**
 - **Compensation for Unbalanced Loads:** Under asymmetric loads (downward forces of 10-40 N in simulation; 1-4 kg payloads in real-world, 4 kg being OOD), ADAPT demonstrated emergent postural adjustments. It tilted the torso away from the loaded side to compensate for the moment, whereas the baseline exhibited visibly unbalanced postures and leaned towards the loaded side.
 - **Maintained Trajectory:** ADAPT maintained a straighter walking trajectory with consistently smaller lateral drift across all load magnitudes and walking speeds (0.8 and 1.1 m/s), indicating better load compensation than the baseline.
- **Conclusion:** The explicit disturbance estimation serves as an effective feedback mechanism for the policy, improving its robustness, generalization, and enabling adaptive, force-aware whole-body responses.

#### 4.3. Reward Shaping for Lighter Locomotion

The research explored the utility of the disturbance estimation for reward shaping to encourage specific behaviors.

- **Reduced Foot-Ground Impact:** By penalizing leg-joint disturbance estimates (light-step reward), ADAPT achieved a consistently lower disturbance residual envelope compared to the baseline during forward walking. This indicated lighter leg loading and reduced foot-ground impact.
- **Emergent Tiptoeing Gait:** Visual observation showed that the ADAPT-trained policy produced a "tiptoeing gait," in contrast to the "stomping gait" exhibited by the baseline, further supporting the reduction in impact forces.
- **Conclusion:** Disturbance-derived envelopes can function as effective reward-shaping signals to refine contact transitions and influence locomotion style.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ADAPT framework presents a significant advancement in humanoid locomotion control, particularly for deployment in human-centered and interactive environments. Its primary significance lies in the demonstration that a physically grounded, analytical disturbance observer can effectively augment learning-based policies.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** By providing policies with an explicit, physics-derived sense of external forces, ADAPT significantly improves robustness against unexpected disturbances. This is crucial for humanoids operating in real-world scenarios where precise environmental models are unavailable and interactions are unpredictable. The observed generalization to out-of-distribution perturbations, such as previously unseen payload magnitudes or directions, indicates a more fundamental understanding of physical interaction.
- **Physically Grounded Perception:** The analytical disturbance observer offers a reliable, sensorless method for inferring whole-body residual forces and torques. Unlike purely data-driven estimators that might struggle with novel situations, ADAPT's physics-informed approach provides a structured perception channel that is inherently designed to generalize beyond training data. This reduces reliance on extensive domain randomization, which can sometimes compromise tracking accuracy.
- **Emergent Adaptive Behaviors:** The integration of disturbance feedback leads to emergent, intelligent behaviors. Examples include the active postural adjustments to compensate for asymmetric payloads and the robust resistance to pushes during standing and walking. These behaviors arise directly from the policy conditioning its response on the estimated disturbances.
- **Versatile Policy Enhancement:** ADAPT demonstrates that analytical disturbance estimates are versatile, serving not only as direct observations for the policy but also as effective signals for reward shaping. This allows for the engineering of specific locomotion styles, such as "light-footed" walking, which could be beneficial for minimizing wear and tear, reducing noise, or for compliant interaction tasks.
- **Practical Applicability:** The methodology's reliance on accessible robot dynamics and proprioceptive state, coupled with robust root linear velocity estimation via FAST-LIO, makes it highly applicable to real-world humanoid platforms without requiring specialized force/torque sensors at every joint.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While ADAPT currently treats fast transients as a lumped disturbance, its success opens avenues for future work, such as separating fast-varying components for improved responsiveness or combining with multimodal policy backbones for more expressive disturbance-aware control.

In summary, ADAPT contributes to making humanoid robots more reliable, adaptable, and robust when performing complex, force-interactive tasks in unstructured environments, pushing closer to the goal of seamless human-robot co-existence.
