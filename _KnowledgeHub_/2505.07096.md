# X-Sim: Cross-Embodiment Learning via Real-to-Sim-to-Real

> [!summary] Summary
> A real-to-sim-to-real framework, X-SIM, enables training robot manipulation policies directly from human videos, achieving effective sim-to-real transfer through object-centric supervision and an online domain adaptation method, thereby removing the requirement for robot teleoperation data.

## Problem

- Robot learning, particularly for foundation models, faces a data scarcity problem due to the high cost and labor intensity of collecting robot-specific teleoperated demonstrations.
- Transferring skills from abundant human videos to robots is challenging because of significant kinematic, dynamic, and visual differences between human and robot embodiments.
- Policies trained exclusively in simulation often struggle with robust deployment in the real world due to the visual and physical discrepancies known as the sim-to-real gap.

## Method

- **X-SIM** transforms human RGBD videos into a photorealistic simulated environment by tracking 6D object poses with ==FoundationPose== and reconstructing the scene using ==2D Gaussian Splatting==.
- Trains a privileged-state ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== policy in simulation using ==object-centric rewards== derived from human object trajectories, then generates a large dataset of synthetic image-action pairs.
- Deploys an image-conditioned policy (==Diffusion Policy==) trained on synthetic data, enhanced by an online **Auto-Calibration** domain adaptation technique that aligns real and simulated image embeddings using a ==contrastive InfoNCE loss==.

## Results

- X-SIM significantly outperformed hand-tracking baselines, achieving over **30%** average gains in task progress in challenging cross-embodiment settings.
- The framework demonstrated strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities with RGB image observations, further enhanced by an average **8%** increase in task progress using online domain adaptation.
- X-SIM showed superior data efficiency, successfully completing tasks with only **1 minute** of human video data, compared to **10 minutes** of robot teleoperation data required for comparable performance via behavior cloning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object motion, rather than direct human actions, serves as a transferable and dense supervisory signal, effectively bridging the cross-embodiment gap for robot policy learning.
> - A photorealistic simulation reconstructed directly from human videos can be effectively leveraged as an intermediary to train robot policies without requiring any robot teleoperation data.
> - Online domain adaptation, which self-calibrates observation encoders by aligning real and simulated image embeddings, offers a robust method for sim-to-real transfer without needing real robot action labels or precise object tracking during deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "X-SIM" addresses a critical challenge in modern robot learning: the scalability of training data. Recent advancements, particularly in robot foundation models, heavily rely on massive datasets of robot-specific demonstrations, typically collected through expensive and labor-intensive teleoperation. This approach is not scalable for diverse tasks and environments, creating a bottleneck for widespread robot deployment.

In contrast, human videos (e.g., from YouTube) are abundant and offer a rich source of task demonstrations in natural settings. However, these videos lack the explicit robot action labels required by standard imitation learning algorithms. Existing approaches to bridge this "cross-embodiment" gap fall into several categories:
- **Retargeting human hand motion to robot end-effectors:** These methods often fail due to significant kinematic and dynamic differences between human hands and robot manipulators, or because human motions are simply infeasible for robots.
- **Reducing visual gaps:** Techniques like overlaying robot arms on human videos or inpainting human hands aim to make human demonstrations visually closer to robot perspectives. However, these still rely on solving inverse kinematics, which can be ill-posed for robots with different morphology.
- **Direct translation requiring paired data:** Some methods directly translate human videos to robot actions but necessitate expensive paired human-robot demonstrations, which again limits scalability.

X-SIM positions itself as a novel solution by departing from direct action-label mapping or visual retargeting. Its core insight is that **object motion**, rather than human actions, provides a dense and transferable supervisory signal. This shifts the focus from *how* the human moves to *what effect* the human produces on the environment, which is directly relevant to robot task completion regardless of embodiment differences.

The work builds upon and extends the "real-to-sim-to-real" paradigm. Prior real-to-sim methods (e.g., RialTo, ResiP) often require some form of real-world robot data or focus on improving robustness rather than learning policies from scratch. Recent works like Video2Policy and Human2Sim2Robot have explored learning from human videos within a real-to-sim-to-real framework. However, Video2Policy only extracts initial/final object states and relies on object segmentation masks at test time, while Human2Sim2Robot uses non-photorealistic simulation and requires object tracking at test time, often focusing on dexterous hands with minimal embodiment gaps. X-SIM distinguishes itself by:
- Utilizing **photorealistic simulation** reconstructed directly from human RGBD videos.
- Defining **object-centric rewards** for RL training based on continuous object trajectories.
- Generating **synthetic image-action data** for an image-conditioned policy, eliminating the need for robot teleoperation data.
- Introducing an **online domain adaptation technique** to align real and simulated observations at deployment, thus improving sim-to-real transfer without requiring object tracking at test time.

By addressing the data scarcity for robot foundation models and overcoming the limitations of previous cross-embodiment and real-to-sim-to-real approaches, X-SIM offers a highly scalable and practical pathway to leverage the vast repository of human video data for robotic policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of X-SIM is to **develop a scalable framework for training robot manipulation policies from action-less human videos without requiring any robot teleoperation data.** This overarching goal is driven by several motivations:

- **Bridging the Robot Data Scarcity Gap:** Current robot learning, especially with foundation models, is heavily reliant on large, expensive datasets of robot-specific teleoperated demonstrations. Human videos are abundant and offer a potentially limitless source of training data, but their direct use is hindered by the lack of explicit robot action labels and the significant embodiment differences between humans and robots.
- **Overcoming Embodiment Mismatch:** Existing methods attempting to transfer skills from human videos often struggle when human kinematics and dynamics are not directly transferable to a robot. X-SIM seeks to bypass this "embodiment gap" by focusing on the *effect* of human actions (object motion) rather than the *actions themselves*.
- **Enabling Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While simulation offers a safe and scalable environment for training, transferring policies to the real world remains a significant challenge due to the "sim-to-real gap." X-SIM aims to achieve robust real-world deployment of policies trained entirely in simulation.
- **Improving Data Efficiency:** The framework intends to reduce the time and resources needed for data collection, allowing for more efficient training of robot policies compared to traditional behavior cloning methods that require extensive robot teleoperation.
- **Enhancing Generalization:** The goal is to develop policies that are robust to variations in environment, camera viewpoints, and other test-time changes, a crucial aspect for deploying robots in unstructured real-world settings.

In essence, X-SIM aims to unlock the potential of human videos for robot learning by providing a complete pipeline that handles the transition from human observation to robot action, from real-world video to simulated training, and back to real-world deployment, all while maximizing scalability and minimizing human intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

X-SIM proposes a "real-to-sim-to-real" framework structured into three main stages: (1) Real-to-Sim Transfer from Human Videos, (2) Generating Robot Actions in Simulation, and (3) Sim-to-Real Transfer of Image-Based Policies.

#### 3.1. Real-to-Sim Transfer from Human Videos

This stage converts human RGBD videos into a photorealistic simulated environment with defined object trajectories.
- **Object Pose Tracking:** High-fidelity 3D meshes of manipulated objects are first obtained (e.g., using a 3D scanning app). The paper then employs **FoundationPose** to track the 6D (position and rotation) poses of these objects throughout the human video. This process starts with a 2D mask of objects in the first frame, generated by Segment Anything (SAM). The output is a trajectory of object poses, $s_H = \{s_t^H\}_{t=1}^T$.
- **Environment Reconstruction:** A geometrically accurate and photorealistic environment mesh is constructed using **2D Gaussian Splatting** from multi-view images of the scene. This reconstructed environment, along with the tracked object states, is then imported into the ManiSkill simulator. Physical properties are initially set to default values, with compatibility for more advanced system identification or domain randomization techniques.

#### 3.2. Generating Robot Actions in Simulation

This stage leverages the simulated environment and object trajectories to train a robot policy and generate synthetic data.
- **Defining Object-Centric Rewards:** Instead of mapping human actions, X-SIM defines a dense, object-centric reward function based on the tracked human object trajectories. The reward encourages the robot to move objects towards their next desired pose as specified by the human demonstration. The reward function, $r_{goal} \propto -d_{pos}(s_B^H, s_t^R) - d_{rot}(s_B^H, s_t^R)$, penalizes deviations in both position and rotation from the target object pose. An additional $r_{approach}$ term encourages the robot end-effector to approach relevant objects. The goal state updates online as sub-goals are achieved, enabling multi-step manipulation.
- **Training Privileged-State RL Policy:** A robot policy is trained using **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** in the ManiSkill simulator, optimizing the object-centric reward. This policy conditions on privileged simulation states (current object states, goal states, robot proprioception) and learns to reproduce the observed object manipulation effects. Object starting poses are randomized during training to enhance robustness.
- **Collecting Synthetic Image-Action Data:** After training, the RL policy is rolled out in the simulator to generate a large synthetic dataset of paired image-action data, $D_{synthetic}$. To ensure diversity and robustness, these rollouts are rendered under varied simulation conditions, including randomized object starting positions, robot poses, camera viewpoints, and lighting conditions. Only successful trajectories are kept.

#### 3.3. Sim-to-Real Transfer of Image-Based Policies

This final stage distills the learned behaviors into an image-conditioned policy and deploys it in the real world with online adaptation.
- **Training Image-Conditioned Policies:** Using the synthetic dataset $D_{synthetic}$, an image-conditioned policy, $\pi_{img}(a|o)$, is trained. This policy operates directly on RGB observations without privileged state access. **Diffusion Policy (DP)**, a state-of-the-art behavior cloning architecture, is employed to predict a sequence of actions given the current RGB image input.
- **Auto-Calibration of Real and Sim (Online Domain Adaptation):** To mitigate the visual sim-to-real gap, X-SIM introduces an online domain adaptation technique. After initial real-world deployment (even with failures), image observations from robot rollouts are collected. The simulation is then spawned with the same initial state as these real rollouts, and the exact robot trajectories are replayed in simulation to create a paired dataset, $D_{paired} = \{(o_{sim}^R, o_{real}^R)\}$. A **contrastive InfoNCE loss** is then applied during policy training. This loss encourages the policy's image encoder, $\phi$, to map corresponding real and simulated images to similar embeddings while pushing apart mismatched ones. This guides the encoder to focus on task-relevant features and prevents overfitting to simulation-specific visual artifacts, without requiring any real robot action data.

In summary, X-SIM masterfully combines advanced 3D vision, reinforcement learning, and domain adaptation techniques to create an end-to-end framework that learns robot manipulation from human videos, bypassing the need for expensive robot teleoperation data and directly tackling the embodiment and sim-to-real gaps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

X-SIM was evaluated across 5 manipulation tasks in 2 real-world environments (Kitchen and Tabletop) using a 7-DOF Franka arm. The experiments aimed to answer four core questions, leading to the following main findings:

1. **Bridging the Embodiment Gap via Simulation:**
 - X-SIM significantly **outperforms hand-tracking baselines** (Hand Mask and Object-Aware IK). It achieves consistently higher task progress, with over 30% average gains in the most challenging and mismatched settings.
 - **Hand Mask** fails due to a large visual domain gap, rarely progressing beyond the approach phase.
 - **Object-Aware IK** performs adequately in tasks with similar human-robot execution styles (Kitchen) but fails in tasks with significant kinematic infeasibility or mismatched dynamics (Tabletop), confirming the difficulty of direct human motion retargeting.
 - X-SIM, by learning feasible strategies in simulation based on object motion, effectively overcomes these limitations.

2. **Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer:**
 - X-SIM, using only **RGB image observations**, shows strong transfer capabilities and significantly **outperforms pose-based sim-to-real baselines** (e.g., Human2Sim2Robot). On the "Letter Arrange" task, X-SIM achieved 83.3% average task progress compared to 43.3% for the state-based baseline.
 - This highlights that raw images provide a more robust and practical observation modality for real-world deployment, being less sensitive to noise and occlusions that can cripple precise object pose tracking at inference time.
 - The **online domain adaptation (Auto-Calibration)** further enhances transfer performance. X-SIM (CALIBRATED) shows an average increase of 8% in task progress across all tasks, with a notable 13% increase for the challenging "Mug Insert" task. Visual analysis using t-SNE embeddings confirms that calibration effectively aligns real and simulated image embeddings, guiding the policy to focus on task-relevant features.

3. **Data Efficiency:**
 - X-SIM demonstrates **superior data efficiency** compared to behavior cloning (BC) methods relying on robot teleoperation.
 - For a modified "Mustard Place" task with broader initial state distribution, X-SIM achieved 90% success with just **1 minute of human video data**, while behavior cloning with robot teleoperation only reached 70% success with **10 minutes of robot demonstrations**.
 - This efficiency stems from the faster acquisition of human videos (20s vs. 60s per robot demo) and the ability to extensively perturb object poses in simulation for broader coverage.

4. **Robustness to Test-Time Changes:**
 - X-SIM effectively generates synthetic data to enable real-world policy robustness, particularly to **novel camera viewpoints**.
 - By collecting simulated rollouts from diverse camera angles (e.g., Side and Frontal for "Shoe on Rack") and training a policy jointly on this data, X-SIM significantly improves generalization, even to unseen camera angles (e.g., Novel viewpoint). For instance, a policy trained on both Side & Frontal views achieved 96.7% and 80.0% success on Side and Frontal test views respectively, and 53.5% on a novel viewpoint, whereas policies trained on single views performed poorly on alternative/novel views.

In essence, the findings confirm that X-SIM successfully leverages object motion from human videos to train robust, image-conditioned robot policies that transfer effectively to the real world, exhibiting high data efficiency and generalization capabilities, all without requiring any robot-specific teleoperation data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

X-SIM represents a significant step forward in robot learning by offering a scalable and practical solution to fundamental challenges in the field. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Scalability of Robot Learning:** By demonstrating a viable pathway to learn robot manipulation from abundant human videos, X-SIM fundamentally addresses the data bottleneck in robotics. This opens up the possibility of training policies for a vast array of tasks that would be impractical or prohibitively expensive to cover with robot teleoperation. This is particularly relevant in the era of large-scale robot foundation models, which constantly demand more diverse data.
- **Overcoming the Embodiment Gap:** The framework's core insight—using object motion as a transferable supervisory signal—is powerful. It elegantly bypasses the complexities and limitations of directly mapping human kinematics or visual cues to robots, which often fail due to mismatched embodiments. This makes the learned policies inherently more robust and generalizable across different robot platforms.
- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** X-SIM pushes the boundaries of real-to-sim-to-real pipelines. The combination of photorealistic environment reconstruction, object-centric RL training in simulation, and crucially, the novel online domain adaptation technique for image alignment, significantly reduces the sim-to-real gap without relying on expensive real-world robot data for co-training or brittle object tracking at deployment. The self-calibrating observation encoder is a particularly innovative aspect.
- **Increased Data Efficiency:** The demonstrated 10x reduction in data collection time compared to teleoperation-based behavior cloning highlights X-SIM's practical utility. This efficiency enables faster iteration cycles for policy development and deployment, making robot skill acquisition more agile.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The ability to train policies that generalize to novel camera viewpoints and test-time changes is critical for real-world deployment, where conditions are rarely static. X-SIM's simulation-based data augmentation naturally provides this robustness.
- **Complementary to Robot Foundation Models:** As highlighted in the discussion, X-SIM offers a natural extension for fine-tuning pre-trained robot learning models, including foundation models like PI-0.5 and OpenVLA. By generating targeted synthetic rollouts conditioned on human video demonstrations, X-SIM could enable low-cost, scalable specialization of generalist policies to new tasks and embodiments, providing a crucial "specialization layer" for these large models.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the reliance on 3D object meshes, rigid object manipulation, and environment scans, these are framed as active areas of research with promising future solutions. The work provides a strong foundation for future research in self-supervised robotics, opening avenues for robots to learn continuously from the vast amount of human interaction data available in the world. Its impact could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots into diverse real-world applications.
