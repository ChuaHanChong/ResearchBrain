# An Optimized Franz-Parisi Criterion and its Equivalence with SQ Lower Bounds

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Yale University propose the Generalized Franz-Parisi (GFP) criterion, which establishes an "almost" two-way equivalence with Statistical Query (SQ) lower bounds for a broad spectrum of statistical models. This refined criterion generalizes the notion of "overlap" and offers a unified framework for predicting and understanding computational hardness across diverse high-dimensional inference problems.

## Problem

- The original Franz-Parisi (FP) criterion, while connecting statistical physics heuristics to LDP lower bounds, was rigorously applicable primarily to Gaussian additive models (GAMs) and showed limitations in other settings.
- A formal gap existed in rigorously linking physics-inspired intuitions about computational hardness (e.g., from spin glass theory) to formal algorithmic complexity frameworks like Statistical Query (SQ) lower bounds.
- The fixed Euclidean "overlap" in the original FP criterion was not always appropriate for capturing computational hardness in various statistical models, leading to one-sided implications or failures.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Generalized Franz-Parisi== (GFP) criterion, which optimizes over possible "overlap" events defined by an invariant finite group, allowing for a more flexible and model-specific measure of signal similarity.
- Developed a theoretical framework to prove an "almost" two-way equivalence between GFP-hardness and Statistical Query (SQ) lower bounds, holding under a mild and verifiable Assumption 3.1.
- The proof strategy involves a two-step process: first simplifying GFP-hardness to a more tractable "optimized overlap" (`ρ_G`-FP) form, then establishing the rigorous equivalence between `ρ_G`-FP hardness and SQ-hardness.

## Results

- Established an "almost" two-way equivalence between GFP-hardness and SQ-hardness, valid for a wide range of models including Planted Sparse Models, Non-Gaussian Component Analysis, Single-Index Models, and Gaussian Convex Truncation models.
- Demonstrated that Assumption **3.1** is satisfied by these diverse models; notably, for Gaussian Convex Truncation, it is equivalent to the Gaussian Correlation Inequality.
- Provided a counterexample showing a detection task that is FP-easy but GFP-hard (and thus SQ-hard), validating the necessity of optimizing the overlap event in the GFP criterion.
- Derived new SQ lower bounds for problems like mixed sparse linear regression and α-Slice Convex Truncation, while also offering unified derivations for existing bounds in other models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing the definition of the 'overlap' event, by considering group-invariant structures, is crucial for a computational hardness criterion that accurately applies beyond Gaussian additive models.
> - A mild, verifiable assumption regarding the correlation structure of likelihood ratios (Assumption 3.1) is sufficient to rigorously bridge the GFP criterion with SQ lower bounds.
> - The GFP-SQ equivalence provides a new, unified, and potentially simpler pathway for deriving SQ lower bounds, unifying statistical physics insights with formal algorithmic barriers.
