# IMAGAgent: Orchestrating Multi-Turn Image Editing via Constraint-Aware Planning and Reflection

> [!summary] Summary
> IMAGAgent is a multi-turn image editing agent built on a plan-execute-reflect closed loop. A VLM planner splits instructions into atomic sub-tasks under explicit constraints, a tool-chain orchestrator schedules retrieval, detection, segmentation and editing tools, and a panel of VLM critics aggregated by an LLM triggers self-correction. It beats prior editors on a new long-horizon benchmark and on MagicBrush.

## Problem

- Single-turn and controllable diffusion editors run a static one-instruction-one-edit regime, so small deviations go undiagnosed and accumulate into error and structural distortion over many turns.
- Existing multi-turn methods rely on rigid predefined workflows and cannot adapt actions to intermediate outcomes, so satisfied constraints are not tracked and later edits overwrite earlier semantics, causing drift.
- Tool-augmented vision agents orchestrate experts for high-level planning but lack editing-aware fine-grained evaluation and closed-loop feedback, so they cannot reliably suppress side effects that corrupt identity and geometry over long horizons.

## Method

- ==Constraint-aware planning==: a VLM planner decomposes a long instruction into ==atomic sub-tasks== governed by three rules, target singularity, semantic atomicity and visual perceptibility, so each sub-task is executable.
- ==Tool-chain orchestration==: a VLM controller builds an execution path from the current image, the current sub-task and the accumulated history, scheduling web search, SAM, GroundingDINO and editing models such as Seedream and Qwen.
- ==Multi-expert collaborative reflection==: several VLM critics score semantic, perceptual, aesthetic and logical quality, an LLM aggregator merges them into positive and negative feedback, retries below a threshold, and logs outcomes into history.

## Results

- On MTEditBench (1,000 sequences of at least 4 turns), IMAGAgent averages DINO **0.766**, CLIP-I **0.875** and CLIP-T **0.248**, beating VINCIE (**0.739** DINO) and GPT-4o (**0.727**), with the gap widening as turns increase.
- On MagicBrush, average DINO is **0.806** and CLIP-I **0.898** versus **0.735** and **0.853** for VINCIE. At turn 3 DINO is **0.766** against **0.645** for VINCIE and **0.586** for OmniGen.
- Ablations on MTEditBench show every part matters: removing reflection drops DINO from **0.766** to **0.689**, removing history drops CLIP-I from **0.875** to **0.772**, and a single expert lowers all metrics. Reflection raises step success from **68.32%** to **95.25%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-turn editing is best framed as sequential decision-making with explicit constraints and verification, rather than repeated isolated edits, because errors compound through each output becoming the next input.
> - Carrying a structured history of past plans, images and feedback matters most for identity preservation; dropping it caused the largest CLIP-I loss, so persistent memory is a core component.
> - Closed-loop critique recovers roughly 27% of initially failing steps within three retries, and a heterogeneous critic panel catches defects a single VLM misses, so ensemble feedback is a cheap route to robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image editing has seen advancements with the emergence of diffusion models and tool-augmented agents, enabling users to refine visuals through sequential instructions. However, a significant challenge in multi-turn image editing scenarios is the maintenance of stability. Existing paradigms are often limited to isolated, single-step execution, leading to issues such as error accumulation, semantic drift, and structural distortion over multiple interactions. This occurs because each intermediate output serves as the input for the subsequent step, propagating any deviations throughout the editing history. Current systems generally lack explicit mechanisms to represent, evaluate, and enforce cross-turn editing constraints.

Previous approaches to image editing can be broadly categorized. Single-turn diffusion-based methods, such as SDEdit and UltraEdit, operate on a "one instruction, one edit" principle, lacking inherent support for sequential decision-making. More recent multi-turn studies, like VINCIE and ICEdit, have attempted to address sequential consistency, often through in-context learning. Nevertheless, these methods frequently rely on predefined workflows and do not adaptively adjust actions based on intermediate outcomes or provide dynamic feedback mechanisms to autonomously identify and rectify generative side effects.

Multimodal agents, exemplified by frameworks like FAST and ChatVLA-2, have demonstrated capabilities in action-oriented domains by integrating hierarchical perception and emphasizing experience retrieval and error recovery. While these agents primarily focus on discrete physical actuation rather than generative image editing, their architectural principles for handling errors and incorporating feedback offer a relevant inspiration.

IMAGAgent aims to bridge these identified gaps by introducing a closed-loop "plan-execute-reflect" mechanism, explicitly designed to model and verify long-horizon visual and semantic constraints across multiple editing turns. This positions IMAGAgent as an effort to integrate robust feedback and planning strategies from general multimodal agents into the specific domain of complex, multi-turn generative image editing, thereby enhancing stability and consistency over extended interaction sequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for developing IMAGAgent stems from fundamental deficiencies observed in existing multi-turn image editing systems:

- **Error Accumulation:** Current methods often execute instructions in isolation without explicit diagnosis or correction of deviations. This leads to minor errors propagating and compounding across turns, resulting in cumulative degradation of the image.
- **Semantic Drift:** A lack of explicit representation for satisfied constraints or unmet goals causes subsequent edits to inadvertently overwrite or violate previously established semantics. The system loses track of the overall editing intent.
- **Structural Distortion:** Local edits frequently lack grounded visual verification, leading to unintended side effects that corrupt object identity, geometry, and textural coherence over prolonged editing sequences. The structural integrity of the image is compromised.
- **Lack of Closed-Loop Decision Mechanisms:** Existing approaches typically operate with static, predefined workflows or lack fine-grained, editing-aware evaluation and feedback mechanisms to reliably suppress the issues mentioned above.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of the IMAGAgent framework are:

- **Develop a Closed-Loop Agent Framework:** Introduce a "plan-execute-reflect" paradigm that achieves deep synergy among instruction parsing, tool scheduling, and adaptive correction within a unified pipeline.
- **Implement Constraint-Aware Planning:** Design a module that leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to precisely decompose complex natural language instructions into executable sub-tasks. This decomposition is governed by strict criteria: target singularity (operations confined to a single entity), semantic atomicity (indivisible sub-tasks), and visual perceptibility (tasks producing tangible visual alterations).
- **Orchestrate Heterogeneous Tools Dynamically:** Create a tool-chain orchestration module that dynamically constructs execution paths based on the current image, sub-task, and historical context. This module enables adaptive scheduling and collaborative operation among diverse vision models (e.g., image retrieval, segmentation, detection, editing).
- **Devise a Multi-Expert Collaborative Reflection Mechanism:** Establish a mechanism where a central Large Language Model (LLM) aggregates critiques from multiple VLM experts. This holistic feedback triggers fine-grained self-correction for erroneous iterations and records feedback outcomes to optimize future decisions, aiming to suppress error accumulation and semantic drift.
- **Construct a Comprehensive Benchmark:** Develop MTEditBench, a new dataset specifically designed for evaluating long-horizon multi-turn image editing to enable systematic assessment of the system's performance across different editing turns and complexities.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** Achieve significantly improved performance over existing methods in terms of instruction consistency, editing precision, and overall quality, particularly in long-horizon multi-turn interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IMAGAgent reformulates multi-turn image editing as a sequential optimization process driven by feedback. Given an initial image ($I_0$) and a complex user instruction ($T_0$), the objective is to generate a stable edited image ($I^*$) through a closed-loop pipeline. This process maintains a dynamic historical context ($C$) that includes completed editing attempts, their orchestration plans, resulting visual states, and critique feedback.

The framework operates via three core modules in a closed-loop pipeline:

#### 3.1. Constraint-Aware Planning Module

This module is responsible for interpreting and structuring complex user instructions.
- **Function:** It maps a potentially ambiguous global instruction ($T_0$) into a structured sequence of atomic sub-tasks ($A = \{t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_n\}$). A VLM-based planner ($\Psi$) performs this decomposition, grounding instructions in the actual spatial layout of $I_0$.
- **Decomposition Criteria:** Each sub-task ($t_i$) must adhere to three rigorous criteria:
 - **Target Singularity:** Operations are restricted to a single entity or a coherent group, preventing simultaneous edits on independent objects.
 - **Semantic Atomicity:** Each sub-task is indivisible; further decomposition would compromise its semantic integrity.
 - **Visual Perceptibility:** The task must result in a tangible visual alteration rather than an abstract semantic shift.
- **Causal Consistency:** Beyond mere decomposition, the planner consolidates redundant or chained sub-tasks and reorders the sequence $A$ according to semantic dependencies. This ensures that prerequisite edits are executed before dependent ones, establishing a causally consistent and executable task sequence. Qwen-VL-MAX is employed as the foundational planner.

#### 3.2. Tool-Chain Orchestration Module

Once the task sequence is established, this module manages the execution phase.
- **Contextual Decision-Making:** It utilizes a dynamic historical context set ($C$) that encapsulates reasoning trajectories, critique feedback, and intermediate states. For each sub-task ($t_i$), the agent operates within a feedback-driven re-execution loop.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Mechanism:** Conditioned on the current image state ($I_{i-1}$), the atomic sub-task ($t_i$), and the historical context ($C$), the agent generates a comprehensive orchestration plan ($R^{(j)}_i$). This plan includes a chain-of-thought rationale, the selected tool-chain, and optimized execution parameters. GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking serves as the editing controller for this module.
- **Execution:** The refined candidate image ($I^{(j+1)}_i$) is generated by executing the plan.
- **Heterogeneous Tools:** The system integrates a diverse suite of invokable tools:
 - **Segmentation:** SAM model for high-precision segmentation.
 - **Detection:** Grounding-DINO-Base for open-set object detection.
 - **Image Retrieval:** CLIP-ViT-Large-Patch14 as the backbone for selecting candidate images.
 - **Generative Editing:** Doubao-Seedream-4.0, Qwen-Image-Edit, and Stable-Diffusion-XL-Base-1.0 to handle various editing requirements.

#### 3.3. Multi-Expert Collaborative Reflection Mechanism

This module is crucial for maintaining semantic fidelity and structural stability over turns.
- **Multi-Dimensional Assessment:** A panel of three distinct VLM experts (Qwen-VL-MAX, Doubao-Seed-1.6-Vision, Doubao-Seed-1.6) conducts a multi-dimensional assessment. The evaluation rubric covers semantic alignment, perceptual quality, aesthetic assessment, and logical consistency.
- **Individual Critiques:** Each expert independently scrutinizes the generated candidate image ($I^{(j+1)}_i$) to provide a raw critique tuple ($f_k = \{f^{(k)}_{pos}, f^{(k)}_{neg}, s^{(k)}\}$), detailing positive traits, negative defects, and a quantitative score.
- **Consensus Aggregation:** A central LLM aggregator (DeepSeek-V3.2) synthesizes these individual critiques into a unified consensus feedback triplet ($F^{(j+1)}_i = \{F_{pos}, F_{neg}, S^{(j+1)}_i\}$). $F_{pos}$ summarizes successful modifications, $F_{neg}$ pinpoints specific remaining defects, and $S_i \in$ is the average score.
- **Iterative Refinement Strategy:** A dual-threshold strategy governs the feedback loop:
 - **Acceptance:** If the consensus score ($S^{(j+1)}_i$) meets or exceeds a success threshold ($\tau_{sr}$), the image is accepted, and the system proceeds to the next sub-task.
 - **Re-generation:** If the score is below $\tau_{sr}$ and the iteration limit ($\tau_{it}$) has not been reached, the system triggers a re-generation step. The agent uses the negative feedback ($F_{neg}$) from the updated historical context ($C$) to rectify errors in the subsequent attempt.
 - **Fallback:** If the maximum iterations ($\tau_{it}$) (set to 3) are reached without convergence, the system selects the candidate with the highest consensus score from all attempts within that sub-task as the final output. The success threshold ($\tau_{sr}$) is set to 7.

This iterative optimization effectively suppresses error accumulation and ensures structural integrity throughout long-horizon editing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of IMAGAgent was evaluated through extensive experiments on a newly constructed benchmark, MTEditBench, and the existing MagicBrush dataset.

#### 4.1. Datasets

- **MagicBrush**: An existing dataset with over 10,000 triplets from 5,313 sessions, but limited to a maximum depth of 3 turns.
- **MTEditBench**: A new benchmark constructed from the VINCIE training corpus, comprising 1,000 high-quality sequences with depths of at least 4 turns. This dataset was specifically curated to assess cumulative semantic consistency in complex, long-horizon editing scenarios, covering diverse operations like object removal, addition, and attribute editing.

#### 4.2. Metrics

Performance was evaluated using a multi-dimensional assessment framework:
- **DINO**: Evaluates visual consistency.
- **CLIP-I (Image-Image Similarity)**: Measures image semantic similarity.
- **CLIP-T (Text-Image Alignment)**: Assesses cross-modal text-image alignment, indicating instruction consistency.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Evaluation

- **MTEditBench (Table 1)**: IMAGAgent exhibited consistent and stable performance, particularly demonstrating a more pronounced advantage as the number of editing turns increased. This suggests an ability to mitigate semantic drift and preserve contextual and logical coherence over long-horizon interactions.
- **MagicBrush (Table 2)**: While several SOTA models performed competitively in the initial turn, IMAGAgent maintained superior stability in multi-turn settings. It achieved higher DINO, CLIP-I, and CLIP-T scores as editing progressed, indicating stronger structural preservation and semantic alignment.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Evaluation

- **Visual Comparisons (Figures 4 and 5)**: Comparisons against SOTA approaches on both MTEditBench and MagicBrush datasets illustrated that IMAGAgent achieved higher visual quality, consistency, and command adherence accuracy. The constraint-aware planning and multi-expert collaborative reflection mechanisms contributed to maintaining image quality without discernible semantic drift and effectively suppressed error accumulation across increasing editing turns.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user study involving 20 expert participants (rank-3 preference) on 50 randomly selected cases evaluated IMAGAgent against baselines across three perspectives: instruction following, visual realism, and overall editing quality. The results (Figure 6) indicated that IMAGAgent consistently achieved favorable scores across all metrics, supporting the practical effectiveness of the system in stable multi-turn image editing.

#### 4.6. Ablation Results

An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of each core component of IMAGAgent (Table 3, Figure 7):
- **w/o Reflection (Linear)**: Removing the feedback loops resulted in a significant performance drop, particularly in DINO scores (from 0.766 to 0.689). This degradation confirmed that a linear execution paradigm is prone to error accumulation, validating the necessity of the closed-loop design for structural stability.
- **w/o Constraints**: This variant showed a notable decline in CLIP-T instruction alignment. Without the constraints of target singularity and semantic atomicity, the planner tended to generate overly complex or ambiguous sub-tasks, indicating that rule-based decomposition is crucial for executable complex instructions.
- **w/o Historical Context**: A substantial degradation in CLIP-I scores (from 0.875 to 0.772) was observed. This suggested that ignoring editing history hinders the agent's ability to track previous modifications, leading to semantic drift where previous edits are inadvertently overwritten or inconsistencies arise.
- **Single-Expert Critique**: Comparing this variant with the full IMAGAgent revealed that the collaborative panel provided a stable improvement across all metrics. While a single VLM performed reasonably well, the ensemble of heterogeneous VLMs offered more comprehensive coverage of aesthetic, logical, and semantic defects, reducing the likelihood of missed errors during critique.

#### 4.7. Analysis of Feedback Mechanism

- **Qualitative Correction Capability (Figure 8a)**: An example demonstrated that the reflection module could identify defects (e.g., structural distortion, unintended object introduction) and provide actionable negative feedback. Guided by this critique, the agent optimized tool selection and parameters to achieve a corrected result, illustrating fine-grained error correction beyond simple binary judgments.
- **Refinement Efficacy (Figure 8b)**: Statistical analysis on the MagicBrush validation set showed that while 68.32% of editing steps succeeded in the first attempt, approximately 27% of cases initially failed but were successfully rectified within the allowed three iterations. This quantitative improvement validated that the iterative refinement significantly boosts the overall success rate by addressing subtle attribute errors or spatial misalignments.
- **Cost vs. Quality Trade-off**: The feedback mechanism increased the average inference time cost by approximately 1.4 times compared to open-loop baselines. The authors argue that this trade-off is justified for creative workflows where output quality is a primary concern.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

IMAGAgent introduces a novel and systematic approach to multi-turn image editing, addressing several critical limitations of existing methods. Its significance lies in:

- **Enhanced Stability and Consistency:** The framework directly tackles the challenges of error accumulation, semantic drift, and structural distortion by implementing a closed-loop "plan-execute-reflect" paradigm. This leads to more reliable and consistent image edits over extended interaction sequences.
- **Robust Instruction Following:** The constraint-aware planning module ensures that complex instructions are precisely decomposed into executable sub-tasks, improving the system's ability to accurately follow user commands.
- **Adaptive Tool Orchestration:** Dynamic scheduling and collaborative operation of heterogeneous vision tools allow the system to adapt its editing strategy based on the specific context and requirements of each sub-task.
- **Effective Self-Correction:** The multi-expert collaborative reflection mechanism provides a robust feedback loop that enables the agent to identify and rectify errors autonomously, leading to iterative refinement and higher-quality outputs.
- **New Evaluation Benchmark:** The introduction of MTEditBench provides a valuable resource for the research community, enabling systematic evaluation of long-horizon multi-turn image editing capabilities, which was previously a limiting factor.

The potential impact of IMAGAgent extends to various applications requiring complex, sequential visual manipulations. It could lead to more intelligent and user-friendly creative tools for professional designers, artists, and general users, by enabling them to achieve intricate editing goals with greater precision and fewer manual corrections. Furthermore, the principles of constraint-aware planning and multi-expert reflection could inform the development of more robust AI agents in other multimodal domains where sequential decision-making and error recovery are crucial.
