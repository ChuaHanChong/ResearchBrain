# Programmable World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Programmable World Model decouples world-state evolution from visual generation. A coding agent turns natural-language instructions into executable programs defining entity states and rules; a lightweight engine maintains an explicit persistent state, including off-screen and non-visual attributes. State-augmented 3D oriented bounding boxes compile deterministically into pixel-aligned controls for a pretrained video renderer, reaching 94% Count Accuracy and 98% State Accuracy on CombatStateBench.

## Problem

- Interactive video world models expose only camera, action, or prompt interfaces, giving no way to address and manipulate individual entities within the generated world.
- They lack an explicit persistent global state independent of the current view, so off-screen entities, inventory, progress, and interaction history are not reliably preserved.
- Users cannot program rules governing world evolution; prompting a desired outcome never establishes an executable rule that consistently governs later interactions.

## Method

- Agent-orchestrated box world: a VLM coding agent writes engine-readable programs over 3D-detected entities, and a lightweight engine executes rules to advance a canonical state of entities, attributes, relations, rules.
- ==Deterministic state compiler== projects state-augmented ==3D OBBs== under the target camera into three rasterized control maps: learnable ==identity-slot embeddings==, text-encoder semantic features, and seven-way ==world-velocity direction states==.
- **Structured Spatial ControlNet** injects these controls into a frozen camera-controlled video backbone; ==chunk-autoregressive rendering== adds multi-scale temporal history plus ==geometry-aligned spatial memory==, fed by an automatic annotation pipeline.

## Results

- On 50 CombatStateBench clips, Count Accuracy reaches **94.00%** versus **40.75%** for LingBot-World-V2 and **32.00%** for YUME, a gain of **53.25** and **62.00** points.
- State Accuracy over 50 death events reaches **98.00%**, exceeding LingBot-World-V2 by **90.00** points and YUME by **40.00** points, judged from RGB frames alone without privileged annotations.
- VBench metrics lead on all four axes: Imaging **67.62**, Subject Consistency **94.74**, Background Consistency **96.98**, Temporal Stability **99.00**; an **897**-frame rollout stays stable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A generative world should not rely on generation alone to remember facts; making state executable and verifiable while keeping appearance generative is the separation that pays off.
> - Representation granularity is a design dial: too coarse under-constrains geometry, too fine forces the system to author low-level motion and widens the training-inference mismatch.
> - Deriving motion cues from world-space velocity rather than image-space displacement disambiguates object motion from camera-induced apparent motion, a transferable trick for camera-controlled generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in generative models, particularly video world models. These models aim to create interactive virtual environments by synthesizing realistic visual observations based on visual histories and user actions. Recent progress has enabled the generation of increasingly plausible and responsive environments, opening avenues for immersive world creation.

However, existing interactive video world models typically operate by implicitly learning the dynamics of the world from visual data. This approach presents several challenges when attempting to build truly interactive and programmable world engines. First, current control interfaces predominantly focus on camera movements, general actions, or high-level prompts, limiting the ability to directly manipulate or address individual entities within the generated world. Second, these models often lack an explicit, persistent global state that can be independently accessed and updated. Such a state is crucial for maintaining information about off-screen entities, non-visual attributes (e.g., inventory, health), task progress, and interaction history, which are fundamental for coherent and long-horizon interactions, especially in complex or multiplayer scenarios. Third, users face difficulties in programming the specific rules that govern world evolution. While prompts can suggest a desired outcome, they do not establish executable rules that consistently dictate how interactions unfold over time.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that explicitly decouples the evolution of the world state from the generation of visual observations. It introduces a mechanism for maintaining a canonical, programmable world state and connecting it to a generative renderer through a structured intermediate representation. This approach contrasts with prior work that primarily focuses on improving visual consistency through temporal or spatial memory within observation-generating models, or those that predict state variables through learned logic engines, which can be prone to accumulating errors. By introducing explicit, user-programmable rules and a deterministic state engine, this work aims to provide a more reliable and controllable foundation for interactive generative worlds, bridging the gap between purely generative visual models and robust, rule-based interactive systems found in traditional game engines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Programmable World Model (PWM) that overcomes the limitations of existing interactive video world models, specifically concerning persistent world state and programmable rules. The core motivation stems from the recognition that while generative video models can produce visually realistic and interactive experiences, they struggle to reliably maintain explicit, consistent world states and enforce user-defined rules over extended interactions.

The key objectives are articulated as follows:

1. **Decouple World-State Evolution from Visual Observation Generation:** The central aim is to separate the logical progression of the world's internal state from the process of rendering visual observations. This decoupling is intended to allow for independent management and evolution of world facts.
2. **Enable Entity-Level Control and Persistent State Management:** The framework seeks to provide direct control over individual entities within the world, allowing for manipulation of their attributes and interactions. Crucially, it aims to establish a persistent global world state that encompasses both visible and off-screen entities, as well as non-visual attributes, ensuring coherence across long interaction sequences.
3. **Support User-Programmable World Rules:** A significant objective is to allow users to define and enforce explicit rules that govern how entities interact and how the world evolves. This moves beyond relying on implicit dynamics learned by generative models, aiming for predictable and verifiable world behavior.
4. **Develop an Effective Intermediate Representation:** A key motivation is to identify and implement a representation that is compact, directly editable by programs, yet sufficiently expressive to guide high-quality visual generation. This representation must support programmable world-state evolution while preserving the spatial and semantic structure necessary for visual consistency. The paper highlights the trade-offs in representation choices, aiming for an abstraction level that offers explicit control without excessive complexity in data acquisition or inference-time state construction.
5. **Create a Practical Data Curation Pipeline:** To facilitate the training of such a system, an objective is to develop an automatic pipeline capable of extracting the necessary spatial and semantic supervision from unlabeled videos, addressing the scarcity of appropriately annotated data for programmable generative worlds.
6. **Establish a Benchmark for Programmable World Models:** Recognizing the need for standardized evaluation, the paper aims to introduce a benchmark specifically designed to assess the consistency between generated videos and engine-maintained world states, particularly focusing on verifiable, explicit properties.

In essence, the motivation is to elevate generative video models from mere observation predictors to robust, programmable interactive world engines where the underlying logic and state are explicit, controllable, and verifiable, akin to traditional game engines but with generative visual flexibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Programmable World Model (PWM) framework is designed to decouple world-state evolution from visual observation generation. It comprises a coding agent, a lightweight state engine, a state compiler, and a generative renderer, all integrated into an interactive loop.

#### 3.1. World Representation and Programming

The core world state is represented by a set of persistent entities in 3D space. Each entity is defined by a **state-augmented 3D oriented bounding box (OBB)**, specifying its position, extent, and orientation. This geometry is augmented with state variables such as persistent identity, semantic category, and functional attributes (e.g., health, faction). The world state also includes inter-entity relations (e.g., hostility) and executable rules that dictate how actions and events modify these states. This OBB-based representation is chosen as an intermediate abstraction to balance explicit control with generative flexibility, avoiding the over-specification of detailed 3D models while providing more robust spatial grounding than 2D or text-based controls.

An **agent orchestrator**, utilizing a VLM-based coding agent, translates natural-language descriptions into executable world programs. This program defines the initial states, attributes, relations, and rules for entities identified from an initial observation (using a 3D detector). These programs are then used to instantiate the initial canonical state.

#### 3.2. Engine Execution

A **lightweight engine** executes these programs. At each interaction step, it evaluates player actions against the current state, applies effects according to the defined rules, and resolves triggered events. This engine deterministically updates the canonical world state, which can include non-visual variables (e.g., inventory, health), ensuring a persistent and explicit record of world facts. This explicit, rule-based evolution of state is a key differentiator from models that rely on learned state transitions, which can be prone to errors.

#### 3.3. Control Compilation

After the engine updates the canonical world state ($s_{t+1}$), a **deterministic state compiler** projects the visually relevant components into renderer-facing spatial controls ($M^{ctrl}_{t+1}$) for the target camera ($C_{t+1}$). This process translates the 3D OBBs and their augmented states into pixel-aligned information.

The compiler generates three spatially aligned control maps:
- **Identity Map ($M^{id}$):** Assigns a persistent identity embedding to each visible entity's projected OBB region. This ensures instance correspondence across frames, even with occlusion or entities leaving/re-entering the view.
- **Semantic Map ($M^{sem}$):** Rasterizes semantic embeddings (derived from text descriptions via a pretrained encoder) over the projected OBBs, providing category-level information.
- **Direction Map ($M^{dir}$):** Represents the entity's camera-relative motion. The entity's world-space velocity is rotated into the target camera's coordinate system and quantized into seven states (forward, backward, left, right, static, up, down). An embedding for the corresponding state is rasterized over the OBB. This map explicitly distinguishes object motion from apparent motion caused by camera movement.

These maps are concatenated to form the structured projected-box control, providing explicit spatial, identity, semantic, and object-motion guidance.

#### 3.4. Generative Renderer

The generative renderer is built upon LingBot-World-v1, a pretrained camera-controlled video generation backbone. A trainable **Structured Spatial ControlNet** is attached to this backbone to incorporate the compiled controls. The ControlNet encodes the structured projected-box controls (identity, semantic, direction maps) and injects layer-wise features into the frozen main backbone. This design allows the pretrained camera module to handle global viewpoint evolution, while the ControlNet applies entity-specific spatial constraints. The generative prior then synthesizes fine visual details (shape, texture, articulation, etc.) not explicitly encoded by the OBBs.

For long-horizon generation, the renderer operates in a **chunk-autoregressive manner**. Information is causally propagated across chunk boundaries through:
- **Temporal History:** A bounded latent history from previously generated chunks, organized into multi-scale representations (recent, mid-range, long-range) to preserve detailed recent information and extend context.
- **Geometry-aligned Spatial Memory:** Adopted from AlayaWorld, this memory lifts completed RGB frames into a world-space representation using estimated depth and camera parameters. Relevant past observations are retrieved and reprojected into new target camera views, providing a persistent global visual memory against camera motion or occlusion.

#### 3.5. Automatic Training Data Pipeline

To acquire the necessary structured annotations (camera geometry, persistent instance identities, semantic information, object motion) from unlabeled videos, an automatic data engine was developed. This pipeline involves:
1. **Camera-parameter estimation:** Uses ViPE to recover camera intrinsics, poses, and metric depth maps.
2. **Semantic-category discovery:** A VLM (Qwen3-VL) analyzes videos to generate global captions and identify relevant object categories.
3. **Instance segmentation and tracking:** SAM3 performs video instance segmentation and tracking, providing masks, 2D bounding boxes, semantic labels, and persistent track IDs.
4. **Object-trajectory recovery:** Wild-Det3D estimates 3D OBBs for each visible instance. These are transformed into a shared world coordinate system, and object motion (world-space displacement) is calculated and quantized.
5. **Conditioning-map generation:** The estimated 3D OBBs are projected into each frame, and rasterization with z-buffering generates the identity, semantic, and direction maps used as control signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the Programmable World Model (PWM) was evaluated through quantitative and qualitative experiments on a custom benchmark, focusing on world-state consistency and video quality.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Training Data:** The model was trained on HUD-free gameplay videos from Cyberpunk 2077, Forza Horizon 6, and Grand Theft Auto V. An automatic data engine (described in Section 4.5) processed these videos to construct paired video-control training data.
- **Benchmark (CombatStateBench):** A controlled benchmark comprising 50 combat scenario clips was introduced. This benchmark verifies consistency between generated videos and engine-maintained world states. It covers diverse camera and entity motions, including interactions with off-screen entities and character death events. Each sequence includes synchronized 3D boxes, entity states, camera parameters, projected controls, and instance masks, rigorously verified for consistency.
- **Baselines:** PWM was compared against LingBot-World-V2 and YUME, two existing interactive video world models. Since these baselines lack explicit instance-level state interfaces, state transitions were communicated via prompt switching (e.g., updating text conditions to describe a character being killed).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **World-State Consistency (VLM-based):** A VLM (Qwen3.6-27B) served as a judge, observing only generated RGB frames without privileged information.
 - **Count Accuracy:** Measures the percentage of sampled frames where the VLM-predicted count of visibly alive characters matches the engine-maintained count.
 - **State Accuracy:** Measures the percentage of engine-recorded death events where the VLM identifies at least one frame depicting a dead character post-transition.
 - **Video Quality (VBench metrics):**
 - **Imaging Quality:** Assesses frame-level clarity, exposure, noise, and overall visual quality.
 - **Subject Consistency:** Evaluates the stability of foreground entity identity, appearance, and structure across frames.
 - **Background Consistency:** Measures the cross-frame stability of the background scene.
 - **Temporal Stability:** Based on the VBench temporal flickering score, with higher values indicating less flicker.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

The results are summarized in Table 1 (from the paper):

| Method | Imaging Subject Cons. | Background Cons. | Temporal Stability | Count Acc. | State Acc. |
| :-------------------- | :-------------------- | :--------------- | :----------------- | :--------- | :--------- |
| LingBot-World-V2 | 67.46 | 81.87 | 91.89 | 96.85 | 40.75 | 8.00 |
| YUME | 64.10 | 92.35 | 93.63 | 98.76 | 32.00 | 58.00 |
| **Ours** | **67.62** | **94.74** | **96.98** | **99.00** | **94.00** | **98.00** |

- **World-State Consistency:**
 - **Count Accuracy:** PWM achieved 94.00%, substantially outperforming LingBot-World-V2 (40.75%) and YUME (32.00%). This indicates a higher reliability in preserving the number of visible alive characters according to the engine's state.
 - **State Accuracy:** PWM achieved 98.00%, significantly exceeding LingBot-World-V2 (8.00%) and YUME (58.00%). This demonstrates a much greater consistency in visually realizing character death events as specified by the engine.
- **Video Quality:** PWM exhibited competitive or superior performance across all VBench metrics.
 - **Imaging Quality:** PWM scored 67.62, slightly higher than LingBot-World-V2 (67.46) and YUME (64.10).
 - **Subject Consistency:** PWM obtained 94.74, surpassing LingBot-World-V2 (81.87) and YUME (92.35), indicating better preservation of entity identity and appearance.
 - **Background Consistency:** PWM achieved 96.98, outperforming LingBot-World-V2 (91.89) and YUME (93.63).
 - **Temporal Stability:** PWM reached 99.00, slightly better than LingBot-World-V2 (96.85) and YUME (98.76).

These quantitative results demonstrate that decoupling explicit state evolution from generative rendering, along with structured spatial controls, leads to significantly more reliable world-state consistency and robust video quality compared to methods relying on implicit state representations.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) highlighted that while baselines could generate plausible responses to prompts, they frequently failed to reliably preserve world state (e.g., characters disappearing, killed characters continuing to move). In contrast, PWM consistently reflected the engine-maintained state while retaining visual realism.

Further qualitative examples (Figure 5) showcased PWM's capabilities across diverse scenarios:
- **Generalization:** The model generalized to novel minotaur scenes and racing game genres, demonstrating adaptability beyond specific training data styles.
- **Off-screen Entities and Scene Consistency:** Under large-angle camera rotations, PWM correctly revealed characters initially off-screen, maintaining scene structure and rendering newly visible entities according to the persistent world state.
- **Heterogeneous Objects:** The system successfully rendered scenes with mixed object categories, such as humans and vehicles.
- **Long-Horizon Autoregression:** In an 897-frame autoregressive sequence with many NPCs progressively entering the scene, the model maintained reasonable visual and temporal stability, suggesting scalability to complex, evolving worlds.

The visualized control signals (global scene coordinates or camera-view 3D OBB maps) consistently aligned with the generated videos, confirming that the model effectively followed specified camera motion, spatial layouts, and entity states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Programmable World Model (PWM) introduces a significant architectural shift in the design of interactive generative world models by explicitly decoupling world-state evolution from visual observation generation. This approach addresses fundamental limitations of prior work, which often rely on implicitly learned dynamics, leading to challenges in maintaining consistent world states, enforcing specific rules, and providing granular control over individual entities.

The primary significance of this work lies in demonstrating that an external, executable, and verifiable world state, when properly integrated with a generative renderer, results in substantially more reliable and controllable interactive experiences. By using a lightweight engine to manage canonical world facts and a state compiler to translate these facts into structured visual controls (state-augmented 3D OBBs, identity, semantic, and direction maps), PWM achieves higher accuracy in maintaining entity counts and correctly realizing state transitions (e.g., character deaths). This explicit control mechanism allows for consistent long-horizon generation, even when entities are initially off-screen, undergo complex interactions, or when the camera perspective changes dramatically.

The development of the automatic training data pipeline is also impactful, as it provides a practical method for acquiring the necessary structured annotations from unlabeled videos. This reduces the substantial manual effort typically required for such detailed supervision, thereby facilitating the scaling of training data for building more complex and open-domain programmable generative worlds. Furthermore, the introduction of CombatStateBench offers a valuable tool for future research, enabling standardized and objective evaluation of programmable world models based on verifiable world-state consistency.

The potential impact of this research is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced Reliability and Predictability:** By making world logic explicit and programmable, PWM enables the creation of generative environments where interactions are predictable and outcomes are consistent with defined rules, moving beyond mere visual plausibility to logical coherence.
- **Improved User Control:** The framework offers a direct interface for users to define game mechanics, control individual entities, and maintain persistent world states. This could empower creators to develop sophisticated interactive experiences with greater ease and precision.
- **Foundational for Interactive AI Agents:** A programmable and verifiable world model can serve as a more robust foundation for training and evaluating AI agents that interact with virtual environments, as agents can rely on a consistent world state rather than inferring dynamics from potentially ambiguous visual observations.
- **Bridging Generative AI and Game Development:** The methodology suggests a pathway to integrate the creative power of generative AI with the structured, rule-based nature of traditional game engines, potentially leading to new paradigms for game creation and interactive narrative experiences.
- **Generalization to Complex Worlds:** The qualitative results, including generalization to novel scenes, diverse object categories, and long autoregressive sequences with increasing entity counts, indicate the system's potential for supporting complex and evolving virtual worlds.

In conclusion, this work posits that separating "what is true about the world" from "how the world appears" is a crucial step towards building more robust, controllable, and truly interactive generative world models. This direction could significantly advance the capabilities of generative AI in creating dynamic, persistent, and logically consistent virtual environments.
