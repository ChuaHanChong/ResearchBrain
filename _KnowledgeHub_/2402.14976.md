# Unsupervised Domain Adaptation within Deep Foundation Latent Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> A finetuning-free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) method is introduced, leveraging the fixed latent spaces of pre-trained Vision Transformers in conjunction with interpretable prototypical networks. This approach achieves superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art UDA baselines on multi-source UDA datasets by effectively transferring knowledge across domains without modifying the feature extractor.

## Problem

- Existing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods often require complex finetuning of feature extractors, leading to high computational costs.
- Deep learning models for UDA typically lack interpretability, making their cross-domain decision-making processes opaque.
- Bridging distribution shifts between a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain remains a core challenge in machine learning.

## Method

- Extracts features from source and target domains using a fixed, pre-trained ==Vision Transformer== backbone without finetuning.
- Applies ==k-means clustering== to the extracted features in both domains to select representative ==prototypes==.
- Matches unlabeled target prototypes to labeled source prototypes using distance metrics (==L2== or ==2-Wasserstein==) to assign target labels.

## Results

- The method achieved superior UDA performance on multi-source datasets, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines like MCD when utilizing a SWAG ViT-H/14 backbone.
- Fine-tuning the Vision Transformer backbone on ImageNet-**1K** provided a consistent performance boost, underscoring the importance of high-quality pre-trained features.
- Qualitative analysis revealed that even misclassifications were often semantically meaningful, demonstrating inherent cross-domain generalization and the interpretability of prototype-based decisions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Transformers provide sufficiently robust and semantically rich latent spaces to achieve effective UDA without finetuning the feature extractor.
> - Prototypical networks offer inherent, "by-design" interpretability for cross-domain classification, allowing for qualitative analysis of decision errors.
> - The quality and pretraining strategy of the foundation model, such as additional ImageNet-1K finetuning, significantly impacts performance in a finetuning-free UDA setup.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research strategically positions itself at the nexus of several highly active and critical areas within artificial intelligence: **foundation models, unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), and interpretable AI**.

**Foundation Models (FMs)**, such as Vision Transformers (ViT) and DINO-V2, represent a paradigm shift in AI. These models are pre-trained on vast datasets (often in a self-supervised manner) to learn highly robust and generalizable feature representations. The promise of FMs is their ability to solve new problems with "little or no finetuning of features," serving as powerful feature extractors that reduce the need for extensive task-specific training. This paper directly investigates this promise in the context of UDA.

**Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** is a long-standing challenge in machine learning. It addresses situations where a model trained on a labeled "source" dataset needs to perform well on an "unlabeled target" dataset, even though the data distributions of the source and target domains differ (e.g., real images vs. sketches). Traditional UDA methods often involve complex techniques like adversarial training (to align source and target feature distributions) or minimizing distribution divergence (e.g., Maximum Mean Discrepancy, Moment Matching). These methods typically require finetuning the model's feature extractor, which can be computationally intensive and may compromise the inherent robustness of pre-trained foundation models.

**Interpretable AI (XAI)** is another crucial and growing field, driven by the need to understand the decision-making processes of complex deep learning models. Many deep learning models are "black boxes," making it difficult to trust or debug them. The paper highlights two main approaches to XAI: *ante hoc* (by-design) interpretability, which integrates transparency into the model architecture (e.g., prototypical networks, xDNN), and *post hoc* explainability, which provides explanations after the model has made a decision (e.g., GradCAM). This work explicitly aligns with the *ante hoc* philosophy by employing prototypical networks.

The novelty of this paper lies in its **intersection of these three fields**. Instead of resorting to complex finetuning or adversarial training for UDA, it proposes a minimalist approach: leveraging the *fixed, pre-trained latent spaces* of powerful vision transformers for UDA, combined with the inherent interpretability of **prototypical networks**. This approach tests the true "foundation" capabilities of FMs – whether their latent spaces are inherently robust enough to handle domain shifts without further modification. By doing so, it attempts to simplify the UDA pipeline, reduce computational burden associated with finetuning, and critically, provide transparency into the cross-domain classification process, distinguishing it from many performance-driven UDA methods that often sacrifice interpretability for accuracy. This work contributes to the growing body of research exploring efficient and transparent ways to adapt pre-trained models to new, challenging scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are clearly articulated, addressing both technical performance and the broader implications of foundation models and interpretability:

**1. To evaluate the efficacy of fixed Foundation Model latent spaces for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) without finetuning.**
 - **Motivation:** Foundation models like ViT and Dino-V2 are designed to provide highly generalizable features. A central question is whether these pre-trained representations are inherently robust enough to handle domain shifts in UDA tasks *without any further training or finetuning* of the feature extractor itself. This objective directly challenges the traditional UDA paradigm, which often necessitates finetuning, and aims to validate the "zero-shot" or "few-shot" adaptation potential of FMs for domain shift scenarios.

**2. To propose a simple and interpretable methodology for UDA based on prototypical networks.**
 - **Motivation:** Many existing UDA methods involve complex end-to-end training, often using adversarial learning or sophisticated distribution matching algorithms, which can be computationally expensive and make the decision-making process opaque. The authors are motivated to shift towards "decision making over the foundation models’ latent spaces" using a straightforward combination of clustering and distribution matching. Crucially, by adopting prototypical networks, the method aims to be "interpretable-by-design," allowing for a clear understanding of classification decisions based on similarity to learned prototypes. This addresses the growing demand for transparent and trustworthy AI.

**3. To quantitatively benchmark the proposed method against state-of-the-art UDA baselines.**
 - **Motivation:** While simplicity and interpretability are valuable, the method must also demonstrate competitive performance. The authors aim to show that this non-finetuning, prototype-based approach can "improve upon existing baselines" and potentially "outcompete purpose-finetuned models for UDA." This objective is crucial for establishing the practical viability and impact of their approach.

**4. To provide qualitative interpretations of decision-making errors through latent space analysis.**
 - **Motivation:** Beyond just achieving good accuracy, understanding *why* a model makes certain predictions or misclassifications is vital. The prototype-based nature of their method facilitates "geometric proximity analysis through latent feature spaces." This objective aims to offer qualitative insights into the model's cross-domain generalization capabilities and limitations, showcasing how interpretations derived from prototypes can explain seemingly incorrect classifications in a semantically meaningful way. This reinforces the value of interpretable AI in debugging and building intuition for model behavior.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to leverage the immense power of foundation models in a computationally efficient and transparent manner for complex domain adaptation challenges, pushing the boundaries of what is possible without extensive model finetuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is elegantly simple, yet powerful, by strategically combining fixed foundation model feature extraction with prototypical networks and inter-domain cluster matching. The approach, summarized in Algorithm 1, meticulously avoids finetuning the underlying feature extractor, relying entirely on the pre-trained knowledge embedded within the foundation models' latent spaces.

The methodology proceeds in four main stages:

**1. Feature Extraction:**
 - The first step involves passing all images from both the source dataset (S) and the target dataset (T) through a pre-trained, *fixed* foundation model, denoted as $\phi$. This model acts solely as a feature extractor, converting raw images into high-dimensional latent space representations. The paper experiments with various backbone architectures for $\phi$, including ResNet-152 and several Vision Transformer (ViT) variants like SWAG ViT-H/14 and Dino-V2 ViT-G/14, some with additional ImageNet-1K finetuning. The critical aspect here is that the weights of $\phi$ are frozen and not updated during the entire UDA process.

**2. Prototype Selection through Clustering:**
 - Once the features for all source and target images are extracted, unsupervised clustering is performed separately on each domain's feature set.
 - **k-means clustering** is applied to the source features (S) to identify cluster centroids ($P_c^S$) and group features into corresponding clusters ($C^S$). Similarly, k-means is applied to the target features (T) to obtain $P_c^T$ and $C^T$. The number of clusters is set to $k \times \text{number of classes}$ (e.g., 5 clusters per class, for a total of $5 \times 345 = 1725$ clusters in their experiments).
 - From these clusters, "prototypes" ($P_S$ and $P_T$) are selected. A prototype for each centroid is defined as the actual data point (image feature vector) from the respective dataset that is closest to that centroid in the latent space.
 - For the source domain, since labels are known, labels ($L_S$) are retrieved for each source prototype and its corresponding cluster.

**3. Matching Prototypes Between Source and Target Domains (Domain Adaptation):**
 - This is the core domain adaptation step. The goal is to establish a correspondence between the unlabeled target clusters/prototypes and the labeled source clusters/prototypes.
 - A **distance matrix ($D_{ST}$)** is computed, quantifying the dissimilarity between each source cluster (or its prototype) and each target cluster (or its prototype). Two distinct methods are explored for measuring this inter-cluster distance:
 - **L2 distance:** A straightforward Euclidean distance between the cluster prototypes (centroids).
 - **Sinkhorn approximation of the 2-Wasserstein distance:** This metric measures the "cost" of transforming one distribution into another. It is generally more robust to shifts in data distributions and can capture more complex relationships between clusters. The `geomloss` library function is used for its implementation.
 - Based on this distance matrix, labels are assigned to the target clusters ($L_T$). Each target cluster receives the label of the *closest* source cluster, according to the chosen distance metric ($D_{ST}$). This effectively transfers the semantic knowledge (labels) from the source domain to the target domain's latent structure.

**4. Source and Target Class Prediction (Decision Making):**
 - For any new query image (either from the source $q_s$ or target $q_t$), its features are extracted using the same fixed foundation model $\phi$.
 - The class prediction ($\hat{y}$) is then made using a **nearest-neighbor prototype classifier**. The query image's feature vector is compared (using L2 distance) to all prototypes in its respective domain ($P_S$ for source queries, $P_T$ for target queries). The label of the closest prototype is assigned as the prediction.

This methodology hinges on the assumption that the latent spaces of powerful foundation models are sufficiently well-structured and semantically rich that meaningful clusters can be formed, and that these clusters can be effectively matched across domains even without finetuning. The use of prototypical networks inherently provides interpretability, as decisions are directly tied to the proximity to understandable prototypes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using the multi-source UDA dataset proposed by Peng et al. (2019), which features six diverse visual domains (sketch, real, quickdraw, painting, infograph, clipart) and 345 common object categories. The authors focus on single-source domain adaptation.

The key findings are presented through a series of comparative tables (Figure 2 in the paper) and qualitative interpretability analysis:

**1. Critical Role of Foundation Model Backbone:**
 - **Initial Baseline (ResNet-152):** When using a ResNet-152 backbone (fine-tuned on ImageNet), the proposed methodology performed "substantially worse" than the state-of-the-art UDA method MCD (Saito et al., 2018), which was based on a ResNet-101. This indicates that a conventional CNN architecture, even a powerful one, provides feature spaces that are not inherently robust enough for effective UDA *without finetuning* in this framework.
 - **Superiority with Vision Transformers:** The performance dramatically "improves for various ViT backbones" and, crucially, "ultimately overtakes MCD baseline." This is a central finding: leveraging the advanced generalization capabilities of Vision Transformers (specifically SWAG ViT-H/14) allows the simple, non-finetuned approach to surpass a purpose-built, finetuning-based UDA method.

**2. Impact of Pretraining and Finetuning of the Foundation Model:**
 - **ImageNet Pretraining Benefit:** The SWAG ViT-H/14 model that was *finetuned on ImageNet-1K* consistently showed "better performance on practically every single task" compared to its counterpart *without* this additional finetuning. This highlights that while the UDA method itself doesn't finetune the feature extractor, the quality and generalizability of the *pre-trained* feature extractor (enhanced by extensive pretraining like ImageNet-1K) are paramount.
 - **DinoV2's Unexpected Performance:** Surprisingly, DinoV2 ViT-G/14, which is known for its strong unsupervised representation learning and superior performance in many transfer learning scenarios, "does not translate into a better performance on this UDA task" compared to the best-performing SWAG ViT-H/14 finetuned on ImageNet-1K. This suggests that the inherent robustness beneficial for general transfer learning might not perfectly align with the specific demands of *unsupervised* domain adaptation within a fixed latent space, or that the specific architecture/pretraining strategy of SWAG ViT with ImageNet-1K finetuning yields a more suitable latent space for this particular UDA method.

**3. Distance Metric Evaluation:**
 - Comparing the L2 distance between cluster prototypes with the Sinkhorn approximation of the 2-Wasserstein distance, the latter "shows some potential to improve the performance." However, it generally "still lags behind ViT H/14, finetuned on ImageNet-1K" using L2 distance. This suggests that while Wasserstein distance is theoretically more robust for distribution matching, its practical benefits in this specific prototype-matching context are marginal compared to the impact of the underlying feature extractor.

**4. Qualitative Interpretability and Insights:**
 - The analysis of decision-making through nearest prototypes reveals "remarkable cross-domain generalisation." Even for incorrect classifications, the closest prototypes often make "semantically meaningful errors" (e.g., mistaking diamonds for blueberries due to visual similarity in shape and color, or confusion between similarly shaped geometric figures like octagons and hexagons).
 - The visualizations demonstrate how the model successfully relates objects across domains (e.g., aircraft carriers sketched vs. real photographs).
 - It also highlights limitations, such as the model preferring "texture to the semantics" in certain cases or struggling to generalize specific object variations across domains (e.g., alarm clocks).
 - These qualitative insights validate the interpretability aspect of the method, showing that the prototype-based classification provides actionable explanations, even when the model fails by benchmark standards.

In summary, the core finding is that by leveraging powerful, well-pretrained Vision Transformers, a simple, finetuning-free, and interpretable UDA method based on clustering and prototype matching can outperform established, purpose-built UDA techniques. The quality of the foundation model's pre-trained features is paramount, with ImageNet-1K finetuning providing a significant boost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented by Kangin and Angelov carries significant implications for the fields of unsupervised domain adaptation, foundation models, and interpretable AI. Its impact can be discussed across several dimensions:

**1. A New Paradigm for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation:**
 - The most significant impact is the proposal of a genuinely *finetuning-free* approach to UDA that achieves state-of-the-art or even superior performance. This challenges the conventional wisdom in UDA, which often relies on complex, computationally expensive finetuning or adversarial training processes. By demonstrating that UDA can be effectively solved within the fixed latent spaces of foundation models, the paper suggests a shift towards simpler, more efficient, and potentially more scalable UDA solutions. This could democratize UDA by reducing the computational resources and specialized expertise required.

**2. Validation and Extension of Foundation Models' Capabilities:**
 - The work provides strong empirical evidence for the extraordinary generalization power of vision transformer-based foundation models. It demonstrates that their learned feature representations are inherently robust enough to handle significant domain shifts, even without task-specific finetuning. This further strengthens the narrative of foundation models as versatile, "universal" feature extractors, capable of solving diverse downstream tasks with minimal adaptation. It encourages further exploration of FMs' intrinsic capabilities for various transfer learning challenges.

**3. Advancing Interpretability in Complex AI Systems:**
 - By explicitly integrating prototypical networks, the methodology offers inherent, *ante hoc* interpretability. This allows for a transparent understanding of classification decisions, even in cross-domain scenarios. The qualitative analysis beautifully illustrates how predictions, even erroneous ones, can be semantically justified by proximity to learned prototypes. This is a crucial step towards building more trustworthy and explainable AI systems, where users can understand *why* a model made a decision, fostering greater confidence and facilitating debugging in critical applications. It exemplifies how interpretability doesn't have to come at the cost of performance.

**4. Simplified Deployment and Reduced Resource Demands:**
 - Eliminating the need for finetuning on source or target data simplifies the deployment pipeline for UDA. It means a single pre-trained foundation model can be used as a fixed feature extractor across various UDA tasks, with only the lightweight clustering and prototype matching steps needing to be performed. This significantly reduces computational costs, training time, and the need for large labeled target datasets (which are unavailable in UDA anyway for training). This is particularly impactful for resource-constrained environments or applications where rapid adaptation is necessary.

**5. Benchmark for Foundation Model Evaluation:**
 - The proposed methodology can "serve as a benchmark for evaluating such representation learning" from foundation models. It offers a standardized, finetuning-free framework to assess the intrinsic cross-domain robustness and semantic quality of features learned by new foundation models, distinct from their performance on tasks requiring finetuning. The finding regarding DinoV2's surprising performance relative to SWAG-ViT highlights the value of such specific benchmarking.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
 - The limitations uncovered (e.g., the mixed performance of Wasserstein distance, the surprising relative performance of DinoV2) open new avenues for research. Future work could explore more sophisticated prototype selection strategies, hybrid distance metrics, or subtle ways to align latent spaces without explicit finetuning (e.g., through optimal transport techniques applied to the entire feature distributions, not just prototypes). Investigating *why* certain foundation models perform better than others in this specific UDA setting could lead to insights into designing even more robust and universal feature extractors.

In conclusion, this paper makes a compelling case for a paradigm shift in UDA, demonstrating that the future of domain adaptation may lie in intelligently leveraging the fixed, interpretable latent spaces of powerful foundation models. Its impact stems from offering a high-performing, resource-efficient, and transparent solution to a long-standing challenge in machine learning.
