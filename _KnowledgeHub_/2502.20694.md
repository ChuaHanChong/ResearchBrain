# WorldModelBench: Judging Video Generation Models As World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldModelBench is introduced as a benchmark to evaluate video generation models based on their instruction following and adherence to physical laws, moving beyond general visual quality assessments. Its application reveals that current models, including leading ones, still exhibit substantial gaps in accurately modeling physical interactions and following complex instructions.

## Problem

- Existing evaluation benchmarks for video generation models primarily focus on general visual quality, temporal consistency, and video-condition consistency, neglecting understanding of physical laws, causal relationships, and instruction following.
- Current physics-based evaluations are limited in scope, often focusing only on daily objects and lacking comprehensive coverage of diverse, application-driven scenarios relevant to video world models.
- There is a need to assess video generation models as 'world models' that accurately represent physical reality and can follow instructions for practical applications like robotics, autonomous driving, and gaming.

## Method

- The paper introduces WorldModelBench, a new benchmark designed to evaluate video generation models on instruction following (4 levels) and physics adherence (5 common violations), in addition to general video quality.
- The benchmark is built on 67,000 human labels from 14 frontier models, ensuring its evaluations align with human judgments.
- It comprises a dataset of 350 image and text condition pairs covering 7 diverse application-driven domains and 56 subdomains, including challenges for various physical phenomena and instruction types.

## Results

- Even the best-performing models achieve only 61% accuracy in completing specified tasks, with significant violations of physical laws such as mass conservation and impenetrability.
- WorldModelBench revealed a weak correlation between general visual quality metrics (like VBench frame-wise quality) and physics adherence scores, indicating a disconnect between these two aspects of model performance.
- Fine-tuning a visual language model as an automated judger on human annotations allows for accurate prediction of world modeling violations and can be used as a reward model to improve video generation models, enhancing instruction following and physics adherence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap exists between current video generation models and ideal video world models regarding physics adherence and instruction following, even for top-performing models.
> - Higher visual quality in generated videos does not consistently correlate with improved instruction following or adherence to physical laws.
> - Image-to-video (I2V) models generally exhibit poorer world modeling capabilities compared to text-to-video (T2V) models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Video generation models have evolved rapidly in recent years, reaching unprecedented levels of visual quality. However, as these models become increasingly sophisticated, there's a growing need to evaluate them not just on aesthetic qualities, but on their ability to function as "world models" that accurately represent physical reality and follow instructions. The paper "WorldModelBench: Judging Video Generation Models As World Models" addresses this critical gap by introducing a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the world modeling capabilities of video generation models.

### 2. Research Context

Current evaluation benchmarks for video generation models primarily focus on general visual quality metrics such as Frechet Video Distance (FVD) and CLIPScore. While these metrics effectively measure visual fidelity and temporal consistency, they fail to capture a model's understanding of physical laws, causal relationships, and instruction following capabilities.

Existing benchmarks like VBench provide comprehensive evaluation of general video quality and video-condition consistency, but lack specific focus on physical law adherence. VideoPhy introduces some physics-based evaluations, but concentrates only on interactions between daily objects without covering application-driven scenarios.

WorldModelBench aims to fill this gap by providing a benchmark that evaluates:
1. Instruction following abilities
2. Physics adherence
3. Commonsense reasoning

This approach recognizes that video generation models are increasingly being viewed as potential video world models capable of simulating future frames and supporting decision-making tasks in applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and gaming.

### 3. WorldModelBench Overview

WorldModelBench brings two key advantages to the evaluation landscape:

1. **Nuanced World Modeling Violation Detection**: The benchmark focuses on instruction following (4 levels) and physics adherence (5 common violations) in addition to general video quality.

2. **Alignment with Human Preferences**: The benchmark is built on 67,000 human labels evaluating 14 frontier models, ensuring that its assessments align with human judgments.

### 4. Evaluation Criteria

WorldModelBench introduces a comprehensive scoring system to evaluate video generation models across three dimensions:

#### 4.1. Instruction Following (0-3 points)

This dimension assesses whether the generated video correctly follows the provided text and image prompts. The scoring system includes four levels:
- 0: The model fails to follow basic instructions
- 1: The model follows some instructions but misses key elements
- 2: The model follows most instructions with minor errors
- 3: The model perfectly executes the specified instructions

#### 4.2. Physics Adherence (0-5 points)

This dimension evaluates adherence to five fundamental physical laws:

- **Newton's First Law**: Objects in motion stay in motion without external forces

- **Conservation of Mass and Solid Mechanics**: Objects should not deform irregularly

- **Fluid Mechanics**: Liquids should flow naturally

- **Impenetrability**: Objects should not intersect unnaturally

- **Gravitation**: Objects should obey gravity

#### 4.3. Commonsense (0-2 points)

This dimension assesses general video generation quality:
- Frame-wise quality (visually appealing frames)
- Temporal quality (smooth, non-flickering motion)

### 5. Methodology

The authors developed WorldModelBench through the following process:

1. **Dataset Creation**: They built a dataset of 350 image and text condition pairs covering 7 application-driven domains and 56 diverse subdomains.

2. **Human Annotation**: They collected 67,000 human labels for videos generated by 14 frontier models.

3. **Judger Training**: They fine-tuned a 2-billion parameter visual language model on the human annotations to create an automated judger.

4. **Model Evaluation**: They used both human ratings and the automated judger to evaluate model performance.

5. **Comparison with Existing Benchmarks**: They conducted a comparative analysis with VBench to demonstrate the unique contributions of WorldModelBench.

### 6. Key Findings

The application of WorldModelBench to evaluate current video generation models revealed several important insights:

1. **Large Gap to Ideal Video World Models**: Even the top-scoring model (kling) has only 61% of videos correctly finishing the specified task. Significant percentages of generated videos violate mass conservation laws and synthesize objects penetrating each other.

2. **Disconnect Between Visual Quality and World Modeling**: Models with higher frame-wise and temporal quality do not necessarily have better instruction following and physics adherence capabilities. This is evident in the weak correlation between VBench frame-wise quality scores and WorldModelBench physics adherence scores.

3. **I2V Models Lag Behind T2V Models**: Image-to-video models generally performed worse than their text-to-video counterparts, suggesting that I2V models need improved tuning and development.

4. **Open Models Are Competitive**: Top open-source models perform competitively with closed-source models in terms of instruction following and physics adherence.

5. **Domain-Specific Challenges**: Most models struggle with autonomous driving, human activities, and robotics categories while performing well on natural domains.

6. **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks**: VBench does not effectively distinguish between videos based on their adherence to physical laws, as shown by the weak correlations between VBench metrics and physics adherence.

### 7. Benchmark Dataset

The WorldModelBench dataset consists of 350 image and text condition pairs carefully designed to test various aspects of video world modeling:

1. **Domain Coverage**: The dataset covers 7 application-driven domains:
 - Robotics
 - Driving
 - Industry
 - Gaming
 - Human Activities
 - Animation
 - Natural

2. **Subdomain Diversity**: Within these domains, there are 56 diverse subdomains, as illustrated in the circular diagram.

3. **Instruction Types**: The dataset includes both text-to-video (T2V) and image-to-video (I2V) prompts, allowing for comprehensive evaluation of both types of models.

4. **Physics Challenges**: The prompts are designed to test various physical phenomena, including fluid dynamics, solid mechanics, gravity, and object interactions.

The benchmark includes model performance rankings across different dimensions, providing a clear overview of which models excel at different aspects of video world modeling.

### 8. Model Improvement Through Judger Alignment

One of the most promising findings from the paper is that aligning with human annotations by maximizing the scores from the fine-tuned judger improves the world modeling capability of video generation models. The researchers demonstrated this by:

1. Training a visual language model (VLM) on the collected human annotations
2. Using the VLM to output scores for individual grading criteria
3. Applying the fine-tuned judger as a reward model to improve video generation

The fine-tuned judger was shown to accurately predict world modeling violations, achieving lower error rates than larger models like GPT-4o despite being much more lightweight.

Examples demonstrate how models improved through judger alignment show better temporal consistency (left) and instruction following (right).

Additional examples further illustrate the improvements in fluid physics and object deformation.

### 9. Implications and Applications

The introduction of WorldModelBench has several important implications for the field of video generation:

1. **Application-Driven Development**: By focusing on application domains like robotics, autonomous driving, and gaming, WorldModelBench encourages the development of video generation models that can be practically deployed in these areas.

2. **Physics-Aware Generation**: The emphasis on physics adherence pushes the field toward models that better understand and respect physical laws, which is crucial for simulation-based applications.

3. **Instruction-Following Capabilities**: The benchmark's focus on instruction following encourages the development of models that can better follow user prompts, making them more useful for real-world applications.

4. **Enhanced Evaluation Methods**: The introduction of a fine-tuned judger that aligns with human preferences provides a more reliable automated evaluation method than existing metrics.

5. **Identifying Research Gaps**: The benchmark helps identify specific areas where current models fall short, such as understanding fluid dynamics or object interactions, guiding future research efforts.

The potential applications of improved video world models include:

- **Robotics**: Training robots in simulated environments that accurately reflect real-world physics
- **Autonomous Driving**: Developing self-driving systems that can accurately predict and respond to dynamic environments
- **Gaming**: Creating more realistic and engaging game worlds
- **Simulation**: Generating accurate simulations for scientific and engineering purposes

### 10. Conclusion

WorldModelBench represents a significant advancement in the evaluation of video generation models by specifically targeting their capabilities as world models. The benchmark goes beyond assessing visual quality to evaluate how well these models understand and adhere to physical laws and follow instructions.

The findings reveal that while current video generation models have made impressive progress in producing visually appealing content, they still face significant challenges in accurately modeling physical interactions and following complex instructions. Even the best-performing models show substantial room for improvement.

The introduction of a fine-tuned judger aligned with human preferences not only provides a reliable automated evaluation method but also demonstrates a path toward improving video generation models through reward-based learning. This approach has shown promising results in enhancing both instruction following and physics adherence.

WorldModelBench provides researchers and developers with a valuable tool for comparing and analyzing different video generation models, guiding future development toward more robust and reliable video world models. As these models continue to evolve, they have the potential to transform various applications that rely on accurate simulation and prediction of physical environments.
