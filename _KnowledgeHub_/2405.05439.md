# How Generalizable Is My Behavior Cloning Policy? A Statistical Approach to Trustworthy Performance Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> A statistical framework offers robust and sample-efficient evaluation of robot learning policies, particularly for generalization in out-of-distribution environments, by providing tight worst-case bounds on performance with user-specified confidence using minimal real-world rollouts.

## Problem

- Current robot policy evaluations often rely on small numbers of empirical rollouts, providing insufficient statistical significance or reliability for real-world deployment.
- Unpredictable performance degradation in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments is difficult to quantify rigorously, with human intuition often proving unreliable.
- Existing statistical bounds are frequently loose, demanding excessive costly real-world rollouts, and often only bound scalar performance metrics, which can mask critical failure modes.

## Method

- The research develops a rigorous statistical framework that employs optimal confidence bounds, specifically the Uniformly Most Accurate (UMA) bound for binary outcomes and a tighter Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz (DKW) bound for continuous metrics.
- A novel, tractable method for computing the Maximum Expected Shortage (MES) is introduced, which quantifies the tightness of the statistical bounds.
- The framework enables users to specify desired confidence and tightness levels, then automatically calculates the minimum number of costly real-world policy rollouts required to meet these criteria.

## Results

- Simulation experiments validated the framework's theoretical guarantees, showing that generated confidence bounds consistently and accurately reflected true OOD performance distributions.
- Hardware experiments with a pouring task demonstrated the framework's ability to provide statistically backed lower bounds for policy success rates (e.g., 3.5% for a 'harmful' OOD setting vs. 64.1% for a 'benign' setting with 95% confidence).
- The framework successfully compared two state-of-the-art policies (RT-2 and VC-1) on hardware, concluding with 95% confidence that RT-2 significantly outperformed VC-1 across three OOD settings due to disjoint confidence intervals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal confidence bounds can provide tight, worst-case guarantees on the entire distribution of black-box robot policy performance, moving beyond simple scalar metrics.
> - Quantifying bound tightness through Maximum Expected Shortage (MES) allows for an optimal trade-off between confidence, tightness, and the number of expensive real-world experiments.
> - Robot policy generalization to out-of-distribution settings is often counter-intuitive, underscoring the necessity of objective, statistically rigorous evaluation over anecdotal evidence or optimistic empirical averages.
