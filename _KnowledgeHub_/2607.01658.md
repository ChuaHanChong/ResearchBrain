# Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models What to See and Where to Look

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called DriveTeach-VLA guides vision-language-action models to develop spatially-grounded visual attention for autonomous driving, leading to improved trajectory prediction. This approach achieved a 90.4 PDM-Score on the NAVSIM benchmark and a 0.30m L2 distance error with a 0.12% collision rate on nuScenes.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving rely heavily on text-centric supervision, leading to spatially ungrounded visual attention that lacks precise spatial dependencies and motion understanding.
- These models often fail to intrinsically identify and prioritize traffic-critical objects or understand which spatial regions in an image correspond to feasible driving paths.
- The learned representations capture semantic knowledge but often lack the action-grounded perception crucial for reliable trajectory planning, leading to diffuse and unfocused attention.

## Method

- Introduce **DriveTeach-VLA**, a dual-model system consisting of a ==TGP-Prompter== and a ==TGP-Planner==, which follows a vision-guided learning sequence: "what to see" (DVD pretraining) "where to look" (TGP-guided SFT) "how to act" (TGP-guided GRPO).
- Employ ==Driving-aware Vision Distillation (DVD)== pretraining, which uses ==bbox-augmented images== and ==self-distillation==, to inject traffic-specific perceptual priors into the vision encoder, teaching the model "what to see".
- Utilize ==2D Trajectory-Guided Prompts (2D-TGP)== during ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== and ==reinforcement learning (GRPO)== to explicitly guide the model's attention towards feasible driving regions, teaching it "where to look" and improving action-grounded planning.

## Results

- Achieved a **PDM-Score of 90.4** on the NAVSIM benchmark in a single inference pass and **92.7** with a best-of-N strategy, outperforming state-of-the-art end-to-end and VLA methods.
- Demonstrated superior trajectory prediction on the nuScenes benchmark with an average **L2 distance error of 0.30 m** and a **collision rate of 0.12%** over a 3-second horizon.
- Qualitative and quantitative analysis confirmed that DriveTeach-VLA's vision encoder produced more spatially grounded attention maps focused on traffic-critical objects compared to baselines, with ablation studies validating the contributions of DVD pretraining and 2D-TGP guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding visual attention using traffic-critical object bounding boxes and projected 2D expert trajectories significantly improves the spatial grounding of VLA models for autonomous driving.
> - A structured, vision-guided learning pipeline, starting with object awareness and progressing to trajectory-aligned spatial focus, effectively bridges the gap between perception and planning in end-to-end driving models.
> - Decoupling the generation of spatial guidance (TGP-Prompter) from the final trajectory planning (TGP-Planner) allows for a more robust and precise integration of visual information into the MLLM's decision-making process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous Driving (AD) research has traditionally followed a modular design, segmenting the driving process into distinct perception, prediction, and planning components. While this modularity offered interpretability and control, it was susceptible to error propagation between modules and exhibited limited cross-task reasoning capabilities.

Recent advancements in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have inspired a shift towards unified, end-to-end autonomous driving systems, capable of mapping raw sensor data directly to trajectory planning outcomes. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent paradigm within this trend, integrating perception, prediction, and planning. These models typically process RGB camera images and predict future vehicle trajectories, often by formulating trajectory generation as a decoding task. This involves either autoregressively generating text-based waypoints or employing an auxiliary planner (e.g., a diffusion model) conditioned on MLLM features.

The training of advanced AD-VLA models commonly follows a three-stage pipeline:
1. **VQA Pretraining:** Initial training on Visual Question Answering (VQA) data, often pseudo-labeled, to inject general driving priors. This stage frequently emphasizes tasks like scene description, traffic condition reasoning, and action explanation.
2. **Imitation Learning (IL) with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Training with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning data and expert trajectories to enable end-to-end trajectory generation.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** A more recent addition, often using algorithms like GRPO, to further align predicted trajectories with human driving preferences, encompassing aspects such as comfort, safety, and traffic rule compliance.

Despite their ability to provide linguistically interpretable reasoning, a critical limitation in existing AD-VLA models has been identified: their reliance on text-centric supervision, particularly in VQA and CoT reasoning, which primarily focuses on semantic question answering rather than action-grounded spatial reasoning. This can lead to learned representations that capture semantic knowledge but lack the precise spatial dependencies and motion understanding crucial for reliable trajectory planning. Consequently, models may learn to identify objects without understanding their relevance to navigation or where to focus attention for planning purposes, resulting in spatially ungrounded visual attention, as observed in prior VLA models. This work positions itself by addressing this fundamental gap through explicit spatial guidance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the identified gap between perception and planning in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving. The authors observe that while current VLA models can process visual information and generate linguistic explanations or meta-behaviors, their underlying visual representations often lack the crucial spatial and motion dependencies required for accurate trajectory planning. This deficiency stems from their training data being predominantly text-centric, emphasizing semantic reasoning over action-grounded perception.

Specifically, the motivations and objectives are:

1. **Address Spatially Ungrounded Attention:** The paper highlights that attention mechanisms in existing VLA models, trained primarily on text-based VQA and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, tend to be diffuse and lack specific spatial grounding relevant to driving tasks. The objective is to cultivate focused, planning-aware visual attention.
2. **Teach "What to See":** Existing VLA models, using vision encoders pretrained on general natural images, often lack intrinsic knowledge about which objects are critically relevant for driving (e.g., other vehicles, pedestrians, traffic lights, lane markings). A key objective is to inject driving-specific perceptual priors into the vision encoder, guiding it to identify and prioritize traffic-critical elements.
3. **Teach "Where to Look":** Beyond identifying relevant objects, the models need to understand which spatial regions in the image correspond to feasible or optimal driving paths. The motivation is to provide explicit spatial conditioning that aligns with viable driving trajectories, thereby connecting visual perception directly to planning actions.
4. **Enhance Action-Grounded Planning:** By integrating explicit "what to see" and "where to look" guidance, the authors aim to move VLA models beyond mere linguistic reasoning to achieve more precise, spatially-grounded, and ultimately more reliable trajectory prediction.
5. **Develop a Vision-Guided Learning Pipeline:** The overarching objective is to propose and implement a structured training framework, DriveTeach-VLA, that systematically introduces these vision-centric priors throughout the learning process, evolving from general visual understanding to precise action generation.

In essence, the research aims to transform VLA models from merely "naming what is seen" to "understanding what should be seen and where to look" for effective autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **DriveTeach-VLA**, is designed to explicitly teach VLA models what to see and where to look. It operates as a dual-model system, comprising a **TGP-Prompter** and a **TGP-Planner**, both built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-3B MLLM backbone. The overall pipeline follows a vision-guided learning sequence: "what to see" (DVD pretraining) → "where to look" (TGP-guided SFT) → "how to act" (TGP-guided GRPO).

#### 3.1. TGP-Prompter: Driving-aware Vision Distillation (DVD) Pretraining

The TGP-Prompter is responsible for generating feasible driving region guidance. Its training, termed DVD pretraining, aims to inject traffic-specific perceptual priors without relying on text-centric VQA data.

- **Bbox-augmented Image Self-Distillation for ViT Encoder:**
 - **Critical Object Detection:** An open-vocabulary detector, Grounding DINO, is employed to identify traffic-critical objects (e.g., cars, pedestrians, traffic lights, barriers) in raw camera images.
 - **Bbox Augmentation:** The detected bounding boxes are overlaid onto the raw images, creating "bbox-augmented images."
 - **Self-Distillation:** A student-teacher architecture is used. A "teacher ViT" processes the bbox-augmented images, while a "student ViT" processes the corresponding raw images. Both are initialized from the same MLLM's ViT encoder.
 - **Feature Alignment:** Block-wise alignment (e.g., 2x4 blocks) is applied to the feature maps of the student and teacher ViT encoders. A Smooth-L1 loss (`L_distill`) minimizes the difference between the mean features of corresponding blocks, enabling the student encoder to internalize traffic-relevant visual cues from the augmented images.
 - **Teacher Update:** The teacher ViT's weights are updated using Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student ViT's weights, a technique adapted from DINO.

- **2D Trajectory-Guided Prompt (2D-TGP) SFT for MLLM Decoder:**
 - **BEV to 2D-TGP Conversion:** Expert trajectories in world (Bird's-Eye-View, BEV) coordinates are projected onto the 2D image plane using a pinhole camera model. These projected 2D trajectories, including x, y coordinates and yaw angle, are termed "ground-truth 2D-TGP." The yaw angle is retained for potential inverse projection.
 - **SFT for Prompter's Decoder:** The TGP-Prompter's MLLM decoder is supervised to predict this ground-truth 2D-TGP trajectory as a sequence of text tokens, using a cross-entropy loss (`L_TGP`).
 - **Overall Training Objective:** The DVD pretraining loss combines both components: `L_DVD = L_distill + λ_TGP · L_TGP`, where `λ_TGP` is a hyperparameter balancing the two objectives.

#### 3.2. TGP-Planner: TGP-guided Learning and Reasoning

The TGP-Planner is responsible for predicting the future BEV trajectory, conditioned on the visual input, ego-state, driving instruction, and the 2D-TGP guidance generated by the TGP-Prompter.

- **Initialization:** The TGP-Planner is initialized with the weights of the TGP-Prompter, inheriting the learned driving-related visual priors.
- **TGP-guided Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:**
 - **Pseudo-labeling:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B is used to generate pseudo-labeled step-by-step CoT reasoning data.
 - **Four Sub-tasks:** The CoT process includes:
 1. **Critical Object Detection:** Identifying objects influencing the ego vehicle's path.
 2. **Natural Language Explanation:** Describing the expert trajectory and its rationale.
 3. **Meta-Behavior Selection:** Classifying the overall driving behavior (e.g., accelerate, turn left).
 4. **Future Trajectory Prediction:** This task is modified to explicitly incorporate 2D-TGP keypoints as spatial guidance, instructing the model to attend to specific regions along the feasible path.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The TGP-Planner is trained using SFT on this CoT reasoning data, learning to generate both reasoning steps and future trajectories.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with GRPO:**
 - Following the SFT stage, the TGP-Planner undergoes further optimization using GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization).
 - **Reward Function:** Trajectory predictions are evaluated using the PDM-Score (PDMS), which incorporates metrics such as No-at-fault Collisions (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Ego Progress (EP), Time to Collision (TTC), and Comfort (C). This stage aligns the model's predictions with human driving preferences.
- **Teacher Forcing Training and Inference Pipeline:**
 - **Training:** During both CoT-SFT and GRPO-RL, ground-truth 2D-TGP is provided to the TGP-Planner as a condition. This "teacher forcing" ensures precise spatial guidance during the learning of driving skills.
 - **Inference:** In real-world operation or testing, the TGP-Prompter first generates the estimated 2D-TGP. This predicted 2D-TGP is then fed into the TGP-Planner to generate the final BEV trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The DriveTeach-VLA framework was evaluated on two autonomous driving benchmarks: NAVSIM (non-reactive closed-loop) and nuScenes (open-loop).

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **NAVSIM Benchmark:**
 - DriveTeach-VLA achieved a PDM-Score (PDMS) of **90.4** on the Navtest split in a single inference pass. This result surpassed several state-of-the-art end-to-end and VLA-based methods, including RecogDrive (89.6), ImagiDrive-S (86.9), and AutoVLA (89.1).
 - When employing a best-of-N (N=12) strategy with a DrivorR selector model, DriveTeach-VLA reached **92.7 PDMS**, outperforming AutoVLA's best-of-N result (92.1).
 - In terms of Extended PDMS (EPDMS), DriveTeach-VLA also reported a leading score of **85.4**, further increasing to **89.0** with the best-of-N selector.

- **NuScenes Benchmark:**
 - On open-loop trajectory prediction, DriveTeach-VLA achieved an average L2 distance error of **0.30 m** and a collision rate of **0.12%** over a 3-second horizon (using ST-P3 metrics).
 - Using UniAD metrics, it achieved an L2 error of **0.60 m** and a collision rate of **0.31%**.
 - These results indicated superior performance compared to previous methods like UniAD (0.69m L2, 0.12% collision), VAD (0.37m L2, 0.14% collision), and Impromptu VLA (0.33m L2, 0.13% collision), demonstrating enhanced precision and safety in trajectory prediction.

#### 4.2. Qualitative and Ablation Analysis

- **Attention Analysis of Vision Encoders:**
 - Qualitative visualizations showed that a CoT-SFT trained Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline exhibited dispersed attention, while DriveTeach-VLA produced spatially grounded attention maps focused on traffic-critical objects.
 - Quantitatively, DriveTeach-VLA's TGP-Planner recorded a mean Attention Mass (AM) score of 3.19 × 10⁻², compared to the baseline's 2.28 × 10⁻², with higher standard deviation (3.77 × 10⁻² vs. 3.12 × 10⁻²), indicating more concentrated and relevant attention.

- **Hyperparameter Analysis (λ_TGP and B_k):**
 - Ablation studies on the `λ_TGP` hyperparameter (balancing 2D-TGP learning and self-distillation during DVD pretraining) showed that a moderate value of **0.10** yielded the best overall PDMS (87.6), indicating an optimal balance.
 - The choice of block partition `B_k` for feature alignment in DVD significantly impacted performance. A **2×4 block partition** achieved the best PDMS (87.6), suggesting that larger receptive fields during distillation effectively capture highlighted regions, whereas patch-by-patch alignment performed worse (86.5).

- **Ablation of DVD Pretraining and 2D-TGP Guidance:**
 - The baseline QwenVL-2.5-3B model achieved 84.8 PDMS.
 - Adding conventional VQA pretraining followed by CoT-SFT increased performance to 86.4 PDMS.
 - Replacing VQA with DVD pretraining and CoT-SFT (TGP-Prompter only) improved it to 87.3 PDMS.
 - Introducing 2D-TGP guidance during CoT-SFT further boosted performance to 88.2 PDMS.
 - The final addition of GRPO reinforcement learning significantly raised the PDMS to **90.4**. This incremental improvement highlights the contributions of both DVD and 2D-TGP.

- **Train-Test Gap Analysis of Predicted 2D-TGP:**
 - A comparison between using predicted 2D-TGP and oracle ground-truth 2D-TGP during inference showed a negligible performance drop of only 0.4 PDMS (90.4 vs. 90.8). This indicates that the TGP-Prompter generates sufficiently accurate spatial guidance and the teacher-forcing strategy does not introduce a significant bottleneck.
 - Analysis of PDMS across different L2 errors between predicted and ground-truth 2D-TGP showed that PDMS remained robust across a range of prediction inaccuracies, gradually declining as errors increased, confirming the policy's robustness.

- **Robustness of DVD:**
 - DVD demonstrated robustness to moderate noise in Grounding DINO detections (20% random bbox drop or jitter perturbation), maintaining performance (86.8/87.1 PDMS vs. 86.2 w/o DVD). However, severe noise (40% drop/jitter) could degrade guidance.

- **Efficiency and Dual-Model Design Ablation:**
 - The dual-model architecture of DriveTeach-VLA (Prompter-Planner) resulted in a higher inference latency (3.03s on H100) compared to a Prompter-only baseline (1.14s).
 - However, a single-model multi-turn QA variant performed worse (87.0 PDMS) than the dual-model Prompter-Planner (88.2 PDMS, without GRPO), suggesting benefits from decoupling the two tasks.
 - Despite the increased latency from the dual-model, DriveTeach-VLA was found to be faster than AutoVLA (3.18s vs. 3.95s for action token variant, 7.65s for text waypoint variant), implying that its vision-grounded spatial enhancement reduces the reliance on extensive CoT reasoning tokens.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DriveTeach-VLA framework introduces several significant contributions and holds potential impact for the field of autonomous driving and vision-language models:

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation in VLA Models:** The work directly confronts the issue of spatially ungrounded attention in existing VLA models, which primarily rely on text-centric training. By explicitly incorporating vision-centric guidance ("what to see" and "where to look"), it helps VLA models develop a more robust and relevant understanding of the visual scene for planning purposes.
2. **Enhanced Trajectory Prediction Performance:** The achievement of state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like NAVSIM and nuScenes demonstrates the practical effectiveness of the proposed methods. This indicates that DriveTeach-VLA can generate more precise, safer, and compliant driving trajectories.
3. **Novel Vision-Guided Learning Pipeline:** The introduction of Driving-aware Vision Distillation (DVD) and 2D Trajectory-Guided Prompts (2D-TGP) establishes a new paradigm for training VLA models. This approach moves beyond generic VQA pretraining by injecting explicit, driving-specific visual priors and spatial conditioning, which could be adapted to other vision-language-action tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning.
4. **Improved Model Interpretability and Grounding:** By guiding the model's attention to critical objects and feasible driving regions, DriveTeach-VLA enhances the visual grounding of the MLLM's decision-making process. This could lead to more interpretable autonomous driving systems, where the model's "reasoning" is more visibly aligned with observable scene elements.
5. **Efficiency through Visual Context:** The finding that vision-grounded spatial enhancement can reduce the need for extensive linguistic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tokens is notable. Despite a dual-model architecture, DriveTeach-VLA demonstrates competitive or superior runtime compared to some baselines that rely more heavily on text-based reasoning, suggesting a path toward more efficient and visually intelligent VLA models.
6. **Advancement of End-to-End Autonomous Driving:** By refining the ability of VLA models to integrate perception, prediction, and planning in a more spatially aware manner, this research contributes to the development of more reliable and robust end-to-end autonomous driving systems, potentially accelerating their deployment in real-world scenarios.
