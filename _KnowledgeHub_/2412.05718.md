# RLZero: Direct Policy Inference from Language Without In-Domain Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> RLZero, developed by researchers at The University of Texas at Austin and collaborators, presents a framework that enables reinforcement learning agents to directly infer policies from natural language instructions or cross-embodied video demonstrations, eliminating the need for in-domain supervision or test-time training. The framework achieved an 83.2% win rate against the best baseline for language-to-behavior tasks and generated policies approximately 400 times faster than competing methods.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods struggle with the complexity and cost of designing effective reward functions, leading to reward hacking and limiting scalability.
- Many language-conditioned RL approaches require extensive, costly in-domain supervision (task-specific labels or expert demonstrations) or time-consuming training for each new task at inference time.
- Developing generalist RL agents that can understand and execute tasks described intuitively by humans (e.g., natural language, arbitrary videos) remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **RLZero** utilizes a three-step '==Imagine, Project, Imitate==' framework to translate language or video into policies without in-domain supervision or test-time training; the 'Imagine' step employs a multimodal video-foundation model (InternVideo2) to generate a video depiction of the task from a language prompt.
- The 'Project' step grounds these imagined (or directly provided cross-embodied) video observations into the agent's real observation space using semantic similarity-based retrieval with an off-the-shelf VLM (SigLIP).
- The 'Imitate' step leverages a pre-trained Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) to infer a closed-loop policy that matches the state visitation distribution of the projected observation sequence in a zero-shot, gradient-free manner.

## Results

- For language-to-behavior tasks in continuous control, RLZero achieved an **83.2%** win rate against the best baseline (GenRL) when evaluated by a multimodal LLM (GPT-4o) and showed statistically significant improvement in distribution matching returns.
- RLZero demonstrated substantial inference efficiency, generating a policy in approximately 25 seconds, which is about 400 times faster than baselines that required 3-5 hours of training per task.
- The framework successfully performed cross-embodied imitation from YouTube or AI-generated videos, achieving an **80%** win rate against SMODICE for Stickman and a **100%** win rate for HumEnv.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unsupervised generative video models can effectively translate natural language instructions into visual task imaginations, bypassing the need for supervised video-text alignment on specific tasks.
> - Semantic similarity-based retrieval using general-purpose vision-language models can robustly ground abstract imagined or out-of-domain video demonstrations into an agent's specific observation space.
> - Pre-trained successor-measure-based Behavior Foundation Models allow for a closed-form, zero-shot inference of policies for arbitrary reward functions (derived from projected trajectories), making policy generation instantaneous and gradient-free.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes, but a significant challenge lies in the design of effective reward functions. Experts often find it difficult to engineer reward functions that align precisely with intended task specifications, leading to issues like "reward hacking" where agents exploit the reward function in ways unintended by humans. This difficulty restricts the scalability of RL agents and limits their accessibility to users without expertise in reward design.

Natural language is recognized as an intuitive and expressive channel for human intent, offering an alternative to manual reward specification. Prior research has explored language-conditioned RL, but these approaches typically fall into categories that present their own limitations:
1. **Costly Supervision**: Many methods require extensive collection and annotation of behavior-language pairs within the agent's specific domain. This process is resource-intensive and impractical for scaling across diverse tasks and environments.
2. **Test-Time Training**: Other approaches necessitate training an RL agent for each new language instruction at inference time. This can be computationally expensive, time-consuming, and unsafe in real-world deployments.
3. **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as Reward Functions**: Recent work has used pretrained VLMs to infer reward functions from language instructions. However, these inferred rewards can still be subject to hacking or require subsequent RL training.
4. **Video Plan Generation**: Some methods generate video plans using diffusion models or energy models. These often require in-domain labeled trajectories for fine-tuning or rely on APIs, inverse dynamics models, or goal-conditioned policies to convert plans into actions, typically resulting in open-loop action sequences.

RLZero addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that aims for *direct policy inference from arbitrary natural language instructions* without requiring *in-domain supervision* (i.e., task-specific labels or expert demonstrations) and *without test-time training*. It positions itself as a step towards true generalist agents that can solve new tasks described naturally by humans, by leveraging large-scale multimodal foundation models for language-to-state translation and unsupervised RL for zero-shot policy generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable reinforcement learning agents to understand and execute tasks specified in natural language, directly translating language commands into behaviors without requiring further training or extensive human supervision. This overarching goal is motivated by several core challenges and aspirations in the field of RL:

1. **Mitigating the Reward Design Challenge**: The fundamental difficulty in designing effective reward functions is a major bottleneck for RL. Reward engineering is complex, prone to misalignment with human intent, and can lead to unintended "reward hacking" behaviors. The paper seeks to bypass this challenge by utilizing natural language as a direct task specification medium.
2. **Reducing Supervisory Costs**: Current language-conditioned RL methods often rely on costly human supervision, such as manually labeled trajectories or expert demonstrations for each specific task in each environment. This dependence on in-domain, task-specific data collection is a significant barrier to scaling RL to a wide array of tasks and environments. RLZero aims to eliminate this requirement, operating without labeled trajectories or expert demonstrations.
3. **Enabling Zero-Shot Policy Inference**: Many existing methods require substantial training of the RL agent for every new language command at inference time. This is inefficient, computationally expensive, and impractical for real-time human-agent interaction. A key motivation is to develop a method that can instantly generate policies in a "zero-shot" manner, meaning without any gradient updates or additional environmental interaction during testing.
4. **Leveraging Foundation Models for Generalization**: The recent advent of large-scale multimodal foundation models, trained on vast amounts of internet data, offers powerful capabilities for understanding and generating content across modalities (e.g., text, images, video). The authors are motivated to harness these models to address the "language-to-state" translation aspect of task specification, specifically by generating visual imaginations of tasks from text.
5. **Utilizing Unsupervised RL for Skill Acquisition**: Concurrently, advancements in unsupervised RL methods, particularly those based on successor features or measures (Behavior Foundation Models - BFMs), have demonstrated the ability to learn a broad repertoire of behaviors from unlabeled, offline interaction data. These models can then infer near-optimal policies for arbitrary reward functions in a zero-shot manner. The motivation is to integrate these BFMs to efficiently translate imagined or demonstrated states into executable policies.
6. **Achieving Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: A broader aspiration is for agents to learn from demonstrations not confined to their own embodiment or domain. The paper is motivated to explore if the proposed framework can extend to generating policies from cross-embodied video demonstrations (e.g., human videos for a robotic agent), further reducing the need for domain-specific data.

In summary, RLZero's objectives are to provide a direct, efficient, and supervision-free mechanism for translating natural language instructions (and cross-embodied videos) into agent behaviors, thereby making RL agents more accessible, scalable, and capable of generalized task execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RLZero framework operates in three distinct, sequential steps: Imagine, Project, and Imitate, designed to achieve zero-shot policy inference from natural language without in-domain supervision.

#### 3.1. Imagine: Unsupervised Generative Text-Conditioned Video Modeling

This initial step addresses the challenge of translating a natural language instruction into a visual representation of the desired task.
- **Purpose**: Given a natural language instruction (e.g., "do a lunge"), the agent generates a sequence of video frames that visually depicts the described task.
- **Tool**: The method utilizes a Multimodal Video-Foundation Model (ViFM), specifically InternVideo2, in an unsupervised video modeling approach.
- **Process**:
 1. The natural language prompt is encoded into a common embedding space using the ViFM's language encoder ($f_l$).
 2. An aligner module ($f_\psi$) processes these embeddings to bridge the multimodality gap between video and text embeddings.
 3. A generative video model (VM), simplified to a GRU architecture for video generation, conditions on these aligned latent embeddings to generate a sequence of image observations ($i_1, i_2..., i_T$). This model is trained to reconstruct original observation frames from an offline dataset without requiring text labels.
 4. Temporal embeddings (one-hot encodings of timesteps) and a world model connector are used to initialize and update deterministic and stochastic latent states, which are then decoded into video frames.
- **Key Feature**: The training of the video generation model does not require text labels mapped to in-domain task examples, relying instead on large-scale internet data and unsupervised learning.

#### 3.2. Project: Grounding to Agent’s Observation Space

The imagined video sequence, generated from general internet data, might not be perfectly representative of the agent's specific environment, potentially containing noise, unrealizable dynamics, or different embodiments. This step grounds these imagined observations into the agent's actual observation space.
- **Purpose**: Convert the imagined video frames into a sequence of real, dynamically feasible observations from the agent's operational environment.
- **Method**: Semantic-similarity based retrieval.
- **Tool**: An off-the-shelf vision-language embedding model, SigLIP, which is pretrained on internet-scale datasets for similarity matching.
- **Process**:
 1. Both the imagined video frames ($i_t$) and the observations from the agent's offline interaction dataset ($d_O$) are mapped into a shared latent embedding space using SigLIP.
 2. For each imagined frame (or a stack of k previous frames to capture temporal information like velocity and acceleration), the algorithm searches the offline dataset for the real observation ($o_t$) that has the highest cosine similarity in the embedding space.
 3. This retrieval process yields a sequence of real observations, and their corresponding proprioceptive states, that closely match the imagined behavior.
- **Flexibility**: This projection step also allows for direct policy inference from *cross-embodied video demonstrations* (e.g., YouTube videos) by bypassing the "Imagine" step and directly using the external video as the input for projection.

#### 3.3. Imitate: Zero-Shot Distribution Matching with Unsupervised RL

With a grounded sequence of target observations (and states), the final step is to derive a policy that can execute this behavior. Directly cloning these states might be difficult if they are dynamically infeasible or not present as complete trajectories in the offline dataset.
- **Purpose**: Obtain an executable, closed-loop policy that mimics the state visitation distribution of the projected observation sequence.
- **Tool**: A pre-trained Behavior Foundation Model (BFM), specifically a successor measure-based unsupervised RL agent (e.g., Forward-Backward).
- **Pre-training BFM**: The BFM ($\phi(s)$, $\pi_z$) is pre-trained on a dataset $d_O$ of reward-free, offline interactions from the agent's environment. This BFM learns a state representation $\phi(s)$ and a latent-conditional policy $\pi_z$, capable of producing near-optimal policies for any reward function defined in the span of its learned features, in a zero-shot manner (without gradient updates).
- **Process - Imitation as Distribution Matching**:
 1. The problem of finding a policy to imitate the projected trajectory is framed as a distribution matching objective: minimize the divergence $D(\rho_{\pi_z}(s), \rho_E(s))$, where $\rho_E$ is the state visitation distribution of the projected "expert" trajectory.
 2. The key insight is that for successor-measure based BFMs, an approximate *closed-form solution* exists to find the optimal latent vector $z_{imit}$ without any further gradient steps or interaction with the environment.
 3. This is achieved by formulating a reward function $r_{imit}(s)$ based on the distribution ratio between the expert projected states ($\rho_E(s)$) and the offline dataset states ($\rho(s)$), specifically $r_{imit}(s) = \rho_E(s)/\rho(s)$.
 4. Using this shaped reward, the optimal latent $z_{imit}$ can be directly computed as $E_{\rho_E}[\phi(s)]$, leveraging the BFM's ability to map reward functions to policy latents.
- **Efficiency**: This "zero-shot" inference means that once the BFM is pre-trained, a policy for any new task (specified by a projected observation sequence) can be generated almost instantaneously, contrasting with methods requiring iterative training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates RLZero across various continuous control tasks, focusing on two primary settings: Language to Behavior generation and Cross-embodied Video to Behavior generation. Crucially, none of the experiments assume in-domain supervision (task-specific annotations or expert demonstrations).

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Environments**: DeepMind Control Suite (Cheetah, Walker, Quadruped, Stickman) and HumEnv (3D Humanoid). These environments offer diverse and complex dynamics with varying state (17-358 dimensions) and action (6-69 dimensions) spaces.
- **Offline Data Collection ($d_O$)**: For DM-control environments, 5-10 million reward-free transitions were collected using a random exploration algorithm (RND). Stickman additionally augmented this with data from trained walking and running policies to increase behavior diversity.
- **BFM Training**: Forward-Backward was used for DM-control environments. For HumEnv, an existing open-source BFM trained on MoCap datasets was leveraged.
- **Baselines**:
 - **Image-language reward (VLM-RM)**: Offline RL (TD3 and IQL) trained with rewards derived from SigLIP cosine similarity between image stacks and text embeddings.
 - **Video-language reward**: Offline RL (TD3 and IQL) trained with rewards from InternVideo2 cosine similarity between video segments and text embeddings.
 - **GenRL**: A model-based RL approach that generates video plans from text and then trains a new policy for each task using model-based RL.
- **Evaluation Protocol**:
 - **Qualitative (Primary)**: Multimodal LLM (GPT-4o) as a judge. For each task, GPT-4o compared videos of behaviors generated by RLZero and baselines against the task prompt (or original video for cross-embodiment), providing a preference (win rate). Videos were subsampled to manage token limits.
 - **Quantitative (Secondary)**: Average return under a distribution-matching reward function. This reward is defined by a discriminator trained to distinguish projected states (from imagination or demonstration) from states in the offline dataset. A higher return indicates better matching of the target behavior.

#### 4.2. Benchmarking Language to Continuous Control

- RLZero was evaluated on 25 tasks across the 4 DM-control environments.
- **Qualitative Performance**: RLZero achieved an 83.2% win rate against the best performing baseline (GenRL, 76.8% win rate) when evaluated by GPT-4o.
- **Quantitative Performance**: RLZero demonstrated statistically significant improvement over baselines in terms of distribution matching returns.
- **Efficiency**: A key finding is RLZero's computational efficiency at inference time. While baselines like GenRL required approximately 3-5 hours of training on an NVIDIA-A40 GPU per task, RLZero output a policy in approximately 25 seconds due to its gradient-free, zero-shot inference mechanism. RLZero also uses a single, pre-trained policy model, making it parameter-efficient compared to baselines that train a new policy for each task.

#### 4.3. Video to Continuous Control: Cross-Embodied Imitation

- The semantic similarity-based projection step allows RLZero to generalize to out-of-domain video demonstrations. This bypasses the "Imagine" step.
- Evaluated on 17 tasks using YouTube/AI-generated videos for Stickman (2D Humanoid) and HumEnv (3D Humanoid).
- **Baseline**: SMODICE, an offline imitation learning method that uses state-only observational data. GenRL was not applicable here due to the lack of a world model for HumEnv.
- **Performance**: RLZero achieved an 80% win rate against SMODICE for Stickman and a 100% win rate for HumEnv, demonstrating its capability for successful direct cross-embodiment transfer.

#### 4.4. Ablation and Limitations

- **Imagination-free RLZero**: An ablation study explored directly mapping language embeddings to the BFM's latent space, bypassing video generation. This variant achieved a 65.71% win rate compared to the full RLZero's 91.4% (on a subset of Walker tasks), indicating that the explicit imagination step contributes positively to performance.
- **Failure Cases**:
 - **Limited Video Generative Model**: The video generation model, being relatively small, struggled with complex behaviors and was sensitive to prompt engineering. Its inability to imagine a behavior directly limited the agent's ability to imitate it.
 - **Semantic Search-based Image Retrieval**: The SigLIP-based retrieval occasionally failed due to:
 - **Background Distractors**: The similarity metric sometimes focused on background features rather than the agent's pose.
 - **Rough Symmetries**: In environments with highly symmetric agents, retrieval could yield incorrect permutations of poses (e.g., swapping similar-looking limbs).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLZero framework represents a notable advance in the field of reinforcement learning by addressing several long-standing challenges in task specification and policy acquisition. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Human-RL Communication**: RLZero directly links intuitive human communication channels (natural language and visual demonstrations) to agent behavior. This makes RL agents more accessible to non-experts and could facilitate more natural human-robot interaction.
2. **Overcoming Reward Engineering and Hacking**: By enabling direct policy inference from language or video, the framework bypasses the arduous and often problematic process of manual reward function design. This inherently reduces the risk of reward hacking, where agents exploit flaws in engineered reward signals.
3. **Achieving Zero-Shot, Efficient Policy Generation**: The ability to generate policies without any test-time training or gradient updates is a substantial contribution. This "zero-shot" capability dramatically reduces the computational cost and time associated with deploying RL agents for new tasks, moving towards truly instantaneous policy inference. This makes the system more practical for real-world applications where rapid adaptation is crucial.
4. **Leveraging Foundation Models**: RLZero effectively integrates the power of large-scale multimodal foundation models (for imagination and projection) with the skill discovery capabilities of unsupervised RL. This demonstrates a synergistic approach to combining advanced perception/generation with robust control.
5. **Facilitating Cross-Embodiment Learning**: The framework's ability to derive policies from cross-embodied video demonstrations (e.g., human videos for a robot) significantly expands the potential sources of learning data. This could unlock vast repositories of internet videos as implicit expert demonstrations, reducing the need for costly and specialized in-domain data collection.
6. **Interpretability of Failures**: The modular "imagine, project, imitate" structure provides interpretable failure points. If a policy fails, it is possible to diagnose whether the video generation model misimagined the task or if the projection step was inaccurate, which can guide future improvements.
7. **Foundation for Generalist Agents**: By enabling agents to perform a variety of tasks from diverse, intuitive instructions without specific supervision, RLZero contributes to the long-term vision of creating generalist AI agents capable of operating across a wide range of tasks and environments.

In conclusion, RLZero offers a practical and efficient paradigm for task specification in RL, potentially accelerating the deployment of advanced autonomous agents in complex, human-centric environments.
