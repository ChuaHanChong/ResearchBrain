# 3DVLA: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models via 3D Spatial and Instance Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed 3DVLA, a plug-and-play framework enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with robust 3D scene understanding without requiring additional manual labels. The framework achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of 86.0% on LIBERO-Plus and improved success rates on RoboTwin 2.0 by up to 8.1 percentage points on hard tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on 2D visual inputs, leading to a lack of comprehensive 3D scene understanding.
- This 2D-centric approach results in weak 3D spatial position extraction, insufficient 3D instance understanding, and fragile reasoning under occlusion, hindering precise 6-DoF pose reasoning and robust interaction.
- Directly integrating mature 3D perception methods into VLA pipelines is challenging due to architectural incompatibilities and the need for costly 3D annotations, potentially sacrificing benefits of VLM pre-training.

## Method

- **3DVLA** introduces a "plug-and-play" framework to inject robust 3D reasoning into pretrained VLAs by leveraging multi-view spatial fusion for globally consistent 3D representations.
- It incorporates an object-centric 3D instance module to extract physical entities directly in 3D space, overcoming limitations of 2D bounding boxes and providing explicit instance awareness.
- A coordinate-driven 3D ==self-supervised== predictor is repurposed to synthesize occluded geometries, enhancing reasoning under partial visibility, while spatially-conditioned geometry aggregation integrates 3D information into the action expert.

## Results

- 3DVLA achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **86.0%** on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, demonstrating enhanced resilience to spatial and visual perturbations compared to existing baselines.
- On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, the framework improved success rates for hard tasks by **6.9** percentage points (from **16.3%** to **23.2%**) with the \pi_0 baseline and by **3.1** percentage points (from **39.0%** to **42.1%**) with the X-VLA baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each proposed module—multi-view spatial fusion, object-centric 3D instance understanding, and the coordinate-driven 3D self-supervised predictor—contributes significantly to the overall performance gains and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly lifting 2D visual features into a unified, globally consistent 3D memory via multi-view spatial fusion establishes robust spatial grounding for VLA models.
> - An object-centric approach that directly extracts and encodes physical entities as high-level 3D instance tokens provides more precise and reliable spatial understanding compared to 2D-only methods.
> - Repurposing a masked self-supervised predictor for 3D completion allows VLA models to infer unseen object parts, significantly improving robustness in situations with partial occlusion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a notable advancement in robotic manipulation. By leveraging extensive vision-language pre-training, these models have demonstrated capabilities in performing diverse tasks across various robotic embodiments. This progress indicates their potential for general-purpose robotics. However, a fundamental limitation in the current VLA paradigm is its predominant reliance on 2D visual inputs, which results in a lack of comprehensive 3D scene understanding. This deficiency restricts their effectiveness in real-world environments that inherently demand robust spatial reasoning.

The paper identifies three interconnected challenges stemming from this 2D-centric approach:
1. **Weak 3D spatial position extraction and absence of multi-view consistency**: Existing VLA architectures typically process single-view observations or loosely integrate multiple views without explicitly reconstructing 3D coordinates or ensuring consistency across different viewpoints. This impedes the policy's ability to ground decisions in a metric 3D reference frame, thus limiting precise 6-DoF pose reasoning.
2. **Insufficient 3D instance understanding**: VLAs tend to interpret objects as flat image regions, lacking information about their volumetric extent, 3D boundaries, or fine-grained geometry. This makes tasks requiring precise spatial interaction, such as grasping specific object parts or relative placement, challenging as the model cannot reliably differentiate object instances in 3D space.
3. **Fragile reasoning under occlusion**: When task-relevant object parts are partially obscured due to viewpoint changes, state-of-the-art VLAs exhibit a significant drop in performance. This vulnerability arises because purely 2D models cannot infer unseen geometry or maintain a coherent spatial memory, leading to actions based on incomplete visual information.

While mature 3D perception methods exist, their direct integration into VLA pipelines is hindered by architectural incompatibilities. Such integration often necessitates a complete redesign, potentially discarding the benefits of VLM pre-training, including generalization and language grounding. Furthermore, many 3D perception models depend on costly instance-level or semantic annotations, which are difficult to acquire at scale for robotic manipulation tasks.

This research aims to bridge this gap by introducing a "plug-and-play" framework, 3DVLA, designed to inject robust 3D reasoning into existing pretrained VLAs. The key distinction of this work is its approach to enhancing 3D understanding without requiring additional manual annotations or discarding the valuable priors learned during VLM pre-training. It also repurposes the predictor from masked self-supervised learning, typically discarded after pre-training, for visual token completion at occluded 3D locations. This positions 3DVLA as an integrative solution within the VLA landscape, seeking to overcome the 3D understanding barrier while preserving the advantages of current VLM-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to augment Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with robust 3D scene understanding capabilities to improve their performance in robotic manipulation tasks, particularly in complex and unconstrained real-world environments. This overarching goal is driven by the recognition that current VLA models, which largely operate on 2D visual inputs, face fundamental limitations in spatial reasoning.

The motivation for 3DVLA stems from addressing three specific, interconnected challenges:

- **Addressing Weak 3D Spatial Position Extraction and Lack of Multi-View Consistency:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing VLA architectures typically process visual information from single views or loosely fuse multi-view data. They often do not explicitly reconstruct objects in 3D space or enforce geometric consistency across different camera perspectives. This means the robotic policy cannot establish a precise, metric 3D reference frame for objects, hindering its ability to reason about and execute actions requiring accurate 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) poses. For instance, tasks requiring precise placement or manipulation of objects in relation to their surroundings become problematic.
 - **Objective:** To enable robust 3D spatial grounding by establishing explicit multi-view consistency throughout the model. This involves lifting 2D visual features into a unified 3D memory and enforcing consistent 3D geometric relations across all input modalities.

- **Addressing Insufficient 3D Instance Understanding:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard VLA models perceive objects primarily as 2D image regions or bounding boxes. They lack an understanding of an object's volumetric extent, its precise 3D boundaries, or its fine-grained geometry. Consequently, the model struggles with tasks that demand specific interactions, such as grasping a particular component of an object or arranging multiple objects relative to each other's actual 3D forms. This limitation arises because the model cannot reliably distinguish individual object instances or their spatial properties in a 3D context.
 - **Objective:** To incorporate an object-centric approach that allows for the direct extraction and encoding of physical entities in 3D space. This includes generating high-level 3D instance tokens that explicitly represent object identity and geometry, thereby providing the policy with awareness of distinct objects and their 3D characteristics.

- **Addressing Fragile Reasoning Under Occlusion:**
 - **Motivation:** In real-world robotic scenarios, objects frequently become partially or fully occluded due to camera viewpoints or other objects. Current 2D-based VLA models are susceptible to severe performance degradation when task-relevant parts of an object are occluded. This brittleness is attributed to their inability to infer unseen geometry or maintain a coherent spatial memory, leading to action generation based on incomplete and potentially misleading visual cues. This highlights the need for a mechanism that can explicitly reconstruct the unseen portions of objects.
 - **Objective:** To integrate a mechanism that can synthesize occluded geometric structures. This is achieved by repurposing a masked self-supervised 3D encoding branch and its predictor to complete visual tokens at occluded locations, enabling the model to reason about invisible parts of objects during task execution.

A critical underlying motivation for all these objectives is to develop a solution that is "plug-and-play." This means the framework must be compatible with existing VLM pre-training architectures and capable of enhancing them without necessitating extensive architectural redesign, discarding valuable VLM priors, or requiring additional, costly manual instance-level annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, 3DVLA, is a plug-and-play framework designed to inject robust 3D reasoning into pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It operates without requiring additional manual labels or discarding existing VLM priors. The framework is composed of four interconnected components: Multi-View Spatial Fusion, Object-Centric 3D Instance Module, Coordinate-Driven 3D Self-Supervised Predictor, and Spatially-Conditioned Geometry Aggregation.

#### 3.1. Multi-View Spatial Fusion

This component aims to create a unified and globally consistent 3D representation from multi-view 2D feature maps.
- **Process**: 2D feature maps from multiple views are flattened into an unordered sequence. Their unprojected 3D coordinate grids are used to ground these visual features in absolute 3D space via a coordinate projection network, which generates 3D position embeddings. These position-aware features are then processed by a multi-layer Transformer Encoder.
- **Multi-View Consistency**: The use of absolute spatial embeddings within the Transformer inherently establishes cross-view geometric correspondences, resulting in a globally consistent 3D memory.
- **Relative Distance Priors**: For fine-grained relative distance reasoning, Continuous 3D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) is applied exclusively to the attention queries and keys, imposing strict Euclidean relative-distance priors.

#### 3.2. Object-Centric 3D Instance Module

This module extracts physical entities directly in 3D space, moving beyond the limitations of 2D bounding boxes and masks.
- **3D Probe Decoding and Multi-Scale Sampling**:
 - The module initializes a set of state probes, each comprising a semantic state vector and a 3D reference point.
 - The globally consistent 3D memory (M) is decoded into a multi-scale semantic pyramid and a high-resolution map for mask supervision.
 - Each 3D reference point is projected to individual camera views using intrinsic and extrinsic matrices, with a depth clamping mechanism to prevent numerical instability.
 - These projected 2D coordinates serve as sampling pivots for extracting visual features from the multi-scale semantic pyramid via Deformable Cross-Attention. This process generates view-specific features for each probe.
 - To manage varying 2D projections of a single 3D entity, view-dependent bounding box and mask regressions are conditioned on view-specific positional encodings, enabling a shared 3D semantic state to handle perspective variations.
- **Occlusion-Aware Gating and Bounded Coordinate Refinement**:
 - An adaptive gating mechanism evaluates the validity of each view's feature for a given probe, suppressing unclear observations. The semantic state of each probe is residually updated by aggregating these gated, normalized view features.
 - The 3D reference point of each probe is iteratively refined using a bounded coordinate refinement mechanism, which employs a tanh activation scaled by a physical factor. This prevents spatial divergence and ensures stable 3D centroid convergence.
- **Global 3D Matching and Joint Optimization**:
 - Label assignment is conducted entirely in 3D space to eliminate cross-view ID conflicts. Pseudo ground-truth 2D bounding boxes and masks, generated by a frozen external perception model, are unprojected to derive 3D centroids.
 - A global matching cost between probes and targets is computed, averaged across all valid views, incorporating classification, 2D boundaries, and 3D distance costs. The Hungarian Algorithm is then used for optimal assignment.
 - The network is optimized using a combination of six loss components: focal loss for classification, L1 and GIoU losses for 2D bounding boxes, cross-entropy and DICE losses for 2D masks, and an L1 loss for 3D positions, ensuring robust 3D anchoring.

#### 3.3. Coordinate-Driven 3D Self-Supervised Predictor

This component addresses reasoning under occlusion by repurposing a masked self-supervised predictor for 3D geometry completion.
- **Target Coordinate Formulation and Geometric Completion**:
 - During training, the model uses Mask Coordinates for self-supervised distillation and Completion Coordinates for synthesizing unobservable geometries. Asymmetric multi-view masking forces the network to infer missing structures using cross-view geometric priors.
 - Completion Coordinates are generated by back-projecting 2D masks into a raw point cloud, denoising, and using a pre-trained completion network to predict dense shapes. Unobservable regions are isolated and uniformly sampled.
 - During inference, masking is disabled, and the predictor directly queries continuous space with Completion Coordinates.
- **Position-View Conditioned Hybrid Attention**:
 - A Hybrid 3D Attention Mechanism is employed, which decouples identity injection from relative distance encoding. It injects a continuous Fourier 3D positional encoding and discrete camera view embedding into both visible context and target queries.
 - Continuous 3D RoPE is applied to the Query and Key matrices to incorporate Euclidean distance priors.
 - Each predictor block sequentially uses Hybrid Attention for cross-attention (extracting features from unmasked context) and self-attention (exchanging spatial information within target queries) to align structural consistencies.
- **Distillation Objective and Anti-Collapse Regularization**:
 - Representations are distilled from an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher network to stabilize continuous feature regression.
 - The self-supervised loss includes Smooth-L1 for magnitude alignment, cosine distance for semantic angle alignment (preventing norm reliance), and a variance regularization loss to avoid spatial homogeneity and feature collapse.

#### 3.4. Spatially-Conditioned Geometry Aggregation

This final component explicitly embeds the derived 3D geometries as affordance-aware instance tokens for the downstream action expert, maintaining the plug-and-play design.
- **End-Effector Centric Relative Positional Encoding**:
 - Object positions are transformed into end-effector-centric relative offsets (Δp = p_obj - p_ee).
 - These offsets are expanded using sinusoidal functions across multiple frequencies and projected via an MLP to match the token dimension.
 - The resulting affordance embeddings are added to the corresponding instance and completion tokens, which are then concatenated with visual tokens and fed into the action expert.
- **Uncertainty-Guided Instance Geometric Routing**:
 - This mechanism adaptively injects 3D geometry into instance probes based on physical proximity and detection uncertainty.
 - **Spatial-Biased Local Context**: For each instance, K-nearest completion points are retrieved. Their local geometric context is aggregated via cross-attention, with an attention bias determined by relative physical offsets. This ensures attention to semantically matched and physically adjacent geometries.
 - **Confidence-Modulated Routing**: An uncertainty score, derived from the 2D classification logit of the instance, quantifies observation ambiguity. A dynamic routing gate, conditioned on the instance feature, local 3D context, and uncertainty score, controls the injection of 3D geometry. The gate's final layer is zero-initialized with a negative bias, ensuring minimal interference with confident 2D features at the start of training. The instance representation is updated by blending with the normalized local geometric context via this gate.

#### 3.5. Two-Stage Training Strategy (Appendix Details)

The training process is decoupled into two stages for stable optimization:
- **Stage 1 (Spatial Fusion and Instance Extraction)**: Focuses on learning view-consistent physical entities by training the Multi-View Spatial Fusion module, 3D Instance Module, and the action expert.
- **Stage 2 (3D Completion and Geometry Aggregation)**: Freezes weights from Stage 1 and trains the 3D Self-Supervised Predictor, Geometry Aggregation module, while continuing to update the action expert.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of 3DVLA was evaluated on two robotic manipulation benchmarks: LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0, using success rate as the primary metric. The findings demonstrate consistent and substantial improvements over baseline VLA models.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Transfer on LIBERO-Plus

On the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, which features seven perturbation categories (e.g., Camera, Layout, Background, Light), 3DVLA achieved an average success rate of 86.0% when integrated with the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline. This represents a new state-of-the-art performance, surpassing $\pi_{0.5}$'s original 84.2%.
- **Perturbation Resilience**: 3DVLA demonstrated enhanced resilience to both spatial shifts (e.g., Camera from 71.2% to 75.6%, Layout from 84.3% to 86.6%) and visual perturbations (e.g., Light from 94.7% to 97.4%, Background from 94.0% to 97.7%). These gains are attributed to the framework's ability to anchor features in continuous 3D space for multi-view consistency and to reconstruct occluded geometries.

#### 4.2. Comprehensive Manipulation on RoboTwin 2.0

On RoboTwin 2.0, which emphasizes physics-rich, contact-heavy manipulation, 3DVLA demonstrated its plug-and-play compatibility and effectiveness by integrating with two different VLA baselines, $\pi_0$ and X-VLA.
- **Improvements with $\pi_0$**: When integrated with $\pi_0$, 3DVLA improved the success rate from 46.4% to 54.5% on Easy tasks and from 16.3% to 23.2% on Hard tasks.
- **Improvements with X-VLA**: When integrated with X-VLA, success rates increased from 70.0% to 72.6% on Easy tasks and from 39.0% to 42.1% on Hard tasks.
- **Efficiency**: These improvements were achieved with an addition of approximately 0.086 billion parameters and negligible computational overhead, underscoring the method's practical viability.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Systematic ablation experiments were conducted to evaluate the contribution of each proposed module.

- **Progressive Integration**:
 - **3D Instance Module (Model A)**: Incorporating the 3D Instance Module into the $\pi_0$ baseline improved RoboTwin 2.0 Hard task performance from 16.3% to 18.9% and raised the LIBERO-Plus average success rate to 85.0%. This supports the benefit of extracting entities directly in 3D space.
 - **Coordinate-Driven 3D Self-Supervised Predictor (Model B)**: Adding this predictor further improved $\pi_0$ on RoboTwin 2.0 Hard tasks to 21.8% and LIBERO-Plus average to 85.7%. A notable gain in the LIBERO-Plus "Camera" split (71.4% to 73.6%) indicates its role in handling occlusions.
 - **Uncertainty-Guided Instance Geometric Routing (Model C - Full 3DVLA)**: The full 3DVLA framework achieved peak performances, pushing LIBERO-Plus average to 86.0% and RoboTwin 2.0 Hard tasks to 23.2%. A +2.0% absolute improvement in LIBERO-Plus "Camera" variations (73.6% to 75.6%) highlights the benefit of dynamically fusing local 3D geometric context based on detection uncertainty.

- **Micro-Ablations on 3D Instance Module**:
 - **Without Spatial Fusion**: Removing the voxel-free spatial fusion component led to a performance drop (85.0% to 84.6% average on LIBERO-Plus), indicating the necessity of multi-view consistency for coherent memory.
 - **2D Matching vs. Global 3D Matching**: Replacing global 3D bipartite matching with heuristic 2D matching reduced the average success rate to 84.5%, demonstrating that 2D matching can reintroduce cross-view ID conflicts.
 - **Without End-Effector Relative Positional Encoding**: Omitting this component degraded performance to 84.8%, validating that providing direct, actionable spatial offsets relative to the robot's gripper is more effective than forcing the policy to learn complex kinematic transformations implicitly.

- **Micro-Ablations on Masking Ratio for Self-Supervised Training**:
 - **25% Masking**: This lower ratio yielded sub-optimal results (85.5% average), suggesting the network might bypass true 3D reconstruction by interpolating features from adjacent visible 2D pixels.
 - **50% Asymmetric Masking (used in Model B)**: This ratio explicitly forced the predictor to engage in cross-view spatial reasoning, leading to the highest structural fidelity and overall performance (85.7% average).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, 3DVLA, addresses a critical limitation in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating robust 3D scene understanding capabilities. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Advancement in VLA Capabilities**: 3DVLA demonstrably enhances the performance of VLA models in complex robotic manipulation tasks. By addressing the fundamental deficiency of 2D-centric perception, it enables VLAs to operate more effectively in real-world environments characterized by diverse viewpoints, partial occlusions, and varied scene layouts. This moves VLA models closer to fulfilling their potential for general-purpose robotics.

- **Practicality and Compatibility**: A key contribution is the "plug-and-play" nature of 3DVLA. It integrates advanced 3D reasoning into existing VLA architectures without requiring the discarding of valuable priors from Vision-Language Model (VLM) pre-training or relying on costly, manually annotated 3D labels. This design principle makes 3DVLA a practical and accessible enhancement for the VLA community, as it minimizes the burden of architectural redesign and data acquisition.

- **Methodological Innovation in 3D Perception for Robotics**: The paper introduces several novel components:
 - **Pervasive Multi-View Consistency**: The Multi-View Spatial Fusion module effectively lifts 2D features into a unified 3D memory, establishing viewpoint-invariant representations crucial for robust spatial grounding.
 - **Object-Centric 3D Instance Understanding**: The Object-Centric 3D Instance Module with its high-level instance tokens provides explicit awareness of 3D object identities and geometries, surpassing the limitations of 2D-based reasoning.
 - **Repurposed Self-Supervised Learning for Occlusion Handling**: The Coordinate-Driven 3D Self-Supervised Predictor innovatively repurposes the predictor from masked self-supervised learning to synthesize occluded geometries. This mechanism allows VLAs to reason about unseen parts of objects, significantly improving robustness under partial occlusion.
 - **Task-Relevant Feature Aggregation**: The Spatially-Conditioned Geometry Aggregation, particularly with end-effector centric relative positional encoding and uncertainty-guided routing, ensures that 3D information is integrated into the action expert in a contextually relevant and robust manner.

- **Demonstrated Generalizability and Efficiency**: The consistent performance gains across multiple VLA baselines (e.g., $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, X-VLA) and challenging benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0) suggest the generalizability of 3DVLA. Furthermore, achieving these improvements with only a minor increase in parameters and computational overhead enhances its practical utility for real-world robotic systems.

In summary, 3DVLA represents a significant step towards developing more spatially intelligent and robust robotic policies. By enabling VLAs to genuinely "understand" scenes in 3D, it lays a foundation for more precise, adaptable, and generalizable manipulation capabilities, thereby expanding the potential applications of robotic systems in complex and dynamic environments.
