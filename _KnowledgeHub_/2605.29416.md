# 3DVLA: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models via 3D Spatial and Instance Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed 3DVLA, a plug-and-play framework enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with robust 3D scene understanding without requiring additional manual labels. The framework achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of 86.0% on LIBERO-Plus and improved success rates on RoboTwin 2.0 by up to 8.1 percentage points on hard tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on 2D visual inputs, leading to a lack of comprehensive 3D scene understanding.
- This 2D-centric approach results in weak 3D spatial position extraction, insufficient 3D instance understanding, and fragile reasoning under occlusion, hindering precise 6-DoF pose reasoning and robust interaction.
- Directly integrating mature 3D perception methods into VLA pipelines is challenging due to architectural incompatibilities and the need for costly 3D annotations, potentially sacrificing benefits of VLM pre-training.

## Method

- **3DVLA** introduces a "plug-and-play" framework to inject robust 3D reasoning into pretrained VLAs by leveraging multi-view spatial fusion for globally consistent 3D representations.
- It incorporates an object-centric 3D instance module to extract physical entities directly in 3D space, overcoming limitations of 2D bounding boxes and providing explicit instance awareness.
- A coordinate-driven 3D ==self-supervised== predictor is repurposed to synthesize occluded geometries, enhancing reasoning under partial visibility, while spatially-conditioned geometry aggregation integrates 3D information into the action expert.

## Results

- 3DVLA achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **86.0%** on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, demonstrating enhanced resilience to spatial and visual perturbations compared to existing baselines.
- On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, the framework improved success rates for hard tasks by **6.9** percentage points (from **16.3%** to **23.2%**) with the \pi_0 baseline and by **3.1** percentage points (from **39.0%** to **42.1%**) with the X-VLA baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each proposed module—multi-view spatial fusion, object-centric 3D instance understanding, and the coordinate-driven 3D self-supervised predictor—contributes significantly to the overall performance gains and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly lifting 2D visual features into a unified, globally consistent 3D memory via multi-view spatial fusion establishes robust spatial grounding for VLA models.
> - An object-centric approach that directly extracts and encodes physical entities as high-level 3D instance tokens provides more precise and reliable spatial understanding compared to 2D-only methods.
> - Repurposing a masked self-supervised predictor for 3D completion allows VLA models to infer unseen object parts, significantly improving robustness in situations with partial occlusion.
