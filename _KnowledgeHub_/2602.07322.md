# Action-to-Action Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> The Action-to-Action Flow Matching (A2A) method from Nanyang Technological University introduces a generative policy paradigm that utilizes historical proprioceptive actions as an informed starting point for action generation. It achieves sub-millisecond inference latency (0.56 ms), faster training convergence, and improved robustness to visual and initial state uncertainties compared to prior diffusion-based policies.

## Problem

- High inference latency of diffusion-based policies (dozens of iterative denoising steps) hinders real-time robotic control applications.
- Existing generative policies start action generation from uninformed random noise, neglecting the valuable temporal continuity and physical priors from historical proprioceptive feedback.
- Difficulty in effectively leveraging continuous proprioceptive and state feedback, which often gets diluted when combined with high-dimensional visual inputs.

## Method

- **A2A** introduces a new ==action-to-action flow matching== paradigm, initiating action generation from historical proprioceptive action sequences rather than random noise.
- Leverages ==flow matching== to learn a ==time-dependent vector field==, transporting a latent representation of past actions to a latent representation of future actions.
- Architecture features distinct pathways for visual conditioning and historical proprioceptive encoding, integrated with a ==flow net== and an ==action autoencoder== in a shared latent space.

## Results

- Achieved **sub-millisecond inference latency (0.56 ms)** for high-quality action generation in as few as **one step**, significantly outperforming generative and regression policies.
- Superior training efficiency: **100% success within 40 epochs** on "Close Box" and with only **30 trajectories** on "Pick Cube".
- Enhanced generalization and robustness to visual perturbations (background, illumination, viewpoint) and initial state uncertainty.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic control policies can achieve faster and more stable generation by leveraging continuous proprioceptive feedback as an informed prior, rather than starting from random noise.
> - Learning an optimal transport path between historical and future action latents significantly reduces the distributional gap, enabling effective single-step inference.
> - Decoupling visual and proprioceptive processing within a generative framework enhances robustness to visual perturbations and improves generalization in unseen environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in imitation learning have enhanced robotic system capabilities, particularly through diffusion-based policies. These policies, drawing inspiration from generative models like Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (Ho et al., 2020), frame action prediction as a conditional denoising process. They have demonstrated effectiveness in modeling the multi-modality inherent in human demonstrations and executing high-precision tasks (Chi et al., 2025; Lipman et al., 2023).

However, a significant limitation of standard diffusion models in robotics is their "denoise-from-scratch" paradigm, which involves initializing the generative process from random Gaussian noise. This approach typically requires numerous iterative denoising steps (often dozens) to produce clean actions, leading to substantial inference latency (Pan et al., 2026). This latency presents a major bottleneck for real-time robotic control, where low cycle times and rapid feedback are essential for stable and responsive execution.

Prior research has attempted to mitigate this inference speed limitation by improving the initialization of the diffusion process. For example, Wagenmaker et al. (2025) proposed using an RL-trained policy to guide the initial sampling distribution, while Scholz and Turner (2025) explored warm-start strategies to identify more informed starting points closer to the data distribution. These methods aim to replace uninformed Gaussian noise with more relevant priors, often through auxiliary models that predict distributional statistics conditioned on the current observation. While these approaches have shown some effectiveness, they generally still rely on stochastic noise initialization and frequently introduce additional modeling complexity.

The fundamental assumption challenged by this paper is the necessity of sampling from random noise for robot policy generation. Diffusion models originated in domains like high-fidelity image and video synthesis (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022), where meaningful priors are often absent, necessitating generation from uninformed noise. In contrast, modern robotic systems are equipped with rich state sensors that provide continuous, low-latency proprioceptive feedback (Jia et al., 2025b, 2024). This structured feedback encodes the robot’s current physical state and recent execution history, offering a potential principled alternative to random noise initialization.

Existing visuomotor policies, including both regression-based methods like ACT (Zhao et al., 2023) and generative methods like Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2025) or Flow Matching-based policies (Intelligence et al., 2025a), typically condition action generation on a direct concatenation of current proprioceptive states/actions and visual observations. This design often overlooks the temporal continuity intrinsic to robotic motion and can lead to the dilution of low-dimensional proprioceptive signals when fused directly with high-dimensional visual representations. Additionally, research suggests that explicit conditioning on proprioceptive states can negatively impact spatial generalization (Zhao et al., 2025a). Consequently, the rich historical information regarding system dynamics and action trends largely remains underexploited in prevalent frameworks.

This work positions itself by proposing that the inherent proximity between the distributions of past executions and future actions can be leveraged. By focusing on learning a direct mapping between these distributions, the goal is to reduce learning complexity, shorten the generative transport path, and achieve more stable policy generation, thereby addressing the latency and generalization limitations of current diffusion-based policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the performance limitations of existing diffusion-based robotic policies, primarily their high inference latency. While these policies have demonstrated success in complex manipulation tasks due to their ability to model multi-modal action distributions, their iterative denoising process from random Gaussian noise leads to computational overhead that makes them unsuitable for real-time control applications where rapid decision-making is critical.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Address High Inference Latency:** The primary objective is to overcome the substantial inference latency associated with the "denoise-from-scratch" paradigm of diffusion models. Generating a single action often requires dozens of iterative steps, which creates a bottleneck for robotic systems needing real-time responsiveness and low cycle times.
2. **Challenge the Necessity of Uninformed Noise Sampling:** The paper questions the fundamental assumption that robotic policies must be generated by sampling from random noise. Unlike image synthesis, robot control inherently benefits from continuous, low-latency proprioceptive feedback that captures the system's current physical state and recent execution history. This feedback represents a strong, structured prior that is currently underutilized.
3. **Exploit Temporal Continuity and Proprioceptive History:** Existing methods largely condition action generation on instantaneous observations or simple concatenations of current proprioceptive and visual data. This overlooks the temporal continuity inherent in robot motion and the rich historical information (e.g., past proprioceptive actions) that can inform future movements. The authors propose to explicitly leverage this historical context.
4. **Prevent Signal Dilution and Enhance Generalization:** Direct concatenation of low-dimensional proprioceptive signals with high-dimensional visual representations can lead to the proprioceptive information being "diluted" or overshadowed. Moreover, explicit proprioceptive conditioning has been shown to potentially harm spatial generalization. The work aims to design a policy that effectively uses complementary strengths of both modalities without these drawbacks, thus improving robustness to visual perturbations and generalization to unseen configurations.
5. **Develop an Efficient Action Generation Paradigm:** The overarching objective is to introduce "Action-to-Action Flow Matching (A2A)," a novel policy paradigm. A2A is designed to shift from random sampling to an initialization informed by previous proprioceptive actions. This approach seeks to bypass costly iterative denoising, capture robot dynamics, maintain temporal continuity, and enable high-quality action generation with significantly fewer (ideally single) inference steps, thereby achieving higher training efficiency, faster inference speed, and improved generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Action-to-Action Flow Matching (A2A) paradigm builds upon the algorithmic foundation of flow matching and introduces a novel approach to policy generation informed by historical proprioceptive actions.

#### 3.1. Algorithmic Foundation: Flow Matching

A2A adopts flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as its core generative mechanism. Flow matching is a simulation-free training objective designed to learn a continuous transformation from a source probability distribution (typically a standard Gaussian, $p_0 = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$) to a target data distribution ($p_1 = p_{\text{data}}$) over $\mathbb{R}^d$. This transformation is achieved by learning a time-dependent vector field $v: \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ that defines a probability path $p_\tau$. The evolution of a state $x_\tau$ along this path is governed by the ordinary differential equation (ODE) $dx_\tau/d\tau = v_\tau(x_\tau)$, where $x_0 \sim p_0$.

The optimal transport displacement map is utilized, which defines the path as $x_\tau = (1 - \tau)x_0 + \tau x_1$. A neural network $f_\theta(x_\tau, \tau, c)$, parameterized by $\theta$ and conditioned on external factors $c$ (e.g., visual observations), is trained to approximate this conditional vector field $v_\tau(x_\tau)$. The training objective is the flow matching loss: $L_{FM} = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim U, x_0 \sim p_0, x_1 \sim p_1} \|f_\theta(x_\tau, \tau, c) - v_\tau(x_\tau)\|^2$. During inference, actions are typically obtained by discretizing and integrating the learned vector field using methods like Euler integration.

#### 3.2. Action-to-Action Flow (A2A)

A2A fundamentally differs from previous generative policies by shifting the source distribution from Gaussian noise to a distribution derived from historical proprioceptive actions. The goal is to learn a policy that maps a sequence of historical actions to a sequence of future actions. The action formulation can be flexible, accommodating joint positions (used in simulation) or end-effector states (used in real-world experiments).

Given a sequence of historical proprioceptive actions $a_{\leq t} = \{a_{t-n+1}, \dots, a_t\}$ and visual observations $I_{\leq t} = \{I_{t-m+1}, \dots, I_t\}$, A2A aims to predict future actions $a_{>t} = \{a_{t+1}, \dots, a_{t+n}\}$. Here, $n$ and $m$ denote the action and observation horizons, respectively. The core idea is to transform the distribution of historical actions directly into the distribution of future actions within a shared high-dimensional latent space $Z$. This leveraging of physical continuity means the starting point for generation ($z_0$) is inherently closer to the target ($z_1$) in the latent space compared to starting from random noise. This reduced distributional gap simplifies the transport mapping, potentially enabling high performance with single-step inference using a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).

To introduce stochasticity and preserve multimodality, subtle noise (e.g., 0.1 STD Gaussian noise) can be injected into the historical actions $a_{\leq t}$ before encoding. This perturbation aims to enhance the model's generalization capabilities against action-level uncertainties, as shown in the supplementary material.

#### 3.3. Architectural Overview

The A2A architecture comprises three main components:

1. **Condition Path:** This path processes visual observations $I_{\leq t}$. It employs a ResNet-18 backbone followed by a linear projector to generate a global condition vector $c = \text{MLP}(E_I(I_{\leq t}))$.
2. **Source Path:** A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with a 5x5 kernel compresses the $n$-frame historical actions $a_{\leq t}$ into a compact latent starting point $z_0 = E_a(a_{\leq t})$. $E_a$ is the action encoder.
3. **Flow-based Generation Process:** The central component is the Flow Net, built with AdaLN-MLP blocks. This network predicts the vector field that transports the latent starting point $z_0$ to the target latent $z_1$ within a unified 512-dimensional latent space. A residual MLP decoder, $D_a$, then transforms $z_1$ into the predicted future action sequence $\hat{a}_{>t} = D_a(z_1)$. The action encoder $E_a$ and decoder $D_a$ effectively form an autoencoder for action trajectories.

#### 3.4. Learning Objectives

The total training objective $L_{\text{total}}$ is formulated as a multi-task loss to ensure both generation accuracy and physical consistency.

1. **Flow Matching Loss ($L_{FM}$):** This is the primary objective, focused on regressing the time-dependent vector field $f_\theta(z_\tau, \tau, c)$ within the latent space $Z$. It ensures the model learns the optimal transport path between the latent historical action $z_0$ and the latent target future action $z_1$.
 $L_{FM} = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim U, z_0, z_1} \|f_\theta(z_\tau, \tau, c) - v_\tau(z_\tau, \tau, c)\|^2$.
2. **Autoencoder Reconstruction Loss ($L_{AE}$):** An $\ell_1$ reconstruction loss is applied to the action autoencoder to ensure the latent space $Z$ accurately preserves the topological structure of the action space. This loss regularizes the encoder $E_a$ and decoder $D_a$ to maintain high-fidelity reconstruction of action chunks.
 $L_{AE} = \mathbb{E}_{a_{>t}} \|a_{>t} - D_a(E_a(a_{>t}))\|_1$.
3. **Inference Consistency Loss ($L_{IC}$):** Inspired by Gao et al. (2026), this loss bridges the gap between abstract latent generation and physical execution. It aligns the ODE-inferred latent vector $\hat{z}_1$ with the ground truth latent $E_a(a_{>t})$, and the decoded action $D_a(\hat{z}_1)$ with the ground truth action $a_{>t}$. This objective is considered critical for preventing latent space collapse.
 $L_{IC} = \mathbb{E}_{\hat{z}_1, a_{>t}} (\|\hat{z}_1 - E_a(a_{>t})\|_1 + \lambda_0 \|D_a(\hat{z}_1) - a_{>t}\|_1)$, where $\lambda_0$ is a weighting coefficient.

The total training objective is a weighted sum of these three losses: $L_{\text{total}} = \lambda_1 L_{FM} + \lambda_2 L_{AE} + \lambda_3 L_{IC}$. The hyperparameters for all experiments were set to $\lambda_0=0.5, \lambda_1=1, \lambda_2=0.5, \lambda_3=1$, with action and observation horizons $n=m=8$, and a batch size of 32.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of A2A was conducted comprehensively across both simulated and real-world robotic environments, benchmarking its performance against eight state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Tasks:** Five tasks were used: "Stack Cube" and "Pick Cube" from ManiSkill (Mu et al., 2021), "Close Box" from RLBench (James et al., 2020), and "Open Drawer" and "Pick-Place Bowl" from LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023a), all within the Roboverse platform (Geng et al., 2025a).
- **Real-World Tasks:** "Pick Cube" and "Open Drawer" tasks were performed on a Franka robot, utilizing dual-view visual input.
- **Baselines:** Eight methods were compared: DDPM-UNet, DDPM-DiT, DDIM-UNet, FM-UNet, FM-DiT, Score-UNet (generative diffusion/flow-based), ACT (Zhao et al., 2023) (regression-based Transformer), and VITA (Gao et al., 2026) (lightweight MLP vision-to-action). Hyperparameters and network scales (including a modified ACT with halved parameters) were standardized for fair comparison.
- **Metrics:** Training efficiency, inference cost (speed and steps), and generalization performance.

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency

- **Convergence Speed:** A2A demonstrated superior convergence speed. On the "Close Box" task, A2A achieved a stable 100% success rate within 40 training epochs, significantly faster than DDPM-UNet and FM-UNet.
- **Sampling Efficiency:** A2A maintained high performance as the number of demonstrations increased in the "Stack Cube" task, exhibiting stability. DDPM-UNet and FM-UNet showed more fluctuations, suggesting they require more training or model capacity for diverse trajectories, although they eventually converged with more epochs.
- **Real-World Performance with Limited Data:** In real-world "Pick Cube" and "Open Drawer" tasks, A2A-Flow achieved 100% in-distribution success with only 30 training trajectories, outperforming DDPM-UNet and FM-UNet.
- **Task Completion Time:** For the "Open Drawer" task, A2A achieved shorter completion times, while baselines exhibited hesitation.
- **Adaptation to Novel Scenarios:** A2A adapted rapidly to new pick-up locations with only 10 demonstrations per location, maintaining high success rates, even when objects were at the edge of the field-of-view, demonstrating superior data efficiency.
- **Overall Success Rates:** Across the 5 simulation tasks, A2A consistently achieved the highest success rates (Table 1). VITA also performed competitively, attributed to its inference consistency mechanism. ACT's performance was comparable to generative methods when using advanced transformer architectures, supporting recent observations by Pan et al. (2026).

#### 4.3. Inference Cost

- **Inference Steps vs. Success:** Increasing inference steps for A2A led to rapid improvements in success rates, but marginal gains diminished significantly beyond 4 steps.
- **Single-Step Inference Capability:** When restricted to a single inference step, A2A's success rate rose above 90% after 32 training epochs.
- **Latent Space Convergence Visualization:** t-SNE embeddings of history and future action latents showed a significant decrease in average distance between paired samples during training, and trajectories connecting these pairs aligned into increasingly parallel paths. This empirically supports the feasibility of single-step flow mapping and the rectilinearity of the learned flow.
- **Inference Latency:** A2A maintained inference latency below 1 ms, reaching 0.56 ms in the single-step inference regime on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 GPU. This efficiency is attributed to the compressibility of sampling steps and its MLP-based architecture, outperforming regression-based ACT due to pure MLP operations versus a Transformer backbone.

#### 4.4. Generalization Performance

- **Visual Uncertainty:** Tested across four levels of randomization in Roboverse ("Close Box" task): Level 0 (training), Level 1 (background textures), Level 2 (illumination), Level 3 (camera viewpoint).
 - A2A (6 steps) maintained a robust success rate of 30-40% on unseen Levels 1-3, consistently outperforming all baselines.
 - A2A (1 step) also exhibited superior generalization compared to other algorithms.
 - Real-world tests showed A2A maintained 80% success when picking an unseen glowing block, whereas baselines failed.
 - This robustness is attributed to a decoupled processing strategy, preventing low-dimensional proprioceptive signals from being overshadowed by high-dimensional visual features, and grounding the generation process in historical actions to enforce physical consistency.
 - Injecting 0.02 standard deviation (STD) Gaussian noise into historical trajectories further narrowed the generalization gap (20% to 52% on Level 1).
- **Initial State Uncertainty:** A2A, being temporally dependent on previous actions, was more sensitive to uncertainties in historical sequences compared to baselines that start from pure noise. However, injecting 0.1 STD Gaussian noise into historical actions significantly boosted its generalization performance against initial robot pose uncertainty. The paper also explored the relationship between noise intensity and success rate.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Regression vs. Generation:**
 - Both Flow-latent (A2A) and Reg-latent (deterministic regression in latent space) achieved high success rates on the training distribution.
 - However, the generative approach (Flow-latent) showed significantly higher resilience to environmental perturbations, while regression failed to generalize. This suggests that the generative aspect and the decoupled processing of action and visual inputs in A2A contribute to better generalization.
- **Action Space vs. Latent Space Flow Matching:**
 - Flow matching performed directly in the raw action space (using U-Net or MLP backbones) resulted in inferior convergence performance compared to the latent-space approach (Flow-latent).
 - This is attributed to the high-dimensional latent space effectively aligning initial and target distributions, facilitating a smoother learning process and enabling high performance even with single-step inference.

#### 4.6. Application to Video Generation

- **Frames-to-Frames (F2F):** The A2A paradigm was extended to video generation for future frame prediction, termed F2F.
- **Results:** F2F achieved significantly higher generation quality compared to a regression-based baseline with the same network configuration for predicting future frames in unseen scenarios of the "pick cube" task. This demonstrates the broader versatility of the A2A approach in temporal modeling tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Action-to-Action Flow Matching" (A2A) framework introduces a notable shift in the paradigm for diffusion-based robotic policies, addressing critical limitations related to inference latency and generalization.

#### 5.1. Contribution and Advantages

- **Reduced Inference Latency:** A2A's core innovation lies in replacing the "denoise-from-scratch" paradigm of traditional diffusion models with an "action-to-action" transport mechanism. By leveraging the physical continuity of sequential robot motions, it significantly shortens the generative path. This enables high-quality action generation in as few as a single inference step, reducing latency to sub-millisecond levels. This directly addresses the major bottleneck for real-time robotic control that has historically hindered the widespread adoption of diffusion policies.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The method achieves competitive or superior success rates across a diverse set of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks compared to a range of state-of-the-art diffusion, flow-matching, and regression-based baselines. It also demonstrates superior training efficiency and data efficiency, converging faster and performing robustly even with limited demonstration data.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** A2A exhibits enhanced robustness to visual perturbations (e.g., background textures, lighting changes, unseen objects) and improved generalization to unseen configurations. This is attributed to its history-informed initialization, which enforces physical consistency over time, and its decoupled processing of low-dimensional proprioceptive and high-dimensional visual signals, which prevents signal dilution.
- **Architectural Efficiency:** The design leverages a lightweight MLP architecture for the core flow-based generation in the latent space, contributing to its fast inference speed. The empirical evidence of latent space convergence validates the feasibility of single-step flow mapping.

#### 5.2. Broader Applicability

The paper also demonstrates the broader versatility of the A2A paradigm by extending it to video generation, referred to as Frames-to-Frames (F2F) flow matching. The success in predicting future video frames suggests that the underlying principle of leveraging temporal continuity for informed initialization is applicable beyond robotic control to other continuous temporal modeling tasks.

#### 5.3. Identified Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge several limitations that offer avenues for future research:
- **Action Space Specificity:** A2A is currently most effective for tasks involving smooth, continuous control signals due to its reliance on physical continuity. Its benefits are limited for discrete or switch-like action dimensions, such as binary gripper open/close commands. Future work could focus on extending A2A to support hybrid continuous-discrete action spaces.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** The current framework requires manual tuning of several loss-weighting coefficients ($\lambda_0, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$). Developing adaptive loss weighting mechanisms could improve ease of use and potentially performance.
- **Stochasticity Optimization:** While injecting subtle noise into historical actions improves generalization and multimodality, the optimal strategy for fusing clean historical data with Gaussian noise to balance determinism and stochasticity remains an open question for future exploration.
- **Integration with Predicted Video:** The successful application to video generation suggests a promising potential for integrating predicted video into the policy architecture to further augment the performance of A2A.

In summary, A2A presents a significant advancement for generative robotic policies by offering a highly efficient, robust, and generalizable approach that overcomes the traditional latency challenges, making diffusion-based methods more viable for real-time robotic applications.
