# Development of a Handheld Actuation Mechanism for a Tendon-driven Robotically Steered Guidewire

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed a handheld actuation mechanism for tendon-driven robotically steered guidewires, integrating a compact spooling system and motor controls into an ergonomic device. This mechanism enables precise feeding, retraction, rotation, and bending of the guidewire, demonstrating successful navigation in an anatomically accurate aorta phantom model without the buckling issues observed in previous designs.

## Problem

- Manual guidewire navigation in endovascular interventions is challenging due to tortuous vessels and limited tip steerability, leading to risks like vessel damage and post-surgical complications.
- Previous robotic guidewire actuation systems, including a prior spooling mechanism from the same group, required external motorized components for roll motion and possessed a non-ergonomic form factor.
- Designing compact yet powerful actuation systems for slender medical robots that can generate sufficient forces and ranges of motion for clinical use remains a challenge.

## Method

- Designed and fabricated a self-contained, **handheld actuation mechanism** (0.836 kg) that integrates a ==compact spooling system== capable of storing and dispensing up to 1.5 meters of robotic guidewire.
- Implemented ==three distinct motor-driven actuation systems==, controlled by an internal ==Teensy 4.0 microcontroller== and ==custom PCB==, to provide independent control over guidewire feeding/retraction, rotation, and tip bending.
- Developed an ==ergonomic user interface== featuring an integrated ==joystick== for translational and rotational control, and a ==momentary rocker switch== for guidewire bending, powered by an internal ==12V Li-ion battery==.

## Results

- Successfully developed and fabricated a handheld actuation device weighing **0.836 kg** that fully integrates all necessary components, eliminating the need for external motorized systems.
- Qualitatively demonstrated successful navigation of a robotic guidewire through an anatomically accurate abdominal aorta phantom model, executing precise feeding, retraction, rotation, and bending maneuvers.
- The developed mechanism effectively dispensed the guidewire throughout the phantom without buckling between the insertion point and the device, resolving a critical functional limitation of previous related work.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is feasible to integrate complex mechanical, electrical, and control systems for robotic guidewire manipulation into a compact, ergonomic handheld device suitable for clinical use.
> - Combining intuitive user controls (joystick, switch) with embedded actuation and spooling mechanisms can provide comprehensive and precise control over multi-degree-of-freedom robotic instruments.
> - Directly addressing prior design limitations, such as external component dependency and guidewire buckling, through an integrated system design significantly enhances the practical applicability and reliability of robotic medical tools.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) represent a significant global health challenge, accounting for a substantial number of deaths annually. Endovascular interventions are minimally invasive procedures widely employed for treating CVDs. These procedures typically involve a clinician manually navigating a guidewire through the vasculature to a target location, which then serves as a pathway for other medical devices like catheters and stents.

Manual guidewire navigation presents several challenges, including the complexity of navigating tortuous vessels and the limited steerability of conventional guidewire tips. These difficulties can lead to adverse events such as vessel damage, perforation, dissection, occlusion, and post-surgical complications like thrombosis. To mitigate these risks and enhance navigation efficiency, robotically steerable guidewires have emerged as a research focus. These devices are slender continuum robots designed to exhibit controlled bending motion at their tips.

Within the domain of robotically steerable guidewires, various actuation strategies exist. The present work specifically concentrates on tendon-driven robotically steerable guidewires. This approach is noted for its ability to generate sufficient actuation ranges and forces while maintaining a relatively compact form factor, which can be advantageous compared to other methods such as magnetic actuation.

This paper builds directly upon previous research by the same group. Their prior work focused on the design of a compact spooling mechanism capable of storing and dispensing clinically relevant lengths of tendon-driven robotically steerable guidewires. However, that mechanism was dependent on an external motorized system for roll motion and possessed a non-ergonomic form factor. The current research aims to address these identified limitations by integrating the compact spooling mechanism into a handheld, self-contained, and ergonomically designed actuation device, thereby advancing the practical applicability of tendon-driven robotic guidewires for clinical use. The development represents a step towards creating more autonomous and user-friendly robotic tools for endovascular interventions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is the development of a handheld actuation mechanism for tendon-driven robotic guidewires. This objective is driven by the aim to improve the safety and efficiency of endovascular interventions by providing clinicians with enhanced control over guidewire navigation.

The detailed objectives and motivations include:

- **Addressing Limitations of Manual Guidewire Navigation:** The current manual method of guidewire navigation can be challenging due to factors such as vessel tortuosity and the lack of steerability at the guidewire tip. This can lead to complications such as vessel damage, perforation, dissection, occlusion, and post-surgical thrombosis. The motivation is to reduce these risks by providing a robotically controlled alternative.
- **Integrating and Enhancing Previous Work:** The authors’ previous research introduced a compact spooling mechanism for tendon-driven robotic guidewires. However, this earlier design required an external motorized system for guidewire roll motion and was not ergonomically suitable for handheld use. A key objective of the current work is to integrate this spooling mechanism into a self-contained, handheld device, thereby overcoming the previous design’s reliance on external components and its non-ergonomic form factor.
- **Enabling Comprehensive Guidewire Control:** The mechanism is designed to allow clinicians to execute multiple degrees of freedom for guidewire manipulation. Specifically, the objective is to enable controlled feeding (advancement), retraction, rotation, and bending motions of up to 1.5 meters of the robotic guidewire. This broad control aims to facilitate precise navigation within complex vascular anatomies.
- **Achieving Handheld and Ergonomic Design:** A significant motivation is to develop a device that is compact enough to be handheld and features an ergonomic design. This focus on ergonomics intends to ensure that clinicians can use the device with comfort and ease, potentially increasing adoption and reducing user fatigue during prolonged procedures. The design integrates a joystick and momentary switch on the handle for intuitive control.
- **Providing Integrated Motorized and Potential Manual Control:** The proposed device aims to offer motorized control for guidewire steering while also allowing for potential manual rotation and translation if desired by the clinician. This hybrid control approach could offer flexibility during complex procedures.
- **Demonstrating Functional Navigation in a Relevant Model:** A practical objective is to demonstrate the functionality and effectiveness of the developed handheld mechanism through navigation experiments in an anatomically accurate phantom aorta model. This demonstration serves to validate the device's capabilities in a simulated clinical environment and to confirm its ability to dispense the guidewire without issues like buckling, which was a shortcoming in prior work.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research involved the design, fabrication, and experimental demonstration of a handheld actuation mechanism for a tendon-driven robotic guidewire.

**Device Design and Fabrication:**
The overall device is engineered to accommodate a robotic guidewire up to 1.5 meters in length and has a total weight of 0.836 kg.

- **Robotic Guidewire:** The guidewire itself features a 20 mm long bending segment fabricated from a 0.48 mm outer diameter (OD) nitinol tube. This segment is created by machining a unidirectional asymmetric notch (UAN) pattern into the tube using a femtosecond laser (WS-Flex Ultra-Short Pulse Laser Workstation, Optec, Frameries, Belgium). A 76 µm nitinol tendon is attached to the distal tip of the guidewire using epoxy (J-B Weld, Sulphur Springs, TX, USA). Tension applied to this tendon facilitates the actuation of the bending joint.
- **Spooling Mechanism:** The guidewire is secured within a main spool using a single screw. It is routed within a groove in the spool and continuously wraps upon itself during retraction. Once the procedure is complete, the guidewire can be removed by loosening the screw, allowing for catheter placement. Inside the main spool, a smaller internal spool is present for controlling the tendon. This tendon spool has an OD of 7.4 mm and a groove with an OD of 5 mm, and its rotation controls the bending of the guidewire tip.
- **Actuation Systems:**
 - **Feeding and Retraction:** The main spool incorporates an embedded gear design, which is rotated by a motor equipped with a driving gear. The gear ratio between the main spool and its driver gear is 29:6, enabling the controlled dispensing and retraction of the guidewire.
 - **Rotation:** The main spool is centrally attached to a supporting brace that can rotate. At the distal end of the brace's neck, an embedded gear controls the rotation of the guidewire. This brace gear interacts with a driver gear, with a gear ratio of 34:25. A polypropylene strip is placed above the spool to ensure the guidewire remains within its groove during operation.
 - **Bending:** The bending motion of the guidewire tip is controlled by the rotation of the internal tendon spool, which is actuated by a dedicated motor.
- **Electronics and Power:** All motors used in the device are sourced from Pololu Robotics and Electronics (NV, United States). They are powered by a 12 V Li-ion battery, which is housed below the rotation mechanism at the distal end of the device. Continuous rotational motion of each motor is facilitated by slip rings strategically placed throughout the device. The motors are controlled by a Teensy 4.0 microcontroller, which is co-located with a custom printed circuit board (PCB) equipped with motor drivers, positioned above the rotation mechanism.
- **User Interface:** The device features an ergonomic handle with an OD of 40 mm, designed for clinician comfort. This handle integrates a joystick and a two-state momentary rocker switch for controlling the guidewire’s movements.
 - **Joystick Control:** Moving the joystick towards the distal end of the device initiates guidewire dispensing (feeding), while moving it towards the proximal end causes retraction. Lateral movements of the joystick control the rotation of the guidewire.
 - **Momentary Switch Control:** Pressing the momentary rocker switch backward facilitates the bending of the guidewire by rotating the tendon spool. Pressing it forward unbends the guidewire.
- **Guidewire Routing:** The guidewire is routed from the spool through the center of the device via a polyimide tube (Zeus Company LLC, SC, USA).

**Experimental Demonstration:**
To demonstrate the functionality of the handheld actuation mechanism, a navigation experiment was conducted using an abdominal aorta phantom model (United Biologics Inc., CA, USA).

- **Setup:** The experimental setup involved mounting a camera above the phantom to record the guidewire's movement during navigation.
- **Procedure:** The robotic guidewire was introduced into the phantom through the left femoral artery. The objective was to navigate the guidewire towards the femoral bifurcation. Once at the bifurcation, the guidewire was bent across it, and then further advanced down the right iliac artery.
- **Assessment:** The experiment was qualitatively assessed based on the successful navigation of the guidewire through the phantom. Particular attention was paid to the ability of the mechanism to dispense the guidewire without buckling between the phantom insertion point and the mechanism exit, which had been identified as a critical limitation in previous related work.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research culminated in the successful development and qualitative demonstration of a handheld actuation mechanism for tendon-driven robotic guidewires.

**Device Development:**
- A handheld device integrating a compact spooling mechanism was developed. This mechanism is capable of storing and dispensing up to 1.5 meters of robotic guidewire.
- The total weight of the developed handheld device is 0.836 kg.
- The device incorporates three distinct motor-driven actuation systems, controlled by a Teensy 4.0 microcontroller and a custom PCB. These systems enable independent control over guidewire feeding/retraction, rotation, and tip bending.
- The user interface is designed for ergonomic operation, featuring an integrated joystick for translational and rotational control, and a momentary rocker switch for guidewire bending.
- The integration of all necessary components, including motors, battery, and control electronics, into a single handheld unit eliminates the need for external motorized systems, which was a limitation of prior spooling mechanism designs.

**Navigation Demonstration:**
- The developed handheld actuation mechanism successfully controlled a robotic guidewire to navigate through an anatomically accurate abdominal aorta phantom model.
- Specific maneuvers demonstrated included introducing the guidewire through the left femoral artery, navigating it to the femoral bifurcation, bending the guidewire across the bifurcation, and subsequently advancing it down the right common iliac artery.
- The qualitative assessment confirmed that the guidewire was successfully navigated throughout the phantom, demonstrating the functional capabilities of the handheld mechanism in controlling complex movements.
- A notable improvement over previous work was observed during the demonstration: the proposed mechanism was able to dispense the guidewire without experiencing buckling between the phantom insertion point and the mechanism exit. This addresses a critical shortcoming where manual intervention was previously required.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of medical robotics, particularly in the context of endovascular interventions, by developing a compact and integrated control solution for robotically steerable guidewires.

- **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency in Endovascular Procedures:** The development of a robotically steered guidewire mechanism with precise control over bending, rotation, and translation has the potential to mitigate the risks associated with manual guidewire navigation, such as vessel damage and procedural complications. This could lead to safer and more efficient endovascular interventions for cardiovascular diseases.
- **Advancement in Robotic Instrument Design:** The research demonstrates the feasibility of integrating complex mechanical and electronic systems, including a spooling mechanism, multiple motors, control electronics, and a battery, into a compact, handheld form factor. This represents an advancement in the design of self-contained, user-friendly robotic medical instruments.
- **Improved Usability and Ergonomics:** The ergonomic handle design, coupled with intuitive joystick and switch controls, aims to enhance clinician comfort and control during procedures. This focus on human-robot interaction is critical for clinical adoption and could reduce operator fatigue during long or complex interventions.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Prior Work:** By successfully integrating the spooling mechanism into a handheld device and eliminating the need for external motors and manual intervention during guidewire dispensing, the research addresses key limitations identified in previous iterations of the technology. This makes the system more practical and self-reliant.
- **Foundation for Clinical Translation:** The qualitative demonstration in an anatomically accurate phantom model suggests the device's potential for clinical application. The authors' stated future work, focusing on aspects such as guidewire replacement, sterilization, and further user comfort modifications, indicates a clear pathway towards increasing the clinical viability of this mechanism.
- **Potential for Training and Simulation:** Beyond direct clinical use, such a device could also serve as a valuable tool for training medical professionals in the navigation of complex vascular anatomies using robotic assistance, providing a realistic and controllable simulation platform.
