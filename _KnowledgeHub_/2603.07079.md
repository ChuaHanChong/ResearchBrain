# Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation of Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD) framework for language models mitigates diversity degradation and learning instability in standard distillation by adaptively applying forward Kullback-Leibler divergence in high-entropy teacher regions, improving mathematical reasoning performance and preserving the teacher's distributional uncertainty. For instance, a Qwen3-4B-Base student showed a +5.05 increase in Pass@8 compared to previous on-policy methods.

## Problem

- Standard on-policy distillation (OPD) methods often use reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, which exhibits a mode-seeking property.
- This mode-seeking behavior leads to diversity degradation and unstable learning in student models, particularly at token positions where the teacher's probability distribution has high entropy.
- Such instability hinders the student's ability to fully capture and transfer the teacher's diverse behavioral repertoire and uncertainty in complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- The paper introduces Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (**EOPD**), a framework that adaptively combines reverse KL and forward KL divergence objectives.
- **EOPD** applies the computationally efficient reverse KL for low-entropy regions, but selectively adds an approximated forward KL divergence term when the teacher's token-level entropy exceeds a predefined threshold.
- The forward KL divergence is efficiently approximated by focusing only on the teacher's top-k most probable tokens, renormalized to maintain a practical balance between accuracy and computational cost.

## Results

- EOPD consistently improved mathematical reasoning performance across various LLM sizes, with a Qwen3-4B-Base student achieving +1.80 in Avg@8 and +5.05 in Pass@8 compared to standard OPD.
- Student models trained with EOPD demonstrated enhanced out-of-domain generalization capabilities on tasks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro, even when trained solely on mathematical data.
- EOPD effectively preserved the teacher's high-entropy token distribution in the student, maintaining significantly more probability mass in the high-entropy region compared to other on-policy distillation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The mode-seeking nature of reverse KL divergence causes student models to become overconfident and lose diversity by ignoring the teacher's uncertainty in high-entropy token distributions.
> - Explicitly modeling and transferring the teacher's uncertainty, represented by high-entropy tokens, is crucial for improving the student's reasoning capabilities and its ability to explore diverse solution paths.
> - An adaptive distillation strategy that blends mode-seeking and mode-covering objectives based on teacher entropy allows for more stable and comprehensive knowledge transfer without excessive computational overhead.
