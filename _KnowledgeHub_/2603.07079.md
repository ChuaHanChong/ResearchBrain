# Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation of Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD) framework for language models mitigates diversity degradation and learning instability in standard distillation by adaptively applying forward Kullback-Leibler divergence in high-entropy teacher regions, improving mathematical reasoning performance and preserving the teacher's distributional uncertainty. For instance, a Qwen3-4B-Base student showed a +5.05 increase in Pass@8 compared to previous on-policy methods.

## Problem

- Standard on-policy distillation (OPD) methods often use reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, which exhibits a mode-seeking property.
- This mode-seeking behavior leads to diversity degradation and unstable learning in student models, particularly at token positions where the teacher's probability distribution has high entropy.
- Such instability hinders the student's ability to fully capture and transfer the teacher's diverse behavioral repertoire and uncertainty in complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- The paper introduces Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (**EOPD**), a framework that adaptively combines reverse KL and forward KL divergence objectives.
- **EOPD** applies the computationally efficient reverse KL for low-entropy regions, but selectively adds an approximated forward KL divergence term when the teacher's token-level entropy exceeds a predefined threshold.
- The forward KL divergence is efficiently approximated by focusing only on the teacher's top-k most probable tokens, renormalized to maintain a practical balance between accuracy and computational cost.

## Results

- EOPD consistently improved mathematical reasoning performance across various LLM sizes, with a Qwen3-4B-Base student achieving +1.80 in Avg@8 and +5.05 in Pass@8 compared to standard OPD.
- Student models trained with EOPD demonstrated enhanced out-of-domain generalization capabilities on tasks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro, even when trained solely on mathematical data.
- EOPD effectively preserved the teacher's high-entropy token distribution in the student, maintaining significantly more probability mass in the high-entropy region compared to other on-policy distillation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The mode-seeking nature of reverse KL divergence causes student models to become overconfident and lose diversity by ignoring the teacher's uncertainty in high-entropy token distributions.
> - Explicitly modeling and transferring the teacher's uncertainty, represented by high-entropy tokens, is crucial for improving the student's reasoning capabilities and its ability to explore diverse solution paths.
> - An adaptive distillation strategy that blends mode-seeking and mode-covering objectives based on teacher entropy allows for more stable and comprehensive knowledge transfer without excessive computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Knowledge distillation (KD) is a technique designed to transfer the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to smaller, more efficient models. This transfer aims to reduce inference costs and improve deployability. Traditional KD approaches typically employ off-policy training, where a student model learns from data generated by a teacher model using supervised loss or forward KL divergence. A known limitation of this method is the potential for a distribution mismatch between the training sequences and those generated by the student during inference.

On-policy distillation (OPD) emerged to address this distribution mismatch. In OPD, the student model generates its own samples, and these are then corrected or guided by the teacher model, commonly through the minimization of reverse KL divergence. This approach is noted for its efficiency and compatibility with reinforcement learning (RL) pipelines. Recent work has indicated that on-policy distillation can achieve performance comparable to RL-trained models in tasks such as mathematical reasoning, but with significantly lower computational overhead. Consequently, OPD has become a standard method for distilling knowledge in LLMs.

However, the reverse KL divergence objective, while efficient for converging on dominant modes, is inherently mode-seeking. This characteristic can lead to a reduction in the student model's generation diversity. The paper identifies that this mode-seeking property yields unstable learning signals, particularly when the teacher's probability distribution has high entropy, signifying uncertainty or multiple plausible outputs. In complex reasoning tasks, high-entropy tokens often represent critical decision points or branching paths where multiple continuations are valid. If the student model fails to capture this distributional breadth, it may converge to a more narrow behavioral repertoire than the teacher, even if the reverse KL objective itself appears well-optimized.

This work addresses this specific limitation by introducing a mechanism that maintains the efficiency of on-policy training while enabling the student to better capture the teacher's uncertainty and diverse distributional structures at ambiguous steps. It extends the current state of on-policy distillation by proposing a method to balance the mode-seeking precision of reverse KL with the mode-covering robustness needed to transfer a comprehensive understanding of the teacher's output distribution. The research positions itself against existing KD methods that face exposure bias, as well as recent RL-based approaches that might incur higher computational costs, while also distinguishing its approach from general entropy-promoting methods in RL that do not specifically leverage teacher uncertainty.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified shortcoming in existing on-policy knowledge distillation (OPD) methods for language models. While OPD is recognized for its efficiency and ability to mitigate distribution mismatch by training students on their own generated trajectories, it predominantly relies on the reverse Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence objective. The authors highlight that this objective, being mode-seeking, tends to reduce the student model's generation diversity. Furthermore, they demonstrate that this mode-seeking property results in unstable learning signals when the teacher model's distribution exhibits high entropy, indicating significant uncertainty or multiple plausible continuations for a given context. This instability is particularly problematic in reasoning tasks, where high-entropy tokens often represent crucial decision points with various valid reasoning paths. The consequence is that the student model may develop a narrower range of behaviors compared to the teacher, even when the reverse KL objective is optimized.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Analyze Diversity Degradation and Training Instability**: To conduct a systematic analysis of token-level entropy in on-policy distilled models, demonstrating empirically how standard reverse KL distillation leads to a collapse in generation diversity. Additionally, to use a controlled toy experiment to illustrate how reverse KL objectives generate unstable gradient signals when the teacher's distribution is high-entropy, thereby hindering proper convergence.
2. **Develop an Entropy-Aware Distillation Framework**: To propose a novel on-policy distillation framework, termed Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD), that dynamically adapts its training objective. This framework aims to strategically combine the efficiency of reverse KL for confident teacher predictions with the mode-covering capability of forward KL for transferring teacher uncertainty and broader distributional structure.
3. **Enhance Knowledge Transfer and Reasoning Performance**: To empirically demonstrate that EOPD can maintain higher generation diversity in student models, leading to improved alignment with the teacher's uncertainty. This, in turn, is hypothesized to translate into superior performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks and better generalization to out-of-domain tasks.
4. **Balance Efficiency and Robustness**: To achieve the aforementioned improvements without sacrificing the computational efficiency inherent in on-policy training, specifically by carefully selecting when and how to apply the more computationally intensive forward KL objective.

These objectives collectively aim to improve the fidelity and robustness of knowledge transfer from large teacher models to smaller student models, particularly in contexts requiring nuanced reasoning and the preservation of diverse problem-solving strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involved a two-pronged approach: first, a systematic analysis to diagnose the limitations of standard on-policy distillation (OPD) using reverse KL divergence, and second, the development and empirical validation of Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD) to address these limitations.

#### 3.1. Analysis of Diversity Degradation and Training Instability

The authors initiated their investigation by analyzing the behavior of student models trained with standard OPD.
- **Token-Level Entropy Analysis**: They examined token-level entropy distributions in responses generated by a teacher model (Qwen3-8B) and an OPD-trained student (Qwen3-1.7B-Base) on mathematical reasoning prompts (AIME24/25). The analysis revealed that the student model retained a significantly lower percentage of high-entropy tokens (6.8%) compared to the teacher (18.5%). This observation suggested that the mode-seeking nature of reverse KL leads to a reduction in generation diversity, causing the student to become overconfident in specific modes rather than preserving the teacher's inherent uncertainty.
- **Controlled Toy Experiment**: To isolate and further investigate the instability caused by reverse KL under teacher uncertainty, a simplified toy experiment was designed. This experiment removed the autoregressive conditioning of LLMs, focusing on categorical distributions over a small vocabulary (V=80). A fixed teacher distribution was created with five dominant modes, and its entropy was controlled by a temperature parameter (T=0.3 for low entropy, T=1.0 for high entropy). A student model, restricted to sampling from its top-10 most probable indices to simulate limited capacity, was optimized using a sample-based negative reverse KL reward. The study tracked the "top-10 change rate" (Jaccard distance between top-10 sets) and "top-1 change count." Results indicated that with a low-entropy teacher, the student converged stably. However, with a high-entropy teacher, training exhibited persistent instability, characterized by frequent changes in the top-1 index and failure of the top-10 set to converge. This confirmed that reverse KL-based rewards provide unstable learning signals when the teacher distribution is uncertain.

#### 3.2. Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD)

To address the identified limitations, the authors proposed EOPD, an adaptive distillation framework.
- **Hybrid Objective Function**: EOPD augments the standard clipped reverse KL loss (L_OPD) with a forward KL loss (L_FKL) that is selectively applied. The token-level objective is defined as:
 `L_EOPD_t(θ; c_t) = L_OPD_t(θ; c_t) + α · I[H_te_t > τ] · L_FKL_t(θ; c_t)`
 - `L_OPD_t`: This is the base on-policy objective, which is the clipped reverse KL divergence between the student (`π_θ`) and teacher (`π_te`) distributions, with an expectation taken over student-generated tokens (`x_t`) in context `c_t`. It leverages a policy-gradient-style update with an advantage term `A_t = log π_te(x_t | c_t) - log π_θ(x_t | c_t)`.
 - `H_te_t`: Represents the teacher's token-level entropy at position `t`.
 - `τ`: An entropy threshold. The indicator function `I[H_te_t > τ]` activates the forward KL term only when the teacher's entropy exceeds this threshold. This means that in low-entropy regions (teacher confident), the objective reverts to standard reverse KL, maintaining efficiency. In high-entropy regions (teacher uncertain), the forward KL term is added.
 - `L_FKL_t`: This is the forward KL divergence, `KL(π_te(· | c_t) ∥ π_θ(· | c_t))`, which encourages the student to cover the teacher's entire support, thus preserving probability mass over multiple plausible continuations.
 - `α`: A weighting coefficient that controls the influence of the forward KL term.
- **Efficient Forward KL Approximation**: Computing forward KL over the entire vocabulary (V) is computationally expensive. EOPD approximates `L_FKL_t` by taking the expectation only over the teacher's top-k most probable tokens (`S_k_t`). The teacher's probabilities are renormalized over these top-k tokens (`˜π_te`). This approximation balances capturing sufficient probability mass with computational efficiency. Through ablation, `k=16` was chosen as a practical value.
- **Training Procedure**: The training follows an on-policy RL-like loop (Algorithm 1). A student policy (`π_θ_old`) generates rollouts. For each token in the rollout, the teacher is queried for its log-probabilities, entropy (`H_te_t`), and top-k tokens (`S_k_t`). These are collected, and then mini-batch gradient steps are performed to minimize the `L_EOPD_t` objective.
- **Experimental Setup**:
 - **Models**: Qwen3-0.6B-Base, Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Qwen3-4B-Base as student models, and Qwen3-8B as the teacher. Additional experiments used Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct as student and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as teacher.
 - **Training Datasets**: MATH and DAPO datasets for mathematical reasoning.
 - **Evaluation Benchmarks**: Six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24/25, AMC23, Minerva, OlympiadBench) and three out-of-domain benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, AlpacaEval 2.0).
 - **Metrics**: Avg@8 and Pass@8 for reasoning, Pass@1, Win Rate (WR), and Length-Controlled Win Rate (LC-WR) for out-of-domain.
 - **Baselines**: Knowledge Distillation (KD), On-Policy Distillation (OPD), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), OPD + Entropy Bonus, OPD + Advantage Shaping, GKD, OPD-FKL, and ablations on FKL placement (Full/Random).
 - **Hyperparameters**: Default `τ = 0.8` and `α = 1.0`, with `k = 16`.
 - **Computational Overhead**: EOPD introduced approximately 4.5% additional computational overhead per training step and was significantly more memory-efficient (7.37x) than applying full forward KL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provided several key findings regarding the efficacy and mechanisms of Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD).

#### 4.1. Mathematical Reasoning Performance (RQ1)

EOPD consistently demonstrated performance improvements across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- **Qwen3-0.6B-Base**: EOPD achieved average gains of +1.16 in Avg@8 and +1.37 in Pass@8 compared to baseline OPD.
- **Qwen3-1.7B-Base**: EOPD showed average gains of +0.99 in Avg@8 and +2.39 in Pass@8.
- **Qwen3-4B-Base**: EOPD exhibited the largest average gains of +1.80 in Avg@8 and +5.05 in Pass@8.
These results indicate that EOPD scales effectively with model size and consistently improves reasoning capabilities. Furthermore, analysis of Pass@k performance revealed that EOPD achieved higher Pass@k values, especially on challenging benchmarks like AIME and as 'k' increased. This suggests that EOPD promotes the exploration of more diverse and potentially correct reasoning trajectories.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Domain Performance (RQ2)

Despite being trained exclusively on mathematical data, the Qwen3-1.7B-Base student trained with EOPD showed stable performance increases on general reasoning and instruction-following benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, AlpacaEval 2.0) compared to KD and GRPO. EOPD generally outperformed standard OPD on these out-of-domain tasks, with the exception of AlpacaEval 2.0 win rate. This suggests that selectively incorporating teacher guidance in high-uncertainty regions provides benefits beyond the specific training distribution.

#### 4.3. Token-Level Uncertainty Transfer (RQ3)

A token-level entropy analysis comparing the Qwen3-8B teacher with Qwen3-1.7B-Base students trained by OPD and EOPD on AIME benchmarks was conducted. While both OPD and EOPD students maintained similar entropy distributions to the teacher in mid-entropy ranges, EOPD preserved substantially more probability mass in the high-entropy region (entropy ≥ 1.0). This finding supports the hypothesis that EOPD more effectively mitigates premature over-confidence and mode collapse, leading to a student distribution that is more faithful to the teacher's uncertainty.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Entropy-Driven Baselines (RQ4)

When compared against other entropy-promoting methods (OPD + Entropy Bonus and OPD + Advantage Shaping), EOPD demonstrated superior performance on several mathematical reasoning benchmarks. Analysis of training dynamics showed that OPD + Advantage Shaping resulted in lower training entropy. Although EOPD and OPD + Entropy Bonus maintained comparable average entropy levels, EOPD consistently achieved lower forward KL divergence values at high-entropy token positions (teacher entropy ≥ 0.8). This indicates that EOPD better aligns with the teacher's distribution in uncertain regions, suggesting that simply promoting entropy is less effective than targeted guidance based on teacher uncertainty.

#### 4.5. Additional Comparisons and Ablations

- **Comparison with GKD**: EOPD consistently outperformed various configurations of GKD (Guided Knowledge Distillation), including its fully on-policy variant, across all tested benchmarks.
- **Effect of KL Objective**: A comparison between OPD (reverse KL), OPD-FKL (forward KL only), and EOPD revealed that reverse KL generally outperformed forward KL-only in on-policy settings. EOPD, by selectively combining both, outperformed both individual approaches, underscoring the complementary nature of the objectives.
- **Impact of Forward KL Placement**: Ablations showed that applying forward KL selectively based on teacher entropy (EOPD) was more effective than applying it randomly to a fixed percentage of tokens. EOPD also demonstrated competitive performance against applying forward KL at all token positions, while being more computationally efficient.
- **Generalizability**: EOPD's effectiveness was also validated on the Llama model series, showing competitive performance and stable training dynamics in high-entropy regions where standard OPD exhibited fluctuations.
- **Comparison with Adaptive KL Baselines**: EOPD achieved superior or comparable performance to other adaptive KL-based methods (BD-KD, AKL, ToDi), while only applying forward KL where necessary.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: Ablation studies on the entropy threshold (τ), forward KL coefficient (α), and top-k selection (k) indicated that EOPD exhibited stable performance across a range of these values, with optimal settings identified for each. For instance, a τ of 0.8 and α of 1.0-1.2 yielded strong results, while k=16 effectively balanced probability mass coverage and memory usage.

Overall, the findings collectively indicate that explicitly accounting for teacher uncertainty through selective application of forward KL divergence is critical for achieving robust knowledge transfer, maintaining generation diversity, and improving student model performance in complex reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the field of knowledge distillation for large language models by identifying and addressing a fundamental limitation of existing on-policy distillation methods. The paper rigorously demonstrates that the mode-seeking nature of reverse KL divergence, a common objective in on-policy training, can lead to a reduction in student generation diversity and instability in learning signals, particularly at token positions where the teacher model exhibits high entropy. These high-entropy regions are often critical in complex reasoning tasks, representing important decision points with multiple valid pathways.

The introduction of Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD) offers a principled solution to this challenge. By dynamically augmenting the reverse KL objective with forward KL divergence in high-entropy contexts, EOPD effectively balances the efficiency of mode-seeking with the robustness of mode-covering. This adaptive strategy allows the student model to better preserve the teacher's uncertainty and diverse distributional structures without incurring the full computational cost of constant forward KL application. The empirical results consistently show EOPD achieving significant performance gains across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks and student model scales (Qwen3-0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, and Llama models), as well as demonstrating improved generalization to out-of-domain tasks. The measured increase in Pass@k performance, especially on harder benchmarks, suggests that EOPD enables student models to explore a wider range of valid reasoning trajectories, thereby increasing the likelihood of successful problem-solving.

The significance of this work extends to several areas:
- **Improved Efficiency in LLM Development**: By enabling smaller models to more faithfully replicate the complex reasoning capabilities of larger teachers, EOPD contributes to the development of more efficient and deployable language models. This can reduce the computational and environmental costs associated with relying solely on very large, resource-intensive models.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities**: The findings underscore the importance of transferring not just dominant modes, but also the nuanced uncertainty and diverse plausible outputs of a teacher model. This insight is particularly valuable for applications requiring complex multi-step reasoning, where understanding and exploring alternative paths are crucial.
- **Advancement in Knowledge Distillation Theory**: The research provides a deeper understanding of the interplay between different KL divergence objectives in the context of on-policy training, offering a novel approach to adapt these objectives based on the teacher's local certainty. This could inspire further research into context-aware or adaptive knowledge transfer mechanisms.
- **Broader Applicability**: The method's effectiveness across different model architectures (Qwen3 and Llama) and scales suggests its potential for wide adoption in the LLM ecosystem.

In conclusion, this work addresses a critical gap in on-policy distillation, providing a method that not only enhances the performance of student models in reasoning tasks but also offers a more robust and faithful mechanism for knowledge transfer by explicitly accounting for teacher uncertainty. This advancement contributes to making advanced language model capabilities more accessible and efficient.
