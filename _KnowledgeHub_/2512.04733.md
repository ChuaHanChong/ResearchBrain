# E3AD: An Emotion-Aware Vision-Language-Action Model for Human-Centric End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> The E3AD framework introduces an emotion-aware vision-language-action model for human-centric end-to-end autonomous driving, integrating continuous Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) emotion modeling and a dual-pathway spatial reasoning module. It reduces trajectory error metrics by up to 20%, significantly improves visual grounding and 3D spatial perception, and achieves high correlation with human emotional annotations, enhancing user comfort and trust.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end autonomous driving systems are emotion-agnostic, leading to a disconnection from human emotional understanding and reduced user acceptance.
- Current vision-language-action (VLA) models for autonomous driving often exhibit weak spatial understanding, particularly in 3D environments, and neglect passenger emotional cues.
- Autonomous vehicles struggle to interpret nuanced natural language commands that convey implicit emotional meaning or urgency beyond purely semantic content.

## Method

- Development of an Emotion-Aware VLA Framework (**E3AD**) that uses a continuous ==Valence-Arousal-Dominance== (VAD) model to capture emotional tone and urgency from natural language commands.
- Implementation of a dual-pathway spatial reasoning module that fuses egocentric and allocentric views for a comprehensive 3D spatial understanding.
- Utilization of a consistency-oriented training scheme, including Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), to ensure coherence between inferred emotional intent, semantic understanding, and resulting driving actions.

## Results

- E3AD demonstrated superior end-to-end performance, reducing trajectory errors such as ADE by **17.01%**, Fréchet by **18.26%**, and FDE by **20.00%** compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- It significantly improved visual grounding accuracy (e.g., +**6.86%** IoU on Talk2Car) and established new state-of-the-art in 3D spatial perception with a Location MAE of **0.47** and Depth MAE of 4.25.
- The framework achieved high correlation with human emotion annotations (Spearman’s ρ and Kendall’s τ > **0.8** for VAD dimensions) and received the highest preference in user studies for command compliance, emotion alignment, safety, and comfort.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating continuous emotion modeling, especially with VAD representation, enables autonomous vehicles to interpret human intent more accurately and respond in emotionally aligned ways.
> - A dual-pathway spatial representation, combining local egocentric and global allocentric views, is crucial for robust visual grounding and precise 3D trajectory planning.
> - Consistency-oriented training, by aligning emotional intent with behavioral outcomes, effectively refines multimodal alignment and strengthens emotion-trajectory consistency in autonomous driving systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving (AD) systems have progressively shifted from modular pipelines to integrated, end-to-end (E2E) vision-language-action (VLA) models. This evolution aims to enhance efficiency and adaptability by unifying perception, prediction, and planning within a single learning framework. Current E2E systems often demonstrate proficiency in control and perception but face challenges related to human acceptance and trust. A significant limitation identified in existing VLA models for AD is their emotion-agnostic nature, which results in a disconnection between computational reasoning and human emotional understanding. These models typically operate on a purely rational, sequence-prediction paradigm, neglecting emotional cues such as anxiety or urgency conveyed through natural language commands.

Previous VLA architectures in AD can be categorized into three paradigms: (1) those producing scene-level explanations via QA-style prompts (e.g., DriveGPT-4, OpenEMMA), which offer interpretability but lack precise spatial grounding and direct control; (2) those employing VLMs to generate discrete "meta-behaviors" to guide low-level controllers (e.g., Senna, LMDrive), providing sparse guidance with limited continuous spatial reasoning; and (3) those coupling VLM-based perception with dedicated planning modules to directly output trajectories (e.g., Simlingo, AutoVLA, FSDrive), which achieve robust performance but frequently exhibit weak spatial understanding (operating largely in 2D) and disregard passenger emotion.

This research positions itself within the third paradigm, aiming to address the identified "emotion gap" and weak spatial understanding. It proposes an emotion-aware, spatially grounded end-to-end VLA framework that extends beyond purely semantic understanding to incorporate emotional and spatially consistent reasoning. The work builds upon the understanding that emotional interaction is a critical factor for user comfort and perceived safety in autonomous vehicles, suggesting that an intelligent system should comprehend not only the content of a passenger's command but also the manner in which it is expressed.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Define Open-Domain End-to-End (OD-E2E) AD:** To establish a new task formulation for human-centric autonomous vehicles that integrates semantic, emotional, and spatial reasoning based on natural-language commands. This involves the autonomous vehicle interpreting free-form natural language, inferring emotional state, and planning a physically feasible trajectory.
2. **Develop an Emotion-Aware VLA Framework (E3AD):** To design and implement a framework that augments semantic understanding with specific cognitive components:
 - A continuous Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) emotion model to capture emotional tone and urgency from language.
 - A dual-pathway spatial reasoning module that fuses egocentric and allocentric views for a more comprehensive spatial understanding, analogous to human cognition.
3. **Propose a Consistency-Oriented Training Scheme:** To ensure coherence between the inferred emotional intent, semantic understanding, and the resulting driving actions. This scheme combines modality pretraining with preference-based alignment.
4. **Demonstrate Improved Performance:** To show that the proposed E3AD framework improves visual grounding, waypoint planning, and emotion estimation compared to existing state-of-the-art methods across various real-world datasets. The aim is to achieve more human-aligned grounding, planning, and feedback by integrating emotion into VLA-style driving.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that despite technical advancements in E2E AD systems, a significant obstacle to public trust and acceptance is the lack of human-centered interaction. Passengers often express uneasiness with autonomous systems that do not acknowledge their intent or emotional state. The distinction between commands like "stop here" and "stop here now!" carries implicit emotional meaning that should influence vehicle response. By recognizing such emotional nuances, an AV can regulate its behavior to align with the passenger's emotional state, thereby providing reassurance, enhancing comfort, and fostering greater acceptance of AD technology. This research aims to transform AVs into empathetic and user-aligned driving agents capable of interacting with humans in a more sophisticated and emotionally intelligent manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The E3AD framework is a cognitively inspired VLA system built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct backbone. It integrates Emotion Modeling, a Two-System Spatial Representation, and Consistency-Oriented Action Planning within a unified pipeline. The training strategy involves three stages to progressively enhance its capabilities.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is defined as Open-Domain End-to-End (OD-E2E) AD, where an autonomous vehicle receives multi-view observations (`I = {I_ego, I_allo}`) and a natural-language command (`C`). The agent must localize the referenced object or location (`ˆb`) and generate a physically feasible trajectory (`ˆτ`) that fulfills the instruction. Unlike prior language grounding setups, OD-E2E learns a single policy `fθ` that jointly predicts (`ˆb, ˆτ`) conditioned on (`I, C`) under a unified objective.

#### 3.2. Emotion Modeling

To capture nuanced emotional communication, E3AD adopts a continuous Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) model, representing emotion as a 3D vector (`e ∈ R^3`). VAD axes correspond to attitude (Valence), alertness (Arousal), and control (Dominance).
- **VAD Label Derivation:** VAD labels are derived from both sentence-level and word-level cues. GoEmotions classifier is used for discrete emotion categories, mapped to VAD scores. Word-level VAD vectors are computed by averaging lexical scores.
- **Emotion-Aware Command Augmentation:** To mitigate the bias of emotionally neutral driving commands, the model generates `K` paraphrases for each command (`C^(i)`) that preserve the driving goal but vary in attitude or intensity. Each augmented command is assigned a VAD label, creating a dataset (`C∗`) of semantically equivalent but affectively distinct commands. This approach encourages the model to attribute VAD changes to tone rather than intent.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The VLM is fine-tuned with instruction templates, framing emotion prediction as conditional generation. The loss (`L_emo`) is defined as the negative log-likelihood of predicting quantized VAD tokens, embedding `e` within the generative reasoning process.

#### 3.3. Spatial Reasoning

Inspired by human spatial perception, the VLA backbone uses a dual-system model:
- **Egocentric Pathway:** Captures the agent’s first-person perceptual field, predicting relative 3D direction, distance, and grounded location in image coordinates from egocentric views (`I_ego`) and the command. A dataset of 30K samples is curated for this pathway.
- **Allocentric Pathway:** Encodes a world-centered representation, similar to a cognitive map. Given BEV input (`I_allo`), the model predicts the target location in BEV coordinates and generates a coarse trajectory (`τ`). This pathway is trained with approximately 17K samples.
These two pathways provide complementary local and global spatial cues for grounding and waypoint generation.

#### 3.4. Action and Feedback

- **Action Decoder:** A lightweight action decoder (`f_act`) is appended to translate high-level VLA outputs into a precise, physically realizable trajectory (`ˆτ`), conditioned on the grounded target (`ˆb`), coarse trajectory (`eτ`), and visual observations (`I`).
- **Human-centric Verbal Feedback:** E3AD provides verbal feedback (`ˆr`) after planning, generated by the trained Qwen2.5-VL backbone. The response is conditioned on the predicted emotion state (`ˆe`), grounded target (`ˆb`), and planned waypoints (`ˆτ`), adapting its tone, length, and specificity to the passenger’s emotional state and urgency.

#### 3.5. Consistency-Oriented Training and Inference

The training strategy is divided into three stages:
1. **Modality Pretraining:**
 - Emotion Modeling is trained on the augmented command dataset (`C∗`) using `L_emo`.
 - Spatial Reasoning is trained on synthetic egocentric and allocentric pathway datasets using negative log-likelihood loss (`L_spatial`) for next-token prediction.
2. **Joint Fine-Tuning:** The VLA is fine-tuned to unify these capabilities. An instruction-based SFT paradigm is used, where the model autoregressively predicts the full output sequence (`T = (ˆe, ˆb, ˆτ)`) in a single forward pass. The joint objective (`L_Joint`) encourages an emotion-aware chain of thought where `ˆe` and `ˆb` inform `ˆτ`.
3. **Emotion-Action Alignment (DPO):** To explicitly enforce behavioral consistency with emotional intent, a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) approach is used. Since standard AD datasets lack preference pairs, pseudo-preference pairs are constructed. For an original command (`C^(i)`) and trajectory (`τ^(i)`), an emotion-augmented variant (`C^(i)_k−`) with the most deviating VAD embedding is identified. This "negative" command is used to generate a dispreferred, emotion-shifted trajectory (`eτ^(i)_k−`). DPO then optimizes the preference (`τ^(i) ≻ eτ^(i)_k−`), encouraging the model to favor trajectories consistent with the original command's intent and suppress emotionally perturbed alternatives.

**Inference:** During inference, E3AD operates end-to-end, processing (`I_ego, I_allo, C`) to directly produce the emotion state (`ˆe`), grounded target (`ˆb`), coarse trajectory waypoints (`ˆτ`), and verbal response (`ˆr`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted experiments on four real-world benchmarks: Talk2Car, DrivePilot, MoCAD, and Talk2Car-Trajectory. Tailored subsets like Long-Text and Corner-Case were also used to assess robustness. Evaluations focused on joint end-to-end performance and sub-task ablations, using metrics such as ADE, FDE, Fréchet, DTW, SSPD, PA_g for trajectories; IoU, MAE for visual grounding and spatial reasoning; and Spearman’s (ρ) and Kendall’s (τ) for emotion awareness.

#### 4.1. Joint Evaluation Results

E3AD demonstrated superior end-to-end performance across all seven standard trajectory metrics compared to state-of-the-art baselines. Against the strongest baseline (PTPC), E3AD achieved notable reductions: 17.01% in ADE, 18.26% in Fréchet, and 20.00% in FDE, indicating more accurate and stable trajectory forecasting. It also improved SSPD and DTW by over 16% and increased planning accuracy (PA2/PA4) by +16.71%/+18.10%. General-purpose VLMs (Qwen-VL) and FSDrive-Finetuned exhibited lower performance, highlighting the limitations of existing VLA models in command-driven scenarios without emotion and sophisticated spatial reasoning.

#### 4.2. Sub-task Evaluation Results

E3AD performed well across individual sub-tasks, despite being trained as a unified end-to-end network, unlike many specialized baselines.
- **Visual Grounding:** E3AD surpassed the strongest baseline (CAVG) with absolute gains of +6.86% on Talk2Car, +10.50% (MoCAD test), +8.72% (MoCAD val), +6.79% (DrivePilot test), and +7.36% (DrivePilot val). Performance improvements were more pronounced in challenging scenarios: +8.26% on occluded, +6.95% on multi-agent, +7.48% on ambiguous corner-case splits, and +11.63% on Long-text commands. Generic VLMs lagged significantly.
- **Spatial Reasoning:** E3AD markedly outperformed large VLM baselines (Qwen2.5/3-VL) in target localization and depth estimation. It achieved a Location MAE of 0.47 and Depth MAE of 4.25, significantly lower than Qwen2.5-VL-72B's MAE of 10.1 (Location) and 22.68 (Depth). Accuracy metrics further showed E3AD achieving 97.7% PA2 (Location) and 53.1% PA2 (Depth), establishing a new state-of-the-art in 3D spatial perception for driving.
- **Emotion Recognition:** E3AD achieved the highest correlation with human annotations for Valence, Arousal, and Dominance. Spearman’s ρ and Kendall’s τ correlations were 0.95/0.84 for valence, 0.94/0.82 for arousal, and 0.94/0.81 for dominance. In contrast, Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B showed near-random correlation levels. This indicates E3AD's effectiveness in modeling continuous human emotions, enabling human-centric planning and feedback.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the contribution of each core component:
- Removing the egocentric pathway resulted in the largest drop in visual grounding (e.g., ↓7.0% IoU on Talk2Car), underscoring its role in localizing linguistic referents.
- Excluding the allocentric map weakened global reasoning and reduced waypoint planning quality (ADE/FDE increased by ↑10.0%/↑10.1%), demonstrating the importance of global priors for spatial awareness.
- Emotion Modeling yielded notable gains, particularly for ambiguous and long-text commands (↑4.5%/↑4.8%), enhancing sensitivity to nuanced emotional cues.
- DPO (Emotion-Action Alignment) provided moderate gains by refining multimodal alignment and strengthening emotion-trajectory consistency, leading to more geometrically coherent and behaviorally consistent paths. For example, higher arousal correlated with straighter, smoother motion, while lower arousal produced more cautious, curved paths.

#### 4.4. Case Study and Qualitative Analysis

Case studies illustrated how E3AD integrates emotion understanding. For instance, a "cautious" command variant shifted the VAD state to increased Arousal and Dominance, causing E3AD to avoid a lane change, unlike the standard maneuver planned for a neutral command. The verbal feedback also adapted to the inferred emotional state. Qualitative comparisons showed E3AD outperforming FSDrive-FT in emotion-rich, multi-agent, and ambiguous scenes, providing timely responses, accurate grounding, and map-feasible plans where FSDrive-FT failed.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user study involving 217 participants evaluated E3AD's real-world utility. Participants ranked systems on Command Compliance, Emotion Alignment, Safety, Comfort, and Overall Preference. E3AD consistently achieved the highest mean scores and most Rank-1 votes across most dimensions, indicating a clear preference over baselines. Participants favored trajectories that matched emotional tone, maintained correct grounding, and remained physically feasible, highlighting the value of emotion cues in end-to-end AD for perceived trust and confidence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that extends end-to-end autonomous driving beyond purely rational control to incorporate emotion-aware grounding and planning. The introduction of Open-Domain End-to-End AD (OD-E2E) as a task formulation signifies a shift towards more human-centric autonomous systems. The proposed E3AD framework, through its continuous VAD-based emotion modeling, dual-pathway spatial reasoning, and consistency-oriented training, contributes to several areas:

- **Enhanced Human-Vehicle Interaction:** By enabling autonomous vehicles to interpret and respond to a passenger's emotional state and intent, E3AD can improve user comfort, trust, and acceptance, addressing a fundamental human-centered obstacle to AD deployment. The generation of human-centric verbal feedback further humanizes the interaction, mitigating "black-box" anxiety.
- **Improved Driving Behavior:** The integration of emotional cues allows for more nuanced and context-aware driving behaviors. The ability to distinguish between urgent and calm commands, for instance, can lead to appropriate adjustments in trajectory, speed, and safety margins, potentially increasing perceived safety and aligning vehicle actions more closely with human expectations.
- **Robust Perception and Planning:** The dual-pathway spatial reasoning, fusing egocentric and allocentric views, provides a more comprehensive understanding of the driving environment, leading to more precise visual grounding and robust trajectory planning, particularly in complex or ambiguous scenarios.
- **Advancement in VLA Models for AD:** E3AD pushes the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action models by explicitly integrating emotion and advanced spatial reasoning, moving beyond purely semantic processing. This could inspire further research into multimodal integration for cognitive and affective intelligence in autonomous systems.

The findings indicate that injecting emotion into VLA-style driving yields more human-aligned grounding, planning, and feedback, fostering calibrated trust in end-to-end AD. Future work plans to incorporate richer multimodal emotion signals beyond language, gather more human preference feedback, and conduct closed-loop evaluations in high-fidelity simulators to further enhance the reliability and acceptance of emotion-aware autonomous driving systems.
