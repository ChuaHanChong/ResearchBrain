# Seeing Globally, Refining Locally: Global Visual Guidance and Local Ultrasound Cues for Robust Freehand 3-D Ultrasound Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-modal framework is presented for robust freehand 3-D ultrasound reconstruction, integrating external visual guidance from dual cameras with local ultrasound cues. The system provides highly accurate probe pose estimation, achieving reduced trajectory drift and improved volumetric reconstructions by leveraging global stability from cameras and local anatomical refinement from B-mode images.

## Problem

- Existing "trackerless" 3-D ultrasound (US) reconstruction methods, relying solely on US images or IMUs, suffer from accumulated pose errors and significant trajectory drift (often >5mm) during freehand scans.
- Conventional 3-D US systems require expensive external optical or electromagnetic trackers, increasing cost, complexity, and susceptibility to interference or occlusion.
- Purely external camera-based methods provide global stability but lack access to fine-grained, anatomy-aware local motion cues from US images, which are critical for precise tissue-probe interaction.

## Method

- A **global-to-local pose estimation framework** integrates an external ==dual-camera branch== for globally stable probe localization and a ==B-mode ultrasound branch== for anatomy-aware local refinement.
- A ==cross-modal fusion module== leverages ==cross-attention== to inject ultrasound features into camera-derived representations, predicting ==residual pose corrections== applied in ==SE(3)== space.
- A ==multi-scale pose loss== constrains relative motion over multiple temporal horizons to suppress accumulated drift, alongside ==correlation== and ==triplet losses== for feature learning.

## Results

- The US + Dual-Cam fusion model reduced average trajectory drift (AD) by **16.50%** (from **2.00 mm to 1.67 mm**) on FUSION-J and **27.12%** (from **1.77 mm to 1.29 mm**) on FUSION-L compared to a dual-camera-only baseline.
- The framework achieved high-quality 3-D US volume reconstruction, with **Hausdorff Distances of 1.58 mm to 2.35 mm** and **Dice coefficients of 0.69 to 0.87** for in vivo forearm arteries.
- The US-only branch, though showing drift, achieved the lowest **final drift rate (FDR) of 2.93%** and **average drift (AD) of 10.37 mm** on the TUS-REC2025 dataset, demonstrating effectiveness in improving long-term consistency within US-based approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The complementary nature of external visual tracking (global stability) and internal ultrasound cues (local anatomical and motion sensitivity) can be effectively integrated to overcome their individual limitations in freehand 3-D US reconstruction.
> - Refining globally stable camera-derived pose estimates with locally anatomy-aware ultrasound features via a residual prediction model significantly improves accuracy and reduces trajectory drift, even over long scans.
> - Employing multi-scale loss functions that consider relative motion over various temporal horizons is crucial for suppressing accumulated errors and maintaining long-term trajectory consistency in sequential pose estimation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Medical ultrasound (US) is a widely utilized imaging modality due to its advantages in cost-effectiveness, real-time capabilities, and absence of ionizing radiation. However, conventional two-dimensional (2-D) US imaging can be challenging to interpret, often requiring significant operator experience to overcome issues like speckle artifacts and acoustic shadowing. Three-dimensional (3-D) US imaging offers a more intuitive volumetric representation, which is valuable for diagnosis, anatomical visualization, and interventional guidance.

Methods for 3-D US generation generally fall into two categories: direct acquisition using matrix-array probes and reconstruction from tracked 2-D US sequences. While matrix-array probes acquire volumetric data directly, their high cost, complexity, and limited field of view restrict their widespread clinical adoption. Tracking-based 3-D US, which reconstructs a 3-D volume by stacking sequential 2-D B-mode frames based on their estimated spatial positions (poses), offers a more practical alternative. Accurate probe pose estimation is fundamental for geometrically faithful reconstruction in this approach.

Historically, accurate probe pose information for freehand 3-D US systems has relied on external tracking devices such as optical or electromagnetic (EM) trackers. These systems can provide high accuracy in controlled environments but introduce increased hardware costs, are susceptible to interference or occlusion, and may impose workflow constraints. Robotic ultrasound systems offer similar accuracy and controlled scanning, but their complexity and cost limit accessibility.

These limitations have driven research into sensorless, image-based, and camera-based freehand 3-D US reconstruction. Purely US image-based methods estimate probe motion by analyzing B-mode speckle patterns and sometimes incorporating inertial measurement unit (IMU) data. Despite advancements, these methods are prone to accumulated pose errors over long scanning trajectories, often resulting in significant trajectory drift (typically exceeding 5 mm). The implicit nature of out-of-plane cues in B-mode images, along with speckle noise and tissue deformation, limits their robustness and global consistency.

Conversely, external camera-based methods track the US probe or markers in the external scene to estimate its pose. While these methods can provide globally stable observations, they lack access to the internal anatomical information provided by US images. Current camera-based approaches still face challenges such as sensitivity to occlusion, visual ambiguity, and the need for structured environments or specialized hardware.

The current work positions itself at the intersection of these two fields, recognizing the complementary strengths of external visual tracking (global stability) and internal ultrasound cues (local anatomical and motion sensitivity). The broader landscape indicates a need for robust, low-cost, and easy-to-use 3-D US reconstruction methods that overcome the individual limitations of purely US-based or purely camera-based approaches, especially regarding accumulated drift over extended freehand scans. This paper aims to integrate these modalities to provide globally consistent and locally refined pose estimation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and accurate pose estimation framework for freehand 3-D ultrasound (US) reconstruction that addresses the limitations of existing trackerless methods, particularly their susceptibility to accumulated pose errors over long scanning trajectories.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges in freehand 3-D US:
1. **Dependence on External Trackers:** Conventional high-precision 3-D US reconstruction often relies on expensive and bulky external optical or electromagnetic tracking systems. These systems increase hardware cost, introduce calibration complexities, and are vulnerable to line-of-sight occlusions or electromagnetic interference, limiting their applicability in routine clinical and point-of-care settings.
2. **Limitations of Purely US Image-based Methods:** "Trackerless" or "sensorless" US pose estimation methods, which derive probe motion solely from B-mode US images (sometimes augmented with IMU data), are attractive for their low cost and ease of use. However, the intrinsic properties of US images, such as speckle noise, acoustic shadowing, tissue deformation, and the implicit nature of out-of-plane motion cues, lead to inherent ambiguities. This results in accumulated errors and significant trajectory drift (often exceeding 5 mm) during extended freehand sweeps, compromising the geometric accuracy of the reconstructed 3-D volume.
3. **Limitations of Purely Camera-based Methods:** External camera-based methods offer a global spatial reference for probe tracking. While they provide more stable global localization compared to US-only approaches, they observe the external scene rather than the scanned tissue. Consequently, they cannot capture the fine-grained, anatomy-aware local motion cues embedded within the US images, which are crucial for precise tissue-probe interaction modeling. These methods can also be sensitive to visual occlusion or ambient lighting conditions.
4. **Complementary Nature of Modalities:** The authors hypothesize that the global stability offered by external visual tracking and the local, anatomy-aware motion cues from B-mode US images are complementary. Integrating these two modalities could overcome their individual limitations by providing a globally consistent pose estimate while preserving anatomically accurate local motion, thereby addressing the issue of accumulated drift in long freehand scans.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a **global-to-local pose estimation framework** that leverages external camera observations for globally stable probe localization and B-mode US images for anatomy-aware local refinement.
- Design a **dual-camera branch** for contextual feature aggregation across camera views and temporal observations, estimating a globally consistent probe trajectory.
- Design a **B-mode branch** for anatomical feature aggregation from sequential US images, capturing tissue-dependent local motion cues.
- Create a **cross-modal fusion module** to integrate contextual camera features and anatomical US features, predicting pose residuals to refine camera-derived estimates in the transformation space.
- Introduce a **multi-scale pose loss** to constrain relative motion over multiple temporal horizons, aiming to suppress accumulated drift during extended scans.
- Validate the proposed framework extensively on phantom and *in vivo* datasets, demonstrating improved pose estimation accuracy, reduced trajectory drift, and higher-quality 3-D US volume reconstruction, particularly under challenging practical conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named "global-to-local pose estimation," integrates external camera observations with internal B-mode ultrasound (US) image cues for robust freehand 3-D US reconstruction. It consists of three main phases: camera-based pose estimation, ultrasound-based pose estimation, and a multi-modal fusion stage for global-to-local refinement.

**Problem Definition:**
The system aims to estimate the 6-DoF rigid transformation $\mathbf{T}_i \in SE(3)$ for each 2-D US image plane at frame $i$, given synchronized B-mode US images $\mathbf{U}_i$ and dual-camera RGB images $\mathbf{W}_i$ over a sequence of $N$ frames. The objective is to recover the full probe trajectory $\hat{\mathcal{T}} = \{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_i\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$ that is geometrically consistent with the ground-truth trajectory.

**Overall Framework:**
The framework utilizes a global-to-local approach:
1. **Camera Branch:** Provides coarse but globally stable probe pose estimates from dual-camera observations using AprilTag markers.
2. **Ultrasound Branch:** Extracts motion-aware latent representations from sequential B-mode frames to characterize tissue appearance changes and probe-tissue interaction.
3. **Cross-modal Fusion Module:** Integrates features from both branches to predict a residual pose correction, refining the camera-derived global estimate.

#### 3.1. Contextual Feature Aggregation from Camera Images (Camera Branch)

- **Initial Pose Estimation:** AprilTag markers are placed on a planar board in the scanning scene. For each of the two cameras, AprilTag detections are used with a calibrated intrinsic matrix (K) and the PnP (Perspective-n-Point) algorithm to estimate the grid-to-camera transformation ($\mathbf{T}_{g \rightarrow c}$). This transformation is then inverted and combined with a calibrated camera-to-ultrasound extrinsic transformation to obtain the absolute 6-DoF pose of the US image center in the tag-grid reference frame.
- **Feature Learning:** These absolute pose sequences are converted into left- and right-view relative motion sequences. A zero vector is prepended to each sequence.
 - A shared Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) embeds the relative motion sequences into a common feature space.
 - A shared Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU) aggregates bidirectional temporal context within each view's feature sequence.
 - An MLP head concatenates and fuses the frame-wise features from both views to predict the camera-derived relative pose sequence ($\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{\text{cam}}$). The fused hidden features represent the contextual camera representation.

#### 3.2. Anatomical Feature Aggregation from US Images (Ultrasound Branch)

- **3-D ResNet-18 Backbone:** Takes an input US clip ($\mathbf{U}$) and transforms it into frame-aligned feature maps. These features encode local anatomical appearance, speckle patterns, and short-range spatiotemporal context.
- **Token-Context Encoder:**
 - Feature maps are converted into frame-wise patch tokens.
 - A motion-aware block uses cross-frame attention to aggregate short-range anatomical variations. It calculates cross-frame attention for temporal lags (1 and 2 frames) and combines lag-specific responses using learnable weights.
 - Cosine dissimilarity between enhanced tokens and shifted counterparts is computed to emphasize spatial regions with pronounced inter-frame changes, yielding motion-aware tokens.
 - These tokens, along with a CLS token and positional embeddings, are processed by a Transformer encoder.
 - Multi-head attention pooling with learnable queries compresses the context tokens, yielding a clip-level representation.
- **Temporal Encoder:** A second Transformer models dependencies across the full clip using the output from the token-context encoder.
- **MLP Pose Head:** Maps the temporal features to frame-aligned 6-DoF pose predictions ($\bar{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{\text{us}}$), from which the relative pose sequence ($\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{\text{us}}$) is derived. The same temporal features are retained as the anatomical and motion-aware ultrasound representation.

#### 3.3. Global-to-Local Pose Refinement via Dual-Modal Fusion

- **Frozen Backbones:** The independently trained camera and ultrasound backbones are frozen during this stage.
- **Feature Projection:** The latent camera features ($\mathbf{Q}_{\text{cam}}$) and ultrasound features ($\mathbf{K}_{\text{us}}$) are projected into a common hidden space.
- **Cross-Attention Blocks:** A stack of four cross-attention blocks progressively injects ultrasound-derived information into the camera representation. Camera features serve as queries, while ultrasound features serve as keys and values. Each block includes multi-head attention and a Feed-Forward Network (FFN), both followed by Layer Normalization (LN). This results in a fused local representation ($\mathbf{Z}_{\text{local}}$).
- **Global-Local Feature Integration:** The camera-derived global pose sequence ($\mathbf{P}_{\text{cam}}$) is embedded and concatenated with the fused local representation.
- **Bi-GRU and Residual Prediction:** A Bi-GRU processes the combined features, and an MLP residual head predicts frame-wise residual poses ($\hat{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}_{\text{res}}$). The final layer of this head is initialized to zero to ensure initial corrections are minimal.
- **SE(3) Residual Application:** The predicted residual correction ($\hat{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}_{\text{res}}$) is converted into a transformation matrix ($\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\text{res},t}$) and applied multiplicatively to the camera-derived pose transformation ($\mathbf{T}_{\text{cam},t}$) in the SE(3) space: $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\text{pred},t} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{cam},t} \hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\text{res},t}$.
- **Trajectory Stitching:** For full-sweep inference, overlapping 16-frame windows with a stride of one are processed. Predictions from multiple windows for the same frame-to-frame motion are combined using triangular weights, giving more importance to predictions near the window center. These relative motions are then composed to recover the continuous probe trajectory.

#### 3.4. Loss Functions

- **Multi-Scale Pose Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{pose}}$):** Applied to both camera and ultrasound branches. It supervises composed transformations over multiple temporal horizons ($k \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 15\}$) using an L1-norm (Huber loss) for translation error and geodesic distance on SO(3) for rotation error. Translation and rotation terms are balanced using learnable uncertainty weights, and horizon-dependent weights ($w_k = 1/\sqrt{k}$) are used to moderate longer-horizon contributions.
- **Correlation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{corr}}$):** Encourages the predicted relative pose sequence to align with the overall motion pattern of the ground truth using cosine correlation.
- **Triplet Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}$):** Applied to the temporal latent features of the US branch to encourage motion-discriminative representations by ensuring that features from similar motions are closer than those from dissimilar motions in the latent space.
- **Fusion Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{fusion}}$):** Used for the fusion module, comprising translation and geodesic rotation errors on the refined poses, plus an L2 regularization term on the predicted residual poses to discourage unnecessarily large corrections.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Data

- **Hardware:** Two low-cost monocular RGB cameras (480x640, 30 fps) rigidly mounted on a 3-D-printed probe holder, observing a planar AprilTag board. An optical tracking system (Ruitong-MAX) provided ground-truth 6-DoF poses at 120 fps. Two clinical US systems were used: Siemens ACUSON Juniper (12L3 probe, 30 fps) and Wisonic Labat (L15-6-H probe, 10 fps). All subsystems were synchronized using ROS2.
- **Datasets:**
 - **FUSION-J:** In-house dataset using Siemens ACUSON Juniper, collected from 8 participants (left/right forearms, 4 trajectory patterns, 3 repetitions each). 164 scans (approx. 1000 frames/scan).
 - **FUSION-L:** In-house dataset using Wisonic Labat, collected from 10 participants (left/right forearms, 4 trajectory patterns, 5 repetitions each). 391 scans (approx. 300 frames/scan).
 - **TUS-REC2025:** Public dataset used for ultrasound-only branch benchmarking, including forearm scans with fanning/rocking motions. 106 scans (approx. 1600 frames/scan).
- **Implementation Details:** US frames resized to 256x256. Full scans divided into overlapping 16-frame windows for training and inference. AdamW optimizer with cosine learning-rate decay. Data augmentation included random sweep reversal and temporally consistent patch cutout.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Pose MAE:** Mean absolute error for 6 global pose components (translation in mm, rotation in degrees).
 - **Trajectory Drift:** Final Drift Rate (FDR), Average Drift Rate (ADR), Maximum Drift (MD), Average Drift (AD), Trajectory Hausdorff Distance ($\text{HD}_{\text{traj}}$).
 - **Reconstruction Quality:** Surface Hausdorff Distance ($\text{HD}_{\text{surf}}$), Chamfer Distance ($d_C$), Dice Coefficient ($D$), Jaccard Index ($J$) for reconstructed 3-D US volumes and segmented artery surfaces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on two in-house datasets (FUSION-J and FUSION-L) and the public TUS-REC2025 dataset, evaluating ultrasound-only, camera-only, and the proposed dual-modal fusion models.

#### 4.1. Ultrasound-only Pose Estimation Results

- **Performance:** On FUSION-J, the proposed US-only model achieved the lowest Z-translation error and competitive rotational accuracy, with the lowest Final Drift Rate (FDR), Average Drift Rate (ADR), Maximum Drift (MD), and Average Drift (AD) compared to state-of-the-art learning-based methods. It reduced ADR to 5.80% and AD to 15.14 mm.
- **Cross-Dataset Consistency:** On FUSION-L, it demonstrated the lowest translational MAE across all three axes and superior performance across all trajectory-level metrics, reducing AD to 13.51 mm and $\text{HD}_{\text{traj}}$ to 24.84 mm. On TUS-REC2025, it achieved the lowest FDR (2.93%), ADR (9.69%), AD (10.37 mm), and $\text{HD}_{\text{traj}}$ (14.90 mm).
- **Interpretation:** These results indicate that the motion-aware ultrasound branch primarily improves long-term trajectory consistency over uniformly minimizing every frame-wise pose component. However, despite these advancements, the accumulated drift in US-only methods (e.g., AD > 13 mm) remained substantial compared to camera-based approaches, highlighting the intrinsic limitations of B-mode images for globally stable pose estimation.

#### 4.2. Camera-only Pose Estimation Results

- **Superiority over US-only:** Single-camera configurations (L-Cam, R-Cam) using AprilTag tracking significantly outperformed the US-only model in both frame-wise pose accuracy and trajectory consistency, reducing average drift by approximately an order of magnitude.
- **Dual-Camera Advantage:** The Dual-Cam configuration generally improved pose accuracy and trajectory consistency over single-camera setups. In the Global setting (where camera pose is estimated relative to the AprilTag board, avoiding sequential composition errors), Dual-Cam reduced AD to 2.00 mm on FUSION-J and 1.77 mm on FUSION-L. The two views provided complementary coverage and redundancy, enhancing stability under view-specific degradations.
- **"Local" vs. "Global" Setting:** The "Local" camera setting, which uses window-based relative motion estimation followed by stitching (similar to the US branch), showed higher accumulated error than the "Global" setting, confirming that sequential composition can introduce additional drift even with robust camera inputs.

#### 4.3. Dual-Modal Fusion (US + Dual-Cam) and Ablation Analysis

- **Overall Best Performance:** The proposed US + Dual-Cam model achieved the best frame-wise pose accuracy for most components and the lowest FDR, MD, AD, and $\text{HD}_{\text{traj}}$ on both FUSION-J and FUSION-L.
- **Quantifiable Improvement:** Compared to the strong Dual-Cam (Global) baseline, the fusion model further reduced Average Drift (AD) from 2.00 mm to 1.67 mm on FUSION-J (16.50% improvement) and from 1.77 mm to 1.29 mm on FUSION-L (27.12% improvement).
- **Complementarity Confirmed:** This ablation demonstrates that the two modalities play complementary roles. The camera provides a stable global reference, while the ultrasound information refines local alignment, leveraging B-mode features related to tissue structure, speckle variations, and probe-tissue interaction not accessible to external cameras.

#### 4.4. Representative Freehand Sweep Evaluation

- **Consistency across Trajectories:** Qualitative results (Fig. 7) showed that US-only trajectories significantly deviated, especially during direction changes, while Dual-Cam offered global stability. The US + Dual-Cam model maintained global consistency and improved local trajectory agreement.
- **Quantitative Improvements:** Across C-shaped, linear, S-shaped, and zigzag trajectories, the US + Dual-Cam model consistently outperformed the US-only model by a substantial margin. It also reduced AD and MD compared to the Dual-Cam (Global) baseline across most patterns. For instance, AD decreased from 3.11 mm to 1.75 mm for C-shaped sweeps on FUSION-J, and from 1.72 mm to 0.73 mm for S-shaped sweeps on FUSION-L.

#### 4.5. Freehand 3-D US Reconstruction Analysis

- **Method:** Voxel-based spatial compounding was used to reconstruct 3-D US volumes from the predicted probe trajectories and corresponding 2-D B-mode frames. Arterial surfaces were extracted from compounded segmentation masks.
- **Reconstruction Fidelity:**
 - **In vivo forearm arteries:** Hausdorff Distance ($\text{HD}_{\text{surf}}$) ranged from 1.58 mm to 2.35 mm, and Dice coefficient ($D$) from 0.69 to 0.87. C-shaped, linear, and S-shaped trajectories generally yielded stronger geometric agreement than the zigzag trajectory.
 - **Phantom vessels:** $\text{HD}_{\text{surf}}$ ranged from 1.73 mm to 1.84 mm, and $D$ from 0.69 to 0.71.
- **Interpretation:** These results indicate that the estimated trajectories support geometrically consistent 3-D reconstruction across various sweep patterns and imaging targets. The surface-distance maps showed that remaining errors were predominantly localized rather than indicative of large global distortions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for freehand 3-D ultrasound (US) reconstruction that addresses the limitations of existing methods by integrating external visual guidance with local ultrasound cues. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Accuracy and Robustness:** The study demonstrates that combining global visual guidance with local ultrasound cues leads to significantly more accurate and robust probe pose estimation compared to either modality alone. The proposed US + Dual-Cam model reduces average trajectory drift to 1.67 mm and 1.29 mm on the FUSION-J and FUSION-L datasets, respectively, representing substantial improvements (16.50% and 27.12%) over a strong dual-camera baseline. This level of accuracy, achieved without expensive conventional tracking systems, is critical for clinical utility.

2. **Principled Multi-Sensor Fusion:** The framework offers a principled approach to multi-sensor fusion, assigning complementary roles to each modality. External cameras provide a globally stable spatial anchor, overcoming the inherent ambiguity and drift of purely US-based methods. Simultaneously, internal B-mode US images contribute fine-grained, anatomy-aware local motion cues, which are not accessible through external vision alone, to refine the camera-derived estimates. This "global-to-local" strategy capitalizes on the strengths of each sensor while mitigating their individual weaknesses.

3. **Improved 3-D Reconstruction Quality:** The enhanced pose estimation accuracy directly translates into higher-quality 3-D US volume reconstruction. The reported Hausdorff distances of 1.58 mm to 2.35 mm and Dice scores of 0.69 to 0.87 for *in vivo* forearm arteries and phantom vessels demonstrate the geometric fidelity of the reconstructed volumes across diverse scanning trajectories. This allows for more intuitive volumetric visualization, which can aid in diagnosis, surgical planning, and interventional guidance.

4. **Cost-Effectiveness and Accessibility:** By utilizing low-cost dual RGB cameras and standard 2-D US probes, the proposed system offers a more affordable and accessible alternative to expensive matrix-array probes or dedicated external optical/electromagnetic tracking systems. This reduction in hardware complexity and cost has the potential to expand the adoption of volumetric US imaging beyond specialized academic or large clinical centers into routine clinical practice and point-of-care settings.

5. **Applicability in Challenging Scenarios:** The method demonstrates improved performance under challenging practical conditions, including long or complex freehand sweeps and variations in image quality or visual ambiguity. Its robustness across different ultrasound machines (validated on FUSION-J and FUSION-L) further underscores its potential for broader clinical deployment.

6. **Contribution to the Research Community:** The authors plan to release two new in-house datasets (FUSION-J and FUSION-L) and the source code upon acceptance. This contribution will facilitate further research, benchmarking, and development in the field of freehand 3-D US reconstruction.

In summary, this work provides a practical and effective solution for robust freehand 3-D US reconstruction, leveraging complementary information from external cameras and internal ultrasound images. Its potential impact lies in making accurate volumetric ultrasound imaging more accessible and deployable, thereby enhancing diagnostic capabilities and guiding interventions in a wider range of clinical applications.
