# A Mechanistic Analysis of Sim-and-Real Co-Training in Generative Robot Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> This work presents a mechanistic analysis of sim-and-real co-training for diffusion-based generative robot policies, identifying structured representation alignment and importance reweighting as two intrinsic effects. The study demonstrates how these effects balance cross-domain knowledge transfer and target domain adaptation to improve robot manipulation performance.

## Problem

- Training robust robot policies is bottlenecked by the scarcity of real-world data.
- Despite empirical success, the underlying mechanisms governing co-training paradigms, which combine limited real data with abundant surrogate data, remain largely uncharacterized.
- A lack of systematic understanding of co-training's internal dynamics hinders principled improvements and targeted algorithm development for robot learning.

## Method

![[2604.13645-method.png]]
*A workflow example of co-training systems for generative robot policies. We identify Structured Representation Alignment as the main intrinsic effect in co-training: it refers to both representation alignment and domain discernibility, which lay the foundation of action transfer and adaptation.*

- A theoretical analysis of ==diffusion-based generative robot policies== trained jointly on multi-domain data identified two intrinsic effects: ==structured representation alignment== and ==importance reweighting==.
- Controlled toy experiments were used to disentangle and individually investigate these effects, followed by extensive empirical validation on realistic sim-and-real robot manipulation tasks.
- The findings informed the design of **CFG-ADDA**, a new co-training method that integrates ==adversarial alignment== with ==explicit domain conditioning==, and explored the use of ==negative Classifier-Free Guidance== scales for knowledge transfer.

## Results

- Controlled experiments confirmed structured alignment as the primary driver, accounting for **~50%** of loss variance, with importance reweighting contributing **~20%**.
- In robot manipulation tasks, strong positive correlation was observed between representation alignment and task success, with optimal performance typically achieved within balanced mixing ratios (**0.016 to 0.3**).
- The proposed CFG-ADDA method consistently outperformed prior techniques, achieving an average success rate of **~74% (21/30)** in real-world robot manipulation tasks and demonstrating robust performance across various mixing ratios.
- It was discovered that utilizing negative Classifier-Free Guidance (λ < 0) can actively transfer knowledge from surrogate domains during inference, leading to improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured representation alignment, which balances cross-domain feature alignment with domain-specific discernibility, is the dominant intrinsic effect, explaining approximately 50% of performance variance.
> - Co-training operates within distinct representation regimes: "disjoint," "structured aligned" (optimal for adaptive transfer), and "overlapping" (leading to negative transfer when representations become indistinguishable yet actions differ).
> - Effective co-training requires a careful balance; implicit representation alignment emerges within optimal mixing ratios, but retaining domain discernibility is crucial, as blind alignment can degrade performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. A Mechanistic Analysis of Sim-and-Real Co-Training in Generative Robot Policies

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is significantly impacted by the scarcity of real-world data, which presents a fundamental bottleneck for training robust robot policies. To address this, co-training paradigms have emerged, integrating limited in-domain real-world data with abundant surrogate data sources such as simulation or cross-embodiment robot data. These methods, which include approaches like sim-and-real and human-to-robot co-training, have demonstrated empirical success in training generative robot policies, particularly diffusion-based models.

However, despite their practical effectiveness, the underlying mechanisms that govern the efficacy of co-training remain largely uncharacterized. Existing literature often treats co-training as a "black box," lacking a systematic understanding of its internal dynamics and the factors contributing to its performance. Prior empirical analyses have provided some insights but have not fully elucidated the intrinsic effects at play.

This work positions itself within this landscape by providing a mechanistic analysis of sim-and-real co-training. It aims to move beyond empirical observation to uncover the fundamental principles governing co-training's effectiveness. By investigating the intrinsic effects that influence performance, the research seeks to offer a unified interpretation of existing co-training techniques, such as optimal transport-based regularization, adversarial discriminative domain adaptation, and classifier-free guidance. The goal is to establish a principled framework that can inform the design of more effective and robust co-training algorithms, thereby addressing the long-standing challenge of data scarcity in robotics in a more systematic manner.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to comprehensively investigate the mechanisms underlying sim-and-real co-training in the context of generative robot policies, with a specific focus on diffusion-based models. The authors aim to identify and characterize the intrinsic effects that determine when and why co-training is effective.

This objective is motivated by several factors:
- **Lack of Mechanistic Understanding:** Co-training has achieved empirical success in various robotic applications, but the reasons for its effectiveness are often unclear. The process is frequently treated as an opaque system, hindering principled improvements and targeted algorithm development. A deeper understanding of its internal workings is necessary to move beyond trial-and-error approaches.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** Data scarcity is a critical challenge in robotics. Leveraging abundant, inexpensive surrogate data (like simulation) alongside limited real-world data through co-training offers a practical solution. However, this introduces significant "domain gaps" that make knowledge transfer difficult. Understanding how co-training navigates these gaps is crucial for maximizing its utility.
- **Unifying Existing Techniques:** Numerous co-training techniques have been proposed, each with varying degrees of success across different scenarios. The authors are motivated to develop a unified explanatory framework that can interpret the behavior of these diverse methods, clarify their strengths and limitations, and provide a common ground for analysis.
- **Guiding Future Algorithm Design:** By identifying the core mechanisms, the research aims to provide insights that can directly inform the design of novel, more effective, and robust co-training algorithms. This includes understanding how to optimize key parameters, such as the data mixing ratio, and how to combine existing strategies for improved performance.
- **Specific Questions:** The work explicitly raises two guiding questions:
 - Do the identified intrinsic effects from theoretical analysis (structured representation alignment and importance reweighting) manifest in realistic robot manipulation tasks?
 - How can these insights be practically applied to design more effective co-training algorithms?

In essence, the motivation is to demystify co-training, transforming it from an empirically successful but poorly understood technique into a scientifically characterized method with clear principles for optimization and development.

#### 1.3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged approach, combining theoretical analysis with extensive empirical validation across controlled toy examples and realistic robot manipulation tasks.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
The study begins by analyzing the learning objective of diffusion-based generative robot policies when trained jointly on data from multiple domains. This analysis focuses on the score parameterization of diffusion models, leading to the hypothesis of two intrinsic effects:
1. **Structured Representation Alignment:** This effect is characterized as a balance between aligning cross-domain representations in a domain-invariant subspace (facilitating knowledge transfer) and preserving domain-specific discernibility (enabling adaptation to the target domain). The theory outlines three potential scenarios for representation alignment: "Disjoint" (no transfer), "Structured Aligned" (optimal balance for adaptive transfer), and "Overlapping" (negative transfer due to collapsed representations).
2. **Importance Reweighting Effect:** This refers to the domain-dependent modulation of action weightings, which influences the contribution of samples from each domain to action decisions during training. This effect is theoretically linked to the data mixing ratio (w), dataset sizes, and domain gaps.

**Controlled Toy Example:**
To disentangle and individually investigate the contributions of these two effects, a simplified toy experiment was designed.
- **Policy Model:** A 4-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) was used as the diffusion model architecture.
- **Data Generation:** Two manifolds, M_S (source) and M_T (target), were manually defined with distinct distributions. Data points were sampled, ensuring a significant disparity in size (N_S ≫ N_T) to simulate real-world data scarcity.
- **Alignment Control:** The distance between these manifolds was varied along a principal direction to simulate the "disjoint," "structured aligned," and "overlapping" representation scenarios.
- **Evaluation:** L2 loss was used to quantify model performance, and an ANOVA-style variance decomposition was conducted to assess the relative influence of representation alignment and the mixing ratio.

**Sim-and-Real Co-Training for Manipulation:**
To validate the theoretical insights in practical robotics scenarios, comprehensive experiments were conducted on manipulation tasks.
- **Robot Policy:** A transformer-based diffusion model, with ResNet18 as the vision backbone, was trained end-to-end.
- **Task Suite:** Three manipulation tasks from the robosuite environment were selected: NutAssembly, MugHang, and MugCleanup, chosen for their demands on precise control, dense object interaction, and long-horizon reasoning.
- **Environment Settings:** Experiments included both "sim-and-sim" and "sim-and-real" setups. For sim-and-sim, domain gaps were explicitly controlled along two dimensions: visual appearance ("visual-only"), environment physics ("physics-only"), and a combination ("visual-physics"). Sim-and-real experiments used calibrated real-world setups.
- **Data Preparation:** Target domains utilized 50 human demonstrations, while source domains employed approximately 3000 synthesized trajectories (generated using MimicGen).
- **Mixing Ratio Sweep:** Policies were co-trained across a range of mixing ratios (w ∈ {0, 0.005, w_n=0.016, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1}, where w_n is the natural mixing ratio).
- **Empirical Measurements:**
 - **Representation Alignment:** UMAP was used for qualitative visualization of latent features (from vision stem and encoder trunk). Quantitative metrics included Gromov-Wasserstein distance (for local geometric similarity) and Wasserstein distance (for global distributional distance) between real and simulation features. Correlation analysis (Pearson and Spearman coefficients) between alignment and task success rate was performed. An ablation study was conducted to explicitly encourage representation separation.
 - **Domain Discernibility:** A linear probing study using a 2-layer MLP was performed to classify domain origin (sim or real) from the encoder trunk's output embeddings, assessing how easily domains could be distinguished.

**Unified View of Co-Training Methods:**
The empirical findings were used to analyze and categorize existing co-training techniques:
- **Prior Methods Benchmarking:** Optimal Transport (OT), Adversarial Domain Adaptation (ADDA), and Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) were re-implemented and evaluated on the same tasks and settings. These methods were analyzed based on their primary emphasis on either promoting cross-domain alignment or preserving domain-specific information.
- **Proposed Method (CFG-ADDA):** Based on the derived insights, a simple combined approach, CFG-ADDA, was proposed. This method integrates adversarial alignment with explicit domain conditioning using one-hot embeddings for environment labels. The study also explored the effect of the CFG guidance scale (λ), investigating negative values for knowledge transfer during inference.
- **Evaluation of Combined Method:** CFG-ADDA was benchmarked against the other methods in both sim-and-sim and sim-and-real experiments across different mixing ratio regimes, with ablations on the guidance scale.

#### 1.4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings through its theoretical analysis and empirical investigations:

**Theoretical Insights and Toy Model Validation:**
- **Two Intrinsic Effects Identified:** The theoretical analysis identified two primary intrinsic effects governing co-training performance: "structured representation alignment" and the "importance reweighting effect." Structured representation alignment refers to the balance between cross-domain representation alignment for knowledge transfer and domain discernibility for adaptation. The importance reweighting effect refers to domain-dependent modulation of action weighting.
- **Structured Alignment as Primary Driver:** Controlled toy experiments confirmed the presence of both effects. Structured representation alignment was identified as the dominant factor, explaining approximately 50% of the loss variance. The importance reweighting effect, influenced by the mixing ratio, accounted for about 20% of the variance, indicating a secondary, modulatory role.
- **Representation Regimes:** The toy model demonstrated three distinct scenarios: "disjoint" (no positive transfer), "structured aligned" (optimal for adaptive action transfer and out-of-distribution generalization), and "overlapping" (negative transfer due to representations becoming indistinguishable while actions differ).

**Empirical Observations in Robotic Manipulation:**
- **Implicit Representation Alignment:** In end-to-end co-training for robot manipulation tasks, representation alignment was observed to emerge implicitly within an appropriate range of data mixing ratios. UMAP visualizations indicated local geometric alignment in shallow visual features and global alignment in deeper observation features (Figure 6).
- **Correlation with Performance:** Quantitative measurements (Gromov-Wasserstein and Wasserstein distances) showed a moderate-to-strong positive correlation (Pearson r and Spearman ρ generally between -0.6 and -0.8, with p-values < 0.04) between representation alignment (smaller distance meaning stronger alignment) and task success rate across sim-to-real and most sim-to-sim settings (Figure 7).
- **Importance of Discernibility:** Despite alignment, features retained domain discernibility. A simple 2-layer MLP could classify domains with near 100% accuracy on encoder trunk output embeddings, suggesting representations were partially aligned rather than fully collapsed.
- **Harmful Blind Alignment:** In scenarios with significant physics-only domain gaps, blind representation alignment could lead to performance degradation. The correlation between alignment and success rate in these settings could even become negative (Figure 7), highlighting that preserving domain discernibility is necessary for effective action adaptation. Explicitly encouraging representation separation through an ablation resulted in consistent performance drops (Table 1).
- **Balanced Mixing Ratios:** Best performance was consistently achieved within a specific range of mixing ratios (referred to as "balanced mixing ratios"), generally between 0.016 and 0.3 in the experiments (Figure 8, Appendix D.3). The mixing ratio was found to not merely re-weight data but also implicitly reshape the learned representation space.

**Unified View of Co-Training Methods and Proposed Approach:**
- **Categorization of Existing Methods:** Existing co-training techniques were categorized based on their primary emphasis:
 - **Alignment-focused:** Optimal Transport (+OT) and Adversarial Domain Adaptation (+ADDA) primarily promote cross-domain alignment. These methods showed improvements in balanced mixing ratio regimes but often degraded under unbalanced mixing (Figure 9).
 - **Discernibility-focused:** Classifier-Free Guidance (+CFG) preserves domain discernibility. It demonstrated greater robustness in unbalanced mixing but had limited peak performance compared to alignment methods in balanced settings.
- **CFG-ADDA Performance:** The proposed combination method, CFG-ADDA (integrating adversarial alignment with explicit domain conditioning), consistently achieved strong performance across both balanced and unbalanced mixing ratio regimes in sim-and-sim experiments (Figure 9).
- **Real-World Effectiveness:** In real-world robotic manipulation tasks, CFG-ADDA yielded stable and substantial improvements over prior methods, achieving an average success rate of approximately 74% (21/30) across challenging tasks (Table 2).
- **Guidance Scale (λ) Insight:** Ablations on the CFG guidance scale revealed that setting λ < 0 (e.g., -0.5) could actively transfer knowledge from surrogate domains during inference, leading to improved performance for both CFG and CFG-ADDA, rather than just amplifying action gaps with positive λ (Figure 10).
- **Mixing Ratio Guideline:** A guideline for selecting mixing ratios was proposed based on the relative dataset sizes (N/M), providing a narrowed search range (w_n, w_q) for effective co-training, empirically validated across different dataset sizes (Appendix D.5, Figure 15).
- **Three Regimes in Practice:** Further experiments confirmed the existence of "overlapping", "structured aligned", and "disjoint" representation regimes in practical settings, exhibiting a non-monotonic, U-shaped relationship between alignment and performance when discernibility is also considered (Appendix D.6, Figure 16).

#### 1.5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic mechanistic analysis of co-training, shifting the understanding of this empirically successful paradigm from a "black box" to a more characterized scientific process. The findings offer several layers of significance and potential impact:

- **Fundamental Understanding of Co-Training:** The identification of "structured representation alignment" and the "importance reweighting effect" as the two intrinsic mechanisms is a core contribution. This offers a foundational theoretical framework for understanding how limited real-world data and abundant surrogate data interact during training to influence robot policy performance. It elucidates the crucial balance between domain-invariant feature learning for knowledge transfer and domain-specific information retention for real-world adaptation.
- **Unified Interpretation of Existing Methods:** The research provides a unified lens through which to analyze and interpret various existing co-training techniques (e.g., Optimal Transport, ADDA, CFG). By categorizing these methods based on their emphasis on alignment versus discernibility, the study clarifies their inherent strengths, limitations, and the conditions under which they are most effective. This framework can help practitioners select or combine methods more strategically.
- **Guidance for Algorithm Design and Optimization:** The derived insights directly inform the design of more principled, robust, and effective co-training algorithms. The proposed CFG-ADDA method, which explicitly balances alignment and discernibility, demonstrates substantial and consistent improvements over prior approaches in challenging manipulation tasks, including real-world settings. This validates the practical utility of the mechanistic understanding. Furthermore, the identification of optimal mixing ratio regimes and the novel perspective on the Classifier-Free Guidance scale (utilizing negative λ for active knowledge transfer) provide concrete guidelines for optimizing co-training implementations.
- **Increased Efficiency in Robotics Research:** By narrowing the search space for effective mixing ratios and understanding the interplay of domain gaps, the research can lead to more efficient and reliable large-scale co-training experiments in robotics. This can reduce the computational resources and human effort required for developing high-performing robot policies.
- **Broader Applicability and Future Directions:** While grounded in imitation learning for generative robot policies, the co-training paradigm is broadly relevant across various machine learning settings, including world modeling and reinforcement learning. The insights from this work may stimulate further research into the underlying mechanisms of multi-domain learning in these areas, potentially leading to more robust and generalizable AI systems. Future work can extend this analysis to other co-training scenarios (e.g., human-robot co-training), delve deeper into the dynamic interaction of mechanisms during training, and characterize the intrinsic structure of learned representations.
