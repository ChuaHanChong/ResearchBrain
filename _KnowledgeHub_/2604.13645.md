# A Mechanistic Analysis of Sim-and-Real Co-Training in Generative Robot Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> This work presents a mechanistic analysis of sim-and-real co-training for diffusion-based generative robot policies, identifying structured representation alignment and importance reweighting as two intrinsic effects. The study demonstrates how these effects balance cross-domain knowledge transfer and target domain adaptation to improve robot manipulation performance.

## Problem

- Training robust robot policies is bottlenecked by the scarcity of real-world data.
- Despite empirical success, the underlying mechanisms governing co-training paradigms, which combine limited real data with abundant surrogate data, remain largely uncharacterized.
- A lack of systematic understanding of co-training's internal dynamics hinders principled improvements and targeted algorithm development for robot learning.

## Method

![[2604.13645-method.png]]
*A workflow example of co-training systems for generative robot policies. We identify Structured Representation Alignment as the main intrinsic effect in co-training: it refers to both representation alignment and domain discernibility, which lay the foundation of action transfer and adaptation.*

- A theoretical analysis of ==diffusion-based generative robot policies== trained jointly on multi-domain data identified two intrinsic effects: ==structured representation alignment== and ==importance reweighting==.
- Controlled toy experiments were used to disentangle and individually investigate these effects, followed by extensive empirical validation on realistic sim-and-real robot manipulation tasks.
- The findings informed the design of **CFG-ADDA**, a new co-training method that integrates ==adversarial alignment== with ==explicit domain conditioning==, and explored the use of ==negative Classifier-Free Guidance== scales for knowledge transfer.

## Results

- Controlled experiments confirmed structured alignment as the primary driver, accounting for **~50%** of loss variance, with importance reweighting contributing **~20%**.
- In robot manipulation tasks, strong positive correlation was observed between representation alignment and task success, with optimal performance typically achieved within balanced mixing ratios (**0.016 to 0.3**).
- The proposed CFG-ADDA method consistently outperformed prior techniques, achieving an average success rate of **~74% (21/30)** in real-world robot manipulation tasks and demonstrating robust performance across various mixing ratios.
- It was discovered that utilizing negative Classifier-Free Guidance (λ < 0) can actively transfer knowledge from surrogate domains during inference, leading to improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured representation alignment, which balances cross-domain feature alignment with domain-specific discernibility, is the dominant intrinsic effect, explaining approximately 50% of performance variance.
> - Co-training operates within distinct representation regimes: "disjoint," "structured aligned" (optimal for adaptive transfer), and "overlapping" (leading to negative transfer when representations become indistinguishable yet actions differ).
> - Effective co-training requires a careful balance; implicit representation alignment emerges within optimal mixing ratios, but retaining domain discernibility is crucial, as blind alignment can degrade performance.
