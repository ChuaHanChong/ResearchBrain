# OASIS: Observation-Action Space Alignment via SE(3) Trajectory Prediction for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> OASIS, a visuomotor policy from Xi
’an Jiaotong University, improves robotic manipulation by explicitly predicting SE(3) end-effector trajectories in the camera frame, thereby aligning its intermediate representation with the action space. The system achieved a 97.6% average success rate on LIBERO tasks and an 89.2% average success rate across real-world multi-task, spatial, and long-horizon robotic challenges.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policies like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs) use intermediate representations predominantly in the observation space.
- This design forces action decoders to implicitly infer complex 6-DoF rigid-body motions (SE(3)) from non-geometric features, leading to ambiguity and error accumulation.
- Current methods, even with pretraining or auxiliary spatial features, do not explicitly inject the geometric structure of SE(3) into the intermediate representation.

## Method

- **OASIS** introduces an "aligned-intermediate design principle" by explicitly predicting a camera-frame SE(3) end-effector trajectory, providing a direct geometric inductive bias.
- The architecture comprises a 3D-aware feature encoder (fusing vision-language with metric depth), a ==Transformer==-based SE(3) trajectory predictor supervised by camera-frame poses, and a learned action decoder.
- The system is trained end-to-end on standard expert demonstrations, with the action decoder conditioned on the pose-supervised hidden states from the trajectory predictor to generate executable actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **97.6%** across LIBERO simulation benchmarks, outperforming Unified-VLA by **2.1%** and ThinkAct by **13.2%** (**24.3%** on Long-horizon tasks).
- Attained an **89.2%** average success rate in real-world settings (Franka Research 3, Kinova Gen3 robots), surpassing π
0.5 by **7.6%**, RDT by **19.7%**, and ACT by **48.7%**.
- Demonstrated strong out-of-distribution generalization (e.g., **94.8%** success with unseen backgrounds) and a potential low-data advantage, matching baseline performance with **60%** fewer demonstrations on a real-world long-horizon task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly aligning intermediate representations with the robot's rigid-body action space via SE(3) end-effector trajectory prediction provides a crucial geometric inductive bias to the action decoder.
> - Predicting trajectories in the camera frame simplifies the learning task for the predictor by deferring the recovery of camera-to-world extrinsic transformations to the learned action decoder.
> - Utilizing metric depth features within the 3D-aware encoder significantly enhances the representation's spatial understanding, improving manipulation precision and generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is significantly advanced by visuomotor policies, which employ neural networks to map image observations, language instructions, and robot states directly to executable 6-DoF actions and gripper commands. Within this domain, two primary families define the current research frontier: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs).

Vanilla VLA models typically leverage pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to construct multimodal features from visual observations and language instructions, subsequently feeding these into an action decoder. Feature-enhanced VLA models build upon this by integrating auxiliary spatial features, such as depth maps, regions of interest, or 2D image-plane motion trajectories, to improve spatial understanding. In contrast, WAMs adopt an approach of learning a world model that predicts future images or latent visual features, from which actions are then decoded.

Despite their empirical successes, a common characteristic of both VLA and WAM models is that their intermediate representations largely remain within the observation space. These representations do not inherently share the rigid-body geometry of the action space, which for a robot end-effector corresponds to the Special Euclidean group SE(3). This disconnect necessitates that the action decoder implicitly infer and recover the complex 6-DoF rigid-body motions from these observation-space features. While methods like pretraining on extensive robotic demonstrations or adding auxiliary spatial supervision can mitigate this ambiguity, they do not explicitly inject the geometric structure of SE(3) into the intermediate representation itself.

OASIS positions itself as an alternative approach by proposing an "aligned-intermediate design principle." It introduces SE(3) end-effector trajectory prediction as a mechanism to explicitly align the intermediate representation with the action space. This approach aims to provide a direct geometric inductive bias, rather than relying solely on enriched observation-space features or predictive visual capacity, thereby addressing a fundamental limitation in current visuomotor policy designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a visuomotor policy that overcomes the inherent ambiguity in decoding 6-DoF robot actions from intermediate representations that are not geometrically aligned with the rigid-body motion of the action space. The authors identify a key limitation in current VLA models and WAMs: their intermediate representations, whether multimodal features, auxiliary spatial features, or future visual states, exist predominantly in the observation domain. This forces the action decoder to implicitly recover the precise SE(3) pose and motion required for robot execution.

The primary motivations driving the development of OASIS are:

- **Geometric Alignment:** To directly address the challenge of aligning the intermediate representation used by visuomotor policies with the rigid-body geometry of the robot's action space (SE(3)). The hypothesis is that an explicit pose readout within the intermediate representation will provide a crucial geometric inductive bias, simplifying the action decoding process.
- **Improved Precision and Generalization:** To demonstrate that providing such a geometrically aligned intermediate representation leads to enhanced manipulation precision and improved generalization capabilities, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios. The authors posit that explicitly supervising the intermediate representation against the rigid-body geometry of the action space will reduce the accumulation of errors inherent in implicitly inferring poses from visual features.
- **Training Efficiency and Accessibility:** To achieve these improvements without requiring computationally intensive large-scale robotic pretraining or labor-intensive spatial annotations. The goal is to develop a system that can be trained end-to-end using standard expert demonstrations, thereby making advanced visuomotor policies more accessible to a wider range of research groups and applications.
- **Explicit Pose Information for Decoder:** To provide the action decoder with explicit, horizon-indexed pose information, allowing it to focus on learning practical residuals (e.g., frame conversion, prediction noise, contact dynamics, gripper timing) rather than implicitly inferring target poses and action parameterizations.
- **Camera-Frame Prediction:** To predict end-effector trajectories in the camera frame to keep the intermediate representation tethered to the observation stream and alleviate the predictor's need to learn the camera-to-world extrinsic transformation, leaving this to the more robust learned action decoder.

By formalizing the concept of a "geometrically aligned" intermediate representation and instantiating it through SE(3) end-effector trajectory prediction, OASIS aims to provide a more direct and effective mechanism for improving visuomotor policy performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OASIS (Observation-Action Space Alignment via SE(3) Trajectory Prediction) is a visuomotor policy designed based on an "aligned-intermediate design principle," where the intermediate representation provides an explicit pose readout geometrically aligned with the action space. The architecture consists of three main components trained end-to-end: a 3D-aware feature encoder, an SE(3) trajectory predictor, and an action decoder.

- **Problem Formulation and Aligned Intermediate Representations:**
 - The policy receives an image observation ($o_t$), language instruction ($l$), and current end-effector state ($e_t = [p_t, \theta_t]^\top$).
 - The goal is to produce an action chunk $A_t = \{(a_{t+h-1}, g_{t+h-1})\}_{h=1}^H$ that drives the end-effector through a world-frame target pose sequence $\{T_{t+h}\}_{h=1}^H$.
 - A key design principle is that a horizon-indexed intermediate representation $m_t = \{m_{t+h}\}_{h=1}^H$ is "geometrically aligned" if it provides an explicit pose readout $r(m_{t+h}) = g \cdot T_{t+h} \in \text{SE(3)}$ at each step, where $g$ is a fixed rigid transform.
 - OASIS achieves this alignment by predicting a camera-frame SE(3) end-effector trajectory $\{T^c_{t+h}\}_{h=1}^H$. This provides the decoder with explicit pose information, up to a fixed camera-to-world transform ($g = T_{c \to w}^{-1}$), allowing the decoder to learn residuals.

- **3D-aware Feature Encoder:**
 - **Purpose:** To generate a 3D-aware representation ($h_{3D_t}$) that fuses vision-language features with metric-depth features, providing calibrated 3D spatial information.
 - **Metric Depth Feature:** Utilizes Depth Anything 3 (DA3METRIC-LARGE model, frozen) to extract multi-scale metric-depth features from the observation $o_t$, projecting them into depth hidden states ($h_{d_t}$). The use of *metric* depth is emphasized for accurate physical distances.
 - **Vision-Language Feature:** Employs a VLM with a Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone (LoRA-tuned) and DINOv2/SigLIP visual encoders. This VLM processes $o_t$ and $l$ jointly to produce visual hidden states ($h_{v_t}$) and language hidden states ($h_{l_t}$).
 - **Output:** The final 3D-aware representation $h_{3D_t}$ is formed by concatenating these hidden states: $h_{3D_t} = [h_{l_t}, h_{v_t}, h_{d_t}]$.

- **SE(3) Trajectory Predictor:**
 - **Purpose:** To align its intermediate representation with the action space by predicting a horizon-$H$ sequence of camera-frame end-effector SE(3) states ($T^c_t$). The pose-supervised hidden states from this predictor ($h_{traj}$) condition the action decoder.
 - **Architecture:** A 4-layer Transformer with $H=8$ learnable trajectory queries. These queries interact via self-attention with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) for temporal coherence and cross-attention on $h_{3D_t}$.
 - **Output:** Trajectory hidden states $h_{traj}$ (which are then passed to the action decoder) and a linear projection from these hidden states to per-step pose vectors $\tau^c_t = \{e^c_{t+h} = [p^c_{t+h}, \theta^c_{t+h}]^\top\}_{h=1}^H$.
 - **SE(3) Parameterization:** Position ($p^c_{t+h} \in \mathbb{R}^3$) and axis-angle vector ($\theta^c_{t+h} \in \mathbb{R}^3$) are used. Axis-angle naturally induces valid SO(3) rotation matrices, avoiding post-hoc corrections. Predictions are made in the camera frame, simplifying the learning task by deferring camera-to-world extrinsic recovery to the action decoder.
 - **Training Objective:** Supervised by ground-truth camera-frame pose vectors, using an $\ell_1$ loss: $L_{traj} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H \|\hat{e}^c_{t+h} - e^c_{t+h}\|_1$.

- **Action Decoder:**
 - **Purpose:** To generate the executable action chunk $A_t$ (6-DoF relative actions $a_{t+h-1}$ and gripper commands $g_{t+h-1}$) by conditioning on the pose-supervised hidden states $h_{traj}$ and the current end-effector state $e_t$. This component learns to approximate the relative-action recovery and absorb practical residuals.
 - **Architecture:** A 2-layer Transformer with $H=8$ learnable action queries. These queries attend to the trajectory hidden states ($h_{traj}$) and a linear projection of the current end-effector state ($h_{state}$).
 - **Training Objective:** Supervised against expert demonstrations using an $\ell_1$ loss: $L_{act} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H \|[\hat{a}_{t+h-1}, \hat{g}_{t+h-1}] - [a_{t+h-1}, g_{t+h-1}]\|_1$.

- **Total Objective:** The overall training objective combines trajectory supervision and executable-action supervision: $L_{total} = \lambda L_{traj} + L_{act}$, where $\lambda=0.1$.
- **Training Details:** OASIS is trained end-to-end on standard expert demonstrations (RLDS datasets). The Qwen2.5-0.5B VLM is LoRA-tuned, DA3METRIC-LARGE is frozen, and the predictor, decoder, and projection layers are trained from scratch. The model has 0.18B trainable parameters out of 1.73B total.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OASIS was conducted across both simulation and real-world environments, systematically comparing its performance against established baselines and through targeted ablation studies.

**Simulation Experiments:**

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** OASIS achieved an average success rate of 97.6% across the four LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), surpassing the next-best baseline, Unified-VLA, by 2.1%. This lead was observed consistently across all suites, with a 1.4% margin on Spatial, 1.6% on Goal, and 1.2% on Long over the strongest prior results in each category, and a tie on Object. Compared to ThinkAct, which predicts 2D image-plane trajectories, OASIS demonstrated a 13.2% average lead and a 24.3% lead on the Long suite.
- **CALVIN ABC→D Benchmark:** On the challenging CALVIN ABC→D setting, which tests out-of-distribution generalization, OASIS attained an average sequence length of 4.57, outperforming all baselines. For five consecutive tasks, OASIS achieved an 83.3% success rate, leading DreamVLA by 5.2%. The authors attribute this widening lead in multi-stage tasks to the geometrically aligned intermediate representation, which potentially results in smaller per-step error accumulation.
- **SE(3) Trajectory Visualization:** Qualitative analysis demonstrated that the predicted translation waypoints and rotation axes closely corresponded to the executed end-effector paths on complex tasks, indicating that the trajectory predictor provided consistent and meaningful rigid-body motion priors for action generation.

**Ablation Study (primarily on LIBERO-Long, consistent on LIBERO-Spatial):**

- **Effectiveness of 3D-aware Feature Encoder (Metric Depth):** Removing the metric depth feature (w/o Metric) from the encoder reduced the LIBERO-Long success rate from 95.2% to 91.8%. Replacing it with relative depth (w/ Rel.) only marginally improved performance to 92.0%, suggesting the *metric* scale, rather than just depth information, is critical for the 3D-aware representation.
- **Impact of SE(3) Trajectory Prediction:** A controlled ablation demonstrated a monotonic increase in performance with richer geometric supervision targets on the pose-supervised hidden states.
 - A variant without the trajectory predictor (`w/o Traj.`) achieved 89.5%.
 - Routing the trajectory loss through a parallel predictor branch without conditioning the decoder (`w/ AuxTraj`) yielded only 89.7%, highlighting the necessity of passing supervised hidden states to the decoder.
 - Predicting a 2D image-plane trajectory (`w/ 2D`) reached 90.7%, indicating limited benefit from image-plane geometry alone.
 - Adding 3D position prediction (`w/ 3D Pos.`) improved to 92.3%.
 - Adding world-frame 3D pose prediction (`w/ World`) reached 93.2%.
 - OASIS, with camera-frame SE(3) prediction, achieved the highest at 95.2%, supporting the design choice of predicting in the camera frame to avoid learning the camera-to-world extrinsic.
- **Choice of SO(3) Parameterization:** Axis-angle (95.2%) outperformed Euler angles (92.2%) and quaternions (91.6%), aligning with axis-angle being a minimal, constraint-free chart suitable for the bounded orientation range in tabletop manipulation.
- **Closed-Form Decoder Ablation:** Replacing the learned action decoder with a hardcoded geometric pipeline (even with privileged gripper information) resulted in significantly lower success rates (12.4% on LIBERO-Spatial and 0.0% on LIBERO-Long). This confirms that a learned decoder is essential for absorbing residuals like uncalibrated camera-to-world extrinsics, prediction noise, and gripper timing, rather than merely performing a direct pose-to-action conversion.

**Real-World Experiments:**

- **Performance:** On Franka Research 3 and Kinova Gen3 robots, OASIS achieved an 89.2% average success rate across multi-task (Goal), spatial-relationship (Spatial), and long-horizon (Long) suites. It outperformed π$_{0.5}$ by 7.6%, RDT by 19.7%, Seer-Large by 30.8%, and ACT by 48.7%. The lead was maintained across all three task categories.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** OASIS demonstrated robustness under OOD perturbations on the Goal task, achieving 94.8% success with unseen backgrounds, 91.2% with altered camera viewpoints, and 86.5% with human interference. This performance consistently surpassed π$_{0.5}$ in these settings, suggesting that the metric-depth feature and camera-frame prediction contribute to OOD robustness. The predictor's ability to regenerate trajectories at each step aids in real-time retargeting during dynamic interference.
- **Data-Scaling Study:** On the real-world Long task, OASIS matched the 25-demonstration success rate of π$_{0.5}$ with only 10 demonstrations, indicating a potential low-data advantage in this specific long-horizon scenario.
- **Failure Analysis:** Residual errors primarily stemmed from sub-centimeter placement/orientation requirements (e.g., "Hang cup," "Place pot") and compounding inaccuracies across multi-stage, long-horizon tasks (e.g., "Long horizon task").
- **Inference Speed:** OASIS operates at approximately 20 Hz, predicting an 8-step SE(3) trajectory and corresponding action steps within 0.05 seconds.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper introduces a significant design principle for visuomotor policies: the explicit alignment of the intermediate representation with the robot's action space through SE(3) end-effector trajectory prediction. This "aligned-intermediate" approach offers a departure from traditional methods that rely on observation-space features, forcing the action decoder to implicitly infer rigid-body geometry.

The primary significance of OASIS lies in its demonstrated ability to enhance robotic manipulation performance. By providing a geometric inductive bias to the action decoder, OASIS consistently achieved superior task success rates and out-of-distribution generalization across a range of simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN) and real-world robot platforms (Franka Research 3, Kinova Gen3). The ablation studies reinforce the criticality of metric depth and, more prominently, the SE(3) trajectory prediction itself, particularly when its pose-supervised hidden states are directly accessible to the action decoder. The effectiveness of camera-frame prediction over world-frame prediction also highlights a practical design choice that leverages the strengths of a learned decoder in handling residuals.

Beyond performance, OASIS presents several practical advantages:
- **Training Efficiency:** It does not necessitate large-scale robotic pretraining or manual spatial annotations, relying solely on standard expert demonstrations. This reduces the data collection burden and computational resources required, potentially lowering the barrier to entry for research groups and organizations with limited resources.
- **Accessibility:** With only 0.18 billion trainable parameters out of a total of 1.73 billion (the majority being frozen pre-trained components), OASIS is computationally efficient, enabling deployment and training on commodity hardware (e.g., four NVIDIA A800 GPUs).
- **Inspectability and Debuggability:** The explicit prediction of an SE(3) trajectory provides a more interpretable intermediate representation compared to abstract latent features. This transparency can facilitate debugging and enhance trust in autonomous robotic systems, a critical factor for safer deployments.
- **Robustness:** The OOD generalization capabilities demonstrated in real-world experiments suggest increased robustness to variations in environments, camera viewpoints, and dynamic interference, which are common challenges in practical robotic applications.

The potential impact of OASIS is broad, particularly in applications requiring precise robotic manipulation. This includes:
- **Assistive Robotics:** Household robots performing complex tasks where accurate object placement and manipulation are crucial.
- **Industrial Automation:** Dexterous manipulation tasks in manufacturing settings that demand high precision and adaptability to slight variations.
- **Laboratory Automation:** Automating experimental procedures that involve delicate handling and precise positioning of samples or equipment.

The authors acknowledge current limitations, such as the focus on tabletop manipulation with a single arm, and suggest future work in extending this design principle to richer action spaces, like coupled SE(2) × SE(3) trajectories for mobile manipulation or contact-conditioned trajectories for dexterous hands. Overall, OASIS offers a practical, data-efficient, and effective framework for improving visuomotor policies, inspiring further exploration into geometrically aligned intermediate representations for robot learning.
