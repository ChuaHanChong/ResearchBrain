# FuseLIP: Multimodal Embeddings via Early Fusion of Discrete Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> FuseLIP proposes a single-encoder architecture for vision-language models that processes both images and text through early fusion of discrete tokens. This approach enables modality interaction at every layer of the transformer encoder, achieving superior performance on tasks requiring fine-grained visual-textual understanding while reducing trainable parameters by more than 50% compared to competitive baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional vision-language models like CLIP use separate encoders for images and text, preventing deep, unified interaction between modalities for tasks requiring joint understanding of image-text pairs.
- Existing late fusion methods for multimodal tasks limit modality interaction to the final processing stages, hindering the development of deeply integrated representations necessary for fine-grained visual-textual understanding.
- Multimodal sequence-to-sequence models, while versatile, often lack the strong zero-shot generalization capabilities of CLIP or incur high computational costs due to their size.

## Method

- FuseLIP introduces a ==single transformer-based encoder== that processes both images and text by converting them into unified sequences of ==discrete tokens== through early fusion.
- It employs a novel ==tokenization strategy== that combines standard text tokens with discrete visual tokens from TiTok family image tokenizers into a single, extended vocabulary.
- The model is trained with a dual-objective strategy that combines a modified ==Sigmoid contrastive loss== with a ==masked multimodal modeling (MMM) objective==, where masking applies to the same input used for contrastive learning.

## Results

- FuseLIP-B achieved top performance across nearly all evaluation benchmarks, leading on **8 out of 9** tasks when trained on CC3M+MM and **7 out of 9** tasks for CC12M+MM training.
- The model demonstrated significantly fewer trainable parameters (**65.6M** for FuseLIP-B vs. **149.7M** for SigLIP-B_SF), resulting in faster training and reduced GPU memory requirements.
- FuseLIP-S outperformed late fusion baselines by **9-24%** on text-guided transformation tasks like CC3M-TGIT, showing particular strength in understanding fine-grained visual changes such as cropping, rotation, and flipping operations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Early fusion of discrete image and text tokens within a single encoder enables richer, layer-wise modality interaction, leading to superior performance on tasks requiring fine-grained understanding.
> - A single encoder can effectively process both unimodal and multimodal data in a CLIP-like framework, challenging the established dual-encoder paradigm for vision-language models.
> - Seamless integration of masked multimodal modeling (MMM) alongside contrastive learning within a single encoder improves performance across tasks and simplifies the training process by eliminating the need for separate passes or auxiliary modules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Modern vision-language models like CLIP have achieved remarkable success in aligning visual and textual representations through contrastive learning. However, these models face a fundamental limitation: they use separate encoders for images and text, making them unable to natively process multimodal inputs—image-text pairs that require deep interaction between modalities—into a single unified representation.

FuseLIP introduces a paradigm shift by proposing a single-encoder architecture that processes both images and text through early fusion of discrete tokens. This approach enables modality interaction at every layer of the transformer encoder, rather than combining features only at the final stages as in traditional late fusion methods. The research demonstrates that this early fusion strategy leads to superior performance on tasks requiring fine-grained visual-textual understanding while maintaining competitive performance on standard benchmarks.

The work is authored by researchers from the Tübingen AI Center at the University of Tübingen and EPFL, bringing together expertise in robust machine learning, optimization, and practical deep learning architectures. This collaboration has produced a method that not only advances the state of multimodal representation learning but also simplifies the training process compared to existing multi-branch architectures.

### 2. Background and Motivation

The landscape of vision-language understanding has been dominated by models that employ separate encoders for different modalities. CLIP's success stems from its ability to align textual and visual features in a shared latent space through contrastive learning, excelling in tasks like zero-shot image classification and retrieval. However, this architecture cannot handle tasks requiring a single, unified representation of image-text pairs, such as visual question answering or text-guided image manipulation.

Existing approaches to address this limitation fall into two main categories:

**Late Fusion Methods** merge features from pre-trained unimodal encoders using additional learnable modules. While effective for many downstream tasks, this approach limits modality interaction to the final processing stages, potentially hindering the development of deeply integrated representations. Methods like MagicLens, FLAVA, and BridgeTower exemplify this approach but face inherent constraints in how thoroughly modalities can interact.

**Multimodal Sequence-to-Sequence Models** train encoder-decoder architectures that can handle various modalities and tasks. While versatile for transfer learning, these models often lack the strong zero-shot generalization capabilities that characterize CLIP. Recent multimodal Large Language Models adapted as encoders (like VLM2Vec) leverage extensive pre-training but incur high computational costs due to their size.

FuseLIP addresses these limitations by proposing early fusion through discrete tokenization. By converting both images and text into sequences of discrete tokens from a unified vocabulary, the method enables a single transformer encoder to process multimodal inputs with interaction occurring at every layer, not just at the end.

### 3. Architecture and Methodology

FuseLIP's core innovation lies in its unified tokenization strategy and single-encoder architecture that processes both modalities simultaneously.

#### 3.1. Tokenization Strategy

The architecture begins with converting both modalities into discrete tokens:

- **Text Tokenization**: Standard natural language tokenization is applied to text inputs
- **Image Tokenization**: Recent discrete image tokenizers from the TiTok family convert images into fixed sequences of discrete visual tokens (typically 128 tokens per image). Crucially, these tokenizers are trained solely for image compression and reconstruction, avoiding any pre-existing text-image alignment biases

The discrete tokens from both modalities are drawn from a single, extended vocabulary, enabling true early fusion. For multimodal inputs, token sequences are concatenated with images first, followed by text, separated by special `<bot>` and `<eot>` tokens.

#### 3.2. Single Encoder Architecture

A single transformer-based encoder, similar to the SigLIP text encoder with bidirectional attention, processes the concatenated token sequences. The architecture includes:

- **Unified Processing**: The same encoder parameters process image-only, text-only, or combined image-text inputs
- **Positional Embeddings**: Token sequences are mapped to d-dimensional vectors via an embedding matrix with positional embeddings
- **Attention Masking**: Padding tokens are excluded from self-attention computations
- **Output Extraction**: The final multimodal embedding is extracted from the output corresponding to the `<eot>` token

#### 3.3. Auxiliary Components

An auxiliary prediction head enables masked modeling objectives. This two-layer classification network maps the final transformer block output to predictions over the entire unified token vocabulary, following the FLAVA architecture design.

### 4. Training Approach

FuseLIP employs a dual-objective training strategy that combines contrastive learning with masked modeling:

#### 4.1. Contrastive Learning Objective

The model uses a modified Sigmoid loss adapted from SigLIP. Unlike traditional dual-encoder setups, FuseLIP's single encoder `f` processes all input types. For a batch of input pairs `(z₁ₖ, z₂ₖ)`, where `z` can represent text, image, or image-text combinations, the loss function encourages alignment of positive pairs while pushing apart negative pairs.

#### 4.2. Masked Multimodal Modeling (MMM)

This objective requires the model to recover randomly masked tokens from input sequences. FuseLIP's unified token space simplifies this process significantly compared to previous approaches:

```
L_MMM = CrossEntropy(h(f(masked_input)), original_tokens)
```

Where `h` represents the auxiliary prediction head. The key advantage is that masking applies to the same input used for contrastive loss, eliminating the need for separate forward passes or auxiliary modules.

#### 4.3. Combined Training Objective

The final training objective combines both losses:

```
min(L_MMSigLIP + α × L_MMM)
```

Where α is set to 0.25 as the balancing hyperparameter. The image tokenizer remains frozen throughout training, focusing learning on the encoder and prediction head.

### 5. Experimental Setup

The researchers designed comprehensive experiments to evaluate FuseLIP's capabilities across diverse scenarios:

#### 5.1. Training Data

The training regimen incorporates both traditional and newly generated datasets:

**Traditional Datasets**: CC3M and CC12M provide standard image-text pairs for general vision-language alignment.

**Novel Multimodal Datasets**: Recognizing the scarcity of challenging multimodal data, the authors generated several new task-specific datasets:

- **Text-guided Image Transformations (TGIT)**: Pairs of original images with transformation descriptions and their corresponding transformed images
- **Generated VQA Data**: Using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct to convert CC3M captions into question-answer pairs
- **Visual Grounding Tasks**: Creating image-region description pairs from Visual Genome annotations
- **HQ-Edit Integration**: Incorporating synthetic image edit pairs for transformation understanding

**Hard Negatives**: Batches explicitly include semantically similar examples to force discriminative learning, such as multiple transformations of the same image or different descriptions for the same query.

#### 5.2. Evaluation Benchmarks

Models are evaluated across comprehensive tasks:

**Standard Benchmarks**: ImageNet-1k classification and various MMEB subtasks covering Classification, VQA, Retrieval, and Grounding.

**Novel Multimodal Benchmarks**: New tasks specifically designed to test multimodal interaction capabilities:
- **OpenImages-Crop (OI-Crop)** and **OpenImages-Position (OI-Pos)**: Testing object retrieval and positional understanding
- **Visual Grounding validation**: Using the newly generated VG-Crop dataset
- **Transformation Understanding**: CC3M-TGIT validation split

**Compositionality Assessment**: SugarCrepe benchmark evaluates understanding of compositional concepts including object, attribute, and relation modifications.

### 6. Results and Analysis

FuseLIP demonstrates superior performance, particularly on tasks requiring deep multimodal interaction:

#### 6.1. Overall Performance

FuseLIP-B achieves top performance across nearly all evaluation benchmarks. When trained on CC3M+MM, it leads on 8 out of 9 tasks, and for CC12M+MM training, it achieves highest scores on 7 out of 9 tasks. This consistent dominance validates the early fusion approach.

#### 6.2. Parameter Efficiency

Despite superior performance, FuseLIP-B requires significantly fewer trainable parameters (65.6M vs. 149.7M for SigLIP-B_SF), primarily due to its frozen image tokenizer. This efficiency translates to faster training and reduced GPU memory requirements.

#### 6.3. Task-Specific Advantages

The most compelling results emerge in text-guided transformation tasks. On CC3M-TGIT, FuseLIP-S outperforms SigLIP-B_MLF by 9-10% and SigLIP-B_SF by 22-24%. Detailed analysis reveals FuseLIP's particular strength in tasks requiring understanding of fine-grained visual changes:

- **Cropping Operations**: Understanding spatial boundaries based on text descriptions
- **Rotation Tasks**: Comprehending directional transformations
- **Flipping Operations**: Grasping mirror-image relationships

These results suggest that late fusion approaches lose crucial visual structural details that early fusion preserves through continuous modality interaction.

#### 6.4. Component Analysis

**Hard Negatives Impact**: Ablation studies confirm that hard negative examples are crucial for multimodal task performance. Their absence leads to significant drops in VG-Crop, OI-Crop, OI-Pos, and CC3M-TGIT performance.

**MMM Loss Effectiveness**: The masked modeling objective consistently improves performance across all tasks. FuseLIP's architecture enables seamless integration of this objective without the computational overhead typical of multi-branch models.

**Compositionality Understanding**: FuseLIP-B achieves highest mean performance on SugarCrepe, indicating that early fusion enhances understanding of complex compositional concepts in vision-language tasks.

### 7. Significance and Impact

FuseLIP represents a fundamental advancement in multimodal representation learning with several key implications:

#### 7.1. Architectural Innovation

The work successfully demonstrates that CLIP-like models can be effectively trained using a single encoder for both unimodal and multimodal data. This challenges the established dual-encoder paradigm and opens new avenues for designing more unified vision-language architectures.

#### 7.2. Training Efficiency

The seamless integration of contrastive and masked modeling objectives within a single framework eliminates the computational overhead typically associated with multi-branch architectures. This simplification makes multimodal pre-training more accessible to researchers with limited computational resources.

#### 7.3. Enhanced Understanding Capabilities

Superior performance on fine-grained visual-textual interaction tasks validates the hypothesis that early fusion produces richer, more integrated representations. This capability is crucial for applications requiring understanding of subtle visual nuances influenced by textual instructions.

#### 7.4. Scalable Data Generation

The paper introduces practical methods for generating diverse multimodal training data from existing unimodal datasets. This addresses the scarcity and expense of large-scale, high-quality multimodal datasets, enabling future research to train more capable models cost-effectively.

#### 7.5. Evaluation Framework

The proposed evaluation tasks specifically target interactive capabilities of multimodal encoders, exposing limitations of late-fusion approaches and providing guidance for future multimodal model development.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations primarily stemming from computational constraints:

**Scalability Exploration**: Limited ability to fully explore effects of scaling FuseLIP to significantly larger models or training datasets, which could reveal additional potential.

**Inference Efficiency**: Current inference costs are higher than baselines because image tokenization and encoding occur sequentially. However, anticipated advances in image tokenizers and larger encoders should mitigate this gap.

**Model Size Constraints**: Computational limitations prevented comprehensive analysis of how FuseLIP's advantages might scale with larger architectures comparable to state-of-the-art foundation models.

Despite these limitations, FuseLIP establishes a compelling foundation for early fusion approaches in multimodal representation learning. The architecture naturally extends to multiple images, interleaved image-text inputs, or additional modalities, positioning it as a valuable building block for future general-purpose multimodal foundation models.

The work demonstrates that unified tokenization and early fusion can simultaneously simplify training procedures while improving performance on challenging multimodal tasks, suggesting a promising direction for the field's continued development.
