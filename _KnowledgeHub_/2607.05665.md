# Efficient Transfer Learning of Robot Dynamic Models Using Morphological Similarity

> [!summary] Summary
> Kupyn et al. developed an autoencoder-based transfer learning framework that aligns the latent dynamic models of morphologically similar soft underwater robots, enabling accurate velocity prediction on a target robot with no labeled data. This method achieved approximately a 40% reduction in RMSE for linear velocity compared to a non-adapted source model.

## Problem

- Traditional analytical models struggle to accurately capture the complex nonlinear fluid-structure interactions inherent in soft, fin-actuated underwater robots.
- Neural network-based dynamics models require extensive volumes of labeled data, which is costly and challenging to acquire for underwater robotic platforms.
- A "domain gap" exists between morphologically similar robots due to differences in physical scale, mass, and hydrodynamic properties, preventing direct application of models trained on one to another.

## Method

- A self-supervised, ==autoencoder==-based **transfer learning framework** is proposed to learn a shared latent representation of robot dynamics across different platforms.
- The framework incorporates a combined loss function that optimizes for input reconstruction, next-state dynamics prediction, and ==Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)== for domain alignment.
- Two morphologically similar fin-actuated underwater robots (U-CAT as source, Micro-CAT as target) are used to validate the approach, with evaluation focused on label-efficient dynamics transfer.

## Results

- PCA visualization confirmed that the autoencoder significantly increased the overlap of latent embeddings between source (U-CAT) and target (Micro-CAT) robots, demonstrating successful domain alignment.
- The proposed autoencoder with a source-only regression head (zero-shot transfer) reduced the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) for linear velocity (Vx) on the target robot by approximately **40%** compared to a source-only model without adaptation.
- In scenarios with limited or no labeled target data, the framework significantly outperformed the non-adapted source-only baseline, establishing reliable dynamics prediction for the target robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An autoencoder architecture effectively learns a shared latent feature space that bridges the domain gap between morphologically similar robots despite significant differences in scale and hydrodynamic properties.
> - The Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) loss successfully aligns the latent feature distributions of source and target robots, making their dynamic representations more interchangeable.
> - Accurate body-frame velocity prediction for a data-scarce target robot can be achieved through zero-shot transfer (without target-specific labels for the prediction head) by leveraging knowledge from a data-rich source robot and its aligned latent representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is continuously advancing, with a particular focus on bio-inspired designs that offer advantages such as quiet and efficient propulsion, especially in underwater environments. However, accurately modeling the dynamics of these soft, fin-actuated robotic systems presents significant challenges. These challenges stem from complex nonlinear fluid–structure interactions, which render traditional analytical models insufficient for precise state estimation and control. Consequently, many existing underwater vehicle systems depend on expensive sensor feedback for state estimation, a limitation that increases operational costs and complexity.

Neural network (NN)–based modeling has emerged as a promising alternative, capable of capturing the intricate dynamics of such systems by predicting robot motion directly from sensor and control inputs. Despite their potential, these data-driven models typically demand large volumes of labeled data for supervised training. The acquisition of such data for underwater platforms is a demanding and costly endeavor, often necessitating specialized equipment to obtain ground truth labels for motion variables.

To mitigate the extensive data requirements, transfer learning has been explored as a method to leverage knowledge gained from one robotic platform to improve learning on another. Previous work in transfer learning for robotics has addressed various aspects, including layer policy transfer for deep reinforcement learning control in underwater robots and self-supervised domain adaptation for enhancing visual models using synthetic data. These efforts primarily focused on perception tasks or control policy transfer, often still requiring substantial labeled data or addressing different modalities. Recent advancements in robotic manipulation have also investigated cross-embodiment transfer through aligned latent representations.

This study differentiates itself by focusing specifically on the *latent dynamic model alignment* between morphologically similar soft underwater robots. Unlike prior work that primarily targets visual or control policy transfer, this research aims to bridge the "domain gap" in dynamic models that arises from differences in geometrical scale and hydrodynamic properties between morphologically similar robots. By developing a label-efficient transfer learning framework, the authors seek to enable accurate dynamics modeling on a target robot with limited or no labeled data, building upon existing efforts to reduce data dependency in robotics. This approach contributes to the broader goal of making advanced robotic systems more deployable and less reliant on costly data collection campaigns.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and evaluate a neural network–based transfer learning framework designed for modeling the dynamics of soft, fin-actuated underwater robots. This framework specifically targets scenarios involving morphologically similar robots that nonetheless exhibit differences in scale and hydrodynamic properties.

The core motivation for this work is to overcome the significant challenges associated with acquiring large labeled datasets for training dynamics models, particularly for underwater robotic platforms. The researchers acknowledge that:
- **Complexity of Soft Robot Dynamics:** Soft, fin-actuated underwater robots involve complex nonlinear fluid–structure interactions, making their dynamics difficult to model accurately using conventional analytical methods.
- **Data Scarcity and Cost:** While neural network models offer a viable solution for capturing these complexities, they typically require extensive labeled datasets. Collecting such data for underwater robots is costly and challenging due to the need for specialized equipment to obtain ground truth labels for motion and environmental parameters.
- **Domain Gap in Morphologically Similar Robots:** Even when robots share similar designs and actuation principles (morphological similarity), differences in physical scale lead to variations in mass and hydrodynamic properties. These variations create a "domain gap" that prevents direct application of a model trained on one robot to another without adaptation.

To address these motivations, the study sets out to:
- **Enable Label-Efficient Transfer:** Develop a method that can adapt a dynamic model trained on a data-rich source robot to a data-scarce target robot, ideally requiring limited or no labeled data from the target domain.
- **Learn a Shared Latent Representation:** Implement an autoencoder-based domain adaptation approach to learn a common latent representation that effectively aligns the dynamics of both source and target robots, thereby bridging the inherent domain gap.
- **Achieve Accurate State Estimation:** Demonstrate that the proposed method can achieve accurate state estimation, specifically for body-frame velocities, on the target platform even when labeled data from that platform is unavailable during the transfer process.
- **Lay Foundation for Control:** Position accurate dynamic forward modeling as a foundational element for developing more robust and efficient model-based control strategies for soft robots.

The researchers validate their proposed framework using two specific morphologically similar fin-actuated underwater robots: U-CAT (designated as the source domain) and Micro-CAT (serving as the target domain).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes an autoencoder-based transfer learning framework for estimating the velocity of underwater robots by transferring dynamic knowledge across morphologically similar platforms. The methodology involves a self-supervised training approach that leverages a shared feature space constructed from both source and target robot domains.

**A. Robot Platforms and Data Collection:**
Two fin-actuated underwater robots, U-CAT (source domain) and Micro-CAT (target domain), were utilized. These robots are considered morphologically similar as they share identical actuation kinematics, a common geometric design, and four independently controlled silicone fins. They differ significantly in absolute scale (e.g., U-CAT is 3.33 times longer and 14.67 times heavier than Micro-CAT) and thus in their mass and hydrodynamic properties, which creates the domain gap.
Data collection involved equipping robots with cameras to track their trajectories using ArUco markers placed in an experimental water tank. The ground truth velocity was derived by differentiating the smoothed position information obtained from these markers.
Input features for the models include: pressure values from a pressure sensor, IMU orientation, IMU angular velocity, IMU linear acceleration (gravity-aligned), and fin actuation parameters (amplitude A_amp, oscillation offset ϕ_c).

**B. Supervised Baseline Models:**
Three supervised baseline models were established for comparison:
- **Target-only model:** Trained exclusively on labeled data from the target robot (Micro-CAT).
- **Source-only model:** Trained solely on labeled data from the source robot (U-CAT) and directly evaluated on the target domain. This baseline highlights the domain gap without transfer.
- **Joint supervised model:** Trained on the combined labeled datasets of both U-CAT and Micro-CAT. This represents an upper bound for supervised performance when all data is available.
All baseline models use a neural network architecture comprising a 1D convolutional layer, a max-pooling layer, and a fully connected output layer. They were trained for 50 epochs using the Adam optimizer with a mean squared error (MSE) loss function.

**C. Proposed Autoencoder-based Framework:**
The core of the proposed method is an autoencoder architecture designed to learn a shared latent representation that captures common dynamic behaviors between platforms.

- **Architecture (Fig. 2):**
 - **Encoder:** Processes time-series sensor and control inputs (structured as 3D tensors after windowing) through a 1D CNN layer, pooling, and dense layers to generate a compact latent vector representing robot-specific dynamic properties.
 - **Dynamics Head:** Predicts the next latent state (ˆz_t+1) from the current latent state (z_t) and control action vector (a_t).
 - **Dual Decoders:** Two domain-specific decoders (dec_ucat and dec_mcat), each consisting of two fully connected layers, are used to reconstruct the original input time-series sequence from the latent representation. Input samples are routed through their corresponding decoder based on a domain label.

- **Training Pipeline (Fig. 3):**
 - **Data Preparation:** A joint dataset is created, combining feature sequences, control inputs, next-state sequences, and a domain label for both source and target samples.
 - **Combined Loss Function:** The model is optimized jointly using three loss components:
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L_recon):** Calculated as the mean squared error (MSE) between the original input sequence and its reconstruction by the corresponding decoder. This ensures that the latent space retains sufficient information to represent the original data.
 - **Dynamics Prediction Loss (L_dyn):** The MSE between the predicted next latent state (ˆz_t+1) and the true next latent state (z_t+1). This loss encourages the latent representation to capture the temporal evolution of the robot's state in response to control inputs.
 - **Domain Alignment Loss (L_mmd):** Utilizes Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to minimize the statistical distance between the latent feature distributions of the source (U-CAT) and target (Micro-CAT) platforms. A Gaussian radial basis function (RBF) kernel is employed for this calculation. This objective aims to make the latent representations from both domains indistinguishable, thereby reducing the domain gap.
 - **Optimization:** The total loss L_total = L_recon + λ_dyn * L_dyn + λ_mmd * L_mmd is minimized. The weighting factors λ_dyn and λ_mmd were both set to 0.5 to balance the objectives equally. The Adam optimizer was used with a learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 256, over 50 epochs.

**D. Evaluation:**
The autoencoder's performance was evaluated under two scenarios for the target robot's motion prediction:
- **Autoencoder (Joint Head):** A linear regression head is trained on labels from both domains after the encoder is frozen, to assess the quality of the aligned latent representation when target labels are available for fine-tuning.
- **Autoencoder (Source Head):** A linear regression head is trained only on source labels, and then evaluated "zero-shot" on the target domain. This scenario directly tests the label-efficient transfer capability.
Prediction accuracy was quantified using Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for linear velocities (Vx, Vy) and angular velocity (ωz) across three different test datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results provide a comparison between the proposed autoencoder framework and the supervised baseline models, demonstrating the efficacy of the transfer learning approach, particularly in label-limited scenarios.

**A. Autoencoder Preliminary Results (Domain Alignment):**
The study first assessed the autoencoder's ability to align feature distributions between the two robot domains. Visualization of the encoded features using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) revealed that the latent embeddings of the source (U-CAT) and target (Micro-CAT) robots became substantially more overlapping after training (Fig. 5). This increased overlap indicates that the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) loss effectively reduced the domain gap in the shared latent feature space, implying that the encoder learned to map the dynamics of both robots into a common representation.

**B. Prediction Comparison:**
The prediction performance was evaluated for the linear velocity components (Vx, Vy) and angular velocity (ωz) of the target robot (Micro-CAT).

- **Qualitative Analysis (Fig. 6):** For linear velocity in the x-direction (forward motion), the autoencoder's predictions (both with joint and source regression heads) generally followed the overall shape and captured sign changes of the ground-truth velocity. While some underestimation of short velocity peaks and a minor timing delay at sign changes were observed, the autoencoder maintained this behavior in both labeled and unlabeled target-domain conditions. In contrast, the source-only baseline model, which lacked domain adaptation, showed noticeable drift and failed to generalize accurately to the target robot's dynamics.

- **Quantitative Analysis (Table II):**
 - **Target-only model:** This baseline, trained exclusively on labeled Micro-CAT data, achieved strong performance (e.g., RMSE Vx: 0.0328, MAE Vx: 0.0268), establishing a reference for the target robot's inherent predictability with sufficient data.
 - **Joint Supervised model:** Training on combined labeled datasets from both U-CAT and Micro-CAT yielded the lowest errors across all metrics (e.g., RMSE Vx: 0.0237, MAE Vx: 0.0185). This result confirms that exposure to multi-domain labeled data, when available, generally improves generalization and predictive accuracy.
 - **Source-only model:** This model, trained on U-CAT data and directly applied to Micro-CAT, exhibited significantly larger errors (e.g., RMSE Vx: 0.0945, MAE Vx: 0.0888). This substantial performance degradation quantifies the existence of a significant domain gap between the two morphologically similar but physically distinct robots, validating the need for domain adaptation.
 - **Autoencoder (Joint Head):** When the autoencoder's latent features were fed to a regression head trained with labels from both domains, its performance was comparable to the fully supervised baselines (e.g., RMSE Vx: 0.0362, MAE Vx: 0.0264). This indicates that the learned shared latent representation is capable of supporting accurate predictions when some target-domain labels are accessible for the final regression layer.
 - **Autoencoder (Source Head):** The most notable finding concerns the autoencoder with a source-only regression head, which represents the zero-shot dynamics transfer scenario (no labeled target data used for training the prediction head). This approach achieved a significant reduction in error compared to the source-only baseline (e.g., RMSE Vx: 0.0596 vs. 0.0945 for Vx, representing approximately a 40% improvement in RMSE). This result demonstrates that the proposed autoencoder framework successfully reduces the domain gap and enables a reliable dynamics prediction for the target robot without requiring any labeled data from that target platform.

In summary, while the fully supervised joint training achieved the highest accuracy, it necessitates extensive labeled data from both platforms. The proposed autoencoder, particularly in its zero-shot configuration (source head), demonstrated effective transfer learning, bridging the domain gap and achieving substantially improved prediction accuracy on the target robot without relying on its labeled data, thus addressing the problem of data scarcity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for efficient transfer learning of robot dynamic models, offering several points of significance and potential impact in robotics and related fields.

**A. Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:**
The primary significance lies in the development of a label-efficient transfer learning framework. By enabling accurate dynamics modeling for a target robot with limited or no labeled data, the proposed autoencoder-based approach directly addresses the substantial cost and logistical challenges associated with acquiring large, labeled datasets for complex robotic systems, especially those operating in demanding environments like underwater. This efficiency can accelerate the development and deployment of new robotic platforms by reducing the dependency on extensive data collection campaigns for each new embodiment.

**B. Foundation for Advanced Robot Control:**
Accurate dynamic forward models are fundamental for various advanced control strategies, including model-predictive control and other model-based approaches. By providing a method for reliable dynamics prediction, even in data-scarce scenarios, this work lays a crucial groundwork for developing more robust, adaptive, and autonomous control systems for soft, bio-inspired robots. The ability to transfer learned dynamics efficiently can enhance the performance and operational capabilities of these robots in diverse applications.

**C. Validation of Morphological Similarity for Transfer Learning:**
The study demonstrates that morphological similarity, even when accompanied by significant differences in scale, mass, and hydrodynamic properties, can be effectively leveraged for transferring learned dynamics. This finding suggests a guiding principle for designing transfer learning strategies, indicating that common actuation principles and geometric layouts can serve as a strong basis for knowledge transfer, provided appropriate domain adaptation techniques are applied to bridge the remaining domain gap.

**D. Generalizability to Other Robotic Systems:**
While validated on fin-actuated underwater robots, the underlying framework, which utilizes shared latent representations and domain alignment techniques (MMD), has potential applicability beyond this specific domain. It could be extended to other soft or bio-inspired robotic systems, or indeed any platform where similar actuation principles are at play and a need for label-efficient cross-platform model transfer exists. This broadens the impact of the methodology to a wider range of robotic applications.

**E. Future Research Directions:**
The research identifies several avenues for future work, indicating its foundational nature. These include:
- **Simulation-to-Real Adaptation:** Extending the framework to transfer dynamic models from simulations to real-world robots, further mitigating real-world data collection needs.
- **Cross-Platform Control:** Utilizing the learned shared latent dynamics representation to implement model-based and data-driven control strategies across different platforms.
- **Advanced Domain Adaptation:** Exploring alternative alignment strategies, such as Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence minimization or adversarial domain adaptation, which could offer different trade-offs in flexibility and training complexity.
- **Encoder Architectures:** Investigating the use of recurrent or attention-based encoders (e.g., GRU, LSTM, Transformer) to potentially improve the modeling of time-series-dependent dynamic features.
- **Ablation Studies and Sensitivity Analysis:** Conducting further analyses to systematically characterize the contributions of individual loss components and parameters, which would help in establishing operational tolerances for future applications.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes to the field of robot learning by providing a practical and efficient solution for dynamics modeling in data-limited environments. Its implications extend to faster development cycles for new robotic hardware, enhanced capabilities for autonomous control, and a deeper understanding of how to exploit morphological similarities for effective knowledge transfer across robotic embodiments.
