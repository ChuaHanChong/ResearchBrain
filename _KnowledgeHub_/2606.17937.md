# ThinkingVLA: Interleaved Vision and Language Reasoning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ThinkingVLA is a Vision-Language-Action model that integrates interleaved textual and visual reasoning with inverse dynamics within a unified autoregressive architecture. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating enhanced robustness and interpretability.

## Problem

- Traditional VLA models lack explicit reasoning mechanisms, limiting their performance on complex, multi-step tasks requiring subgoal decomposition and spatial anticipation.
- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches for VLA models are either unimodal (lacking spatial precision or structured task decomposition) or decoupled (suffering from weak cross-modal coherence and implicit inverse dynamics reasoning).
- There was a need for a principled decomposition of manipulation planning into explicit prediction of future states and inference of inverse dynamics, connected by clear reasoning steps within a unified architecture.

## Method

- **ThinkingVLA** explicitly models intermediate planning variables—forward CoT (textual reasoning), predicted future images, and inverse CoT (action reasoning)—generated in a single, unbroken causal sequence.
- It employs a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture, comprising a thinking expert for interleaved textual and visual reasoning, and an action expert for generating robot motor commands.
- All inputs and outputs (language, current observation, and future images) are mapped into a shared token space using unified tokenization, enabling end-to-end training and direct gradient propagation across modalities.

## Results

- ThinkingVLA achieved the highest average success rates on the RoboTwin **2.0** benchmark, showing a **15.2**-**19.6** percentage point improvement over baselines on Horizon=2 tasks and significant gains on Horizon=3 tasks.
- On real-world long-horizon tasks with an ALOHA bimanual robot, the model outperformed state-of-the-art methods, achieving 10 pp higher success rates on tasks like 'Make Breakfast' and 'Assemble Equation' compared to strong baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of Inverse CoT, whose removal led to a 15 pp reduction in overall success, particularly impacting long-horizon tasks, and demonstrated improved robustness to varied lighting conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factoring manipulation planning into explicit visual state prediction and inverse dynamics reasoning, mediated by textual CoT, significantly enhances a VLA model's capacity for complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Interleaving textual reasoning with image generation and visually-grounded action inference within a unified autoregressive architecture (MoT) improves cross-modal coherence and provides human-inspectable intermediate outputs.
> - A multi-stage progressive training strategy, including generative reasoning pretraining and end-to-end policy learning, is crucial for acquiring diverse manipulation capabilities and improving data efficiency and transferability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a notable advancement in generalizable robot control by integrating visual perception, language comprehension, and action generation within a unified framework. Traditionally, VLA models operate by directly mapping observations to actions through a single, implicit forward pass. This approach involves a vision-language backbone encoding the current observation and instruction, followed by an action head decoding motor commands. While effective for short-horizon or well-demonstrated tasks, this monolithic mapping lacks explicit mechanisms for complex reasoning processes such as decomposing instructions into subgoals, anticipating the consequences of potential actions, or verifying plan efficacy for complex manipulation tasks like multi-step assembly or long-horizon instruction following.

To address these limitations, recent research has explored incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, an approach that has improved reasoning capabilities in large language models. Within VLA, CoT methods generally fall into two categories:

1. **Unimodal CoT**: These methods augment policies with reasoning within a single modality. Text-only approaches enhance multi-step task performance but frequently lack spatial precision. Conversely, visual-only approaches provide spatial grounding but do not offer structured task decomposition.
2. **Decoupled CoT**: These approaches leverage both textual and visual reasoning but often as separate, sequential stages. A limitation of decoupled methods is that textual reasoning may not directly guide image generation, which can reduce cross-modal coherence. Furthermore, an explicit reasoning step following the predicted image is typically absent, leaving inverse dynamics reasoning implicit.

ThinkingVLA aims to advance beyond these existing approaches by proposing a unified architecture that interleaves textual and visual reasoning within a single generation process. This builds upon recent developments in unified vision-language generation, which enable the generation of both text and images using discrete image tokenizers and shared vocabularies within autoregressive models. The work repurposes this capability for robotic planning, using generated image tokens as direct functional intermediates that bridge language reasoning and action generation in a closed-loop system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to equip VLA models with structured reasoning capabilities necessary for complex robotic manipulation tasks. The authors identify a core limitation in most existing VLA models: their inability to perform explicit reasoning, which restricts their performance on long-horizon tasks requiring subgoal decomposition and spatial anticipation.

The motivation for ThinkingVLA stems from the observation that current CoT reasoning approaches in VLA models exhibit shortcomings:
- **Unimodal CoT approaches**: These either lack spatial precision (text-only) or structured task decomposition (visual-only).
- **Decoupled CoT approaches**: These suffer from weak cross-modal coherence, as textual reasoning does not explicitly guide image generation. Furthermore, they often lack explicit inverse reasoning based on a target state, leaving the inference of actions implicit after visual prediction.

The authors hypothesize that manipulation planning can be naturally decomposed into two fundamental components:
1. **Prediction**: Anticipating the next visual state of the environment.
2. **Inverse Dynamics**: Inferring the specific actions required to reach that anticipated state.

To effectively bridge these two components, the research posits the need for two key ingredients:
1. **Principled Decomposition**: An explicit factorization of planning into prediction and inverse dynamics, with a clear reasoning step connecting the predicted future state to action generation. Existing unimodal approaches only address one subproblem, and decoupled approaches lack an explicit bridge.
2. **Unified Autoregressive Architecture**: An architecture that seamlessly interleaves textual reasoning, visual prediction, and visually-grounded inverse dynamics reasoning within a single, continuous causal sequence. This allows the predicted image to serve as a direct bridge, ensuring that each subsequent reasoning step or action generation is explicitly conditioned on all preceding outputs across modalities, thereby enhancing cross-modal coherence.

ThinkingVLA is motivated to implement this decomposition within a unified Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture, providing human-inspectable intermediate outputs and enabling end-to-end training across diverse manipulation domains and robot platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ThinkingVLA addresses instruction-conditioned robotic manipulation as a sequential decision-making problem by explicitly modeling intermediate planning variables, rather than a direct observation-to-action mapping.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

At each decision step, the robot receives a task instruction ($l$) and a visual observation ($o_t$). ThinkingVLA predicts an action chunk ($a_t$) by first generating three structured intermediate variables:
1. **Forward CoT ($r_{fwd_t}$)**: Interprets the task instruction and reasons about the desired next state.
2. **Predicted Future Images ($\hat{o}_{t+1}$)**: Visualizes the anticipated next state from available robot viewpoints, guided by the forward reasoning.
3. **Inverse CoT ($r_{inv_t}$)**: Reasons about the specific actions required to reach the predicted state.

This process factorizes action generation as follows:
$p_{\theta}(r_{fwd_t}, \hat{o}_{t+1}, r_{inv_t}, a_t | o_t, l) = p_{\theta}(r_{fwd_t} | o_t, l) p_{\theta}(\hat{o}_{t+1} | o_t, l, r_{fwd_t}) \times p_{\theta}(r_{inv_t} | o_t, l, r_{fwd_t}, \hat{o}_{t+1}) p_{\theta}(a_t | o_t, l, r_{fwd_t}, \hat{o}_{t+1}, r_{inv_t})$.

#### 3.2. Architecture

ThinkingVLA employs a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture comprising two coupled experts: a thinking expert for generating the reasoning chain and an action expert for producing actions.

- **Unified Tokenization**: All inputs and outputs (language, current observations, future images) are mapped into a shared token space. Language is tokenized using a pretrained VLM text tokenizer, current observations are encoded via a SigLIP vision encoder, and future images are discretized into vision tokens using a Cosmos image tokenizer. Text and vision tokens share a unified vocabulary, allowing the thinking expert to process both modalities in a single autoregressive pass.
- **Training Sequence**: The training sequence follows a structured format: `[BOS] {o_t} {l} [think] {r_fwd_t} [/think] [gen] {$\hat{o}_{t+1}$} [/gen] [think] {r_{inv_t}} [/think] [EOS]`. Special tokens (`[think]/[/think]`, `[gen]/[/gen]`) denote modality switches. This forms an unbroken causal sequence, ensuring direct gradient propagation from image generation through preceding forward CoT tokens and grounding of inverse CoT in individual image tokens.
- **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)**:
 - **Thinking Expert**: A multi-layer transformer (based on PaliGemma / Gemma-2B) responsible for generating the interleaved reasoning sequence ($[r_{fwd_t}, \hat{o}_{t+1}, r_{inv_t}]$).
 - **Action Expert**: A 300M-parameter transformer (based on Gemma-300M) dedicated to producing the action chunk ($a_t$), conditioned on the full reasoning context provided by the thinking expert.
 - **Shared Attention**: Both experts share attention at every layer. They compute their own Q/K/V projections, but K/V pairs are concatenated for a single joint attention application. The output is then split and passed through expert-specific feed-forward networks.
- **Attention Mask**: An adaptive attention mask manages token visibility. Context tokens attend bidirectionally, while reasoning and image tokens are generated causally. The action expert attends to the entire thinking prefix but is masked from it, leveraging the reasoning without influencing its generation. For multi-view prediction, wrist-view images attend to all third-view tokens for consistency but not to other wrist views.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

The full training objective combines losses for the thinking and action experts:
- **Text Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{text}$)**: Standard cross-entropy for CoT generation, supervising the thinking expert on $r_{fwd_t}$ and $r_{inv_t}$. The $r_{inv_t}$ is autoregressively conditioned on preceding image tokens.
- **Image Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{img}$)**: Standard cross-entropy for future-image generation, supervising the thinking expert on $\hat{o}_{t+1}$ (and all generated views in multi-view prediction).
- **Action Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{action}$)**: A flow-matching loss for the action expert. It learns to predict the target velocity from a perturbed action, conditioned on time and the full reasoning context.

The total loss is $\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{text} \mathcal{L}_{text} + \lambda_{img} \mathcal{L}_{img} + \lambda_{action} \mathcal{L}_{action}$, with specific loss weights for each stage.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference Strategy

ThinkingVLA employs a multi-stage progressive training strategy to acquire diverse capabilities:
1. **Stage 1: Generative Reasoning Pretraining**: Only the thinking expert is trained to generate forward CoT ($r_{fwd_t}$) and a single future image ($\hat{o}_{t+1}$). Loss weights: $\lambda_{text} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{img} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{action} = 0$. This stage utilizes a subset of the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset.
2. **Stage 2: End-to-End Policy Learning**: The action expert is introduced, and the complete pipeline is trained under the full objective. Loss weights: $\lambda_{text} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{img} = 2.0$, $\lambda_{action} = 10.0$. This stage also uses the OXE dataset.
3. **Stage 3: Target Robot Adaptation**: The full model is fine-tuned on specific target datasets (RoboTwin and real-world demonstrations). Multi-view prediction is enabled to match camera configurations. Loss weights: $\lambda_{text} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{img} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{action} = 10.0$.

**Thinking Data Construction**: Forward and inverse CoT annotations are generated offline by prompting a multimodal language model with the task instruction, current observation, and a ground-truth future observation. Both CoTs are constrained to a single paragraph (max 60 words). The inverse CoT explicitly includes a "subtask: [action]" tag.

**Inference Strategy**:
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**: Applied exclusively to future-image token generation to improve quality. It fuses logits from three condition-ablated branches (full, image-only, unconditional) during inference.
- **Reasoning Dropout**: During training, $r_{fwd_t}$ and $r_{inv_t}$ are independently dropped with a probability of 0.1. This trains the model to function even without explicit reasoning, enabling selective skipping of CoT generation during inference to reduce latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ThinkingVLA was conducted in both simulation and real-world settings, supported by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation

Experiments were performed on 20 tasks from the RoboTwin 2.0 dual-arm manipulation benchmark, categorized by task horizon (1, 2, or 3). ThinkingVLA was compared against $\pi_0$, XVLA, UP-VLA, and BagelVLA.
- **Overall Performance**: ThinkingVLA achieved the highest average success rate on Easy tasks (77.9%) and competitive performance on Hard tasks (29.3%).
- **Task Horizon Sensitivity**: The performance advantage of ThinkingVLA increased with task complexity. On Horizon=2 tasks, ThinkingVLA demonstrated a 15.2 percentage point (pp) improvement on Easy tasks and 19.6 pp on Hard tasks compared to BagelVLA. For Horizon=3 tasks, ThinkingVLA achieved 60.0% (Easy) and 23.6% (Hard) success rates, surpassing BagelVLA (55.8% Easy / 6.8% Hard) and XVLA (44.6% Easy / 18.6% Hard). These results suggest that the interleaved reasoning design contributes to tighter cross-modal coupling, which is particularly beneficial for more complex tasks.

#### 4.2. Real-World Deployment

ThinkingVLA was deployed on an ALOHA bimanual robot platform, evaluated on five manipulation tasks (three basic, two long-horizon). It was compared against $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, and UP-VLA.
- **Task Success**: ThinkingVLA achieved the highest success rate on four out of five tasks.
- **Long-Horizon Task Gains**: The most significant performance gains were observed on long-horizon tasks. ThinkingVLA outperformed $\pi_{0.5}$ by 10 pp on both "Make Breakfast" and "Assemble Equation." UP-VLA, which incorporates visual prediction, showed lower performance on these complex tasks (30% and 20% respectively), indicating that visual prediction alone is insufficient without structured textual reasoning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on the real-world task suite investigated the contribution of reasoning components and the pretraining strategy.

- **Reasoning Component Ablation**:
 - **Inverse CoT**: This was identified as the most critical component. Its removal led to a 15 pp reduction in overall success (from 85.0% to 70.0%), with long-horizon tasks being most affected (e.g., "Make Breakfast" reduced from 70% to 45%, "Assemble Equation" from 90% to 60%).
 - **Forward CoT**: Its removal resulted in a smaller but consistent overall gain of 6 pp, mainly benefiting multi-step tasks by guiding visual prediction.
 - **Comparison to Single-Modality Variants**: Variants using only visual prediction (67.0% success) or only subtask text (69.0% success) performed similarly but significantly below the full model. This indicates that the interleaved combination of textual reasoning, visual prediction, and visually-grounded action reasoning is essential, rather than either modality in isolation.
 - **Task Complexity Scaling**: The impact of ablations scaled with task complexity; on single-step tasks like "Hang Cup," all variants achieved 90% success, while on long-horizon tasks, the full model outperformed single-modality variants by 25–40 pp.

- **Effect of Stage 2 Pretraining**: Comparing the full model (with Stage 2 pretraining) to a variant that skipped this stage, fine-tuning directly from Stage 1 to Stage 3, revealed:
 - **Data Efficiency**: Stage 2 pretraining consistently improved data efficiency, with the benefit increasing with task complexity.
 - **Performance Gap**: At 50 fine-tuning episodes, the performance gap was 5 pp on "Hang Cup" (Horizon=1), increased to 25 pp on "Place Cubes" (Horizon=2), and 50 pp on "Assemble Equation" (Horizon=3), where the variant without pretraining failed completely at 10 episodes. These results indicate that end-to-end action pretraining imparts transferable manipulation priors that are not fully compensated by additional domain-specific data alone.

#### 4.4. Robustness Experiments

Evaluation under varied lighting conditions demonstrated ThinkingVLA's visual robustness.
- **Performance Under Lighting Variation**: ThinkingVLA consistently outperformed all baselines across different colored illuminations (blue, red, pink, green, warm-white), achieving the highest success rate on every task.
- **Task Specificity**: Tasks requiring fine-grained object identification, such as "Arrange Blocks" and "Assemble Equation," experienced notable performance drops across all methods under varied lighting. However, ThinkingVLA maintained a clear advantage (e.g., 48% and 68% vs. 32% and 56% for the next-best method on these tasks). This robustness is attributed in part to the visual prediction module's resilience, which is trained with data augmentation, providing a more stable visual context for downstream action reasoning. The interleaved reasoning structure, with textual CoT mediating observation and image generation, may also contribute to stabilizing visual prediction under distribution shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a principled decomposition of manipulation planning into prediction and inverse dynamics, realized through ThinkingVLA’s unified autoregressive architecture. This approach addresses identified limitations in existing VLA models, particularly their capacity for reasoning-intensive, long-horizon tasks.

The key contributions and potential impacts are:
- **Enhanced Reasoning for Manipulation**: By interleaving textual subgoal identification, future image prediction, and visually-grounded action reasoning within a single coherent generation process, ThinkingVLA provides a structured and explicit reasoning pathway. This represents an advancement over unimodal and decoupled CoT approaches.
- **Improved Task Performance**: The consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art baselines in both simulation and real-world settings, especially on complex, long-horizon tasks, suggests the effectiveness of explicit multimodal reasoning for robust robotic manipulation.
- **Data Efficiency and Transferability**: The progressive training pipeline, which includes generative reasoning pretraining and end-to-end action policy learning, enhances data efficiency and enables the acquisition of transferable manipulation priors across diverse robot platforms and task distributions.
- **Increased Interpretability**: The generation of human-inspectable intermediate reasoning chains (forward CoT, predicted images, inverse CoT) offers insights into the model's decision-making process, which can be valuable for debugging and trust in autonomous systems.
- **Robustness to Visual Variation**: The demonstrated robustness to challenging lighting conditions suggests the model's capacity to generalize under environmental variations, an important aspect for real-world deployment.

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

A noted limitation of ThinkingVLA, inherent to models that generate extensive reasoning, is the added latency due to the generation of the full reasoning chain. While the proposed reasoning dropout mechanism offers a way to selectively skip CoT generation for efficiency, future work could explore adaptive reasoning strategies. Such strategies would involve invoking the explicit reasoning chain only for particularly complex or uncertain steps, thereby optimizing the trade-off between reasoning depth and execution speed.
