# 6-DOF Grasping for Target-driven Object Manipulation in Clutter

> [!summary] Summary
> A cascaded, learning-based system plans 6-DOF grasps for a specified target object in structured clutter from a single-view segmented point cloud. A variational autoencoder samples object-centric grasps, an evaluator scores and refines them, and CollisionNet predicts gripper-scene collisions from raw partial point clouds. Trained purely in simulation, it reaches 80.3% real-robot grasp success and can rank blocking objects for removal.

## Problem

- Most data-driven grasping restricts itself to top-down planar grasps, which lack diversity and often fail under task or arm constraints in packed, structured scenes.
- Learning methods for clutter mostly target bin-picking with small light objects, where collisions matter less than in packed shelves of larger, heavier items.
- Model-based planners for cluttered grasping assume full object knowledge, known poses, or occupancy grids that miss collisions hidden by occlusion.

## Method

- Cascaded factorization splits the grasp posterior into an object-centric grasp distribution conditioned on the segmented object cloud and a separate clutter-conditioned collision term.
- A ==PointNet++== conditional ==VAE== samples 6-DOF poses, trained with gripper-point reconstruction loss plus KL penalty; an evaluator scores grasps and ==Metropolis-Hastings== refinement replaces slower gradient search.
- **CollisionNet** classifies gripper-scene collisions from the merged scene and gripper point clouds with an indicator feature, trained with balanced sampling over positives, negatives, hard negatives, and free-space grasps.

## Results

- Real-robot grasping over 9 scenes, 23 unknown objects, and 51 picks reached **80.3%** success, beating a clutter-agnostic grasp-generation plus segmentation baseline by **17.6** points.
- Removing CollisionNet dropped success to **60.7%**, and CollisionNet correctly classified **89.7%** of the collision cases where the voxel-based heuristic failed.
- In simulation on 15 held-out objects across 100 scenes, the cascaded model reached **0.22** success-coverage AUC versus **0.02** for the instance-agnostic single-stage variant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling grasp quality from collision feasibility into separate learned modules beats one monolithic predictor, and makes the collision score reusable for downstream decluttering decisions.
> - Voxel or occupancy representations silently miss collisions in occluded regions and reject valid grasps that graze the target; learned collision checking on raw partial clouds avoids both biases.
> - Simulating segmentation noise, boundary salt-and-pepper plus random object merging, is what lets a purely synthetic pipeline survive imperfect real-world instance masks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic grasping is a foundational capability in automation, yet it presents significant challenges, particularly in complex, unstructured environments. Traditionally, grasping approaches have relied on explicit object models, often assuming perfect or near-perfect perception of object pose and shape. These model-based methods typically involve physics-based grasp analysis or database lookups of pre-computed grasps. However, such approaches are often slow, sensitive to perception errors, and struggle to generalize to novel objects.

The increasing availability of data and advancements in machine learning have led to a surge in data-driven methods for robotic grasping. Many early data-driven works focused on simplified scenarios, such as planar grasping (e.g., bin-picking where objects are small and light, allowing for top-down grasps) or grasping isolated objects. While these methods demonstrated the potential of learning-based approaches, they are often insufficient for real-world tasks that demand more versatile manipulation capabilities, such as picking up objects with complex geometries or handling them in tightly packed spaces.

Grasping in cluttered environments exacerbates these challenges. In such scenarios, significant portions of object geometry are occluded by surrounding items, making accurate perception difficult. Furthermore, effective grasping requires explicit reasoning about potential collisions between the gripper, the manipulator arm, and the surrounding environment or other objects. Many existing methods for cluttered environments either rely on full observability and prior object knowledge (model-based task and motion planning) or utilize simplified collision representations (e.g., occupancy grids) that may lack reliability in dynamic or partially observed settings. Some data-driven approaches address clutter but are often confined to bin-picking setups or are limited to planar grasps. Those that do explore full 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DOF) grasp poses in clutter may still incorporate non-learned components or rely on hand-crafted features.

This research positions itself within the landscape of data-driven, model-free, and target-driven 6-DOF grasping in structured clutter. Structured clutter, as defined by the authors, involves packed configurations of larger, heavier objects (e.g., kitchen cupboards, supermarket shelves), where successful grasps are sparser and collision avoidance is critical. The work distinguishes itself by tackling the problem of generating 6-DOF grasps for *unknown* objects in *structured clutter* from *partial point cloud observations* obtained from a *single view*. It explicitly addresses the challenge of collision checking within the cluttered scene using a learned module, a departure from traditional geometric or voxel-based methods. By combining state-of-the-art instance segmentation with a cascaded learning approach for grasp synthesis and collision detection, the authors aim to overcome limitations related to occlusions, unknown object models, and robust collision avoidance in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method capable of planning 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DOF) grasps for a specified target object within a cluttered scene, based solely on partial point cloud observations. This overarching objective is motivated by several fundamental challenges in robotic manipulation:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Planar Grasping:** Many existing data-driven grasping approaches simplify the problem by focusing on top-down or planar grasps. While suitable for some bin-picking tasks, these methods greatly limit the diversity and kinematic feasibility of possible grasps, rendering them insufficient for complex real-world scenarios where objects may require precise orientation and diverse approach vectors. The authors aim to enable full 6-DOF grasping, which is crucial for handling objects in confined and structured cluttered environments.

2. **Overcoming Perception Challenges in Clutter:** Grasping in cluttered environments is inherently difficult due to severe occlusions, where significant portions of an object's geometry are hidden by other items. Traditional shape-matching techniques struggle under such conditions, even with known object geometries. Furthermore, acquiring high-quality 3D information often requires specialized depth sensors or multiple views, which may not be feasible in constrained spaces. The work seeks to perform robust grasping from partial point cloud observations obtained from a single view, making the system more deployable.

3. **Robust Collision Avoidance:** A critical aspect of grasping in clutter is ensuring that the gripper and manipulator do not collide with surrounding objects or the environment. Existing model-based approaches often assume perfect object knowledge or rely on occupancy-grid representations for collision checking, which can be unreliable, computationally intensive, or impractical in real-world settings with unknown objects and partial observations. The authors are motivated to develop a robust and learned collision detection mechanism that operates directly on raw partial point cloud observations.

4. **Generalization to Novel Objects:** Traditional grasping methods often require prior models or pre-computed grasp databases, limiting their ability to generalize to novel, previously unseen objects. The goal is to develop a learning-based approach that can generate stable grasps for unknown objects without requiring explicit models.

5. **Enabling Target-Driven Manipulation:** In practical applications, a robot often needs to grasp a specific item from a collection. Many existing data-driven methods are target-agnostic, simply identifying graspable regions without specifying which object to manipulate. This research aims for a target-driven approach, where the robot is commanded to grasp a particular object.

6. **Facilitating Sequential Manipulation Tasks:** Beyond simple single-object grasping, the ability to manipulate objects in a sequence is vital for more complex tasks. This includes scenarios where a desired object is initially inaccessible due to blocking items. The authors are motivated to develop a system that can not only identify collisions but also reason about which blocking objects to remove to achieve a target grasp.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines specific contributions:
- The development of a learning-based framework for 6-DOF grasp synthesis for novel objects in structured clutter, incorporating a learned collision checker conditioned on gripper information and raw scene point clouds.
- Demonstration that this approach, trained exclusively with synthetic data, achieves a high grasp accuracy (80.3%) on real-world test objects in clutter and surpasses a state-of-the-art clutter-agnostic baseline by 17.6%.
- Illustrating an application of the method for effectively removing blocking objects to enable grasping of an initially occluded target.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a cascaded grasp synthesis approach designed for 6-DOF grasping of unknown objects in cluttered environments from partial point cloud observations. The core idea is to factor the complex problem of estimating the distribution of successful grasps in clutter into two distinct, learned models: one for object-centric grasp success and another for clutter-centric collision detection.

**A. Overview of Approach:**
The method takes as input a depth image of the scene and a binary mask identifying the target object. This information is processed into a point cloud. The overall framework involves:
1. **Instance Segmentation:** A state-of-the-art instance segmentation algorithm (e.g.,) is used to identify the target object and generate its segmented point cloud ($X_o$).
2. **Object-centric Grasp Synthesis:** A generative model, trained on isolated objects, proposes 6-DOF grasps for the target object ($P(G^*|X_o)$), initially disregarding clutter. This module includes a sampler, an evaluator, and a refiner.
3. **Clutter-centric Collision Detection:** A discriminative model, termed **CollisionNet**, evaluates these proposed grasps against the full scene point cloud ($X$) to predict potential collisions ($P(C|X, g)$) with surrounding clutter.
4. **Grasp Selection:** Grasps are then ranked and filtered based on a combination of their object-centric quality and their collision-free probability in the cluttered scene.

This factorization is intended to simplify learning by disentangling the geometry of the target object from environmental collisions, allowing for more interpretable failure modes and enabling advanced reasoning, such as identifying and removing blocking objects.

**B. 6-DOF Grasp Synthesis for Isolated Objects:**
This module focuses on generating and evaluating grasps based solely on the target object's geometry, as represented by its segmented point cloud ($X_o$).

- **Variational Grasp Sampling:** A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (VAE) based on the PointNet++ architecture is used as the grasp sampler.
 - **Encoder:** Maps a pair of object point cloud ($X_o$) and ground truth grasp ($g$) to a latent space ($z$).
 - **Decoder:** Reconstructs a grasp ($\hat{g}$) from $X_o$ and a sampled latent variable $z$.
 - **Training:** The VAE is trained to minimize a reconstruction loss (based on the transformation of predefined points on the gripper) between ground truth and predicted grasps, combined with a KL-divergence penalty between the encoder's latent distribution and a standard normal distribution.
 - **Input Data:** The VAE's input is the point cloud of the target object, segmented from the scene. To enhance robustness to real-world imperfections, the simulation data generation includes random salt-and-pepper noise at object boundaries and merging of partially occluded objects to mimic segmentation errors.
 - **Inference:** The encoder is discarded, and latent values are sampled from a normal distribution to generate diverse grasps.

- **Grasp Evaluation:** A separate evaluator module, also based on PointNet++, predicts a grasp quality score ($P(S|X_o, g)$) for each generated grasp.
 - **Training:** The evaluator is trained on a dataset comprising positive grasps (known to be successful) and negative grasps (unsuccessful). Hard negative grasps are generated by perturbing positive grasps to be in slight collision or too far from the object, helping the model learn fine-grained failure conditions.
 - **Purpose:** At test time, this evaluator filters out potentially failed grasps and ranks the remaining ones.

- **Grasp Refinement:** To improve sub-optimal grasps, a refinement step is implemented. Instead of computationally expensive gradient descent, the authors employ Metropolis-Hastings sampling. This involves perturbing a grasp ($g$) by a small $\Delta g$ and accepting the new grasp ($g + \Delta g$) with a probability proportional to the ratio of their success probabilities ($P(S|g+\Delta g,X_o) / P(S|g,X_o)$). This method is described as achieving similar performance to gradient-based refinement but with twice the computational speed.

**C. Collision Detection for Grasps in Clutter: CollisionNet:**
CollisionNet is a separate, discriminative neural network designed to predict a collision score ($P(C|X, g)$) for a given grasp $g$ within the full scene point cloud $X$.

- **Input:** The network takes the full scene point cloud ($X$) and the gripper's point cloud at the proposed grasp pose ($X_g$). An indicator feature distinguishes points belonging to the target object from those of the gripper and other clutter.
- **Architecture:** Similar to other modules, CollisionNet uses the PointNet++ architecture to reason about relative spatial information between the gripper and the point clouds.
- **Training Data:** Ground truth labels for collision ($C$) or no-collision ($\neg C$) are generated in simulation using a physics-based collision checker with full state information. The training data includes positive grasps (no collision with clutter), negative grasps (in collision with clutter), hard-negative grasps (perturbed positive grasps that collide), and grasps in free space. Balanced sampling is used during training to improve stability and generalization.
- **Optimization:** CollisionNet is optimized using a cross-entropy loss.

**D. Implementation Details:**
- **Data Generation:** Training data is synthesized online by randomly arranging 126 objects (from, including various categories) in stable poses. Rejection sampling ensures objects do not initially collide.
- **Point Cloud Processing:** For each scene, multiple 3D crops centered on the target object (with noise) are taken. These cropped point clouds are down-sampled to 4096 points.
- **Architecture:** All VAEs and samplers are based on PointNet++ architectures. The latent space dimension for the VAE is set to 2.
- **Inference Pipeline:** During inference, 200 latent values are sampled from the VAE, and the generated grasps are refined using 20 iterations of Metropolis-Hastings.
- **Computational Performance:** The entire inference process takes approximately 2.5 seconds on an NVIDIA Titan XP GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed method involved both simulation-based ablation studies and real robot experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating 6-DOF grasps for novel objects in structured clutter.

**A. Ablation Analysis and Discussion:**
Ablation studies were conducted in simulation using a held-out test set of 15 unseen objects arranged in 100 different cluttered scenes, with physical interactions simulated using FleX. Two metrics were used: **success rate** (percentage of successful grasps without collision) and **coverage** (percentage of ground truth grasps within 2cm of generated grasps). The Area Under Curve (AUC) of the success-coverage plot was used for comparison.

- **Learned vs. Surface Normal Based Grasp Sampler:** The learned VAE sampler demonstrated superior grasp coverage compared to a geometric baseline that samples points on object surfaces along normals. The VAE sampler had a higher AUC, indicating its ability to generate a wider range of viable grasps, especially for objects with complex geometries like rims or handles where the surface-normal sampler struggled.

- **CollisionNet vs. Voxel-Based Collision Checking:** CollisionNet significantly outperformed a common voxel-based heuristic (similar to MoveIt!) for collision detection.
 - CollisionNet showed higher precision and coverage.
 - The voxel-based approach frequently failed to predict collisions when the gripper intersected with occluded parts or areas with missing depth information (false negatives). In such cases, CollisionNet achieved 89.7% accuracy in correctly classifying collisions.
 - The voxel-based method also generated false negatives by rejecting valid grasps that slightly penetrated voxels representing the target object, due to the spatial expansion of voxels. Removing target object voxels improved coverage marginally but reduced grasp success by not pruning grasps colliding with the target. CollisionNet, by contrast, learned to reason about relative spatial information in partial point clouds without such biases.

- **Single-stage vs. Cascaded Evaluator:** The cascaded approach, which separates object-centric grasp evaluation from clutter-centric collision checking, outperformed a single-stage model that directly estimated success and non-collision ($P(S, \neg C|X, g)$). The cascaded model's advantage was attributed to the VAE's greater proficiency in learning grasps from object-centric observations and the explicit abstraction that allowed the grasp evaluator to focus on grasp robustness and CollisionNet on collision prediction.

- **Role of Object Instance Segmentation:** The cascaded approach, leveraging object instance information via segmentation, achieved a significantly higher AUC (0.22) compared to an instance-agnostic baseline (AUC 0.02). The instance-agnostic model frequently failed because its variational sampler became confused about which object to grasp in a scene, leading to degraded grasp quality across all objects. This highlights the importance of target-driven instance segmentation for effective grasping in clutter.

**B. Real Robot Experiments:**
Experiments were conducted on a physical setup consisting of a 7-DOF Franka Panda robot with a parallel-jaw gripper and an Intel Realsense D415 RGB-D camera. Grasps were executed in an open-loop fashion after a single scene observation. An occupancy voxel-grid collision checker was additionally used to prevent manipulator arm collisions, as CollisionNet primarily focuses on gripper-clutter collisions. The method was tested on 9 different cluttered scenes, involving 23 unknown objects and 51 total pick attempts, with half of the target objects initially occluded. A grasp was deemed successful if the object was picked within two attempts.

- **Performance Comparison:**
 - The proposed framework (Object Instance + CollisionNet) achieved a grasp success rate of **80.3%** (41/51 trials).
 - A baseline using 6-DOF GraspNet with state-of-the-art instance segmentation achieved 62.7% (32/51 trials).
 - The proposed model *without* CollisionNet (relying only on object instance information for grasp evaluation) achieved 60.7% (31/51 trials), indicating a substantial performance degradation without explicit learned collision checking.
- **Key Real-world Success:** The results demonstrated that the proposed approach, trained exclusively on synthetic data, successfully transferred to the real world and significantly outperformed the baseline, improving grasp success by 17.6%.
- **Failure Analysis:** Primary failure modes included grasps colliding with the object (not pruned by the object-centric evaluator but intended to be filtered by CollisionNet) and limitations of the auxiliary voxel-based arm collision checking.

**C. Application: Removing Blocking Objects:**
The paper demonstrated an application of the approach to multi-step manipulation, specifically for retrieving an occluded target object by first removing blocking objects.
- The system inferred potential grasps for the occluded target object, even if initially unreachable (indicated by low CollisionNet scores).
- It then identified blocking objects by calculating the expected increase in collision scores for target grasps if a specific object was hypothetically removed.
- The object causing the largest increase in collision-free opportunities was identified as the primary blocking object.
- This blocking object was then grasped and removed from the scene, after which collision-free grasps for the now-accessible target object could be generated and executed. This qualitative experiment showcased the ability to reason about sequential manipulation beyond simple pick-and-place.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for 6-DOF robotic grasping that addresses several critical challenges in cluttered and unstructured environments, demonstrating significant potential impact in various robotic applications.

**1. Enhanced Robustness in Clutter:** The core contribution lies in enabling robust 6-DOF grasping of novel objects in structured clutter from partial, single-view point cloud observations. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in real-world scenarios such as industrial automation, logistics, domestic assistance, and retail, where items are often tightly packed and not fully observable.

**2. Improved Collision Avoidance:** The development and successful application of CollisionNet, a learned collision checker, represents a notable advancement. Unlike traditional geometric or voxel-based methods, CollisionNet operates on raw partial point clouds and demonstrates superior accuracy in identifying potential collisions, especially with occluded object parts. This reduces the risk of damaging objects or the robot, thereby increasing the reliability and safety of robotic manipulation.

**3. Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The fact that the system, trained entirely on synthetic data, achieves an 80.3% success rate on real-world objects in clutter is a significant achievement. This high rate of sim-to-real transfer indicates the robustness of the learned models and their ability to generalize to physical complexities not explicitly seen during training. This reduces the immense data collection burden typically associated with real-world robotic learning.

**4. Enabling Target-Driven and Sequential Manipulation:** The framework's target-driven nature, facilitated by instance segmentation, allows robots to perform specific manipulation tasks rather than merely picking arbitrary objects. Furthermore, the demonstrated ability to reason about and remove blocking objects to access an occluded target is a foundational step towards more complex, multi-step task planning in robotics. This capability is essential for operations in confined spaces like kitchen cabinets or densely packed shelves, moving beyond isolated pick-and-place operations.

**5. Advancement in Learning-Based Grasping:** The cascaded architecture, which separates object-centric grasp quality from scene-centric collision reasoning, offers a modular and effective approach. This design allows for specialized learning components and provides a more interpretable understanding of grasp failures, contributing to the broader field of learning-based robotic manipulation.

**6. Foundation for Future Work:** The authors identify avenues for future research, such as integrating trajectory generation into grasp planning and extending the framework to more challenging scenarios like retrieving stacked objects. This suggests that the current work provides a strong foundation upon which more sophisticated and versatile robotic manipulation systems can be built.

In summary, this research advances the state-of-the-art in 6-DOF grasping by providing a robust, data-driven solution for cluttered environments, bridging the gap between perception and action in complex real-world settings. Its successful real-world deployment on a robotic platform, despite synthetic training, highlights its practical applicability and potential to expand the capabilities of autonomous robots.
