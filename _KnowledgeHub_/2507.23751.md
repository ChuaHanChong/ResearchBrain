# CoT-Self-Instruct: Building high-quality synthetic prompts for reasoning and non-reasoning tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR at Meta developed CoT-Self-Instruct, a method for generating and curating high-quality synthetic training data for large language models across both reasoning and non-reasoning tasks. The approach leverages Chain-of-Thought reasoning during data creation and employs advanced filtering mechanisms like Answer-Consistency and RIP, resulting in up to 57.2% average accuracy on reasoning benchmarks and 67.1% on instruction-following tasks, outperforming standard Self-Instruct and human-annotated datasets.

## Problem

- Human annotation for high-quality LLM training data is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and prone to biases.
- Existing synthetic data generation techniques, like Self-Instruct, struggle to ensure high quality and effectiveness, particularly for complex reasoning tasks.
- A comprehensive solution for generating high-quality synthetic data for both verifiable reasoning and general instruction-following tasks, coupled with robust automatic filtering, was lacking.

## Method

- A two-stage pipeline where an LLM first generates synthetic instructions using ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning==.
- For verifiable reasoning tasks, the LLM generates both the instruction and its verifiable answer simultaneously, integrating step-by-step problem-solving. For general tasks, only the instruction is generated.
- Synthetic instructions are curated using advanced filtering: ==Answer-Consistency== for reasoning tasks (comparing LLM-generated responses to the creation-time target) and ==Rejecting Instruction Preferences (RIP) filtering== for general instruction-following tasks (evaluating multiple responses with a Reward Model).

## Results

- On verifiable reasoning tasks, CoT-Self-Instruct with Answer-Consistency filtering achieved an average accuracy of **57.2%** across benchmarks (MATH500, AMC23, AIME24, GPQA-Diamond), outperforming standard Self-Instruct's **42.7%**.
- For general instruction-following tasks, CoT-Self-Instruct with RIP filtering achieved an average score of **54.7%** (offline DPO) and **67.1%** (online DPO), surpassing standard Self-Instruct (**47.4%**) and human instructions from WildChat.
- The proposed Answer-Consistency filtering consistently yielded the highest performance among filtering strategies for reasoning tasks, and longer CoT reasoning chains during generation provided greater gains for all task types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning directly into the synthetic data generation process significantly enhances the quality and logical soundness of generated examples for both reasoning and non-reasoning tasks.
> - Rigorous, automatic filtering mechanisms like Answer-Consistency and RIP are crucial for curating high-quality synthetic data, allowing LLMs to effectively learn from self-generated examples.
> - Synthetic data generated and filtered using CoT-Self-Instruct can lead to LLMs that outperform models trained on comparable standard synthetic or even human-annotated datasets across various benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models has been largely predicated on the availability of vast amounts of high-quality training data. However, acquiring such data through human annotation is often prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, ethically complex, and prone to biases and errors. This challenge has driven the growing interest in **synthetic data generation**, where LLMs are used to create their own training examples, thereby offering a scalable and potentially more controlled alternative.

This paper directly addresses a significant limitation of existing synthetic data generation techniques, particularly the **Self-Instruct** method (Wang et al., 2022a). While Self-Instruct pioneered the use of LLMs to generate instruction-following examples from a small set of seed tasks, its primary challenge has been ensuring the *quality* and *effectiveness* of the generated data for diverse tasks, especially complex reasoning. Prior variants of Self-Instruct have focused on increasing query complexity, maintaining semantic diversity, or scaling data generation. However, a comprehensive solution for generating high-quality synthetic data for *both* reasoning and non-reasoning tasks, coupled with robust filtering, remained an insufficiently understood area.

CoT-Self-Instruct enters this landscape by integrating **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning** directly into the synthetic data generation process. CoT has been shown to enhance LLMs' reasoning abilities by prompting them to articulate intermediate steps. By applying CoT to *data generation itself*, the authors aim to produce more logically sound and challenging examples. Furthermore, the paper introduces sophisticated **automatic filtering mechanisms** (Answer-Consistency and RIP) to curate only the highest quality synthetic data, building upon recent work in data selection which emphasizes that quality often trumps quantity in LLM training. This work represents a crucial step towards making LLM self-improvement more effective, reducing reliance on costly human labor, and potentially pushing the performance ceiling of LLMs across a wider range of tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly effective method for **generating and curating high-quality synthetic data for training LLMs across both verifiable reasoning and non-verifiable instruction-following tasks**.

The motivations behind this objective are multi-faceted:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost**: Human-generated high-quality data is expensive and difficult to scale. Synthetic data offers a viable, cost-effective, and scalable alternative.
- **Improving Data Quality**: Human-generated data can suffer from biases and errors. LLM-generated data, especially when guided by reasoning and filtered rigorously, can potentially surpass human data in specific quality metrics, leading to better-performing models.
- **Enhancing LLM Capabilities**: Existing synthetic data methods often struggle to produce examples that adequately challenge or teach LLMs advanced reasoning. By integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into the data generation process, the authors aim to create more complex, logically valid, and high-quality reasoning examples.
- **Developing Robust Curation**: Not all synthetically generated data is useful. The need for effective, automatic filtering methods is critical to ensure that only the most beneficial examples are used for training, optimizing model performance despite potentially reduced dataset size.
- **Generalizability Across Task Types**: The method aims to be effective for both tasks with verifiable answers (e.g., math problems) and open-ended instruction-following tasks, demonstrating broad applicability.

Ultimately, the goal is to enable more efficient and powerful LLM development by creating a self-sustaining and quality-driven data generation pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CoT-Self-Instruct method is a two-stage pipeline designed for both verifiable reasoning and general instruction-following tasks, leveraging LLM reasoning and automatic curation.

**Stage 1: Synthetic Instruction Creation with Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**
Unlike standard Self-Instruct which directly generates new instructions, CoT-Self-Instruct prompts the LLM to first engage in a multi-step reasoning process:
- **Analysis of Seed Instructions**: The LLM analyzes a small set of human-annotated "seed" instructions (few-shot examples), identifying their domain, complexity, purpose, and quality characteristics.
- **Plan Generation**: Based on this analysis, the LLM devises a step-by-step plan to create a *new, self-contained instruction* of similar quality and complexity.
- **Instruction and Answer Generation**:
 - **Verifiable Reasoning Tasks**: For tasks with deterministic answers (e.g., math problems), the LLM is instructed to generate both the *instruction* and its *verifiable answer* simultaneously, performing step-by-step problem-solving as part of the creation process. This integrated approach ensures logical validity and answer correctness from the outset. A strict formatting template is used to easily separate the question from its boxed final answer.
 - **General Instruction-Following Tasks**: For open-ended tasks, the LLM generates only the *instruction*, not a specific response. This is because these tasks are later trained with reward models that assess policy model responses, making a reference answer unnecessary during generation. To maintain domain consistency, seed instructions are first grouped into categories, and new examples are generated from within the same category.

**Stage 2: Synthetic Instruction Curation**
To ensure only high-quality data is used for training, a crucial filtering step is applied:
- **For Verifiable Reasoning Tasks – Answer-Consistency Filtering**:
 - Given a generated instruction and its target answer (from Stage 1), the LLM is prompted to generate *K* independent responses to the instruction at inference time.
 - A majority vote is taken over these *K* responses' final answers.
 - If the majority-voted answer *does not match* the target answer generated during the instruction creation phase, the synthetic example is rejected. This leverages the insight that the CoT-Self-Instruct generated target answer, derived during the problem creation process, possesses a deeper understanding than an answer generated purely at inference, providing a strong consistency check. This is stated to be superior to standard Self-Consistency filtering which only relies on majority vote among generated solutions.
- **For General Instruction-Following Tasks – Rejecting Instruction Preferences (RIP) Filtering**:
 - This method (from Yu et al., 2025) is used because there are no deterministic "answers" to compare.
 - For each generated instruction, *K* responses are generated by an LLM.
 - A separate Reward Model (RM) evaluates each of these *K* responses, assigning a score.
 - The lowest rating among these *K* responses is used as the overall quality score for the synthetic instruction.
 - Instead of a global threshold (which could cause topic distribution shifts), the method selects the instruction with the highest RIP score from multiple generated instructions within each few-shot prompt.

**Self-Training with Synthetic Data**:
The curated synthetic datasets are then used for reinforcement learning (RL) training to improve the performance of a base LLM.
- **Verifiable Reasoning Tasks**: Trained with GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Learning from Pairwise Preferences).
- **General Instruction-Following Tasks**: Trained with DPO (Direct Preference Optimization), including both offline and online variants, potentially combined with length normalization to mitigate response length inflation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of CoT-Self-Instruct across both reasoning and instruction-following domains.

**For Verifiable Reasoning Tasks (Tables 1, 3-10):**
- **CoT-Self-Instruct Outperforms Self-Instruct**: Models fine-tuned on CoT-Self-Instruct generated data (Qwen3-4B generated, GRPO-trained Qwen3-4B-Base) significantly outperformed those trained on standard Self-Instruct data. For instance, without filtering, CoT-Self-Instruct achieved an average accuracy of 53.0% across MATH500, AMC23, AIME24, and GPQA-Diamond, compared to 42.7% for Self-Instruct. This highlights the crucial role of CoT reasoning in generating high-quality reasoning instructions and target answers.
- **Filtering Provides Further Gains**: Applying filtering methods further improved performance for both approaches. With Answer-Consistency filtering, CoT-Self-Instruct reached 57.2% average accuracy, surpassing Self-Instruct with CoT-generated targets and Self-Consistency filtering (53.6%) or RIP filtering (54.5%). The proposed Answer-Consistency filtering specifically yielded the highest performance among all filtering strategies.
- **Superior to Existing Datasets**: CoT-Self-Instruct generated data, even when filtered to comparable sizes, outperformed well-known human-curated and large public reasoning datasets. For example, 2926 filtered CoT-Self-Instruct examples (with Answer-Consistency) achieved 57.2%, significantly higher than the 44.6% achieved by training on 893 s1k questions with R1 labels, or the 47.5% from 10,000 OpenMathReasoning questions. Scaling CoT-Self-Instruct data to 10,000 examples with Answer-Consistency filtering further boosted performance to 58.7%.
- **Consistency Across Target Generation Methods**: The observed trends held true even when using different models or methods (e.g., majority-vote, Best-of-K using a reward model) to construct targets, consistently demonstrating the superiority of CoT-Self-Instruct with Answer-Consistency.

**For General Instruction Following Tasks (Tables 2, 11):**
- **CoT-Self-Instruct Outperforms Self-Instruct**: For non-reasoning tasks (DPO-trained Llama 3.1-8B-Instruct), CoT-Self-Instruct also significantly improved synthetic data quality. Without filtering, it achieved an average score of 53.9% across AlpacaEval 2.0 and Arena-Hard, compared to 47.4% for standard Self-Instruct.
- **RIP Filtering Boosts Performance**: Applying RIP filtering further enhanced CoT-Self-Instruct, raising its average score to 54.7%. This also improved Self-Instruct and human data (WildChat), indicating its general effectiveness in curating high-quality instructions.
- **Superior to Human Instructions**: The best DPO-trained model was achieved using CoT-Self-Instruct with RIP filtering (54.7%), outperforming training on human instructions from WildChat (46.8% without filtering, 50.7% with RIP filtering).
- **Online DPO Further Improvements**: With online DPO training, CoT-Self-Instruct + RIP achieved an average score of 67.1%, surpassing human instructions from WildChat (63.1%), further cementing its leading performance.
- **Longer CoT is Better**: Experiments showed that longer CoT reasoning chains during generation yielded greater gains than shorter CoTs, even for non-verifiable tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CoT-Self-Instruct method represents a significant advancement in the field of synthetic data generation for LLMs, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Elevated LLM Performance**: The most direct impact is the demonstrated ability to train LLMs that achieve state-of-the-art performance on challenging reasoning and instruction-following benchmarks. By creating higher-quality training data, this method pushes the capabilities of LLMs beyond what is achievable with current human-annotated or standard synthetic datasets.
- **Reduced Reliance on Human Annotation**: By providing a robust, automated pipeline for generating and curating high-quality training data, CoT-Self-Instruct significantly reduces the cost, time, and logistical challenges associated with acquiring large-scale human-annotated datasets. This enables more rapid iteration and development cycles for LLMs.
- **Scalability and Accessibility**: The method offers a scalable solution for data generation, potentially democratizing access to high-quality training data, especially for researchers and organizations with limited resources for human annotation. This could accelerate AI research and development across the board.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities**: The core innovation of integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning into the data generation process is particularly impactful. By teaching LLMs to generate problems and solutions *with* reasoning, the method inherently fosters a deeper understanding of logical steps, which then translates into improved reasoning capabilities of the trained models.
- **Improved Data Quality Control**: The introduction of sophisticated filtering mechanisms like Answer-Consistency and RIP marks a critical step towards automated quality control for synthetic data. These methods ensure that only truly beneficial examples are used for training, mitigating the risks of introducing noise or low-quality data that could degrade model performance.
- **Towards Autonomous LLM Development**: This work moves closer to a future where LLMs can autonomously generate, evaluate, and refine their own training data, leading to self-improving AI systems. This paradigm shift could unlock unprecedented levels of efficiency and capability in LLM development.
- **Broader Applicability**: The method's effectiveness across both verifiable reasoning and open-ended instruction-following tasks highlights its versatility, suggesting it can be broadly applied to improve various facets of LLM behavior.

In conclusion, CoT-Self-Instruct offers a powerful and comprehensive framework for synthetic data generation, addressing critical limitations of previous approaches and setting a new standard for building high-quality training datasets that significantly enhance LLM performance.
