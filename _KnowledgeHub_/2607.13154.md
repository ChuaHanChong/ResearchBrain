# Worlds in One Demo: A Synthetic Data Engine for Learning Open-World Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University researchers developed WANDA, a synthetic data engine that generates extensive, diverse data for open-world mobile manipulation from a single human demonstration. Policies trained with WANDA data achieved 75.6% success in simulation with 100 generated demos and an average progress score of 54.8% in real-world tasks across 16 diverse environments, while also demonstrating zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization.

## Problem

- Collecting extensive real-world data for open-world mobile manipulation is resource-intensive and costly due to high degrees of freedom, large workspaces, and long task horizons.
- Existing synthetic data generation methods often lack the diversity required for open-world scenarios, typically focusing on tabletop tasks or relying on pre-scanned environments and manually scanned objects.
- Current data collection paradigms underutilize the rich information embedded within a single robot demonstration, which could provide comprehensive task and environmental insights.

## Method

- **WANDA** reconstructs diverse 3D environments and robot-object interactions from a single RGBD demonstration and everyday photos using techniques like ==Gaussian Splatting==, ==BundleSDF==, and ==VLM-guided spatial configuration==.
- The system synthesizes novel whole-body trajectories for mobile manipulation by chaining contact-rich segments with ==motion planning== and employing ==Corrective State Expansion (CSE)== to simulate policy drifts and enhance robustness.
- Photo-realistic visual observations are generated through ==factorized rendering==, combining Gaussian splatting for backgrounds with ==Isaac Sim== rendering for foreground robots and objects, reducing rendering time.

## Results

- Policies trained with **100** WANDA-generated demonstrations achieved **75.6%** success in simulation, comparable to baselines trained on **40-60** teleoperated demonstrations, demonstrating significant data efficiency.
- WANDA-trained policies achieved an average progress score of **54.8%** across **five** long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks in **16** diverse real-world environments, showing strong spatial and cross-environment generalization.
- The system demonstrated the first reported zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization in mobile manipulation, achieving a **40%** progress score on a Linearbot when trained solely on data generated from an Agibot G1 demonstration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single human demonstration, when comprehensively leveraged, contains sufficient task and environmental information to bootstrap a scalable synthetic data generation process for open-world mobile manipulation.
> - Corrective State Expansion (CSE) is critical for generating data that enhances long-horizon robustness and spatial generalization, by simulating policy drifts and increasing coverage of manipulation configurations.
> - Factorized rendering and VLM-guided scene generation allow for efficient synthesis of diverse, photo-realistic visual data and 3D scenes from minimal inputs, enabling broad cross-environment generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction and Broader Research Landscape

Achieving mobile manipulation in diverse, unstructured environments such as homes, cafes, and offices represents a long-standing challenge in robotics. The development of learning-based foundation models has demonstrated potential in robot manipulation; however, extending these to open-world mobile manipulation presents significant data acquisition challenges. Mobile manipulators possess a greater number of degrees of freedom and operate within a considerably larger workspace compared to fixed-base manipulators. This necessitates policies capable of strong spatial generalization across varied configurations, robustness to compounding errors over long task horizons, and generalization across diverse environments. These requirements typically demand extensive datasets, often comprising thousands of demonstrations for a single task.

Current primary data collection methods, including teleoperation and Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI), are resource-intensive, requiring substantial human effort and financial investment to scale. Collecting mobile manipulation data is particularly costly, as it involves physically deploying robots in multiple environments and repeating tasks. Furthermore, these methods often depend on high-precision odometry and localization, which can be difficult to achieve reliably in real-world, noisy settings.

An alternative approach involves using synthetic data to train robot policies. Methods such as MimicGen and its extensions have explored automatic demonstration generation to reduce the need for manual data collection. MoMaGen specifically extended this to mobile manipulation within simulation environments. However, these simulation-based approaches typically require additional real-world data for sim-to-real co-training to mitigate the simulation-to-real-world gap. Other real-world extensions, like DemoGen, often operate in sparse point-cloud observation spaces, which may not be suitable for training large robot foundation models. A common limitation across these existing synthetic data generation methods is their inability to synthesize diverse demonstrations specifically for open-world mobile manipulation, with many focusing on tabletop scenarios or relying on pre-scanned environments or manually scanned objects.

The authors identify a gap, arguing that the informational richness embedded within a single robot demonstration, particularly for mobile manipulation, is currently underutilized. A single demonstration can provide views suitable for consistent 3D scene reconstruction, capture 4D object motions, and specify critical contact-rich interactions. This work aims to leverage this information to address the data bottleneck in learning open-world mobile manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to maximize the utility of a single human demonstration for learning open-world mobile manipulation policies by developing a scalable synthetic data engine. The motivation stems from the prohibitive human effort and cost associated with collecting vast datasets for mobile manipulation through conventional methods like teleoperation or UMI.

Specifically, the paper aims to achieve the following:
- **Data Efficiency:** Enable the training of robust, end-to-end open-world mobile manipulation policies from a single human demonstration, thereby significantly reducing the need for extensive manual data collection.
- **Spatial Generalization:** Develop a mechanism to synthesize diverse trajectories that cover a wide range of spatial configurations, allowing trained policies to perform tasks effectively irrespective of the exact starting positions or object placements.
- **Long-Horizon Robustness:** Generate data that accounts for and simulates policy drifts and compounding errors common in long-duration mobile manipulation tasks, thereby enhancing the robustness of learned policies.
- **Cross-Environment Generalization:** Facilitate the creation of diverse 3D environments from simple inputs (e.g., everyday photos) to synthesize trajectories, enabling policies to generalize to novel, unseen real-world environments.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** Support the generation of synthetic data for different robot morphologies from a single demonstration, aiming for zero-shot deployment on varied mobile manipulators.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to overcome the limitations of current data collection paradigms and existing synthetic data generation methods, which largely fall short in providing the scale and diversity required for open-world mobile manipulation. The underlying hypothesis is that a single demonstration contains sufficient task and environmental information to bootstrap a comprehensive synthetic data generation process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, WANDA (learning open-World mobile mANipulation from one demonstration via a synthetic DAta engine), operates through a multi-stage pipeline as depicted in Figure 2.

#### 3.1. Worlds Reconstruction and Generation

WANDA begins by processing a single real RGBD demonstration and leveraging daily photographs to create a diverse set of 3D environments.
- **3D Background Scene Reconstruction:** The system subsamples diverse mobile camera views from the source demonstration. Foreground elements are inpainted using Nano Banana Pro. The inpainted background images are then fed into MAtCha, a pose-free sparse-view 2D Gaussian Splatting method, to reconstruct both Gaussian splats and a mesh of the scene. To establish real-world scale, the reconstructed scene is registered by aligning the predicted camera height with the robot's camera height derived from forward kinematics, bypassing the need for high-precision SLAM.
- **Robot-Object Interaction Reconstruction:** BundleSDF is employed to simultaneously reconstruct the mesh and track the 6D pose of foreground objects from RGBD observation streams. This approach aims for high-fidelity object texture and geometry, accommodating articulated objects. For cases of incomplete object observation during the demonstration, a generative completion method inspired by UA-Pose and SAM3D is used to infer the unseen parts of the object geometry, which are then fused with real observations to generate complete textured meshes.
- **Scalable 3D Scene Generation:** To achieve broad scene generalization, Marble is utilized to generate new 3D Gaussian Splats and extract meshes from single everyday photos. This expands the dataset's environmental diversity.
- **VLM-guided Spatial Configuration:** Contact-rich end-effector (EE)-object interaction segments are identified. A Vision-Language Model (VLM), Gemini 3.1 Pro, is prompted with rendered orbit views of the scene and the task instruction to identify suitable regions for initializing manipulated objects. SAM3 is then used to predict entity masks, which are back-projected into 3D to define randomization regions. For Marble-generated scenes, which may lack metric scale, the VLM infers plausible entity heights to recover the scene's metric scale.

#### 3.2. Novel Trajectory Generation for Mobile Manipulation

Once the diverse worlds are established, WANDA synthesizes new trajectories.
- **Whole-Body Motion Planning:** Rearranged contact-rich segments are chained into complete trajectories using whole-body motion planning, encompassing both navigation and manipulation phases.
 - **Navigation Phase:** The system samples feasible base positions, solves whole-body Inverse Kinematics (IK) to reach the segment's starting EE pose, and uses RRT-Connect to plan a collision-free base path. A visibility cost is applied to keep objects in view.
 - **Manipulation Phase:** RRT-Connect plans a collision-free arm path to reach the target EE pose from the navigation pose. During contact-rich segments, the object's tracked pose and the EE motion are jointly replayed in object-relative coordinates, with IK solving for arm joint angles.
- **Corrective State Expansion (CSE):** To enhance long-horizon robustness and state coverage, CSE simulates policy drifts.
 - **Object State Expansion (OSE):** After the robot reaches a target base position, object poses are perturbed (translation and yaw) around their centroid. IK and arm motion planning are re-executed to adapt to these new object poses, simulating navigation drift and increasing manipulation spatial coverage.
 - **Robot State Expansion (RSE):** During the navigation phase, new navigation start positions are sampled within a stratified cone behind the original target, and the robot is instructed to turn towards the object and move directly. Arm joints are also randomly perturbed during navigation, easing back to a default carried pose to counter arm drift. In the manipulation phase, approaching-object segments are replaced with a cone-cycloid EE trajectory, starting from a sampled cone behind the target gripper pose, to broaden approach configurations.
Every generated sample is validated for kinematic feasibility and collision before acceptance.

#### 3.3. Factorized Rendering

Finally, photo-realistic visual observations are generated using factorized rendering. Gaussian splatting renders the background, while the foreground robot and objects are rendered in Isaac Sim with lighting domain randomization. These layers are then composited to produce the final frames. This approach aims to reduce rendering time compared to full scene rendering in Isaac Sim.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated WANDA extensively through simulation and real-world experiments, across various policy architectures.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Bigym Benchmark:** Using ACT for policy learning, WANDA generated 100 demonstrations from one source demonstration. Policies trained with WANDA achieved an average success rate of 75.6%, comparable to baselines trained on 40-60 teleoperated demonstrations (78.0%). This demonstrated sample efficiency for localized mobile manipulation.
- **BEHAVIOR Challenge:** For a long-horizon mobile manipulation task, WANDA generated 1360 demonstrations from one official demonstration to fine-tune a π0.5 policy. WANDA achieved a Q-score of 16.67, outperforming policies trained on 20 (3.33) and 50 (11.11) official demonstrations, although it was lower than policies trained on 200 demonstrations (62.22). This indicated effectiveness in generating high-quality trajectories for extended tasks and improved data efficiency (approximately 50x in simulation).

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Five long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks (Lunch Box, Utensil, Drop Trash, Pour, Fridge) were designed for an Agibot G1 robot. Policies were trained using π0.5 with data synthesized from a single real demonstration per task.
- **Overall Performance:** WANDA achieved an average progress score of 54.8% across 16 different environments, demonstrating broad spatial and cross-environment generalization from a single real demonstration.
- **Task-Specific Results:**
 - **Lunch Box:** The robot successfully completed a multi-stage task involving navigation, folding, grasping, and placement.
 - **Drop Trash:** The robot consistently navigated to, grasped, and disposed of objects across various tables.
 - **Utensil:** The robot navigated a long distance and executed precise grasping of a knife.
- **CSE Ablation:** A variant of WANDA without Corrective State Expansion (CSE) showed a significant degradation in performance, achieving only a 15.7% average progress score. This indicates that CSE is critical for robustness against compounding state deviations and for enhancing spatial generalization during manipulation and recovery from arm drift.

#### 4.3. Scalability Study

- **In-domain Data Scaling:** Scaling the quantity of generated demonstrations (e.g., from a single scene) resulted in steady performance improvements in both the BEHAVIOR Challenge simulation and the real-world Drop Trash task, suggesting the scalability of WANDA for improving in-domain performance.
- **Scene Diversity Scaling:** WANDA was tested for generalization in novel real-world environments by training policies on data synthesized from Marble-generated worlds (created from single photos). While a source-only baseline failed to generalize (near zero progress score), WANDA maintained substantial progress across four tasks and three novel scenes. This indicated that WANDA facilitates open-world data generation with minimal environmental input, bridging visual and geometric discrepancies between generated and real scenes.
- **Data Generation Cost:** WANDA demonstrated efficient data generation. It took approximately 3 GPU-hours to generate one hour of 30 fps multi-view demonstration data. On an 8x L40S node, this equated to about 0.38 wall-clock hours, which is 6.4 times faster than MoMaGen and approximately 4 times faster than human teleoperation (considering setup and reset times). The initial human effort for one demonstration amortizes as the generated dataset scales.

#### 4.4. Zero-shot Cross-Embodiment Generalization

WANDA supported cross-embodiment data generation from an Agibot G1 to a Linearbot. Despite significant morphological and camera differences between the robots, 400 Linearbot demonstrations were generated from a single Agibot G1 demonstration for the Drop Trash task. A policy trained on this data achieved a 40% progress score when deployed zero-shot on a physical Linearbot, demonstrating the first reported zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization capability in mobile manipulation.

#### 4.5. Visualization

Visualizations confirmed the generation of photo-realistic observations for both source and diverse generated scenes. Visualization of Corrective State Expansion showed a broader spatial distribution of object positions during manipulation and increased diversity of robot poses, consistent with its intended function of enhancing robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents WANDA, a synthetic data engine designed to address the data bottleneck in learning open-world mobile manipulation policies. The system's primary significance lies in its ability to leverage a single human demonstration to generate extensive, diverse, and high-quality synthetic data, thereby substantially reducing the human effort and cost typically associated with data collection.

The work demonstrates the capability to train policies that exhibit:
- **Enhanced Generalization:** Strong spatial generalization across diverse configurations, long-horizon robustness against compounding errors, and cross-environment generalization to novel scenes derived from single photographs.
- **Data Efficiency:** A significant improvement in data efficiency, achieving comparable or superior performance to policies trained on many more teleoperated demonstrations in simulation and facilitating meaningful progress on complex real-world tasks from minimal input.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The potential for zero-shot deployment on robots with different morphologies, indicating a step towards more versatile and rapidly deployable robotic systems.

By making open-world data generation more accessible and scalable, WANDA has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of generalist mobile manipulation robots. The methodology for world reconstruction, novel trajectory generation with corrective state expansion, and factorized rendering provides a framework for generating high-fidelity visual and motion data.

Future directions highlighted by the authors include extending the reconstruction stage to handle soft and deformable objects using techniques like 4D Gaussian Splatting, incorporating physics into WANDA for more efficient and reproducible real-to-sim evaluation, and exploring co-training with UMI data and human videos to further broaden policy diversity and coverage. These advancements could further expand the applicability and robustness of learned mobile manipulation skills in increasingly complex real-world scenarios.
