# RoboMemory: A Brain-inspired Multi-memory Agentic Framework for Interactive Environmental Learning in Physical Embodied Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> A brain-inspired multi-memory agentic framework, RoboMemory, unifies spatial, temporal, episodic, and semantic memory with parallel processing to facilitate interactive environmental learning and long-horizon planning in physical embodied systems. The framework achieves a 70.5% success rate on EmbodiedBench, surpassing existing VLM-based agents, and shows cumulative learning in real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- VLM/VLA models struggle with long-horizon reasoning and consistent environmental understanding in partially observable real-world environments due to reliance on immediate observations and limited context windows.
- Existing memory systems for embodied agents often suffer from fragmented spatial awareness, sequential updates leading to latency, and rigid knowledge representations that hinder adaptation.
- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models are not inherently optimized for long-horizon, spatially grounded embodied planning trajectories, limiting their generalization to novel interactive scenarios.

## Method

- **RoboMemory** introduces a brain-inspired 'Perception – Memory – Retrieval – Planning – Execution' loop, featuring an Information Preprocessor (Thalamus-inspired) and a Closed-Loop Planning Module (Prefrontal Cortex-inspired) with a Planner-Critic mechanism.
- A Comprehensive Embodied Memory system (Hippocampus-inspired) unifies four distinct memory modules: Temporal (short-term buffer), Spatial (dynamic Knowledge Graph with incremental updates), Semantic (long-term facts), and Episodic (task trajectories), all updated and retrieved in parallel.
- Low-level execution is managed by a Cerebellum-inspired module utilizing a ==LoRA==-finetuned VLA model for manipulation and SLAM-based navigation for locomotion.

## Results

- RoboMemory achieved an average Success Rate (SR) of **70.5%** and Goal Condition Success Rate (GC) of **79.7%** across the EmbodiedBench benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art closed-source VLM-Agents (e.g., Claude-3.5-Sonnet at **69.5%** SR) and other VLM-Agent frameworks.
- Efficiency analysis showed parallel memory updates were comparable to single-module updates, and the dynamic Spatial KG update algorithm efficiently localized modifications, retrieving a decreasing proportion of relations as the KG grew (e.g., **28%** at iteration 20).
- Real-world robot deployments confirmed interactive environmental learning, with success rates for second attempts significantly higher than first attempts (e.g., **26.67%** SR to **46.67%** SR for repeated tasks without memory resets), demonstrating the framework's ability to learn cumulatively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified and parallelized multi-memory architecture, inspired by the brain's hierarchical memory system, significantly enhances long-horizon planning and interactive environmental learning in embodied agents.
> - Dynamic, retrieval-driven incremental updates to a Spatial Knowledge Graph provide a scalable and efficient method for maintaining consistent spatial awareness in partially observable and changing environments.
> - The integration of long-term episodic and semantic memories enables cumulative interactive learning, allowing agents to improve performance on repeated tasks by leveraging past experiences and analyzing failure causes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied intelligence aims to develop robots capable of learning, reasoning, and generalizing effectively in complex, dynamic real-world environments. Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have enabled progress in this field. VLMs facilitate high-level textual planning from multimodal inputs, while VLAs offer direct, low-level robot control. However, these two approaches exhibit complementary limitations: VLAs often lack long-horizon reasoning due to reliance on immediate observations, and VLM-based agents, while capable of strategic planning, typically struggle with direct motor control and maintaining consistent environmental understanding.

A current paradigm attempts to bridge this gap by combining VLM-based planners with VLA executors. While this improves performance on multi-step tasks, existing implementations face two primary challenges in real-world settings. First, environments are partially observable, and VLM agents often rely on chat-style context windows, which are insufficient for robust spatial awareness and maintaining a consistent representation of the environment's layout and object locations over time. This leads to fragmented spatial reasoning. Second, pre-trained VLMs are not inherently trained on long-horizon, spatially grounded embodied planning trajectories, limiting their generalization to novel real-world scenarios. This necessitates frameworks that support interactive environmental learning, enabling agents to acquire, integrate, and retrieve diverse forms of knowledge (spatial, episodic, semantic) through experience.

Previous work on memory systems for embodied agents has explored time-ordered context buffers for short-term memory, experience buffers for long-term semantic memory, and skill libraries for procedural memory. However, these often suffer from limitations such as lack of spatial awareness, sequential memory updates leading to latency, and rigid knowledge representations that hinder adaptation to dynamic environments. Cognitive psychology provides hierarchical memory models (sensory, short-term, long-term, episodic, semantic, working memory) as a conceptual foundation, but most robotic frameworks have only partially implemented these, often treating memory modules as independent buffers without dynamic interactions observed in biological systems. This research aims to address these identified limitations by introducing a brain-inspired, unified, and parallelized multi-memory framework that directly tackles the challenges of partial observability, spatial reasoning, and interactive learning in physical embodied systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable framework, named RoboMemory, that enables embodied agents to perform long-horizon planning and interactive learning in complex, dynamic, and partially observable real-world environments. The framework aims to achieve this by unifying various memory types within a brain-inspired, parallelized architecture.

The motivation for RoboMemory stems from several critical limitations observed in existing embodied intelligence approaches:
- **Partial Observability and Fragmented Spatial Reasoning:** Current VLM-based agents often lack mechanisms for maintaining a consistent, long-term understanding of the environment's spatial layout and object locations. Their reliance on limited context windows prevents them from reliably tracking objects or recognizing previously visited states, which is crucial for navigation and manipulation tasks that span multiple locations or time steps.
- **Inefficient Integration of Heterogeneous Memories:** While various memory types (e.g., temporal, semantic) have been explored, their integration often occurs sequentially, leading to high inference latency. This inhibits real-time interaction and adaptive decision-making in dynamic environments. A more efficient, unified approach is required to handle diverse information streams.
- **Limited Capacity for Long-Horizon Adaptation and Generalization:** Pre-trained VLMs are typically not optimized for embodied planning, especially for long-horizon tasks requiring continuous interaction and learning from environmental feedback. This restricts their ability to generalize to novel scenarios and improve performance through experience. The capacity for "interactive environmental learning"—acquiring and leveraging spatial, episodic, and semantic knowledge during task execution—is a recognized gap.
- **Bridging Biological Cognition and Robotics:** The research is motivated by insights from cognitive neuroscience, which highlight the hierarchical organization and parallel processing nature of human memory. Mimicking this biological architecture is hypothesized to lead to more generalizable and adaptive embodied systems that can better manage layered information processing.

In summary, RoboMemory seeks to overcome these challenges by providing a comprehensive memory system that integrates different memory modalities, supports dynamic spatial awareness, facilitates efficient knowledge accumulation, and enables agents to learn and adapt from their experiences in real-time, physical settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboMemory adopts a brain-inspired hierarchical agent system operating on a "Perception – Memory – Retrieval – Planning – Execution" loop. This architecture maps neural components to agent modules, designed to enable long-term planning and interactive learning.

**A. Information Preprocessor (Thalamus-inspired):**
At each time step, this module processes multimodal sensory inputs (RGB frames or video clips) into textual representations. It operates two Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in parallel:
- **Step Summarizer (S):** Converts the observation ($O_t$) into a concise textual description ($s_t$) of the executed action, which is stored in working memory.
- **Query Generator (Q):** Generates a list of natural language queries ($q_t$) from the same observation ($O_t$), used to retrieve relevant information from the memory system.

**B. Comprehensive Embodied Memory (Hippocampus-inspired):**
This system unifies four distinct memory modules, all updated and retrieved in parallel to enhance efficiency. Functionally, these modules mirror the hierarchy of human memory:
- **Sensory Memory:** Implicitly represented by the real-time textual summaries ($s_t$) generated by the Step Summarizer.
- **Temporal Memory (Short-term):** Stores sequential interaction summaries ($s_t$) in a FIFO (First-In, First-Out) buffer. When capacity is reached, older entries are compressed into a single summarized entry using a VLM, maintaining context without unbounded growth. All existing memory in text is provided for retrieval.
- **Spatial Memory (Short-term):** A dynamic Knowledge Graph (KG) that records high-level spatial relationships between objects and positions. Objects and locations are vertices, and spatial relations are edges. This KG focuses on semantically meaningful relations (e.g., "cup on table"). To handle dynamic environments, a retrieval-driven, incremental update algorithm is introduced. This algorithm involves:
 1. Retrieving the most relevant sub-KG around new observations.
 2. Injecting new relations extracted from the current observation via a VLM-based Relation Retriever.
 3. Detecting and resolving conflicts between new and existing relations using a VLM-based resolver.
 4. Merging changes back into the main KG and pruning isolated vertices.
 This localized update mechanism ensures efficiency, with provable guarantees bounding the number of processed vertices per update (O($D^K$), where D is max degree, K is hop distance).
- **Semantic Memory (Long-term):** Stores time-independent facts, task-relevant experiences, and environmental knowledge. It updates when new, meaningful information arises (e.g., after subtask completion). Implemented as a vector database (DB) where memory items (natural language descriptions) are converted into semantic vectors. A VLM-based Long-term Memory Creator generates new items, and a VLM-based updater compares them with top-S similar existing items to decide on addition, update, removal, or no operation.
- **Episodic Memory (Long-term):** Records task-specific execution trajectories, including action sequences and environmental feedback. It provides "autobiographical" records of past attempts, guiding future task completion by leveraging successful trajectories. It shares the same VLM-driven vector DB storage and update mechanism as Semantic Memory.

**C. Closed-Loop Planning Module (Prefrontal Cortex-inspired):**
This module uses a Planner-Critic mechanism, powered by the same VLM with different prompts.
- **Planner (P):** Generates a multi-step, long-term action plan based on retrieved memory ($r_t$), current observation ($O_t$), and task description ($T$).
- **Critic (C):** Before executing each subsequent action (i.e., not the first step), the Critic evaluates its appropriateness given the latest environment state and memory. If the Critic rejects an action, the Planner triggers a re-planning phase. A modification is introduced to always execute the first step of a plan to prevent infinite replanning loops.

**D. Low-Level Executor (Cerebellum-inspired):**
This module translates high-level textual action instructions from the Closed-Loop Planning Module into concrete robot movements. It utilizes a LoRA-finetuned VLA model ($\pi_0$) for manipulation actions and a SLAM-based navigation model for locomotion. In simulated environments (EB-ALFRED), the provided high-level action APIs are used instead of the VLA-based executor.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmarks:** EmbodiedBench (EB-ALFRED and EB-Habitat, Base and Long subsets) for task planning, comprising 200 complex embodied tasks. A real-world kitchen environment for evaluating interactive environmental learning.
- **Baselines:** Single VLM agents (closed-source: GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet; open-source: Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Ins, Llama-3.2-90B-Vision-Ins) and VLM-Agent frameworks (Reflexion, Voyager, Cradle, RoboOS).
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR) and Goal Condition Success Rate (GC).
- **Core VLM:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Ins for RoboMemory's internal VLM components; Qwen3-Embedding for RAG embeddings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate RoboMemory's capabilities across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

**A. Performance on EmbodiedBench:**
RoboMemory, instantiated with Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Ins, achieved an average Success Rate (SR) of 70.5% and a Goal Condition Success Rate (GC) of 79.7% across the EB-ALFRED and EB-Habitat benchmarks.
- **Comparison with Single VLM-Agents:** RoboMemory outperformed state-of-the-art closed-source VLM-Agents, specifically Claude-3.5-Sonnet (69.5% SR, 71.8% GC), by 1% in SR and 7.9% in GC. This indicates that an agent framework with an open-source model can surpass closed-source models when augmented with a robust memory system.
- **Comparison with VLM-Agent Frameworks:** RoboMemory showed substantial improvements over other VLM-Agent frameworks, such as Voyager (46.5% SR), Reflexion (38.3% SR), Cradle (44.5% SR), and RoboOS (25.5% SR, when using the same Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Ins backbone). This performance gain is attributed to RoboMemory's brain-like memory system, which provides accurate and persistent contextual information, and its Planner-Critic mechanism for closed-loop, adaptive planning.

**B. Efficiency Analysis:**
The Comprehensive Embodied Memory module, with its parallel update and retrieval strategy, demonstrated significant efficiency gains. When tested on long-horizon tasks, the wall-clock time for updating multiple memory modules in parallel was comparable to updating a single base memory module (Temporal Memory). This validates the effectiveness of the parallelization architecture. Furthermore, the dynamic Spatial Knowledge Graph (KG) update algorithm showed that as the KG grows, the proportion of relationships retrieved for update at each iteration decreases (e.g., from 76% initially to 28% at iteration 20), indicating efficient localization of modifications.

**C. Ablation Studies (on EB-ALFRED):**
Systematic removal of RoboMemory's components revealed their contributions to overall performance:
- **Long-term Memory (Semantic + Episodic):** Removing these modules resulted in a significant drop in SR (from 67% to 57%), highlighting their role in interactive environmental learning, skill property acquisition, and avoiding ineffective task attempts.
- **Spatial Memory:** Performance dropped substantially without spatial memory (from 67% to 47% SR), underscoring its criticality for spatial reasoning in partially observable environments.
- **Critic Module:** The absence of the critic module led to a decrease in SR (from 67% to 55%), demonstrating its importance for closed-loop planning, adapting to dynamic environments, and improving failure recovery.

**D. Real-World Robot Deployment:**
RoboMemory was deployed in a physical kitchen environment to evaluate its interactive environmental learning capability. Tasks were executed sequentially without memory resets between attempts.
- **Cumulative Learning:** The success rates for second attempts were significantly higher than first attempts (e.g., RoboMemory improved from 26.67% SR on the first attempt to 46.67% SR on the second attempt), confirming that RoboMemory's long-term memory effectively guides subsequent tasks by learning from environmental familiarization and past experiences.
- **Key Capabilities:** Observations highlighted closed-loop error recovery (retrying failed actions), spatial reasoning (remembering object locations and relationships), and interactive environmental learning (analyzing failure causes to inform future decisions).
- **Real-world Challenges:** A noted performance degradation compared to simulated environments was primarily attributed to limitations of the low-level VLA executor, including unreliable instruction following and inadequate video understanding for dynamic state changes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboMemory represents a contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by addressing several fundamental challenges in developing autonomous, learning agents.

**A. Brain-Inspired Architecture for Generalizable Agents:** By integrating a unified, brain-inspired multi-memory system (Spatial, Temporal, Episodic, and Semantic memory) within a parallelized architecture, RoboMemory provides a novel framework for efficient long-horizon planning and interactive learning. This architecture offers a blueprint for creating more generalizable and robust embodied agents, drawing insights from cognitive neuroscience to inform robotic system design.

**B. Scalable and Dynamic Spatial Reasoning:** The introduction of a retrieval-driven incremental update algorithm for Spatial Knowledge Graphs (KGs) is significant. This approach transforms KGs from static representations into dynamic, scalable modalities for spatial memory in real-time embodied interaction. By localizing updates and resolving conflicts efficiently, it overcomes previous scalability bottlenecks, enabling agents to maintain a consistent and up-to-date worldview in complex, dynamic environments.

**C. Demonstrating Interactive Environmental Learning in Physical Systems:** RoboMemory's successful deployment in real-world environments, showing improved performance over repeated tasks without memory resets, highlights its capacity for cumulative, interactive learning. This capability is a step towards practical long-term autonomous learning in physical scenarios, allowing robots to adapt and refine their behaviors through continuous experience accumulation.

**D. Advancing Performance Benchmarks:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like EmbodiedBench, surpassing both advanced closed-source VLMs and other VLM-agent frameworks, validates the effectiveness of RoboMemory's architectural design and its memory mechanisms. This performance indicates that a well-structured memory and planning framework can leverage open-source models to compete with or exceed the capabilities of larger, proprietary models.

**E. Foundation for Future Research:** RoboMemory provides a robust foundation for future work in embodied AI. Its modular design and emphasis on memory-driven learning offer avenues for refining reasoning capabilities, enhancing execution robustness (particularly with low-level executors), and exploring more sophisticated interactions between different memory modalities. This work contributes to bridging the gap between theoretical cognitive models and practical robotic autonomy, fostering the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
