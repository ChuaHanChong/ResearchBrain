# Rearrangement: A Challenge for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers defined 'Rearrangement' as a canonical task for Embodied AI, requiring agents to modify an environment from an initial state to a specified goal using realistic sensory input and physical interaction. They introduced a detailed framework for task definition, goal specification, embodiment characterization, and evaluation, alongside five experimental testbeds to foster end-to-end research.

## Problem

- Embodied AI research lacked a unifying task definition beyond navigation, hindering coordinated knowledge accumulation and objective comparison of diverse approaches.
- Existing evaluation protocols often focused on narrower tasks or specific algorithmic approaches, failing to assess end-to-end capabilities from raw sensory input to actuation.
- Developing and evaluating embodied agents for strong generalization to unseen environments and novel objects under realistic perceptual and physical constraints remained a challenge.

## Method

- The paper formally defined **Rearrangement** as an ==end-to-end task== for embodied AI, using ==POMDP== notation, where agents transform object configurations based on sensory observations and goal specifications.
- It proposed a taxonomy of five ==goal specification mechanisms== (Geometric, Image, Language, Experience, Predicate) and characterized agent embodiment and task complexity across a spectrum of realism.
- A standardized ==evaluation protocol== was introduced, featuring a primary task completion metric based on ==binary predicate tests== and secondary efficiency metrics, supported by the release of five diverse experimental testbeds.

## Results

- A comprehensive and generalized framework for the 'Rearrangement' task was established, formally defining its scope and requirements for embodied agents.
- Five distinct mechanisms for specifying rearrangement goals were detailed, accommodating various research paradigms and levels of human-agent interaction.
- **Five experimental testbeds**, spanning different simulation environments (SAPIEN, RLBench, AI2-THOR, Habitat) and varying complexities, were released to provide immediate platforms for research and benchmarking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The rearrangement task inherently integrates a broad array of AI capabilities—perception, navigation, manipulation, memory, planning—making it a suitable challenge for holistic embodied agent development.
> - Standardizing a complex, application-grounded task with clear definitions and evaluation can significantly accelerate progress in embodied interaction and manipulation.
> - Emphasizing realistic sensor inputs, physical dynamics, and strong generalization to novel environments is crucial for developing robust agents whose performance in simulation translates effectively to real-world applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied AI focuses on developing intelligent systems equipped with physical or virtual embodiments that can perceive and interact with their environments. Recent advancements in this field have primarily centered on navigation tasks within previously unseen environments, benefiting from dedicated software platforms and standardized experimental protocols. This progress in navigation research has been attributed to robust infrastructure and established methodologies.

However, a significant frontier in Embodied AI involves tasks requiring active interaction and contact between an agent and its environment, where the agent modifies the environment to achieve its goals. While several software platforms support such interaction scenarios by simulating realistic perception and physical dynamics, the field has lacked a unifying task definition. A common task framework, with clear definitions and evaluation metrics, has been instrumental in driving progress in fields like computer vision (e.g., image classification, object detection) and natural language processing (e.g., language modeling, machine translation). These standard tasks foster the development and validation of foundational techniques, architectures, and serve as sources of pretrained models transferable to other applications.

Existing work in robotics and AI has explored aspects related to "rearrangement planning," focusing on transforming an initial object configuration to a goal configuration. This body of work, spanning task planning (e.g., STRIPS, PDDL) and motion planning, often assumes idealized perception (e.g., provided object poses). While valuable, these methods are not always easily comparable due to differing input/output assumptions. Prior standardized evaluation protocols in Embodied AI often focus on narrower tasks like grasping or navigation, or specific algorithmic approaches like reinforcement learning, rather than broad, end-to-end capabilities.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing "rearrangement" as a canonical, general, and application-grounded task for Embodied AI. It emphasizes end-to-end evaluation, encompassing the full system from raw sensory input to actuation, thereby integrating perception, planning, and control in a unified framework. This approach aims to align research efforts, accelerate cumulative knowledge, and enable comprehensive assessment of embodied intelligence across diverse algorithmic paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to define and promote "Rearrangement" as a canonical task for research and evaluation in Embodied AI. The motivations for this proposal are multifaceted:

- **Standardization and Alignment of Research:** The authors observe that the lack of a clear, standard task definition has hindered coordinated knowledge accumulation across different research groups in Embodied AI, unlike in other AI fields. By establishing rearrangement as a common task, the paper aims to focus the development of new techniques and facilitate fair comparison of diverse approaches.
- **Comprehensive Skill Integration:** Rearrangement inherently demands a wide array of capabilities from an embodied intelligent system. Successful execution can require:
 - **Perception:** Recognizing object states, estimating forces, and predicting action effects.
 - **Inference:** Inferring differences between current and goal states.
 - **Manipulation:** Interacting with objects, including in cluttered environments, and understanding preconditions/postconditions of actions.
 - **Planning:** Sequencing actions to achieve subgoals and the overall objective.
 - **Navigation:** Traversing complex environments while maintaining an internal representation.
 The task thus serves as a framework that subsumes and integrates component skills like navigation and manipulation into a broader roadmap towards embodied intelligence.
- **Practical Relevance:** The rearrangement task naturally unifies numerous real-world scenarios of practical interest, such as setting a table, cleaning a room, loading a dishwasher, or picking and placing items in a fulfillment center. This direct applicability is intended to drive research towards solutions with tangible utility.
- **Emphasis on Realistic Conditions:** The proposal prioritizes developing systems that operate under realistic constraints. This includes:
 - **Perceptual Input:** Acting based on noisy and incomplete onboard perception from realistic sensors, rather than idealized maps or abstract representations.
 - **Physical Dynamics:** Moving objects via forces, typically through contact, in physically simulated or real-world environments.
 - **Strong Generalization:** Evaluating agents in previously unseen environments and with novel objects, without access to privileged information (e.g., 3D models, ground-truth poses) encountered during training.
- **Catalyzing Infrastructure Development:** By defining a clear task and evaluation protocol, the authors anticipate stimulating the release of new datasets and the development of new simulation platforms to support training and deployment of rearrangement agents on physical systems.
- **Foundational for Transfer Learning:** Similar to how tasks like image classification have provided pretrained backbones for computer vision, rearrangement is envisioned to serve as a source of trained models that can be transferred or adapted to other embodied tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a formal definition of the rearrangement task, characterization of its complexity, specification of agent embodiments and sensory input, and a detailed evaluation protocol, complemented by the release of experimental testbeds.

#### 3.1. Task Definition

Rearrangement is formally defined using the notation of Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs). The world state space ($S$) is restricted to configurations of rigid bodies and kinematic trees thereof (e.g., robots, objects). The task involves transforming an environment from an initial starting state ($s_0$) to a desired goal state ($s^* \in S^*$), where $S^*$ is a set of acceptable goal states. Crucially, the agent operates solely based on sensory observations ($o$) and a goal specification ($g$), reflecting the partial observability inherent in Embodied AI.

The paper outlines five mechanisms for **Goal Specification**:
- **GeometricGoal:** Specifies target object poses (e.g., coordinates, bounding box transformations, full rigid-body poses). Suitable for single or multi-object rearrangement, focusing on precise placement.
- **ImageGoal:** Provides a visual rendering (e.g., 3rd-person image or video) of the desired goal state. It can be intuitive for humans but may be underspecified for certain object configurations or occluded items.
- **LanguageGoal:** Uses natural language descriptions (e.g., "move the chair to the right of the sofa"). Language is often underspecified, requiring robust interpretation and careful evaluation.
- **ExperienceGoal:** The agent is immersed in the goal state ($s^*$) to build its own representation of what to achieve. This is akin to "make it like it was before," suitable for scenarios where the robot has prior experience of the desired state.
- **PredicateGoal:** Defines the goal as a set of binary predicates (e.g., `on(plate1, table1)`) that must be satisfied. This provides a precise, programmatically evaluable specification that can serve as a common substrate for other goal types.

#### 3.2. Embodiment: Actuators and Sensors

The framework acknowledges a spectrum of choices for agent embodiment, ranging from abstract to realistic, while recommending the most realistic options where possible. Clear descriptions of the chosen embodiment are emphasized for reproducibility and fair comparison.

**Actuation (Manipulation Abstraction):**
- **Magic Pointer Abstraction:** Agent navigates and controls a virtual "mouse pointer" or raycast. A "pick" action grabs the closest object intersecting the ray, allowing it to be held, rotated, and placed. This allows basic physical interaction but abstracts away complex grasping dynamics.
- **Kinematic Articulated Arm with Abstracted Grasping:** The agent controls a realistic robotic arm (e.g., Cartesian end-effector or joint space control), but grasping is simplified. Any object near the end-effector is "grasped" automatically upon command, bypassing intricate contact force dynamics.
- **Full Physical Simulation:** Provides the highest fidelity, requiring the agent to control a robotic arm to physically grasp objects using accurately simulated contact forces. Actions map directly to joint actuation commands, necessitating continuous feedback control and robust physical interaction.

**Sensors (Perception Abstraction):**
- **Ground Truth Positions Abstraction:** Provides direct access to object positions and orientations, possibly with realistic noise. This simplifies the problem to focus on planning and control in isolation.
- **Intermediate Representations:** Assumes mid-level visual processing, providing segmented images and/or depth maps. This abstracts away low-level pixel processing and lighting variations but requires noise simulation in the intermediate data.
- **Full Simulated Perception:** Employs realistic simulations of onboard sensors (e.g., RGB cameras, depth sensors, LiDAR, contact sensors, microphones) with realistic noise, uncertainty, and imperfect calibration. This is recommended as the default for high-fidelity real-world deployment simulation.

#### 3.3. Task Characterization and Generalization

The paper characterizes rearrangement task complexity along several dimensions:
- **Agent-centric:** Agent mobility (fixed, wheeled, bipedal), manipulation capabilities (e.g., magic pointer, parallel-jaw gripper, humanoid hand), and sensory suite (e.g., color vision, depth, tactile, LiDAR).
- **Environment-centric:** Environment interactability (rigid, articulated, containers), complexity (number of objects, clutter, occlusion, ordering importance), and dynamicity (changes independent of agent actions, unrecoverable states).

A **Task Generalization Spectrum** is defined, ranging from "weak generalization" (known objects in known environments, novel arrangements) to "strong generalization" (new objects in new environments). Strong generalization is recommended as the default mode to foster robust, generalizable agents.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol

The evaluation protocol proposes a primary metric and several secondary metrics.

**Primary Metric: Task Completion**
- Measured as the percentage of binary target state tests successfully passed for "objects of interest," without causing harm to other parts of the scene.
- **Object Placement Accuracy:** Evaluated via a norm ($N(s_i, s^*_i)$) comparing an object's final pose to its target pose. Norms can focus on translation, combine translation and rotation, or use 3D Intersection-over-Union (IoU).
- **Binary Thresholding:** Individual object distances are converted into binary success/failure based on task-specific thresholds, then aggregated to a percentage. This accounts for heterogeneous tolerances.
- **"Do No Harm" Test:** An agent must pass an additional binary predicate test, defined by the task designer, to achieve a non-zero completion score. This typically checks that non-task objects have not been moved or broken.
- **General Predicate Evaluation:** Completion can be generalized to a set of logical predicate tests ($P_j(s, s^*)$) that capture relationships (e.g., relative poses, logical states) between multiple objects.
- **Evaluation Programs:** Task completion should be automatically computed by an evaluation program that compares final and target states. These programs should be clearly defined and public, potentially using a domain-specific language.
- **Natural Language Predicates:** Acknowledges the challenge of automatically evaluating free-form natural language goals, potentially requiring trained neural network classifiers.

**Secondary Metrics (Efficiency)**
- **Simulation Time:** Total simulated time or number of simulation "ticks" taken.
- **Simulated Energy:** Virtual physical work done by the agent, integrating forces and movements.
- **Computational Measures:** FLOPS, memory usage per "tick" or episode, and the agent's real-time factor relative to the simulator. These metrics are crucial for assessing practical deployability.

#### 3.5. Experimental Testbeds

To facilitate immediate research, the paper releases five suites of rearrangement scenarios across four existing simulation environments:
- **T1: Bimanual Sweeping (SAPIEN):** Two fixed-base robot arms sweep small objects into a bin.
- **T2: Table Organization (SAPIEN & Real robots):** A fixed-base robot arm rearranges tabletop objects into a specific configuration, with a companion real-robot challenge.
- **T3: Storing Groceries (RLBench):** A fixed robotic arm picks up scattered grocery items and places them into a constrained shelf space, requiring planning for order and placement.
- **T4: Room Rearrangement (AI2-THOR):** A mobile agent rearranges household objects and changes their states (e.g., opening/closing cabinets) in a room, using a "magic pointer" manipulator and an "ExperienceGoal" specification.
- **T5: House Cleanup (Habitat):** A mobile agent with a "magic pointer" manipulator navigates a house to find scattered objects and moves them to specified goal locations, involving longer-range navigation.

These testbeds span a spectrum of environment complexities and agent capabilities (navigation, manipulation type, perception).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's primary contribution is the establishment and detailed articulation of the "Rearrangement" task as a foundational challenge for Embodied AI. It does not present experimental results from agents solving this task but rather defines the framework *for* such experimentation. The main findings and results are thus the proposed conceptual and practical infrastructure:

- **Formal Task Definition:** A concrete and generalized definition of the rearrangement task is provided, leveraging POMDP notation and focusing on rigid and articulated piecewise-rigid objects, with clear boundaries on its scope (excluding non-rigid dynamics or within-object transformations for initial focus).
- **Comprehensive Goal Specification Taxonomy:** Five distinct mechanisms for specifying rearrangement goals (Geometric, Image, Language, Experience, Predicate) are identified and elaborated, accommodating diverse research needs and human-agent interaction paradigms.
- **Characterization of Embodiment and Task Complexity:** The paper systematically maps the spectrum of actuation and sensing realism, from abstract to full physical simulation, and delineates key dimensions of task complexity (agent mobility, manipulation, sensory suite; environment interactability, complexity, dynamicity). This provides a taxonomy for researchers to define and compare tasks.
- **Standardized Evaluation Protocol:** A primary task completion metric, based on binary predicate tests for object poses and a "do no harm" condition, is proposed. This, along with recommended secondary efficiency metrics (simulation time, energy, computation), aims to enable objective, comparable, and comprehensive evaluation of embodied agents.
- **Identification of Core AI Abilities:** The rearrangement task is explicitly shown to exercise and integrate a broad array of AI capabilities, including perception, navigation, manipulation, memory, planning, and communication. This highlights its potential to drive holistic advancements in Embodied AI.
- **Release of Experimental Testbeds:** Five concrete rearrangement scenarios, implemented across four distinct simulation environments (SAPIEN, RLBench, AI2-THOR, Habitat), are released. These testbeds provide immediate, diverse platforms for researchers to begin developing and evaluating rearrangement agents, spanning varied complexities and agent capabilities.

In essence, the paper's findings are the framework itself: a clear, detailed, and actionable proposal for a canonical Embodied AI task, coupled with practical resources to initiate research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed rearrangement framework holds significant potential for advancing the field of Embodied AI and related disciplines:

- **Acceleration of Embodied AI Research:** By providing a clear, unifying, and challenging task, the framework is expected to focus and accelerate research in embodied interaction and manipulation, moving beyond predominantly navigation-centric studies. This standardization can foster coordinated efforts and enable cumulative progress across research groups.
- **Holistic Development of Intelligent Agents:** The comprehensive nature of rearrangement, demanding integration of perception, navigation, manipulation, memory, and planning, encourages the development of more sophisticated, general-purpose embodied agents rather than specialized solutions for isolated sub-problems.
- **Enabling Fair Comparisons and Benchmarking:** The detailed task definition, characterization of complexity, and proposed evaluation metrics provide a robust foundation for objectively comparing diverse algorithmic approaches (e.g., modular vs. end-to-end learning systems). The released testbeds offer concrete environments for such benchmarking.
- **Bridging Simulation and Reality:** The emphasis on realistic sensor inputs, physical dynamics simulation, and strong generalization to novel environments is designed to mitigate the "sim-to-real gap," making research progress in simulation more directly transferable and predictive of performance on physical robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Complex Tasks:** Rearrangement is positioned as a stepping stone towards even more complex real-world tasks. The paper explicitly outlines promising future extensions, such as handling deformable objects, transforming object states (e.g., cooking), multi-agent coordination, and intuitive human-robot interaction, demonstrating the extensibility of the framework.
- **Stimulating Cross-Disciplinary Research:** The task naturally draws upon insights from computer vision, robotics, machine learning, and natural language processing, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration and innovation, particularly in areas like grounding language in perception and action.
- **Practical Applications:** The direct relevance of rearrangement to numerous household, industrial, and service robotics applications means that advancements in this area can lead to tangible benefits in assistive robotics, automation, and intelligent environments.
