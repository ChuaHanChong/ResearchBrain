# Finite-Sample Analysis of Policy Evaluation for Robust Average Reward Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides the first finite-sample analysis for policy evaluation in robust average-reward reinforcement learning, establishing order-optimal O~(ϵ−2)O(ε^{-2})O~(ϵ−2) sample complexity guarantees. It achieves this by proving a contraction property for the robust average-reward Bellman operator under a novel semi-norm and by introducing a truncated Multi-Level Monte Carlo estimator for efficient estimation of worst-case transitions.

## Problem

- Real-world deployment of RL agents faces the 'simulation-to-reality gap' and unmodeled uncertainties, necessitating robust policies for guaranteed performance.
- Prior work in robust average-reward RL primarily offered asymptotic convergence guarantees, lacking explicit finite-time performance bounds or sample complexity analyses.
- The robust average-reward Bellman operator typically lacks a contraction property under standard norms, hindering the application of stable iterative methods for finite-sample analysis.

## Method

- The robust average-reward ==Bellman operator== is shown to be a contraction with respect to a specifically constructed semi-norm, enabling the use of stochastic approximation.
- A biased stochastic approximation framework is developed that explicitly accounts for small, persistent biases in the robust Bellman operator estimators.
- A truncated Multi-Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimator is introduced for Total Variation and Wasserstein uncertainty sets, ensuring finite expected sample complexity for worst-case transition estimation.

## Results

- The robust value function and robust average reward are estimated to ϵ-accuracy with an order-optimal sample complexity of O~(ϵ−2)O(ε^{-2})O~(ϵ−2).
- The truncated MLMC estimator for support functions demonstrates finite expected sample complexity (O(Nmax)O(N_{max})O(Nmax)), exponentially decaying bias (O(2−Nmax/2)O(2^{-N_{max}/2})O(2−Nmax/2)), and linear variance (O(Nmax)O(N_{max})O(Nmax)).
- The biased stochastic approximation framework achieves convergence rates with an O(1/T)O(1/T)O(1/T) term for variance and an O(ϵbiaslog⁡T)O(\epsilon_{bias} \log T)O(ϵbiaslogT) term for bias in the expected squared error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The robust average-reward Bellman operator, despite lacking a discount factor, can exhibit a contraction property under a carefully designed semi-norm.
> - Stochastic approximation algorithms can converge with provable rates even when faced with bounded, persistent noise bias terms.
> - Truncating the geometric sampling in MLMC estimators provides finite expected sample complexity while maintaining an exponentially decaying bias.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated success across various domains, including robotics, finance, healthcare, transportation, and large language models. A common challenge in deploying RL agents in real-world scenarios is the discrepancy between simulated training environments and actual operational conditions, often referred to as the "simulation-to-reality gap." This gap can lead to performance degradation when learned policies encounter unmodeled uncertainties. Robust Reinforcement Learning (Robust RL) addresses this by formulating learning as an optimization problem over an uncertainty set of transition probabilities, aiming to guarantee reliable performance under worst-case conditions.

RL problems are typically studied under two time horizon formulations: discounted-reward and average-reward. While the discounted-reward setting is prevalent, it can lead to myopic policies in applications requiring sustained long-term efficiency, such as queuing systems or inventory management. The average-reward setting, which focuses on optimizing long-term performance, is more suitable for such applications.

Prior research in Robust RL, particularly for the average-reward criterion, has predominantly offered asymptotic convergence guarantees. These approaches often rely on techniques like ordinary differential equation (ODE) analysis or martingale theory to establish almost sure convergence, but they do not provide explicit finite-time performance bounds or sample complexity guarantees. This contrasts with robust discounted-reward RL, where significant progress has been made in establishing finite-sample guarantees by leveraging the contraction property of the robust Bellman operator under a discount factor. However, this property is absent in the average-reward setting, making direct extension of these methods problematic.

This paper addresses a fundamental gap in the robust average-reward RL literature by providing the first finite-sample analysis for policy evaluation. Specifically, it focuses on evaluating the robust value function and estimating the robust average reward for a given policy using data sampled from a nominal simulator. The work aims to bridge the theoretical divide between asymptotic and finite-sample guarantees, thereby enabling the development and analysis of robust average-reward RL algorithms with concrete performance assurances.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to present the first finite-sample analysis of policy evaluation in robust average-reward Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). This objective is motivated by several challenges and theoretical gaps:

1. **Addressing the Simulation-to-Reality Gap and Real-World Uncertainty:** In critical real-world applications such as robotic control, autonomous driving, and healthcare, agents trained in simulators must perform reliably in complex and uncertain physical environments. Unmodeled uncertainties or mismatches between simulation and reality can lead to catastrophic failures. Robust RL directly addresses this by optimizing policies against worst-case environmental dynamics, ensuring a certain level of performance guarantees under these uncertainties. This motivates the need for robust policy evaluation methods to quantify the performance of such policies.

2. **Importance of Average-Reward Criterion for Long-Term Performance:** While discounted rewards are common, they can lead to short-sighted behaviors, as future rewards are exponentially devalued. In contrast, the average-reward criterion is crucial for applications where sustained long-term operational efficiency is paramount, such as resource management, network control, and manufacturing systems. Policy evaluation in this setting needs to reflect this long-term perspective accurately.

3. **Lack of Finite-Sample Guarantees in Robust Average-Reward RL:** Prior work in robust average-reward RL has largely been limited to asymptotic convergence results. These guarantees indicate that an algorithm will eventually converge to the optimal solution given infinite data, but they provide no insight into how much data or computation is required to achieve a certain level of accuracy within a finite timeframe. This absence of finite-sample analysis hinders practical deployment, algorithm design, and theoretical understanding of learning efficiency.

4. **Challenges in Policy Evaluation for Robust Average-Reward MDPs:**
 - **Worst-Case Optimization:** Unlike standard policy evaluation, robust policy evaluation requires computing value functions and average rewards under the worst-case transition probabilities chosen by an adversary from an uncertainty set. This necessitates inferring adversarial dynamics using only samples from a nominal (non-adversarial) model, adding a layer of complexity.
 - **Absence of Contraction Property:** A fundamental difficulty lies in the robust average-reward Bellman operator. In discounted RL, the discount factor naturally induces a contraction property under standard norms (e.g., sup-norm), which facilitates stable iterative methods and their finite-sample analysis. However, in the average-reward setting, the absence of a discount factor means the Bellman operator does not inherently possess this contraction property with respect to any standard norm, making conventional fixed-point iteration and error bounding techniques inapplicable.
 - **Efficient Estimation of Support Functions:** For certain types of uncertainty sets (e.g., Total Variation and Wasserstein distance), computing the worst-case transition effect involves evaluating a support function, which can be computationally intensive and requires sampling. Existing Multi-Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) based estimators for these support functions often suffer from infinite expected sample complexity due to the nature of their underlying geometric sampling schemes, which limits their practical utility and theoretical analysis to asymptotic regimes.

The key objective is to overcome these challenges by developing a theoretical framework and algorithmic techniques that yield explicit finite-sample complexity guarantees for robust average-reward policy evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research develops a novel theoretical and algorithmic framework to provide finite-sample analysis for policy evaluation in robust average-reward MDPs. The methodology addresses the core challenges by introducing a carefully constructed semi-norm, a biased stochastic approximation framework, and an efficient estimator for worst-case transitions.

#### 3.1. Semi-Norm Contraction of Robust Bellman Operators

The central theoretical challenge is the lack of a contraction property for the robust average-reward Bellman operator. The authors address this by demonstrating that the operator *is* a contraction under a specifically designed semi-norm, denoted as $\lVert \cdot \rVert_{\mathcal{P}}$.
- **Motivation from Non-Robust Case:** The paper first demonstrates this concept for the non-robust average-reward Bellman operator. Under the assumption that the Markov chain induced by the policy and nominal transition is irreducible and aperiodic, a semi-norm $\lVert \cdot \rVert_{P}$ is constructed. This semi-norm's kernel (the set of vectors for which its value is zero) is precisely the space of constant vectors, which is relevant because average-reward value functions are unique only up to an additive constant. The construction involves leveraging the property that the fluctuation matrix (transition matrix minus its stationary projector) has all eigenvalues strictly inside the unit circle, allowing for the construction of a norm under which it is a contraction. A small "quotient" correction term is added to ensure the semi-norm vanishes only on constant functions while preserving the one-step shrinkage.
- **Extension to Robust Case:** For the robust setting, the paper extends this idea. Under the nominal model's ergodicity (Assumption 3.1) and compactness of the uncertainty set $\mathcal{P}$, with specific restrictions on the radius of the uncertainty sets, the robust Bellman operator $T_g$ is shown to be a contraction. The construction of $\lVert \cdot \rVert_{\mathcal{P}}$ for the robust case generalizes the non-robust version by considering a family of fluctuation matrices for all possible worst-case transitions within the uncertainty set. The "worst-case gain" of these linear mappings over any number of steps is shown to be strictly below 1, enabling the construction of an "extremal norm" (defined as the supremum of scaled norms over all possible products of fluctuation matrices from the uncertainty set). This extremal norm uniformly contracts all non-constant components. Similar to the non-robust case, a small quotient correction is added to account for constant shifts, resulting in a semi-norm under which $T_g$ is a strict contraction.
- **Radius Restrictions:** The paper details the necessary radius restrictions for three types of uncertainty sets:
 - **Contamination Uncertainty Set:** If the nominal model is irreducible and aperiodic, the joint spectral radius remains strictly less than 1 without additional radius restrictions (for $\delta < 1$).
 - **Total Variation (TV) Uncertainty Set:** A specific condition on the radius $\delta$ (e.g., $\delta < b_0/m$, where $b_0$ is a minimum entry of an $m$-step nominal matrix) is required to ensure that all models within the uncertainty set remain ergodic and the joint spectral radius is less than 1.
 - **Wasserstein Distance Uncertainty Set:** Similarly, a radius restriction related to the minimum distance between states and the mixing time is necessary to ensure the contraction property (e.g., $\delta < \delta_{min} b_0/m$).

#### 3.2. Biased Stochastic Approximation Framework

The derived contraction property enables the application of stochastic approximation (SA) techniques. The paper develops a novel SA framework tailored for the robust average-reward setting.
- **Handling Biased Noise:** A key aspect is that the estimators for the robust Bellman operator (specifically, the support functions) can introduce a small, persistent bias, unlike traditional unbiased SA settings. The framework explicitly accounts for this "biased noise."
- **Convergence Analysis:** The authors prove the convergence of this biased SA under semi-norm contraction, demonstrating that if the noise terms have bounded variance and a small, bounded bias, the iterates will converge to a solution of the Bellman equation. The analysis, building on Lyapunov function approaches, shows that the bias term results in an additional error term that depends logarithmically on the total number of iterations $T$.

#### 3.3. Efficient Estimators for Uncertainty Sets

Accurately and efficiently estimating the robust Bellman operator, particularly its support function component $\sigma_{\mathcal{P}_a^s}(V)$, is critical.
- **Contamination Uncertainty Set:** For this linear model, a simple, unbiased estimator is used, requiring only one sample per state-action pair ($s' \sim \tilde{P}_a^s$). The estimator is defined as $(1 - \delta)V(s') + \delta \min_x V(x)$, which is proven to be unbiased and have bounded variance.
- **Total Variation and Wasserstein Distance Uncertainty Sets:** These uncertainty sets involve non-linear support functions, posing a greater challenge. Previous Multi-Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimators suffered from infinite expected sample complexity due to their reliance on unbounded geometric distributions for sampling levels.
 - **Truncated MLMC (Algorithm 1):** The paper introduces a modified MLMC approach with a *truncation mechanism* based on a *truncated geometric distribution* with a parameter of 0.5. This ensures a finite expected sample complexity.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of sampling from an infinite geometric distribution, the level $N'$ is set to $\min\{N, N_{max}\}$, where $N \sim \text{Geom}(0.5)$. This choice of parameter (0.5) is critical as it leads to a linear growth of expected sample complexity with $N_{max}$ (i.e., $O(N_{max})$), rather than an exponential one.
 - **Bias and Variance Control:** The truncation introduces a small bias, but the algorithm incorporates a correction term $\Delta_{N'}(V)$ to manage this. The bias is shown to decay exponentially with $N_{max}$, while the variance grows linearly with $N_{max}$.

#### 3.4. Robust Average-Reward TD Learning (Algorithm 2)

The overall policy evaluation algorithm is a two-phase robust Temporal Difference (TD) learning method.
- **Phase 1: Robust Value Function Estimation:**
 - Iteratively updates the robust value function $V_t(s)$ using the biased stochastic approximation.
 - For each state-action pair $(s,a)$, the estimated Bellman operator $\hat{T}_{g_0}(V_t)(s)$ is computed. This involves using the appropriate support function estimator $\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}_a^s}(V_t)$ (either the direct one for contamination or the truncated MLMC for TV/Wasserstein).
 - A decreasing learning rate $\eta_t$ is employed.
 - To resolve the additive constant ambiguity inherent in average-reward value functions, $V_{t+1}$ is centered by setting $V_{t+1}(s_0) = 0$ for a chosen anchor state $s_0$.
- **Phase 2: Robust Average Reward Estimation:**
 - After $V_T$ is obtained from Phase 1, the algorithm estimates the robust average reward $g_t$.
 - The expected Bellman residual $\hat{\delta}_t(s)$ is computed for all states using $V_T$ and averaged across states to get $\bar{\delta}_t$.
 - A separate stochastic approximation update $g_{t+1} \leftarrow g_t + \beta_t(\bar{\delta}_t - g_t)$ with learning rate $\beta_t$ refines the average reward estimate.

The total sample complexity is analyzed by considering the sum of samples needed for all state-action pairs over all iterations, scaled by the expected samples per $\hat{\sigma}$ estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings that establish finite-sample guarantees for policy evaluation in robust average-reward MDPs.

1. **Contraction Property of Robust Bellman Operator (Theorem 4.2):**
 - A foundational theoretical result is the proof that the robust average-reward Bellman operator $T_g$ is a contraction.
 - This contraction is established with respect to a carefully constructed semi-norm $\lVert \cdot \rVert_{\mathcal{P}}$, whose kernel is the set of constant functions.
 - This property holds under the ergodicity assumption of the nominal model (Assumption 3.1) and certain radius restrictions for the uncertainty sets (contamination, total variation, Wasserstein distance), which ensure the joint spectral radius of the family of fluctuation matrices is less than 1.
 - This finding is crucial as it overcomes the long-standing challenge of applying standard fixed-point analysis techniques to robust average-reward problems, enabling the use of stochastic approximation for finite-time analysis.

2. **Convergence of Biased Stochastic Approximation (Theorem B.1):**
 - The paper provides a theoretical result for the convergence rate of stochastic approximation algorithms in the presence of biased noise.
 - Under semi-norm contraction, bounded noise variance, and bounded noise bias ($\epsilon_{bias}$), the expected squared error $\mathbb{E}[\lVert x_T - x^* \rVert_P^2]$ is shown to decay as $O(1/T)$ (for the variance term) and $O(\epsilon_{bias} \log T)$ (for the bias term) when using a step size of $O(1/t)$. This result generalizes prior analyses that typically assume unbiased noise.

3. **Properties of Truncated Multi-Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) Estimator (Algorithm 1):**
 - For nonlinear uncertainty sets (Total Variation and Wasserstein distance), the proposed truncated MLMC estimator addresses the problem of infinite expected sample complexity found in previous MLMC approaches.
 - **Finite Expected Sample Complexity (Theorem 5.1):** By employing a truncated geometric sampling scheme with parameter $\Psi = 0.5$, the expected number of samples required for each support function estimation is shown to be finite and grows linearly with the truncation level $N_{max}$, specifically $E[M] = O(N_{max})$.
 - **Exponentially Decaying Bias (Theorem 5.2):** The bias of the truncated MLMC estimator decays exponentially with the truncation level $N_{max}$, i.e., $|\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma} - \sigma]| \leq O(2^{-N_{max}/2})$. This ensures that the truncation does not introduce a significant, persistent error.
 - **Linear Variance (Theorem 5.4):** The variance of the truncated MLMC estimator is shown to grow linearly with $N_{max}$, i.e., $\text{Var}(\hat{\sigma}) = O(N_{max})$. This demonstrates that the estimator remains stable as the truncation level increases.

4. **Order-Optimal Sample Complexity for Robust Policy Evaluation (Algorithm 2):**
 - The paper presents the first non-asymptotic sample complexity guarantees for robust policy evaluation in average-reward MDPs.
 - **Robust Value Function Estimation (Theorem 6.1):** Algorithm 2 achieves an order-optimal $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ sample complexity for estimating the robust value function $V_T$ to $\epsilon$-accuracy in the $\ell_\infty$ norm. The $\tilde{O}$ notation hides logarithmic factors related to problem parameters and $1/\epsilon$.
 - **Robust Average Reward Estimation (Theorem 6.2):** The algorithm also achieves an order-optimal $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ sample complexity for estimating the robust average reward $g_T$ to $\epsilon$-accuracy.
 - The sample complexity bounds depend on factors like state space size ($S$), action space size ($A$), mixing time ($t_{mix}$), and the contraction factor ($1/(1-\gamma)^2$). For TV and Wasserstein uncertainty sets, the complexity also includes factors related to $N_{max}$, which is chosen to balance bias and variance terms to achieve the overall $\epsilon$-accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the theoretical understanding and practical applicability of robust reinforcement learning, particularly for problems involving long-term performance optimization.

1. **Foundation for Finite-Sample Theory in Robust Average-Reward RL:** The most significant impact is bridging the gap between asymptotic and finite-sample analysis in robust average-reward MDPs. By providing the first explicit finite-time convergence rates and sample complexity guarantees, this work lays a theoretical foundation comparable to that which exists for robust discounted RL. This enables rigorous analysis, algorithm design, and performance benchmarking for a class of problems previously lacking such guarantees.

2. **Algorithmic Innovations for Policy Evaluation:** The development of a contraction-inducing semi-norm for the robust average-reward Bellman operator is a key theoretical innovation. This property is crucial for the application of stable iterative methods like stochastic approximation. Furthermore, the introduction of the truncated Multi-Level Monte Carlo estimator with finite expected sample complexity, exponentially decaying bias, and linear variance addresses a practical bottleneck in estimating worst-case transitions, making such estimations feasible for deployment.

3. **Enhanced Reliability and Generalization in Real-World Applications:** Robust average-reward RL is critical for real-world systems (e.g., robotics, autonomous control, industrial process control, queueing systems) where long-term operational efficiency and robustness to environmental uncertainties are paramount. The ability to evaluate policies with finite-time guarantees enhances the confidence in deploying learned policies, as their worst-case performance can be quantified before deployment. This directly contributes to safer and more reliable AI systems.

4. **Enabling Robust Policy Optimization:** Policy evaluation is a fundamental subroutine in policy optimization algorithms. By establishing finite-sample guarantees for policy evaluation, this work paves the way for the development of robust average-reward policy *optimization* algorithms (e.g., policy gradient or actor-critic methods) that can also come with finite-time performance guarantees, a frontier largely unexplored in this setting.

5. **New Perspectives on Stochastic Approximation:** The analysis of stochastic approximation in the presence of bounded, but non-zero, bias, and its application in a semi-norm setting, contributes to the broader field of stochastic optimization. The methods for managing bias propagation and showing logarithmic dependence on iteration count are valuable.

While the work advances the field, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the requirement for ergodicity of the nominal model and the open problem of scaling these results via function approximation to larger state spaces. Nevertheless, this paper provides crucial theoretical groundwork and practical tools that significantly advance the state-of-the-art in robust average-reward reinforcement learning.
