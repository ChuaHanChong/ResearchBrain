# Finite-Sample Analysis of Policy Evaluation for Robust Average Reward Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides the first finite-sample analysis for policy evaluation in robust average-reward reinforcement learning, establishing order-optimal O~(ϵ−2)\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})O~(ϵ−2) sample complexity guarantees. It achieves this by proving a contraction property for the robust average-reward Bellman operator under a novel semi-norm and by introducing a truncated Multi-Level Monte Carlo estimator for efficient estimation of worst-case transitions.

## Problem

- Real-world deployment of RL agents faces the 'simulation-to-reality gap' and unmodeled uncertainties, necessitating robust policies for guaranteed performance.
- Prior work in robust average-reward RL primarily offered asymptotic convergence guarantees, lacking explicit finite-time performance bounds or sample complexity analyses.
- The robust average-reward Bellman operator typically lacks a contraction property under standard norms, hindering the application of stable iterative methods for finite-sample analysis.

## Method

- The robust average-reward ==Bellman operator== is shown to be a contraction with respect to a specifically constructed semi-norm, enabling the use of stochastic approximation.
- A biased stochastic approximation framework is developed that explicitly accounts for small, persistent biases in the robust Bellman operator estimators.
- A truncated Multi-Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimator is introduced for Total Variation and Wasserstein uncertainty sets, ensuring finite expected sample complexity for worst-case transition estimation.

## Results

- The robust value function and robust average reward are estimated to ϵ\epsilonϵ-accuracy with an order-optimal sample complexity of O~(ϵ−2)\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})O~(ϵ−2).
- The truncated MLMC estimator for support functions demonstrates finite expected sample complexity (O(Nmax)O(N_{max})O(Nmax​)), exponentially decaying bias (O(2−Nmax/2)O(2^{-N_{max}/2})O(2−Nmax​/2)), and linear variance (O(Nmax)O(N_{max})O(Nmax​)).
- The biased stochastic approximation framework achieves convergence rates with an O(1/T)O(1/T)O(1/T) term for variance and an O(ϵbiaslog⁡T)O(\epsilon_{bias} \log T)O(ϵbias​logT) term for bias in the expected squared error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The robust average-reward Bellman operator, despite lacking a discount factor, can exhibit a contraction property under a carefully designed semi-norm.
> - Stochastic approximation algorithms can converge with provable rates even when faced with bounded, persistent noise bias terms.
> - Truncating the geometric sampling in MLMC estimators provides finite expected sample complexity while maintaining an exponentially decaying bias.
