# RLVR-World: Training World Models with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Tsinghua University Machine Learning Group developed RLVR-World, a framework that fine-tunes pre-trained language and video world models using reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. This approach directly optimizes for task-specific prediction metrics, leading to improved accuracy by up to 44.8% in text game state prediction, a 30.3% F1 score increase for web page state prediction, and a 9.2% LPIPS improvement in robot video prediction, all while significantly reducing training steps and mitigating generative artifacts like repetition.

## Problem

- Traditional Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) for world models often leads to a misalignment between training objectives and desired task-specific prediction goals.
- MLE can result in artifacts like blurry video predictions or repetition in generated sequences and does not directly optimize for metrics like accuracy or perceptual quality.
- A unified, efficient post-training method for diverse world models (language, video) to directly improve their utility for downstream tasks was lacking.

## Method

- A unified framework transforms language and video world modeling into an ==autoregressive token sequence generation== task using decoder-only Transformers, initially pre-trained with MLE.
- ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== is applied post-training, where the model generates multiple candidate predictions, and modality-specific decoders convert them back to the original form.
- Rule-based, verifiable rewards are computed by directly comparing predictions against ground truth, and ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== is used to fine-tune the model without a separate value function.

## Results

- For language world models, RLVR-World improved text game state prediction accuracy by up to **44.8%** and web page state prediction F1-score by **30.3%** over supervised fine-tuning baselines, also boosting web agent success rates by **18.4%**.
- Video world models showed substantial gains on robot manipulation tasks, including a **9.2%** relative LPIPS improvement and a **26.1%** relative MSE improvement for multi-step prediction.
- RLVR fine-tuning achieved these performance boosts with only a few **hundred gradient steps**, drastically more efficiently than the hundreds of thousands of steps for MLE pre-training, and reduced video repetition rates from **48.6%** to **9.9%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) provides a highly efficient and effective post-training paradigm to align world models directly with task-specific metrics across diverse modalities.
> - Formulating world modeling tasks as a unified token-based autoregressive sequence generation problem enables the broad application of RLVR to both language and video.
> - Direct optimization with verifiable rewards can significantly reduce common generative model artifacts, such as repetition, and improve overall prediction quality with minimal training steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of world modeling, reinforcement learning, and generative artificial intelligence, addressing a fundamental challenge in training advanced predictive models.

**World Models:** World models, which learn to predict future states of an environment given current states and actions, are a cornerstone of model-based reinforcement learning (RL) and intelligent agent design. They enable agents to plan, evaluate policies, and reason about the consequences of their actions in a simulated future. Historically, world models have been developed across diverse modalities, from structured text environments in games to complex visual and sensory data in robotics and autonomous driving. Recent advancements have seen the integration of powerful generative architectures, such as Transformers and diffusion models, to capture the intricate dynamics of these environments.

**Challenges with Traditional Training:** A common practice for training these expressive world models, especially large-scale ones, is Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE). While effective for learning complex data distributions, MLE-based objectives (e.g., next-token prediction, variational lower bounds) often suffer from a fundamental misalignment with the true, task-specific goals of a world model. For instance, MLE can lead to issues like repetition and hallucination in language models, or blurry predictions in video models, even if the model captures the overall data distribution well. Furthermore, these surrogate objectives do not directly optimize for the actual metrics by which world models are evaluated, such as prediction accuracy for discrete states or perceptual quality for visual outputs.

**Emergence of RL for Generative Models:** In parallel, reinforcement learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for fine-tuning pre-trained generative models to better align with specific objectives or human preferences. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has famously improved the helpfulness and safety of Large Language Models (LLMs). More recently, Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has gained traction, particularly in domains like mathematical reasoning and code generation. Unlike RLHF, which relies on learned reward models that can be susceptible to overoptimization, RLVR uses faithful, rule-based reward functions that are directly computed from verifiable metrics.

**RLVR-World's Contribution:** "RLVR-World" bridges these two landscapes by applying the RLVR paradigm directly to world models. It proposes that task-specific prediction metrics (e.g., accuracy, F1-score, LPIPS) can serve as verifiable rewards, allowing for direct optimization of world models without relying on surrogate MLE objectives or potentially unstable learned reward models. This work extends the proven utility of RLVR beyond LLM reasoning tasks to a broader class of generative models—world models—across diverse modalities, specifically language and video. By doing so, it offers a novel post-training paradigm that aims to enhance the *utility* and *task-alignment* of world models, promising more accurate, reliable, and perceptually superior predictions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the RLVR-World framework can be summarized as follows:

1. **Address Misalignment between Training Objectives and Task Goals:** The core motivation is to overcome the inherent mismatch between standard world model training objectives (like MLE) and their ultimate task-specific goals (e.g., high prediction accuracy, superior perceptual quality). MLE, while effective for learning data distributions, often falls short in directly optimizing for these downstream metrics, leading to suboptimal performance or artifacts like blurry video predictions and repetitive text.

2. **Directly Optimize Task-Specific Prediction Metrics:** The authors aim to develop a method that allows world models to be directly optimized for quantifiable, verifiable prediction metrics. This ensures that the models are explicitly tuned for what truly matters in their application, rather than indirectly via a proxy objective.

3. **Unify World Modeling across Diverse Modalities:** A significant objective is to establish a unified framework that can apply reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards to world models operating across different data types, specifically language and video. This involves formulating world modeling as a general autoregressive sequence generation task, abstracting away modality-specific complexities through tokenization.

4. **Leverage the Power of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Motivated by the success of RLVR in improving LLM performance on complex, verifiable tasks (like math and code), the authors seek to explore if this paradigm can similarly enhance the utility of generative models more broadly, starting with world models. The appeal of RLVR lies in its use of rule-based, faithful reward functions, avoiding the complexities and potential instability of learned reward models used in RLHF.

5. **Improve Efficiency and Reduce Training Artifacts:** The research aims to demonstrate that RLVR can achieve significant performance gains with substantially fewer training steps compared to conventional MLE, leading to more efficient fine-tuning. Additionally, a key motivation is to mitigate common generative model artifacts, such as repetition in sequence generation, by directly optimizing against metrics that penalize such issues.

6. **Enhance Downstream Applications:** Ultimately, the motivation extends to improving the performance of intelligent agents that rely on world models for planning and control. By developing more accurate and reliable world models, the authors anticipate improvements in model-predictive control, policy evaluation, and other model-based RL applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RLVR-World framework proposes a unified approach to train world models across diverse modalities by leveraging reinforcement learning with verifiable, task-aligned rewards.

#### 3.1. Unified World Models as Sequence Modeling

The core methodological innovation lies in unifying world modeling across language and video modalities under a general autoregressive sequence generation framework.
- **Tokenization:** States and actions, regardless of their original modality, are converted into sequences of discrete tokens.
 - **Language:** Standard text tokenization (e.g., BPE) is used for verbal state representations (e.g., JSON objects) and actions.
 - **Video:** Learned visual tokenizers (variants of VQGAN or iVideoGPT's compressive tokenizer) discretize image and video frames into sequences of visual tokens. Low-dimensional continuous actions (e.g., robot joint angles) are quantized into uniform bins and represented as tokens.
- **Autoregressive Generation:** A decoder-only Transformer model is employed to predict the next state's token sequence, conditioned on the current state and action token sequences. This mirrors the successful architecture of large language models, allowing the world model to learn the probabilistic state transition function $p(s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t)$ (or higher-order Markov processes).
- **Pre-training with MLE:** Before RLVR, the world models are pre-trained using standard Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), minimizing the negative log-likelihood of predicting the next tokens in the sequence. This initial phase provides a strong generative foundation.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

After pre-training, the world model undergoes a post-training phase using RLVR to directly optimize for task-specific prediction metrics.
- **Sampling Predictions:** Given a current state-action input, the pre-trained world model (as the "behavior policy") generates a *group* of diverse candidate next-state predictions.
- **Modality-Specific Decoding:** These tokenized predictions are then decoded back into their original modality:
 - **Language:** Rule-based extractors interpret the generated JSON-like token sequences into structured state changes.
 - **Video:** A visual decoder reconstructs the predicted video frames from the visual tokens.
- **Verifiable Reward Computation:** A crucial step is the computation of a verifiable reward function $R_i$ for each generated prediction $\hat{s}'_i$ by directly comparing it against the ground-truth next state $s'$. This reward is *rule-based* and *task-aligned*, explicitly measuring the desired quality of the prediction.
 - **Language World Models:** Rewards can be binary (1 for exact match, 0 otherwise) or task-specific (e.g., F1-score for item changes in web pages, or a weighted sum of accuracy components for text game state prediction).
 - **Video World Models:** Rewards are based on established visual quality metrics, such as negative L1 distance, negative LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity), or a combination thereof, where lower values indicate better perceptual quality. The sign is adjusted so that higher reward corresponds to better predictions.
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm (GRPO):** The paper employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to fine-tune the world model. GRPO, an alternative to PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization), simplifies the training process by eliminating the need for a separate value function. It estimates advantages by normalizing rewards within a group of sampled responses, promoting diversity and enhancing exploration. The GRPO objective includes a clipped term to stabilize updates and a KL divergence penalty to keep the fine-tuned policy close to a reference policy (e.g., the SFT-tuned model), preventing divergence.

#### 3.3. Downstream Application Evaluation

The utility of the RLVR-trained world models is further demonstrated in practical downstream applications:
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** For web agents, the world model predicts the outcome of candidate actions proposed by a policy model. A value model then scores these predicted outcomes, and the action leading to the highest-scoring future state is executed.
- **Real2Sim Policy Evaluation:** For robotic manipulation, the RLVR-trained video world model serves as a simulator. Policies are rolled out in this neural simulator from real initial observations, and the simulated trajectories are evaluated (e.g., by human annotators) to assess policy success without real-world deployment.

In essence, the RLVR-World framework systematically transforms diverse world modeling tasks into a unified token-based autoregressive prediction problem, then applies a powerful and verifiable reinforcement learning paradigm to directly align the model's outputs with the true metrics of prediction quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RLVR-World framework demonstrates substantial performance gains across both language- and video-based world models, highlighting its effectiveness and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Language World Models

- **Text Game State Prediction:** On the ByteSized32-State-Prediction dataset, RLVR-World significantly improved accuracy. For a 1.5B parameter base model, it achieved a remarkable +34.7% accuracy for "unchanged" cases and +8.9% for "changed" cases over supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with a minimalist binary reward. Using a tailored, task-specific reward further boosted accuracy to +44.8% (unchanged) and +9.6% (changed). Scaling to a 7B model, RLVR-World's overall performance surpassed GPT-4, demonstrating its ability to enhance LLMs for world modeling tasks involving verbal state transitions.
- **Web Page State Prediction:** For web navigation, RLVR-World achieved substantial gains in predicting web page state changes. It improved the F1 score by +30.3% over the SFT baseline for the 1.5B model, with precision increasing by +48.5% and recall by +15.1%. This indicates that RLVR effectively fine-tunes LLMs to extract and predict precise item changes within accessibility trees, a more challenging task than generating natural language descriptions.
- **Application to Web Agents (Model Predictive Control):** The enhanced language world models translated directly into improved performance for web agents. Agents equipped with RLVR-trained world models showed an +18.4% relative improvement in success rate on the WebArena benchmark compared to those using SFT-trained models, demonstrating practical utility in downstream tasks.

#### 4.2. Video World Models

- **Robot Manipulation Trajectory Prediction (RT-1 Dataset):** RLVR-World achieved significant improvements across all visual metrics for single-step and multi-step video prediction on the RT-1 dataset. For multi-step prediction, it showed a +26.1% relative improvement in MSE, +4.5% in PSNR, +1.9% in SSIM, and a notable +9.2% relative improvement in LPIPS over the base model.
- **Training Efficiency:** A key finding is the extreme efficiency of RLVR fine-tuning. These substantial gains were achieved with merely a *few hundred* RLVR gradient steps, in stark contrast to the *hundreds of thousands* of steps required for MLE pre-training to reach comparable (though still inferior) performance. This highlights RLVR's power as a post-training optimization paradigm.
- **Mitigation of Repetition Artifacts:** The paper shows that RLVR effectively mitigates the common issue of repetition in multi-step video generation, reducing the repetition rate from 48.6% in the base model to 9.9%. This directly addresses a known limitation of likelihood-based objectives.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art:** On other robotic manipulation datasets (PushT, Rope, Granular), RLVR-World demonstrated performance comparable to, and in some cases surpassing (e.g., on the challenging Granular dataset), advanced world models including recurrent latent space models and diffusion models.
- **Metric-Oriented Optimization:** Experiments confirmed that models fine-tuned with a specific metric (e.g., LPIPS) as a reward generally performed best when evaluated on that same metric, providing strong evidence for the direct alignment capability of RLVR.
- **Application to Real2Sim Policy Evaluation:** RLVR-trained video world models proved more effective as real-world simulators for policy evaluation. They yielded smaller discrepancies between real-world and simulated policy success rates compared to both the base world model and handcrafted SIMPLER simulators, bridging the sim-to-real gap more effectively.

Overall, the main findings consistently demonstrate that RLVR-World is a powerful and efficient post-training paradigm that significantly boosts the accuracy, perceptual quality, and utility of world models across diverse modalities by directly optimizing for verifiable, task-aligned metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLVR-World framework presents a significant contribution to the field of generative models and reinforcement learning, with broad implications for future research and practical applications.

**1. General Post-training Paradigm for Generative Models:** The most far-reaching significance is the proof-of-concept that RLVR can serve as a *general post-training paradigm* for enhancing the utility of generative models beyond just reasoning-focused LLMs. By demonstrating its effectiveness on both language and video world models, the work suggests that any generative model whose outputs can be objectively and verifiably evaluated against a ground truth can benefit from this direct optimization approach. This could inspire applications in areas like image generation (optimizing for compressibility or specific feature presence), audio synthesis, or other multimodal generative tasks.

**2. Elevating World Model Utility:** For world models specifically, RLVR-World addresses a fundamental limitation of MLE-based training: the misalignment between training objectives and real-world task metrics. By directly optimizing for prediction accuracy, perceptual quality, and other verifiable metrics, the framework enables world models to become significantly more reliable and useful. This enhanced utility directly benefits model-based reinforcement learning, planning, and agentic AI, which rely heavily on accurate environmental predictions.

**3. Efficient Fine-tuning:** The remarkable efficiency of RLVR—achieving substantial gains with hundreds of gradient steps compared to hundreds of thousands for MLE—is highly impactful. This efficiency makes it more feasible to fine-tune large, pre-trained world models for specific tasks or domains without extensive computational resources, accelerating research and deployment cycles.

**4. Mitigating Generative Model Artifacts:** The framework's ability to mitigate common issues like repetition in multi-step video generation demonstrates its power in producing higher-quality, more natural outputs. This aspect is crucial for building robust and trustworthy generative AI systems.

**5. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The successful application of RLVR-trained video world models for real-to-sim policy evaluation is particularly impactful for robotics. By creating more faithful neural simulators, the framework reduces the costly and time-consuming reliance on real-world robot experiments or labor-intensive physical simulator development. This could accelerate the development and deployment of robotic policies.

**6. Future Research Directions:** The paper opens several avenues for future exploration:
 - **Breaking Performance Barriers:** Investigating how to achieve continual improvements beyond the initial rapid convergence, perhaps by exploring architectural, data, or algorithmic advancements in RL.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** Studying whether RLVR can enhance world models' ability to generalize to novel situations or counterfactual actions, a critical aspect for robust decision-making.
 - **Foundation World Models:** Emphasizing the need for general-purpose, large-scale pre-trained world models. RLVR's full potential will likely be unleashed when applied as a fine-tuning stage atop such powerful foundation models, mirroring the success observed in LLMs.
 - **Broader Application Across Model Classes:** Extending RLVR to other generative architectures beyond autoregressive Transformers, such as diffusion models, as ongoing research is already exploring.
 - **Sophisticated Reward Design:** Incorporating more advanced, domain-specific reward functions that capture physical rules, temporal consistency, and nuanced user-intended qualities beyond simple pixel-level or token-level metrics.

In conclusion, RLVR-World offers a potent and practical method for elevating the capabilities of world models and, more broadly, generative AI. Its significance lies in its potential to make these models more aligned with their intended tasks, more efficient to deploy, and ultimately more impactful in driving progress across diverse fields from autonomous agents to robotics.
