# Decouple to Generalize: Context-First Self-Evolving Learning for Data-Scarce Vision-Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The DoGe framework from Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and collaborators introduces a context-first self-evolving learning approach that decouples cognitive processes for data-scarce vision-language reasoning. It achieves stable performance improvements of 5.7% for 3B-series models and 2.3% for 7B-series models across seven benchmarks, while enhancing policy exploration and mitigating reward hacking.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with data scarcity in specialized domains, making RL post-training prohibitively expensive due to high annotation costs.
- Existing data synthesis and self-rewarding methods for VLMs suffer from limited data distributions, poor alignment with reasoning objectives, and 'reward hacking' leading to policy entropy collapse.
- Current VLM supervision often overlooks rich contextual information in problem statements, reducing learning to superficial question-answering loops without fostering generalizable reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces ==DoGe (Decouple to Generalize)==, a dual-decoupling framework that separates the VLM's cognitive process into a ==Thinker== and a ==Solver== component.
- Employs a two-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training approach, where the Thinker is trained on question-masked context and the Solver evaluates its generated analysis.
- Implements an ==iterative curriculum data synthesis pipeline== to continuously generate diverse and challenging multimodal training data, adapting to the model's evolving capabilities.

## Results

- DoGe achieves stable self-evolution with an average performance increase of **5.7%** for 3B-series models and **2.3%** for 7B-series models across seven benchmarks after three iterations.
- The framework enhances general visual reasoning capabilities and reduces hallucination, demonstrating an average improvement of **2.0%** on the HallBench benchmark.
- DoGe effectively increases initial policy entropy and maintains higher entropy throughout training compared to naive GRPO, promoting better exploration and preventing policy entropy collapse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the cognitive process into a context-analyzing Thinker and a problem-solving Solver helps prevent reward hacking and fosters deeper, more generalizable reasoning.
> - Training the Thinker to first understand contextual information, rewarded by the Solver's performance on the original problem, guides the model toward high-level thinking patterns.
> - Continuously synthesizing challenging, domain-specific data, informed by both general knowledge and 'occasionally solvable' problems, enables stable self-evolution in data-scarce environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Decouple to Generalize: Context-First Self-Evolving Learning for Data-Scarce Vision-Language Reasoning" (DoGe) is situated at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas in artificial intelligence: Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and the development of self-evolving AI agents.

**Current State of VLMs and RL Post-training:** Recent years have witnessed remarkable progress in VLMs, which integrate visual and linguistic understanding to perform complex reasoning tasks. A significant advancement in enhancing these models has been the adoption of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for post-training. RL allows VLMs to generate more coherent and extended "chain-of-thought" responses, moving beyond simple perception to tackle multi-step problems. This approach is seen as a key enabler for "self-evolving Agents" – a paradigm shift from models reliant on human-annotated data to those that continuously learn and improve through experience.

**Challenges in Data-Scarce Specialized Domains:** While RL post-training holds immense promise, its efficacy is critically dependent on the availability of abundant, high-quality multimodal training data. This presents a major hurdle for specialized, high-value domains such as chemistry, earth sciences, and multimodal mathematics. In these fields, manual annotation of domain-specific multimodal knowledge and the creation of high-quality reasoning training data are prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This data scarcity severely limits the scalable application of RL post-training for VLM self-evolution in critical professional contexts.

**Limitations of Existing Solutions:** To circumvent data scarcity, existing strategies have explored synthetic data generation and self-rewarding mechanisms. However, these methods suffer from inherent limitations:
- **Limited Distributions:** Synthetic data often converges to narrow, repetitive distributions, failing to capture the rich diversity and complexity required for robust reasoning in specialized domains.
- **Alignment Difficulties:** Self-rewarding mechanisms, while effective for improving visual perception, often lack precise alignment with the nuanced reasoning objectives of specialized tasks.
- **Reward Hacking:** A critical problem identified is "reward hacking," where models learn to exploit superficial patterns to gain high rewards rather than developing genuine reasoning capabilities. This leads to policy entropy collapse, diminished exploration, and ultimately, a lack of generalizable performance. The paper highlights that current supervision paradigms, often based solely on "answering questions—receiving rewards," overlook the rich contextual information in problem statements, contributing to this issue.

**DoGe's Contribution:** DoGe directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel framework that fundamentally rethinks how VLMs learn in data-scarce environments. Instead of solely focusing on problem-solving outcomes, DoGe emphasizes a "context-first" learning approach. By decoupling the cognitive process and refining reward signals, it aims to foster deeper understanding and prevent the pitfalls of reward hacking, thereby enabling more stable and generalizable self-evolution for VLMs, especially in critically important specialized domains. This work represents a significant step towards realizing truly autonomous and adaptable AI agents capable of continuous learning and improvement in real-world, complex scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the DoGe framework are multi-faceted, stemming from the acknowledged limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms, particularly in specialized, data-scarce environments.

#### 2.1. Specific Objectives

1. **Enhance Generalization in Data-Scarce Domains:** The paramount objective is to enable VLMs to achieve remarkable reasoning capabilities and continuous self-evolution even when high-quality multimodal training data is scarce, especially in specialized domains like chemistry, earth sciences, and multimodal mathematics.
2. **Mitigate Reward Hacking and Policy Entropy Collapse:** DoGe aims to overcome the common pitfalls of RL post-training, specifically reward hacking, where models exploit shortcuts for high rewards, leading to a collapse in policy entropy and instability during training.
3. **Foster Deeper Contextual Understanding:** The framework seeks to guide models to first learn from the rich contextual information surrounding a problem, rather than immediately jumping to problem-solving. This "context-first" approach is intended to stimulate general reasoning thinking.
4. **Develop a Stable and Scalable Self-Evolving Learning Pathway:** DoGe endeavors to create a robust and scalable method for realizing self-evolving Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) that can continuously improve their performance over time.
5. **Align with Human Cognitive Processes:** The design of DoGe is inspired by human cognitive processes, aiming to establish an evolutionary cycle of "learning-application-internalized understanding," thereby providing insights for advanced, continuously self-learning agents.

**Motivation:**
The authors are primarily motivated by the high costs and impracticality of manually annotating vast amounts of domain-specific multimodal data required for effective RL post-training of VLMs in specialized fields. Existing methods, such as synthetic data and self-rewarding mechanisms, are insufficient because:
- They often generate data with limited or repetitive distributions, failing to capture the complexity of real-world reasoning.
- Their training objectives lack proper alignment with complex reasoning tasks, especially in nuanced domains.
- Critically, these methods often overlook the rich contextual information inherent in problem statements, reducing learning to a superficial "answering questions—receiving rewards" loop. This oversight prevents models from developing a generalizable understanding and makes them susceptible to reward hacking, where they learn to exploit high-reward patterns rather than engaging in genuine reasoning.

By addressing these fundamental issues, DoGe aims to unlock the full potential of RL for VLM self-evolution, making advanced AI reasoning capabilities accessible and sustainable even in the most challenging, data-limited specialized domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DoGe (Decouple to Generalize) proposes a dual-decoupling framework and a two-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training approach, complemented by an iterative curriculum data synthesis pipeline. It is built upon multimodal Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

#### 3.1. Dual-Decoupling of Cognitive Process (Thinker and Solver)

Inspired by human cognition, DoGe decouples the VLM's learning process into two distinct functional components, both derived from the same base VLM:

- **Thinker (π_T):** This component is responsible for performing comprehensive contextual analysis. Critically, the Thinker receives a *question-masked input* ($\tilde{x} = \text{Mask}(x, q)$), where the explicit question and direct answer cues are removed. Its task is to freely explore and generate a detailed textual analysis (`a`) of the context, representing a deep understanding of the problem scenario. The Thinker is learnable.
- **Solver (π_S):** This component is designed to solve the original problem. It receives the original context (`x`), the question (`q`), and crucially, the textual analysis (`a`) generated by the Thinker. The Solver's parameters are *frozen* during the first stage of training.

At the beginning of each training round, both Thinker and Solver are initialized from the parameters of the base model from the previous round.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Reinforcement Learning Framework

DoGe employs a two-stage RL framework guided by the GRPO algorithm, restructuring the traditional "problem-solving" loop. The general policy objective aims to maximize expected reward `J(θ) = E[(r(x, q, ŷ))]`.

- **Stage 1: Learning from Context (Thinker Training)**
 - **Objective:** The Thinker is trained to understand contextual information *without* explicitly solving the problem.
 - **Process:** For a given training sample `(x, q, y)`, a question-masked input `~x` is created. The Thinker `π_T` generates a set of candidate contextual analyses `a_k`.
 - **Reward Quantification:** A frozen copy of the Solver `π_S` assesses the practical usefulness of these analyses. The Solver attempts to solve the *original* problem `(x, q)` conditioned on each `a_k`. The reward for the Thinker, `r_context`, is computed based on the Solver's solution accuracy, with a bonus for correct output formatting. This creates a self-supervised feedback loop where the Thinker is rewarded for generating analyses that help the (frozen) Solver correctly answer the original question.
 - **Outcome:** This stage encourages the Thinker to develop a deep, general understanding of the knowledge domain and explore diverse reasoning paths, moving beyond superficial pattern matching. The updated Thinker `π_T^(t+1)` captures richer contextual reasoning patterns.

- **Stage 2: Learning from Application (Thinker as Evolved Model Training)**
 - **Objective:** The updated Thinker `π_T^(t+1)` applies its acquired high-level thinking to solve the original problem, thereby internalizing contextual knowledge into direct reasoning capabilities.
 - **Process:** During this phase, the Thinker is optimized on the original training problems `(x, q)`.
 - **Reward Quantification:** The reward, `r_app`, is assigned directly based on whether the final generated answer `ŷ` is correct, also with a format bonus.
 - **Outcome:** The updated parameters `π_T'^(t+1)` are then used to initialize the Solver for the *next* iteration (`π^(t+1)`), creating a continuous self-bootstrapping cycle where improved context understanding empowers progressively more generalizable reasoning.

#### 3.3. Iterative Curriculum Data Synthesis

DoGe implements a data synthesis pipeline to continuously generate diverse and challenging training data:

- **Multimodal Knowledge Pool:** Unlabeled raw multimodal data (textual and visual) is collected from the web and databases using tools (e.g., Geometry3k, PubChem API, NASA EPIC API). For "information-poor" samples, frontier VLMs are prompted to generate expert-level analysis reports to enrich their content. These processed, information-rich samples form the Multimodal Knowledge Pool.
- **Seed Problem Pool:** This pool stores high-quality, "occasionally solvable" problems (e.g., 0.1 ≤ pass rate ≤ 0.3) that are challenging but not impossible for the current model policy. It is initialized either from modified existing datasets (e.g., SMolInstruct for chemistry) or synthetic problems from the first round. After each iteration, the current policy model updates this pool by selecting problems meeting the "occasionally solvable" criterion.
- **Iterative Synthesis:** In each iteration, large models are prompted to synthesize challenging reasoning Question-Answer pairs by sampling from both the Multimodal Knowledge Pool (for new domain knowledge) and the Seed Problem Pool (for challenging variants). Overly simple problems are filtered out based on accuracy rates.

This pipeline ensures a continuous supply of diverse and appropriately challenging data, preventing the model from stagnating due to limited data distributions.

**Policy Optimization:** Both stages of RL are performed using the GRPO algorithm, which uses group relative advantages to replace the value model, reducing VRAM overhead. The loss function includes an importance-weighted clipped objective and a KL divergence term to maintain stability, with decoupled epsilon values for clipping to enhance exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate that DoGe consistently outperforms baseline methods, providing a stable and scalable pathway for self-evolving Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), particularly in data-scarce specialized domains.

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvement Across Benchmarks

- **Overall Gains:** DoGe enables VLMs to achieve stable self-evolution and performance improvement across all seven benchmark tests. The 3B-series models show an average performance increase of **5.7%**, while the 7B-series models achieve an average increase of **2.3%** over 7 benchmarks after three iterations.
- **Domain-Specific Reasoning:** DoGe demonstrates strong improvements in specialized domains. For instance, the 3B model shows significant gains in MSEarthMCQ (+7.5%) and ChemBench (+4.3%) compared to the base model.
- **General Visual Reasoning & Hallucination:** DoGe also enhances general visual reasoning capabilities and reduces hallucination. It achieves an average improvement of **2.0% on HallBench**, indicating that the "context-first" training effectively strengthens the model’s visual perception and reduces reliance on text priors, a common issue in VLMs.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** DoGe consistently shows superior performance compared to previous works like Visionary-3B and Vision-R1-7B across multiple benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Stability of Self-Evolving Learning

- **Robust Performance Growth:** Unlike naive GRPO algorithms, which exhibit significant fluctuations in performance during multiple iterations, DoGe consistently improves model performance over successive iterations (Table 1, Figure 1). This stability is a crucial advantage for realizing reliable self-evolving agents.
- **Mitigation of Reward Hacking:** The stability of DoGe is attributed to its ability to prevent reward hacking, even when faced with fluctuations in the quality of synthetic training data.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Policy Entropy

- **Enhanced Exploration:** DoGe effectively increases the initial policy entropy of subsequent reinforcement learning training and maintains a higher entropy value throughout the training process compared to directly applying naive GRPO (Figure 4).
- **Avoiding Entropy Collapse:** By encouraging the Thinker to deeply understand contextual information and pre-learn high-level thinking patterns, DoGe promotes greater exploration. This avoids the problem of excessively low entropy and reduced exploration capacity observed in baseline methods, which often struggle to learn generalizable reasoning paradigms.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on DoGe's Two-Stage Training

- **Superiority over Naive GRPO:** An ablation study comparing DoGe with naive GRPO (Table 2) further confirms the benefits of the decoupled two-stage training strategy. DoGe stably outperforms the baseline across various benchmarks, particularly in reasoning-intensive tasks like multimodal mathematics and general reasoning. The naive GRPO tends to show more unstable performance and even degradation in certain iterations, likely due to reward hacking and policy entropy collapse.

#### 4.5. Insights from Data Synthesis Analysis

- **Diverse Synthetic Data:** Visualization of synthetic training data (Figure 5) shows that while synthetic data shares similar contexts with manually annotated data, the model-synthesized data exhibits a wider range of "question surface designs." This implies that existing manually annotated datasets often have higher requirements for image diversity; otherwise, repetitive image-text questions can lead to models continuously outputting similar actions, which get rewarded, further reducing policy entropy and affecting generalization.
- **Underutilized Contextual Information:** The analysis suggests that there is often more unused information in the question context than captured by a single question, underscoring the value of DoGe's "context-first" approach to encourage deeper understanding.

In summary, DoGe successfully demonstrates that by decoupling cognitive processes, focusing on context understanding, and employing a two-stage RL with an iterative data synthesis pipeline, it can enable stable, generalizable, and self-evolving performance in VLMs, particularly addressing the critical challenge of data scarcity in specialized domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DoGe framework introduces a significant paradigm shift in how Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can achieve continuous self-evolution, particularly in data-scarce and specialized domains. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Enabling Robust AI in Data-Scarce Domains:** DoGe provides a critical solution for fields like chemistry, earth sciences, and multimodal mathematics, where acquiring abundant, high-quality annotated data is prohibitively expensive or practically impossible. By enabling VLMs to learn effectively from limited, unannotated raw data through context-first understanding and iterative self-improvement, DoGe can accelerate AI adoption and capability development in these high-value professional domains. This opens up opportunities for AI-driven discovery, analysis, and problem-solving that were previously hindered by data limitations.

2. **Mitigating Reward Hacking and Enhancing Generalization:** The core innovation of decoupling learning into Thinker and Solver components, with a focus on contextual understanding, directly addresses the pervasive problem of "reward hacking" in RL. By preventing models from exploiting superficial patterns and instead fostering genuine reasoning and deeper exploration (as evidenced by increased policy entropy), DoGe significantly improves the generalizability and robustness of VLMs. This means models are less likely to fail on out-of-distribution or novel problems, making them more trustworthy and reliable for real-world deployment.

3. **Advancing Self-Evolving AI Agents:** DoGe contributes directly to the realization of "self-evolving Agents" – intelligent systems that continuously learn and adapt without constant human intervention. By establishing a stable and effective iterative learning cycle ("learning-application-internalized understanding"), DoGe pushes the boundaries of autonomous AI. This has profound implications for developing more sophisticated agents capable of continuous skill acquisition, knowledge refinement, and adaptive behavior in complex, dynamic environments, from scientific research to interactive systems.

4. **Rethinking RL for VLMs:** The framework challenges the traditional "question-answering-reward" paradigm in multimodal RL, advocating for a more nuanced, human-inspired cognitive process. This "context-first" approach encourages broader, more flexible thinking, which can lead to new directions in RL algorithm design and reward function engineering for complex AI tasks.

5. **Scalable Data Synthesis:** The iterative curriculum data synthesis pipeline presented in DoGe offers a practical and scalable method for generating diverse and challenging training data. This reduces reliance on costly manual annotations and provides a dynamic way to tailor training data to the evolving capabilities of the model, ensuring sustained improvement.

6. **Improved Model Interpretability and Trust:** By encouraging models to first generate a "Deep-Thinking Report" (Thinker's analysis) before solving a problem, DoGe implicitly paves the way for potentially more interpretable AI systems. Understanding the model's contextual analysis could offer insights into its reasoning process, fostering greater trust and enabling better debugging or validation in critical applications.

In essence, DoGe offers a reliable and foundational approach for building more intelligent, adaptable, and generalizable VLMs that can learn effectively from experience, even when data is scarce. This represents a significant step towards the next generation of AI systems capable of continuous learning and advanced reasoning in highly specialized and complex domains.
