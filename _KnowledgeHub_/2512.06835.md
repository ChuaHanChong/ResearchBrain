# Decouple to Generalize: Context-First Self-Evolving Learning for Data-Scarce Vision-Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The DoGe framework from Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and collaborators introduces a context-first self-evolving learning approach that decouples cognitive processes for data-scarce vision-language reasoning. It achieves stable performance improvements of 5.7% for 3B-series models and 2.3% for 7B-series models across seven benchmarks, while enhancing policy exploration and mitigating reward hacking.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with data scarcity in specialized domains, making RL post-training prohibitively expensive due to high annotation costs.
- Existing data synthesis and self-rewarding methods for VLMs suffer from limited data distributions, poor alignment with reasoning objectives, and 'reward hacking' leading to policy entropy collapse.
- Current VLM supervision often overlooks rich contextual information in problem statements, reducing learning to superficial question-answering loops without fostering generalizable reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces ==DoGe (Decouple to Generalize)==, a dual-decoupling framework that separates the VLM's cognitive process into a ==Thinker== and a ==Solver== component.
- Employs a two-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training approach, where the Thinker is trained on question-masked context and the Solver evaluates its generated analysis.
- Implements an ==iterative curriculum data synthesis pipeline== to continuously generate diverse and challenging multimodal training data, adapting to the model's evolving capabilities.

## Results

- DoGe achieves stable self-evolution with an average performance increase of **5.7%** for 3B-series models and **2.3%** for 7B-series models across seven benchmarks after three iterations.
- The framework enhances general visual reasoning capabilities and reduces hallucination, demonstrating an average improvement of **2.0%** on the HallBench benchmark.
- DoGe effectively increases initial policy entropy and maintains higher entropy throughout training compared to naive GRPO, promoting better exploration and preventing policy entropy collapse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the cognitive process into a context-analyzing Thinker and a problem-solving Solver helps prevent reward hacking and fosters deeper, more generalizable reasoning.
> - Training the Thinker to first understand contextual information, rewarded by the Solver's performance on the original problem, guides the model toward high-level thinking patterns.
> - Continuously synthesizing challenging, domain-specific data, informed by both general knowledge and 'occasionally solvable' problems, enables stable self-evolution in data-scarce environments.
