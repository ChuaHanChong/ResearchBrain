# MS-MEM: Multi-Skill Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping via Uncertainty- and Disturbance-Aware Action Selection

> [!summary] Summary
> MS-MEM is an evidential framework for uncertainty-aware metric-semantic mapping of confined cluttered shelves. It jointly selects among active viewpoints, pushes, and grasps under one Disturbance- and Occlusion-aware Information Gain objective, using a full-evidential grasp model over affordance and orientation plus a collateral disturbance constraint. It reaches 0.899 occupancy IoU while cutting object displacement from 1.571 m to 0.707 m.

## Problem

- Prior manipulation-enhanced mapping restricts interaction to pushing alone, which needlessly rearranges objects, invalidates accumulated map evidence, and forgoes more selective occluder removal.
- Earlier interactive-perception work fixes the viewpoint during manipulation, so occluded regions stay unobservable and active view selection cannot refine beliefs across changing camera poses.
- Existing evidential grasp models capture only baseline directional uncertainty, leaving affordance confidence unmodeled and preventing consistent multi-view grasp fusion under partial observability.

## Method

- **Full-Evidential vMF-Contact** models grasp affordance as a ==Beta distribution== and both baseline and approach directions as ==von Mises-Fisher== distributions, with orthogonal projection enforcing the approach-perpendicular-to-baseline grasp constraint.
- **Full-Evidential Uncertainty-guided Multi-view Grasp Fusion** maintains a global grasp buffer, aggregating Beta evidence from newly matched candidates while decaying historical evidence, keeping grasp hypotheses consistent with the evolving belief.
- **Disturbance- and Occlusion-aware Information Gain** adds a ==Lagrangian-relaxed== **Collateral Disturbance Constraint** penalizing semantic class flips in confident voxels, making viewpoints, pushes, and grasps directly comparable under one criterion.

## Results

- In 25 simulated shelf scenes, combining push and grasp reached **0.905** occupancy and **0.791** semantic IoU without the constraint, versus **0.887/0.756** for push-only and **0.880/0.681** for grasp-only.
- Adding the disturbance constraint cut cumulative object displacement from **1.571 m** (push-only) to **0.707 m**, sacrificing only **0.024** semantic IoU relative to the unconstrained variant.
- Zero-shot real-world transfer on 5 scenes with 69 objects found **44** objects correctly versus **40** and **38** for baselines, reduced displacement **28.0%** versus push-only, and improved grasp success **30%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Placing heterogeneous perception and manipulation skills under one shared information-gain currency lets a planner sequence them synergistically rather than hand-coding when each primitive applies.
> - Penalizing changes to already-confident belief regions is a general way to keep interactive perception from destroying the evidence it has accumulated, mostly suppressing disruptive pushes.
> - Pushing actively manufactures grasp opportunities: the multi-skill agent executed more grasps than a grasp-only policy, so skill complementarity exceeds simply widening the action set.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Service robots operating in confined and cluttered environments, such as shelves, require robust scene understanding to perform tasks like object localization and retrieval. However, severe occlusions and restricted accessibility often limit the effectiveness of purely visual sensing. This challenge has led to the development of methods that couple perception with active manipulation to reveal occluded regions and reduce environmental uncertainty.

Earlier research in this domain often assumed fixed viewpoints during manipulation or focused on combining active vision with grasping in unrestricted cluttered scenes. More recent approaches, such as Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping (MEM), frame mapping as a sequential decision-making process under uncertainty, where active view selection is combined with non-prehensile interactions like pushing. MEM utilizes learned metric-semantic belief maps to guide action selection based on uncertainty reduction.

However, existing manipulation-enhanced mapping methods have primarily been limited to pushing as the sole manipulation primitive. This can be suboptimal because pushing may cause widespread disturbance, affecting already mapped regions and potentially being less efficient than more selective actions. Conversely, research in synergistic push-grasp manipulation in unconfined areas has demonstrated the complementary nature of these two primitives: pushing can create space and reveal occlusions, while grasping allows for the targeted removal of specific objects with less collateral disturbance.

This paper addresses the gap by integrating grasping into the manipulation-enhanced mapping framework, alongside pushing and active vision. It seeks to leverage the synergistic benefits of multiple manipulation skills within a unified decision-making process, explicitly accounting for both uncertainty reduction and the minimization of collateral scene disturbance. The work builds upon the foundation of MEM by extending its capabilities to a multi-skill context and by introducing a more comprehensive, uncertainty-aware representation for grasp planning. It also distinguishes itself by applying push-grasp synergy not just for goal-oriented object retrieval, but specifically for improving scene understanding and mapping accuracy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing Multi-Skill Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping (MS-MEM) are detailed below:

- **Addressing Limitations of Single-Skill Manipulation in Mapping:** Existing manipulation-enhanced mapping frameworks, such as MEM, are typically restricted to a single manipulation primitive, predominantly pushing. While pushing is effective for revealing occlusions and rearranging clutter, it can lead to unnecessary disturbance of the scene, invalidate previously acquired map information, and miss opportunities where a more precise action, like grasping, would be beneficial. The motivation is to overcome these limitations by introducing a broader range of interaction primitives.
- **Leveraging Push-Grasp Synergy for Enhanced Mapping:** The research is motivated by the recognized complementary roles of pushing and grasping in cluttered environments. Pushing can create free space, separate objects, and expose occluded regions, while grasping enables the selective removal of specific objects, potentially with less collateral impact on the surrounding environment. A key objective is to exploit this synergy to improve both the completeness and accuracy of scene maps by more effectively revealing and identifying hidden objects.
- **Developing an Uncertainty-Aware Grasp Representation:** Accurate grasp planning under partial observability is crucial for effective manipulation in cluttered spaces. The paper aims to develop a comprehensive evidential grasp representation that explicitly models both grasp affordance and orientation uncertainty. This representation, termed Full-Evidential vMF-Contact (FE-vMF), is intended to facilitate uncertainty-guided multi-view grasp fusion and allow for consistent grasp refinement as the robot acquires more observations from various viewpoints.
- **Establishing a Unified Action Selection Criterion:** To enable direct comparison and selection among diverse actions (active viewpoint changes, pushing, and grasping), a unified decision objective is required. The motivation is to create a common information gain criterion that can evaluate the expected utility of each action type in terms of uncertainty reduction.
- **Minimizing Collateral Scene Disturbance:** A significant motivation is to balance the need for information gain through manipulation with the desire to preserve the integrity of already mapped, confident regions of the scene. The paper aims to introduce a Collateral Disturbance Constraint (CDC) within the action selection framework to explicitly penalize actions that cause excessive and unnecessary changes to the scene. This is motivated by the need for robust and reliable mapping, where previously accumulated evidence should not be needlessly invalidated.
- **Enabling Autonomous and Adaptive Scene Exploration:** Ultimately, the objective is to create a closed-loop framework where a robot can autonomously select the most informative and least disruptive action from a repertoire of perception and manipulation skills. This adaptive decision-making process aims to lead to more geometrically complete and semantically accurate maps in complex, cluttered, and confined environments.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for service robots to reliably understand and map cluttered environments, and it aims to achieve this by integrating a multi-skill manipulation approach guided by uncertainty and disturbance awareness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MS-MEM is an evidential framework designed for uncertainty-aware mapping that integrates active viewpoint selection, object pushing, and grasping into a unified decision-making process. The system operates in a closed-loop, iteratively updating a shared evidential scene belief.

#### 3.1. Evidential Scene Belief Representation (MEM Foundation)

MS-MEM builds upon the Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping (MEM) framework, which models mapping as a sequential decision-making problem under uncertainty. The scene belief $\Phi_t = \{\Phi_O_t, \Phi_S_t\}$ at time *t* comprises a volumetric occupancy belief $\Phi_O_t$ and a 3D semantic belief $\Phi_S_t$ over known classes. Crucially, MEM uses an evidential representation $\lambda_t = \{\lambda_O_t, \lambda_S_t\}$, where Beta parameters $(\alpha, \beta)$ model occupancy probabilities and Dirichlet parameters $(\eta)$ model semantic probabilities for each map element. This evidential formulation allows for calibrated reasoning about uncertainty and occlusions. Belief updates after an action $a_t$ and observation $o_t$ are performed using learned Calibrated-Neural Accelerated Belief Updates (CNABU) networks, denoted as $\sigma_o$ for observation updates and $\sigma_p$ for push-specific updates.

#### 3.2. Multi-Skill Action Selection Pipeline

At each time step, MS-MEM evaluates candidate actions from three modalities:
1. **Occlusion-aware viewpoint selection:** Viewpoint candidates ($v_t$) from a predefined set $V$ are evaluated using Next-Best View (NBV) planning.
2. **Uncertainty-informed push selection:** Push actions ($p_t$) are generated to target occluded regions, following prior work.
3. **Uncertainty-informed grasp selection:** Grasp candidates ($g_t$) are predicted using a novel evidential grasp model.

All candidate actions are evaluated under a common objective, the Disturbance- and Occlusion-aware Information Gain (DOIG). The action maximizing DOIG is selected and executed, and the scene belief is updated accordingly. If a manipulation action is chosen, a subsequent NBV is executed to acquire an observation for belief update.

#### 3.3. Disturbance- and Occlusion-Aware Information Gain (DOIG)

The DOIG formulation is a shared decision metric designed to evaluate and compare all action modalities. It extends volumetric Information Gain (IG).

- **Occlusion-aware Information Gain (OIG):** For manipulation actions $a_t \in \{p_t, g_t\}$, OIG combines the expected IG from the best subsequent viewpoint $v_{t+1}$ given the predicted post-action belief $\tilde{\Phi}_{a_t}^{t+1}$, with a semantic entropy difference term $\Delta H(\Phi_t, \tilde{\Phi}_{a_t}^{t+1})$ that penalizes uncertainty introduced by scene changes. For pure viewpoint actions $a_t \in V$, OIG combines immediate IG with the best subsequent gain.
- **Collateral Disturbance Constraint (CDC):** To prevent unnecessary changes to confident regions, the OIG is augmented with a CDC. This constraint penalizes semantic changes in voxels identified as confident (semantic Dirichlet evidence $\chi_{S_{t+1}}(u) < \tau_\chi$) and whose estimated semantic class has changed ($\rho_{t+1,u} \neq \rho_{t,u}$). The number of such "disturbed confident voxels" $|U_{\text{diff}}|$ is constrained to be below a threshold $\Omega_c$. This constraint is incorporated into the DOIG objective via a Lagrangian relaxation with multiplier $\zeta_{CDC}$, where $DOIG(a_t) := OIG(a_t) - \zeta_{CDC}|U_{\text{diff}}(\Phi_t, \tilde{\Phi}_{a_t}^{t+1})|$. For grasp actions, the region of the grasped object is explicitly excluded from the CDC penalty, ensuring that intentional object removal is not penalized.

#### 3.4. Uncertainty-Informed Grasp Selection (UGS)

The UGS pipeline extends MEM with selective occluder removal:

##### 3.4.1. Full-Evidential Grasp Learning (FE-vMF)

This module predicts uncertainty-aware grasp hypotheses. FE-vMF extends prior work to a full-evidential representation of SE(3) grasp configurations, modeling both grasp affordance and orientation uncertainty.
- **Dual Orientational Uncertainty:** Unlike previous methods that modeled only the baseline direction uncertainty, FE-vMF models both the baseline ($b$) and an intermediate approach estimate ($\hat{a}$) as independent von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distributions. The final approach direction ($a$) is obtained by projecting $\hat{a}$ orthogonal to $b$, allowing for anisotropic orientation uncertainty.
- **Evidential Affordance Representation:** Grasp affordance ($q_c$) at a contact point $c$ is modeled by a Beta distribution $Beta(\alpha_c, \beta_c)$. The evidence parameters ($\alpha_c, \beta_c$) are derived from learned logits, allowing the model to express both the predictive affordance and associated epistemic uncertainty.
- **Learning:** FE-vMF is trained using point clouds extracted from the expected occupancy belief. A Point Transformer v3 backbone processes the point cloud, followed by an MLP that predicts the evidential grasp parameters. A composite evidential loss supervises orientation distributions and affordance evidence.

##### 3.4.2. Full-Evidential Uncertainty-guided Multi-view Grasp Fusion (FE-UMGF)

This temporal fusion mechanism refines grasp hypotheses over time by integrating evidential grasp predictions from multiple views. A global grasp buffer is maintained and updated. For a fused grasp, Beta evidence for affordance is aggregated from newly assigned candidates and decayed historically. Orientation uncertainty for both $b$ and $\hat{a}$ is fused independently within clusters.

##### 3.4.3. Final Grasp Selection

After fusion, each grasp hypothesis is converted into an executable grasp pose. A dedicated Grasp CNABU ($\sigma_g$) predicts the post-grasp evidential belief, which is then used to evaluate the grasp and its best subsequent viewpoint under the DOIG objective.

#### 3.5. Multi-Skill Action Selection (MSAS)

The MSAS module compares the optimal grasp, push, and viewpoint candidates based on their respective DOIG scores to select the overall best action $a^*_t$. If a manipulation action is chosen, its associated NBV is executed, and the observation updates the global evidential belief via the observation CNABU ($\sigma_o$). The process continues until the scene is sufficiently mapped or a maximum step budget is reached.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated MS-MEM's mapping performance, multi-skill action selection behavior, scene preservation capabilities, and uncertainty-aware grasp reasoning in both simulation and real-world setups.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

The evaluation used 25 challenging simulated shelf scenes, comparing MS-MEM against several baselines: "Grasp Only," "Push Only" (original MEM), and "w/o CDC" (MS-MEM without the Collateral Disturbance Constraint). Metrics included occupancy and semantic Intersection over Union (IoU), cumulative object displacement (Position Change), and action statistics.

- **Mapping Performance:**
 - Methods combining pushing and grasping (Ours and w/o CDC) generally outperformed single-skill baselines in mapping accuracy.
 - "w/o CDC" achieved the highest occupancy IoU (0.905) and semantic IoU (0.791), followed by "Ours" (0.899 occupancy IoU, 0.767 semantic IoU).
 - "Push Only" (0.887 occupancy IoU, 0.756 semantic IoU) outperformed "Grasp Only" (0.880 occupancy IoU, 0.681 semantic IoU). These results indicate a synergistic benefit from combining pushing and grasping for mapping.

- **Scene Preservation and Action Selection:**
 - "Grasp Only" resulted in the lowest Position Change (0.228 m), but had the lowest semantic IoU, suggesting conservative interaction.
 - "Push Only" achieved higher mapping accuracy than "Grasp Only" but caused the largest Position Change (1.571 m), indicating significant scene disturbance.
 - The "w/o CDC" variant, while having high mapping accuracy, also resulted in substantial Position Change (1.231 m), performing more grasps than "Grasp Only" and a similar number of pushes to "Push Only." This suggests that without the CDC, the system leverages manipulation more aggressively for information gain, even at the cost of disturbance.
 - The full "Ours" system achieved a Position Change of 0.707 m, significantly reducing disturbance compared to "Push Only" and "w/o CDC," while maintaining competitive mapping performance (0.899 occupancy IoU, 0.767 semantic IoU). This demonstrates the effectiveness of the CDC in balancing information gain with scene preservation. The CDC particularly reduced the average number of pushes, indicating it suppresses disruptive rearrangements.

- **Evidential Grasp Learning Performance (FE-vMF):**
 - Visualizations of FE-vMF showed that high affordance uncertainty occurred at partially observed contact points, collision-sensitive regions, or intrinsically ambiguous grasp points.
 - Orientation uncertainty, quantified by the inverse of concentration parameters, increased when multiple collision-free approach directions were plausible for less constrained objects. It decreased when nearby obstacles constrained feasible approach directions.
 - Quantitative calibration statistics demonstrated that the orientation estimates accurately captured directional uncertainty, with lower predicted precision corresponding to larger angular errors.

#### 4.2. Real-World Performance

MS-MEM was evaluated zero-shot on a physical shelf setup using a UR5 manipulator and RealSense L515 camera across 5 challenging scenes with 69 objects. Object recognition metrics and physical position change were measured using a Vicon motion capture system.

- **Object Recognition and Scene Preservation:**
 - "Ours" achieved the highest number of correctly identified objects (44), compared to "Push Only" (40) and "Grasp Only" (38). This improvement was attributed to a reduction in missed objects (8 for "Ours" vs. over 10 for others).
 - "Grasp Only" caused the least object disturbance (0.198 m Position Change) but missed the most objects.
 - "Push Only" improved coverage but produced the largest scene disturbance (0.567 m Position Change).
 - "Ours" achieved its superior object recognition performance while reducing Position Change by 28.0% (from 0.567 m to 0.408 m) compared to "Push Only."
 - The grasp success rate of "Ours" was 30% higher compared to "Grasp Only."
These real-world results corroborate the simulation findings, indicating that MS-MEM effectively combines pushing and grasping to improve object recognition and grasp success while simultaneously reducing scene disturbance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MS-MEM represents an advancement in robotic scene understanding and manipulation, particularly for cluttered and confined environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Mapping Accuracy and Completeness:** By integrating multiple manipulation skills (grasping and pushing) with active vision, MS-MEM demonstrates superior performance in generating geometrically complete and semantically accurate maps compared to single-skill approaches. This is crucial for robots performing tasks that require a precise understanding of their environment, such as inventory management, object retrieval, and facility maintenance in complex settings.
- **Improved Manipulation Strategy:** The framework highlights the synergistic benefits of combining pushing and grasping. Pushing can effectively clear clutter and create free space, while grasping enables the targeted and selective removal of objects. This adaptive multi-skill approach allows robots to choose the most appropriate interaction based on the current scene state and uncertainty, leading to more efficient and robust manipulation.
- **Reduced Collateral Scene Disturbance:** The introduction of the Collateral Disturbance Constraint (CDC) is a significant contribution, allowing the robot to explicitly balance information gain with the preservation of already confident scene elements. This capability is vital for maintaining the integrity of the environment, reducing unnecessary re-mapping efforts, and ensuring the reliability of accumulated scene knowledge. It leads to more "thoughtful" robotic interactions, where manipulation is purposeful and minimized when possible.
- **Robust Grasp Planning under Uncertainty:** The proposed Full-Evidential vMF-Contact (FE-vMF) model and Full-Evidential Uncertainty-guided Multi-view Grasp Fusion (FE-UMGF) provide a comprehensive and robust framework for grasp planning in partially observable and cluttered scenes. By explicitly modeling and fusing uncertainty in grasp affordance and orientation across multiple views, the system can make more informed grasp decisions, leading to higher grasp success rates, as demonstrated in the real-world experiments.
- **Unified Decision-Making Framework:** The unified Disturbance- and Occlusion-aware Information Gain (DOIG) objective provides a common metric for evaluating heterogeneous perception and manipulation actions. This allows for a principled and adaptive selection among diverse actions, moving beyond heuristic-based approaches and enabling more intelligent autonomous behavior in complex tasks.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The successful zero-shot transfer of the action selection pipeline from simulation to a physical robot demonstrates the potential for developing and testing complex robotic behaviors in simulated environments, with direct applicability to real-world scenarios. This can accelerate research and development in robotics.

In summary, MS-MEM contributes to the field by providing a more comprehensive and intelligent approach to active robotic mapping in cluttered environments. Its ability to synergistically combine perception and multiple manipulation skills, while explicitly managing uncertainty and minimizing scene disturbance, has the potential to significantly enhance the autonomy and capabilities of service robots in a wide range of practical applications.
