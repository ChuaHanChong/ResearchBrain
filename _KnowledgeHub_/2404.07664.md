# Finding Dino: A Plug-and-Play Framework for Zero-Shot Detection of Out-of-Distribution Objects Using Prototypes

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fraunhofer IKS and Technical University of Munich developed PROWL, a plug-and-play framework for zero-shot, unsupervised detection and segmentation of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) objects. The system, leveraging frozen DINOv2 features and prototype matching, achieved state-of-the-art or comparable performance on road driving OOD benchmarks and generalized effectively to rail (83.29 IoU) and maritime (73.30 IoU) domains without retraining.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems operating in open-world environments (e.g., vehicles, trains) require the ability to detect Out-of-Distribution (OOD) objects not encountered during training for safety reasons.
- Current supervised anomaly segmentation methods necessitate extensive annotations or auxiliary OOD samples, which is impractical and often impossible for truly novel unknowns.
- Existing Open-World Object Detection (OWOD) approaches frequently struggle to differentiate relevant unknown objects from generic background clutter, leading to noisy or imprecise detections.

## Method

- The ==PROWL framework== utilizes a frozen, self-supervised pre-trained foundation model (DINOv2) as a robust feature extractor.
- It establishes a =="prototype feature bank"== for known object classes by extracting features from a minimal set of segmented instances in an offline, one-time process.
- During inference, OOD objects are identified by calculating the ==Inverse Normalised Cosine Similarity (INCS)== of test image pixels to all known prototypes, classifying pixels with low similarity as OOD.
- An optional refinement module integrates unsupervised foreground masks from models like CutLER to aggregate pixel-level OOD detections into coherent object instances, improving segmentation precision.

## Results

- PROWL + CutLER achieved state-of-the-art or comparable performance on road driving OOD benchmarks (RoadObstacle, RoadAnomaly, FS Static) against supervised methods trained without auxiliary OOD data.
- The framework successfully generalized to new domains, achieving an IoU of **83.29** and F1 of **90.38** on an in-house rail dataset, and IoU of **73.30** and F1 of **84.08** on the maritime MODD dataset.
- It significantly outperformed a supervised baseline (Maskomaly) in binary OOD segmentation, demonstrating an IoU of **75.22** and F1 of **85.25** on RoadAnomaly compared to Maskomaly's **46.86** IoU and **56.76** F1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised pre-trained foundation models like DINOv2 learn highly generalizable visual features that are effective for differentiating known objects from Out-of-Distribution (OOD) elements without explicit OOD training.
> - Zero-shot OOD object detection and segmentation can be achieved through an inference-only prototype matching approach, eliminating the need for domain-specific training or auxiliary OOD data.
> - Integrating unsupervised instance-level foreground masks significantly refines pixel-wise OOD predictions, leading to more precise, coherent, and less noisy OOD object segmentations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of computer vision and artificial intelligence for autonomous systems: the detection and localization of unknown or Out-of-Distribution (OOD) objects. In open-world scenarios, autonomous vehicles, trains, and unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) constantly encounter objects not present in their training data. Current State-of-the-Art (SOTA) AI models, primarily trained on closed sets of object categories, fail to recognize these "unknowns," posing significant safety risks.

Traditionally, approaches to OOD detection and anomaly segmentation have largely fallen into two categories:
- **Supervised Anomaly Segmentation:** These methods often require training on exhaustively annotated datasets for every domain or rely on selected OOD samples (auxiliary OOD data) during training or fine-tuning. This reliance on prior knowledge of OOD samples is a severe limitation when attempting to detect truly novel, unforeseen objects.
- **Open-World Object Detection (OWOD):** While gaining attention, OWOD methods typically struggle with the fundamental distinction between an unknown relevant object and generic background clutter, often requiring incremental learning to adapt to novel classes.

The existing landscape highlights a significant gap: a lack of methods that can perform OOD detection in a *zero-shot*, *unsupervised*, and *plug-and-play* manner, meaning without requiring any domain-specific training, fine-tuning, or exposure to OOD samples. This is where "Finding Dino" positions itself.

The paper capitalizes on the recent emergence of **self-supervised pre-trained foundation models** (e.g., DINO, DINOv2). These models, trained on vast and diverse image corpora without explicit human labels, have demonstrated an remarkable ability to learn robust, general-purpose visual features that capture deep semantic and perceptual groupings. The authors leverage these powerful, frozen feature extractors as the backbone for their system.

By proposing PROWL (PRototype-based OOD detection Without Labels), the authors contribute a novel framework that bridges this gap. It represents a shift from data-dependent, supervised learning to an inference-based approach that exploits the inherent generalization capabilities of foundation models. This work pushes the boundaries towards more adaptable and resilient AI systems, critical for safe operation in dynamic, unpredictable real-world environments. It is presented as the first end-to-end framework for zero-shot unsupervised OOD object detection and segmentation, setting a new benchmark for practical OOD awareness in autonomous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a novel, highly adaptable framework, named PROWL (PRototype-based OOD detection Without Labels), for the accurate detection and localization of unknown or Out-of-Distribution (OOD) objects in diverse visual scenes. The framework is specifically designed to operate in a **zero-shot, unsupervised, and plug-and-play manner**, meaning it requires no explicit training or fine-tuning on domain-specific data, nor does it need auxiliary OOD samples.

The motivation for this ambitious objective stems from pressing challenges in deploying Artificial Intelligence (AI) in safety-critical autonomous systems:

- **Safety Imperative in Open-World Settings:** Autonomous systems, such as vehicles, trains, and unmanned surface vehicles (USVs), operate in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments. They must be able to perceive their surroundings comprehensively, which includes identifying and reacting to objects they have never encountered during training. Failure to detect an unexpected obstacle (e.g., a fallen tree, an animal on the tracks, or an unknown floating debris) can lead to severe accidents and endanger lives.
- **Limitations of Closed-Set AI:** Conventional deep neural networks (DNNs) are typically trained on a pre-defined, closed set of object categories. While they achieve high performance on these known classes, they fundamentally lack the ability to recognize or even signal the presence of objects outside this training distribution. This "blind spot" is a critical vulnerability for systems operating in open-world settings.
- **Impracticality of Exhaustive Supervision:** The sheer diversity and unpredictability of potential OOD objects make it virtually impossible to train AI models exhaustively with annotated datasets covering all possible known and unknown object variations. Creating such datasets for every operational design domain (ODD) is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and ultimately unfeasible.
- **Challenges with Existing OOD Approaches:** Many existing anomaly segmentation or open-world object detection methods either rely on supervised training with some form of OOD data (defeating the "unknown" principle) or struggle with distinguishing a relevant unknown object from mere background noise. Pixel-level anomaly detection often yields noisy results, while mask-based methods, though more precise, have largely remained within a supervised paradigm.

PROWL aims to overcome these limitations by leveraging the powerful, generalized feature representations learned by self-supervised foundation models (e.g., DINOv2). By establishing "prototypes" for known classes using these robust features and then identifying anything that significantly deviates from these prototypes, PROWL offers a practical and scalable solution. Its plug-and-play nature allows for rapid adaptation to new domains by simply defining the expected "known" classes, thereby addressing the urgent need for flexible and reliable OOD detection in critical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, PROWL (PRototype-based OOD detection Without Labels), is an inference-based method composed of three interconnected modules designed for zero-shot, unsupervised OOD detection and segmentation.

#### 3.1. Plug-and-play Prototype Matching Module

This module is central to defining "known" objects and is divided into two phases:

- **Creating the Prototype Feature Bank:** This is an offline step. The authors assume a predefined list of *K* known object classes (C = {c1, c2..., cK}) for a given Operational Design Domain (ODD). For each class, a minimal number of segmented object instances (e.g., L ∈ {5, 20}) are selected from a training dataset of that domain. A *frozen*, self-supervised pre-trained foundation model, specifically DINOv2's ViT-S/14, is used as a feature extractor. For each object instance, its ground truth (GT) mask is multiplied with the extracted feature map to isolate instance-specific features. A spatial averaging operation then converts these into 1D feature representations, which are stored in the "prototype feature bank" (P). The frozen nature of the foundation model means no training or fine-tuning is performed here; it's purely feature extraction.
- **Prototype Matching (Inference):** For any new test image, features are extracted using the *same frozen foundation model*. For every pixel in the test image's feature map, its feature vector is compared against all prototype vectors in the feature bank using cosine similarity. The maximum cosine similarity score (v) across all prototypes, along with the corresponding class label (y), is recorded for each pixel. This process generates *K* prototype heatmaps (H = {h1..., hK}), where each heatmap indicates the similarity of image regions to a specific known class.

#### 3.2. OOD Detection Module

Following the prototype matching, this module identifies OOD pixels:

- **Inverse Normalised Cosine Similarity (INCS):** For each pixel, an INCS score is calculated based on its maximum similarity score (v) to any known prototype. The formula is `w = 1 - norm(v)`, where `norm(a)` normalizes `a` between 0 and 1. A high INCS score indicates low similarity to *all* known prototypes, suggesting an OOD pixel.
- **Thresholding:** Pixels whose INCS score (w) exceeds a predetermined threshold (t ∈) are designated as OOD ("unknown"). Pixels below this threshold retain their assigned known class label (y). While reliable, this pixel-level detection can sometimes be noisy.

#### 3.3. Refinement Module Using Foreground Masks

To mitigate noise and improve localization, PROWL offers an optional but highly beneficial refinement step:

- **Unsupervised Foreground Mask Generation:** The framework integrates SOTA unsupervised segmentation methods, such as STEGO or CutLER. These models, trained on large generic datasets in a self-supervised manner, are capable of generating instance-level foreground masks for objects in a scene *without prior knowledge of their classes*. This means they identify "object-like" regions irrespective of whether they are known or unknown to PROWL.
- **Mask-based OOD Classification:** Instead of relying solely on pixel-level decisions, PROWL combines the generated foreground masks (M = {m1..., mN}) with the prototype heatmaps. For each foreground mask, if a *majority* of its pixels are identified as OOD by the INCS thresholding (from the previous step), then the *entire mask* is classified as an OOD object. This refinement step ensures that OOD detections correspond to coherent object instances rather than scattered pixels, leading to more precise and less noisy segmentations.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details and Datasets

- **Feature Extractor:** DINOv2 ViT-S/14, pre-trained on 142 million images, is used in a frozen state.
- **Hyperparameters:** Default INCS threshold of 0.55 and CutLER detector threshold of 0.2 were found effective on a validation set.
- **Evaluation Domains:**
 - **Road driving:** Prototypes from Cityscapes (19 classes); OOD evaluation on RoadAnomaly21, RoadObstacle21 (from SMIYC benchmark), and FS Static (Fishyscapes benchmark).
 - **Rail:** Prototypes from RailSem19 (6 classes); OOD evaluation on an in-house created dataset with in-painted OOD objects.
 - **Maritime:** Prototypes from MaStr1325 (sky, sea as known); OOD evaluation on corresponding test data (MODD).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** AUPRC and FPR@TPR95% (pixel-wise, common in anomaly segmentation benchmarks) and Intersection over Union (IoU) and F1 score (for binary segmentation of OOD objects), addressing the lack of direct zero-shot benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of PROWL across various scenarios and benchmarks, highlighting its effectiveness and generalizability.

#### 4.1. Performance on Road Driving Scenes (Comparison with SOTA)

- **Outperformance on RoadObstacle:** PROWL, particularly with the CutLER refinement module (PROWL + CutLER), achieves state-of-the-art results on the challenging RoadObstacle dataset. It outperforms all supervised methods that do not use auxiliary OOD data, demonstrating superior AUPR and FPR metrics.
- **Comparable Performance on RoadAnomaly and FS Static:** On the RoadAnomaly and FS Static datasets, PROWL + CutLER shows comparable performance to highly-ranked supervised methods (trained without auxiliary OOD data) in terms of AUPR and FPR. Specifically, it achieves the best FPR and second-best AUPR on RoadAnomaly compared to supervised methods without auxiliary data.
- **Significant Improvement over Supervised Baseline (Maskomaly):** When evaluated as a binary segmentation task using fixed thresholds (IoU and F1 scores), PROWL + CutLER dramatically outperforms Maskomaly, a supervised baseline also operating in a zero-shot inference manner but relying on supervised pre-training. For example, on RoadAnomaly, PROWL + CutLER achieved an IoU of 75.22 and F1 of 85.25, significantly higher than Maskomaly's 46.86 IoU and 56.76 F1. This shows PROWL's superior ability to precisely localize and segment OOD objects.
- **Impact of Refinement Module:** The refinement module, especially when integrated with CutLER for foreground mask generation, consistently and substantially improves OOD detection and segmentation quality over vanilla pixel-wise PROWL. This refinement reduces noisy detections and localizes OOD objects as coherent instances. STEGO also provides improvements, but CutLER's instance-centric masks yield better results.
- **Qualitative Superiority:** Qualitative results demonstrate that PROWL + CutLER provides visually superior OOD detection and segmentation across all road driving datasets, even in challenging scenarios with varying object sizes, scenes, and textures where supervised baselines struggle with partial or false detections.

#### 4.2. Plug-and-Play Application to Other Domains

- **Generalizability Confirmed:** The framework's "plug-and-play" capability is successfully demonstrated by applying it to entirely different domains: rail and maritime scenes, without any retraining or fine-tuning. This involves simply defining a new list of ODD classes and creating a corresponding prototype bank.
- **Strong Performance in New Domains:** For the rail domain (using an in-house in-painted OOD dataset), PROWL + CutLER achieved an IoU of 83.29 and F1 of 90.38. In the maritime domain (MODD dataset), it achieved an IoU of 73.30 and F1 of 84.08. In both cases, PROWL + CutLER significantly outperformed vanilla PROWL, reconfirming the importance of instance-level mask refinement.
- **Adaptability of CutLER:** The success in new domains is partly attributed to CutLER's unsupervised training on generic datasets (ImageNet), allowing it to provide zero-shot instance segmentation in any domain, unlike domain-specific supervised methods.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study Insights

- **Influence of Prototype Count:** The study revealed that PROWL achieves good performance with as few as 5 prototypes per class, with performance saturating around 15-20 prototypes. This highlights the efficiency of the prototype bank creation and the robustness of DINOv2 features.
- **Feature Extractor Choice:** While larger DINOv2 models (e.g., ViT-g/14) offer marginal performance gains, the ViT-S/14 model provides a better trade-off between performance and inference time, making it a practical choice.
- **Threshold Optimization:** Optimal CutLER detector and INCS thresholds vary slightly across datasets, depending on OOD object size and scene complexity. For instance, smaller objects often require lower detector thresholds. However, the default thresholds proved to be generally effective, reinforcing the zero-shot nature.
- **OOD Object Size Impact:** The performance correlates with OOD object size; smaller, distant objects (e.g., in RoadObstacle) are inherently more challenging to detect reliably than larger ones (e.g., in RoadAnomaly).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Finding Dino" paper introduces a highly significant advancement in the field of computer vision for autonomous systems, with profound potential impacts across several dimensions:

- **Enabling Safer Autonomous Systems:** The most immediate and critical impact of PROWL lies in its ability to enhance the safety and reliability of autonomous systems operating in open-world environments. By providing a robust mechanism for detecting truly unknown or unforeseen objects, PROWL directly addresses a major Achilles' heel of current AI perception systems. This is vital for autonomous vehicles, trains (as highlighted by the "safe.trAIn" project funding), and unmanned surface vehicles, mitigating risks associated with unpredictable obstacles that could lead to accidents.
- **Paradigm Shift in OOD Detection:** PROWL represents a significant conceptual shift from data-intensive, supervised OOD detection methods to a purely inference-based, zero-shot, and unsupervised framework. This eliminates the prohibitive costs and logistical challenges of collecting and annotating exhaustive OOD datasets, or the need for auxiliary OOD data during training, which is often impossible for truly novel unknowns. This paradigm offers a more practical and scalable solution for real-world deployment.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models for Practical Applications:** The work effectively showcases the immense potential of self-supervised vision foundation models (like DINOv2) as powerful, general-purpose feature extractors. It demonstrates that the robust representations learned by these models, without human labels, can be directly leveraged for complex downstream tasks like OOD object detection, opening new avenues for applied AI research and development.
- **High Adaptability and "Plug-and-Play" Capability:** PROWL's plug-and-play nature is a game-changer. It allows rapid adaptation to new operational design domains (ODDs) or specific environmental contexts by simply defining a new list of "known" classes and creating a small prototype bank. This significantly reduces development cycles, costs, and the time-to-deployment for AI systems in varied applications, from urban driving to rail networks and maritime navigation.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Benchmarking:** By establishing the first end-to-end framework for zero-shot unsupervised OOD object detection and segmentation, PROWL sets a crucial baseline. This will undoubtedly spur further research in this underserved area. Moreover, the authors explicitly identify a critical need for harmonized evaluation metrics and benchmarks specifically designed for zero-shot approaches, which is essential for fair comparisons and accelerating progress in the field. This recognition itself is an important contribution, guiding the community towards more relevant evaluation strategies.
- **Efficiency in Resource Utilization:** The ability to achieve strong OOD detection with very few prototype samples (as few as 5 per class) and without any model retraining or fine-tuning makes PROWL highly efficient in terms of computational resources and data requirements. This is particularly valuable for resource-constrained applications or for quick deployment in novel environments.

In conclusion, "Finding Dino" is a groundbreaking contribution that provides a pragmatic and highly effective solution to a long-standing challenge in AI safety. Its innovative use of foundation models and its zero-shot, plug-and-play design pave the way for more robust, adaptable, and trustworthy autonomous systems in an increasingly unpredictable world.
