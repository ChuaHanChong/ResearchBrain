# Finding Dino: A Plug-and-Play Framework for Zero-Shot Detection of Out-of-Distribution Objects Using Prototypes

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fraunhofer IKS and Technical University of Munich developed PROWL, a plug-and-play framework for zero-shot, unsupervised detection and segmentation of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) objects. The system, leveraging frozen DINOv2 features and prototype matching, achieved state-of-the-art or comparable performance on road driving OOD benchmarks and generalized effectively to rail (83.29 IoU) and maritime (73.30 IoU) domains without retraining.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems operating in open-world environments (e.g., vehicles, trains) require the ability to detect Out-of-Distribution (OOD) objects not encountered during training for safety reasons.
- Current supervised anomaly segmentation methods necessitate extensive annotations or auxiliary OOD samples, which is impractical and often impossible for truly novel unknowns.
- Existing Open-World Object Detection (OWOD) approaches frequently struggle to differentiate relevant unknown objects from generic background clutter, leading to noisy or imprecise detections.

## Method

- The ==PROWL framework== utilizes a frozen, self-supervised pre-trained foundation model (DINOv2) as a robust feature extractor.
- It establishes a =="prototype feature bank"== for known object classes by extracting features from a minimal set of segmented instances in an offline, one-time process.
- During inference, OOD objects are identified by calculating the ==Inverse Normalised Cosine Similarity (INCS)== of test image pixels to all known prototypes, classifying pixels with low similarity as OOD.
- An optional refinement module integrates unsupervised foreground masks from models like CutLER to aggregate pixel-level OOD detections into coherent object instances, improving segmentation precision.

## Results

- PROWL + CutLER achieved state-of-the-art or comparable performance on road driving OOD benchmarks (RoadObstacle, RoadAnomaly, FS Static) against supervised methods trained without auxiliary OOD data.
- The framework successfully generalized to new domains, achieving an IoU of **83.29** and F1 of **90.38** on an in-house rail dataset, and IoU of **73.30** and F1 of **84.08** on the maritime MODD dataset.
- It significantly outperformed a supervised baseline (Maskomaly) in binary OOD segmentation, demonstrating an IoU of **75.22** and F1 of **85.25** on RoadAnomaly compared to Maskomaly's **46.86** IoU and **56.76** F1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised pre-trained foundation models like DINOv2 learn highly generalizable visual features that are effective for differentiating known objects from Out-of-Distribution (OOD) elements without explicit OOD training.
> - Zero-shot OOD object detection and segmentation can be achieved through an inference-only prototype matching approach, eliminating the need for domain-specific training or auxiliary OOD data.
> - Integrating unsupervised instance-level foreground masks significantly refines pixel-wise OOD predictions, leading to more precise, coherent, and less noisy OOD object segmentations.
