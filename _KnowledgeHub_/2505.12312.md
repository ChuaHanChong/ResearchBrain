# Visuospatial Cognitive Assistant

> [!summary] Summary
> Kyoto University's ViCA introduces a 322K-pair dataset and a 7B-parameter model, ViCA-7B, for video-based spatial cognition, achieving leading performance on the VSI-Bench benchmark over larger models. The research also presents ViCA-7B-Thinking, which generates explicit reasoning chains for enhanced interpretability.

## Problem

- Existing Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack sophisticated video-based spatial cognition, struggling with fine-grained 3D relationships, geometric changes, and precise estimations from video.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, diverse datasets providing targeted supervision for intricate spatial skills, particularly those with precise 3D ground truth from real-world videos.
- Current VLM reasoning processes are often opaque, limiting interpretability and trust for critical applications requiring spatial decision-making.

## Method

- A large-scale dataset, ==ViCA-322K==, was created from real-world indoor videos with 3D metadata-grounded and complex spatial reasoning queries.
- ==ViCA-7B==, a 7B-parameter VLM, was developed by fine-tuning LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2 on the ViCA-322K dataset for enhanced spatial cognition.
- ==ViCA-Thinking-2.68K==, a dataset with explicit reasoning chains, was used to fine-tune ViCA-7B into ViCA-7B-Thinking for improved model interpretability.

## Results

- ViCA-7B established new state-of-the-art performance on the VSI-Bench benchmark, surpassing larger open-source models and several proprietary systems.
- The Complex Spatial Reasoning (CSR) portion of the ViCA-322K dataset contributed a **5.17** average score improvement on VSI-Bench.
- ViCA-7B-Thinking provided transparent, step-by-step reasoning processes, though this came with an average **22.1-point** decrease in VSI-Bench accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted fine-tuning on diverse, 3D-grounded spatial reasoning data significantly boosts VLM performance, enabling smaller models to outperform larger generalist models.
> - Explicit temporal instructions or simply increasing input frames do not improve spatial reasoning, indicating architectural limitations in effectively leveraging structured temporal cues.
> - Achieving explicit, human-like reasoning chains in VLMs currently incurs a trade-off with raw predictive accuracy, particularly for numerical tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen tremendous progress with Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in multimodal understanding and generation for tasks like visual question answering and image captioning. However, this paper identifies a critical frontier where current VLMs often fall short: **sophisticated video-based spatial cognition**. This specific capability—the ability to accurately perceive, reason about, and interact with the three-dimensional (3D) structure and dynamics of environments from video input—is paramount for the advancement of robotics, augmented reality (AR), and embodied AI.

The authors position their work against the backdrop of existing VLM research, acknowledging the impressive general visual understanding capabilities of models like GPT-4V, Gemini, LLaVA, and BLIP, as well as their extensions to video understanding such as Video-LLaMA and LLaVA-NeXT-Video. However, they highlight that these generalist models frequently struggle with tasks demanding nuanced spatial understanding beyond mere object recognition or temporal event tracking. The challenges include comprehending fine-grained spatial relationships, tracking geometric changes, precisely estimating sizes and distances, and reasoning about navigability or functional affordances within a 3D space.

This gap is primarily attributed to two factors:
1. **Data Scarcity:** A lack of large-scale, diverse datasets that offer rich, targeted supervision for intricate spatial skills, particularly those grounded in precise 3D information from real-world videos. Existing datasets often focus on static images, offer limited spatial query diversity, or lack the crucial 3D ground truth needed for robust geometric understanding.
2. **Model Opacity:** The internal reasoning processes of current VLMs often remain opaque, hindering interpretability, debugging, and trust in critical applications where spatial decisions are vital.

The research directly addresses these limitations. It contributes to the landscape by providing:
- **A specialized dataset (ViCA-322K):** This dataset fills a significant void by offering a diverse collection of 3D metadata-grounded and complex video-based spatial reasoning queries from real-world indoor environments. It moves beyond generic video understanding benchmarks (like Video-mme, Egoschema, Mvbench, Video-Bench) to focus specifically on fine-grained spatial cognition, utilizing the VSI-Bench as its evaluation standard.
- **A high-performing model (ViCA-7B):** By achieving state-of-the-art results on VSI-Bench, even outperforming larger and proprietary models, ViCA-7B demonstrates that targeted data and fine-tuning can unlock superior spatial reasoning capabilities in relatively smaller models. This challenges the prevailing notion that only ever-larger models with generic training data can achieve leading performance.
- **An interpretability solution (ViCA-Thinking-2.68K and ViCA-7B-Thinking):** This component directly tackles the transparency problem in VLM reasoning, a crucial step towards building more trustworthy and explainable AI systems, particularly vital for human-robot interaction and safety-critical applications.

In essence, this work pushes the frontier of multimodal AI from general visual understanding towards genuinely intelligent spatial cognition, laying a foundational groundwork for more capable and trustworthy embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **advance video-based spatial cognition** to unlock more sophisticated capabilities for robotics, augmented reality, and embodied AI. The authors are driven by the observed shortcomings of current Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when faced with tasks requiring a nuanced understanding of 3D space and its dynamics within video sequences.

The primary motivations underpinning this work stem from two critical challenges:

1. **Addressing the Limitations of Existing VLMs in Spatial Reasoning:** While contemporary VLMs excel at many multimodal tasks, they struggle with the intricacies of spatial cognition. This includes difficulties in discerning fine-grained relationships between objects, tracking precise geometric changes over time, accurately estimating real-world sizes and distances, and performing complex reasoning related to navigability or functional affordances within an environment. Without these capabilities, embodied AI agents cannot truly "understand" and interact intelligently with the physical world.
2. **Bridging the Data Gap for Targeted Spatial Supervision:** A significant barrier to developing robust spatial reasoning models is the scarcity of appropriate training data. Existing large-scale datasets often consist of static images, offer limited diversity in spatial queries, or lack the precise 3D ground truth information essential for learning robust geometric and contextual understanding from videos. The authors argue for a concentrated effort to create comprehensive datasets that specifically cover a wide spectrum of spatial reasoning tasks, grounded in rich, real-world video data.

To tackle these motivations, the paper sets forth several key objectives, which are also its primary contributions:

- **Dataset Introduction (ViCA-322K):** To provide comprehensive and targeted supervision, the authors aim to introduce ViCA-322K, a large-scale, diverse dataset of 322,003 high-quality question-answer pairs. This dataset is designed to span direct spatial queries grounded in 3D metadata and more complex reasoning tasks derived from real-world indoor videos (ARKitScenes, ScanNet, ScanNet++).
- **Model Development and Performance (ViCA-7B):** A key objective is to develop a Vision-Language Model, ViCA-7B, by fine-tuning an existing state-of-the-art VLM on the ViCA-322K dataset. The goal is for ViCA-7B to achieve new state-of-the-art performance on a challenging benchmark like VSI-Bench, demonstrating superior visuospatial understanding and efficiency by outperforming larger models.
- **Interpretability Enhancement (ViCA-Thinking-2.68K and ViCA-7B-Thinking):** Recognizing the critical need for transparency, the authors aim to construct ViCA-Thinking-2.68K, a novel dataset incorporating explicit reasoning chains for spatial cognitive tasks. Subsequently, they intend to fine-tune ViCA-7B to create ViCA-7B-Thinking, a model capable of articulating its step-by-step spatial reasoning process, thereby enhancing model interpretability.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** A final, crucial objective is to publicly release all developed resources, including the ViCA-7B and ViCA-7B-Thinking models, evaluation code, fine-tuning scripts, and training logs. This aims to foster broader research, ensure reproducibility, and contribute to the collective advancement of visuospatial intelligence within the AI community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a multi-pronged approach, focusing on both **data creation** and **model development and evaluation**, with a particular emphasis on enhancing both performance and interpretability in video-based spatial cognition.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction: ViCA-322K

The cornerstone of this work is the **ViCA-322K dataset**, meticulously designed to provide rich, targeted supervision for visuospatial understanding.

- **Video Sources:** The dataset draws from three real-world indoor video datasets: ARKitScenes, ScanNet, and ScanNet++. Crucially, only the training splits of these datasets are used, with strict filtering to prevent any overlap with the VSI-Bench evaluation set, ensuring unbiased assessment. These sources provide diverse indoor environments with high-quality annotations.
- **Data Composition - Base Data (281,359 QA pairs):** This subset focuses on direct spatial queries derived from precise **3D oriented bounding box annotations**. Tasks include object count, relative and absolute distance estimation, object size estimation, object appearance order, and room size calculation. A deliberate choice was made to *omit* relative direction and route planning tasks from this subset. This encourages the model to learn generalizable spatial understanding rather than task-specific pattern matching, aiming for transferability to novel scenarios.
- **Data Composition - Complex Spatial Reasoning (CSR) (40,644 QA pairs):** This subset is designed to probe deeper spatial understanding, requiring interpretation from **video observations alone** (though the questions themselves are generated using metadata and/or sampled frames).
 - **General Category:** This involves medium-length, multi-turn dialogues between a human and a GPT agent, discussing spatial relationships and functional scene understanding (e.g., repositioning objects for better visibility). These Q&A pairs are generated by GPT-4o-mini, using *only metadata* (object names, coordinates, sizes, orientations) as input, without any visual frames. Loss is computed solely on the GPT agent's responses during training.
 - **Specific Category:** This targets fine-grained spatial reasoning with four distinct tasks: assessing furniture addition possibilities, evaluating space capacity and usage scenarios, determining wheelchair accessibility and describing navigation paths, and generating holistic spatial descriptions from random viewpoints. These specific Q&A pairs are generated by GPT-4o-mini, conditioned on **16 uniformly sampled frames** from each video, alongside carefully designed prompts.
- **Linguistic Diversity:** To enhance generalization and robustness, 10 alternative phrasings are designed for each question type within both Base Data and CSR subsets, with one phrasing randomly sampled during generation.

#### 3.2. Model Development: ViCA-7B

- **Base Model Selection:** The authors chose LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2, a state-of-the-art open-source vision-language model built upon LLaVA-OneVision, known for its strong video understanding capabilities.
- **Fine-tuning:** ViCA-7B is fine-tuned on the full ViCA-322K dataset. The most comprehensive version was trained for 55 hours using 8 NVIDIA H100 (80GB) GPUs and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 Offload for memory optimization.
- **Ablation Study Model:** ViCA-Base-7B was trained using only the Base Data portion of ViCA-322K, excluding the Complex Spatial Reasoning (CSR) data, to assess the impact of the CSR component.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

- **Benchmark:** All models are rigorously evaluated on the newly released VSI-Bench, a high-quality benchmark specifically designed for visuospatial reasoning and memory in real-world indoor videos. It comprises eight tasks: Object Count, Absolute Distance, Object Size, Room Size, Relative Distance, Relative Direction, Route Planning, and Appearance Order.
- **Standardization:** Evaluation strictly follows VSI-Bench prompts, with 64 frames uniformly sampled from each video.
- **Probing Experiments:**
 - **Time Instructions:** An experimental variant included explicit timing information in the prompt, listing timestamps for the 64 sampled frames.
 - **Increased Frames:** Another variant tested using a higher frame sampling rate of 128 frames instead of 64.

#### 3.4. Interpretability Model: ViCA-7B-Thinking

- **Dataset Construction (ViCA-Thinking-2.68K):** To enable explicit reasoning, a novel dataset was constructed. The authors leveraged Gemini 2.5 Pro Preview 03-25, which internally generates "Thoughts" (reasoning chains) not exposed via its API. Five high-quality "Thoughts" samples were manually collected via Google AI Studio. For each video-question pair, Gemini was prompted to generate ten candidate answers; correct answers (matching multiple-choice or MRA > 0.7 for numeric) were selected, and their corresponding "Thoughts" were extracted. ChatGPT-4o was then used to score these thoughts, selecting the highest-quality ones for the dataset. The prompting strategy for generation included detailed guidance on structuring and articulating the reasoning process.
- **Fine-tuning:** ViCA-7B was further fine-tuned on this ViCA-Thinking-2.68K dataset to create ViCA-7B-Thinking. This model was trained to produce a structured output format: `<|im_start|>think ... <|im_start|>response ... <|im_start|>final`.
- **Evaluation:** Qualitative analysis focuses on the interpretability and coherence of the generated reasoning chains. Quantitative evaluation on VSI-Bench required a post-processing step using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct to canonicalize the structured final answers into numerical or multiple-choice formats for scoring consistency.

This comprehensive methodology ensures a robust investigation into spatial cognition, from data generation to model performance and interpretability, with a strong commitment to open-source contributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings across dataset effectiveness, model performance, and insights into VLM capabilities and interpretability.

#### 4.1. ViCA-322K Dataset Effectiveness

- **Scale and Diversity:** ViCA-322K comprises a substantial 322,003 question-answer pairs, derived from ARKitScenes, ScanNet, and ScanNet++. This includes 281,359 Base Data questions grounded in 3D annotations and 40,644 Complex Spatial Reasoning (CSR) questions. This scale and structured diversity provide broad coverage of visuospatial semantics.
- **Impact of CSR Data:** Ablation studies demonstrated that the inclusion of CSR data significantly enhances model performance. The model trained on the full ViCA-322K dataset consistently achieved higher average scores across VSI-Bench tasks compared to the model trained only on Base Data, with a notable +5.17 average score improvement. This highlights that CSR data provides crucial inductive biases and richer structural supervision, enabling better generalization for complex visuospatial tasks.

#### 4.2. ViCA-7B State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Overall SOTA:** ViCA-7B achieved new state-of-the-art performance across all eight VSI-Bench tasks.
- **Outperformance of Larger Models:** Remarkably, ViCA-7B, a 7B-parameter model, consistently outperformed existing open-source models, including much larger 72B-scale models (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B, LLaVA-OneVision-72B), and even several proprietary systems (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5 Flash/Pro) on most tasks.
- **Significant Gains:** Particularly striking improvements were observed in numerical answer tasks. For instance, ViCA-7B surpassed the second-best result on the Absolute Distance task by an impressive 26.1 percentage points.
- **Transfer Learning Evidence:** Despite the Base Data specifically omitting relative direction and route planning tasks, ViCA-7B showed improvements on these, suggesting that the model successfully transferred learned spatial understanding from other tasks.
- **Temporal Precision:** High performance on the Appearance Order task was attributed to the dataset's use of YOLO-based precise temporal detection for object first appearances during data preparation, underscoring the value of high-quality, task-specific ground truth.

#### 4.3. Data Scale and Saturation

- **Performance Scaling:** Model performance significantly increased as the training data percentage grew from 5% to 60%.
- **Saturation Point:** Performance gains plateaued and slightly decreased between 95% and 100% of the dataset usage. This indicates that the ViCA-322K dataset size is well-matched to the 7B parameter capacity of the model, and the model reached a saturation point with the provided data, suggesting sufficient data volume without significant overfitting.

#### 4.4. Probing Experiments: Limitations in Temporal-Spatial Modeling

- **Ineffectiveness of Time Instructions:** Explicitly adding detailed timing information (64 timestamps) to prompts did *not* improve performance; in fact, it slightly degraded it. This suggests that current VLM architectures struggle to effectively leverage explicit temporal alignment cues, and the verbosity of such instructions may interfere with the reasoning flow.
- **No Benefit from Increased Frames:** Increasing the input visual content from 64 to 128 frames also did *not* improve model performance, leading to a slight drop. This indicates that simply providing more raw visual input does not automatically lead to better spatial understanding; models might be conditioned to a fixed number of visual tokens, and more sophisticated strategies are needed to integrate richer temporal information.

#### 4.5. ViCA-7B-Thinking: Interpretability vs. Accuracy Trade-off

- **Structured Output Adoption:** Fine-tuning ViCA-7B on the ViCA-Thinking-2.68K dataset for explicit reasoning led to rapid adoption of the desired structured output format (`<|im_start|>think ... <|im_start|>response ... <|im_start|>final`), stabilizing after only 15% of training data.
- **Accuracy Degradation:** Quantitatively, ViCA-7B-Thinking showed a significant drop in VSI-Bench accuracy compared to ViCA-7B (an average decrease of 22.1 points). This decline was most pronounced in numerical prediction tasks (e.g., Object Count, Absolute Distance, Room Size).
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** Qualitatively, ViCA-7B-Thinking demonstrated a significant gain in interpretability. By articulating step-by-step reasoning processes, it offers a transparent view into its decision-making, mirroring a human-like chain of reasoning. This, despite the accuracy trade-off, is crucial for understanding, debugging, and building trust in AI systems. The authors interpret this as a revelation of structural limitations in current decoder-only architectures when adapting to multi-phase output formats, rather than a mere performance loss.

In summary, the results underscore the power of targeted data in achieving SOTA spatial cognition, while simultaneously highlighting architectural limitations in processing explicit temporal information and the inherent trade-off between explicit reasoning and raw predictive accuracy in current models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Visuospatial Cognitive Assistant" research presents several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in areas requiring embodied intelligence and human-AI collaboration.

#### 5.1. Advancing Video-Based Spatial Cognition

This work makes a direct and substantial advancement in video-based spatial cognition, a critical bottleneck for intelligent agents. By enabling VLMs to reason about 3D environments from video with unprecedented accuracy, ViCA pushes the boundaries of how AI perceives and understands the physical world. This is fundamental for robots navigating complex spaces, AR systems overlaying contextually relevant information, and embodied AI agents performing intricate tasks that require detailed spatial awareness.

#### 5.2. Pioneering Dataset for Targeted Spatial Reasoning

The introduction of **ViCA-322K** is a major contribution to the research community. It addresses a critical scarcity of large-scale, diverse datasets specifically designed for fine-grained, 3D-grounded spatial understanding in videos. The dataset's dual structure, combining precise metadata-derived queries with complex, video-observation-based reasoning, sets a new standard for training and evaluating visuospatial intelligence. The empirical evidence of the Complex Spatial Reasoning (CSR) component's impact on generalization underscores the importance of carefully designed, richer supervision, influencing future dataset construction efforts.

#### 5.3. Democratizing High-Performance Spatial AI

ViCA-7B's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance, outperforming significantly larger models (e.g., 72B parameters) and even some proprietary systems, is highly impactful. It demonstrates that superior spatial cognition is not solely a function of model size, but critically depends on targeted, high-quality data and effective fine-tuning strategies. This finding has implications for resource-constrained research groups and practical applications where smaller, more efficient models are preferred for deployment.

#### 5.4. Enhancing Model Interpretability and Trust

The development of **ViCA-Thinking-2.68K** and **ViCA-7B-Thinking** represents a crucial step towards more transparent and trustworthy AI. By enabling models to articulate their step-by-step reasoning processes, this work tackles a long-standing challenge in AI: the "black box" problem. In fields like robotics or medical imaging, understanding *why* an AI makes a particular spatial judgment is as important as the judgment itself. This increased interpretability allows for better debugging, easier validation by human experts, and fosters greater trust in AI systems, paving the way for more reliable human-in-the-loop applications.

#### 5.5. Illuminating Future Research Directions

The probing experiments offer valuable insights into the limitations of current VLM architectures. The finding that naive scaling of temporal inputs (explicit timestamps, increased frame density) does not improve—and can even degrade—spatial reasoning performance is highly significant. It challenges prevailing assumptions and strongly suggests the need for developing **more sophisticated temporal-spatial modeling techniques**. Future research will likely focus on architectural innovations that can effectively integrate and reason over structured temporal information rather than simply consuming more raw data. Furthermore, the observed trade-off between explicit reasoning and raw accuracy in ViCA-7B-Thinking opens new avenues for exploring hybrid generation schemes, modular decoding strategies, or multi-objective training to reconcile both goals.

#### 5.6. Fostering Open Science and Reproducibility

The public release of all datasets, models, evaluation code, fine-tuning scripts, and training logs is a commendable commitment to open science. This democratizes access to advanced spatial cognition tools and benchmarks, lowering the barrier for entry for other researchers, ensuring reproducibility, and accelerating collective progress in the field.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances visuospatial intelligence, offers foundational resources for the community, and provides critical insights that will guide the development of next-generation, spatially-aware, and interpretable multimodal AI systems.
