# Visuospatial Cognitive Assistant

> [!summary] Summary
> Kyoto University's ViCA introduces a 322K-pair dataset and a 7B-parameter model, ViCA-7B, for video-based spatial cognition, achieving leading performance on the VSI-Bench benchmark over larger models. The research also presents ViCA-7B-Thinking, which generates explicit reasoning chains for enhanced interpretability.

## Problem

- Existing Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack sophisticated video-based spatial cognition, struggling with fine-grained 3D relationships, geometric changes, and precise estimations from video.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, diverse datasets providing targeted supervision for intricate spatial skills, particularly those with precise 3D ground truth from real-world videos.
- Current VLM reasoning processes are often opaque, limiting interpretability and trust for critical applications requiring spatial decision-making.

## Method

- A large-scale dataset, ==ViCA-322K==, was created from real-world indoor videos with 3D metadata-grounded and complex spatial reasoning queries.
- ==ViCA-7B==, a 7B-parameter VLM, was developed by fine-tuning LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2 on the ViCA-322K dataset for enhanced spatial cognition.
- ==ViCA-Thinking-2.68K==, a dataset with explicit reasoning chains, was used to fine-tune ViCA-7B into ViCA-7B-Thinking for improved model interpretability.

## Results

- ViCA-7B established new state-of-the-art performance on the VSI-Bench benchmark, surpassing larger open-source models and several proprietary systems.
- The Complex Spatial Reasoning (CSR) portion of the ViCA-322K dataset contributed a **5.17** average score improvement on VSI-Bench.
- ViCA-7B-Thinking provided transparent, step-by-step reasoning processes, though this came with an average **22.1-point** decrease in VSI-Bench accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted fine-tuning on diverse, 3D-grounded spatial reasoning data significantly boosts VLM performance, enabling smaller models to outperform larger generalist models.
> - Explicit temporal instructions or simply increasing input frames do not improve spatial reasoning, indicating architectural limitations in effectively leveraging structured temporal cues.
> - Achieving explicit, human-like reasoning chains in VLMs currently incurs a trade-off with raw predictive accuracy, particularly for numerical tasks.
