# KORGym: A Dynamic Game Platform for LLM Reasoning Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> KORGym is introduced as a dynamic game platform for evaluating large language models' (LLMs) intrinsic reasoning, featuring over fifty knowledge-orthogonal games across six dimensions. Developed by ByteDance Seed, M-A-P, and Beihang University, the platform provides comprehensive, multi-turn, and multimodal assessments, revealing consistent model behavioral patterns and significant performance gains from reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Existing LLM benchmarks often conflate memorization with genuine reasoning, struggling to evaluate intrinsic problem-solving skills in novel contexts.
- Current evaluation methods are frequently domain-specific, limited to single-turn interactions, and lack robust support for assessing long-term planning or adaptive decision-making.
- Many interactive benchmarks introduce confounding factors or lack inherent compatibility with reinforcement learning (RL), making it difficult to isolate pure reasoning ability.

## Method

- **KORGym** provides a modular, game-based platform with over fifty novel '==knowledge-orthogonal==' games designed to minimize reliance on pretraining knowledge across six distinct reasoning dimensions.
- The platform supports dynamic, multi-turn interactions via standardized APIs and is built on the Gymnasium framework to inherently support reinforcement learning scenarios for strategic planning and adaptation.
- It includes dedicated multimodal games that require visual recognition, image matching, and spatial perception, enabling comprehensive reasoning evaluation for Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

## Results

- O3-mini achieved the highest overall score among evaluated models, excelling particularly in spatial reasoning, while Gemini-**2.5**-pro led in mathematical and puzzle reasoning.
- Average scores on textual versions of games were consistently higher than on visual versions, though some closed-source VLMs showed stronger performance on visual tasks.
- A strong positive correlation was observed between reasoning performance and response length, with reasoning models producing broader token distributions, but with diminishing returns beyond a certain verbosity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Closed-source models generally exhibit superior reasoning performance, and 'thinking models' consistently outperform their non-thinking counterparts within similar size ranges.
> - Models within the same series demonstrate consistent profiles of strengths and weaknesses, frequently employing explicit reasoning paradigms (code, mathematical, natural language), although over-reliance on these paradigms can sometimes impede generalization.
> - Reinforcement learning-driven enhancements yield substantial gains in reasoning capabilities, leading to both improved overall performance and more balanced strengths across different reasoning dimensions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of KORGym is a direct response to critical limitations in the current evaluation landscape for large language models (LLMs), particularly concerning their reasoning capabilities.

**Challenges with Existing LLM Benchmarks:**
Historically, LLM evaluation has relied heavily on benchmarks like MMLU, CommonsenseQA, and MATH. While valuable, these benchmarks often conflate memorization (drawing on pretraining data) with genuine reasoning. They tend to be:
- **Domain-specific:** Benchmarks like AIME and PHYBench focus on particular domains, failing to capture general reasoning.
- **Pretraining-data-influenced:** Even broader benchmarks such as SuperGPQA and HLE are susceptible to models performing well due to exposure during pretraining, rather than intrinsic reasoning.
- **Limited in interactivity:** Many only support single-turn interactions (e.g., LogicGame), precluding the assessment of long-term planning or dynamic decision-making.
- **Confounding factors:** Some interactive benchmarks like TextArena and SPINBench introduce opponent dynamics that can inject extraneous variability, complicating the isolation of pure reasoning ability and making them less suitable for reinforcement learning (RL) scenarios due to exploitable strategies.
- **Lack of robustness:** Benchmarks like gg-bench might heavily rely on generative capacity or lack robustness in gameplay fidelity and RL integration.

**Introduction of Knowledge Orthogonality:**
A crucial conceptual anchor for KORGym is the principle of **Knowledge Orthogonality**, first introduced by KOR-Bench. This paradigm advocates for decoupling reasoning assessment from prior knowledge. The formal definition provided in the appendix clarifies that a task satisfies knowledge orthogonality if:
1. **Knowledge-Rule Decoupling (R ⊥ K):** The core rules (R) required for the task are logically self-contained and independent of general background knowledge (K).
2. **Knowledge Assistiveness:** While background knowledge (K) may support or interfere, it does not play a central role, quantified by a small Knowledge Impact Factor (β).
3. **Rule Centrality:** Correctness predominantly relies on understanding and applying the rules (R), with R having significantly greater influence than K.
This shift prioritizes evaluating a model's ability to follow rules in novel, out-of-distribution scenarios, thereby isolating core abilities like systematic generalization and hypothesis testing, rather than merely recalling learned patterns.

**KORGym's Position:**
KORGym emerges as a sophisticated advancement within this evolving landscape. Inspired by the knowledge-orthogonal framework of KOR-Bench and built upon the widely adopted Gymnasium for RL environments, KORGym offers a dynamic and comprehensive platform that addresses the aforementioned shortcomings. It directly tackles the need for evaluating intrinsic, general reasoning by:
- **Knowledge-orthogonal game design:** Ensuring games are less influenced by pretraining data.
- **Diverse and dynamic game scenarios:** Offering over fifty games across six reasoning dimensions, supporting interactive, multi-turn assessments.
- **Reinforcement learning compatibility:** Designed to support RL scenarios, enabling evaluation of strategic planning and adaptability over time.
- **Multimodal capabilities:** Including visual tasks to assess reasoning beyond text.

By integrating these features, KORGym aims to provide a "fairer framework" for measuring cognitive agility, ensuring models demonstrate genuine reasoning and adaptability in complex, interactive environments. Its comparison with other game-based benchmarks (Table 2) clearly highlights its unique combination of multi-turn support, RL integration, controllable difficulty, and multimodal capabilities, positioning it as a leading-edge tool for next-generation LLM evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind KORGym is to overcome the limitations of existing LLM evaluation benchmarks and provide a more accurate, comprehensive, and robust assessment of LLMs' intrinsic reasoning capabilities.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Accurately Assess Intrinsic Reasoning:** The primary objective is to move beyond evaluations that conflate memorization with reasoning. Existing benchmarks are often domain-specific or heavily influenced by pretraining data, making it difficult to gauge a model's genuine problem-solving skills in novel contexts. KORGym aims to provide a "knowledge-orthogonal" environment where success hinges on rule-following and logical deduction rather than factual recall.
- **Enable Comprehensive, General Reasoning Evaluation:** Current benchmarks struggle to capture an LLM's general reasoning potential. KORGym seeks to address this by offering a broad suite of over fifty diverse games spanning six distinct reasoning dimensions (Mathematical and Logical, Control Interaction, Puzzle, Spatial and Geometric, Strategic, and Multimodal Reasoning). This diversity ensures a holistic assessment of various cognitive skills.
- **Support Dynamic and Interactive Assessments:** Many benchmarks are limited to single-turn interactions, failing to evaluate capabilities like long-term planning, adaptive decision-making, and understanding environmental feedback. KORGym is designed for multi-turn scenarios, allowing for interactive assessments and the evaluation of models in dynamic, evolving environments.
- **Facilitate Reinforcement Learning (RL) Integration:** Recognizing the importance of learning and adaptation in complex environments, KORGym is built on the Gymnasium framework to inherently support RL scenarios. This allows researchers to assess how LLMs perform and improve through interactive learning, a critical aspect of agentic behavior.
- **Evaluate Multimodal Reasoning:** With the rise of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the need for benchmarks that can assess reasoning across different modalities is crucial. KORGym includes specific multimodal games to evaluate visual recognition, image matching, and spatial perception, providing a comprehensive assessment in both textual and visual contexts.
- **Provide a Scalable and Extensible Platform:** The framework is designed with modular components (Inference, Game Interaction, Evaluation, Communication Modules) to be extensible, supporting the continuous addition of new games and evaluation methodologies. This ensures KORGym remains a valuable and evolving resource for the research community.
- **Uncover Deeper Insights into LLM Behavior:** Beyond just scoring, a key motivation is to use KORGym for in-depth analysis. This includes revealing consistent reasoning patterns within model families, examining the effects of modality, understanding how different reasoning strategies (e.g., code, mathematical, natural language) are employed, and quantifying the impact of reinforcement learning techniques on problem-solving capabilities.

In essence, KORGym is motivated by the desire to build a more challenging, realistic, and insightful benchmark that can truly push the boundaries of LLM reasoning research and guide the development of more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KORGym's methodology revolves around the design and implementation of a dynamic, game-based platform to evaluate LLM reasoning, coupled with a robust scoring and analysis framework.

#### 3.1. Framework Architecture: Modular Design

KORGym is structured into four modular components, enhancing its extensibility and functionality:
- **Inference Module:** Manages the LLM inference processes, including asynchronous acceleration and intermediate result checkpointing, allowing for efficient and traceable model responses.
- **Game Interaction Module:** Encapsulates the core game logic and interaction APIs. Key APIs include `generate` (initializes game environment), `print_board` (renders the current game state and generates prompts), and `verify` (updates the game state based on LLM actions and computes intermediate scores).
- **Evaluation Module:** Handles input parameter parsing, establishes communication protocols, encapsulates and transmits data packets, and logs final evaluation scores. It acts as the orchestrator of the evaluation process.
- **Communication Module:** Facilitates seamless communication between the other modules, ensuring data flow and state updates.

#### 3.2. Inference Workflow

The typical workflow for evaluating a model on KORGym involves:
1. **Parameter Initialization:** Loading initial parameters such as game type, seed for reproducibility, and model information.
2. **Parameter Parsing and Protocol Startup:** Parsing these parameters and setting up the communication protocols.
3. **Game Environment Initialization:** Generating a specific game instance based on the parameters.
4. **Acquisition of Game Information:** The Game Interaction Module's `generate` and `print_board` APIs are invoked to obtain the current environment state (e.g., game board, instructions).
5. **Game Information Transmission:** This information is packaged and sent to the Inference Module.
6. **Generation of LLM Action:** The LLM (via the Inference Module) processes the game information and generates an action.
7. **Transmission of LLM Action:** The generated action is sent back to the Game Interaction Module's `verify` API, which updates the game state. If the game is not concluded, the process loops back to "Acquisition of Game Information" for the next turn.
8. **Score Calculation and Result Output:** Once the game ends, the final score is calculated and recorded.

#### 3.3. Task Design: Knowledge-Orthogonal Game Suite

Central to KORGym are its **over fifty novel games** designed to be "knowledge-orthogonal" (i.e., minimizing reliance on pretraining knowledge). These games span **six distinct reasoning dimensions**:
- **Mathematical and Logical Reasoning:** E.g., Sudoku, Alien (counting species based on complex traits).
- **Control Interaction Reasoning:** E.g., Plants vs. Zombies (PVZ), Minesweeper, Tetris (requiring continuous interaction and state updates).
- **Puzzle Reasoning:** E.g., Wordle, Crypto Word (evaluating linguistic capabilities and deductive reasoning).
- **Spatial and Geometric Reasoning:** E.g., Maze, Sokoban, Free the Key (emphasizing navigation, path planning, and geometric manipulation).
- **Strategic Reasoning:** E.g., 2048, Evolution of Trust, N-point (involving game theory, adversarial decision-making, and planning).
- **Multimodal Reasoning:** E.g., Jigsaw Puzzle (Visual), Circle the Cat (Visual), Wordle (Visual) (tasks requiring visual recognition, image matching, and spatial perception from visual inputs).

The games are categorized into traditional puzzles, adaptations of classic video games, game-theoretic challenges, and multimodal tasks. They support multi-turn interactions via standardized APIs, are tailored for RL (providing environment states and reward signals), and allow for configurable difficulty levels.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Method: Robust Scoring and Aggregation

KORGym employs a comprehensive scoring scheme to accurately reflect model performance:
- **Binary Scoring:** For single-objective games (success/failure, 0 or 1).
- **Proportional Scoring:** For multiple-choice or partial-credit games (number of correct responses / total options).
- **Cumulative Scoring:** For games with incremental points (accumulating points earned throughout the game).

To address score variability and skewness across diverse games, the **Capability Dimension Aggregated Mean** metric is introduced. This involves:
1. **Log-p Transformation:** Applying `ln(1 + x)` to raw scores if `max_m S_g,m > 1` to compress large values and reduce skewness.
2. **Min-Max Normalization:** Normalizing adjusted scores for each game into the `` range across all evaluated models, preserving relative differences. If all models perform identically, a normalized score of 0.5 is assigned.
3. **Dimension-wise Aggregation:** Averaging the normalized scores for all games within each specific reasoning capability dimension.

#### 3.5. Experimental Settings

- **Models Evaluated:** 19 LLMs (11 "thinking" models, 8 instruction-tuned) and 8 Vision-Language Models (VLMs) from various series (GPT, Claude, Doubao, DeepSeek, Qwen, Gemini, InternVL). "Thinking models" refer to models designed to explicitly engage in step-by-step reasoning or thought processes.
- **Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **Single-epoch Games:** Each model evaluated on 50 independently initialized game instances (varying "seed" parameter).
 - **Multiple-epoch Games:** 20 game environments initialized, with up to 100 interaction rounds per episode, and varying "seed" from 1 to 50 for reproducibility.
- **Prompting:** All assessments use a zero-shot prompting setup to minimize influence from in-context examples.
- **Hardware:** Open-source models were evaluated on eight NVIDIA A100-80G GPUs.

This rigorous methodology ensures that KORGym provides a fair, detailed, and insightful evaluation of LLM reasoning across a broad spectrum of challenges, pushing beyond the limitations of prior benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on KORGym with 19 LLMs and 8 VLMs yielded several key insights into the reasoning capabilities and behavioral patterns of these models.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Model Series Consistency

- **Superiority of Closed-Source Models:** Closed-source models generally demonstrated superior reasoning performance. O3-mini achieved the highest overall score, particularly excelling in spatial reasoning. Gemini-2.5-pro and Claude-3.7-thinking also showed strong performance, with Gemini-2.5-pro leading in mathematical and puzzle reasoning.
- **Consistent Strength-Weakness Profiles:** Models within the same series exhibited consistent profiles of strengths and weaknesses across reasoning dimensions (e.g., O1 and O3-mini excelled in spatial reasoning, while the Gemini series performed well in mathematical and puzzle reasoning).
- **Impact of Model Scale and Architecture:** Model performance scaled positively with model size. Notably, "thinking models" consistently outperformed their size-matched "non-thinking" variants, indicating the benefit of architectures or training strategies designed for explicit reasoning. For example, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, despite being smaller, outscored Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct.

#### 4.2. RQ1: Does Modality Affect Reasoning Performance?

Comparing performance on textual vs. multimodal versions of six representative games revealed:
- **General Advantage of Textual Versions:** Average scores on textual versions were consistently higher than on visual versions across most models.
- **Open-Source VLM Limitations:** Open-source VLMs performed better on text-based reasoning tasks, suggesting limitations in their visual grounding or multimodal alignment.
- **Closed-Source VLM Strengths in Visual Tasks:** Some closed-source VLMs scored higher on visual versions, indicating stronger visual reasoning capabilities or superior multimodal integration.
- **Symbolic Advantage in Math:** In mathematics-related games, models scored significantly higher on textual versions, underscoring the advantage of symbolic representation for numerical reasoning.

#### 4.3. RQ2: Do Different Model Series Exhibit Consistent Behavioral Patterns?

Analysis using mean/standard deviation of scores and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) revealed distinct behavioral patterns:
- **Homogeneous Profiles in Top-Tier Models:** Top-tier models (e.g., O1, O3-mini) formed tight clusters in PCA space, showing consistently strong and balanced reasoning performance across dimensions.
- **Distinct Patterns for Thinking vs. Non-Thinking Models:** PCA clearly separated thinking models into their own clusters (Clusters 0 and 1) from non-thinking models (Cluster 2 and 3), highlighting fundamental differences in their reasoning approaches.
- **Explicit Reasoning Paradigms:** LLMs in KORGym frequently adopted explicit reasoning paradigms during problem-solving:
 - **Code Paradigm:** Generating executable code (e.g., Python scripts).
 - **Mathematical Paradigm:** Applying algebraic equations or arithmetic rules.
 - **Algorithm-Specific Paradigm:** Invoking established algorithms (e.g., Dijkstra's, Eulerian path).
 - **Natural Language Reasoning Paradigm:** Conducting spatial, logical, or causal analysis in natural language.
- **Model-Specific Paradigm Preferences:** Models within the same series exhibited distinct preferences (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro favored code, O3-mini mathematical/natural language, Doubao-1.5-thinking-pro algorithm-specific).
- **Paradigm Constraints and Performance:** Ablation experiments (disabling specific paradigms via prompt constraints) surprisingly led to *increased* average performance across all models in many cases. This suggests that LLMs can sometimes over-rely on pretraining data rich in these paradigms, which may impede generalization to novel tasks.
- **Mathematical Reasoning as Core:** Disabling the mathematical paradigm often led to a decline or no improvement in performance, indicating its critical role.
- **Robustness of Stronger Models:** More capable models (e.g., O3-mini, Doubao-1.5-thinking-pro) showed greater robustness to having their preferred reasoning strategies constrained, maintaining near-original performance.

#### 4.4. RQ3: What is the Impact of Reinforcement Learning (RL) on Problem Solving Capabilities?

- **Significant Gains from RL:** RL-driven enhancements (e.g., specialized algorithmic frameworks like DAPO and VAPO, training on STEM and classic games) yielded substantial gains in reasoning capabilities.
- **Improved and Balanced Performance:** Doubao-1.5-thinking-pro, which incorporated extensive RL fine-tuning, achieved a high mean score and excelled in puzzle reasoning, demonstrating that appropriate RL can lead to both enhanced reasoning and more balanced performance across different dimensions.

#### 4.5. RQ4: Is There a Correlation Between Response Length and Reasoning Performance?

- **Positive Correlation:** A strong positive correlation was observed between reasoning performance and response length; models with longer responses generally achieved higher KORGym scores.
- **Distinct Distributions:** Reasoning models produced responses with a broader and more varied token distribution compared to non-reasoning models.
- **Diminishing Returns:** While longer responses were beneficial, the incremental score gains became marginal beyond a certain point, suggesting an upper limit to the benefits of verbosity.

These findings collectively offer a nuanced understanding of current LLM capabilities, their architectural influences, strategic reasoning patterns, and the potential for targeted training via reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

KORGym represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model evaluation, with profound potential impacts on both research and development.

#### 5.1. Advancing LLM Evaluation Methodologies

- **Comprehensive and Dynamic Assessment:** KORGym provides a more holistic and dynamic approach to evaluating LLM reasoning, moving beyond static, domain-specific, or single-turn benchmarks. Its suite of over fifty diverse games across six reasoning dimensions offers an unparalleled breadth of assessment.
- **Knowledge-Orthogonal Evaluation:** By implementing the principle of knowledge orthogonality, KORGym directly tackles the critical issue of conflating memorization with genuine reasoning. This ensures that models are tested on their ability to apply rules and logical deduction in novel contexts, rather than merely recalling pretraining data. This foundational shift helps measure intrinsic intelligence more accurately.
- **Interactive and Reinforcement Learning Integration:** The platform's support for multi-turn interactions and reinforcement learning (RL) scenarios allows for the evaluation of capabilities essential for intelligent agents, such as long-term planning, adaptive decision-making, and learning from environmental feedback. This moves LLM evaluation closer to real-world, agentic applications.
- **Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** The inclusion of multimodal tasks directly addresses the growing need to evaluate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) across different data modalities, crucial for developing integrated AI systems that perceive and reason in complex environments.

#### 5.2. Deeper Insights into LLM Behavior and Development

- **Revealing Intrinsic Reasoning Patterns:** The extensive experiments and analysis provide invaluable insights into the inherent strengths and weaknesses of different LLM architectures and model families. Understanding consistent profiles within model series and the distinct behavioral patterns between "thinking" and "non-thinking" models can guide future architectural designs.
- **Understanding Reasoning Paradigms:** The discovery and analysis of explicit reasoning paradigms (code, mathematical, algorithmic, natural language) employed by LLMs offer a window into their cognitive strategies. The finding that disabling certain paradigms can *improve* performance highlights potential over-reliance on pretraining data and suggests avenues for training more adaptable models.
- **Quantifying the Impact of RL:** Demonstrating that appropriate reinforcement learning can substantially enhance and balance reasoning capabilities provides strong evidence for RL's role in developing more robust and versatile LLMs. This encourages further research into RL-based fine-tuning for complex reasoning tasks.
- **Guiding Model Optimization:** Insights into the correlation between response length and performance, as well as the diminishing returns of verbosity, can inform strategies for optimizing LLM outputs, balancing explanatory detail with efficiency.

#### 5.3. Practical Impact and Future Directions

- **Standard Resource for Research:** KORGym is poised to become a valuable, extensible resource for the broader AI research community. Its open-source project page on GitHub indicates a commitment to community engagement and continuous development, allowing researchers to build upon its foundation, add new games, and contribute to evaluation methodologies.
- **Benchmarking Next-Generation LLMs:** The platform offers a robust testbed for benchmarking emerging LLMs and VLMs, providing a standardized way to compare models on truly challenging reasoning tasks. This will accelerate the development of more advanced and genuinely intelligent AI systems.
- **Informing AI Safety and Robustness:** By evaluating reasoning in out-of-distribution scenarios and understanding model robustness under constraints, KORGym contributes to the development of safer and more reliable AI, capable of handling unforeseen situations.

In conclusion, KORGym is more than just another benchmark; it is a meticulously designed platform that pushes the boundaries of how we evaluate and understand the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Its focus on knowledge orthogonality, dynamic interactivity, and multimodal challenges, coupled with its robust analytical framework, positions it as a foundational tool for fostering the next generation of truly intelligent and adaptable AI.
