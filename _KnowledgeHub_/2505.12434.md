# VideoRFT: Incentivizing Video Reasoning Capability in MLLMs via Reinforced Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named VIDEORFT, developed by researchers from Beijing Institute of Technology and Shenzhen University, enables multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to perform human-like, visually grounded video reasoning through reinforcement fine-tuning. The approach leverages a novel data curation pipeline and a specialized semantic-consistency reward, achieving state-of-the-art performance and outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o on several video reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current video MLLMs are predominantly "answer-driven," providing outputs without explicitly revealing their internal reasoning processes, limiting transparency and interpretability.
- Applying Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) to video reasoning is hampered by a scarcity of high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets with complex temporal, causal, and logical structures.
- A major challenge in cross-modal video reasoning is ensuring that generated reasoning steps are faithfully aligned with and supported by visual evidence, preventing hallucinations.

## Method

- A ==multi-expert-driven, cognition-inspired pipeline== automatically curates large-scale, high-quality video Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations, resulting in the ==VideoRFT-CoT-102K== and ==VideoRFT-RL-310K== datasets.
- A novel rule-based reward system for Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) is introduced, comprising format, accuracy, and a unique ==semantic-consistency reward==.
- The semantic-consistency reward explicitly guides MLLMs to produce visually grounded reasoning by measuring cosine similarity between video description tokens and visual features, activated only for factually correct answers.

## Results

- VIDEORFT achieved state-of-the-art performance on six challenging video reasoning benchmarks, surpassing its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) by up to **+7.2%** and outperforming GPT-4o on VSI-Bench by **+2.8%**.
- Diagnostic experiments confirmed that the cross-modal CoT refinement stage effectively mitigates visual hallucinations, and the full two-stage SFT and RL training paradigm, including the semantic-consistency reward, significantly improved performance over individual components.
- The generated VideoRFT-CoT-102K dataset exhibits more elaborate and video-centric reasoning processes, with broader distribution and longer average token lengths compared to existing video Chain-of-Thought datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, cognitively-inspired Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, curated through cross-modal refinement, is essential for training MLLMs to perform robust video reasoning and mitigate visual hallucinations.
> - Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) with a carefully designed reward system can effectively incentivize MLLMs to generate explicit, visually grounded reasoning steps for complex video content.
> - A conditional semantic-consistency reward, activated only when an answer is factually correct, is crucial for promoting visually faithful reasoning while preventing the reinforcement of visually plausible but incorrect explanations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable reasoning capabilities, particularly with the advent of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), extending these advanced reasoning abilities to complex video data remains a significant challenge. VIDEORFT directly addresses this critical gap, aiming to cultivate human-like cognitive reasoning in MLLMs for video understanding.

Historically, video understanding models primarily focused on perceptual-level tasks like action recognition or object detection. More recently, MLLMs designed for video, such as Video-ChatGPT, VILA, and Video-LLaVA, have emerged, offering improved capabilities in processing and generating responses based on video content. However, these models are predominantly "answer-driven," providing outputs without explicitly revealing their internal reasoning processes, a limitation that hampers transparency, interpretability, and true cognitive understanding.

Earlier attempts to introduce structured reasoning in video MLLMs, like VoT and STEP, relied on rigid, template-based frameworks to decompose video reasoning into predefined steps. While providing some structure, these approaches lack the flexibility and adaptability characteristic of human cognition, which can dynamically adjust reasoning based on perceptual inputs.

The broader landscape of LLM research has recently seen a significant shift towards models that "think before answering," leveraging RFT with CoT annotations (e.g., OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, Kimi-1.5). These models learn to generate explicit, step-by-step reasoning paths, leading to more robust and generalized problem-solving. This paper situates itself by extending this highly successful RFT paradigm from the language and image domains to the more challenging video domain.

VIDEORFT explicitly identifies and tackles two fundamental bottlenecks hindering the application of RFT to video reasoning:
1. **Scarcity of high-quality video CoT datasets:** Unlike text or even images, creating large-scale, detailed chain-of-thought annotations for videos, which often involve complex temporal, causal, and logical structures, is extremely challenging. Current video CoT datasets are acknowledged as lacking the necessary complexity and granularity.
2. **Ensuring visual grounding during reasoning:** In a cross-modal setting like video, it is crucial that the generated reasoning steps are faithfully aligned with and supported by the visual evidence in the video, rather than hallucinating or deriving conclusions unsupported by the visual input. Previous works have largely underexplored this aspect.

By proposing a novel data curation pipeline to generate high-quality video CoT datasets and introducing a specialized semantic-consistency reward, VIDEORFT positions itself at the forefront of research aiming to imbue MLLMs with genuinely cognitive, human-like reasoning capabilities for dynamic, real-world video content. It builds upon the success of RFT in LLMs and image MLLMs, pushing the boundaries into the more complex spatio-temporal reasoning required for comprehensive video understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **incentivize human-like video reasoning capabilities in MLLMs through a novel Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) framework, dubbed VIDEORFT.** This goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of existing video understanding systems:

1. **Bridging the Gap to Human-Level Cognition in Video:** Humans, even infants, possess an innate and sophisticated ability to reason about videos—understanding events, predicting future actions, and explaining causal relationships. Current AI models, despite advancements, are predominantly limited to perceptual-level understanding and struggle with the depth, efficiency, and interpretability of human video cognition. The paper aims to replicate this fundamental aspect of human intelligence in AI systems.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Answer-Driven MLLMs:** While recent video MLLMs have improved video understanding, they often produce direct answers without explicitly revealing their reasoning process. This "black box" nature hinders trust, interpretability, and the ability to debug or refine models. The motivation is to move towards models that "think before answering," akin to recent advancements in LLMs.
3. **Addressing the Rigidity of Template-Based Reasoning:** Previous attempts to introduce reasoning in video MLLMs, such as VoT, often rely on fixed, predefined templates for multi-step inference. This rigid structure contrasts with the adaptive and flexible nature of human cognition, limiting generalization across diverse and complex video scenarios. The objective is to enable more flexible and adaptive reasoning.
4. **Tackling the Scarcity of High-Quality Video Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Data:** A critical enabler for RFT and human-like reasoning in LLMs is the availability of extensive, high-quality CoT datasets. For the video domain, such datasets are scarce, complex to create, and fundamentally limit the ability of MLLMs to emulate advanced cognitive capabilities. A primary motivation is to overcome this data bottleneck.
5. **Ensuring Visual Grounding in Cross-Modal Reasoning:** In video reasoning, it is paramount that the model's textual reasoning traces are faithfully grounded in and consistent with the actual visual content of the video. The paper notes that how to ensure such visual faithfulness remains underexplored in existing works. Motivated by this, the research aims to develop mechanisms that explicitly promote alignment between textual reasoning and visual evidence, reducing visual hallucinations.

To address these motivations, VIDEORFT sets forth two core objectives:
- **Establish a strong CoT foundation for video RFT:** This involves designing and implementing a scalable, cognitively-inspired pipeline to automatically curate large-scale, high-quality video CoT annotations, leading to the creation of the VideoRFT-CoT-102K and VideoRFT-RL-310K datasets.
- **Develop a novel reward mechanism for effective video RFT:** This entails introducing a semantic-consistency reward that explicitly guides the MLLM during the reinforcement learning phase to produce reasoning outputs that are coherent, context-aware, and visually grounded in the input video content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VIDEORFT proposes a novel reinforcement fine-tuning framework that follows the standard two-stage scheme: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and rule-based reinforcement learning (RL) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The core innovations lie in its unique approach to generating high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data for videos and its specialized reward modeling for cross-modal reasoning.

#### 3.1. CoT Dataset Curation Pipeline: Cognitively Inspired Generation

Recognizing the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality video CoT datasets, VIDEORFT introduces a multi-expert-driven, cognition-inspired pipeline comprising three main stages:

1. **Structured Video Representation:** For each input video, the pipeline first employs a specialized MLLM (GPT-4o-mini) to extract rich, structured textual descriptions. This involves generating a high-level video caption and detailed, frame-level metadata. The metadata is structured in a JSON schema, including timestamps, captions for each frame, and key visual elements such as objects, actions, scenes, spatial relations, and potential interactions. This step effectively transforms raw video content into a comprehensive textual representation ($S_v$).

2. **Cognitively Inspired CoT Generation:** An advanced reasoning LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) is then prompted to generate initial CoT annotations based *solely* on the question and the structured video representation ($S_v$). This stage is guided by a composite prompt ($P_{cog}$) comprising five sub-prompts:
 - **Simulated observation prompt:** Instructs the model to form an initial high-level understanding of the video content.
 - **Task understanding prompt:** Encourages analysis of the question to infer the task type (e.g., factual, causal).
 - **Selective focus prompt:** Directs attention to relevant temporal segments.
 - **Visual reasoning prompt:** Grounds reasoning in visual content, analyzing objects, actions, and spatio-temporal relations.
 - **Reflective answering prompt:** Guides to the final answer, incorporating self-verification.
 This "blind reasoning" approach leverages the LLM's strong logical inference capabilities.

3. **Cross-modal CoT Refinement:** A crucial step to mitigate visual hallucinations inherent in blind reasoning. An MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) is employed to revise the initial CoTs. This MLLM compares the generated CoT with the *actual video* input, identifies inconsistencies between the textual reasoning and visual evidence, and performs necessary revisions. This process ensures that the refined CoT ($CoT_v$) is visually grounded while preserving its original logical structure.

Finally, a filtering stage ensures data quality by excluding samples with incorrect final answers (for structured tasks) or low semantic consistency between the generated answer and reference (for open-ended tasks). This pipeline results in two datasets: VideoRFT-CoT-102K for SFT and VideoRFT-RL-310K for RL.

#### 3.2. Video Reinforcement Fine-Tuning with Rule-based Reward Modeling

VIDEORFT utilizes a two-stage training paradigm:

1. **SFT Cold Start (Warm-up Phase):** The base MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is initially fine-tuned using the VideoRFT-CoT-102K dataset. This stage equips the model with the foundational ability to generate structured CoT responses and correct answers.

2. **Rule-based RL (Refinement Phase):** The model's generalization capabilities are further refined using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO is a computationally efficient RL algorithm that simplifies traditional RL by removing the need for a value model. It optimizes the policy model by sampling candidate responses, evaluating them with defined reward functions, and calculating advantages.

The core of the RL phase is a novel rule-based reward system comprising three components:

- **Format Reward ($R_f$):** A binary reward ensuring the model's output adheres to a predefined structured format, with reasoning within `<think>...</think>` tags and answers within `<answer>...</answer>` tags.
- **Accuracy Reward ($R_a$):** Task-specific metrics (e.g., Exact Match, ROUGE, WER) providing reliable supervision for factual correctness across diverse tasks.
- **Novel Semantic-Consistency Reward ($R_s$):** This is a key innovation for cross-modal grounding. Motivated by the observation that reasoning traces typically involve question parsing, *video describing*, and abstract reasoning, this reward specifically targets the "video describing" segment. It measures the cosine similarity between the token representations of the video description part (extracted using regular expressions) and the visual features of the input video. Both are encoded using a lightweight SigLIP model. The reward is scaled and bounded to stabilize training.
 The overall reward $R$ for a sample is computed as: $R = R_f + R_a + 1[R_a > 0] \cdot R_s$. The indicator function $1[R_a > 0]$ is crucial; it ensures the semantic-consistency reward is activated *only when the answer is factually correct*, preventing the reinforcement of visually plausible but incorrect reasoning.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

The base model used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Training is performed on 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs. For efficiency, video input is limited to 16 frames at 128x28x28 resolution during training, and 32 frames at 256x28x28 during inference. The training regimen involves one epoch of SFT followed by 1K steps of RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VIDEORFT demonstrates significant advancements in incentivizing video reasoning capabilities in MLLMs, achieving state-of-the-art performance across a diverse set of benchmarks.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- VIDEORFT achieves leading performance on six challenging video reasoning and understanding benchmarks: VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU, MVBench, TempCompass, and VideoMME.
- Compared to its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VIDEORFT shows substantial improvements across all benchmarks, with gains such as +5.0% on VSI-Bench, +7.2% on MMVU, and +7.0% on VideoMME. This clearly validates the effectiveness of the proposed approach.
- The model consistently outperforms all other non-RL open-source MLLMs evaluated.
- Notably, VIDEORFT surpasses the proprietary GPT-4o on VSI-Bench by +2.8%, highlighting the strong potential of RFT in bridging the performance gap with closed-source models in complex video reasoning tasks.
- When compared to concurrent R1-based models (e.g., Video-R1, TinyLLaVA-Video-R1, VideoChat-R1), VIDEORFT delivers the best overall performance, ranking first on four out of six benchmarks. This demonstrates the superiority and generalization ability of the method over other contemporary efforts.

#### 4.2. Diagnostic Experiments (Ablation Studies)

A series of diagnostic experiments provided crucial insights into the contributions of individual components:

- **Impact of Cross-modal CoT Refinement:** Ablating the cross-modal refinement stage (i.e., using initial CoTs without refinement) consistently led to performance drops across all six benchmarks (e.g., -2.3% on VSI-Bench, -7.0% on VideoMME). This strongly confirms that the refinement step effectively mitigates errors and visual hallucinations in the initial CoTs, thereby enhancing data quality and ultimately improving model performance.
- **Effectiveness of the RFT Training Paradigm:**
 - Training with SFT only (without RL) resulted in lower performance compared to the full VIDEORFT model.
 - Training with RL only (without prior SFT, dubbed "zero" model) generally surpassed the SFT-only counterpart on four out of six datasets, indicating RL's capacity to stimulate more generalized reasoning.
 - The full VIDEORFT model, combining both SFT for stable initialization and RL for reasoning enhancement, achieved the best results, substantially outperforming both SFT-only and RL-only baselines across all datasets. This validates the complementary strengths of the two-stage RFT paradigm.
- **Contribution of Semantic-Consistency Reward:**
 - Adding the semantic-consistency reward ($R_s$) to the baseline of Format and Accuracy Rewards ($R_f + R_a$) consistently improved performance.
 - The full reward formulation, which conditionally activates $R_s$ using an indicator function ($1[R_a > 0]$), yielded the best overall results. This conditional activation notably brought substantial gains on video reasoning benchmarks (e.g., +2.2% on VSI-Bench, +3.3% on MMVU, and +3.5% on VideoMME), demonstrating its effectiveness in encouraging visually grounded reasoning only for factually correct answers.

#### 4.3. CoT Data Analysis

A comparison between VideoRFT-CoT-102K and Video-R1 revealed that the CoTs in VideoRFT-CoT-102K exhibit a broader distribution and longer average token length, indicating more elaborate, fine-grained, and nuanced reasoning processes. The word cloud analysis showed VideoRFT-CoT-102K's dominance by dynamic, video-centric concepts ("video", "main", "happen", "first"), reflecting an emphasis on narrative structure and temporal progression, which are crucial for complex video understanding. This contrasts with Video-R1's bias towards static or declarative content, underscoring the superior expressiveness and depth of reasoning provided by the new dataset.

#### 4.4. Aha Moments and Scalability

Qualitatively, VIDEORFT demonstrated "Aha Moments" in its reasoning traces, where it exhibited human-like behavior by pausing and double-checking its inferences before finalizing an answer (e.g., "Wait, let me double-check it"). This suggests the model engages in internal feedback loops to re-evaluate evidence. Furthermore, an additional 3B parameter variant of VIDEORFT showed competitive performance and outperformed a contemporary lightweight 3B model (TinyLLaVA-Video-R1-3B) on several benchmarks, indicating robustness and strong generalization abilities even under resource-constrained conditions. Hyperparameter analysis also confirmed that a scaling factor of `w=2` for the semantic reward provides an optimal balance for performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VIDEORFT marks a substantial step forward in the field of multimodal AI, particularly in endowing MLLMs with advanced video reasoning capabilities. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

#### 5.1. Advancing Human-like Video Reasoning

VIDEORFT directly addresses the challenge of making AI systems understand and reason about video content in a manner more akin to human cognition. By moving beyond mere perceptual tasks and explicit answer generation towards models that can "think before answering" with verifiable reasoning traces, it pushes the frontier of video understanding from shallow processing to deeper cognitive engagement. This could pave the way for MLLMs that can not only identify objects and actions but also infer intentions, predict consequences, and explain complex spatio-temporal dynamics in videos.

#### 5.2. Addressing Critical Bottlenecks in Multimodal RFT

The work successfully tackles two major limitations that have hindered the application of Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) to video: the scarcity of high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets and the challenge of ensuring visual grounding. The proposed multi-expert, cognition-inspired CoT curation pipeline is a novel and effective solution to the data bottleneck, providing a blueprint for generating sophisticated reasoning annotations in complex multimodal domains.

#### 5.3. Novel Contributions to Reward Modeling

The introduction of the semantic-consistency reward is a crucial methodological innovation. It provides a targeted mechanism to explicitly guide MLLMs towards producing visually grounded reasoning, reducing hallucinations—a pervasive problem in MLLMs. The conditional activation of this reward, ensuring it only reinforces correct answers, is a clever design choice that enhances training stability and effectiveness. This reward modeling strategy could be adapted and extended to other cross-modal reasoning tasks where faithful grounding to perceptual input is essential.

#### 5.4. Creation of Valuable Research Resources

The development of the VideoRFT-CoT-102K and VideoRFT-RL-310K datasets represents a significant contribution to the research community. These large-scale, high-quality datasets, curated with a cognitively inspired pipeline, will serve as invaluable resources for future efforts in RFT-based video reasoning, enabling researchers to further explore and develop models capable of deeper video understanding.

#### 5.5. Broadening the Applicability of MLLMs

By enhancing video reasoning capabilities, VIDEORFT can significantly broaden the applicability of MLLMs across various domains:
- **Education:** Intelligent tutoring systems that can analyze educational video content and provide personalized explanations or assessments.
- **Security and Surveillance:** More efficient and accurate analysis of surveillance footage for anomaly detection, incident response, and forensic investigations.
- **Content Moderation:** Improved capabilities for automatically detecting and filtering inappropriate or harmful content in vast amounts of video data.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Better understanding of dynamic environments, leading to more intelligent decision-making for robots and self-driving cars.
- **Healthcare:** Analysis of medical videos (e.g., surgical procedures, diagnostic imaging) for training, assistance, and improved patient outcomes.

#### 5.6. Potential Negative Impacts and Ethical Considerations

As with any powerful AI technology, there are potential risks and ethical implications:
- **Misinformation and Bias:** Enhanced video analysis capabilities could be misused to generate convincing but false narratives, or amplify existing biases present in training data, leading to the spread of misinformation.
- **Privacy Concerns:** In surveillance contexts, improved video reasoning could intensify privacy concerns by enabling more sophisticated tracking and analysis of individuals' activities without consent.
- **Over-reliance and Misinterpretation:** Over-reliance on automated video analysis without human oversight could lead to critical errors or misinterpretations, especially in high-stakes applications.
- **Job Displacement:** Automation of tasks currently performed by humans (e.g., manual video analysis) could lead to job displacement in certain sectors.

The authors acknowledge these potential impacts, underscoring the need for careful consideration of ethical guidelines, robust evaluation, and transparent deployment of such advanced AI systems. Future research will need to continue addressing limitations, such as handling challenging video scenarios like rapid motion or severe visual occlusion, and exploring even more advanced reasoning LLMs for CoT generation to further refine and stabilize these capabilities.
