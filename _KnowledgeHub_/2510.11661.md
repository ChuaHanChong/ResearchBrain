# SR-Scientist: Scientific Equation Discovery With Agentic AI

> [!summary] Summary
> An agentic framework named SR-SCIENTIST enables large language models to function as autonomous AI scientists, discovering scientific equations through interactive data analysis, hypothesis generation, and iterative refinement. This approach consistently achieves higher precision, better out-of-domain generalization, and improved robustness to noise compared to traditional and LLM-augmented symbolic regression methods.

## Problem

- Scientific equation discovery (symbolic regression) is an NP-hard problem due to a vast search space, often leading to inefficient and unscalable solutions with traditional methods.
- Existing LLM-based symbolic regression approaches typically restrict LLMs to passive roles, like proposing equations within predefined human pipelines, limiting their autonomy for active data analysis and iterative refinement.
- Traditional genetic programming and deep learning methods for symbolic regression often yield black-box models or struggle with interpretability, which is crucial for scientific insight.

## Method

- The SR-SCIENTIST framework employs an LLM as an ==autonomous agent==, guided by a goal and utilizing a ReAct-like trajectory for iterative reasoning, tool invocation, and observation over long horizons.
- The agent interacts with the environment through a code interpreter providing specialized tools: a `data_analyzer` for flexible data inspection and an `equation_evaluator` for constant optimization and metric reporting.
- An end-to-end reinforcement learning pipeline, including a mixed data synthesis strategy and a continuous log-linear reward function, is used with the GRPO algorithm to enhance the agent's problem-solving capabilities.

## Results

- SR-SCIENTIST consistently outperformed all baseline methods (traditional and LLM-augmented) on the LSR-Synth benchmark, achieving an absolute performance margin of **6%** to **35%** in precision across various LLM backbones.
- The framework demonstrated superior generalization to out-of-domain data and maintained higher performance under Gaussian noise, indicating enhanced robustness to imperfect or noisy scientific observations.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of the `data_analyzer` tool and the experience buffer for achieving high performance, and increasing interaction turns significantly improved results for long-horizon optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming LLMs into autonomous agents, capable of independent data analysis and iterative equation refinement via tools, fundamentally shifts their role from passive proposers to active scientific investigators.
> - An experience buffer that stores and retrieves top-performing equations effectively serves as long-term memory, mitigating LLM context limitations and enabling more efficient long-horizon optimization.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with a continuous reward function, allows the agent to self-evolve its scientific discovery capabilities and problem-solving strategies without rigid human-defined pipelines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Scientific equation discovery, also known as symbolic regression (SR), is a fundamental yet challenging task in science. It involves identifying concise and interpretable mathematical expressions that accurately describe the underlying relationships within observed data. Historically, SR has been an NP-hard problem due to its vast search space.

Traditionally, the field has relied on two main families of methods:
- **Genetic Programming (GP)-based Methods:** These approaches use evolutionary algorithms to search through a combinatorial space of symbolic operators, constants, and variables. While effective for certain problems, they often face limitations in efficiency and scalability as the complexity of the search space grows.
- **Deep Learning Methods:** More recently, deep neural networks have been employed to learn direct mappings from numerical data to mathematical expressions, either through online or offline learning. These methods, while powerful, can also suffer from efficiency and scalability issues and often yield black-box models that lack the interpretability crucial for scientific insight.

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has introduced a new paradigm to symbolic regression. LLMs, with their vast embedded scientific knowledge, have been integrated into traditional techniques, primarily as "equation proposers" within GP algorithms. This integration has shown promise in enhancing search efficiency by generating more informed hypotheses. However, a significant limitation of these existing LLM-based methods is that LLMs largely function as static components within human-crafted pipelines. They lack the autonomy to actively analyze data, experiment with hypotheses, and refine their equations through continuous interaction with the environment.

Concurrently, the broader field of Artificial Intelligence has witnessed the rise of "Agentic AI." This paradigm shifts LLMs from simple knowledge retrievers to autonomous agents capable of completing complex, long-horizon tasks by interacting with dynamic environments. These agents exhibit characteristics analogous to human scientists, such as sustained interaction with feedback, operational autonomy, and reduced reliance on rigid, predefined workflows. While Agentic AI has seen applications in areas like software engineering and user interface operations, its application to scientific discovery, particularly symbolic regression, has been limited.

SR-SCIENTIST positions itself at the intersection of LLM-based symbolic regression and Agentic AI. It aims to address the shortcomings of current LLM-SR methods by transforming the LLM from a passive hypothesis generator into an active, autonomous AI scientist. This work represents a significant step towards empowering LLMs to drive the entire scientific discovery lifecycle, rather than merely serving as tools within human-orchestrated processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind SR-SCIENTIST stems from the recognized limitations of existing LLM applications in scientific discovery. While LLMs possess extensive scientific knowledge valuable for hypothesis generation, current approaches typically confine them to passive roles, such as proposing equations within predefined search algorithms like genetic programming. This static role prevents LLMs from fully leveraging their potential for autonomous exploration, data analysis, and iterative refinement, which are hallmarks of human scientific inquiry.

The researchers identified a critical gap: despite the rise of Agentic AI, its application to complex scientific discovery tasks, like symbolic regression, has been underdeveloped. They argue that building a scientific discovery framework around agentic models could fundamentally shift the paradigm, empowering LLMs to act as autonomous drivers of discovery rather than mere passive tools.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the SR-SCIENTIST framework are:

1. **Elevate LLM's Role to an Autonomous AI Scientist:** The primary goal is to transform the LLM from a simple equation proposer to an intelligent agent capable of independently analyzing data, implementing equations as code, submitting them for evaluation, and optimizing them based on experimental feedback.
2. **Enable Long-Horizon Optimization:** To facilitate more sophisticated hypothesis generation and refinement, the framework aims to allow the LLM agent to interact with data and tools over extended sequences of turns, gathering sufficient information for effective equation design.
3. **Minimize Human-Defined Pipelines:** A crucial objective is to reduce reliance on rigid, human-defined workflows, granting the agent the flexibility and autonomy to determine its own problem-solving strategy for a given scientific task.
4. **Develop an End-to-End Reinforcement Learning Framework:** To enhance the agent's capabilities and enable self-evolution, the project aims to integrate a comprehensive reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline, covering training data construction and reward design.
5. **Achieve Superior Performance and Robustness:** Empirically, the framework seeks to significantly outperform existing state-of-the-art symbolic regression methods across multiple scientific disciplines, demonstrating improved precision, generalization to out-of-domain data, robustness to noise, and higher symbolic accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SR-SCIENTIST proposes a comprehensive methodology for autonomous scientific equation discovery, structured around an LLM agent that interacts with its environment through specialized tools and an experience buffer, further refined by a reinforcement learning (RL) framework.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as symbolic regression: given a dataset $D = \{(x_i, y_i)\}$, find a concise and interpretable symbolic function $f$ such that $f(x_i) \approx y_i$. The primary metric for optimizing the equation during the agent's interaction is the Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), aiming for a specified precision goal. For final evaluation and comparison with other methods, the Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) is used.

#### 3.2. Inference Framework

The inference framework (Algorithm 1, Figure 1) outlines how the LLM agent autonomously conducts long-horizon optimization.
- **LLM Agent:** The central component, guided by a goal $G_i$ (a desired MAPE precision). It operates iteratively, interleaving internal reasoning with external environment feedback, typically following a ReAct-like trajectory of (reasoning, tool invocation, observation). A maximum number of interaction turns ($M$) is set to manage inference time.
- **Tool Design:** The agent interacts with the environment via a code interpreter wrapped into two specialized tools:
 - **`data_analyzer` (T1):** This tool provides the agent with the ability to write and execute arbitrary Python code to inspect and analyze the observed data. This includes tasks like examining data samples, performing statistical analyses, or analyzing residuals of proposed equations. The tool is linked to the data, and prompt examples demonstrate data access, but the agent is not constrained to these examples, allowing for diverse analysis patterns.
 - **`equation_evaluator` (T2):** This tool accepts an equation skeleton in Python code format, with placeholders for constants. During execution, it uses the BFGS algorithm from SciPy to optimize these constants against the observed data and then reports performance metrics (Mean Squared Error, Normalized Mean Squared Error, and Mean Absolute Percentage Error) and success status. This design streamlines equation evaluation, preventing the agent from repeatedly writing optimization code.
- **Experience Buffer:** To mitigate the context length limitations of LLMs and to leverage past explorations, an experience buffer $E = \{(e_i, s_i)\}$ stores explored equations and their corresponding MAPE scores. At the beginning of each iteration, the top-$K$ best-performing equations from the buffer are fetched and provided to the agent as in-context examples. The optimization goal $G_i$ is dynamically updated if the previous goal was achieved. This mechanism serves as a long-term memory for the agent.
- **Minimal Human-Defined Pipelines:** A core principle is to allow the agent to determine its own workflow for problem-solving, rather than following rigid, predefined human pipelines.
- **Stopping Condition:** The process concludes either when a maximum number of iterations is reached or when an equation achieves a sufficiently small error. The best-performing equation is then selected for final evaluation.

#### 3.3. Training Framework

SR-SCIENTIST incorporates an end-to-end reinforcement learning pipeline to enhance the LLM agent's capabilities.
- **Training Data Construction:** A mixed strategy of rule-based and model-based data synthesis is employed. An LLM is prompted to synthesize scientific scenarios and potential equation skeletons (combining known and novel terms). For each skeleton, the LLM determines constant values based on physical significance, followed by human validation. Data points are then generated by sampling variables within defined ranges, producing training, in-domain (ID), and out-of-domain (OOD) sets. A rigorous filtering process removes scientifically anomalous data, resulting in 1024 distinct problems for RL training.
- **Reward Design:** To guide the RL process, a continuous reward function is designed based on the MAPE score ($s$) of the best equation found within an iteration. A log-linear reward function (Equation 2) is used: $R = \text{clip}\left(\frac{\lg s_{\text{max}} - \lg s}{\lg s_{\text{max}} - \lg s_{\text{goal}}}, 0, 1\right)$, where $s_{\text{max}}$ is the maximum MAPE for a non-zero reward (100%) and $s_{\text{goal}}$ is the target MAPE (0.1%). This continuous reward avoids sparsity commonly found in binary reward systems.
- **Training Algorithm:** The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is applied for optimization. To encourage exploration, the KL penalty term against a reference model is omitted, which was found to accelerate convergence and maintain performance. The objective (Equation 3) maximizes the average advantage over a group of sampled outputs from the old policy. Training is conducted on 32 NVIDIA H200 GPUs using the verl framework, with SGLang for asynchronous rollouts and FSDP for training, for 60 steps, with a maximum of 20 turns per rollout.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SR-SCIENTIST was rigorously evaluated against multiple baselines on the LSR-Synth benchmark, a dataset carefully curated to prevent LLMs from relying on memorized equations. The evaluation primarily used accuracy-to-tolerance (Acc_τ) and symbolic accuracy (SA).

- **Precision (Table 1):** SR-SCIENTIST consistently outperformed all baseline methods, including traditional GP-based, deep learning, and other LLM-based approaches. Across five different LLM backbones, SR-SCIENTIST achieved an absolute performance margin of 6% to 35% over the baselines. Notably, when powered by GPT-OSS-120B, it achieved the highest overall accuracy (Acc_0.01 of 63.57% and Acc_0.001 of 49.35%). Furthermore, for the Qwen3-Coder-30B model, end-to-end RL training significantly boosted its performance across all scientific subjects, underscoring the agent's ability to evolve its problem-solving capabilities.

- **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Performance (Figure 2, Appendix D.3):** The discovered equations demonstrated strong generalization capabilities. SR-SCIENTIST consistently achieved superior performance on out-of-domain data compared to other methods, suggesting that the agent learns fundamental relationships rather than merely overfitting to the training distribution. This generalization was evident across various scientific disciplines.

- **Robustness to Noise (Figure 3):** When Gaussian noise was introduced to the training data, all methods experienced performance degradation. However, SR-SCIENTIST maintained superior performance, especially with larger LLM backbones like Qwen3-Coder-480B and GLM-4.5-Air. This indicates its enhanced robustness to imperfect or noisy observational data, a common challenge in real-world scientific experiments.

- **Symbolic Accuracy (Table 2, Figure 4):** Identifying equations identical to the ground truth remains a significant challenge. Nevertheless, SR-SCIENTIST achieved the best symbolic accuracy among all tested methods, correctly identifying more ground truth equations. Qualitative examples from physics problems (PO10 and PO37) demonstrated SR-SCIENTIST's ability to discover the correct structure and constants for non-linear oscillators, often yielding simpler and more accurate equations than baselines. The framework also generates detailed derivation trajectories, which can provide valuable insights for human scientists.

- **Ablation Studies (Table 3):** Critical components of SR-SCIENTIST were investigated:
 - **Data Analysis (w/o T1):** The ability of the agent to perform data analysis via the `data_analyzer` tool was found to be crucial, particularly for powerful LLM backbones like GPT-OSS-120B and Qwen3-Coder-480B. Its removal led to significant performance drops.
 - **Experience Buffer:** Both directly removing the experience buffer ("w/o experience") and randomly sampling from it instead of fetching the top-K equations ("w/o top-k") resulted in notable performance degradation. This highlights the importance of the experience buffer for long-term memory and efficient exploration.

- **Effect of Turn Number (Figure 5):** Increasing the maximum number of turns for agent-environment interaction from 10 to 25 significantly improved overall performance. This validated the importance of long-horizon optimization. However, performance stagnated or slightly decreased beyond 25 turns, suggesting that excessively long individual explorations might not be optimal and that resources should be balanced with initiating new iterations.

- **Tool Call Analysis (Figure 6):** An analysis of tool usage revealed distinct patterns. Qwen and GLM families typically allocated about 80% of calls to `equation_evaluator` and 20% to `data_analyzer`, with `data_analyzer` use primarily for data statistics (e.g., correlations). GPT-OSS models, conversely, showed a greater propensity to write custom code for residual error analysis and even to directly determine equation constants through data analysis, indicating higher flexibility in their analytical approach. RL training also influenced Qwen3-Coder-30B to increase its use of data statistics for more advanced analysis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SR-SCIENTIST framework represents a significant advancement in the application of Large Language Models to scientific discovery, particularly in the domain of symbolic regression. Its core contribution is the transformation of LLMs from passive equation proposers into autonomous, agentic scientists capable of driving the entire discovery process through interactive data analysis, hypothesis generation, and experimental refinement.

**Key Contributions:**
1. **Autonomous Agent Framework:** SR-SCIENTIST is a novel framework that empowers LLMs to act as autonomous agents in scientific equation discovery, utilizing long-horizon, tool-driven data analysis and equation evaluation.
2. **Superior Performance:** The framework consistently and significantly outperforms existing baseline methods (both traditional and LLM-augmented) across diverse scientific disciplines in terms of precision, generalization to out-of-domain data, robustness to noise, and symbolic accuracy.
3. **Reinforcement Learning for Self-Evolution:** The development of an end-to-end reinforcement learning pipeline enables the agent to continuously learn and enhance its scientific discovery capabilities through experience.

**Potential Impact and Implications:**
- **Paradigm Shift in Scientific Discovery:** SR-SCIENTIST heralds a shift from AI as a computational assistant to AI as an active, independent scientific investigator. This could accelerate scientific progress by enabling automated discovery of fundamental physical laws and relationships, reducing the manual effort involved in formulating and testing hypotheses.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** By focusing on symbolic equations, the framework ensures that the discovered models are not only accurate but also interpretable, a critical requirement for scientific understanding and theory building, differentiating it from opaque deep learning models.
- **Advancement of Agentic AI:** This work demonstrates the profound potential of agentic LLMs in tackling complex, open-ended scientific challenges. It extends the applicability of Agentic AI beyond traditional software or information tasks into the realm of scientific research, paving the way for more sophisticated autonomous AI systems.
- **Democratization of Science:** By automating parts of the discovery process, SR-SCIENTIST could make advanced scientific research more accessible and efficient, allowing researchers to focus on higher-level conceptualization and validation.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its strengths, the authors acknowledge several limitations that present avenues for future research:
- **Text-Only Modality:** The current framework primarily uses text-only LLMs. Integrating multimodal inputs, such as visual plots and charts, could enable richer data analysis patterns and potentially greater insights.
- **Noise Handling Strategies:** While robust to noise, SR-SCIENTIST still experiences performance drops in highly noisy environments. Future work could involve explicitly incorporating advanced noise-handling strategies into the agent's prompts or tools to guide its analysis in such scenarios.
- **Memory Optimization:** The experience buffer currently stores and retrieves top-performing equations. Optimizing the agent's memory system, perhaps by distilling higher-level "lessons learned" or "strategies" from past experiences rather than just equations, could prevent redundant exploration and further enhance efficiency.
- **Broader Scientific Tasks:** The current focus is on equation discovery. Future work could extend the agentic framework to other facets of scientific discovery, such as experimental design, material synthesis, or theoretical model construction.

In conclusion, SR-SCIENTIST represents a pivotal step towards realizing the vision of autonomous AI scientists. By endowing LLMs with the ability to actively engage in the scientific method—analyzing data, forming hypotheses, testing them, and refining them through iterative feedback—this work not only pushes the boundaries of symbolic regression but also significantly contributes to the broader field of Agentic AI and AI for Science.
