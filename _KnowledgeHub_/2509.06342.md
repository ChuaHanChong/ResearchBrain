# Towards bridging the gap: Systematic sim-to-real transfer for diverse legged robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The PACE framework systematically aligns simulation physics with reality using a minimal set of physically interpretable parameters, enabling zero-shot policy transfer for diverse legged robots. It trains energy-efficient locomotion policies by integrating a physics-grounded energy model, resulting in a 32% reduction in Cost of Transport for ANYMAL D (CoT 1.27) and a CoT of 0.97 for TYTAN.

## Problem

- The notorious simulation-to-reality gap causes policies trained in simulation to perform poorly or fail on real robotic hardware, especially due to actuator dynamics inaccuracies.
- Many existing RL policies for legged locomotion neglect actuator-specific energy losses, leading to suboptimal endurance and limited operational time for real robots.
- Designing effective RL controllers often relies on complex, high-dimensional, and labor-intensive hand-tuned reward functions that lack clear physical interpretations.

## Method

- The Precise Adaptation through Continuous Evolution (PACE) framework uses a three-stage bottom-up approach, starting with isolated in-air joint data collection.
- Simulator alignment is achieved by optimizing a compact set of physically interpretable parameters (inertia, damping, friction, bias, command delay) via Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) to match real robot joint dynamics.
- Locomotion policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in the precisely aligned simulator, integrating a physics-grounded energy model for Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motors (PMSMs) into a compact, four-term reward function.

## Results

- PACE-fitted simulators achieved near-overlap with real measurements for in-air trajectories across TYTAN, ANYMAL, and MINIMAL, outperforming URDF-only baselines and matching state-of-the-art actuator networks.
- Policies trained with PACE demonstrated a 32% reduction in Cost of Transport (CoT) for ANYMAL D (CoT of 1.27) compared to previous work, and an even lower CoT of 0.97 for TYTAN, by enabling more efficient gaits.
- The framework allowed for successful zero-shot deployment of policies on diverse robots, enabling dynamic behaviors such as two-legged balancing, high-speed running (near 3 m/s), and continuous stair climbing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A minimal set of physically interpretable parameters (e.g., 4n+1 for n joints) is sufficient to accurately model joint-space dynamics, bridging the sim-to-real gap with as little as 20 seconds of encoder-only real-world data per robot.
> - Integrating a physics-grounded energy model directly into the RL reward function effectively optimizes for electrical and mechanical efficiency, leading to substantial reductions in the robot's Cost of Transport.
> - Precise simulator alignment via parameter identification eliminates the need for dynamics randomization during RL training, simplifying the learning process and enabling robust zero-shot policy transfer to hardware.
