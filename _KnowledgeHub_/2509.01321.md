# Towards High Data Efficiency in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> The DEPO (Data-Efficient Policy Optimization) framework significantly improves the data efficiency of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for large reasoning models by implementing a two-stage data selection pipeline. This approach achieves comparable performance to training on full datasets while substantially reducing training time by up to 1.85x and using less than 60% of total training time and 40% of rollout budgets.

## Problem

- Current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on extensive data and numerous reasoning rollouts, leading to prohibitively high computational costs and low data efficiency.
- Previous offline data selection techniques often require initial training or extensive rollouts on the full dataset to compute selection metrics, while online methods lack fine-grained metrics for evaluating the exploration potential of samples.
- The scaling strategy of simply increasing training data and rollouts introduces significant drawbacks, making advanced LLM training resource-intensive and environmentally costly.

## Method

- DEPO introduces a ==two-stage pipeline== combining offline data curation and online rollout pruning to optimize data efficiency in RLVR training.
- The offline stage utilizes a ==PageRank-weighted Determinantal Point Process (DPP)== on a representation-augmented sample graph to prune data for diversity and influence, followed by ==difficulty-aware normal distribution sampling== to create a high-quality, compact training subset.
- The online stage dynamically prunes rollouts during training using a novel ==explorability metric== to prioritize samples with high exploration potential, complemented by a dynamic replay mechanism for under-explored samples and an adaptive decay rate for high-explorability focus.

## Results

- DEPO, using only **20%** of the training data, achieved performance nearly matching GRPO trained on the full dataset, outperforming all other offline baselines across **five** reasoning benchmarks.
- When combined with online pruning, DEPO demonstrated a **1.85x** speed-up on AIME24 and **1.66x** speed-up on AIME25, using less than **60%** of total training time and only **40%** of rollout budgets while maintaining comparable accuracy.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all DEPO components, particularly difficulty-aware sampling and explorability measurement, are critical for its efficiency and performance, with under-explored sample replay being especially beneficial for challenging tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curating a dataset based on sample diversity, influence, and especially moderate difficulty (via normal distribution sampling) can achieve performance similar to full datasets with significantly fewer samples.
> - Dynamically measuring and prioritizing samples with high "explorability" (based on entropy and recent rewards) during online training can drastically reduce the number of computational rollouts without sacrificing model performance.
> - Integrating a mechanism to dynamically replay under-explored samples is crucial to prevent valuable samples from being permanently ignored, particularly for more challenging tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper directly addresses a critical and highly relevant challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their application to complex reasoning tasks. Recent advancements in LLMs, such as OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and Kimi, have showcased remarkable reasoning capabilities, largely attributable to the integration of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR paradigms, including methods like PPO and GRPO, train LLMs by having them explore multiple reasoning trajectories (known as "rollouts") and receive binary rewards based on the correctness of their final answers. This iterative refinement process is essential for unlocking the full reasoning potential of LLMs.

However, the prevailing strategy to enhance RLVR performance—scaling up the amount of training data and the number of rollouts—introduces significant drawbacks. This "brute-force" scaling leads to prohibitively high training costs and inherently low data efficiency, creating a major bottleneck for further development and widespread adoption of these advanced models.

Prior work has attempted to mitigate these issues through various data selection strategies, categorized into offline and online approaches:
- **Offline Data Selection:** Previous studies have explored curating high-quality RLVR datasets *before* training. Methods have focused on metrics such as historical reward trends, reward variance, and gradient alignment to identify beneficial samples. While these approaches can activate reasoning abilities, they often suffer from high computational costs themselves, as they typically require initial training or extensive rollouts over the *entire dataset* to compute the necessary metrics for selection. They also often overlook the interdependencies between training samples, focusing primarily on individual sample properties.
- **Online Data Selection:** These methods aim to improve rollout efficiency *during* RLVR training, as rollout generation is a significant computational bottleneck. For instance, methods like GRESO identify and filter out samples that contribute minimally to policy updates, such as those with zero historical reward variance. However, a limitation of such approaches is their lack of fine-grained metrics for evaluating the "exploration potential" of samples that do exhibit non-zero variance, treating them all equally and potentially missing opportunities for more strategic resource allocation.

DEPO (Data-Efficient Policy Optimization) directly addresses these limitations by proposing a comprehensive, two-stage data selection pipeline. It uniquely combines optimized strategies for *both* offline and online data selection, offering a more holistic solution than previous fragmented approaches. By tackling data redundancy and computational inefficiency at both the dataset curation and dynamic training phases, DEPO positions itself as a significant advancement in making large-scale RLVR training more sustainable, accessible, and efficient, thereby accelerating the progress of reasoning-capable LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the **data efficiency** of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in the context of large reasoning models. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific aims:

- **Reduce extensive rollout computation:** RLVR training requires LLMs to generate numerous reasoning trajectories (rollouts) to explore solution spaces. This process is computationally very expensive and is a major bottleneck in training speed. A core objective is to minimize these wasteful computations.
- **Mitigate high training costs:** The necessity of large datasets and extensive rollouts in current RLVR methods translates directly into high monetary and environmental costs. The research seeks to lower these costs, making advanced LLM training more accessible.
- **Improve overall data efficiency:** Beyond just reducing computation, the aim is to ensure that the data used for training provides the most effective learning signals. This means optimizing for which samples are chosen and when their rollouts are performed, to maximize the policy improvement per unit of computational effort.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the current state of LLM development. While RLVR has proven highly effective in boosting the reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art LLMs, the scaling strategies currently employed (more data, more rollouts) are becoming unsustainable. The authors highlight that this "scaling strategy introduces significant drawbacks, that is, it substantially increases training cost and results in low data efficiency." By developing a data-efficient pipeline like DEPO, the researchers aim to unlock further reasoning potential in LLMs without the prohibitive resource expenditure, thereby democratizing access to and accelerating the development of next-generation reasoning AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed solution, **DEPO (Data-Efficient Policy Optimization)**, is a two-stage pipeline designed to optimize data efficiency in RLVR training by integrating strategies for both offline and online data selection. The overall framework is illustrated in Figure 2.

**Stage 1: Offline Data Selection (Diversity, Influence, and Difficulty-aware Data Curation)**
This stage aims to curate a high-quality, compact subset of the RLVR dataset before the main reinforcement learning training begins.

1. **Representation-augmented Sample Graph Construction:**
 - The first step involves creating a graph representation of the dataset to capture relationships between samples.
 - For each sample, the last token embedding from the LLM's final layer is used as its representation, as these embeddings are known to effectively capture sample characteristics.
 - A sample graph $G = (V, E, P)$ is constructed, where vertices ($v_i$) are samples, and the edge weight matrix ($P$) encodes pairwise similarities between their embeddings.

2. **PageRank-weighted Determinantal Point Process (DPP) Data Pruning:**
 - This step prunes redundant data from the initial dataset, focusing on two key properties: **diversity** and **influence**.
 - **Diversity** is promoted using Determinantal Point Process (DPP), which selects a subset maximizing the determinant of its similarity submatrix. Geometrically, this corresponds to selecting samples that form a larger volume in the feature space.
 - **Influence** is measured using PageRank, which assigns a weight ($w_i$) to each sample, reflecting its representativeness or importance within the graph structure.
 - The two objectives are unified by constructing a combined kernel matrix $L_Y = \text{diag}(w_Y^{1/2}) \cdot S_Y \cdot \text{diag}(w_Y^{1/2})$ and maximizing its determinant (Equation 1).
 - An efficient greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) is employed to approximate the solution, iteratively selecting samples based on a dynamically updated probability distribution and using Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization.

3. **Difficulty-aware Normal Distribution Sampling:**
 - After pruning, the resulting subset ($Y$) is further refined based on sample difficulty, aligning it with the model's learning needs.
 - **Offline rollouts** are performed *only on this pruned subset* (not the full dataset) using the current LLM policy ($\pi_\theta$) to compute an accuracy score ($Acc_i$) for each sample (Equation 2). This serves as a measure of difficulty.
 - Samples are then drawn from $Y$ according to a normal distribution $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation of the accuracies in the *final selected subset*. This strategy assigns higher sampling probabilities to samples of **moderate difficulty**, which are deemed most informative for policy optimization, avoiding samples that are too easy or too hard. The final RL training subset $D_{sub}$ is thus created.

**Stage 2: Online Data Selection (Explorability-guided Rollout Pruning)**
During active RLVR training, this stage dynamically filters samples to reduce computationally expensive rollouts while ensuring adequate training.

1. **Sample-level Explorability Measurement:**
 - A novel explorability metric ($E$) is introduced to quantify the "exploration potential" of each sample.
 - This metric aggregates entropy-based signals over a sliding window of recent epochs (Equation 4). It prioritizes samples with high entropy, as these are more likely to yield diverse reasoning paths and offer new learning signals.
 - To prevent instability from pathological (e.g., nonsensical) high-entropy negative samples, a threshold ($\lambda$) is applied to exclude them. The metric also incorporates normalized rewards.
 - Samples with low explorability are strategically skipped for rollouts, saving computational resources.

2. **Dynamic Replay of Under-explored Samples:**
 - To prevent samples that might become valuable later from being permanently ignored due to initial low explorability, a dynamic replay mechanism is integrated.
 - Each training batch is composed of two types of samples: (1) those ranked in the top $\alpha_e$% by their current explorability score, and (2) a $\rho$% proportion of samples that have been the *least explored* throughout the entire training process.
 - The proportion of high-explorability samples ($\alpha_e$) is adaptively reduced over epochs using a linear decay rate ($d$) (Equation 6), shifting the focus from broad exploration early on to more refined training later.
 - The overall optimization objective for DEPO combines the standard policy gradient update with this dynamic sample selection (Equation 5, with indicator function $I_h$ for selection).

By combining these offline and online strategies, DEPO aims to reduce both the initial data volume and the computational cost associated with rollouts during training, while maintaining or improving model performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across five reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, GPQA, LiveCodeBench) using three different LLMs (Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Deepseek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, Qwen2.5-7B-Math) demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of DEPO.

#### 4.1. Performance and Efficiency Gains

- **Superior Performance in Offline Selection:** DEPO, when applied offline to select only 20% of the training data, achieved performance nearly matching that of GRPO trained on the *full* dataset. This significantly outperforms all other offline baselines (Random, PPL-Top, PPL-Middle, LIMR, Learnalign). This indicates DEPO's ability to curate a high-quality subset that retains most of the learning signal.
- **Dramatic Speed-ups with Online Pruning:** When DEPO's offline selection is combined with its online explorability-guided rollout pruning, the efficiency gains are substantial.
 - Notably, using only 20% of the training data, DEPO achieved a **1.85x speed-up on AIME24** and a **1.66x speed-up on AIME25** compared to the GRPO baseline trained on the full dataset, while maintaining comparable accuracy (Figure 1).
 - Overall, DEPO achieved performance comparable to full rollouts while using less than 60% of the total training time and only 40% of the rollout budgets (Table 1).
- **Improved Training Dynamics:** Analysis of training dynamics (Figure 3) showed that DEPO consistently selected samples with higher initial entropy, lower initial rewards, and longer response lengths. This implies that DEPO effectively identifies more challenging and diverse samples that offer greater exploration potential, leading to faster reward growth and more efficient learning throughout the training process.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- Ablation studies (Table 2) confirmed that all components of DEPO are crucial.
- **Difficulty-aware Sampling** in the offline phase showed the most significant performance drop when removed, underscoring the importance of selecting samples with appropriate difficulty.
- **Explorability Measurement** in the online phase was also critical; replacing it with random filtering led to a substantial performance decline, validating explorability as a key indicator of sample value.
- **Under-explored Sample Replay** was particularly beneficial for challenging tasks (e.g., AIME25), suggesting its importance for ensuring comprehensive training.

#### 4.3. Detailed Analysis

- **Robustness Across Datasets and Ratios:** DEPO demonstrated strong performance across different RLVR training datasets (HARP, DAPO, Open-R1) and varying offline sampling ratios. Performance plateaued around 20% data usage, indicating the presence of significant redundancy in larger datasets.
- **Optimal Difficulty Distribution:** Sampling based on a normal distribution of difficulty outperformed random and stratified sampling, highlighting that moderate-difficulty samples are most beneficial. Further analysis revealed that while medium-difficulty samples accelerate early learning, incorporating a wider range of difficulty (larger standard deviation) is crucial for higher final convergence.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Appropriate settings for hyperparameters, such as the decay rate ($d$) for explorability and the replay sample ratio ($\rho$), were found to significantly reduce rollouts while maintaining performance, demonstrating the fine-tuning potential of the system (Figure 7, Figure 8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DEPO represents a significant advancement in the field of large language models and reinforcement learning, with several profound implications:

- **Addresses a Critical Bottleneck in LLM Scaling:** The most immediate impact of DEPO is its ability to tackle the prohibitive computational costs and data inefficiency inherent in scaling RLVR methods for advanced reasoning tasks. By drastically reducing the need for extensive rollouts and large datasets, DEPO makes the training and fine-tuning of powerful reasoning LLMs more feasible and sustainable.
- **Democratization of Advanced LLM Training:** The high resource demands of current RLVR techniques often limit their application to institutions with massive computational infrastructure. DEPO's efficiency gains lower this barrier, potentially democratizing access to and accelerating research for smaller labs, universities, and organizations.
- **Accelerated Research and Development Cycles:** Reduced training times mean faster iteration cycles for researchers and developers. This allows for quicker experimentation with new models, architectures, and training strategies, ultimately speeding up the pace of innovation in LLM capabilities.
- **Improved Sustainability:** Large-scale LLM training is notoriously energy-intensive. By significantly reducing the computational load (fewer rollouts, smaller effective datasets), DEPO contributes to more environmentally sustainable AI development practices.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Robustness:** The method's effectiveness across multiple LLMs, diverse reasoning benchmarks (mathematics, general Q&A, code generation), and varying dataset sizes suggests its general applicability and robustness, making it a valuable tool across different problem domains.
- **Foundation for Future Data-Efficient RL:** DEPO's multi-faceted approach, combining offline data curation with dynamic online filtering and replay, sets a new benchmark for data efficiency in RLVR. It inspires future research into more sophisticated ways of quantifying sample value, dynamic curriculum learning, transfer learning, and meta-learning strategies tailored for data-efficient RL in LLMs.
- **Practical Industry Impact:** The involvement of Ant Group underscores the practical relevance of this work for industry. Efficient RLVR training can lead to more cost-effective development and deployment of LLMs for real-world applications, such as complex problem-solving in finance, scientific discovery, or advanced automation, where reliable reasoning is paramount.
