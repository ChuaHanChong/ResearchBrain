# RecSim: A Configurable Simulation Platform for Recommender Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> RecSim is a configurable open-source platform for authoring recommender simulation environments that support sequential user interaction. It composes a document model, a user model with latent state and transition dynamics, and a user choice model, wrapped as an OpenAI Gym environment with stackable hierarchical agent layers. Case studies show exploration and slate RL agents lift simulated click-through rate substantially over myopic baselines.

## Problem

- Static recommender datasets, even temporally extended ones, cannot answer counterfactual questions about how alternative action sequences would change user behaviour, blocking reinforcement learning on logged data.
- Logs come from myopically engagement-optimising systems, so their action distributions diverge from long-horizon policies and undermine off-policy correction such as importance weighting.
- Existing RL platforms benchmark fixed environments, and prior recommender simulators offer bandit-style feedback without evolving user latent state, so key recommender challenges go untested.

## Method

- A three-part environment, document model, user model with latent features and a transition model, user choice model (==multinomial logit==, ==exponentiated cascade==), formalised as a ==dynamic Bayesian network== over slates, choices and observations.
- A simulator control loop wrapping the environment in ==OpenAI Gym==, logging each episode as a TensorFlow SequenceExample for batch RL, with a separate evaluation process for robustness testing under shifted choice models.
- Stackable hierarchical agent layers, `ClusterClickStatsLayer`, `FixedLengthHistoryLayer`, `TemporalAggregationLayer`, that pre/post-process observations and slates around base agents, plus baselines `TabularQAgent`, `FullSlateQAgent`, `RandomAgent` and bandit algorithms.

## Results

- In the high-topic-affinity latent-bandit environment, UCB1 reached **25.17%** average CTR and FullSlateQ **23.28%**, versus **14.97%** for random and **17.56%** for the omniscient greedy agent.
- Under low topic affinity the spread collapsed: UCB1 **9.76%** and FullSlateQ **9.64%** against random **7.86%**, roughly **24%** relative gain, confirming exploration matters mainly when interest dominates quality.
- Slate-decomposed TD-learning with greedy serving captured much of full Q-learning's long-term engagement gain over myopic policies and stayed robust to user-choice-model mismatch; temporal aggregation nearly matched fully observed satisfaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stylised, deliberately imperfect simulators beat high-fidelity ones for algorithm research: their value is stressing modelling assumptions, not sim-to-real transfer of deployable policies.
> - Exploration's payoff depends on environment structure: when latent interest dominates item quality, bandit and RL agents beat greedy; otherwise myopic policies are nearly optimal.
> - Separating agent-side history statistics into reusable wrapper layers decouples feature engineering from algorithm design, letting one regulariser or aggregator be applied to any base agent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Evolution of Recommender Systems

The research landscape of recommender systems (RSs) is rapidly evolving. Traditionally, RSs, often based on collaborative filtering, focused on myopically maximizing predicted user engagement (e.g., through item ratings). However, advances in AI, machine learning, and natural language understanding are facilitating the development of **collaborative interactive recommenders (CIRs)**. CIRs explicitly use sequences of interactions, often through conversational methods, preference elicitation, or bandit-based exploration, to maximize long-term user engagement or satisfaction over extended horizons. This shift moves beyond static recommendations to dynamic, interactive processes that better reflect real-world user experiences.

#### 1.2. Challenges in Applying Reinforcement Learning to RS

The paper identifies CIRs as a natural application area for **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, given their sequential and interactive nature. Despite increasing research activity, the practical deployment of CIRs remains limited due to several fundamental challenges that mainstream RL literature often overlooks:
- **Generalization across users:** Real-world RSs interact with millions of unique users, each potentially representing a distinct partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP). RL algorithms must generalize across these diverse users, often by modeling them as contextual MDPs, as individual user data is often insufficient for robust learning.
- **Combinatorial, large, dynamic, and stochastic action spaces:** Recommending slates (multiple items simultaneously) creates vast combinatorial action spaces. Furthermore, the pool of recommendable items is often dynamic, with constant flux in content availability, popularity, and freshness, posing significant challenges for fixed-action-space RL algorithms.
- **Severe partial observability and stochasticity:** User latent states (interests, satisfaction, mood) are inherently unobservable and must be inferred from noisy signals. Exogenous events further complicate behavioral interpretation, leading to low signal-to-noise ratios, which hinders effective learning.
- **Long horizons:** Some aspects of user state evolve very slowly, requiring RL agents to reason about extremely long interaction horizons to optimize for long-term engagement, a challenging task for many current RL methods.
- **Other practical challenges:** These include accurate off-policy estimation from logged data, costly live policy evaluation, and the complexities of optimizing multiple, often conflicting, evaluation criteria.

Existing approaches often rely on static datasets (which don't support counterfactual action predictions needed for RL) or proprietary, specially crafted synthetic user models, making systematic and reproducible research difficult.

#### 1.3. RECSIM's Position

RECSIM is positioned as a critical tool to address these challenges. It aims to provide a **configurable platform for authoring simulation environments** specifically designed for sequential interactive recommendation problems. Its goal is not to create a "perfect" simulator for direct deployment but to serve as a controlled environment for developing, evaluating, and comparing recommender models and algorithms, particularly those designed for sequential user-system interaction. RECSIM allows researchers to create stylized environments that reflect specific aspects of user behavior found in real systems, pushing the limits of current RL and RS techniques. By being open-source, it also promotes reproducibility and sharing within the research community, fostering greater engagement at the intersection of RL and RecSys. For practitioners, RECSIM can challenge assumptions made in standard RL algorithms, identify pitfalls, and reduce live experiment cycle times by enabling rapid development and refinement in simulation.

#### 1.4. Comparison with Related Work

The paper contrasts RECSIM with existing simulation and RL platforms:
- **RL Platforms (e.g., OpenAI Gym, Dopamine, ELF, Natural RL Benchmarks):** While sharing some goals, RECSIM differs in its core emphasis. OpenAI Gym provides a collection of environments for benchmarking, but RECSIM focuses on *authoring* environments to push algorithm development for *new domain characteristics*. Dopamine provides RL algorithms but not environment authoring. ELF allows configuration for real-time strategy games but prioritizes fast performance and specific training regimes. Natural RL benchmarks augment tasks with richer state inputs, while RECSIM focuses on configuring basic new structures in state space, action space, observability, and dynamics using stylized models to challenge fundamental RL assumptions. RECSIM integrates with OpenAI Gym to facilitate RL experimentation.
- **RecSys and Dialogue Environments (e.g., RecoGym, Rule-based dialogue systems, GAN-based user simulators):** RecoGym, while RL-based and supporting sequential interaction, doesn't allow user state transitions; its focus is more on bandit-style feedback within the learning agent. Rule-based dialogue systems and learning-enhanced rule-based environments aim to aid dialogue agent evaluation. More recently, GANs have been used to generate virtual users for high-fidelity environments for "sim-to-real" transfer. RECSIM, however, emphasizes the *ease of authoring environments using stylized models*, rather than focusing on high-fidelity "sim-to-real" transfer. This approach allows industrial researchers to share qualitative and quantitative observations of user interaction without revealing sensitive data, thereby facilitating academic-industrial collaboration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind RECSIM is to **facilitate the study and advancement of Reinforcement Learning algorithms in the context of recommender systems**. This overarching goal breaks down into several specific objectives:

- **Address Challenges of Sequential Interaction:** To create environments that naturally support sequential interaction with users, moving beyond traditional static datasets that hinder the application of RL.
- **Enable Configurability and Flexibility:** To allow researchers and practitioners to easily configure environments by varying assumptions about user preferences, item familiarity, user latent state dynamics, and choice models. This flexibility is crucial for isolating and studying specific aspects of user behavior and algorithmic challenges.
- **Push the Limits of RL and RS Techniques:** To provide abstract, yet representative, environments where existing RL and RS methods can be challenged and extended, particularly in solving complex sequential interactive recommendation problems that present issues like combinatorial action spaces, partial observability, and long horizons.
- **Aid Reproducibility and Sharing:** To serve as an open-source platform that enhances reproducibility of research and facilitates the sharing of models and environments within the research community, thereby accelerating progress.
- **Reduce Live Experiment Cycle Time and Risk:** For practitioners, RECSIM aims to reduce the need for costly and potentially user-impacting live experiments by providing a rapid development and model refinement environment in simulation.
- **Foster Academic-Industrial Collaboration:** To act as a vehicle for collaboration by allowing industrial researchers to share "stylized user models" that reflect real-world user behavior and system characteristics, without compromising user data or proprietary practices. This allows academia to address realistic problems while respecting commercial sensitivities.

**Non-objectives:** The paper explicitly states that RECSIM does not directly provide learning algorithms (like collaborative filtering or RL agents). Instead, it supports their development and comparison. Furthermore, the goal is not to create a "perfect" or high-fidelity simulator for direct "sim-to-real" transfer, but rather to enable the creation of "stylized user models" that illuminate specific aspects of user behavior relevant to particular research questions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RECSIM is designed as a modular and configurable simulation platform that mirrors the interactions between a recommender agent and users within an environment.

#### 3.1. Core Simulation Components

The RECSIM environment fundamentally consists of three main components:
- **User Model:** Samples users from a prior distribution over configurable user features. These can be latent (e.g., personality, satisfaction, interests), observable (e.g., demographics), or behavioral (e.g., session length, time budget). Crucially, it includes a **User Transition Model** that dictates how a user's state evolves after consuming a document (e.g., interest in a topic increasing/decreasing, budget depleting).
- **Document Model:** Samples items from a prior over document features. These can also be latent (e.g., document quality) or observable (e.g., topic, length, global statistics like popularity).
- **User Choice Model:** Determines how a user responds to a recommended slate of documents. This includes which document (if any) is chosen, and other behavioral responses like time spent or post-consumption ratings. The choice depends on both observable document features and all user features (latent and observable). Examples include multinomial logit or exponentiated cascade models.

The interplay of these components defines a dynamic Bayesian network that describes the probability distribution over interaction trajectories (slates, choices, observations, and latent user states).

#### 3.2. Simulator Architecture and Control Flow

The **Simulator** acts as the interface between the environment and the recommender agent. The interaction follows a structured, multi-step control flow (currently sequential for single users, with future plans for concurrent execution):
1. **Environment State Query:** The simulator requests observable and latent user features from the User Model, and a set of candidate documents from the Document Model.
2. **Observation to Agent:** The observable user state and candidate documents are sent to the agent. The agent does not have direct access to latent user features.
3. **Agent Recommendation:** The agent, using its current policy, returns a slate of recommended documents to the simulator.
4. **User Choice Determination:** The simulator forwards the recommended slate and the *full* user state (observable and latent) to the User Choice Model.
5. **User Response:** The User Choice Model, based on its configured functions, generates the user's choice and other responses, returning them to the simulator.
6. **State Update and Feedback:** The simulator sends the user's choice and response to:
 - The User Model, which updates the user's state via the User Transition Model.
 - The Agent, allowing it to update its policy based on the feedback.

An "episode" consists of a multi-turn history with a single user, terminating when the episode is long enough or the user enters a terminal state. An "iteration" groups multiple episodes for bookkeeping and metric aggregation. RECSIM also supports logging interaction traces as Tensorflow SequenceExamples for offline (batch) RL training.

#### 3.3. Environment Configuration and Integration

RECSIM's core strength is its configurability. Developers can:
- Customize the level of observability for user and document features.
- Define arbitrary complexity for user states and their relationships to documents.
- Vary the user transition and choice models to reflect different behaviors.

Once defined, a RECSIM environment can be wrapped in an **OpenAI Gym** environment, making it compatible with a wide range of existing RL algorithms and frameworks. This integration ensures easy adoption by the RL community. Developers are required to define a reward function within the Gym wrapper, representing the primary evaluation criterion for the agent.

RECSIM also leverages **Tensorflow's Tensorboard** for visualizing aggregate metrics during training and evaluation. It includes a separate evaluation process that can run in parallel across multiple users, allowing for robustness testing (e.g., evaluating an agent trained under one user choice model against users with a different choice model).

#### 3.4. Recommender Agent Architecture

RECSIM provides abstractions and APIs for building configurable agents with reusable modules, moving beyond specific RL algorithms. It features a **stackable hierarchical agent layer** architecture, similar to Keras layers:
- A hierarchical layer receives observations and rewards, preprocesses them, passes them to one or more base agents, and then post-processes their outputs to form a concrete action (slate).
- These layers can serve various roles: feature injectors (extracting features from observation history), regularizers (modifying rewards), or implementing stateful and dynamic mechanisms (like parameter updates).
- Examples include `ClusterClickStatsLayer` (logging per-topic impression/click stats for bandit-style exploration), `FixedLengthHistoryLayer` (recording recent observations), and `TemporalAggregationLayer` (reducing control frequency or adding switching cost regularization to improve performance in low-SNR, slow-evolving environments).

RECSIM also ships with **baseline agents** to facilitate evaluation:
- `RandomAgent`: Recommends random slates.
- `TabularQAgent`: Implements Q-learning for small, discretized state-action spaces.
- `FullSlateQAgent`: A deep Q-Network (DQN) agent that treats each slate as a single action.
- Adapters for integrating Dopamine's DQN.
- Standard multi-armed bandit algorithms (e.g., UCB1) for exploration experimentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results (Case Studies)

The paper illustrates RECSIM's utility through three case studies, each highlighting a different research problem in sequential recommendation:

#### 4.1. Latent State Bandits

This case study investigates the value of exploration in environments where user latent topic interests are unobservable to the agent. The environment is configured such that user choice is influenced by both topic affinity and document quality.
- **Configuration:** Single-item recommendation with documents having a single topic and quality. Users have static interest vectors.
- **Finding:** When topic affinity strongly influences user choice (high topic affinity environment), exploration and planning strategies (UCB1 and Q-learning agents utilizing `ClusterClickStatsLayer`) significantly outperform myopic (greedy) and random agents in terms of total user clicks. UCB1 and `FullSlateQAgent` showed gains of 68.14% and 55.51% over the random agent, respectively, in the high topic affinity setting. This demonstrates that RECSIM can be used to quantitatively show the importance of actively discovering user latent states for long-term engagement.

#### 4.2. Tractable Decomposition for Slate RL

This study, based on prior work by Ie et al. (2019) using RECSIM, addresses the challenge of large combinatorial action spaces in slate recommendation by testing decomposition techniques for estimating Q-values.
- **Configuration:** User topic interests are observable but shift with document exposure. Topics have varying average quality. Document quality impacts the user's time budget decay.
- **Finding:** RL approaches that plan for long-term interactions significantly improve overall engagement compared to myopic policies. A decomposed TD/Q-learning algorithm that exploits assumptions about user choice behavior was shown to capture substantial gains, even with greedy slate construction at serving time. Crucially, RECSIM demonstrated that this decomposition technique is **robust to user choice model shifts**, meaning performance gains are maintained even if the assumed user choice model deviates from the true model. This highlights RECSIM's value in testing algorithm robustness under environmental uncertainties.

#### 4.3. Advantage Amplification over Long Horizons

This case study, drawing from Mladenov et al. (2019), explores challenges in environments where user latent states evolve slowly and signals are noisy (low SNR).
- **Configuration:** Documents vary in "quality" (chocolate vs. kale), with "chocolate" content leading to immediate high engagement but "kale" content increasing long-term user satisfaction. User satisfaction evolves stochastically and slowly.
- **Finding:** The study investigated hierarchical policies that use **temporal aggregation** (repeating actions for a period) or **temporal regularization** (penalizing action switching). These methods, implemented as hierarchical agent layers in RECSIM, were shown to amplify the advantage function, making the learned policy less susceptible to low-SNR effects. Temporal aggregation, in particular, improved the quality of learned policies to a level comparable to scenarios where user satisfaction was fully observed, showcasing a powerful approach for overcoming long-horizon and partial observability challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RECSIM represents a significant contribution to both the Reinforcement Learning and Recommender Systems research communities, as well as to industrial practitioners.

- **Advancing RL for Real-World Applications:** By providing a structured and configurable environment that models the complexities of user interaction, RECSIM offers a crucial stepping stone for applying RL beyond traditional game or control environments to real-world RSs. It helps researchers tackle specific challenges like combinatorial action spaces, partial observability, and long-term user dynamics that are often overlooked in mainstream RL literature.
- **Systematic RS Research:** RECSIM enables more systematic exploration of RL methods in RS research, moving beyond ad-hoc synthetic models or static datasets. Its configurability allows researchers to isolate variables and test the robustness of algorithms under varying assumptions about user behavior and system dynamics. This fosters a deeper understanding of which algorithms perform best under what conditions.
- **Reproducibility and Collaboration:** As an open-source platform integrated with popular frameworks like OpenAI Gym and Tensorflow, RECSIM promotes reproducibility of research findings and facilitates the sharing of environments and models. This communal resource can accelerate research at the intersection of RL and RecSys.
- **Bridging Academic and Industrial Research:** A key impact is its potential to foster academic-industrial collaboration. By allowing industrial researchers to share "stylized user models" that capture specific aspects of real-world user behavior and system characteristics (without revealing sensitive data), RECSIM provides a common ground for research. This aligns academic efforts with practical challenges faced by industry, leading to more relevant and impactful research outcomes. It can help reduce the development cycle time for new RS features and minimize the risk of negative user impact by testing innovations in simulation first.

**Future Directions and Broader Vision:** The authors outline exciting next steps, including methodologies to **fit stylized user models using production usage logs**. This "sim-to-real" alignment (rather than direct transfer) will make RECSIM environments even more representative of specific commercial RSs, further enhancing collaboration and research relevance. Additionally, plans to support **concurrent execution** will improve simulation throughput and enable the investigation of multi-user phenomena like "distributed exploration." Future releases also aim to incorporate **richer mixed-mode interactions**, including preference elicitation, example critiquing, and natural language interaction, acknowledging the evolving complexity of modern CIRs. Overall, RECSIM is poised to become a foundational tool for pushing the boundaries of interactive recommendation systems research.
