# ARMADA: Augmented Reality for Robot Manipulation and Robot-Free Data Acquisition

> [!summary] Summary
> The ARMADA system, developed by researchers from Apple and the University of Colorado Boulder, uses Apple Vision Pro to enable robot-free data acquisition for imitation learning. It provides real-time augmented reality feedback of a virtual robot's execution, leading to a substantial increase in the replay success rate of human demonstrations on physical robots, with improvements of up to 85.4% observed.

## Problem

- Robot imitation learning faces a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality human demonstration datasets due to limitations in traditional data collection methods.
- Teleoperation requires constant access to physical robot hardware, creating a bottleneck that limits the quantity and diversity of collectible data.
- Learning directly from passive human videos is challenging due to the 'embodiment gap,' as human kinematics and dynamics differ significantly from robot capabilities, making actions difficult to directly translate.

## Method

- The **ARMADA** system leverages ==Apple Vision Pro== to provide real-time augmented reality feedback of a virtual robot overlaid on a user's bare hands, enabling robot-free data acquisition.
- Human hand and finger movements are tracked by ARKit and translated into virtual robot control commands via inverse kinematics for the robot arm and a threshold for gripper control.
- Real-time visual cues (e.g., color changes for singularity, red walls for workspace limits, 'Slow Down' text for velocity) are provided to guide human demonstrators towards robot-compatible actions.

## Results

- Demonstrations collected with real-time AR feedback achieved substantially higher replay success rates on physical robots, with increases of **76.0%** for 'Pick Tissue', **48.0%** for 'Declutter', and **85.4%** for 'Bimanual Wipe' compared to conditions without feedback.
- Even without live AR feedback, prior exposure to the system improved replay success rates (e.g., 'Pick Tissue' rose from **2.7%** to **37.3%**), indicating implicit learning by human demonstrators.
- Participants rated the robot visualization as highly intuitive (mean **6.4** 1 **0.6**) and useful (mean **6.0** 1 **1.2**) for understanding robot motion and predicting behavior across varying levels of prior robotics and VR/AR experience.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time augmented reality feedback significantly guides human demonstrators to produce motions that are directly compatible with robot kinematics and operational constraints.
> - The system effectively bridges the 'embodiment gap' by allowing humans to intuitively understand and adapt to robot limitations without needing a physical robot during demonstration.
> - Consumer-grade AR devices enable a scalable and accessible method for collecting high-quality robot demonstration data, democratizing data collection beyond specialized labs.
