# SynAgent: Generalizable Cooperative Humanoid Manipulation via Solo-to-Cooperative Agent Synergy

> [!summary] Summary
> SynAgent, a framework for cooperative humanoid manipulation, enables generalizable and physically plausible control by leveraging abundant single-human interaction data to bootstrap multi-agent behaviors. It achieves higher success rates and lower object manipulation errors on complex cooperative tasks compared to existing methods, facilitating precise, trajectory-conditioned control across diverse object geometries.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality, large-scale datasets for human-object-human interaction (HOHI) with physical fidelity.
- The exponential increase in joint action space for cooperative manipulation leads to difficulties in policy optimization, convergence, and generalization.
- Existing methods struggle to generalize across diverse interaction patterns and object geometries, often lacking precise trajectory-conditioned control.

## Method

- An interaction-preserving motion retargeting method and a "train-to-filter" strategy to create a high-quality, physics-validated HOHI dataset from raw motion capture.
- A solo-to-cooperative agent synergy paradigm that pretrains decentralized policies on single-agent data before adapting them to multi-agent cooperative behaviors.
- A generalizable trajectory-conditioned policy, implemented as a conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), which is distilled from multi-teacher imitation policies and utilizes geometry-aware interaction graphs.

## Results

- Achieved a 45.00% success rate and an average episode length of 78.74 for multi-agent imitation, substantially outperforming the state-of-the-art InterMimic method (7.26% success, 51.91 length).
- Demonstrated improved cooperative object transportation with a 19.16% success rate and 0.7939m average distance error, surpassing CooHOI (7.28% success, 1.3074m error) on diverse, complex objects.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of solo-to-cooperative skill transition, data refinement (retargeting and filtering), and the interaction graph for achieving robust performance and generalization in cooperative manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging abundant single-human interaction data for pretraining is crucial for bootstrapping and stabilizing learning in complex multi-agent cooperative tasks, overcoming HOHI data scarcity.
> - Data quality, ensured by interaction-preserving retargeting and automatic filtering, is paramount for training robust physics-based control policies for multi-agent systems.
> - Decentralized policy architectures with shared weights prove more effective for multi-agent imitation than centralized policies, despite the lack of explicit inter-agent communication.
> - Embedding geometry-aware interaction graphs within a VAE-based policy significantly enhances generalizability and precision for trajectory-conditioned manipulation across diverse object shapes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence focuses on developing systems that can perceive, reason, and act within physical environments. A significant portion of existing research in this area addresses single-robot locomotion and isolated object manipulation. However, real-world applications often necessitate multi-robot systems capable of synergistic operation in shared, dynamic environments. This requires robust cooperative manipulation and precise control, which remain challenging to achieve.

A primary limitation in advancing multi-agent manipulation is the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale datasets capturing human-object-human interaction (HOHI). While datasets for single-person motion, dual-human interaction, and human-object interaction (HOI) exist, HOHI data is often limited in scale and physical fidelity. Furthermore, cooperative manipulation inherently involves an exponentially larger joint action space compared to single-agent tasks, leading to difficulties in optimization, convergence, and generalization across diverse interaction patterns, object geometries, and coordination scenarios. Existing methods for HOHI often simplify objects or rely on highly engineered reward systems, limiting their generalizability.

This work, SynAgent, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenges of data scarcity and complexity in controllable cooperative humanoid manipulation. It aims to bridge the gap between single-agent motor proficiency and multi-agent collaborative synergy, moving beyond isolated robotic skills to achieve more complex multi-agent system capabilities. It specifically contributes a unified framework designed for scalable and physically plausible cooperative manipulation that generalizes across diverse object geometries and supports trajectory-conditioned control, an aspect largely unaddressed by prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SynAgent is to enable generalizable and physically plausible cooperative manipulation for humanoid agents by overcoming significant challenges in embodied intelligence. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current research:

- **Data Scarcity for Human-Object-Human Interaction (HOHI):** Most available datasets focus on single-person or single-human-object interactions, leaving a substantial gap in high-quality data that captures complex multi-human, multi-object scenarios. This data bottleneck hinders the development of physically grounded control policies for cooperative manipulation.
- **Complexity of Multi-Agent Coordination:** The joint action space in multi-agent systems expands rapidly with the number of agents, leading to increased difficulty in training stability, optimization, and convergence of control policies. Methods effective in restricted single-agent settings often fail to scale or generalize to cooperative scenarios.
- **Limited Generalization:** Existing approaches frequently struggle to generalize across diverse interaction patterns, novel object geometries, and unseen coordination scenarios due to reliance on specific task configurations or overly simplified object representations.
- **Lack of Trajectory-Conditioned Control:** Many cooperative manipulation systems focus on goal-conditioned object transportation (moving an object to a target position) rather than precise, trajectory-conditioned control, which is necessary for executing complex manipulation paths.

To address these motivations, SynAgent sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Develop an Interaction-Preserving Retargeting Method:** To mitigate skeletal discrepancies and maintain semantic integrity during motion transfer from human demonstrators to humanoid agents, particularly in interaction scenarios.
- **Introduce a Solo-to-Cooperative Agent Synergy Paradigm:** To leverage abundant single-human interaction data for bootstrapping multi-agent cooperative behaviors, thereby addressing the HOHI data scarcity problem.
- **Propose a Multi-Teacher Distillation Framework:** To synthesize diverse motion priors into a unified trajectory-conditioned policy, enabling stable and controllable object-level trajectory execution and generalization across various object geometries.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a robust and scalable framework that can transfer individual manipulation skills into coordinated multi-humanoid behaviors, advancing the capabilities of embodied intelligence in collaborative tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SynAgent is a unified framework designed with three core components: interaction-preserving motion retargeting, a solo-to-cooperative agent synergy paradigm, and a generalizable trajectory-conditioned policy.

#### 3.1. A. Interaction-Preserving Motion Retargeting

This component addresses the challenge of transferring human motion capture (MoCap) data, which often contains skeletal discrepancies and artifacts, to simulated humanoid agents. The process involves four steps:
1. **Shape Fitting:** A differentiable SMPL-X proxy model is used to bridge the morphological gap between MoCap actors and target agents. This is achieved by optimizing SMPL-X shape parameters ($\beta_b$) to match the target agent's initial joint positions, transforming the retargeting problem into a mesh-to-mesh alignment (Eq. 1).
2. **Retargeting:** To preserve interaction semantics (spatial relationships among humans and objects), an "Interact Mesh" is constructed using Delaunay tetrahedralization. For each interaction frame, tetrahedrons are formed by selecting two joints from one human, one object mesh vertex, and one joint from the other human. The retargeting objective minimizes the Laplacian deformation energy of these Interact Meshes, enforcing their invariance. Additional terms in the objective function (Eq. 2) include temporal smoothness, joint position and velocity limits, and a foot-sliding penalty to ensure physically plausible motions.
3. **Smooth:** A post-processing step is applied to reduce jitter in the retargeted motion. Root translation trajectories are smoothed using Sobolev norm regularization, solving linear equations. Joint rotations are de-jittered using a pre-trained SmoothNet filter, which processes rotation sequences in a sliding-window manner.
4. **Filter:** A "train-to-filter" strategy iteratively cleans the dataset. A policy is initially trained on the full data, and motion clips resulting in consistently early terminations (episode lengths below average) are identified as low-quality and removed. The policy is then retrained on the filtered data, repeating until performance converges. This process aims to ensure the training data is reliable and physically consistent.

#### 3.2. B. Single-Agent Pretraining for Cooperation

This paradigm enables the transfer of skills from single-agent human-object interaction to multi-agent scenarios using a decentralized training scheme with shared weights.
1. **Reinforcement Learning Formulation:**
 - **State:** The policy observes two consecutive states (o_t, o_t+Δt). Each observation (o) includes an agent-object observation (o_ao), an interaction graph (ig), and a delta interaction graph (Δig). The interaction graph encodes vectors from agent joints to nearest object vertices, providing geometric contact information. The agent observation (o_a) covers joint positions, rotations, linear/angular velocities, and contact indicators, along with their deviations from reference. The object observation (o_o) includes object position, orientation, linear/angular velocities, and deviations from the reference object trajectory.
 - **Reward:** The reward function (Eq. 3) is designed to encourage motion imitation and physical realism. It consists of an imitation reward penalizing state deviations, a contact reward promoting desired contacts within an adaptive threshold (0.07m for contact, 0.07-0.2m buffer, >0.2m penalty), and an energy efficiency term penalizing high-frequency joint motion and excessive contact forces. Importantly, a consistent set of hyperparameters is used across all tasks and objects.
 - **Policy & Action:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) within an actor-critic architecture parameterizes the policy. It takes the concatenated observation vector and outputs joint PD targets (exponential map) for one agent, which are converted into torques.
2. **Training Stages of Imitation Policies:**
 - **Stage I (Solo Skill Acquisition):** Single-human HOI data is partitioned into N subsets, and N separate policies are trained. The single-human data is then augmented by duplicating the human, either identically or with random nearby positioning. A multi-agent policy is trained on this augmented data using Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO), with shared weights initialized from the single-agent policies. Inter-agent collisions are initially disabled to allow agents to occupy similar space, then enabled for subsequent training/inference. Agents perceive each other through object dynamics.
 - **Stage II (Cooperative Skill Adaptation):** The set of policies from Stage I ({π_s}) is distilled into a unified "Base Model" to provide robust initialization. This Base Model is then adapted and trained on multi-human HOHI data (CORE4D) to yield a set of multi-agent policies ({π_m}). These {π_s} and {π_m} form the "Motion Imitation Policies" for the subsequent stage.

#### 3.3. C. Generalizable Trajectory-Conditioned Policy

The final model, SynAgent, is implemented as a conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE).
- **Architecture:** It removes reference agent state observations from its input. The encoder receives agent states, interaction graph, and object states (including reference object state as a control signal). The condition for the CVAE is a T × 3 sequence of object translations (trajectory). The encoder outputs a latent vector (z), which is concatenated with object states and passed to the decoder to generate actions.
- **Distill from Imitation to Generation:** To address the ill-posed nature of trajectory-conditioned control, pre-trained imitation policies (π_imit) are used to process raw reference data, generating physically consistent state-action pairs (s_e, a_e). These pairs serve as supervision for the CVAE, transforming the open-ended problem into a more constrained one.
- **Progressive Skill Acquisition:** A DAgger-style schedule (Algorithm 1) controls the learning process using hyperparameters ϵ, κ, and T_imit. Initially (t <= κ), only actions from the imitation policies (a_e) are executed in simulation. As training progresses (κ < t < ϵ+κ), the probability of executing actions from π_imit and the weight of the action imitation loss are linearly annealed. After κ+ϵ episodes, only actions from the CVAE are executed. For the first T_imit epochs, the full imitation reward is used. Beyond T_imit, a sparse reward based solely on object trajectory tracking error is employed, encouraging the policy to explore autonomous action sequences and improve generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SynAgent's effectiveness was evaluated through extensive experiments, comparisons with baselines, and ablation studies, utilizing a curated dataset combining OMOMO (single-human HOI) and CORE4D (multi-human HOHI).

#### 4.1. A. Datasets

- **OMOMO:** Approximately 10 hours of single-human HOI data, partitioned into 17 subsets based on actor body shapes. Refined via physics-based re-tracking with trained imitation policies, yielding 2,888 physically plausible motions for single-agent pretraining.
- **CORE4D:** Initially 11,000 collaboration sequences. Synthetic portions were excluded due to low physical fidelity. Real-world MoCap data was distilled to 961 sequences, and processed into 3 usable subsets for multi-human training after discarding low-quality categories (e.g., boards, sticks) that caused non-convergence.
- **Final Dataset:** Aggregated 2,960 physically validated motion sequences from 20 subsets (17 HOI, 3 HOHI), covering 9 major object categories and 25 unique object geometries.

#### 4.2. B. Imitation and Control Performance

- **Comparison of Imitation:**
 - SynAgent demonstrated substantial superiority over InterMimic, a state-of-the-art HOI imitation method, in dual-agent scenarios (Table I, IV, V).
 - InterMimic, while strong in single-human HOI, showed significant performance degradation in multi-human settings when an additional agent introduced physical interference. Its success rate was 7.26%, with an average episode length of 51.91.
 - SynAgent (using MAPPO-based joint training) achieved a 45.00% success rate and an average episode length of 78.74, along with higher rewards and lower human and object errors (E_h = 0.2376, E_o = 0.2387). This indicates that MAPPO-based training is critical for robust multi-agent imitation.
- **Comparison of Control:**
 - SynAgent consistently outperformed CooHOI, a goal-conditioned object manipulation method, in cooperative object transportation across nearly all object categories, especially non-box-shaped and complex geometries (Table II, VIII).
 - CooHOI exhibited severe generalization failures due to its abstraction of objects into simple 8-keypoint bounding boxes and lack of dexterous hand modeling. Its success rate was 7.28% with an average distance error of 1.3074m across all objects.
 - SynAgent (Ours-2 variant) achieved a 19.16% success rate and a significantly lower average distance error of 0.7939m. This highlights the advantage of SynAgent's VAE-based trajectory-conditioned control, geometry-aware interaction graph, and ability to handle dexterous hands.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation and Analysis

- **Centralized vs. Decentralized Policy (Table I):** Decentralized policy architecture (shared-weight policies controlling individual agents) yielded better empirical performance (19.92% success rate) compared to a centralized policy (12.61% success rate). The centralized approach, despite allowing explicit information sharing, led to increased learning difficulty due to the larger action space.
- **Solo-to-Cooperative Skill Transition (Table III, VII):**
 - Initializing multi-human imitation policies from the Base Model (distilled from single-human policies) was crucial. Without this initialization ("w/o init"), performance degraded significantly (3.33% success rate, 16.37 average reward, high errors), often leading to non-convergence.
 - This confirms that leveraging skill priors from abundant single-human data is essential for stable and effective learning in multi-agent cooperative manipulation.
- **Data Refinement (Table III, VI, VII):**
 - **Interaction-Preserving Retargeting:** Eliminating this component ("w/o retarget") resulted in lower performance (2.42% success rate, 20.25 reward) due to uncorrected motion artifacts from skeletal discrepancies.
 - **Automatic Filtering:** Removing the train-to-filter strategy ("w/o filter") led to the poorest performance (1.43% success rate, 13.74 reward, very high errors). This demonstrated that raw MoCap data contains a substantial amount of erroneous and low-quality sequences that retargeting alone cannot fix. Filtering was shown to improve success rates and enable the model to learn motions previously deemed unlearnable.
- **Interaction Graph in CVAE (Table II):** The "Ours-2" variant, which explicitly includes the Interaction Graph in the CVAE's latent vector concatenation, performed marginally better than "Ours-1" (19.16% vs. 18.41% success rate, 0.7939m vs. 0.8192m distance error). This indicates that providing the decoder with precise spatial relationship cues enhances the model's ability to execute stable and accurate trajectory-conditioned manipulation across diverse object shapes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SynAgent introduces a comprehensive framework that addresses fundamental challenges in controllable cooperative humanoid manipulation, making several contributions to embodied intelligence and multi-agent robotics.

- **Scalable and Physically Plausible Cooperative Manipulation:** By effectively transferring skills from single-agent human-object interaction to multi-agent scenarios, SynAgent offers a scalable pathway to develop complex collaborative behaviors. The decentralized training with shared weights is inherently more scalable than centralized approaches.
- **Enhanced Data Utilization:** The interaction-preserving retargeting method and the train-to-filter data cleaning strategy significantly improve the quality and usability of scarce HOHI data. This is crucial for learning robust physics-based control policies, especially given the current limitations of available datasets.
- **Generalization Across Diverse Objects and Trajectories:** The trajectory-conditioned generative policy, trained through multi-teacher distillation from motion imitation priors and leveraging geometry-aware interaction graphs, enables precise and stable object-level control. This allows the system to generalize across a broader range of object geometries and execute specific manipulation paths, moving beyond simple goal-reaching.
- **Bridging Isolated Skills to Coordinated Behaviors:** The solo-to-cooperative agent synergy paradigm demonstrates that robust multi-agent coordination can be bootstrapped from abundant single-human experience. This approach provides a practical foundation for advancing from individual manipulation skills to complex, coordinated multi-humanoid behaviors without requiring extensive, specialized multi-agent supervision.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** SynAgent is presented as a pioneering exploration in physics-based, dual-agent cooperative manipulation. While current limitations include the scarcity of high-quality HOHI data preventing learning of more diverse and complex interaction behaviors beyond object transportation, the framework is designed to scale efficiently as better datasets become available. Its advancements lay groundwork for future research in embodied intelligence, physics-based animation, and human-robot interaction involving complex multi-agent collaboration.

The work's demonstrated improvements in imitation success and trajectory completion rates over existing methods, especially in the context of challenging HOHI tasks, highlight its potential to accelerate progress in creating intelligent agents capable of synergistic operation in human-centric environments.
