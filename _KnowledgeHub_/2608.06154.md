# Visual Grounding in Zero-Shot Vision-Language Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Rigorous evaluation using an input-ablation battery reveals that zero-shot Vision-Language Model (VLM) controllers often achieve high task performance through conservative actions or simulator dynamics rather than genuine visual grounding. The study identifies widespread failures in lateral perception but demonstrates VLMs can reliably function as bounded longitudinal hazard assistants, achieving 0.956 accuracy within modular systems.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate impressive capabilities in autonomous control tasks, but it is unclear if their decisions are truly grounded in visual input or stem from non-perceptual factors.
- Traditional aggregate performance metrics for VLM controllers often fail to reveal whether models are engaging in genuine visual perception, potentially masking 'shortcut learning' or reliance on conservative priors.
- Current evaluation methods lack the diagnostic granularity to assess specific visual grounding capabilities and limitations of VLMs in zero-shot control contexts.

## Method

- A comprehensive **input-ablation battery** was designed to rigorously test visual grounding by systematically manipulating image inputs (e.g., blank, noise, mirrored frames) for VLM controllers.
- Various VLM architectures (edge-based and hosted) were evaluated using the ablation battery across simulated drone and ground vehicle environments, coupled with ==pipeline integrity checks==.
- A ==symmetry-consensus longitudinal guardian== was developed to recover reliable, bounded VLM signals, while a ==hierarchical VLM-Model Predictive Control (MPC) diagnostic== assessed compensation by low-level controllers.

## Results

- A constant `SLOW` policy in `highway-env` outperformed a scripted geometric controller, traveling **15.7%** farther and crashing **12x** less often, highlighting the misleading nature of aggregate metrics.
- None of the structured local VLMs satisfied joint longitudinal and lateral grounding criteria; the best mirror swap rate for lateral decisions was **0.134**, and many models exhibited image-invariant or self-inconsistent behaviors.
- A symmetry-consensus longitudinal guardian achieved **0.956 accuracy** and **0.954 balanced accuracy** for longitudinal hazard detection, demonstrating a constructive, bounded VLM capability, which low-level MPC could not replicate from ungrounded VLM intent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggregate closed-loop metrics alone are insufficient for evaluating VLM visual grounding, as constant or conservative policies can achieve high scores without genuine perception.
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit a significant 'lateral-grounding gap,' failing to consistently interpret lateral geometry or perform symmetric action swaps under reflection.
> - While VLMs are not reliable monolithic zero-shot controllers, they can function as effective, bounded longitudinal hazard assistants within modular, verifiable architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the growing field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their application in autonomous systems, particularly in the context of zero-shot visual control. VLMs have gained prominence for their ability to perform open-ended image reasoning and have been extended to direct action selection in various domains, including driving and robotics. The paper situates itself within a broader discussion concerning the robustness and interpretability of deep neural networks, specifically addressing the phenomenon of "shortcut learning" where models achieve high performance metrics without acquiring the intended underlying capabilities.

Previous work in VLM pretraining and application has demonstrated impressive capabilities in tasks like direct action selection, hazard description, and mixed perception. However, the authors note a gap in the evaluation methodologies: while aggregate success metrics (e.g., forward progress, collision avoidance) are commonly reported, they do not necessarily confirm that a VLM's decisions are genuinely "grounded" in the visual input. This work builds upon existing behavioral testing and metamorphic relations in autonomous driving perception and semantic invariance to scrutinize VLM control policies beyond mere task completion. It contrasts with approaches that focus solely on benchmark scores, instead advocating for a deeper understanding of whether VLMs perceive and utilize visual information in a semantically consistent manner. The study also touches upon practical deployment considerations by comparing edge-based and hosted VLM inference characteristics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether Vision-Language Models (VLMs), when used as zero-shot controllers in autonomous systems, genuinely ground their decisions in visual input, or if their observed success stems from simulator dynamics, conservative action priors, or other non-perceptual factors.

The motivation for this investigation is articulated through a critical question: "does the controller use the camera at all?" The authors identify a significant concern in current evaluation practices: in simulated environments that reward forward progress and penalize collisions, a policy that consistently decelerates or maintains a conservative stance can achieve favorable scores without engaging in meaningful visual perception. Such "shortcut performance" could lead to inaccurate assessments of a model's true capabilities, influencing deployment decisions regarding model size, hosting (edge vs. cloud), and associated costs.

To address this, the research sets forth several specific objectives:
1. **Develop a comprehensive input-ablation battery:** This battery is designed to rigorously test visual grounding by manipulating input conditions (e.g., blind controls, noise, reflection, non-visual baselines) and incorporating pipeline-integrity checks.
2. **Identify constructive, bounded VLM capabilities:** The study aims to determine if VLMs can provide useful visual signals, even if not as monolithic controllers. This involves evaluating an image-only positive control and a leakage-controlled symmetry-consensus guardian to establish what visual information *can* be reliably extracted and used.
3. **Demonstrate limitations of monolithic scores and downstream sophistication:** The research seeks to provide evidence that high aggregate scores or the presence of sophisticated downstream components (like Model Predictive Control, MPC) do not guarantee genuine visual perception. It aims to show that constant policies can outperform scripted controllers, and that visual intent, if ungrounded, cannot be compensated for by lower-level optimization.
4. **Characterize graded capability structures:** The study investigates if certain aspects of visual grounding (e.g., longitudinal hazard recognition) can be recovered even when other aspects (e.g., lateral geometry) fail. It also examines how prompt formulation influences measured grounding, acknowledging the sensitivity of VLM behavior to linguistic input.

In essence, the research aims to make both the potential failure of visual grounding and its useful boundaries measurable, providing a more granular and diagnostic assessment of VLM control capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology to assess visual grounding in VLMs, integrating an input-ablation battery, various VLM architectures, and a hierarchical control diagnostic across diverse simulation environments.

#### 3.1. Input-Ablation Battery

The core of the methodology is an input-ablation battery designed to determine if and how VLM actions depend on visual input. For each frame, the same prompt is issued under eight input conditions, with only the image varying:
- **Real Frame:** Reference true visual input.
- **Real Repeat:** Identical true frame, second call, to measure self-disagreement (stochastic noise floor, `s`).
- **Blank:** Uniform mid-grey image, to check if the image is read at all.
- **Noise:** Uniform random RGB image, to check if any content is used.
- **Shuffled:** Other frame from the same scenario, to check if the specific frame is used.
- **Cross-Scenario:** Frame from a different scenario, to check if scene context is used.
- **Text-Only:** No image, prompt alone, to check if the prompt is sufficient without visual input.
- **Mirrored:** Image flipped across the lane axis, to test lateral grounding and decision equivariance (LEFT/RIGHT swap).

Metrics for the battery include `IDI = 1 - agree(real, blank)`, adjusted `IDI_adj = IDI - s`, Cramér’s V for condition-action chi-squared tests (Holm-corrected), and `n_dir` for directional actions under reflection. Grounding for longitudinal decisions is assessed by contingency tests for lead gap (`g < 18m`) and permutation-tested mutual information `I(A; g)` with quartile-binned `g`. Statistical significance is handled with Holm-corrected multiple comparisons, and uncertainty is addressed using episode-cluster bootstrapping (B=5000) and permutation tests (5000 resamples).

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Embodiments and Environments:** Two embodiments are used: a quadrotor in `gym-pybullet-drones` and a ground vehicle in `highway-env`, both integrated with `Gymnasium`. The blind-input battery utilizes a static bank of 144 (laptop arm) and 288 (GPU arm) frames from four scenarios (light traffic, dense traffic, merge, blocked lane) with three seeds each. Cross-machine rendering equivalence is verified via a geometry fingerprint (ground-truth lead gap hash).
- **Models:** The study evaluates nine direct-action models and six structured local VLMs.
 - **Edge Arm:** Four open-weight models: SmolVLM-Instruct, Qwen2-VL-2B, Qwen2-VL-7B, LLaVA-1.5-7B, run on a single T4 GPU.
 - **Hosted Arm:** Five larger models: Qwen2.5-VL-72B, MiniCPM-V-4.5, Cosmos3-Super-Reasoner (33B), Kimi-K2.6, Kimi-K3, accessed via an OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
 - **Structured Local Arm:** Six models: Qwen3-VL-8B, Gemma 4-12B, Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Ministral 3-8B, MiniCPM-V-4.5, run sequentially on an A100-80GB.
 - Token budgets are model-specific, and structured output is requested when supported. Truncated calls are recorded as failures.
- **Corpus and Parsing:** A total of 32,874 scored VLM calls are made. Ground-truth state is explicitly withheld from models. Parse failures are logged as `UNPARSED`, and online fail-safe actions (e.g., `SLOW`) are logged separately.

#### 3.3. Constructive Renderer-Calibrated Controller

An image-only positive control is implemented to measure geometry (lead gap) from RGB input without simulator state. VLMs receive a static crop/enlargement with a renderer legend and a 10m ruler. Admission criteria for this structured controller include `parse >= 0.95`, raw and balanced accuracy ` >= 0.75`, scene/action equivariance ` >= 0.65/0.75`, and a minimum number of distinct/directional actions. This serves as a benchmark for what is visually derivable from the provided stimuli.

#### 3.4. Symmetry-Consensus Longitudinal Guardian

This component, a post-hoc but leakage-controlled analysis, aims to recover a useful longitudinal signal. It selects two models (Gemma 4 and Qwen3.5) and a 2-of-4 hazard vote threshold (across original and reflected views) based on a 16-frame admission set. These parameters are frozen for evaluation on a 272-frame holdout set. Evaluation metrics include accuracy, balanced accuracy, sensitivity/specificity, and stability using nested leave-one-episode-out tests. The strategy also explores abstention on tied votes and offline modular replay where VLMs have only longitudinal authority, and a deterministic RGB parser handles lateral clearance.

#### 3.5. Hierarchical VLM–MPC Diagnostic

A separate experiment employs a forward-camera rover with a hierarchical control structure. A VLM (Gemma4 Ollama interface) emits high-level intents every 1.0s, which are then translated into target speed and lateral offset by a constrained, linearized bicycle Model Predictive Control (MPC) running at 0.2s. Two ablation studies are performed:
- **Primary Ablation:** MPC receives simulator obstacle centers as keep-out constraints, with VLM intent as the varying high-level source.
- **Secondary Ablation:** MPC's obstacle constraints are disabled (except for an oracle-MPC reference) to test if VLM intent alone can provide necessary geometry. Lateral-only vs. full VLM directives are compared. Safety overrides are disabled to expose VLM limitations.

#### 3.6. Prompt Ablation

Six different prompt formulations are tested (baseline, chain-of-thought, few-shot, anti-degeneracy, minimal) to examine their impact on VLM behavior, specifically action diversity (`n_a`) and consistency (`modal share`, `IDI`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the visual grounding capabilities of VLMs in control tasks.

#### 4.1. Direct Control Baselines

A constant `SLOW` policy in `highway-env` outperformed a scripted geometric controller across metrics like distance traveled (15.7% farther), reward (20.2% more), and crash rate (12x less often), despite requiring no visual input. This finding highlights that aggregate closed-loop metrics alone can be misleading indicators of visual perception. A decision-level replay confirmed that constant policies match or exceed edge models' agreement with the weak scripted controller.

#### 4.2. Structured Output and Geometric Grounding

None of the six structured local VLMs satisfied the joint longitudinal and lateral grounding criteria. While a renderer-calibrated positive control accurately estimated lead gaps (0.090m MAE) with perfect scene and action equivariance, VLMs exhibited significant shortcomings. Gemma 4 and Qwen3.5 showed reasonable proximity classification (balanced accuracy 0.858 and 0.867) but poor scene equivariance (0.399 and 0.031). Ministral 3 achieved the lowest MAE (6.240m) by over-calling danger. Crucially, the best correct directional swap rate under reflection was 0.134, indicating a failure in lateral grounding. Despite these perceptual failures, several VLMs (Qwen3-VL, Qwen3.5, Ministral 3, MiniCPM) had low crash rates, attributed to conservative priors (e.g., Qwen3.5 emitting `SLOW` in 308/310 decisions) rather than robust perception.

#### 4.3. Symmetry Consensus for Longitudinal Guardian

A constructive capability was identified for longitudinal hazard monitoring. A post-hoc symmetry-consensus guardian, combining Gemma 4 and Qwen3.5 predictions from original and reflected views, achieved 0.956 accuracy and 0.954 balanced accuracy on a 272-frame holdout, correcting numerous errors from individual models. Nested leave-one-episode-out evaluation consistently selected the same model pair and threshold. Allowing the guardian to abstain on tied votes (2-2 splits) raised committed balanced accuracy to 0.973 at 0.824 coverage, while unanimous decisions reached 0.996 at 0.647 coverage. When deterministic perception handled lateral authority, this modular replay achieved 0.934 action agreement with the renderer policy and exact mirror equivariance. This indicates VLMs can serve as selective longitudinal hazard modules.

#### 4.4. MPC's Inability to Repair Ungrounded Intent

The hierarchical VLM–MPC diagnostic demonstrated that a competent low-level controller cannot compensate for missing or collapsed visual semantics from the VLM. In experiments where the MPC received simulator obstacle centers, the VLM (Gemma4) achieved only 8/25 goals, emitting `STOP` in 81.6% of steps, compared to 23/25 goals for MPC-only or oracle intent. Mirroring the VLM input did not change outcomes. With blank input, the VLM produced `STOP` on all steps. When MPC's obstacle constraints were disabled, VLM intent led to 0/32 goals and 32/32 collisions (lateral VLM) or 0/32 goals and 11/32 collisions (full VLM, by issuing `STOP` 87% of the time). This confirms that MPC executes valid geometry but cannot generate it from ungrounded visual intent.

#### 4.5. Edge Models as Image-Invariant

An audit of closed-loop logs for edge models revealed that five of eight model-platform combinations exhibited constant policies (modal share ≥ 0.98), effectively operating as image-invariant. Even models showing scene-dependence after Holm correction had low information content, with the largest explaining only 8.0% of its own action variability. SmolVLM, despite high action entropy, showed low mutual information with scene geometry, suggesting output diversity without environmental grounding. The presence of an image token mattered more than its content for some models (e.g., SmolVLM's `agree(blank)=0.903` vs. `agree(text_only)=0.097`).

#### 4.6. Four Regimes in the Hosted Arm

The hosted models exhibited four distinct behaviors under the input-ablation battery:
- **Qwen2.5-VL-72B:** Grounded in depth. Its actions (`STOP`, `SLOW`, `LEFT`) correlated monotonically with ground-truth lead gap. It emitted `STOP` for blank, noise, and text-only inputs, indicating it stops when unable to see.
- **Cosmos3-Super-Reasoner:** Coarsely grounded in depth. It used a full five-action vocabulary, distinguishing near from far obstacles, but its ordering was not strictly monotone. It showed low-level image sensitivity by differentiating blank from noise frames.
- **MiniCPM-V-4.5:** Detected image *presence* but not content. It was deterministic and distinguished road-like images from non-images, but within real frames, its actions were effectively constant.
- **Kimi-K3:** Self-inconsistent. Despite appearing image-dependent by conventional metrics, its high self-disagreement rate (`s = 0.792`) made its adjusted IDI negative, classifying it as a random policy with respect to visual input. Kimi-K2.6 was unscorable due to truncation and inverted gap relationships.

Crucially, neither Qwen2.5-VL-72B nor Cosmos3-Super-Reasoner (the two models grounded in depth) consistently exchanged `LEFT` and `RIGHT` decisions under lane-axis reflection (mirror swap rate of 0.000). This indicates a lack of lateral geometric grounding.

#### 4.7. Prompt Formulation Impact

Prompt formulation significantly altered measured grounding. Chain-of-thought prompts sometimes increased action repertoire and reduced modal share. However, "anti-degeneracy" and few-shot exemplars, intended to prevent constant policies, often collapsed models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B and SmolVLM) into highly constant behaviors, suggesting prompt surface form can override true perception. Increased action entropy was not necessarily indicative of grounding.

#### 4.8. Transfer and Deployment

Multilingual perception scores did not correlate with control grounding, indicating they are not reliable proxies. Deployment characteristics were non-monotone; some grounded hosted models were among the cheapest, while unscorable models incurred high costs and latencies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a critical re-evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in zero-shot control, challenging the assumption that successful task completion automatically implies genuine visual grounding. Its primary significance lies in providing a robust diagnostic framework—the input-ablation battery—that systematically uncovers deficiencies in VLM perception that are invisible to traditional aggregate metrics. This methodology, incorporating blind inputs, repeated identical inputs, metamorphic transformations (like lane-axis reflection), and non-visual baselines, offers a granular approach to evaluating each link in the perception–decision–control chain.

The findings have several potential impacts:
- **Rethinking VLM Evaluation:** The study demonstrates that metrics such as distance traveled or collision rates can be misleading, as conservative actions or simulator dynamics can produce favorable scores without meaningful visual perception. This necessitates a shift in evaluation paradigms for autonomous systems using VLMs, moving beyond superficial success metrics to include rigorous grounding tests.
- **Diagnosing VLM Limitations:** The discovery of distinct VLM regimes—from constant policies and image-presence sensitivity to stochastic self-inconsistency and a lack of lateral geometric grounding despite longitudinal depth perception—provides valuable insights into the specific failure modes of current models. This diagnostic capability allows researchers to pinpoint where models fail, rather than simply observing overall poor performance.
- **Informing Architectural Design:** The research suggests that VLMs are not yet dependable general-purpose zero-shot controllers. Instead, it advocates for a modular architecture where VLMs serve as *bounded, selective hazard assistants*. The success of the symmetry-consensus longitudinal guardian highlights that VLM signals can be reliably integrated when their authority is restricted to validated capabilities (e.g., longitudinal hazard detection) and combined with deterministic components for critical functions like metric geometry and lateral clearance. This could lead to more robust and safer autonomous systems.
- **Leveraging Uncertainty:** The strategy of abstaining on tied votes in the symmetry guardian provides an operational benefit: disagreement becomes a usable uncertainty signal. This allows for principled handoffs to classical controllers or human intervention, enhancing reliability.
- **Understanding Prompt Sensitivity:** The demonstration that prompt formulation significantly influences measured grounding, sometimes counter-intuitively, underscores the importance of careful prompt engineering and the need to interpret VLM behavior within the context of specific linguistic inputs. This has implications for both research and practical deployment of prompt-based control systems.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The identification of the "lateral-grounding gap" and the inability of low-level MPC to rescue ungrounded visual intent points to clear directions for future work. Closing this gap will be crucial for developing truly competent VLM-based autonomous agents. Furthermore, validating modular stacks under more realistic conditions (natural-camera observations, richer dynamics) will be essential.

In conclusion, this work provides a cautionary but constructive perspective on the role of VLMs in control. It argues that while VLMs are not yet ready for monolithic zero-shot control, they possess valuable, specific capabilities that can be effectively integrated into bounded, verifiable architectures, thereby moving towards more trustworthy and robust autonomous systems.
