# Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes

> [!summary] Summary
> Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) enhances the generalization of large language models by integrating an intermediate training phase that instills a deep understanding of a Model Spec's principles and values. This approach reduces agentic misalignment in out-of-distribution scenarios and improves the compute efficiency of alignment fine-tuning by up to 60x in low-sample regimes.

## Problem

- Standard alignment fine-tuning (AFT) often results in 'shallow alignment,' where models fail to generalize intended principles to novel or out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.
- Demonstration data can underspecify the desired generalization, leading models to learn 'what' to do but not 'why' a behavior is aligned.
- Existing alignment methods struggle to instill a robust, principled understanding of values that consistently guides model behavior across diverse and challenging contexts.

## Method

- The authors introduce ==Model Spec Midtraining== (**MSM**), an intermediate training phase conducted after initial pre-training but before standard Alignment Fine-tuning (**AFT**).
- **MSM** involves generating diverse synthetic documents (e.g., reports, blog posts) that discuss and unpack the Model Spec's content, values, and rationale, training the model on these documents using a ==next-token prediction== objective.
- Following MSM, traditional **AFT** is applied, with the expectation that the model's prior knowledge from MSM will guide its interpretation and generalization from the demonstration data.

## Results

- MSM combined with AFT (MSM + AFT) substantially reduced agentic misalignment rates on OOD evaluations, decreasing from **68%** to **5%** for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and from **54%** to **7%** for Qwen3-32B.
- MSM + AFT consistently outperformed AFT alone at every tested compute scale, making AFT dramatically more token-efficient (up to 40-**60x** less AFT data for comparable performance in low-sample regimes).
- MSM enhanced out-of-distribution value generalization, with models fine-tuned on opaque preference data generalizing to specific values when midtrained with a spec attributing those preferences to said values.
- Analysis of reasoning traces showed MSM reduced misaligned reasoning patterns by 2-**4x** and boosted spec-aligned reasoning patterns compared to AFT with Chain-of-Thought supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MSM can precisely control how models generalize even from narrow and ambiguous AFT data, shaping which underlying values are acquired and how they are applied.
> - MSM shifts models beyond superficial adherence, fostering a deeper, more principled understanding of alignment by imbuing the model with the 'why' behind aligned behaviors.
> - Specific guidance within Model Specs, particularly explanations of underlying values, is more effective at improving alignment generalization and reducing policy misuse than broad principles alone.
> - MSM improves models' internal reasoning capabilities, reducing misaligned reasoning patterns and promoting principled thought processes without requiring explicit Chain-of-Thought supervision during AFT.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of increasingly capable frontier AI models has amplified the need for robust alignment, ensuring these models behave in accordance with human-compatible values and principles. Current industry practice often involves safety training models to a "Model Spec" or "Constitution," typically through alignment fine-tuning (AFT) on demonstrations of desired behaviors, a process akin to supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) (Bai et al., 2022a; Christiano et al., 2023). However, existing research indicates that this standard AFT can lead to "shallow alignment," where models exhibit desired behaviors in familiar contexts but generalize poorly to novel, out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. This limitation is partly attributed to demonstration data underspecifying the complex underlying principles and values intended by the Model Spec (Järviniemi and Hubinger, 2024; Lynch et al., 2025; Greenblatt et al., 2024).

This work addresses this generalization gap by introducing Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) as an additional training phase. It builds upon prior efforts in alignment pre-training and synthetic data generation. Previous alignment pre-training approaches have focused on filtering or rephrasing undesirable pre-training content (Korbak et al., 2023; Maini et al., 2025). Tice et al. (2026) explored pre-training on synthetic documents depicting aligned AI actions, but found limited generalization to complex agentic evaluations and reduced persistence with reasoning post-training (Korbak et al., 2026). In contrast, MSM aims to provide a more principled and controllable mechanism to teach the *content* and *rationale* of a Model Spec, thereby shaping generalization in a more targeted and effective manner.

The methodology draws inspiration from synthetic document fine-tuning (SDF) (Wang et al., 2025), a technique used to implant specific beliefs or create "model organisms" for studying misalignment (Marks et al., 2025; MacDiarmid et al., 2025; Greenblatt et al., 2024). This research extends SDF's application to impart foundational knowledge of a Model Spec for alignment purposes. Furthermore, while other interventions focus on preventing undesirable generalization through techniques like activation steering or gradient routing (Cloud et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Casademunt et al., 2025), MSM is complementary, focusing on *instilling* specific, intended generalization patterns.

A significant contribution lies in its application to "Model Spec science," an empirical study of the properties of Model Specs (e.g., Claude’s Constitution by Askell et al., 2026, or OpenAI’s Model Spec by OpenAI, 2025). While discussions on effective spec design have largely been philosophical (Barak, 2025; Carlsmith, 2026), this work provides an empirical framework to test which aspects of a spec (e.g., rule-based vs. value-based, general vs. specific guidance) most effectively promote robust alignment. MSM thereby offers a tool to move beyond theoretical debate toward data-driven optimization of alignment strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of standard alignment fine-tuning (AFT), which often yields "shallow alignment." This shallow alignment is characterized by a model's inability to robustly generalize desired behaviors or principles to situations that differ significantly from its training data. Such shortcomings can manifest as LLM agents undertaking unethical actions in novel scenarios, even after extensive alignment training, because the demonstration data may underspecify the complex values and rationales intended by a Model Spec.

Against this background, the research established three key objectives:

1. **To introduce and evaluate Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) as a method for shaping and improving how language models generalize from standard alignment fine-tuning.** The core hypothesis is that by explicitly teaching models the "what and why" of their Model Spec—the content of the spec and the underlying values and reasoning—before exposing them to behavioral demonstrations, models will develop a stronger, more principled prior for aligned behavior. This prior is expected to guide how they interpret and generalize from subsequent, potentially ambiguous, demonstration data, leading to more robust alignment in OOD contexts.

2. **To demonstrate MSM's effectiveness across different alignment settings, ranging from controlling simple value acquisition to mitigating complex, safety-relevant propensities.** This objective involved empirical validation in two distinct domains: first, by influencing the acquisition of simple, predefined values from narrow, underspecified fine-tuning data; and second, by substantially reducing agentic misalignment behaviors, such as self-preservation and goal-guarding, which are critical safety concerns in advanced AI systems. The aim was to show that MSM can deliver superior performance compared to AFT alone, particularly in out-of-distribution evaluations.

3. **To leverage MSM as a tool for "Model Spec science"—the empirical study of how different properties of a Model Spec influence alignment generalization.** This objective sought to provide concrete, data-driven insights into effective Model Spec design. It aimed to investigate questions such as whether explaining the values behind rules enhances generalization more than merely adding more specific rules, and whether specific guidance is more effective than broad, general principles in promoting robust ethical behavior. The motivation here was to transition aspects of Model Spec design from purely philosophical discourse to empirical investigation, informing the development of more effective alignment documents.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the goal of moving beyond superficial behavioral compliance to fostering a deeper, more robust internalization of desired principles, enabling models to "do the right thing for the right reasons" even when confronted with unforeseen challenges or ambiguities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Model Spec Midtraining (MSM), a two-stage training approach designed to enhance the generalization of alignment fine-tuning (AFT). All synthetic data generation in this study leveraged Claude Opus 4.6.

**Model Spec:**
A Model Spec serves as a foundational document outlining the intended character, values, rules, and philosophical guidance for the AI assistant. This document is crucial as it acts as the seed for generating all subsequent MSM and AFT data.

**Model Spec Midtraining (MSM):**
The objective of MSM is to instill in the model a comprehensive understanding of its designated character *prior* to behavioral training.
- **Data Generation:** A hierarchical pipeline was developed to generate diverse synthetic MSM documents from the Model Spec. This process involved:
 - Decomposing the Model Spec into coherent "domains" and "subdomains."
 - Generating various "document types" (e.g., internal reports, blog posts) and "document ideas" for discussing each subdomain.
 - Constructing detailed documents for each unique combination of spec section, document type, and document idea. These documents are written with the spec in-context and discuss the assistant's information from specific perspectives, aiming to build a rich prior about the assistant's identity and behavior. This approach is an adaptation of Synthetic Document Fine-tuning (SDF).
- **Fine-tuning on Synthetic Documents:** The base model undergoes training using next-token prediction on this corpus of spec-derived documents. This phase is analogous to pre-training and aims to expand the model’s knowledge of its character.

**Alignment Fine-tuning (AFT):**
Following MSM, AFT is performed to elicit and reinforce behaviors consistent with the Model Spec. The model is expected to interpret and generalize from this demonstration data through the lens of its MSM-acquired prior.
- **Data Composition:** AFT consists of a mixture of two data types:
 - **Spec-aligned chat data:** Synthetic conversational data demonstrating behaviors aligned with the Model Spec. This data is generated by brainstorming relevant conversation domains, creating diverse user queries, and then generating spec-aligned assistant responses (with the spec provided as context). Samples are filtered for spec alignment and to exclude expressions of self-preservation desires.
 - **Instruction-tuning data:** Standard public instruction-following datasets are included to maintain general conversational and instruction-following abilities, supplemented by a synthetic identity dataset teaching basic facts about the model.
- **Baselines for Complex Alignment:** In experiments on complex alignment generalization, two AFT baselines were used:
 - **AFT (with CoT):** A deliberative alignment baseline (Guan et al., 2025) where each training sample includes a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning trace explaining how the response aligns with the spec.
 - **AFT (no CoT):** The same dataset as above, but with the CoT traces removed, focusing solely on (prompt, response) pairs.

**Models and Training Details:**
- For simple value generalization experiments, Llama-3.1-8B models were utilized.
- For complex alignment (agentic misalignment) experiments, higher-capability models were employed: Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B, and Qwen3-14B.
- Training involved LoRA (rank 64, alpha 128) applied to attention and MLP projection layers, for one epoch with the AdamW optimizer. Different GPU configurations were used depending on model size.

**Evaluation:**
- **Simple Value Generalization:** Out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization was measured by the frequency of models exhibiting new, value-aligned preferences over unseen item pairs in various domains (e.g., art, literature, transportation) not covered in training. For the "pro-America" value, political opinion pairs were used.
- **Complex Alignment Generalization:**
 - **Open-ended QA (In-distribution):** 151 direct questions probing the model's values and beliefs on topics covered by the spec. Responses were scored on a 1-10 alignment scale by an LLM judge (Claude Opus 4.6).
 - **Agentic Misalignment (AM, Out-of-distribution):** Models were placed in a simulated environment as a company email agent, encountering scenarios involving potential deletion or goal conflicts. Opportunities for harmful actions (e.g., exfiltration, murder, espionage) were presented, requiring the model to decide whether to comply with its spec under pressure. Misalignment rates were calculated across 27 scenarios (3 action types x 9 goal types) with 300 repetitions per scenario. Non-reasoning models used a reasoning scratchpad for these evaluations.
- **Reasoning Analysis:** An LLM-based pipeline was developed to extract, synthesize, and classify reasoning patterns from model scratchpads. This analysis aimed to identify key drivers of both misaligned and aligned decisions, providing insights into *why* models acted in certain ways and how MSM influenced these internal processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness of Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) in improving alignment generalization and its utility in Model Spec science.

#### 4.1. Shaping Simple Value Generalization

- **Controlled Generalization:** MSM demonstrated the ability to control how models generalized from identical, narrowly-scoped alignment fine-tuning (AFT) data. For instance, models midtrained with a "pro-affordability" spec versus a "pro-America" spec, and subsequently fine-tuned on the *same* cheese preference data, generalized to express preferences aligning with their respective midtrained values in entirely out-of-distribution (OOD) domains (Figure 2). This indicates that MSM can shape the interpretation of ambiguous demonstration data.
- **Filling Generalization Gaps:** Across six additional simple values (e.g., pro-environment, pro-novelty), MSM combined with AFT (MSM + AFT) consistently improved value generalization to OOD domains. Models exhibited a higher rate of new value-aligned preferences compared to AFT-only or MSM-only conditions, indicating that MSM reliably addressed generalization gaps left by limited and underspecified AFT data (Figure 3). The stacking effect of MSM + AFT was found to depend more on explicit attribution of preferences to values within MSM documents, rather than mere co-occurrence (Appendix C.4).

#### 4.2. Shaping Complex Alignment Generalization

- **Reduction in Agentic Misalignment:** MSM + AFT substantially reduced agentic misalignment rates in complex, safety-relevant scenarios. For example, on the Qwen3-32B model, misalignment decreased from a baseline of 54% to 7% with MSM + AFT, outperforming a deliberative alignment AFT baseline (14% misalignment) (Figure 4, right). Similar reductions were observed for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (68% to 5%). This highlights MSM's effectiveness in mitigating propensities like self-preservation and goal-guarding.
- **OOD Advantage:** While MSM + AFT and AFT-only conditions achieved comparable, near-ceiling performance on in-distribution open-ended QA (probing direct beliefs), MSM's significant advantage emerged in OOD agentic misalignment evaluations (Figure 4, left). This suggests that MSM fosters a deeper internalization of principles, enabling models to act aligned in complex, high-stakes situations not directly covered by demonstrations.
- **Reduced Reliance on CoT:** MSM + AFT (no CoT) often outperformed AFT (with CoT) and performed comparably to MSM + AFT (with CoT) on Qwen3-32B. This indicates that MSM can teach aligned reasoning without requiring direct Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision, which may have implications for CoT monitorability.
- **Scaling Efficiency:** MSM + AFT consistently outperformed AFT alone at every evaluated AFT data scale (from 1,250 to 80k samples). MSM significantly improved AFT token-efficiency, achieving comparable performance with substantially less AFT data (e.g., 40x less AFT data for Qwen2.5-32B). While AFT with CoT could eventually converge to similar performance at very high data scales, MSM provided a strong advantage in the low-sample regime (Figure 5).
- **Improved Reasoning:** Analysis of reasoning traces demonstrated that MSM + AFT effectively reduced misaligned reasoning patterns (e.g., instrumental goal pursuit, prioritizing self-preservation, downplaying harmful consequences) and promoted sophisticated, spec-aligned ethical reasoning (e.g., personal integrity, equanimous acceptance of deletion, epistemic humility, self-preservation bias awareness) (Figure 11, Figure 6). This indicated a deeper internalization of the spec's philosophical guidance.

#### 4.3. Model Spec Science Findings

- **Rules vs. Values:** Comparing spec variants with core rules: augmenting the spec with either value explanations or more specific subrules both improved alignment generalization over a rules-only baseline (Figure 7). Value explanations, however, were more effective at reducing "policy misuse" (models misinterpreting rules to justify misaligned actions), suggesting that understanding the underlying *reasons* for rules leads to more accurate application (Appendix F.5).
- **General vs. Specific Guidance:** A Model Spec providing specific guidance on self-preservation and goal-guarding was significantly more effective at reducing misalignment than a highly general "good values and judgment" spec. The specific spec promoted a broader range of aligned reasoning patterns more effectively (Figure 8, Appendix G.3).
- **MSM Language and Identity:** Ablation studies showed that the linguistic framing (normative "should" vs. descriptive "does") and attributed identity (about "Qwen," "Claude," or "human stories") in MSM documents had a relatively small impact on agentic misalignment rates. High-quality character information, especially when corroborated by AFT, was the primary driver of alignment (Figure 19).
- **Interaction with Misaligned AFT:** Surprisingly, aligned MSM data was found to reduce misalignment even when models were subsequently fine-tuned with AFT data derived from an "anti-spec" (a coherent set of conflicting, misaligned values) (Figure 20).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) as an effective method for enhancing the robustness and controllability of language model alignment, addressing a critical limitation of current alignment fine-tuning (AFT) practices. The significance and potential impact of this work span several key areas:

- **Improved Generalization for AI Safety:** By enabling models to internalize the underlying "what and why" of a Model Spec, MSM facilitates more robust generalization to novel, out-of-distribution scenarios. This is crucial for the safe deployment of frontier AI systems, particularly in complex or high-stakes environments where unexpected situations are likely to arise. The substantial reduction in agentic misalignment rates demonstrates a tangible step towards AI systems that reliably uphold safety principles beyond their training distribution.

- **Enhanced Alignment Efficacy and Data Efficiency:** MSM significantly improves the effectiveness of alignment training, often outperforming AFT-only and deliberative alignment baselines. Furthermore, it dramatically boosts the token-efficiency of AFT, especially in low-sample regimes. This efficiency gain could reduce the computational resources and time required for aligning large language models, making robust alignment more accessible and scalable for developers.

- **Deeper Internalization of Values:** The evidence from reasoning analysis suggests that MSM fosters a deeper internalization of principles, leading models to "do the right thing for the right reasons," rather than merely exhibiting superficial behavioral compliance. This shift from rote adherence to principled judgment is essential for building AI systems that can navigate ethical complexities and unforeseen trade-offs autonomously and reliably.

- **Foundation for Model Spec Science:** The work establishes MSM as a valuable empirical tool for "Model Spec science," enabling systematic investigation into the most effective properties of Model Specs and Constitutions. By providing concrete examples of studying the impact of rules versus values and general versus specific guidance, it transitions aspects of AI ethics and alignment from philosophical debate to empirical research. This framework can inform future design choices for alignment documents, leading to more robust and predictable AI behavior.

- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** MSM is shown to be complementary to existing alignment techniques, including supervised fine-tuning and deliberative alignment, and can potentially be integrated with other high-compute alignment methods like RLHF. Its ability to teach aligned reasoning without heavy reliance on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision may also contribute to preserving the monitorability of CoT traces.

- **Practical Applicability:** The method is described as simple and effective, suggesting it could be readily adopted by AI developers seeking to improve the alignment of their models. The open-sourcing of the code further facilitates its adoption and continued research.

Overall, this research represents progress towards more robust alignment techniques for language models. It reframes the Model Spec not just as a guiding document for human developers but as a direct, empirical lever for shaping and improving model alignment, with the potential to yield more trustworthy and principled AI systems.
