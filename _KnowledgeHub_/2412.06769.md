# Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta introduced Coconut, a paradigm training Large Language Models to perform reasoning in a continuous latent space by iteratively feeding hidden states. This approach demonstrates emergent breadth-first search capabilities, achieving higher accuracy on logical tasks like ProsQA with significantly greater token efficiency compared to traditional language-based Chain-of-Thought methods.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) relying on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) are constrained by natural language, which is often suboptimal and inefficient for complex reasoning.
- CoT generates many 'fluency tokens' that contribute little to core reasoning, wasting computational resources.
- Autoregressive language generation forces LLMs to commit prematurely to a single reasoning path, hindering complex planning and exploration of alternatives.

## Method

- The ==Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought)== paradigm utilizes the LLM's last ==hidden state== as a continuous representation of 'thought,' feeding it back as the next input embedding instead of generating word tokens.
- A ==multi-stage training curriculum== gradually replaces initial language reasoning steps from existing CoT data with continuous thoughts, guiding the model to learn latent reasoning.
- The model operates in distinct 'language mode' for textual output and =='latent mode'== for internal continuous thought processing, enabling end-to-end optimization.

## Results

- Coconut significantly outperforms language-based CoT on logical reasoning tasks like ProntoQA and the novel ProsQA dataset, achieving **97.0%** accuracy on ProsQA compared to CoT's **77.5%**.
- The method demonstrates a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off, achieving comparable or better accuracy on tasks like GSM8k while generating substantially fewer tokens during inference.
- Analysis revealed that Coconut's latent space maintains diverse high-probability candidate paths at early reasoning stages, progressively converging to the most promising solution, showcasing a non-greedy search strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning in a continuous latent space allows LLMs to encode and explore multiple alternative reasoning steps simultaneously, leading to emergent breadth-first search (BFS) behavior.
> - The multi-stage training curriculum is crucial for LLMs to effectively learn and leverage continuous latent thoughts for sophisticated reasoning.
> - Continuous thoughts implicitly encode intermediate variables or concepts, serving as efficient, compact representations for problem-solving steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper directly challenges and seeks to extend the dominant paradigm of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, particularly the **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)** approach. CoT, where LLMs generate explicit step-by-step reasoning in natural language, has been remarkably effective in enhancing LLM reasoning abilities. However, this paper argues that CoT, while powerful, is fundamentally constrained by its reliance on the "language space."

The authors contextualize their work by referencing human cognition studies that suggest language networks are largely inactive during various reasoning tasks, implying that human reasoning might occur in a non-linguistic, abstract space. This biological inspiration forms a critical backdrop to their argument against the universality of language-based reasoning for LLMs.

Within the LLM research landscape, this paper connects to several related lines of inquiry:

- **Limitations of CoT:** The paper highlights the inefficiency of CoT, where many generated tokens contribute to textual fluency rather than core reasoning, and the challenge LLMs face in complex planning within a strictly autoregressive language generation framework.
- **Latent Reasoning in LLMs:** Previous works have explored the concept of "latent reasoning" as the hidden computations within transformer models (Yang et al., 2024; Biran et al., 2024). Some studies even note the "unfaithfulness" of CoT, where the explicit linguistic reasoning might not entirely reflect the model's true internal thought process (Wang et al., 2022; Turpin et al., 2024). This paper explicitly aims to *enhance* and *utilize* this latent reasoning space.
- **Augmenting LLMs with Special Tokens:** Other efforts have introduced learnable `<pause>` tokens (Goyal et al., 2023) or filler tokens (Pfau et al., 2024) to provide LLMs with additional computational steps without generating full language. Coconut extends this by making the "thought" itself a continuous, differentiable hidden state rather than a discrete token.
- **Internalizing CoT:** Work like iCoT (Deng et al., 2024) and knowledge distillation (Deng et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2024b) aims to distill explicit CoT into more implicit, latent reasoning within the model, often by shortening or removing explicit reasoning steps during training. Coconut's multi-stage training strategy is directly inspired by iCoT, replacing language steps with continuous thoughts.
- **Planning and Search in LLMs:** The paper also relates to research that equips LLMs with explicit tree search algorithms (Xie et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023; Hao et al., 2024) or trains them on search dynamics (Lehnert et al., 2024). Coconut distinguishes itself by showing that a similar breadth-first search (BFS) pattern *emerges* from its continuous latent space reasoning, even without explicit training for such a mechanism.

In essence, this work represents a significant step towards moving LLMs beyond purely linguistic processing for reasoning, proposing a more flexible and potentially more efficient paradigm by directly engaging with the model's internal continuous representations for thought. It positions itself at the intersection of understanding and enhancing the internal mechanisms of LLMs for complex problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **explore the potential of LLM reasoning in an unrestricted latent space** as an alternative to reasoning solely in the "language space." The authors introduce and validate a novel paradigm, Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought), designed to achieve this.

The core motivation stems from several critical observations and arguments regarding the limitations of language-based reasoning in LLMs, particularly Chain-of-Thought (CoT):

1. **Suboptimality of Language for Reasoning:** The authors argue that natural language, primarily optimized for communication, is not always the optimal medium for complex reasoning. They cite neuroimaging studies suggesting that human language networks are inactive during certain reasoning tasks, implying a non-linguistic mode of thought.
2. **Inefficiency and Wasted Computation in CoT:** When LLMs use language for reasoning, a significant portion of generated tokens (e.g., filler words, grammatical connectors) primarily ensures textual coherence but contributes little to the actual logical process. This means LLMs allocate computing resources to predict "fluency tokens" rather than solely focusing on "critical reasoning tokens" which require complex planning. This leads to computational inefficiency.
3. **Premature Commitment in Autoregressive Decoding:** The autoregressive nature of language generation forces LLMs to commit to a single reasoning path early on. If an initial decision is suboptimal, the model might get stuck, hallucinate, or fail to backtrack, hindering its ability to perform complex planning and search. The authors note that this contrasts with human problem-solving, which often involves exploring multiple possibilities.
4. **Desire for Unconstrained Reasoning:** Ideally, LLMs should have the freedom to reason internally without the constraints of natural language, translating their findings into language only when a final answer or explanation is required. This would allow for more flexible and potentially more powerful reasoning mechanisms.

By addressing these limitations, Coconut aims to foster more efficient, accurate, and advanced reasoning patterns in LLMs, specifically by leveraging the continuous, differentiable nature of the model's hidden states for iterative thought.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around **Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought)**, a novel paradigm that modifies how LLMs progress through reasoning steps.

**a. Coconut Paradigm (Chain of Continuous Thought):**
Instead of generating word tokens for each step in a reasoning chain, Coconut utilizes the last hidden state of the LLM as a representation of the "reasoning state" or "continuous thought." This continuous thought is then directly fed back into the LLM as the subsequent input embedding.

- **Language Mode vs. Latent Mode:** The LLM operates in two modes. In "language mode," it functions as a standard autoregressive language model, generating word tokens. In "latent mode," marked by special `<bot>` (begin of thought) and `<eot>` (end of thought) tokens, it uses the continuous hidden state feedback loop.
- **Continuous Thought Mechanism:** When in latent mode, instead of processing `e(x_t)` (the embedding of a word token `x_t`), the model processes `h_{t-1}` (the last hidden state from the previous step) as its input embedding for the current step. This process creates a chain of continuous thoughts, fully differentiable, allowing for end-to-end optimization via gradient descent.
- **Inference:** During inference, `<bot>` is inserted after the question. For `<eot>`, the authors found that either training a binary classifier or simply padding latent thoughts to a constant length worked comparably, opting for the latter for simplicity in their experiments.

**b. Multi-Stage Training Strategy:**
Inspired by iCoT (Deng et al., 2024), Coconut employs a multi-stage training curriculum to guide the learning of latent reasoning, leveraging existing language CoT data.

- **Curriculum Design:**
 - **Initial Stage:** The model is trained on regular CoT instances, generating full language reasoning chains.
 - **Subsequent Stages:** In each progressive stage (k-th stage), the first `k * c` language reasoning steps of the CoT are replaced with `k * c` continuous thoughts (where `c` is a hyperparameter, typically 1 or 2, controlling how many continuous thoughts replace one language step). The model then predicts the *remaining* language reasoning chain.
 - **Loss Function:** The training optimizes the negative log-likelihood loss, but the loss is masked for the question and latent thoughts, focusing only on the remaining language tokens. This objective encourages continuous thoughts to facilitate the prediction of future reasoning, not necessarily to compress the removed language.
- **Training Details:** The optimizer state is reset when switching stages. Training involves multiple forward passes (n+1 for n latent thoughts), with KV cache used for efficiency. The training is conducted on a pre-trained GPT-2 model.

**c. Datasets:**
The effectiveness of Coconut is evaluated on three distinct reasoning tasks:

- **Math Reasoning (GSM8k):** A dataset of grade-school-level math word problems (Cobbe et al., 2021), using a synthetic dataset for training (Deng et al., 2023).
- **Logical Reasoning (ProntoQA):** A dataset requiring the application of logical rules to determine conclusions (Saparov and He, 2022).
- **ProsQA (Proof with Search Question-Answering):** A *newly proposed dataset* by the authors specifically designed to demand sophisticated planning and search strategies. ProsQA instances are based on directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) of logical relationships, where models must find valid paths. This dataset is crucial for demonstrating Coconut's emergent BFS capabilities due to its complex exploration paths.

**d. Baselines and Variants:**
The study compares Coconut against several baselines:
- **CoT:** Standard language chain-of-thought.
- **No-CoT:** Direct answer generation without any intermediate reasoning.
- **iCoT:** A method that internalizes CoT by gradually shortening reasoning chains.
- **Pause Token:** Inserts special learnable `<pause>` tokens for additional computation.

They also analyze variants of Coconut, such as training without the multi-stage curriculum, without continuous thoughts (similar to iCoT), and using pause tokens as continuous thoughts, to isolate the impact of the core Coconut mechanism and training strategy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings demonstrating the efficacy and unique emergent properties of the Coconut paradigm:

**a. Emergence of Breadth-First Search (BFS) Behavior:**
- **Key Insight:** One of the most striking findings is that reasoning in a continuous latent space allows the model to encode *multiple alternative next reasoning steps simultaneously*. This enables a reasoning process akin to a **breadth-first search (BFS)**, where the model can explore several paths concurrently and progressively eliminate incorrect options, rather than committing to a single deterministic path like traditional CoT.
- **Evidence from ProsQA:** On the newly introduced ProsQA dataset, which requires extensive planning, Coconut significantly outperforms language-based CoT. Probing the latent space reveals that the model, at early steps, maintains diverse high-probability candidate paths (Figure 5, 6). As reasoning progresses, it converges on the most promising path, even if it wasn't the top candidate initially. This demonstrates a non-greedy, search-like strategy.
- **Benefit for Planning:** The authors hypothesize and show that nodes explored in early reasoning stages (far from the target) are harder to evaluate accurately. By delaying deterministic decisions and exploring towards terminal states in the latent space, Coconut improves the model's ability to differentiate correct from incorrect paths (Figure 7), enhancing planning performance.

**b. Enhanced Reasoning Accuracy and Efficiency:**
- **Outperformance on Logical Reasoning:** Coconut, and some of its variants, significantly surpass language-based CoT methods on logical reasoning tasks like ProntoQA and ProsQA. For example, on ProsQA, Coconut achieves 97.0% accuracy compared to CoT's 77.5%.
- **Superior Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off:** While Coconut does not always surpass CoT on GSM8k in raw accuracy, it offers a substantially better trade-off between reasoning accuracy and efficiency. It achieves comparable or better accuracy while generating **significantly fewer tokens** during inference (Table 1, Figure 8). This suggests that continuous thoughts are more efficient representations of reasoning steps than natural language tokens.
- **Scalability with Continuous Thoughts:** Increasing the number of continuous thoughts (`c`) per language reasoning step consistently improves performance on tasks like GSM8k, indicating that chaining more continuous thoughts can scale to solve harder problems (Figure 8, II).

**c. Critical Role of Multi-Stage Training Curriculum:**
- **Necessity of Guidance:** The multi-stage training curriculum is crucial for Coconut's success. Models trained "w/o curriculum" (directly with continuous thoughts from the start) perform no better than the No-CoT baseline (Table 1), indicating that LLMs need explicit guidance to learn effective latent reasoning representations.
- **Effectiveness:** With the curriculum, Coconut outperforms other architectures trained with similar strategies, including those using pause tokens or "internalization" approaches like iCoT (though a small gap remains with iCoT, possibly due to its finer-grained token removal schedule).

**d. Nature of Continuous Thoughts:**
- **Implicit Variable Encoding:** When continuous thoughts are decoded, they often correspond to intermediate variables or concepts relevant to the problem-solving process (Figure 9), further supporting their role as efficient reasoning representations.
- **Generalizability (Limited to GPT-2):** While successful on GPT-2, experiments with larger models like Llama 3.2-3B and Llama 3-8B showed consistent gains over No-CoT, but the improvements were less pronounced than with GPT-2. This suggests that extensive language-focused pre-training in larger models might make the transition to latent reasoning more challenging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Coconut and the demonstration of its capabilities mark a significant step towards a new paradigm for reasoning in Large Language Models. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

**a. Advancing LLM Reasoning Beyond Language:** This work offers a compelling argument and practical framework for moving LLM reasoning beyond the strict confines of natural language. By allowing LLMs to "think" in a continuous, differentiable latent space, it opens avenues for more flexible, efficient, and potentially more human-like reasoning processes.

**b. More Efficient LLMs:** The finding that Coconut can achieve higher accuracy with significantly fewer generated tokens translates directly into greater computational efficiency during inference. This is crucial for deploying LLMs in real-world applications where speed and resource consumption are critical. Reducing token generation also helps mitigate issues like long context windows and associated quadratic complexity.

**c. Emergence of Sophisticated Search Strategies:** The discovery of emergent breadth-first search behavior is profound. It suggests that if LLMs are given the right architectural freedom, they can intrinsically develop complex planning and exploration strategies without explicit architectural design or explicit training for search algorithms. This could lead to LLMs that are more robust in solving problems requiring extensive planning and dealing with uncertainty.

**d. Bridging Cognitive Science and AI:** By drawing parallels between non-linguistic human reasoning and latent space reasoning in LLMs, this research contributes to a deeper understanding of intelligence in both biological and artificial systems. It encourages further interdisciplinary exploration.

**e. Future Research Directions:** The paper lays strong groundwork for several future research avenues:
- **Scaling Latent Reasoning:** The challenges observed with larger models suggest a need for "latent space pre-training" or more specialized training methodologies to help massive, language-centric LLMs adapt to this new paradigm. Integrating Coconut with recent advancements in latent representation learning (e.g., Barrault et al., 2024; Gladstone et al., 2025; Geiping et al., 2025) is a promising direction.
- **Refining Training Curricula:** Developing more general and robust training strategies for latent reasoning, perhaps without heavy reliance on existing language CoT supervision, is an important next step.
- **Hybrid Reasoning:** Exploring hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of both language-based (for structured explanations) and latent-based (for efficient planning) reasoning could offer the best of both worlds.
- **Theoretical Understanding:** The references to follow-up theoretical work (Zhu et al., 2025b, 2025a) indicate ongoing efforts to understand *how* emergent behaviors like superposition states arise during Coconut training, deepening the theoretical foundation of this approach.

In conclusion, Coconut represents a compelling step forward in LLM research, demonstrating that freeing models from the explicit constraints of language can unlock more advanced and efficient reasoning capabilities. This work promises to influence the design and training of future generative AI models, potentially leading to more intelligent and versatile AI systems.
