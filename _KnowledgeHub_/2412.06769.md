# Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta introduced Coconut, a paradigm training Large Language Models to perform reasoning in a continuous latent space by iteratively feeding hidden states. This approach demonstrates emergent breadth-first search capabilities, achieving higher accuracy on logical tasks like ProsQA with significantly greater token efficiency compared to traditional language-based Chain-of-Thought methods.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) relying on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) are constrained by natural language, which is often suboptimal and inefficient for complex reasoning.
- CoT generates many 'fluency tokens' that contribute little to core reasoning, wasting computational resources.
- Autoregressive language generation forces LLMs to commit prematurely to a single reasoning path, hindering complex planning and exploration of alternatives.

## Method

- The ==Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought)== paradigm utilizes the LLM's last ==hidden state== as a continuous representation of 'thought,' feeding it back as the next input embedding instead of generating word tokens.
- A ==multi-stage training curriculum== gradually replaces initial language reasoning steps from existing CoT data with continuous thoughts, guiding the model to learn latent reasoning.
- The model operates in distinct 'language mode' for textual output and =='latent mode'== for internal continuous thought processing, enabling end-to-end optimization.

## Results

- Coconut significantly outperforms language-based CoT on logical reasoning tasks like ProntoQA and the novel ProsQA dataset, achieving **97.0%** accuracy on ProsQA compared to CoT's **77.5%**.
- The method demonstrates a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off, achieving comparable or better accuracy on tasks like GSM8k while generating substantially fewer tokens during inference.
- Analysis revealed that Coconut's latent space maintains diverse high-probability candidate paths at early reasoning stages, progressively converging to the most promising solution, showcasing a non-greedy search strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning in a continuous latent space allows LLMs to encode and explore multiple alternative reasoning steps simultaneously, leading to emergent breadth-first search (BFS) behavior.
> - The multi-stage training curriculum is crucial for LLMs to effectively learn and leverage continuous latent thoughts for sophisticated reasoning.
> - Continuous thoughts implicitly encode intermediate variables or concepts, serving as efficient, compact representations for problem-solving steps.
