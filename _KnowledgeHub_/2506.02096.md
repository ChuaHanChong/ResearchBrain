# SynthRL: Scaling Visual Reasoning with Verifiable Data Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> NUS-TRAIL researchers develop SynthRL, an automated three-stage pipeline that synthesizes more challenging visual reasoning problems while preserving original answers to enhance reinforcement learning training for vision-language models, generating 3,380 verified harder questions from 8,072 MMK12 seed samples through difficulty-based selection, Gemini-2.5-Flash synthesis, and guaranteed verification using Monte Carlo rollouts, achieving consistent +1.0% average accuracy gains across five out-of-domain benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, WeMath, DynaMath) with pronounced improvements on medium (+1.7%) and hard (+1.6%) evaluation samples, demonstrating that strategically augmenting training distributions with verifiably correct yet more complex variants provides richer learning signals for RLVR than traditional data scaling approaches.

## Problem

- RLVR shows promise for enhancing VLM reasoning but faces data scalability challenges, as existing selection methods are constrained by original dataset volume and bias
- Creating challenging yet verifiable training data for visual reasoning requires expensive human annotation and step-by-step reasoning supervision
- Traditional data augmentation lacks correctness guarantees and difficulty calibration needed for effective RL training where unreliable rewards lead to suboptimal policies

## Method

- ==Three-stage automated pipeline==: difficulty-based seed selection using Monte Carlo rollouts to identify easy questions, synthesis using powerful VLMs prompted to create harder variants without seeing answers, and guaranteed verification ensuring correctness and increased difficulty
- ==Target model-aligned verification== using rollout pass counts with dual criteria: correctness (C_cand ≥ 4) and difficulty enhancement (C_cand ≤ C_ori - 2)
- ==Augmentation strategy== combining original and synthesized data rather than replacement to create richer training distributions

## Results

- Generated **3,380** verified challenging questions from **8,072** seeds, achieving **58.0%** average accuracy versus **57.0%** baseline across five benchmarks
- Largest improvements on medium (**+1.7%**) and hard (**+1.6%**) evaluation samples while showing slight decrease (**-0.5%**) on easy samples, confirming targeted enhancement of complex reasoning
- Ablation studies confirm necessity of verification (**1.4%** drop without), multi-pass evaluation (**0.7%** drop with single-pass), and augmentation over replacement strategy (**1.1%** performance difference)

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - More challenging yet still correct training samples provide richer learning signals for RLVR than volume increases alone
> - Verification alignment with target model capabilities is crucial - using non-target verifiers reduces performance by 1.5%
> - Benefits scale with seed dataset size, widening from +0.2% at 2K samples to +1.0% at 8K samples, indicating complementary value to traditional scaling

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, and **Data-Centric AI**.

**VLMs for Reasoning:** VLMs have seen rapid advancements, moving from foundational integration to sophisticated mathematical and visual reasoning capabilities. While models like GPT-4o and Gemini show impressive general understanding, a significant gap remains in robust visual reasoning that demands deep analysis and complex inference. Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often struggles to scale this capability due to the high cost of acquiring richly annotated reasoning data.

**Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** RL has emerged as a promising paradigm to enhance the reasoning abilities of large models. RLVR, specifically, allows models to learn from verifiable rewards based on final answers, eliminating the need for explicit step-by-step reasoning annotations. This approach has shown success in scaling test-time compute effectively for language and vision-language models by incentivizing correct reasoning chains.

**Data-Centric AI and Data Synthesis:** Concurrently, there's a growing recognition that "data is king" in AI development. Data-centric approaches, focusing on improving the quality, diversity, and scale of training data, are critical for pushing the boundaries of model intelligence. Data synthesis, in particular, offers a scalable way to generate large volumes of training examples, initially used for instruction tuning and alignment. More recently, it has been applied to create sophisticated datasets for complex visual reasoning tasks, sometimes using techniques like reverse chain-of-thought.

**SynthRL's Contribution and Niche:**
SynthRL directly addresses a critical and underexplored challenge: **how to scale RLVR training data with correctness and distribution guarantees**. Existing data selection methods, while useful, are constrained by the volume and bias of original datasets. SynthRL proposes data synthesis as a complementary and more practical solution, specifically tailored for the RLVR setting.

The key distinction of SynthRL within this landscape is its focus on generating **more challenging yet verifiably correct training samples** for RL. Unlike general data augmentation or instruction tuning, where synthesized data quality might be a concern, SynthRL introduces a **guaranteed verification stage**. This verification ensures near-perfect correctness and difficulty enhancement, which is paramount for effective reinforcement learning, where unreliable rewards can lead to suboptimal policies. By providing a streamlined, automated pipeline for generating such high-quality, difficulty-controlled data, SynthRL advances the field by:
1. **Enabling broader exploration in RL:** By offering a richer learning signal from harder problems.
2. **Bridging the gap between data synthesis and RL:** Demonstrating a practical method for leveraging synthetic data in RL contexts, which has been largely unexplored.
3. **Improving VLM reasoning robustness:** Specifically targeting complex visual mathematical reasoning, where models benefit from exposure to more challenging problems.

In essence, SynthRL acts as a critical data infrastructure layer for advanced RL-tuned VLMs, pushing beyond simple data volume increases to intelligent data quality and difficulty scaling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind SynthRL stems from two critical observations in the contemporary AI landscape:
1. **Effectiveness of RLVR:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has proven to be a promising paradigm for significantly enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language and vision-language models. This approach allows models to learn complex reasoning paths by receiving feedback (rewards) based on the correctness of their final answers, circumventing the need for expensive, step-by-step human annotations.
2. **Importance of Data-Centric Approaches:** There is a growing consensus that the quality and scale of training data are paramount for advancing model intelligence. Data-centric methods are increasingly recognized as crucial for improving model performance and generalization.

Despite these insights, a significant, underexplored challenge remains: **Can we scale the RLVR training data with correctness and distribution guarantees to achieve better performance?** The authors identify that directly addressing this challenge is non-trivial, as it cannot be easily formulated as a standard optimization problem. Existing data selection methods are limited by the inherent volume and biases of original datasets, becoming less effective when data is scarce or skewed.

**Motivated by the intuition that "under RLVR settings, more challenging yet still correct training samples can provide richer learning signals,"** the key objectives of this work are:

- To propose **SynthRL**, a scalable and guaranteed pipeline for automatically generating challenging and verifiable data specifically for reasoning-oriented RL training of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
- To demonstrate empirically that this synthesized data can further improve RLVR, leading to **consistent gains across out-of-domain visual math reasoning benchmarks**.
- To ensure the **correctness and difficulty enhancement** of the synthesized data through a robust verification mechanism.
- To show that the benefits of SynthRL are most pronounced on **challenging evaluation samples**, indicating its effectiveness in eliciting deeper and more complex reasoning patterns from VLMs.

In essence, the authors aim to overcome the data bottleneck in RLVR by intelligently synthesizing high-quality, harder-to-solve problems that nevertheless retain the original ground-truth answers, thereby providing a more potent and efficient learning signal for VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SynthRL proposes a streamlined, automated, and guaranteed pipeline for synthesizing more challenging RL training data for VLMs. The approach comprises three main stages:

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

The paper first outlines the RLVR framework, which is the foundation for their data synthesis. In RLVR, a policy model ($\pi_\theta$) is trained on a dataset $D = \{(x, y^*)\}$ of inputs ($x$, e.g., image-question pairs) and correct outputs ($y^*$, e.g., answers). The model generates its own reasoning steps, and a verifiable reward function $r(y, y^*)$ is used to assign a reward (e.g., binary, 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) based on the final generated answer $y$ and the ground truth $y^*$. The goal is to maximize the expected reward. The authors note that a key challenge in RLVR is data scalability, which SynthRL aims to address. They implement GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) as their RL algorithm, which is an adaptation of PPO, allowing for effective policy updates.

#### 3.2. Difficulty-Based Seed Selection

The first step in SynthRL is to identify "easy" seed questions from an existing dataset ($D_{seed}$) that can be augmented into more challenging variants.
- **Difficulty Assessment:** The difficulty of a question is defined relative to the specific VLM being trained, referred to as the "target model" ($\pi_{target}$). For each image-question-answer triplet $(I, Q, A)$, a **Monte Carlo rollout procedure** is applied. The `rollout pass count` ($C_{pass}$) is calculated by performing $N$ stochastic forward passes (e.g., $N=16$) of the target model and counting how many times the model correctly predicts the answer $A$. A higher $C_{pass}$ indicates an easier question for $\pi_{target}$.
- **Difficulty-Aware Selection:** The distribution of pass counts often shows many samples consistently solved (high $C_{pass}$) or consistently failed (low $C_{pass}$). Questions consistently solved by the target model offer limited gradient signals for RL training. Therefore, SynthRL focuses on selecting questions with a high $C_{pass}$ (e.g., $C_{ori} \ge 12$ out of 16) as "seed data" for synthesis. These are questions the model finds relatively easy and are ripe for complexity enhancement.

#### 3.3. Data Synthesizer

Once easy seed questions are identified, the Data Synthesizer module generates more challenging versions.
- **Synthesizer Model:** A powerful general-purpose VLM ($\phi$, e.g., Gemini-2.5-Flash-Preview-04-17) is used for this task.
- **Prompting Strategy:** For each selected sample $(I, Q_{ori}, A)$, the synthesizer VLM is prompted with the image $I$ and the original question $Q_{ori}$. Crucially, the **ground truth answer $A$ is deliberately withheld** from the synthesizer. The prompt instructs the VLM to "transform it into a significantly more challenging version that requires deeper reasoning but maintains the same answer." This design forces the synthesizer to focus on the semantic relationship between the original question and the image, promoting the generation of questions that genuinely require deeper visual reasoning while implicitly preserving the answer.
- **Output:** The synthesizer produces a candidate triplet $(I, Q_{cand}, A)$, where $Q_{cand}$ is the newly synthesized question.

#### 3.4. Correctness and Difficulty Guaranteed Verifier

This is the most critical stage, ensuring the quality and relevance of the synthesized data.
- **Candidate Evaluation:** For each candidate question $Q_{cand}$, its difficulty is assessed using the same `rollout pass count` metric, $C_{cand} = C_{pass}(I, Q_{cand}, A; \pi_{verifier})$, where $\pi_{verifier}$ is typically the target model itself.
- **Verification Criteria:** A candidate question is accepted if it meets two conditions:
 1. **Correctness Criterion:** $C_{cand} \ge T_{min}$ (e.g., $T_{min}=4$). This ensures that the original ground truth answer $A$ is still correct and verifiable for the new, more challenging question $Q_{cand}$. A minimum number of successful rollouts confirms the question's validity.
 2. **Difficulty Criterion:** $C_{cand} \le C_{ori} - \Delta_{hard}$ (e.g., $\Delta_{hard}=2$). This quantitatively confirms that the new question $Q_{cand}$ is measurably harder than the original $Q_{ori}$ for the target model.
- **Guaranteed Synthesis:** This two-pronged verification mechanism is the "guaranteed" aspect of SynthRL. It filters out questions that are either incorrectly formulated (not verifiable with $A$) or insufficiently difficult, ensuring that only high-quality, valuable training examples are added to the dataset.

#### 3.5. Dataset and Training Details

- **Seed Dataset:** MMK12, consisting of 8,099 question-answer pairs, preprocessed to 8,072 open-ended answers to prevent reward hacking. Subsets of 2K and 4K samples are also used for scaling analysis.
- **Synthesized Data:** Using Gemini-2.5-Flash-Preview-04-17 as the synthesizer, with $T_{min}=4$ and $\Delta_{hard}=2$, SynthRL generated 3,380 verified harder questions from 8,072 seed samples. The combined dataset (original MMK12 + synthesized variants) is named A-MMK12, totaling 11,452 samples.
- **Training Setup:** The policy model is initialized with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. The RL training uses the EasyR1 framework, which supports VLMs, and GRPO. The vision encoder is unfrozen during training. No KL divergence constraint is applied in GRPO to promote broader exploration.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is assessed on five out-of-domain visual reasoning benchmarks: MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, WeMath, and DynaMath. A standardized evaluation suite using vLLM for inference and Gemini-2.0-Flash-001 as a judge is employed.
- **Difficulty Analysis:** The Bradley-Terry model and Elo rating system, powered by Gemini-2.0-Flash-001 pairwise comparisons, are used to objectively rank evaluation samples by difficulty and categorize them into easy, medium, and hard tiers.

This meticulously designed pipeline ensures that the data augmentation process is not just about increasing volume but strategically enhancing the training signal by providing targeted, higher-quality, and more challenging examples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the scalability and effectiveness of SynthRL, particularly in enhancing visual reasoning capabilities of VLMs.

#### 4.1. Out-of-Domain Generalization

- **Consistent Gains:** SynthRL consistently improves performance across five diverse out-of-domain visual reasoning benchmarks.
- **Overall Improvement (8K scale):** Models trained with the SynthRL-augmented A-MMK12 dataset achieved an average accuracy of **58.0%**, a significant improvement over the baseline models trained solely on the original MMK12 seed dataset (57.0%). This represents a +1.0% absolute gain.
- **Benchmark-Specific Gains:** Notable improvements were observed on individual benchmarks. For instance, MathVerse accuracy increased from 51.6% to 53.5% (+1.9%), and WeMath from 70.6% to 72.6% (+2.0%). These results underscore that the synthetic data enhances generalization to unseen problem distributions, which is crucial for robust VLM performance.

#### 4.2. Data Scaling Effect

- **Widening Performance Gap:** The benefits of SynthRL become more pronounced with larger initial seed datasets. At the 2K data scale, the performance gap between A-MMK12 and MMK12 was modest (+0.2% average accuracy). However, this gap widened to +0.7% with 4K seed examples and a substantial +1.0% with 8K seed examples.
- **Higher Peak Performance:** Analysis of training curves across various data scales (2K, 4K, 8K) showed that while both baseline and SynthRL-trained models exhibited similar learning patterns, models trained on A-MMK12 consistently achieved higher peak performance.
- **Complementary to Traditional Scaling:** These findings indicate that SynthRL's synthesis approach is not merely a replacement for traditional data scaling but complements it, offering additional performance gains that are not achievable by simply increasing the volume of original data. The targeted generation of challenging variants creates a more effective training distribution.

#### 4.3. Difficulty-Based Performance Analysis

- **Targeting Challenging Problems:** To understand where SynthRL provides the most value, evaluation samples were categorized into easy, medium, and hard difficulty tiers using the Bradley-Terry model and Elo rating system.
- **Pronounced Gains on Medium and Hard Examples:** The analysis revealed that SynthRL-augmented training (A-MMK12) yielded the largest improvements on the medium and hard subsets of evaluation examples. At the 8K data scale, while A-MMK12 showed a slight decrease on easy examples (-0.5%), it demonstrated clear gains on medium (+1.7%) and hard (+1.6%) examples. This pattern was consistent across all data scales.
- **Deeper Reasoning Patterns:** This outcome confirms that SynthRL successfully targets and improves model capabilities on complex reasoning challenges that are not adequately addressed by training on existing seed data alone. The shift in performance towards harder examples aligns with the objective of eliciting deeper and more complex reasoning patterns.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on the Verifier

These studies highlight the crucial role of the verification stage in SynthRL's effectiveness.
- **Importance of Target Model Alignment:** When a non-target model (Gemini-2.0-Flash-001) was used as the verifier instead of the target model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), average accuracy dropped from 57.2% to 55.7% (a 1.5% decrease). This demonstrates that effective verification requires the verifier's capabilities to be aligned with those of the model being trained to properly calibrate difficulty.
- **Multi-Pass Verification (Monte Carlo Rollouts):** Using a "single-pass" verification (only one rollout per question) instead of multiple Monte Carlo rollouts resulted in a performance drop to 56.5% (a 0.7% decrease). This confirms the value of stochastic multi-pass evaluation for robust difficulty assessment.
- **Necessity of Verification:** Training with completely unverified synthetic data yielded an average accuracy of 55.8% (a 1.4% decrease compared to A-MMK12). This conclusively shows that the rigorous correctness and difficulty verification steps are essential for SynthRL's overall performance gains.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies on Data Strategy

This study compared different ways of incorporating the synthesized data.
- **Augmentation vs. Replacement:** SynthRL's augmentation approach (A-MMK12, adding synthesized samples to the original dataset) achieved the highest average accuracy at 57.2%. In contrast, a "replacement" strategy (R-MMK12, where synthesized samples replaced their corresponding seed samples to maintain dataset size) performed worse than even the original baseline (56.1% vs. 56.5%).
- **Complementary Value:** This finding suggests that synthesized questions provide maximum benefit when they *complement* the original data distribution rather than replacing it. The improvements stem from both the overall increase in data volume and the targeted difficulty enhancement, creating a richer and more diverse training environment.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate SynthRL's ability to create high-quality, challenging training data that effectively boosts VLM reasoning capabilities, especially for complex problems, and that the rigorous verification process is indispensable for these gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SynthRL represents a significant advancement in the development of robust visual reasoning capabilities for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in the context of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Automated and Scalable Data Generation for RL:** The most direct impact of SynthRL is its ability to **automate the creation of verifiable, challenging training data** for RL. This addresses a critical bottleneck in RL training: the high cost and time-consuming nature of human annotation, especially for complex reasoning tasks. By generating high-quality, guaranteed-correct data without human intervention, SynthRL significantly reduces the resource requirements for developing advanced VLMs, making sophisticated RL training more accessible and scalable.

2. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that models trained with SynthRL's synthesized data achieve **measurable and consistent performance gains** across diverse, out-of-domain visual math reasoning benchmarks. Crucially, these improvements are most pronounced on the **most challenging evaluation samples**, indicating that SynthRL effectively cultivates deeper and more complex reasoning patterns within VLMs. This leads to more robust and capable models that can tackle problems requiring multi-step inference and sophisticated analysis.

3. **Advancing Data-Centric AI for RL:** SynthRL offers a novel and smart approach to data scaling for reinforcement learning. Instead of merely increasing the volume of data, it strategically generates *smarter*, *harder*, and *verifiable* data. This data-centric focus on quality and targeted difficulty enhancement provides a more effective training signal, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved through traditional data augmentation or selection methods alone. It highlights the potential for intelligent data synthesis to drive model performance in complex domains.

4. **Guaranteed Data Quality:** The "guaranteed verification" stage is a cornerstone of SynthRL's reliability. By ensuring near-perfect correctness and a verifiable increase in difficulty, the pipeline mitigates the risks associated with synthetic data (e.g., hallucination, incorrect answers, insufficient challenge). This built-in quality control mechanism is vital for RL, where erroneous rewards can lead to detrimental policy updates.

5. **Democratization of Advanced VLM Development:** By automating the creation of high-quality RL training data, SynthRL has the potential to democratize access to advanced VLM development. Researchers and practitioners with limited human annotation resources can leverage this pipeline to build and fine-tune more capable visual reasoning models, fostering innovation across a broader community.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The success of SynthRL opens several avenues for future research. It encourages further exploration into:
 - The optimal balance between seed data volume and synthetic data generation.
 - Comparative analysis of different synthesizer models and their impact on synthetic data quality.
 - Application of similar verifiable data synthesis paradigms to other complex reasoning domains beyond visual mathematics.
 - Investigating the theoretical underpinnings of why generating "harder" correct samples provides richer learning signals in RL.

In conclusion, SynthRL presents a practical, effective, and verifiable method for scaling visual reasoning in VLMs through automated data synthesis. It not only addresses a significant challenge in RL data scarcity but also pushes the frontier of data-centric AI by demonstrating how intelligent data generation can directly translate into superior model performance, particularly for complex, reasoning-intensive tasks.
