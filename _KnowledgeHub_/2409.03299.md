# Bringing the RT-1-X Foundation Model to a SCARA robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Universiteit van Amsterdam's Intelligent Robotics Lab investigated the RT-1-X robotic foundation model's capacity for generalization to a 40-year-old SCARA robot, an embodiment with distinct kinematics. While zero-shot transfer failed, fine-tuning with 100 demonstrations enabled the robot to achieve a 23% success rate on a pick-up task, though object-specific knowledge transfer proved limited.

## Problem

- Current robotic foundation models struggle to generalize to entirely new robotic embodiments, particularly those with fundamentally different kinematic structures not represented in their training datasets.
- Traditional robotic systems typically require extensive, specific training data for each unique task, environment, and robot form, hindering widespread adaptability.
- Existing large-scale datasets and foundation models primarily focus on robots with spherical workspaces, leaving a gap in understanding generalization to distinct kinematics like SCARA robots.

## Method

- The ==UMI-RTX==, a 40-year-old ==SCARA robot== with a unique kidney-shaped workspace not present in the [[2310.08864|RT-1-X]] training data, was used as the novel robotic embodiment.
- The ==RT-1-X transformer-based model== was fine-tuned using 100 manual ==teleoperated demonstrations== for a specific task: "picking up a banana from the workspace."
- Performance was evaluated for in-distribution task execution and the transfer of object-specific knowledge, using both the fine-tuned object (banana) and a novel object (coke can) from RT-1-X's pre-training data.

## Results

- Initial experiments confirmed that RT-1-X failed to generalize in a zero-shot manner to the UMI SCARA robot.
- After fine-tuning, the model achieved a **23%** success rate for the "picking up a banana" task, with **80%** of attempts resulting in a success or a "near miss" (within 5cm of the target).
- When instructed to pick up an unseen object (coke can) that was part of the original pre-training data, the robot achieved a **10%** success rate, but its ability to differentiate and pick up specific objects deteriorated significantly to **0%** success when both the banana and coke can were present.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art robotic foundation models like RT-1-X do not generalize zero-shot to robots possessing fundamentally different kinematic structures.
> - Fine-tuning with a relatively small dataset of demonstrations (100 episodes) can effectively adapt powerful pre-trained models to new, kinematically distinct robotic embodiments.
> - Skill transfer (e.g., general manipulation actions) can occur, but object-specific knowledge transfer is limited and fragile when confronted with new kinematic contexts or multiple objects.
