# DiffusionOPD: A Unified Perspective of On-Policy Distillation in Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces DiffusionOPD, a two-stage on-policy distillation framework that effectively integrates multi-task capabilities into text-to-image diffusion models. The approach achieved an average min-max normalized score of 0.929 across evaluated metrics, outperforming existing multi-task reinforcement learning methods in both performance and training efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing multi-task reinforcement learning (RL) methods for diffusion models suffer from objective conflicts, task-difficulty imbalance, and catastrophic forgetting, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Users demand a single generative model capable of excelling across multiple objectives (e.g., aesthetics, prompt fidelity, text rendering) simultaneously, which current task-specific models cannot reliably provide.
- Directly optimizing multiple rewards in diffusion models is computationally intensive and leads to slow convergence and lower performance ceilings.

## Method

- **DiffusionOPD** employs a two-stage ==decouple-then-distill== paradigm: first, independent teacher models specialize in single tasks, and second, a unified student model learns from these teachers via on-policy rollouts.
- It adapts ==On-Policy Distillation (OPD)== from discrete-token LLMs to ==continuous-state Markov processes== in diffusion models, using a ==round-robin, on-policy distillation== approach across tasks.
- The framework derives a ==closed-form per-step Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence objective== to unify distillation for both stochastic SDE and deterministic ODE samplers, providing lower-variance gradients than PPO-style methods.

## Results

- DiffusionOPD achieved the highest average min-max normalized score of **0.929** across diverse metrics, consistently outperforming multi-task RL and cascade RL baselines.
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster convergence and attained a higher performance ceiling than competing multi-task RL and other distillation methods like DMD, TDM, and SFT.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the closed-form KL objective yielded faster reward improvement and higher performance compared to PPO-style policy gradients, and deterministic ODE sampling proved **up to 5x more efficient** than SDE sampling with noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling single-task exploration from multi-task integration effectively mitigates issues like objective conflict and catastrophic forgetting prevalent in existing multi-task RL paradigms.
> - A closed-form per-step KL divergence objective is more efficient and stable for continuous-state policy distillation in diffusion models, yielding lower-variance gradients compared to PPO-style approaches.
> - Deterministic ODE samplers facilitate significantly more efficient and higher-performing distillation compared to stochastic SDE samplers, by offering a more stable training signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the improvement and multi-task application of diffusion-based text-to-image models. Recent advancements have seen Reinforcement Learning (RL) effectively applied to enhance the performance of these models, often by optimizing them against single, task-specific reward signals, such as aesthetic quality or text rendering accuracy. However, this often results in models specialized for individual tasks, falling short of the practical user expectation for a single model to perform well across multiple objectives simultaneously (e.g., generating aesthetically pleasing images that are also faithful to textual instructions).

The broader research landscape for multi-task RL in diffusion models currently comprises two main paradigms:
- **Joint Optimization:** This approach attempts to train all tasks concurrently within a unified framework. While conceptually straightforward, it frequently encounters challenges such as objective conflicts (different tasks leading to inconsistent optimization directions) and task-difficulty imbalance (easier tasks dominating learning dynamics).
- **Cascade RL:** This paradigm optimizes the policy on different tasks sequentially. It aims to avoid direct gradient conflicts but is often practically cumbersome due to requirements for multiple training stages, specific schedules, and task-specific hyperparameters. A significant drawback is its susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting, where learning new tasks degrades performance on previously acquired ones.

Additionally, the paper relates to **diffusion distillation**, a field primarily focused on compressing many-step teacher diffusion models into fewer-step student models for more efficient inference. This typically involves trajectory distillation (imitating intermediate transitions) or distribution matching (aligning model distributions at specific timesteps). In contrast, DiffusionOPD utilizes distillation not for step reduction, but for integrating capabilities from multiple reward-specialized teachers into a single, unified student model in a multi-task context.

DiffusionOPD positions itself as a new paradigm to address the limitations of existing multi-task RL approaches by decoupling single-task policy exploration from multi-task capability integration, drawing inspiration from On-Policy Distillation (OPD) previously applied to large language models (LLMs).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DiffusionOPD stems from the observed limitations of current methods for enhancing diffusion-based text-to-image models, particularly in a multi-task setting.

**Motivation:**
1. **Single-Task Specialization vs. Multi-Objective User Demand:** While Reinforcement Learning has proven effective in improving diffusion models, these improvements are typically task-specific. Real-world users often require a single generative model to fulfill several criteria concurrently, such as aesthetic appeal, prompt fidelity, and accurate text rendering. This discrepancy highlights a need for models capable of handling multiple objectives without specialized training for each.
2. **Challenges of Existing Multi-Task RL Paradigms:**
 - **Joint Optimization:** Directly optimizing multiple rewards simultaneously often leads to severe objective conflict, where gradients from different tasks interfere with each other, hindering effective learning. Furthermore, an imbalance in task difficulty can cause easier tasks to overshadow and suppress signals from more complex ones, leading to suboptimal performance across the board.
 - **Cascade RL:** Sequential training, while avoiding direct gradient conflicts, is operationally complex. It demands careful staging, scheduling, and hyperparameter tuning for each task. Crucially, it is prone to catastrophic forgetting, meaning a model might lose proficiency in earlier tasks as it learns new ones. This makes it inefficient and limits the ceiling of its final performance.
3. **Need for Decoupled Optimization:** The authors argue that multi-task RL for diffusion models would benefit from a decoupled approach: separating the process of single-task exploration from the integration of multiple capabilities. This would allow individual tasks to be mastered without interference, followed by a systematic method to combine these proficiencies.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Propose a New Multi-Task Training Paradigm:** Develop DiffusionOPD, a framework based on Online Policy Distillation (OPD), that addresses the limitations of joint and cascade multi-task RL by decoupling single-task exploration and multi-task integration.
2. **Extend On-Policy Distillation to Diffusion Models:** Lift the OPD framework, which originated in discrete token-based autoregressive models (LLMs), to the continuous-state Markov processes inherent in diffusion denoising. This involves formulating a suitable objective function for continuous state spaces.
3. **Derive a Unified, Closed-Form Per-Step KL Objective:** Mathematically derive a per-step Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence objective that can effectively unify distillation for both stochastic SDE (Stochastic Differential Equation) and deterministic ODE (Ordinary Differential Equation) refinement processes in diffusion models. A key aim is to achieve this with lower variance compared to traditional policy gradient methods.
4. **Demonstrate Superiority in Efficiency and Performance:** Empirically show that DiffusionOPD consistently outperforms existing multi-reward RL and cascade RL baselines in terms of training efficiency and the final performance ceiling across diverse benchmarks (e.g., aesthetics, OCR, compositional generation).
5. **Validate Design Choices:** Conduct ablation studies to understand the impact of key design elements, such as the choice of distillation objective, loss formulation (e.g., closed-form KL vs. PPO-style policy gradients), and sampler noise level.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more robust, efficient, and higher-performing multi-task diffusion model by reimagining how knowledge from specialized policies is integrated, moving beyond the inherent conflicts and inefficiencies of direct multi-task RL approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffusionOPD introduces a two-stage training paradigm designed to integrate multiple task-specific capabilities into a single diffusion model efficiently and effectively.

**Core Idea:**
The fundamental approach involves a "decouple-then-distill" strategy. First, individual "teacher" models are trained to specialize in single tasks without inter-task interference. Second, a unified "student" model then learns from these teachers by distilling their knowledge through on-policy rollouts generated by the student itself.

**Stage 1: Per-Task Teacher Training**
- The multi-task problem is decomposed into *M* individual tasks.
- For each task *m*, a separate, task-specific teacher model, denoted `v^(m)_φ_m`, is trained.
- These teachers are optimized independently using existing, off-the-shelf Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms tailored to diffusion models (e.g., Flow-GRPO, DiffusionNFT). This ensures each teacher can fully specialize in its reward objective without being affected by conflicts or imbalances from other tasks.
- For example, GRPO-Guard was used for OCR and Aesthetics tasks, while DiffusionNFT was employed for GenEval, based on their observed performance characteristics for respective objectives.

**Stage 2: Multi-Task On-Policy Distillation**
- A unified student model, `v_θ`, is initialized from a pre-trained diffusion policy (`v_ref`).
- Training proceeds in a round-robin, on-policy manner across all *M* tasks.
- For each task *m*:
 1. Prompts are sampled from the task-specific dataset `C^(m)`.
 2. The current student model `v_θ` generates an on-policy denoising trajectory `{x_t_j}_N_j=0`. This means the student produces its own samples, which are then used as the context for distillation.
 3. Along this sampled trajectory, the corresponding task teacher `v^(m)_φ_m` is evaluated.
 4. A Monte Carlo estimate of the On-Policy Distillation (OPD) objective is computed, aiming to match the student's and teacher's transition means at every denoising step.

**Lifting OPD to Continuous-State Markov Chains:**
- The original OPD framework for LLMs decomposes a sequence-level KL divergence into a sum of per-step conditional KLs. DiffusionOPD reinterprets this as applicable to any discrete-time Markov chain where student and teacher share state space and transition kernel structure.
- The diffusion denoising process is modeled as a discrete-time Markov chain induced by the reverse-time SDE.
- **Per-step Gaussian Transitions:** The reverse-time SDE is discretized using Euler-Maruyama on a denoising schedule. This results in one-step transition kernels `p_S` (student) and `p_T` (teacher) that are Gaussian distributions. Crucially, these Gaussian kernels share the same per-step covariance (`¯σ^2_j I_d`), as it depends only on the scheduler and global noise level, which are identical for student and teacher.
- **Closed-Form Reverse KL:** Since the student and teacher Gaussian transition kernels share the same covariance and are evaluated at the same student-rollout state, their reverse KL divergence has a closed-form expression: `KL(N(μ_1, Σ) || N(μ_2, Σ)) = 1/2 (μ_1 - μ_2)^T Σ^-1 (μ_1 - μ_2)`. When `Σ = ¯σ^2_j I_d`, this simplifies to `∥μ_S - μ_T∥^2_2 / (2¯σ^2_j)`. This expression is exact, avoiding Monte-Carlo variance often associated with policy gradient methods.
- **Unified Perspective (SDE and ODE):**
 - For stochastic SDE samplers, the objective is the derived closed-form reverse KL (Equation 11).
 - For deterministic ODE samplers (where the noise level is zero), the distribution matching reduces to pointwise transition matching. The reverse-KL objective naturally transforms into a direct squared L2 loss between the student's and teacher's deterministic transition targets (Equation 12). This provides a unified principle for distillation across different sampler types.

**Loss Formulation: Closed-form KL vs. PPO-style Policy Gradient:**
- The paper theoretically demonstrates that the closed-form KL minimization and PPO-style policy gradient methods (using negative KL as a dense reward) have the same expected gradient.
- However, the closed-form KL is preferred for two reasons:
 1. **Lower Variance Gradient:** The closed-form KL gradient is entirely pathwise, obtained via direct backpropagation. The PPO formulation introduces an additional score-function term proportional to Gaussian noise, which, while unbiased, increases gradient variance.
 2. **Broader Applicability:** The closed-form KL remains valid for both stochastic (SDE) and deterministic (ODE) sampling regimes. A PPO-style objective is inherently tied to a stochastic policy density and importance ratios, making it less natural for deterministic samplers.

**Multi-Task Optimization Strategy:**
- To stabilize multi-task optimization and mitigate bias towards individual tasks, losses are accumulated over a full round-robin cycle.
- The gradient accumulation factor is set to *M* (number of tasks), meaning one accumulation step per task.
- A single backward pass and optimizer step are performed only after all *M* tasks have been visited and their respective losses averaged. This ensures each parameter update reflects supervision from the complete task set.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Base Model:** SD3.5-Medium at 512x512 resolution.
- **Finetuning:** LoRA (α=64, r=32).
- **Sampler:** 40-step first-order ODE sampler for evaluation.
- **Reward Models:** GenEval, OCR (rule-based); PickScore, Clip-Score, HPSv2.1, Aesthetics, ImageReward, UnifiedReward (model-based).
- **Datasets:** FlowGRPO splits for GenEval and OCR; Pick-a-Pic for training and DrawBench for evaluation of model-based rewards.

This comprehensive methodology allows DiffusionOPD to leverage specialized teacher knowledge efficiently and integrate it into a single, capable student model, aiming for robust multi-task performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DiffusionOPD was conducted across various benchmarks and compared against multiple baselines, focusing on overall performance, training efficiency, and the impact of key design choices.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Multi-Task RL Methods

- **Overall Performance:** DiffusionOPD achieved the highest average min-max normalized score of 0.929 across all evaluated metrics, surpassing all multi-task RL and cascade RL baselines. This indicates its effectiveness in achieving comprehensive multi-domain preference optimization.
- **Single-Task Teachers:** Individual teachers demonstrated high specialization in their respective domains (e.g., GenEval Teacher for compositional alignment, OCR Teacher for text rendering, Aes Teacher for aesthetics). However, they exhibited limited generalization capabilities beyond their specific optimization targets, performing poorly on other metrics.
- **Multi-Task RL Baselines (GRPO-Guard, NFT):** These methods showed improvement in overall task coverage compared to single-task teachers but required substantially longer training times. They also exhibited slower convergence and encountered stronger optimization interference across heterogeneous rewards, particularly on challenging objectives such as aesthetics, resulting in lower performance ceilings.
- **Cascade NFT:** While achieving relatively competitive performance in some areas, Cascade NFT was identified as the slowest and most cumbersome strategy due to its sequential, multi-stage training process. It also showed susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting, which limited its final performance.
- **Qualitative Results:** Figures 2 and 7 provided visual evidence suggesting superior image quality generated by DiffusionOPD compared to multi-task RL and single-task teacher baselines.

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency

- DiffusionOPD demonstrated significantly faster convergence and attained a higher performance ceiling than multi-task RL baselines (Figure 1a, Figure 3). The two-stage training paradigm, where teachers are trained independently before distillation, contributed to this efficiency by avoiding the optimization burden of solving all tasks jointly from scratch. The total training time for DiffusionOPD (maximum teacher training time + OPD training time) was also competitive or lower than multi-task baselines.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Distillation Methods

- DiffusionOPD was compared against other distillation techniques, including Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), Trajectory Distribution Matching (TDM), and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- **Performance:** DiffusionOPD consistently achieved the fastest convergence and the highest final performance ceiling among all compared distillation methods (Figure 5).
- **Qualitative Results:** Figures 4 and 8 provided qualitative comparisons, indicating superior visual quality for images generated by DiffusionOPD compared to DMD, TDM, and SFT.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Loss Formulation

- The paper compared the proposed closed-form KL objective against a PPO-style policy gradient approach, both evaluated in a multi-task setting with the same SDE sampler noise level (a=0.7).
- **Result:** The closed-form KL objective exhibited faster reward improvement and achieved a higher performance ceiling than the PPO-style policy gradient (Figure 6). This empirical finding supports the theoretical analysis that the closed-form KL provides a lower-variance gradient estimator.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies on Sampler Noise Level

- An investigation into the SDE sampler noise level during distillation revealed a direct correlation with performance and efficiency.
- **Result:** Reducing the noise level consistently led to faster convergence and higher evaluation scores for the student model (Figure 6). Notably, using a deterministic ODE sampler (equivalent to a noise level of 0) was observed to be up to five times more efficient than an SDE sampler with a noise level of 0.7, while also achieving the highest performance. This suggests that deterministic transitions facilitate more stable and efficient distillation.

In summary, the findings indicate that DiffusionOPD offers a more efficient and effective approach to multi-task preference alignment in diffusion models by leveraging a principled on-policy distillation framework, outperforming existing multi-task RL and other distillation methods across various quantitative and qualitative metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DiffusionOPD represents a significant contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in the realm of text-to-image diffusion models and their multi-task capabilities. Its potential impact spans theoretical understanding, practical application, and future research directions.

**Theoretical Significance:**
1. **Extension of On-Policy Distillation:** The work successfully extends the On-Policy Distillation (OPD) framework from discrete token-based autoregressive models (LLMs) to continuous-state Markov processes inherent in diffusion models. This generalization broadens the applicability of OPD to a new and important domain of generative AI.
2. **Principled Framework for Continuous-State Distillation:** By deriving a closed-form per-step KL objective, DiffusionOPD establishes a principled theoretical framework for distillation in continuous state spaces. This objective unifies stochastic SDE and deterministic ODE refinement, offering a coherent perspective on how knowledge transfer can occur across different sampling mechanisms.
3. **Lower-Variance Optimization:** The theoretical and empirical demonstration that the closed-form KL objective yields lower-variance gradients compared to PPO-style policy gradients is a notable contribution. This insight can guide future algorithm design in areas where high gradient variance is a known challenge, leading to more stable and efficient training.

**Practical Impact:**
1. **Enhanced Multi-Task Performance:** DiffusionOPD addresses a critical limitation of current diffusion models by enabling a single model to perform well across multiple, often conflicting, objectives simultaneously (e.g., aesthetics, compositional accuracy, OCR). This directly meets real-world user expectations for versatile generative tools.
2. **Improved Training Efficiency:** The decoupled two-stage training paradigm of DiffusionOPD (independent teacher training followed by student distillation) demonstrates significantly faster convergence compared to joint multi-task RL approaches. This efficiency can reduce the computational resources and time required to develop high-performing multi-task generative models.
3. **Mitigation of RL Challenges:** By separating single-task exploration from multi-task integration, DiffusionOPD effectively bypasses the common pitfalls of multi-task RL, such as cross-task interference, task-difficulty imbalance in joint optimization, and catastrophic forgetting in cascade RL. This makes the training process more robust and reliable.
4. **State-of-the-Art Results:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on evaluated benchmarks (aesthetics, OCR, GenEval) indicates the practical superiority of DiffusionOPD over existing methods, making it a competitive candidate for deployment in practical text-to-image generation systems.

**Future Research Directions:**
1. **Foundation for Preference Alignment:** DiffusionOPD can serve as a foundation for future work on preference-aligned diffusion modeling, where models learn to generate content that aligns with diverse and nuanced human preferences across various dimensions.
2. **Exploration of Distillation Objectives:** The framework's flexibility, noted by the authors, to incorporate a broader family of existing distillation objectives (beyond just reverse-KL) suggests avenues for exploring even more effective knowledge transfer mechanisms.
3. **Scaling to Larger Models and Tasks:** Investigating the scalability of DiffusionOPD to even larger diffusion models and a greater number of more complex, diverse tasks would be a natural next step, further pushing the boundaries of generative AI capabilities.

In summary, DiffusionOPD offers a robust and efficient solution to the multi-task challenge in diffusion models, providing both theoretical clarity and practical performance gains, thereby advancing the field towards more capable and versatile generative AI systems.
