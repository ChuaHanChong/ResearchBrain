# DiffusionOPD: A Unified Perspective of On-Policy Distillation in Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces DiffusionOPD, a two-stage on-policy distillation framework that effectively integrates multi-task capabilities into text-to-image diffusion models. The approach achieved an average min-max normalized score of 0.929 across evaluated metrics, outperforming existing multi-task reinforcement learning methods in both performance and training efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing multi-task reinforcement learning (RL) methods for diffusion models suffer from objective conflicts, task-difficulty imbalance, and catastrophic forgetting, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Users demand a single generative model capable of excelling across multiple objectives (e.g., aesthetics, prompt fidelity, text rendering) simultaneously, which current task-specific models cannot reliably provide.
- Directly optimizing multiple rewards in diffusion models is computationally intensive and leads to slow convergence and lower performance ceilings.

## Method

- **DiffusionOPD** employs a two-stage ==decouple-then-distill== paradigm: first, independent teacher models specialize in single tasks, and second, a unified student model learns from these teachers via on-policy rollouts.
- It adapts ==On-Policy Distillation (OPD)== from discrete-token LLMs to ==continuous-state Markov processes== in diffusion models, using a ==round-robin, on-policy distillation== approach across tasks.
- The framework derives a ==closed-form per-step Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence objective== to unify distillation for both stochastic SDE and deterministic ODE samplers, providing lower-variance gradients than PPO-style methods.

## Results

- DiffusionOPD achieved the highest average min-max normalized score of **0.929** across diverse metrics, consistently outperforming multi-task RL and cascade RL baselines.
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster convergence and attained a higher performance ceiling than competing multi-task RL and other distillation methods like DMD, TDM, and SFT.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the closed-form KL objective yielded faster reward improvement and higher performance compared to PPO-style policy gradients, and deterministic ODE sampling proved **up to 5x more efficient** than SDE sampling with noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling single-task exploration from multi-task integration effectively mitigates issues like objective conflict and catastrophic forgetting prevalent in existing multi-task RL paradigms.
> - A closed-form per-step KL divergence objective is more efficient and stable for continuous-state policy distillation in diffusion models, yielding lower-variance gradients compared to PPO-style approaches.
> - Deterministic ODE samplers facilitate significantly more efficient and higher-performing distillation compared to stochastic SDE samplers, by offering a more stable training signal.
