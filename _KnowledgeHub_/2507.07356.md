# UniTracker: Learning Universal Whole-Body Motion Tracker for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> UniTracker introduces a multi-stage training framework for humanoid robots that learns a universal whole-body motion tracker. It leverages a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) and a fast adaptation module to enable a single policy to accurately and robustly track over 8,100 diverse human motions on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning policies for humanoid motion tracking often exhibit limited expressiveness, diversity, and generalization to unseen motion sequences.
- Deployment of these policies under partial observations frequently suffers from issues like orientation drift and global inconsistencies due to a lack of global contextual information.
- Current methods lack robust and scalable mechanisms for quickly adapting to challenging or out-of-distribution motion sequences without extensive retraining.

## Method

- A three-stage training framework is proposed: an oracle teacher policy learns high-fidelity tracking in simulation, followed by an online distillation stage.
- The distillation uses a ==Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)== to learn a universal student policy, modeling motion diversity and implicitly injecting global context by aligning its partial-observation prior with a globally-informed encoder.
- A lightweight residual decoder is appended for fast adaptation, allowing the pretrained universal policy to quickly specialize for challenging or out-of-distribution motions through targeted reinforcement learning fine-tuning.

## Results

- UniTracker achieves state-of-the-art performance, with a **91.82%** success rate and significantly lower Mean Per Keypoint Position Error (**82.57**) across the entire AMASS training dataset, outperforming existing baselines.
- The system demonstrates enhanced generalization to unseen and out-of-distribution motions, successfully tracking complex behaviors (e.g., text-to-motion, video-reconstructed) in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 robot.
- The CVAE-based policy effectively mitigates orientation drift, maintains global consistency, and exhibits superior robustness to observation noise compared to non-CVAE approaches, enabling reliable real-world deployment on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot for over 8,100 diverse motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling the student policy as a CVAE, where the partial-observation prior is aligned with a privileged-observation encoder, effectively injects global context and enhances motion diversity and generalization capabilities.
> - A modular, multi-stage training approach, separating high-fidelity teacher learning from robust student distillation and rapid adaptation, improves scalability and practical applicability for complex robotic tasks.
> - Leveraging a pretrained universal policy with a lightweight residual adaptation module enables efficient specialization for difficult or novel motions, significantly reducing the training time required for edge cases.
