# UniTracker: Learning Universal Whole-Body Motion Tracker for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> UniTracker introduces a multi-stage training framework for humanoid robots that learns a universal whole-body motion tracker. It leverages a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) and a fast adaptation module to enable a single policy to accurately and robustly track over 8,100 diverse human motions on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning policies for humanoid motion tracking often exhibit limited expressiveness, diversity, and generalization to unseen motion sequences.
- Deployment of these policies under partial observations frequently suffers from issues like orientation drift and global inconsistencies due to a lack of global contextual information.
- Current methods lack robust and scalable mechanisms for quickly adapting to challenging or out-of-distribution motion sequences without extensive retraining.

## Method

- A three-stage training framework is proposed: an oracle teacher policy learns high-fidelity tracking in simulation, followed by an online distillation stage.
- The distillation uses a ==Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)== to learn a universal student policy, modeling motion diversity and implicitly injecting global context by aligning its partial-observation prior with a globally-informed encoder.
- A lightweight residual decoder is appended for fast adaptation, allowing the pretrained universal policy to quickly specialize for challenging or out-of-distribution motions through targeted reinforcement learning fine-tuning.

## Results

- UniTracker achieves state-of-the-art performance, with a **91.82%** success rate and significantly lower Mean Per Keypoint Position Error (**82.57**) across the entire AMASS training dataset, outperforming existing baselines.
- The system demonstrates enhanced generalization to unseen and out-of-distribution motions, successfully tracking complex behaviors (e.g., text-to-motion, video-reconstructed) in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 robot.
- The CVAE-based policy effectively mitigates orientation drift, maintains global consistency, and exhibits superior robustness to observation noise compared to non-CVAE approaches, enabling reliable real-world deployment on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot for over 8,100 diverse motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling the student policy as a CVAE, where the partial-observation prior is aligned with a privileged-observation encoder, effectively injects global context and enhances motion diversity and generalization capabilities.
> - A modular, multi-stage training approach, separating high-fidelity teacher learning from robust student distillation and rapid adaptation, improves scalability and practical applicability for complex robotic tasks.
> - Leveraging a pretrained universal policy with a lightweight residual adaptation module enables efficient specialization for difficult or novel motions, significantly reducing the training time required for edge cases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has long pursued the grand challenge of enabling robots to perform complex, human-like tasks in unstructured, human-centric environments. A critical enabler for this is sophisticated whole-body motion control, which coordinates numerous joints and limbs for stability, expressiveness, and adaptability.

Historically, humanoid control relied on traditional optimization-based methods, which, while precise, suffered from limitations in adapting to environmental variations, robustness, and computational demands. The advent of reinforcement learning (RL) has revolutionized this domain, offering a data-driven approach to learn robust and adaptive control policies directly from interaction.

This paper positions itself directly within the cutting edge of RL-based whole-body control, specifically focusing on *motion tracking* using reference motion sequences. The existing landscape of RL-based humanoid controllers can be broadly categorized by their control signal sources:
- **Teleoperation:** Directly controlling robots with human input (e.g., motion capture), as seen in works like Twist, H2O, and OmniH2O. These aim for high-fidelity mimicry.
- **Offline Motion Datasets:** Using pre-collected human or humanoid motion sequences as references for imitation learning. This is a highly active area, with examples like Exbody, Exbody2, and the recently notable GMT, which claims to be the first to track 8,000 motions with a single policy. UniTracker directly competes and builds upon this category.
- **Video-based Motion Estimation:** Extracting motion data from videos to guide robot control (e.g., VideoMimic).
- **High-level Task Commands:** Sparse control signals specifying desired outcomes (e.g., walking direction) rather than detailed joint motions (e.g., Hover, HugWBC).

UniTracker specifically addresses the limitations of prevalent *teacher-student frameworks* used for learning motion tracking policies from datasets. These frameworks, often employing simple MLP-based DAgger architectures, exhibit three key shortcomings:
1. **Limited Expressiveness and Diversity:** They tend to average out diverse behaviors, resulting in less expressive and often robotic motions.
2. **Poor Generalization:** They struggle to generalize to unseen or out-of-distribution motion sequences.
3. **Global Inconsistencies:** Lack of global context during training leads to orientation drift and overall inconsistent global behavior in real-world deployments.

By proposing a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)-based approach and a fast adaptation module, UniTracker directly aims to overcome these specific challenges. It seeks to establish a *universal* motion tracker that not only tracks a large variety of motions (over 8,100, directly competing with GMT's claim) but does so with enhanced expressiveness, generalization, robustness, and global consistency under partial observations. This work contributes to making humanoid robots more capable and versatile for real-world deployment, pushing the boundaries of what a single, unified policy can achieve in complex motion tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of UniTracker is to develop a generalizable, expressive, and robust whole-body motion tracking system for humanoid robots that can operate effectively in real-world environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations, rooted in the limitations of existing state-of-the-art methods:

1. **Enhance Motion Expressiveness and Diversity:** Current MLP-based policies, when distilled from a privileged teacher to a student policy operating under partial observations, often fail to preserve the rich diversity of reference motions. This results in averaged, less expressive, and somewhat monotonous robot behaviors. UniTracker aims to capture and reproduce the full spectrum of motion diversity.
2. **Improve Generalization to Unseen Motions:** A significant challenge for current methods is their limited representational capacity, leading to poor generalization when faced with motion sequences not present in the training data. The objective is to create a policy that can robustly track novel or out-of-distribution movements.
3. **Ensure Global Consistency and Prevent Orientation Drift:** The deployment of policies trained under partial observations often suffers from a lack of global contextual information, manifesting as undesirable phenomena like orientation drift and general global inconsistencies. UniTracker seeks to implicitly inject global awareness into the policy, even with limited sensory input.
4. **Enable Scalable and Adaptive Tracking:** While a universal policy should handle most motions, specific, highly dynamic, or rare sequences might remain challenging. The motivation is to provide a mechanism for rapid adaptation to these "edge cases" without retraining the entire policy, thus extending the system's practical applicability and scalability.
5. **Achieve Real-world Deployment Robustness:** Ultimately, the aim is to develop a policy that performs reliably and accurately not only in simulation but also on physical humanoid robots, demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer.

In essence, UniTracker is motivated by the need to bridge the gap between high-fidelity motion tracking in privileged simulation environments and robust, diverse, and globally consistent deployment on real humanoid hardware, all while addressing the computational and generalization challenges of learning from large motion datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniTracker proposes a three-stage training framework designed for scalable and adaptive whole-body motion tracking. This modular pipeline addresses the limitations of existing teacher-student approaches through innovative uses of generative modeling and reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Humanoid Motion Dataset Curation

The foundation of UniTracker is a large-scale, high-quality motion dataset.
- **Source:** Primarily derived from the publicly available AMASS dataset.
- **Filtering:** Initial exclusion of human-object interactions and short clips (less than 10 frames). Further refinement using the PHC filtering protocol ensures physical feasibility and suitability for whole-body tracking. This yields 8,179 human motions, represented as SMPL parameters.
- **Retargeting:** A two-stage approach, inspired by H2O, is used to bridge the embodiment gap between the SMPL human model and the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. This involves optimizing shape parameters and then performing gradient descent over robot root and joint positions to minimize link distances throughout motion sequences, with regularization for smoothness.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Oracle Policy Training in Simulation

This stage focuses on learning a high-fidelity "teacher" policy under privileged conditions in a simulator.
- **Problem Formulation:** Goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (RL) using PPO, where the policy learns to track reference motions. The Unitree G1 robot's 29 DoF are reduced to 23 by locking wrist joints.
- **Oracle State Space:** The teacher policy uses `s_p-oracle` (robot proprioception: position, orientation, velocities, joint positions/velocities, previous action) and `s_g-oracle` (goal state: one-frame difference between reference and current pose for various body parts). All states are rotated to the local coordinate frame.
- **Reward Design:** A comprehensive reward function combines task rewards (tracking keypoints, feet, body rotation, joint positions/velocities, linear/angular velocities), regularization (action rate, torque), and penalties (slippage, early termination). Curriculum learning is applied to regularization and penalty terms.
- **Training Enhancements:**
 - **Early Termination:** Conditions (e.g., orientation exceeding threshold, average link distance > 0.5m) prevent invalid data collection.
 - **Reference State Initialization:** Randomly samples reference motion starting points, allowing the policy to learn different motion phases in parallel.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Only asset properties (friction, mass, CoM) are randomized at this stage, focusing the teacher on high-fidelity tracking while generating robust reference actions.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Hierarchical Controller via Online Distillation (CVAE-based Universal Student Policy)

This stage distills the teacher's knowledge into a deployable "student" policy that operates under partial observations, leveraging a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE).
- **Deployable State Space:** The student policy uses `s_p-deploy` (stacked past 25 steps of joint positions/velocities, root angular velocity, gravity vector, previous action) and `s_g-deploy` (stacked future 5 steps of reference pose height, root orientations, velocities, and rigid body/root positions). This reflects real-world sensor limitations.
- **Modeling Diversity with CVAE:**
 - The student policy `π_deploy` is modeled as a CVAE to capture motion diversity and handle ambiguity from partial observations.
 - **Variational Encoder ε:** Takes *privileged* oracle observations (`s_p-oracle`, `s_g-oracle`) to compute the latent code distribution `z_t`. This is crucial for injecting global context.
 - **Conditional Prior ρ:** Learns a distribution of `z_t` based on *partial* deployable observations (`s_p-deploy`, `s_g-deploy`).
 - **Decoder D:** Generates the action `a_t` from `s_p-deploy` and `z_t`.
 - **Alignment:** The objective function (Evidence Lower Bound) includes a KL divergence term `D_KL(ε(z_t|s_p-o,s_g-o) || ρ(z_t|s_p-d,s_g-d))`. This forces the partial-observation prior to align with the globally-informed encoder, implicitly injecting global intent into the latent space even when the policy operates under partial observability.
 - **Optimization:** Trained via online distillation, minimizing a combined loss `L = L_action + βL_KL`, where `L_action` is the squared error between student and teacher actions, and `L_KL` is the KL divergence. The encoder is modeled as a residual to the prior.

#### 3.4. Stage 3: Fast Adaptation on top of a Universal Policy

This final stage fine-tunes the universal policy for challenging or out-of-distribution motions.
- **Architecture:** A lightweight *residual decoder* `D(ea_t|s_t_p-deploy, s_t_g-deploy)` is appended to the frozen universal policy.
- **Training:** Only the residual decoder is fine-tuned using RL (same reward function as Stage 1).
- **Input:** The residual decoder directly uses explicit motion references (not latent representations) as input, as out-of-distribution motions might not be well-captured by the learned CVAE prior. The goal is fast overfitting rather than generalization.
- **Action:** The final action is `a_final_t = a_t + ea_t`, combining the universal policy's output with the residual correction.
- **Flexibility:** Supports both per-instance and batch-mode adaptation.

#### 3.5. Experiment Setup

- **Simulation:** Isaac Gym with 8192 parallel environments, domain randomization. Validated in MuJoCo.
- **Real-world:** Unitree G1 humanoid robot (1.3m tall, 29 DoF, controlling 23 DoFs).
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR), Mean Per Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE), Velocity Distance (Vel-Dist), Acceleration Distance (Acc-Dist).

This comprehensive methodology builds a robust, expressive, and adaptable whole-body motion tracker by systematically addressing the challenges of data curation, high-fidelity teacher training, generalized student learning with latent space modeling for diversity and global context, and targeted adaptation for difficult motions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniTracker's experimental evaluation in both simulation and real-world environments demonstrates its superior performance and effectiveness across several key aspects:

- **Superior Tracking Accuracy and Robustness:**
 - UniTracker consistently outperforms all baselines, including state-of-the-art methods like OmniH2O, Exbody2, and a DAgger framework without CVAE, as well as a policy trained from scratch (Table IIa).
 - It achieves the highest Success Rate (SR) of 91.82% and the lowest Mean Per Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE) of 82.57, Velocity Distance (4.22), and Acceleration Distance (1.79) across the entire AMASS training dataset. The performance gap is even more pronounced on successfully tracked motions.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capability:**
 - The CVAE-based architecture is crucial for generalization. UniTracker successfully tracks challenging, unseen motions (e.g., a crescent kick) in MuJoCo simulation, while a variant without CVAE fails to reproduce them effectively (Figure 4).
 - This improved generalization extends to diverse downstream applications, including tracking motions generated from text prompts (via MDM) and reconstructed from monocular video (via GVHMR), both in simulation and on the real robot (Figure 3).

- **Improved Global Consistency:**
 - The CVAE-based framework, by aligning the partial-observation prior with a globally-informed encoder, effectively addresses the issue of orientation drift. In a walking sequence, UniTracker allows the robot to walk in a straight line, closely following the reference, whereas the non-CVAE variant gradually deviates from the path (Figure 4).

- **Robustness to Observation Noise:**
 - UniTracker exhibits significantly higher robustness to increasing levels of observation noise compared to the DAgger baseline without CVAE. It maintains a higher SR and lower MPKPE, with slower performance degradation as noise intensity increases (Table III). This is critical for real-world deployment where sensor noise is inevitable.

- **Efficient and Fast Adaptation:**
 - The lightweight fast adaptation module demonstrates remarkable efficiency. For challenging motions, it converges substantially faster (in terms of episode length and cumulative reward) than training from scratch, highlighting the effectiveness of leveraging the pretrained universal policy for rapid specialization (Figure 5).

- **Successful Real-World Deployment:**
 - Crucially, UniTracker is validated on a real 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot. It successfully tracks over 8,100 diverse human motions, including highly dynamic behaviors, using a single unified network, confirming its practical viability and robustness in hardware.

- **Ablation Studies Confirm Design Choices:**
 - **Actor Architecture:** Ablations show that excluding explicit reference motion from the actor's input (forcing reliance on the latent variable `z`) is vital for the CVAE's influence and superior performance (Table IIb).
 - **KL Residual:** Using a KL residual (where the encoder is a residual to the prior) is beneficial, yielding better performance (Table IIc).
 - **KL Coefficient:** An optimal KL coefficient of 0.1 balances latent regularization and reconstruction quality, leading to the best policy performance (Table IId).
 - **Future Window Size:** A future window size of 5 frames for reference motion in the CVAE prior provides the best trade-off between responsiveness and stability (Table IIe).
 - **Latent Dimension:** A latent dimension of 64 yielded the best overall performance, indicating that larger dimensions do not necessarily lead to improvements and can make training harder (Table IIf).

In summary, UniTracker delivers state-of-the-art performance in humanoid whole-body motion tracking, demonstrating significant advancements in accuracy, generalization, robustness, and real-world applicability, primarily due to its innovative CVAE-based architecture and multi-stage training pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniTracker represents a substantial advancement in the field of humanoid robot control, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Truly Universal Humanoid Control:** The ability to track over 8,100 diverse motion sequences with a single, unified policy marks a critical step towards universal whole-body control. This level of generality simplifies policy deployment and removes the need for task-specific controllers for a vast range of behaviors, accelerating the development and application of humanoids.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap with Robustness:** The method's demonstrated robustness to observation noise and successful deployment on a physical Unitree G1 robot is paramount. This validates the effectiveness of the training methodology, including domain randomization and the CVAE's ability to handle partial observations, in producing policies that reliably transfer from simulation to the real world.
- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations of Imitation Learning:** By tackling the issues of limited expressiveness, poor generalization, and orientation drift inherent in traditional teacher-student imitation learning frameworks, UniTracker significantly enhances the quality and reliability of learned robot behaviors. The CVAE's role in modeling motion diversity and implicitly incorporating global context through latent space alignment is a key architectural innovation.
- **Scalability for Complex Behaviors:** The fast adaptation module allows the universal policy to quickly learn and execute challenging, out-of-distribution motions with minimal overhead. This adaptability is crucial for handling the unforeseen scenarios and rare skills required for complex real-world tasks, making the system more practical and scalable.
- **Foundation for Downstream Applications:** The successful integration with text-to-motion generation and video-based motion estimation demonstrates UniTracker's versatility as a foundational motion tracking primitive. This opens doors for humanoids to interpret high-level commands (text) or learn directly from human demonstrations (video) more effectively, enabling intuitive human-robot interaction and novel applications.
- **Advancement in Generative Models for Control:** The effective use of CVAE for continuous control in a partial observation setting, particularly with the careful alignment of prior and encoder distributions, showcases a powerful technique for injecting structured latent representations into complex robotic tasks. This could inspire further research into generative modeling for robust and diverse control policies.
- **Paving the Way for Autonomous Humanoid Deployment:** By providing a highly capable and adaptable motion tracking system, UniTracker brings humanoid robots closer to autonomous operation in human-centric environments. Such robots could eventually perform a wide range of services, manufacturing, or assistive tasks with human-like dexterity and responsiveness, impacting industries and daily life.

In conclusion, UniTracker makes significant contributions to robust and generalizable humanoid whole-body control. Its innovative multi-stage framework, centered around a CVAE-based universal policy and a fast adaptation module, effectively addresses long-standing challenges in motion tracking. This work not only pushes the boundaries of current humanoid capabilities but also lays a strong foundation for future research and real-world deployment of advanced humanoid robots.
