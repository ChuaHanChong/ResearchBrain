# CronusVLA: Towards Efficient and Robust Manipulation via Multi-Frame Vision-Language-Action Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Lab and collaborating institutions introduce CronusVLA, an efficient framework that extends single-frame Vision-Language-Action models to leverage multi-frame observations for robust robotic manipulation. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks in simulation and real-world, significantly improving resilience against visual disturbances while maintaining fast inference speeds.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily operate within a single-frame paradigm, failing to fully utilize temporal information critical for understanding motion cues and resolving state ambiguities.
- Directly extending large-scale VLA models to a multi-frame paradigm incurs prohibitive computational overhead and inference latency due to the quadratic scaling of self-attention with input tokens.
- Current robotic manipulation benchmarks often lack comprehensive evaluation for 'observational robustness,' leaving a gap in assessing how VLA models perform under real-world visual disturbances like blur, noise, or occlusions.

## Method

- A two-stage framework: single-frame VLA pretraining followed by efficient ==multi-frame post-training==, using continuous 'learnable features' and ==feature chunking== with a first-in, first-out (FIFO) queue to aggregate historical information while reusing computed VLM features for fast inference.
- Employs a ==DiT-based cross-frame decoder== with a feature modulator to dynamically balance current and past frame contributions, and a multi-frame regularization technique using stop-gradients to efficiently update the VLA backbone without disrupting single-frame perception.
- Developed ==SimplerEnv-OR==, a novel benchmark with 24 types of temporal and spatial observational disturbances across over 120 severity levels, to quantitatively assess and compare model robustness.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average success rates of **70.9%** on SimplerEnv Google Robot and WidowX Robot tasks, and **97.0%** on the LIBERO benchmark, showing significant improvements (e.g., **40.3%** over OpenVLA on LIBERO long-horizon tasks).
- Demonstrated superior observational robustness on the SimplerEnv-OR benchmark, consistently outperforming prior methods under various temporal and spatial disturbances, with high R-Scores (e.g., **96.2 R-Score** for Sparse 1:3 disturbances).
- Maintained fast inference speeds (e.g., **8.7 Hz** for the 7B model with 7 frames), addressing the computational challenges of multi-frame processing and making advanced temporal modeling practical for real-time robotic control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient integration of multi-frame temporal information is crucial for enhancing the performance, fluidity, and robustness of robotic manipulation, moving beyond the limitations of instantaneous single-frame perception.
> - Decoupling single-frame perception from multi-frame temporal modeling allows for leveraging powerful pre-trained VLM backbones while achieving significant computational efficiency during multi-frame inference.
> - Rigorous evaluation of observational robustness against diverse disturbances is essential for bridging the gap between laboratory performance and the reliable deployment of VLA models in complex, unpredictable real-world environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as backbones. These VLMs provide rich vision-language representations, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute complex tasks. Recent prominent VLA methods such as RT-2, OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, 3D-VLA, and Magma, primarily adapt these advanced VLMs by re-engineering tokenizers or integrating specialized action heads to generate robot actions. They generally operate within a "single-frame paradigm," where the robot's decision-making is based on a single current observation image.

However, this single-frame approach has inherent limitations. It struggles to fully capitalize on the temporal information embedded in multi-frame histories, which is crucial for understanding motion cues, resolving state ambiguities, and enhancing observational robustness. While low-level policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy) have demonstrated the benefits of multi-frame observations for performance, directly extending this to large-scale VLA models built on VLM backbones introduces substantial computational overhead and inference latency. This quadratic scaling of self-attention computation with the number of input tokens makes large-scale embodied pretraining and real-world deployment impractical. Some existing multi-frame approaches, like RoboVLMs, attempt to use memory-based LSTMs but often train embodiment capabilities from scratch, overlooking the potential of efficiently adapting already pre-trained single-frame models. Other methods like TraceVLA use visual prompting to draw past traces on current observations, which is an indirect way of integrating temporal information.

CronusVLA strategically positions itself within this landscape by addressing these critical shortcomings. It proposes an efficient and robust framework to extend *existing single-frame VLA models* to a multi-frame paradigm without incurring prohibitive computational costs or sacrificing inference speed. Unlike approaches that train multi-frame models from scratch, CronusVLA capitalizes on the robust vision-language foundation established by single-frame pretraining and then introduces multi-frame capabilities through a targeted post-training phase. Furthermore, CronusVLA highlights and rigorously evaluates "observational robustness"—a crucial aspect for real-world robot deployment that is often under-emphasized in existing benchmarks. By introducing SimplerEnv-OR, it pushes the research community to consider the resilience of VLA models against various temporal and spatial disturbances, thereby broadening the scope of evaluation beyond mere task success rates. This work represents a significant step towards more practical, adaptable, and deployable generalist robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of CronusVLA are multi-faceted, aiming to overcome the inherent limitations of current VLA models and propel the field toward more robust and efficient robotic manipulation:

1. **To enable Efficient Multi-frame Modeling for VLA Models:** The foremost objective is to extend the capabilities of single-frame VLA models to leverage multi-frame historical observations without the prohibitive computational costs and inference latency associated with directly feeding multiple images into VLM backbones. The authors are motivated by the understanding that temporal information provides valuable motion cues and helps disambiguate robot states, which are critical for effective manipulation.
2. **To Enhance Performance in Robotic Manipulation Tasks:** By effectively incorporating temporal information, CronusVLA aims to achieve state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments. The motivation stems from the belief that motion cues derived from consecutive observations can significantly improve the accuracy and fluidity of robot actions, leading to higher success rates, especially in complex or long-horizon tasks.
3. **To Improve Observational Robustness against Disturbances:** A key objective is to make VLA models significantly more robust to real-world observational disturbances, such as blur, noise, occlusions, and jittering. The authors recognize that current benchmarks often overlook these factors, which are ubiquitous in practical deployments. By building policies that can reliably infer actions even when the current input is corrupted, CronusVLA seeks to bridge the gap between lab performance and real-world applicability.
4. **To Establish a Comprehensive Benchmark for Robustness Evaluation:** Motivated by the lack of adequate tools to quantitatively assess robustness, the authors introduce SimplerEnv-OR. This benchmark is designed to systematically evaluate how manipulation models perform under various temporal and spatial observational disturbances, encouraging the development of more resilient policies in the community.
5. **To Facilitate Practical Real-world Deployment:** Ultimately, the project aims to create VLA models that are not only highly capable but also practical for real-world deployment. This involves ensuring fast inference speeds, strong performance across various embodiments, and superior robustness to unexpected visual challenges.

In essence, CronusVLA is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of temporal information in VLA models, making them more intelligent, efficient, and resilient agents for complex robotic tasks in unpredictable environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CronusVLA proposes a unified, two-stage framework for multi-frame training and inference, meticulously designed to achieve efficient and robust manipulation.

**Stage 1: Single-frame Pretraining**
The process begins by establishing a foundational single-frame Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. This stage adapts off-the-shelf pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as those built on Llama 2 or Qwen2.5 with vision encoders like SigLip and Dinov2, to large-scale embodied manipulation datasets (e.g., OXE, Bridge-v2, Fractal).
- **Action Representation:** Continuous robot actions (e.g., 7D end-effector poses) are discretized into a fixed number of bins (e.g., 256) using an extended action tokenizer. These discrete action values are treated as tokens within the VLM's vocabulary.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a standard next-token prediction objective, where, given an observation image `I_t` and a language instruction `l`, it autoregressively predicts the subsequent action tokens `a_t = VLA(I_t, l)`.
- **Purpose:** This stage efficiently transfers the visual perception and language understanding capabilities of the VLM to embodied scenes, establishing a strong vision-language foundation. It prioritizes the preservation of single-frame visual perception and cost-effective training on vast datasets.

**Stage 2: Multi-frame Post-training**
After single-frame pretraining, the model undergoes a multi-frame post-training phase on high-quality cross-embodiment datasets. This stage adapts the model to explicitly handle temporal information.
- **From Discrete Action Tokens to Learnable Features:** Instead of predicting discrete action tokens, the VLA backbone is modified to output continuous "learnable features" `f_t ∈ R^d` from its hidden layers. These features serve as continuous representations that summarize the embodied vision-language information for a single frame. The vision-language backbone still processes each image within a single-frame formulation.
- **Feature Chunking:** To aggregate historical information, CronusVLA introduces "feature chunking" `F^M_t = {f_{t-M+1}..., f_t}`, which is a sequence of `M` historical learnable features.
 - During **training**, `M`-step image inputs are restructured at the batch level, allowing the VLA backbone to process `B × M` single-frame inputs independently, maintaining efficiency.
 - During **inference**, a first-in, first-out (FIFO) queue mechanism is used to maintain the feature chunking. This strategy reuses previously computed VLM features, eliminating redundant computations and ensuring fast inference.
- **Cross-frame Decoder:** A dedicated decoder processes the aggregated feature chunking to predict a chunk of future actions `a_{t:t+K-1} = Decoder(F^M_t)`.
 - **Architecture:** The decoder is DiT-based (Diffusion Transformer), comprising self-attention networks and MLP layers.
 - **Feature Modulator:** A critical component, the feature modulator dynamically balances the contributions of the current frame's feature `f_t` and past frames' features `f_{t-M+1:t-1}`. It divides `f_t` to match the number of past features and processes them through an MLP, producing modulated features `Z_f`. This prevents irrelevant historical information from dominating current cues.
 - **Cross-Attention:** `Z_f` is used as keys and values in a cross-attention mechanism, while noised actions (`a^`) serve as queries. This enables effective interaction between the aggregated temporal context and the action prediction process.
 - **Training Objective:** The decoder is trained using a diffusion loss (multi-step denoising), where noised actions are iteratively denoised conditioned on `Z_f`.
- **Multi-frame Regularization:** A novel regularization technique is introduced to decouple the multi-frame modeling of the decoder from the single-frame perception of the VLA backbone.
 - The past learnable features `f_{t-M+1:t-1}` (from `k=1..., M-1` previous frames) are treated as auxiliary inputs to the decoder.
 - A `stop-gradient` operation is applied to these past features (`sg(VL(I_{t-k}, l))`), meaning their gradients do not propagate back to update the VLA backbone. Only the current frame's feature `f_t` updates the backbone.
 - Advantages: Reduces computational and memory overhead during training, preserves the VLA backbone's original single-frame perceptual capabilities, and accelerates convergence.

**SimplerEnv-OR Benchmark**
To address the critical need for robustness evaluation, the authors introduce SimplerEnv-OR (Observational Robustness), a novel benchmark built upon the WidowX Robot Visual Matching (WR-VM) setting of SimplerEnv.
- **Disturbance Dimensions:** It introduces 24 types of observational disturbances categorized into two dimensions:
 - **Spatial:** Global (blurring, jittering, frame dropping, full occlusion), Local (overexposing, partial occlusions), and Discrete (Gaussian noise, impulse noise).
 - **Temporal:** Disturbances are applied at varying frequencies: Constant (1:0 disturbed:clean frames), Cyclic (1:1), and Sparse (1:3, 1:5, etc.). This results in over 120 severity levels across 2,300 trials.
- **Robustness Score (R-Score):** A quantitative metric `R-Score_i = 100 * (SR_i / SR)` is defined, where `SR_i` is the success rate under a specific disturbance `i`, and `SR` is the baseline success rate without disturbances. This allows for standardized comparison of model resilience.

This comprehensive methodology allows CronusVLA to efficiently integrate temporal information, enhance performance, and rigorously evaluate robustness, paving the way for more practical robotic systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CronusVLA demonstrates leading performance, superior robustness, and efficient inference across a range of simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

**1. Fast Inference:**
- CronusVLA significantly improves inference speed compared to prior VLA models, particularly when scaling to multiple frames. By predicting learnable features in a single forward pass and employing cached historical features (feature chunking), it avoids the latency of autoregressive prediction and redundant VLM computations.
- For the 7B model, CronusVLA achieves an inference speed of 8.7 Hz with 7 frames, while the 0.5B model reaches 11.1 Hz with 4 frames. In contrast, a naive multi-frame OpenVLA (7B) drops to 3.1 Hz with 7 frames from its single-frame 5.2 Hz. This highlights a substantial speed improvement without sacrificing performance.

**2. High Performance in Simulation Benchmarks:**
- **SimplerEnv:** CronusVLA-7B achieves a state-of-the-art average success rate of 70.9% across Google Robot and WidowX Robot tasks.
 - **Google Robot:** It recorded 78.6% in Visual Matching (VM) and 73.8% in Variant Aggregation (VA), significantly outperforming other multi-frame VLAs like TraceVLA and RoboVLMs. Notably, it showed strong performance on long-horizon tasks such as "Put in Drawer" (64.8% VM, 65.1% VA), where most prior methods struggled.
 - **WidowX Robot:** CronusVLA-7B also achieved a high average success rate, surpassing SpatialVLA by 41.5% and CogACT by 9.4%.
 - **Efficiency of Smaller Models:** Even CronusVLA-0.5B, with a much smaller language backbone, outperformed many 2B-7B parameter models, suggesting the effectiveness of its modeling approach over sheer parameter count.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** CronusVLA-7B achieved the highest average success rate of 97.0%, demonstrating strong in-domain learning and long-horizon execution capabilities. It achieved an impressive 94.0% success rate on Long-horizon tasks, a 40.3% improvement over OpenVLA.

**3. Strong Observational Robustness (SimplerEnv-OR Benchmark):**
- CronusVLA consistently surpassed previous approaches in robustness score (R-Score) and success rate under various observational disturbances.
- **Temporal Dimension:** It exhibited strong resistance under constant high-frequency disturbances and near immunity under sparse disturbances (96.2 R-Score for Sparse 1:3). Single-frame models and even other multi-frame models like RoboVLMs and TraceVLA struggled, often defaulting to inaction or out-of-distribution actions when temporal information was compromised.
- **Spatial Dimension:** CronusVLA showed superior robustness under Global (e.g., blurring, jittering) and Local (e.g., partial occlusions) disturbances. For instance, SpatialVLA, which relies on zero-shot depth estimation, was highly sensitive to local disturbances. CronusVLA's ability to integrate temporal context effectively allowed it to maintain performance.

**4. Real-world Experimental Results (Franka Platform):**
- Evaluations on the Franka Research 3 Robot showed CronusVLA outperforming DP3 and OpenVLA across almost all tasks.
- **Simple Tasks:** Demonstrated better precision for complex manipulations like stacking.
- **Long-horizon Tasks:** Exhibited stronger long-horizon learning capabilities, such as successfully completing the "Press buttons in order" task where OpenVLA often failed due to state confusion or repeated actions.
- **Generalization and Observational Robustness:** Achieved the best performance in distracted situations (e.g., human interference, unseen objects, variable lighting, camera occlusions), with a 72.6% success rate under various challenges. Its multi-frame modeling effectively withstood frequent visual dropouts during camera occlusions.

**5. Ablation Studies:**
- **Post-training Strategies:** The full CronusVLA design (Multi-frame + Decoder + Vision-Language backbone training + Regularization) yielded the highest performance (70.9%) and inference speed (8.73 Hz). Multi-frame regularization was crucial for both performance and faster convergence by decoupling the VLA backbone from the multi-frame decoder.
- **Cross-frame Decoder Designs:** The DiT-based decoder with cross-attention and a feature modulator was found to be optimal. The modulator was critical for balancing current and past features, preventing performance degradation from irrelevant historical information. Cross-attention offered linear scaling with frame number, unlike quadratic self-attention.
- **Impact of Frame Number:** Performance initially increased with more frames but then decreased, suggesting an optimal range (e.g., 7 frames for 7B, 4 for 0.5B). Critically, CronusVLA maintained high inference speed regardless of the number of frames, demonstrating its long-horizon efficiency.

In summary, CronusVLA successfully addresses the dual challenges of efficient multi-frame modeling and robust performance in VLA systems, delivering state-of-the-art results across rigorous benchmarks and real-world deployments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CronusVLA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its contributions and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Efficient and Effective Multi-frame VLA Modeling:** The core significance of CronusVLA lies in its novel approach to extending single-frame VLA models to the multi-frame paradigm without the traditional computational and latency penalties. By separating single-frame pretraining from multi-frame post-training, utilizing learnable features, and employing feature chunking with a DiT-based cross-frame decoder and multi-frame regularization, the framework efficiently processes temporal information. This is critical for robots operating in dynamic, real-world environments where context over time is indispensable for accurate decision-making and fluid execution. The demonstrated fast inference speeds make these advanced capabilities practical for real-time robotic control.

2. **Addressing a Critical Gap in Observational Robustness:** Current VLA benchmarks often focus on task success under ideal conditions, overlooking the pervasive observational disturbances encountered in real-world scenarios. CronusVLA directly tackles this limitation by introducing SimplerEnv-OR, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating robustness against 24 types of temporal and spatial disturbances. This contribution is highly significant as it provides a standardized, quantitative method to assess a crucial aspect of real-world robot deployment. The superior robustness achieved by CronusVLA on this benchmark highlights its potential for dependable performance outside controlled laboratory settings, marking a step forward in making robots more reliable and resilient.

3. **Advancing Generalist Robotic Policies:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse manipulation tasks in both simulation (SimplerEnv, LIBERO) and real-world (Franka platform), CronusVLA pushes the boundaries of generalist robotic policies. Its ability to handle long-horizon tasks and generalize to unseen conditions, distractors, and environmental variations underscores its potential to build more versatile and adaptable robots. The architecture's efficiency also allows for scaling to different model sizes (0.5B and 7B), making powerful VLA capabilities accessible to a wider range of hardware constraints.

4. **Inspiration for Future Research Directions:** CronusVLA opens several avenues for future research. Its explicit focus on temporal modeling and robustness encourages the community to move beyond instantaneous perception. The limitations discussed by the authors, such as the potential for redundant token processing and the need for stronger language-driven reasoning and multi-view/proprioceptive integration, provide clear directions for enhancing VLA architectures. The framework's modularity, building upon existing VLA checkpoints, also promotes resource-efficient development and encourages collaboration.

5. **Accelerating Real-world Deployment of Embodied AI:** Ultimately, the combined advantages of efficiency, high performance, and robustness make CronusVLA a more viable candidate for real-world deployment in assistive robotics, manufacturing, logistics, and other domains. Robots powered by such robust multi-frame VLA models can better understand complex instructions, adapt to unforeseen visual challenges, and execute tasks with greater precision and safety, bringing the promise of embodied AI closer to practical realization.

In conclusion, CronusVLA offers a foundational framework that tackles key challenges in VLA model development, solidifying the role of temporal information in creating truly intelligent, efficient, and robust robotic agents.
