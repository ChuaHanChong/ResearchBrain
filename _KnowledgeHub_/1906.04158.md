# Towards Social Artificial Intelligence: Nonverbal Social Signal Prediction in A Triadic Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> This work formulates nonverbal social interaction as a signal prediction problem: regress a target person's emitted signals from the signals of conversational partners. It introduces the Haggling dataset, roughly 180 markerless motion-capture sequences of triadic buyer-two-seller negotiations with 3D body, face, and hand motion, speaking labels, and 500-plus camera views, plus convolutional baselines for speaking, social formation, and body gesture prediction.

## Problem

- Nonverbal communication rules are poorly formalized, so hand-coded rule-based conversational agents cannot capture the subtle, continuous signals humans actually exchange during interaction.
- Data-driven modeling is blocked by data scarcity: unlike text, nonverbal signals lack a recording format, and raw internet video needs full semantic pose extraction first.
- Existing datasets capture upper body only at tables, or record just position and orientation, or capture single isolated subjects rather than freely interacting groups.

## Method

- ==Social signal prediction== formulation: learn a function mapping partners' signal histories to the target person's concurrent signals, subsuming chatbots and motion forecasting as special cases.
- **Haggling game** protocol in the Panoptic Studio: two sellers pitch similar items to a buyer for one minute with no behavioral instruction, markerlessly capturing 73-D body, 5-D face expression, position, and orientation.
- Three fully ==convolutional== baselines: 1-D conv classifier for binary speaking status, conv autoencoder for formation, and a frozen Holden-style motion-manifold decoder driven by trajectory or partner body motion.

## Results

- Speaking classification reaches **88.93%** from the target's own face and **73.12%** from own body; the other seller's face still gives **80.14%**, versus **49.65%** chance for an unrelated person.
- Masking ablations on the Face+Body model drop accuracy to **56.59%** without face but only **82.48%** without body; the mouth-opening face component and the right hand dominate.
- Formation error improves from **29.83 cm** position and **15.24 degrees** body orientation using position only, to **24.59 cm** and **12.33 degrees** using position, face, and body cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interpersonal signals are strongly predictive across people: a partner's face predicts your speech better than your own body, with turn-taking as the underlying mechanism.
> - Objectively measured signals sidestep semantic annotation entirely, letting correlation structure among channels be quantified without labeling what any gesture means.
> - Caution: a fixed mean-pose baseline beat all gesture models at 7.83 cm joint error, showing per-joint distance metrics fail to capture motion naturalness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge at the intersection of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Computer Vision, Social Signal Processing (SSP), and Human-Computer Interaction (HCI): enabling machines to understand and genuinely participate in human social communication, particularly through nonverbal channels. The authors frame this grand objective as the pursuit of "Social Artificial Intelligence (AI)."

Traditionally, understanding nonverbal communication has been difficult, often relying on rule-based systems or manual behavioral coding procedures in fields like psychology and behavioral science. However, these methods struggle with the inherent subtlety, high dimensionality, and poorly understood rules governing human nonverbal cues. The authors draw a direct analogy to the remarkable success seen in Natural Language Processing (NLP), where data-driven deep learning models, trained on vast datasets, have achieved "free" communication without extensive reliance on centuries of linguistic rules. This analogy positions their work as a **paradigm shift** for nonverbal communication: moving from rule-based, semantic-focused approaches towards a data-driven, dynamics-modeling framework.

The paper highlights a critical gap in the research landscape: the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale datasets capturing the full spectrum of nonverbal signals from naturally interacting human groups. Existing datasets are often limited in scope (e.g., upper body only, dyadic interactions, only location/orientation), in naturalness (e.g., marker-based, restrictive setups), or in the range of captured signals (e.g., lacking face/hand details). By introducing a new task, "social signal prediction," and a novel dataset captured using the Panoptic Studio, the authors directly tackle this data bottleneck, providing a foundational resource for the emerging field of Social AI.

This work contributes to several specific research areas:
- **Computer Vision and Machine Learning:** By leveraging advanced markerless motion capture and deep neural networks, it pushes the boundaries of human pose, face, and hand estimation, and applies these techniques to a complex, multi-person interaction scenario. It also extends the nascent field of human motion forecasting to explicitly social contexts.
- **Social Signal Processing (SSP):** It offers a computational, data-driven framework for analyzing and predicting social cues, moving beyond traditional methods and opening new avenues for quantitative study of phenomena like proxemics, kinesics, and turn-taking.
- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The ability to predict and generate appropriate nonverbal responses is critical for building robots that can effectively cooperate and genuinely communicate with humans, a key goal for future robotic systems.
- **Behavioral Science:** The dataset and predictive models provide new tools for researchers to quantitatively explore and validate theories about nonverbal communication and social dynamics, offering a macroscopic view of interactions that was previously difficult to obtain.

In essence, this paper provides a concrete methodology and a crucial resource for bridging the gap between sophisticated human sensing technologies and the ambitious goal of creating truly socially intelligent machines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching, long-term objective of this research is **to endow machines with the ability to genuinely communicate with humans, specifically by encoding and decoding the broad channel of nonverbal social signals.** The authors coin the term "Social Artificial Intelligence (AI)" to refer to machines possessing this capability.

To achieve this ambitious goal, the paper outlines several key immediate objectives:

1. **Formulate a New Research Task: "Social Signal Prediction."** Instead of attempting to formalize complex, poorly understood rules of nonverbal communication or directly map signals to abstract semantic meanings (like emotion recognition), the authors propose modeling the *dynamics* of social signals exchanged among interacting individuals. This reframes social interaction as a signal prediction problem, where an individual's output signals (e.g., gestures, speaking status) are predicted based on the input signals received from communication partners and their own past behavior. This approach has two key advantages:
 - It allows tackling the problem using objectively measurable social signal data, avoiding subjective annotation of underlying meanings.
 - It enables modeling subtle details by considering the continuous, high-dimensional signal space.

2. **Develop a New, Large-Scale, Full-Spectrum Social Interaction Dataset.** The motivation here is paramount: the fundamental difficulty in building data-driven nonverbal communication models stems from the extreme rarity of suitable data. Unlike verbal language, nonverbal signals are hard to "record" in their full complexity (3D body, face, hands, position, orientation, voice) from multiple, naturally interacting individuals simultaneously. The authors aim to collect a dataset that meets critical requirements:
 - Capturing a broad spectrum of 3D nonverbal cues (face, body, hands).
 - Involving naturally interacting groups (more than two people for turn-taking, attention switching).
 - Achieving a significant scale of data collection.

3. **Establish Baseline Approaches for Social Signal Prediction.** Using the newly formulated framework and dataset, the paper aims to demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of computational methods for predicting specific nonverbal cues. This involves:
 - Quantitatively investigating correlations between diverse behavioral channels, both within an individual and across interacting individuals.
 - Providing a foundation for future, more complex Social AI models.

The primary motivation for this data-driven approach is the observed success in Natural Language Processing (NLP). The authors hypothesize that if large-scale language datasets and deep neural networks could lead to "free" verbal communication without extensive prior linguistic knowledge, a similar paradigm could unlock the secrets of nonverbal communication, given sufficient data and powerful modeling tools. The explicit lack of such comprehensive data for nonverbal interaction serves as a strong impetus for their dataset creation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research revolves around formulating and solving the "social signal prediction" problem using a novel, purpose-built dataset and deep neural networks.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Social Signal Prediction

The authors define social signal prediction as learning the dynamics between input signals `X` that an individual receives and output social signals `Y` that the individual emits. Formally, they aim to find a function `F` such that `Y(t+1) = F(X(t0:t), Y(t0:t))`, where `X` includes signals from conversational partners (`Xc`) and the environment (`Xe`), and `Y` includes the target person's own historical signals. For this paper, they simplify to predicting concurrent signals in a triadic interaction: `Y(t0:t) = F(Xc1(t0:t), Xc2(t0:t))`, predicting the target's signals given the concurrent signals of two partners.

The specific signals considered for input and output are comprehensive:
- **Body Motion (B):** A 73-dimensional vector representing skeletal joint rotations, root position/velocity, and footstep signals in a person-centric coordinate system.
- **Face Motion (F):** A 5-dimensional vector representing key facial expression parameters from a deformable face model.
- **Global Position (x):** 2D coordinates of the body's root joint.
- **Body Orientation (θ):** A 2D unit vector representing torso direction on the ground plane.
- **Face Orientation (φ):** A 2D unit vector representing face normal direction on the ground plane.
- **Speaking Status (S):** A binary label (1 for speaking, 0 for not speaking), manually annotated from audio.

For the triadic interaction, the target's full set of signals `Y = [x0, θ0, φ0, B0, F0, S0]` is predicted based on the equivalent sets of signals from two other partners, `X1` and `X2`.

#### 3.2. Dataset Creation: The Haggling Game

To collect the necessary large-scale, full-spectrum data, the authors devised the "Haggling Game" protocol and captured interactions using the Panoptic Studio.
- **Interaction Scenario:** A triadic setup (two sellers, one buyer) was chosen to elicit complex social behaviors like turn-taking and attention switching, which are absent in dyadic interactions. The game involved sellers promoting similar products to a buyer, incentivizing natural negotiation and persuasion.
- **Naturalness:** Participants (122 subjects, 180 sequences, ~3 hours of data) were not given behavioral restrictions, ensuring spontaneous social behaviors.
- **Capture System:** The Panoptic Studio at CMU was used, a markerless motion capture system employing over 500 RGB cameras, 10 RGB+D sensors, and 23 microphones. This system allows for:
 - **3D Full Body Motion Reconstruction:** Markerlessly tracking 3D anatomical keypoints for bodies, faces, and hands.
 - **Multimodal Data:** Synchronized RGB videos, depth maps, and individual voice recordings.
 - **Automated Annotation:** Speaking status was manually annotated from audio, while 3D signals were automatically extracted.
- **Data Pre-processing:** Raw 3D motion data was retargeted to a standardized skeleton to remove individual-specific cues (height, limb length), focusing purely on motion. Body motion was decoupled from global translation/orientation. Face motion parameters were extracted. The dataset was partitioned into training and testing sets, with quality control to ensure error-free sequences.

#### 3.3. Prediction Tasks and Baseline Models

The paper explores three specific social signal prediction tasks using deep neural networks (specifically, fully convolutional networks and autoencoders):

1. **Predicting Speaking Status (Binary Classification):**
 - **Objective:** Predict whether the target person is speaking (S0).
 - **Inputs:**
 - **Intra-personal:** Target's own face motion (F0), body motion (B0), or both.
 - **Inter-personal:** Other seller's face motion (F2), body motion (B2), or both.
 - **Model:** A four-layer 1-D convolutional neural network with ReLU activations and a sigmoid output, trained with Cross Entropy loss.
 - **Analysis:** Compares performance of intra-personal vs. inter-personal signals, and ablates contributions of face vs. body cues.

2. **Predicting Social Formations (Regression):**
 - **Objective:** Predict the target's global position (x0) and orientations (θ0, φ0).
 - **Inputs:** Global positions and orientations of the two communication partners (X1p, X2p).
 - **Model:** An autoencoder-like structure with two 1-D convolutional layers in the encoder and a transposed convolution layer in the decoder.
 - **Analysis:** Evaluates prediction accuracy in terms of position error (cm) and orientation error (degrees).

3. **Predicting Body Gestures (Kinesic Signals - Regression):**
 - **Objective:** Predict the target's full 73-dimensional body motion (B0).
 - **Approach 1: Using Social Formation Only (Fp→B0):** Regresses body motion from the *predicted* social formation (position and orientation) of the target person. This uses a pre-trained body motion autoencoder's decoder.
 - **Approach 2: Using Other Body Motions as Input (F(B1,B2)→B0):** Directly uses the full body motions of the two partners (B1, B2) to predict the target's body motion.
 - **Approach 3: Hybrid Method:** Combines leg and root motion from Approach 1 (which handles global formation well) with upper body motion from Approach 2 (which is more responsive to partners).
 - **Model:** Autoencoder architecture adapted from prior work, where a decoder generates body motion from a latent code. The encoder part varies based on input source.
 - **Analysis:** Evaluates average joint error (cm) and discusses qualitative naturalness, acknowledging the limitations of L2 metrics for motion.

The experiments standardize inputs, use adaptive gradient descent (AmsGrad), and l1 regularization. A significant aspect is the use of the "left seller" as the target person to simplify the problem for formation prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate significant correlations among nonverbal social signals, both within an individual and across interacting individuals, thereby validating the data-driven social signal prediction framework.

#### 4.1. Speaking Status Classification

- **Intra-personal Signals:** Strong correlations were found between an individual's own nonverbal cues and their speaking status.
 - **Face motion (F0)** yielded the highest accuracy (88.93%), primarily attributed to the strong link between lip motion and speech.
 - **Body motion (B0)** also showed a substantial correlation (73.12% accuracy).
 - Combining **Face+Body (F0+B0)** achieved 88.40%, only marginally improving over face-only, suggesting facial cues dominate. An ablation study confirmed this: masking out body cues in the trained Face+Body model had minimal impact (82.48%), while masking out face cues caused a significant drop (56.59%).
- **Inter-personal Signals:** Surprisingly strong correlations were observed between a communication partner's signals and the target's speaking status.
 - The **other seller's facial motion (F2)** predicted the target's speaking status with 80.14% accuracy, which is *higher* than using the target's *own* body motion (73.12%).
 - The **other seller's body motion (B2)** achieved 70.48% accuracy.
 - Combining **Face+Body from other seller (F2+B2)** yielded 78.42%.
 - This strong inter-personal predictive power is largely attributed to **turn-taking** behavior in social communication. Sequences where turn-taking rules were strictly followed showed higher prediction accuracies.
- **Ablation Study (Detailed):** Further analysis revealed that the first component of face motion (corresponding to mouth opening) had the strongest predictive power for speaking status. Among body parts, the right hand showed stronger predictive power than the left hand.
- **Baseline:** Using signals from a random, non-interacting person resulted in chance-level accuracy (around 50%), confirming the significance of actual social interaction cues.

#### 4.2. Social Formation Prediction

Predicting the target's global position and orientations showed that combining different social signal channels improved performance.
- Using **all signals** (position, body orientation, face orientation from partners) achieved the best results:
 - Average position error: 24.59 cm
 - Body orientation error: 12.33 degrees
 - Face orientation error: 17.01 degrees
- Using **position cues only** from partners yielded the highest errors but still demonstrated reasonable predictability.
- The results indicate that the neural network can learn to estimate a target's spatial configuration from the cues of their partners, reflecting established theories like Proxemics and F-formations.

#### 4.3. Body Gestures Prediction (Kinesic Signals)

Predicting the high-dimensional body gestures proved more challenging, particularly when evaluated quantitatively using average joint error (cm).
- **Formation-based prediction (Fp→B0):** This method, which predicts body motion from the target's estimated social formation, produced reasonable leg motions that followed trajectory movements, but resulted in minimal upper body motion. Average Joint Error: 8.31 cm.
- **Other Body Gestures as Input (F(B1,B2)→B0):** This approach, using partners' full body motions as input, generated more dynamic and realistic upper body movements, responsive to the partners' actions. Average Joint Error: 8.72 cm.
- **Hybrid Method:** Combining the lower body/formation from the first method with the upper body from the second qualitatively produced the most natural results, satisfying various social rules (distance, orientation, natural hand gestures). However, its quantitative error was 8.61 cm.
- **Baseline (Mean Pose):** A simple baseline of always generating a fixed "mean pose" from the training set yielded the lowest average joint error (7.83 cm). This crucial finding highlights a limitation of standard L2 error metrics for evaluating the *naturalness* and *social appropriateness* of complex motions, suggesting that quantitatively "accurate" poses might not be qualitatively "natural" or "socially intelligent." The qualitative improvements shown in supplementary videos for the hybrid method, despite higher L2 error, underscore this point.

Overall, the findings provide strong empirical evidence for the existence of predictable dynamics in nonverbal social signals and the potential of data-driven approaches to model them.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step "Towards Social Artificial Intelligence" by laying a foundational computational framework and providing a unique dataset for understanding human nonverbal social interactions. Its potential impact spans several domains:

1. **Advancing Social AI and Human-Robot Interaction:** The primary significance lies in offering a clear, data-driven roadmap for equipping machines with nonverbal communication capabilities. Robots, virtual agents, and other AI systems currently lack the subtlety and responsiveness in nonverbal cues that are essential for genuine, fluid human interaction. By demonstrating that social signals are predictable and that their dynamics can be learned computationally, this work opens the door for:
 - **More natural and empathetic robots:** Robots could anticipate human actions, adapt their postures, gestures, and expressions to match social contexts, and engage in meaningful turn-taking.
 - **Improved human-computer collaboration:** AI systems could become more intuitive partners, understanding unspoken cues and responding in socially appropriate ways, leading to more effective and comfortable interactions.

2. **A Novel Research Paradigm for Nonverbal Communication:** The "social signal prediction" task reframes the study of nonverbal communication, moving away from subjective semantic interpretations or rigid rule-based systems. This data-driven, dynamics-modeling approach provides a robust, measurable framework for computational analysis, potentially accelerating discovery in a field that has historically been challenging to formalize. It provides a means to "decode" the implicit rules of social behavior from observed data.

3. **Creation of a Critical Dataset: The Haggling Game:** The **Panoptic Studio Haggling Game dataset** is perhaps one of the most immediate and impactful contributions. It addresses a long-standing bottleneck in nonverbal communication research: the lack of high-quality, large-scale, multimodal datasets capturing natural group interactions. This dataset, with its full-spectrum 3D body, face, and hand motions, global positions, orientations, and speaking statuses from diverse, naturally interacting individuals, will serve as a vital resource for researchers across AI, computer vision, and behavioral science. It enables the systematic study of phenomena like turn-taking, attention switching, and the intricate interplay of cues that emerge in genuine social contexts.

4. **Quantitative Validation of Social Science Theories:** The research provides empirical, computational evidence supporting social science theories like Proxemics and the importance of F-formations and turn-taking. By showing strong correlations in interpersonal signals, it quantitatively reinforces the idea that individuals' nonverbal behaviors are deeply intertwined and mutually predictive, offering a new lens for behavioral scientists to explore and validate their hypotheses.

5. **Benchmarking and Future Directions:** The presented baseline models and their results (e.g., strong speaking prediction, challenges in gesture naturalness evaluation) establish benchmarks for future research. The discussion on the limitations of current metrics for evaluating motion naturalness (e.g., L2 error vs. qualitative realism) highlights important avenues for developing more perceptually relevant evaluation techniques. The paper also points to future work on integrating verbal and nonverbal signals, and exploring more diverse social scenarios, laying out a clear agenda for the field.

In summary, "Towards Social Artificial Intelligence" not only introduces a novel problem formulation and a groundbreaking dataset but also provides compelling evidence for the feasibility of data-driven modeling of nonverbal social signals. This work is a crucial building block for developing truly interactive and socially aware AI systems, with far-reaching implications for human-machine interaction and our fundamental understanding of human social behavior.
