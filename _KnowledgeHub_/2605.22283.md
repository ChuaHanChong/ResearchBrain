# Spatial Memory for Out-of-Vision Manipulation in Vision-Language-Action

> [!summary] Summary
> SOMA, a framework for Vision-Language-Action models, integrates a persistent spatial memory constructed from multi-view observations to enable robust robotic manipulation of objects initially or temporarily outside the camera's field of view. The approach yielded improved success rates across five real-world out-of-vision tasks, reducing target localization time by 40-59% and head search path length by 40-57%, while also achieving state-of-the-art performance in fully observable simulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models implicitly assume task-relevant objects remain within the robot's current camera view, leading to task failure when objects move out of sight.
- Internal spatial reasoning or learned priors in MLLMs are often brittle, with spatial estimates deteriorating rapidly without direct perceptual evidence of unseen targets.
- Current memory-centric VLA designs primarily store short-term observations rather than maintaining a globally consistent and persistent spatial representation that supports reasoning beyond the immediate camera frustum.

## Method

- **SOMA** constructs a persistent spatial-semantic memory using a multi-view scanning strategy with a movable head camera, lifting 2D detections and features into a unified 3D scene coordinate system via geometric priors and semantic encoders.
- It employs a dynamic memory refinement mechanism that continuously updates the scene memory during manipulation through instance-level association and adaptive similarity-aware fusion of new observations.
- A contextual memory retrieval module uses ==cross-attention== to inject instruction-grounded global spatial-semantic knowledge from the refined memory into the VLM's multimodal representation, guiding action generation.

## Results

- SOMA achieved the highest success rates in five real-world out-of-vision pick-and-place tasks, with up to **30%** pick success and **25%** place success in complex scenarios like "Invisible-to-Invisible PnP".
- Qualitative results in real-world tasks showed SOMA reduced first-fixation time by 40-**59%**, head search path length by 40-**57%**, and time-to-grasp by 41-**60%** compared to baselines.
- In simulation, SOMA achieved an average success rate of **52.0%** on RoboCasa Tabletop GR1, outperforming other baselines, and demonstrated strong sample efficiency by achieving **48.3%** success with only 30 demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equipping VLA models with a persistent, dynamically refined spatial memory is crucial for overcoming limitations of view-bound perception and enabling robust out-of-vision manipulation.
> - Both geometric and semantic priors are essential for constructing a robust and queryable spatial-semantic representation of the environment, significantly impacting manipulation performance.
> - The integration of active multi-view perception with a coherent, constantly updated scene memory leads to qualitatively different and more efficient robot behaviors, such as faster target acquisition and more decisive grasps.
