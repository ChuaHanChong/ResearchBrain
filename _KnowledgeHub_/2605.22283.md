# Spatial Memory for Out-of-Vision Manipulation in Vision-Language-Action

> [!summary] Summary
> SOMA, a framework for Vision-Language-Action models, integrates a persistent spatial memory constructed from multi-view observations to enable robust robotic manipulation of objects initially or temporarily outside the camera's field of view. The approach yielded improved success rates across five real-world out-of-vision tasks, reducing target localization time by 40-59% and head search path length by 40-57%, while also achieving state-of-the-art performance in fully observable simulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models implicitly assume task-relevant objects remain within the robot's current camera view, leading to task failure when objects move out of sight.
- Internal spatial reasoning or learned priors in MLLMs are often brittle, with spatial estimates deteriorating rapidly without direct perceptual evidence of unseen targets.
- Current memory-centric VLA designs primarily store short-term observations rather than maintaining a globally consistent and persistent spatial representation that supports reasoning beyond the immediate camera frustum.

## Method

- **SOMA** constructs a persistent spatial-semantic memory using a multi-view scanning strategy with a movable head camera, lifting 2D detections and features into a unified 3D scene coordinate system via geometric priors and semantic encoders.
- It employs a dynamic memory refinement mechanism that continuously updates the scene memory during manipulation through instance-level association and adaptive similarity-aware fusion of new observations.
- A contextual memory retrieval module uses ==cross-attention== to inject instruction-grounded global spatial-semantic knowledge from the refined memory into the VLM's multimodal representation, guiding action generation.

## Results

- SOMA achieved the highest success rates in five real-world out-of-vision pick-and-place tasks, with up to **30%** pick success and **25%** place success in complex scenarios like "Invisible-to-Invisible PnP".
- Qualitative results in real-world tasks showed SOMA reduced first-fixation time by 40-**59%**, head search path length by 40-**57%**, and time-to-grasp by 41-**60%** compared to baselines.
- In simulation, SOMA achieved an average success rate of **52.0%** on RoboCasa Tabletop GR1, outperforming other baselines, and demonstrated strong sample efficiency by achieving **48.3%** success with only 30 demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equipping VLA models with a persistent, dynamically refined spatial memory is crucial for overcoming limitations of view-bound perception and enabling robust out-of-vision manipulation.
> - Both geometric and semantic priors are essential for constructing a robust and queryable spatial-semantic representation of the environment, significantly impacting manipulation performance.
> - The integration of active multi-view perception with a coherent, constantly updated scene memory leads to qualitatively different and more efficient robot behaviors, such as faster target acquisition and more decisive grasps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper addresses a limitation in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. VLA models are a significant direction in robotic action modeling, typically integrating large-scale pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with an action module to map multimodal inputs (visual observations, natural language instructions) into robotic actions. These models have demonstrated capabilities in generalization and task versatility across various manipulation scenarios.

However, a common assumption in the development of many existing VLA systems is that task-relevant objects are consistently visible within the robot's current camera view. This "view-bound" assumption stems from prevailing fixed-view tabletop manipulation setups, which simplify calibration, state estimation, and data collection. Consequently, when a target object moves outside the camera's field of view, the robot's perception–action loop is disrupted, often leading to task failure. Current approaches relying on internal spatial reasoning or learned spatial priors to infer unseen targets under static camera setups have shown limitations. Such inference can be brittle, as MLLMs' spatial estimates may deteriorate without direct perceptual evidence, causing errors in localization and motion. This indicates that spatial reasoning alone, without grounded perceptual evidence or persistent memory, is insufficient for robust manipulation.

Previous work in robotic manipulation has explored movable cameras to improve exploration under partial observability, but these methods often lack instruction-agnostic policies and persistent spatial memory. Similarly, while some memory-centric VLA designs exist, they primarily focus on storing recent observations or key frames for short-term reasoning, rather than maintaining a globally consistent and persistent spatial representation that supports reasoning beyond the immediate camera frustum.

This work, SOMA (Spatial memory framework for Out-of-Vision Manipulation in Vision-Language-Action), aims to bridge this gap by explicitly equipping VLA models with a persistent spatial memory. It deviates from purely reactive, view-dependent policies by integrating active, multi-view perception with a structured memory, enabling manipulation even when target objects are not directly observable. This approach seeks to enhance the robustness and operational scope of VLA systems in real-world scenarios where objects frequently move out of view or require manipulation in partially observed environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to perform robust manipulation tasks even when target objects are initially or temporarily outside the robot's camera's field of view (out-of-vision, OOV). This contrasts with most existing VLA systems that implicitly assume continuous visibility of task-relevant objects, leading to brittle and reactive behaviors when this assumption is violated.

The motivation for developing SOMA stems from several observations:
- **Limitations of View-Bound Perception:** Current VLA models, often developed under fixed-view tabletop setups or relying on single static cameras, struggle when objects are occluded or leave the camera's frustum. This reliance on instantaneous, view-bound visual observations results in task failure as the perception-action loop breaks down without visual input of the target. Figure 1 in the paper illustrates this issue, showing a robot failing to pick a "pink cup" because it is out of sight, despite multiple cameras.
- **Brittleness of Internal Spatial Reasoning:** While some efforts have been made to compensate for out-of-view perception using internal spatial reasoning (e.g., learned spatial priors), these methods are often unreliable. MLLMs can infer unseen targets only when partial visual evidence or strong object-related cues are available, and their spatial estimates rapidly degrade once targets are fully unobservable. Errors propagate, leading to inaccurate localization and failed manipulation.
- **Need for Persistent Spatial Representation:** The authors argue that merely increasing model capacity or reasoning depth cannot compensate for missing perceptual grounding when objects have never been observed or past observations are not retained. A fundamental requirement for robust OOV manipulation is a mechanism that both acquires spatial evidence beyond the current view and retains it in a persistent scene representation.
- **Integration of Active Perception and Memory:** The paper posits that integrating angular-wise observations into a coherent spatial-semantic memory is crucial for globally consistent reasoning. This necessitates equipping the robot with a movable head-mounted camera to acquire diverse viewpoints and a global spatial memory to unify these observations.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
1. Develop a VLA framework that enables manipulation of objects beyond the current camera view.
2. Introduce a memory-centric perception architecture that maintains a persistent, queryable spatial-semantic representation of the scene.
3. Facilitate instruction-aware action selection and manipulation grounded in this global spatial memory, rather than solely on immediate visual inputs.
4. Demonstrate that such a framework leads to more efficient and reliable manipulation behaviors under OOV conditions, including faster target localization and reduced search efforts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SOMA framework addresses out-of-vision manipulation by integrating a persistent spatial memory into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The methodology comprises three interconnected components: Spatial Memory Construction, Dynamic Memory Refinement, and Contextual Memory Retrieval, along with an active head camera for multi-view observations.

**General Workflow:**
Initially, if a task-relevant target object is not visible in the current camera view, the robot initiates a dedicated scene observation phase using its movable head camera. This phase is used to build the initial spatial memory. During manipulation, new observations continuously update this memory, and instruction-relevant spatial cues are retrieved to guide actions.

#### 3.1. Spatial Memory Construction

This phase aims to build a unified spatial-semantic map of the environment.
- **Multi-view Scanning:** A movable head camera performs a predefined scanning strategy to acquire a sequence of multi-view observations (V). A subset of frames (˜V) is uniformly sampled from this sequence.
- **Perception Pipeline:** Each sampled frame f_i ∈ ˜V is processed by a unified perception pipeline:
 - **Geometry Prior Network (VGGT):** Estimates camera pose and coarse scene geometry, establishing a consistent global 3D scene coordinate system.
 - **Semantic Perception Module (YOLO):** Performs object localization and categorization, detecting 2D bounding boxes (b_j^(i)) for instances.
 - **Semantic Encoder (DINOv3):** Extracts high-dimensional visual features (F^(i)) for each frame. Instance-level appearance embeddings (f_j^(i)) are obtained by cropping and spatially pooling features from detected 2D bounding boxes.
- **3D Lifting and Association:** Each 2D detection is lifted into the global 3D scene coordinate system using VGGT-predicted geometric priors, yielding 3D bounding boxes (b_j^(i) ∈ R^(8x3)). These 3D boxes are expressed in the global reference frame. Across all sampled frames, detected instances are associated and fused based on a joint appearance-geometry similarity (cosine similarity for DINOv3 embeddings and 3D spatial consistency for bounding boxes). Merged instances have their features and geometries aggregated by averaging, resulting in global collections of appearance features (F), 3D bounding boxes (B_3D), and 3D centroids (C_3D). Placeholder embeddings and pseudo bounding boxes are injected for robustness against imperfect detections.
- **Spatial-Semantic Embedding:** Each global instance (f_k, c_k, b_k) is transformed into a memory token m_0_k. The 3D bounding box b_k is embedded into a spatial positional vector p_k = Φ_pos(b_k) using a learnable 3D positional embedding module. Object features f_k are projected into a shared embedding space via Φ_mem(·). The final memory token m_0_k = Φ_mem(f_k) + p_k jointly encodes appearance semantics and 3D spatial context. The collection of these tokens forms the initial overview scene memory M_0 = {m_0_k}_(k=1)^(N_I).

#### 3.2. Dynamic Memory Refinement

This component iteratively updates the scene memory (M_0) during manipulation to maintain an up-to-date and globally coherent scene representation.
- **Current Observation Processing:** At each time step t, the current head-view observation o_h^t is processed by the same embedding pipeline (Φ_mem and positional embedding) to produce current memory tokens M_t = {m_j^t}.
- **Instance-Level Association and Fusion:** For each new observed instance m_j^t, an instance-level association is performed against same-class entries in the previous memory state M_(t-1) using the same appearance-geometry similarity criterion.
- **Adaptive Similarity-Aware Fusion:** If a match is found (m_k^(t-1)), its representation is refined. Semantic similarity (s_k_j^t) and a dynamic fusion score (g_k_j^t) are computed using dedicated MLPs (Φ_sim, Φ_fuse). These scores determine an adaptive update coefficient α_k_j^t = g_k_j^t ⋅ s_k_j^t. The matched memory token is then updated via a temporal exponential moving average: m_k^t = α_k_j^t m_j^t + (1 - α_k_j^t) m_k^(t-1).
- **Memory Management:** Instances without matches are appended as new memory entries. Unmatched past memory instances are retained to handle temporary occlusions. This process yields the refined current memory ˆM_t.

#### 3.3. Contextual Memory Retrieval

This module injects global spatial-semantic knowledge into the multimodal representation for instruction-grounded reasoning.
- **Query Formulation:** Vision-language token embeddings (X_vl) from the VLM are treated as the query set (Q).
- **Memory as Key-Value Bank:** The refined scene memory ˆM_t acts as the key-value memory bank (K, V). Each memory token m_k^t is projected into the VLM's latent space via an alignment function Φ_align(·) to become ˜m_k^t.
- **Cross-Attention Mechanism:** A cross-attention module performs retrieval, where X_boost = softmax(QK^T/√C)V. This enhances each token x_i with spatially grounded memory cues.
- **Action Generation:** The memory-enhanced features (X_boost) are injected into Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks as a spatial-semantic prior. These DiT blocks fuse current observations with the structured scene memory. The refined tokens, along with original VLM tokens, robot states, and noised action embeddings, are then passed to an embodiment-specific action decoder to generate the next action chunk.

The framework utilizes offline preprocessing for Spatial Memory Construction by leveraging external perception models (VGGT, YOLO, DINOv3) and uniform frame sampling from both scanning and manipulation phases. A back-mapping procedure ensures that every timestep in a trajectory is paired with valid memory observations during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SOMA through extensive experiments in both real-world and simulation environments, focusing on out-of-vision (OOV) manipulation and the general effectiveness of its memory design.

#### 4.1. Real-World Out-of-Vision Manipulation Tasks

SOMA was evaluated on five self-designed, challenging real-world OOV pick-and-place (PnP) tasks, including scenarios with multi-step and dual-arm manipulation where target objects were initially invisible.
- **Improved Success Rates:** SOMA demonstrated the highest success rates across all five OOV tasks compared to baselines such as Diffusion Policy, StarVLA, SpatialVLA, and GR00T N1.5. For instance, in "Invisible-to-Invisible PnP," SOMA achieved a pick success rate of 30% and a place success rate of 25%, while GR00T N1.5 managed 15% for both. The performance gap increased with task complexity (e.g., in "Sequential Dual-Object PnP" and "Dual-Arm Coordination PnP"). The "Fixed Head Camera" baseline consistently failed when targets or goals left the field of view, underscoring the limitations of view-bound policies.
- **Qualitatively Different Behaviors:** Beyond improved success rates, SOMA induced distinct manipulation behaviors:
 - **Faster First-Fixation Time:** SOMA reduced the time to visually acquire the target object by 40-59% compared to GR00T-N1.5 across tasks (e.g., 4.2s vs. 7.6s in Task 1).
 - **Reduced Head Search Path Length:** The total angular displacement of the head before target visibility was reduced by 40-57% (e.g., 27.8 deg vs. 50.5 deg in Task 1).
 - **Reduced Viewpoint Correction Count:** SOMA exhibited 42-57% fewer head reorientation events after initial target observation (e.g., 0.9 vs. 1.6 in Task 1).
 - **Near One-Shot Grasping:** The number of grasp attempts was reduced by 40-57% (e.g., 1.0 vs. 1.8 in Task 1), indicating more decisive and spatially grounded execution.
 - **Reduced Time-to-Grasp:** Overall time from episode start to successful grasp was reduced by 41-60% (e.g., 32.3s vs. 58.0s in Task 1).
- **Ablation Study on Memory Components (Real-world OOV):**
 - "Scan+GR00T" (scanning without persistent memory) yielded the lowest performance, indicating that scanning alone is insufficient.
 - "No-Scan SOMA" (memory from single-view initialization) slightly outperformed "Scan+GR00T," highlighting the benefit of explicit memory.
 - "Scan-only SOMA" (multi-view scans for memory but no dynamic refinement) improved further.
 - "Full SOMA" (with dynamic refinement) consistently achieved the best performance, suggesting that persistent spatial memory and its ongoing refinement contribute significantly more to performance gains than scanning alone.

#### 4.2. Simulation Results

SOMA's memory design was also validated on conventional fully observable settings in RoboCasa Tabletop GR1 and SimplerEnv.
- **RoboCasa Tabletop GR1:** SOMA achieved the highest average success rate of 52.0% with 300 demonstrations, outperforming baselines like Diffusion Policy, GR00T, and StarVLA across five task categories (Container Interaction, Cooking Preparation, Tabletop Serving, Dish Transfer, Tray Organization). SOMA showed strong sample efficiency, achieving an average success rate of 48.3% with only 30 demonstrations, surpassing GR00T-N1.5 with the full dataset. This suggests that explicit scene representation and instruction-conditioned retrieval enhance learning stability and generalization.
- **SimplerEnv Fractal Suite:** SOMA achieved the highest average success rates in both Visual Matching (63.2%) and Variant Aggregation (52.5%) settings, surpassing other SOTA VLAs. This indicates robustness and spatial consistency even under significant visual perturbations and task variants.
- **Ablation Study on Memory Components (GR1):**
 - Removing positional cues ("w/o Geo.") or object semantics ("w/o Obj.") from the overview scene memory led to performance drops of 4.2% and 5.6%, respectively, demonstrating the necessity of both geometric and semantic priors.
 - Excluding the dynamic update mechanism ("w/o Update") caused the largest degradation (7.8% drop), confirming the importance of temporal refinement for memory consistency.
 - The complete adaptive similarity-aware fusion mechanism in Dynamic Memory Refinement was found to be most effective.
 - A "Middle" cross-attention layer configuration (3 layers) in Contextual Memory Retrieval achieved an optimal balance of performance and complexity.
 - An optimal frame sampling interval of 20 was identified for Spatial Memory Construction, balancing information density and temporal diversity.

#### 4.3. Latency Evaluation

SOMA exhibited comparable online inference latency per action chunk to prior VLA baselines (e.g., StarVLA, GR00T-N1.5) in real-world experiments. This suggests that the spatial memory design does not impose a prohibitive computational overhead during online execution.

#### 4.4. Failure Case Analysis

- **Real-world OOV tasks:** Dominant failures (48%) stemmed from inaccurate grasp pose estimation during active visual search. Other issues included placement timing/precision errors (32%) and head-arm coordination mismatches (20%), indicating challenges in translating spatial localization into precise motor execution under dynamic viewpoints.
- **Fully observable simulation:** Failures primarily involved noisy spatial tokens from imperfect 3D estimation (44%) and irrelevant memory activation during retrieval (32%). A lack of task-phase awareness in memory refinement (24%) also contributed to failures in multi-stage tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SOMA framework addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation: the inability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to reliably operate when task-relevant objects are outside the robot's immediate field of view. By equipping robots with a persistent, spatial memory constructed from multi-view observations, SOMA moves beyond the limitations of view-bound, reactive perception.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Enhanced Robustness in Partial Observability:** SOMA enables VLA models to reason about and manipulate objects that are temporarily or initially out of sight. This represents a step towards more robust and capable robotic systems that can operate effectively in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments, where full observability is often not guaranteed.
- **Qualitative Behavioral Improvements:** The observed changes in manipulation behavior—faster target localization, reduced head movement, more stable viewpoint alignment, and near one-shot grasping—indicate that memory-guided manipulation can lead to more efficient and decisive actions compared to purely reactive policies. This suggests improved operational efficiency for robots.
- **Generalizability of Memory Design:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated in both real-world OOV tasks and fully observable simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa GR1, SimplerEnv) highlight that SOMA's memory mechanism is not limited to OOV scenarios but also enhances learning stability and generalization in conventional settings. This implies a broadly beneficial architectural component for VLA systems.
- **Foundation for Complex Tasks:** By maintaining a globally consistent spatial representation, SOMA facilitates more complex manipulation tasks, such as multi-step sequences and dual-arm coordination, which require persistent spatial awareness and cross-stage information retention.
- **Modular and Adaptable Framework:** The modular design, building on standard perception components (YOLO, DINOv3, VGGT) and integrating with existing VLA architectures (Diffusion Transformer), suggests that SOMA can benefit from future advances in these underlying technologies and be integrated into various robotic platforms.

Potential impacts include:
- **Broader Real-World Applications:** The ability to manipulate out-of-view objects is critical for practical applications in household assistance, industrial automation, logistics, and exploration, where objects might be occluded, stored in drawers, or require reaching beyond the direct line of sight.
- **More Autonomous and Intelligent Robots:** By enabling robots to build and refine an internal model of their environment, even when not directly observing everything, SOMA contributes to the development of more autonomous and contextually aware robotic agents.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as robustness in highly dynamic scenes, fine-grained semantic state transitions (e.g., open vs. closed drawer), and scalability to larger environments, offer clear pathways for future research. This includes developing adaptive memory update mechanisms, integrating explicit task-phase awareness, and scaling hierarchical memory structures for mobile manipulation.
- **Safety Considerations:** The work acknowledges that persistent memory also introduces potential risks related to stale or incorrect information and privacy concerns in human-shared environments, prompting a focus on confidence scoring, re-scan triggers, memory expiration, and human-in-the-loop validation as crucial mitigation strategies.
