# What Matters for Simulation to Online Reinforcement Learning on Real Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper empirically investigates crucial design choices for successfully fine-tuning simulation-trained reinforcement learning policies on real robots in an online setting. It demonstrates that standard off-policy algorithms can achieve stable performance on diverse robotic platforms by employing techniques like data retention, warm starts, and asymmetric actor-critic updates.

## Problem

- Transferring reinforcement learning policies trained in simulation to real robots often faces a "sim-to-real gap," making online adaptation on physical hardware challenging.
- Online reinforcement learning on real robots is frequently unstable and sample-inefficient, often leading to a "downward spiral" where fine-tuning degrades performance.
- Existing deep RL applications in robotics often rely on customized setups or lack systematic empirical studies of practical deployment challenges across diverse platforms.

## Method

- A systematic empirical study was conducted, involving over 100 real-world training runs on three distinct robotic platforms (manipulation, locomotion, navigation), using standard off-policy RL algorithms like SAC.
- Key design choices were investigated: retaining simulation or past real-world data in the replay buffer, using warm starts to pre-fill the buffer, and implementing asymmetric actor-critic updates.
- An open-source training pipeline and robotic stack for the Franka Emika Panda were provided to facilitate reproducibility and lower entry barriers for real-world RL experimentation.

## Results

- The study demonstrated that retaining simulation data and previous real-world data significantly improved learning efficiency and stability, often by acting as a regularizer during online fine-tuning.
- Warm-starting the online replay buffer with initial real-world data collected by the prior policy was shown to improve learning stability for locomotion and navigation tasks.
- Asymmetric actor-critic updates, updating the actor less frequently and with a smaller learning rate, were essential for preventing performance degradation and enabling stable online policy improvement across all tested robotic platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effectively scaling off-policy algorithms in simulation for robust sim-to-real transfer requires a high update-to-data ratio and a large number of domain-randomized environments (N_e ~ 10^3).
> - Stabilizing online fine-tuning on real robots relies heavily on practical data management strategies, such as retaining simulation data and previous online data within the replay buffer, and utilizing warm starts.
> - Asymmetric actor-critic updates, specifically delaying actor updates and using a reduced actor learning rate, are critical for preventing policy divergence and ensuring stable learning in the presence of distribution shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and persistent challenge in modern robotics: effectively transferring and adapting reinforcement learning (RL) policies trained in simulation to real-world robotic systems. This domain, often referred to as "sim-to-real," has seen significant advancements, but the transition from *offline simulation training* to *online adaptation on physical hardware* remains a bottleneck.

Historically, early applications of RL on real robots (dating back to the 1990s) were limited to simpler tasks with low-dimensional state-action spaces. The advent of "deep RL" techniques, exemplified by works like Haarnoja et al.'s Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), demonstrated the potential for efficient learning on real robots. However, much of this literature, while showcasing algorithmic innovation, often relied on customized hardware, proprietary software, or narrow experimental setups, making reproduction and broad applicability difficult. These works typically focused on *demonstrating a concept* rather than systematically investigating the *practical engineering challenges* of deployment.

A key limitation of many existing RL-in-robotics systems is that learning occurs predominantly offline—either entirely within a simulator or using a fixed dataset. While this approach yields some success, its performance is ultimately constrained by the quality of the "priors" (simulators or pre-collected data). Simulators are inherently imperfect, and acquiring high-quality real-world robotics data is orders of magnitude more expensive than in other AI domains (e.g., language modeling). This leads to the "sim-to-real gap," where policies optimal in simulation often perform poorly on physical robots.

Prior work has explored sim-to-real fine-tuning, such as Yin et al. which proposed reward shaping to mitigate transfer challenges, or Smith et al. which focused on legged locomotion tasks. Ibarz et al. provided a comprehensive review identifying data reuse as crucial but lacked empirical validation on real robots. Tirumala et al. showed the effectiveness of data reuse in simulated environments. This paper distinguishes itself by *systematically and empirically studying* the "sim-to-online" setting across *multiple, diverse real robotic platforms* (manipulation, locomotion, navigation). Instead of proposing complex new algorithms, it investigates *specific design choices* within *standard off-policy RL algorithms* (like SAC and TD3) that are often left implicit or treated as defaults in prior work.

Crucially, the paper tackles the "downward spiral" problem, where fine-tuning simulation-trained policies can lead to instability and even "unlearning" due to distribution shifts and approximation errors. By providing the "first large-sample empirical study of such design choices," this work fills a significant gap, offering practical, actionable guidance to practitioners and researchers aiming to deploy online RL with lower engineering effort, thereby democratizing access to real-world RL experimentation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from the recognition that as robotic tasks grow in complexity and the desire for autonomous systems to continually adapt to open-world scenarios increases, future robots *must* learn online through embodied interaction. Relying solely on offline learning, either in simulators or from fixed datasets, presents fundamental limitations:

1. **Simulator Imperfection (Sim-to-Real Gap):** Simulators, no matter how sophisticated, are imperfect models of reality. Unmodeled dynamics, sensor noise, and environmental variations invariably create a gap between simulated and real-world performance.
2. **Data Scarcity and Cost:** Obtaining high-quality, diverse real-world robotics data for pre-training is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, unlike the vast, Internet-scale datasets available for other AI domains.
3. **Limitations of Priors:** Without online adaptation, performance is ultimately bounded by the quality of the initial knowledge (priors) encoded in the simulator or offline dataset. When tasks require novel interactions or encounter unforeseen circumstances, the robot must be able to adapt.

Given these challenges, the authors explicitly state their overarching goal: **to empirically study the "sim-to-online" setting and demonstrate a working recipe for online reinforcement learning on real robots.**

To achieve this, the paper sets out several key objectives:

- **Identify Enabling Design Choices:** Systematically investigate which specific algorithmic, systems, and experimental design choices are crucial for successful online RL on physical robots. This involves ablating factors often left implicit in prior work.
- **Demonstrate Robustness Across Platforms:** Validate the identified design choices across a diverse set of three distinct real-world robotic platforms (manipulation, locomotion, and navigation) to ensure broad applicability.
- **Lower the Entry Barrier:** Develop and open-source a comprehensive training pipeline that facilitates pre-training in simulation (using MuJoCo Playground) and seamless continuation of online training on real robots. Crucially, for the Franka Emika Panda, they open-source the full robotic stack using "off-the-shelf" hardware to enhance reproducibility.
- **Mitigate Instabilities:** Understand and address the stability challenges that arise when transferring simulation-trained policies to real robots, particularly the "downward spiral" where fine-tuning can lead to unlearning.
- **Provide Actionable Guidance:** Offer practical, evidence-based recommendations that practitioners can directly apply to reduce the engineering burden associated with deploying RL on real robots.
- **Enable Effective Pretraining:** Empirically study and demonstrate techniques for effective pretraining of off-policy RL algorithms in massively-parallel simulators, which is a prerequisite for robust sim-to-real transfer.

In essence, the paper aims to shift the paradigm towards making online, adaptive learning on real robots a more standard and accessible practice, moving beyond isolated demonstrations of algorithmic novelty.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a rigorous empirical approach, conducting over 100 real-world training runs on three distinct robotic platforms to systematically investigate design choices for sim-to-online reinforcement learning.

**Problem Setting:**
The study focuses on infinite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) within an episodic online learning framework. This means agents learn over finite episodes, after which the robot is manually reset. The core challenge is fine-tuning a "prior policy" ($\pi_0$), initially learned in simulation (or from an offline dataset $\mathcal{D}_0$), on a real robot to overcome the sim-to-real gap. The prior policy is assumed to be suboptimal on the real system, necessitating further online data collection and learning.

**Core Algorithms:**
The primary reinforcement learning algorithm used is **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)**, an off-policy, maximum-entropy deep RL algorithm known for its sample efficiency and competitive results. Some additional experiments are conducted using **TD3 (Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient)** to validate findings across different off-policy methods. The choice of off-policy algorithms is motivated by their ability to reuse past data, which is crucial for sample-efficient online learning on real robots.

**Robotic Platforms:**
To ensure broad applicability, experiments are conducted on three diverse real-world robots:
1. **Franka Emika Panda (Manipulation):** A popular research and industrial robot. Task: locate, grasp, and lift a cube to a goal position, based on grayscale image observations. This task is challenging due to vision-based control, complex contact dynamics, and sparse rewards.
2. **Unitree Go1 (Locomotion):** A quadruped robot. Task: follow joystick commands (linear and angular velocities) by mapping them to joint position targets.
3. **Race Car (Navigation):** A remote-controlled car. Task: park at a specified goal position as quickly as possible. This involves high agility, a fast control loop, and difficult-to-model tire friction and drifting.

**Key Design Choices and Techniques Investigated:**
The paper focuses on three crucial techniques to stabilize learning under deployment shifts, particularly against the "downward spiral" problem:

1. **Data Retention:** This involves keeping the data $\mathcal{D}_0$ (collected in simulation during pretraining or from previous real-world trials) in the replay buffer. During online learning, minibatches for critic updates (Equation 3) are sampled as a mixture of $\mathcal{D}_0$ and newly collected online data ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{online}}$) using a blending factor $\alpha$. The authors propose annealing $\alpha$ from an initial value (e.g., 0.5) to 1, gradually reducing reliance on simulation data. This provides a stabilizing prior by continuing to expose the critic to data where its approximation errors are typically smaller.
2. **Warm Starts:** As an alternative or complement to retaining $\mathcal{D}_0$, this technique involves pre-filling the online replay buffer $\mathcal{D}_{\text{online}}$ by running the initial policy $\pi_0$ for a number of initial episodes ($N^*$) *without* performing any updates. This provides the online learning process with a rich initial dataset from the prior policy, which can mitigate instabilities caused by starting with an empty buffer.
3. **Asymmetric Updates:** This addresses the observation that increasing the update-to-data (UTD) ratio ($\eta$) in off-policy RL can amplify approximation errors. The authors investigate mitigating this by:
 - **Delaying Actor Updates:** Updating the actor less frequently than the critic (e.g., every $M$ critic updates, where $M > 1$).
 - **Reducing Actor Learning Rate:** Using a smaller learning rate for the actor compared to the critic.
 This approach, grounded in two-timescale stochastic approximation, aims to ensure that the policy improvement step is based on a more stable and accurate value function estimate.

**Pretraining in Simulation:**
- Policies $\pi_0$ are pretrained in massively parallel simulators. The Franka Emika Panda and Unitree Go1 use MuJoCo Playground, while the Race Car uses a first-principles model implemented with Brax.
- **Domain Randomization:** Applied extensively (especially for Franka Emika Panda's vision-based task) to improve sim-to-real transfer by exposing the policy to diverse visual and physical variations.
- **Scaling SAC:** The authors demonstrate that scaling SAC effectively in parallel simulation requires increasing the UTD ratio ($\eta$) proportionally to the number of parallel environments ($N_e$) to prevent "undertraining." They also show that a large $N_e$ (e.g., $~10^3$) is crucial for robust transfer, not just good simulation performance.

**Experimental Protocol:**
- Each real-world experiment is run with three random seeds, and performance (undiscounted accumulated rewards $\hat{J}(\pi)$) is reported with mean and standard error.
- Episodes have fixed time steps (e.g., $T=250$ for Panda/Race Car, $T=1000$ for Go1).
- The authors also identify and call out common "pitfalls" in implementation (e.g., not restoring optimizer state or target networks) that can lead to silent failures or distorted results.
- An open-source pipeline (https://github.com/yardenas/panda-rl-kit) is provided to enable reproducibility.

This comprehensive methodological approach allows the authors to systematically tease out the factors that truly matter for stable and efficient sim-to-online reinforcement learning on real-world robots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings derived from its extensive empirical study, highlighting the crucial design choices that enable successful sim-to-online reinforcement learning on real robots.

#### 4.1. Learning $\pi_0$ in Simulation (Pretraining Insights)

- **Scaling Soft Actor-Critic (SAC):** The study found that effectively scaling SAC in massively parallel simulators requires increasing the Update-to-Data (UTD) ratio ($\eta$) proportionally to the number of parallel environments ($N_e$). While higher $\eta$ improves sample efficiency (fewer environment steps), there are diminishing returns beyond a task-dependent saturation point in terms of wall-clock training time (Figure 5).
- **Importance of Scale for Sim-to-Real:** A critical finding is that a large number of domain-randomized environments ($N_e \sim 10^3$) is essential for robust sim-to-real transfer (Figure 11). Even if a policy appears to converge well in simulation with fewer environments, its real-world performance can be significantly degraded, indicating that broader generalization through extensive randomization is paramount.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap Quantification:** As expected, prior policies ($\pi_0$) trained purely in simulation consistently exhibit a notable performance drop when deployed on real robots across all three tasks (Figure 6). This empirically validates the necessity of online fine-tuning. The zero-shot performance of SAC and PPO shows similar degradation, indicating the sim-to-real gap, not the choice of off-policy algorithm, is the primary issue (Figure 12).

#### 4.2. Real-World Results (Effectiveness of Design Choices)

The core results demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed design choices in stabilizing and accelerating online learning on physical robots:

- **Recycling Data Accelerates Learning (Data Retention):**
 - Retaining data from *previous real-world trials* significantly boosts learning performance and robustness (Figure 8). As more online data is accumulated and reused across trials, the robot's ability to learn and adapt improves consistently.
 - Even more fundamentally, retaining the *simulation data* ($\mathcal{D}_0$) used for pretraining substantially improves both learning efficiency and stability in the online fine-tuning phase (Figure 15). The simulation data acts as a regularizer, biasing minibatches towards samples with lower approximation error, thereby dampening sharp distribution shifts and stabilizing the learning process. Annealing the mixing parameter $\alpha$ from 0.5 to 1 ensures that the policy eventually relies solely on real-world data to achieve optimal performance.
 - The initial choice of the mixing parameter $\alpha_0$ was found to be somewhat robust; as long as offline data is used early and online data dominates later, good performance can be achieved (Figure 14).

- **Warm Starts as a Proxy for Data Retention:**
 - Pre-filling the online replay buffer with data collected by running $\pi_0$ for an initial period ($N^*$ iterations) (a "warm start") improves learning stability for the Unitree Go1 and Race Car robots (Figure 9). This provides a valuable initial dataset of real-world experiences.
 - Interestingly, for the Franka Emika Panda, learning succeeded even without a warm start, suggesting task-dependent sensitivity to this particular technique. However, it generally provides a benefit when $\mathcal{D}_0$ cannot be fully retained.

- **Asymmetric Updates are Critical for Stability:**
 - This was identified as a crucial technique across all robotic platforms. The baseline approach, where the actor is updated with the same frequency and learning rate as the critic, universally failed to improve performance due to training instability (Figure 10).
 - In contrast, employing asymmetric updates—updating the actor less frequently (e.g., every 20 critic updates) and reducing its learning rate—enabled efficient and stable transfer (Figure 10). This finding was further supported by sim-to-sim experiments with SAC (Figure 4) and TD3 (Figure 13), where increasing the actor update delay ($M$) significantly improved stability under dynamics mismatch. This confirms that more conservative actor updates are essential for preventing the policy from diverging based on noisy or erroneous value function estimates during distribution shifts.

In summary, the paper empirically demonstrates that standard off-policy RL algorithms like SAC can be successfully fine-tuned on real hardware within realistic time budgets for various tasks, including vision-based ones. This success hinges not on complex algorithmic modifications, but on adopting robust, readily available design choices related to data management (retention, warm starts) and optimization stability (asymmetric actor-critic updates).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in making online reinforcement learning on real robotic systems a more practical and accessible endeavor. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Demystifying Sim-to-Online Transfer:** The paper provides the first large-sample empirical study systematically ablating critical design choices for sim-to-online RL. By focusing on practical challenges rather than solely algorithmic novelty, it demystifies many implicit assumptions and common practices, offering clarity on "what matters" for real-world deployment. This reduces the trial-and-error often associated with robot learning.
- **Actionable Guidance for Practitioners:** The findings translate directly into actionable recommendations for engineers and researchers. Implementing techniques such as retaining data (simulation and past online trials), utilizing warm starts, and employing asymmetric actor-critic updates can dramatically improve the stability and efficiency of real-world RL fine-tuning. This guidance is especially valuable as it requires no major modifications to standard off-policy algorithms like SAC.
- **Lowering the Entry Barrier to Real-World RL:** The open-sourcing of the training pipeline, particularly the full robotic stack for the Franka Emika Panda with off-the-shelf hardware, is a substantial contribution. This initiative promotes reproducibility and lowers the financial and technical hurdles for new researchers to engage in real-world RL experiments, fostering a more collaborative and accelerated research ecosystem.
- **Validation of Off-Policy Methods for Real Robots:** The work confirms that sample-efficient off-policy algorithms are well-suited for real-world fine-tuning. The ability to adapt policies within realistic time budgets (e.g., roughly ten minutes for the Franka Emika Panda) makes online learning a viable strategy, even for complex vision-based tasks with sparse rewards.
- **Addressing the "Downward Spiral" Empirically:** The paper provides empirical evidence that simple mitigation techniques can effectively counteract the "downward spiral" problem, where distribution shifts lead to unlearning. This strengthens the argument for using simulation-trained priors followed by online adaptation.
- **Challenging Harmful Defaults:** The study reveals that some widely used defaults in RL implementation can be detrimental in the sim-to-online setting (e.g., symmetric actor-critic updates). This prompts a re-evaluation of common practices and encourages more informed decision-making during implementation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While providing clear guidance, the paper also opens up several important avenues for future research. These include:
 - **Optimal Sample Selection:** How to optimally select samples from offline data ($\mathcal{D}_0$) to maximize online sample efficiency.
 - **Cross-Task Data Reuse:** Exploring strategies for effectively reusing data across different robotic tasks, moving towards more generalizable learning.
 - **Improved Regularization:** Investigating alternative or superior regularization strategies to enable even faster and more stable online learning.
 - **Fully Autonomous Learning:** Extending the research beyond the current semi-automated episodic setting (which still requires manual resets) towards practical algorithmic solutions for fully autonomous, non-episodic learning in the open world.

In conclusion, "What Matters for Sim-to-Online Reinforcement Learning on Real Robots" provides a critical empirical foundation for the practical deployment of RL in robotics. Its focus on robust, accessible, and empirically validated design choices positions it as a highly impactful reference for researchers and practitioners alike, accelerating the development of truly adaptive and capable robotic systems.
