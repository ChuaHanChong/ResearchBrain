# Inpainting-Guided Policy Optimization for Diffusion Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Inpainting-Guided Policy Optimization (IGPO) framework enables efficient reinforcement learning for masked diffusion large language models (dLLMs) by leveraging their unique inpainting capabilities to overcome challenges in policy optimization. This approach yields an average 7.7% absolute accuracy improvement on mathematical reasoning benchmarks like GSM8K, Math500, and AMC compared to baselines, achieving new state-of-the-art for this dLLM architecture.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning for large language models struggles with sparse reward signals and inefficient exploration in complex tasks.
- Group-based policy optimization methods encounter a "zero-advantage dilemma" where uniform incorrect rewards within a group prevent meaningful policy updates.
- Existing RL approaches for diffusion LLMs do not fully exploit their unique bidirectional architecture and inpainting capabilities.

## Method

- **IGPO** leverages dLLMs' ==inpainting== to provide partial ground-truth reasoning hints, selectively triggered when a "zero-advantage dilemma" is detected in policy optimization.
- Correct responses generated with hints replace original incorrect samples, restoring a non-degenerate advantage signal and guiding the policy.
- Training stability is maintained by applying gradient updates only to high-entropy hint token positions, complemented by a Length-Aligned SFT strategy with concisely rewritten reasoning traces.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art mathematical reasoning performance for full-attention masked dLLMs, with an average **7.7%** absolute accuracy increase across GSM8K, Math500, and AMC benchmarks.
- Demonstrated specific improvements of +**4.9%** on GSM8K, +**8.4%** on Math500, and +**9.9%** on AMC over the LLaDA-**8B**-Instruct baseline.
- IGPO reduced the occurrence of "all-wrong groups" by approximately **60%**, ensuring more stable training and outperforming other reinforcement learning methods like UniGRPO and DiffuGRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The bidirectional nature and inpainting ability of diffusion LLMs offer a powerful mechanism for guided exploration in RL, particularly for resolving "zero-advantage" scenarios.
> - Providing partial, rather than full, ground-truth hints allows the model to engage in self-generated reasoning, effectively bridging supervised fine-tuning and on-policy reinforcement learning.
> - Selectively updating gradients based on the entropy of hint token positions is critical for stabilizing RL training when incorporating off-policy guidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), diffusion models, and reinforcement learning (RL), a highly active and critical area in contemporary AI.

**Emergence of Diffusion LLMs:** The paper highlights masked diffusion large language models (dLLMs) as a promising alternative to the widely prevalent autoregressive (AR) LLMs. While AR LLMs (like GPT, LLaMA) generate text token-by-token from left to right, dLLMs, exemplified by models like LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025) and Dream (Ye et al., 2025), iteratively unmask tokens in parallel. This distinct architecture offers several advantages:
- **Unique Generation Capabilities:** Foremost among these is "inpainting," the ability to fill in missing content within existing text, which AR models inherently struggle with due to their unidirectional nature.
- **Potential for Faster Inference:** As noted with models like Mercury (Inception Labs et al., 2025) and Gemini Diffusion (DeepMind, 2025), parallel unmasking can lead to faster generation.
- **Flexible Inductive Bias:** dLLMs can naturally integrate multimodal data, as explored in recent works (Li et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; You et al., 2025).

**Challenges in LLM Alignment via RL:** Reinforcement learning (RL) has become indispensable for aligning LLMs with human preferences and complex task objectives (e.g., through RL from Human Feedback or RLHF). However, a fundamental challenge across *all* LLM architectures is exploration. In challenging tasks, policies often struggle to discover correct solutions, leading to sparse reward signals and significant sample waste. This inefficiency burdens the computational cost of online RL, a problem that affects both AR and dLLMs.

**The Unique Opportunity for dLLMs:** While dLLMs face the same exploration hurdle as AR LLMs, their bidirectional generative structure and inherent inpainting capability present a unique opportunity. Prior work on RL for dLLMs (e.g., DiffuGRPO by Zhao et al., 2025; UniGRPO by Yang et al., 2025) largely adapted methods initially developed for AR LLMs, without fully capitalizing on the distinct architectural advantages of diffusion models. This paper explicitly targets this gap.

**Bridging SFT and RL:** IGPO introduces a novel form of guided exploration that interpolates between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning. SFT provides dense supervision but can suffer from distribution shift during policy rollouts. RL offers fine-grained optimization but struggles with exploration. IGPO aims to harness the benefits of both: SFT-like guidance through inpainting and on-policy learning for the self-generated parts. This approach addresses a common challenge in LLM training, where moving from SFT to RL often involves a difficult transition.

**Addressing the Zero-Advantage Dilemma:** The paper also directly tackles a specific vulnerability in group-based policy optimization methods, such as GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), which are increasingly popular for their computational efficiency. When all samples within a group yield identical rewards (e.g., all incorrect), the advantage signal collapses to zero, preventing any meaningful policy update. This "zero-advantage dilemma" is a critical practical issue in sparse-reward environments, and IGPO offers a targeted solution for dLLMs.

In summary, this work pushes the frontier of dLLM training by developing an RL framework that is specifically tailored to the unique strengths of the diffusion architecture, rather than merely porting AR LLM techniques. It aims to make RL for dLLMs more efficient, stable, and powerful, ultimately improving their performance on complex reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of reinforcement learning for masked diffusion large language models (dLLMs) by systematically leveraging their unique inpainting capabilities.**

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific challenges:

- **Mitigate the RL Exploration Challenge:** A fundamental hurdle in applying RL to LLMs, both autoregressive and diffusion-based, is the sparse reward signal. For complex tasks like mathematical reasoning, models frequently generate incorrect solutions, leading to many zero-reward samples. This makes it difficult for the policy to discover rewarding behaviors, resulting in inefficient exploration and substantial sample waste. The paper aims to address this inefficiency for dLLMs.

- **Solve the "Zero-Advantage Dilemma" in Group-Based RL:** Policy optimization methods like Group-relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are vulnerable to situations where all sampled responses within a group yield identical rewards (e.g., all incorrect). In such "all-wrong groups," the relative advantage signal collapses to zero, resulting in degenerate gradients and stalled learning. This phenomenon is particularly problematic in challenging reasoning domains. A core motivation is to introduce a mechanism that restores meaningful gradient signals in these scenarios.

- **Leverage dLLMs' Unique Architectural Advantage:** Unlike autoregressive models, dLLMs possess an inherent ability to perform inpainting due to their bidirectional attention mechanism and training procedure involving stochastic masking. The authors are motivated to explore how this distinctive capability, which is often underutilized in existing RL approaches for dLLMs, can be harnessed to guide exploration more effectively than traditional RL methods.

- **Bridge Supervised and Reinforcement Learning:** The paper seeks to find a middle ground between the strong guidance of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and the on-policy adaptation of RL. By strategically injecting partial ground-truth hints, the goal is to steer the policy towards promising solution spaces while still allowing the model to generate its own reasoning, thereby preserving on-policy characteristics and mitigating distribution shift issues associated with pure SFT.

- **Improve Model Initialization and Reduce Distribution Mismatch:** A secondary objective is to provide a stronger and better-aligned initialization for RL training. Standard SFT datasets for reasoning tasks often contain verbose traces that do not match the constrained generation lengths used during RL or evaluation, leading to distribution mismatch. The authors aim to address this by systematically rewriting and condensing reasoning traces.

- **Ensure Training Stability:** Introducing ground-truth hints can potentially destabilize RL training if not handled carefully, as these hints represent off-policy information. Therefore, another objective is to develop mechanisms, such as entropy-based filtering, to maintain stability when incorporating such guidance.

In essence, the paper's primary motivation is to innovate RL for dLLMs by exploiting their architectural strengths, specifically inpainting, to make the learning process more robust, efficient, and capable of solving complex tasks where sparse rewards pose a significant challenge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces **IGPO (Inpainting Guided Policy Optimization)**, a novel RL framework for masked dLLMs, along with complementary techniques to enhance training. The methodology can be broken down into three main components: IGPO itself, a mechanism for stable hint integration, and an improved SFT strategy.

#### 3.1. IGPO: Inpainting Guided Policy Optimization

The core of IGPO revolves around strategically injecting partial ground-truth reasoning traces during the online RL sampling process, specifically when the policy struggles to find correct solutions.

- **Zero-Advantage Dilemma Detection:** IGPO is an "elastic" method, meaning it is only triggered under specific conditions. When sampling a group of `G` responses for a given prompt `q` and detecting that *all* responses receive identical incorrect rewards (i.e., `r(o_i) = 0` for all `i`), the algorithm recognizes a zero-advantage scenario. In this situation, standard GRPO would yield zero gradients, halting learning.

- **Inpainting-Triggered Sampling:** To overcome the zero-advantage dilemma, IGPO generates an additional set of `G` responses (`~o_i`) through an inpainting process.
 - **Hint Construction:** Ground-truth reasoning traces (`y*`) associated with the problem are segmented into variable-length contiguous chunks. The length of each chunk is sampled from a uniform distribution (e.g., `U[s_min, s_max]`). Crucially, the final answer tokens are explicitly *excluded* from being chunked to prevent the model from merely predicting the answer without reasoning.
 - **Hint Injection:** For each inpainted response `~o_i`, a hint injection ratio `η_i` is randomly sampled from a range (e.g., `U[η_low, η_high]`). Based on this ratio, a corresponding number of chunks (e.g., `⌊η * N⌋`) are randomly selected from `y*`. These selected chunks' tokens are then "fixed" as hints at their corresponding positions within the masked completion sequence.
 - **Bidirectional Denoising:** The masked dLLM then performs its iterative bidirectional denoising process on the input (prompt + partially masked completion with fixed hints). The fixed hint tokens remain unchanged throughout denoising, guiding the generation.

- **Augmented RL Sampling Group:** After generating the additional `G` inpainted responses, only those that lead to *correct* solutions (`r(~o_i) = 1`) are considered for replacement. IGPO replaces `K = min(|{`~o_i : r(~o_i) = 1}|, ⌊λG⌋)` of the original incorrect responses with these verified correct inpainted responses. The `λ` parameter (replacement fraction) ensures that not all original incorrect responses are replaced, maintaining some degree of original policy rollout and enabling non-zero advantage variance in the augmented group.

- **Policy Optimization with DiffuGRPO:** The policy optimization objective `L_IGPO(θ)` modifies the standard GRPO formulation to incorporate this augmented sampling procedure. IGPO leverages the mean-field approximation methods from DiffuGRPO (Zhao et al., 2025) to efficiently estimate token-level importance ratios and KL regularization terms for dLLMs, which otherwise lack a simple chain-rule factorization for likelihoods. The prompt tokens are not randomly masked during log-probability estimation to reduce overfitting during the small number of policy iterations.

#### 3.2. Entropy-based Gradient Filtering for Hint Tokens

A potential issue with injecting ground-truth tokens is that they are "off-policy," originating from a different distribution than the current policy `π_θ`. This can cause training instability, especially if the model is forced to update strongly held beliefs at low-entropy positions.

- **Solution:** For hint token positions (those fixed with ground-truth chunks), the policy gradient updates are restricted. Gradients are applied only to the top `τ` percentile of hint token positions that exhibit the *highest entropy*. High entropy indicates model uncertainty, making these positions more receptive to external guidance and less prone to generating large, destabilizing gradients.

#### 3.3. Length-Aligned SFT via Concise Reasoning Trace Rewriting

To provide a robust initial checkpoint for RL and address distribution mismatch, the authors propose an improved supervised fine-tuning (SFT) strategy.

- **Problem:** Traditional reasoning SFT datasets (e.g., OpenR1) often contain verbose reasoning traces that can exceed typical dLLM context lengths (e.g., LLaDA's 4096 tokens) or are significantly longer than the generation lengths used in RL sampling (256 tokens) and evaluation (512 tokens). This leads to truncation, distribution shift, and inefficient training.
- **Solution:** The authors employ a powerful large language model, LLaMA-4-Maverick (Meta, 2025), to systematically rewrite these verbose reasoning traces. The rewriting process aims to:
 - Eliminate redundant reflections and excessive verbiage.
 - Condense multi-sentence elaborations into concise, mathematically rigorous statements.
 - Preserve essential logical flow and reasoning steps.
 - Constrain the length of rewritten traces to a more manageable range (e.g., under 1500 tokens), better aligning with the downstream RL and evaluation phases.
- **SFT with Rewritten Traces:** LLaDA is then fine-tuned exclusively on these "Length-Aligned" rewritten traces. This strategy is designed to provide a stronger, more efficient, and context-consistent initialization for the subsequent RL phase.

**Experimental Setup:** The methodology is evaluated on the LLaDA-8B-Instruct model. SFT is performed on the OpenR1-Math-220K dataset with rewritten traces. RL (IGPO) is then applied using the MetaMathQA dataset. Performance is measured on GSM8K, MATH500, and AMC mathematical benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates substantial improvements in mathematical reasoning performance for full-attention masked dLLMs through the proposed IGPO framework and complementary techniques.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance for Masked dLLMs:**
 - The complete training recipe (Length-Aligned SFT on rewritten traces followed by RL with IGPO) achieves new state-of-the-art results among full-attention masked dLLMs across all three mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
 - Specifically, the model achieves **+4.9% on GSM8K**, **+8.4% on Math500**, and **+9.9% on AMC** relative to the LLaDA-8B-Instruct baseline. This cumulative improvement is significant, pushing the average accuracy across benchmarks to 52.7%, a 7.7% absolute improvement over the baseline 45.0%.
 - The final model (LLaDA + SFT + IGPO) outperforms all baseline approaches, including the previous LLaDA-1.5 and other RL methods for full-attention dLLMs.

2. **Effectiveness of Length-Aligned SFT:**
 - The initial Length-Aligned SFT with rewritten traces alone yields substantial improvements: **+2.1% on GSM8K**, **+6.2% on MATH500**, and **+7.8% on AMC** compared to the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct.
 - This confirms that aligning SFT data length with RL/evaluation lengths and providing concise reasoning traces offers a significantly stronger initialization for subsequent RL. Figure 6 clearly shows that SFT on rewritten traces leads to higher performance and better RL stability.

3. **IGPO's Superiority and Training Stability:**
 - When applied on top of the Length-Aligned SFT model, IGPO further improves performance by **2.8% on GSM8K** (reaching 86.4%) and **2.2% on MATH500** (reaching 47.4%).
 - Even without the Length-Aligned SFT, IGPO applied directly to LLaDA-Instruct achieves better performance than other RL methods (UniGRPO, DiffuGRPO) and LLaDA-1.5, demonstrating its inherent strength.
 - IGPO consistently exhibits more stable training dynamics compared to standard GRPO sampling, regardless of the initialization checkpoint (Figure 3). This stability is crucial for effective online RL.

4. **Mitigation of the Zero-Advantage Dilemma:**
 - IGPO effectively reduces the prevalence of "all-wrong groups" by approximately **60%** (as shown in Figure 1(b)). This reduction is critical because it restores non-degenerate gradient signals, allowing for meaningful policy updates and accelerating convergence that would otherwise be stalled.

5. **Benefits of Partial vs. Full Hint Injection:**
 - A key ablation study (Figure 4) reveals that **partial hint injection (η ~ U[0.2, 0.6]) consistently outperforms full ground-truth hint injection (η = 1.0)**.
 - This finding supports the hypothesis that allowing the model to generate a significant portion of its reasoning ("self-generated reasoning") while being guided by partial hints provides a better learning signal. It strikes a balance between strong guidance and maintaining an "on-policy" learning experience, bridging SFT and online RL more effectively.

6. **Efficacy of Entropy-based Gradient Filtering:**
 - Experiments on entropy clipping thresholds (Figure 5) confirm that learning only from a subset of hint token positions with the highest entropy (e.g., top 20%, τ = 0.2) achieves the best performance and most stable training dynamics.
 - Learning from all hint token positions (τ = 1.0) leads to more unstable training and performance fluctuations, validating the necessity of entropy-based filtering to mitigate distribution mismatch and instability arising from off-policy ground-truth hints.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that by thoughtfully integrating dLLMs' inpainting capabilities into an RL framework, combined with data alignment and stability mechanisms, significant breakthroughs in mathematical reasoning performance and training efficiency can be achieved.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Inpainting-Guided Policy Optimization for Diffusion Large Language Models" carries significant implications for the field of large language models, reinforcement learning, and the future development of generative AI.

1. **Novel Paradigm for RL in Diffusion LLMs:** This paper represents a seminal contribution by being the *first to systematically leverage the unique inpainting capabilities of diffusion LLMs for reinforcement learning*. Previously, RL approaches for dLLMs largely adapted methods from autoregressive models. IGPO demonstrates that dLLM-specific architectural properties can be a powerful tool for overcoming core RL challenges, opening up a novel research direction.

2. **Addressing Core RL Challenges (Exploration & Zero-Advantage):** The fundamental challenge of exploration in sparse-reward environments is a persistent bottleneck in LLM alignment. IGPO directly tackles this by providing targeted, partial guidance through inpainting. Its ability to mitigate the "zero-advantage dilemma" in group-based policy optimization methods like GRPO is particularly impactful, as this dilemma often leads to stalled learning and wasted computational resources in practical applications. By restoring meaningful gradient signals, IGPO makes online RL more robust and efficient.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Stability:** The combination of elastic inpainting-guided sampling, entropy-based gradient filtering, and Length-Aligned SFT contributes to a more efficient and stable RL training process for dLLMs. This efficiency translates to faster convergence and better utilization of computational resources, which is crucial given the high costs associated with training large models. The improved stability means less susceptibility to performance fluctuations and easier hyperparameter tuning.

4. **Advancing Mathematical Reasoning Capabilities:** The significant, state-of-the-art performance gains across challenging mathematical benchmarks (GSM8K, Math500, AMC) demonstrate the practical utility and effectiveness of IGPO for complex reasoning tasks. This indicates that dLLMs, with the right training paradigms, can become highly competitive, or even superior in certain aspects, to autoregressive models for such applications.

5. **Broader Implications for dLLM Research:** This work strengthens the argument for diffusion models as a powerful and flexible architecture for LLMs. It showcases that their unique bidirectional nature and inpainting ability are not merely curiosities but can be strategically leveraged for advanced training and improved performance, potentially positioning dLLMs as a more versatile alternative to AR models in various generative tasks. It also encourages further exploration of other dLLM-specific features for training and application.

6. **Methodological Contributions:** Beyond the overall framework, the paper introduces concrete methodological innovations that are valuable to the community:
 - **Elastic, hint-triggered sampling:** A smart way to apply guidance only when needed, maintaining policy integrity otherwise.
 - **Partial vs. Full Hinting analysis:** Providing empirical evidence for the benefits of self-generated reasoning guided by partial hints, which bridges the gap between SFT and RL effectively.
 - **Entropy-based gradient filtering:** A practical technique for stabilizing learning when integrating off-policy information into policy gradients, applicable beyond this specific context.
 - **Length-Aligned SFT through trace rewriting:** A valuable data preprocessing and SFT strategy for improving initial model quality and reducing distribution shift, relevant for any LLM fine-tuning scenario with length constraints.

In conclusion, IGPO offers a sophisticated and effective solution to critical challenges in dLLM training, providing a robust framework that harnesses the unique architectural advantages of diffusion models. Its significance lies in both its immediate impact on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and its broader implications for guiding future research in efficient and powerful reinforcement learning for next-generation generative AI architectures.
