# SceneAlign: Aligning Multimodal Reasoning to Scene Graphs in Complex Visual Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces SceneAlign, a framework designed to enhance multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by aligning their reasoning processes more closely with the visual structure of complex scenes. It employs scene graphs to systematically generate structured counterfactuals for Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), improving both answer accuracy and visually grounded reasoning across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently exhibit 'reasoning unfaithfulness,' where generated rationales decouple from actual visual evidence despite potentially correct final answers.
- This unfaithfulness manifests as object hallucination, relation mis-grounding, skipped reasoning steps, or over-specified details, hindering trustworthiness.
- Existing preference-based alignment methods are often 'structure-agnostic,' allowing MLLMs to rely on strong language priors rather than addressing true visual grounding errors directly.

## Method

- SceneAlign leverages detailed ==scene graphs== to create 'structure-consistent' positive chains of thought, anchoring MLLM reasoning to explicit visual structures.
- It constructs =='hard negative rationales'== by applying four controlled perturbation strategies (swap, replace, shorten, overthink) to critical elements within the scene graphs, creating linguistically plausible but visually inaccurate counterfactuals.
- A two-step filtering process (scene-graph filtering and diversity-based sampling) selects informative hard negative examples, which are then used in a ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)== pipeline to fine-tune MLLMs.

## Results

- SceneAlign consistently outperforms base models and SFT baselines across **five** MLLMs and **seven** diverse visual reasoning benchmarks, improving both answer accuracy and reasoning faithfulness.
- The approach yields the most pronounced improvements on reasoning-intensive tasks, showing gains up to **+23.97%** on HallusionBench and **+3-5%** on EMMA benchmarks for specific models.
- Ablation studies confirm that all four perturbation operators are critical for performance, with relation perturbations yielding the largest gains, and that optimizing negative sampling strategies is essential for effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating hard negative examples through controlled structural perturbations of scene graphs is a highly effective method for explicitly teaching MLLMs to identify and avoid visual grounding errors.
> - Different perturbation types (swapping roles, replacing entities, shortening details, overthinking) target distinct failure modes in multimodal reasoning, all contributing to overall performance gains.
> - Careful selection of 'medium-difficulty' negative rationales, balanced by scene-graph similarity and diversity, provides the most potent contrastive supervision for DPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): their struggle with faithful and visually grounded reasoning in complex visual scenes. While MLLMs have achieved impressive performance in tasks like visual question answering and captioning, they frequently exhibit "reasoning unfaithfulness." This phenomenon manifests as hallucinated entities, mis-grounded relations, skipped reasoning steps, or over-specified reasoning, where the generated rationales decouple from the actual visual evidence, even if the final answer appears correct.

Existing approaches to mitigate these issues primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Preference-based alignment of reasoning chains (e.g., RLHF, DPO):** These methods often generate positive and negative rationale pairs by perturbing textual tokens or final answers. While effective for encouraging coherent reasoning, they are largely "structure-agnostic." They allow models to exploit language priors, failing to directly address visual grounding errors. For instance, replacing a word in a rationale or using an incorrect answer as a basis for a negative rationale doesn't guarantee that the model learns to identify *visual* inconsistencies.
2. **Scene graph integration for multimodal reasoning:** Recent work has explored using scene graphs as structured visual information to enhance MLLMs. These methods typically employ scene graphs for zero-shot prompting, extracting compositional knowledge, or as part of knowledge graphs to improve coherence. However, most of these efforts integrate scene graphs during *inference* or for *initial training*, without deeply incorporating them into the *alignment process itself* to explicitly teach models about correct visual grounding versus common grounding failures.

SceneAlign distinguishes itself by bridging these two areas. It leverages scene graphs not just as an input for reasoning, but as a mechanism for *generating structured, visually-grounded negative supervision* during preference optimization. Instead of perturbing text or answers, SceneAlign perturbs scene graph elements (objects, attributes, relations) to create "hard negative rationales" that are linguistically plausible but visually inaccurate. This novel approach directly tackles the "reasoning unfaithfulness" problem by forcing the model to learn fine-grained, structure-faithful grounding, making it a significant advancement in robust and trustworthy multimodal reasoning. It pushes the boundary beyond mere textual coherence towards genuine visual fidelity in MLLM rationales.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for SceneAlign stems from the observed limitations of current MLLMs in performing visually grounded reasoning, especially within complex scenes. The authors highlight that MLLMs frequently generate rationales that are "unfaithful" to the visual evidence, even when the final answers are correct. This unfaithfulness is a significant concern for the trustworthiness and reliability of MLLMs, manifesting in several critical ways:
- **Object hallucination:** Inventing objects not present in the image.
- **Relation mis-grounding:** Incorrectly linking objects or confusing subject-object roles (e.g., "man riding dog" instead of "dog riding man" if depicted).
- **Incomplete grounding / Skipped steps:** Omitting crucial visual evidence necessary for a complete reasoning chain.
- **Over-specification:** Introducing irrelevant or tangential visual details into the reasoning.

The primary objective of SceneAlign is to address this "reasoning unfaithfulness" by explicitly aligning MLLMs' reasoning processes with the underlying visual structure of a scene. The authors argue that existing preference-based methods are insufficient because they allow models to bypass true visual grounding by relying on strong language priors.

To overcome this, SceneAlign aims to:
1. **Develop a framework for controllable structural interventions:** This involves using scene graphs as a structured representation of visual information to systematically generate counterfactual scenarios.
2. **Construct hard negative rationales:** These negatives should be linguistically plausible to challenge the model's language priors but factually inconsistent with the actual visual scene, specifically targeting common grounding failures.
3. **Employ Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** By training MLLMs with these contrastive pairs (visually faithful vs. structurally perturbed rationales), SceneAlign intends to steer models towards fine-grained, structure-faithful reasoning.
4. **Improve both answer accuracy and reasoning faithfulness:** The ultimate goal is to enhance the overall performance of MLLMs on complex visual reasoning tasks, ensuring that not only the answers are correct but also the underlying logical steps are truly grounded in visual evidence.

In essence, the motivation is to make MLLMs not just "fluent" but also "truthful" in their visual explanations, fostering a deeper understanding and adherence to visual facts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SceneAlign proposes a comprehensive framework that integrates scene graphs into a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) pipeline to enhance the faithfulness of multimodal reasoning. The methodology comprises four key stages:

#### 3.1. Positive Reasoning Generation

The process begins by establishing a "ground truth" for faithful reasoning. For each image-question pair, a detailed scene graph (SG) is extracted using GPT-4o, capturing entities, attributes, and relational triples (e.g., (man, look at, motorcycle)). This scene graph acts as an explicit structural representation of the visual scene. Subsequently, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) is prompted with the image, question, and the generated scene graph to produce a "structure-consistent chain of thought (CoT)." This positive CoT is explicitly anchored to elements within the scene graph, encouraging the MLLM to reference concrete visual evidence at each reasoning step.

#### 3.2. Construction of Negative Scene Graphs

This is the core innovative component of SceneAlign. To teach the MLLM what *incorrect* grounding looks like, the framework systematically constructs structured counterfactuals.
First, a "CoT-grounded subgraph" ($SG^c_q$) is identified, consisting only of the scene graph elements explicitly referenced in the positive CoT. This ensures that perturbations are reasoning-critical.
Then, four controlled perturbation strategies are applied to elements within $SG^c_q$, while the remaining, unreferenced parts of the original scene graph ($SG^+_q \setminus SG^c_q$) are kept unchanged. This ensures relevance while altering grounding. The four strategies are:
- **Swap:** Exchanges the subject and object of a relation (e.g., (man, look at, motorcycle) becomes (motorcycle, look at, man)). This mimics mis-grounding errors where roles are confused.
- **Replace:** Substitutes a key element (entity or attribute) with a candidate from the "residual pool" ($P_{res}$), which contains non-critical elements from the *same* scene graph. This simulates subtle grounding errors or hallucination-like substitutions.
- **Shorten:** Removes a key element (entity, attribute, or relation) from the subgraph. This mimics omitted reasoning steps or incomplete grounding.
- **Overthink:** Augments the subgraph by adding a redundant or spurious element from $P_{res}$. This simulates over-specification, where irrelevant details distract from core reasoning.
Each perturbation creates a negative scene graph ($SG^-_{q,i}$), which is then used to generate a corresponding negative CoT by prompting the MLLM (with the perturbed scene graph and question, but without the image, to force reliance on the *graph*).

#### 3.3. Selection of Negative Reasoning Chains

Not all generated negative CoTs are equally informative. SceneAlign employs a two-step filtering process to select "hard negatives" that are challenging but not trivial or irrelevant:
- **Scene-Graph Filtering:** The Jaccard overlap is computed between each negative scene graph and the original positive scene graph. Negatives with overlap values too high (near-duplicates) or too low (irrelevant outliers) are discarded. This ensures that the selected negatives are semantically close enough to be plausible but structurally distinct enough to highlight specific errors.
- **Diversity-based Sampling:** From the filtered set, a fixed number (e.g., 3) of diverse negative CoTs are selected using a max-min criterion on their embeddings. This maximizes the dissimilarity between chosen negative examples, preventing overfitting to narrow error modes and ensuring a rich set of counterfactuals.

#### 3.4. Scene-Graph Preference Optimization

Finally, the MLLM is fine-tuned using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is applied over pairs consisting of the original image-question-positive scene graph context, the positive CoT (chosen response), and the selected diverse negative CoTs (rejected responses). The DPO objective encourages the MLLM to assign higher likelihood to rationales consistent with the true scene structure and lower likelihood to those generated from structurally inconsistent (perturbed) scene graphs. This explicit alignment process enhances the model's ability to produce visually grounded and faithful reasoning chains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SceneAlign demonstrates consistent and significant improvements across a diverse suite of visual reasoning benchmarks, confirming its effectiveness in enhancing both answer accuracy and reasoning faithfulness in MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **Consistent Gains:** Across all five tested MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B, and LLaVA-Next-8B) and seven benchmarks, SceneAlign consistently outperforms both the base (pretrained) models and SFT (supervised fine-tuning with only positive CoTs) baselines. This highlights that structural alignment provides benefits beyond simple positive example training.
- **Strongest on Reasoning-Intensive Tasks:** The most pronounced improvements are observed on benchmarks requiring complex, step-wise compositional reasoning and multi-entity grounding, such as EMMA (+3-5%), HallusionBench (up to +23.97% on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and +7.70% on Qwen3-VL-4B), and MMMU-Reasoning. This indicates that SceneAlign effectively addresses the fine-grained, structure-level grounding errors that these tasks expose.
- **Scalability and Generality:** The gains are observed across models of varying scales and architectures, from lightweight Qwen2.5-VL-3B to larger InternVL3-8B and LLaVA-Next-8B, suggesting that SceneAlign offers orthogonal improvements to model capacity and is a generalizable framework.
- **Outperforming Prior Methods:** When compared against prior CoT-based preference construction methods like LLaVA-Reasoner and AoT (using LLaVA-Next-8B backbone), SceneAlign surpasses them across nearly all metrics, with significant margins on HallusionBench and EMMA. This validates the superior quality of grounding-aware contrastive pairs over text-only perturbations.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies Insights

- **Effect of Perturbation Operators:** All four perturbation strategies (swap, replace, shorten, overthink) contribute to the overall performance. Removing any single operator leads to a reduction in performance, particularly on benchmarks sensitive to the specific error type that operator mimics. This confirms that each perturbation addresses a distinct and valuable failure mode, and their combination yields the most balanced and comprehensive improvements.
- **Effect of Scene Graph Elements:** Perturbing relations yields the largest performance gains, underscoring the critical importance of correctly binding subjects and objects for grounded reasoning. Entity-level perturbations also show substantial benefits, while attribute grounding, though smaller, still provides consistent complementary cues.
- **Effect of Negative Sampling Strategies:** A comparison between random negatives, diversity-only sampling, and the full SceneAlign method (scene-graph filtering + diversity sampling) reveals that the full method achieves the highest performance. This highlights the crucial role of carefully selecting "medium-difficulty" hard negatives—neither too similar nor too irrelevant—to provide effective contrastive supervision.
- **Effect of Overlap Thresholds and Number of Negatives:** There is an optimal range for Jaccard overlap thresholds (γℓ = 0.3, γu = 0.7) that balances data coverage and informativeness, showing an inverted U-shape performance curve. Similarly, using three negatives per instance provides the best trade-off between structural supervision and computational efficiency, with diminishing returns for more negatives.

#### 4.3. Case Studies

A case study effectively illustrates how SceneAlign generates structure-consistent positive CoTs and how specific perturbations (swap, replace, shorten, overthink) lead to distinct, yet plausible, failure modes. This qualitative analysis visually reinforces the quantitative findings, demonstrating how the method exposes reasoning drift that purely text-based perturbations miss.

In summary, SceneAlign successfully tackles the challenge of reasoning unfaithfulness by providing a novel, controlled, and visually-grounded preference alignment mechanism. The consistent gains across models and benchmarks, supported by detailed ablations, validate its design principles and superior performance compared to existing techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SceneAlign represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of trustworthy and robust multimodal large language models, with several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhancing Trustworthiness and Reliability of MLLMs:** By directly addressing reasoning unfaithfulness and hallucination, SceneAlign makes MLLMs' explanations and decisions more transparent and verifiable against visual evidence. This is crucial for applications where errors in reasoning, even with correct answers, can have serious consequences, such as in medical imaging analysis, autonomous driving, or complex scientific inquiry. The ability to generate "structure-faithful" rationales builds greater confidence in MLLM outputs.

2. **Principled Generation of Hard Negatives:** The framework introduces a novel and highly effective method for generating hard negative examples for preference optimization. Unlike prior textual or answer-based perturbations that can be superficial, SceneAlign's scene-graph-guided structural interventions create counterfactuals that are linguistically plausible but visually inaccurate in a controlled, interpretable manner. This provides a richer and more targeted learning signal for MLLMs, pushing them to learn deeper visual grounding rather than relying on language priors. This approach could inspire similar structured perturbation techniques in other domains.

3. **Improved Interpretability and Debuggability:** By explicitly exposing common grounding failure modes (e.g., subject-object swaps, entity hallucinations, omitted steps), SceneAlign offers a clearer understanding of *why* an MLLM might fail. The structured nature of the negatives allows for localization and categorization of errors, which is invaluable for debugging and iteratively improving MLLM architectures and training methodologies.

4. **Broader Applicability to Multimodal Reasoning:** The consistent performance gains across diverse MLLM architectures and a wide range of benchmarks (from perception to complex reasoning) suggest that SceneAlign is a generalizable framework. Its principles could be adapted to other multimodal tasks requiring fine-grained visual-language alignment, such as image manipulation with natural language, visual storytelling, or even more complex embodied AI scenarios.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Dynamic and Temporal Scene Graphs:** The authors acknowledge the limitation to single-image reasoning. Extending SceneAlign to multi-image sequences or video could involve dynamic scene graphs to capture temporal and cross-view consistency challenges.
 - **Beyond GPT-based Scene Graph Generation:** While GPT-4o is effective, exploring alternative or more efficient methods for scene graph generation, especially for large-scale datasets, could be beneficial.
 - **Human-Centered Evaluation:** While automatic metrics show clear improvements, incorporating more comprehensive human-centered evaluation protocols could provide deeper insights into the nuanced aspects of grounding fidelity in open-ended multimodal reasoning.
 - **Integration with Other Alignment Techniques:** SceneAlign's structural interventions could be combined with other preference alignment methods or reward modeling techniques to further refine MLLM behaviors.

In conclusion, SceneAlign represents a significant step forward in making MLLMs more intelligent by making them more *truthful* to the visual world. By providing a principled way to teach models to distinguish between faithful and unfaithful visual reasoning, it contributes substantially to the development of more reliable, interpretable, and ultimately more capable multimodal AI systems.
