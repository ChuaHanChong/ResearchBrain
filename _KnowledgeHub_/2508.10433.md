# We-Math 2.0: A Versatile MathBook System for Incentivizing Visual Mathematical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> WE-MATH 2.0 introduces a comprehensive framework to enhance multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) mathematical reasoning, featuring a structured knowledge system, model-centric data, and progressive reinforcement learning. The system, leveraging a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, achieved over 5% performance improvement on multiple benchmarks with only 9.8K training samples, demonstrating improved generalization and efficiency.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex mathematical reasoning, especially in visual contexts, due to issues with generalization.
- Existing MLLM training approaches lack a comprehensive knowledge system, model-centric difficulty modeling, and sufficient emphasis on reasoning generalization, often leading to rote memorization.
- Current datasets for mathematical reasoning exhibit uneven performance across subfields and do not effectively align problem difficulty with MLLMs' learning patterns.

## Method

- The ==MathBook Knowledge System==, a five-level hierarchical framework with 491 knowledge points and 1,819 principles, was developed to provide fine-grained mathematical supervision.
- Multi-dimensional datasets (MathBook-Standard and MathBook-Pro) were created using GeoGebra-rendered diagrams and a 3D difficulty modeling approach (step, visual, and contextual complexity) to facilitate structured, progressive learning.
- MathBook-RL, a two-stage progressive alignment reinforcement learning framework, was introduced, combining cold-start fine-tuning with dynamic scheduling RL and incremental learning mechanisms for robust generalization.
- MathBookEval, a comprehensive benchmark, was designed to rigorously evaluate MLLMs' reasoning depth and breadth across diverse knowledge points and reasoning steps.

## Results

- MathBook-**7B** consistently improved MLLM performance by over **5%** across four widely used mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, We-Math, MathVerse) compared to strong baselines.
- The system demonstrated improved generalization on the We-Math benchmark and achieved strong performance using only **9.8K** training samples, highlighting its efficiency and the quality of its structured data.
- MathBookEval revealed that MLLM performance negatively correlates with reasoning depth (number of knowledge points) and that models consistently struggle with geometry, while ablation studies confirmed the significant contributions of both pre-aligned and dynamic scheduling RL stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured, hierarchical mathematical knowledge system provides foundational, interpretable supervision that enables MLLMs to develop genuine understanding and balanced performance across mathematical domains.
> - Model-centric difficulty modeling, coupled with high-precision GeoGebra diagrams and variant construction, allows for effective progressive training and enhances MLLMs' generalization capabilities to unseen problems.
> - A two-stage reinforcement learning paradigm, incorporating knowledge-guided SFT and dynamic curriculum scheduling, effectively aligns MLLMs toward robust, knowledge-oriented chain-of-thought reasoning from foundational understanding to advanced problem-solving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen rapid advancements, demonstrating impressive capabilities across a wide spectrum of tasks, from natural language understanding to visual question answering and object detection. However, the paper identifies a critical challenge: MLLMs still "struggle with complex mathematical reasoning," especially in visual contexts where generalization remains a fundamental hurdle.

Current research efforts to improve mathematical reasoning in MLLMs primarily converge on three directions:
1. **Dataset Construction:** Creating richer and more diverse datasets to train models.
2. **Preference Optimization:** Refining model outputs through human or AI feedback to guide step-by-step reasoning.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Employing RL techniques, often with curriculum-based training, to enhance performance on complex reasoning tasks.

Despite progress in these areas, the authors pinpoint several fundamental limitations in existing approaches:
- **Lack of a Comprehensive Knowledge System:** Current MLLMs exhibit uneven performance across mathematical subfields due to datasets lacking systematic knowledge coverage and domain diversity.
- **Lack of Model-Centric Difficulty Modeling:** Existing datasets often categorize difficulty based on human learning stages, which do not necessarily align with MLLMs' learning patterns, leading to ineffective progressive training.
- **Lack of Emphasis on Reasoning Generalization:** Models tend to memorize problems rather than acquiring generalizable reasoning skills, performing poorly on subproblems or similar tasks.

WE-MATH 2.0 directly addresses these critical gaps by introducing a unified system that integrates a **structured mathematical knowledge system, model-centric data space modeling, and a reinforcement learning-based training paradigm**. This work aims to shift the paradigm from mere data aggregation or superficial preference tuning to a more principled, knowledge-driven, and progressively aligned training approach. By manually constructing high-quality, GeoGebra-rendered data samples with fine-grained knowledge annotations and difficulty controls, WE-MATH 2.0 stands out from existing datasets like Geometry3K, MathV360K, We-Math (the previous version), and GeoSense, which often have limited knowledge coverage or lack principle-level annotations and progressive difficulty levels (as highlighted in Table 1). The introduction of a dedicated benchmark, MathBookEval, also contributes to a more rigorous and comprehensive evaluation of MLLM reasoning capabilities, addressing the limitations of existing benchmarks like MathVista and MathVision in evaluating multi-level reasoning depth and full knowledge coverage.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of WE-MATH 2.0 is to **comprehensively enhance the mathematical reasoning abilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**, particularly in visual contexts. This is driven by the identified shortcomings of current MLLMs, which, despite their general impressive capabilities, struggle with complex mathematical tasks that require deep conceptual understanding, multi-step reasoning, and robust generalization.

The specific motivations and objectives that underpin the WE-MATH 2.0 framework are fourfold:

1. **To establish a comprehensive and systematic mathematical knowledge system:** Recognizing that existing datasets lack broad and structured coverage of mathematical knowledge, a primary objective is to build a hierarchical system that systematically organizes mathematical concepts. This "MathBook Knowledge System" aims to provide fine-grained supervision, enabling MLLMs to develop a more balanced and thorough understanding across different mathematical domains, from primary to university levels.
2. **To develop datasets with model-centric difficulty modeling for structured and progressive learning:** The authors observe that human-defined difficulty levels do not always align with how MLLMs learn. Therefore, a key motivation is to create datasets ("MathBook-Standard" and "MathBook-Pro") that incorporate a principled, multi-dimensional framework for defining problem difficulty. This aims to facilitate structured, progressive learning, allowing models to master concepts incrementally and improve reasoning across varying complexity levels. The goal is to move beyond mere problem memorization towards genuine conceptual understanding.
3. **To propose an advanced reinforcement learning (RL) framework that leverages knowledge-driven design and dynamic data scheduling:** To effectively utilize the structured knowledge system and progressive datasets, the paper aims to introduce "MathBook-RL." This framework is designed to align MLLMs with knowledge-oriented chain-of-thought reasoning from a "cold start" and then progressively refine their reasoning through a curriculum-based RL paradigm with dynamic data scheduling. The motivation is to improve the generalization and robustness of MLLMs in mathematical problem-solving.
4. **To introduce a comprehensive and interpretable benchmark for evaluating multi-modal mathematical reasoning:** To rigorously assess the advancements made by their proposed system and to provide a valuable tool for the broader research community, the authors aim to develop "MathBookEval." This benchmark is designed to cover a vast range of knowledge points and diverse reasoning step distributions, offering a systematic means to evaluate MLLMs' reasoning depth and breadth, thereby promoting transparency and interpretability in model development.

In essence, WE-MATH 2.0 is motivated by the need for a more principled and systematic approach to teaching MLLMs mathematical reasoning, moving beyond ad-hoc data collection and superficial training methods to foster genuine understanding and generalizable problem-solving skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WE-MATH 2.0 is a unified system composed of three core methodological pillars: a structured mathematical knowledge system, multi-dimensional data construction, and a reinforcement learning-based training paradigm, complemented by a new evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. MathBook Knowledge System

The foundation of WE-MATH 2.0 is the **MathBook Knowledge System**, a meticulously constructed five-level hierarchical framework.
- **Structure:** It encompasses 491 distinct knowledge points and 1,819 fundamental principles, spanning mathematics from primary to university levels, organized under a "Definition–Theorem–Application" paradigm.
- **Construction:** The system is built through a human-AI collaborative process. Human experts establish an initial structure (K_human) based on authoritative sources (textbooks, Wikipedia, national curriculum). Concurrently, GPT-4o processes 30K problems from existing datasets to assign multi-level topic tags, which are then hierarchically clustered to generate an AI-assisted structure (K_auto). The final knowledge point set (K) is an expert-guided integration and refinement of both K_human and K_auto.
- **Fine-Grained Principle Annotation:** For each knowledge point, GPT-4o annotates step-level knowledge points within Chain-of-Thought (CoT) solutions of problems, mapping each step to corresponding K. It also summarizes theorems and principles used across these solution paths, resulting in a detailed mapping (k_i → P_i). These AI-extracted principles are cross-checked and refined by human experts to ensure completeness and accuracy.

#### 3.2. Multi-Dimensional Data Construction

This module focuses on creating two versatile datasets: MathBook-Standard and MathBook-Pro.
- **MathBook-Standard: Seed and Variant Problem Construction**
 - **Seed Problem Construction:** Problems are constructed following strict guidelines: 1) Diagrams are rendered with **GeoGebra** for precision, avoiding superficial cues. 2) Problems focus on mathematical essence. 3) Each problem strictly corresponds to its designated principle set. A "model-assisted, expert-led" workflow is used, where an LLM generates draft problems (question, answer, XML script), and GeoGebra automates diagram generation. Human experts then meticulously revise or rework almost all drafts to ensure alignment with mathematical principles.
 - **Variant Problem Expansion:** To enhance diversity and generalization, two types of variants are created for each seed problem:
 - **One-Problem-Multi-Image Variants (D_ImgVar):** The problem statement and knowledge annotation are fixed, but multiple images are generated by varying GeoGebra parameters (e.g., acute/obtuse/right triangles for the same problem), providing visual diversity.
 - **One-Image-Multi-Problem Variants (D_QstVar):** A single high-quality GeoGebra image is reused to construct multiple new problems targeting different knowledge points and principles, curated by experts with LLM assistance, enhancing semantic diversity.

- **MathBook-Pro: Three-Dimensional Difficulty Modeling**
 - To systematically model difficulty from a model-centric perspective, a three-dimensional difficulty space is defined for each seed problem:
 - **Step Complexity (ϕ_s):** Quantified by the number of involved knowledge points. Variants are constructed to require progressively more knowledge points (e.g., 6+).
 - **Visual Complexity (ϕ_v):** Increased by adding auxiliary elements (lines, geometric configurations) to the GeoGebra diagram while preserving the core structure.
 - **Contextual Complexity (ϕ_c):** Varies the textual context from concise mathematical descriptions to complex linguistic scenarios or real-world problems.
 - Each seed problem is expanded into **seven progressive variants** by varying a single dimension at a time and then composing changes across multiple dimensions, allowing for structured, progressive learning paths.

#### 3.3. MathBook-RL: Progressive Alignment Reinforcement Learning

This two-stage RL framework guides MLLMs from foundational understanding to advanced reasoning.
- **Cold-Start Fine-tuning (SFT):** The initial stage involves supervised fine-tuning using the MathBook-Standard dataset. Samples are rewritten by GPT-4o to include natural language explanations explicitly referencing relevant knowledge points. This aims to instill knowledge-oriented CoT reasoning and prevent rote memorization.
- **Progressive Alignment RL:** This curriculum-based RL paradigm is applied to MathBook-Pro.
 - **Pre-aligned RL:** Initial RL training on D_ImgVar (one-problem-multi-image variants) uses a **mean-based reward function** (r = (1/m) Σ r^(t)) across multiple variants of the same knowledge principle. This encourages consistent and robust performance by providing a more comprehensive critic, promoting analogical reasoning.
 - **Dynamic Scheduling RL:** Training progresses along increasing difficulty trajectories defined by MathBook-Pro's 3D difficulty space (e.g., x₀ → ϕ_s(x₀) → ϕ_s ◦ ϕ_v(x₀)). An **Incremental Learning Mechanism** is employed: if the model fails on a harder variant (ϕ(x)) after succeeding on an easier one (x), it is adaptively redirected to an incremental set (Δ(x, ϕ)) of auxiliary problems that isolate the new knowledge or modality introduced by ϕ.
 - *Knowledge Increment Scheduling:* For increased step complexity (ϕ_s), the model is redirected to problems targeting the new knowledge points.
 - *Modality Increment Scheduling:* For increased visual (ϕ_v) or contextual (ϕ_c) complexity, the model is guided through single-modality incremental problems.
 - The overall RL objective is optimized using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an extension of PPO, which estimates baselines from group scores.

#### 3.4. MathBookEval

A comprehensive benchmark introduced to systematically evaluate MLLM reasoning.
- **Design Principles:** Comprehensive knowledge coverage (491 KPs), multi-level reasoning depth (1-10 KPs per problem, categorized into Level 1, 2, 3), and rigorous annotation (expert step-by-step annotation with explicit knowledge-point mapping, independent cross-validation).
- **Data:** 1,000 fully annotated problems (600 collected from existing benchmarks, 400 newly curated).
- **Dimensions:** Problems are divided by Reasoning Dimension (1-3, 4-6, 7-10 steps) and Knowledge Dimension (4 domains, 13 subdomains).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Employs LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-4o) and reports overall accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the effectiveness of WE-MATH 2.0 in significantly enhancing the mathematical reasoning capabilities of MLLMs.

1. **Overall Superiority and Effectiveness of MathBook:** MathBook-7B, built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, consistently achieves over a 5% improvement across four widely used mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, We-Math, MathVerse). This demonstrates the overall effectiveness of the integrated knowledge system, data construction, and RL training.
2. **Improved Generalization:** On the We-Math benchmark, which specifically tests generalization by requiring models to solve both complex multi-step questions and their corresponding subproblems, MathBook-7B outperforms strong RL-based baselines. This highlights the success of the progressive alignment reinforcement learning in fostering knowledge generalization rather than mere problem memorization.
3. **Efficiency with Limited Data:** Remarkably, MathBook-7B achieves strong performance using only 9.8K training samples (1K for SFT + 9.8K for RL). The authors attribute this efficiency to the high-quality, structured mathematical knowledge system and data, which enable effective alignment and generalization even with a relatively small dataset compared to models trained on millions of samples.
4. **MathBookEval Insights on MLLM Capabilities:**
 - **Reasoning Depth Correlation:** MLLMs' performance negatively correlates with the number of required knowledge points. Accuracy steadily declines as reasoning steps increase, dropping below 50% for problems requiring 7-10 knowledge points. This confirms the multi-step reasoning challenge and validates knowledge point count as a robust difficulty indicator.
 - **Domain-Specific Challenges:** MLLMs perform well on algebra (accuracies above 50%) but consistently struggle with geometry, indicating persistent challenges in spatial reasoning.
 - **Scaling Benefits:** Within model families (InternVL2.5, Qwen2.5-VL), larger models generally yield more consistent improvements across all difficulty and knowledge dimensions, emphasizing the role of scale.
5. **Ablation Studies on Training Stages:**
 - **Contribution of RL Stages:** Both the pre-aligned RL and dynamic scheduling RL stages significantly contribute to performance gains over a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) only baseline (M4). Pre-aligned RL (M2 vs M4) shows notable improvements on MathVista and We-Math, underscoring the importance of knowledge learning.
 - **SFT's Enabling Role:** While SFT alone (M4) provides marginal gains over the backbone, it is crucial for unlocking the full potential of RL. The combination (M0) substantially boosts overall performance, suggesting SFT is critical for shifting the model's reasoning paradigm for effective RL optimization.
6. **Analysis of SFT Data Paradigm and Scale:**
 - **Natural Language CoT Advantage:** Natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in SFT leads to better RL performance compared to a structured, step-wise CoT format. This implies that natural prompts might cultivate more flexible reasoning, which translates to stronger visual mathematical reasoning.
 - **Minimal SFT Sufficiency:** Scaling up SFT data (from 1K to 10K) does not consistently lead to better performance. Minimal, well-curated SFT data appears sufficient to establish the necessary reasoning paradigm for effective subsequent RL, emphasizing quality over quantity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WE-MATH 2.0 represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal mathematical reasoning, with profound implications for AI research and education.

1. **Foundational Shift in MLLM Training for Reasoning:** The introduction of the **MathBook Knowledge System** is a critical contribution. By providing a five-level hierarchical framework with 491 knowledge points and 1,819 fundamental principles, the work establishes a principled and systematic approach to mathematical knowledge supervision. This moves beyond opaque, data-driven patterns to offer a structured, interpretable foundation for MLLMs, fostering genuine understanding rather than rote memorization. This could inspire similar knowledge-driven designs in other complex reasoning domains.
2. **High-Quality, Model-Centric Data Resources:** The development of **MathBook-Standard** and **MathBook-Pro** offers invaluable resources for the community. MathBook-Standard's dual expansion strategies ("multi-images per question" and "multi-questions per image") enrich visual and semantic diversity, while the GeoGebra-based diagram generation ensures unprecedented precision and mathematical rigor. More importantly, MathBook-Pro's **three-dimensional difficulty modeling** (step, visual, contextual complexity) provides a groundbreaking approach to structured, progressive learning, designed specifically to align with MLLMs' learning patterns. This enables robust and generalizable training, addressing a key limitation of existing datasets.
3. **Innovative Reinforcement Learning Paradigm:** **MathBook-RL** introduces a novel two-stage RL framework that effectively integrates knowledge-guided supervision and dynamic data scheduling. The cold-start fine-tuning instills foundational knowledge, while the progressive alignment RL, especially with its mean-based reward and incremental learning mechanisms (knowledge increment and modality increment scheduling), significantly boosts MLLMs' generalization and robustness. This approach offers a powerful blueprint for developing more adaptable and efficient RL curricula for complex, multi-modal reasoning tasks.
4. **Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** **MathBookEval** fills a crucial gap in evaluation methodologies. By covering all 491 knowledge points and diverse reasoning step distributions, it provides a rigorous and interpretable benchmark for thoroughly assessing MLLMs' mathematical capabilities. This fosters transparency in model development, allowing researchers to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses across different mathematical domains and reasoning depths.
5. **Bridging AI and Education:** The meticulously crafted datasets, particularly the GeoGebra files accompanying each problem, hold substantial **educational value**. They can serve as high-quality teaching materials, facilitating personalized learning paths, targeted exercises, and interactive learning experiences. This work demonstrates a clear pathway for AI research to directly enhance mathematics education and support the development of intelligent tutoring systems.
6. **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to releasing all curated data under the CC BY 4.0 license ensures **open access** for the research community. This fosters reproducibility, encourages further research, and accelerates progress in multimodal mathematical reasoning, making the sophisticated resources available to a wider audience.

In summary, WE-MATH 2.0 is not merely an incremental improvement; it proposes a holistic, knowledge-first approach to multimodal mathematical reasoning. By systematically addressing fundamental challenges in knowledge representation, data design, and training paradigms, it sets a new standard for developing MLLMs that can reason more like humans—with understanding, generalization, and adaptability across varying complexities and contexts.
