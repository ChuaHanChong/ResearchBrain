# We-Math 2.0: A Versatile MathBook System for Incentivizing Visual Mathematical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> WE-MATH 2.0 introduces a comprehensive framework to enhance multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) mathematical reasoning, featuring a structured knowledge system, model-centric data, and progressive reinforcement learning. The system, leveraging a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, achieved over 5% performance improvement on multiple benchmarks with only 9.8K training samples, demonstrating improved generalization and efficiency.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex mathematical reasoning, especially in visual contexts, due to issues with generalization.
- Existing MLLM training approaches lack a comprehensive knowledge system, model-centric difficulty modeling, and sufficient emphasis on reasoning generalization, often leading to rote memorization.
- Current datasets for mathematical reasoning exhibit uneven performance across subfields and do not effectively align problem difficulty with MLLMs' learning patterns.

## Method

- The ==MathBook Knowledge System==, a five-level hierarchical framework with 491 knowledge points and 1,819 principles, was developed to provide fine-grained mathematical supervision.
- Multi-dimensional datasets (MathBook-Standard and MathBook-Pro) were created using GeoGebra-rendered diagrams and a 3D difficulty modeling approach (step, visual, and contextual complexity) to facilitate structured, progressive learning.
- MathBook-RL, a two-stage progressive alignment reinforcement learning framework, was introduced, combining cold-start fine-tuning with dynamic scheduling RL and incremental learning mechanisms for robust generalization.
- MathBookEval, a comprehensive benchmark, was designed to rigorously evaluate MLLMs' reasoning depth and breadth across diverse knowledge points and reasoning steps.

## Results

- MathBook-**7B** consistently improved MLLM performance by over **5%** across four widely used mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, We-Math, MathVerse) compared to strong baselines.
- The system demonstrated improved generalization on the We-Math benchmark and achieved strong performance using only **9.8K** training samples, highlighting its efficiency and the quality of its structured data.
- MathBookEval revealed that MLLM performance negatively correlates with reasoning depth (number of knowledge points) and that models consistently struggle with geometry, while ablation studies confirmed the significant contributions of both pre-aligned and dynamic scheduling RL stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured, hierarchical mathematical knowledge system provides foundational, interpretable supervision that enables MLLMs to develop genuine understanding and balanced performance across mathematical domains.
> - Model-centric difficulty modeling, coupled with high-precision GeoGebra diagrams and variant construction, allows for effective progressive training and enhances MLLMs' generalization capabilities to unseen problems.
> - A two-stage reinforcement learning paradigm, incorporating knowledge-guided SFT and dynamic curriculum scheduling, effectively aligns MLLMs toward robust, knowledge-oriented chain-of-thought reasoning from foundational understanding to advanced problem-solving.
