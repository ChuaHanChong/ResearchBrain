# Improving the Reasoning of Multi-Image Grounding in MLLMs via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xiaohongshu Inc. and affiliated universities developed a two-stage training framework that combines Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-tuning (CoT-SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enhance multi-image grounding reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance, including a 9.04% improvement over the SFT baseline on MIG-Bench and outperforming GPT-4o on the BLINK multi-image understanding benchmark, demonstrating robust generalization.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex real-world tasks that require reasoning across multiple images and intricate multi-modal instructions.
- Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods for multi-image grounding often lead to pattern memorization rather than robust, generalizable reasoning, limiting performance on novel scenarios.
- Direct application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to MLLMs for complex multi-image queries faces instability, as base models lack the foundational capacity to handle such tasks.

## Method

- A two-stage training framework was developed, starting with a Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model.
- Stage 1: ==Cold-start CoT-SFT initialization==. A high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset was synthesized using Qwen2.5-VL-72B and filtered for accuracy, then used to fine-tune the base MLLM via LoRA.
- Stage 2: Rule-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==. ==Rejection sampling== was applied to curate informative training samples, and a combined accuracy and format reward function guided the learning process.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art results on multi-image grounding benchmarks, with a **9.04%** improvement over the SFT baseline on MIG-Bench (**58.83%** accuracy) and a **6.37%** gain on MC-Bench (**58.49%** accuracy).
- Demonstrated superior generalization, outperforming the 72B-scale Qwen2.5-VL-72B and even the closed-source GPT-4o model on the BLINK multi-image understanding benchmark (**61.18%** average accuracy).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the cold-start CoT-SFT initialization, the rejection sampling strategy for efficient RL training, and the optimized KL divergence weight in the GRPO objective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured two-stage approach, initiating with CoT-SFT, is essential for stabilizing and effectively applying RL to MLLMs for complex multi-image reasoning tasks.
> - Reinforcement Learning, specifically GRPO with targeted reward design and rejection sampling, can transition MLLMs from rote memorization to robust, generalizable reasoning capabilities in multimodal contexts.
> - Enhancing MLLM reasoning through RL in multi-image grounding tasks leads to improved performance across broader multi-image understanding, zero-shot grounding, and out-of-domain object detection tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has demonstrated capabilities in understanding and processing both language and visual information. A significant area within this field is visual grounding, which involves localizing specific objects or regions within an image based on textual descriptions. Historically, research in visual grounding has largely focused on single-image scenarios, where an MLLM identifies a target within a single provided image using a natural language query. MLLMs, often built upon the foundation of large language models (LLMs), have shown improvements in this area due to their enhanced language comprehension and ability to generate descriptive outputs.

However, real-world applications frequently present scenarios that extend beyond single-image analysis. These scenarios, termed "multi-image grounding," necessitate cross-image reasoning, contextual understanding across multiple visual inputs, and the ability to respond to more complex, dynamic instructions. Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches for MLLMs in multi-image tasks, such as those used by models like Migician, tend to "memorize" patterns and instruction-following behaviors. This characteristic limits their ability to generalize and perform true reasoning, which is essential for novel or intricate multi-image queries.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) in purely language-based tasks. This work integrates these two distinct research trajectories by exploring the application of RL-based post-training to MLLMs specifically for multi-image grounding. It aims to bridge the gap between MLLMs' proficiency in single-image grounding and their struggle with complex, reasoning-intensive multi-image tasks, thereby advancing their applicability to more demanding real-world multimodal reasoning challenges. The paper seeks to move beyond mere pattern recognition towards cultivating robust reasoning abilities in MLLMs for complex visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) specifically for multi-image grounding tasks. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

Firstly, while MLLMs have achieved considerable success in single-image visual grounding, their performance degrades significantly in real-world scenarios that necessitate cross-image reasoning and the interpretation of complex, multi-modal instructions. Traditional visual grounding often relies on fixed instruction templates and localizes targets within a single, static visual context. However, practical applications, such as identifying an object in one image that is required by a person in another, demand a more sophisticated understanding of relationships and semantics across multiple visual inputs.

Secondly, existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches for multi-image tasks have primarily focused on training MLLMs to recognize patterns and follow instructions. This "memorization" paradigm, as highlighted by prior research, limits the models' capacity for genuine reasoning and generalization to unseen or subtly different scenarios. The authors observed that direct application of RL to MLLMs for multi-image queries often resulted in failures, indicating a fundamental lack of initial reasoning capacity in the base models. This limitation prevents MLLMs from effectively tackling dynamic, context-sensitive instructions inherent in multi-image grounding.

Thirdly, the recent successes of reinforcement learning (RL) in improving the reasoning abilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) in text-based tasks provided a strong motivation. The authors posit that a similar RL-based post-training strategy could be adapted for MLLMs to cultivate robust reasoning pathways for multimodal data. The challenge lies in adapting RL to the unique complexities of multimodal inputs and structured outputs (bounding boxes alongside reasoning chains).

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
1. Develop an RL-based post-training strategy that explicitly teaches MLLMs to perform cross-image reasoning for multi-image grounding.
2. Equip a relatively smaller MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) with reasoning capabilities comparable to or exceeding larger models, while maintaining computational efficiency.
3. Ensure the enhanced model can generalize its reasoning abilities across a diverse range of multi-image and related visual tasks, beyond the specific training distribution.
4. Address the stability and data quality challenges associated with applying RL to MLLMs for complex reasoning tasks, specifically by incorporating strategies for effective initialization and informative data selection.

By addressing these points, the research aims to move beyond simple instruction-following towards models that can infer, deduce, and justify their grounding decisions in complex multi-image contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a two-stage training paradigm designed to improve the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs for multi-image grounding tasks. This paradigm consists of a cold-start Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) initialization followed by a rule-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) training phase. The base MLLM used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

#### 3.1. Cold-start CoT-SFT Initialization (Stage 1)

This initial stage aims to equip the base MLLM with fundamental multi-image comprehension and reasoning abilities, addressing the instability observed when applying RL directly to an uninitialized model.

##### 3.1.1. CoT Reasoning Dataset Preparation

The core of this stage involves creating a high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning dataset.
- **Dataset Foundation**: The dataset is built upon MGrounding-630k, a multi-image grounding dataset. To ensure the model retains proficiency in single-image grounding, it is augmented with a small portion of data from RefCOCO/+/g (single-image grounding) and ODINW and V3Det (object detection). This hybrid approach facilitates comprehensive training.
- **CoT Generation**: A larger, more capable MLLM, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, is employed to synthesize the CoT data. For each question-image pair, the model is prompted with a fixed instructional format: "{Question} First output the thinking process in <think> </think> tags and then output the final answer in <answer> </answer> tags. Output the bounding box coordinates in JSON format." This structure encourages explicit reasoning before providing the final answer.
- **Data Filtering**: To ensure high quality, four distinct responses are generated for each sample using Qwen2.5-VL-72B. Only samples where all four responses exhibit perfect agreement (Intersection over Union (IoU) accuracy = 1.0) for the bounding box prediction are retained. This filtering yields 56,000 high-confidence CoT samples.

##### 3.1.2. Cold-start Supervised Fine-tuning

- **Training Strategy**: The curated CoT dataset is used to fine-tune the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model. Supervised fine-tuning is performed via next-token prediction, allowing the model to learn both the explicit reasoning traces within the `<think>` tags and the spatial grounding in the `<answer>` tags.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)**: To mitigate the risks of full-parameter fine-tuning (e.g., reduced reasoning flexibility, weakened generalization), Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is utilized. LoRA updates only a small number of low-rank matrices, preserving the base model's core knowledge while enhancing its perceptual and reasoning capabilities for multi-image grounding.
- **Model Output**: The trained LoRA modules are then merged with the base MLLM, producing the "Stage-1 CoT-SFT model" which serves as an initialized model for the subsequent RL phase.

#### 3.2. Rule-based RL Training (Stage 2)

The second stage refines the model's reasoning using a rule-based reinforcement learning approach, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), enhanced by rejection sampling.

##### 3.2.1. Rejection Sampling

- **Problem Statement**: During RL training, if a batch of generated responses is either uniformly correct or uniformly incorrect, the reward variance becomes zero. This leads to vanishing advantage signals, rendering optimization ineffective.
- **Solution**: Before GRPO training, rejection sampling is applied. The fixed Stage-1 CoT-SFT model generates multiple predictions for each sample. Samples are discarded if all predictions are entirely correct or entirely incorrect. Only "partially correct" cases (where there's a mix of correct and incorrect predictions) are retained. This ensures non-zero reward variance, providing stronger learning signals. This filtering yields 174,000 informative samples for Stage 2.

##### 3.2.2. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

- **Policy Update Mechanism**: The MLLM, using its current policy (πθ), generates a group of *G* complete responses for a given query. Each response includes a full CoT reasoning process and a final bounding box prediction.
- **Advantage Estimation**: For each generated response *oᵢ*, a scalar reward *rᵢ* is calculated. These rewards are then normalized across the group to estimate a group-relative advantage *Aᵢ*, reflecting how much better or worse a specific response is compared to others in the same group.
- **GRPO Objective**: The training objective incorporates the advantage *Aᵢ* to encourage the generation of higher-reward responses. It also includes a clipping mechanism (similar to PPO) to prevent overly aggressive policy updates and a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty (βKL(πθ(oᵢ|q)|πref(oᵢ|q))) against the fixed CoT-SFT policy (πref). This penalty prevents the policy from drifting too far from the initial supervised learned behavior, maintaining stability.

##### 3.2.3. Reward Design

Two types of reward functions are used, combined into a weighted sum:
- **Accuracy Reward (r_acc)**: This is calculated as the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the predicted bounding box (B̂) and the ground truth bounding box (B). A higher IoU yields a higher accuracy reward.
- **Format Reward (r_format)**: This binary reward enforces adherence to the specified output format: "<think>...</think> <answer>...</answer>". A reward of 1 is given if the format is strictly followed and if the bounding boxes are in valid JSON format; otherwise, it is 0.
- **Total Reward**: The total reward *r* is a weighted sum: *r = λ_acc * r_acc + λ_format * r_format*, with λ_acc = 1.0 and λ_format = 0.5.

The model is trained on NVIDIA H800-80G GPUs, using specific learning rates and batch sizes for SFT and GRPO, and generating 8 rollouts per prompt during RL training. Evaluation is primarily based on Acc@0.5, where a prediction is correct if its IoU with the ground truth exceeds 0.5.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed two-stage RL-based post-training strategy for improving multi-image grounding and broader multimodal reasoning in MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Performance on Multi-image Grounding Benchmarks

- **MIG-Bench Performance**: The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on the MIG-Bench multi-image grounding benchmark. The model records the highest results across all seven subsets of referential grounding.
 - It achieves an overall average score of **58.83%** on MIG-Bench.
 - This represents a **+9.04%** gain over the SFT baseline (49.79%).
 - Compared to the base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (13.19%), the proposed approach demonstrates a substantial improvement of **45.64%**.
 - The model also surpasses the larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B model (46.23%) by **12.60%**, despite using significantly fewer parameters.

#### 4.2. Generalization Across Out-of-Domain Benchmarks

To assess robustness and generalization, the full model was evaluated across seven diverse datasets spanning zero-shot grounding, object detection, and multi-image understanding.

- **Overall Average Performance**: The full model achieves the highest overall average score of **58.23%** across these benchmarks.
 - This is a **+12.13%** improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (46.10%).
 - It also outperforms the SFT baseline (53.82%) by **+4.41%**.

- **Zero-shot Grounding**: On multi-image grounding datasets (MC-Bench, ReVOS) and single-image grounding datasets (LISA, Ref-L4), the method shows an average improvement of **5.06%** over the SFT baseline.
 - Specifically, on ReVOS, performance improved from 46.33% (SFT) to 49.16%.
 - On MC-Bench, performance improved from 52.12% (SFT) to 58.49%.
 - On LISA, performance improved from 54.64% (SFT) to 59.44%.
 - On Ref-L4, performance improved from 69.03% (SFT) to 75.26%.

- **Object Detection**: On the ODINW dataset, the model achieves an average score of **54.15%**, which is **+7.24%** higher than Qwen2.5-VL-7B (46.91%) and **+4.33%** higher than the SFT baseline (49.82%). This indicates robust performance under distribution shifts.

- **Multi-image Understanding**: On BLINK and MMIU, the RL-enhanced training achieves an average improvement of **3.17%** over the SFT baseline.
 - On MMIU, performance improved from 48.21% (SFT) to 49.93%.
 - On BLINK, performance improved from 56.56% (SFT) to 61.18%. This suggests that the strategy strengthens grounding while also improving higher-level reasoning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on MIG-Bench provided insights into the contribution of individual components:

- **Effectiveness of Cold Start**:
 - Direct application of GRPO-based RL to Qwen2.5-VL-7B without cold-start (w/o CS) yielded limited gains of **+3.74%** over the base model, achieving 17.79%.
 - With CoT-SFT initialization, the performance significantly improved to 48.82% (full-parameter) and 52.41% (LoRA), demonstrating the critical role of cold-start in providing initial reasoning capacity. LoRA-based CoT-SFT slightly outperformed full-parameter CoT-SFT by **+0.59%**.

- **Effectiveness of Rejection Sampling**:
 - Training the Stage-1 CoT-SFT model with GRPO but *without* rejection sampling (w/o RS) resulted in a performance of 55.20%.
 - Enabling rejection sampling, as in the full model, improved performance to 58.47%, a gain of **+3.27%**. This highlights the importance of filtering out uninformative samples for efficient RL optimization.

- **Ablations for KL Divergence**:
 - Removing the KL regularization term (w/o KL) in Stage-2 RL training led to a performance reduction of **-3.14%** (from 58.47% to 55.33%), indicating its role in stabilizing policy updates and preventing excessive drift from the initial CoT-SFT policy.

Overall, the findings indicate that the combination of cold-start CoT-SFT initialization, rejection sampling, and rule-based GRPO with appropriate reward design significantly enhances the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs for multi-image grounding and related visual tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the advancement of multimodal large language models by addressing their limitations in complex, real-world multi-image grounding scenarios. The proposed RL-based post-training strategy offers a structured approach to cultivate reasoning abilities in MLLMs, moving beyond mere instruction-following or pattern recognition.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that reinforcement learning, previously successful in enhancing reasoning in text-only LLMs, can be effectively adapted to MLLMs for multimodal reasoning. The two-stage framework, comprising a cold-start CoT-SFT initialization and rule-based RL training, addresses the challenge of directly applying RL to MLLMs that lack inherent complex reasoning capabilities. The use of synthesized Chain-of-Thought data for initialization provides a crucial foundation, while the subsequent GRPO-based RL refines the model's ability to discover optimal reasoning paths.

The integration of rejection sampling is a notable methodological contribution, mitigating the issue of vanishing advantage signals in RL by ensuring that only informative samples, those with varied prediction quality, are used for training. This technique enhances training efficiency and stability, which are critical considerations for complex RL setups. The carefully designed reward functions, combining accuracy (IoU) and format adherence, guide the model toward both precise localization and structured, human-interpretable reasoning outputs.

The experimental results indicate that the approach substantially improves performance on multi-image grounding benchmarks, outperforming both the base MLLM and strong supervised fine-tuning baselines. Furthermore, the demonstrated generalization across diverse zero-shot grounding, object detection, and multi-image understanding datasets suggests that the enhanced reasoning capabilities are robust and transferable. The ability of a 7B-scale MLLM to surpass larger 70B-scale models in certain benchmarks, while leveraging a larger model for data synthesis, highlights a potential pathway for developing capable models with reduced computational footprints for inference.

The potential impact of this work extends to real-world applications requiring nuanced visual understanding and cross-image inference, such as advanced human-robot interaction, complex visual search, intelligent assistants, and content moderation systems that need to analyze relationships between multiple images. By fostering genuine reasoning in MLLMs for multimodal tasks, this research encourages further exploration into how such models can autonomously learn and adapt to increasingly complex visual-linguistic challenges, potentially leading to more intelligent and versatile AI systems.
