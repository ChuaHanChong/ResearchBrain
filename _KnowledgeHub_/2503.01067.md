# All Roads Lead to Likelihood: The Value of Reinforcement Learning in Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers explain the empirical superiority of two-stage online Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) over offline Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) for fine-tuning foundation models. This advantage stems from a “generation-verification gap,” where learning a simpler reward model (verifier) is statistically more efficient than directly learning a complex policy (generator), effectively constraining the policy search space.

## Problem

- Complex two-stage online Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) consistently outperforms simpler offline Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) in fine-tuning foundation models, despite theoretical arguments suggesting otherwise.
- From an information-theoretic perspective, passing data through an intermediary reward model should only lead to a loss of information, and on-policy sampling does not create new ground-truth information, posing a paradox for RLHF's empirical success.
- Traditional justifications for online RL (e.g., learning from exploration, simpler reward functions) do not readily apply to auto-regressive language models, where rewards can be as complex as policies and 'mistakes' are permanent.

## Method

- The authors establish theoretical equivalences between online and offline ==Preference Fine-Tuning== (PFT) under idealized assumptions (isomorphic function classes, exact optimization) to formally define the discrepancy with empirical observations.
- A series of controlled empirical experiments using Pythia models on a summarization task systematically test and largely falsify several existing and novel hypotheses for the online-offline performance gap.
- A novel hypothesis, the 'generation-verification gap,' is proposed and validated through critical experiments that manipulate the inherent complexity of the generation and verification tasks.

## Results

- Theoretical proofs confirm that online and offline PFT are equivalent under ideal conditions, providing a formal baseline for the observed empirical discrepancy.
- Controlled experiments demonstrate that online DPO consistently and robustly outperforms standard offline DPO on the `tl;dr` summarization task, empirically validating the persistent performance gap.
- The performance gap between online and offline methods significantly diminishes or vanishes when the generation-verification gap is reduced (e.g., in a two-word summarization task) or when the reward function is made complex (e.g., using ROUGE-L as reward), providing strong empirical support for the generation-verification gap hypothesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Under ideal theoretical conditions (isomorphic function classes, exact optimization), online and offline PFT methods are mathematically equivalent and should yield identical optimal policies.
> - The observed empirical superiority of online RLHF for LLM fine-tuning is primarily explained by a 'generation-verification gap,' where the optimal reward function (verifier) is often statistically simpler to learn than the optimal policy (generator).
> - Online PFT implicitly constrains the search space to policies optimal for these simpler verifiers, effectively reducing the statistical complexity of the learning problem compared to offline methods that search the entire policy space.
