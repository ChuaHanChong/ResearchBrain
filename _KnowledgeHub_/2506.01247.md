# Visual Sparse Steering: Improving Zero-shot Image Classification with Sparsity Guided Steering Vectors

> [!summary] Summary
> Rutgers University researchers developed Visual Sparse Steering (VS2), a suite of methods that leverage sparse autoencoders to directly steer Vision Foundation Models at inference time, leading to absolute top-1 accuracy gains of up to 4.12% with VS2 and over 21% with its retrieval-augmented variant (VS2++) in zero-shot image classification across various datasets.

## Problem

- Traditional fine-tuning of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) is computationally expensive and data-intensive, limiting their adaptability for new tasks.
- Applying 'steering vectors' to the vision domain is challenging due to the redundancy of visual representations and the lack of clear, interpretable 'anchors' to define steering directions.
- Existing VFM control methods often rely on external language signals or aggregate class-level statistics from training data, limiting test-time unsupervised operation.

## Method

- ==Visual Sparse Steering (VS2)== extracts sparse concept activations from VFM embeddings using a ==Top-k Sparse Autoencoder== and amplifies these features to create an inference-time steering vector.
- ==VS2++== extends VS2 by leveraging cached unlabeled data to construct more precise, contrastive steering vectors based on pseudo-labeled positive and negative neighbors.
- ==Prototype-Aligned Sparse Steering (PASS)== enhances SAEs by incorporating a prototype-alignment loss during training, aligning learned sparse features with class-discriminative information for improved downstream relevance.

## Results

- Visual Sparse Steering (VS2) improved zero-shot top-1 accuracy over CLIP by up to **4.12%** on CIFAR-100 and **1.84%** on Tiny-ImageNet without external data.
- The retrieval-augmented VS2++ achieved up to **21.44%** absolute top-1 accuracy gain over CLIP in an oracle setting and generally outperformed standard CLIP Steering Vectors in realistic conditions.
- Per-class accuracy for confusing categories increased by up to **25%** with VS2 and **38%** with VS2++, demonstrating targeted disambiguation of fine-grained visual concepts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparse autoencoder (SAE) features capture interpretable, disentangled visual concepts within Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), enabling principled steering.
> - Steering vectors derived from amplifying these sparse features effectively modulate VFM behavior at inference time without requiring explicit positive/negative concept pairs or additional supervision.
> - The proposed sparse steering methods primarily improve classification accuracy for visually or taxonomically similar categories, disambiguating fine-grained confusions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned within the field of mechanistic interpretability, specifically focusing on Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) and their application to vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP. Historically, SAEs have been primarily utilized for post-hoc analysis, decomposing model activations into sparse features to explain what a model represents and which features activate on a given input (Bricken et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024a). This work addresses an underexplored area: the transition of sparse features from being purely analytical tools to serving as an actionable intervention space for improving downstream task performance in a label-free manner.

Adapting frozen foundation models, such as CLIP, typically involves methods like labeled data, prompt tuning (Zhou et al., 2022b), test-time optimization (Shu et al., 2022), or contrastive examples for steering vectors (Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023). These approaches often incur substantial computational costs, require backpropagation through large encoders, or depend on explicit supervision. This paper contrasts with such methods by proposing a technique that leverages SAEs, trained on unlabeled activations, to discover interpretable sparse directions without requiring labeled data, contrastive anchors, or test-time backpropagation, thereby offering a more computationally efficient and adaptable approach to model steering.

While other works have explored SAEs for feature analysis, generative modeling, or concept disentanglement in the visual domain (Bhalla et al., 2024; Stevens et al., 2025; Surkov et al., 2024), they often prioritize interpretability over direct downstream performance improvement. This paper extends the application of sparse steering to vision models, where its utility for performance enhancement in a label-free, test-time setting has been comparatively underexplored. It differentiates itself from methods like Joseph et al. (2025), which studies CLIP's steerability on typographic attacks, and Patch-SAE (Lim et al., 2024), which uses class-conditioned latent masking, by not requiring class labels or class-based activation aggregation during training. The research contributes to the broader effort of making large models more controllable and reliable with minimal additional resources.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether sparse features learned by Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) can be used to steer frozen vision-language models for downstream tasks, moving beyond their traditional role in post-hoc interpretability. The authors aim to establish SAEs as an actionable intervention space in vision.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Develop a Label-Free Steering Method:** A primary goal is to introduce Visual Sparse Steering (VS2), a novel method that trains a top-k SAE on unlabeled activations from a frozen CLIP image encoder. The motivation here stems from the observation that many existing VLM adaptation techniques are either computationally intensive (e.g., test-time optimization requiring backpropagation) or reliant on labeled data (e.g., prompt tuning, contrastive steering vectors). VS2 aims to bypass these requirements by leveraging the inherently interpretable and unsupervised nature of SAEs.
- **Improve Zero-Shot Classification Performance:** The authors seek to demonstrate that VS2 can consistently enhance zero-shot classification accuracy across various image classification datasets and different CLIP backbones. This objective directly addresses the practical utility of sparse features for improving model performance without task-specific training.
- **Provide a Geometric Interpretation of Steering:** The research aims to offer a theoretical understanding of *why* VS2 is effective. This involves demonstrating that the steering procedure admits a closed-form decomposition, interpreting it as "centroid-deviation steering." The motivation for this is to provide transparency and a mechanistic explanation for the observed empirical gains, showing that the method amplifies an input's distinctiveness by moving it away from a population centroid learned by the SAE.
- **Address Reliability and Distribution Shift:** A crucial motivation is to tackle the challenge of reliability when applying sparse steering, particularly under distribution shifts. When an SAE's reconstruction of target activations is poor, steering can potentially degrade performance. The objective is to formalize this failure mode using the Fraction of Variance Unexplained (FVU) as a metric and to develop a label-free reliability gate that can prevent harmful interventions by reverting to the original zero-shot CLIP prediction when SAE reconstruction is deemed unreliable.
- **Identify and Quantify the "Reconstruction-vs-Task Saliency Gap":** The authors motivate an investigation into a fundamental limitation: whether features salient for reconstruction (what SAEs are trained for) are necessarily the most useful for downstream prediction. They introduce VS2++ as a controlled study to quantify the potential accuracy headroom achievable if task-relevant sparse features could be selectively amplified. This objective aims to delineate the inherent constraints of SAE-based steering and guide future research toward more task-aligned feature selection.

In summary, the motivation is to unlock the potential of SAEs for efficient, label-free, and interpretable intervention in frozen VLMs, while simultaneously understanding their underlying mechanisms, limitations, and ensuring their reliable deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around Visual Sparse Steering (VS2), a label-free approach designed to steer frozen vision-language models using features learned by Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Top-k Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)

The foundation of VS2 is a top-k SAE. Given a CLIP image encoder's CLS-token activation `x` (a `d`-dimensional vector), the SAE learns to encode `x` into sparse features `c` (an `n`-dimensional vector, where `n` is the latent width, typically an expansion of `d`) and then reconstruct `x` as `x_tilde`.
- **Encoding:** `c = TopK_k(W_enc(x - b_pre))`, where `W_enc` is the encoder weight matrix, `b_pre` is a learned bias (initialized to the empirical mean of `x`), and `TopK_k(·)` retains the `k` entries with the largest absolute values, setting others to zero.
- **Decoding:** `x_tilde = W_dec * c + b_pre`, where `W_dec` is the decoder weight matrix.
The SAE is trained to minimize the mean squared error `||x - x_tilde||^2_2` over unlabeled training activations.

#### 3.2. Visual Sparse Steering (VS2) Construction

VS2 operates in three steps at test time for a given input image `x`:
1. **Sparse Feature Extraction:** The input `x` is fed through the SAE encoder to obtain its sparse features `c`.
2. **Steering Vector Generation:** An amplification factor `γ > 1` is applied to the sparse features. A steering vector `v` is then constructed by decoding the amplified features and subtracting the decoded un-amplified features: `v = D_c(γc) - D_c(c)`. This formulation ensures that the SAE's centering bias (`b_pre`) cancels out, isolating the change induced purely by the active sparse features. Since the decoder is affine, this simplifies to `v = (γ-1) * W_dec * c`.
3. **Embedding Adjustment:** The original CLIP embedding `x` is then adjusted by adding a scaled version of the steering vector `v`: `x_hat = x + λv`, where `λ > 0` is a steering scale factor. To maintain comparability with standard CLIP classification, `x_hat` is finally rescaled to preserve the original embedding's norm: `x_hat = (x + λv) * (||x||_2 / ||x + λv||_2)`.
This process is label-free, instance-specific, and requires no contrastive anchors or gradient updates.

#### 3.3. Geometric Interpretation: Centroid-Deviation Steering

The paper provides a closed-form decomposition of `x + λv`. By substituting `W_dec * c = (x - b_pre) + ϵ(x)` (where `ϵ(x)` is the per-sample reconstruction residual), the steered embedding can be expressed as `x + λv = x + α(x - b_pre) + α ϵ(x)`, with `α = λ(γ-1)`.
This decomposition reveals that VS2 performs "centroid-deviation steering": it moves the embedding `x` along the direction of its deviation from the SAE-learned population centroid (`b_pre` ≈ `μ`). This process aims to amplify the distinctive features of `x` while suppressing common, shared structures. The residual term `α ϵ(x)` has a magnitude controlled by `α * sqrt(FVU(x)) * ||x - μ||`, where `FVU(x)` is the per-sample fraction of variance unexplained by the SAE's reconstruction.

#### 3.4. Reliability Mechanism: FVU-Gated Fallback

To address potential performance degradation when the SAE poorly reconstructs activations (e.g., under distribution shift), a reliability mechanism is introduced. The Fraction of Variance Unexplained (FVU) for each sample serves as a diagnostic:
- `FVU(x) = ||ϵ(x)||^2_2 / ||x - μ||^2_2`. Higher FVU indicates poorer reconstruction.
- A label-free FVU-gated fallback is implemented: if a test sample's `FVU(x)` exceeds a calibrated threshold `τ`, steering is disabled, and the original zero-shot CLIP embedding is used instead. The threshold `τ` is determined by taking a `q`-quantile of FVU scores on an unlabeled in-domain dataset. This mechanism prioritizes safety by preventing potentially harmful steering when the SAE's representation is unreliable.

#### 3.5. Headroom Analysis: Selective Amplification (VS2++)

To quantify the "reconstruction-vs-task saliency gap" – the difference between features salient for reconstruction and those useful for prediction – a controlled study called VS2++ is introduced. It explores the upper bound of accuracy gains achievable through *selective* amplification of sparse features:
1. **Neighbor Retrieval:** For a query image `x_q`, its `N` nearest neighbors are retrieved from an unlabeled corpus using DINOv2.
2. **Pseudo-labeling and Grouping:** CLIP pseudo-labels are assigned to the query and its neighbors. Neighbors agreeing with `x_q`'s pseudo-label form a positive set (`S+`), and the rest form a negative set (`S-`).
3. **Contrastive Steering:** A contrastive steering vector `v_bar` is formed by averaging the individual VS2 directions (`v_i`) for `S+` and subtracting the average of `v_i` for `S-`: `v_bar = (γ-1) * W_dec * (c_bar+ - c_bar-)`. This directs the embedding based on class-discriminative sparse features.
4. **Steering:** `x_q` is steered using `v_bar`. An oracle version of VS2++ uses ground-truth labels for neighbor grouping to establish an upper bound. VS2++ serves as an analytical tool to measure the potential gains from task-aligned feature selection, rather than a direct deployment method.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted on nine image-classification datasets (CIFAR-100, CUB-200, Tiny-ImageNet, Aircraft, Food101, Flower102, Caltech101, SUN, EuroSAT) using frozen CLIP models with ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, and ViT-L/14 backbones. Top-k SAEs were trained on unlabeled CLS-token activations from the final layer. Hyperparameters (e.g., `k`, expansion factor) were set (e.g., `k=64` for ViT-B/32, `k=256` for ViT-B/16, `4x` expansion) and their sensitivity analyzed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness, mechanisms, and limitations of Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) for visual steering.

- **Consistent Zero-Shot Performance Improvement with VS2:**
 - VS2 consistently improved zero-shot image classification accuracy across all nine tested datasets (CIFAR-100, CUB-200, Tiny-ImageNet, Aircraft, Food101, Flower102, Caltech101, SUN, EuroSAT) and two CLIP backbones (ViT-B/32 and ViT-B/16). Gains ranged up to +4.12% on CIFAR-100 (ViT-B/16).
 - This improvement was achieved without requiring labels, contrastive anchors, or test-time optimization, and with negligible inference overhead (less than 0.1% additional compute).
 - Compared to prompt-based test-time adaptation (TTA) methods, VS2 either matched or exceeded their performance while incurring significantly lower computational and memory overhead.
 - The benefits were shown to stem from the additive steering direction rather than simply replacing CLIP embeddings with SAE reconstructions, as naive reconstruction often degraded performance.
 - VS2 demonstrated robustness to variations in its steering hyperparameters (sparse amplification `γ` and steering magnitude `λ`), exhibiting broad regions of near-optimal performance when `λγ` was between 2 and 3.
 - Qualitative analysis indicated that individual SAE latents often clustered coherent visual concepts, and VS2 corrections frequently aligned with class-discriminative features (e.g., distinguishing "tractor" from "lawn mower").
 - The method's effectiveness extended to larger models, showing positive accuracy gains on CLIP ViT-L/14 across all tested datasets.

- **Identification and Quantification of the Reconstruction-vs-Task Saliency Gap (VS2++):**
 - The controlled study using VS2++ (selective amplification based on retrieved neighbors) revealed substantial "headroom" for accuracy gains. In an oracle setting (using ground-truth labels for neighbors), VS2++ achieved gains up to +21.44% on CIFAR-100, +7.08% on CUB-200, and +20.47% on Tiny-ImageNet.
 - This finding suggests that the primary limitation of SAE-based downstream steering is not the absence of useful sparse features, but rather the challenge of reliably selecting the task-relevant subset of features.
 - In a non-oracle setting (using CLIP pseudo-labels), performance was reduced due to noise but still often outperformed baseline CLIP steering and avoided negative transfer.
 - Per-class analysis on CIFAR-100 showed that VS2 yielded significant gains (up to +25% absolute) on visually ambiguous classes (e.g., "tractor," "forest," "man") where zero-shot CLIP faced confusions. VS2++ further amplified these gains (up to +38% absolute) for similar cases (e.g., "spider," "tiger," "flatfish"). This indicated that steering helps emphasize distinctive visual attributes crucial for distinguishing confusable categories.

- **Reliability Enhancement with FVU-Gated Fallback:**
 - The research validated Proposition 3.1, demonstrating that high Fraction of Variance Unexplained (FVU) correlated with larger residual magnitudes in the steering direction and less reliable steering.
 - When a generalized SAE (trained on a union of datasets) was applied to out-of-distribution datasets, ungated VS2 could degrade performance where FVU was high.
 - The proposed label-free FVU-gated fallback mechanism effectively mitigated these harmful interventions. By setting a threshold based on a quantile of FVU scores, steering was selectively disabled when reconstruction was unreliable. This mechanism acted as a conservative safety measure, returning accuracy to the zero-shot baseline when steering was detrimental, while largely retaining gains when reconstruction was reliable. This provided a model-internal reliability signal for safe deployment.

- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - SAE training, particularly with cached activations, was found to be significantly more computationally efficient than alternative adaptation methods like LoRA or full fine-tuning (e.g., 0.014 GFLOPs/sample for SAE vs. 26.365 GFLOPs/sample for LoRA).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the fields of mechanistic interpretability and efficient model adaptation by demonstrating a new functional role for Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs).

- **Expanding the Role of SAEs:** The work moves SAEs beyond their established role as tools for post-hoc interpretation to a more proactive function as an *actionable intervention space*. This indicates that the interpretable features learned by SAEs can directly manipulate the behavior of frozen foundation models, opening new avenues for model control and debugging.

- **Enabling Label-Free and Efficient Model Steering:** Visual Sparse Steering (VS2) introduces a label-free, unsupervised, and computationally lightweight method for improving the performance of frozen vision-language models. This is significant because many existing model adaptation techniques require labeled data, extensive fine-tuning, or computationally expensive test-time optimization. VS2's minimal inference overhead (less than 0.1%) makes it a practical solution for deploying adapted models in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications.

- **Providing a Mechanistic Understanding:** The derivation of VS2 as "centroid-deviation steering" offers a clear geometric and theoretical explanation for its effectiveness. This interpretable mechanism, which amplifies distinctive features by shifting representations away from learned population centroids, enhances transparency and provides a foundation for further theoretical development in sparse feature-based interventions.

- **Enhancing Reliability in Deployment:** The introduction of an FVU-based reliability gate provides a crucial safety mechanism for sparse steering. By utilizing the SAE's internal reconstruction error as a diagnostic, VS2 can intelligently choose to disable steering when representations are unreliable, thereby preventing harmful interventions, particularly under distribution shifts. This self-awareness capability is a significant step towards more robust and trustworthy AI systems.

- **Identifying Key Research Challenges:** The study identifies the "reconstruction-vs-task saliency gap" as a central challenge. By quantifying the substantial headroom available through selective feature amplification (VS2++), the research highlights that the limitation is not the absence of useful sparse features, but rather the difficulty of reliably selecting task-relevant subsets. This observation provides a clear direction for future work in developing more sophisticated, task-aligned sparse feature selection techniques, potentially incorporating weak supervision or prototype information.

- **Broad Applicability and Scalability:** Demonstrated improvements across nine diverse datasets and multiple CLIP vision backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) suggest the broad applicability of VS2. Its ability to scale to larger models indicates its potential utility for state-of-the-art foundation models.

In conclusion, this research presents a practical, analytically grounded, and reliability-aware approach to label-free visual steering using sparse autoencoders. Its impact lies in expanding the utility of interpretability tools for performance enhancement, offering a cost-effective adaptation strategy, and providing a principled framework for understanding and mitigating the risks associated with model interventions.
