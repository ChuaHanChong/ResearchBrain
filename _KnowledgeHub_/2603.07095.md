# ACLM: ADMM-Based Distributed Model Predictive Control for Collaborative Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed a distributed model predictive control framework for teams of legged robots collaboratively transporting heavy payloads. Leveraging ADMM to decompose the control problem and integrating a wrench-aware whole-body controller, the system achieved up to 11.4x speedup over centralized methods while maintaining real-time performance and robustness on various challenging terrains.

## Problem

- Centralized planning for multi-robot collaborative loco-manipulation faces severe scalability limitations with increasing numbers of robots and degrees of freedom, making real-time application impractical.
- Hierarchical or fully decentralized approaches often neglect critical force and dynamic interactions between robots and the shared payload, leading to conservative motions, degraded performance, and stability issues on rough terrain.
- Current methods struggle to balance computational tractability with the detailed modeling of dynamic and force interactions, which are essential for stable and efficient multi-robot loco-manipulation with legged robots.

## Method

![[2603.07095-method.png]]
*The ACLM framework decomposes the collaborative transport task into a payload subproblem and individual robot subproblems, solved iteratively via ADMM. The resulting plans are executed by a wrench-aware Whole-Body Controller (WBC).*

- The collaborative loco-manipulation task is formulated as a centralized Optimal Control Problem (OCP) and then re-formulated for distributed computation using consensus Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM).
- The OCP is decomposed into parallel, robot-level subproblems coupled only through the shared payload, by using approximated state copies and enforcing consensus primarily on manipulation wrenches.
- An integrated 'wrench-aware' Whole-Body Controller (WBC) executes planned trajectories, utilizing a hierarchical Quadratic Program to prioritize arm contact wrench tracking for precise force consistency.

## Results

- Distributed MPC demonstrated significant computational speedups, achieving up to **11.4x** for 4 robots on flat terrain and **7.3x** on rough terrain, compared to centralized MPC, while maintaining real-time planning frequencies (50-100 Hz).
- The framework enabled successful collaborative transportation across diverse rough terrain scenarios, including stepped gaps, slopes, narrow turns, and annular platforms, showcasing adaptive motion and stable payload transport.
- The controller exhibited robustness to significant modeling errors in cargo mass and inertia (up to ~**67%** error before failure), maintaining low pose tracking errors. Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of full wrench tracking for rotational stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exploiting the payload-induced 'star-shaped' coupling structure through ADMM enables efficient distributed computation that scales positively with robot team size while preserving essential dynamic and force interactions.
> - Enforcing consensus primarily on interaction wrenches, rather than full state consensus, significantly simplifies subproblems and improves computational tractability without compromising critical coupling.
> - Elevating arm contact wrench tracking to the highest priority in the Whole-Body Controller is crucial for maintaining rotational stability and achieving accurate force and torque consistency during collaborative manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of collaborative transportation of heavy payloads using teams of legged robots equipped with manipulators (loco-manipulation) in complex, unstructured environments. This capability is considered essential for various applications such as logistics, mining, construction, agriculture, and search-and-rescue.

Current approaches to multi-robot collaborative loco-manipulation generally fall into two categories, each with distinct limitations:

1. **Centralized Planning Methods:** These methods, exemplified by holistic trajectory optimization, inherently capture dynamic coupling among robots and payloads. However, they face significant scalability issues, particularly with a high number of degrees of freedom (DoFs), hybrid contact dynamics, and the complexities of rough terrain associated with quadrupedal manipulators. This computational burden restricts their real-time applicability as the team size increases.
2. **Hierarchical and Fully Decentralized Approaches:** While offering scalability by decoupling payload and robot planning, these methods often simplify coordination by neglecting critical force and dynamic interactions between robots and the payload. This simplification can lead to conservative motions, degraded performance, and stability issues, especially on challenging terrains.

The existing research landscape for collaborative loco-manipulation with legged robots is less explored compared to wheeled mobile manipulators or quadrotors. While some works have utilized centralized MPC with simplified models or hierarchical planning frameworks, they either suffer from scalability limitations or require strong hierarchical assumptions. Approaches using holonomically constrained systems (e.g., cables) offer scalability but are limited by restrictive mechanical assumptions and reduced interaction flexibility. Prehensile manipulation, which allows for flexible grasp-based interaction, has been addressed, but often relies on centralized optimization or complex reward designs for learning-based strategies. Non-prehensile methods (pushing, carrying) are suitable for long-horizon tasks but lack direct force control and manipulation precision.

This work positions itself to bridge the gap between these two extremes. It aims to develop a distributed control framework that achieves scalability without sacrificing the detailed modeling of force and dynamic interactions, which is crucial for stable and efficient loco-manipulation on rough terrain. By leveraging the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) and exploiting the inherent "star-shaped" coupling structure (where robots interact with the shared payload, but not directly with each other), the authors propose a method to decompose a centralized optimal control problem into tractable subproblems. This approach aims to provide the computational advantages of distributed methods while retaining much of the solution quality associated with centralized optimization, thereby addressing a significant limitation in the field of multi-robot collaborative loco-manipulation. The integration with a wrench-aware Whole-Body Controller further distinguishes this work by providing a comprehensive pipeline from high-level planning to low-level execution, specifically accounting for interaction wrenches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust, real-time, and scalable collaborative transportation of heavy payloads by a team of quadrupedal manipulators in complex, unstructured environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and necessitates distinct objectives:

1. **Addressing Scalability Limitations:** Collaborative loco-manipulation with multiple high-degree-of-freedom legged robots presents a complex optimal control problem. Centralized planning, while capable of modeling full dynamic coupling, becomes computationally prohibitive as the number of robots increases, making real-time application impractical. The motivation is to find a method that maintains the benefits of comprehensive dynamic modeling without the linear increase in computational cost with team size.
 - **Objective:** To formulate the collaborative loco-manipulation task as a centralized Optimal Control Problem (OCP) and then develop an ADMM-based distributed Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework that decomposes this OCP into parallel, robot-level subproblems.

2. **Preserving Dynamic and Force Interactions:** Many existing hierarchical or decentralized approaches simplify the problem by neglecting the strong dynamic and force coupling between the robots and the shared payload. For legged manipulators operating on rough terrain, these interactions are critical for stability, performance, and avoiding conservative behavior.
 - **Objective:** To design the distributed optimization to explicitly exploit the payload-induced "star-shaped" coupling structure, treating the payload as an independent subsystem and enforcing consensus primarily on interaction wrenches. This aims to maintain essential force and dynamic interactions while enabling parallel computation.

3. **Achieving Real-time Performance in Receding-Horizon Control:** For robots to operate effectively in dynamic and uncertain environments, planning must occur in real-time within a receding-horizon MPC framework, allowing continuous replanning and adaptation.
 - **Objective:** To implement the distributed planner in a receding-horizon fashion, incorporating warm-start strategies to ensure fast convergence (few ADMM iterations per planning cycle) and real-time operational frequencies.

4. **Enabling Robust and Accurate Execution:** A high-level planner requires a robust lower-level controller that can accurately execute planned trajectories and precisely control interaction forces.
 - **Objective:** To integrate the distributed MPC framework with a "wrench-aware" Whole-Body Controller (WBC) that tracks both planned motion trajectories (end-effector poses, foot placements) and the crucial interaction wrenches. This WBC is designed with a specific task hierarchy to prioritize safety and wrench consistency.

5. **Validation Across Diverse Scenarios:** The effectiveness and practicality of the proposed framework must be empirically validated under various challenging conditions.
 - **Objective:** To conduct extensive simulations with varying numbers of robots (up to four) on diverse collaborative transportation tasks, including obstacle avoidance and rough-terrain traversal, to demonstrate scalability, real-time performance, and robustness to model uncertainty.

In summary, the motivation stems from the limitations of current centralized and decentralized control strategies for complex multi-robot loco-manipulation, specifically the trade-off between computational tractability and fidelity of dynamic interaction modeling. The objectives are structured to overcome these limitations by introducing a distributed optimization approach that is both scalable and interaction-aware, culminating in a practical, real-time control system for teams of quadrupedal manipulators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on an Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)-based distributed Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, integrated with a wrench-aware Whole-Body Controller (WBC). This approach decomposes a complex centralized optimal control problem (OCP) for collaborative loco-manipulation into manageable, parallelizable subproblems.

**A. Centralized Optimization Formulation**

The initial step involves formulating the collaborative loco-manipulation task as a centralized OCP. This problem aims to minimize a cost function over a finite horizon (N+1 steps), subject to system dynamics, initial conditions, and various equality and inequality constraints.
- **State and Control Variables:** The state `x[k]` and control `u[k]` vectors at each time step `k` are concatenated from `R` robots and a single payload. The payload (`i=0`) has no direct control input; its motion is governed by interaction forces from the robots.
 - Payload State `x0[k]`: Position, linear velocity, Euler angles, and angular momentum.
 - Robot State `xi[k]`: Position, linear velocity, Euler angles, angular momentum, and foot positions.
 - Robot Control `ui[k]`: Foot contact forces, foot velocities, and manipulation force and torque at the arm end-effector (EE).
- **Dynamics:** The system dynamics `D(x[k], u[k])` are based on second-order rigid body equations for both the payload and each robot. Payload dynamics are driven by the sum of manipulation forces/torques from all robots. Robot dynamics are influenced by ground contact forces and the manipulation wrench exerted on the payload. These continuous-time dynamics are discretized using the backward Euler method.

**B. Distributed Optimization via ADMM**

To enable distributed computation, the centralized OCP is reformulated using consensus ADMM, exploiting the star-shaped coupling where each robot interacts only with the payload.
- **Decomposed System Dynamics:**
 - **Payload Control Variables:** To separate the payload, `u0[k]` is introduced, comprising global copies of the manipulation wrenches (`¯fi,h[k]`, `¯τi,h[k]`) from each robot.
 - **Consensus Constraints:** Newton's third law dictates that the manipulation wrench applied by robot `i` (`fi,h[k]`, `τi,h[k]`) and the corresponding wrench perceived by the payload (`¯fi,h[k]`, `¯τi,h[k]`) must be equal and opposite. These are formulated as linear equality constraints: `fi,h[k] + ¯fi,h[k] = 0` and `τi,h[k] + ¯τi,h[k] = 0`. These constitute the only coupling constraints between subproblems.
 - **Approximated State Copy:** A key design choice to maintain compact subproblem size and computational efficiency is to avoid enforcing state consensus. For dynamic equations and inter-system constraints that involve state variables from other subsystems (e.g., payload state in robot dynamics, or robot states in payload arm kinematics constraints), values from the *previous ADMM iteration* (`xm0[k]`, `xmi[k]`) are used. This approximation is justified by the dominance of interaction forces in coupling and has been empirically shown to have minimal performance impact.
- **Cost Function:** The total cost function `C` is decomposed into individual cost functions `li[k]` for each subsystem (payload and `R` robots). These consist of standard tracking costs (deviation from reference trajectories) and regularization terms (e.g., minimizing foot/arm EE velocities, contact forces, and wrenches for motion smoothness).
- **Constraints:**
 - **Independent (Local) Constraints:** These apply to individual subsystems without direct coupling to others.
 - Frictional and Contact Constraints: For manipulation contact and point-foot ground contacts (e.g., positive normal force, friction cones, moment bounding box for arm, zero velocity during stance, zero force during non-contact).
 - Foot Kinematics: Limits foot EE movement within a nominal region.
 - Foot Placement: Ensures footholds adhere to terrain height or safe regions from an elevation map (perceptive mode).
 - Collision Avoidance: Uses a Control Barrier Function (CBF) formulation for individual robots and the payload, and terrain-aware constraints for the payload.
 - **Inter-System (Coupling) Constraints:** These involve states from multiple subsystems but are handled in a distributed manner by using the approximated state copies from the previous ADMM iteration.
 - Arm Kinematics: Bounds the relative pose between each robot base and the grasping handle within a feasible workspace.
 - Formation: Keeps each robot's center of mass near its nominal offset from the payload in the payload frame.
- **ADMM Update Sequence:** For each ADMM iteration `m`, the following three steps are executed:
 1. **Payload Subproblem Update (ϕm+10):** Solves for the payload trajectory, minimizing its local cost function and the augmented Lagrangian terms related to consensus with robots.
 2. **Robot Subproblems Update (ϕm+1i):** Each robot `i` solves its subproblem in parallel, minimizing its local cost and augmented Lagrangian terms, utilizing the *newly updated* payload variables.
 3. **Dual Variable Update (wm+1i):** The dual variables for each robot `i` are updated based on the current violation of the consensus constraints.
- **Solver and Stopping Criteria:** A Riccati-like Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) solver is used for efficiently solving the nonlinear, high-dimensional subproblems. Inequality constraints are handled with a relaxed log barrier. The ADMM iterations stop when a time-scaled sum squared error of the consensus residual falls below a specified tolerance.

**C. Real-time Planning and Control**

The ADMM-based distributed MPC operates in a receding-horizon fashion.
- **Warm-Starting:** Two strategies are employed to accelerate convergence:
 1. Within a single MPC solve: The solution from the previous ADMM iteration warm-starts the current iteration's subproblems.
 2. Across MPC solves: The primal and dual solutions from the previous MPC window warm-start the first ADMM iteration of the new window.
- **Wrench-Aware Whole-Body Control (WBC):** A lower-level WBC, operating at 500 Hz, executes the planned trajectories.
 - **Task Hierarchy:** A hierarchical Quadratic Program (QP) with three priority levels is used:
 1. Highest Priority: Enforces floating-base equations of motion, joint torque limits, friction cones (for feet and arm), no-contact motion for swing legs, and crucially, *arm contact wrench tracking* (elevated to highest priority to ensure strict wrench consistency).
 2. Second Priority: Base pose, swing-leg, and arm EE pose tracking.
 3. Lowest Priority: Leg contact force tracking and regularization of torque/joint acceleration.
 - **Execution:** Inverse kinematics (IK) provides joint references, and the final torque command combines WBC feedforward torques with low-gain PD feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive simulation results to evaluate the performance of the proposed ADMM-based distributed MPC framework, focusing on scalability, convergence, terrain navigation, obstacle avoidance, robustness, and the impact of wrench tracking.

**A. Scalability and Real-time Performance**
- **Computational Advantage:** Distributed MPC demonstrates significant computational speedups compared to centralized MPC, which scale positively with the number of robots.
 - On flat terrain: Distributed MPC achieved 3.6×, 7.1×, and 11.4× speedups for 2, 3, and 4 robots, respectively. Median CPU times for distributed MPC ranged from 6.73 ms to 11.63 ms, while centralized MPC ranged from 24.38 ms to 133.13 ms.
 - On Gap-Slope terrain with perceptive constraints: Speedups were 1.7×, 5.0×, and 7.3× for 2, 3, and 4 robots, with median times of 18.01 ms, 16.39 ms, and 23.03 ms for distributed MPC.
- **Real-time Operation:** Distributed MPC maintained a planning frequency of 50 Hz (100 Hz on flat terrain) across all tested team sizes. In contrast, centralized MPC dropped below 30 Hz for teams of 3 and 4 robots.
- **Reason for Scalability:** The superior scalability is attributed to the decomposition of the multi-robot optimization into smaller, parallelizable subproblems, leading to nearly uniform computational time for distributed MPC as team size increases, unlike the linearly growing complexity of centralized MPC.

**B. Convergence Analysis of ADMM-SQP Iterations**
- **Residual Behavior:** With only 1 ADMM iteration, the consensus residual reduced slowly, and trajectory deviations increased, particularly during challenging phases like gap traversal. Incorporating more SQP iterations offered marginal improvements in reducing constraint violations.
- **Optimal Balance:** Two ADMM iterations were found to keep the residual within the specified threshold with rare violations, offering a balance between constraint satisfaction and computational efficiency. Increasing to 5 ADMM iterations further improved residual reduction but incurred higher initial computational costs.
- **Computational Cost:** More ADMM iterations resulted in faster initial convergence of the residual but also led to increased computational time. More SQP iterations substantially increased computation time. Based on this analysis, 2 ADMM iterations and 1 SQP iteration per MPC solve were selected for practical implementation to balance performance and efficiency.

**C. Rough Terrain Navigation**
- The framework was evaluated across diverse rough terrain scenarios:
 - **Gap Terrain (Stepped):** A two-robot team successfully traversed stepped terrain, demonstrating adaptive swing heights and stable payload transport.
 - **Sloped Surface:** The system traversed a 10° incline, with robot bases and cargo orientations adjusting to compensate for terrain tilt.
 - **Narrow Turn:** Coordinated replanning enabled successful navigation through a 90° turn in a confined passage, highlighting adaptation to constrained configurations.
 - **Annular Platform:** The system demonstrated stable formation tracking on an elevated circular path with continuous curvature.

**D. Obstacle Avoidance**
- **Safe Trajectory Generation:** In a physical simulator scenario, the robot–cargo system successfully planned and executed collision-free trajectories among obstacles, even when initialized with a simple start-to-goal reference. This indicated the MPC's ability to find feasible paths by leveraging manipulator flexibility to navigate narrow passages.
- **Pose Tracking Accuracy:** The integrated MPC-WBC pipeline achieved accurate pose tracking during obstacle avoidance. Over a 110-second duration, maximum linear errors were 0.018 m, and maximum angular errors were 1.64°. Transient spikes related to ground impacts were observed but remained bounded, demonstrating reliable real-time performance.

**E. Robustness and Ablation Study**
- **Robustness to Model Uncertainty:** The controller exhibited robustness to significant modeling errors in cargo mass and inertia. Despite deliberate variations, median errors and interquartile ranges for cargo pose tracking remained low and consistent. Performance degraded to failure when modeling errors reached approximately 67%, indicating a practical operating margin.
- **Ablation Study on Wrench Tracking:**
 - **Force-Only Tracking:** When torque tracking was disabled (force-only tracking), the angular tracking error progressively increased, eventually leading to instability of the system.
 - **Full Wrench Tracking:** Enabling torque tracking, particularly along the robot-cargo alignment axis (x-axis), significantly improved rotational stability and reduced angular error. This result supports the design choice of optimizing and tracking the full wrench (both force and torque) at the grasped handles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of multi-robot collaborative loco-manipulation, with several areas of potential impact:

1. **Advancement in Multi-Robot Control Scalability:** The proposed ADMM-based distributed MPC framework offers a practical solution to the long-standing challenge of scalability in multi-robot optimal control. By effectively decomposing a high-dimensional, coupled problem into smaller, parallelizable subproblems, it enables the application of model predictive control to larger teams of complex robots without prohibitive computational costs. This methodological approach could be influential for other multi-agent control problems exhibiting similar sparse coupling structures.

2. **Enabling Advanced Legged Robot Applications:** Legged robots are designed for mobility in complex, unstructured environments where wheeled platforms may struggle. Equipping them with collaborative loco-manipulation capabilities, as demonstrated here, expands their utility for tasks in logistics, construction, disaster response, and mining, which often involve transporting heavy or awkward payloads over challenging terrain. This work pushes the boundary of what legged robots can collectively achieve in practical scenarios.

3. **Real-time and Robust Operation:** Achieving real-time planning frequencies (e.g., 50-100 Hz) for complex, high-DOF robotic systems is crucial for dynamic environments where rapid adaptation is necessary. The framework's demonstrated real-time performance, combined with its robustness to model uncertainties and diverse rough terrains, enhances the reliability and practical deployability of multi-quadruped systems. This is particularly important for real-world scenarios where perfect environmental models or payload parameters are rarely available.

4. **Integrated Planning and Control Pipeline:** The seamless integration of the distributed MPC planner with a wrench-aware Whole-Body Controller is a critical aspect. This ensures that the high-level optimal plans, including desired motion and interaction forces, are faithfully executed by the robot's physical hardware. Elevating wrench tracking to the highest priority in the WBC's task hierarchy is a specific design insight that guarantees force consistency, which is vital for stable and safe manipulation of shared loads.

5. **Methodological Generalizability:** The specific ADMM decomposition strategy, particularly the treatment of the payload as an independent subsystem and the enforcement of consensus primarily on interaction wrenches while approximating state copies, provides a template for addressing other coupled multi-agent optimal control problems. This approach judiciously balances computational efficiency with the preservation of critical dynamic interactions.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several avenues for future work, including validation on physical hardware platforms, exploring GPU implementations for further computational acceleration, and leveraging the framework to guide decentralized reinforcement learning training. This indicates the potential of this work to serve as a strong foundation for continued advancements in collaborative robotics.
