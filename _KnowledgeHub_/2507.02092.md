# Energy-Based Transformers are Scalable Learners and Thinkers

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs), a new class of models that enable scalable System 2 thinking through unsupervised learning by reframing prediction as an optimization process over a learned energy function. EBTs demonstrate superior scaling rates compared to standard Transformers in language and video, improve performance by up to 29% with increased inference-time computation, and achieve better generalization on out-of-distribution data across diverse modalities.

## Problem

- Current Transformer-based models and LLMs excel at 'System 1 thinking' (fast pattern recognition) but struggle with 'System 2 thinking' (slow, deliberate reasoning, complex math, OOD data).
- Existing System 2 approaches are often modality/problem-specific, require additional supervision (e.g., RL, human feedback), or lack dynamic computation allocation.
- Traditional EBMs have faced issues with scalability, stability, and long training times, preventing their use in foundation models.

## Method

- Develop ==Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs)== that combine the scalability of Transformers with the verification capabilities of Energy-Based Models (EBMs).
- Implement prediction as an ==iterative gradient descent optimization== on a learned energy function, allowing for dynamic computation per prediction and explicit uncertainty modeling.
- Introduce ==energy landscape regularization techniques== (e.g., replay buffer, Langevin Dynamics, varying optimization paths) and optimize training for second-order derivatives to ensure scalable and stable EBM learning.

## Results

- Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs) show up to a **35%** higher scaling rate with respect to data than Transformer++ on autoregressive language modeling, indicating improved data efficiency.
- EBTs improve language modeling performance by up to **29%** and significantly outperform Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) in image denoising (**99%** fewer forward passes) and representation learning (**10x** higher ImageNet-1k accuracy) by leveraging inference-time 'thinking'.
- EBTs exhibit greater performance improvements on out-of-distribution data (e.g., in language tasks and image denoising), demonstrating enhanced generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing prediction as an iterative optimization process over a learned energy function allows models to exhibit System 2 thinking facets: dynamic computation allocation, uncertainty modeling, and intrinsic prediction verification.
> - Energy-Based Models (EBMs) can be scaled using Transformer architectures and novel regularization techniques, overcoming historical scalability and stability challenges.
> - The ability to 'think longer' and self-verify through energy minimization leads to improved generalization, particularly on out-of-distribution data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current AI research landscape is heavily dominated by large language models (LLMs) and generative models, primarily built upon the Transformer architecture. While these models excel at "System 1 thinking" – rapid, intuitive pattern recognition and generation – they notoriously struggle with "System 2 thinking" – slow, deliberate, and logical reasoning requiring conscious effort and iterative refinement. This limitation manifests in areas like complex mathematics, robust coding, multi-step reasoning, and handling out-of-distribution (OOD) data.

Existing attempts to imbue AI models with System 2 capabilities generally fall into a few categories, each with limitations:
- **Modality-Specific/Problem-Specific Approaches:** Many "reasoning models" (e.g., OpenAI O1, DeepSeek-R1, Grok 3, Claude 3.7 Sonnet) are highly effective in verifiable domains like math and coding, often leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL) with rule-based rewards. However, their applicability is narrow, and performance can degrade in other tasks like creative writing, suggesting they might increase the probability of known reasoning patterns rather than inducing new ones.
- **External Supervision/Training:** RL-based methods often require additional supervision (human feedback, verifiable rewards) beyond unsupervised pretraining. This dependency limits their generalization to domains where such explicit verification is difficult or unavailable.
- **Architectural Limitations:**
 - **Traditional Transformers & RNNs:** These models typically perform a fixed amount of computation per prediction, making them unable to dynamically allocate more "thinking time" to complex problems. They also struggle with reliable uncertainty estimation in continuous state spaces.
 - **Diffusion Models:** While offering iterative inference through denoising steps, they primarily learn to predict noise rather than an explicit likelihood or energy. They often fail to benefit from steps beyond their training regime and typically require external verifiers to enhance System 2 capabilities. Their reliance on a fixed denoising schedule also limits adaptive computation.
 - **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) with Dynamic Compute:** Some RNN variants (e.g., Universal Transformer, looped transformers) enable iterative computation, but often amortize gradient prediction of an energy function, preventing explicit uncertainty modeling or prediction verification.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by asking if System 2 thinking can emerge *solely* from unsupervised learning, without modality or problem specificity, and without additional supervision. It proposes that this is possible by fundamentally reframing prediction as an optimization process over a learned "verifier" or Energy-Based Model (EBM). EBMs learn an energy function that quantifies the compatibility between an input and a candidate prediction. By performing gradient descent on this energy landscape, the model iteratively refines its predictions, mimicking System 2 thinking. This approach allows for dynamic computation, explicit uncertainty modeling, and intrinsic prediction verification—the three "facets of System 2 Thinking" identified by the authors.

The work builds upon the theoretical foundation of EBMs, particularly Yilun Du's prior research on learning iterative reasoning through energy minimization. However, it takes a significant step forward by integrating EBMs with the highly scalable Transformer architecture, overcoming traditional EBM scalability issues, and demonstrating their effectiveness across diverse modalities and at scales previously unachieved for EBMs. By showing that EBTs not only scale efficiently during training but also exhibit superior "thinking scalability" and generalization, this research presents a promising new paradigm that challenges the dominance of conventional autoregressive and diffusion models in their respective capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core research question driving this paper is: **"Is it possible to generalize these System 2 Thinking approaches, and develop models that learn to think solely from unsupervised learning?"**

This question is motivated by the recognized limitations of existing AI models, particularly large language models and generative models, in performing complex, deliberate reasoning (System 2 thinking), despite their impressive System 1 capabilities. The authors identify several specific motivations and objectives:

- **Generalization of System 2 Thinking:** Current System 2 approaches are often limited to specific modalities (e.g., text) or problem domains (e.g., math, coding) where explicit, verifiable rewards or external supervision are easily obtained. The authors seek a universal approach that is modality- and problem-agnostic.
- **Unsupervised Learning of Thinking:** A key constraint is to achieve System 2 capabilities purely from unsupervised pretraining, avoiding the need for additional human feedback, reward models, or external verifiers. This aligns with the long-term goal of AI systems that can learn to think on their own.
- **Dynamic Allocation of Computation (Facet 1):** Humans naturally adjust their computational effort based on task difficulty. The objective is to enable models to dynamically allocate resources *per prediction*, allowing them to "think longer" on challenging or novel problems.
- **Modeling Uncertainty in Continuous State Spaces (Facet 2):** Traditional generative models often struggle to provide reliable uncertainty estimates, especially in continuous domains like vision. EBMs, by assigning an unnormalized probability (energy) to input-prediction pairs, naturally model uncertainty, which is crucial for cautious and adaptive behavior.
- **Prediction Verification (Facet 3):** The ability to verify one's own predictions is fundamental to effective reasoning, guiding decisions on when to stop thinking or which prediction is most accurate. The paper aims to build models that learn an explicit verifier, intrinsically coupled with the generation process, unlike approaches requiring separate verifier models.
- **Scalability of EBMs:** Energy-Based Models have historically struggled with scalability, stability, and long training times. A major objective is to address these challenges by integrating EBM principles with the highly scalable Transformer architecture, demonstrating that EBMs can form the basis of "Foundation EBMs."
- **Improved Learning and Thinking Scaling Rates:** Beyond just enabling System 2 thinking, the authors aim to show that their proposed Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs) can scale *faster* than dominant Transformer architectures during pretraining (learning scalability) and exhibit superior performance gains with increased inference-time computation (thinking scalability).
- **Enhanced Generalization, especially to OOD Data:** System 2 thinking in humans is crucial for generalizing to novel or out-of-distribution situations. The paper aims to demonstrate that EBTs achieve better generalization, particularly on OOD data, by leveraging their intrinsic thinking capabilities.

In essence, the motivation is to develop a new paradigm for AI that more closely mimics human-like reasoning, allowing models to learn to *think* and *understand* their own predictions, rather than merely generating them, all while being inherently scalable and generalizable across diverse domains without external supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this paper revolves around the development of **Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs)**, which reframe the prediction problem as an optimization procedure with respect to a learned verifier. This approach leverages the principles of Energy-Based Models (EBMs) and integrates them with the scalable Transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. Energy-Based Models (EBMs) as Verifiers

- **Foundation:** EBMs learn an energy function $E_\theta(x, \hat{y})$ that assigns a scalar energy value to any given input ($x$) and candidate-prediction ($\hat{y}$) pair. Lower energy indicates higher compatibility or likelihood. This energy function intrinsically acts as a verifier of the coherence between $x$ and $\hat{y}$.
- **Prediction as Optimization:** Unlike traditional generative models that directly predict $\hat{y}$, EBTs make predictions by starting from an initial random candidate $\hat{y}_0$ (e.g., random noise) and iteratively refining it. This refinement is achieved through gradient descent on the learned energy landscape: $\hat{y}_{i+1} = \hat{y}_i - \alpha \nabla_{\hat{y}_i} E_\theta(x, \hat{y}_i)$, where $\alpha$ is the step size. This iterative process simulates "thinking" by progressively minimizing energy until convergence.

#### 3.2. Achieving System 2 Thinking Facets

This EBM-based optimization framework naturally supports the three identified facets of System 2 thinking:
- **Dynamic Allocation of Computation (Facet 1):** By allowing the model to perform multiple iterative optimization steps, EBTs can dynamically adjust the amount of computation ("thinking time") spent on each prediction. More steps can be allocated for harder problems or when higher precision is required.
- **Modeling Uncertainty (Facet 2):** The energy scalar $E_\theta(x, \hat{y})$ itself serves as an unnormalized likelihood. A higher energy for a particular prediction or slow convergence of energy during optimization indicates greater uncertainty. This provides a direct measure of prediction confidence, crucial for continuous state spaces where explicit likelihoods are difficult to model.
- **Prediction Verification (Facet 3):** At each step of the optimization, the EBM provides an energy scalar, acting as an explicit verifier of the current prediction's quality. This enables dynamic inference behaviors such as early stopping or selecting the best prediction from multiple candidates (self-verification). The authors emphasize that verification is often exponentially easier and generalizes better than generation.

#### 3.3. Scalable EBM Learning

Traditional EBMs have struggled with scalability due to challenges in training stability and long training times (e.g., contrastive methods grappling with the curse of dimensionality). The authors propose to train EBMs by framing learning as an optimization problem:
- **Optimization-Based Training:** The EBM is trained to optimize an initial prediction towards the ground truth solution. This implicitly regularizes the energy landscape, forcing it to be convex around the true data manifold.
- **Second-Order Derivatives:** Backpropagating the loss through the entire optimization process requires second-order derivatives (gradients of gradients). The authors note that these are computed efficiently via Hessian-vector products, which scale linearly with model size, similar to standard first-order backpropagation.
- **Loss Functions:** Standard loss functions are used (e.g., categorical cross-entropy for language modeling, mean squared error for image denoising). The loss is applied to the final prediction after the optimization steps.

#### 3.4. Energy Landscape Regularization Techniques (for Scalable EBM Thinking)

To ensure smooth and convex energy landscapes conducive to strong System 2 thinking, the authors introduce three key regularization techniques:
- **Replay Buffer:** Helps simulate longer optimization trajectories, ensuring the energy landscape is well-defined near its minimum.
- **Langevin Dynamics:** Adds random noise to the gradient descent steps ($\hat{y}_{i+1} = \hat{y}_i - \alpha \nabla_{\hat{y}_i} E_\theta(x, \hat{y}_i) + \eta_i$, where $\eta_i \sim N(0, \sigma)$). This encourages broader exploration of the energy landscape, preventing optimization from getting stuck in poorly defined regions.
- **Varying Optimization Paths:** Randomizing the gradient descent step size ($\alpha$) and the number of optimization steps taken during training significantly improves generalization.

#### 3.5. Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs) Architecture

- **Transformer Backbone:** EBTs leverage the Transformer architecture (attention mechanisms, parallelizability, stability, scalability) to overcome historical EBM challenges.
- **Variants:** Two main variants are developed:
 - **Decoder-only EBT:** Inspired by GPT, designed for autoregressive modeling, parallelizing all predictions simultaneously. This involves non-trivial implementation challenges to prevent information leakage while processing predicted states in the input space.
 - **Bidirectional EBT:** Similar to BERT or Diffusion Transformers (DiT), enabling bidirectional attention across entire sequences for tasks like masked modeling or infilling.
- **Efficient Implementation:** The paper details a custom, efficient implementation for autoregressive EBTs that manages the complex attention calculations when predictions are made in the input space, effectively doubling the sequence length.

#### 3.6. "Thinking" Approaches During Inference

- **Thinking Longer:** Increasing the number of optimization steps for a single prediction.
- **Self-Verification (Best-of-N):** Generating multiple candidate predictions from an EBM (each through its own optimization process) and then choosing the one with the minimum energy (highest compatibility). This generalizes Best of N (BoN) sampling without requiring external verification.

Overall, the methodology combines the interpretability and verification capabilities of EBMs with the architectural strengths of Transformers, augmented by novel regularization techniques, to enable intrinsically unsupervised System 2 thinking that scales both in learning and inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling experimental results across discrete (text) and continuous (visual) modalities, evaluating both learning and thinking scalability.

#### 4.1. Autoregressive Language Modeling (Text)

- **Learning Scalability (Pretraining):**
 - EBTs consistently demonstrate a **higher scaling rate** (faster performance improvement with increased resources) than the Transformer++ recipe across six different axes: data, batch size, depth, parameters, FLOPs, and embedding dimension.
 - Notably, EBTs achieve an **up to 35% higher scaling rate with respect to data**, implying significantly improved data efficiency. This is a critical finding as data scarcity is becoming a bottleneck for scaling LLMs.
 - The authors claim EBTs are the "first approach to out-scale the standard feed-forward Transformer++ recipe across several modalities and axes" without using a different tokenizer.
 - This suggests that at the scale of modern foundation models (e.g., Llama3), EBTs would likely achieve significantly better pretraining performance.
- **Thinking Scalability (Inference):**
 - EBTs can **improve language modeling performance by as much as 29%** by increasing inference-time computation ("thinking longer" through more optimization steps and "self-verification" by selecting the minimum energy prediction from multiple candidates).
 - In contrast, the **Transformer++ shows no performance improvement** with increased inference-time computation on a per-token level, reinforcing the claim that traditional models cannot dynamically allocate computation for deeper reasoning.
 - The thinking capabilities of EBTs **scale with pretraining data**: the benefits from self-verification (BoN sampling) increase as EBTs are trained on larger datasets (e.g., from 4-8% to 10-14% improvement with more data).
 - **Uncertainty Modeling (Facet 2):** EBTs learn to capture uncertainty without explicit supervision. Easier-to-predict tokens (e.g., "the", "but") result in faster energy convergence to lower values, while harder-to-predict tokens (e.g., "fox", "problem") exhibit higher energies and slower convergence, indicating greater uncertainty.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Data:**
 - System 2 thinking with EBTs yields **larger performance improvements on data that is farther OOD**. A strong linear trend is observed between the magnitude of OOD shift and the performance gains from thinking longer and self-verification.
 - Despite having **slightly worse pretraining perplexity** than Transformer++ models with identical training setups, EBTs consistently achieve **lower (better) perplexity on most downstream tasks** (GSM8K, BigBench Math QA, BigBench Dyck Languages), indicating superior generalization capabilities.

#### 4.2. Autoregressive Video Experiments (Continuous Modality)

- **Learning Scalability:** EBTs exhibit a **more than 33% faster scaling rate** than the Transformer++ for next-frame prediction on the Something Something V2 dataset, specifically concerning embedding dimension and non-embedding parameter count. This suggests EBTs would significantly outperform Transformers at larger scales in video.
- **Uncertainty Modeling (Facet 2):** EBTs successfully learn to express uncertainty across continuous video frames. Scenes with high unpredictability (e.g., empty frames) show higher energy (uncertainty), which decreases as predictable objects appear and increases again when they leave, demonstrating implicit uncertainty capture in continuous spaces.

#### 4.3. Bidirectional Image Experiments (Image Denoising & Classification)

- **Image Denoising:**
 - EBTs significantly **outperform Diffusion Transformers (DiTs)** in Peak Signal to Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Mean Squared Error (MSE) on both in-distribution and OOD (higher noise) image denoising.
 - Crucially, EBTs achieve this while using **99% fewer forward passes** than DiTs (e.g., 3 passes vs. 300 for comparable or better performance).
 - The **System 2 thinking scaling rate** (performance improvement with more forward passes) is much higher for EBTs than for DiTs, indicating more efficient use of additional computation.
 - Qualitative results also show EBT denoised images are less blurry and have better overall visual quality than DiT images.
- **Image Representation Learning (Classification):**
 - EBTs learn **significantly better image representations** than DiTs, achieving approximately **10x higher Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy** on a linear probe evaluation on ImageNet-1k, suggesting EBTs develop a deeper "understanding" of images through their verification mechanism.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on System 2 Thinking

- Ablation studies confirm the importance of the proposed energy landscape regularization techniques (random step size, random number of steps, Langevin Dynamics, replay buffer) for maximizing System 2 thinking performance, particularly when combining "thinking longer" and "self-verification." Removing these techniques degrades performance gains from thinking.

In summary, the results strongly support the paper's central hypotheses: EBTs are more scalable learners, demonstrate robust and scalable System 2 thinking capabilities (dynamic compute, uncertainty, verification), and generalize better, especially to OOD data, compared to current state-of-the-art Transformer and Diffusion Transformer architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "Energy-Based Transformers are Scalable Learners and Thinkers" represent a significant advancement in the field of AI, particularly in the pursuit of more human-like intelligence. The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:

#### 5.1. A New Paradigm for Foundation Models

The most profound impact lies in positioning EBTs as a **promising new paradigm for scaling both the learning and thinking capabilities of models**.
- **Superior Scaling Laws:** The consistent out-scaling of EBTs over the Transformer++ across various axes (data, batch size, depth, parameters, FLOPs) suggests that at the scale of modern foundation models (trillions of tokens, hundreds of billions of parameters), EBTs could fundamentally outperform current architectures even without their System 2 capabilities. This implies a more efficient use of computational resources and data.
- **Data Efficiency:** The finding that EBTs are more data-efficient is crucial, as data availability is becoming a major bottleneck for further AI scaling. EBTs could enable training more capable models with less data.
- **Unified Framework for Learning and Thinking:** By intrinsically linking prediction to an optimization process via a learned verifier, EBTs provide a more unified and principled way for models to "learn to think" from unsupervised data alone, addressing a long-standing challenge in AI.

#### 5.2. Enabling Robust System 2 Thinking

- **Intrinsic Dynamic Compute, Uncertainty, and Verification:** EBTs natively incorporate dynamic computation allocation, explicit uncertainty modeling, and self-verification, which are critical for tackling complex, real-world problems. This addresses a core limitation of many existing models that either lack these capabilities or require external, often problem-specific, supervision.
- **Improved Generalization to OOD Data:** The demonstration that System 2 thinking in EBTs leads to greater performance improvements on OOD data is highly significant. This suggests a path towards models that are not just good at memorizing training distributions but can genuinely reason and adapt to novel scenarios, mirroring human cognitive abilities.
- **Reliable Self-Correction:** The observed decrease in "adversarial dynamics" (i.e., less likelihood of selecting poor samples with low energy) as EBTs scale indicates that their self-verification process becomes more reliable and robust, overcoming issues reported in prior work on decoupled verifier-generator systems.

#### 5.3. Advancing Generative Models

- **Beyond Diffusion Models:** EBTs' superior performance in image denoising with significantly fewer forward passes compared to DiTs suggests a more efficient and powerful approach for generative tasks in continuous domains.
- **Better Representations and Understanding:** The fact that EBTs learn significantly better image representations (as evidenced by 10x higher ImageNet-1k classification accuracy) indicates that their learning mechanism (optimization on an energy landscape) fosters a deeper "understanding" of the data rather than just superficial generation. This could lead to generative models that are not only capable of high-quality synthesis but also exhibit stronger discriminative abilities and a better grasp of the content they create, addressing "The Generative AI Paradox."

#### 5.4. Addressing Fundamental AI Challenges

- **Reversal Curse (Future Work):** The hypothesis that EBTs might mitigate the "reversal curse" in LLMs by making predictions in the input space and allowing for bidirectional gradient updates is a fascinating direction that could fundamentally improve relational understanding and generalization.
- **World Models and Decision Making:** The discussion of EBTs' potential in world models for planning and decision-making by optimizing actions to achieve target states is a compelling vision, offering a route to more sophisticated embodied AI and reinforcement learning applications.
- **Complementary AI Systems:** EBTs could serve as "System 2 Backbones" complementing lighter, "System 1" models, creating hybrid architectures that balance speed with depth of reasoning.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges several limitations. EBTs introduce more hyperparameters, leading to increased tuning complexity and sensitivity to chosen values. Training and inference are more computationally expensive than standard feed-forward models due to additional gradient computations and second-order derivatives, though the efficient Hessian-vector products mitigate this. The largest models tested are 800M parameters, so scaling trends at the trillion-parameter level remain unverified (though the observed scaling rates strongly suggest positive outcomes). Finally, EBTs currently struggle with highly multimodal distributions (e.g., class-conditional image generation with many modes), indicating that the convex energy landscape assumption needs further refinement for such scenarios.

Despite these limitations, the paper makes a strong case for EBTs as a paradigm shift. By demonstrating principled ways to scale both learning and thinking, and showing improved generalization and understanding across diverse modalities, EBTs pave the way for a new generation of AI models that are not just powerful generators but also capable, verifiable, and adaptive thinkers.
