# Energy-Based Transformers are Scalable Learners and Thinkers

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs), a new class of models that enable scalable System 2 thinking through unsupervised learning by reframing prediction as an optimization process over a learned energy function. EBTs demonstrate superior scaling rates compared to standard Transformers in language and video, improve performance by up to 29% with increased inference-time computation, and achieve better generalization on out-of-distribution data across diverse modalities.

## Problem

- Current Transformer-based models and LLMs excel at 'System 1 thinking' (fast pattern recognition) but struggle with 'System 2 thinking' (slow, deliberate reasoning, complex math, OOD data).
- Existing System 2 approaches are often modality/problem-specific, require additional supervision (e.g., RL, human feedback), or lack dynamic computation allocation.
- Traditional EBMs have faced issues with scalability, stability, and long training times, preventing their use in foundation models.

## Method

- Develop ==Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs)== that combine the scalability of Transformers with the verification capabilities of Energy-Based Models (EBMs).
- Implement prediction as an ==iterative gradient descent optimization== on a learned energy function, allowing for dynamic computation per prediction and explicit uncertainty modeling.
- Introduce ==energy landscape regularization techniques== (e.g., replay buffer, Langevin Dynamics, varying optimization paths) and optimize training for second-order derivatives to ensure scalable and stable EBM learning.

## Results

- Energy-Based Transformers (EBTs) show up to a **35%** higher scaling rate with respect to data than Transformer++ on autoregressive language modeling, indicating improved data efficiency.
- EBTs improve language modeling performance by up to **29%** and significantly outperform Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) in image denoising (**99%** fewer forward passes) and representation learning (**10x** higher ImageNet-1k accuracy) by leveraging inference-time 'thinking'.
- EBTs exhibit greater performance improvements on out-of-distribution data (e.g., in language tasks and image denoising), demonstrating enhanced generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing prediction as an iterative optimization process over a learned energy function allows models to exhibit System 2 thinking facets: dynamic computation allocation, uncertainty modeling, and intrinsic prediction verification.
> - Energy-Based Models (EBMs) can be scaled using Transformer architectures and novel regularization techniques, overcoming historical scalability and stability challenges.
> - The ability to 'think longer' and self-verify through energy minimization leads to improved generalization, particularly on out-of-distribution data.
