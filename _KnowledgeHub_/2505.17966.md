# Is Single-View Mesh Reconstruction Ready for Robotics?

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical study evaluates the suitability of state-of-the-art single-view 3D mesh reconstruction models for generating digital twins in robotic applications, revealing significant shortfalls in meeting requirements for reconstruction accuracy, physical plausibility, and computational efficiency in realistic environments. Researchers found that models often exceed the 2mm accuracy target and produce unstable or colliding meshes, hindering their use in physics-based simulation for tasks like real-time planning.

## Problem

- Robot planning and learning often rely on 'learnt world models' which suffer from generalization issues, high data requirements, and limited physical interpretability.
- Traditional methods for creating 'digital twins' for physics-based robot simulation are manual and labor-intensive, preventing their use in online, real-time robotics workflows.
- While single-view 3D reconstruction offers automated scene understanding, its specific suitability for the stringent demands of robotic manipulation, such as physical fidelity and computational immediacy, remained largely unevaluated.

## Method

- The authors defined ==five robotics-specific desiderata== for single-view 3D mesh reconstruction: accuracy (<2mm Chamfer distance), no object collisions, physical object stability (within 5° tilt), robust partial occlusion resolution (occluded error <10% higher than visible), and computational efficiency (<2s for scene).
- They conducted an ==empirical evaluation== of 12 state-of-the-art ==single-view object-level and scene-level mesh reconstruction models==.
- Models were tested on two real-world datasets, ==YCB-Video== and ==Project Aria Digital Twin==, using quantitative metrics for each desideratum, including ==Chamfer distance==, collision checks, stability simulations, and reconstruction times.

## Results

- Most models failed to achieve the target **2mm** reconstruction accuracy, exhibiting median Chamfer distances of approximately **5mm** on YCB-Video and up to **1cm** on the Aria dataset, leading to low grasp transfer success rates (around **50%**).
- A majority of reconstructed scenes contained colliding objects and produced unstable object poses, making them unsuitable for direct use in physics simulations without extensive post-processing.
- Computational efficiency was a major limitation, with most models taking multiple seconds per object or tens/hundreds of seconds for entire scenes, far exceeding the **2-second** target for real-time robotic deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap exists between the performance of single-view 3D reconstruction models on computer vision benchmarks and the practical requirements for robotics applications.
> - Future research needs to focus on physics-aware reconstruction, developing models that inherently avoid collisions and generate stable object poses, potentially by integrating differentiable physics.
> - Robotics-relevant training datasets, incorporating diverse capture conditions (sensor noise, non-canonical views) and real-world scene complexities (occlusions, support relationships), are crucial for advancing model capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research evaluates the suitability of single-view 3D mesh reconstruction models for creating digital twins in robotics, specifically for real-time planning and dynamics prediction in physics simulators. The broader landscape involves ongoing efforts to develop robust robot planning and learning systems capable of simulating future world states from perception.

Historically, robot planning and learning have leveraged "learnt world models"—neural networks that predict forward dynamics from raw observations. However, these models often exhibit poor generalization, opaque failure modes, high data requirements, and limited physical interpretability. An alternative approach involves creating a "digital twin" in simulation, a geometric reconstruction of a physical scene or object that allows robots to reason using physics-based simulators. Traditionally, creating such digital twins has been a labor-intensive, manual process.

Recent advances in "real-to-sim" pipelines have focused on automating this process. Early progress in this area primarily utilized multi-view 3D reconstruction methods, which require comprehensive scene coverage from multiple viewpoints. Parallel to this, the computer vision community has achieved significant advancements in category-agnostic, single-view, full-shape 3D mesh reconstruction, inferring complete object geometry, including unobserved surfaces, from a single image.

This paper addresses a critical gap by assessing whether these single-view reconstruction advances, which relax the multi-view requirement, are adequate for robotic manipulation tasks. Prior work often assumes dense viewpoints or focuses on domain-randomized environments rather than exact digital twins, or lacks comprehensive robotic experimentation. This study aims to bridge the gap between computer vision progress in single-view 3D reconstruction and the specific, stringent requirements of robotic applications, particularly regarding physical fidelity, immediacy, and simulation readiness. It complements existing surveys that broadly categorize 3D reconstruction methods or focus on specific representations, by providing a focused, empirical evaluation of single-view mesh reconstruction models against robotics-specific desiderata.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central research question driving this work is: "Can recent advancements in single-view mesh reconstruction models be effectively applied to create accurate digital twin environments for online, real-time, and high-throughput robotic manipulation?"

The motivation for addressing this question stems from several challenges and opportunities in robotics:
- **Limitations of Learned World Models:** While promising, learned world models for robot planning and control suffer from generalization issues, data intensity, and lack of physical interpretability. Digital twins offer an alternative for physics-based reasoning.
- **Manual Digital Twin Creation:** Traditional methods for creating simulation environments are labor-intensive and costly, hindering their integration into online robotics workflows like model-predictive control, planning, and real-time learning.
- **Need for Automated Real-to-Sim:** A general, automated pipeline that maps RGB(-D) observations into a simulation instance by reconstructing individual, physically plausible, and complete 3D meshes is highly desirable for scene-agnostic and robust dynamics prediction.
- **Advantages of Single-View Reconstruction:** Relaxing the multi-view requirement offers significant practical benefits for robotics. Collecting multiple viewpoints often requires physical robot movement, which introduces delays, viewpoint planning challenges, and collision risks. In certain scenarios, such as cluttered drawers or objects on shelves, obtaining multiple views may be physically impossible without prior interaction. Single-view approaches offer algorithmic simplicity and are crucial for real-time workflows where viewpoint acquisition time directly impacts task performance.
- **Underexplored Suitability:** Despite advances in computer vision for single-view 3D reconstruction, their specific suitability for physics simulations and robotics applications under practical constraints (immediacy, physical fidelity, simulation readiness) remains largely underexplored.

To systematically address the research question, the authors formulate five robotics-specific desiderata for single-view 3D reconstruction models:

1. **Reconstruction Accuracy:** Chamfer distances between reconstructed and ground truth meshes should be within 2mm. This is driven by the high precision required for robot manipulation tasks (e.g., 1mm for grasping, sub-millimeter for assembly).
2. **Object Collision Constraints:** No reconstructed object should violate space occupied by other objects or parts of the environment. This is critical for physical stability and accurate interaction simulation, as collisions at initialization can cause simulator breakdown.
3. **Object Stability:** Reconstructed meshes should offer physically stable poses within a 5-degree tilt of their scene pose. This ensures objects remain in a stable resting state and scene integrity is maintained during physics simulation, assuming observed scenes are static.
4. **Partial Occlusion Resolution:** For common household objects, reconstructed meshes should resolve partial occlusions with Chamfer distances no more than 10% higher than those of visible regions when compared to ground truth. This is necessary for accurate simulation, as occlusions are ubiquitous in real-world environments and affect object completeness.
5. **Computational Efficiency:** The reconstruction pipeline should produce a complete scene reconstruction within 2 seconds, even for multiple objects. This is crucial for online, real-time robotic deployment, which often has limited on-board compute and cannot afford long latency or reliance on off-device servers.

The overall aim is to inform robotics practitioners and computer vision researchers about existing capability gaps, guiding tool selection and identifying areas for future research at the intersection of these fields.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs an empirical evaluation methodology to assess selected state-of-the-art single-view 3D reconstruction models against the formulated robotics-specific desiderata.

**Model Selection Criteria:** The evaluation focuses on models that:
1. **Work with single-view inputs:** Essential for real-time robotics workflows where multi-view acquisition is impractical or impossible.
2. **Produce complete meshes for individual objects:** Meshes are the de-facto standard for rigid body simulation in most robotics simulators.
3. **Are trained for category-agnostic reconstruction:** Necessary for handling the diverse range of objects encountered in real-world robotics.

The authors explicitly exclude models that generate 3D structures solely from text, require multiple or dense viewpoints, or rely on slow optimization-based routines (e.g., score distillation) due to computational constraints. Models without publicly available open-source implementations were also excluded.

**Evaluated Models:**
- **Object-Level Reconstruction Models:**
 - SF3D
 - InstantMesh
 - One2345
 - LGM
 - Michelangelo
 - ZeroShape
 - Real3D
 - DSO
- **Scene-Level Reconstruction Models:**
 - MIDI
 - Gen3DSR
 - PhysGen3D

**Evaluation Data:**
Two richly annotated real-world datasets representative of robotics applications were used:
1. **YCB-Video dataset:** Features standardized objects with varying geometric complexity, common in robotic manipulation benchmarks.
2. **Project Aria Digital Twin dataset:** Contains everyday household items in naturalistic, cluttered configurations.

Both datasets provide ground-truth meshes, object poses, segmentation masks, and camera intrinsics, enabling quantitative evaluation against true object geometry. Objects and frames were manually selected to cover different types, viewpoints, and poses.

**Experimental Setup and Common Preprocessing:**
- All models received identical single-view inputs.
- Data preparation, including cropping and resizing objects, followed the routines provided by each model.
- Ground-truth object masks were provided for all methods relying on off-the-shelf segmentation models to ensure a consistent comparison.
- **Mesh Alignment Procedure:**
 - **Scale Estimation:** Standard deviations of mesh vertices along the top three principal components were used to estimate a rescaling parameter as the median ratio between ground truth and reconstructed meshes.
 - **Translation and Rotation Alignment:** 512 approximately equidistant unit quaternions were sampled from the 3-sphere as initializations for an Iterative Closest Point (ICP) optimization. The transformation with the lowest root mean squared error was selected to avoid local optima.

**Evaluation of Desiderata:**
Each desideratum was assessed quantitatively:
- **Reconstruction Accuracy (Desideratum 1):**
 - Evaluated on 177 entirely visible objects from YCB-Video and 136 from Aria.
 - Accuracy measured using **Chamfer distance (CD)** between 10,000 uniformly sampled points from the surfaces of ground truth and reconstructed meshes.
 - For scene reconstruction models, CD was computed for the entire scene, aligned as a whole.
 - Grasp transfer success rates (using) from reconstructed to ground truth meshes were also evaluated on YCB-Video to assess practical accuracy for manipulation.
- **Object Collision Constraints (Desideratum 2):**
 - Evaluated on 96 object pairs from YCB-Video and 64 from Aria.
 - Objects were reconstructed individually, placed at their scene poses (from ICP), and collisions between them were computed using **FCL**.
- **Object Stability (Desideratum 3):**
 - Evaluated on 146 YCB-Video samples and 133 Aria samples for objects resting on planes.
 - Stable orientations were computed as those where the center of mass is within the 2D convex hull of minimal-elevation vertices.
 - P.oses were considered stable if the z-axis was at most 5 degrees tilted from the ground truth and if, after 8 equally spaced 5-degree perturbations, the object reverted to its unperturbed pose in a **PyBullet** physics simulation.
- **Partial Occlusion Resolution (Desideratum 4):**
 - Evaluated on 80 partially occluded objects from YCB-Video and 76 from Aria.
 - The ICP alignment procedure was modified to ignore occluded points (both ground truth and reconstruction) using object masks, re-computed at each iteration. Symmetric objects were corrected for rotation misalignment.
 - Chamfer distances of unoccluded object parts were compared with those of occluded parts.
- **Computational Efficiency (Desideratum 5):**
 - Mean reconstruction time per object and peak memory allocation (VRAM) were measured during inference.
 - Experiments were run on a server node with four 2.1GHz CPU cores, 20GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU with 48GB VRAM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation revealed significant limitations in the current state of single-view 3D mesh reconstruction models for robotics applications.

**a) Reconstruction Accuracy (Desideratum 1: < 2mm Chamfer Distance)**
- **Object-Level Models:** On YCB-Video, most models (except SF3D and DSO) achieved median Chamfer distances of approximately 5mm. On the Aria Digital Twin dataset, accuracy was significantly worse, with median errors roughly double, reaching about 1cm. This substantially exceeds the 2mm target for robotic manipulation. Models struggled particularly with objects in non-canonical poses.
- **Grasp Transfer:** Grasp poses computed on reconstructed meshes showed low transfer success rates (around 50%) to ground truth meshes on YCB-Video (Fig. 3). This indicates insufficient accuracy for practical manipulation tasks.
- **Scene-Level Models:** On YCB-Video, scene reconstruction models performed worse than single-object models, with median Chamfer distances between 1cm and 1.5cm (Fig. 6). This level of error is too large for robust robotic manipulation and physics simulation.
- **Relative Pose Estimation:** Scene reconstruction models did not preserve relative object poses, with noticeable errors in Chamfer distance differences between object pairs in reconstructed versus ground truth scenes (Fig. 11), often overestimating distances.

**b) Object Collision Constraints (Desideratum 2: No Collisions)**
- **Object-Level Models:** A majority of reconstructed scenes from single-object models (SF3D, InstantMesh, One2345, LGM, Michelangelo, ZeroShape, Real3D, DSO) exhibited mesh collisions (Fig. 5). Such collisions at initialization would cause unwanted behavior (e.g., instantaneous acceleration, scene collapse) in physics simulations.
- **Scene-Level Models:** Even scene reconstruction models, which process entire scenes, showed collision frequencies comparable to or only slightly better than single-object models (Fig. 13). Only PhysGen3D exhibited significantly reduced collisions, which the authors suggest might be an artifact of it overestimating relative object distances (Fig. 11).

**c) Object Stability (Desideratum 3: Stable Poses within 5° Tilt)**
- **Object-Level Models:** The vast majority of YCB-Video reconstructions did not yield stable poses near the observed scene pose (Fig. 4). Michelangelo performed comparatively better on YCB-Video (two-thirds stable), but this advantage did not hold for the Aria dataset, where all models produced even fewer stable reconstructions (Fig. 15). This means most reconstructed meshes would be unusable in physics simulations without substantial post-processing, as they would topple over.
- **Scene-Level Models:** Scene reconstruction models also showed low stability. PhysGen3D had the fewest stable meshes among all evaluated models (Fig. 8). While individual alignment of reconstructed objects to ground truth improved stability (Fig. 9), it indicated that the scene reconstruction models struggled with accurate object pose estimation, even if the mesh geometry itself was somewhat stable.

**d) Partial Occlusion Resolution (Desideratum 4: Occluded Error < 10% Higher than Visible)**
- **Object-Level Models:** Occlusion led to significantly increased reconstruction inaccuracy across all single-object models, with median Chamfer distances 40-95% higher in occluded regions compared to unoccluded regions (Fig. 10). This is problematic for robotic manipulation as occluded parts are often relevant and affect simulation accuracy.
- **Scene-Level Models:** Scene reconstruction models performed notably better (Fig. 16). The increase in median Chamfer distance due to occlusion was at most about 10% across all models, with MIDI and PhysGen3D being significantly below this threshold. This suggests that leveraging scene-level context or image inpainting (used by PhysGen3D and Gen3DSR) is beneficial for resolving occlusions.

**e) Computational Efficiency (Desideratum 5: < 2s for Scene Reconstruction)**
- **Object-Level Models:** Most evaluated models required multiple seconds, sometimes tens of seconds, to reconstruct a single object (Fig. 12). SF3D (approx. 0.5s) and ZeroShape (approx. 1s) were faster, but reconstruction time still scales linearly with the number of objects. This exceeds the 2-second target for an entire scene.
- **Scene-Level Models:** Scene reconstruction models required substantially more time (multiple tens or hundreds of seconds) than single-object models to process entire scenes (Fig. 7), making real-time execution impractical.
- **Memory Allocation:** VRAM utilization for most single-object models was below 10GB, aligning with on-robot compute capabilities, but InstantMesh and One2345 required significantly more (up to 25GB). Scene-level models showed comparable VRAM usage but with much longer processing times.
- **Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off:** No clear trend was observed where computationally more expensive models consistently produced more accurate reconstructions.

**Overall:** Despite advances in computer vision benchmarks, existing single-view mesh reconstruction models largely fail to meet robotics-specific requirements for accuracy, collision avoidance, stability, and computational efficiency in realistic robotic environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this paper hold significant implications for both computer vision and robotics research:

- **Identified Capability Gap:** The study rigorously quantifies a substantial gap between the current capabilities of state-of-the-art single-view 3D reconstruction models and the practical needs of robotic manipulation. While these models perform well on computer vision benchmarks, their direct application in robotics for creating physics-compatible digital twins is currently limited due to insufficient accuracy, widespread collision, instability, and poor occlusion handling.
- **Guidance for Robotics Practitioners:** The detailed evaluation provides robotics practitioners with an informed perspective on the current limitations of single-view reconstruction. This knowledge can guide tool selection, highlight areas where post-processing or alternative approaches are necessary, and manage expectations for real-world deployment. The low grasp transfer success rates, for example, directly indicate that current models are not ready for manipulation tasks without further refinement.
- **Directions for Computer Vision Research:** The paper identifies critical areas for future research at the intersection of computer vision and robotics:
 - **Robotics-Relevant Training Data:** A strong recommendation is made for developing training datasets that specifically address robotics constraints. This includes incorporating more diverse capture conditions (sensor noise, varied lighting, non-canonical views, low resolution) and realistic scene compositions (occlusions, support relationships) beyond high-resolution synthetic object datasets.
 - **Physics-Aware Reconstruction:** Future models need to prioritize physical plausibility during reconstruction. This involves developing methods that inherently avoid collisions, generate stable object poses with stricter thresholds, and can integrate differentiable physics simulation more effectively into the learning process. The success of scene-level models in handling occlusions using inpainting or joint denoising highlights the value of incorporating contextual information and physics-aware reasoning.
 - **Enhanced Occlusion Handling:** The significant performance drop in occluded regions necessitates more robust strategies. Physics-aware inpainting and training with varied occlusion patterns could improve how models complete unseen object parts.
 - **Integration of Depth Information:** The paper advocates for explicitly incorporating depth information from RGB-D cameras into reconstruction pipelines. Given the prevalence and cost-effectiveness of these sensors in robotics, leveraging their 2.5D data could resolve ambiguities and provide stronger supervision, even if the depth data is noisy or incomplete.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Continued work on faster, more memory-efficient models is crucial for on-device deployment, as current state-of-the-art models are too slow for real-time robotic feedback.
- **Call for Collaboration:** The findings underscore the need for closer collaboration between the computer vision and robotics communities to develop reconstruction techniques tailored to the unique demands of robotic manipulation. This cross-disciplinary effort is essential to realize the potential of instant digital twin creation for autonomous systems operating in unstructured, real-world environments.

In summary, this work provides a rigorous and quantitative assessment of a promising avenue (single-view 3D reconstruction) for a critical application (robotic digital twins). By clearly articulating current limitations and proposing concrete research directions, it serves as a foundational reference point for advancing the state-of-the-art in automated real-to-sim pipelines for robotics.
