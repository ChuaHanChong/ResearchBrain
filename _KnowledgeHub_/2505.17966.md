# Is Single-View Mesh Reconstruction Ready for Robotics?

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical study evaluates the suitability of state-of-the-art single-view 3D mesh reconstruction models for generating digital twins in robotic applications, revealing significant shortfalls in meeting requirements for reconstruction accuracy, physical plausibility, and computational efficiency in realistic environments. Researchers found that models often exceed the 2mm accuracy target and produce unstable or colliding meshes, hindering their use in physics-based simulation for tasks like real-time planning.

## Problem

- Robot planning and learning often rely on 'learnt world models' which suffer from generalization issues, high data requirements, and limited physical interpretability.
- Traditional methods for creating 'digital twins' for physics-based robot simulation are manual and labor-intensive, preventing their use in online, real-time robotics workflows.
- While single-view 3D reconstruction offers automated scene understanding, its specific suitability for the stringent demands of robotic manipulation, such as physical fidelity and computational immediacy, remained largely unevaluated.

## Method

- The authors defined ==five robotics-specific desiderata== for single-view 3D mesh reconstruction: accuracy (<2mm Chamfer distance), no object collisions, physical object stability (within 5° tilt), robust partial occlusion resolution (occluded error <10% higher than visible), and computational efficiency (<2s for scene).
- They conducted an ==empirical evaluation== of 12 state-of-the-art ==single-view object-level and scene-level mesh reconstruction models==.
- Models were tested on two real-world datasets, ==YCB-Video== and ==Project Aria Digital Twin==, using quantitative metrics for each desideratum, including ==Chamfer distance==, collision checks, stability simulations, and reconstruction times.

## Results

- Most models failed to achieve the target **2mm** reconstruction accuracy, exhibiting median Chamfer distances of approximately **5mm** on YCB-Video and up to **1cm** on the Aria dataset, leading to low grasp transfer success rates (around **50%**).
- A majority of reconstructed scenes contained colliding objects and produced unstable object poses, making them unsuitable for direct use in physics simulations without extensive post-processing.
- Computational efficiency was a major limitation, with most models taking multiple seconds per object or tens/hundreds of seconds for entire scenes, far exceeding the **2-second** target for real-time robotic deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap exists between the performance of single-view 3D reconstruction models on computer vision benchmarks and the practical requirements for robotics applications.
> - Future research needs to focus on physics-aware reconstruction, developing models that inherently avoid collisions and generate stable object poses, potentially by integrating differentiable physics.
> - Robotics-relevant training datasets, incorporating diverse capture conditions (sensor noise, non-canonical views) and real-world scene complexities (occlusions, support relationships), are crucial for advancing model capabilities.
