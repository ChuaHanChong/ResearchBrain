# An Embodied Generalist Agent in 3D World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Beijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence (BIGAI) introduce LEO, an embodied multi-modal generalist agent that perceives, grounds, reasons, plans, and acts in 3D environments. It achieves performance comparable to or exceeding task-specific models across 3D vision-language understanding, scene-grounded dialogue, planning, robotic manipulation, and object navigation tasks.

## Problem

- Current generalist AI models primarily operate in 2D domains, limiting their comprehension and interaction with the 3D physical world.
- Tasks intrinsically defined in 3D, such as 3D grounding, embodied reasoning, navigation, and manipulation, remain largely unexplored by generalist agents.
- Scarcity of large-scale, high-quality 3D-language and 3D-action datasets hinders the development of robust 3D-aware generalist agents.

## Method

- A unified architecture centered on a decoder-only LLM that converts all multi-modal inputs (egocentric 2D, global 3D, text) into a single sequence of tokens.
- A two-stage learning process involving 3D vision-language alignment followed by 3D vision-language-action instruction tuning.
- An innovative LLM-assisted pipeline for generating large-scale, high-quality 3D-language and 3D-action datasets from 3D scene graphs, incorporating Object-centric Chain-of-Thought (O-CoT) for better grounding.

## Results

- LEO achieves performance exceeding or comparable to state-of-the-art task-specific models across 3D dense captioning, 3D Question Answering, robotic manipulation, and object navigation.
- It demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization capabilities to novel scenes and objects in both manipulation and navigation tasks.
- The agent exhibits proficiency in generating scene-grounded responses for 3D dialogue and task planning, providing informative spatial relations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An object-centric 3D representation provides a simple yet effective method for connecting 3D scenes with large language models, leveraging their prior knowledge efficiently.
> - Training with a generalist-style instruction tuning approach across a broad range of scenes and tasks significantly improves generalization compared to specialist models.
> - The initial 3D vision-language alignment stage is crucial for improving downstream task performance, particularly for detailed scene understanding and captioning.
