# An Embodied Generalist Agent in 3D World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Beijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence (BIGAI) introduce LEO, an embodied multi-modal generalist agent that perceives, grounds, reasons, plans, and acts in 3D environments. It achieves performance comparable to or exceeding task-specific models across 3D vision-language understanding, scene-grounded dialogue, planning, robotic manipulation, and object navigation tasks.

## Problem

- Current generalist AI models primarily operate in 2D domains, limiting their comprehension and interaction with the 3D physical world.
- Tasks intrinsically defined in 3D, such as 3D grounding, embodied reasoning, navigation, and manipulation, remain largely unexplored by generalist agents.
- Scarcity of large-scale, high-quality 3D-language and 3D-action datasets hinders the development of robust 3D-aware generalist agents.

## Method

- A unified architecture centered on a decoder-only LLM that converts all multi-modal inputs (egocentric 2D, global 3D, text) into a single sequence of tokens.
- A two-stage learning process involving 3D vision-language alignment followed by 3D vision-language-action instruction tuning.
- An innovative LLM-assisted pipeline for generating large-scale, high-quality 3D-language and 3D-action datasets from 3D scene graphs, incorporating Object-centric Chain-of-Thought (O-CoT) for better grounding.

## Results

- LEO achieves performance exceeding or comparable to state-of-the-art task-specific models across 3D dense captioning, 3D Question Answering, robotic manipulation, and object navigation.
- It demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization capabilities to novel scenes and objects in both manipulation and navigation tasks.
- The agent exhibits proficiency in generating scene-grounded responses for 3D dialogue and task planning, providing informative spatial relations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An object-centric 3D representation provides a simple yet effective method for connecting 3D scenes with large language models, leveraging their prior knowledge efficiently.
> - Training with a generalist-style instruction tuning approach across a broad range of scenes and tasks significantly improves generalization compared to specialist models.
> - The initial 3D vision-language alignment stage is crucial for improving downstream task performance, particularly for detailed scene understanding and captioning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist artificial intelligence has been a long-standing pursuit, with recent breakthroughs in Large Language Models (LLMs) and "foundation models" accelerating progress in specific domains like natural language processing, computer vision, and robotics. Models such as GPT-3/4, DALL-E, Segment Anything Model (SAM), Gato, PaLM-E, and RT-2 have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across a multitude of tasks within their respective modalities.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: most of these successful generalist models primarily operate within **2D domains**, relying on 2D images and exhibiting limited capacity for comprehending and interacting with the **3D physical world**. Tasks inherently defined in 3D, such as 3D grounding, embodied reasoning, navigation, and manipulation, remain largely underexplored by generalist agents. This limitation significantly hinders their ability to perform real-world tasks and approach human-level general intelligence, which is intrinsically embodied and interacts with a 3D environment.

"An Embodied Generalist Agent in 3D World" directly addresses this fundamental challenge by proposing **LEO**, an embodied multi-modal generalist agent designed to bridge the gap between large language models and 3D physical environments. It extends the concept of generalist models from primarily text- or 2D-image-centric domains to encompass robust 3D understanding and embodied action.

Existing 3D vision-language (VL) models often rely on synthetic data, lack unified architectures, or fall short in embodied tasks. LEO differentiates itself by:
- **Unifying modalities:** Integrating 2D egocentric images, 3D point clouds, and text instructions into a single model.
- **Comprehensive task coverage:** Tackling a wide range of tasks from passive scene understanding (captioning, QA) to active embodied interaction (navigation, manipulation, dialogue, planning).
- **Leveraging LLMs for 3D:** Adapting powerful pre-trained LLMs to understand and act in 3D, moving beyond traditional vision-language models that might have separate reasoning modules.
- **Addressing data scarcity:** Innovating a large-scale, high-quality 3D-language dataset generation pipeline, a crucial step given the high cost and difficulty of collecting 3D data compared to 2D.

This work positions itself at the forefront of embodied AI, aiming to move beyond domain-specific solutions towards a more holistic, human-like general intelligence that can perceive, reason about, and interact within complex 3D environments. It builds upon the successes of 2D foundation models while extending their capabilities into the crucial dimension of real-world physical embodiment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to answer a fundamental question: "how to equip the generalist agent with a comprehensive understanding of and the ability to interact with the real 3D world?" To achieve this, the authors introduce LEO, an embodied multi-modal generalist agent.

The motivation for developing LEO stems from several significant challenges observed in current machine learning models, particularly general-purpose task solvers leveraging large language models (LLMs):

1. **Limited 3D Input Capacity:** Most existing successful models primarily rely on 2D images. While powerful, this inherently restricts their ability to process and comprehend the volumetric and spatial information crucial for real-world scenarios.
2. **Neglected 3D-Defined Tasks:** Current models rarely explore tasks that are intrinsically defined in the 3D world, such as 3D grounding (understanding object positions and relations in 3D space), embodied reasoning (making decisions based on spatial context), and embodied acting (performing actions in a physical environment).
3. **Hindrance to Real-World Application and General Intelligence:** The authors argue that these limitations are significant obstacles preventing current AI models from effectively performing real-world tasks and truly approaching general intelligence, which necessitates seamless interaction with the 3D physical environment.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives for LEO are:
- **Unified Perception and Interaction:** To create an agent capable of perceiving, grounding, reasoning, planning, and acting within the 3D world using a unified task interface, model architecture, and objective.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** To collect large-scale, diverse 3D vision-language (VL) and vision-language-action (VLA) datasets, particularly through innovative LLM-assisted data generation techniques, to enable comprehensive training.
- **Efficient LLM Adaptation:** To develop an efficient framework that connects object-centric 3D representations with LLMs, leveraging the powerful prior knowledge of LLMs for downstream 3D tasks.
- **Demonstrating Versatility and Proficiency:** To empirically demonstrate LEO's remarkable proficiency across a wide spectrum of 3D tasks, including 3D captioning, question answering, embodied reasoning, navigation, and manipulation, and to show that it can surpass or be comparable to task-specific models.
- **Providing Insights for Future Research:** To conduct thorough ablative studies and scaling analyses to provide valuable insights for the development of future embodied generalist agents.

In essence, the motivation is to advance the generalist agent paradigm from a primarily 2D/textual domain to a truly embodied, 3D-aware agent, bringing AI closer to human-like understanding and interaction with the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LEO's methodology is built upon two core design principles: handling multi-modal inputs (egocentric 2D, global 3D, text) and outputs (textual responses, embodied actions) in a unified architecture, and leveraging pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) as powerful priors.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Unified Interface

LEO employs a **decoder-only LLM** (specifically, Vicuna-7B) as its central component. All inputs, regardless of modality, are converted into a unified sequence of tokens.
- **Input Modalities:**
 - **Egocentric 2D images:** Processed by a pre-trained OpenCLIP ConvNext encoder to obtain image token embeddings. Used for embodied views.
 - **3D point clouds:** Object-centric 3D point clouds (derived from Mask3D-based object proposals) are encoded by a pre-trained PointNet++. A Spatial Transformer then processes these embeddings to capture 3D relations between objects, outputting object-centric 3D tokens. Used for a third-person global view.
 - **Text instructions:** Encoded using a SentencePiece tokenizer.
 - **Embodied Action Commands:** Continuous actions (e.g., in manipulation) are discretized, and both discrete and continuous actions are mapped to the least used tokens in SentencePiece, unifying the action space with the text vocabulary.
- **Token Embedding:** MLP adapters are used to match the dimensions of embeddings from different modalities. CLIP's text encoder is used to provide semantic guidance by updating visual tokens.
- **LLM Adaptation:** LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to introduce additional tunable parameters to the frozen pre-trained LLM, enabling efficient alignment and grounding of multi-modal tokens while preserving the LLM's vast knowledge.
- **Unified Objective:** All tasks are formulated as autoregressive sequence prediction, following a GPT-style prefix language modeling objective, allowing for a single training loss function across diverse tasks.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Learning Scheme

LEO adopts a two-stage learning approach, similar to successful 2D VLMs:
1. **3D Vision-Language (VL) Alignment:** This initial stage focuses on bridging the gap between 3D scene representation and natural language. It involves training LEO to generate captions given various 3D inputs.
2. **3D Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Instruction Tuning:** In this subsequent stage, LEO is fine-tuned to follow instructions and accomplish a wide range of 3D tasks, encompassing perception, grounding, reasoning, planning, and acting.

#### 3.3. Large-Scale Dataset Collection (LEO-align and LEO-instruct)

Recognizing the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale 3D data, the authors meticulously collect and generate two comprehensive datasets:
- **LEO-align:** Comprises three types of 3D captioning data:
 - **Object-level captions:** Aligning 3D individual objects with descriptions (from Cap3D/Objaverse).
 - **Object-in-the-scene captions:** Generating referring expressions for objects within a 3D scene context (from ScanScribe, ReferIt3D, and LLM-generated data).
 - **Scene-level captions:** Depicting global 3D scenes using natural language, generated via LLM-assisted pipeline.
- **LEO-instruct:** Curated for instruction-following across diverse 3D VLA tasks:
 - **3D Captioning and Question Answering:** General descriptions and answers to 3D-specific questions (from Scan2Cap, ScanQA, SQA3D, and LLM-generated 3RScanQA).
 - **3D Dialogue and Task Planning:** Generating coherent multi-round conversations and step-by-step plans for high-level tasks in 3D scenes (both LLM-generated on 3RScan).
 - **Embodied Navigation:** Mapping instructions and 3D/2D inputs to discrete motor commands for object navigation (from Habitat-web/MP3D shortest-path episodes).
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** Executing manipulation tasks in a simulated environment by discretizing continuous actions (from CLIPort demonstrations).

#### 3.4. LLM-assisted 3D-Language Data Generation Pipeline

A crucial innovation is the pipeline for generating high-quality 3D-VL data using LLMs (ChatGPT).
- **Scene-graph-based Prompting:** The pipeline starts with 3D scene graphs (from 3DSSG), which provide rich object attributes and accurate spatial relation information. These scene graphs serve as context for prompting LLMs. Manual seed tasks are designed for various types of data (captioning, QA, dialogue, planning).
- **Object-centric Chain-of-Thought (O-CoT):** To mitigate hallucination and enhance grounding, LLMs are prompted to explicitly provide object labels and IDs as "thoughts" during text generation. This helps ensure generated text is directly tied to scene entities. Subgraph sampling further enhances data diversity.
- **Refinement Procedures:** A series of human-defined filters, using regular expression matching and scene graph retrieval, are applied to raw LLM responses. This includes removing negative/unanswerable responses, rewriting unnatural narratives, and manually fixing logical errors (e.g., counting) or hallucinations, ensuring high data quality and reliability.

By combining a unified, LLM-centric architecture with a sophisticated data generation and curation strategy, LEO aims to achieve generalist capabilities in the 3D world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LEO demonstrates remarkable proficiency across a comprehensive suite of 3D tasks, showcasing its generalist capabilities in perception, grounding, reasoning, planning, and acting.

#### 4.1. 3D Vision-Language Understanding and Reasoning

- **Superior Performance:** LEO significantly surpasses state-of-the-art task-specific models and task-specific fine-tuned generalist models (e.g., 3D-VisTA, 3D-LLM) on 3D dense captioning (Scan2Cap) and 3D Question Answering (ScanQA, SQA3D).
- **Unified Generalization:** Notably, LEO achieves these results with a single, unified architecture and without task-specific fine-tuning, highlighting its strong generalist capabilities.
- **Object-centric Advantage:** The results suggest that the object-centric 3D representation is a simple yet effective way to connect 3D scenes with LLMs, harnessing LLM's inherent knowledge more efficiently than more complex feature aggregation methods.

#### 4.2. Scene-grounded Dialogue and Planning

- **Qualitative Proficiency:** LEO exhibits strong capabilities in generating high-quality, scene-grounded responses for 3D dialogue and task planning, even in unseen scenarios.
- **Grounded and Informative:** Responses are precisely grounded to the 3D scenes, involving concrete objects and plausible actions. They often include rich, informative spatial relations, aiding in identifying specific objects in complex environments.

#### 4.3. Embodied Action in 3D World (Navigation and Manipulation)

- **Robotic Manipulation:** LEO achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art specialized models like CLIPort, and even outperforms them on some challenging unseen manipulation tasks. This is significant given that LEO directly produces motor commands without inductive biases like heatmaps, demonstrating its capacity for learning embodied actions within a generalist framework.
- **Object Navigation (ObjNav):** LEO demonstrates a success rate comparable to baselines and achieves better SPL (Success weighted by Path Length) on MP3D-val, indicating efficient path-finding. It also shows remarkable zero-shot generalization to novel scenes (HM3D-val).
- **Semantic Generalization:** The two-stage learning scheme endows LEO with semantic-level generalization to novel objects and environments in both manipulation and navigation.
- **Limitations in Recurrence:** While strong, LEO's performance in ObjNav is achieved with a simplified feed-forward policy, without complex recurrent modules common in specialized agents, leading to slightly lower success rates compared to some baselines that utilize recurrence.

#### 4.4. Ablative Studies and Insights

- **Importance of Alignment:** The VL alignment stage consistently improves downstream performance, particularly for detailed scene understanding and captioning tasks.
- **Generalist vs. Specialist:** Generalist-style instruction tuning with broad coverage of scenes and tasks significantly outperforms a specialist model trained only on specific scenes, especially in generalization across scenes and tasks.
- **VL vs. VLA Trade-off:** Incorporating embodied acting tasks can sometimes lead to slight performance drops on 3D VL tasks. This suggests a potential gap between language generation and embodied action prediction, possibly due to imbalanced data scales, highlighting a future research direction.
- **Dialogue and Planning Data Benefits:** Training with dialogue and planning data improves LEO's ability to handle complex instructions, cultivate flexible responses, reduce hallucination, and enhance overall NLP skills.
- **Data Balancing:** Augmenting data with negative samples effectively mitigates hallucination (e.g., over-answering "Yes" to existence questions) and generalizes zero-shot to novel scenes.
- **Object-centric Masks:** Using Mask3D proposals instead of ground-truth masks leads to only moderate performance drops, confirming the practical applicability of LEO with off-the-shelf perception.

#### 4.5. Scaling Law Analysis

- **Conformity to Scaling Laws:** LEO's instruction-tuning loss decreases log-linearly with increasing data scale, confirming conformity to established scaling laws in neural language models.
- **LLM Scale Benefits:** Scaling up the underlying LLM (from OPT-1.3B to Vicuna-7B) leads to consistent performance improvements. However, the gains from 7B to 13B models appear less significant, suggesting potential saturation without a corresponding increase in data scale.
- **Alignment's Role in Scaling:** The alignment stage consistently leads to lower instruction-tuning losses, reinforcing its importance for efficient learning.

Overall, the findings strongly support LEO as a highly capable embodied generalist agent that successfully extends the generalist paradigm to the 3D world, providing concrete evidence of its effectiveness and offering valuable insights for future advancements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LEO represents a significant step forward in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the realm of embodied generalist agents. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Core Contributions

- **First Embodied Generalist Agent in 3D:** LEO is presented as the pioneering embodied generalist agent capable of following human instructions to perceive, ground, reason, plan, and act directly in the 3D world. This moves beyond the limitations of predominantly 2D-focused generalist models.
- **Unified Framework for 3D VL A:** It proposes a simple yet highly effective framework that seamlessly connects object-centric 3D representations with Large Language Models (LLMs), bridging the gap between vision, language, and embodied action efficiently. This unified approach simplifies model design and training compared to task-specific architectures.
- **Large-Scale, High-Quality 3D Data:** The collection and generation of extensive, comprehensive datasets (LEO-align and LEO-instruct) are crucial. The innovative LLM-assisted pipeline, incorporating scene-graph-based prompting, Object-centric Chain-of-Thought (O-CoT), and meticulous refinement procedures, addresses the critical data scarcity issue in 3D-language learning, setting a new standard for data curation.
- **Empirical Validation and Insights:** Extensive experiments demonstrate LEO's proficiency across a broad spectrum of 3D tasks, often outperforming or matching state-of-the-art task-specific models with a generalist approach. The in-depth analyses and ablative studies provide valuable insights into the design principles, data importance, and scaling properties of embodied generalist agents.
- **Open-Source Release:** The commitment to releasing data, code, and model weights is a substantial contribution to the research community, enabling reproducibility, fostering further research, and accelerating advancements in embodied generalist AI.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancing Embodied AI:** LEO pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by demonstrating effective grounding and interaction in 3D environments, which is essential for intelligent agents to operate in the real world.
- **Robotics:** The ability of LEO to handle robotic manipulation and navigation tasks, including zero-shot generalization to novel scenes and objects, holds immense potential for developing more versatile and intelligent robots. Such robots could understand complex natural language instructions and adapt to new environments and tasks with minimal re-training.
- **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** LEO's capabilities in 3D scene understanding, dialogue, and planning could revolutionize AR/VR applications, enabling more interactive, intelligent, and context-aware virtual assistants that can understand and manipulate virtual 3D environments in response to user commands.
- **Assistive Technologies:** For accessibility and assistive robotics, an agent that can comprehend and act within a 3D home environment could provide invaluable support for individuals with disabilities, automating tasks and responding to complex needs.
- **Environmental Planning and Simulation:** The detailed 3D understanding and planning capabilities could be applied to urban planning, architectural design, and complex simulations, allowing for more intelligent and interactive tools.

#### 5.3. Ethical Considerations (as per Impact Statement)

The authors explicitly acknowledge the importance of responsible AI development. As LEO ventures into complex human-machine interaction and real-world task execution, ethical implications related to **safety, privacy, and fairness in automated decision-making** become paramount. The paper highlights the necessity for ongoing ethical consideration as applications evolve, ensuring that advancements in embodied generalist AI contribute positively to society and are developed with robust safeguards against potential misuse or unintended consequences.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The authors identify key limitations and promising avenues for future work:
- **Enhanced 3D VL Understanding:** Leveraging even larger-scale VL data from richer 3D domains to further improve LEO's comprehension.
- **Bridging VL and Embodied Action:** Continuing to explore how to effectively bridge the gap between vision-language learning and embodied action control, especially given the observed trade-offs in joint training. This includes investigating more sophisticated policy architectures (e.g., recurrence) for embodied tasks.
- **Safety and Alignment:** Deeply investigating safety and alignment issues for embodied generalist agents, particularly as models and data scale up, to ensure robust and beneficial real-world deployment.

In conclusion, LEO represents a critical initial stride towards building truly embodied generalist agents, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve by grounding them firmly in the complexities of the 3D physical world.
