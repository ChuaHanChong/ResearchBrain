# Can You Learn to See Without Images? Procedural Warm-Up for Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from AIML demonstrates that pretraining Vision Transformers on procedurally generated symbolic data, rather than images, instills generic computational biases that improve downstream vision task performance by an average of 3.4% absolute accuracy. This "procedural warm-up" also enhances data efficiency, enabling models to achieve similar performance with 28% fewer natural images on ImageNet-1K compared to standard initialization.

## Problem

- Training Vision Transformers (ViTs) demands vast amounts of real image data and computational resources, limiting their accessibility and efficiency.
- Existing approaches for pretraining vision models, even with synthetic data, largely depend on visual stimuli, potentially missing more generalizable inductive biases.
- The challenge of instilling generic, modality-agnostic computational mechanisms into ViTs that can transfer effectively to diverse vision tasks.

## Method

- Proposing a **procedural warm-up** pretraining phase for ViTs using synthetically generated, non-visual symbolic data.
- Employing ==formal grammars== (regular, context-free, context-sensitive) to create structured token sequences, ensuring no visual or semantic content.
- Adapting ViTs to process these abstract tokens with a ==masked-token prediction== objective, critically freezing the ==discrete embedding layer== to force learning in ==attention== and ==MLP== layers, before transitioning to standard image training.

## Results

- Procedural warm-up consistently boosts ViT performance across multiple image classification datasets, yielding an average absolute accuracy improvement of **+3.4%** compared to default random initialization.
- The method proved more effective than alternative initialization strategies, including structured mimetic initialization and visual-analogue FractalDB warm-up, for enhancing ViT performance.
- It significantly improved data efficiency, allowing models to achieve comparable performance to standard ImageNet-1K pretraining using **28%** fewer natural images (a reduction of approximately **108 million** examples).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision models can acquire fundamental computational mechanisms from abstract, non-visual symbolic data, which are demonstrably beneficial for downstream image-based tasks.
> - The type of procedural data matters, with context-free grammars like k-DYCK, characterized by hierarchical dependencies, yielding the most substantial performance gains.
> - Learned inductive biases are distributed across both attention and MLP layers, primarily accumulating in the deeper, later layers of the Vision Transformer, contrasting with typical visual feature learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of machine learning (ML) has seen a growing trend towards the development of increasingly generic approaches, moving away from domain-specific methods. Transformer architectures, initially prominent in natural language processing (NLP), exemplify this trend by demonstrating versatile applicability across various modalities, including vision. This versatility suggests the existence of underlying generic inductive biases that can benefit learning processes regardless of the data type.

Prior research has explored instilling domain-agnostic mechanisms in models. For instance, training large language models (LLMs) on computer code has been observed to enhance reasoning capabilities, and text-trained models have shown capacities for handling visual data. These examples suggest that certain computational mechanisms can be learned independently of a specific domain or modality.

Within computer vision, a considerable body of work has investigated training vision models using synthetic or abstract images. These typically involve handcrafted algorithms to generate abstract images that mimic structural or statistical properties of natural data, such as fractals, contours, or structured noise. The motivations for such approaches often include addressing privacy or fairness concerns, or enabling learning from smaller datasets by replacing natural images.

This current work extends this line of inquiry by proposing a departure from visual data altogether. Instead of using abstract *images*, it investigates pretraining vision transformers (ViTs) on *procedurally generated symbolic data* that lacks any visual or semantic content and does not attempt to imitate visual structures. This approach seeks to determine if generic computational priors, previously shown to benefit LLMs when trained on formal grammars, can similarly enhance vision models. The research positions itself as a method to complement, rather than replace, standard image-based training by providing a qualitatively different type of training signal to instill universally useful computational mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to explore a novel method for instilling generic inductive biases into Vision Transformers (ViTs) through a pretraining phase. This "procedural warm-up" utilizes procedurally generated symbolic data that is devoid of visual or semantic content, aiming to improve the efficiency and performance of ViTs in subsequent image-based tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and hypotheses:
1. **Generality of Transformers:** Transformers have demonstrated remarkable versatility across modalities, suggesting that they possess or can acquire generic inductive biases beneficial for diverse data types. The researchers hypothesize that these biases can be explicitly instilled in ViTs using non-visual data.
2. **Acquisition of Domain-Agnostic Mechanisms:** Existing work shows that models can acquire domain-agnostic capabilities from abstract data (e.g., LLMs on computer code or formal grammars). This research aims to extend this concept to vision models, suggesting that capabilities relevant to vision tasks might be learned from non-visual, abstract data.
3. **Limitations of Visual-Analogue Synthetic Data:** While previous efforts in vision have used synthetic or abstract *visual* data (e.g., fractal images) to mimic properties of natural images, this approach often remains constrained by visual imitation. The authors hypothesize that completely abstract, non-visual data, generated by simple algorithms like formal grammars, might capture more broadly useful structures, independent of specific visual properties. The core idea is that reasoning over images is fundamentally a reasoning problem, not solely an image problem.
4. **Improving Data Efficiency and Convergence:** Training large-scale vision models requires extensive datasets and computational resources. The researchers aim to develop a lightweight pretraining strategy that, despite using minimal computational cost for data generation and warm-up, can significantly improve data efficiency, accelerate convergence, and enhance the final performance of ViTs when subsequently trained on natural images.
5. **Complementary Training Signal:** The study seeks to determine if the inductive biases acquired from procedural warm-up are distinct from those learned during standard image-based training. If so, this would imply that procedural data provides complementary information, leading to better overall model performance rather than merely an early-stage acceleration or substitution.
6. **Understanding Inductive Bias Formation:** By analyzing the effects of different types of procedural data and observing which parts of the ViT architecture benefit most, the research aims to gain insights into how generic computational mechanisms are learned and represented within transformer networks, particularly in the context of transfer to a different modality.

In summary, the detailed objectives include demonstrating that ViTs can "learn to see without images" by acquiring abstract computational priors from symbolic data, quantifiably showing improvements in downstream vision task performance, and analyzing the nature and location of these learned biases within the model architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed "procedural warm-up," involves a two-phase training pipeline for Vision Transformers (ViTs). First, ViTs undergo a brief pretraining phase on procedurally generated symbolic data. Second, they proceed with standard image-based training.

#### 3.1. Generating Procedural Data

The core of the procedural warm-up relies on synthetic, non-visual data generated using formal languages. This data is designed to be inexpensive to produce and to contain predictable structures of varying complexity, without semantic meaning.

- **Selected Formal Grammars:** The study employs three types of formal grammars, representative of different levels in the Chomsky hierarchy:
 - **WW (Regular Language):** Generates sequences by concatenating a random substring with its exact copy (e.g., "abcabc"). This captures global one-to-one correspondence.
 - **k-DYCK (Context-Free Language):** Produces sequences of balanced parentheses with hierarchical structures (e.g., "([ ] ) < >"). The parameter *k* determines the number of unique bracket pairs, influencing structural variety and nesting complexity. This requires stack-based dependencies.
 - **k-DYCK SHUFFLE (Context-Sensitive Language):** A variation of k-DYCK that allows for crossing dependencies by relaxing the well-nested constraint, while still requiring every opening bracket to be closed (e.g., "( [ ) < ] >"). This represents multiple intertwined hierarchies.
- **Generating Token Sequences:** For each grammar, a stochastic generator creates sequences. WW sequences involve sampling and concatenating substrings. k-DYCK strings use a stack-based process to enforce proper nesting. k-DYCK SHUFFLE sequences stochastically open and close brackets.
- **Vocabulary and Sequence Size:** A fixed vocabulary of 128 tokens is used (setting *k*=64 for Dyck languages). Each procedural sequence is generated to a fixed size of N=H×W, matching the expected input token count for a ViT. The data generation process is designed to be computationally negligible.

#### 3.2. Pretraining ViTs with Procedural Data

This phase adapts standard ViTs to process the symbolic procedural data and learn underlying algorithms.

- **Feeding Abstract Tokens to ViTs:**
 - **Bypassing Visual Patch Embeddings:** Standard ViTs begin with a linear patch embedding layer for image content. For procedural data, this is bypassed.
 - **Discrete Embedding Layer:** A discrete embedding layer, analogous to those in language models (a lookup table), is used to map abstract symbols to feature vectors.
 - **Frozen Embeddings:** Crucially, this embedding layer is kept frozen with random vectors. This design choice ensures that the model cannot solve the pretraining objective by simply modifying the embeddings. Instead, it is forced to adapt its attention and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers, encoding learned knowledge within the core transformer blocks. Positional encodings are also frozen.
- **Pretraining Objective:** The procedural warm-up utilizes a masked-token prediction objective. For each sequence, 50% of structurally important tokens (e.g., closing brackets in DYCK, or the repeated sequence in WW) are masked. The model is then required to predict these original tokens. Only the attention and MLP layers are updated during this phase.
- **Handling Ambiguous Grammars:** For k-DYCK and WW, the loss mask ensures a unique valid completion. For k-DYCK SHUFFLE, which can have multiple valid completions, "teacher-forcing" supervision is used with one valid continuation per example. This encourages the model to learn tracking stack depth and bracket types to assign probabilities to valid continuations.
- **Transition to Standard Image-Based Training:** After the procedural warm-up, the discrete token embeddings and prediction head are discarded. The procedurally warmed-up weights of the attention and MLP layers serve as the new initialization for the ViT. The entire architecture is then updated using standard learning rates and hyperparameters for image-based pretraining or fine-tuning on natural image datasets.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Baselines

- **ViT Architectures:** ViT-B/16 is used for ImageNet-1K experiments, and ViT-T/16 for smaller datasets.
- **Warm-up Budget:** The procedural warm-up typically consumes a small budget, equivalent to 1% of the total ImageNet-1K examples used in subsequent training stages.
- **Datasets:** Evaluation is performed on ImageNet-1K, Tiny-ImageNet, Food-101, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 for image classification.
- **Baselines for Comparison:**
 - **Default initialization:** Standard random sampling of weights from a truncated-normal distribution.
 - **Mimetic initialization:** A structured initialization scheme that instills diagonal patterns in attention matrices to imitate pretrained models.
 - **FractalDB warm-up (sample-matched):** A form of procedural *visual* data designed to train vision models. To ensure a fair comparison, the number of training examples is matched, and patch embeddings/classifier layers are reset after pretraining, similar to the proposed method.

Ablation studies are conducted to analyze the effects of different grammar types, sequence order, warm-up duration, and the location of learned information within the network's layers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy and characteristics of procedural warm-up for Vision Transformers.

#### 4.1. Improving Vision without Images

Experiments demonstrated that ViTs can acquire information useful for vision tasks from abstract, non-visual procedural data.
- **Consistent Performance Gains:** Procedural warm-up consistently improved downstream performance across all evaluated image classification benchmarks. On average, it yielded a +3.4% absolute improvement in accuracy over the default random initialization.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** The procedural warm-up produced larger improvements compared to the Mimetic initialization and outperformed the sample-matched FractalDB warm-up, despite FractalDB utilizing visual data. For example, on ImageNet-1K, a ViT-B model achieved a +1.72% improvement in top-1 accuracy with procedural warm-up. This suggests that the generic inductive biases from symbolic data were more beneficial than structured attention patterns or visually-inspired procedural data.
- **Scalability:** The improvements persisted even with larger models and datasets, such as ViT-B on ImageNet-1K, indicating that the benefits are not limited to smaller-scale scenarios.

#### 4.2. Complementarity with Standard Training

The study found that procedural warm-up provides a distinct and complementary training signal to standard large-scale visual pretraining.
- **Distinct Optimization Trajectory:** Models initialized with procedural warm-up followed a different optimization path and converged to higher accuracies compared to those with default initialization, even when followed by identical ImageNet pretraining. This suggests more than just a head-start; rather, a qualitatively different form of learning.
- **Persistent Gains in Additive Setting:** When procedural warm-up was applied before large-scale ImageNet pretraining (additive setting), performance improvements consistently persisted across various fine-tuning benchmarks (e.g., Tiny-ImageNet, Food-101, CIFAR-10/100, STL-10). The warmed-up models continued to outperform baselines, indicating that the acquired biases complement knowledge from natural images.
- **Data Efficiency in Substitutive Setting:** In a substitutive setting, replacing only 1% of the total pretraining budget with procedural data allowed models to achieve equivalent performance to models trained solely on ImageNet-1K, but using 28% fewer natural image examples (approximately 108 million fewer images). This quantifies the data efficiency benefit.

#### 4.3. Important Properties of the Procedural Data

Ablation studies identified key characteristics of the procedural data responsible for the observed benefits.
- **Language Type:** Context-free languages, specifically k-DYCK, yielded the strongest performance improvements, suggesting that hierarchical, stack-based dependencies are particularly valuable. The context-sensitive k-DYCK SHUFFLE also provided benefits but less than k-DYCK. The regular language WW, which lacks nested structure, offered no discernible advantage, indicating that simple repetition is insufficient.
- **Order Preservation:** Shuffling the token order within k-DYCK sequences completely negated the benefits of procedural warm-up, leading to performance degradation below random initialization. This confirmed that the gains arise from the precise structural dependencies and order within the data, rather than mere token distributions or low-order statistics.
- **Pretraining Length:** Downstream performance peaked at an intermediate number of procedural warm-up steps. Both excessively short and excessively long warm-up durations resulted in lower accuracy, suggesting that over-specialization to the procedural data can hinder subsequent adaptation to visual tasks.

#### 4.4. Which Layers Contain Useful Information from Procedural Warmup?

Analysis of the model architecture revealed how the learned inductive biases are expressed.
- **Importance of Precise Weight Structure:** Shuffling the weight values within layers after procedural warm-up eliminated nearly all performance gains, indicating that the benefits are due to specific learned weight structures corresponding to the data-generating algorithms, not just adjusted weight magnitudes.
- **Distributed Information:** Substantial performance degradation occurred when either attention or MLP layer weights were shuffled. This suggests that the learned information and inductive biases are distributed throughout the network, rather than being confined to a single component type (e.g., only attention layers, as in some prior structured initialization schemes).
- **Concentration in Late Layers:** A layer-wise analysis demonstrated that the benefits of procedural warm-up were predominantly concentrated in the later (deeper) layers of the ViT. Transferring only the final one-third of transformer blocks accounted for most of the gains. This contrasts with established knowledge in standard visual pretraining, where early layers typically capture low-level, transferable features, suggesting a qualitatively different training signal from procedural data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the understanding of inductive biases in Vision Transformers and offers a novel, efficient pretraining strategy. The key significance lies in demonstrating that vision models can acquire fundamental computational mechanisms from non-visual, abstract symbolic data, which subsequently benefits their performance on image-based tasks.

**Scientific Contributions:**
- **Demonstration of Modality-Agnostic Learning:** The work empirically establishes that generic inductive biases, previously observed in language models, can be effectively instilled in vision transformers using procedurally generated, non-visual data. This challenges the notion that initial learning for vision models must inherently involve visual stimuli.
- **Qualitatively Different Training Signal:** The findings suggest that procedural warm-up provides a training signal distinct from natural image data. The benefits are complementary, leading to enhanced performance and data efficiency even after large-scale visual pretraining, rather than merely accelerating the initial stages of visual learning.
- **Insights into Inductive Bias Localization:** The analysis indicating that learned biases are distributed across both attention and MLP layers, and predominantly reside in the *later* layers of the transformer, offers new insights into how abstract computational priors are encoded and leveraged within deep neural networks. This contrasts with traditional views of early layers capturing low-level features in vision models.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The most direct impact is the potential for significantly reducing the data requirements for training high-performing Vision Transformers. By demonstrating that a small fraction of procedural data can be equivalent to a much larger quantity of real image data, this method could lead to more efficient and less resource-intensive model development, particularly beneficial in domains with limited annotated datasets.
- **Domain-Agnostic Pretraining:** The "procedural warm-up" offers a generic and computationally lightweight alternative to traditional random or structured initialization schemes. A "warmed-up model" could serve as a universal starting point for diverse tasks across different modalities, amortizing the initial training cost over multiple applications.
- **Foundation for Universal AI:** The concept of instilling fundamental computational mechanisms through abstract data aligns with broader efforts towards developing more general and robust AI systems. It suggests a path toward building models that possess versatile reasoning capabilities, independent of specific sensory inputs.
- **Future Research Directions:** This work opens avenues for exploring a wider range of procedural generators and formal languages, investigating other vision architectures (e.g., Swin, XCiT), and evaluating the benefits in diverse tasks such as segmentation, out-of-distribution generalization, and multimodal learning. The notion of developing a "closed-form instantiation" of procedurally pretrained weights, similar to algorithmic weight initialization, is an ambitious yet intriguing prospect for future research.

In conclusion, this research provides evidence that the foundational "learning to see" can be jump-started from abstract computational principles, pointing toward a promising new direction for data-efficient and domain-agnostic pretraining strategies in machine learning.
