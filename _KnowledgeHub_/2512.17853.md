# AnyTask: an Automated Task and Data Generation Framework for Advancing Sim-to-Real Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AnyTask is an automated framework that pairs massively parallel GPU simulation with foundation models to design manipulation tasks and synthesize robot data from only a high-level textual goal. LLM-driven object retrieval, task proposal, and simulation-code generation feed three demonstration agents (VIPR, VIPR-Eureka, VIPR-RL). Behavior-cloning policies trained purely on this synthetic data transfer zero-shot to a physical robot at 44% average success.

## Problem

- Building simulation systems still demands heavy human labor: proposing tasks, selecting assets, designing success metrics, and collecting demonstrations, which caps the diversity of generated data.
- Prior LLM-driven task generators target small task sets and rarely address sim-to-real transfer, scalable dataset size, or systematic, diverse data-collection strategies.
- Classical teacher policies are narrow: planning needs expert-specified domain knowledge in PDDL, and reinforcement learning needs hand-crafted reward functions, so neither alone covers diverse manipulation.

## Method

- **VIPR**: a task-and-motion-planning agent that writes skill-API Python plans, then iteratively refines them using a VLM judging K parallel rollouts plus dense simulation annotations.
- **VIPR-Eureka**: ==Eureka==-style LLM reward search plus mesh-based contact sampling, where barycentric grasp candidates are filtered by batched ==cuRobo== inverse kinematics and collision rejection to seed RL states.
- Supporting pipeline: VLM-annotated object database with FAISS retrieval, LLM-generated reset/success/reward/state/policy code, dense step-level language annotation, and two-stage state-collection-then-rendered-replay data generation; **VIPR-RL** hybridizes planning with trained RL skills.

## Results

- With an improved prompt, o3-mini produced **96%** runnable environment code versus 84% unprompted, 76% for DeepSeek-R1 and 64% for o1-mini; `reset()` caused most errors.
- Agents complement each other, jointly solving **90%** of lifting, **87%** pick-and-place, **70%** pushing, **54%** stacking, **33%** drawer tasks; VLM refinement improved 86.4% of tasks by 13.6% average.
- Contact sampling lifted RL collection success from **37% to 57%** average, and eight point-cloud diffusion policies trained purely in simulation reached **44%** average real-world success over 30 trials each.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ensembling heterogeneous demonstration generators (planning, reward-learning RL, hybrid) covers far more task types than any single teacher; agent diversity matters as much as task diversity.
> - Decoupling state-only rollout collection from rendered replay slashes data-generation cost, yielding roughly four-fold speedups precisely on hard tasks where successful trajectories are rare.
> - Efficient collection does not imply distillable data: reward-hacked RL trajectories yield weak cloned policies, and transfer here depended on uncolored point clouds rather than RGB.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of deep learning, including its application in robotics, relies heavily on access to large-scale, high-quality datasets. While domains such as language modeling and visual understanding have benefited from vast datasets, collecting comparable interaction data for robotic manipulation is often time-consuming and expensive due to the necessity of physical interaction with the real world. Robot simulation offers a scalable alternative for data collection. However, current simulation-based approaches still require substantial human effort for various stages, including task design, scene generation, definition of success metrics, and synthesis of expert demonstrations. This human involvement typically limits the diversity and scale of the generated data.

Recent advancements in foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have shown promise in automating aspects of robotic applications, such as scene understanding, task planning, and motion synthesis. Existing work has explored using LLMs for proposing tasks or generating simulation content. However, these efforts often do not sufficiently address scaling data generation, developing diverse data collection strategies, or ensuring robust sim-to-real transfer, which is the ultimate goal for deploying trained policies in physical environments. Some approaches either require significant human input for task and demonstration design or struggle with direct transfer to the real world.

ANYTASK positions itself within this landscape by seeking to fully automate the entire data generation pipeline, from high-level task specification to zero-shot real-world policy deployment, while explicitly addressing the sim-to-real gap. It integrates massively parallel GPU simulation with foundation models to reduce human intervention in task design, scene configuration, data collection, and policy training, offering a more holistic and scalable solution compared to prior, more fragmented approaches. The framework aims to bridge the gap by producing synthetic data that facilitates the training of visuomotor policies directly applicable to physical robots without requiring real-world data for fine-tuning.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the challenge of data scarcity and the intensive human effort required in scaling robot learning. The success of deep learning models across various domains is largely attributed to the availability of extensive, high-quality datasets. In robotics, however, acquiring large-scale, diverse, and high-quality interaction data through real-world collection is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

Simulation environments offer a promising avenue for scaling data collection due to their inherent ability to be parallelized and controlled. Nevertheless, the creation and utilization of these simulated environments still demand significant human intervention. This includes:
- **Task Design**: Manually conceiving and defining a variety of robotic tasks.
- **Task-Aware Scene Generation**: Curating and arranging appropriate object assets and scene configurations relevant to specific tasks.
- **Expert Demonstration Synthesis**: Generating high-quality, successful trajectories that robots can learn from.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Ensuring that policies trained in simulation can effectively operate on physical robots, a process often hindered by the "reality gap" and demanding careful domain adaptation.

The overarching objective of ANYTASK is to develop a scalable and automated framework that significantly reduces this human effort across the entire pipeline. Specifically, the key objectives are:
1. **Automated Task and Scene Generation**: To leverage foundation models (LLMs and VLMs) to automatically design diverse manipulation tasks and generate corresponding simulation scenes based on high-level textual objectives and an intelligent object database.
2. **Scalable Expert Demonstration Synthesis**: To develop robust agents capable of generating high-quality expert demonstrations for a wide range of automatically generated tasks, utilizing massively parallel GPU-based simulation. This objective includes addressing limitations of traditional planning and learning approaches.
3. **Facilitating Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: To produce synthetic data that enables training visuomotor policies capable of being deployed directly onto a physical robot without requiring any real-world data collection or fine-tuning. This involves identifying and incorporating factors critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap, such as domain randomization and robust policy architectures.
4. **End-to-End Automation**: To integrate all these components into an almost autonomous pipeline, from task generation to policy training and deployment, requiring minimal human intervention beyond initial high-level goal specification.

By achieving these objectives, ANYTASK aims to accelerate the development of generalist robot learning policies by providing a continuous, automated stream of diverse and high-quality synthetic data.

### 3. Methodology and approach

ANYTASK is designed as an automated framework to generate text-based task descriptions and executable simulation code for robot data collection. The system integrates an object database, a task generator, and a simulation generator, orchestrated by LLMs, to produce diverse, large-scale manipulation datasets.

**Object Database:** An intelligent object database is constructed to store and retrieve object information using natural language queries. This database encodes object names, colors, textures, materials, bounding boxes, joint information (for articulated objects), and overall descriptions. The creation process involves rendering each object and part from multiple viewpoints and using a VLM (GPT-4o) to generate visual annotations. Sentence-level embeddings (Sentence-T5-Large) and a faiss index enable efficient nearest neighbor search, minimizing human effort in database creation and querying.

**High-level Task and Scenario Information:** Human input provides high-level guidance, such as a task family (e.g., "pick-and-place") and robot/workspace constraints, allowing the LLM to generate diverse and physically plausible robotic tasks.

**Task Generator:** This component proposes detailed tasks and relevant objects. Two variants are supported:
1. **Object-based task generation:** Objects are sampled from the database first, then the LLM proposes a task involving them.
2. **Task-based object proposal:** The LLM suggests objects suitable for a given task (e.g., a drawer for "open a drawer"), then the system retrieves a matching asset, and the LLM generates the final task description.
The output includes a natural language task description and structured object metadata.

**Simulation Generator:** This module translates the task description and object information into executable Python code for the IsaacLab GPU-based simulator. The LLM generates code for five key functions:
- `reset()`: For randomizing the scene, including object poses.
- `check_success()`: To define the task's goal condition.
- `compose_state()`: To provide a state representation for RL policies.
- `reward_function()`: For an initial version of the RL reward function.
- `scripted_policy()`: To define an expert policy for data collection.
The `check_success()` function is generated first and used to guide the LLM in generating the other four functions to ensure consistency. A set of environment and robot skill APIs are provided to the LLM as part of the prompt.

**Dense Annotation:** To enrich the generated data, an automated dense annotation system transforms privileged simulation information into natural language descriptions summarizing environmental states before and after actions. The `log_step()` API, called by the LLM during policy execution, generates these annotations, providing detailed textual context for each trajectory segment.

**ANYTASK Agents:** Three distinct agents are developed to generate expert demonstrations for diverse task types:

1. **VIPR (VLM-in-the-loop Parallel Refinement):** A Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) agent that uses an LLM to produce a Python-based task-motion plan by calling parameterized skill APIs (e.g., `move_to`, `grasp`). To overcome LLM limitations in spatial understanding, an iterative refinement process is introduced. This involves executing K parallel rollouts of the plan in simulation to collect images and dense annotations, which a VLM then evaluates, providing natural-language feedback and a binary success/failure judgment with confidence. This feedback is aggregated to refine the plan.

2. **VIPR-EUREKA:** An enhanced version of the Eureka framework for reinforcement learning (RL) agents. It refines and samples LLM-proposed reward functions. A novel **Mesh-based Contact Sampling** algorithm generates high-quality grasp candidates by sampling a triangle on an object mesh, determining a contact position using barycentric interpolation, and positioning the gripper along a perturbed surface normal. Gripper orientation is randomly sampled around a VLM- or human-defined z-axis. Rejection sampling with batched collision checking and inverse kinematics (using cuRobo) ensures feasibility, speeding up sampling. The number of environments using contact sampling gradually decays as training success improves.

3. **VIPR-RL:** A hybrid TAMP+RL approach that combines the strengths of both. It leverages motion planning for free-space movements and trained RL skills for contact-rich sub-tasks. The code generation process incorporates trained RL skill APIs. For each sub-task, motion planning moves the gripper to an object part (sampled by the grasp sampler), followed by the invocation of trained RL skills. RL skills are trained using PPO on 1024 environments for 1500 epochs with LLM-produced sparse rewards.

**Infrastructure Design:**
- **Multi-GPU Data Collection:** A Metaflow pipeline orchestrates data collection across multiple GPU nodes. This pipeline has three stages: optional policy refinement, primary state-based data gathering (without rendering), and replay of successful trajectories to render high-fidelity vision data (RGB-D, point clouds from multiple cameras). This decoupling optimizes computational overhead.
- **Demonstration Replay:** Successful trajectories are replayed using two methods: `State Replay` (directly setting the simulation to stored states) and `Action Replay` (re-executing action sequences). This approach significantly improves data generation throughput by avoiding redundant rendering during failed attempts.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several findings regarding the framework's capabilities in code generation, task diversity, agent robustness, data generation efficiency, and policy transferability.

**Code Runability:**
An evaluation of LLM-generated simulation code for `compose_state()`, `reset()`, and `check_success()` functions showed varying success rates. The o3-mini LLM achieved the highest initial runability at 84%. After analyzing common errors, particularly in the `reset()` function which involves complex object placement and spatial transforms, an improved prompt was developed. This refined prompt enhanced the o3-mini's code runability to 96%, indicating the effectiveness of LLM guidance and iterative refinement in generating functional simulation code.

**Task Diversity:**
The diversity of generated task descriptions was assessed using the self-BLEU score, where a lower score indicates higher diversity. ANYTASK achieved a self-BLEU score of 0.352, which was lower than existing systems like RoboGen (0.494), RLBench (0.590), and GenSim2 (0.692). This result suggests that ANYTASK generates more diverse task descriptions compared to the reference methods, contributing to broader learning opportunities for robotic policies.

**Robustness of ANYTASK Agents in Demonstration Generation:**
The agents (VIPR, VIPR-EUREKA, VIPR-RL) were evaluated across over 400 tasks in five categories: lifting, pick-and-place, pushing, stacking, and drawer opening.
- **Complementary Strengths:** No single agent excelled at all tasks. VIPR demonstrated high success in lifting (81%), pick-and-place (76%), and showed improvement with refinement. VIPR-EUREKA performed well in lifting (69%) and pushing (60%), while VIPR-RL achieved 33% in drawer opening, where others struggled. The ensemble of all agents collectively achieved higher success rates across categories (e.g., 90% for lifting, 87% for pick-and-place, 70% for pushing), highlighting the necessity of agent diversity.
- **VIPR Refinement:** The VLM-in-the-loop parallel refinement mechanism improved VIPR's success rates in 86.4% of tasks with non-zero initial success, yielding an average gain of 13.6%. This indicates that VLM feedback significantly enhances the robustness of TAMP-generated plans.
- **Contact Sampling in VIPR-EUREKA:** An ablation study comparing VIPR-EUREKA with standard Eureka demonstrated the effectiveness of LLM-guided contact sampling. VIPR-EUREKA achieved an average success rate of 57% across lifting, pushing, stacking, pick-and-place, and drawer opening tasks, significantly outperforming Eureka's 37% average. This improvement was particularly notable in lifting (73% vs. 40%) and stacking (57% vs. 0%), suggesting better exploration and grasping for complex objects.

**Data Generation Speed:**
ANYTASK employs a two-stage pipeline—state-based demonstration recording followed by trajectory replay with rendering—to optimize throughput. This method allowed for the collection of 500 RGB-D and point cloud demonstrations (from 4 cameras, 11-second duration each) in approximately 36 minutes on a single L4 GPU, including all overhead. Action replay, specifically, significantly improved data generation throughput, achieving a four-fold speedup on more challenging tasks by eliminating wasted rendering time during unsuccessful attempts.

**Behavior Cloning Policy Training in Simulation:**
Diffusion policies trained on ANYTASK-generated data were evaluated in simulation across over 70 policies (Table VI).
- VIPR data generally performed better on tasks requiring long-horizon or multi-step processes (e.g., Lifting: 42.0%, Pick and Place: 40.7%).
- Data collected by RL-based methods (VIPR-RL, VIPR-EUREKA) showed comparable or superior performance on tasks involving continuous contact, although VIPR-EUREKA data proved more challenging to distill into BC policies, occasionally leading to "reward hacking" behaviors (e.g., pushing instead of picking for pick-and-place).
- Overall, the results demonstrate that policies can be trained effectively on the synthetic data, with performance varying based on the agent type and task characteristics.

**Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer:**
Eight diverse tasks (e.g., LiftBanana, OpenDrawer, PutObjectInClosedDrawer) were selected for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. VIPR generated 1,000 expert demonstrations per task, varying in length (10-30 seconds). These demonstrations were distilled into single-task, point-cloud-based policies using 3D Diffusion Policy, trained on 4 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for 500 epochs.
- **Input Modalities and Augmentations:** Policies used uncolored point clouds (from four RealSense D455 cameras) as visual input, supplemented with small pose jitter, simulated "ghost" points to mimic sensor noise, and uniform downsampling. Robot end-effector pose and gripper state served as proprioceptive inputs.
- **Execution:** An asynchronous policy runner with temporal ensembling executed 32 actions from a predicted 64-action chunk at 30Hz on an A6000 GPU.
- **Real-World Performance:** Evaluated over 30 trials with randomized object poses, the policies demonstrated generalization to novel object configurations, achieving an average success rate of 44% across the eight real-world manipulation tasks. This indicates the framework's capability to generate data suitable for effective zero-shot transfer without real-world fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The ANYTASK framework represents a significant advancement in addressing the data bottleneck that constrains generalist robot learning. By automating the entire pipeline from high-level task specification to zero-shot real-world policy deployment, this work substantially reduces the manual effort traditionally associated with creating large-scale, diverse, and high-quality robotic datasets.

The key contributions and their potential impact include:

1. **Reduced Human Effort and Enhanced Scalability:** The automated generation of tasks, scenes, success criteria, and expert demonstrations using foundation models and massively parallel simulation significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing complex robot behaviors. This scalability enables the creation of vast datasets that would be impractical to collect manually, fostering the development of more generalist and capable robotic agents.
2. **Increased Task and Data Diversity:** The ability to automatically generate a wide range of tasks and associated data, as evidenced by the lower self-BLEU scores for task descriptions, is crucial for training policies that can generalize to novel situations. This diversity moves robot learning closer to developing policies that are robust across various objects, environments, and manipulation challenges.
3. **Advanced Demonstration Synthesis:** The introduction of diverse ANYTASK agents (VIPR, VIPR-EUREKA, VIPR-RL), each excelling at different types of tasks (TAMP for long-horizon, RL for contact-rich), provides a comprehensive approach to generating high-quality expert trajectories. The VLM-in-the-loop refinement for VIPR and the Mesh-based Contact Sampling for VIPR-EUREKA represent methodical improvements in synthesizing effective demonstrations.
4. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A core achievement is the successful deployment of policies trained purely on synthetic data to a physical robot without any real-world fine-tuning, achieving a 44% average success rate across diverse tasks. This result validates the quality of the generated data and the effectiveness of the chosen sim-to-real transfer strategies, such as domain randomization and point-cloud-based policies. This capability could dramatically accelerate real-world robot deployment by bypassing the costly and time-consuming process of real-world data collection.

In summary, ANYTASK offers a comprehensive solution for generating synthetic data that is both diverse and high-fidelity enough to bridge the sim-to-real gap. Its impact lies in paving the way for more autonomous, scalable, and generalized robot learning, potentially accelerating research and development in areas requiring robust robotic manipulation. The framework's limitations, such as performance on high-precision tasks and reliance on point-cloud inputs for sim-to-real, also point to clear avenues for future research, including expanding to RGB-based policies and more complex mobile manipulation.
