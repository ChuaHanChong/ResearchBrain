# X-Token: Projection-Guided Cross-Tokenizer Knowledge Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> X-Token introduces a projection-guided cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation framework that enables efficient knowledge transfer between language models with different tokenizers. It consistently outperforms prior methods like GOLD, achieving an average gain of +3.82 points with a Qwen3-4B teacher and +0.5 points with a Phi-4-mini teacher, and demonstrates benefits in multi-teacher distillation.

## Problem

- Traditional knowledge distillation methods rely on shared tokenizers, limiting the choice of teacher models and hindering multi-teacher distillation across diverse tokenizer families.
- Existing logit-distribution-based cross-tokenizer methods, like GOLD, suffer from "uncommon-token failure" where critical tokens (e.g., multi-digit numerals) receive degraded supervision.
- These methods also exhibit "over-conservative matching" due to strict string-equality criteria, overlooking functionally equivalent token pairs and losing useful alignment signals.

## Method

- A dynamic programming-based span alignment method identifies text-consistent chunks across teacher and student token sequences, providing robust positional correspondence.
- A sparse projection matrix, W, is deterministically constructed to map student-token probabilities into the teacher's vocabulary space, bridging incompatible vocabularies.
- Two complementary loss formulations, Projection-Guided KL (P-KL) and Hybrid Loss with Relaxed Matching (H-KL), are introduced to address specific shortcomings of prior hybrid loss designs, selected based on token coverage audit.

## Results

- X-Token consistently outperformed the state-of-the-art GOLD method, showing an average gain of +3.82 points with a Qwen3-4B teacher and +0.5 points with a Phi-4-mini teacher.
- P-KL dramatically improved GSM8k accuracy by approximately 6-fold (from 2.56 to 15.54) with a Qwen3-4B teacher, outperforming even same-tokenizer KD from a weaker teacher.
- Multi-teacher distillation, combining Phi-mini (H-KL) with Llama-3B (standard KL), yielded the highest overall average accuracy of 40.48, exceeding the best single-teacher cross-tokenizer run by +1.3 points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - P-KL effectively resolves "uncommon-token failure" by projecting the student's entire distribution into the teacher's vocabulary space, ensuring all tokens receive identity-aware supervision.
> - H-KL mitigates "over-conservative matching" by expanding the "common" token set through relaxed matching using the projection matrix W, capturing broader semantic correspondences.
> - Optimal distillation performance depends on dynamically selecting either P-KL or H-KL based on whether critical tokens are adequately covered by the common set for a given teacher-student tokenizer pair.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Knowledge Distillation (KD) is a technique that involves transferring "dark knowledge" from a large, high-performing "teacher" model to a smaller, more efficient "student" model. Traditional KD methods typically rely on a shared tokenizer between the teacher and student models, often using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence over their next-token probability distributions. This requirement limits the choice of teacher models, as a student model with a specific tokenizer cannot directly learn from teachers with incompatible vocabularies. This constraint also hinders multi-teacher distillation across different tokenizer families, preventing the combination of diverse teachers with complementary strengths (e.g., reasoning, code, multilingual capabilities).

The field of cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation aims to address this limitation by enabling knowledge transfer between models that utilize different tokenizers. Existing methods in this area generally fall into two categories:
1. **Representation-based approaches:** These methods align teacher and student models at the embedding or hidden-state level. Examples include DSKD and ZETT, which often require auxiliary trainable components or modifications to the model architecture.
2. **Logit-distribution-based approaches:** These methods operate directly on the output probability distributions of the models. They are often preferred for their ease of integration, as they can function as drop-in replacements for standard KD loss without modifying the model architecture or requiring additional forward passes. ULD and GOLD are prominent examples within this family.

The current state-of-the-art logit-distribution-based method, GOLD, employs a hybrid loss that partitions tokens into a 1-to-1 string-matched common subset for KL divergence and an uncommon remainder for rank-based L1 matching. While this approach enables cross-tokenizer KD, it introduces two structural limitations:
- **Uncommon-token failure:** Critical tokens, such as multi-digit numerals in mathematical reasoning tasks, may fall into the unmatched uncommon subset due to differences in tokenization strategies (e.g., Qwen splitting numerals while Llama packs them). These tokens then receive degraded supervision from identity-agnostic rank-based matching and suppressive gradients from the common-KL term.
- **Over-conservative matching:** The strict string-equality criterion of GOLD excludes functionally equivalent token pairs across tokenizers or over teacher multi-token decompositions, leading to a loss of potentially useful alignment signals.

The "X-Token" work positions itself within the logit-distribution-based family, aiming to resolve these specific shortcomings of prior methods by introducing novel loss formulations and a projection mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation method that overcomes the limitations of existing logit-distribution-based approaches. The authors are motivated by the need to enable flexible and efficient transfer of knowledge between language models with incompatible tokenizers, thereby expanding the utility of knowledge distillation in practical applications.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Address the "uncommon-token failure" in existing hybrid loss formulations:** The paper highlights that when critical tokens (e.g., multi-digit numbers in arithmetic tasks) are fragmented differently by teacher and student tokenizers, they are relegated to the "uncommon" subset in methods like GOLD. This results in them receiving misdirected or suppressive gradients, leading to poor performance on tasks where these tokens are crucial. The motivation is to provide a mechanism that ensures critical tokens receive appropriate, identity-aware supervision, even when they lack a direct 1-to-1 string match across vocabularies.
2. **Mitigate "over-conservative matching":** Current methods' reliance on strict string equality for token matching can discard useful alignment signals between "near-equivalent" tokens or a student token and a multi-token decomposition from the teacher. The objective is to relax this strict matching criterion to capture a broader range of semantic correspondences, thereby exploiting more of the available knowledge transfer opportunities.
3. **Enable effective multi-teacher distillation across diverse tokenizer families:** By providing a more general and robust cross-tokenizer distillation framework, the work aims to facilitate combining multiple teachers, each potentially with a different tokenizer and specialized strengths (e.g., in reasoning, coding, or multilingual capabilities). This would allow for the creation of student models that inherit a broader and more complementary set of skills.
4. **Provide a drop-in replacement for standard KD loss:** A core motivation for logit-distribution-based methods is their operational simplicity, avoiding architectural modifications or auxiliary components. The authors aim to maintain this characteristic, ensuring their proposed method integrates seamlessly into existing training pipelines.
5. **Develop a deterministic and scalable alignment mechanism:** Accurate alignment of student and teacher sequences is fundamental to cross-tokenizer KD. The motivation is to provide a reliable and efficient method for text-consistent span alignment that can handle discrepancies arising from different tokenizers.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to unlock the full potential of cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation, freeing practitioners from tokenizer lock-in and enabling more powerful and versatile student models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

X-Token is a logit-distribution-based approach comprising three main components: (i) span alignment for text-consistent units, (ii) a sparse projection matrix $\mathbf{W}$ to bridge vocabularies, and (iii) two complementary loss formulations, P-KL and H-KL, with an optional multi-teacher extension.

#### 3.1. Sequence Alignment

To address the lack of positional correspondence between token sequences from different tokenizers ($\mathcal{T}_S$, $\mathcal{T}_T$), X-Token employs a dynamic programming (DP) based span alignment. This process groups tokens into chunks $\{(A_S^{(k)}, A_T^{(k)})\}$ where each pair of spans decodes to the same underlying text substring. A chain-rule merge then combines per-token probabilities within each aligned span to obtain chunk-level distributions $\hat{p}_S^{(k)}$ and $\hat{p}_T^{(k)}$, which serve as the aligned units for distillation. This DP-based approach is designed to be robust against local tokenization edge cases and inconsistent alignments that can arise in simpler substring-equality methods.

#### 3.2. X-Token Projection Matrix $\mathbf{W}$

A sparse projection matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}_S| \times |\mathcal{V}_T|}$ is introduced to map student-token probabilities into the teacher vocabulary space. $\mathbf{W}$ is constructed deterministically in two passes:
1. **Exact-match pass:** For student-teacher token pairs $(s, t)$ whose decoded strings match after canonicalization (unifying space prefixes, newline markers, etc.), $\mathbf{W}[s, t]$ is set to 1.
2. **Multi-token-rule pass:** For remaining student tokens $s$, their decoded text is re-tokenized under the teacher tokenizer, yielding a sequence of teacher tokens $(\tau_0, \ldots, \tau_L)$. $\mathbf{W}[s, \tau_i]$ is set with an exponentially decaying weight $\beta \gamma^i$ (e.g., $\beta=0.9, \gamma=0.1$), concentrating mass on leading sub-tokens.
Each row of $\mathbf{W}$ is then truncated to its top-4 entries and normalized. This matrix is constructed once before training and can be optionally fine-tuned during KD for P-KL. The matrix $\mathbf{W}$ acts as a probability-preserving operator, ensuring valid student distributions over the teacher vocabulary.

#### 3.3. Knowledge Distillation Loss Formulations

The paper proposes two complementary loss formulations, P-KL and H-KL, to address the identified shortcomings of previous hybrid loss designs. Both operate on the chunk-level distributions obtained via span alignment.

#### 3.3.1. P-KL: Projection-Guided KL

P-KL addresses the "uncommon-token failure" and the associated erroneous/suppressive gradients of hybrid losses (like GOLD). It entirely removes token partitioning and projects the student's distribution $\hat{p}_S^{(k)}$ into the teacher's vocabulary space $\tilde{p}_S^{(k)}$ using the projection matrix $\mathbf{W}$:
$\tilde{p}_S^{(k)}[t] = \sum_{s \in \mathcal{V}_S} \mathbf{W}[s, t] \cdot \hat{p}_S^{(k)}[s]$.
The loss is then a direct KL divergence between the teacher's distribution $\hat{p}_T^{(k)}$ and the projected student distribution $\tilde{p}_S^{(k)}$: $\mathcal{L}^{(k)}_P = \text{KL}(\hat{p}_T^{(k)} \parallel \tilde{p}_S^{(k)})$. This formulation provides teacher-aware supervision for all tokens, including those that would otherwise be in the "uncommon" set.

#### 3.3.2. H-KL: Hybrid Loss with Relaxed Matching

H-KL addresses the "over-conservative matching" issue of strict 1-to-1 string equality. It retains the hybrid loss structure but expands the "common" set $\mathcal{C}$ by relaxing the matching criterion. For each student token $s$, it identifies its top-ranked teacher token $t^*$ based on the highest weight in $\mathbf{W}$ ($t^* = \arg\max_{t' \in \mathcal{V}_T} \mathbf{W}[s, t']$ with $\mathbf{W}[s, t^*] > 0$). This extends $\mathcal{C}$ beyond exact string matches to include high-confidence alignments exposed by $\mathbf{W}$. The hybrid loss (similar to GOLD's $\mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\text{GOLD}} = \lambda_{\text{KL}} \mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\text{common}} + \lambda_{\text{ULD}} \mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\text{ULD}}$) is then applied over this expanded $\mathcal{C}$.

#### 3.4. Loss Selection Criteria

The choice between P-KL and H-KL is guided by a coverage audit over token categories within the student vocabulary. For instance, if critical tokens like multi-digit numerals fall into the uncommon set for a given teacher (e.g., with Qwen3-4B), P-KL is selected. If these critical tokens remain in the common set (e.g., with Phi-4-mini), H-KL is chosen to leverage its sharper identity-aligned supervision.

#### 3.5. Multi-Teacher Distillation

X-Token naturally extends to multi-teacher scenarios by aggregating per-teacher losses: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD,multi}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \alpha_m \mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{*,m}$, where $\mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{*,m}$ is the chosen loss (P-KL, H-KL, or standard KL for same-tokenizer teachers) for teacher $m$, and $\alpha_m$ are teacher-specific weights. While adaptive weighting schemes based on teacher confidence were explored, simple static weighting was found to perform effectively. The approach emphasizes the importance of teacher complementarity for gains.

#### 3.6. Dynamic KD/CE Scaling

To stabilize optimization, the distillation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}$ (or $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD,multi}}$) is dynamically rescaled at each step to match the magnitude of the next-token cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$: $\mathcal{L} = \text{sg}(\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} / \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}) \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$. This ensures a consistent balance between the two loss components throughout training.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

Experiments use Llama-3.2-1B as the student, trained on the Nemotron-ClimbMix dataset. Teachers include Llama-3.2-3B (same tokenizer), Qwen3-4B, and Phi-4-mini-Instruct (cross-tokenizer). Evaluation is conducted on 3-shot accuracy across five benchmarks: MMLU, GSM8k, MATH-Hendrycks, Winogrande, and HellaSwag. Baselines include no distillation, continued pre-training, same-tokenizer KD, ULD, and GOLD, all reimplemented under identical training settings to isolate distillation mechanism differences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy of X-Token in cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation:

- **Overall Performance Improvement:** X-Token consistently outperformed the current state-of-the-art GOLD method. With a Qwen3-4B teacher, X-Token (P-KL) achieved an average gain of +3.82 points. With a Phi-4-mini teacher, X-Token (H-KL) achieved an average gain of +0.5 points.

- **P-KL Effectiveness in Uncommon-Token Regimes (Qwen3-4B Teacher):**
 - When critical tokens (e.g., multi-digit numerals in GSM8k) fall into the uncommon subset due to tokenizer differences (as observed with Qwen3-4B), GOLD's performance significantly degrades (2.56 on GSM8k, resulting in 35.03 average accuracy), performing worse than even continued pre-training without a teacher.
 - P-KL, by removing the partition and projecting student mass through $\mathbf{W}$, addressed this issue effectively. It improved GSM8k accuracy by approximately 6-fold (from 2.56 to 15.54) and overall average accuracy to 38.85. This improvement surpassed even same-tokenizer KD from a weaker teacher (Llama-3.2-3B, which achieved 12.89 on GSM8k), indicating the value of cross-tokenizer KD with P-KL for specific tasks.
 - P-KL also outperformed ULD (36.77 average), demonstrating the benefit of identity-aware projection over rank-based matching.

- **H-KL Effectiveness in Sound-Partition Regimes (Phi-4-mini Teacher):**
 - When critical tokens remained within the common subset (as observed with Phi-4-mini), GOLD's partition was structurally sound, yielding an average accuracy of 38.66.
 - H-KL, by relaxing strict string matching to incorporate top-ranked teacher mappings from $\mathbf{W}$, achieved further gains, reaching 39.18 average accuracy (+0.52 over GOLD). This highlights the benefit of improved coverage through relaxed matching while retaining the hybrid loss structure.
 - In this regime, P-KL performed worse than H-KL (37.50 vs. 39.18), indicating that when the partition is sound, removing it sacrifices sharper, identity-aligned supervision.

- **Tokenizer-Dependent Loss Selection:** Empirical results confirmed that selecting the appropriate loss formulation (P-KL or H-KL) based on the specific tokenizer pair and its impact on critical token coverage is crucial for optimal performance. Each loss mode showed a sharp drop when applied to the "wrong" teacher.

- **Multi-Teacher Distillation:**
 - **Complementary Gains:** Combining Phi-mini (H-KL) with Llama-3B (same-tokenizer standard KL) under static weighting yielded the highest overall average accuracy of 40.48. This result exceeded the best single-teacher cross-tokenizer run (39.18) by +1.3 points and the same-family reference by +2.1, demonstrating that combining teachers with complementary strengths (e.g., math/reasoning from Phi-mini, commonsense from Llama-3B) can be beneficial.
 - **Redundancy/Interference:** Combining two cross-tokenizer reasoning teachers (Phi-mini + Qwen-4B) achieved 38.49 average accuracy, which was below the best single-teacher result, suggesting overlapping capabilities or potential interference. Adding a third teacher (Qwen-4B) to the Phi-mini + Llama-3B setup resulted in 40.15 average, with slight trade-offs in performance across different benchmarks, further indicating potential redundancy.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Learnable Projection Matrix $\mathbf{W}$:** Jointly learning $\mathbf{W}$ during KD (under P-KL) yielded a modest but consistent improvement (38.85 vs. 38.37 average) compared to a frozen, rule-based $\mathbf{W}$, indicating that the rule-based initialization is strong but can be further refined.
 - **Dynamic KD/CE Scaling:** Dynamic scaling for balancing KD and cross-entropy losses performed marginally better than fixed-weight combinations across various settings, suggesting its utility in maintaining stable optimization.
 - **Multi-Teacher Weighting:** Simple static weighting schemes, particularly those emphasizing the stronger teacher, consistently outperformed adaptive confidence-based weighting schemes, indicating that static weighting can be sufficient and avoid tuning complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The X-Token framework offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of large language model training and deployment:

1. **Enabling Flexible Teacher Selection:** By effectively addressing the challenges of incompatible vocabularies, X-Token frees practitioners from the constraint of tokenizer lock-in. This allows them to leverage stronger or more specialized teacher models, irrespective of their native tokenization, for distilling knowledge into a student model with a different tokenizer. This expands the pool of available teachers and enables more strategic knowledge transfer.

2. **Improving Performance on Challenging Tasks:** The paper demonstrates that by correctly handling "uncommon-token failure," X-Token can lead to substantial performance gains on tasks sensitive to tokenization, such as mathematical reasoning (e.g., GSM8k). This directly impacts the ability to distill specific, critical skills that might otherwise be degraded by tokenizer mismatches.

3. **Facilitating Multi-Teacher Distillation from Diverse Sources:** X-Token's ability to seamlessly integrate multiple teachers with heterogeneous tokenizers opens avenues for creating more versatile student models. By combining teachers specializing in different domains (e.g., reasoning, commonsense, code), a student model can accumulate a broader set of complementary capabilities, potentially leading to more balanced and powerful distilled models.

4. **Advancing Logit-Distribution-Based KD:** The work refines logit-distribution-based KD, a preferred method due to its architectural simplicity (no auxiliary components or extra forward passes). By overcoming the limitations of previous methods like GOLD through the projection matrix $\mathbf{W}$ and its complementary loss formulations, X-Token pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with output distribution alignment alone.

5. **Robust and Deterministic Alignment:** The development of a robust DP-based span alignment method, which localizes and manages tokenization disagreements, provides a reliable foundation for cross-tokenizer interactions, contributing to the overall stability and effectiveness of the distillation process.

In summary, X-Token represents a methodological advancement in cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation, providing a practical and effective solution to a fundamental challenge in LLM development. Its impact extends to greater flexibility in model design, enhanced performance on diverse tasks, and improved capabilities for combining knowledge from multiple, disparate sources. Future work may explore its application to instruction-tuned models, larger students, and even more diverse tokenizer families, alongside more sophisticated multi-teacher weighting strategies.
