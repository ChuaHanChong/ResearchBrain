# Unified Human-Scene Interaction via Prompted Chain-of-Contacts

> [!summary] Summary
> UniHSI is a unified Human-Scene Interaction framework that leverages Large Language Models for high-level planning and a physics-based controller to enable versatile, natural language-driven control of diverse human-scene interactions. It achieves an 85.5% success rate for simple tasks on PartNet scenarios and generates physically plausible movements by defining interactions as a "Chain of Contacts," significantly reducing the need for manual interaction annotations.

## Problem

- Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) methods struggle with either physical plausibility (kinematics-based) or require task-specific policies and extensive, costly motion datasets (physics-based).
- Integrating natural language control into HSI often lacks precision, struggling to ground high-level commands to specific objects and exact contact regions in 3D environments.
- Existing systems lack a unified framework to handle a broad spectrum of HSI, particularly for long-horizon, multi-object interactions, making them hard to scale and generalize.

## Method

- Introduces "Chain of Contacts" (CoC), a uniform definition representing interactions as ordered sequences of contact steps, each specifying object parts, humanoid joints, contact types, and relative directions.
- Employs an ==LLM Planner== (GPT-3.5/GPT-4) to translate natural language commands into CoC task plans, leveraging LLM world knowledge to generate interaction sequences without requiring interaction-specific motion annotations.
- Develops a Unified Controller based on physics-based reinforcement learning (AMP), which uses a TaskParser to convert CoC steps into uniform task observations and objectives for physically plausible and natural motion execution.

## Results

- UniHSI achieved an **85.5%** success rate for simple tasks on PartNet scenarios and generalized well to real scanned scenes (ScanNet), with a **73.2%** success rate on simple tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of adaptive contact weights, which improved simple task success rate from **21.2%** to **85.5%**, and the importance of ego-centric heightmaps for robustness in complex environments.
- The framework consistently outperformed or matched baseline methods in quantitative metrics and exhibited faster convergence, generating more natural and semantically faithful motions, as validated by user studies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing diverse human-scene interactions as a structured "Chain of Contacts" allows for a flexible, generalizable, and unified approach to interaction modeling.
> - Large Language Models can effectively serve as high-level planners, translating natural language into executable physical interaction plans, thereby eliminating the need for labor-intensive, interaction-specific motion datasets.
> - A unified physics-based reinforcement learning controller, augmented with adaptive reward weighting and ego-centric scene awareness, can robustly execute complex CoC plans, ensuring both physical realism and natural motion quality across various interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) is a fundamental component of various applications, particularly in embodied AI and virtual reality. The broader research landscape for HSI is characterized by ongoing efforts to improve motion quality, physical plausibility, and user control.

Historically, HSI research has largely fallen into two categories:
- **Kinematics-based methods:** These approaches focus on synthesizing realistic humanoid movements. While they have achieved significant advancements in motion quality and diversity, they often struggle with physical plausibility, leading to artifacts like penetration or floating, and usually require extensive post-processing. They typically lack a robust mechanism for scene interaction or rely on pre-defined interaction gestures.
- **Physics-based methods:** More recent advancements leverage physics-aware simulators to ensure physical realism. These methods address the plausibility issues of kinematic models. However, they frequently suffer from limitations such as requiring separate policy networks for each specific task, a lack of fine-grained control over interactions, and a heavy reliance on large, annotated motion datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire for diverse interactions.

Furthermore, the integration of **language understanding** into human motion control has been an emerging area. Previous work in this domain has often focused on scene-agnostic motion synthesis. When applied to HSI, language-based control faces challenges in grounding natural language commands to specific objects and precise contact areas within a 3D environment. Existing language-driven HSI systems often either generate static interaction gestures, require pre-defined tasks with separate low-level policies, or struggle with adequately grounding target objects and contact regions.

"UNIFIED HUMAN-SCENE INTERACTION VIA PROMPTED CHAIN-OF-CONTACTS" (UniHSI) positions itself as a crucial advancement that bridges several of these gaps. It addresses the limitations of both kinematics and traditional physics-based methods by:
- **Unifying diverse interactions:** Unlike methods that require task-specific policies, UniHSI aims for a single, unified controller.
- **Leveraging natural language interfaces:** It offers a user-friendly way to control complex interactions through language, moving beyond action-based or manually designed controls.
- **Ensuring physical plausibility:** Built on a physics-based reinforcement learning framework, it guarantees realistic interactions.
- **Reducing annotation dependency:** By utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level planning, it minimizes the need for labor-intensive, interaction-specific motion annotations, a significant bottleneck in HSI research.
- **Enabling long-horizon and fine-grained control:** It supports multi-step interactions and precise contact definitions, going beyond simple, short-duration tasks.

The paper directly tackles the "versatile interaction control" and "user-friendly interfaces" issues that are noted as requiring further exploration for practical HSI applications. By introducing "Chain of Contacts" as a generalizable interaction definition and combining it with LLM planning and unified physics-based control, UniHSI pushes the boundaries of intuitive, realistic, and scalable human-scene interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **unified Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) framework, named UniHSI, that supports versatile control of diverse human-scene interactions through natural language commands**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations in current HSI systems:

1. **Versatile Interaction Control:** Existing HSI methods often focus on a limited set of interactions or require task-specific models. UniHSI aims to provide a single framework capable of handling a broad spectrum of human-scene interactions.
2. **User-Friendly Interface:** The authors recognize natural language commands as the most intuitive and accessible interface for users. A key objective is to effectively translate these high-level linguistic instructions into precise, executable interaction sequences.
3. **Unified Modeling for Scalability:** To achieve versatility, the framework needs to unify the representation of diverse interactions within a single model, ensuring scalability and reducing the complexity of developing separate solutions for each interaction.
4. **Physical Plausibility and Realism:** While aiming for versatility, maintaining physically plausible and realistic motion is crucial for practical applications in embodied AI and virtual reality, avoiding unnatural or artifact-ridden movements.
5. **Long-Horizon and Multi-Object Interactions:** Current methods often deal with short, single-action interactions. UniHSI seeks to enable continuous, multi-step interactions involving one or more objects, reflecting more complex real-world scenarios.
6. **Reduced Annotation Burden:** Traditional physics-based HSI methods heavily rely on labor-intensive, annotated motion sequences for training. A significant motivation is to mitigate this requirement by leveraging the vast knowledge embedded in Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate interaction plans, thereby making training interaction annotation-free.
7. **Fine-Granularity Control:** Beyond broad actions, the framework aims to offer fine-grained control over specific body joints and object parts involved in an interaction.

In essence, the motivation stems from the perceived gap between the current capabilities of HSI systems and the practical demands of embodied AI and virtual reality for truly intuitive, versatile, and realistic human-avatar interactions. By offering a unified, language-driven, and physically plausible solution, UniHSI seeks to significantly advance the state of the art in this domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniHSI proposes a novel two-component framework: an **LLM Planner** for high-level linguistic interpretation and a **Unified Controller** for low-level physical execution, built upon a new interaction definition called **Chain of Contacts (CoC)**.

#### 3.1. Chain of Contacts (CoC) Definition

The core innovation is the uniform representation of interactions as "Chain of Contacts." Inspired by the strong correlation between interaction types and contact regions, CoC defines an interaction as an ordered sequence of contact steps ($C = \{S_1, S_2...\}$). Each step ($S_i$) comprises multiple contact pairs. A contact pair is a five-element tuple: `{object, object part, humanoid joint, contact type, relative direction}`.
- **Object (o):** The target object in the scene.
- **Object Part (p):** A specific part of the object (e.g., "chair," "seat surface").
- **Humanoid Joint (j):** A specific human joint (e.g., "pelvis," "left hand").
- **Contact Type (c):** Specifies "contact," "not contact," or "not care."
- **Relative Direction (d):** Indicates the contact direction (e.g., "up," "down," "front").
This structured yet flexible definition allows UniHSI to model diverse interactions, including multi-round transitions and fine-grained details, in a standardized format.

#### 3.2. Large Language Model Planner

The LLM Planner acts as the high-level cognitive component, translating natural language commands into CoC.
- **Inputs:** Language commands, detailed background scenario information (objects, their parts, spatial layouts), humanoid joint information, and predefined instructions, rules, and examples.
- **Process:** Leverages prompt engineering to instruct LLMs (specifically GPT-3.5 and GPT-4) to generate step-by-step task plans in the CoC format. This includes planning for approaching objects, performing contacts, and transitioning between states.
- **Key Advantage:** By using LLMs, the framework significantly reduces the need for manual interaction annotations for training. The LLM's world knowledge allows it to generate plausible plans for versatile interactions, making the training process interaction annotation-free.

#### 3.3. Unified Controller

The Unified Controller takes the CoC plans from the LLM Planner and executes them as realistic, physically plausible movements.
- **Foundation:** It is built upon the Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) framework, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning system that uses an adversarial discriminator to model motion priors for realistic motion synthesis. The physical simulation is handled by IsaacGym.
- **TaskParser (Core of Unified Controller):**
 - **Uniform Task Formulation:** Converts the CoC steps into uniform task observations and task objectives. For each contact pair, it extracts the joint position, object part point clouds, and determines the nearest object point. The observation is `{nearest_point - joint_position, contact_type, relative_direction}`.
 - **Reward Function ($R_G$):** A summation of individual contact pair rewards, $R_k$.
 - For "contact": Rewards proximity and correct relative direction.
 - For "not contact": Rewards being far from the specified part.
 - For "not care": Provides a maximum reward.
 - **Step Sequencing:** Evaluates the completion of the current CoC step based on predefined criteria (e.g., distance and direction thresholds) and sequentially fetches the next step, enabling multi-round and long-horizon control.
- **Adaptive Contact Weights:** To address the challenge of balancing rewards for multiple contact pairs, UniHSI adaptively adjusts the weights for each contact reward ($w_k$). Weights are dynamically reduced for easily optimized or unused pairs and increased for more challenging ones, facilitating efficient learning.
- **Ego-centric Heightmap:** To enhance scene awareness and prevent collisions, especially in densely populated or complex scenes, the controller samples surrounding information as an ego-centric heightmap. This helps the humanoid navigate and interact safely within the environment.
- **Motion Discriminator:** The AMP framework's motion discriminator is retained to ensure that the generated movements are natural and realistic, learned from unlabeled motion datasets (SAMP and CIRCLE).

#### 3.4. Dataset (ScenePlan)

To support training and evaluation, the authors created a novel dataset, ScenePlan. This dataset comprises diverse indoor scenarios constructed from PartNet (for objects with fine-grained parts suitable for LLM planning) and ScanNet (for real-world scanned scenes). The LLM Planner generates thousands of interaction plans for these scenarios, encompassing simple, medium, and hard tasks with varying horizons and object involvement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniHSI demonstrates significant advancements in versatile, language-conditioned human-scene interaction, supported by comprehensive experiments and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Performance on ScenePlan Dataset

- **Versatile Task Execution:** UniHSI achieves high success rates (85.5%) and low contact errors (0.035) on "simple" tasks (up to 3 steps, single object) in PartNet scenarios.
- **Long-Horizon and Complex Interactions:** While performance shows a noticeable decline for "medium" (more than 3 steps, single object) and "hard" (multiple objects) tasks, the "Success Steps" metric indicates that the model still effectively completes significant portions of these complex plans (e.g., 4.8 average successful steps for hard tasks on PartNet).
- **Generalizability to Real Scanned Scenes:** The framework demonstrates good generalization ability to real-world scanned scenes from ScanNet, achieving a 73.2% success rate on simple tasks and 22.3% on hard tasks, despite the modality gap with the training data. This highlights its robustness to real-world scene complexities.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Adaptive Weights:** Removing the adaptive weights from the controller leads to a substantial performance drop across all task levels (e.g., simple task success rate drops from 85.5% to 21.2%), underscoring their critical role in effectively balancing and optimizing rewards for various contact pairs.
- **Ego-centric Heightmap:** The absence of the ego-centric heightmap results in performance degradation, particularly for difficult tasks, emphasizing its importance for collision avoidance, scene perception, and generalization to complex scanned scenes.
- **Choice of LLMs:** A comparison between GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 for the LLM Planner reveals that GPT-4 significantly outperforms GPT-3.5 in both task Execution Success Rate (ESR) and Planning Correctness (PC). However, both LLMs still lag behind human performance, indicating that LLM planning for highly nuanced physical interactions remains an area for further improvement, with issues like incomplete planning, out-of-distribution actions, and spatial reasoning errors.

#### 4.3. Design Comparison with Previous Methods

- **Quantitative Superiority:** UniHSI consistently outperforms or matches baseline implementations derived from previous methods (NSM, SAMP, InterPhys, AMP) on common interaction tasks like "Sit," "Lie Down," and "Reach." The performance advantage is particularly notable in more challenging tasks like "Lie Down."
- **Efficient and Effective Training:** UniHSI demonstrates higher success rates and faster convergence during training compared to baseline implementations.
- **Unified vs. Vanilla Combination:** A "vanilla combination" of AMP for multiple tasks results in a significant performance drop, attributed to interference and inefficiencies during training. In contrast, UniHSI's unified representation through CoC and its TaskParser enable effective multi-task learning without such degradation.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** UniHSI generates more natural and accurate movements than baselines. This is because the LLM Planner decomposes tasks into natural, sequential movements, avoiding the unnatural simultaneous execution of sub-tasks often seen in baselines that combine objectives heuristically.

#### 4.4. User Study on Motion Reality

A user study evaluating the naturalness and semantic faithfulness of the generated motions shows that UniHSI achieves higher scores compared to a baseline AMP model, both in PartNet and ScanNet scenarios. This subjective evaluation reinforces the objective findings that UniHSI produces more realistic and command-aligned interactions.

In summary, the results highlight UniHSI's effectiveness in generating versatile, physically plausible, and natural human-scene interactions driven by natural language, demonstrating robust performance and generalization capabilities across various scenarios and levels of complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniHSI represents a significant step forward in the field of Human-Scene Interaction, with profound implications for embodied AI, virtual reality, and related domains. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unified and Versatile HSI Control:** By proposing the "Chain of Contacts" (CoC) as a universal interaction definition and developing a unified controller, UniHSI moves beyond task-specific solutions. This unification is crucial for building scalable and general-purpose agents capable of a wide array of interactions with diverse environments, a long-standing challenge in embodied AI.
2. **Intuitive Language-Based Interaction:** The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) as high-level planners provides a highly intuitive and user-friendly interface. Users can describe complex interactions in natural language, democratizing access to sophisticated HSI control and making it more accessible for non-expert users in applications like virtual reality, interactive storytelling, or character animation.
3. **Reduced Annotation Burden:** Leveraging LLMs to generate interaction plans in the CoC format largely eliminates the need for labor-intensive, interaction-specific motion datasets. This "interaction annotation-free" approach is a game-changer for scalability, significantly accelerating the development and deployment of HSI systems for new tasks and environments.
4. **Enhanced Physical Realism and Naturalness:** By building upon physics-based reinforcement learning (AMP) and incorporating a motion discriminator, UniHSI ensures that the generated interactions are not only task-compliant but also physically plausible and natural-looking, avoiding common artifacts found in kinematics-based methods. The qualitative and user study results confirm this improvement.
5. **Support for Long-Horizon and Fine-Grained Interactions:** The step-by-step planning enabled by CoC and the TaskParser facilitates multi-round, long-horizon interactions, and the ability to specify human joint-object part contact pairs allows for fine-granularity control. This capability is essential for creating truly intelligent agents that can perform complex, sequential tasks in dynamic environments.
6. **Generalizability to Real-World Scenes:** The demonstrated ability of UniHSI to generalize to real scanned scenes (ScanNet) is a critical step towards practical applications beyond synthetic environments. This robustness is vital for deployment in real-world scenarios, such as robotic assistants or digital twins.
7. **New Dataset (ScenePlan):** The creation of the ScenePlan dataset, encompassing LLM-generated plans for diverse scenarios from PartNet and ScanNet, serves as a valuable resource for future research and benchmarking in language-conditioned HSI.

In conclusion, UniHSI introduces a robust, scalable, and intuitive paradigm for human-scene interaction. Its core contributions in interaction representation, LLM-based planning, and unified physics-based control are likely to inspire further research in embodied AI, robotics, and virtual content creation, pushing these fields closer to achieving truly intelligent and interactive virtual agents and environments. The identified limitations, such as handling movable objects and seamless LLM integration into training, also point to exciting avenues for future exploration, ensuring continued advancement in this critical area.
