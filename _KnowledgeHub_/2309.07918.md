# Unified Human-Scene Interaction via Prompted Chain-of-Contacts

> [!summary] Summary
> UniHSI is a unified Human-Scene Interaction framework that leverages Large Language Models for high-level planning and a physics-based controller to enable versatile, natural language-driven control of diverse human-scene interactions. It achieves an 85.5% success rate for simple tasks on PartNet scenarios and generates physically plausible movements by defining interactions as a "Chain of Contacts," significantly reducing the need for manual interaction annotations.

## Problem

- Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) methods struggle with either physical plausibility (kinematics-based) or require task-specific policies and extensive, costly motion datasets (physics-based).
- Integrating natural language control into HSI often lacks precision, struggling to ground high-level commands to specific objects and exact contact regions in 3D environments.
- Existing systems lack a unified framework to handle a broad spectrum of HSI, particularly for long-horizon, multi-object interactions, making them hard to scale and generalize.

## Method

- Introduces "Chain of Contacts" (CoC), a uniform definition representing interactions as ordered sequences of contact steps, each specifying object parts, humanoid joints, contact types, and relative directions.
- Employs an ==LLM Planner== (GPT-3.5/GPT-4) to translate natural language commands into CoC task plans, leveraging LLM world knowledge to generate interaction sequences without requiring interaction-specific motion annotations.
- Develops a Unified Controller based on physics-based reinforcement learning (AMP), which uses a TaskParser to convert CoC steps into uniform task observations and objectives for physically plausible and natural motion execution.

## Results

- UniHSI achieved an **85.5%** success rate for simple tasks on PartNet scenarios and generalized well to real scanned scenes (ScanNet), with a **73.2%** success rate on simple tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of adaptive contact weights, which improved simple task success rate from **21.2%** to **85.5%**, and the importance of ego-centric heightmaps for robustness in complex environments.
- The framework consistently outperformed or matched baseline methods in quantitative metrics and exhibited faster convergence, generating more natural and semantically faithful motions, as validated by user studies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing diverse human-scene interactions as a structured "Chain of Contacts" allows for a flexible, generalizable, and unified approach to interaction modeling.
> - Large Language Models can effectively serve as high-level planners, translating natural language into executable physical interaction plans, thereby eliminating the need for labor-intensive, interaction-specific motion datasets.
> - A unified physics-based reinforcement learning controller, augmented with adaptive reward weighting and ego-centric scene awareness, can robustly execute complex CoC plans, ensuring both physical realism and natural motion quality across various interactions.
