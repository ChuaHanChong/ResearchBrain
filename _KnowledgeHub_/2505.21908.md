# Reinforcement Learning for Out-of-Distribution Reasoning in LLMs: An Empirical Study on Diagnosis-Related Group Coding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mayo Clinic and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign developed DRG-SAPPHIRE, a reinforcement learning-based model for automated Diagnosis-Related Group (DRG) coding that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on the MIMIC-IV dataset while providing physician-validated, explainable reasoning.

## Problem

- Automated Diagnosis-Related Group (DRG) coding is challenging due to the critical need for accuracy in hospital reimbursement, traditionally requiring labor-intensive manual assignment by highly trained specialists.
- Existing LLM methods often treat DRG coding as a black-box classification problem, lacking transparency and explainability, which hinders their adoption in clinical settings where trust and validation are crucial.
- DRG coding is an out-of-distribution (OOD) task for general LLMs because pretraining data typically lacks exposure to private clinical or billing records, and the task involves a high-dimensional search space (over 700 codes) with complex hierarchical rules.

## Method

- Introduced **DRG-SAPPHIRE**, a large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) approach using a variant of ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) to automate MS-DRG code assignment from unstructured clinical notes.
- Implemented a three-step training pipeline consisting of bootstrapping Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the Qwen2.5-7B base model, and subsequent large-scale RL with verifiable rule-based rewards.
- Proposed and evaluated several algorithmic enhancements to GRPO, including dynamic resampling, targeted interventions for specific CoT reasoning patterns, KL divergence decay, and various reward shaping strategies.

## Results

- The optimal DRG-SAPPHIRE model achieved a state-of-the-art accuracy of **54.8%** on the MIMIC-IV dataset for automated DRG coding, outperforming previous proprietary and open-source models.
- DRG-SAPPHIRE generates interpretable, physician-validated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, with expert physicians rating its helpfulness and accuracy at a median score of 4 out of 5 across 30 evaluated cases.
- GRPO consistently improved Pass@1 accuracy by approximately 10 percentage points over SFT baselines on a reduced dataset, primarily by sharpening the model's output distribution rather than by expanding its capacity for diverse reasoning exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL performance on knowledge-intensive, out-of-distribution tasks such as DRG coding is fundamentally constrained by the domain-specific knowledge instilled in the base model through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) prior to RL training.
> - Scaling Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) data to infuse domain knowledge is more computationally efficient and effective for improving model performance in OOD domains compared to solely scaling RL training.
> - For DRG coding, the 'Answer-First' reasoning pattern (predicting the DRG code before the explanation) demonstrated superior performance over explicit CoT-First or Differential Thinking approaches, suggesting implicit knowledge leverage.
