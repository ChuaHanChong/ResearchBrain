# What Would Fix This RAG Failure? Auditing Counterfactual Response with Paired Evidence Interventions

> [!summary] Summary
> Pair-ID is an offline audit that takes an already-failed RAG answer and, holding query, retrieval order, reader, and decoding fixed, crosses two evidence edits: adding missing gold support and deleting verified nonsupport. The resulting three-cell counterfactual response vector shows support addition repairs 32.8% of joint-stratum failures and pooled deletion 13.6%, yet the original failed view cannot recover the exact vector beyond a majority baseline.

## Problem

- Observational RAG diagnostics like retrieval recall, answer presence, citation overlap, and claim checking describe the failed trace but never measure the response to a repair never executed.
- Different failure types demand different actions, retrieve, filter, both, or stop editing, yet they can share an identical final wrong answer, so observed error alone cannot validate a diagnosis.
- Prior evidence-intervention and failure-repair work varies evidence or routes failure states, but none crosses two verified repairs on the same already-failed instance to test reader dependence.

## Method

- Pair-ID factorial estimand crosses support addition with nonsupport deletion on one failed query, defining a JOINT stratum with four generated cells and a COMPLETE stratum where addition is structurally aliased.
- A deterministic funnel over both benchmarks plus a pinned DeBERTa entailment nonsupport verifier yields the eligible-failure frame; a prospectively fixed SHA-256 ordering selects the sample before any response is generated.
- Length- and position-matched sham blocks isolate semantic from prompt-size effects; nested out-of-fold observation-only attackers over BGE plus full-context Qwen features test action regret and direct vector recovery.

## Results

- Support addition repairs 197/600 JOINT failures (0.328, CI [0.292, 0.367]); pooled deletion repairs 162/1,190 (0.136, [0.117, 0.155]); matched shams retain contrasts of 0.223 and 0.101.
- The best loss-aligned full-view attacker reaches regret 0.100 versus 0.101 for constant always-joint, never approaching the prespecified 0.02 absorption threshold; exact-vector accuracy 0.637 trails the 0.646 majority baseline.
- Across four readers both marginal sensitivities recur (addition 0.102 to 0.334), but exact response-vector agreement is only 0.675 to 0.765 pooled and 0.538 to 0.691 on JOINT-only cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low action regret from a near-constant policy does not validate a diagnosis: coarsening distinct response vectors into one action hides which repair actually works.
> - Evidence sensitivity is a property of the failure-and-reader pair, not of the query, so failure taxonomies learned on one reader should not be stored as intrinsic labels.
> - Partial per-cell calibration gains alongside no exact-vector recovery show observed traces carry signal without identifying outcomes; claims stay frame-scoped, not information-theoretic impossibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by addressing the challenge of diagnosing and understanding RAG system failures. RAG systems combine the knowledge retrieval capabilities of search mechanisms with the generative power of large language models (LLMs) to produce informed and contextually relevant answers. However, these systems can fail for various reasons, such as retrieving irrelevant information, omitting necessary support, or generating incorrect answers despite having adequate context.

Existing work in RAG evaluation and robustness has focused on decomposing failures into fine-grained metrics (e.g., RAGChecker), benchmarking noise robustness and negative rejection (e.g., RGB), or studying how irrelevant context affects LLM performance. Another line of research involves evidence intervention and attribution, with studies like CUE-R, ONCU, RAGONITE, and Bridge Evidence exploring how variations in evidence (addition, deletion, replacement) affect system output and causal utility. Additionally, diagnosis-to-repair systems (e.g., Skill-RAG, D2R-RAG, Doctor-RAG, RePAIR) aim to identify failure states and route them to appropriate repair actions, such as rerunning retrieval or modifying context.

This paper differentiates itself by introducing Pair-ID, a methodology that specifically audits the *counterfactual response* of an already-failed RAG system to *crossed* evidence interventions. Unlike prior work that often focuses on single interventions or observational diagnostics, Pair-ID systematically measures the outcome when both adding missing support and deleting verified nonsupport are independently toggled for the *same failed query*. The central object of this study is the resulting "same-failure crossed response vector," which captures whether support addition, nonsupport deletion, their joint application, or neither repairs the original failure. This approach aims to provide direct reference labels for understanding evidence sensitivity, which can then be used to test the validity of observational RAG diagnoses. The research also investigates the prevalence of these responses, their semantic interpretability, their recoverability from the original failed state, and their stability across different reader models, thereby addressing gaps in understanding the intrinsic nature and predictability of RAG failures under intervention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from a fundamental challenge in diagnosing RAG system failures: an observed incorrect answer can be consistent with multiple underlying causes and potential repairs, but the system's actual response to an unexecuted repair remains unknown. For instance, a RAG system might fail because it omitted necessary supporting evidence, included interfering or distracting information, or both, or because the answer would remain incorrect even with evidence editing. Each of these scenarios calls for a different intervention (e.g., improved retrieval, context filtering, combined operations, or ceasing evidence manipulation), yet they may all manifest as the same final error.

While fine-grained observational diagnostics (e.g., retrieval recall, answer presence, citation overlap) provide useful insights into the observed trace, they offer predictions *from* the observed state rather than measuring the system's *response under* an intervention. This distinction is critical because an action recommendation based on an observational diagnosis might successfully reduce action loss without validating whether the underlying diagnosis (e.g., "support-sensitive" or "interference-sensitive") accurately reflects the system's behavior under an actual repair. A constant recommendation, such as "always try joint repair," might appear effective by collapsing different counterfactual responses into a single action, but it fails to provide diagnostic granularity.

The key objectives of this paper are:

1. **To define and measure the "same-failure counterfactual response vector":** The primary objective is to operationalize and quantify how a failed RAG system responds to two specific, verified evidence interventions: adding missing support and deleting verified nonsupport. This is achieved through Pair-ID, which keeps the query, retrieval state, reader, and decoding constant while independently applying these two edits.
2. **To assess the prevalence of evidence sensitivities:** The research aims to estimate the rates at which adding missing support or deleting nonsupport repairs failures within a defined frame of eligible RAG failures.
3. **To interpret the semantic nature of evidence changes:** Through the use of length- and position-matched sham controls, the study seeks to determine whether observed changes in response are attributable to the semantic content of the evidence interventions rather than mere alterations in prompt length or structure.
4. **To evaluate the observability of counterfactual responses from the original failed state:** A significant objective is to determine if the exact counterfactual response vector (i.e., which specific combination of edits leads to success) can be accurately predicted from the original failed query and context using advanced observation-only models. This investigates whether an "identifiability gap" exists between observable failure diagnostics and actual counterfactual responses.
5. **To assess the reader dependence of counterfactual responses:** By replicating the intervention factorial across multiple LLM readers, the study aims to ascertain whether the counterfactual response to evidence interventions is a universal property of the query or is conditional on the specific large language model used as the reader.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond observational diagnostics to establish a robust, empirically measured framework for understanding *what truly fixes* a RAG failure under specific evidence manipulations, thereby providing foundational insights for developing more effective and diagnostically valid RAG repair strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study introduces Pair-ID, an offline audit designed to measure counterfactual responses to evidence interventions for RAG failures. The methodology is structured around defining the "same-failure response object" and evaluating it through four complementary tests.

#### 3.1. The Same-Failure Response Object

For a given query `q`, the framework defines `Y(a, d)` as an alias-aware normalized exact match, where `a ∈ {0, 1}` indicates support addition and `d ∈ {0, 1}` indicates nonsupport deletion. `Y(a, d) = 1` signifies a correct answer. A query is eligible for the study only if its original answer is incorrect (`Y(0, 0) = 0`).

The methodology distinguishes between two strata:
- **JOINT stratum:** Applies when `G_q ≠ ∅` (missing support exists). All four combinations of `(a, d)` are independently generated: original (`Y(0, 0)`), add-only (`Y(1, 0)`), delete-only (`Y(0, 1)`), and joint (`Y(1, 1)`).
- **COMPLETE stratum:** Applies when `G_q = ∅` (no missing support). In this case, `Y(1, 0) ≡ Y(0, 0)` and `Y(1, 1) ≡ Y(0, 1)`, effectively making it a two-response deletion layer.

The primary factor estimands quantify the average effect of interventions:
- `τ_A = E[Y(1, 0) - Y(0, 0) | JOINT]` (JOINT addition effect)
- `τ_J_D = E[Y(0, 1) - Y(0, 0) | JOINT]` (JOINT deletion effect)
- `τ_C_D = E[Y(0, 1) - Y(0, 0) | COMPLETE]` (COMPLETE deletion effect)
Pooled deletion combines `τ_J_D` and `τ_C_D`. Token F1 is also used for factor contrasts. A secondary estimand, `Δ`, measures the JOINT interaction effect.

#### 3.2. From Response Vectors to Action Labels

An "observation attack" aims to predict a minimum-loss action `z` from `X_q` (the original view) based on a defined action loss function `L_q(z) = 1 - Y_q(z) + 0.05 c(z)`, where `c(z)` is the number of edit operators. The goal is to estimate the "identifiability gap," `G_F`, which quantifies how much regret a diagnostic family `F` incurs by predicting actions solely from `X_q` compared to the optimal action derived from the counterfactual matrix. A threshold of 0.02 is set for this gap to consider the observation to have "absorbed" the derived action object.

#### 3.3. Study Design - Four Complementary Tests

1. **Frame and Hash-Selected Sample:**
 - **Datasets:** HotpotQA (distractor validation split) and 2WikiMultiHopQA (development split).
 - **Screening Reader:** Qwen3-4B.
 - **Eligibility:** Out of 19,981 queries, 11,105 were identified as "native-eligible original failures." Eligibility required an incorrect original answer, benchmark support semantics, at least one removable paragraph, and full-input nonsupport verification. Nonsupport verification used a pinned DeBERTa-v3 model for NLI checks.
 - **Sampling:** A prospectively fixed hash function (SHA256) was used to select the first 1,200 eligible queries without replacement, ensuring prospective selection before any responses were generated. After regeneration, 1,190 queries remained "condition-valid."
 - **Constants:** Primary reader, prompt, evidence order, alias-aware exact-match normalization, and deterministic decoding were held constant.

2. **Matched Semantic Controls:**
 - **Purpose:** To isolate the semantic effect of evidence changes from simple length or position alterations in the prompt.
 - **Method:** In an independent sample, missing support was replaced with an equivalent number of cross-query, NLI-nonentailing sham blocks. Verified nonsupport was replaced with token-matched material at the same positions.

3. **Observation-Only Absorption Attack:**
 - **Purpose:** To test whether the original failed state (and related observable information) can predict the counterfactual response.
 - **Representations:**
 - **Baseline:** BGE-base-en-v1.5 encoder mapping question, context head, context tail, and failed response to 3,072 features.
 - **Stronger:** Qwen3-4B encoder using complete original context, question, and original response with mean and last-token pooling (8,192 features).
 - **Prediction Targets:** Minimum-loss action `z` and the uncoarsened three-bit vector `(Y_10, Y_01, Y_11)`.
 - **Learners:** Logistic regression and ExtraTrees, selected by inner-fold regret or mean cell Brier.
 - **Evaluation:** Nested out-of-fold predictions using metrics like AUROC, Brier score, accuracy, F1, Hamming accuracy, and multiclass Brier/log loss, compared against marginal-probability and majority-vector baselines.

4. **Four-Reader Replication:**
 - **Purpose:** To assess the stability and reader dependence of the counterfactual responses.
 - **Readers:** Qwen3-4B, Gemma-3-4B-IT, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3.
 - **Method:** A fixed hash permutation selected 600 queries from the frame sample. The factorial was regenerated for these queries using all four LLMs, conditioning effects on each reader's valid original failures.
 - **Recurrence Criterion:** Positive pooled addition and deletion estimates in at least three readers, with no dataset-level aggregate sign reversal.
 - **Metrics:** Exact three-bit agreement, Hamming agreement, per-cell agreement, and Cohen's κ for pairwise reader comparisons.

A **secondary probability-scale interaction audit** was also conducted on a subset of 180 JOINT queries with Qwen3-4B at temperature 0.2 and five fixed seeds to estimate success probabilities and interaction intervals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the counterfactual responses of RAG systems to evidence interventions:

#### 4.1. Reproducible Evidence Sensitivities

The hash-selected sample of eligible failures demonstrated measurable sensitivities to evidence interventions:
- **Support Addition:** Among 600 JOINT failures, adding missing support repaired 197 cases, resulting in a repair rate of 0.328 (95% CI [0.292, 0.367]).
- **Nonsupport Deletion:** Deleting verified nonsupport repaired 53/600 JOINT failures and 109/590 COMPLETE failures. The pooled deletion rate across 1,190 eligible cases was 0.136 (95% CI [0.117, 0.155]).
- Both addition and deletion effects were positive across both HotpotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA datasets.
- **Response Categories:** Among 1,190 valid failures, 769 (64.6%) remained incorrect after all evaluated edits (residual). The remaining 421 cases were categorized as: 152 (12.8%) addition-sensitive, 128 (10.8%) deletion-sensitive, 89 (7.5%) requiring complementary edits, 25 (2.1%) substitutable, and 27 (2.3%) antagonistic or nonmonotonic.

#### 4.2. Semantic Contrasts Isolated by Matched Shams

Matched semantic controls confirmed that the observed repair effects were largely due to the semantic content of the evidence changes, rather than merely alterations in prompt length or position:
- **Support Addition (Sham-rendered):** The effect was 0.223 (95% CI [0.181, 0.267]) on 382 JOINT failures.
- **Pooled Deletion (Sham-rendered):** The effect was 0.101 (95% CI [0.077, 0.126]) on 636 deletion-eligible failures.
These results indicate that the factors are not reducible to simple prompt-size effects.

#### 4.3. Partial Signal but Unresolved Exact Response from Full-Context Attacks

While the original view of a failed query contained some predictive signal for individual response cells, it struggled to recover the exact counterfactual response vector:
- **Cell-level Prediction:** The selected observation-only model achieved a macro cell AUROC of 0.678 and a Brier score of 0.152, which was a modest improvement over the marginal baseline (0.160). This suggests partial signal for individual success/failure of interventions.
- **Exact Vector Recovery:** However, exact-vector accuracy was 0.637, which did not exceed the majority-vector baseline of 0.646. Vector balanced accuracy was 0.172, and macro-F1 was 0.170, indicating weak performance in predicting the precise combination of responses.
- **Action Regret:** A loss-aligned attack with complete context achieved a mean regret of 0.100 (95% CI [0.092, 0.108]) over all 1,190 failures. While substantially stronger than a baseline BGE head/tail attack (0.238), it did not meet the specified 0.02 absorption threshold. The optimal policy in this setting was nearly constant, selecting "joint" for 1,171 out of 1,190 queries.

#### 4.4. Factor Recurrence Coexists with Reader-Conditional Response

The replication across four different LLM readers revealed both consistency in marginal effects and variability in query-level responses:
- **Marginal Recurrence:** Addition effects were positive for all four readers (ranging from 0.102 for Mistral to 0.334 for Qwen). Pooled deletion effects were also positive for all four (ranging from 0.024 to 0.128). This satisfied the recurrence criterion, implying that evidence sensitivities are not unique to a single reader.
- **Reader-Conditional Response:** Despite recurrent marginal effects, query-level exact response vectors were not stable across readers. Pooled pairwise exact agreement on the three-bit vector `(Y_10, Y_01, Y_11)` ranged from 0.675 to 0.765 (and 0.538–0.691 for JOINT-only cases), with lower multiclass vector Cohen's κ values. This indicates that the counterfactual response for a specific query is conditional on the reader, rather than being an intrinsic property of the query itself or supporting a reader-independent taxonomy of failures.

#### 4.5. Interaction and Acquisition Boundaries

The deterministic interaction rate was 0.058, showing heterogeneity across datasets, and a low-temperature probability audit estimated interaction at 0.063 (95% CI included zero), supporting factor sensitivity as the thesis rather than a universal interaction law. The study emphasizes that offline response characterization does not directly imply runtime value or a superior deployable policy, but rather provides a boundary for policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the understanding and diagnosis of RAG failures:

**A Framework for Auditing Counterfactual Responses:** Pair-ID introduces a systematic and robust methodology for empirically measuring how RAG systems respond to specific, verified evidence interventions. This is crucial for distinguishing between observational correlates of failure and the actual behavioral response of the system under repair, providing "reference labels" for diagnostic validity.

**Quantified Prevalence of Evidence Sensitivities:** The study quantifies that a meaningful proportion of RAG failures (approximately one-third for JOINT cases with missing support, and over one-tenth for deletion-eligible cases with verified nonsupport) are repairable through targeted evidence interventions. This provides a baseline understanding of how often these types of failures occur and are addressable.

**Isolation of Semantic Effects:** By using matched sham controls, the research confirms that the observed improvements are due to the semantic content of the added support or deleted nonsupport, rather than superficial changes to prompt length or structure. This reinforces the interpretability of "support sensitivity" and "interference sensitivity."

**Identification of an Observational Gap:** The findings indicate that even with comprehensive observable information from the original failed state, current diagnostic models struggle to precisely predict the *exact* counterfactual response vector. This "counterfactual action gap" suggests limitations in purely observational RAG failure diagnosis and highlights the need for more sophisticated predictive models or a different approach to diagnostics.

**Evidence for Reader-Conditional Responses:** The replication across multiple LLM readers demonstrates that while marginal evidence sensitivities recur, the specific counterfactual response for a given query is conditional on the LLM being used as the reader. This challenges the notion of a universal, reader-independent taxonomy of RAG failures and implies that repair strategies or diagnostic labels may need to be tailored to specific LLMs.

#### 5.1. Implications for RAG System Development and Evaluation

- **Diagnostic Tooling:** The methodology can serve as a benchmark for evaluating the effectiveness of RAG failure diagnostic tools. A diagnostic tool's predictions can be validated against the ground truth counterfactual responses generated by Pair-ID.
- **Adaptive RAG Systems:** Understanding the prevalence and nature of evidence sensitivities can inform the design of more adaptive RAG systems that can dynamically apply interventions.
- **Benchmarking and Robustness:** The study provides a deeper understanding of RAG system robustness to various evidence manipulations, complementing existing RAG evaluation benchmarks.

The research explicitly bounds its claims, stating it supports a "frame-scoped offline response audit" and a test of "diagnostic sufficiency," rather than an "information-theoretic impossibility result," a "reader-independent taxonomy," or a "runtime repair policy." This precise framing ensures a factual interpretation of its impact on the field.
