# Recurrent Reasoning with Vision-Language Models for Estimating Long-Horizon Embodied Task Progress

> [!summary] Summary
> R
												
																
																		2VLM, developed by researchers including those from Renmin University of China and Tsinghua University, introduces a recurrent reasoning Vision-Language Model that estimates long-horizon embodied task progress by processing short video snippets and updating an evolving Chain of Thought. The system achieves a mean absolute error (pmae) of 2.19 on ALFRED and 19.25 on Ego4D, demonstrating improved computational efficiency and accuracy for complex multi-step tasks while also proving effective for downstream applications like policy learning and reward modeling.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) based methods for embodied task progress estimation often underutilize VLMs' reasoning potential, struggling with complex temporal dependencies between subtasks.
- Processing entire, lengthy video trajectories with VLMs is computationally intensive, impractical for real-time applications, and can lead to attention dispersion and hallucination.

## Method

- A ==recurrent reasoning== framework (R
												
																
																		2VLM) that processes short, local video snippets and leverages a historical Chain of Thought (CoT) to incrementally update task understanding and progress.
- An evolving Chain of Thought (CoT) mechanism that generates an initial CoT and dynamically updates it by integrating historical context with new video observations, adapting task decomposition and step completion.
- A two-stage training strategy involving supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on an automatically generated CoT dataset and multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) with specific reward functions to refine reasoning.

## Results

- R
												
																
																		2VLM achieved best performance across all metrics on ALFRED (pmae 2.19, acc **0.988**) and Ego4D (pmae 19.25), outperforming existing VLM baselines and task-specific methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the snippet-based input maintained constant inference time and improved accuracy over full-video processing, while the full reward design, especially the Improvement Reward, was essential for optimal performance.
- R
												
																
																		2VLM's progress signals enhanced embodied policy learning (e.g., +**2.2%** goal-condition success rate on ALFRED) and provided effective dense rewards for online reinforcement learning across tasks of varying lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Processing short video snippets with historical CoT significantly improves computational efficiency and inference time, preventing attention dispersion and maintaining accuracy for long-duration tasks.
> - Explicit CoT generation and dynamic updating enable VLMs to perform structured reasoning about temporal dependencies, maintaining global task context and yielding more accurate and interpretable progress estimations.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with an 'Improvement Reward' incentivizing error reduction from previous turns, is critical for refining the model's reasoning beyond supervised signals and learning generalizable adaptive strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurately estimating task progress is a critical capability for embodied agents operating in complex, long-horizon, and multi-step environments. This research addresses a gap in current Vision-Language Model (VLM) based approaches, which, despite recent advancements (e.g., GVL, ROVER), primarily leverage VLMs for video understanding through large context windows. These existing methods often do not fully explore the complex reasoning potential of VLMs. Furthermore, processing entire long video trajectories using VLMs typically incurs substantial computational and time costs, which limits their practicality for real-world deployment.

In the broader context of embodied AI, progress estimation directly informs long-horizon planning and context-aware decision-making. For instance, knowing the completion status of subtasks allows an agent to sequence its actions or trigger recovery behaviors if a task stalls. The paper also connects to reward modeling in reinforcement learning (RL) for long-horizon tasks. Traditional inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) methods can be unstable or difficult to scale. Hierarchical reward frameworks exist but require manual decomposition of tasks. This work aims to provide an end-to-end solution for progress estimation that can also serve as a source of dense reward signals without requiring explicit hierarchical decomposition, thereby improving the efficiency and interpretability of agent training.

This research contributes to the development of more stable, accurate, and practical progress estimation models by integrating recurrent reasoning and explicit temporal dependencies, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior VLM applications in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and accurate method for estimating the progress of long-horizon embodied tasks. The authors identify two main challenges that motivate their work:

1. **Complexity of Temporal Dependencies:** Embodied tasks frequently involve multiple subtasks with intricate temporal relationships. An effective progress estimation model must be capable of reasoning about these dependencies and accurately aligning them with dynamic visual observations. Existing VLM-based approaches often focus on video understanding, potentially underutilizing the VLMs' capacity for complex, explicit reasoning over task structure.

2. **Computational Prohibitions of Long Video Trajectories:** Processing entire, lengthy video trajectories with VLMs is computationally intensive. The significant cost in terms of processing power and time renders such an approach impractical for real-world applications where real-time or near real-time progress estimation is required. The substantial visual token count in long videos can also lead to attention dispersion and potential "hallucination" in VLMs, degrading prediction accuracy.

To address these challenges, the research aims to achieve the following:
- **Enhance Reasoning Capabilities:** Endow VLMs with a structured reasoning mechanism to handle complex temporal dependencies between subtasks, moving beyond mere video understanding.
- **Improve Computational Efficiency:** Develop a framework that avoids the high cost of processing full, long videos, ensuring feasibility for real-world deployment.
- **Maintain Global Context:** Preserve essential global task context and historical information even when processing local, short video snippets.
- **Support Downstream Applications:** Provide a robust progress estimation signal that can be effectively utilized in various embodied AI applications, such as policy learning, reward modeling for reinforcement learning, and proactive assistance.

By tackling these motivations, the authors seek to create a progress estimation model that is both accurate and practical, thereby contributing to the advancement of embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce the Recurrent Reasoning Vision-Language Model (R$^2$VLM), which incorporates two primary innovations: a recurrent reasoning framework and an evolving Chain of Thought (CoT).

**Task Formulation:**
The model operates on streamed video input, where an agent continuously receives egocentric video (v) related to a task query (τ). At each iteration (t), the goal is to predict normalized task progress (p$_t$ ∈). Instead of processing the full video, R$^2$VLM processes short, local video snippets (v$_t$), each containing K uniformly sampled image frames. The core of the recurrent formulation is that the model (f$_θ$) takes the task query, the current video snippet (v$_t$), and the historical CoT (c$_{t-1}$) as input to incrementally update the CoT to c$_t$ and then output the current progress p$_t$. The CoT explicitly serves as a memory of previous video snippets and reasoning.

**Recurrent Reasoning with Chain of Thought (CoT):**
1. **Initial CoT Generation:** At the beginning of a task, R$^2$VLM prompts a VLM to generate an initial CoT (c$_0$) using its inherent commonsense knowledge. An ideal CoT for a multi-step task includes: (i) a decomposition of the long-horizon task into key subtasks, (ii) an analysis of which key subtasks have been observed and which have not, and (iii) a progress estimate derived from the proportion of completed subtasks.
2. **Dynamic CoT Update:** In subsequent iterations, the model integrates the historical CoT (c$_{t-1}$) with the newly observed video snippet (v$_t$) to update the CoT to c$_t$. This update process allows for dynamic adjustment of the task decomposition, including merging, splitting, or reordering steps, to align with the agent's actual execution observed in v$_t$. The completion status of steps is also updated.
This recurrent framework offers benefits in accuracy and interpretability due to explicit CoT generation, maintains global task coherence by integrating historical context, and ensures logical consistency across multi-step estimations.

**Dataset Construction:**
A large-scale, multi-turn incremental video interaction dataset was automatically generated for training and evaluation.
1. **Source Data:** Expert trajectories from ALFRED (simulated) and Ego4D (real-world) datasets were utilized. These datasets provide task descriptions (T), video demonstrations (E), and step-level decompositions (s$_i$) with start (t$_{s_i}$) and end (t$_{e_i}$) timestamps.
2. **Video Snippets with Progress Labels:** Videos were divided into short snippets (4s for ALFRED, 2s for Ego4D), with 4 frames sampled from each. Progress was defined as the proportion of completed steps, assuming linear variation within each step, using a formula based on completed steps (k) and total steps (n).
3. **Distractor Task Descriptions:** To improve model robustness against superficial visual cues, distractor task descriptions were generated using powerful VLMs with a constrained prompting strategy. These distractors matched initial steps of the expert demonstrations but diverged later, allowing for controllable and derivable progress assignments.
4. **Chain-of-Thought Training Data:** An automated pipeline leveraged large models to generate high-quality CoT data. The process integrated prior knowledge (task decomposition, observed/unobserved steps, and progress) available in the dataset to construct structured CoTs, which are crucial for subsequent inference.
The resulting datasets consisted of 11,499 ALFRED trajectories (124,821 dialogue tuples) and 13,965 Ego4D trajectories (127,694 dialogue tuples). Manually reviewed benchmarks for unseen scenarios were also constructed (93% retention for ALFRED, 74% for Ego4D).

**Training Strategy:**
Training of R$^2$VLM (f$_θ$) involves two stages:
1. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The model undergoes SFT using the automatically generated CoT data to learn the reasoning patterns for task decomposition, step-wise reasoning, and progress estimation.
2. **Multi-turn Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Starting from a cold-start checkpoint from the SFT stage, RL (using Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO) is applied to further enhance performance on multi-step reasoning tasks.
A suite of reward functions guides the RL process:
- **Format Reward (R$_{fmt}$):** Ensures the model's output adheres to the specified `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>` format.
- **Bin Reward (R$_{bin}$):** Provides a coarse-grained constraint, rewarding the model if its predicted progress falls within the correct step-completion interval (bin).
- **MAE Reward (R$_{mae}$):** Offers a fine-grained constraint, encouraging accurate progress predictions by penalizing the mean absolute error between predicted and ground-truth progress.
- **Improvement Reward (R$_{imp}$):** Specific to multi-turn RL, this reward incentivizes the model to produce more accurate predictions in the current turn compared to the previous one, reflecting its self-correction capability.
- **Finish Reward (R$_{fin}$):** Evaluates the model's ability to correctly identify overall task completion.
The **Overall Reward (R$_{overall}$)** combines these, ensuring that MAE reward is only applied when the bin prediction is correct for stability. The model is implemented based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, utilizing LLaMA-Factory for SFT and Verl for RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of R$^2$VLM was conducted using three model variants: R$^2$VLM-Zero (direct RL on the base model), R$^2$VLM-SFT (SFT on the full generated CoT dataset), and R$^2$VLM (RL continued from an SFT cold-start checkpoint). Performance was assessed against API-based models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen3-VL-Plus), open-source models (MiniCPM-V-2.6, GLM-4.1V-Thinking, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL), and task-specific methods (LIV, ROVER, GVL-SFT). Four metrics were used: p$_{mae}$ (mean absolute error of progress), ∆p$_{mae}$ (MAE of progress increment), bin (accuracy of progress falling into correct step bin), and acc (accuracy of task completion prediction).

**Overall Performance:**
- R$^2$VLM achieved the best performance across all four metrics on both the ALFRED and Ego4D benchmarks, demonstrating state-of-the-art results in long-horizon task progress estimation.
- Both R$^2$VLM-SFT and R$^2$VLM showed substantial improvements over baseline pretrained VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B and -72B). For instance, on ALFRED, R$^2$VLM achieved a p$_{mae}$ of 2.19, significantly lower than Qwen2.5-VL-7B's 27.87 and Qwen2.5-VL-72B's 24.88. This indicates that domain-specific fine-tuning and CoT integration are crucial, as pretrained models exhibited poor zero-shot performance.
- Applying RL after CoT-based SFT (R$^2$VLM) further improved performance over SFT alone (R$^2$VLM-SFT), suggesting that RL helps the model learn more generalizable and adaptive reasoning strategies beyond supervised signals. For example, R$^2$VLM's task completion accuracy (acc) on ALFRED was 0.988, compared to R$^2$VLM-SFT's 0.961. R$^2$VLM-Zero, which used direct RL without SFT, performed poorly, indicating the necessity of initial supervised learning for effective reasoning.
- Performance was consistently higher on the simulated ALFRED dataset than on the real-world Ego4D dataset across all models, including baselines. This difference is attributed to the increased complexity and noise of real-world environments and the variability in human execution paths in Ego4D, which challenges adaptive reasoning and progress estimation. Nonetheless, R$^2$VLM still achieved a p$_{mae}$ of 19.25 on Ego4D, outperforming other models significantly.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Video Snippet Input vs. Full Video Input:** Experiments on Ego4D demonstrated that processing video snippets with historical CoT provided advantages over processing the full video. The snippet-based method maintained a constant inference time regardless of video duration, while full video input showed a nearly linear increase in inference time. Moreover, the snippet-based approach yielded progressively better accuracy (lower MAE) as video duration increased, whereas full-video processing suffered from attention dispersion and degradation with longer sequences.
- **Reward Design:** Ablation of the Bin, MAE, and Improvement rewards on the ALFRED benchmark showed that the combination of all three rewards resulted in optimal performance. Removing the MAE reward led to a slight gain in bin metrics but degraded overall progress estimation (p$_{mae}$, ∆p$_{mae}$) and task completion accuracy. The Improvement Reward, which encourages reducing error from the previous turn, was found to be the most essential. Its removal increased p$_{mae}$ by approximately 10.5% and reduced task completion accuracy, confirming its role in guiding iterative reasoning refinement.

**Downstream Applications:**
- **Progress-enhanced Embodied Policy Learning:** R$^2$VLM's progress signals, based on the proportion of completed steps, improved the performance of Seq2Seq models for embodied agents on ALFRED. Agents trained with R$^2$VLM-estimated progress achieved higher goal-condition success rates (e.g., a 2.2% increase on the valid seen split) compared to those using time-based progress, which sometimes even degraded performance.
- **Reward Modeling for Online Reinforcement Learning:** R$^2$VLM proved effective in providing dense, general reward signals for online reinforcement learning. When used to fine-tune a pretrained SPRINT policy model on AI2-THOR, R$^2$VLM-derived rewards consistently improved agent performance across tasks of varying lengths (2–5 steps), surpassing sparse step-completion rewards and those from Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct.
- **Step-wise Proactive Assistance:** R$^2$VLM enabled proactive assistance by processing streaming video snippets and continuously updating its CoT. This allowed a lightweight LLM to extract structured lists of completed, in-progress, and pending steps in near real-time, accurately detecting key transitions and supporting task tracking.

**Cross-Domain Generalization:**
Evaluation of cross-domain generalization demonstrated that models trained on either ALFRED or Ego4D could outperform zero-shot baselines when tested on the other dataset. Furthermore, training on a combination of both datasets consistently improved performance on most metrics, particularly on Ego4D. This suggests that R$^2$VLM acquires generalizable reasoning and analytical capabilities rather than memorizing dataset-specific patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of R$^2$VLM represents a step in addressing critical challenges in embodied AI, specifically in the domain of long-horizon task progress estimation. The significance of this work lies in its integration of recurrent reasoning with an evolving Chain of Thought (CoT) and its efficient processing of local video snippets, which collectively yield practical and accurate results.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** By processing local video snippets instead of entire long trajectories, R$^2$VLM mitigates the computational and time costs associated with traditional VLM-based approaches. This makes the model more suitable for real-time deployment in embodied agents.
- **Enhanced Reasoning and Interpretability:** The explicit generation and dynamic updating of a CoT allow R$^2$VLM to reason about complex temporal dependencies and maintain global context, leading to more accurate and interpretable progress estimations. This move towards more structured reasoning within VLMs provides insights into their decision-making process.
- **Generalizability and Robustness:** Demonstrated strong performance across both simulated (ALFRED) and real-world (Ego4D) environments, indicating robustness to varying levels of environmental complexity and noise. The success in cross-domain generalization further supports the model's ability to acquire transferable reasoning skills.
- **Broad Applicability in Embodied AI:** The accurate and dense progress signals generated by R$^2$VLM have shown utility in diverse downstream applications:
 - **Embodied Policy Learning:** Providing a semantically grounded progress signal can significantly enhance an agent's ability to learn and execute complex tasks.
 - **Reward Modeling for Reinforcement Learning:** R$^2$VLM offers a solution to the challenge of sparse or inaccurate rewards in long-horizon RL, enabling more effective policy optimization.
 - **Proactive Assistance Systems:** The model's ability to continuously track step-level completion status in near real-time enables intelligent assistance for human operators or autonomous systems in various contexts, such as education, accessibility, or industrial automation.

Overall, R$^2$VLM offers a practical solution for task progress estimation, bridging the gap between VLM capabilities and the demands of real-world embodied AI applications. Its advancements in efficiency, reasoning, and broad applicability could accelerate the development of more capable and autonomous embodied agents.
