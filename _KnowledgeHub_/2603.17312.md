# Recurrent Reasoning with Vision-Language Models for Estimating Long-Horizon Embodied Task Progress

> [!summary] Summary
> R
												
																
																		2VLM, developed by researchers including those from Renmin University of China and Tsinghua University, introduces a recurrent reasoning Vision-Language Model that estimates long-horizon embodied task progress by processing short video snippets and updating an evolving Chain of Thought. The system achieves a mean absolute error (pmae) of 2.19 on ALFRED and 19.25 on Ego4D, demonstrating improved computational efficiency and accuracy for complex multi-step tasks while also proving effective for downstream applications like policy learning and reward modeling.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) based methods for embodied task progress estimation often underutilize VLMs' reasoning potential, struggling with complex temporal dependencies between subtasks.
- Processing entire, lengthy video trajectories with VLMs is computationally intensive, impractical for real-time applications, and can lead to attention dispersion and hallucination.

## Method

- A ==recurrent reasoning== framework (R
												
																
																		2VLM) that processes short, local video snippets and leverages a historical Chain of Thought (CoT) to incrementally update task understanding and progress.
- An evolving Chain of Thought (CoT) mechanism that generates an initial CoT and dynamically updates it by integrating historical context with new video observations, adapting task decomposition and step completion.
- A two-stage training strategy involving supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on an automatically generated CoT dataset and multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) with specific reward functions to refine reasoning.

## Results

- R
												
																
																		2VLM achieved best performance across all metrics on ALFRED (pmae 2.19, acc **0.988**) and Ego4D (pmae 19.25), outperforming existing VLM baselines and task-specific methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the snippet-based input maintained constant inference time and improved accuracy over full-video processing, while the full reward design, especially the Improvement Reward, was essential for optimal performance.
- R
												
																
																		2VLM's progress signals enhanced embodied policy learning (e.g., +**2.2%** goal-condition success rate on ALFRED) and provided effective dense rewards for online reinforcement learning across tasks of varying lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Processing short video snippets with historical CoT significantly improves computational efficiency and inference time, preventing attention dispersion and maintaining accuracy for long-duration tasks.
> - Explicit CoT generation and dynamic updating enable VLMs to perform structured reasoning about temporal dependencies, maintaining global task context and yielding more accurate and interpretable progress estimations.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with an 'Improvement Reward' incentivizing error reduction from previous turns, is critical for refining the model's reasoning beyond supervised signals and learning generalizable adaptive strategies.
