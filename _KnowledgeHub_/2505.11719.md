# Zero-Shot Visual Generalization in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Southern California researchers developed a robot manipulation framework that leverages disentangled representations and associative latent dynamics for zero-shot visual generalization. This approach, when integrated with diffusion-based imitation learning, outperformed baselines on diverse visual perturbations in simulation and real-world tasks, while a learned canonicalization finetuning achieved ~96% success under severe image rotations.

## Problem

- Vision-based robot manipulation policies often fail to generalize to novel visual environments and out-of-distribution data, despite extensive data augmentation or domain randomization.
- Promising disentangled representation learning techniques, like Associative Latent DisentAngle-ment (ALDA), had not been scaled to complex robotic manipulation tasks or deployed in real-world settings.
- Existing robot policies lack robustness to camera perturbations (e.g., rotations), and methods for achieving equivariance typically require expensive retraining from scratch.

## Method

- ==Leverages Associative Latent DisentAngle==-ment (ALDA) to learn factorized visual representations that can robustly handle out-of-distribution visual inputs by "recalling" known observations.
- Integrates ALDA with Diffusion Policy (ALDA-DP) for efficient behavior cloning, enabling its application to complex robotic manipulation tasks using expert demonstrations.
- Introduces a finetuning method for pre-trained policies using learned canonicalization to adapt them for equivariance to discrete planar camera rotations without full retraining.

## Results

- ALDA-based methods (ALDA-SAC and ALDA-DP) demonstrated superior zero-shot visual generalization across various visual perturbations in complex simulation tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- The equivariant adaptation finetuning procedure increased policy success rates from **1.6**-**4.19%** to **76.86**-**96.93%** on the PickCube task under simulated camera rotations.
- In real-world PickCube experiments, ALDA-DP achieved an **80%** success rate and maintained robustness (e.g., **60%** with distracting objects, **70%** with directed light from left) where baseline methods frequently collapsed to **0%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured, factorized representations learned through associative latent dynamics can confer robust zero-shot visual generalization to robot policies, outperforming data-centric baselines in diverse environments.
> - The principles of disentangled representation learning can be effectively integrated with sample-efficient imitation learning methods, like Diffusion Policy, making them practical for real-world robot manipulation.
> - Pre-trained robot policies can be made robust to camera rotations through a lightweight finetuning process using learned canonicalization, avoiding the need for extensive retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of vision-based manipulation policies that can operate robustly across diverse visual environments remains a significant challenge in robot learning. Current methodologies predominantly address this problem through several established trends. One approach involves relying on invariant representations, such as point clouds or depth information, which inherently filter out certain visual variabilities. Another common strategy is to force generalization through extensive visual domain randomization in simulation or by training on very large, visually diverse datasets, often leading to the development of "Robot Foundation Models" like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models learn from varied contexts, aiming to generalize through data scale, akin to sophisticated domain randomization.

However, the efficacy of these data-centric approaches in achieving true extrapolative generalization has been questioned in recent literature. Studies have indicated that vision-language models can perform poorly on out-of-distribution (OOD) data, and state-of-the-art classifiers exhibit significant accuracy drops when confronted with objects in unusual poses. Furthermore, neural networks trained via supervised learning have been shown to struggle with extracting and generalizing fundamental symmetries, such as SO(2) rotations, suggesting that data scaling alone may be insufficient for robust generalization.

In contrast to these data-intensive paradigms, biological systems demonstrate a remarkable ability to extract structure from high-dimensional sensory inputs and adapt rapidly, even under severe visual perturbations. Neuroscience research suggests that factorized and modular representations are key enablers of this structural generalization. In machine learning, similar principles are explored through disentangled and object-centric representation learning, which have shown promise in facilitating visual generalization in continuous control tasks within simulation environments. However, these techniques have largely been confined to simpler benchmarks and controlled settings, not yet scaled to complex manipulation tasks or real-world deployment.

A recent development, Associative Latent DisentAngle-ment (ALDA), an reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm, has combined factorized representation learning with principles of associative memory (inspired by Hopfield Networks) to demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in visual generalization benchmarks for continuous control. While promising, ALDA has not been extensively tested in complex robotic manipulation or real-world scenarios, and its direct application as an RL algorithm struggles with the sample efficiency demands of real-world interaction, making imitation learning a more practical alternative for such contexts.

Another aspect of real-world deployment concerns camera perturbations. Equivariant Neural Networks, which encode symmetry structures into their architecture, offer a solution to robustly handle transformations like rotations. However, these methods typically require training from scratch, which can be computationally expensive and limit architectural choices. Learned canonicalization, a more recent technique, proposes adapting pre-trained models to be equivariant without complete retraining by using a lightweight surrogate network. This approach has primarily been explored in simpler supervised learning tasks like image classification, and its applicability to robot learning policies under dynamic, real-world conditions remained an open area.

This work contributes to this landscape by bridging these gaps, specifically by scaling disentangled representation learning and associative memory to complex manipulation tasks and real-world scenarios, extending it to imitation learning, and adapting learned canonicalization for robot policy equivariance to camera perturbations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop robot manipulation policies that exhibit robust zero-shot visual generalization across diverse visual environments and are resilient to camera perturbations, thereby enabling more adaptable and reliable real-world robot deployment. The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Lack of Robustness to Visual Distribution Shifts:** Current vision-based manipulation policies often struggle with changes in visual conditions that were not explicitly encountered during training. While domain randomization and large datasets aim to provide visual diversity, evidence suggests these methods may not achieve true *extrapolative* generalization, performing poorly on out-of-distribution data or failing to generalize fundamental symmetries. This implies that agents trained this way might not be inherently robust to novel visual disturbances encountered in unstructured real-world settings. The paper is motivated by the hypothesis that more structured, factorized representations, inspired by biological systems, could offer a path toward more robust generalization without requiring exhaustive data coverage.

2. **Scaling Disentangled Representation Learning:** While disentangled representation learning and associative memory (as exemplified by ALDA) have shown promise in simpler continuous control benchmarks for visual generalization, their application has not been scaled to more complex, visually rich, and dynamically challenging robotic manipulation tasks. A key motivation is to investigate if the principles of ALDA can be effectively applied to the domain of robotic manipulation and demonstrate its utility in real-world scenarios.

3. **Practicality for Real-World Manipulation:** Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, including the original ALDA, often suffer from poor sample efficiency, making them impractical for direct training from real-world interaction for complex manipulation tasks. This motivates the need to extend the benefits of disentangled representations and associative memory to more sample-efficient paradigms, specifically imitation learning (behavior cloning), which can leverage expert demonstration datasets. The paper aims to integrate ALDA's principles with state-of-the-art imitation learning methods, such as Diffusion Policy.

4. **Addressing Camera Perturbations:** Beyond visual variations in the scene, real-world robotic deployments are also subject to camera perturbations, such as rotations, which can further degrade policy performance. While equivariant neural networks can encode rotational symmetries, they typically require training a new model from scratch, limiting flexibility and increasing training time. This motivates the development of a method to efficiently adapt *pre-trained* robot policies to be robust to such perturbations. The paper seeks to introduce a novel technique, adapted from learned canonicalization literature, to transform existing neural network policies into ones invariant to 2D planar rotations.

In summary, the specific objectives articulated as contributions are:
1. To evaluate the performance of ALDA on complex manipulation tasks, demonstrating its visual generalization capabilities.
2. To extend ALDA to diffusion-based behavior cloning (ALDA-DP) and show improved generalization compared to existing state-of-the-art imitation learning baselines.
3. To propose a finetuning method for adapting any pre-trained robot policy to be equivariant to discrete planar rotations.
4. To validate the effectiveness of these combined approaches on a real robot under realistic visual perturbations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a comprehensive approach to achieve zero-shot visual generalization and robustness to camera perturbations in robot manipulation, primarily building upon the Associative Latent DisentAngle-ment (ALDA) framework and integrating it with state-of-the-art imitation learning and a novel equivariant adaptation technique.

#### 3.1. Disentangled Representations with Associative Latent Dynamics (ALDA Core)

The foundational component is the ALDA model, which learns factorized representations from high-dimensional visual inputs.
- **Encoder:** An encoder network, denoted as $f_{\theta}$, processes an image observation $o$ and maps it to a continuous latent representation $z_{\text{cont}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_z}$.
- **Associative Latent Dynamics:** Each dimension $z_j$ of $z_{\text{cont}}$ is then mapped to a discrete value $z_d_j$ using a collection of scalar codebooks $Z = V_1 \times V_2 \times \dots \times V_{n_z}$, one for each latent dimension. This mapping is performed via an attention-based association mechanism:
 $z_{d_j} = \text{Softmax}(\beta\text{Sim}(z_j, V_j)) \odot V_j$
 where $\text{Sim}(\cdot, \cdot)$ is a similarity function (negative L1 distance is used), and $\beta$ controls the degree of separation.
- **Decoder:** A decoder network $g_{\phi}$ uses the discrete representation $z_d$ to reconstruct the original observation, establishing a feedback loop for visual information.
- **Disentanglement Enforcement:** The discrete nature of $z_d$, coupled with large network activation penalties applied to the encoder and decoder weights, facilitates the disentanglement process.
- **Out-of-Distribution Handling:** During inference, if an observation is in-distribution, the association step acts approximately as a no-op. However, for out-of-distribution (OOD) observations due to visual distractions, Equation (2) forces the representation $z_d$ to conform to the in-distribution space before being processed by the policy, effectively "recalling" the most related known observation.

#### 3.2. ALDA Integration with Diffusion Policy (ALDA-DP)

For complex manipulation tasks and real-world deployment, ALDA's principles are extended to imitation learning, specifically Diffusion Policy, which is a generative model for learning visuomotor policies.
- **Policy Architecture:** The diffusion model learns to iteratively denoise action sequences ($\tau = a_{t:t+k}$) conditioned on the disentangled latent representation $z_d$ (derived from the image observation $o$) and the robot's proprioceptive state $s$.
- **Action Representation:** Actions are defined as 4-dimensional vectors comprising delta xyz position commands for the end-effector and a binary gripper open/close command.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained on expert action trajectories. A random trajectory timestep $t$ and diffusion timestep $i$ are sampled, and noise $\epsilon$ is added to the ground truth action sequence $\tau_0$. The diffusion model learns to predict this noise. The training objective for Diffusion Policy is $J(\text{DP}) = ||\epsilon_{\theta}(z_d, s, \tau_i, i) - \epsilon||^2_2$.
- **Overall Training:** The final training objective for ALDA-DP combines the ALDA objective ($J(\text{ALDA})$), which includes reconstruction loss, commitment loss, and activation penalties, with the Diffusion Policy objective: $J(\text{ALDA}) + J(\text{DP})$.
- **ALDA-SAC Variant:** A reinforcement learning variant, ALDA-SAC, is also utilized for comparison. It combines ALDA's representation learning with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC). For ManiSkill3 tasks, a simplified implementation without frame stacking or projection of latent states is used, where a single observation's disentangled latent is directly fed to actor/critic networks.

#### 3.3. Equivariant Adaptation via Learned Canonicalization

To address robustness to camera perturbations, specifically planar rotations, the paper introduces a finetuning method based on learned canonicalization for pre-trained policies.
- **Concept:** The goal is to make a pre-trained policy $\pi(a|z)$ (comprising an encoder $f$, latent model $l$, and policy network $\pi$) equivariant to 2D planar rotations. Instead of retraining the entire policy, a lightweight equivariant neural network, $C_{\phi}$, is initialized as a *canonicalization function*.
- **Mechanism:** $C_{\phi}$ learns to transform any rotated input observation $o$ into a "canonical" orientation $o_{\text{canon}}$. The policy then operates on this canonical input, and the output action can be transformed back if necessary (though for manipulation, the canonicalized action might be directly applicable). The core idea is to ensure that applying a group action (e.g., rotation) on the input $o$ results in a predictable transformation of the policy's output.
- **Finetuning Objective:** The finetuning process trains $C_{\phi}$ and the original policy's components ($f, l, \pi$) to minimize the difference between the actions predicted by the finetuned policy on canonicalized inputs and the actions predicted by a frozen "oracle" clone of the original, un-finetuned policy on the original (potentially rotated) inputs. The objective is:
 $|| \pi(a|l(f(C_{\phi}(o)))) - \pi_*(a|l_*(f_*(o))) ||^2_2 + \beta \cdot L_{\text{prior}} + L_{\text{commit}}$
 where $\pi_*, l_*, f_*$ are frozen versions of the original policy's components.
- **Loss Components:**
 - **Action Reconstruction Loss:** The primary term, $|| \pi(a|l(f(C_{\phi}(o)))) - \pi_*(a|l_*(f_*(o))) ||^2_2$, ensures the finetuned policy's output on canonicalized input matches the oracle's output on original input.
 - **Canonicalization Prior ($L_{\text{prior}}$):** A regularizer, $-\mathbb{E}_{o \sim D} [D_{KL}(P_D || P_{C_{\phi}(o)})]$, ensures the canonicalized inputs $C_{\phi}(o)$ remain as close as possible to the original observation distribution, preventing degenerate canonicalizations.
 - **Commitment Loss ($L_{\text{commit}}$):** From ALDA's training objective, this term ($|| \text{StopGrad}(z_{\text{canon_d}}) - z_{\text{canon_cont}} ||^2_2$) maintains the proximity between continuous and discrete latent representations during finetuning.
- **Data Source:** The finetuning uses the replay buffer (for ALDA-SAC) or expert demonstration dataset (for ALDA-DP) as a source of training transitions.
- **Restriction:** Due to challenges with continuous rotations in prior work, this method is restricted to discrete planar rotations ($C_n$ groups), where $n$ defines the granularity of rotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and on real hardware provide empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (ManiSkill Visual Generalization Benchmark - MVGB)

The research used ManiSkill3's tabletop manipulation suite, augmented with a custom MVGB, including Distracting Backgrounds (DBG), Random Colors, and Random Lighting, as well as combinations thereof.

- **ALDA-SAC Performance (RL tasks: PickCube, PushCube, PullCube):**
 - On **PickCube**, ALDA-SAC consistently demonstrated superior performance across all visual variations, often by significant margins, compared to baselines like SAC, SAC-AE, and TD-MPC2.
 - On **PushCube** and **PullCube**, ALDA-SAC also outperformed other RL baselines with substantial margins.
- **ALDA-DP Performance (BC tasks: PickCube, PushCube, PushT):**
 - On **PickCube**, ALDA-DP showed strong performance and significant gains in generalization compared to state-of-the-art imitation learning baselines, Diffusion Policy (DP) and Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT).
 - On **PushCube**, ALDA-DP and Diffusion Policy performed similarly, both outperforming ACT. The authors suggest that simpler non-prehensile pushing tasks might inherently be more robust to visual perturbations for behavior cloning, reducing the necessity for structured representations.
 - On the more challenging **PushT** task, overall success rates were lower across all methods. However, ALDA-DP still outperformed baselines on most visual variations, indicating that the structured representations provided by ALDA offer benefits even when the underlying BC algorithm struggles with task complexity.
- **Overall Simulation Trend:** ALDA-based methods (ALDA-SAC and ALDA-DP) generally exhibited superior zero-shot visual generalization across a range of manipulation tasks and visual perturbations in simulation.

#### 4.2. Equivariant Adaptation Results

The finetuning procedure for equivariant adaptation was applied to ALDA-DP and ALDA-SAC on the PickCube task for discrete cyclic groups ($C_8, C_{12}, C_{24}$), representing image rotations by 45, 30, and 15 degrees, respectively.

- **ALDA-SAC with Equivariant Adaptation:**
 - Achieved high success rates, maintaining approximately 96-97% success across all tested rotation granularities ($C_8, C_{12}, C_{24}$).
 - This represents a substantial improvement compared to the non-finetuned ALDA-SAC, which collapsed to success rates between 1.60% and 1.78% under rotations.
- **ALDA-DP with Equivariant Adaptation:**
 - Also benefited from the finetuning, with success rates of 76.86% for $C_8$.
 - Performance showed a gradual decline with finer rotational granularities, dropping to 60.94% for $C_{12}$ and 45.57% for $C_{24}$.
 - Despite the drop, this was significantly better than the non-finetuned ALDA-DP, which had success rates between 3.16% and 4.19% under rotations.
 - The authors hypothesize that ALDA-DP's larger model size might make finetuning more challenging, potentially requiring more training iterations or deeper canonicalization networks.
- **Conclusion on Equivariance:** The finetuning procedure effectively enhanced the robustness of both ALDA-SAC and ALDA-DP to image rotations, a critical aspect for mitigating camera perturbations in real-world deployments.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Experiments were conducted using a Franka Emika Panda arm, a RealSense D515 camera, and 200 teleoperated demonstrations for a PickCube task. Policies were evaluated under six types of visual perturbations: basic (no perturbation), directed light (left, middle, right), ambient light, gray cube (color change), and distracting objects.

- **ALDA-DP Performance:**
 - Achieved an 80% success rate in the basic setup.
 - Demonstrated strong robustness across various perturbations: 85% with ambient light, 30% with a gray cube, and 60% with distracting objects.
 - For directed light, it achieved 70% success when the light was from the left, but performance collapsed (0%) for middle and right directed light.
- **Baseline Performance (DP and ACT):**
 - Diffusion Policy (DP) and ACT achieved 80% success in the basic setup.
 - However, both baselines largely failed (0% success) across most visual perturbations, with the exception of ACT achieving 55% success for directed light from the left.
- **Real-World Limitations:** The authors observed performance collapse in certain extreme conditions, such as changing table color without corresponding training data, and sunlight bleeding into the camera frame causing oversaturation. Directed light from specific angles (middle/right) also proved challenging, potentially due to occlusions or drastic perceived color shifts.
- **Overall Real-World Trend:** ALDA-DP significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on the majority of realistic visual perturbations in a real-world setting, affirming its practical utility.

#### 4.4. Latent Traversals (Qualitative Analysis)

Qualitative analysis via latent traversals (Appendix A) demonstrated that ALDA-DP successfully learns disentangled representations where individual latent dimensions correspond to distinct factors of variation (e.g., object x-axis position, gripper state). The presence of discontinuities in some traversals (e.g., cube on table vs. picked up) is noted, potentially attributed to the discrete nature of the latent representation or the policy's focus on critical task states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an alternative paradigm to visual generalization in robot learning, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Visual Generalization without Exhaustive Data Augmentation:** The work provides strong empirical evidence that disentangled representations, when coupled with associative latent dynamics, can enable robust zero-shot visual generalization for complex manipulation tasks. This is achieved without extensive reliance on data augmentation, heavy domain randomization, or explicit camera calibration, which are often computationally intensive and require broad data collection. This approach suggests a path to building more generalist agents that are less dependent on exhaustive visual data coverage.

2. **Scalability to Complex Tasks and Real-World Scenarios:** By successfully extending ALDA to visually rich and dynamically complex manipulation tasks in simulation and demonstrating its effectiveness on real hardware, the research addresses a critical limitation of previous disentanglement work, which was largely confined to simpler benchmarks. This advancement marks a step towards deploying robots in uncontrolled, visually diverse environments.

3. **Integration with Imitation Learning for Practicality:** The successful integration of ALDA's principles with diffusion-based behavior cloning (ALDA-DP) addresses the sample efficiency challenges of reinforcement learning for real-world manipulation. This makes the robust generalization capabilities of disentangled representations more accessible and practical for applications where expert demonstrations are feasible to collect.

4. **Robustness to Camera Perturbations:** The introduction of a finetuning method to make pre-trained robot policies equivariant to discrete planar rotations significantly enhances their robustness to common camera perturbations. This learned canonicalization approach offers an efficient way to adapt existing policies without costly retraining from scratch, thereby increasing the reliability of robot systems in varied operational setups.

5. **Alternative to Data-Centric Generalization:** By focusing on structured representations and associative memory, this research offers a conceptual alternative or complement to the current trend of scaling up data and model sizes (e.g., foundation models). It suggests that inherent architectural properties and representation learning strategies can contribute significantly to generalization, potentially mitigating some of the limitations observed with purely data-driven approaches regarding extrapolative generalization.

6. **Paving the Way for Generalist Agents:** The combination of visual robustness and resilience to camera perturbations constitutes meaningful progress towards developing generalist robotic agents capable of robust, out-of-the-box adaptation in dynamic and complex real-world settings. This could reduce deployment costs and increase the versatility of robotic systems across various industries and applications.

While the research acknowledges limitations, such as challenges with extreme lighting conditions or specific combinations of randomized factors, it highlights exciting opportunities for future work, including scaling these techniques to larger models and datasets to potentially combine the benefits of structured representations with broad data diversity.
