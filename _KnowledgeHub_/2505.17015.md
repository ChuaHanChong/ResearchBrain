# Multi-SpatialMLLM: Multi-Frame Spatial Understanding with Multi-Modal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta AI and CUHK researchers develop Multi-SpatialMLLM by creating the MultiSPA dataset—1.2 million multi-frame instruction-following samples from real-world 3D/4D datasets—to train language models for spatial reasoning across image pairs, achieving 36% performance gains over InternVL2 baseline on tasks including depth perception, visual correspondence, camera movement detection, and object tracking while demonstrating transferable spatial understanding on external benchmarks and showing potential as a multi-frame reward annotator for robotics applications.

## Problem

- Current multi-modal large language models lack robust spatial understanding capabilities, particularly across multiple frames
- Existing MLLMs cannot effectively reason about depth, visual correspondence, or dynamic changes between images
- Scarcity of large-scale training data specifically designed for multi-frame spatial reasoning tasks
- Limited evaluation frameworks for assessing multi-frame spatial understanding in vision-language models

## Method

- Create ==MultiSPA dataset== with 1.2 million instruction-following samples from Aria Digital Twin, Panoptic Studio, ScanNet, and TAPVid3D datasets
- Develop ==data engine== that samples image pairs with uniform overlap distribution and establishes pixel correspondences through ==backprojected point clouds==
- Design five core spatial reasoning tasks: depth perception, visual correspondence, camera movement, object movement, and object size perception
- Fine-tune InternVL2 model using ==LoRA== on MultiSPA dataset mixed with general instruction-following samples

## Results

- Multi-SpatialMLLM achieves **36%** performance improvement over InternVL2 baseline on MultiSPA benchmark
- Model outperforms proprietary systems including Claude-3.5, Gemini 2.0, and GPT-4o on multi-frame spatial tasks
- Demonstrates good generalization on BLINK benchmark while maintaining standard VQA proficiency
- Shows preliminary emergent behavior in challenging spatial tasks and potential for robotics reward annotation applications

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-task training on diverse spatial reasoning tasks provides synergistic benefits and improved generalization
> - Model performance scales with both training data size and model capacity, particularly for challenging vector prediction tasks
> - Rigid body segmentation for point cloud clustering enhances training data diversity and model robustness
> - Multi-frame spatial understanding transfers effectively to external benchmarks while preserving original VQA capabilities

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated rapid advancements in a wide array of visual tasks, establishing themselves as versatile AI assistants. This progress, however, has primarily focused on single-image understanding, limiting their spatial reasoning capabilities to static visual contexts. This limitation restricts the deployment of MLLMs in physical-world applications such as robotics and autonomous vehicles, which inherently require a comprehensive understanding of dynamic, multi-frame environments.

Prior research, exemplified by works like SpatialVLM and SpatialRGPT, has addressed deficiencies in MLLMs' single-image spatial understanding by introducing specialized training data. These efforts demonstrated notable improvements but were confined to static fields-of-view, largely relying on off-the-shelf modules for noisy annotations from in-the-wild images. This paper identifies a critical gap: the absence of robust multi-frame spatial understanding in MLLMs.

Drawing inspiration from the "Structure-from-Motion" problem in 3D computer vision, this work extends MLLMs' spatial understanding to integrate information across multiple images. It aims to equip MLLMs with fundamental spatial skills: depth perception (inferring 3D structure and distances), visual correspondence (matching overlapping regions across images), and dynamic perception (understanding camera and object movement). The challenge lies in the scarcity of suitable, large-scale, high-quality multi-frame spatial training data, as manual annotation is cost-prohibitive, and aligning unstructured in-the-wild images is complex.

The proposed approach leverages existing annotated 3D and 4D datasets, distinguishing itself from prior single-image efforts. While a concurrent work (SAT) also explores multi-frame spatial reasoning using simulated data, this research positions itself with a significantly larger dataset derived from real-world images, covering a broader range of spatial reasoning tasks, and aiming to mitigate the sim-to-real gap. By developing a novel data pipeline and a comprehensive benchmark, this study contributes to bridging the gap between MLLMs' current capabilities and the demands of real-world embodied AI applications that necessitate human-like spatial reasoning across dynamic scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to equip Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with robust multi-frame spatial understanding, a capability that has been largely overlooked in previous spatial understanding research focused predominantly on single-image scenarios.

The detailed motivations and specific objectives driving this work are as follows:

1. **Address Limitations in Current MLLMs for Real-World Applications**: Existing MLLMs, despite their advancements in visual tasks, exhibit limited spatial understanding, often struggling with even basic concepts like left-right differentiation. This deficiency renders them ill-suited for real-world applications such as robotics and autonomous vehicles, which necessitate sophisticated interactions with dynamic physical environments and a human-like comprehension of space and motion. The motivation is to move beyond MLLMs as "brains in a jar" for digital platforms and enable their integration into embodied AI.

2. **Overcome the Scarcity of Multi-Frame Spatial Training Data**: A significant hurdle in developing multi-frame spatial understanding is the lack of suitable, large-scale, and high-quality training data. Previous attempts to improve spatial understanding often relied on noisy, single-view data or off-the-shelf modules, which are insufficient for multi-frame reasoning. Manual annotation for multi-frame data is prohibitively expensive, and aligning spatial and temporal information in unstructured "in-the-wild" images remains an open challenge. This motivates the development of a novel data pipeline to generate synthetic yet high-fidelity multi-frame spatial data from existing annotated 3D and 4D datasets.

3. **Integrate Fundamental Spatial Skills into MLLMs**: The research is motivated by the need to incorporate three core spatial capabilities essential for multi-frame understanding, inspired by principles from 3D computer vision:
 - **Depth Perception**: The ability to infer relative distances and reconstruct three-dimensional structures from visual input.
 - **Visual Correspondence**: The capacity to accurately match overlapping regions and points across multiple images, ensuring consistent scene association over time or different viewpoints.
 - **Dynamic Perception**: The skill to perceive and quantify movement, encompassing both ego-motion (camera movement) and the movement of objects within the scene.

4. **Support Diverse Input Referencing and Output Formats**: Current spatial understanding benchmarks and datasets often constrain input referencing to semantic labels or object masks and limit outputs to qualitative descriptions or multiple-choice formats. The motivation is to broaden these capabilities by supporting multiple input modalities (visual point annotations, pixel coordinates, semantic labels) and diverse quantitative outputs (scalar values, 2D pixel locations, 3D displacement vectors), thereby enhancing the model's versatility and utility for downstream applications.

5. **Develop a Comprehensive Evaluation Framework**: To accurately assess the progress in multi-frame spatial understanding, there is a need for a standardized and comprehensive evaluation framework. This motivates the creation of the MultiSPA benchmark, designed to test a wide spectrum of spatial tasks under uniform metrics, allowing for rigorous comparison against baselines and proprietary systems.

In essence, the research aims to fill a critical gap in MLLM capabilities by enabling them to process and reason about spatial information across multiple temporal or multi-view frames, thereby paving the way for more capable and versatile embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on developing a framework to equip MLLMs with multi-frame spatial understanding by integrating fundamental spatial skills. This involves a novel data generation pipeline to create a large-scale dataset, MultiSPA, and a comprehensive benchmark for evaluation.

#### 3.1. MultiSPA Dataset Design

The dataset is designed to train MLLMs on five key multi-frame spatial understanding tasks:
- **Depth Perception**: Inferring object distances and comparing depths of two points.
- **Visual Correspondence**: Matching a point from one image to its corresponding location in another.
- **Camera Movement Perception**: Estimating the camera's relative translation and orientation between frames.
- **Object Movement Perception**: Quantifying the displacement of an object or point across frames.
- **Object Size Perception**: Determining the 3D dimensions (height, width, length) of an object from multiple views.

The data generation supports diverse input referencing methods:
- **Visual dots (points)**: Direct annotation on images.
- **Pixel coordinates**: Numerical (u,v) values, normalized by image width and height (scaled to 0-1000).
- **Semantic labels**: Textual descriptions of objects.

Output formats are also diverse, including:
- **Qualitative text**: Descriptive answers.
- **Multiple-choice questions (MCQ)**: Selecting from predefined options.
- **Scalar values**: Numerical answers (e.g., depth in mm, rotation in degrees).
- **2D Pixel Coordinates**: Normalized (u,v) values for corresponding points.
- **3D Displacement Vectors**: [x, y, z] vectors for movement, in millimeters.

#### 3.2. MultiSPA Data Generation Pipeline

The pipeline leverages existing annotated 3D and 4D datasets to generate high-quality question-answer (QA) pairs at scale.

#### 3.2.1. Source Datasets

- **Static Scenes**: ScanNet (3D indoor scans with point clouds, camera poses, depth maps, instance/semantic masks) for Depth Perception, Visual Correspondence, Camera Movement Perception, and Object Size Perception.
- **Dynamic Scenes**: Aria Digital Twin (ADT) and Panoptic Studio (PStudio), with 3D tracking annotations from TAPVid3D, for Object Movement Perception.

#### 3.2.2. Data Generation for Static Scenes

1. **Visible Points Calculation**: For each image in a ScanNet scene, 3D world points are transformed into camera coordinates and projected onto the 2D image plane. Points whose projections fall within image bounds and are not occluded (checked against depth maps) are considered visible.
2. **Depth Perception Data**: Randomly sample images and one or two visible points. Record their 2D coordinates and 3D depth, then use LLM-generated templates (GPT-4o) to form QA pairs for direct depth estimation or depth comparison.
3. **Image Pairs Sampling**: To create multi-frame data, pairs of images with overlapping regions are required. The overlap ratio is defined as the Intersection over Union (IoU) of their visible points. Pairs with 6% to 35% overlap are selected. A binning strategy is employed to ensure balanced sampling across different overlap magnitudes, preventing over-representation of low-overlap pairs.
4. **Visual Correspondence Data**: For each sampled image pair, a point visible in both images (from their co-visible set) is randomly selected. Its projected pixel coordinates in both images are used to construct qualitative (MCQ with distractors) or quantitative (coordinate output) QA pairs.
5. **Camera Movement Perception Data**: For an image pair, the relative camera pose (extrinsic matrix transformation) is computed. This yields a 3D displacement vector ([x, y, z] in mm) and rotation angles (yaw, pitch in degrees). These are then converted into various QA formats, including qualitative directions (e.g., "right," "up," "forward"), scalar distances/degrees, and vector predictions.
6. **Object Size Perception Data**: This task requires a set of images that jointly cover an entire target object. A BFS-based minimum-coverage-set search algorithm is used to find minimal sets of images that collectively capture the object's full 3D dimensions (height, width, length) from its 3D bounding box. For efficiency, this task is primarily limited to two-image sets.

#### 3.2.3. Data Generation for Dynamic Scenes (Object Movement)

Using TAPVid3D annotations, which provide temporally aligned 3D point tracking sequences:
1. **Point and Image Pair Sampling**: A point from a tracked sequence is randomly selected, and two frames (I_i, I_j) where it is visible are chosen.
2. **Rigid Body Segmentation**: To ensure diverse motion patterns, a clustering-based rigid body segmentation method is applied to group points that move together. This helps in sampling points from different moving parts (e.g., human torso vs. arm) rather than being skewed towards dominant parts.
3. **Image Pairs Bin Sampling**: Similar to static scenes, image pairs are binned based on the object's translation distance between frames. Balanced sampling ensures diversity across small and large displacements.
4. **QA Pair Construction**: The point's 3D displacement vector and translation distance between frames are computed using camera extrinsics and used to generate QA pairs.

#### 3.3. MultiSPA Benchmark

- **Scale**: Over 27 million QA samples are generated from 1.1 million unique images.
- **Evaluation Sets**: 300 samples are held out for each of the 26 subtasks, totaling 7,800 benchmark samples. These samples come from scenes or scenarios distinct from the training split to ensure generalization.
- **Evaluation Metric**: A unified metric is used.
 - **Qualitative/MCQ**: Exact matching accuracy.
 - **Scalar/Vector**: A prediction `v_pred` is correct if `||v_pred - v_gt||_2 <= 0.2 * ||v_gt||_2`.
 - **Pixel Coordinates**: A prediction is correct if it is within 5% of the image width pixels of the ground truth.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture and Training

- **Base MLLM**: InternVL2-8B is chosen due to its instruction-following capabilities.
- **Fine-tuning Strategy**: LoRA (rank R=16) is applied to the LLM backbone, while the image encoder and projection layer are initially frozen. (Later experiments explored tuning the vision encoder for further gains).
- **Training Data Mix**: A subset of 3 million MultiSPA QA samples is used, mixed with 60,000 general image-based instruction-following samples to prevent catastrophic forgetting and preserve the base model's general abilities.
- **Optimization**: AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate scheduler (lr = 4 × 10^-5). Training is performed on 24 nodes of 8x32G V100 GPUs.
- **Baselines**: InternVL2 (8B, 26B), SpatialRGPT, and proprietary models (Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini2.0-Flash, GPT-4o) are used for comparison. Proprietary models are prompted to adhere to specific answer formats, with GPT-4 used for post-processing their outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings regarding the Multi-SpatialMLLM's capabilities, scalability, generalization, and emergent behaviors.

#### 4.1. Multi-Frame Spatial Understanding Performance

- **Superior Performance on MultiSPA Benchmark**: Multi-SpatialMLLM significantly outperforms all baselines, including larger InternVL2 variants, SpatialRGPT, and state-of-the-art proprietary MLLMs (Claude-3.5, Gemini-2.0, GPT-4o). It achieves an average gain of 36% over the base InternVL2-8B model.
- **High Accuracy on Qualitative Tasks**: The model attains 80-90% accuracy on relatively easier qualitative tasks such as depth comparison and camera translation direction, surpassing all proprietary models.
- **Significant Improvement on Quantitative Tasks**: For challenging quantitative tasks like coordinate-based visual correspondence and predicting camera or object movement vectors, Multi-SpatialMLLM demonstrates much higher accuracy, while all baselines perform near zero. This validates the effectiveness of the MultiSPA dataset and the training approach.
- **SpatialRGPT's Limitations**: SpatialRGPT, trained on extensive single-image spatial data, performs worse than zero-shot InternVL on MultiSPA, confirming that single-image supervision does not effectively transfer to multi-image tasks.

#### 4.2. Scalability of Multi-SpatialMLLM

- **Consistent Gains with Data and Capacity**: The model's multi-frame spatial understanding ability scales with increasing training data volume and trainable parameters. For instance, in the camera movement vector prediction task, performance consistently improves with more data and when using the 26B variant, which achieves around 72% accuracy with 2.5M samples.
- **Impact of Vision Encoder Tuning**: Experiments with different fine-tuning strategies reveal that tuning the vision encoder significantly boosts performance, particularly on fine-grained spatial perception tasks like camera and object motion prediction.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Expert Models

- Multi-SpatialMLLM, despite being a general-purpose VLM, exhibits 3D perception capabilities comparable to specialized 3D vision models. The 26B model achieves 72% accuracy on camera vector prediction, which is on par with specialized models like VGGT. It also matches LoFTR for image matching and remains competitive with Depth-Anything for depth comparison.

#### 4.4. Generalization Abilities

- **Held-out Benchmarks (BLINK)**: The model demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to the BLINK benchmark, achieving nearly 90% accuracy on Visual Correspondence and Multi-View Reasoning tasks, and an average 26.4% improvement over the base model. It even outperforms proprietary models on these tasks, despite never seeing BLINK images during training.
- **Synthetic Data (3RScan)**: Multi-SpatialMLLM generalizes to multi-view images rendered from unseen 3RScan scene meshes, achieving 18.7% (8B) and 27.7% (26B) accuracy on camera vector prediction, while zero-shot baselines remain at 0%.
- **Standard VQA Benchmarks**: Evaluation on popular VQA benchmarks (POPE, VizWiz, OCRVQA, MathVista, MMStar, CCBench, MMMU, MME, MMVet) shows that the model largely retains its original standard VQA proficiency, indicating that fine-tuning for multi-frame spatial reasoning does not lead to significant catastrophic forgetting. Performance remains stable throughout training iterations.

#### 4.5. Multi-Task Generalization and Synergy

- **Benefits of Diverse Tasks**: Multi-task training on the MultiSPA dataset provides notable benefits. Training on a broader set of tasks (e.g., 3M samples across all tasks) improves performance on specific tasks like camera movement (from 9.3% to 18.0%) and object movement (from 17.5% to 22.04%) compared to training on single-task subsets.
- **Cross-Dataset Transfer**: This synergy is observed even when the additional data comes from different datasets (e.g., ScanNet for static tasks helping PStudio/ADT for dynamic tasks), suggesting that spatial understanding learned from various datasets and task types can transfer. Depth perception and visual correspondence tasks contribute most significantly to this synergy.

#### 4.6. Emergence of Spatial Understanding

- Preliminary signs of emergent behavior on challenging spatial tasks are observed. In a "Hard" version of the multiple-choice visual correspondence task (with closely placed distractors), only the larger 26B model demonstrates effective learning and improvement (82.33% accuracy), while smaller models (8B, 13B) fail to learn effectively or even show reduced performance. This suggests that certain complex spatial reasoning abilities may only manifest in sufficiently large models.

#### 4.7. Demonstrations and Applications

- **Zero-shot Robotics**: Multi-SpatialMLLM can accurately identify static objects and estimate movement distances in out-of-distribution robot scenes (e.g., a robot arm stacking cubes), where GPT-4o and base models fail.
- **Multi-Frame Reward Annotator**: The model demonstrates potential as a multi-frame reward annotator for robot learning, accurately estimating increasing movement distances of objects across consecutive frames, which could be used to guide tasks like "move the object by N meters."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a notable advancement in the capabilities of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by extending their spatial understanding from static, single-image contexts to dynamic, multi-frame scenarios. This capability is critical for enabling MLLMs to interact effectively with the physical world.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

1. **Enabling Embodied AI**: By endowing MLLMs with multi-frame spatial understanding, this work provides a foundational capability for integrating these models into real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and augmented reality. MLLMs can move beyond purely digital platforms to become more capable "brains" for physical systems, allowing for more nuanced perception and interaction with dynamic 3D/4D environments.

2. **Addressing a Critical Data Gap**: The introduction of MultiSPA, the first large-scale dataset specifically designed for multi-frame spatial understanding, is a significant contribution. It fills a crucial void in existing training data, which previously focused on single-image or limited multi-frame scenarios. The novel data generation pipeline, leveraging existing high-quality 3D/4D annotations, offers a scalable and cost-effective method to create diverse and detailed spatial QA pairs, including challenging quantitative outputs (vectors, coordinates, scalars) and flexible referencing.

3. **Comprehensive Benchmark for Evaluation**: Alongside the dataset, the MultiSPA benchmark provides a standardized and rigorous evaluation framework for multi-frame spatial reasoning. Its diverse tasks and output formats enable a more holistic assessment of MLLMs' spatial intelligence, driving future research and development in this domain.

4. **Demonstrated Scalability and Generalization**: The research provides evidence that multi-frame spatial understanding in MLLMs is scalable with both data volume and model capacity. Crucially, the Multi-SpatialMLLM demonstrates generalization to unseen real-world and synthetic datasets (e.g., BLINK, 3RScan) and retains its proficiency on standard VQA benchmarks, indicating that this specialized spatial training does not compromise general vision-language abilities. This suggests a robust pathway for developing more powerful and versatile MLLMs.

5. **Insights into Multi-Task Learning and Emergence**: The observation of synergistic benefits from multi-task training on diverse spatial tasks suggests that learning across different spatial reasoning types can be mutually reinforcing. Furthermore, the preliminary findings on emergent spatial capabilities in larger models, where complex spatial tasks are only effectively learned by sufficiently capacious models, open new avenues for fundamental research into how advanced spatial cognition arises in AI systems.

6. **Direct Practical Applications**: The successful demonstration of Multi-SpatialMLLM as a multi-frame reward annotator for robot learning highlights an immediate and tangible impact. This capability can streamline the development and training of robotic policies, enabling robots to understand and achieve goals related to precise object manipulation and movement over time (e.g., "move an object by N millimeters"), thus accelerating progress in robot learning and control.

In summary, this work pushes the boundaries of MLLMs by significantly enhancing their multi-frame spatial understanding, providing essential tools (dataset, benchmark), and offering valuable insights into the development of truly embodied and intelligent AI agents.
