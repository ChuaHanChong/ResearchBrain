# SafeAdapt: Provably Safe Policy Updates in Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SafeAdapt introduces a framework for deep reinforcement learning that enables policies to adapt to new tasks while provably preserving safety on previously learned tasks. The method consistently maintains a perfect critical-state safety rate of 1.00 +- 0.00 on source tasks, even as unconstrained or regularization-based baselines experience degradation as low as 0.88 +- 0.00, while achieving competitive performance on new tasks.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (RL) agents need to continually adapt in non-stationary environments, but policy updates can lead to catastrophic forgetting of safety constraints from prior tasks.
- Existing safe RL methods often provide only probabilistic safety guarantees or require expensive post-hoc verification, lacking formal a priori assurances in parameter space.
- Standard continual learning techniques, like regularization, mitigate catastrophic forgetting but do not offer formal guarantees specifically for safety preservation, allowing strong reward signals to override safety.

## Method

- Defines a "Rashomon set" in policy parameter space, representing a certified region where all policies are guaranteed to be safe on a source task based on a distribution-independent safety constraint.
- Computes this certified safe region by maximizing its volume using a sound, differentiable safety surrogate in conjunction with Interval Bound Propagation (IBP) to establish lower bounds on safety.
- Integrates policy adaptation with projected gradient descent, constraining parameter updates to remain within the certified safe region to ensure continuous, provable safety throughout the learning process.

## Results

- SafeAdapt consistently achieved a perfect critical-state safety rate of 1.00 +- 0.00 on source tasks across all tested environments, demonstrating complete safety retention.
- In contrast, baselines (UnsafeAdapt, EWC) exhibited significant safety degradation, with critical-state safety rates as low as 0.88 +- 0.00 and trajectory safety rates as low as 0.10 +- 0.30 on source tasks.
- The method maintained competitive or optimal performance on downstream tasks, matching or outperforming baselines in smaller environments and showing only a modest performance drop on the largest environments while ensuring safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formal safety guarantees for RL policy updates can be achieved a priori by certifying regions in parameter space, shifting from reactive runtime intervention to proactive policy assurance.
> - A differentiable safety surrogate combined with Interval Bound Propagation (IBP) provides a practical way to compute provably safe parameter bounds (Rashomon sets) for deep neural network policies.
> - Catastrophic forgetting of safety can be rigorously prevented by explicitly projecting policy parameters onto a pre-certified safe region during adaptation, rather than relying on soft regularization penalties.
