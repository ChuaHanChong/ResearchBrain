# Arcadia: Toward a Full-Lifecycle Framework for Embodied Lifelong Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Arcadia, a full-lifecycle framework for embodied lifelong learning, integrates real-world data acquisition, generative simulation updates, shared representation learning, and structured feedback to enable continuous self-improvement for robots. This approach enhances generalization, leading to a 46% success rate in real-world navigation and a 27% success rate in manipulation on a physical robot, significantly outperforming previous baselines.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI systems depend on exogenous data, often misaligned with specific robot control objectives and deployment contexts.
- Traditional simulators utilize static, pre-rendered environments, lacking the editability and generativity needed to incorporate real-world observations.
- Embodied systems frequently suffer from architectural fragmentation, treating navigation and manipulation as separate problems, hindering shared understanding and knowledge transfer.
- Real-world deployment strategies provide sparse feedback, insufficient for localizing errors, adapting to environmental shifts, or guiding continuous policy refinement.

## Method

- The framework employs self-evolving exploration for autonomous, task-aligned data acquisition from physical environments using frontier-based policies.
- It uses generative scene reconstruction to convert real-world multimodal sensory data into editable, realistic simulation assets by synthesizing 3D objects from multi-view observations.
- Arcadia utilizes a shared embodied representation architecture, a unified multimodal backbone (VLM) jointly trained across Visual-Language-Navigation and Visual-Language-Action tasks.
- A sim-from-real evaluation and evolution mechanism logs real-world execution traces to extract dense, structured feedback (task, scene, and robot levels) for continuous refinement of policies and simulation assets.

## Results

- Arcadia achieved superior performance in simulation, with a 50.1% success rate on VLN-CE-Isaac (vs. 45.1% for NaVILA) and significant gains over OpenVLA on VLA benchmarks like BridgeData V2 (52.4% SR vs. 39.6%).
- On a physical Unitree G1 robot, Arcadia demonstrated substantially better real-world generalization, completing 46% of navigation tasks (vs. 13% for NaVILA) and 27% of manipulation tasks (vs. 9% for OpenVLA).
- Ablation studies confirmed that each of Arcadia's four key modules (self-evolving exploration, generative scene reconstruction, shared representation, and structured feedback) contributes critically to overall performance.
- The shared multimodal VLM backbone effectively unified navigation and manipulation tasks, demonstrating minimal performance degradation when ablated, supporting its potential for general-purpose embodied agents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodied learning is optimally viewed as a continuous, closed-loop problem across a full lifecycle, rather than isolated stages of optimization.
> - Generating editable simulation assets directly from real-world observations and autonomously collecting task-aligned data are crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap and enabling robust generalization.
> - A unified multimodal backbone can effectively learn shared embodied representations for diverse tasks like navigation and manipulation, promoting knowledge transfer and coherent reasoning.
> - Dense and structured feedback from real-world deployments is vital for localizing errors, adapting to environmental shifts, and continuously improving both policies and simulation environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Arcadia addresses a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence, particularly in robotics: enabling agents to learn and adapt continually in complex, dynamic real-world environments. This work is positioned within the burgeoning field of **embodied lifelong learning**, moving beyond the traditional paradigm of training models on static datasets or optimizing for isolated, single-stage tasks.

The paper critically identifies four key limitations in contemporary embodied AI systems that Arcadia aims to overcome:
1. **Exogenous Data Dependence:** Many systems rely on pre-collected, often off-distribution, data (e.g., YouTube videos, datasets from different robot morphologies) that lack strong alignment with the specific control objectives and deployment contexts of a target robot. This mismatch hinders effective policy updates and lifelong improvement.
2. **Pre-Rendered Environments:** Traditional simulators often use static or pre-rendered environments (e.g., Matterport3D, Habitat v1). These environments lack the editability and generativity required to incorporate real-world observations and deployment-time variations, thereby slowing down or even blocking the crucial data-asset-policy feedback loop.
3. **Model Architectural Fragmentation:** Most embodied systems treat navigation and manipulation tasks as separate problems, employing distinct perception stacks and action spaces. This fragmentation prevents shared understanding across tasks, limits transfer learning, and makes it difficult to achieve coherent policy updates for long-horizon, multi-skill objectives.
4. **Real-world Feedback Sparsity:** Current deployment strategies often treat robots as one-shot evaluators, capturing only coarse success/failure labels. This sparse feedback is insufficient for localizing errors, adapting to environmental shifts, or guiding continuous refinement of both policies and simulation assets.

Arcadia builds upon recent advancements in embodied intelligence that have begun to integrate previously isolated stages. Works like GRUtopia expand simulation capabilities, while NaVILA connects language instructions to low-level control for physical robots. However, the authors argue that these efforts, while significant, "stop short of closing the lifecycle." They either primarily extend the simulation side or the execution span without establishing a persistent, editable path from deployment experience back into simulation assets or a persistent mechanism for routing in-deployment signals to supervise subsequent training.

In this context, Arcadia's contribution is to propose a **"full-lifecycle" framework** that explicitly closes the "real-to-sim-to-real" loop. This positions Arcadia as a holistic solution to achieve continuous self-improvement and robust generalization by tightly coupling all stages of the embodied learning pipeline – from autonomous data acquisition in the real world to generative simulation updates, shared representation learning, and adaptive feedback mechanisms. This comprehensive approach marks a significant step towards creating truly adaptive and intelligent general-purpose embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central thesis of Arcadia is that **embodied learning is fundamentally a life-cycle problem rather than a single-stage optimization**. This perspective forms the core motivation for the entire framework. The authors draw inspiration from human learning, which occurs across an entire lifespan, where experience is continuously accumulated, reorganized, and generalized beyond initial training contexts. They contend that for robots to achieve similar levels of competence, their learning systems must also operate as closed loops that continually integrate real-world experience with updates to their internal representations, policies, and the simulators used for training.

Based on this overarching philosophy, the key objectives and motivations driving the development of Arcadia are:

- **To address the limitations of existing embodied AI systems:** The four limitations previously outlined (exogenous data dependence, pre-rendered environments, architectural fragmentation, and sparse real-world feedback) serve as direct bottlenecks that Arcadia is designed to resolve. The framework is specifically engineered to counter these weaknesses by enabling:
 - **Task-aligned data acquisition:** Robots should autonomously collect data relevant to their deployment contexts, eliminating reliance on mismatched external sources.
 - **Editable, generative simulation environments:** Simulators must be dynamic and capable of ingesting real observations to create editable assets, allowing deployment experience to directly inform scene variations.
 - **Shared embodied representations:** A unified architectural backbone should facilitate knowledge transfer and coherent policy updates across diverse tasks like navigation and manipulation.
 - **Dense, structured real-world feedback:** Deployment should not be an endpoint but a continuous source of multi-level feedback to refine both simulator assets and learning policies.
- **To operationalize embodied lifelong learning:** The ultimate goal is to move beyond episodic learning to a system that exhibits sustained improvement and robustness over an agent's operational lifespan. Arcadia aims to provide the mechanisms for continuous self-adaptation and learning through constant interaction with its environment.
- **To achieve broad generalization and skill transfer:** By tightly coupling all stages of the learning lifecycle, Arcadia seeks to enable robots to acquire skills that generalize robustly to novel scenarios and transfer effectively across different tasks and embodiments.
- **To release a foundational framework:** The authors aim to provide open-source tools and standardized interfaces to enable reproducible evaluation and cross-model comparison, thereby serving as a scalable foundation for developing future general-purpose embodied agents and accelerating research in this domain.

In essence, Arcadia is motivated by the vision of intelligent robots that learn adaptively and autonomously, much like humans, continuously evolving their capabilities through a symbiotic relationship between real-world experience and simulated learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Arcadia proposes a **closed-loop, full-lifecycle framework** that tightly integrates four key stages, ensuring continuous feedback and self-improvement for embodied agents. The methodology can be broken down into these interconnected components:

1. **Self-Evolving Exploration and Grounding (Real-World Data Acquisition):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Exogenous data dependence and weak task alignment.
 - **Approach:** Instead of relying on external datasets, robots autonomously explore their physical environments to collect task-relevant data. This ensures high fidelity and alignment between training data and deployment conditions.
 - **Mechanism:** Built upon Isaac ROS and Nvblox for robust SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) and 3D reconstruction. It employs a **frontier-based exploration policy**, where the robot intelligently identifies and moves towards boundaries between explored and unexplored regions (frontier points), prioritizing areas that maximize information gain or semantic relevance for downstream tasks.
 - **Output:** Synchronized multimodal data (RGB-D, LiDAR, IMU, odometry, poses) that capture complete observation histories, providing dense, temporally grounded supervision.

2. **Generative Scene Reconstruction and Augmentation (Real-to-Sim Asset Creation):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Pre-rendered, static environments with limited editability.
 - **Approach:** Converts real-world sensory data into editable, realistic simulation assets, blending real-world realism with simulation scalability.
 - **Mechanism:** Multimodal inputs from exploration (videos, point clouds) are parsed into a structured 3D scene graph. Crucially, instead of retrieving existing meshes, Arcadia **synthesizes assets directly from multi-view observations** using a Gaussian-splat-based reconstructor (e.g., InstantMesh). These synthesized assets are then imported into Isaac Sim as USD objects, maintaining consistent geometry, texture, and semantics.
 - **Output:** High-fidelity, extensible, and task-aligned virtual environments that can be dynamically updated and augmented, reducing asset bias and preserving real-world task semantics.

3. **Shared Embodied Representation Architecture (Unified Learning):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Model architectural fragmentation between navigation and manipulation.
 - **Approach:** Employs a unified multimodal backbone jointly trained across both Visual-Language-Navigation (VLN) and Visual-Language-Action (VLA) tasks.
 - **Mechanism:** A high-level vision-language model (VLM, such as Qwen2.5-VL) acts as the shared backbone. This backbone processes language-conditioned inputs. Lightweight, task-specific decoders then generate actions.
 - For **navigation**, start-goal pairs are sampled in simulation, and A* produces collision-free paths in a 7-primitive discrete control space.
 - For **manipulation**, RRT (Rapidly-exploring Random Tree) generates physically feasible trajectories for robotic arms.
 - **Benefit:** Joint training forces the learning of a shared embodied state representation, enabling a common semantic and spatial understanding across tasks, which facilitates knowledge transfer and coherent reasoning for long-horizon instructions.

4. **Sim-from-Real Evaluation and Evolution (Feedback Loop Closure):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Sparse real-world feedback.
 - **Approach:** Treats real-world deployment as a continuous supervision stage, where execution traces are logged, decomposed into structured feedback signals, and reintegrated into simulation.
 - **Mechanism:** Feedback is extracted along three channels:
 - **Task Feedback:** Decomposes tasks into step-level actions and provides a composite signal (rewards, state transition magnitude, perceptual consistency, distance to goal) for global scoring and localized error attribution.
 - **Scene Feedback:** Utilizes multimodal sensory streams to characterize environmental dynamics and perception quality (e.g., degraded mapping in low light, unseen objects). This information is used to update the simulator by instantiating new assets or injecting perturbations, making future simulations more reflective of deployment conditions.
 - **Robot Feedback:** Monitors hardware telemetry (joint states, actuator load, communication stability). Constraint violations (e.g., exceeding step height) are recorded to adapt motion policies to platform limits and for safety gating.
 - **Impact:** This bidirectional "real-to-sim-to-real" loop transforms simulation from a static proxy into an active driver of embodied adaptation, continually refining policies and environment assets based on real-world experience.

These four stages are designed to function both independently and, critically, as a tightly coupled closed-loop system, where each component feeds into and refines the others, enabling continual self-improvement and robust adaptation throughout the robot's lifecycle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Arcadia demonstrates consistent and substantial performance gains across both simulated and real-world benchmarks for embodied tasks, validating its full-lifecycle approach.

#### 4.1. VLN Task Performance (Simulation)

- **Comparison with Baselines:** Arcadia consistently outperforms baseline methods on various Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) benchmarks, including VLN-CE-Isaac, R2R, RxR, and ScanQA.
- **Impact of Self-Collected Data:** "Arcadia w/o feedback" (using autonomously collected, task-aligned data but without real-world feedback) shows an average success rate (SR) improvement of 2.7% over NaVILA, which relies on mismatched external corpora. This highlights the direct benefit of grounding data acquisition in the robot's actual deployment environment.
- **Impact of Real-World Feedback:** The full "Arcadia w/ feedback" framework achieves the best performance across all VLN benchmarks. For instance, on VLN-CE-Isaac, it achieves an SR of 50.1% and SPL (Success weighted by Path Length) of 45.0%, significantly higher than NaVILA's SR of 45.1% and SPL of 40.1%. These gains underscore the effectiveness of continuously refining policies and data through a closed feedback loop.
- **Navigation-oriented Question Answering:** On ScanQA, Arcadia w/ feedback achieves a Meteor score of 19.1, outperforming NaVILA (16.3), indicating better language grounding and scene understanding.

#### 4.2. VLA Task Performance (Simulation)

- **Comparison with Baselines:** Arcadia significantly outperforms OpenVLA, a baseline for Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models, on manipulation benchmarks such as LIBERO and BridgeData V2.
- **Overall Improvement:** Across LIBERO (Spatial, Object, Goal, and 10-task) and BridgeData V2, Arcadia shows clear advantages.
- **Impact of Feedback in Manipulation:** On BridgeData V2, "Arcadia w/ feedback" achieves a success rate of 52.4%, which is a substantial improvement over "Arcadia w/o feedback" (47.3%) and OpenVLA (39.6%). This indicates that real-world feedback is particularly effective in enhancing object grounding and long-horizon stability for manipulation tasks.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

- **Physical Robot Deployment:** Experiments were conducted using a Unitree G1 robot equipped with a Dex-3 manipulator in a physical testing environment, comprising 100 navigation and 100 manipulation tasks. Navigation tasks were zero-shot.
- **Superior Real-World Generalization:** Arcadia demonstrated markedly stronger generalization and reliability compared to baselines.
 - **Navigation:** Arcadia successfully completed 46% of navigation trials, substantially outperforming NaVILA (13%).
 - **Manipulation:** Arcadia completed 27% of manipulation trials, significantly better than OpenVLA (9%).
- **Complex Scenarios:** In challenging multi-destination navigation and multi-object manipulation scenarios (where baselines typically failed), Arcadia maintained a 17% success rate, showcasing its ability to handle more complex, compositional instructions, even if still with room for improvement. These results highlight Arcadia's potential as a robust foundation for real-world embodied intelligence.

#### 4.4. Ablation and Analysis

- **Contribution of Components:** An ablation study systematically replaced each of Arcadia's four key modules with suboptimal alternatives. The results confirmed that removing any single component significantly degraded overall performance, highlighting the crucial role of each stage in the full lifecycle.
- **Static Training Set:** Replacing Arcadia's self-evolving data collection with a static training set (ScaleVLN and RLBench) led to a notable performance drop (e.g., VLN-CE-Isaac SR from 50.1% to 43.0%), emphasizing the value of task-aligned, dynamic data.
- **Retrieval Scene Construction:** Using retrieval-based scene construction instead of generative synthesis also reduced performance (e.g., VLN-CE-Isaac SR from 50.1% to 46.1%), showing the benefit of editable, generative simulation assets.
- **Sparse Feedback:** Limiting real-world feedback to a binary success/failure signal (sparse feedback) resulted in a performance decrease (e.g., VLN-CE-Isaac SR from 50.1% to 48.8%), confirming the importance of dense, structured feedback.
- **Joint Training:** The performance drop from removing joint training (i.e., using a non-unified VLM backbone) was minimal (VLN-CE-Isaac SR from 50.1% to 49.8%). This finding is significant as it suggests that VLN and VLA tasks can effectively share a unified VLM backbone, motivating further integration towards a truly general-purpose agent capable of both navigation and manipulation within a single framework.

In summary, the results collectively indicate that Arcadia's tightly coupled lifecycle—encompassing continuous real-world data acquisition, generative simulation updates, and shared-representation learning—is highly effective in supporting lifelong improvement, robust generalization, and successful transfer to physical robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Arcadia represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, proposing a holistic and impactful shift from traditional fragmented approaches to a truly **full-lifecycle framework for lifelong learning**. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering a Full-Lifecycle Paradigm:** The most profound contribution of Arcadia is its conceptualization and operationalization of embodied learning as a continuous, closed-loop problem. By tightly integrating data collection, generative simulation, shared representation learning, and real-world feedback, Arcadia establishes a new paradigm that promises sustained self-improvement, robust generalization, and adaptability for embodied agents. This moves beyond merely "bridging the sim-to-real gap" to actively using real-world experience to *evolve* both the simulation and the agent's capabilities.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains in both simulation and, critically, on physical robots underscore Arcadia's ability to achieve robust generalization. This is particularly evident in its superior performance in multi-destination navigation and multi-object manipulation scenarios, where baselines struggled. This increased reliability in real-world deployment is a crucial step towards practical, deployable robotic systems.
- **Reducing the Sim-to-Real Gap at its Source:** By making simulation assets generative and editable based on real-world observations, and by using real-world feedback to update both policies and the simulation environment, Arcadia directly addresses the reality gap. This "real-to-sim-to-real" cycle transforms simulation from a static training ground into an active, adaptive component of the learning process, which can continuously evolve to match the complexities of the physical world.
- **Promoting Unified Architectures:** The success of the shared embodied representation architecture, particularly its minimal performance drop in ablation, highlights the feasibility and benefits of unifying navigation and manipulation tasks within a single multimodal backbone. This can lead to more efficient learning, better transfer of knowledge between tasks, and a more coherent understanding of the environment and task objectives for general-purpose robots.
- **Accelerating Research through Open-Source Contributions:** The commitment to open-sourcing standardized interfaces and reusable environments (via GitHub) is highly impactful. By reducing the engineering complexity often associated with setting up embodied AI experiments, Arcadia empowers researchers to focus on core model innovation rather than implementation overhead. This fosters reproducibility, facilitates cross-model comparison, and can significantly accelerate the pace of progress in the embodied AI community.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Agents:** Arcadia lays a scalable foundation for the development of general-purpose embodied agents. By enabling continuous adaptation and learning across diverse tasks and environments, it moves towards a future where robots can acquire and refine skills throughout their operational lifespan, much like humans do.
- **Future Directions and Broader Applicability:** While currently confined to the Unitree G1 platform and Isaac Sim, the framework's principles are generalizable. Future work aims to extend Arcadia to a broader range of embodiments and simulation environments, potentially integrating with emerging open frameworks. This indicates a long-term vision for broad applicability and impact across the entire robotics domain.

In conclusion, Arcadia is more than just an incremental improvement; it proposes a fundamental shift in how we approach embodied intelligence. By advocating for and demonstrating the efficacy of a full-lifecycle, closed-loop learning system, it brings us closer to realizing truly autonomous, adaptive, and intelligent robots capable of lifelong learning in complex real-world settings.
