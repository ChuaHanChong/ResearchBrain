# ECo-MoE: Embodiment-Conditioned Mixture of Experts Increases the Evolvability of Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Northwestern University researchers introduced ECo-MoE, a control framework that co-optimizes robot morphology and control by using a mixture of experts whose activation is conditioned on the robot's latent design. This approach led to increased robot evolvability in challenging simulated environments like upright locomotion and potholes, and facilitated directing evolution towards specific morphological characteristics.

## Problem

- Co-optimization of high-resolution robot morphology and adaptable controllers presents significant computational challenges.
- Existing methods either incur high computational costs by training individual policies for each robot or lead to conservative, generalized designs when using monolithic universal controllers.
- Simpler morphological representations used in some evolutionary robotics studies limit their relevance to complex physical robots.

## Method

- An Embodiment-Conditioned Mixture of Experts (**ECo-MoE**) framework is introduced, where multiple control experts are dynamically weighted by a gating network conditioned on the robot's latent genotype.
- The system co-optimizes the latent design genome (which decodes to high-definition morphology) and the ECo-MoE controller using PPO for behavior and CMA-ES for design fitness.
- "Evo by Demo" mechanisms, including predesigned latent priors and the injection of pretrained experts, are used to guide evolution towards specific morphological traits.

## Results

- ECo-MoE demonstrated increased robot evolvability in challenging environments like upright locomotion and potholes, achieving higher peak fitness compared to a monolithic universal controller baseline.
- The framework exhibited parameter efficiency, achieving performance gains with a comparable or slightly smaller total policy size and fewer parameters per individual expert.
- "Evo by Demo" settings, particularly combining predesigned latent priors and frozen pretrained experts (CoSteering), effectively guided evolution towards specific predesigned morphological traits and achieved significantly higher fitness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modularity in the control architecture, conditioned on robot embodiment, can increase robot evolvability without necessarily broadening the morphological search space, primarily through more effective control of encountered designs.
> - A routing diversity regularization term is essential to prevent expert collapse and ensure diverse utilization of the specialized control modules within the Mixture of Experts.
> - Injecting prior knowledge through pretrained experts or predesigned latent space initializations (Evo by Demo) provides an effective method to steer freeform morphological evolution toward desired structures.
