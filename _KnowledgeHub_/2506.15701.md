# Compiler-R1: Towards Agentic Compiler Auto-tuning with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Compiler-R1 introduces a framework that integrates Large Language Models with reinforcement learning for automated compiler tuning, optimizing compilation pass sequences. This approach achieved an average 8.46% reduction in Intermediate Representation instruction count compared to `opt -Oz` baselines, significantly outperforming traditional methods in efficiency with markedly lower runtime.

## Problem

- The absence of high-quality, reasoning datasets specifically designed for training LLM agents in compiler auto-tuning, which limits interactive learning.
- Current LLM-based methods, often relying on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), exhibit limited effective interaction with the compilation environment, hindering autonomous exploration and adaptation.
- Traditional compiler auto-tuning methods often suffer from inherent inefficiencies, substantial overheads, and limited generalization capabilities across diverse programs.

## Method

- A two-stage LLM training framework, **Compiler-R1**, combining ==Supervised Fine-Tuning== (SFT) for initial protocol learning and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for outcome-driven policy optimization.
- Construction of a curated, high-quality auto-tuning reasoning dataset that includes Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories, external tool integration demonstrations, and simulated environment feedback.
- Integration of core external tools (`instrcount` and `find_best_pass_sequence`) and a composite reward function to guide the RL agent's learning through autonomous environment exploration.

## Results

- Compiler-R1's GRPO-**7B** variant achieved an average **8.46%** reduction in IR instruction count compared to the `opt -Oz` baseline, outperforming SFT-only models and traditional autotuners.
- The framework demonstrated significantly higher efficiency, completing each program in approximately 26 seconds (GRPO-**7B**) compared to hundreds or thousands of seconds required by traditional autotuners.
- Compiler-R1 models exhibited high task success rates (e.g., **96.71%** for GRPO-**7B**), indicating reliable adherence to the predefined interaction protocol and effective tool usage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-stage SFT+RL pipeline is crucial for equipping LLM agents with both high interaction reliability and strong optimization performance in complex engineering tasks like compiler auto-tuning.
> - Compact statistical features, such as those from AutoPhase, can effectively represent complex program characteristics for LLMs, performing comparably to raw LLVM IR while reducing context window limitations.
> - LLM agents, when augmented with external tools and trained via reinforcement learning, can learn to navigate large action spaces and make sequential, adaptive decisions based on dynamic feedback from an environment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Compiler auto-tuning is a field focused on automatically selecting and ordering compilation passes—modular optimization or analysis steps—within modern compilers like LLVM, with the objective of improving program performance metrics such as Intermediate Representation (IR) instruction count. This task is inherently complex due to the combinatorial explosion of possible pass sequences and the intricate, often non-linear, interactions between individual passes.

Historically, compiler auto-tuning methods have evolved through several generations. Early approaches, termed Heuristic-based Methods, employed iterative compilation techniques such as genetic algorithms and simulated annealing to search for optimal sequences. Examples include iterative compilation efforts and algorithms like genetic algorithms (GA) and Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE). Subsequently, Machine Learning (ML)-enhanced Methods emerged, leveraging ML models to improve tuning efficiency and generalization to unseen benchmarks. These included supervised learning approaches, often relying on labeled optimal sequences, and reinforcement learning (RL)-based methods like AutoPhase, which dynamically adjust optimization strategies. While these traditional methods offered improvements, they often suffered from inherent inefficiencies, substantial overheads, and limited generalization capabilities.

More recently, the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has introduced a new paradigm to compiler auto-tuning. Researchers have begun exploring LLMs' reasoning and problem-solving capabilities to generate or suggest optimization pass sequences. However, directly applying LLMs to this domain faces specific challenges. Existing LLM-based approaches, typically relying on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), often lack high-quality training datasets tailored for auto-tuning that incorporate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, external tool integration, and environment feedback. Furthermore, these SFT-based models exhibit limited interaction with the actual compilation environment, restricting their ability to autonomously explore, adapt, and learn from feedback, which in turn limits their generalization to novel programs.

Compiler-R1 positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing these limitations of current LLM-based approaches. It introduces an RL-driven framework designed to augment LLM capabilities for compiler auto-tuning, aiming to move beyond simple imitation learning to enable autonomous, interactive optimization. By developing a high-quality reasoning dataset and a novel two-stage RL training pipeline, Compiler-R1 seeks to enhance LLMs' ability to interact with the compilation environment, learn from outcomes, and generalize more effectively, thereby advancing the state of the art in agentic compiler optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an advanced framework, Compiler-R1, that enhances the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for compiler auto-tuning by integrating reinforcement learning (RL) with tool-augmented interactive learning. This framework aims to automatically optimize compiler pass sequences to improve program performance, specifically by reducing Intermediate Representation (IR) instruction count.

The motivation for this work stems from two significant challenges identified in the application of current LLM technologies to compiler auto-tuning:

1. **Absence of High-Quality Reasoning Datasets for Agent Training:** Existing LLM applications often lack comprehensive training datasets that are specifically designed for agentic learning in the compiler domain. Effective interactive learning for LLM agents requires datasets that go beyond simple input-output pairs. These datasets need to encompass Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, demonstrate the integration of external tools for environmental interaction, and incorporate feedback from the environment. Such datasets are crucial for teaching LLMs not just what to do, but *how* to reason through a problem and interact with a dynamic system. The lack of such resources limits the LLM's ability to develop robust problem-solving strategies in the auto-tuning context.

2. **Limited Effective Interactions with the Compilation Environment:** Current LLM-based methods for compiler auto-tuning primarily rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). While SFT can teach models to generate plausible optimization sequences, it inherently restricts the model's capacity for autonomous exploration and adaptive learning. SFT models typically operate in a non-interactive, feed-forward manner, generating a sequence without receiving real-time feedback from the compilation environment about the efficacy of their choices. This limitation constrains the model's ability to adjust its strategy based on the actual impact of an optimization, leading to poor generalization when faced with unseen programs or nuanced optimization scenarios. The goal is to move beyond mere imitation towards an agent that can actively experiment, observe outcomes, and refine its policy.

To address these challenges, Compiler-R1 introduces two main innovations:
- **Curated, High-Quality Auto-tuning Reasoning Dataset:** The researchers aim to construct a comprehensive dataset that includes diverse and representative tuning scenarios, incorporating CoT reasoning and tool-use demonstrations, specifically designed to facilitate interactive learning for compiler optimization tasks.
- **Novel Two-Stage End-to-End RL Training Pipeline:** The objective is to develop an RL framework that equips LLM agents with the ability to invoke external tools for autonomous environment exploration. This pipeline leverages an outcome-based reward function to guide the learning process, enabling the agent to understand both individual pass characteristics and their complex compositional interactions. This approach is intended to significantly enhance generalization capability and tuning efficiency.

By achieving these objectives, Compiler-R1 seeks to demonstrate the potential of RL-trained LLMs to advance compiler auto-tuning, enabling more efficient, adaptive, and effective program optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Compiler-R1 is presented as a two-stage LLM training framework designed for compiler auto-tuning, integrating supervised learning and reinforcement learning (RL) to enable models to autonomously learn optimization strategies through structured interaction with a simulated compilation environment.

#### 3.1. Foundation Dataset Construction

The process of constructing a high-quality dataset for LLM training involves several pipelines:

- **Raw LLVM IR Dataset Preparation:** Public datasets from CompilerGym are aggregated. Coreset-inspired sampling is applied to ensure diversity, and programs exceeding 10,000 IR instructions are excluded for computational feasibility. The training set consists of six uncurated datasets, while evaluation uses four curated benchmarks (cbench-v1, chstone-v0, mibench-v1, npb-v0) for out-of-distribution assessment.
- **Synergy Pass Pair Identification:** For each training program, synergistic optimization pass pairs (A, B) are identified. A pair is considered synergistic if applying pass B alone reduces the IR instruction count, and applying pass A followed by pass B results in a further reduction compared to applying B alone. This process captures beneficial interactions between passes on a per-program basis.
- **Global Synergy Graph Construction:** The per-program synergistic pass pairs are aggregated to form a Global Synergy Graph. In this graph, nodes represent individual passes, and edges denote pairs that were found to be synergistic for at least one program. This graph provides a global perspective on beneficial pass interactions.
- **Graph-Guided Optimal Sequence Selection:** From the Global Synergy Graph, 100 candidate pass sequences are generated per program using random walks that prioritize synergistic combinations. Each candidate sequence is evaluated based on its relative improvement over the `-Oz` baseline (a standard LLVM optimization preset), quantified by `OverOz`. The sequence that maximizes `OverOz` is selected as the `PassSeq_opt(P)`, serving as the target solution for the subsequent Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage.
- **Feature Extraction and Representation:** To manage LLM context limits, programs are compressed into 56 statistical features derived from AutoPhase. These features capture structural properties (e.g., type distributions, control-flow properties). Along with the program's initial instruction count, these features constitute the "question" component of the SFT samples, representing the program to the LLM.

#### 3.2. Training Pipeline

The training pipeline consists of two stages:

#### 3.2.1. Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Cold Start

This stage initializes the LLM's understanding of reasoning over program features and interacting with external tools.

- **Core External Tools:**
 - `instrcount`: This tool evaluates a candidate pass sequence by computing its improvement (based on IR instruction count reduction) relative to the standard `-Oz` optimization.
 - `find_best_pass_sequence` (FBPS): This tool is invoked if an initial candidate sequence proves ineffective (i.e., `OverOz ≤ 0`). It performs a guided search to identify a potentially better optimization sequence.
- **SFT Sample Construction:** Training samples are structured as interactive problem-solving sessions, following a "thought–action–feedback" loop. Each sample includes:
 - **Input:** Program's AutoPhase features, task instruction (e.g., "find an IR-reducing pass sequence"), and initial IR instruction count.
 - **Output (Simulated LLM Thought and Action Trajectory):** A Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectory demonstrating step-by-step reasoning. This involves:
 - `<think>` blocks for internal analysis and planning (e.g., planning to verify a sequence with `instrcount`).
 - `<tool_call>` for invoking external tools (e.g., `instrcount` or `find_best_pass_sequence`).
 - `<tool_response>` for simulating tool feedback (e.g., execution status, `OverOz` metric, or a new `best_pass_sequence`).
 - Further `<think>` blocks to process tool feedback and decide on the next action (e.g., accepting a sequence or calling FBPS).
 - An `<answer>` block concluding with the final recommended sequence.
 - This structured output trains the LLM to emulate interactive problem-solving and tool usage.

#### 3.2.2. Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Policy Optimization

Following SFT, this stage refines the LLM's policy, enabling it to move beyond imitation and autonomously discover optimization strategies through trial-and-error interactions.

- **Agent-Environment Interaction:** The LLM acts as an agent, emitting token sequences for high-level actions (e.g., `<think>` statements, `<tool_call>` invocations, `<answer>` tags). The environment simulates compilation tasks or tool executions and provides updated state information along with scalar rewards.
- **Reward Design:** A composite reward function, `R_final = w_f * R_format + w_a * R_answer`, guides learning:
 - `R_format`: A binary reward (1.5 for correct, 0 for incorrect) encouraging adherence to the predefined interaction protocol and structured trajectory (e.g., valid thought-tool-answer path).
 - `R_answer`: Quantifies the effectiveness of the final generated pass sequence (`PassSeq_answer`) in reducing the IR instruction count compared to the *original unoptimized program* (`P_original`). This `OverOrig` metric provides a denser and more consistent reward signal than `OverOz` (which compares against `-Oz`), facilitating stable RL training. It is scaled by `α`.
- **Learning Algorithm:** On-policy RL algorithms, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Generalized Policy Optimization (GRPO), and REINFORCE++ (RPP), are applied. These algorithms are suitable for LLMs due to their ability to handle large action spaces and long-horizon credit assignment, iteratively refining the policy to generate higher-quality optimization sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Compiler-R1 involved comparisons against various baselines across seven datasets, focusing on optimization performance, task success rate, and the impact of input feature representations.

#### 4.1. Experiment 1: Optimization Performance Comparison

- **Overall Performance:** Compiler-R1 models consistently achieved significant IR instruction count reductions. The GRPO-7B variant demonstrated the highest average improvement of 8.46% OverOz (reduction relative to `opt -Oz`). Lower-scale variants like GRPO-1.5B (3.87%) and GRPO-3B (5.12%) also showed competitive performance.
- **Comparison with SFT-only Models:** Compiler-R1 variants, especially GRPO-7B, substantially outperformed SFT-only baselines. SFT-Qwen-7B achieved an average 4.66% OverOz, but required N=40 inference attempts per program, indicating its reliance on stochastic sampling to find good sequences. Compiler-R1 achieved its results with fewer inference attempts and significantly lower runtime.
- **Comparison with Traditional Autotuners:** Compiler-R1 demonstrated comparable or superior optimization quality compared to traditional methods (e.g., OpenTuner, GA, TPE, RIO, CompTuner, BOCA) while exhibiting markedly lower runtime. For instance, GRPO-7B completed each program in 26 seconds, whereas OpenTuner took 200s, GA 561s, and CompTuner over 9800s. This efficiency highlights Compiler-R1's practical viability.
- **AutoPhase Baselines:** While AutoPhase variants (PPO-LSTM and PPO-noLSTM) offered very fast inference times (around 2 seconds), their optimization quality was substantially lower, with PPO-noLSTM even showing a detrimental average OverOz of -50.35%.

#### 4.2. Experiment 2: Task Success Rate and Interaction Efficiency

- **Task Success Rate:** Compiler-R1 models exhibited high task success rates, indicating their ability to reliably follow the predefined interaction protocol (thought-tool-answer trajectory). GRPO-1.5B achieved 83.36% success, while GRPO-7B reached 96.71%. This suggests the effectiveness of RL in training agents for protocol compliance and effective environment interaction.
- **GRPO-3B Anomaly:** Interestingly, GRPO-3B achieved a lower success rate (45.56%) but still a higher OverOz (5.12%) than GRPO-1.5B. This was attributed to GRPO-3B occasionally circumventing strict protocol checks (e.g., directly calling `find_best_pass_sequence` without `instrcount` validation), which, while lowering protocol compliance, could still yield effective optimization sequences. GRPO-7B, benefiting from larger capacity and hyperparameter tuning, balanced both protocol compliance and optimization quality effectively.
- **SFT-only Model Interaction Efficiency:** SFT-only models, lacking environmental feedback, required extensive sampling (N attempts) to achieve improved performance. SFT-Qwen-7B, for example, improved from -14.78% OverOz at N=1 to 4.66% at N=40, demonstrating a dependence on stochastic inference rather than adaptive refinement.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation results confirmed that neither SFT-only (cold-start) nor RL-only (without SFT initialization) training strategies were sufficient on their own. The two-stage SFT+RL pipeline was necessary for achieving high interaction reliability and optimization performance. The repetition penalty hyperparameter was also found to be crucial for success rate stability.

#### 4.3. Experiment 3: Input Representation - AutoPhase vs. Raw LLVM IR

- **Performance Equivalence:** This experiment evaluated the impact of using 56-dimensional AutoPhase features versus raw LLVM IR as input representations for SFT models (Qwen-1.5B). The results showed that AutoPhase features yielded performance closely comparable to raw LLVM IR inputs. With N=40 sampled decoding, the AutoPhase-based model achieved an average `OverOrig` score of 0.4837, slightly surpassing the 0.4795 obtained using raw IR.
- **Advantages of AutoPhase:** AutoPhase features provide a compact and semantically rich representation, offering advantages in terms of reduced input length and better interpretability, which can be critical for LLMs constrained by context window limits. They also abstract away non-essential syntactic variations present in raw IR, potentially leading to more stable model behavior.
- **Decoding Strategy:** Performance generally improved with increased sampling (N), saturating around N=30-50. Greedy decoding (N=1) consistently outperformed single-shot sampled decoding, indicating the stability of deterministic inference for this specific task.

#### 4.4. Summary of Experimental Findings

The experiments collectively demonstrate that Compiler-R1's two-stage interactive RL framework is effective for compiler auto-tuning. It achieves strong optimization results with notable efficiency, largely due to the combination of SFT for initial protocol learning and RL for outcome-driven policy optimization. The utility of compact AutoPhase features as input representation was also confirmed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Compiler-R1 presents a significant contribution to the field of compiler auto-tuning and the broader application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in complex, interactive engineering tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be delineated as follows:

- **Addressing Key LLM Limitations in Compiler Optimization:** The work directly tackles two critical challenges faced by LLM-based compiler auto-tuning: the scarcity of high-quality, domain-specific reasoning datasets and the limited interactive capabilities of LLMs when dealing with dynamic environments. By providing a curated dataset and a novel RL-driven framework, Compiler-R1 offers a robust solution that moves beyond static, imitation-based learning.

- **Advancing Agentic LLMs for Engineering Tasks:** Compiler-R1 validates the "agentic LLM+RL paradigm" for a complex, interactive problem like compiler optimization. It demonstrates that LLMs, when augmented with tools and trained through reinforcement learning, can effectively navigate large action spaces, reason over dynamic feedback, and make sequential decisions to achieve specific objectives. This finding has implications for other engineering domains requiring intelligent, adaptive automation.

- **Improved Optimization Performance and Efficiency:** The framework achieves notable performance gains, with the GRPO-7B variant demonstrating an 8.46% average IR instruction count reduction compared to `opt -Oz`. Crucially, these performance improvements are achieved with significantly greater efficiency than many traditional autotuners, completing optimization tasks in seconds rather than minutes or hours. This balance of performance and speed makes Compiler-R1 a practical and scalable solution for real-world compiler optimization pipelines.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The RL stage of Compiler-R1, by enabling autonomous environment exploration and learning from outcome-based rewards, is expected to foster better generalization to unseen programs and optimization scenarios. This adaptability is a key advantage over static SFT-only models that rely heavily on the diversity of their training data and stochastic sampling at inference.

- **Structured Interaction and Reliability:** The high task success rates achieved by Compiler-R1 models indicate their ability to reliably execute complex interaction protocols involving tool calls and reasoned decision-making. This reliability in following a predefined "thought-tool-answer" trajectory is essential for deploying autonomous agents in sensitive engineering contexts where incorrect actions can lead to undesirable outcomes.

- **Validation of Compact Program Representations:** The finding that AutoPhase features perform comparably to raw LLVM IR for optimization prediction is significant. It suggests that compact, semantically rich feature sets can effectively represent complex program characteristics to LLMs, circumventing context window limitations and potentially improving model robustness by abstracting away irrelevant syntactic variations.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines future research avenues, including leveraging LLMs for improving the interpretability of auto-tuning processes, further developing LLM-RL agents to explicitly understand and exploit synergies between compiler passes, and exploring interactive, turn-by-turn pass application dialogues. These directions underscore the foundational nature of Compiler-R1 as a stepping stone towards more sophisticated intelligent compiler agents.

In summary, Compiler-R1 represents a substantive step towards intelligent compiler auto-tuning by integrating LLMs with reinforcement learning. Its contributions in dataset construction, training methodology, and demonstrated performance highlight the potential for agentic AI to revolutionize complex software optimization tasks.
