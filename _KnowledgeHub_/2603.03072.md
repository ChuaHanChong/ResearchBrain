# TikZilla: Scaling Text-to-TikZ with High-Quality Data and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TIKZILLA, a family of small, open-source LLMs, generates high-quality TikZ code from textual descriptions, demonstrating performance superior to larger proprietary models. This was achieved by developing DaTikZ-V4, a large-scale, high-quality dataset, and a two-stage training pipeline combining supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning, guided by a novel domain-specific visual reward model.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-TikZ datasets were small and noisy, leading to low compilation rates and inaccuracies in generated figures from LLMs.
- Raw figure captions lacked the detail needed for precise figure reconstruction, hindering model training for accurate visual output.
- Previous LLM training relied solely on supervised fine-tuning, which failed to provide direct feedback on the visual semantics of rendered figures, resulting in visually suboptimal or incorrect outputs.

## Method

- Constructed ==DaTikZ-V4==, a dataset of over 2 million high-quality TikZ graphics, by sourcing from GitHub and arXiv, implementing LLM-based debugging to repair uncompilable code, and using VLMs to generate semantically rich figure descriptions.
- Developed a two-stage training pipeline for **TikZilla** models, starting with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for syntactic grounding, followed by reinforcement learning (RL) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- Introduced a domain-specific visual reward model, based on a retrained DeTikZify-V2 image encoder and Earth Mover’s Distance, to provide semantic feedback during RL by measuring similarity between ground-truth and generated image embeddings.

## Results

- TikZilla-**3B**-RL and TikZilla-**8B**-RL models achieved aggregate scores of **0.385** and **0.384** on automatic metrics, outperforming GPT-5 (**0.365**) and GPT-4o (**0.320**).
- Reinforcement learning boosted compilation rates for TikZilla models to 95–**98%** and reduced token length, indicating more efficient and reliable code generation.
- Human evaluation showed TikZilla-**8B**-RL matching GPT-5 on image-based assessment, and the domain-specific reward model R_Sim demonstrated a strong correlation (ρ=**0.714**) with human judgments, validating its effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM-generated descriptions significantly enhance the quality and usefulness of training data, outperforming raw human captions and enabling more accurate figure generation.
> - An LLM-based debugging pipeline effectively repairs uncompilable TikZ code, substantially increasing the volume of usable training data and improving overall dataset quality.
> - The integration of a reinforcement learning stage with a domain-specific visual reward model (R_Sim) is crucial for aligning LLM outputs with visual semantics, leading to higher compilation rates and visually faithful figures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of high-quality scientific figures from textual descriptions is a recognized challenge in scientific workflows. Graphics programming languages such as TikZ are widely used in academia due to their precision, interpretability, and seamless integration within the LaTeX ecosystem. However, their steep learning curve and varied syntax present difficulties for both human users and automated systems like Large Language Models (LLMs).

Prior research has explored LLM-based methods to generate vector graphics, including TikZ and SVG. Projects such as AutomaTikZ (Belouadi et al., 2024a) and TikZero (Belouadi et al., 2025) have finetuned LLMs on caption-TikZ pairs. However, these efforts have faced limitations primarily stemming from the scarcity and noise of available datasets. Existing datasets for Text-to-TikZ are often small and contain mismatches between textual descriptions and rendered figures, leading to issues such as low compilation rates, hallucinations, irrelevant content, and incorrect spatial relations in generated outputs. Additionally, many prior approaches rely solely on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which grounds models in syntax but does not provide direct feedback on the visual semantics of the rendered figure. This absence of visual feedback contributes to the generation of visually inaccurate or suboptimal figures.

This research addresses these limitations by developing a significantly larger and higher-quality dataset and introducing a two-stage training pipeline that incorporates reinforcement learning (RL) with a domain-specific visual reward signal. The work aims to advance the capabilities of LLMs in generating precise scientific figures, thereby enhancing scientific communication and reducing the manual effort involved in figure creation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to overcome the limitations of existing Text-to-TikZ generation systems through improvements in data quality and model training methodology. The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality:** Prior Text-to-TikZ datasets were identified as being too small and noisy, failing to capture the complexity of TikZ code and leading to inaccuracies in generated figures. The researchers aimed to construct a substantially larger and higher-quality dataset to provide richer supervision for model training. This involved sourcing data from diverse repositories and implementing rigorous data filtering and enhancement techniques.
- **Improving Figure Description Accuracy:** Existing figure captions were found to be insufficient for precise figure reconstruction, often lacking crucial details about structural elements, visual attributes, and spatial relations. A key objective was to leverage Vision Language Models (VLMs) to generate more accurate, detailed, and semantically rich descriptions of TikZ figures, thereby mitigating the reliance on noisy human-written captions.
- **Enhancing TikZ Code Compilability:** A significant challenge in dataset creation was the low compilation success rate of collected TikZ code snippets, particularly from sources like arXiv. The research aimed to develop an automated LLM-based debugging pipeline to repair uncompilable TikZ code, thereby increasing the proportion of usable instances in the dataset.
- **Incorporating Visual Semantic Feedback into Model Training:** Relying solely on SFT was observed to lead to errors in rendered figures that relate to visual semantics (e.g., looping, irrelevant content, incorrect spatial relations). A central objective was to introduce a reinforcement learning stage into the training pipeline to expose the model to the rendered semantics of figures. This required the development of a reward mechanism capable of providing semantically faithful feedback based on visual characteristics.
- **Developing Efficient and High-Performing Models:** The goal was to train a family of small, open-source LLMs that could generate high-quality TikZ code, ideally outperforming larger proprietary models, and demonstrating the feasibility of reproducible and efficient text-to-image generation systems without extensive computational resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a multi-faceted approach involving the construction of a large-scale, high-quality dataset, followed by a two-stage training pipeline for Text-to-TikZ models.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction: DaTikZ-V4

The core of the data strategy was the creation of **DaTikZ-V4**, a dataset designed to be significantly larger and of higher quality than its predecessors.

- **Data Sourcing:** The dataset was expanded by integrating new sources. GitHub was identified as a large-scale repository, yielding over 400,000 unique TikZ samples from approximately 5,500 repositories. Data sourcing from arXiv was extended to include submissions post-2021 to mid-2025, contributing an additional 1 million samples. Combined with existing data from TeX StackExchange (TeX SE) and synthetic data, DaTikZ-V4 totaled over 2 million unique TikZ graphics.
- **Filtering and Standardization:** Beyond traditional `tikzpicture` environments, the extraction process included `tikz-cd` (for mathematical diagrams) and `circuitikz` (for electronics). Subfigures were recursively split and extracted. TikZ code was standardized by wrapping it in `\documentclass[tikz]{standalone}` environments. A dynamic package detection approach using regular expressions ensured the inclusion of necessary LaTeX packages. Code depending on external files (e.g., `\input`, `\includegraphics`) and inline comments were removed to improve compilation rates and reduce noise. Exact deduplication was performed, and samples with character counts outside the 100-4000 range were dismissed.
- **LLM Debugging:** Recognizing the low compilation success rate of raw TikZ code (e.g., 31.3% from arXiv), an LLM-based debugging pipeline was introduced. Given a code snippet and its compiler error log, an LLM (Qwen-32B) was instructed to fix the TikZ code. This process successfully repaired approximately 600,000 out of 1.3 million uncompilable instances in the first pass, substantially boosting the proportion of usable TikZ programs.
- **VLM-based Image Description:** To address the inadequacy of raw captions for figure reproduction, VLMs were employed to generate precise descriptions. Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct was used to annotate around 1.3 million compilable samples, creating a large-scale dataset of TikZ paired with semantically rich textual descriptions. Few-shot in-context learning with high-Semantic Textual Similarity (STS) human descriptions was used for VLM prompting.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture and Training Pipeline

The research developed **TikZilla**, a family of small Qwen-based models (3B and 8B parameters), trained using a two-stage pipeline.

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The models were initially finetuned on the DaTikZ-V4 dataset by minimizing the standard autoregressive negative log-likelihood. This stage aimed to ground the models in TikZ syntax and task-specific token distributions, enabling them to generate syntactically valid code aligned with textual prompts.
- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL):** To overcome the SFT limitation of lacking rendered semantics feedback, reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) was applied. For each figure description, multiple TikZ sequences were sampled from the SFT model (reinterpreted as a stochastic policy). Each generated sequence was assigned a scalar reward, and the model was updated using group-centered advantages. The "Dr.GRPO" variant (Liu et al., 2025) with token-level normalization and the "Clip-Higher" strategy from DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) were implemented to enhance training stability and exploration.
- **Domain-Specific Reward Model:** A key innovation was the development of a domain-specific reward model for Text-to-TikZ. This model leveraged the image encoder of DeTikZify-V2 (Belouadi et al., 2024b), which was originally trained for inverse graphics (image → TikZ) on DaTikZ-V3. The DeTikZify-V2 model was retrained end-to-end on the larger DaTikZ-V4 dataset, allowing its image encoder to learn richer, more generalizable low-dimensional representations of scientific figures. This retrained image encoder was then used as the reward model in the online RL environment.
 - **Reward Computation:** Instead of pooled cosine similarity, an Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) formulation was adopted to measure semantic similarity between patch embeddings of ground-truth and predicted images. This yielded a scalar reward in the range for semantic alignment. A format reward was also included, assigning zero reward to TikZ code that did not begin and end with valid document environments. The image encoder was kept frozen during RL optimization to mitigate reward hacking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding dataset quality, model performance, and the efficacy of the proposed training methodology.

#### 4.1. Caption Quality Analysis

An analysis of 200 samples from DaTikZ-V3 indicated that raw captions often lacked key details (e.g., figure types, components, labels) and received low usefulness scores (1-2 on a 5-point Likert scale) from human annotators. Quantitative comparison using BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, and Semantic Textual Similarity (STS) showed that VLM-generated descriptions (e.g., by GPT-4o) were substantially richer and more faithful than raw captions, approaching human-human agreement levels and capturing more details.

#### 4.2. Main Results on Automatic Metrics

On a contamination-free test set of 1,047 samples from DaTikZ-V4, the SFT+RL-tuned TikZilla models achieved superior performance across automatic metrics (CLIPScore, DreamSIM, TeX Edit Distance, Compilation Rate, Average Tokens).
- **TikZilla-3B-RL** achieved an aggregate score (AVG) of 0.385, and **TikZilla-8B-RL** achieved 0.384. These scores surpassed **GPT-5** (0.365), a powerful proprietary LLM, and **GPT-4o** (0.320).
- Compared to **TikZero-Plus-10B**, TikZilla-3B-RL showed substantial improvements across metrics, including +0.085 on CLIP, +0.334 on DSim, a 37% higher compilation rate, and 261 fewer tokens on average.
- RL consistently improved compilation rates to 95–98% for TikZilla models and reduced token length, indicating more efficient code generation.

#### 4.3. Model Size and Training Regime

- The smaller **Qwen2.5-3B model**, when trained with SFT+RL, not only closed the performance gap with **Qwen3-8B** but slightly outperformed it.
- SFT was observed to be crucial for providing syntactic grounding for smaller models, while larger models benefited more directly from RL, suggesting existing TikZ knowledge could be amplified by visual feedback.

#### 4.4. Human Evaluation

Human evaluation by nine expert annotators (6 PhD, 2 postdoc, 1 faculty member) using a 1–5 Likert scale assessed textual and image alignment.
- **GPT-5** achieved the highest textual alignment score (4.18) and tied with **TikZilla-8B-RL** on image-based evaluation (3.48 vs. 3.46).
- **TikZilla-3B-RL** also performed competitively (3.40 text, 3.30 image).
- Reinforcement learning significantly boosted both Qwen models (by +0.75 and +0.67 points) over their SFT-only baselines.
- Most models, especially GPT-5, scored higher on textual evaluation than image evaluation, potentially due to VLM-generated caption limitations or stricter human criteria when comparing to ground-truth images.

#### 4.5. Correlation with Metrics

Spearman’s ρ correlation between automatic metrics and human scores indicated that CLIP (ρ=0.260) and TeX Edit Distance (ρ=0.307) showed weak correlation, DreamSIM (ρ=0.586) showed moderate correlation, and the proposed domain-specific reward model R_Sim (ρ=0.714) showed a strong correlation, validating its alignment with human judgment.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Captions vs. Descriptions:** VLM-generated descriptions consistently outperformed raw captions both at inference (GPT-4o: 0.315 AVG with descriptions vs. 0.270 with captions) and during training. Mixing or oversampling captions degraded performance, suggesting low-quality captions dilute supervision.
- **LLM-based Debugging:** Models trained without the LLM debugging step performed worse (0.288 AVG vs. 0.324), highlighting the necessity of the pipeline for increasing the usable TikZ corpus size.
- **Reward Model Training:** The proposed domain-specific reward model R_Sim achieved the strongest AVG performance compared to CLIPScore and DreamSIM. Retraining DeTikZify-V2 on DaTikZ-V4 further improved the reward model's efficacy (0.389 vs. 0.375 AVG) and its correlation with human judgments (ρ=0.714 vs. 0.698).
- **Dataset Sizes:** Performance of SFT models on Qwen2.5-3B increased sharply with data scale up to 25% of DaTikZ-V4, with more gradual improvements thereafter, suggesting further data scaling could be beneficial.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Data:** On the SPIQA benchmark (OOD for TikZ generation), TikZilla-3B-RL and TikZilla-8B-RL notably outperformed GPT-5, demonstrating robustness under distribution shift.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of scientific content generation by addressing critical limitations in Text-to-TikZ synthesis.

- **Advancement in Text-to-Image Generation:** The construction of **DaTikZ-V4** represents a significant leap in the scale and quality of datasets available for Text-to-TikZ. By combining extensive data sourcing, meticulous filtering, LLM-based debugging, and VLM-generated descriptions, the research provides a robust foundation for training high-performance models.
- **Novel Training Paradigm:** The two-stage training framework, integrating Supervised Fine-tuning with Reinforcement Learning guided by a domain-specific visual reward model, offers a principled approach to aligning LLM outputs with both syntactic correctness and visual semantics. The strong correlation of the R_Sim reward with human judgments demonstrates its effectiveness in capturing nuanced figure quality.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility:** The development of **TikZilla**, a family of small, open-source Qwen-based models, that outperforms larger proprietary systems like GPT-4o and matches GPT-5, highlights the feasibility of building efficient and reproducible text-to-image generation solutions. This reduces reliance on costly, closed-source models and promotes broader access to advanced tools for scientific figure creation.
- **Practical Implications for Scientists:** By enabling the generation of publication-ready scientific figures from textual descriptions, TikZilla has the potential to significantly streamline scientific workflows. It could reduce the time and specialized skills required for creating complex diagrams, allowing scientists to focus more on research content.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work identifies key areas for future research, including developing more reliable VLM annotation methods to reduce biases and hallucinations, designing automatic metrics with stronger alignment to human perception, and extending the approach to other structured generation tasks like LaTeX tables, CAD, or flowcharts.

The limitations primarily involve the VLM-generated figure descriptions, which, despite being superior to raw captions, may still introduce omissions or hallucinations, potentially biasing training and reward optimization. Addressing these issues with more reliable annotation methods and fine-grained reward functions remains a crucial area for continued development.
