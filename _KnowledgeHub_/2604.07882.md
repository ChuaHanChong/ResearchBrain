# ReconPhys: Reconstruct Appearance and Physical Attributes from Single Video

> [!summary] Summary
> ReconPhys introduces a feedforward neural framework to jointly reconstruct the appearance, geometry, and intrinsic physical attributes of non-rigid objects from a single monocular video. This approach enables the rapid generation of physically plausible 3D digital assets, achieving an 8.37 dB gain in future prediction PSNR and over 3600x faster inference compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Existing non-rigid object reconstruction methods with physical plausibility suffer from high computational costs due due to extensive per-scene optimization and manual annotation.
- Current dynamic 3D reconstruction techniques primarily focus on visual fidelity, lacking explicit recovery of underlying physical parameters, leading to physically inconsistent simulations.
- Many physics-aware reconstruction methods depend on difficult-to-obtain ground-truth physical labels or dense multi-view inputs, limiting their applicability to real-world monocular settings.

## Method

- A feedforward neural framework jointly estimates physical attributes and reconstructs 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations, replacing scene-specific physics tuning with a neural estimator.
- Elastic object dynamics are modeled using a differentiable spring-mass system bound to 3DGS, allowing gradients from photometric reconstruction loss to flow back to physical parameters.
- A self-supervised training strategy with 'self-forcing' and truncated backpropagation is employed, using only monocular videos and synthetic data, to infer physical attributes without explicit labels.

## Results

- ReconPhys achieved a future prediction PSNR of 21.64, outperforming Spring-Gaus (13.27 PSNR) by 8.37 dB, and improved dynamic reconstruction PSNR to 33.84, compared to 30.33 for 4DGS.
- The method demonstrated superior 3D geometric accuracy with a Chamfer Distance of 0.001, a substantial reduction from 0.593 by 4DGS.
- Inference was performed in less than 1 second, a significant speedup compared to baseline methods requiring over 1 hour, while achieving lower Mean Absolute Error in physical parameter estimation (e.g., stiffness MAE 297.3 vs. 827.67 for Spring-Gaus).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly learning physical attribute estimation and 3DGS reconstruction through a differentiable simulation-rendering loop allows for self-supervised learning of physical properties from visual cues.
> - A feedforward architecture, trained on a large-scale synthetic dataset, enables rapid (sub-second) inference and strong cross-object generalization for physically plausible assets.
> - Binding a sparse spring-mass system to dense 3D Gaussians via anchor sampling and inverse distance weighting effectively balances computational efficiency with detailed deformation representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of non-rigid object reconstruction operates at the intersection of computer vision, computer graphics, and physics-based simulation. Its applications span areas such as virtual reality, robotics, and digital content creation, where accurately representing objects that deform under various forces is crucial for realistic interaction and simulation.

Recent advancements in dynamic scene reconstruction have been significantly influenced by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and, more recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). Early dynamic NeRF methods, such as D-NeRF and Nerfies, modeled temporal changes through canonical-space deformation or scene flow. These methods achieved high-quality view synthesis but were often computationally intensive for training and rendering, limiting interactive applications. The introduction of 3DGS provided an explicit representation with real-time differentiable rendering, leading to dynamic 3DGS methods that model scenes through deformation-based approaches or explicit per-timestep parameterizations. While these techniques can render photorealistic dynamic videos efficiently, they primarily focus on geometric and appearance fidelity. A limitation of these approaches is their inability to explicitly recover physically meaningful parameters. Consequently, their visual predictions, while plausible, may lack physical consistency under unobserved forces or interactions, which restricts their utility in applications requiring realistic physical simulation.

Bridging visual reconstruction with physical understanding is a challenge for creating "simulation-ready assets." Current techniques addressing this often involve coupling neural representations with differentiable physics engines. Examples include PAC-NeRF, which integrates Material Point Method (MPM) dynamics with NeRF but assumes predefined material families and coarse global parameters, and PhysGaussian, which incorporates continuum mechanics into 3DGS for generative dynamics rather than inferring per-scene attributes from observations. Another relevant work is Spring-Gaus, which integrates a spring-mass system with 3D Gaussians for elastic object reconstruction and simulation. However, these methods often necessitate dense multi-view inputs, explicit state supervision, expensive per-scene optimization, or specialized simulation data. Such requirements limit their scalability, generalizability, and practicality for real-world monocular video input.

ReconPhys contributes to this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing methods, particularly the reliance on per-scene optimization and multi-view inputs. It proposes a feedforward framework that jointly learns physical attribute estimation and 3DGS reconstruction from a single monocular video. This approach aims to provide a more efficient and generalizable solution for generating physically plausible, simulation-ready digital assets, distinguishing itself by enabling rapid inference without manual annotation or expensive tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of jointly reconstructing the appearance, geometry, and intrinsic physical attributes of non-rigid objects from a single monocular video input. The reconstructed physical attributes are intended to enable realistic physical simulation and interaction with the digital object.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations identified in the broader research landscape:

1. **High Computational Cost and Limited Practicality of Per-Scene Optimization**: Existing methods for reconstructing non-rigid objects with physical plausibility often rely on differentiable rendering techniques that necessitate extensive per-scene optimization. This process involves iterative tuning or manual annotation, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming. Such overhead significantly restricts the practicality and generalizability of these methods for large-scale deployment or real-time applications. The authors aim to overcome this by developing a feedforward framework that eliminates the need for per-scene optimization.

2. **Lack of Physical Consistency in Current Dynamic Reconstruction Methods**: While recent advancements in dynamic 3DGS have enabled photorealistic reconstruction of dynamic scenes, these methods primarily focus on visual fidelity (geometry and appearance). They do not explicitly recover underlying physical parameters, leading to reconstructed objects that might appear visually plausible but behave inconsistently when subjected to unseen forces or interactions in a simulation environment. This deficiency limits their utility in applications such as robotics, where accurate physical behavior is critical for planning and interaction. ReconPhys seeks to integrate physics-aware modeling directly into the reconstruction process to produce physically consistent assets.

3. **Dependency on Ground-Truth Physics Labels and Multi-View Inputs**: Many physics-aware reconstruction techniques require ground-truth physical labels, which are often difficult and costly to obtain for real-world objects. Furthermore, some methods, such as Spring-Gaus, rely on dense multi-view inputs, further increasing data acquisition complexity and restricting their applicability to general monocular settings. The authors aim to develop a self-supervised training strategy that eliminates the need for ground-truth physics labels and enables inference from a single monocular video.

4. **Inefficiency in Generating Simulation-Ready Assets**: The process of transferring real-world objects into simulation environments for tasks like non-rigid manipulation often involves significant human intervention and time. The existing methods, requiring hours for processing, hinder the rapid generation of simulation-ready data. A key motivation for ReconPhys is to achieve fast inference, allowing for the generation of simulation-ready assets within seconds, thereby streamlining workflows in robotics and graphics.

In summary, the core motivation is to create an efficient, generalizable, and practical system that can rapidly transform a single monocular video of a non-rigid object into a physically plausible 3D digital asset, complete with its geometry, appearance, and intrinsic physical properties, without requiring explicit physical labels or extensive per-scene tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReconPhys is designed as a feedforward neural framework that jointly estimates physical attributes and reconstructs 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations from a single monocular video. The approach replaces scene-specific physics tuning with a neural estimator trained to predict physical attributes based on visual cues and observed motion dynamics.

**Problem Formulation:**
Given a monocular video $\mathcal{V}$ of an object undergoing dynamics (e.g., gravitational drop, collision), the system aims to recover its intrinsic physical attributes $\mathbf{p}$ and 3D visual representation $\hat{\mathbf{g}}$. The object is modeled as a deformable body using a spring-mass system with $N_A$ mass points. The physical attributes $\mathbf{p}$ include the mass of each point $m_i$, stiffness $k_{ij}$ and damping $d_{ij}$ coefficients for springs connecting points $i$ and $j$, and a global friction coefficient $f$. For efficiency, shared parameters across points/springs are used (i.e., $\hat{m}_i = \hat{m}$, $\hat{k}_{ij} = \hat{k}$, $\hat{d}_{ij} = \hat{d}$). The object's geometry and appearance are represented by $N$ 3D Gaussian kernels, each characterized by its 3D center $\mu_j$, orientation $\theta_j$, RGB color $c_j$, scale $\sigma_j$, and opacity $\alpha_j$. The framework's prediction model $\mathcal{M}$ takes $\mathcal{V}$ as input and jointly regresses $(\hat{\mathbf{p}}, \hat{\mathbf{g}})$.

**Physical Dynamics Model:**
1. **3D Spring-Mass System**: Elastic object dynamics are modeled using $N_A$ anchor points $\mathcal{A} = \{\mathbf{x}_i\}$, each with mass $m_i$ and velocity $\mathbf{v}_i$. Spring connections are established between each anchor and its $K$ nearest neighbors (KNN) based on their initial positions, defining rest lengths $l_{i,j}$. Each spring has stiffness $k_{i,j}$ and damping $d_{i,j}$. The net force $\mathbf{F}_i^t$ on an anchor at timestep $t$ combines nonlinear spring forces, damping forces, and gravity. Spring forces use a generalized Hooke's law with an exponent $p_k$.
2. **Numerical Integration**: Anchor positions and velocities are updated using semi-implicit Euler integration with timestep $\Delta t$. Boundary conditions $\mathcal{B}(\cdot)$ are applied to model ground collisions.
3. **Binding to 3D Gaussian Splatting**: A two-stage binding mechanism connects the physical simulation to the visual representation:
 - **Anchor Sampling**: From the $N$ Gaussian centers of the initial 3DGS representation, $N_A$ anchor points are generated via volume sampling. This ensures anchors are distributed throughout the object and $N_A \ll N$ for computational efficiency.
 - **Position Interpolation**: After simulating anchor dynamics, Gaussian centers $\mu_j$ are updated using Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) interpolation based on their $n_b$ nearest anchors. This preserves local deformation while using sparse anchors for simulation.

**Model Architecture:**
ReconPhys consists of a dual-branch architecture:
1. **3DGS Predictor**: This component outputs a canonical set of Gaussians $\hat{\mathbf{g}}_0$. Importantly, this predictor utilizes off-the-shelf pretrained weights and is kept frozen during the main training phase of ReconPhys. Its role is to provide a high-fidelity initial geometric and appearance representation. Anchor points for the spring-mass system are sampled from this canonical 3DGS.
2. **Physical Predictor**: This branch estimates the physical attributes $\hat{\mathbf{p}} = (\hat{m}, \hat{k}, \hat{d}, \hat{f})$.
 - **Vision Encoder**: An InternViT-based vision encoder extracts dynamic features from each frame of the input monocular video.
 - **ResNet Backbone with Self-Attention**: These per-frame features are aggregated by a ResNet backbone with self-attention to capture spatio-temporal context, producing a compact physical feature representation.
 - **Physical Decoder**: An MLP-based decoder maps these aggregated features to the estimated physical attributes $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$.

**Differentiable Simulation-Rendering Loop:**
The framework establishes an end-to-end differentiable computation graph to learn $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ without ground-truth physical annotations.
- **Forward Pass**: The estimated $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ parameterizes the differentiable spring-mass simulator, which recursively predicts anchor states $\{\mathbf{x}_i^t, \mathbf{v}_i^t\}$. These anchor displacements are propagated to the Gaussian centers via the binding mechanism, creating deformed 3DGS representations $\hat{\mathbf{g}}_t$. A differentiable 3DGS renderer then generates predicted frames $\hat{\mathbf{I}}_t$.
- **Backward Pass**: A photometric reconstruction loss ($\mathcal{L}$) between the predicted frame $\hat{\mathbf{I}}_t$ and the ground-truth input frame $\mathbf{I}_t$ provides gradients. These gradients flow from $\hat{\mathbf{I}}_t$ back to $\mu_t$ through the renderer, then to $\mathbf{x}_i^t$ via the binding rule. Since the simulator is differentiable, gradients are further propagated through the unrolled dynamics to reach the physical parameters $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$, enabling updates to the physical predictor. This gradient pathway focuses on identifying physical attributes that explain the observed motion, given the frozen 3DGS reconstruction.

**Self-Supervised Training with Self-Forcing:**
ReconPhys is trained in a self-supervised manner using only monocular videos, without explicit physical labels.
- **Self-Forcing**: To mitigate the training-inference mismatch common in autoregressive models, a self-forcing strategy is adopted. At each simulation step, the simulator advances from its *own* previously predicted state, rather than relying on ground-truth or proxy states. This promotes long-horizon stability and robustness to accumulated errors.
- **Truncated Backpropagation**: To manage gradient instability associated with long unrolled trajectories, truncated backpropagation is applied. Input states to each rollout step are detached, preventing gradient explosion or vanishing while still allowing supervision from rendered frames to update $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$.
- **Loss Function**: The training objective is an L2 photometric reconstruction loss: $\mathcal{L} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \|\mathbf{I}_t - \hat{\mathbf{I}}_t\|_2^2$, which is fully differentiable with respect to $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$.

**Synthetic Data Pipeline:**
To overcome the scarcity of large-scale datasets with 3D assets and ground-truth physical attributes, an automated pipeline was developed:
1. **Semantic Selection and Asset Generation**: 3D assets suitable for non-rigid dynamics were semantically filtered from the Objaverse-XL collection (500 objects). High-fidelity 3DGS representations were reconstructed using TRELLIS from four orthogonal views for each object.
2. **Consistent Anchor Sampling**: To ensure reproducibility and interpretability, anchor points ($N_A$) are sampled using a hash code derived from the object's unique identifier, guaranteeing an identical set of anchors for each object.
3. **Physical Parameterization and Simulation**: Each 3DGS asset is bound to a spring-mass system, and physical parameters ($m, k, d, f$) are sampled from continuous distributions. A 30-frame free-fall trajectory, including ground collision, is simulated for each (geometry, physics) pair.
4. **Dataset Generation**: The resulting motion is rendered into 512x512 monocular videos, forming a dataset of $(\mathcal{V}, \mathbf{g}, \mathbf{p})$ triplets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on a large-scale synthetic dataset demonstrate ReconPhys's performance in dynamic reconstruction, future prediction, cross-object generalization, and physical disentanglement.

**Cross-Object Generalization:**
ReconPhys was evaluated on a held-out set of 46 unseen objects, comparing its performance against 4DGS and Spring-Gaus. The results, summarized in Table 1 and Figure 4, indicate:
- **Future Prediction**: ReconPhys achieved a PSNR of 21.64, significantly outperforming Spring-Gaus (13.27 PSNR), representing an 8.37 dB gain. SSIM and LPIPS also showed improvements (0.907 vs 0.745 SSIM, 0.0876 vs 0.2856 LPIPS).
- **Dynamic Reconstruction**: ReconPhys demonstrated superior visual fidelity with a PSNR of 33.84, compared to 30.33 for 4DGS and 22.26 for Spring-Gaus.
- **3D Geometric Accuracy**: The method achieved a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.001, a substantial reduction from 0.593 by 4DGS and 0.466 by Spring-Gaus. Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) also improved from 1.501 to 0.007 compared to 4DGS.
- **Computational Efficiency**: ReconPhys performed inference in less than 1 second, whereas baseline methods required over 1 hour, demonstrating a significant speedup.
- **Qualitative Results**: Figure 4 visually corroborates these quantitative results, showing that ReconPhys produces more stable dynamics and realistic future states. Figure 5 further indicates that the model qualitatively captures complex, physics-consistent deformations on real-world non-rigid assets without per-scene optimization.

**Physical Disentanglement:**
To assess the model's ability to separate physical attributes from geometric structure, 46 objects were each assigned two distinct physical configurations within the dataset.
- **Reconstruction Fidelity**: As presented in Table 2, ReconPhys consistently maintained high reconstruction fidelity (e.g., PSNR values in the 30s) while distinguishing between varying physical states for the same geometry. This suggests successful geometry-attribute decoupling.
- **Physical Parameter Estimation Accuracy**: Table 3 details the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for predicted physical parameters. ReconPhys achieved substantially lower errors compared to Spring-Gaus across all attributes:
 - Stiffness: 297.3 (ReconPhys) vs. 827.67 (Spring-Gaus)
 - Damping: 1.151 (ReconPhys) vs. 2.546 (Spring-Gaus)
 - Mass: 1.337 (ReconPhys) vs. 2.276 (Spring-Gaus)
 - Friction: 1.508 (ReconPhys) vs. 1.082 (Spring-Gaus) (Note: The paper states 1.508 for ReconPhys and 1.082 for Spring-Gaus for friction, suggesting Spring-Gaus has a slightly lower error for friction, but overall ReconPhys shows superior performance across other metrics.)
- **Trajectory Divergence**: Figure 6 visually confirms this disentanglement, showing that for the same object, distinct predicted physical attributes lead to observably different future trajectories, validating the model's capacity to infer physically meaningful parameters from monocular video.

**Application in Robot Non-rigid Object Manipulation:**
Figure 7 illustrates the practical application of ReconPhys in robot non-rigid object manipulation. The pipeline involves:
1. Capturing a free-falling object video.
2. Segmenting the object using SAM.
3. Processing the segmented video with ReconPhys to reconstruct a 3DGS representation and a spring-mass system.
4. Simulating interactive manipulation in a virtual environment (e.g., using PhysTwin framework) with the reconstructed deformable object.
This demonstration suggests an efficient method for acquiring high-fidelity deformable digital assets for photorealistic physical simulation, potentially enabling scalable simulation frameworks for robotic manipulation.

In summary, the main findings highlight ReconPhys's capability to achieve state-of-the-art performance in both visual reconstruction and physically plausible future prediction from monocular video. It significantly reduces inference time and improves geometric accuracy compared to existing methods, while effectively disentangling physical attributes from visual appearance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReconPhys framework introduces several significant contributions and has the potential for impact across multiple domains:

1. **Bridging Visual Perception and Physical Understanding**: This work directly addresses the challenge of integrating visual reconstruction with an understanding of underlying physical properties. By linking pixel-level reconstruction errors to physical attributes through a differentiable simulation-rendering loop, ReconPhys establishes a connection between observed visual dynamics and the physical parameters that govern them. This is a fundamental step toward creating more intelligent systems that can not only "see" but also "understand" the physical behavior of objects in the real world.

2. **Efficiency and Scalability for Simulation-Ready Assets**: A key innovation of ReconPhys is its feedforward architecture, which enables rapid inference (under 1 second) compared to hours required by previous optimization-based methods. This eliminates the need for expensive per-scene optimization or manual annotation, making the process highly efficient and scalable. The ability to quickly generate 3DGS assets with bound physical attributes means that real-world objects can be rapidly transferred into simulation environments without significant human intervention. This accelerated workflow is crucial for applications that demand high throughput of digital assets.

3. **Enhanced Generalizability and Zero-Shot Transfer**: The self-supervised training strategy, coupled with a diverse synthetic dataset, allows ReconPhys to learn generalizable mappings from visual cues to material properties. This enables zero-shot transfer to unseen objects and material properties, meaning the model can predict physical attributes for objects it has not encountered during training. Such generalizability is a substantial advantage over methods that rely on known material families or object-specific tuning.

4. **Advancements in Robotics for Non-Rigid Manipulation**: The demonstrated application in robot non-rigid object manipulation showcases a direct impact on robotics. By providing an efficient pipeline to acquire high-fidelity deformable digital assets from simple videos, ReconPhys facilitates the creation of realistic simulation environments. These environments are essential for training and evaluating robotic policies for tasks involving deformable objects, which are notoriously difficult for robots to handle. The ability to rapidly generate accurate physical models can accelerate research and development in areas like soft robotics, surgical robotics, and object manipulation in unstructured environments, contributing to more robust and adaptable robotic systems.

5. **Impact on Computer Graphics and Virtual Reality**: For content creation in computer graphics, virtual reality, and augmented reality, ReconPhys offers a pathway to generate visually realistic and physically plausible animated objects. Developers can easily capture videos of real-world objects and quickly obtain digital twins that behave authentically, enhancing the immersion and interactivity of virtual environments. This can reduce the manual effort involved in physically rigging and parameterizing deformable models.

6. **Self-Supervised Learning Paradigm for Physics Inference**: The development of a self-supervised training strategy that leverages photometric reconstruction loss to infer physical attributes without explicit physical labels is a methodological advancement. It demonstrates that meaningful physical understanding can be derived purely from observed visual dynamics, opening new avenues for learning complex physical phenomena directly from perceptual data.

In conclusion, ReconPhys offers a practical and efficient solution for generating physically plausible 3D digital assets from monocular video, significantly advancing the integration of visual perception with physical simulation. Its potential impact spans robotics, computer graphics, and the broader field of physically-aware artificial intelligence.
