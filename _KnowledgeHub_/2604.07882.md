# ReconPhys: Reconstruct Appearance and Physical Attributes from Single Video

> [!summary] Summary
> ReconPhys introduces a feedforward neural framework to jointly reconstruct the appearance, geometry, and intrinsic physical attributes of non-rigid objects from a single monocular video. This approach enables the rapid generation of physically plausible 3D digital assets, achieving an 8.37 dB gain in future prediction PSNR and over 3600x faster inference compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Existing non-rigid object reconstruction methods with physical plausibility suffer from high computational costs due due to extensive per-scene optimization and manual annotation.
- Current dynamic 3D reconstruction techniques primarily focus on visual fidelity, lacking explicit recovery of underlying physical parameters, leading to physically inconsistent simulations.
- Many physics-aware reconstruction methods depend on difficult-to-obtain ground-truth physical labels or dense multi-view inputs, limiting their applicability to real-world monocular settings.

## Method

- A feedforward neural framework jointly estimates physical attributes and reconstructs 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations, replacing scene-specific physics tuning with a neural estimator.
- Elastic object dynamics are modeled using a differentiable spring-mass system bound to 3DGS, allowing gradients from photometric reconstruction loss to flow back to physical parameters.
- A self-supervised training strategy with 'self-forcing' and truncated backpropagation is employed, using only monocular videos and synthetic data, to infer physical attributes without explicit labels.

## Results

- ReconPhys achieved a future prediction PSNR of 21.64, outperforming Spring-Gaus (13.27 PSNR) by 8.37 dB, and improved dynamic reconstruction PSNR to 33.84, compared to 30.33 for 4DGS.
- The method demonstrated superior 3D geometric accuracy with a Chamfer Distance of 0.001, a substantial reduction from 0.593 by 4DGS.
- Inference was performed in less than 1 second, a significant speedup compared to baseline methods requiring over 1 hour, while achieving lower Mean Absolute Error in physical parameter estimation (e.g., stiffness MAE 297.3 vs. 827.67 for Spring-Gaus).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly learning physical attribute estimation and 3DGS reconstruction through a differentiable simulation-rendering loop allows for self-supervised learning of physical properties from visual cues.
> - A feedforward architecture, trained on a large-scale synthetic dataset, enables rapid (sub-second) inference and strong cross-object generalization for physically plausible assets.
> - Binding a sparse spring-mass system to dense 3D Gaussians via anchor sampling and inverse distance weighting effectively balances computational efficiency with detailed deformation representation.
