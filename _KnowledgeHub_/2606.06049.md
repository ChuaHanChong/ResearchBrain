# L-SDPPO: Policy Optimization of Spiking Diffusion Policy for Intra-vehicular Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> L-SDPPO, developed by researchers at Harbin Institute of Technology and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, integrates spiking neural networks with diffusion policies and reinforcement learning to enable energy-efficient and precise robotic manipulation in microgravity. This approach achieves a 97% success rate across diverse simulated intra-vehicular tasks while reducing energy consumption to approximately 36.45% of traditional diffusion policies.

## Problem

- Intra-vehicular robots operating in microgravity need high-precision control for unconstrained, free-floating objects, which traditional data-driven methods struggle to model with multimodal action distributions.
- Spacecraft platforms have strict power limitations, rendering computationally intensive methods like diffusion policies (DPs) impractical due to their high energy consumption.
- Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), despite their energy efficiency, typically exhibit insufficient perception of dynamic spatiotemporal features, crucial for complex manipulation in dynamic microgravity scenarios.

## Method

- The **L-SDPPO** framework combines a ==Spiking Diffusion Policy== (SDP) for robust action distribution modeling with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for online fine-tuning and enhanced robustness.
- The diffusion policy's noise prediction network is parameterized using a ==Spiking Neural Network== (SNN) to leverage event-driven, sparse computation, drastically reducing energy consumption compared to ANNs.
- A novel ==State-Dependent Latency Injection== (SDLI) mechanism is introduced to dynamically modulate the timing of state features within the SNN, improving spatiotemporal perception critical for dynamic microgravity manipulation.

## Results

- L-SDPPO achieved a mean success rate of **0.97** and an average reward of 478.25 across five simulated intra-vehicular tasks, outperforming all baselines, including DPPO (**0.79** SR, 350.60 AR).
- The framework demonstrated superior energy efficiency, consuming an average of **229.18 pJ**, which is approximately **36.45%** of the **628.75 pJ** used by the ANN-based DPPO, attributed to SNNs' temporal sparsity.
- The State-Dependent Latency Injection (SDLI) mechanism specifically improved the average success rate from **0.94** (SDPPO) to **0.97** (L-SDPPO), with a notable impact on precision-demanding tasks like container capping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating energy-efficient Spiking Neural Networks with Diffusion Policies provides a viable pathway to implement complex, robust control strategies in power-constrained environments like spacecraft.
> - Dynamically controlled temporal encoding, such as State-Dependent Latency Injection, significantly enhances SNNs' ability to perceive and utilize spatiotemporal information, improving precision in dynamic robotic tasks.
> - Combining pre-training on expert data with online reinforcement learning fine-tuning allows spiking diffusion policies to achieve high performance and robustness, overcoming limitations of pure imitation learning in complex environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Intra-vehicular robots (IVRs) are being developed to support astronauts and enhance mission efficiency during extended space missions. These robots operate in microgravity environments where objects exhibit unconstrained drift, necessitating robust, high-precision control. Concurrently, space platforms are characterized by stringent power limitations, demanding energy-efficient control models.

Early approaches to precision space operations primarily utilized model-based impedance control. While effective for specific hardware configurations, these methods often required extensive parameter tuning and demonstrated limited generalization capabilities to unstructured tasks. The research community subsequently explored data-driven methods, such as reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL), which offer increased flexibility. However, RL often faces challenges with low sample efficiency in environments with sparse rewards, and both RL and IL can struggle to model multimodal action distributions common in dynamic microgravity scenarios.

More recently, diffusion policies (DP) have emerged as a method capable of modeling complex, multimodal action distributions by framing robot behavior as a conditional denoising process. This approach has shown promise in capturing intricate distributions and maintaining execution stability. Nevertheless, the iterative sampling inherent in diffusion models is computationally intensive, leading to high energy consumption that often exceeds the constrained power budgets of spacecraft.

This work addresses this specific energy efficiency gap in DP-based robotic manipulation by integrating it with Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs). SNNs are known for their event-driven, sparse computation, which can lead to significantly lower power consumption compared to conventional Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs). The paper positions itself as an effort to leverage the representational capabilities of diffusion policies while mitigating their energy footprint, thereby enabling their practical deployment in power-limited space environments. Furthermore, it introduces a mechanism to enhance spatiotemporal perception within SNNs, a critical requirement for dynamic manipulation in microgravity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a low-energy, precise, and robust robotic manipulation framework for intra-vehicular tasks in microgravity environments. The authors aim to overcome several specific challenges inherent in this domain:

1. **Addressing Microgravity-Induced Manipulation Challenges:** In microgravity, free-floating objects are highly susceptible to displacement, even under minimal interaction forces. This necessitates compliant grasping and high-precision control to prevent target drift. Traditional methods often struggle with this level of precision or with generalizing to the unpredictable nature of unconstrained object movement and multimodal action distributions. Diffusion policies (DPs) have shown potential in modeling these complex action distributions, but their computational demands are a barrier.

2. **Mitigating High Energy Consumption for Spacecraft Operation:** Space cabins have strict power limitations, which constrain the complexity and operational duration of onboard robotic systems. Current state-of-the-art deep learning methods, including DPs, typically require significant computational power for inference, making them unsuitable for deployment on energy-constrained space platforms. A core motivation is to reduce this energy footprint without compromising manipulation accuracy.

3. **Enhancing Spatiotemporal Perception in Microgravity:** The dynamic nature of objects in microgravity means that robots require acute perception of spatiotemporal features. Traditional deep learning models, when converted to SNNs, might lose some of this temporal sensitivity. The authors identify an "insufficient perception of dynamic spatiotemporal features" as a specific problem that needs to be addressed to improve the adaptability of spiking policies in this environment.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose the L-SDPPO framework. Their motivation is to combine the strengths of different methodologies:
- Leveraging **Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs)** for their event-driven, low-power inference capabilities to meet strict energy budgets.
- Utilizing **Diffusion Policies (DPs)** to effectively model the complex, multimodal action distributions required for robust manipulation in unpredictable microgravity conditions.
- Integrating **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, specifically PPO, to fine-tune pre-trained policies, thereby moving beyond the limitations of expert datasets and enhancing robustness against real-world disturbances and cumulative errors.
- Introducing the **State-Dependent Latency Injection (SDLI)** mechanism to refine spatiotemporal perception within the SNN, dynamically regulating the timing of input information to better capture dynamic temporal cues, mimicking biological neural delays.

Through this integrated approach, the research aims to deliver a viable robotic manipulation method that achieves high success rates and substantially lower energy consumption in simulated intra-vehicular tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, L-SDPPO (Low-Energy Spiking Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization), integrates a Spiking Diffusion Policy (SDP) with a reinforcement learning algorithm (PPO) and a novel State-Dependent Latency Injection (SDLI) mechanism. The framework operates in two main phases: pre-training and fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. A. Spiking Diffusion Policy (SDP)

The SDP architecture adapts the conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) to a spiking neural network paradigm.
1. **Policy Formulation:** The policy $\pi_\theta$ is defined as a K-step reverse denoising process conditioned on the environment observation $s$. It generates physical actions by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise. The mean $\mu_\theta$ of each denoising step is parameterized by a neural network.
2. **SNN Parameterization:** To achieve energy efficiency, the noise prediction network $\mu_\theta$ is parameterized using an SNN. Each denoising step involves a complete forward pass of the SNN backbone. The SNN's internal dynamics evolve over a microscale time window $T$. The architecture comprises hybrid input encoding, Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) dynamics, and full-spiking residual blocks.
 - **Hybrid Input Encoding:** Multi-modal inputs, including the discrete diffusion timestep $k$ (encoded sinusoidally) and continuous observation $s$ (processed by a state encoder), are concatenated with the noisy action $x_k$ to form a joint input $Z_{in}$. This $Z_{in}$ is injected as a constant current into the SNN across all internal simulation timesteps $\tau$.
 - **Spatiotemporal Forward Propagation:** Signals propagate through $M$ full-spiking residual blocks. Each block integrates linear transformations, numerical normalization (LayerNorm-like), and LIF neuronal dynamics. LIF neurons integrate current, generate a spike when the membrane potential exceeds a threshold, and reset the potential. A surrogate gradient approximation is used for backpropagation through the non-differentiable spike generation. Residual fusion is performed using sparse accumulate (AC) operations, passing a fused multi-level spiking vector to the next block.
 - **Global Temporal Decoding:** A global feature vector is computed by averaging the temporal sequence from the final SNN block. This averaged representation is then decoded to produce the final continuous noise prediction $\hat{\epsilon}_k$, which parameterizes the mean $\mu_\theta$ for the current diffusion step.
3. **Training Objective:** During pre-training, the SDP is trained via supervised learning on an expert dataset. The objective is to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the ground-truth noise $\epsilon$ (used to create $x_k$ from $x_0$) and the SNN's predicted noise $\hat{\epsilon}_k$.

#### 3.2. B. State-Dependent Latency Injection (SDLI)

The SDLI mechanism is introduced during the fine-tuning phase to dynamically control the timing of state features entering the spiking network, addressing spatiotemporal perception.
1. **Latency Mapping:** For a raw state vector $s_i$, its intensity is normalized. This normalized intensity is then mapped to a specific temporal delay $\delta(\bar{s}_i)$ within the SNN's internal time window $[T_{min}, T_{max}]$. This mimics the biological time-to-first-spike (TTFS) coding, where higher stimulus intensity results in shorter latencies.
2. **Soft Gating Mechanism:** The latency injection is implemented as a soft gate to facilitate gradient propagation. The effective state input $\tilde{s}_i^{(\tau,n)}$ received by the SNN at internal timestep $\tau$ is modulated by the raw state $s_i$ and a sigmoid function of the difference between $\tau$ and the calculated delay $\delta(\bar{s}_i)$. The strength of this injection is controlled by $\psi(n)$, which follows a linear warm-up schedule to ensure stable convergence.

#### 3.3. C. PPO-based SDP Optimization

To enhance robustness and overcome the limitations of expert dataset coverage, the pre-trained SDP is fine-tuned online using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
1. **Dynamic Conditioning:** The SDLI-modulated, time-varying state sequence $\tilde{S}_i$ provides dynamic conditioning for the spiking backbone.
2. **Optimization Objective:** The policy is optimized by minimizing a total objective function, $L_{total}$, which is a weighted sum of four components:
 - **PPO Clipping Objective ($L_{CLIP}$):** Prevents destabilizing policy updates by clipping the probability ratio $p_r(\theta)$ (ratio of new to old policy probabilities) with respect to the advantage estimate $\hat{A}_i$.
 - **Value Function Loss ($L_{VF}$):** Minimizes the MSE between the predicted value function $V_\phi(s_i)$ and the target return $R_i$.
 - **Behavior Cloning Loss ($L_{BC}$):** Maintains consistency with the expert distribution from pre-training by penalizing the deviation of the spiking network’s output noise from the ground-truth noise.
 - **Entropy Regularization ($L_{ENT}$):** Encourages exploration and prevents convergence to local optima by adding a negative empirical variance of the sampled action distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The L-SDPPO framework was evaluated on five simulated intra-vehicular tasks designed to challenge robotic manipulation capabilities in microgravity. These tasks included hatch opening, dynamic tumbling target stowing, precision container capping, sequential panel operation, and long-horizon tumbling target stowing. The framework's performance was benchmarked against Gaussian-based Behavior Cloning (GBC), PPO, GBC pre-training with PPO fine-tuning (GPPO), Diffusion Policy (DP), and Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO). Evaluation metrics included Success Rate (SR), Average Reward (AR), and Energy Consumption (EC).

#### 4.1. Experimental Settings

- **Expert Data:** 200 expert demonstration trajectories per task for pre-training.
- **Training Phases:**
 - **Pre-training:** SDP trained via imitation learning for 3,000 epochs.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Online refinement via PPO, leveraging 50 parallel environments, 10,000 transitions per batch, and 10 update epochs.
- **Hyperparameters:** A unified set of hyperparameters was used across all tasks.
- **Energy Model:** Energy consumption was calculated based on a theoretical model using 45nm CMOS technology, assigning 4.6 pJ for Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) operations (ANNs) and 0.9 pJ for Accumulate (AC) operations (SNNs).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis of Task Execution

L-SDPPO consistently generated trajectories that exhibited precision and stability across all five tasks:
- **Task I (Hatch Opening):** End-effector trajectory aligned with handle displacement post-grasp, showing distinct approach, rotate, and open phases. Hatch actuation occurred only after handle rotation reached a threshold.
- **Task II (Dynamic Tumbling Target Stowing):** End-effector trajectory smoothly aligned with the target object, effectively counteracting microgravity disturbances during approach and placement phases.
- **Task III (Precision Container Capping):** End-effector precisely tracked the lid across all dimensions, with trajectories converging smoothly and remaining stable during the final "closing" phase, indicating high-precision alignment.
- **Task IV (Sequential Panel Operation):** The end-effector navigated through target switches to a final plug location, with state transitions demonstrating temporal consistency and precision for multi-step "open, close, and pull" sequences.
- **Task V (Long-horizon Tumbling Target Stowing):** Spatial trajectories for end-effector and object showed successful long-horizon drawer manipulation. State profiles accurately tracked sequential actions (grasp handle, open drawer, grasp target, close drawer), validating sequential planning capabilities.
Comparatively, Figure 6 visually depicted that baseline methods like GBC and PPO often exhibited erratic trajectories or overshooting in Task III, failing to align the container lid. DPPO, while approaching, showed visible oscillation during fine alignment. L-SDPPO maintained stable, smooth, and precise spatial trajectories.

#### 4.3. Performance and Efficiency Evaluation

Quantitative comparison demonstrated L-SDPPO's performance:
- **Overall Performance:** L-SDPPO achieved a mean SR of 0.97 and an average AR of 478.25 across all tasks. This surpassed all baselines, including DPPO (mean SR of 0.79, AR of 350.60).
- **Energy Efficiency:** L-SDPPO recorded an average EC of 229.18 pJ, which is approximately 36.45% of the DPPO's average EC of 628.75 pJ. This indicates a substantial reduction in energy consumption while maintaining high performance. SNNs' temporal sparsity contributed to this efficiency.
- **Convergence:** SNN-based methods (SDPPO and L-SDPPO) demonstrated faster convergence and achieved higher final SR and AR compared to ANN baselines (GPPO and DPPO), particularly in precision-demanding tasks like Task III where ANN baselines struggled to achieve high success rates.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of the PPO fine-tuning and SDLI mechanism:
- **SDP (Base SNN via IL):** Achieved an average SR of 0.78 and AR of 208.14, demonstrating the initial capability of the spiking diffusion model.
- **SDPPO (SDP + PPO Fine-tuning):** The addition of PPO fine-tuning significantly improved performance, raising the average SR to 0.94 and AR to 452.85. This highlights the importance of active exploration and online refinement in microgravity environments.
- **L-SDPPO (SDPPO + SDLI):** The full framework, incorporating the SDLI module, achieved the highest overall metrics with an average SR of 0.97 and AR of 478.25. The SDLI module's impact was particularly notable in Task III (Precision Container Capping), where it increased the SR from 0.83 (SDPPO) to 0.91 (L-SDPPO) and the AR from 457.68 to 502.36. This indicates that SDLI is important for tasks requiring precise and stable control by enhancing spatiotemporal sensitivity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The L-SDPPO framework represents a step towards addressing critical challenges in intra-vehicular robotic manipulation within microgravity environments. The work's significance lies in its integrated approach that simultaneously tackles the demand for high-precision control, the need for robustness against unpredictable object dynamics, and the strict power limitations of spacecraft.

The primary impact is the demonstration of a robotic control system that achieves a balance between performance and energy efficiency. By leveraging Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), L-SDPPO significantly reduces the energy consumption associated with complex generative models like Diffusion Policies, making advanced control strategies viable for power-constrained space platforms. The reported energy consumption, approximately 36.45% of traditional continuous diffusion models, indicates a substantial practical advantage for extended mission durations and operational flexibility.

The introduction of the State-Dependent Latency Injection (SDLI) mechanism is also notable. This mechanism enhances the SNN's ability to perceive dynamic spatiotemporal features, which is crucial for precise interaction with free-floating objects in microgravity. Its contribution to improving success rates in high-precision tasks, as shown in the ablation study, suggests a potential pathway for improving the adaptability and fine-grained control capabilities of neuromorphic systems in dynamic environments.

The framework's high success rates across diverse intra-vehicular tasks, ranging from hatch opening to precision container capping and long-horizon sequential operations, indicates its potential applicability to a broad spectrum of astronaut assistance and mission efficiency tasks. This could lead to a reduction in astronaut workload, improved safety, and enhanced productivity for future space missions.

While the current evaluations are based on simulations, the theoretical energy savings and demonstrated performance suggest a strong foundation for future hardware implementations. The authors identify future work in evaluating the algorithm on physical neuromorphic hardware and extending the framework to more complex scenarios like bimanual coordination and soft-object manipulation, which could further broaden its utility for advanced on-orbit servicing missions. This research contributes to the growing field of embodied intelligence for space applications, advocating for neuromorphic computing as a viable solution for intelligent, energy-efficient robotics beyond Earth.
