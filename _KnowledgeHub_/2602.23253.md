# SPARR: Simulation-based Policies with Asymmetric Real-world Residuals for Assembly

> [!summary] Summary
> The SPARR framework bridges the sim-to-real gap in robotic assembly by combining a simulation-trained base policy with a vision-conditioned real-world residual policy, achieving 95-100% success rates on diverse tasks without requiring human supervision during real-world adaptation. This hybrid approach significantly improves real-world performance and robustness against pose estimation errors and object displacements.

## Problem

- Simulation-trained robotic assembly policies often suffer significant performance degradation (sim-to-real gap) when deployed in physical environments, leading to moderate success rates below industrial requirements.
- Current real-world reinforcement learning (RL) methods for contact-rich assembly tasks are heavily reliant on human expertise for demonstrations, supervision, and interventions, limiting their scalability and autonomy.
- Robotic assembly solutions need robustness to real-world variations such as part pose uncertainties, manufacturing tolerances, and sensor noise, which existing approaches often struggle to handle.

## Method

- Pre-trains a state-based base policy in simulation using PPO and dense imitation rewards, employing domain randomization to establish a strong initial behavioral prior.
- Deploys the base policy in the real world to autonomously generate demonstrations for a residual policy by injecting Gaussian noise, eliminating the need for human expert demonstrations.
- Learns a vision-conditioned residual policy directly in the real world using the RLPD algorithm, combining sparse rewards with a dynamically updated demonstration buffer to correct base policy actions and adapt to real-world discrepancies and visual cues.

## Results

- SPARR achieved 95-100% success rates on 10 diverse assembly tasks from the AutoMate dataset, demonstrating a 38.4% relative improvement over AutoMate and performance comparable to human-supervised HIL-SERL, but without any human intervention.
- The framework showed enhanced robustness to state noise and socket displacements, outperforming the base policy by 38.6% and state-based residual policies by 20.8% on average across varying physical socket positions.
- SPARR generalized to three unseen NIST assembly tasks, achieving up to 100% success for rectangular peg insertion by correcting rotational mismatches and showing a 74.5% relative improvement in success rate and 36.5% reduction in cycle time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid approach, combining a strong simulation-trained base policy with a real-world vision-conditioned residual policy, can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap and achieve industrial-grade assembly success rates without human supervision.
> - Visual observations in the real-world residual policy are crucial for enhancing robustness to real-world variations like pose estimation errors and physical displacements, providing vital information not captured by state-based simulation.
> - Autonomous demonstration generation by the base policy and the use of a dynamic demonstration buffer significantly improve data efficiency and learning performance for sparse reward real-world adaptation.
