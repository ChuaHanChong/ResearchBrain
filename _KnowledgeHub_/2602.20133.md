# AdaEvolve: Adaptive LLM Driven Zeroth-Order Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaEvolve, an adaptive framework developed at UC Berkeley, reframes LLM-driven program evolution as a hierarchical optimization problem, employing an "accumulated improvement signal" to dynamically manage search processes. The framework achieved state-of-the-art results on 4 out of 6 mathematical optimization tasks, surpassing human SOTA on Circle Packing and consistently outperforming baselines across 185 diverse problems.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-guided search systems use static schedules and fixed hyperparameters, leading to computational waste and premature stagnation in discrete, non-differentiable search spaces.
- These systems lack adaptability to the non-stationary dynamics of evolutionary search, often getting trapped in local optima and requiring extensive manual tuning for each new problem.
- The semantic capabilities of LLMs are underutilized for overarching search control and strategic redirection beyond simple program mutations.

## Method

- AdaEvolve introduces a ==hierarchical adaptive optimization framework== that uses an =="accumulated improvement signal"== to unify and coordinate adaptation across three levels of control.
- It dynamically modulates exploration/exploitation within islands, allocates global computational resources to the most productive islands via a ==multi-armed bandit approach== with globally normalized rewards, and spawns new islands during stagnation.
- A "System 2" ==meta-LLM== is triggered when global stagnation persists to generate high-level "solution tactics" which are then injected into mutation prompts to shift search strategy.

## Results

- AdaEvolve consistently outperformed open-sourced baselines (OpenEvolve, GEPA, ShinkaEvolve) across all categories of **185** diverse problems, including mathematical optimization, systems benchmarks, and Frontier-CS tasks.
- It achieved state-of-the-art results in **4** of **6** mathematical optimization problems, notably surpassing human SOTA on Circle Packing (Square, N=26) with a score of **2.636** (vs. **2.634**).
- Comprehensive ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of all three adaptive control levels, with meta-guidance proving most impactful for enabling breakthroughs by breaking conceptual stalemates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An "accumulated improvement signal," reflecting the productivity of the search trajectory, can effectively drive multi-level adaptive control in zeroth-order optimization.
> - Hierarchical adaptation, encompassing local search intensity, global resource allocation, and meta-level strategy shifts, is crucial for breaking both numerical and conceptual local optima in complex search spaces.
> - Globally normalizing bandit rewards for island selection prevents bias towards trivial improvements on low-performing islands, ensuring resources are directed towards globally significant progress.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

AdaEvolve addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and automated algorithm discovery/optimization. The broader research landscape for LLMs has seen a significant shift from focusing solely on scaling model parameters during training to scaling inference-time computation. This shift acknowledges that reasoning performance can substantially improve by expending more compute during search, moving from one-shot generation to sequential decision-making processes.

Existing methods for scaling inference compute, such as Beam Search, Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), or Tree-of-Thoughts, often treat the LLM as a static operator. They use fixed sampling temperatures and rigid prompt templates, struggling to maintain diverse, long-horizon context or effectively leverage the LLM's capacity for semantic feedback. These approaches typically discard "failed" attempts, losing valuable information about the search landscape.

In response to these limitations, LLM-guided Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) have emerged as a dominant strategy for complex program generation, combinatorial optimization, and algorithmic discovery. Pioneering works like FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024) and AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., 2025) demonstrated LLMs acting as sophisticated semantic mutation operators, capable of navigating discrete, non-differentiable search spaces far more effectively than traditional EAs with random syntactic mutations. Open-source frameworks like OpenEvolve (Sharma, 2025) and ShinkaEvolve (Lange et al., 2025) further democratized these approaches.

However, a critical disparity persists: while the LLM mutation operator is highly sophisticated and context-aware, the underlying search algorithms controlling it often remain surprisingly simple and static. These canonical systems typically rely on pre-determined schedules and fixed hyperparameters (e.g., mutation rates, population sizes, exploration strategies). This rigidity leads to several problems: developers must manually tune these parameters for each new problem, search processes can easily stagnate in local optima if parameters are too conservative, or fail to refine solutions if too aggressive. The lack of adaptation to the non-stationary dynamics of evolutionary search results in significant computational waste.

AdaEvolve directly addresses this gap. It draws an insightful analogy to adaptive gradient methods in continuous optimization (like AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam), which dynamically adjust learning rates based on gradient moments. AdaEvolve extends this concept to the zeroth-order, gradient-free domain of LLM-based program generation. By using the trajectory of fitness improvements as a signal analogous to gradient magnitudes, it introduces a principled, hierarchical adaptive framework. This positions AdaEvolve as a significant advancement in making LLM-guided evolutionary search more autonomous, robust, and computationally efficient, moving beyond the brittle, human-tuned static schedules of prior art. It aims to elevate the level of adaptation within the optimization hierarchy, allowing the system to self-regulate exploration-exploitation dynamics and generate meta-level guidance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AdaEvolve is to introduce a novel framework that reformulates LLM-driven program evolution as a *hierarchical adaptive optimization problem*. The overarching goal is to overcome the limitations of current LLM-guided search systems, which suffer from static schedules and rigid control policies, leading to computational waste and premature stagnation.

The key motivations for developing AdaEvolve stem from several critical observations about existing LLM-driven evolutionary frameworks:

1. **Computational Waste from Static Schedules:** Current systems allocate computational resources indiscriminately, even when a population of solution candidates is stagnating. This leads to inefficient use of expensive LLM calls, as resources are not dynamically re-routed to more promising search frontiers.
2. **Lack of Adaptability to Non-Stationary Dynamics:** Evolutionary search is a dynamic process. A fixed exploration-exploitation balance, mutation rate, or prompt structure, determined at the start, cannot effectively respond to changes in the search landscape (e.g., periods of rapid progress followed by plateaus). This rigidity often traps the search in local optima.
3. **High Manual Tuning Burden:** Developers are often required to manually tune hyperparameters (e.g., exploration rates, population sizes, specific "solution tactics") for each new problem, or even intervene during a run (as exemplified by OpenEvolve's need for human-triggered "refinement" configurations). This makes the process labor-intensive and non-generalizable.
4. **Underutilization of LLM's Semantic Capabilities:** While LLMs are used as sophisticated semantic mutation operators, the overarching search control mechanism often fails to leverage the LLM's capacity for context-aware feedback and strategic redirection beyond simple program mutations.
5. **Stagnation in Conceptual Local Optima:** When numerical adaptation fails, it often implies that the core algorithmic approach is flawed. Existing systems lack a mechanism to trigger a meta-level shift in strategy, leading to persistent stagnation.

AdaEvolve aims to address these motivations by:
- Dynamically modulating exploration intensity within subpopulations.
- Intelligently allocating global computational resources across different subpopulations.
- Generating novel, high-level solution tactics when progress stalls at a fundamental level.
- Unifying these adaptive decisions under a single, data-driven "accumulated improvement signal."
- Achieving robust performance across a diverse set of optimization problems with minimal user configuration.

Ultimately, the objective is to create a more autonomous, efficient, and robust LLM-guided evolutionary system that can consistently discover high-quality solutions across a broad range of challenging algorithmic and systems optimization tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaEvolve reformulates LLM-driven evolution as a hierarchical adaptive optimization problem. Its core innovation lies in using an "accumulated improvement signal" ($G_t$) to unify and coordinate adaptation across three distinct levels of control, thereby replacing ad-hoc heuristics with a cohesive adaptive optimizer. The framework requires minimal user input, only the LLM name and total iteration count, handling all other configurations dynamically.

**Core Mechanism: The Accumulated Improvement Signal ($G_t$)**

For each island (subpopulation) $k$, AdaEvolve tracks $G_t^{(k)}$, an exponential moving average of squared normalized improvements. When a new program $p'$ is generated with fitness $f'$, the normalized improvement $\delta_t^{(k)}$ is calculated as $\max(\frac{f' - f_k^*}{f_k^*}, 0)$, where $f_k^*$ is the current best fitness of island $k$. This signal is scale-invariant and reflects the productivity or "volatility" of the search trajectory: high $G_t^{(k)}$ indicates productive gains, while low $G_t^{(k)}$ signifies convergence or stagnation.

**Hierarchical Adaptive Control:**

1. **Level 1: Local Adaptation (Within Island Exploration Intensity)**
 - **Goal:** Dynamically balance exploration (searching new regions) and exploitation (refining promising solutions) within each island.
 - **Mechanism:** The accumulated improvement signal $G_t^{(k)}$ is used to continuously modulate an exploration intensity $I_t^{(k)} \in [I_{\min}, I_{\max}]$.
 - **Logic:** If $G_t^{(k)}$ is high (island is productive), $I_t^{(k)}$ decreases towards $I_{\min}$, favoring exploitation. If $G_t^{(k)}$ is low (island is stagnating), $I_t^{(k)}$ increases towards $I_{\max}$, encouraging exploration.
 - **Implementation:** At each iteration, the search mode (exploration or exploitation) is sampled stochastically based on $I_t^{(k)}$. Exploration involves uniform random parent selection and prompts encouraging orthogonal solutions, while exploitation samples parents proportionally to fitness and prompts for refinement.

2. **Level 2: Global Adaptation (Across Island Resource Allocation)**
 - **Goal:** Allocate the global computational budget (LLM calls) to the most productive islands.
 - **Mechanism:** This is modeled as a multi-armed bandit problem, where each island is an "arm."
 - **Novelty: Globally Normalized Rewards:** To avoid "poor island bias" (where trivial improvements on low-performing islands receive disproportionate credit), AdaEvolve normalizes bandit rewards by the *global best fitness* ($f_{\text{global}}^*$) rather than each island's local best. The reward $r_t^{(k)}$ for an improvement on island $k$ is $\frac{f' - f_k^*}{f_{\text{global}}^*}$. This ensures resources are directed towards globally significant progress.
 - **Island Selection:** Decayed cumulative rewards $R^{(k)}$ and decayed visit counts $V^{(k)}$ are maintained for each island. Islands are selected using an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) approach, balancing exploitation of currently productive islands with exploration of less-visited ones.
 - **Migration:** Every $M$ iterations, top programs are exchanged between islands via ring migration to propagate good solutions and maintain diversity. Migrated programs update the receiving island's local best and $G_t$ but do not affect UCB statistics.
 - **Dynamic Island Spawning:** When $G_t^{(k)} \le \tau_S$ for *all* islands (indicating widespread stagnation), a new island is dynamically created, initialized with a randomly sampled seed program from the archive to explore entirely new solution avenues.

3. **Level 3: Meta-Guidance (Solution Tactics Generation)**
 - **Goal:** Break out of conceptual local optima when numerical adaptation (Levels 1 and 2) proves insufficient. This implies the underlying algorithmic strategy is suboptimal.
 - **Mechanism:** When global stagnation persists (i.e., $G_t^{(k)} \le \tau_M$ for all islands), a "System 2" meta-analysis is triggered.
 - **Process:** A *separate LLM* is invoked to analyze the problem specification, evaluator code, and recent failed attempts. This meta-LLM generates high-level "solution tactics" (e.g., "switch from greedy to dynamic programming," "use constrained minimization with SLSQP").
 - **Injection:** These tactics are then injected into the mutation prompts for subsequent LLM generations, forcing a qualitative shift in the search direction. If a tactic fails, alternatives are tried, or new tactics are generated.

The three levels operate at different timescales, with local intensity adapting every iteration, island selection operating across iterations, and meta-guidance triggered intermittently upon sustained global stagnation. This multi-layered adaptive control system allows AdaEvolve to autonomously regulate the search process effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AdaEvolve demonstrates strong, consistent performance improvements over existing LLM-guided evolutionary baselines across a remarkably diverse and extensive set of 185 optimization and algorithm discovery problems.

1. **Consistent Outperformance Across Diverse Benchmarks:**
 - AdaEvolve consistently outperforms strong open-sourced baselines, including OpenEvolve, GEPA, and ShinkaEvolve, across all problem categories.
 - The performance gains are robust across different LLM backbones (GPT-5 and Gemini-3-Pro), indicating that the adaptive control mechanism itself is the primary driver of improvement, rather than backbone-specific tuning.

2. **Mathematical Optimization Benchmarks (6 problems):**
 - AdaEvolve achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) results, matching or exceeding the best known human or prior AI solutions (including proprietary models like AlphaEvolve) in 4 out of 6 mathematical optimization tasks.
 - For instance, on the Circle Packing (Square, N=26) problem, AdaEvolve reaches 2.636, surpassing the Human SOTA (2.634) and AlphaEvolve (2.635).
 - Significant gains are observed on problems with deceptive fitness landscapes (e.g., Heilbronn Triangle, MinMaxDist), where fixed-policy baselines often plateau prematurely.

3. **ADRS Systems Benchmarks (7 problems):**
 - AdaEvolve achieves the strongest aggregate performance, winning on all seven real-world systems optimization tasks.
 - This is particularly notable for tasks characterized by sparse or bursty improvements (e.g., TXN, CBL), where fixed strategies struggle to reallocate resources after stagnation. The adaptive nature of AdaEvolve allows it to navigate these challenges effectively.
 - Even on tasks with smoother reward signals (Prism, LLM-SQL), AdaEvolve matches or slightly improves upon the strongest fixed baselines, demonstrating that adaptivity does not harm performance when static strategies are already well-aligned.

4. **Frontier-CS Benchmark (172 problems):**
 - On this challenging suite of open-ended computer science problems, AdaEvolve significantly outperforms all baselines. It achieves a mean score of 61.33 and a median of 75.15, compared to OpenEvolve's mean of 50.75 (median 56.37) and GPT-5's single-call mean of 20.64 (median 0).
 - The median score of 0 for a single GPT-5 call highlights the necessity of sophisticated search algorithms like AdaEvolve for these complex problems.

5. **ARC-AGI-2 Tasks (exploratory analysis):**
 - In an exploratory analysis on abstract and compositional reasoning tasks, AdaEvolve showed performance gains (e.g., 49% vs. 42% accuracy for GPT-5 backbone) even though these tasks are not explicitly designed for evolutionary search. This suggests a broader robustness and generalization capability beyond traditional optimization settings.

6. **Ablation Studies:**
 - Comprehensive ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of each adaptive component:
 - Disabling Level 1 (Local Adaptation) led to performance degradation, especially for Circle Packing.
 - Disabling Level 2 (Adaptive Island Selection) by using round-robin or fixed island counts also reduced performance, notably for Signal Processing, indicating the value of dynamic resource allocation.
 - Disabling Level 3 (Meta-Guidance) resulted in the most significant performance drops across both tested problems (Circle Packing and Signal Processing), underscoring its crucial role in breaking out of conceptual local minima.

7. **Case Studies:**
 - Detailed case studies on Signal Processing and Circle Packing visually demonstrate how AdaEvolve's adaptive components respond to search dynamics.
 - In Signal Processing, the framework adapts from exploration to refinement, and Meta-Guidance introduces novel smoothing operators when numerical methods saturate.
 - In Circle Packing, early random initialization is followed by local refinement. When progress stalls, Meta-Guidance injects an "optimization-based refinement tactic" (using SLSQP), leading to significant breakthroughs and near-optimal layouts, where runs without meta-guidance remained stuck.

In summary, AdaEvolve consistently delivers superior performance by effectively mitigating stagnation, efficiently allocating resources, and adaptively shifting search strategies based on a unified improvement signal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AdaEvolve represents a significant leap forward in the field of LLM-guided program synthesis and zeroth-order optimization, with profound implications across several domains:

1. **Advancement in Autonomous AI Agents:** By introducing a truly adaptive and hierarchical control system, AdaEvolve moves beyond static, human-tuned evolutionary strategies. It enables LLM-driven agents to autonomously regulate their search behavior, making them more intelligent and self-sufficient in discovering novel algorithms and solutions. This is a critical step towards more general and robust AI discovery systems.

2. **Increased Efficiency and Reduced Computational Waste:** The dynamic allocation of computational resources, guided by the globally normalized improvement signal, ensures that expensive LLM calls are directed towards the most promising avenues. This efficiency is crucial for tackling complex, real-world problems where LLM inference costs can be substantial, making LLM-guided search more practical and scalable.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** AdaEvolve's ability to consistently outperform baselines across 185 diverse problems – spanning mathematical optimization, complex systems design, and open-ended algorithmic challenges – highlights its robustness. The minimal configuration requirement (only LLM name and iteration budget) signifies a major step towards generalizable, "plug-and-play" LLM-guided optimization frameworks that can adapt to entirely new problem contexts without extensive human tuning.

4. **Breaking Stagnation and Escaping Local Optima:** The hierarchical nature of AdaEvolve, particularly the Meta-Guidance level, provides a principled mechanism for escaping both numerical and *conceptual* local optima. The ability to generate and inject high-level algorithmic tactics offers a powerful way to redirect search when iterative refinements become unproductive, enabling breakthroughs that fixed-policy systems cannot achieve. This is particularly impactful for deceptive search landscapes.

5. **Accelerating Scientific and Algorithmic Discovery:** By matching or exceeding human and proprietary AI (AlphaEvolve) benchmarks in complex domains, AdaEvolve has the potential to significantly accelerate discovery in scientific research and engineering. It can help researchers quickly explore vast solution spaces for new algorithms, optimal system configurations, or efficient code implementations, thereby pushing the boundaries of what automated tools can achieve.

6. **Democratization of LLM-based Program Generation:** The reduction in manual configuration complexity lowers the barrier to entry for a wider range of users to leverage the power of LLM-guided evolution. Researchers and practitioners in various fields, who may not be experts in evolutionary algorithm tuning, can more easily apply sophisticated LLM capabilities to their specific optimization challenges.

7. **Inspiration for Future Adaptive Systems:** The novel concept of using a unified "accumulated improvement signal" to drive multi-level adaptation can inspire similar adaptive control mechanisms in other areas of AI, such as reinforcement learning agents, multi-agent systems, or even self-improving meta-learning frameworks.

In conclusion, AdaEvolve makes a fundamental contribution by synergizing the semantic power of LLMs with sophisticated, data-driven adaptive control. It transforms LLM-guided evolution from a brittle, hand-tuned process into a robust, autonomous, and highly efficient search paradigm, poised to significantly impact automated discovery and problem-solving across various scientific and engineering disciplines.
