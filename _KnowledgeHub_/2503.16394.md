# Do Visual Imaginations Improve Vision-and-Language Navigation Agents?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Oregon State University investigate how visual imaginations, synthesized from natural language instructions using text-to-image models, can enhance vision-and-language navigation agents. Integrating these imaginations improves success rates on benchmarks like R2R by up to 2.0 points and REVERIE by 1.3 points, providing more robust visual grounding.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents often struggle with robust visual grounding, implicitly associating language with visual referents, especially for novel or out-of-distribution landmarks.
- The learned cross-modal alignment in standard VLN methods can degrade during downstream training, making the agents brittle when encountering new visual concepts.
- Agents lack an explicit mechanism to bridge abstract linguistic descriptions of sub-goals with diverse visual observations in complex, unseen environments.

## Method

- A pipeline generates 'visual imaginations' by segmenting navigation instructions, filtering for visually referable noun phrases, and feeding them into a text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL).
- These imagination embeddings are integrated into existing VLN agents (e.g., HAMT, DUET) by concatenating them with instruction embeddings for early fusion into the policy network.
- An auxiliary cosine similarity loss explicitly aligns imagination embeddings with their corresponding linguistic noun phrase embeddings during a multi-stage finetuning process, alongside the base agent's loss.

## Results

- Integrating imaginations improved HAMT's Success Rate (SR) on R2R val-unseen by ~1.0 point and DUET's SR on the R2R test set by 2.0 points, alongside lower Navigation Error (NE).
- On the REVERIE val-unseen split, DUET-Imagine achieved a 1.3-point increase in SR and a 0.82-point increase in Remote Grounding Success (RGS), demonstrating benefits for coarse-grained navigation.
- Providing sequential imaginations for multiple landmarks along a path yielded greater improvements (1.0 SR over baseline) compared to providing an imagination only for the final goal (0.5 SR over baseline).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual imaginations serve as effective navigational cues, directly bridging abstract language to concrete visual representations and improving agents' ability to ground referents.
> - Training with imaginations provides a valuable regularization effect, enhancing the agent's overall performance even when imaginations are not explicitly used during inference.
> - Leveraging the vast visual knowledge encoded in modern text-to-image diffusion models allows for the synthesis of open-distribution visual concepts, simplifying the semantic matching task for VLN agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN)**, a challenging embodied AI task where an agent must navigate an unseen environment based on natural language instructions. VLN agents typically rely on learning complex cross-modal alignments between linguistic descriptions and visual observations to infer navigational actions.

Historically, VLN research has progressed through several stages:
- **Early approaches** often utilized recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for sequential decision-making.
- **More recent advancements** have seen the rise of transformer architectures, such as HAMT and DUET, which excel at encoding complex spatial, temporal, and cross-modal dependencies.
- **Data scaling techniques** have also played a crucial role, involving augmenting environments with synthetic instructions or expanding to larger datasets like HM3D and Gibson.
- **Emerging trends** include the integration of pre-trained large language models (LLMs) for zero-shot or few-shot VLN, though these often incur significant computational costs.

This paper tackles a fundamental challenge in VLN: **robust visual grounding**—the ability to correctly associate linguistic descriptions (e.g., "pool table," "kitchen") with their visual referents in the environment. Existing methods typically learn this implicitly through extensive pre-training on image caption datasets, but this learned alignment can degrade during downstream VLN training, especially for novel or out-of-distribution landmarks.

The authors contrast their work with several related approaches:
- **ADAPT**: This method augments VLN agents with visual observations retrieved from a training set based on language-observation pairs. The key distinction is that ADAPT's visual base is limited to training environments, whereas this paper's "imaginations" are *synthesized* using text-to-image models, allowing for an *open distribution* of visual concepts and the visualization of novel objects or scenarios not present in the training data (e.g., "kitchen with blue walls").
- **LAD (Layout-aware Dreamer)**: LAD uses generative modules to predict layouts and goals within a topological graph for coarse-grained instructions. This work differentiates itself by being model-agnostic, supporting sequential imaginations of multiple landmarks along a path (not just the goal), and employing early fusion of imagination and language embeddings, in contrast to LAD's architecture-specific, goal-focused late fusion.
- **Image Generation in VLN**: Previous works like Pathdreamer and PanoGen have explored generative models to predict future visual observations or augment datasets. However, these methods either do not condition on instruction text, focus on next-step predictions, or use generated imagery purely for data augmentation rather than as an active input modality during inference, as proposed in this paper. VLN-Sig predicts quantized features of future visual observations, relying on the agent's learned predictive capabilities rather than external text-to-image models.
- **Image Generation in Robot Control**: Other works leverage text-to-image models for tabletop manipulation tasks to synthesize goals or sub-goals. This paper distinguishes VLN from these settings by highlighting the challenge of operating in unconstrained, partially observed environments where sub-goals are often yet-to-be-seen landmarks rather than edits to existing observations.

In essence, this work introduces a novel paradigm by **explicitly leveraging the powerful generative capabilities of modern text-to-image diffusion models to create "visual imaginations" of linguistic landmarks** *before* navigation. These imaginations serve as direct visual cues, providing a concrete bridge between abstract language and diverse visual scenes, thereby augmenting the agent's understanding and improving its navigation performance. It represents a significant step towards enabling VLN agents to better handle visual ambiguity and generalize to novel concepts by pre-computing visual referents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether providing visual imagery corresponding to described landmarks can improve the performance of agents engaged in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks. The authors aim to determine if these "visual imaginations" of sub-goals, synthesized from natural language instructions, can act as effective navigational cues.

The motivation behind this investigation stems from several key observations and hypotheses:
- **Addressing Visual Grounding Weakness:** Standard VLN agents rely on implicitly associating noun phrases with their visual referents through learned cross-modal alignment mechanisms. However, this process can be brittle, especially for out-of-distribution landmarks or after extensive downstream training where these capabilities might degrade. The explicit generation of visual imaginations aims to reinforce this visual grounding.
- **Leveraging Text-to-Image Model Knowledge:** Modern text-to-image diffusion models possess vast and diverse visual knowledge, learned from web-scale datasets. The authors hypothesize that leveraging this broad knowledge to synthesize relevant imagery might make the semantic matching task (imagination-to-observation) easier for VLN agents than solely relying on implicit language-to-vision grounding.
- **Providing Explicit Cues:** Synthesized images offer known correspondences to specific noun phrases in instructions. This explicit link can be exploited through auxiliary losses during training, ensuring that the agent directly associates the generated visual cue with its linguistic origin, thereby strengthening visual understanding.
- **Pivot Between Modalities:** Visual imaginations can serve as a convenient "pivot" between language and environmental observations. By converting linguistic references into visual representations, the task of matching language to vision can be rephrased as an image-to-image matching problem, potentially simplifying the grounding process, especially for novel objects.
- **Inspiration from Cognitive Science:** The work draws inspiration from cognitive science, particularly dual-coding theory, which suggests that concrete imagery can be more effective for certain types of concept learning compared to linguistic stimuli alone. Studies on human and animal cognition also indicate that mentally imagining environmental snapshots or potential trajectories can improve decision-making in navigation.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond purely implicit visual grounding in VLN by integrating explicit, high-fidelity visual representations of instructional landmarks, thereby enhancing agent performance, robustness, and generalization capabilities in complex navigation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology consists of two primary components: (1) a pipeline for generating visual imaginations from navigation instructions, and (2) a model-agnostic approach to integrating these imaginations into existing VLN agents.

#### 3.1. Visual Imagination Generation Pipeline

The process begins with a natural language navigation instruction, which is typically composed of a sequence of sub-instructions.
1. **Instruction Segmentation:** The authors utilize FG-R2R to segment instructions into individual sub-instructions. For instance, "Go straight, take a left at the pool table to enter the kitchen. Walk to the bedroom and stop" would be segmented into phrases like "Go straight," "take a left at the pool table," "to enter the kitchen," etc.
2. **Sub-instruction Filtering Scheme:** Not all sub-instructions contain meaningful visual landmark references. Therefore, a filtering step is applied:
 - Sub-instructions lacking noun phrases (identified using SpaCy) are ignored.
 - Noun phrases are further filtered by a manual blacklist to exclude uninformative terms such as counts ("one"), directions ("left"), or ambiguous pronouns ("it"), ensuring that only specific, visually-referable landmarks are targeted.
3. **Visual Imagination Generation:** The filtered sub-instructions are then fed to a text-conditioned diffusion model, specifically **SDXL**. To ensure the generated images (imaginations) are relevant to indoor navigation environments (like Matterport3D), positive prompts ("indoor," "house," "realistic," "real estate") and negative prompts ("outdoor," "text," "humans," "collage") are used.
4. **Dataset Creation:** This pipeline is applied to the entire R2R dataset, resulting in the **R2R-Imagine dataset**, comprising over 41,000 synthesized imaginations.

#### 3.2. Model-Agnostic Integration with Existing Models

The authors demonstrate their integration method on two representative VLN architectures: **HAMT** and **DUET**. The approach involves an imagination encoder, an auxiliary alignment loss, and a multi-stage finetuning regimen.

1. **Imagination Encoder and Integration:**
 - Each generated imagination image (Z_i) is independently encoded using a pre-trained **Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16)**.
 - The resulting d-dimensional embedding is then passed through a **three-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)**.
 - A modality-specific **type embedding (t_Im)** for imaginations is added to the ViT output before the MLP transformation. The output of the MLP is the final imagination embedding (h_i).
 - These imagination embeddings (H), representing a sequence of visual cues, are **concatenated with the instruction embeddings** (from the agent's text encoder f_T(W)). This combined representation is then fed into the VLN agent's cross-modal policy network (f_X) alongside visual observations. This ensures early fusion of visual imaginations with linguistic context.

2. **Auxiliary Alignment Loss (L_cos):**
 - To explicitly encourage alignment between imaginations and their corresponding linguistic descriptions, an auxiliary loss is introduced.
 - For each sub-instruction (S_i) with a generated imagination (Z_i), the mean embedding vector of the noun phrase tokens within S_i (from the text encoder, denoted as ¯S_i) is computed.
 - A **cosine similarity loss** is calculated between the imagination embedding (h_i) and the mean noun phrase embedding (¯S_i).
 - This loss (L_cos) is added to the base agent's existing loss (L_base) during finetuning, weighted by a scaling factor λ (empirically set to 0.5): L_overall = L_base + λL_cos.

3. **Finetuning Regimen:**
 - To mitigate catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained base agent, a three-stage finetuning process is employed:
 1. **Stage 1:** Only the newly introduced MLP imagination encoder and type embeddings are trained, with all other base agent parameters frozen.
 2. **Stage 2:** All modules are trained jointly, but with a decreased learning rate for the base model parameters.
 3. **Stage 3:** All parameters are trained at a common learning rate.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** R2R (fine-grained instructions) and REVERIE (coarse-grained, goal-oriented instructions requiring object grounding) datasets built on the Matterport3D simulator.
- **Metrics:** Standard VLN metrics including Success Rate (SR), Success scaled by inverse Path Length (SPL), Navigation Error (NE), and Trajectory Length (TL). For REVERIE, Remote Grounding Success (RGS) and RGS penalized by path length (RGSPL) are also used.
- **Computational Resources:** Finetuning takes approximately 1.5 days on a Tesla V100 GPU with a batch size of 8 for 100k iterations. Imagination generation is an upfront cost.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a systematic study of the impact of visual imaginations, providing a robust framework for integration into diverse VLN architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate that integrating visual imaginations into Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents consistently leads to improved performance across various metrics and datasets.

Here are the main findings:

- **Improved VLN Agent Performance with Imaginations:**
 - On the R2R val-unseen split, HAMT-Imagine showed an increase of approximately 1.0 point in Success Rate (SR) and up to 0.5 points in Success scaled by inverse Path Length (SPL) compared to the baseline HAMT.
 - DUET-Imagine similarly improved on R2R val-unseen by about 0.6 SR.
 - For the DUET model, this improvement translated to a notable 2-point increase in SR on the R2R test set.
 - These gains were generally accompanied by lower Navigation Error (NE), indicating more precise navigation.

- **Effectiveness in Coarse-Grained Navigation (REVERIE):**
 - The proposed method also proved beneficial for coarse-grained instructions. On the REVERIE val-unseen split, DUET-Imagine achieved a 1.3-point increase in SR and a 0.82-point increase in Remote Grounding Success (RGS). This suggests that imaginations aid not only in reaching the goal but also in correctly identifying and grounding the target object within the scene.

- **Imaginations Provide Training Regularization and Require Alignment:**
 - **Regularization Effect:** When imaginations were "nullified" (attention masks set to zero) during test-time, VLN-Imagine agents still performed better than their baseline counterparts. This suggests that imagination-based training provides a valuable regularizing effect during fine-tuning, improving the overall agent even without direct imaginative input at inference.
 - **Importance of Alignment:** Feeding agents with randomly sampled (wrong) imaginations from the R2R-Imagine dataset led to worse performance than the baseline, emphasizing that semantic alignment between instructions and imaginations is crucial for benefit.

- **Sequential Imagination Outperforms Goal Imagination:**
 - Providing imaginations for a sequence of sub-instructions along the path (sequential imagination, as in the proposed method) yielded greater improvements (1.0 SR over baseline) compared to providing an imagination only for the final goal (0.5 SR over baseline). While goal imagination is useful, sequential imaginations appear to offer more comprehensive guidance throughout the trajectory.

- **General-Purpose Vision Encoders are Sufficient:**
 - Comparing an off-the-shelf, pre-trained ViT for encoding imaginations against a ViT fine-tuned on R2R navigation episodes showed comparable performance. This indicates that a readily available, general-purpose vision encoder is sufficient, promoting generality and avoiding potential issues like catastrophic forgetting.

- **Auxiliary Alignment Loss is Beneficial:**
 - The auxiliary cosine similarity loss (L_cos) designed to align imagination embeddings with their corresponding noun phrase embeddings contributed positively, resulting in a 0.5 SR and 0.4 SPL increase over training without any auxiliary loss.
 - Exploring an InfoNCE (contrastive) loss variant for alignment did not yield significantly different results compared to the simpler cosine similarity loss.

- **High Fidelity of Generated Imaginations:**
 - An evaluation using an open-vocabulary object detector (LangSAM) on the generated imaginations revealed high fidelity. For 98.78% of sub-instructions, at least one noun phrase was successfully detected in its corresponding imagination, and for 94.99%, all noun phrases were detected. This validates the quality and semantic relevance of the synthesized visual cues.

- **Qualitative Evidence of Imagination as a Pivot:**
 - Qualitative visualizations of attention patterns in HAMT-Imagine demonstrated that imaginations can act as a pivot between language tokens and visual observations. Specifically, imaginations attended strongly to observations of matching semantic concepts. They also helped in disambiguating similar concepts, for example, attending to a unicycle observation when the instruction referred to a "unicycle," even when a "bicycle" was also visible in the environment.

In conclusion, the results strongly support the hypothesis that visual imaginations serve as effective navigational cues, leading to more successful and accurate VLN agents. The benefits stem from improved visual grounding, a regularization effect during training, and the ability of imaginations to bridge the gap between abstract language and concrete visual observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and embodied AI, demonstrating a novel and effective method to enhance agent performance. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Visual Grounding and Understanding:** The most direct impact is the demonstrable improvement in visual grounding. By explicitly providing visual imaginations of landmarks referenced in instructions, the agents gain a more robust and direct understanding of what they are looking for, moving beyond purely implicit cross-modal learning. This is particularly valuable for handling novel or out-of-distribution objects, which are often challenging for traditional VLN models.
- **Leveraging Foundational Generative Models:** This work effectively bridges advanced text-to-image diffusion models (like SDXL) with embodied AI tasks. It showcases how the vast knowledge encoded in these foundational generative models can be strategically harnessed to provide tangible benefits for downstream tasks, opening avenues for similar integrations in other multimodal AI challenges.
- **Model-Agnostic and Generalizable Approach:** The proposed method is shown to be model-agnostic, improving the performance of two distinct VLN architectures (HAMT and DUET), and effective across different granularities of instructions (R2R's fine-grained vs. REVERIE's coarse-grained). This broad applicability suggests its potential for widespread adoption and integration into future VLN systems.
- **Inspired by Cognitive Science:** The work draws inspiration from cognitive science, specifically the role of mental imagery in human learning and navigation. By providing empirical evidence that "visual imaginations" improve AI agents, it not only advances AI but also potentially offers a new perspective for interdisciplinary research between AI and cognitive psychology.
- **Practical Implications for Robotics and Embodied AI:** Improved VLN capabilities have direct implications for real-world robotics. Agents that can better understand and act upon natural language commands, especially in unseen environments, are crucial for applications like personal assistants, service robots in complex spaces, and search-and-rescue operations. While current computational costs for imagination generation are noted, the pre-computation strategy makes it more feasible than real-time generative tasks.
- **Rich Avenues for Future Research:** The paper identifies several exciting future directions:
 - **Understanding Complementary Roles:** Further research into the precise interplay and complementary nature of visual imaginations and language instructions could lead to more optimized fusion strategies.
 - **Sim2Real Gap Bridging:** Imaginations, being ungrounded in specific environments, might offer a unique way to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap by providing abstract yet semantically rich visual cues that generalize better than environment-specific observations.
 - **World Models and Image-to-Image Reasoning:** Imaginations could unlock new capabilities for VLN world models, facilitating more sophisticated image-to-image reasoning for planning and prediction.
 - **Life-long Learning of Groundings:** Integrating imaginations with mechanisms for lifelong learning could address the challenge of persistent groundings for unique or personalized objects/locations.

While the increased computational cost of generating and encoding imaginations is a limitation, the upfront nature of this process makes it manageable. The primary limitation of imaginations not being "environment-grounded" (i.e., not reflecting the exact appearance or context of an object in a specific scene) is acknowledged, setting the stage for future work that might combine generative imagination with environment-specific fine-tuning.

Overall, this research successfully demonstrates the fundamental utility of integrating explicit visual imaginations into VLN agents, pushing the boundaries of multimodal understanding and robust navigation in complex environments.
