# Shikra: Unleashing Multimodal LLM's Referential Dialogue Magic

> [!summary] Summary
> Shikra introduces a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that integrates natural referential dialogue by processing and generating precise spatial coordinates directly within natural language. This model achieved state-of-the-art performance on PointQA tasks and demonstrated improved reasoning and reduced visual hallucinations through the use of grounded Chain of Thoughts.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs cannot engage in precise referential dialogue, lacking the ability to understand or generate responses tied to specific spatial regions within an image.
- Existing vision-language positioning tasks are fragmented, typically handling either input or output of positional information but not both flexibly and simultaneously within a natural dialogue.
- Prior MLLMs inherently lack precise spatial awareness, performing at random chance (25.96% accuracy) on tasks requiring absolute spatial localization, as shown by the 'chessboard test'.

## Method

- Shikra employs a simple, unified three-component architecture consisting of a ==frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder==, a single fully connected alignment layer, and a fine-tuned Vicuna-7B or Vicuna-13B LLM.
- ==Spatial coordinates (bounding boxes or points)== are represented as ==normalized numerical values== directly integrated into natural language sequences without requiring special tokens or architectural modifications.
- The model is trained on a comprehensive instruction dataset, which includes reorganized public vision-language datasets and new, high-quality ==referential dialogue data generated by GPT-4==, utilizing a two-stage tuning process.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on PointQA tasks, including Visual-7W and LookTwice-QA, demonstrating effective comprehension and utilization of positional inputs.
- Showed competitive performance on Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) tasks across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, as well as on general VQA and Image Captioning benchmarks (VQAv2, OK-VQA, COCO, Flickr30k).
- Demonstrated competitive performance in reducing object hallucination on the POPE evaluation pipeline, comparable to InstructBLIP and surpassing several other popular MLLMs like **MiniGPT-4** and **LLaVA**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating numerical positional annotations into Chain of Thoughts (Grounding CoT) significantly improves reasoning and reduces visual hallucinations, yielding a 5.9-point performance increase on the CLEVR dataset compared to direct answering.
> - Representing coordinates as natural language numerical values is more effective and flexible than using extra vocabulary tokens, offering greater precision control and eliminating the need for vocabulary modification.
> - Existing general MLLMs, despite coarse-grained vision-language alignment, do not inherently capture precise spatial positions, validating the need for direct referential dialogue capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

**Emergence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):**
The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has experienced rapid advancements in recent months, with models like Flamingo, BLIP-2, Mini-GPT4, LLaVA, and the proprietary GPT-4 demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating text based on visual inputs. These models have effectively given "eyes" to Large Language Models (LLMs), enabling users to engage in general conversations about images.

**Current MLLM Limitations and the "Referential Gap":**
Despite this progress, a significant limitation persists: current MLLMs cannot engage in precise referential dialogue. They can perceive image content broadly but lack the ability to understand or generate responses tied to specific spatial regions within an image. Users cannot explicitly point to areas of interest, nor can the models provide exact locations of described content in their replies. This absence of "Referential Dialogue" (RD)—the natural human ability to indicate and discuss specific regions within a scene—represents a crucial gap in achieving truly human-like multimodal interaction. The authors aptly term this a "referential gap," noting that it hinders numerous exciting applications.

**Existing Vision-Language Positioning Tasks:**
The paper acknowledges various existing vision-language tasks that involve localization, but often in a fragmented manner.
- **Tasks with output boxes:** Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and Described Object Detection aim to localize objects described by language. VQA Grounding associates answers with visual regions.
- **Tasks with input boxes:** Grounding Caption (GC), Referring Expression Generation (REG), and PointQA require models to generate descriptions or answer questions based on a given location (point or box).
The key distinction highlighted by Shikra is that these prior models typically handle *either* input *or* output of positional information, but not both flexibly and simultaneously within a natural dialogue. They often rely on specific architectures or vocabularies for localization.

**Shikra's Novelty and Unification:**
Shikra's contribution lies in its ability to fill this referential gap by creating a unified MLLM that can *naturally* handle spatial coordinate inputs and outputs in a continuous dialogue. Unlike previous approaches that might use separate modules, specific vocabularies, or pre-/post-detection steps for localization, Shikra integrates this capability directly into the MLLM framework using a simple, natural language numerical representation. This allows for a seamless, bidirectional referential interaction, positioning Shikra as a significant step towards more intuitive and precise human-AI communication in multimodal contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Shikra are clear and directly address the identified limitations of existing MLLMs:

1. **Addressing the Lack of Referential Dialogue:** The core motivation is to enable MLLMs to mimic human-like referential communication. Just as humans naturally point to objects or regions while speaking, Shikra aims to empower MLLMs to process and generate spatial coordinates within natural language. This capability is seen as essential for intuitive and efficient information exchange in multimodal contexts.

2. **Developing a Unified Model for Spatial and Linguistic Interaction:** Shikra seeks to create a single, generalist MLLM capable of handling both vision-language understanding and generation, *including* the input and output of precise spatial coordinates. This objective aims to move beyond fragmented approaches where different modules or pipelines are required for various localization tasks, integrating them all into a cohesive dialogue system.

3. **Achieving Simplicity and Generalizability:** A key design principle for Shikra is simplicity. The authors explicitly aim to achieve referential dialogue without introducing complex architectural modifications. This means avoiding extra vocabularies for coordinates, dedicated position encoders, pre-/post-detection modules, or external plug-in models. The goal is a "straightforward and simple" architecture that maintains the elegance and generalizability of existing MLLMs while adding a powerful new capability.

4. **Enabling Novel Applications and Enhancing Existing Ones:** By mastering referential dialogue, Shikra is motivated by the potential to unlock a plethora of exciting applications. These include integration with Mixed Reality (XR) headsets (like Apple Vision Pro) for contextual AI assistance, improved communication for visual robots, enhanced online shopping experiences where users can inquire about specific items, and more accurate visual reasoning through grounded chains of thought. The ability to provide spatial explanations alongside verbal answers is a crucial driver.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Shikra's methodology is characterized by its simplicity and unified approach to integrating spatial awareness into a standard MLLM architecture.

**Architecture Overview:**
Shikra employs a straightforward three-component architecture:
1. **Vision Encoder:** A pre-trained ViT-L/14 from CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) is used to extract visual features from input images. This leverages the robust visual understanding capabilities of CLIP.
2. **Alignment Layer:** A single fully connected layer maps the high-dimensional output embedding of the ViT (16x16x1024) to a dimension suitable for the LLM (e.g., 256xD, where D is 4,096 for Vicuna-7B or 5,120 for Vicuna-13B). This layer is responsible for aligning the visual features with the language model's input space.
3. **Large Language Model (LLM):** Vicuna-7B or Vicuna-13B serves as the core language model. Visual embeddings are flexibly inserted anywhere into the input sequence of the LLM.
Crucially, the architecture is designed to be unified and simple, without introducing any special vocabularies, position encoders, pre-/post-detection modules, or external plug-in models specifically for spatial coordinate handling. Both the fully connected layer and the entire language model are fine-tuned during training, while the visual encoder remains frozen.

**Numerical Representation of Position:**
A cornerstone of Shikra's design is its intuitive and natural language-based representation of spatial coordinates.
- **Format:** Bounding boxes are represented as `[xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax]`, and region center points as `[xcenter, ycenter]`.
- **Normalization:** All x and y coordinates are normalized to the image size, ranging from 0 to 1.
- **Precision:** By default, three decimal places are maintained for each number, allowing for precise localization.
- **Integration:** These numerical coordinate representations can appear anywhere within the input and output sequences, seamlessly blending with natural language. They are tokenized like regular text, without requiring any special tokens or modifications to the LLM's vocabulary. This flexibility enables expressions like "the jacket [0.268, 0.372]" within a sentence.

**Instruction Data Construction:**
To train Shikra for referential dialogue, a comprehensive and diverse instruction dataset was constructed from two main sources:
1. **Reorganization of Public Data:** Existing vision-language datasets were adapted to Shikra's referential format. This included:
 - VQA (VQAv2), Image Captioning (LLaVA-Pretraining).
 - Datasets with positional annotations: RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg for Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and Generation (REG); Visual Genome for Grounding Caption.
 - PointQA datasets: PointQA-Local/Twice and Visual-7W.
 - **New Task Forms:** The authors also defined "Spotting Captioning" (using Flickr30K Entities), where the model describes an image and explicitly spots mentioned objects or regions with points or boxes. This process ensured a wide range of tasks and data types for robust training. Test/validation images were meticulously excluded from the training set to prevent data leakage.
2. **GPT-4 Generated Referential Dialogue Data (Shikra-RD):** Recognizing the insufficiency of existing data for natural referential communication, the authors leveraged GPT-4 to generate high-quality RD annotations.
 - **Source:** Flickr30K Entities, which provides images with five descriptions and bounding box labels for mentioned objects.
 - **Generation Process:** Although GPT-4 could not "see" images, it was provided with the existing descriptions and bounding box formats. GPT-4 was then prompted to design diverse Question-Answer (Q&A) pairs where coordinate information could appear in both questions and answers, ensuring that answers could be derived from the provided information. This process yielded 5,922 QA pairs, forming the "Shikra-RD" dataset, designed to capture natural dialogue with spatial references.

**Task Prompts and Tuning Details:**
- **Variable Task Templates:** Shikra employs variable task templates for different types of tasks (e.g., spotting captioning, PointQA, REC). To ensure flexibility and prevent the model from becoming rigid, these templates were first described, then sample templates were written, and finally, GPT-4 was used to rewrite them into hundreds of diverse linguistic variations conveying the same meaning. During training, prompts are randomly selected from these variations.
- **Two-Stage Tuning:**
 1. **Stage 1:** Initial training on the reorganized public VL datasets (Section 5.3.1) for approximately 100,000 steps (1.5 epochs).
 2. **Stage 2:** The sampling ratio for LLaVA-Instruct-150K and the newly generated Shikra-RD data (Section 5.3.2) was increased to 50%.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a cosine annealing scheduler, an initial learning rate of 2e-5, and a global batch size of 64 were used. Training was conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Shikra's comprehensive evaluation demonstrates its efficacy in referential dialogue and its competitive performance on various vision-language tasks.

**Validation of Referential Gap: The Chessboard Test:**
To quantitatively assess whether prior MLLMs could inherently understand absolute spatial positions, the authors devised a "chessboard test." An image was divided into 2x2 parts, and LLaVA-13B was asked to identify which part a described object was in. LLaVA-13B achieved an accuracy of only 25.96%, comparable to random guessing (25%). This result strongly confirmed that existing MLLMs, despite coarse-grained vision-language alignment, lacked the capability to capture precise spatial positions, underscoring the necessity for Shikra's approach.

**Impact of Grounding Chain of Thoughts (GCoT):**
The paper explored whether incorporating positional annotations into Chain of Thoughts (CoT)—termed Grounding CoT (GCoT)—could improve performance and reduce visual hallucinations. Using the CLEVR dataset, three settings were compared:
- **Q→A (Question to Answer):** Direct answering.
- **Q→CA (Question to CoT then Answer):** Traditional verbal CoT, which surprisingly decreased performance compared to Q→A.
- **Q→C_Point_A (Question to Grounded CoT then Answer):** GCoT with center point annotations for mentioned objects. This setting yielded a significant performance improvement of 13 points over Q→CA and 5.9 points over Q→A.
This finding suggests that training with positional annotations effectively suppresses visual hallucination and enhances reasoning, highlighting GCoT as a promising direction.

**Comparison of Position Representations:**
The study also investigated the optimal way to represent coordinates:
- **Extra Vocabularies:** Similar to methods like Pix2seq, using dedicated tokens (e.g., `<bin_0>`, `<bin_1000>`).
- **Numerical Representation:** Shikra's approach of using natural language numerical values directly (e.g., `[0.123, 0.456...]`).
Experiments on REC tasks using RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg demonstrated that the numerical representation consistently achieved better results (e.g., 87.40% vs. 86.94% on RefCOCO test-A). Beyond performance, the numerical approach offers greater elegance, eliminates the need for vocabulary modification, and allows flexible precision control without retraining, despite requiring more tokens for dense objects.

**Quantitative Performance on Conventional VL Tasks:**

- **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC):** Shikra-7B and Shikra-13B showed promising performance on REC tasks across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg datasets. While specialist models or heavily fine-tuned generalist models still achieve higher scores, Shikra's results are competitive with other generalist VL models that perform multiple tasks without specific finetuning, demonstrating its strong grounding capabilities within a unified framework.

- **PointQA Capabilities:** Shikra achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on PointQA tasks, including Visual-7W and LookTwice-QA. These tasks, which require answering questions based on user-specified points or boxes, directly validate Shikra's ability to comprehend and utilize positional inputs effectively.

- **General VQA and Image Captioning:** When evaluated on conventional VL tasks that do not involve coordinates in input or output, such as VQA (VQAv2, OK-VQA) and Image Captioning (COCO, Flickr30k), Shikra achieved promising results, competitive with other SOTA generalist models. This indicates that integrating referential dialogue capabilities does not compromise its performance on broader VL tasks.

- **Object Hallucination Benchmarking:** Using the POPE evaluation pipeline, Shikra-7B demonstrated competitive performance in reducing object hallucination, comparable to InstructBLIP and significantly surpassing several other popular MLLMs (MiniGPT-4, LLaVA, MM-GPT, mPLUG-Owl). This further supports the finding that grounding mechanisms, like those in Shikra, can mitigate a common issue in MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Shikra represents a significant leap forward in the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models, offering far-reaching implications across various domains.

**Enabling Human-like Interaction:** The most profound impact of Shikra is its ability to facilitate natural referential dialogue. This bridges a critical gap in human-AI communication, moving beyond mere conversational understanding of images to precise, spatially grounded interactions. By allowing users to point and ask, and models to refer to specific regions in their replies, Shikra makes human-AI collaboration more intuitive, efficient, and aligned with human cognitive processes.

**Diverse Application Scenarios:** The introduction of referential dialogue unlocks a wide array of exciting practical applications:
- **Mixed Reality (XR) Headsets:** In environments like Apple Vision Pro, users could naturally interact with an AI assistant by pointing at objects in their field of view. The AI could then highlight relevant areas for clarification or response, creating a seamless, immersive experience.
- **Visual Robots:** Robots could better understand human instructions that include spatial references ("Pick up *this* [coordinates] object") and communicate their actions with precise localization ("I am now moving *to this area* [coordinates]").
- **Online Shopping:** Users could point to specific features of a product in an image and ask detailed questions, leading to a more engaging and informative shopping experience.
- **Scientific and Medical Imaging:** Researchers and clinicians could query specific regions in complex images, receiving grounded explanations and data.
- **Accessibility:** Enhancing visual descriptions for visually impaired users by providing precise spatial context for objects mentioned.

**Future Research Directions:** Shikra's work also opens new avenues for research:
- **Grounding Chain of Thoughts (GCoT):** The preliminary success of GCoT in reducing hallucination suggests a promising direction for more reliable and explainable MLLM reasoning. Further exploration into how spatial grounding can enhance complex reasoning is warranted.
- **Coordinate Representation Optimization:** While numerical representation is effective, further research could explore hybrid approaches or more efficient methods for dense object scenarios.
- **Multilingual Referential Dialogue:** Expanding Shikra's capabilities to support non-English languages would significantly broaden its global impact.

**Acknowledged Limitations:** The authors transparently acknowledge several limitations:
- **Language Support:** Shikra currently only supports English, limiting its accessibility for non-English speakers.
- **Dense Object Tasks:** The current numerical representation, while flexible, may be less suitable for tasks requiring dense object detection or segmentation due to the increased token count.
- **LLM Harms:** Like most LLMs, Shikra is susceptible to generating harmful or counterfactual responses, a general challenge in the field.

### 6. Conclusion

The paper "Shikra: Unleashing Multimodal LLM’s Referential Dialogue Magic" presents a compelling solution to a fundamental limitation in current Multimodal Large Language Models: the absence of natural referential dialogue. By introducing Shikra, a unified and straightforward MLLM capable of handling spatial coordinate inputs and outputs in natural language, the authors have significantly advanced the field of human-AI interaction.

Shikra's innovative use of numerical coordinates directly within natural language, without complex architectural additions, demonstrates that powerful new capabilities can be integrated elegantly. The rigorous evaluation, including the revealing "chessboard test" and the promising results on Grounding Chain of Thoughts, validates the model's core contributions. Quantitative results on a broad spectrum of vision-language tasks, from REC and PointQA to VQA and Image Captioning, confirm Shikra's generalist capabilities and its competitive performance. Furthermore, its ability to mitigate object hallucination highlights its robustness.

By enabling MLLMs to engage in precise, spatially grounded conversations, Shikra paves the way for a new generation of intelligent assistants in diverse applications such as Mixed Reality, robotics, and online commerce. While acknowledging limitations such as language support and dense object handling, Shikra represents a crucial step towards more intuitive, human-like, and contextually aware multimodal AI systems.
