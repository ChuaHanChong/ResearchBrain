# Shikra: Unleashing Multimodal LLM's Referential Dialogue Magic

> [!summary] Summary
> Shikra introduces a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that integrates natural referential dialogue by processing and generating precise spatial coordinates directly within natural language. This model achieved state-of-the-art performance on PointQA tasks and demonstrated improved reasoning and reduced visual hallucinations through the use of grounded Chain of Thoughts.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs cannot engage in precise referential dialogue, lacking the ability to understand or generate responses tied to specific spatial regions within an image.
- Existing vision-language positioning tasks are fragmented, typically handling either input or output of positional information but not both flexibly and simultaneously within a natural dialogue.
- Prior MLLMs inherently lack precise spatial awareness, performing at random chance (25.96% accuracy) on tasks requiring absolute spatial localization, as shown by the 'chessboard test'.

## Method

- Shikra employs a simple, unified three-component architecture consisting of a ==frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder==, a single fully connected alignment layer, and a fine-tuned Vicuna-7B or Vicuna-13B LLM.
- ==Spatial coordinates (bounding boxes or points)== are represented as ==normalized numerical values== directly integrated into natural language sequences without requiring special tokens or architectural modifications.
- The model is trained on a comprehensive instruction dataset, which includes reorganized public vision-language datasets and new, high-quality ==referential dialogue data generated by GPT-4==, utilizing a two-stage tuning process.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on PointQA tasks, including Visual-7W and LookTwice-QA, demonstrating effective comprehension and utilization of positional inputs.
- Showed competitive performance on Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) tasks across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, as well as on general VQA and Image Captioning benchmarks (VQAv2, OK-VQA, COCO, Flickr30k).
- Demonstrated competitive performance in reducing object hallucination on the POPE evaluation pipeline, comparable to InstructBLIP and surpassing several other popular MLLMs like **MiniGPT-4** and **LLaVA**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating numerical positional annotations into Chain of Thoughts (Grounding CoT) significantly improves reasoning and reduces visual hallucinations, yielding a 5.9-point performance increase on the CLEVR dataset compared to direct answering.
> - Representing coordinates as natural language numerical values is more effective and flexible than using extra vocabulary tokens, offering greater precision control and eliminating the need for vocabulary modification.
> - Existing general MLLMs, despite coarse-grained vision-language alignment, do not inherently capture precise spatial positions, validating the need for direct referential dialogue capabilities.
