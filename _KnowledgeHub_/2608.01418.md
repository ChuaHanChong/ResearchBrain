# Reusing Rollouts under Policy Lag: Prefix-Normalized Policy Optimization for LLM Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Prefix-Normalized Policy Optimization (PNPO) addresses policy lag in large language model reinforcement learning by efficiently reusing rollout data. The method improves mathematical reasoning performance on benchmarks by up to 3.00 percentage points over baselines and achieves similar final performance with only one-quarter of generated rollouts in high off-policy training regimes.

## Problem

- Autoregressive generation of rollout batches for large language models (LLMs) is computationally expensive, leading to the need for rollout reuse to improve training efficiency.
- Reusing rollouts introduces policy lag, causing the learner policy to diverge from the behavior policy and making existing proximal policy optimization (PPO) methods less effective due to their local approximations.
- Exact importance sampling using cumulative importance ratios (C_t) can lead to an 'unwieldy dynamic range' in log-weights, causing numerical instability or high variance.

## Method

- Introduces **Prefix-Normalized Policy Optimization (PNPO)**, a token-level objective that defines a ==prefix-normalized policy weight== as the geometric mean of likelihood ratios along the causal prefix.
- Applies an ==acceptance gate==, `M_i,t`, which dynamically scales its interval based on token position and response length to filter score terms, improving stability under off-policyness.
- Combines the prefix-normalized weight and acceptance gate with a ==group-relative advantage estimator== within a ==detached score-function surrogate objective==.

## Results

- PNPO achieved a macro Avg@32 of **50.24%** in a high-policy-lag regime (four PPO epochs), outperforming GSPO (**47.24%**) by **3.00 percentage points** and GRPO (**47.05%**) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- In the high-policy-lag setting, PNPO matched the final performance of one-epoch PNPO while utilizing only **one-quarter (150 vs. 600)** of the newly generated rollout batches, demonstrating enhanced rollout reuse efficiency.
- Four-epoch PNPO reached a mean rollout reward of **0.25** in approximately **16.0 hours** of training, which was significantly faster than four-epoch GSPO (**22.4 hours**) and all one-epoch methods (**37.7-38.0 hours**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Normalizing cumulative log-likelihood ratios by prefix length (geometric mean) effectively compresses the weight scale while preserving crucial causal-prefix dependence.
> - A dynamically adjusted acceptance window for importance weights, widening for earlier tokens and narrowing for later ones, helps manage the trade-off between off-policyness and exploration.
> - More aggressive rollout reuse (e.g., four PPO epochs per batch) is feasible and beneficial when using weighting schemes that robustly account for increasing policy lag.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training frequently employs policy-gradient methods from reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance model capabilities. A significant computational bottleneck in this training paradigm is the autoregressive generation of rollout batches, which can consume a substantial portion of the overall training time. To mitigate this cost, a common strategy is to reuse collected rollout batches for multiple learner updates. However, this practice introduces policy lag, causing later updates to become increasingly off-policy as the learner policy deviates from the behavior policy that generated the data.

Existing proximal policy optimization (PPO) methods, such as PPO itself and GRPO, address this mismatch through local approximations. They typically use a local ratio (the ratio of the learner policy's probability to the behavior policy's probability for the current action) and control policy movement via trust regions or clipping mechanisms. These local ratios, however, only correct the action distribution at a fixed state and do not account for the shift in the state distribution itself. This omission defines a proximal approximation, making these methods less effective when policy drift is substantial, as occurs with repeated rollout reuse.

In autoregressive generation, the exact state-action density ratio, known as the cumulative importance ratio (C_t), can be computed. This ratio explicitly corrects both the current action and the probability of reaching its prefix, thus accounting for causal-prefix dependence. However, the cumulative nature of this product form means its log-weight scale can grow significantly with prefix length and policy drift, potentially leading to high variance or numerical instability.

Other approaches, such as Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), attempt to control this scale by using a length-normalized full-response statistic shared across all token positions. While this manages scale, it compromises causal-prefix dependence, as early-position weights become dependent on the entire sampled suffix.

This paper introduces Prefix-Normalized Policy Optimization (PNPO) as an alternative that seeks to reconcile the need for causal-prefix dependence with manageable weight scales. It positions itself as an intermediate solution between token-level local ratios (like PPO/GRPO) and sequence-shared statistics (like GSPO), by applying a prefix-dependent normalization to the cumulative likelihood ratio. The work contributes to the ongoing effort to develop more robust and efficient off-policy RL algorithms for LLMs, particularly those that can effectively reuse costly generated data under conditions of increasing policy lag.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to formulate and evaluate Prefix-Normalized Policy Optimization (PNPO), a token-level policy objective designed for off-policy reinforcement learning of large language models. The authors aim to demonstrate that PNPO can more effectively manage the challenges associated with significant policy lag compared to existing methods.

The motivation for this work stems from several interconnected issues in LLM reinforcement learning:

1. **High Computational Cost of Rollout Generation:** Autoregressive generation of responses is a computationally intensive process. To amortize this cost and improve training efficiency, it is common to reuse collected rollout batches for multiple policy updates.
2. **Problem of Policy Lag and Off-Policyness:** Reusing fixed rollout data causes the learner policy to progressively diverge from the behavior policy that generated the data. This "policy lag" leads to off-policy updates, where the data no longer accurately reflects the current policy's performance or state visitation distribution. Existing proximal policy optimization methods, like PPO, inherently make local approximations by freezing the state distribution at the behavior policy. This approximation becomes increasingly inaccurate as the learner policy drifts further from the behavior policy, limiting the effectiveness of rollout reuse.
3. **Limitations of Exact Importance Sampling:** While the exact state-action density ratio (C_t, the cumulative importance ratio) can theoretically correct for off-policyness in autoregressive settings, its product form leads to an accumulation of token-level log-ratios. This can result in an "unwieldy dynamic range" for the importance weights, potentially causing instability or high variance in updates.
4. **Trade-offs in Existing Scale Control Methods:** Approaches like GSPO address the dynamic range issue by normalizing a full-response statistic and sharing it across all positions. However, this method sacrifices causal-prefix dependence, as the weight for an early token becomes dependent on the entire sampled suffix, which may not be optimal for token-level decision making.
5. **Need for a Balanced Approach:** There is a tension between accurately correcting for causal-prefix dependence (which implies cumulative ratios) and maintaining a manageable weight scale. The motivation for PNPO is to develop a method that preserves causal-prefix dependence at each token position while compressing the log-weight scale, thereby offering a more stable and effective way to handle increasing off-policyness without losing critical information about the sequence history.
6. **Empirical Validation under Controlled Off-Policy Regimes:** The authors are specifically motivated to empirically investigate whether PNPO provides a performance advantage over existing methods, particularly GSPO, when training scenarios are intentionally designed to induce greater off-policyness (e.g., through more policy updates per rollout batch). They aim to quantify this advantage in terms of both performance metrics and the efficiency of rollout reuse.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Prefix-Normalized Policy Optimization (PNPO) and evaluates its performance against baseline methods under controlled off-policy conditions.

**Core PNPO Formulation:**
PNPO defines a prefix-normalized policy weight, `w_PN_i,t`, which is the geometric mean of the likelihood ratios (`ρ_i,k`) along the causal prefix up to position `t` for a response `i`. Specifically, `w_PN_i,t = C_i,t^(1/t) = exp(1/t * Σ(k=1 to t) log ρ_i,k)`. This transformation takes the cumulative log-ratio and normalizes it by the prefix length `t`. This design choice aims to compress the cumulative log-weight scale while preserving the causal-prefix dependence, meaning `w_PN_i,t` depends only on the observed prefix `y_i,≤t`. The authors acknowledge that for `t > 1`, this transformation results in a biased importance weight, as it no longer represents an exact target-to-behavior density ratio. However, it is a monotonic transformation of `C_i,t`, preserving the sign of its log and ordering across responses.

**Complete PNPO Objective:**
The practical PNPO objective combines this prefix-normalized weight with an acceptance gate and a group-relative advantage estimator within a detached score-function surrogate:
- **Acceptance Gate:** A position-dependent gate, `M_i,t`, is applied. It retains the score term at position `t` only if `w_PN_i,t` falls within an interval `[1 - ϵ_lower * h(t, L_i), 1 + ϵ_upper * h(t, L_i)]`. The scaling factor `h(t, L_i) = sqrt(L_i / t)` causes the acceptance interval to widen for earlier token positions and narrow towards the base tolerances (`ϵ_lower`, `ϵ_upper`) at the terminal position (`t=L_i`). If `M_i,t = 0`, the token's contribution to the objective is removed, but subsequent tokens remain eligible.
- **Advantage Estimator:** The method utilizes GRPO's group-relative outcome advantage, `b_A_i,t`. This advantage is calculated by standardizing the return `R_i` for response `i` relative to the mean and standard deviation of returns from a group of `G` responses generated from the same prompt.
- **Surrogate Objective:** The objective `J_PNPO(θ)` is formulated as an expectation over prompts and sampled responses. It averages token-level score terms, weighted by `M_i,t`, `w_PN_i,t`, and `b_A_i,t`, first over valid tokens within each response (normalized by `1/L_i`) and then across responses in a batch (normalized by `1/G`). A stop-gradient operator is applied to the weighting coefficients, ensuring gradients flow only through the current-token log probability `log π_θ(y_i,t | x, y_i,<t)`.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Model and Data:** All methods are initialized from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and fine-tuned using reinforcement learning on DAPO-Math-17k.
- **Off-Policy Regimes:** The study intentionally creates two distinct off-policy regimes:
 1. **One PPO Epoch:** One learner update epoch is applied per rollout batch.
 2. **Four PPO Epochs:** Four learner update epochs are applied per rollout batch, inducing greater policy lag and off-policyness.
- **Training Details:** Each training step involves collecting one rollout batch (256 prompts, 8 responses/prompt, total 2,048 responses). Maximum prompt length is 1,024 tokens, and response length is 15,360 tokens. A learning rate of 10^-6 is used with minibatches of 64 prompt groups (512 responses).
- **Matched Update Budget:** Training is conducted for 600 steps with one epoch (total 2,400 optimizer updates) and 150 steps with four epochs (also total 2,400 optimizer updates). This setup allows for a direct comparison of rollout reuse efficiency.
- **Baselines:**
 - **GRPO:** Uses local token ratios, token-mean aggregation, and specified clipping tolerances (0.2, 0.28).
 - **GSPO:** Employs a length-normalized response ratio, response-mean aggregation, and specific clipping tolerances (3e-4, 4e-4).
- **Evaluation:** Mathematical reasoning performance is assessed on AMC 2023, AIME 2024, and AIME 2025 benchmarks. The metric used is Avg@32 (mean binary correctness over 32 sampled responses per problem), and macro Avg@32 (unweighted mean across the three benchmarks). Evaluations are performed periodically throughout training.
- **Hardware:** 32 NVIDIA H20 GPUs were used for all training runs.

The methodology is designed to isolate the impact of different policy weighting schemes under varying degrees of off-policyness, providing a controlled environment to evaluate PNPO's effectiveness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments focused on evaluating PNPO's performance in long-context mathematical reasoning tasks under two controlled off-policy regimes: one and four PPO policy-update epochs per rollout batch.

**Performance under One PPO Epoch (Lower Mismatch):**
- In the regime with one policy update epoch per rollout batch, PNPO did not consistently outperform GSPO.
- The macro Avg@32 peaks were relatively close: GSPO achieved 49.42%, while PNPO reached 50.05%, suggesting a slight, but not consistent, advantage for PNPO in this setting. GRPO achieved 48.25%.
- On individual benchmarks, PNPO showed a notable lead on AIME 2024 (40.52% vs. GSPO's 37.81%), but performed slightly below GRPO and GSPO on AMC 2023 and AIME 2025, respectively.

**Performance under Four PPO Epochs (Higher Mismatch):**
- Under the four-epoch regime, which induces greater policy lag and off-policyness, PNPO demonstrated a more pronounced advantage.
- PNPO attained the highest observed Avg@32 on all three benchmarks: 80.94% on AMC 2023, 38.85% on AIME 2024, and 30.94% on AIME 2025.
- The unweighted mean of these three benchmark peaks for PNPO was 50.24%, which is 3.00 percentage points higher than GSPO's 47.24% in this regime. GRPO performed lowest at 47.05%.
- Analysis of the evaluation trajectories showed that PNPO maintained its lead over GSPO for 14 out of 15 evaluations in the four-epoch setting, finishing 2.66 percentage points ahead of GSPO.

**Rollout Reuse Efficiency and Training Budget:**
- The study matched the total number of optimizer updates (2,400) across both regimes. This meant the four-epoch runs used 150 rollout batches, while the one-epoch runs used 600 rollout batches.
- Four-epoch PNPO reached a final macro Avg@32 of 49.66% after 150 rollout batches. This performance is comparable to the 49.56% achieved by one-epoch PNPO after 600 batches.
- This indicates that four-epoch PNPO achieved similar final performance using only one-quarter of the newly generated responses, providing evidence for more effective rollout reuse in scenarios with greater policy lag.

**Training Dynamics and Time to Threshold:**
- The training reward curves showed that PNPO with four epochs reached a mean rollout reward of 0.25 after an estimated 16.0 hours of training.
- In comparison, four-epoch GSPO took 22.4 hours, and both one-epoch methods (GRPO, GSPO, PNPO) required between 37.7 and 38.0 hours to reach the same reward threshold. This suggests a notable speed-up in training under the four-epoch setting, particularly for PNPO.
- GRPO did not sustain its early reward gains and its evaluation curve declined in the four-epoch setting, suggesting instability under higher off-policyness for this method.

These results collectively indicate that PNPO's approach to weighting, which normalizes cumulative log-ratios along the causal prefix, offers advantages in scenarios where training involves significant off-policy updates, leading to improved performance and more efficient rollout reuse.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Prefix-Normalized Policy Optimization (PNPO) and its empirical evaluation present several significant implications for reinforcement learning in large language models:

**Addressing Off-Policy Challenges:** The most direct significance of this work is its contribution to mitigating the challenges posed by policy lag and off-policy updates in LLM reinforcement learning. By demonstrating that PNPO can achieve superior performance and stability in high-mismatch scenarios (e.g., four PPO epochs), it offers a potentially more robust method than existing proximal policy optimization algorithms like PPO, GRPO, and GSPO under these conditions.

**Improved Training Efficiency through Rollout Reuse:** The finding that four-epoch PNPO can achieve comparable final performance to its one-epoch counterpart using only one-quarter of the newly generated rollouts is substantial. Autoregressive generation is a major computational cost in LLM RL. More effective reuse of existing rollouts can significantly reduce the overall training time and resource consumption, making LLM fine-tuning more accessible and scalable. This could translate to faster experimentation cycles and reduced operational costs for developing advanced LLMs.

**Balanced Importance Weighting:** PNPO's approach of using a prefix-normalized policy weight represents a novel attempt to balance the need for causal-prefix dependence with the necessity of a manageable log-weight scale. This balance addresses a key tension identified in the literature, where exact cumulative importance ratios can be unstable, and length-normalized response-level statistics might sacrifice important local information. This could inspire further research into hybrid or dynamically adaptive importance weighting schemes.

**Potential for Broader Application:** While the current study focuses on specific mathematical reasoning benchmarks and a controlled induction of off-policyness, the principles behind PNPO could be applicable to other RL tasks for LLMs, particularly those involving long sequences and sequential decision-making where causal prefix information is crucial.

**Impact on Algorithmic Design:** The work contributes to the understanding of how different granularities of importance weighting (token-local, sequence-level, prefix-normalized) affect learning dynamics and performance in off-policy LLM RL. It suggests that a position-dependent, prefix-aware weighting mechanism can be beneficial when policies diverge significantly.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also point to avenues for future impact:
- **Limited Scope of Evaluation:** The findings are based on a single 1.5B-parameter model and three mathematical reasoning benchmarks. Further validation on larger models, diverse tasks (e.g., summarization, dialogue, code generation), and more varied datasets would broaden the generalizability of these results.
- **Single Run per Configuration:** Using only one run per configuration limits the robustness of statistical conclusions. Replicating experiments with multiple random seeds would provide a stronger empirical foundation.
- **Lack of Ablation Studies:** The reported PNPO configuration includes an acceptance gate and response-level aggregation. Without ablation studies, the specific contribution of the prefix-normalized weight, distinct from these other components, cannot be fully isolated. Future work could dissect these elements to understand their individual and combined effects.
- **Controlled Off-Policy Induction:** The study exclusively induced mismatch through repeated updates on fresh batches. Evaluating PNPO in other common off-policy scenarios, such as replay buffers, asynchronous actor-learner systems, or with entirely offline datasets, would be critical to establish its broader utility in diverse distributed RL setups.

Despite these limitations, PNPO offers a promising direction for developing more efficient and stable off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms for LLMs, with the potential to significantly impact the computational cost and performance ceiling of instruction-following and reasoning capabilities in large language models.
