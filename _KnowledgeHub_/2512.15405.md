# EUBRL: Epistemic Uncertainty Directed Bayesian Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EUBRL (Epistemic Uncertainty Directed Bayesian Reinforcement Learning) introduces a principled algorithm that directly uses epistemic uncertainty to guide exploration in Bayesian RL, achieving nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for online learning in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without a generative model. The National University of Singapore researchers empirically demonstrate its superior sample efficiency and consistency on challenging sparse-reward and stochastic tasks.

## Problem

- Effective exploration in Reinforcement Learning remains challenging, especially in complex environments with sparse rewards, long horizons, or high stochasticity.
- Traditional Bayesian RL methods, particularly those based on optimism, can suffer from inefficient exploration due to inaccurate reward estimation propagating through value functions.
- Achieving simultaneous nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for online algorithms in infinite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Processes without a generative model has been an open theoretical problem.

## Method

- Proposes **EUBRL**, a Bayesian Reinforcement Learning algorithm that directly integrates ==epistemic uncertainty== into the reward function within a Bayes-adaptive MDP framework.
- Reformulates the RL objective as a probabilistic inference problem, introducing an "uncertainty" variable and deriving an epistemically guided reward function by marginalizing over it.
- The resulting reward function dynamically decouples exploration and exploitation, focusing on intrinsic uncertainty rewards in unknown areas and mean rewards in well-known ones.

## Results

- EUBRL achieved nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for online algorithms in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, notably without requiring a generative model.
- Empirical evaluations showed EUBRL consistently outperformed several frequentist and Bayesian baselines in sample efficiency, scalability, and consistency on tasks with sparse rewards and high stochasticity.
- In the DeepSea environment, EUBRL with an exploratory prior demonstrated perfect task completion in its stochastic variant, indicating robust performance in deep exploration scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Epistemic uncertainty can be directly integrated into the RL objective to form "Epistemic Resistance," which adaptively reduces per-step regret.
> - The theoretical optimality guarantees of EUBRL depend on the characteristics of the prior distribution, with specific conjugate priors (e.g., Dirichlet-Normal) shown to be "sufficiently expressive."
> - Properly quantified and integrated epistemic uncertainty provides a principled and adaptive mechanism for guiding exploration, outperforming heuristic and fixed-bonus approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents operating in unknown environments must effectively balance exploration—seeking new information—with exploitation—using current knowledge to maximize rewards. Traditional exploration strategies, such as epsilon-greedy or Boltzmann exploration, often exhibit limitations in complex environments characterized by sparse rewards, extended horizons, or high stochasticity.

Bayesian RL (BRL) offers a framework for explicitly modeling uncertainty over environment dynamics and rewards. Agents update their belief about the environment through interaction, using this belief to inform decision-making. A common approach within BRL is optimism in the face of uncertainty, where an uncertainty bonus is added to rewards to incentivize exploration of less-known regions. However, inaccuracies in reward estimation, even when augmented by uncertainty bonuses, can propagate through value functions, potentially leading to inefficient or misdirected exploration. Previous theoretical work in RL has established minimax-optimality benchmarks for regret (cumulative difference from optimal performance) and sample complexity (number of steps to reach an epsilon-optimal policy). While some algorithms have achieved strong theoretical guarantees in either regret or sample complexity, particularly in finite-horizon or generative model settings, simultaneous achievement of nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity in online, infinite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) without assuming a generative model has remained an open challenge.

This paper positions its work within this landscape by addressing the effective leveraging of epistemic uncertainty—uncertainty arising from limited knowledge that is reducible with more data—to guide exploration. It aims to develop a principled Bayesian RL algorithm that improves upon existing optimism-based and sampling-based BRL methods by integrating epistemic uncertainty more directly into the objective function. The work specifically targets the theoretical gap of achieving nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without a generative model, alongside robust empirical performance in challenging exploration scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenge of exploration in reinforcement learning, particularly in complex environments where conventional heuristics are insufficient. The authors highlight that epistemic uncertainty, which quantifies the degree of "unknownness," offers a principled foundation for guiding exploration. However, the effective integration of this uncertainty into the learning objective for principled exploration remains an area of active investigation.

The paper identifies specific shortcomings in existing approaches:
- **Limitations of Heuristic Exploration:** Simple methods like epsilon-greedy often perform poorly in environments with sparse rewards, long horizons, or high stochasticity, where sustained and directed exploration is critical.
- **Challenges with Optimism-Based Methods:** While optimism in the face of uncertainty (adding bonuses to rewards) is a common strategy in BRL, even small errors in reward estimation can lead to inaccurate value functions, potentially causing inefficient exploration or slow convergence.
- **Limited Theoretical Guarantees:** Despite advancements in BRL, achieving theoretical guarantees such as nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for online learning in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, especially without a generative model, has presented difficulties.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a Principled Exploration Algorithm:** Propose a Bayesian reinforcement learning algorithm, EUBRL (Epistemic Uncertainty Directed Bayesian Reinforcement Learning), that uses epistemic uncertainty as a direct guide for exploration, aiming to reduce per-step regret from estimation errors.
2. **Establish Strong Theoretical Guarantees:** Prove that EUBRL achieves nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for infinite-horizon discounted MDPs. This includes demonstrating how epistemic uncertainty adaptively influences regret reduction.
3. **Instantiate Prior-Dependent Bounds:** Derive and demonstrate the application of prior-dependent theoretical bounds for commonly used conjugate priors.
4. **Demonstrate Empirical Efficacy:** Evaluate EUBRL on a range of challenging tasks, including those with sparse rewards, long horizons, and stochastic dynamics, to showcase its superior sample efficiency, scalability, and consistency compared to existing frequentist and Bayesian RL baselines.
5. **Achieve Novel Sample Complexity Result:** To address a specific gap in the literature, establish the first nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity result for an online algorithm in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without assuming the existence of a generative model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EUBRL algorithm integrates epistemic uncertainty into the reward function within a Bayesian RL framework.

#### 3.1. Bayesian RL Framework

The work operates within the Bayes-adaptive MDP (BAMDP) framework, where uncertainty over transition dynamics and rewards is explicitly modeled using a prior distribution over possible MDPs. An agent maintains a belief `b` over MDPs, updating this belief after each interaction with the environment. To manage the computational intractability of full Bayesian planning, the agent approximates Bayes optimality by repeatedly solving a "mean MDP." This mean MDP is defined by the posterior predictive transition model `P_b` and posterior predictive mean reward `r_b`, derived from the current belief `b_t`. The goal is to learn the optimal policy `π*` by alternating between posterior belief updates and policy optimization based on the current mean MDP.

#### 3.2. Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification

Epistemic uncertainty is defined as systematic uncertainty arising from limited knowledge, which is reducible by observing more data. It reflects the degree of disagreement within the agent's belief distribution over model parameters (`w`). The paper adopts a generalized formulation: `E_b(s, a) := h(E_T(s, a), E_R(s, a))`, where `E_T` and `E_R` represent epistemic uncertainty in transitions and rewards, respectively. The chosen combination function is `h(x, y) = η(√x + √y)`, with `η` as a scaling factor.
- **Transition Uncertainty (`E_T(s, a)`):** For categorical distributions, representing state transitions, the variance-based epistemic uncertainty is calculated by one-hot encoding states and applying a specific formula derived from the Dirichlet-Multinomial model.
- **Reward Uncertainty (`E_R(s, a)`):** For continuous rewards, uncertainty is often represented by the variance of the mean reward under the belief distribution. For a Normal-Normal prior, `E_R(s, a)` is proportional to `1/n`, where `n` is the number of visits. For a Normal-Gamma prior, the expression is more complex and depends on sample variance.
- **Mutual Information (MI):** As an alternative information-theoretic measure, MI quantifies the reduction in uncertainty after collecting more evidence. A closed-form solution for MI is provided for the Dirichlet distribution.

#### 3.3. Probabilistic Inference and Epistemic Guidance

The core innovation lies in reformulating the RL objective as a probabilistic inference problem. Standard RL maximizes cumulative reward, which can lead to exploitative behavior. The authors introduce a binary "optimality" random variable `O_t` for standard RL. They then extend this by introducing a binary "uncertainty" variable `U_t` to model the degree of uncertainty. By marginalizing over `U_t` and applying an exponential transformation that intensifies higher uncertainty, they derive an epistemically guided reward function:
`r_EUBRL_b(s, a) := (1 - P(U=1|s, a)) * r_b(s, a) + P(U=1|s, a) * E_b(s, a)`.
Here, `P(U=1|s, a)` represents the probability of uncertainty, chosen simply as `E_b(s, a) / E_max`, where `E_max` is the maximum possible epistemic uncertainty (determined by the prior). This formulation intuitively decouples exploration and exploitation: when uncertainty is high (`P_U(s, a)` is large), the agent focuses more on the intrinsic reward provided by `E_b(s, a)`, encouraging exploration. When confident (`P_U(s, a)` is small), it prioritizes the mean reward `r_b(s, a)`, promoting exploitation.

#### 3.4. Algorithm Design (EUBRL)

The EUBRL algorithm operates iteratively:
1. **Initialize:** Start with a prior `b_0` and solve the initial mean MDP `M = (S, A, P_b0, r_EUBRL_b0, γ)` to obtain `π_0`.
2. **Act and Interact:** At each time step `t`, the agent executes action `a_t = π_t(s_t)`, observes the next state `s_{t+1}` and reward `r_t`.
3. **Update Belief:** The belief `b` is updated using observed data. Due to conjugacy, posterior updates for standard priors (Dirichlet, Normal-Gamma) are in closed form, as are calculations for `P_b`, `r_b`, and `E_b`.
4. **Update Policy:** Periodically (or at every step in infinite-horizon settings), a new policy `π_{t+1}` is computed by solving the mean MDP `M = (S, A, P_b_{t+1}, r_EUBRL_b_{t+1}, γ)` using value iteration.
The algorithm is designed to generalize to both infinite-horizon discounted MDPs (policy updated every step, no reset) and finite-horizon episodic MDPs (policy updated and episode reset every `H` steps). It claims simplicity by avoiding intricate designs common in other optimism-based methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical analyses of EUBRL's performance guarantees and empirical evaluations across a suite of challenging RL tasks.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Regret Decomposition and Epistemic Resistance:** The per-step regret `Δ_t = V*(s_t) - V_πt(s_t)` is decomposed into three terms: Quasi-optimism, Complexity, and Accuracy. The analysis introduces "Epistemic Resistance" `R_t(s) := 2P_U(s, π_t(s)) + (9/7)P_U(s, π*(s))`, demonstrating that this term adaptively reduces per-step regret based on the uncertainty of the current and optimal policies' actions. This highlights a direct mechanism for epistemic uncertainty to influence regret.

- **Nearly Minimax-Optimal Frequentist Bounds:**
 - **Regret (Theorem 2):** For infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, EUBRL achieves a regret bound of `O(√SAT / (1-γ)^1.5 + S^2A / (1-γ)^2)`. This bound is nearly minimax-optimal when `T` is sufficiently large (`T ≥ S^3A / (1-γ)`), improving upon prior state-of-the-art frequentist bounds.
 - **Sample Complexity (Theorem 3):** EUBRL is proven to achieve a sample complexity of `O(SA / (ϵ^2(1-γ)^3) + S^2A / (ϵ(1-γ)^2)) * log(1/δ)`. This result is claimed to be the first online algorithm to achieve nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without assuming a generative model, for a specific range of `ϵ`.

- **Prior-Dependent Bayesian Bounds:**
 - **Class of Priors (Theorem 4):** The theoretical results extend to a class of "sufficiently expressive priors" (denoted `C`), which are either decomposable or weakly informative and exhibit an epistemic uncertainty decay rate of `Θ(1/√n)`. For priors within this class that are also uniform and bounded, EUBRL achieves nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity.
 - **Concrete Instantiation (Corollary 1):** The work demonstrates that a combination of Dirichlet priors for transitions and Normal priors (with known precision) for rewards falls within the `C` class and satisfies uniformity and boundedness, thus guaranteeing nearly minimax-optimal performance with EUBRL.

- **Limitations and Counterexamples:**
 - **Normal-Gamma Prior (Proposition 1):** For Normal-Gamma priors, the epistemic uncertainty can depend on the sample variance. In deterministic or nearly deterministic environments, this term can approach zero too quickly (`Ω(1/n^2)` rate), which may violate the quasi-optimism assumption and prevent efficient exploration. However, this issue can be mitigated by careful prior parameterization.
 - **Prior Misspecification (Theorem 5):** If the prior is misspecified such that initial epistemic uncertainty is very low and confidently wrong, EUBRL can fail to converge to the optimal policy, leading to unbounded suboptimality. This highlights the importance of the scaling factor `η` and appropriate prior selection.

#### 4.2. Empirical Findings

EUBRL's exploration capabilities were evaluated on tasks with sparse rewards, long horizons, and stochasticity, comparing its sample efficiency, scalability, and consistency against several frequentist (RMAX, MBIE-EB) and Bayesian (PSRL, BOSS, BEB, VBRB, BEETLE, Mean-MDP) baselines.

- **Chain and Loop Environments:** EUBRL consistently outperformed all relevant baselines in both Chain (highly stochastic) and Loop (deterministic, emphasizing state-space structure), demonstrating lower variability and higher average returns (Tables 1 & 3). Mean-MDP's subpar performance underlined the need for explicit exploration bonuses.
- **Scaling with Problem Size (Loop and DeepSea):**
 - In the generalized Loop environment, EUBRL scaled more favorably than RMAX, suggesting that Bayesian priors can provide a smoothing effect beneficial for sparse environments.
 - In DeepSea (a deep exploration problem), EUBRL, especially with an "exploratory" prior (EUBRL+), showed higher sample efficiency and consistency. For the stochastic variant, EUBRL+ achieved perfect task completion without failure, a result not observed in previous works. Other methods like PSRL showed scaling issues due to excessive sampling, and BEB struggled due to its fixed reward bonus design (Figure 2).
- **LazyChain Environment:** A newly introduced environment designed to test long horizons, sparse rewards, and myopia. EUBRL consistently outperformed other methods in both deterministic and stochastic variants, demonstrating better sample efficiency and scalability even under heavy noise. The use of a tied prior for EUBRL (EUBRL Tied Prior) improved sample efficiency and success rate, and using mutual information as an uncertainty measure (EUBRL MI) led to the highest overall success rate (Figure 3).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of EUBRL represents a methodological advance in principled exploration for reinforcement learning. Its primary significance lies in providing a theoretically grounded framework that leverages epistemic uncertainty to adaptively guide exploration and exploitation.

**Theoretical Impact:**
- EUBRL contributes to the theoretical understanding of exploration efficiency by establishing nearly minimax-optimal regret and, notably, nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs for online algorithms without assuming a generative model. This addresses a previously open problem in the field.
- The regret decomposition into quasi-optimism, complexity, and accuracy terms, coupled with the introduction of "Epistemic Resistance," offers a detailed understanding of how uncertainty can directly influence learning efficiency and per-step regret.
- The characterization of a class of priors (`C`) for which these optimal bounds hold, and the demonstration with common conjugate priors (Dirichlet and Normal), provides clear guidance for applying the theoretical results in practice.

**Practical Impact:**
- The empirical evaluations demonstrate that EUBRL consistently achieves superior sample efficiency, scalability, and consistency across a diverse set of challenging RL tasks, including those with sparse rewards, long horizons, and high stochasticity. This suggests EUBRL could be a more robust and effective exploration strategy than many existing frequentist and Bayesian approaches.
- The method's ability to maintain performance in large-scale and highly stochastic environments, and its adaptability through different prior choices and uncertainty measures (e.g., mutual information), indicates its potential for broader applicability in real-world problems.

**Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges that scaling EUBRL to complex, high-dimensional environments, typically involving deep function approximators, remains an open challenge. Future research directions include:
- Developing efficient and well-calibrated methods for epistemic uncertainty estimation in deep learning contexts, potentially without heavy reliance on sampling.
- Improving the efficiency of Bayesian planning when integrated with function approximation, possibly through sparse sampling or latent space simulations.
- Conducting more comprehensive theoretical analyses to account for approximation errors in complex settings.

Overall, EUBRL offers a robust and theoretically sound approach to exploration, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in terms of learning efficiency and performance in challenging RL environments, while also highlighting clear avenues for future research in scalable Bayesian RL.
