# EUBRL: Epistemic Uncertainty Directed Bayesian Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EUBRL (Epistemic Uncertainty Directed Bayesian Reinforcement Learning) introduces a principled algorithm that directly uses epistemic uncertainty to guide exploration in Bayesian RL, achieving nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for online learning in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without a generative model. The National University of Singapore researchers empirically demonstrate its superior sample efficiency and consistency on challenging sparse-reward and stochastic tasks.

## Problem

- Effective exploration in Reinforcement Learning remains challenging, especially in complex environments with sparse rewards, long horizons, or high stochasticity.
- Traditional Bayesian RL methods, particularly those based on optimism, can suffer from inefficient exploration due to inaccurate reward estimation propagating through value functions.
- Achieving simultaneous nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for online algorithms in infinite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Processes without a generative model has been an open theoretical problem.

## Method

- Proposes **EUBRL**, a Bayesian Reinforcement Learning algorithm that directly integrates ==epistemic uncertainty== into the reward function within a Bayes-adaptive MDP framework.
- Reformulates the RL objective as a probabilistic inference problem, introducing an "uncertainty" variable and deriving an epistemically guided reward function by marginalizing over it.
- The resulting reward function dynamically decouples exploration and exploitation, focusing on intrinsic uncertainty rewards in unknown areas and mean rewards in well-known ones.

## Results

- EUBRL achieved nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity for online algorithms in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, notably without requiring a generative model.
- Empirical evaluations showed EUBRL consistently outperformed several frequentist and Bayesian baselines in sample efficiency, scalability, and consistency on tasks with sparse rewards and high stochasticity.
- In the DeepSea environment, EUBRL with an exploratory prior demonstrated perfect task completion in its stochastic variant, indicating robust performance in deep exploration scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Epistemic uncertainty can be directly integrated into the RL objective to form "Epistemic Resistance," which adaptively reduces per-step regret.
> - The theoretical optimality guarantees of EUBRL depend on the characteristics of the prior distribution, with specific conjugate priors (e.g., Dirichlet-Normal) shown to be "sufficiently expressive."
> - Properly quantified and integrated epistemic uncertainty provides a principled and adaptive mechanism for guiding exploration, outperforming heuristic and fixed-bonus approaches.
