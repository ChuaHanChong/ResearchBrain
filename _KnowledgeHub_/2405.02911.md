# Multimodal Sense-Informed Prediction of 3D Human Motions

> [!summary] Summary
> SIF3D forecasts future 3D human motion by fusing observed motion with a 3D scene point cloud and human gaze, using two novel attention mechanisms: ternary intention-aware attention for global scene-gaze-motion salience and semantic coherence-aware attention for local pose-relevant scene points. On GIMO and GTA-1M it achieves state-of-the-art trajectory and pose prediction accuracy.

## Problem

- Existing scene-aware motion prediction encodes entire 3D scene as single global embedding, treating all points equally relevant, ignoring salient subset.
- Prior methods overlook human gaze as an intention signal indicating destination, missing a key cue for long-term trajectory planning.
- Global embeddings fail to capture local scene details, causing predicted motion to violate physical constraints like mesh intersection or wall penetration.

## Method

- ==Ternary Intention-Aware Attention (TIA)== fuses motion embedding, global scene features, and gaze-indexed scene features via ==cross-modal attention== to compute global point salience for trajectory planning.
- ==Semantic Coherence-Aware Attention (SCA)== computes per-frame local scene-point salience with a spatial-proximity bias, feeding fine-grained scene context into pose prediction.
- **SIF3D**'s full pipeline combines a transformer ==MotionEncoder==, ==PointNet++== scene encoder, TIA/SCA-driven ==TrajectoryPlanner== and ==PosePredictor==, and a ==GCN== ==MotionDecoder== supervised by a ==geometry discriminator== on SMPL-X motion.

## Results

- On GIMO, SIF3D with scene and gaze reaches **666mm** destination-trajectory deviation and **195.7mm** destination MPJPE, beating the BiFu baseline's **727mm** and **205.0mm**.
- On GTA-1M, SIF3D achieves **836mm** destination-trajectory deviation and **227.7mm** destination MPJPE, versus BiFu's **903mm** and **234.2mm**, its closest competitor.
- Ablation shows removing TIA increases destination-trajectory deviation by **111mm** and removing SCA increases it by **37mm**, confirming both attention mechanisms are load-bearing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting scene salience into separate global (trajectory) and local (pose) attention heads outperforms one shared global embedding, matching the task's own structure.
> - Human gaze is a cheap, high-value intention prior for trajectory prediction, useful even when only approximated rather than directly measured.
> - Scene point cloud density trades accuracy against VRAM and speed with diminishing returns past roughly 4096 points, a useful default for scene-conditioned tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D human motion prediction is a component of machine intelligence with applications in autonomous systems, robotics, and human-robot interaction. Initial approaches to this task frequently leveraged Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to model temporal dependencies in motion sequences, though these often encountered issues such as discontinuity and error accumulation. Subsequent advancements introduced Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), transformer-based architectures, and Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to capture complex spatial and temporal relationships within human poses.

A persistent limitation across many of these earlier methods was the insufficient consideration of the external environment. Human motion is inherently influenced and constrained by its physical surroundings. Neglecting this context often resulted in predicted motions that were physically implausible or contained noticeable artifacts, such as human models intersecting with scene geometry.

More recent research efforts have begun to address this by developing scene-aware motion generation techniques. These approaches have incorporated various forms of scene context, including object bounding boxes, 2D scene images, depth maps, and directly accessible 3D point clouds. Notable advancements in this area include methods that contextualize scenes, estimate interactions between humans and objects, and plan collision-averse trajectories. The adoption of 3D point clouds, processed by architectures like PointNet and PointNet++, represents a trend towards leveraging rich geometric data for scene representation.

Despite these advancements in scene-aware motion generation, a gap remained concerning the nuanced utilization of scene information. Many existing methods encoded the entire scene into a unified global embedding. This approach often failed to distinguish between salient (relevant) and underlying (less relevant) scene points, potentially overlooking intricate local details crucial for realistic interactions. Additionally, a significant aspect of human behavior—gaze—was often not explicitly integrated as a cue for human intention or desired destination.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a multimodal framework that explicitly integrates both external 3D scene information and internal human gaze. It proposes mechanisms to identify and utilize salient points within the 3D scene at both global and local scales, guided by human intention inferred from gaze. By doing so, it aims to produce more physically plausible, coherent, and high-fidelity motion predictions that better align with real-world scenarios, advancing beyond methods that consider global scene embeddings uniformly or do not incorporate gaze as a distinct modality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and multimodal approach for forecasting 3D human motions that are physically plausible and contextually coherent within real-world 3D environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and leads to distinct methodological objectives:

1. **Addressing Physical Implausibility in Motion Predictions:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing human motion prediction methods frequently overlook the constraining effects of the external environment. This oversight often results in predicted motions where human meshes intersect with scene geometry, distort in unrealistic ways, or otherwise violate physical constraints, leading to pronounced artifacts and implausible outcomes.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a novel framework capable of generating high-fidelity future human poses and trajectories that explicitly consider and respect the physical boundaries and semantic elements of a given 3D scene.

2. **Enhancing Scene Information Utilization:**
 - **Motivation:** While some previous scene-aware methods incorporate 3D scene representations (e.g., point clouds), they often encode the entire scene into a single global embedding. This approach treats all scene points equally, failing to distinguish between salient points (those critical for human interaction or navigation) and underlying points (less relevant). Consequently, such methods may miss intricate local details and produce motions that deviate from intended scene semantics.
 - **Objective:** To develop mechanisms that explicitly identify and leverage both globally and locally salient points within a 3D scene. This involves discerning which parts of the environment are most relevant to human activity and interaction at different granularities (e.g., global path planning versus local pose adjustments).

3. **Integrating Human Intention through Gaze Information:**
 - **Motivation:** Human gaze is a valuable indicator of intention, often signaling where a person is likely to move or interact within an environment. Current motion prediction models rarely integrate this internal cue, potentially leading to less accurate or less human-like predictions of trajectory and interaction.
 - **Objective:** To incorporate human gaze as a distinct input modality, treating it as a direct manifestation of human intention. This information is intended to guide the prediction process, ensuring that generated motions align with the subject's probable destination or interaction targets.

4. **Developing Multimodal Attention Mechanisms:**
 - **Motivation:** To effectively combine and selectively attend to the diverse information from motion sequences, 3D scenes, and human gaze, specialized attention mechanisms are required. A simple concatenation or global embedding may not adequately capture the nuanced relationships between these modalities.
 - **Objective:** To propose two novel cross-modal attention mechanisms:
 - **Ternary Intention-Aware Attention (TIA):** Designed to integrate human gaze, motion, and global scene features to identify globally salient points and inform long-term trajectory planning by matching where the human intends to reach.
 - **Semantic Coherence-Aware Attention (SCA):** Focused on identifying local salient scene points by analyzing their semantic connections with human poses, thereby ensuring reasonable interaction of the generated sequence with immediate 3D scene details for per-frame pose prediction.

5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - **Motivation:** To demonstrate the efficacy and advantages of the proposed multimodal, sense-informed framework compared to existing methods.
 - **Objective:** To achieve superior performance on established real-world benchmarks for 3D human motion and trajectory prediction, establishing a new state-of-the-art in this domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named SIF3D (Sense-Informed Forecasting of 3D human motions), integrates multimodal inputs—past motion sequences, 3D scene point clouds, and human gaze—to predict future human motions. The architecture consists of several interconnected modules.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The task is defined as predicting a future motion sequence Y$_{1:∆T}$ given an observed motion sequence X$_{1:T}$, a 3D scene point cloud S, and a human gaze point sequence G$_{1:T}$. Each pose x$_k$ includes global translation (t$_k$), orientation (o$_k$), and a body pose embedding (p$_k$). The SMPL-X body model and VPoser are used to reconstruct joint positions. The prediction horizon (∆T = 5 seconds) is longer than in some previous works (e.g., 1 second), while the observation duration is T = 3 seconds.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Encoding

SIF3D processes the three input modalities:
- **Motion Encoding:** A transformer-based MotionEncoder is employed to convert the observed motion sequence X$_{1:T}$ into a motion embedding f$_m$. To facilitate long-term prediction, the observed sequence is padded with the last observed pose for ∆T times, creating a virtual sequence of length T+∆T. The MotionEncoder processes this padded sequence to generate f$_m \in \mathbb{R}^{(T+∆T) \times c_m}$.
- **Scene Encoding:** A PointNet++ model is used to encode the 3D scene point cloud S. This yields per-point scene features $\hat{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times c_s}$ and a global scene embedding $\hat{S}_{global} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_s}$, where n is the number of points in the scene cloud.
- **Gaze Feature Extraction:** The human gaze point sequence G$_{1:T}$, defined as the intersection of the human eye ray with the 3D scene, is used to index the per-point scene features $\hat{S}$. This extracts gaze-related scene features, which are then processed by a GazeEncoder. Similar to motion encoding, the gaze feature sequence is padded with the last gaze point for ∆T frames to align with the prediction length, resulting in encoded gaze features f$_{gaze}$.

#### 3.3. Ternary Intention-Aware Attention (TIA)

TIA is designed to capture global salient scene features and human intention for long-term trajectory planning.
- The motion embedding f$_m$ is first fed into a TrajectoryEncoder to produce f$_{TIA_m}$, which is then condensed into a global motion representation f$_{TIA_{gm}}$ using a temporal aggregator (e.g., taking the last motion embedding).
- A cross-modal attention mechanism calculates global salience s$_g \in \mathbb{R}^n$ for each scene point relative to f$_{TIA_{gm}}$. Query (Q$_{TIA}$) is derived from f$_{TIA_{gm}}$, and Key (K$_{TIA}$) and Value (V$_{TIA}$) are derived from $\hat{S}$.
- The global salient scene features f$_{TIA_{sm}}$ are computed by incorporating $\hat{S}_{global}$ and the salient scene points (s$_g \cdot$ V$_{TIA}$). These features are broadcasted across the entire motion sequence length.
- Finally, TIA concatenates f$_m$, f$_{TIA_{sm}}$, and f$_{gaze}$ and processes them through a two-layer MLP to produce the TIA output f$_{TIA_o}$.

#### 3.4. Semantic Coherence-Aware Attention (SCA)

SCA focuses on integrating local salient scene details for per-frame pose prediction.
- The motion embedding f$_m$ is passed through a PoseEncoder to generate pose embedding f$_{SCA_m}$.
- Local scene point salience s$_l \in \mathbb{R}^{(T+∆T) \times n}$ is calculated for each frame by applying a cross-modal attention mechanism between f$_{SCA_m}$ (as Query) and $\hat{S}$ (as Key and Value).
- A spatial salience bias s$_{spatial} \in \mathbb{R}^{(T+∆T) \times n}$ is introduced, derived from the relative positions of scene points with respect to the predicted human translation and orientation. This bias emphasizes points closer to the subject and aligned with the human body.
- The local salient scene features f$_{SCA_{sm}}$ are obtained by multiplying (s$_l$ + s$_{spatial}$) with V$_{SCA}$.
- The SCA output f$_{SCA_o}$ is produced by concatenating f$_{SCA_m}$ and f$_{SCA_{sm}}$ and passing them through a two-layer MLP.

#### 3.5. Motion Sequence Generation

- **Trajectory Planning:** The TrajectoryPlanner, implemented as a linear layer, utilizes f$_{TIA_o}$ to predict the global translation $\hat{T}$ and orientation $\hat{O}$ for the future sequence.
- **Pose Prediction:** The PosePredictor, also a linear layer, uses f$_{SCA_o}$ to predict the body pose embeddings $\hat{P}$.
- **Motion Decoding:** The predicted trajectory ($\hat{T}, \hat{O}$) and poses ($\hat{P}$) are fed into the frozen pre-trained SMPL-X model and VPoser to reconstruct the joint positions $\hat{J}$. A MotionDecoder, consisting of a GCN architecture, then processes $\hat{J}$ to generate the final predicted motion sequence $\hat{Y}$.
- **Geometric Discriminator:** To enhance the physical stability and authenticity of the predicted motions, a geometric discriminator, structured as a 3-layer transformer decoder, is employed to differentiate between generated and ground truth motion sequences, providing adversarial supervision.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The MotionEncoder uses a 6-layer transformer encoder. TrajectoryEncoder and PoseEncoder utilize single-layer self-attention. The MotionDecoder is a 6-layer GCN. TIA and SCA each contain 2 blocks. All embedding dimensions (attention, motion, scene) are set to 256, and MLPs have a hidden dimension of 1024. The model is trained using an AdamW optimizer with an exponential learning rate scheduler for 100 epochs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SIF3D on two real-world benchmarks: GIMO and GTA-1M, measuring performance using trajectory deviation (Traj-path, Traj-dest) and Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE-path, MPJPE-dest). Results are presented in millimeters (mm).

#### 4.1. Ablation of Multiple Modalities (Table 1)

The study found that the inclusion of gaze and 3D scene information generally improved the performance of all tested methods, including LTD, SPGSN, AuxFormer, BiFu, and SIF3D.
- **Scene Information:** Incorporating scene information led to performance gains across baselines, with SIF3D demonstrating the most substantial improvements. For instance, on the GIMO dataset, SIF3D with scene information reduced Traj-dest from 888mm to 823mm compared to SIF3D without scene. This is attributed to SIF3D's ability to integrate scene features from both global and local perspectives via TIA and SCA.
- **Gaze Information:** Human gaze was observed to significantly enhance trajectory planning. On GIMO, SIF3D with gaze reduced Traj-dest from 888mm to 714mm. This indicates that gaze provides insights into subject intentions, aligning predictions more closely with the motion sequence's destination.
- **Combined Modalities:** The simultaneous incorporation of both scene and gaze information yielded the most favorable results across all metrics and methods. SIF3D with both modalities achieved 590mm Traj-path, 666mm Traj-dest, 156.6mm MPJPE-path, and 195.7mm MPJPE-dest on GIMO, and 626mm Traj-path, 836mm Traj-dest, 164.9mm MPJPE-path, and 227.7mm MPJPE-dest on GTA-1M. These figures represent the lowest deviations compared to other configurations and baselines.

#### 4.2. Detailed Performance Analysis (Table 2 & 3)

SIF3D consistently demonstrated strong performance across various scenarios and prediction horizons, particularly excelling in long-term predictions (5-second horizon).
- **Comparison with Baselines:** AuxFormer and BiFu, both transformer-based, generally outperformed GCN-based methods (LTD, SPGSN) in longer-term predictions, suggesting the effectiveness of attention mechanisms in cross-modal feature extraction. However, SIF3D achieved superior metrics in nearly all long-term (5-sec) evaluations.
 - On GTA-1M for 5-second prediction, SIF3D recorded a Traj-dest of 836mm and MPJPE-dest of 227.7mm, compared to AuxFormer+Scene+Gaze (916mm Traj-dest, 236.8mm MPJPE-dest) and BiFu (903mm Traj-dest, 234.2mm MPJPE-dest).
- **GCN Limitations:** GCN-based methods showed proficiency in short-term predictions but struggled in longer-term scenarios (e.g., 5-second predictions where Traj-dest averaged over 400mm), attributed to difficulties in obtaining comprehensive whole-person pose features and effective cross-modal interaction with scene features.
- **SIF3D's Strengths:** The superior performance of SIF3D across both global trajectory and local pose predictions, especially long-term, is attributed to the effectiveness of TIA in understanding intention and SCA in locally scene-aware pose prediction.

#### 4.3. Visualizations (Figure 3)

Qualitative results illustrate SIF3D's ability to generate physically plausible motions that interact realistically with the 3D scene, in contrast to baseline methods like BiFu.
- **Scenario (a) - Living Room:** SIF3D predicted the person navigating an aisle without passing through a coffee table, and the final pose was physically grounded, unlike BiFu which showed penetration and suspension. SIF3D's global scene point salience heatmap highlighted interactive objects (chairs, sofa, coffee table), indicating its ability to comprehend the 3D scene and align with human gaze for plausible trajectory planning.
- **Scenario (b) - Bedroom:** While BiFu predicted an accurate destination, the motion sequence demonstrated physical inconsistencies (penetration of walls and screens). SIF3D's prediction avoided these penetrations. Its global salience heatmap revealed understanding of the entire 3D scene, even for parts not visible at the end of observation, and local salience heatmaps showed awareness of obstacles like walls.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies of Components (Table 4)

- **TIA (Ternary Intention-Aware Attention):** Removal of TIA resulted in a significant increase in Traj-dest (+111mm), indicating a reduced capacity to understand the 3D scene and human intentions for global trajectory planning. This also indirectly affected SCA's performance due to inaccurate predicted trajectories.
- **SCA (Semantic Coherence-Aware Attention):** Removing SCA led to a 37mm increase in Traj-dest and a 4.7mm rise in MPJPE-dest, demonstrating its role in capturing local scene details for accurate pose prediction.
- **MotionDecoder:** Without the MotionDecoder, Traj-dest increased by 20.0mm and MPJPE-dest by 3.2mm, suggesting its contribution to precise adjustment of joint positions and distinct trajectory/pose generation.
- **Discriminator:** The geometry discriminator contributed to improved physical stability, evidenced by a 7.8mm decrease in Traj-dest and a 1.1mm decrease in MPJPE-dest.
- **PointNet++:** Replacing PointNet++ with a simpler MLP for scene encoding resulted in a substantial increase in both Traj-dest and MPJPE-dest, confirming PointNet++'s critical role in extracting effective scene features.

#### 4.5. Scene Point Cloud Size (Table 5)

Performance improved with increasing scene point cloud size up to 4096 points. Beyond this, further increases yielded marginal performance gains while significantly escalating computational overhead (VRAM and processing speed). A size of 4096 was selected as a balance.

#### 4.6. Motion Aggregator in TIA (Table 6)

The choice of temporal motion aggregator in TIA had limited impact on performance, with the "Last" frame aggregation strategy demonstrating the best results. Surprisingly, weighted methods (Convolutional, Transformer) performed worse, which the authors attribute to potential limitations in dataset size and supervision for aggregated global motion embedding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SIF3D, a multimodal framework that advances the field of 3D human motion prediction by systematically integrating external 3D scene information and internal human gaze cues. The work addresses a critical limitation in previous methods, which often generated physically implausible motions due to a lack of detailed environmental context and human intent.

The introduction of Ternary Intention-Aware Attention (TIA) and Semantic Coherence-Aware Attention (SCA) marks a methodological contribution, enabling the model to selectively process globally and locally salient scene features, respectively. By grounding trajectory planning in human gaze as an indicator of intention and refining pose predictions with fine-grained local scene details, SIF3D achieves a more comprehensive understanding of human-environment interaction.

The consistently superior performance of SIF3D on real-world benchmarks, particularly in long-term predictions, establishes a new state-of-the-art. This improvement is demonstrated across both global trajectory and local pose metrics, and is further supported by qualitative visualizations that show predictions avoiding collisions and adhering to physical constraints, unlike previous methods. The extensive ablation studies quantitatively underscore the individual contributions of each architectural component and the necessity of multimodal inputs.

The significance of this work extends to several application domains:
- **Robotics:** Enhanced prediction of human movement can lead to more intuitive and safer human-robot collaboration, allowing robots to anticipate human actions and plan their movements accordingly in shared 3D spaces.
- **Autonomous Vehicles:** Improved forecasting of pedestrian and occupant motions can contribute to safer navigation and better decision-making for self-driving cars.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Generating realistic, scene-aware character animations could elevate immersion and interaction quality in virtual environments.
- **Action Recognition and Understanding:** The model's ability to discern salient scene points and human intention could also inform more robust and context-aware human activity recognition systems.

By demonstrating the importance of "scene-awareness" and "gaze-guidance," this research establishes a foundation for developing intelligent systems that can operate more effectively and naturally in dynamic human-centric environments. The findings suggest a promising direction for future research in generating diverse and high-fidelity human motions that are seamlessly integrated with complex 3D scenarios.
