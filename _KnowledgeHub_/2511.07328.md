# Q-RAG: Long Context Multi-step Retrieval via Value-based Embedder Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Q-RAG introduces a multi-step retrieval-augmented generation method that fine-tunes only an embedder model using reinforcement learning, rather than the LLM itself. It achieves high accuracy on commonsense reasoning and Needle-in-a-Haystack tasks over ultra-long contexts up to 10 million tokens while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Most existing RAG systems are designed for single-step retrieval, limiting their effectiveness for complex questions that require multi-step reasoning and synthesis of information from various document segments.
- Current multi-step retrieval techniques often involve computationally intensive fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs), making them resource-prohibitive and inaccessible for many practitioners.
- LLMs struggle with ultra-long contexts due to issues like computational inefficiency, attention dilution, and increased hallucination, necessitating effective retrieval mechanisms for documents spanning millions of tokens.

## Method

- **Q-RAG** models multi-step retrieval as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP) and fine-tunes a separate embedder model using temporal difference reinforcement learning, specifically an adaptation of the PQN method.
- The Q-function is approximated by the inner product of embeddings from a state embedder (for query and previously selected chunks) and an action embedder (for candidate chunks), enabling efficient latent-space search.
- A relative positional encoding mechanism is integrated into the action embedder, allowing the agent to reason about the spatial and temporal relationships of facts to enhance multi-step search in long, narrative texts.

## Results

- Q-RAG achieved the highest average performance across BabiLong commonsense reasoning tasks, demonstrating robust generalization and minimal degradation on contexts up to 10 million tokens.
- It attained near-perfect accuracy on all RULER Needle-in-a-Haystack subtasks for contexts up to 1 million tokens, generalizing effectively from **4K**-token training data.
- On open-domain QA benchmarks, Q-RAG showed competitive performance on HotPotQA and superior performance on the out-of-distribution MuSiQue dataset compared to other baselines and LLM-fine-tuning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training only a lightweight embedder with reinforcement learning provides a resource-efficient approach to multi-step RAG, enabling its use with large, frozen, or proprietary LLMs without costly fine-tuning.
> - Factoring the Q-function into state and action embeddings allows for efficient retrieval planning and generalization to varying context lengths at inference time.
> - Explicitly encoding relative positional information within the action embedder enhances the agent's ability to perform temporal reasoning, which is crucial for tasks requiring sequential understanding over long documents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate advanced capabilities across various tasks but encounter limitations such as static knowledge, computational inefficiency with long contexts, attention dilution, and the tendency to hallucinate. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods have emerged as a technique to mitigate these issues by selectively providing LLMs with relevant external information from a large corpus. This approach aims to reduce inference costs and improve output quality by focusing the LLM on a more concise and pertinent input.

Most existing RAG systems are designed for single-step retrieval, which is effective for simple fact-finding tasks like Needle-in-a-Haystack. However, complex questions often necessitate multi-step reasoning, requiring iterative interactions with the context to gather distributed information. The research landscape for multi-step retrieval includes several directions:

- **Knowledge Graph Construction:** Methods such as GraphReader and HippoRAG build knowledge graphs from retrieved information. While offering enhanced retrieval quality, these methods can be computationally intensive at inference time as the LLM processes the entire context to construct the graph for each new query.
- **LLM Agents:** Approaches like Search-o1 and AriGraph involve LLM agents that interleave RAG queries with LLM-generated instructions. These systems can be susceptible to noisy or inaccurate retrieved passages, which may disrupt subsequent query generation.
- **LLM Fine-tuning for Retrieval:** More recent methods, including IM-RAG, R1-Searcher, Search-R1, RAG-RL, and ReSearcher, fine-tune LLMs to interact more effectively with retrieval tools. These approaches often exhibit improved performance but are resource-intensive due to the need for fine-tuning the LLM itself, which can be impractical for large models and limits their accessibility.
- **Retriever Fine-tuning:** RePlug focuses on fine-tuning the retriever based on LLM feedback, though it did not specifically address multi-step reasoning or use reinforcement learning in that context. BeamRetriever trains a reranker for BeamSearch-style planning for short-context QA.

This paper introduces Q-RAG as an alternative within the multi-step retrieval landscape. Q-RAG distinguishes itself by fine-tuning only the embedder model for multi-step retrieval using reinforcement learning, rather than the LLM itself. This approach aims to provide a resource-efficient solution that can operate with large or proprietary LLMs while offering competitive performance, particularly for ultra-long contexts. It positions itself against LLM fine-tuning methods by reducing computational cost and against transformer-based reranking (like BeamRetriever) by leveraging efficient vector similarity for faster inference and scalability. The work also indirectly competes with specialized long-context models (e.g., Mamba, RMT, Titans, ATLAS, ARMT, LongRoPE2) by achieving high performance on long-context reasoning tasks through a retrieval-based mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a resource-efficient multi-step Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach. This approach aims to enhance the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) by enabling them to effectively answer complex questions that require information from multiple, non-contiguous parts of a very long document.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current RAG systems and LLM applications:

1. **Limitations of Single-Step Retrieval:** Most existing RAG methods employ single-step retrieval, which is inadequate for complex queries demanding multi-step reasoning or synthesis of information from various document segments. Such tasks are common in real-world applications but are not effectively addressed by systems designed for simple fact extraction.
2. **Resource Intensity of LLM Fine-tuning:** Emerging multi-step retrieval techniques often involve fine-tuning the LLM itself to generate intermediate search queries or integrate retrieval tools. This process is computationally demanding, requiring significant hardware resources (e.g., clusters of high-end GPUs), making it inaccessible for many researchers and practitioners and impractical for very large or proprietary LLMs.
3. **Need for Long-Context Reasoning:** LLMs face challenges with ultra-long contexts, including computational inefficiency, attention dilution, and increased hallucination rates. Effective multi-step retrieval is crucial for maintaining LLM performance and accuracy when dealing with documents spanning millions of tokens, especially for tasks requiring commonsense or temporal reasoning over distributed facts.
4. **Enhancing Embedder Capabilities:** Traditional embedders are trained for single-shot relevance ranking. There is a motivation to extend their capabilities to understand the context of previously retrieved information and to dynamically adjust their retrieval strategy, thereby performing a more sophisticated "search-based reasoning."
5. **Incorporating Temporal and Positional Awareness:** For narrative texts or tasks requiring sequential understanding, simply retrieving relevant chunks may not be enough. The temporal or relative positional relationship between facts is critical. Current embedders often lack an explicit mechanism to leverage this information during multi-step retrieval.

To address these motivations, the specific contributions proposed in this work are:

- To propose a novel method for training a multi-step retrieval agent using temporal difference reinforcement learning.
- To achieve state-of-the-art results on benchmarks requiring commonsense reasoning and Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) tasks over ultra-long contexts (up to 10 million tokens).
- To introduce a new mechanism for incorporating temporal information into the multi-step embedder, enabling temporal reasoning during retrieval and ensuring generalization to long contexts at inference time.

By focusing on fine-tuning the embedder with reinforcement learning, Q-RAG aims to provide a computationally lighter, yet highly effective, solution for complex, long-context retrieval tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Q-RAG proposes a novel approach to multi-step retrieval by fine-tuning an embedder model using reinforcement learning (RL). The core idea is to train an agent that performs iterative retrieval directly in the latent space of text chunk embeddings, thereby avoiding the high computational cost associated with fine-tuning a large LLM.

#### 3.1. Multi-step Retrieval as a Markov Decision Process (MDP)

The process of multi-step retrieval is formally modeled as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by a tuple (S, A, p, r, γ), where:
- **Context (C):** A long document pre-segmented into non-overlapping text chunks C = {c^(i)}_m_i=1.
- **Query (q):** An initial question or claim.
- **Goal:** To identify information in C necessary to produce the correct answer (y).
- **State Space (S):** An ordered list s_t = ord([q, a_0..., a_{t-1}]) comprising the initial query and all previously selected chunks, sorted by their original document order. The initial state s_0 contains only the query.
- **Action Space (A):** At each step t, the action set A_t consists of the chunks in C that have not yet been selected (A_t = C \ {a_0..., a_{t-1}}). An action a_t selects one chunk.
- **Transition Function (p):** Deterministic, transitioning from s_t to s_{t+1} = ord([s_t, a_t]) upon selecting action a_t.
- **Reward Function (r):** A sparse terminal reward. If a set of ground-truth support facts F* ⊆ C is provided, the reward is 1 if all support facts are included in the final state, and 0 otherwise. Intermediate steps yield a reward of 0.
- **Discount Factor (γ):** A value in that weights future rewards.
- **Termination:** An episode ends when a predefined step budget T is reached or a special STOP action is taken.

#### 3.2. Value-Based Reinforcement Learning for Embedder Fine-tuning

Q-RAG employs maximum-entropy reinforcement learning to train the retrieval agent. The Q-function, Q_π(s, a), is approximated using two neural network embedders:
- **State Embedder (E_s(s_t; θ_1)):** Produces a vector embedding for the current state s_t.
- **Action Embedder (E_a(a_i, i; θ_2)):** Produces a vector embedding for a candidate chunk a_i, incorporating its content and its document-position index i using rotary position embeddings.

The Q-value for retrieving a chunk is estimated by the inner product of these two embeddings: Q_θ(s, a_i) = ⟨E_s(s; θ_1), E_a(a_i, i; θ_2)⟩. This factorization is supported by theoretical convergence guarantees provided in Appendix A.
The agent's policy π(a_t|s_t) is a Boltzmann policy, where chunk selection probability is proportional to the exponential of its Q-value, scaled by a temperature parameter α that controls exploration.

The training algorithm is based on an adaptation of the PQN method (Policy-based Q-Network), which is an on-policy value-based approach. Key modifications in Q-RAG include the use of soft value functions (from maximum-entropy RL) and target networks (common in Deep Q-Networks for stability).
The training objective is to minimize the mean squared error between the predicted Q-value and a λ-return target, G_λ_t, which provides a more stable and faster learning signal than a one-step return. Target network parameters (θ') are updated slowly using an exponential moving average (EMA) of the online network parameters (θ).

#### 3.3. Temporal Reasoning for Long-Context Search

To enable the agent to reason about the temporal or sequential relationships of facts, Q-RAG introduces a relative positional encoding for chunks. When dealing with narrative texts, the absolute position of a chunk might be less important than its position relative to facts already retrieved.
- At each step t, if S_t = {i_1 <... < i_k} are the sorted document indices of selected chunks, these indices partition the document into k+1 intervals.
- A relative positional mapping ρ_t: N → R+ is assigned to every chunk index. This mapping identifies the interval a chunk belongs to and preserves its relative order within that interval.
- Formally, ρ_t(i) is calculated based on the interval boundaries and a linear interpolation within the interval, using parameters δ (inter-interval step) and ℓ (within-interval resolution).
- This relative position ρ_t(i) replaces the absolute position i in the action embedder: E_a(a_i, i; θ_2) ⇒ E_a(a_i, ρ_t(i); θ_2). This allows the Q-function to explicitly leverage the spatial relationship of candidate chunks to already retrieved evidence.

#### 3.4. Computational Efficiency

The Q-RAG architecture prioritizes efficiency:
- **Training:** Only the embedder is fine-tuned, not the LLM. This significantly reduces computational resources. Training for a single model is reported to take approximately 12 hours on a single A100-80GB GPU.
- **Inference:** The LLM Answerer sees only the retrieved chunks, making its time and memory complexity O(1) with respect to the original long context length (N). The retrieval agent's chunk embedding is O(N), state embedding is O(1), and search policy (inner product with all action embeddings) is O(N) in a naive implementation, or sub-linear with kNN methods. Overall time complexity for retrieval is O(N). Space complexity for storing chunk embeddings is O(N), but GPU memory is managed by capping batch sizes during action embedder forward passes.
- **Planning for Multi-step Retrieval:** An optional planning component (Plan Q-RAG) can use beam search, where candidates are ranked by the Q-function. This is computationally cheaper than transformer-based rerankers as Q-values are derived from pre-computed embeddings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Q-RAG involved a range of benchmarks, demonstrating its capabilities across commonsense reasoning, Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) tasks, and open-domain multi-hop question answering (QA) over contexts up to 10 million tokens.

#### 4.1. Commonsense Reasoning on Ultra-Long Contexts (BabiLong)

- Q-RAG achieved the highest average performance across BabiLong tasks (Q1-QA5) when operating with ultra-long contexts ranging from 1 million to 10 million tokens. This indicates strong generalization to extended context lengths compared to specialized long-context methods such as Titans, Atlas, ARMT, and RMT.
- On QA3, the most challenging subtask requiring multi-step search and temporal awareness, Q-RAG showed minimal performance degradation with increasing context length. This contrasted with other long-context approaches, which exhibited greater performance drops. The largest performance gap over baselines was observed on this specific subtask.

#### 4.2. Needle-in-a-Haystack and Long Context QA (RULER)

- Q-RAG attained near-perfect performance (100% accuracy in most cases) on all NIAH subtasks (Single-needle, Multi-keys, Multi-values, Multi-query) for context lengths up to 1 million tokens. The embedder was trained on 4K-length documents and demonstrated robust generalization to much longer contexts without loss of accuracy.
- For the Multi-hop QA subtask within RULER, Q-RAG exhibited significantly better results than all tested baselines across all evaluated context lengths, with some degradation observed only at 1M tokens.

#### 4.3. Open-Domain Question Answering (HotPotQA and MuSiQue)

- On the HotPotQA dataset, Q-RAG achieved fact retrieval accuracy on par with Beam Retriever (Fact F1 0.93 vs 0.97) and surpassed other baselines. Its answer generation (Ans F1 0.76) was also competitive, matching the performance of the LLM-fine-tuning method Search-R1.
- On the out-of-distribution MuSiQue dataset, Q-RAG demonstrated superior performance over all alternatives in both fact retrieval and answer generation (Ans F1 0.52 for Q-RAG vs. 0.40 for Beam Retriever and 0.51 for Search-R1), indicating better generalization capabilities.
- An additional variant, Plan Q-RAG (combining Q-RAG value function with beam search planning), showed similar competitive performance.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on BabiLong-QA3 investigated the impact of architectural choices:
- **Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning:** `Multi-step RAG w.o. FT` (no fine-tuning) and `Multi-step RAG w. SFT` (supervised fine-tuning) performed significantly worse (Fact F1 around 15-20%) compared to Q-RAG (Fact F1 ~97%). This highlights the necessity and effectiveness of RL fine-tuning for multi-step retrieval.
- **Target Networks:** `Q-RAG w.o. Target` showed highly unstable training and substantially lower performance (Fact F1 ~75-79% with large variance), underscoring the importance of target networks for stability in PQN-based value learning.
- **Soft-Q Learning:** `Q-RAG w.o. Soft-Q` resulted in lower performance (Fact F1 ~93-95%) than the full Q-RAG, demonstrating the benefit of entropy regularization and the soft value formulation for retrieval performance.

#### 4.5. Sensitivity to Retrieval Budget and Early Stopping

- Experiments on HotPotQA revealed that increasing the number of retrieval steps from 2 to 3 improved both correct fact retrieval and overall answer quality across different LLMs (Qwen3-4B, 14B, 32B). This suggests that within a reasonable range, answer accuracy benefits from retrieving sufficient correct chunks and is not substantially degraded by the inclusion of some noisy chunks.
- Analysis of a Q-value threshold-based early stopping policy showed a trade-off. Moderate thresholds (e.g., Q_threshold ≈ 0.1-0.3 for HotPotQA, 0.2-0.6 for BabiLong QA2) achieved a high proportion of "perfect stops" (where all ground-truth chunks are collected without unnecessary extra steps) and maintained strong Fact F1 and Ans F1 scores, while reducing the average episode length. ROC curves indicated good performance for the stopping rule (AUC of 0.96 for HotPotQA and 0.97 for BabiLong QA2).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Q-RAG research introduces a method for multi-step retrieval that offers several practical and theoretical implications, potentially impacting the development and deployment of LLM-based applications.

#### 5.1. Resource Efficiency and Accessibility

One of the most significant contributions of Q-RAG is its computational efficiency. By fine-tuning only the embedder model using reinforcement learning, rather than the entire LLM, the method drastically reduces the computational resources required for training. The reported training time of approximately 12 hours on a single A100-80GB GPU contrasts with the multiple A100 GPUs typically required for LLM fine-tuning approaches. This makes advanced multi-step RAG accessible to a broader range of researchers and practitioners who may not have access to large-scale computing clusters.

#### 5.2. Flexibility and Compatibility

Since the LLM itself remains frozen, Q-RAG can be paired with any pre-trained or proprietary LLM of varying sizes. This flexibility allows users to leverage powerful, readily available LLMs without incurring the cost and complexity of fine-tuning them for retrieval-specific tasks. This decoupling also future-proofs the retrieval component, allowing it to benefit from advances in LLM architecture without requiring re-training.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance on Long-Context Reasoning

Q-RAG demonstrates strong performance on tasks requiring reasoning over ultra-long contexts, including commonsense reasoning (BabiLong) and Needle-in-a-Haystack scenarios (RULER) for documents up to 10 million tokens. Its ability to maintain performance with minimal degradation at extreme context lengths, particularly on complex multi-step reasoning tasks, addresses a critical limitation of many current LLM and RAG systems. This capability is crucial for applications dealing with extensive legal documents, scientific literature, or historical archives.

#### 5.4. Robust Multi-Step Retrieval

The method's competitive results on open-domain multi-hop QA benchmarks like HotPotQA and its superior performance on out-of-distribution data like MuSiQue indicate the robustness and generalization capabilities of the learned multi-step retrieval strategy. This suggests that Q-RAG can effectively extract and synthesize distributed information to answer complex questions across diverse domains.

#### 5.5. Novel Temporal Reasoning Mechanism

The introduction of a relative positional encoding within the action embedder is a novel contribution. This mechanism allows the retrieval agent to explicitly consider the spatial and temporal relationships between candidate chunks and already retrieved information. This is particularly impactful for narrative texts or tasks where sequential understanding is critical, enabling more nuanced and accurate fact retrieval.

#### 5.6. Improved Understanding of RAG Components

The comprehensive ablation study provides insights into the necessity of various components of Q-RAG, confirming the benefits of RL fine-tuning, target networks, and soft Q-learning. This contributes to a better understanding of how to design and optimize multi-step retrieval systems. The analysis of early stopping also offers practical guidance on controlling retrieval budget while maximizing accuracy.

In summary, Q-RAG presents a computationally efficient, flexible, and high-performing solution for multi-step retrieval in long-context scenarios. Its potential impact lies in making advanced RAG techniques more accessible, scalable, and effective for real-world applications requiring deep contextual understanding and complex reasoning with large language models.
