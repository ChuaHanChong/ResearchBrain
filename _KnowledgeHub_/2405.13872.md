# Image-of-Thought Prompting for Visual Reasoning Refinement in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Westlake University, City University of Hong Kong, and Johns Hopkins University developed Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting, a method that allows Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to autonomously generate and utilize both visual and textual rationales for complex reasoning. This approach improved MLLM performance in visual question-answering tasks across benchmarks like MMBench, MME, and MMVet, particularly enhancing cognitive reasoning capabilities without requiring model fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from 'superficial integration' of visual data, often relying predominantly on textual rationales.
- MLLMs struggle with hallucination and lack genuine visual grounding in their reasoning processes, as their internal thinking remains largely language-centric rather than deeply engaging with visual information.
- Current multimodal reasoning approaches often require pre-dictated external tools or fixed templates, preventing MLLMs from achieving true automatic, step-by-step multimodal thinking.

## Method

- ==Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting== enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to generate and leverage both visual and textual rationales in a step-by-step manner.
- The method empowers MLLMs to autonomously select and apply external image processing tools (e.g., FastSAM, GroundingDINO, PIL-based functions) to extract ==visual rationales== (e.g., segmented objects, zoomed areas).
- A two-stage process involves ==Chain-of-Multimodal-Rationales Generation== where MLLMs break down problems into sub-goals and create multimodal rationales, followed by ==Hybrid-Rationale-based Answer Refining== to produce the final answer.

## Results

- Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting consistently improved the performance of GPT-4o and Gemini-Pro-1.5 across MMBench, MME, and MMVet benchmarks, with GPT-4o seeing an increase from **86.2%** to **87.6%** on MMBench (dev).
- Notable performance gains were observed in higher-level cognitive tasks, such as 'Cognition' on MME (**+5.6%** for GPT-4o, **+23.9%** for Gemini-Pro-1.5) and categories requiring precise spatial understanding like 'Physical Relation' (**+20.98%**) on MMBench.
- An ablation study confirmed the necessity of visual rationales, showing performance declines without them, while analysis of action statistics revealed MLLMs predominantly use 'Object Detection', 'Segmentation', and 'Zoom-in' to process visual information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-like multimodal reasoning benefits from direct visual engagement, which Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting emulates by enabling MLLMs to autonomously design and execute visual processing operations.
> - Integrating visual rationales (processed images) with textual rationales is crucial for grounding MLLM reasoning, mitigating issues like hallucination, and enhancing performance in complex visual tasks.
> - The 'training-free' nature of IoT makes it an efficient and highly adaptable method for improving MLLM interpretability and performance without the need for extensive fine-tuning.
