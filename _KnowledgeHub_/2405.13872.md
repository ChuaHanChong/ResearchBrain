# Image-of-Thought Prompting for Visual Reasoning Refinement in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Westlake University, City University of Hong Kong, and Johns Hopkins University developed Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting, a method that allows Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to autonomously generate and utilize both visual and textual rationales for complex reasoning. This approach improved MLLM performance in visual question-answering tasks across benchmarks like MMBench, MME, and MMVet, particularly enhancing cognitive reasoning capabilities without requiring model fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from 'superficial integration' of visual data, often relying predominantly on textual rationales.
- MLLMs struggle with hallucination and lack genuine visual grounding in their reasoning processes, as their internal thinking remains largely language-centric rather than deeply engaging with visual information.
- Current multimodal reasoning approaches often require pre-dictated external tools or fixed templates, preventing MLLMs from achieving true automatic, step-by-step multimodal thinking.

## Method

- ==Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting== enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to generate and leverage both visual and textual rationales in a step-by-step manner.
- The method empowers MLLMs to autonomously select and apply external image processing tools (e.g., FastSAM, GroundingDINO, PIL-based functions) to extract ==visual rationales== (e.g., segmented objects, zoomed areas).
- A two-stage process involves ==Chain-of-Multimodal-Rationales Generation== where MLLMs break down problems into sub-goals and create multimodal rationales, followed by ==Hybrid-Rationale-based Answer Refining== to produce the final answer.

## Results

- Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting consistently improved the performance of GPT-4o and Gemini-Pro-1.5 across MMBench, MME, and MMVet benchmarks, with GPT-4o seeing an increase from **86.2%** to **87.6%** on MMBench (dev).
- Notable performance gains were observed in higher-level cognitive tasks, such as 'Cognition' on MME (**+5.6%** for GPT-4o, **+23.9%** for Gemini-Pro-1.5) and categories requiring precise spatial understanding like 'Physical Relation' (**+20.98%**) on MMBench.
- An ablation study confirmed the necessity of visual rationales, showing performance declines without them, while analysis of action statistics revealed MLLMs predominantly use 'Object Detection', 'Segmentation', and 'Zoom-in' to process visual information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-like multimodal reasoning benefits from direct visual engagement, which Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting emulates by enabling MLLMs to autonomously design and execute visual processing operations.
> - Integrating visual rationales (processed images) with textual rationales is crucial for grounding MLLM reasoning, mitigating issues like hallucination, and enhancing performance in complex visual tasks.
> - The 'training-free' nature of IoT makes it an efficient and highly adaptable method for improving MLLM interpretability and performance without the need for extensive fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have ushered in a new era of AI capabilities, particularly in complex reasoning tasks. A significant breakthrough in this domain has been the advent of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which encourages LLMs to articulate intermediate reasoning steps, thereby improving both performance and interpretability. This success has naturally led researchers to explore how these powerful reasoning techniques can be extended to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which process information from both text and images.

However, applying CoT directly to MLLMs presents unique challenges. Existing approaches, such as MMCoT and DDCoT, primarily focus on generating *textual* rationales. While these methods show promise, they often suffer from "superficial integration" of visual data, where visual information is not deeply synthesized with the textual reasoning. This can lead to issues like hallucination, where models generate plausible but incorrect or unsubstantiated content because their reasoning remains predominantly language-centric, rather than genuinely grounded in visual reality. Other efforts have involved invoking external detection tools or designing agents that follow fixed template rules to process multimodal information. However, these methods often pre-dictate the tools and sequences, failing to achieve true "automatic step-by-step thinking" within MLLMs themselves.

This paper positions itself directly in this critical gap. The authors highlight that human reasoning with multimodal data often involves directly interacting with visual information—creating drafts, marking figures, and intuitively processing visual cues rather than solely transferring visual information to text. Current MLLMs lack this intuitive, direct visual engagement.

The Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting method proposed in this paper seeks to bridge this gap by enabling MLLMs to automatically design and execute visual information extraction operations step-by-step. By integrating both visual and textual rationales, IoT aims to move beyond superficial multimodal reasoning towards a more integrated and perceptual approach that mirrors human cognition. This represents a significant shift from relying solely on textual interpretation or external, pre-programmed tools, pushing the boundaries of MLLM capabilities towards more robust, interpretable, and human-like multimodal reasoning. The "train-free" nature of IoT also distinguishes it, offering a more flexible and less resource-intensive solution compared to fine-tuning-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the capability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in tackling complex multimodal reasoning problems, particularly in visual question-answering (VQA) tasks. This enhancement is sought through the innovative **Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting method**, which enables MLLMs to generate and leverage both visual and textual rationales concurrently.

The core motivations driving this research stem from several critical observations and limitations of existing MLLM reasoning paradigms:

- **Mimicking Human Multimodal Cognition:** The authors are deeply motivated by the way humans naturally process multimodal information. When confronted with complex visual information, such as figures or geometric problems, humans don't solely convert visual data into text for reasoning. Instead, they often directly interact with the visual medium—sketching, highlighting, or focusing on specific areas—to construct a chain of thought that integrates both visual and linguistic understanding. The paper aims to develop a method that mimics this intuitive human approach to multimodal reasoning.

- **Addressing Limitations of Text-Centric CoT in MLLMs:** While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has proven highly effective for Large Language Models (LLMs) in text-based reasoning, its direct application to MLLMs often results in "superficial integration" of visual data. Current MLLMs tend to over-rely on textual interpretations, leading to issues like **hallucination** (generating plausible but factually incorrect content) because their reasoning is not sufficiently grounded in visual reality. A key motivation is to overcome this limitation by ensuring that reasoning steps are firmly anchored in visual evidence.

- **Achieving MLLM-Centric Automatic Step-by-Step Thinking:** Existing multimodal CoT approaches often involve external tools or predefined sequences for visual processing. The authors seek to empower the MLLM itself to autonomously design the necessary visual and textual steps, selecting appropriate image processing operations based on the input question and image. This "MLLM-centric" reasoning aims for a more genuine and flexible step-by-step thinking process without artificial pre-dictates.

- **Improving Interpretability and Transparency:** By explicitly generating multimodal rationales (both visual cues and corresponding textual explanations at each step), the IoT method aims to make the MLLM's internal reasoning process more transparent and understandable to human observers. This enhanced interpretability is crucial for debugging, building trust, and analyzing the cognitive processes of these complex models.

- **Developing a Training-Free and Data-Free Paradigm:** A significant practical motivation is to create a method that is "training-free" and "data-free." This eliminates the need for expensive and time-consuming fine-tuning processes typically required by other models, making IoT a more accessible and efficient solution for enhancing MLLM performance and interpretability across various visual understanding tasks.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a robust, interpretable, and efficient prompting strategy that allows MLLMs to engage more profoundly with visual information, thereby improving their accuracy and reliability in complex multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting method is a novel, train-free approach that enhances MLLMs' visual reasoning by compelling them to generate and utilize both visual and textual rationales in a step-by-step manner. The methodology is structured into two main stages: Chain-of-Multimodal-Rationales Generation and Hybrid-Rationale-based Answer Refining.

#### 3.1. Concept Definition

The paper first defines key concepts:
- **Multimodal Reasoning:** Tasks requiring the integration of inputs from multiple modalities (e.g., images and text) to produce accurate responses.
- **Hybrid Rationales:** A combination of textual rationales (step-by-step reasoning generated by MLLMs) and visual rationales (extracted relevant visual features from images, guided by textual reasoning), which enhance interpretability and accuracy.

#### 3.2. Image-of-Thought Prompting: Chain-of-Multimodal-Rationales

This stage focuses on enabling the MLLM to autonomously design and execute a sequence of image processing actions, thereby generating multimodal rationales.

- **MLLM-Centric Reasoning with Actions:** The MLLM is given the capability to break down complex visual reasoning problems into discrete sub-goals (SG). For each sub-goal, the MLLM intelligently selects and applies suitable external image processing tools (Actions) to perform specific manipulations and generate visual rationales (VR). These VRs are then seamlessly integrated with textual rationales (TR), also generated by the MLLM, to form comprehensive Multimodal Rationales (MR). The MLLM orchestrates the entire process, from action planning to execution, ensuring consistency.

- **Action Choices (Toolbox):** To facilitate intricate visual analysis, the IoT method incorporates a curated set of image processing actions. The specific tools used for these actions include:
 - **Segmentation / Edge Detection:** Utilizes **FastSAM** to identify and delineate object boundaries, aiding recognition and spatial analysis.
 - **Geometric Transformations (Zoom-in):** Leverages **GroundingDINO** to identify and zoom into specific areas of interest for detailed examination of subtle features.
 - **Dense Object Detection / Referring Object Detection:** Employs **GroundingDINO** to detect objects based on textual descriptions, linking visual elements with textual queries.
 - **Spatial Ruler:** Custom functions built on the **Python Imaging Library (PIL)** to divide images into quadrants, aiding in perceiving spatial relationships.
 - **Color Space Conversion:** Custom functions based on **PIL** to transform image color spaces, enhancing contrast and aiding segmentation or categorization.
 The MLLM dynamically determines which action to apply based on the sub-goal. For instance, if a sub-goal is "identify the buses," the MLLM selects "Object Detection."

- **Multimodal Rationales Generation:** After executing an action to obtain a VR, the MLLM generates a corresponding TR for that step. The MR is then formulated as a triple: `<sub-goal, visual rationale, textual rationale>`. This explicit alignment ensures that each reasoning step is grounded in both modalities.

#### 3.2.2. Hybrid-Rationale-based Answer Refining

Once the sequence of multimodal rationales is generated, it is used to refine the MLLM's final answer.

- **Chain-of-Image-Thought:** The generated MRs are concatenated sequentially to form a Multimodal Rationale Series (MRS). This MRS serves as a transparent visualization of the MLLM's internal reasoning logic across both modalities.

- **Hybrid Rationale Refining:** The complete MRS, along with the original question, is then fed back into the MLLM. The MLLM leverages this comprehensive multimodal context to refine its decision-making process and produce the final answer. This iterative self-refinement is a key aspect of the "training-free" paradigm, as the model refines its output using the autonomously generated rationales without requiring additional data or fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. IoT Algorithm (Algorithm 1 Summary)

The process can be summarized algorithmically:
1. **Input:** Question (Q), Image (I).
2. **Initialize:** Empty Multimodal Rationale Series (MRS).
3. **Step Decomposition:** MLLM `auto_designs` sub-goals (SG_1..., SG_n) from Q.
4. **Iterative Rationale Generation:** For each SG_i:
 - MLLM `selects_action` (Action_i) based on SG_i.
 - `executes` Action_i on I to get Visual Rationale (VR_i).
 - MLLM `generates_text_rationale` (TR_i) for SG_i.
 - Forms Multimodal Rationale (MR_i) = `<SG_i, VR_i, TR_i>`.
 - Appends MR_i to MRS.
5. **Rationale Concatenation:** MLLM `concatenates_rationales` to form the final MRS.
6. **Answer Refinement:** MLLM `refines_answer` using Q and MRS to produce the final Answer (A).
7. **Output:** A, MRS.

#### 3.4. IoT Prompting Properties

The method highlights three key properties:
1. **Systematic Integration of Image Processing:** Grounds reasoning steps in visual evidence.
2. **Effective Dissection of Data:** Equips MLLMs to analyze visual and textual elements efficiently.
3. **Improved Interpretability:** Provides transparent visualization of reasoning steps.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Tasks and Datasets:** Evaluated on Visual Question-Answering (VQA) benchmarks: MMBench, MME, and MMVet, which cover diverse abilities (perception, cognition, spatial awareness, etc.).
- **Baselines:** GPT-4o and Gemini-Pro-1.5, chosen for their strong visual reasoning and multi-round conversational capabilities.
- **Toolbox Implementation:** Specific use of FastSAM, GroundingDINO, and Python Imaging Library.
- **Prompting Details:** Carefully crafted prompts guide the MLLM to design steps and select tools. GPT-4 API settings (temperature=0, max_tokens=500) were used for stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting significantly enhances the zero-shot visual reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly GPT-4o and Gemini-Pro-1.5.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvement

- **Consistent Gains:** IoT prompting consistently improved performance across all three major benchmarks: MMBench, MME, and MMVet. For instance, on MMBench (dev), GPT-4o saw its accuracy increase from 86.2% to 87.6% with IoT, while Gemini-Pro-1.5 improved from 71.0% to 79.6%.
- **Cognition and Reasoning Boost:** The most notable improvements were observed in categories demanding higher-level cognitive reasoning.
 - On the **MME benchmark**, IoT had a substantial positive impact on **Cognition tasks** for both GPT-4o (+5.6%) and Gemini-Pro-1.5 (+23.9%). This suggests that IoT's explicit expansion of the question-answering process provides valuable contextual information, thereby enhancing the models' ability to process and reason with complex cognitive challenges.
 - For **MMBench**, GPT-4o showed significant gains in "Physical Relation" (+20.98%), "Object Localization" (+16.34%), and "Spatial Relationship" (+12.45%). Similarly, Gemini-Pro-1.5 saw dramatic increases in "Action Recognition" (+30.24%), "Image Emotion" (+24.81%), and "Image Topic" (+17.92%). These improvements highlight IoT's effectiveness in tasks requiring precise object identification, spatial understanding, and the interpretation of complex visual interactions.
 - On **MMVet**, IoT improved GPT-4o's performance across various categories, including OCR, Knowledge, Spatial Awareness (+8.19%), and Math (+7.61%), further affirming its broad utility in reasoning tasks.

#### 4.2. Observed Limitations

- **Resolution and Information Loss:** The paper notes a slight performance loss in certain categories for both models. This is hypothesized to be due to the multi-image processing approach, which might lead to reduced resolution and information quality in the processed visual rationales, potentially hindering the model in tasks sensitive to fine-grained visual details.

#### 4.3. Rationale Ablation Study

A crucial ablation study on the MMVet dataset investigated the impact of hybrid rationales by comparing IoT (visual + textual rationales) against a text-only rationale approach.
- **Importance of Visual Rationales:** Removing visual rationales resulted in a decline in model performance, particularly in "Knowledge" and "Spatial Awareness" categories (e.g., Knowledge: -6.76%, Spatial Awareness: -4.85%). This empirically validates the paper's core hypothesis that textual rationales alone are insufficient to effectively capture image-specific knowledge and spatial information, underscoring the critical role of visual rationales in grounding multimodal reasoning.
- **Unexpected Result for Math:** Interestingly, for "Math" questions, pure textual rationales sometimes outperformed hybrid rationales and zero-shot approaches. The authors speculate that visual rationales might introduce additional symbols or visual noise, potentially leading to visual hallucination and negatively impacting the model's abstract mathematical reasoning. This suggests a nuanced interaction between modalities depending on the task's nature.

#### 4.4. Action Statistics

An analysis of the types of actions chosen by the MLLM in the MMBench Dev dataset provided insights into its "cognitive process."
- **Dominant Actions:** The most frequently employed actions were "Object Detection," followed by "Segmentation" and "Zoom-in." This pattern mirrors human visual processing, where identifying and isolating a target object often precedes detailed analysis.
- **Less Frequent Actions:** "Spatial Ruler" and "Color Transform," which operate on the entire scene or involve more specialized visual properties, were used less often. However, these actions proved particularly beneficial for specific categories:
 - "Image Quality" questions benefited from "Color Transform" for examining factors like brightness or noise.
 - "Spatial Relation" tasks found "Spatial Ruler" valuable for comprehending object arrangement and positions, aligning with how humans might use measurements for precise spatial analysis.

Overall, the findings strongly support the effectiveness of IoT prompting in enhancing MLLM performance in complex visual reasoning tasks by integrating a human-like, step-by-step multimodal reasoning process. The ablation study further validates the necessity of visual rationales, while the action statistics provide valuable insights into the model's adaptive strategy for visual information extraction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Image-of-Thought (IoT) prompting method marks a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with far-reaching implications for AI research and applications. Its significance can be summarized across several key dimensions:

- **Pioneering Multimodal Rationales:** The most significant contribution of this work is its novelty in explicitly generating and utilizing *multimodal rationales*—combining both visual and textual explanations in a step-by-step reasoning chain. Unlike previous Chain-of-Thought (CoT) extensions for MLLMs that primarily relied on textual rationales or superficial visual integration, IoT provides a deeper, more integrated approach. This sets a new paradigm for how MLLMs can process and explain their reasoning in complex visual tasks.

- **Enhanced Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** IoT prompting demonstrably improves MLLMs' performance in challenging visual reasoning tasks across various benchmarks (MMBench, MME, MMVet). By compelling MLLMs to directly engage with visual information through designed image processing operations, the method mitigates common issues like hallucination and over-reliance on linguistic biases. This results in more robust and accurate answers, particularly for tasks requiring precise object localization, spatial understanding, and complex attribute comparisons, which have been traditional weak points for MLLMs.

- **Improved Interpretability and Transparency:** By visualizing each reasoning step with both the processed image (visual rationale) and its corresponding textual explanation, IoT makes the MLLM's internal "thought process" transparent. This human-comprehensible breakdown of the decision-making chain is invaluable for researchers and developers to understand, debug, and build trust in these powerful models. It facilitates a deeper analysis of how MLLMs "think" and where they might go wrong, bridging the gap between black-box AI and explainable AI.

- **Training-Free and Efficient Paradigm:** The "train-free" nature of IoT prompting is a substantial advantage. It eliminates the need for expensive and time-consuming fine-tuning processes, which are often required for training specialized multimodal models or adapting existing LLMs to multimodal tasks. This makes the method highly adaptable, resource-efficient, and readily applicable to a wide range of existing MLLMs without significant computational overhead, promoting broader adoption and experimentation.

- **Mimicking Human Cognition:** The method's inspiration from human multimodal reasoning—where individuals often interact directly with visual aids to construct a logical chain of thought—is a notable aspect. By allowing the MLLM to "autonomously design" visual information extraction operations, IoT moves closer to emulating human-like cognitive processes in AI.

- **Extensibility and Future Potential:** The framework's design, which allows for the selection and integration of "extendable external tools" at each step, provides immense flexibility. This means that as new image processing models or tools emerge, they can be seamlessly incorporated into the IoT framework, continuously enhancing its capabilities.
 - **Robotics:** A direct potential impact highlighted by the authors is in robotics research. IoT could enable robots to autonomously design action sequences based on plausible hybrid rationales, leading to more intelligent and adaptive robotic systems for complex real-world tasks.
 - **Broader Applications:** Beyond VQA, the core principle of integrating dynamically chosen visual and textual rationales could be applied to various multimodal AI challenges, including visual storytelling, complex scene understanding, medical image analysis, and human-computer interaction, where transparent and grounded reasoning is paramount.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (e.g., potential resolution loss, specific cases of visual hallucination in abstract tasks like math), the foundational contribution of IoT prompting represents a significant leap forward in making MLLMs more capable, interpretable, and aligned with human-like reasoning processes. It opens new avenues for research into truly integrated multimodal intelligence.
