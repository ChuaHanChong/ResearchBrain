# Everything in Its Place: Benchmarking Spatial Intelligence of Text-to-Image Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces SpatialGenEval, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the spatial intelligence of Text-to-Image (T2I) models using information-dense prompts and fine-grained multi-choice questions. The paper reveals that T2I models possess only rudimentary spatial reasoning capabilities, with top models achieving around 60% accuracy, and demonstrates that fine-tuning with the SpatialT2I dataset improves performance by 4.2% to 5.7%.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Text-to-Image (T2I) models frequently fail to accurately represent complex spatial relationships, leading to misplaced objects, incorrect orientations, and illogical interactions.
- Existing T2I benchmarks are insufficient for diagnosing nuanced spatial deficiencies, as they rely on short prompts and coarse-grained evaluation metrics, overlooking higher-order spatial reasoning.
- This limitation points to a fundamental deficit in 'spatial intelligence' (perception, reasoning, interaction) in current T2I models, hindering their ability to generate coherent and logically structured scenes.

## Method

- Introduces ==SpatialGenEval==, a novel benchmark consisting of ==1,230 long, information-dense prompts== across 25 real-world scenes, each evaluated with 10 omni-dimensional multi-choice question-answer pairs targeting specific spatial sub-domains.
- Develops a ==hierarchical framework of spatial intelligence== encompassing 4 domains and 10 sub-domains (e.g., spatial position, occlusion, causality) for systematic evaluation.
- Constructs ==SpatialT2I==, a dataset of 15,400 text-image pairs with carefully rewritten prompts, used for supervised fine-tuning of state-of-the-art foundation models to improve their spatial generation capabilities.

## Results

- The highest overall score on SpatialGenEval was approximately **60%**, with open-source Qwen-Image (**60.6%**) closely matching closed-source Seed Dream 4.0 (**62.7%**).
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using the SpatialT2I dataset consistently improved the performance of models like Stable Diffusion-XL, UniWorld-V1, and OmniGen2 on SpatialGenEval by **+4.2%**, **+5.7%**, and **+4.4%** respectively.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that higher-quality data subsets within SpatialT2I contribute more significantly to performance gains, and increasing the quantity of spatial-aware training data consistently improves spatial intelligence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current T2I models exhibit only rudimentary spatial intelligence, with even the most advanced models achieving around 60% accuracy on the SpatialGenEval benchmark, indicating significant room for improvement.
> - Spatial reasoning, encompassing tasks like spatial comparison and occlusion, is identified as the primary bottleneck, with models often scoring below 30% in these sub-domains.
> - Models leveraging stronger text encoders (based on LLMs) and unified multimodal architectures generally outperform others, highlighting the importance of robust language understanding for complex spatial generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has experienced substantial advancements, with models demonstrating a capacity to produce photorealistic and high-fidelity images. These developments have been largely driven by architectural innovations, evolving from Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to diffusion models, often augmented by large language model (LLM) text encoders or integrated into unified multimodal architectures. Current T2I models exhibit proficiency in compositional generation, effectively rendering specified objects and binding attributes such as color, material, and shape, thereby achieving a high degree of fidelity for basic semantic prompt following.

However, a recognized limitation within this landscape is the inconsistent performance of these models when tasked with handling complex spatial relationships. While they can generate "what" is in a scene, they frequently struggle with accurately depicting "where" objects are located, "how" they are arranged, and "why" they interact. This indicates a gap in their spatial intelligence, encompassing spatial perception, reasoning, and interaction capabilities.

Existing benchmarks for T2I models typically employ short or information-sparse prompts, which primarily focus on verifying the presence of objects, their attributes, or simple binary relations. These evaluations often rely on coarse-grained metrics like classification or limited yes-or-no questions. Consequently, they often do not adequately probe a model's higher-order spatial capabilities, thus overlooking critical deficiencies in complex spatial reasoning and interaction.

This research addresses this gap by introducing a benchmark, SpatialGenEval, designed to systematically evaluate these overlooked aspects of spatial intelligence. By focusing on long, information-dense, and spatial-aware prompts with fine-grained multi-choice evaluations, this work aims to provide a more comprehensive assessment of T2I models' spatial understanding. Furthermore, it explores a data-centric approach to model improvement by constructing a supervised fine-tuning dataset, SpatialT2I, which leverages the principles of spatial-aware data design. This positions the work as a contribution to both the evaluation and enhancement of spatial intelligence in T2I models, guiding future research toward addressing more nuanced visual-language alignment challenges beyond basic object composition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically evaluate the spatial intelligence of Text-to-Image (T2I) models and to explore methods for improving these capabilities. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Limitations in Current T2I Models:** Despite significant progress in generating high-fidelity and photorealistic images, existing T2I models demonstrate limitations when dealing with complex spatial relationships. These models frequently misplace objects, orient them incorrectly, disregard relative numerical comparisons, or fail to render causal interactions. This indicates a deficiency in "spatial intelligence," which encompasses abilities such as spatial perception, reasoning, and interaction within real-world scenes, extending beyond merely generating "what" is in a scene to precisely depicting "where," "how," and "why" elements are arranged.

2. **Overcoming Deficiencies in Current Benchmarks:** Existing T2I benchmarks are largely designed with short or information-sparse prompts. This design confines their evaluative scope to basic object presence, attribute binding, or simple binary relations. Evaluation metrics are often coarse-grained, such as classification or limited yes-or-no questions, which are insufficient for diagnosing subtle failures in complex spatial understanding. These benchmarks thus overlook critical aspects of higher-order spatial reasoning and interaction, failing to provide a comprehensive assessment of a model's true spatial capabilities. The motivation is to create a benchmark that explores long, information-dense, spatial-aware prompts and fine-grained evaluations to address this gap.

3. **Introducing a Comprehensive Spatial Intelligence Framework:** There is a need for a structured approach to define and evaluate spatial intelligence. The paper is motivated to propose a hierarchical framework that decomposes spatial intelligence into foundational, perceptual, reasoning, and interactional domains, covering a comprehensive range of abilities from object position and layout to occlusion and causality. This framework is intended to enable a more granular and diagnostic evaluation.

4. **Demonstrating the Utility of Spatially-Aware Data for Model Improvement:** Beyond evaluation, the research is motivated to show that the principles used for benchmark design (information-dense and spatial-aware data) can be leveraged to create datasets for supervised fine-tuning. This aims to validate a data-centric paradigm as a practical and effective pathway to enhance the spatial intelligence of existing foundation T2I models, shifting focus from purely architectural innovations to data quality and density.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Introduce **SpatialGenEval**, a novel benchmark with 1,230 long, information-dense prompts across 25 real-world scenes, each integrating 10 spatial sub-domains, paired with 10 omni-dimensional multiple-choice question-answer pairs for fine-grained evaluation.
- Systematically **evaluate 23 state-of-the-art T2I models** using SpatialGenEval to identify performance bottlenecks, particularly in higher-order spatial reasoning.
- Construct **SpatialT2I**, a supervised fine-tuning dataset containing 15,400 text-image pairs with rewritten prompts, to demonstrate how spatially-aware data can be used to **improve the spatial intelligence** of existing T2I models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces SpatialGenEval, a benchmark designed to evaluate spatial intelligence, and SpatialT2I, a dataset for improving spatial intelligence in T2I models. The methodology involves a multi-stage process for data construction, model evaluation, and fine-tuning experiments.

#### 3.1. SpatialGenEval Benchmark Design Principles

SpatialGenEval is built upon four core principles:
- **Long & information-dense prompts:** Unlike conventional short prompts, SpatialGenEval employs prompts that are approximately 60 words long, densely packed with multiple and interdependent spatial constraints to simulate real-world complexity and thoroughly probe model capabilities.
- **Omni-dimensional multi-choice questions:** Instead of coarse metrics, each prompt is associated with 10 multiple-choice questions, each targeting a distinct spatial sub-domain. This allows for fine-grained diagnostic evaluation. A crucial "E: None" option is included to allow evaluators to refuse a forced choice when no option is faithful to the generated image, addressing answer leakage.
- **Image-dependent answer:** To prevent "answer leakage" where Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) might answer based on textual cues alone, the evaluation process ensures that the MLLM evaluator only receives the generated image and questions, without the original text-to-image prompt. Human checks are also performed to avoid such leakage.
- **Refuse to answer:** The "E: None" option mitigates forced guessing when none of the provided choices accurately reflect the generated image.

#### 3.2. Focused Aspects of Spatial Intelligence

Spatial intelligence is hierarchically decomposed into 4 primary domains and 10 sub-domains, drawing from spatial cognition and intelligence studies:

- **Spatial Foundation (S1: Object Category, S2: Object Attribution):** Evaluates compositional completeness and attribute binding (e.g., generating specified objects, assigning correct colors/shapes).
- **Spatial Perception (S3: Spatial Position, S4: Spatial Orientation, S5: Spatial Layout):** Assesses the model's ability to interpret and render geometric and relational arrangements (e.g., object localization, facing directions, collective configurations).
- **Spatial Reasoning (S6: Spatial Comparison, S7: Spatial Proximity, S8: Spatial Occlusion):** Probes higher-order cognitive abilities to understand abstract, implicit, and 3D-aware spatial relationships (e.g., relative quantitative attributes, fine-grained distances, partial/full object obscuration).
- **Spatial Interaction (S9: Spatial Motion Interaction, S10: Spatial Causal Interaction):** Evaluates the depiction of dynamic events and physical causality (e.g., objects in motion, cause-effect relationships).

#### 3.3. Benchmark Construction

The construction of SpatialGenEval involves two main stages with human-in-the-loop refinement:

- **Information-Dense & Spatial-Aware Prompt Generation:**
 - **Automated Generation:** Gemini 2.5 Pro is used to synthesize 1,230 prompts across 25 real-world scenes (e.g., Nature, Indoor, Outdoor, Human, Design). Each prompt integrates all 10 spatial sub-domains and targets approximately 60 words in length.
 - **Human Refinement:** Expert annotators review each prompt for stylistic fluency, logical soundness, and consistent coverage of all 10 sub-domains. Ambiguous or unusual words are replaced with common synonyms to focus on spatial reasoning.

- **Omni-Dimensional QAs Generation:**
 - **Automated Generation:** For each of the 1,230 prompts, Gemini 2.5 Pro generates 10 multiple-choice questions, each targeting one specific spatial sub-domain. Each question includes a ground-truth answer and three plausible but incorrect options.
 - **Human Refinement:** Annotators validate QA pairs to ensure questions precisely target sub-domains, prevent answer leakage (where the answer is explicit in the question), and correct phrasing inaccuracies. A programmatic "E: None" option is appended to each question post-validation.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Text-to-Image Models:** 23 state-of-the-art models were evaluated, including diffusion models (e.g., SD-series, PixArt-series, FLUX-series, Qwen-Image), autoregressive models (e.g., OmniGen2, NextStep-1, Infinity), unified models (e.g., Janus-Pro, UniWorld-V1, Bagel), and closed-source models (e.g., DALL-E-3, GPT-Image-1, Nano Banana, Seed Dream 4.0).
- **MLLM as a Judge:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B is used as the primary zero-shot, multiple-choice VQA evaluator. GPT-4o-250306 is used for comparison. The MLLM receives the generated image and 10 questions, selecting the best-matching option from five choices (A-D or E: None).
- **Evaluation Details:** To enhance stability, a 5-round voting mechanism is employed; a response is correct if the ground-truth is selected in at least 4 of 5 rounds. The final score is accuracy per sub-domain.

#### 3.5. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with SpatialT2I

To demonstrate the utility of spatially-aware data beyond evaluation, SpatialT2I dataset is constructed for SFT:
- **Prompt and Omni-dimensional QAs generation:** Another 1,230 prompts and 12,300 QAs are generated following SpatialGenEval principles, ensuring no overlap with the evaluation benchmark.
- **Rewrite prompt to obtain text-image pair:** Images are generated from these prompts using 14 top-performing open-source T2I models (accuracy > 50%). A powerful MLLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) then rewrites the prompts to better match the generated images, ensuring text-image alignment while preserving information density and spatial intelligence dimensions. Data from "Design" scenes (130 prompts) are excluded due to low image quality. This yields 15,400 text-image pairs from 22 scenes.
- **Fine-tuning:** SOTA models (Stable Diffusion-XL, UniWorld-V1, OmniGen2) are fine-tuned using SpatialT2I.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 23 state-of-the-art T2I models on SpatialGenEval and subsequent fine-tuning experiments yielded several findings:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Bottlenecks

- **Spatial Intelligence Challenge:** Even advanced T2I models possess only a rudimentary grasp of complex spatial intelligence, with the highest overall scores remaining around 60%. The gap between leading open-source models (e.g., Qwen-Image at 60.6%) and closed-source models (e.g., Seed Dream 4.0 at 62.7%) is narrowing but spatial intelligence remains a significant challenge for all.
- **Imbalanced Performance:** A distinct performance disparity exists between basic spatial foundation tasks and higher-order spatial intelligence. Models typically achieve scores above 70% for object and attribute generation (spatial foundation) but show significant performance drops on tasks requiring complex thinking in spatial perception, reasoning, and interaction.
- **Spatial Reasoning as Primary Bottleneck:** Spatial reasoning sub-domains (Comparison, Proximity, Occlusion) consistently exhibit the lowest scores, often below 30%, approaching random selection levels (20%). This indicates a fundamental inability of current T2I models to bind semantic properties of objects to the structural logic of a scene, such as relative size or physical layering. This weakness is also where the largest performance gap between open-source and closed-source models is observed.
- **Importance of Text Encoder Capability:** Models incorporating stronger text encoders, particularly those leveraging powerful LLMs, consistently outperform those reliant on standard CLIP encoders. For instance, Qwen-Image (LLM encoder) achieved 60.6%, significantly surpassing models like SD-1.5 (28.5%) and even those using T5 encoders like FLUX.1 and SD-3. This suggests that a deeper understanding of complex, information-dense prompts is crucial for high-fidelity spatial generation.
- **Pathways for Improvement: Scale and Architecture:** Model performance on spatial intelligence generally correlates with model size for diffusion models, with larger models demonstrating better capabilities (e.g., 20B Qwen-Image > 8B SD-3.5-L). Unified multimodal architectures also show promise, achieving comparable scores with greater parameter efficiency by integrating understanding and generative abilities (e.g., 7B Bagel vs. 12B FLUX.1-krea).

#### 4.2. Failure Cases Analysis

Analysis of failure cases revealed a hierarchical difficulty:
- **Basic Composition:** Models generally succeed with low error rates.
- **Visual Perception:** Presents a greater challenge, with errors highest in complex "Nature" scenes (28.5%).
- **Relational Reasoning:** This skill is identified as the primary failure point, with error rates frequently exceeding 35% across models. This suggests a critical weakness in processing relational logic.
- **Motion Interaction:** A less frequent source of error, typically below 18%.

#### 4.3. Benchmark Robustness and Evaluator Consistency

- **Correlation with Other Benchmarks:** Model rankings on SpatialGenEval show strong consistency with meta-rankings from four other major T2I benchmarks (GenEval, DPG-Bench, Wise, TIIF-Bench), validating SpatialGenEval as a reliable indicator of overall generative capability.
- **MLLM Judge Consistency:** Evaluation using both Qwen2.5-VL-72B and GPT-4o-250306 as judges yielded similar model rankings and scores, confirming the robustness of the benchmark and its independence from a single evaluator choice.
- **Human Alignment:** A human alignment study demonstrated that MLLMs, particularly Gemini-2.5-Pro, align well with human judgment on SpatialGenEval, with balanced accuracy scores nearing 80%. Alignment was higher on simpler sub-domains (Spatial Foundation/Perception/Interaction) and slightly lower on Spatial Reasoning, correlating with task difficulty.

#### 4.4. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with SpatialT2I

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** Fine-tuning state-of-the-art models (Stable Diffusion-XL, UniWorld-V1, OmniGen2) with the SpatialT2I dataset consistently resulted in improved spatial abilities. Performance gains were observed across models, including +4.2% for SD-XL, +5.7% for UniWorld-V1, and +4.4% for OmniGen2.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Fine-tuned models generated images with more realistic and accurate depictions of spatial relations.
- **Data Scaling and Quality Impact:** An ablation study showed that higher-quality subsets of SpatialT2I data (generated by higher-performing T2I models) yielded more significant performance gains during fine-tuning. A clear data scaling trend was observed, with performance consistently improving as more spatially-aware training data was incrementally added. This indicates the value of information-dense, spatial-aware data and suggests that further scaling could lead to additional improvements.

#### 4.5. Impact of Prompt Rewriting

- **Enhanced Prompt Decomposition:** Rewriting original prompts to make spatial dimensions more explicit led to performance improvements across T2I models, particularly for those initially struggling with text reasoning (e.g., SD-3.5-L showed a +2.3% gain). This suggests that clarifying prompt instructions can aid models in decomposing complex commands.
- **Greater Gains on Explicit Relations:** Rewriting was most effective for clarifying explicit spatial relationships such as Position, Comparison, and Layout, leading to substantial score increases in these categories.
- **Limited Impact on Implicit Reasoning:** The benefits of prompt rewriting were minimal for complex visual reasoning tasks like Occlusion and Orientation, suggesting that failures in these areas stem from a core lack of 3D and physical reasoning in the generative models themselves, rather than merely text comprehension issues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of text-to-image (T2I) generation and its evaluation in several ways:

#### 5.1. Systematic Evaluation of Spatial Intelligence

The introduction of **SpatialGenEval** provides a novel and systematic benchmark specifically designed to assess the complex spatial intelligence of T2I models. Unlike previous benchmarks that primarily focused on basic object composition or simple attributes, SpatialGenEval uses long, information-dense prompts and omni-dimensional multi-choice questions covering 10 distinct spatial sub-domains. This detailed framework allows for a more granular and diagnostic evaluation of T2I model capabilities.

#### 5.2. Identification of Key Bottlenecks

The extensive evaluation of 23 state-of-the-art T2I models using SpatialGenEval has revealed that while models excel at basic object generation, they exhibit significant deficiencies in higher-order spatial tasks. The research explicitly pinpoints **spatial reasoning** (encompassing comparison, proximity, and occlusion) as the primary bottleneck. This finding is significant as it provides a clear direction for future research, indicating that advancements are needed not just in generating objects, but in accurately understanding and rendering their structural and logical relationships within a scene.

#### 5.3. Validation of a Data-Centric Approach for Improvement

Beyond evaluation, the work demonstrates the utility of spatially-aware data for model improvement. The creation of **SpatialT2I**, a supervised fine-tuning dataset built on the same principles of information density and spatial awareness, has shown consistent performance gains when used to fine-tune existing foundation models. This validates a data-centric paradigm as a practical and effective pathway to enhance spatial intelligence in T2I models, complementing ongoing architectural innovations. The observed data scaling trends further support the potential for continued improvement through expanded and refined datasets.

#### 5.4. Guidance for Future T2I Model Development

The findings provide insights into crucial factors influencing spatial intelligence. The observed correlation between stronger text encoders (LLM-based) and improved spatial performance suggests that deeper semantic comprehension of complex prompts is vital. Additionally, the analysis of failure cases indicates that addressing relational logic and 3D reasoning capabilities in generative models is more critical than linearly improving basic visual perception.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact and Applications

The advancements fostered by this research have implications beyond the immediate field of T2I generation:
- **Enhanced Creative Tools:** Improved spatial intelligence in T2I models can lead to more precise and controllable generative tools for artists, architects, designers, and other creative professionals, allowing them to dictate complex scene layouts and object arrangements with greater fidelity.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI:** A stronger grasp of spatial relationships is a foundational requirement for developing more capable AI agents in robotics and embodied intelligence. Models that can accurately understand and interact with the physical world, depicted in generated images, are a crucial step towards creating intelligent systems that can navigate, manipulate, and reason within real environments.
- **Ethical Considerations:** By transparently identifying the limitations of current AI models, this work promotes the development of more robust and reliable generative AI, fostering responsible innovation in the field.

In summary, this research not only provides a robust framework for evaluating a critical aspect of T2I model performance but also offers a data-driven strategy for addressing identified weaknesses, thereby advancing the capabilities and applicability of generative AI.
