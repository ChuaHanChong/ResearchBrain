# RichMap: A Reachability Map Balancing Precision, Efficiency, and Flexibility for Rich Robot Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> RichMap presents a high-precision robot reachability map that refines grid-based structures using theoretical capacity bounds and an asynchronous, GPU-accelerated construction pipeline. It achieves accuracy on par with compact 4D maps, offers microsecond-level query speeds for large batches, and demonstrates an 11% average success rate improvement in cross-embodiment diffusion policy transfer.

## Problem

- Traditional grid-based reachability maps are inefficient in storage and computation, especially at high spatial or rotational resolutions, due to the curse of dimensionality.
- Compact reachability representations, while efficient, often lack the structural flexibility of grid-based maps, complicating certain downstream applications.
- Achieving rigorous coverage and high prediction accuracy with low false positive rates for robot end-effector reachability remains a challenge across various map types.

## Method

- Developed **RichMap**, a refined ==grid-based reachability map== that discretizes the workspace into 3D cells, storing reachable end-effector orientations based on ==geodesic distance== and ==theoretical capacity bounds==.
- Implemented an asynchronous, GPU-accelerated producer-consumer pipeline for map construction, decoupling CPU-based forward kinematics sampling from batched GPU insertion to achieve high throughput.
- Incorporated rotation range tracking to incrementally represent full SO(3) orientations, enhancing map flexibility and allowing for progressive relaxation of the free wrist assumption.

## Results

- **RichMap** achieved prediction accuracy comparable to compact 4D maps (e.g., **0.975** for Panda) and reduced False Positive Rates by **25-35%** compared to traditional capability maps, further lowering FPR to **~1.6%** at finer resolutions.
- Demonstrated microsecond-level query times (**2-3 μs/query for 106 batch size) for large batches, outperforming sequential counterparts by approximately 18 times.
- Improved cross-embodiment diffusion policy transfer by an average of **10.97%** absolute success rate in a block pushing task, reaching **89.56%** overall with similarity-guided energy landscapes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A refined grid-based reachability map structure, combined with theoretical bounds for orientation storage and GPU acceleration, can balance high precision, efficiency, and structural flexibility.
> - Theoretical capacity bounds for orientation storage on S2 or SO(3) enable rigorous coverage and principled parameter selection, leading to high prediction accuracy and low false positive rates.
> - An asynchronous CPU/GPU pipeline for map construction significantly accelerates the process, making it feasible to build high-resolution maps from massive pose datasets within practical timeframes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics extensively utilizes reachability maps to encode the set of poses an end-effector can attain, serving as fundamental priors for various manipulation tasks such as base placement optimization and motion planning. Historically, the classical spatial-rotational grid structure, which discretizes the robot's workspace into 3D grids and stores reachability information, has been a prevalent approach. However, these grid-based methods face challenges due to the curse of dimensionality, leading to inefficiencies in storage and computation, especially when seeking high resolution or complete kinematic information.

To mitigate these limitations, subsequent research has explored various strategies. Some approaches prioritize compactness by employing analytical methods that leverage mathematical structures of the kinematic manifold, aiming to reduce data redundancy (e.g., RM4D). These methods often simplify the representation or use implicit forms, which can, however, introduce complexity in map structure and potentially hinder fast or broad application due to their specialized nature. Other advancements include learning-based methods that approximate reachability boundaries using neural networks to achieve continuous representations, but these often involve implicit representations that may complicate direct interpretation or specific queries.

RichMap positions itself within this landscape by seeking to optimize the trade-off between precision, efficiency, and flexibility. It refines the classic grid-based structure, addressing its inefficiencies while aiming to retain its structural flexibility, which compact representations often sacrifice. By integrating theoretical capacity bounds for orientation storage, an asynchronous construction pipeline, and GPU acceleration, RichMap endeavors to achieve performance comparable to compact forms (like RM4D) in terms of accuracy and query speed for large batches, while maintaining the explicit and versatile structure characteristic of detailed capability maps. This approach aims to bridge the gap between highly compact but potentially less flexible representations and comprehensive but potentially inefficient traditional grids, offering a balanced solution for a range of robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop RichMap, a high-precision reachability map representation that achieves a balance among three critical aspects: precision, efficiency, and flexibility, for versatile robot manipulation tasks. The motivation behind this objective stems from the identified limitations of existing reachability map paradigms:

1. **Addressing Inefficiency of Classical Grid-Based Maps:** Traditional spatial-rotational grid structures, while intuitive and capable of storing detailed information, suffer from the curse of dimensionality. This makes them inefficient in terms of storage and computational cost when high spatial or rotational resolution is required, leading to prohibitive construction times and memory footprints for massive datasets. The authors aim to streamline this classical structure to improve performance without entirely abandoning its inherent benefits.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Compact Map Forms:** While methods like RM4D achieve compactness and efficiency by reducing dimensionality (e.g., to 4D) or using implicit representations, they often sacrifice the structural flexibility and simplicity that explicit grid-based maps offer. This can make certain applications, such as direct robot similarity comparison or integration with energy-based guidance for learning, more challenging to implement. A key motivation is to approach the performance of these compact forms while retaining the flexibility of a more explicit structure.

3. **Ensuring Rigorous Coverage and High Prediction Accuracy:** A fundamental requirement for any reachability map is to accurately determine whether a given end-effector pose is reachable. The authors aim for high prediction accuracy (> 98%) and low false positive rates (1-2%), which is crucial for reliable robot operation. This motivated the derivation of theoretical capacity bounds for orientation storage on S² (or SO(3)) to guide parameter selection and ensure rigorous coverage within each grid cell.

4. **Achieving Efficient Construction and Query Performance:** For practical utility, a reachability map must be constructible within a reasonable timeframe, even for massive datasets (e.g., 10⁸ poses), and capable of fast query operations. The motivation here is to design an efficient construction framework using an asynchronous pipeline and GPU acceleration to enable high throughput during map generation and achieve microsecond-level query latencies for large batches, which is beneficial for sampling-based planning or learning algorithms.

5. **Enabling Extended and Novel Applications:** Beyond traditional reachability checks, the authors sought to develop a framework whose structure facilitates novel applications. The explicit separation of spatial and rotational components in RichMap is designed to allow for functionalities like quantitative robot workspace similarity analysis using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) metrics and energy-based guidance for learning-based tasks, such as diffusion policy transfer across different robot embodiments. This reflects a motivation to enhance the utility of reachability maps beyond basic kinematic feasibility.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a reachability map that overcomes the drawbacks of both traditional grid-based methods (inefficiency) and compact representations (lack of flexibility) by carefully balancing precision, efficiency, and flexibility through a combination of theoretical analysis, an optimized construction pipeline, and a versatile data structure.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RichMap's methodology centers on refining grid-based workspace discretization, coupled with a theoretically bounded orientation coverage strategy and an asynchronous, GPU-accelerated construction pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined as efficiently answering the query: given a target end-effector pose $p^* \in SE(3)$ (represented by a 7-dimensional vector $[t, q]^\top$ for position $t$ and quaternion $q$), determine the existence of a collision-free joint configuration $j^*$ such that the forward kinematics $f_k(j^*) \approx p^*$ within a specified tolerance.

#### 3.2. Grid-Based Workspace Discretization

The robot's workspace $W \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is discretized into a uniform 3D grid with a specified cell size $\Delta$. Workspace bounds are defined as $[x_{min}, x_{max}] \times [y_{min}, y_{max}] \times [z_{min}, z_{max}]$. Each position $t$ is mapped to a grid cell index $(i_x, i_y, i_z)$ using the formula $i_d = \lfloor (t_d - t_{d,min}) / \Delta \rfloor$. This approach yields $N_{grid} = O(n^3)$ cells, where $n$ relates to workspace size and cell resolution. RichMap specifically targets high spatial precision, using $\Delta = 0.02 \text{ m}$ in some evaluations, which is finer than some previous works.

#### 3.3. Orientation Coverage with Geodesic Distance

Within each grid cell, a compact set of reachable end-effector approach directions is stored.
- **Dimensionality Reduction:** The initial design assumes a freely rotating wrist joint, allowing orientations to be represented by approach directions on the unit sphere $S^2$, rather than full quaternions (SO(3)). This significantly reduces storage requirements.
- **Geodesic Distance Metric:** For two unit direction vectors $v_1, v_2 \in S^2$, the geodesic distance is defined as $d_{S^2}(v_1, v_2) = \arccos(v_1 \cdot v_2)$.
- **Insertion Criterion:** A new pose with approach direction $v$ is added to a cell $C(t)$ only if its minimum geodesic distance to any existing direction $v' \in V_{C(t)}$ (the set of stored directions in the cell) is greater than or equal to an angular threshold $\theta$: $\min_{v' \in V_{C(t)}} d_{S^2}(v, v') \ge \theta$. This ensures spatial distribution and avoids redundancy.
- **Theoretical Capacity Bounds (S²):** By considering non-overlapping spherical caps of radius $\theta/2$ assigned to each stored direction, the maximum number of directions $N_{max}$ that can be packed into a cell is approximately $N_{max} \approx \frac{2}{1 - \cos(\theta/2)}$. For $\theta = 0.1 \text{ rad}$, this yields an upper bound of approximately 1600 directions per cell. In practice, robots like UR5e or Franka Panda typically store 300-500 directions per cell, with 35-50% cell coverage.
- **Full Quaternion Representation (SO(3)):** The framework is extendable to store full SO(3) quaternions. The geodesic distance $d_{SO(3)}(q_1, q_2) = 2 \arccos(|q_1 \cdot q_2|)$ is used. The theoretical capacity bound for SO(3) is $N_{max} \approx \frac{\pi}{\theta/2 - \sin(\theta/2)}$. For $\theta = 0.4 \text{ rad}$, this suggests $N_{max} \approx 2360$ quaternions per cell.

#### 3.4. Map Construction and Workflow Acceleration

Map construction involves forward kinematics (FK) sampling and pose insertion based on the geodesic distance criterion. The query operation involves locating the target cell, finding the nearest stored direction, and returning the associated joint configuration if within the threshold.

- **Asynchronous Pipeline Architecture:** To handle massive datasets (10⁷-10⁸ poses), an asynchronous producer-consumer pipeline decouples CPU-based FK sampling from GPU-based batched insertion.
 - **CPU Generation Stage:** Multiple parallel CPU worker processes (4-16) independently perform joint sampling, forward kinematics evaluation, self-collision detection (via physics simulation), and pose formatting. They communicate collision-free poses to the main process via a thread-safe queue.
 - **GPU Insertion Stage:** The main process vectorizes batches of poses (10⁶-10⁸) for GPU-accelerated insertion. Dynamic batch sizing adjusts to maintain GPU utilization as map density increases.
- **Batched GPU Insertion:** Large chunks of data are processed concurrently:
 1. **Voxel Grouping:** Incoming poses are sorted by destination cell index.
 2. **Feature Caching:** 3D axis vectors are computed and cached from quaternions.
 3. **Two-Phase Conflicts Detection:**
 - Phase 1 (New vs Old): Geodesic distances between new candidates and existing cell data are computed using batch matrix multiplication (BMM).
 - Phase 2 (New vs New): Conflicts among new candidates are resolved using greedy selection with geodesic distance checking and stable sorting.
- **Rotation Range Tracking:** The framework supports progressively relaxing the free wrist rotation assumption. For each inserted pose, a set of observed wrist rotation angles from generated (even rejected) poses that share similar approach directions is maintained. This allows augmenting the rotation set of an existing pose with the wrist angle range of a rejected pose, gradually approaching a complete SO(3) representation.

#### 3.5. Design Advantages

RichMap's design prioritizes:
- **High precision with minimal assumptions:** Uses a single kinematic assumption (free wrist joint), supported by theoretical capacity bounds, and progressive relaxation via rotation range tracking.
- **Flexibility through spatial-rotational separation:** Maintains a separated data structure (spatial grid and orientation set), similar to Capability Maps, enabling diverse applications despite higher memory/computational costs compared to highly compact forms.
- **Efficiency via asynchronous pipeline:** Leverages decoupled CPU/GPU processing, faster FK evaluation over IK solving, and vectorized GPU insertion to achieve high throughput and acceleration.

#### 3.6. Applications

The modular structure of RichMap facilitates several applications:
- **Map Compression:** For memory-constrained scenarios, orientation coverage can be compressed into a single scalar value per cell (coverage fraction $\rho_c$), computed using quasi-uniform samples on $S^2$ and the indicator function.
- **Classic grasp-related tasks:** Supports traditional tasks like reachability-aware mobile base placement, feasible pose filtering for grasping, and motion planning.
- **Robot similarity analysis:** Employs cell-wise Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with a multi-scale Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel to quantify workspace capabilities across different robot models, yielding a similarity grid $S_{A \leftrightarrow B}$.
- **Learning-based Manipulation Tasks:** Integrates the robot similarity metric into diffusion policies as energy-based guidance. An energy landscape is created through seed initialization, wavefront propagation, and Gaussian smoothing, which then modifies the predicted noise $\bar{\epsilon}(a|o) = \epsilon(a|o) + \lambda \nabla E(o_x)$ to bias trajectories towards regions of higher reachability similarity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of RichMap involved comprehensive benchmarking against counterpart methods and demonstration of its utility in extended applications.

#### 4.1. Reachability Map Verification

- **Experimental Setup:** Experiments were conducted on Franka Panda, KUKA iiwa7, Universal Robots UR5e, and Kinova Gen3. Test datasets of 2 million poses (50% reachable, 50% unreachable verified by IKFlow) were used. Reachability maps were built with workspace bounds of $[-1, 1] \times [-1, 1] \times [-1 + \triangle_z, 1 + \triangle_z]$ meters and an angular threshold $\theta = 0.1 \text{ rad}$. Two spatial resolutions, $\Delta = 0.05 \text{ m}$ and $\Delta = 0.02 \text{ m}$, were tested. Map construction completed within one day using a consumer-level GPU.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance was assessed using Accuracy, True Positive Rate (TPR), False Positive Rate (FPR), Insertion Rate (IR), and Query Time (μs/query) for various batch sizes.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Counterparts

At $\Delta = 0.05 \text{ m}$, RichMap was compared against RM4D (compact 4D storage) and Capability Map (CM, full SO(3) orientations).
- **Accuracy and FPR:** RichMap achieved accuracy comparable to RM4D (e.g., 0.975 for Panda vs. 0.980 for RM4D) and FPR within 0.5% (e.g., 0.037 vs. 0.035 for Panda). RichMap consistently outperformed CM in FPR (e.g., 0.037 vs. 0.052 for Panda), demonstrating a 25-35% FPR reduction.
- **Map Size:** RichMap utilized approximately 10 times more inserted poses than RM4D (e.g., 11.3M for Panda RichMap vs. 1.1M for RM4D) but had a similar number of inserted poses as CM.
- **Query Efficiency:** While RM4D exhibited relatively constant query times (around 40-50 μs/query) due to CPU-based sequential processing, RichMap and CM, leveraging GPU acceleration, showed significant speedup for large batches. For a batch size of $10^6$, RichMap achieved query times around 2-3 μs/query, which is approximately 18 times faster than RM4D for bulk queries despite its larger map size.

#### 4.3. Effect of Spatial Resolution

Comparing $\Delta = 0.05 \text{ m}$ and $\Delta = 0.02 \text{ m}$ for RichMap revealed:
- **FPR Reduction:** The most notable improvement was a dramatic reduction in FPR (approximately 2.5 times) when moving from $\Delta = 0.05 \text{ m}$ (FPR 0.036-0.063) to $\Delta = 0.02 \text{ m}$ (FPR 0.015-0.028). This supports the hypothesis that finer resolution better approximates irregular reachability boundaries, thus reducing false positives.
- **Map Size Scaling:** The finer resolution ($\Delta = 0.02 \text{ m}$) led to a substantial increase in total inserted poses (13-14 times more), with average poses per cell remaining stable (e.g., Panda: 503 at 0.05m to 501 at 0.02m). This reflects the increased grid cell count and consistent orientation coverage density.
- **Robot-Specific Characteristics:** UR5e consistently showed the largest workspace coverage (occupancy ~50-53%) and higher FPR, attributed to more boundary voxels accumulating approximation errors. Panda and Kinova3 had lower occupancy (~32-35%) and constrained joint configurations.
- **Accuracy-Resolution Trade-off:** While $\Delta = 0.02 \text{ m}$ offered superior FPR and overall accuracy (~0.984 vs. ~0.978), it incurred ~14 times more storage and longer construction times (13-15 hours vs. 1-1.3 hours for 0.05m). Query time also increased to 13-15 μs for large batches due to larger data overheads, but remained significantly faster than RM4D.

#### 4.4. Similarity-Guided Diffusion Policy Transfer: Block Pushing Experiment

- **Energy Landscape Generation:** A smooth energy landscape was created from the discrete MMD similarity grid. This involved:
 1. **Seed Initialization:** Cells with high similarity (MMD $\le \tau$) were marked as fixed zero-energy seed cells; others were unfilled.
 2. **Wavefront Energy Propagation:** Energy propagated layer by layer from seed cells to unfilled neighbors, with a fixed increment $\delta = 0.01$, ensuring each cell was assigned an energy value once.
 3. **Smoothing:** A 3D Gaussian filter was applied to yield a differentiable energy field $E: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$.
- **Guidance Mechanism:** This energy field was integrated into a diffusion policy by modifying the predicted noise $\bar{\epsilon}(a|o) = \epsilon(a|o) + \lambda \nabla E(o_x)$, where $\lambda$ is the guidance strength, effectively biasing trajectories towards regions of higher reachability similarity (lower energy).
- **Block Pushing Experiment:** A multi-modal block pushing task was used, transferring policies from an xArm6 source robot to UR3, UR5, and UR10 target robots.
- **Results:**
 - **Overall Improvement:** The guided transfer approach achieved an average success rate of 89.56%, an 10.97% absolute improvement over direct transfer (78.58%).
 - **Robot-Dependent Gains:** Significant improvements were observed for UR3 (+26.70%) and UR10 (+6.76%). UR5 showed minimal change (-0.54%).
 - **Correlation with Similarity:** The transfer outcomes correlated with workspace reachability similarity patterns revealed by MMD. UR3 and UR10 exhibited substantial reachability divergence from xArm6 in a critical region near the robot base, leading to self-constrained poses during direct transfer and lower baseline success rates.
 - **Energy-Based Guidance Effect:** The gradient field $\nabla E$ successfully steered trajectories away from low-similarity regions. For UR3, increasing guidance strength $\lambda$ led the robot to adapt its behavior, pushing blocks towards target regions farther from the base, avoiding kinematically problematic arm configurations near its base. UR5, having high similarity with xArm6 in the critical region, benefited minimally from guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RichMap contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a balanced approach to reachability mapping, addressing a long-standing challenge of simultaneously achieving high precision, computational efficiency, and structural flexibility.

The work's significance lies in several key aspects:
- **Bridging the Gap:** RichMap successfully bridges the gap between traditional, often inefficient, grid-based reachability maps and highly compact but potentially less flexible representations. It demonstrates that a refined grid-based structure can achieve prediction accuracy comparable to compact 4D maps (e.g., RM4D, within 0.5% FPR) while significantly outperforming traditional capability maps in false positive rates (25-35% reduction).
- **Enhanced Efficiency:** The asynchronous CPU/GPU pipeline and batched GPU insertion represent a notable advancement in map construction and query efficiency. Achieving microsecond-level query speeds for large batches (up to 18 times faster than sequential counterparts) is crucial for real-time applications, sampling-based motion planning, and learning-based approaches that require rapid and frequent reachability queries.
- **High Precision and Theoretical Foundation:** The use of theoretical capacity bounds for orientation storage on S² and SO(3) provides a rigorous basis for parameter selection, ensuring comprehensive coverage and contributing to the high prediction accuracy and low false positive rates. The ability to further reduce FPR to approximately 1.6% with finer spatial discretization underscores its precision capabilities.
- **Versatile Applications:** The explicit separation of spatial and rotational components fosters flexibility, enabling novel applications beyond standard reachability checks. The demonstration of robot workspace similarity quantification using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) provides a quantitative tool for comparing robot capabilities.
- **Impact on Learning-Based Manipulation:** The integration of reachability information into diffusion policies through energy-based guidance offers a significant step towards more robust and generalizable robotic policy transfer. The demonstrated average 11% improvement in success rates for cross-embodiment scenarios in the block pushing experiment, particularly for kinematically dissimilar robots, highlights the potential of using structured kinematic priors to overcome challenges in learning-based control and adaptation across different robot platforms. This guidance mechanism allows diffusion policies to explore kinematically feasible modes of task completion, enhancing performance in challenging transfer scenarios.

**Potential Impact:**
RichMap has the potential to impact various areas:
- **Robot Design and Selection:** The similarity analysis feature can inform the selection of robots for specific tasks or provide insights into workspace commonalities and differences, aiding in system design.
- **Motion Planning and Control:** Faster and more accurate reachability queries can lead to more efficient and reliable motion planners, especially for complex manipulation tasks and in dynamic environments.
- **Reinforcement Learning and Policy Transfer:** The energy-based guidance mechanism offers a blueprint for injecting valuable kinematic priors into learning-based robot control, making policies more robust, transferable, and less prone to generating infeasible actions. This could accelerate the deployment of learned skills across diverse robot hardware.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By accurately representing reachable spaces, RichMap could help bridge the sim-to-real gap by ensuring that learned policies in simulation are kinematically feasible in the real world.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges that current similarity guidance operates only on 3D positions, which might be insufficient for orientation-sensitive tasks. Future work includes expanding to full SE(3) similarity, combining with other value fields (e.g., collision), integrating with trajectory planning for safe reachable sets, and leveraging motion similarity analysis for dynamic properties. Exploring neural network compression is also proposed to further reduce memory usage and accelerate prediction. These areas represent avenues for continued development and further broadening the impact of RichMap.
