# IcFuzz: Fuzzing Isaac Sim with Semantic Stage Guidance and Multi-level Mutation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and collaborators introduced IcFuzz, the first dedicated fuzzing approach for NVIDIA Isaac Sim, which leverages semantic stage guidance and multi-level mutation to identify software defects. The system discovered 11 bugs, with 9 confirmed or fixed by developers, and achieved 190-205% higher code coverage compared to existing fuzzing baselines.

## Problem

- Robotics simulators like NVIDIA Isaac Sim are complex and critical for embodied AI, but are prone to software defects that can compromise reliability and development efficiency.
- Existing general-purpose fuzzers often generate semantically invalid inputs for Isaac Sim due to its context-aware object semantics and hierarchical control, leading to shallow exploration.
- Specialized robotics fuzzers lack the fine-grained control necessary to thoroughly explore Isaac Sim's extensive functionalities and vast simulation state space.

## Method

- **IcFuzz** employs Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform ==semantic stage segmentation== of Isaac Sim programs, decomposing them into context-aware execution stages to ensure semantically valid input generation.
- It utilizes ==multi-level mutation operators== (object, operation, and argument) to systematically test Isaac Sim's hierarchical control mechanisms across various granularities.
- A ==multi-armed bandit (MAB) algorithm== adaptively schedules mutation operators, balancing exploration and exploitation to efficiently guide fuzzing within Isaac Sim's vast state space under budget constraints.

## Results

- IcFuzz detected **11 bugs** in Isaac Sim (versions 5.0.0 and 5.1.0), with **9** confirmed or fixed by developers, demonstrating its efficacy in finding real-world software defects.
- In comparative 12-hour tests, IcFuzz detected an average of **3.7 unique crashes** and achieved **20,771 lines** of code coverage, outperforming baselines (Atheris and GzFuzz) which found no crashes and achieved only **10,136** and **10,940.7 lines** respectively.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of semantic stage guidance, multi-level mutation, and UCB-based operator selection, each significantly contributing to IcFuzz's superior code coverage and valid script generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging LLMs for semantic stage segmentation is an effective strategy for generating valid and context-consistent test cases for complex, script-driven simulation environments.
> - A multi-level mutation approach, targeting object composition, method invocation, and argument values, is crucial for comprehensively exercising the hierarchical control structures in advanced robotics simulators.
> - Adaptive scheduling of mutation operators via a multi-armed bandit algorithm significantly enhances fuzzing efficiency by dynamically prioritizing operators that yield higher code coverage and bug discovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotics simulators serve as critical infrastructure for the development and validation of embodied AI systems, enabling safe and scalable experimentation before real-world deployment. NVIDIA Isaac Sim is identified as a widely adopted simulator, known for its GPU-accelerated physics engine and photorealistic rendering capabilities, which facilitate high-fidelity modeling of complex environments. The inherent complexity of such advanced software, however, inevitably introduces software defects that can compromise simulation reliability.

Automated software testing techniques, particularly fuzzing, have demonstrated efficacy in uncovering previously unknown vulnerabilities and bugs in complex software systems. Fuzzing involves generating random or malformed inputs to expose software defects. While general-purpose fuzzers (e.g., AFL++, Atheris) and even specialized fuzzers for other robotics simulators (e.g., GzFuzz for Gazebo) exist, their application to a sophisticated platform like Isaac Sim presents unique challenges. General-purpose fuzzers often generate inputs at a low level (e.g., byte streams), which typically violate the semantic and structural constraints of Isaac Sim, leading to shallow exploration. GzFuzz, while designed for robotics simulators, operates primarily through a command-line interface, focusing on model manipulation and lacking fine-grained control over simulation functionalities and their parameters, thereby limiting its exploration of Isaac Sim's extensive capabilities.

This work positions itself as the first dedicated fuzzing approach for NVIDIA Isaac Sim. It addresses the limitations of existing fuzzing methods by incorporating domain-specific knowledge and leveraging large language models (LLMs) to understand and manipulate the structured nature of Isaac Sim programs. By focusing on semantic stage guidance and multi-level mutation, the research aims to navigate the complexities of Isaac Sim's input space more effectively than prior approaches, thereby contributing to the reliability of robotics simulation environments, which are foundational for embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate IcFuzz, a novel fuzzing approach specifically designed to detect software bugs in NVIDIA Isaac Sim. This objective is driven by several key motivations and challenges inherent in testing complex robotics simulators:

1. **Ensuring Simulation Reliability in Isaac Sim:** Isaac Sim's role as a foundational platform for embodied AI means that any software bugs within it can lead to significant issues. Crashes, for instance, can cause data loss during model training, disrupt digital twin systems, and incur substantial troubleshooting efforts, thereby impeding development efficiency and potentially leading to safety problems in real-world applications. The motivation is to enhance the trustworthiness and stability of Isaac Sim by systematically identifying and reporting these critical defects.

2. **Addressing Context-aware Object Semantics:** Isaac Sim programs feature tight coupling between object declarations and their execution context. Objects exhibit complex contextual dependencies and require strict logical ordering. For example, the `SimulationApp` must be initialized before any other simulator-specific objects, and `Articulation` objects depend on pre-existing `prim` elements. Inputs that violate these semantic rules are immediately rejected, severely limiting the exploration of the simulator's internal logic by conventional fuzzers. A key motivation is to devise a method that respects these semantic constraints to generate valid, executable test cases.

3. **Handling Hierarchical Simulation Control:** Isaac Sim's control mechanisms are structured hierarchically across various granularities. This includes instantiating high-level objects like `WheeledRobot` or `Manipulator`, applying distinct operations to them (e.g., setting wheel speeds or joint positions), and specifying parameters for these operations (e.g., velocities or precise coordinate values). Different granularities of control exert varied influences on the simulator. The motivation is to enable systematic testing across these multiple levels of control to uncover bugs that might only manifest through specific combinations or sequences of actions.

4. **Navigating a Vast Simulation State Space:** Isaac Sim encompasses a vast simulation state space, resulting from the combinatorial interplay of diverse models (geometries, robots), complex physical interactions (collisions, lighting), and sophisticated AI workflows (e.g., reinforcement learning). Exhaustive exploration is computationally infeasible. Traditional random fuzzing approaches are often inefficient, struggling to reach deep or subtle states where bugs are likely to reside. Therefore, a crucial motivation is to develop efficient strategies to guide fuzzing, ensuring broader and deeper exploration within this extensive state space.

In response to these challenges, IcFuzz aims to:
- Decompose simulation programs into structured "semantic stages" using LLMs to capture context-aware object semantics.
- Design "multi-level mutation operators" that systematically exercise Isaac Sim across object, operation, and argument granularities.
- Employ a "multi-armed bandit (MAB) algorithm" to adaptively schedule mutation operators, guiding efficient exploration of the simulation state space.
- Focus on detecting "crash bugs" as the primary test oracle, given their severity and prevalence in Isaac Sim.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IcFuzz's methodology is structured around a fuzzing loop that integrates semantic understanding with multi-level mutation and adaptive scheduling. The workflow comprises seed pool construction, multi-level mutation, and oracle-based feedback.

#### 3.1. Seed Pool Construction

1. **Seed Collection:** The process begins by collecting standalone Python scripts from the official Isaac Sim repository. These scripts serve as initial test cases, selected for their successful execution without errors and their representativeness of real-world simulator usage, covering various packages, functional categories, robot platforms, and sensor modalities. The initial seed pool consists of 116 such scripts.
2. **Semantic Stage Segmentation:** To address context-aware object semantics, IcFuzz introduces semantic stage segmentation. This involves decomposing each seed script into distinct code segments that align with a predefined taxonomy of domain-specific execution stages. This taxonomy, derived from a systematic review of official scripts and the robot development lifecycle, includes stages such as Simulator Startup, Scene Setup (e.g., Creating the Scene, Adding Objects, Assigning Physical Properties, Adding High-Level Object Management, Adding Sensors), Interacting, Export, and Simulator Shutdown. Large Language Models (LLMs) are utilized to perform this segmentation, guided by a chain-of-thought prompt containing the script, stage descriptions, and an example. The segmented information is then stored alongside the seed in the seed pool.
3. **Seed Sampling:** In each fuzzing iteration, a seed is sampled from the pool using a strategy where selection probability is inversely proportional to its historical sampling frequency, promoting exploration of less-frequently used seeds and newly added coverage-increasing seeds.

#### 3.2. Multi-level Mutation

IcFuzz employs mutation operators at three granularities: object, operation, and argument, and utilizes LLMs for context-consistent code generation. Before mutation, IcFuzz crawls Isaac Sim documentation for class, method, and object-type information.

1. **Context-aware Object Selection:** This mechanism guides object-level mutation to ensure semantic validity. Based on the semantic stage of a code segment, a stage-specific set of permissible object types (for addition or replacement) is identified from a pre-defined mapping (Table 1 in the paper). This restricts object selection, preserving semantic integrity while promoting diversity. For selecting existing objects within a seed, a program-analysis-based strategy extracts objects from the Abstract Syntax Tree (AST), ensuring they are relevant to Isaac Sim. Essential objects like `SimulationApp` are protected from deletion or replacement.

2. **Mutation Operators:**
 - **Object-Level Mutation:** Modifies the composition of objects.
 - **Addition:** Selects a target semantic stage and an addable object type via context-aware selection. An LLM then instantiates the chosen object's class constructor from documentation and inserts the corresponding code into the seed.
 - **Replacement:** Selects an existing object within a target stage using program analysis and a replacement object type via context-aware selection. An LLM replaces the original object and its associated dependencies, or removes dependencies if replacement is infeasible.
 - **Deletion:** Selects an existing object using program analysis and instructs an LLM to remove it along with its dependents.
 - **Operation-Level Mutation:** Modifies method invocations of existing objects.
 - **Addition:** Selects a target object, samples a method from its class's documentation, and uses an LLM to generate code for inserting the method invocation.
 - **Replacement:** Samples a new method and replaces an existing invocation of the target object with the new one, using an LLM.
 - **Deletion:** Randomly selects and removes an existing method invocation of the target object, using an LLM.
 - **Argument-Level Mutation:** Mutates arguments of object operations, performed directly on the AST without LLMs for efficiency.
 - **Deletion:** Removes an argument if it has a documented default value.
 - **Nullification:** Sets an argument to `None` if allowed by documentation.
 - **Replacement:** Modifies the value for numeric, boolean, or list/array arguments (e.g., random generation for numbers, negation for booleans).

3. **Mutation Feasibility Analysis:** Before execution, each candidate mutation undergoes two types of feasibility analysis to reduce wasted execution time:
 - **Rule-based analysis:** Checks if the mutation satisfies the prerequisites (e.g., target stage has addable objects, mutable argument types).
 - **LLM-based analysis:** For LLM-generated mutations, the LLM is prompted to determine if a semantically valid script can be produced. This leverages the LLM's understanding of dynamic semantics and contextual compatibility. Infeasible mutations are discarded.

4. **Mutation Operator Selection:** To efficiently explore the state space under a limited budget, mutation operator selection is modeled as a K-armed bandit problem and solved using the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithm. Each mutation operator represents an "arm." The UCB algorithm selects the operator with the highest UCB score, balancing exploitation (operators with high historical rewards) and exploration (less-frequently selected operators). The reward for each operator is a weighted sum of new code coverage gain (`NewCoverageGain`) and crash discovery (`CrashFound`), reflecting the objective of maximizing both coverage and bug detection.

#### 3.3. Oracle and Feedback

1. **Test Oracle:** IcFuzz focuses on detecting "crash bugs," which are severe failures causing abrupt simulation termination (e.g., indicated by "crash detected" messages or dump file generation). Crashes are identified, and their triggering scripts, exception types, messages, and stack traces are collected. Unique crashes are determined by comparing exception types, top stack frames, and triggering conditions, and subsequently reported to developers.
2. **Execution Feedback:** After execution, IcFuzz assesses whether a mutated test case is valid (executes without errors) and measures its code coverage. Valid scripts that increase cumulative code coverage are segmented and added back to the seed pool. The validity, coverage gain, and crash occurrence information are fed back to the UCB algorithm to update the UCB scores of the mutation operators, guiding future selection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of IcFuzz involved a comprehensive comparison against baselines and ablation studies, yielding several findings regarding bug detection, overall effectiveness, and the contribution of its components.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Bug Detection

IcFuzz conducted a four-month longitudinal fuzzing campaign on Isaac Sim versions 5.0.0 and 5.1.0. Across this period, IcFuzz reported a total of 11 bugs, 9 of which were either confirmed or fixed by developers.

- **Bug #280 (Object-level mutation):** This bug was triggered by adding a `Cylinder` object with transformation parameters (e.g., `radii`, `heights`). The issue stemmed from an incorrect default value (`False`) for the `reset_xform_op_properties` parameter in the `Cylinder` constructor. This led to immediate crashes and affected 15 classes across `Shapes`, `Lights`, and `Meshes` categories, which are experimental but intended for future releases and had lacked sufficient testing. The developers confirmed this bug.
- **Bug #243 (Operation-level mutation):** This bug occurred when IcFuzz added a call to the `is_done` method of the `World` object and prematurely terminated the simulation loop based on its return value. This prevented the simulation from completing necessary warm-up steps before valid camera data was acquired, causing `get_rgba` to return an unexpected one-dimensional array instead of the expected three-dimensional one, leading to an out-of-bounds memory access and a crash. The documentation was subsequently updated to reflect this behavior.
- **Bug #338 (Argument-level mutation):** This bug was detected after a two-step mutation. First, IcFuzz added the `store_measurements` method call. Subsequently, it mutated the `stop_recording_time` argument from its default `True` to `False`. This revealed that the functionality for `stop_recording_time=False` was not fully implemented, triggering a crash only when this specific argument value was applied. Developers confirmed the incomplete implementation.

These cases demonstrate that bugs in Isaac Sim can be complex, involving interactions across different levels of simulation control, which IcFuzz's multi-level mutation is designed to explore.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Effectiveness

**Overall Effectiveness:**
IcFuzz was compared against Atheris (general-purpose Python fuzzer) and GzFuzz (state-of-the-art robotics simulator fuzzer) over three independent 12-hour runs.

- **Crash Detection:** IcFuzz detected an average of 7 total crashes, including 3.7 unique crashes. In contrast, both Atheris and GzFuzz failed to detect any crashes across all runs.
- **Code Coverage:** IcFuzz achieved an average code coverage of 20,771 lines, which represents approximately 205% of Atheris (10,136 lines) and 190% of GzFuzz (10,940.7 lines). These differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05).
- **Coverage Growth:** IcFuzz exhibited steady coverage growth over time, reaching a plateau around 600 minutes, while baselines plateaued much earlier (around 100 minutes).
- **LLM and Reward Setting Variations:** IcFuzz's performance showed slight variations across different LLM configurations (GPT-5-mini, DeepSeek-V4, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) and UCB reward weight settings. All IcFuzz configurations, however, substantially outperformed the baselines in both coverage and crash detection. For instance, the lowest-coverage LLM configuration still achieved approximately 173% of GzFuzz's coverage. Higher `w_cov` weights tended to increase coverage, while crash detection, being sparse, did not proportionally increase with `w_crash`.

**Effectiveness of LLM-dependent Steps:**
- **Semantic Stage Segmentation:** Manual inspection of a 20% sample of 454 segmentation results showed an average accuracy of 93.5%. Errors typically occurred in less common code patterns, such as misclassifying complex camera initialization as 'Interacting' instead of 'Adding Sensors.'
- **LLM-based Mutation Feasibility Analysis:** A 20% sample of 139 rejected mutations revealed only one false negative. This indicates high consistency with manual validation, as IcFuzz accurately assessed the semantic validity of mutations (e.g., rejecting functionally incompatible object replacements like `Articulation` with `XformPrim`). The single false negative involved a mutation that, while technically valid, resulted in an empty, uninteresting simulation.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted over three independent 2-hour runs for 10 configurations.

- **Context-aware Object Selection:** Compared to an LLM-based object selection baseline, context-aware selection in IcFuzz's object-level mutations (addition and replacement) led to higher exploration diversity and coverage. For addition, it covered 10 more unique classes and achieved 545.7 additional lines of coverage, despite generating slightly fewer valid coverage-increasing scripts. For replacement, it significantly outperformed the baseline, covering 16.7 more unique object pairs and increasing coverage by 1,267.3 lines. The LLM-based approach often repeatedly selected common classes or generated semantically invalid replacement pairs.
- **Mutation Operators:** Removing any of the three mutation levels (object-, operation-, or argument-level) from IcFuzz resulted in a decrease in code coverage. IcFuzz with all three levels achieved the highest coverage (16,532 lines), demonstrating the collective contribution of systematic exploration at all granularities. The absence of object- and operation-level operators led to statistically significant coverage drops (p < 0.05).
- **Mutation Operator Selection:** Comparing UCB-based selection with random selection, IcFuzz with UCB generated 4.4 more valid scripts and 5.3 more valid coverage-increasing scripts, while producing 3.4 fewer invalid scripts. This led to a statistically significant increase of 1,200.7 lines in code coverage, indicating UCB's effectiveness in balancing exploration and exploitation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

IcFuzz represents a notable advancement in the automated testing of complex robotics simulators. Its primary significance lies in being the first dedicated fuzzing approach for NVIDIA Isaac Sim, a widely adopted platform in embodied AI development. The research introduces several methodological contributions that address the inherent complexities of fuzzing such a system:

1. **Semantic Stage Guidance:** The concept of semantic stage segmentation, leveraging LLMs to decompose simulation programs into context-aware stages, provides a novel mechanism for enforcing semantic constraints during test generation. This approach ensures the generation of syntactically and semantically valid inputs, which is crucial for deep exploration of complex, context-sensitive software. This method could potentially be adapted to other script-driven simulators with similar lifecycle structures.
2. **Multi-level Mutation:** The design of mutation operators operating at object, operation, and argument levels enables systematic and comprehensive exercise of Isaac Sim's hierarchical simulation control. This multi-granularity approach allows IcFuzz to uncover intricate bugs that might only manifest through specific combinations of simulation entities, actions, and parameter values.
3. **Adaptive Mutation Scheduling:** The integration of a multi-armed bandit algorithm for adaptively scheduling mutation operators is significant for efficient exploration of Isaac Sim's vast simulation state space. By dynamically biasing operator selection based on execution feedback (coverage gains and crash discovery), IcFuzz optimizes its fuzzing efforts under limited computational budgets.

The practical impact of IcFuzz is demonstrated by its empirical results:
- **Superior Bug Detection:** IcFuzz significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines, detecting an average of 3.7 unique crashes in comparative 12-hour tests, whereas baselines found none.
- **High Code Coverage:** It achieved substantially higher code coverage (approximately 190-205% more than baselines), indicating a broader and deeper exploration of Isaac Sim's codebase.
- **Real-world Bug Discovery:** The identification and reporting of 11 bugs in Isaac Sim, with 9 confirmed or fixed by developers, directly contributes to the reliability and trustworthiness of a foundational platform for robotics research and development. These bugs, ranging from incorrect default parameter values to incompletely implemented functionalities, highlight critical areas previously overlooked.

The methodology's generalizability is a potential area of impact. While implemented for Isaac Sim, aspects such as lifecycle-style stage segmentation, stage-based semantic constraints, multi-level mutation, and feedback-based scheduling are designed around common characteristics of script-driven robotics simulators. This suggests that the core principles of IcFuzz could offer insights and a foundational framework for testing other simulators like Genesis, MuJoCo, or Webots, albeit requiring adaptation of specific components. Future work could extend IcFuzz to detect non-crashing bugs through domain-specific semantic oracles and integrate static analysis for enhanced mutation feasibility reasoning.
