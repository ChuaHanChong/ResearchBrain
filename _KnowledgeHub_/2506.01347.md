# The Surprising Effectiveness of Negative Reinforcement in LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Virginia and Princeton University dissected Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLMs into positive and negative reinforcement components. Their analysis revealed that negative reinforcement, which penalizes incorrect responses, surprisingly enhances reasoning and maintains output diversity, often outperforming or matching full RL methods.

## Problem

- The precise mechanisms by which Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) improves LLM reasoning, particularly how it utilizes correct versus incorrect samples, remain underexplored.
- Many RL-trained LLMs show improved greedy accuracy (Pass@1) but often suffer from degraded inference-time scaling and reduced output diversity at higher sampling budgets (Pass@k).
- A clear understanding of the individual contributions of positive sample reinforcement (reinforcing correct outputs) and negative sample reinforcement (penalizing incorrect outputs) within RLVR was lacking.

## Method

- The standard RLVR objective was mathematically decomposed into two distinct learning signals: ==Positive Sample Reinforcement== (PSR) and Negative Sample Reinforcement (NSR).
- LLMs were trained exclusively with PSR-only and NSR-only objectives on mathematical reasoning tasks and evaluated comprehensively using Pass@k metrics (from k=1 to k=256).
- A token-level gradient analysis was conducted to mechanistically understand how PSR and NSR alter the model's output probability distributions and influence knowledge refinement.

## Results

- NSR-only training consistently improved Pass@k performance across the entire spectrum of `k` values, often matching or surpassing complex PPO/GRPO algorithms, even achieving Pass@1 scores comparable to or better than explicit positive reinforcement.
- PSR-only training improved Pass@1 accuracy but caused a significant degradation in Pass@k performance for `k > 8`, indicating a loss of output diversity and exploration capacity.
- A proposed Weighted-REINFORCE (W-REINFORCE) objective, which moderately down-weights positive reward contributions (λ=**0.1**) while fully retaining negative reinforcement, achieved superior overall Pass@k performance on multiple math benchmarks, effectively balancing accuracy and diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Negative Sample Reinforcement (NSR) is surprisingly effective, improving Pass@k performance by implicitly preserving prior knowledge and constructively redistributing probability mass, which helps maintain output diversity.
> - Positive Sample Reinforcement (PSR) can lead to diversity collapse by overly concentrating probability on a few correct responses, resulting in degraded Pass@k performance at higher sampling rates.
> - The effectiveness of RL training is highly dependent on the LLM backbone, with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct showing degradation from RL, while NSR caused the least decline in scaling behavior.
