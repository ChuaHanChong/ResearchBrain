# Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT and ETH Zurich developed Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT), a method that enables large language models to continually acquire new skills and knowledge from demonstrations without experiencing catastrophic forgetting. SDFT achieves this by using the model itself as an in-context learning teacher, consistently outperforming traditional fine-tuning methods and enhancing generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Foundation models suffer from catastrophic forgetting when sequentially learning new skills and knowledge from demonstrations, leading to performance degradation on previously mastered tasks.
- Traditional methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) are inherently off-policy, resulting in poor generalization and an inability to adapt to the model's evolving behavior, while on-policy learning from demonstrations typically requires complex Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) or explicit reward functions.
- Training reasoning models often requires expensive, explicitly labeled chain-of-thought annotations; using SFT with only final answers can degrade a model's internal reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- **SDFT** introduces a novel framework where the foundation model acts as both a student and an ==in-context learning (ICL) teacher==.
- The teacher model, conditioned on expert demonstrations, generates an ==on-policy signal== that guides the student through minimizing a ==reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence== objective, with updates based on trajectories the student itself generates.
- Teacher parameters are maintained as an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA)== of the student's parameters to ensure stable and effective ==self-distillation== training, tracking student progress while smoothing updates.

## Results

- SDFT consistently achieved superior continual learning, allowing models to acquire new skills (e.g., Tool Use, Science Q&A) while retaining performance on previously learned and general capabilities, in contrast to SFT which exhibited severe catastrophic forgetting.
- The method demonstrated enhanced generalization, achieving higher new-task accuracy for in-distribution examples and near-perfect accuracy on 'indirect' out-of-distribution knowledge acquisition questions.
- SDFT successfully fine-tuned reasoning models using only final answers, preserving internal reasoning depth and significantly improving accuracy where SFT caused a 'reasoning collapse,' with its effectiveness scaling positively with model size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The SDFT objective of minimizing reverse KL divergence is mathematically equivalent to an on-policy Reinforcement Learning algorithm that maximizes an implicit reward function derived from the demonstration-conditioned teacher, circumventing the need for explicit reward engineering.
> - Large pretrained models' inherent in-context learning (ICL) abilities can be effectively leveraged to create high-quality, demonstration-conditioned teaching signals for self-distillation, eliminating reliance on external expert models.
> - Ablation studies confirm that the on-policy nature of SDFT is critical for mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enhancing generalization, as offline distillation methods consistently underperform.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses one of the most fundamental challenges in modern AI: **continual learning** in foundation models. Despite their impressive capabilities in various domains like language, vision, and robotics, today's large AI models are largely static after their initial training. They can adapt their behavior during inference through prompting or retrieval, but they do not inherently update their parameters to acquire new skills or knowledge over time without explicitly being retrained. This limitation contrasts sharply with human intelligence, which is characterized by continuous learning and adaptation throughout life.

The broader research landscape for continual learning is marked by several key approaches and challenges:
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** A pervasive issue where models, when trained on new tasks, rapidly lose their performance on previously learned tasks.
- **On-Policy vs. Off-Policy Learning:** A growing body of work, including previous research by some of the current authors (Shenfeld et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025), has emphasized the importance of *on-policy learning* for mitigating catastrophic forgetting and improving generalization. On-policy methods learn from data generated by the model's *current* policy, allowing for self-correction and adaptation to its own evolving behavior. In contrast, *off-policy learning*, such as traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), trains on a fixed, offline dataset, leading to distributional shifts and compounding errors when the model's behavior deviates from the training data.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Many successful on-policy approaches have emerged from RL, where an explicit reward function guides learning. However, specifying explicit reward functions is often difficult or impractical in many real-world scenarios, especially when learning from human demonstrations.
- **Learning from Demonstrations:** In the absence of explicit rewards, learning from expert demonstrations is a primary alternative. This is typically done via SFT, which is inherently off-policy and thus suffers from the aforementioned limitations.
- **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL):** A conceptually elegant solution that aims to infer an explicit reward function from demonstrations, which can then be used for on-policy RL. However, IRL methods often require strong priors, are computationally expensive, or can be ill-posed, limiting their practical widespread adoption.
- **Context Distillation:** A related paradigm where a model conditioned on additional information (e.g., few-shot examples, behavioral guidelines) acts as a teacher for a student version of itself. Prior work in this area has often relied on *offline distillation* from *static contexts*, focusing on prompt compression rather than true continual learning or dynamic behavioral adaptation.

This paper, "Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning" (SDFT), directly positions itself within this landscape by seeking to **reconcile the benefits of on-policy learning with the practical availability of expert demonstrations, without resorting to the complexities of explicit reward engineering or traditional IRL.** It leverages the emergent property of **in-context learning (ICL)** in large language models to create an on-policy signal from demonstrations. By doing so, it offers a novel, scalable, and effective path towards truly continual learning for foundation models, filling a critical gap between theoretical advantages of on-policy methods and practical constraints of real-world data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT) are multifaceted:

1. **Overcome Catastrophic Forgetting in Demonstration-Based Learning:** The primary motivation is to enable foundation models to continually acquire new skills and knowledge from expert demonstrations without suffering from catastrophic forgetting, a pervasive problem when using traditional off-policy methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
2. **Enable On-Policy Learning from Demonstrations Directly:** The authors aim to bridge the gap between the empirically observed benefits of on-policy learning (reduced forgetting, better generalization) and the common scenario where only expert demonstrations are available, not explicit reward functions. SDFT seeks to generate an on-policy learning signal directly from these demonstrations.
3. **Improve Generalization Capabilities:** Beyond mitigating forgetting, a key objective is to enhance the model's ability to generalize to new, unseen instances of a task, both in-distribution and out-of-distribution, a known advantage of on-policy methods over off-policy ones.
4. **Circumvent Limitations of Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL):** While IRL is a conceptual solution for learning rewards from demonstrations, its practical application is often hindered by the need for strong priors or high computational costs. SDFT aims to achieve similar benefits (on-policy updates from demonstrations) without inferring an explicit reward function, offering a more scalable and accessible approach.
5. **Leverage In-Context Learning (ICL) as a Teacher Signal:** The research is motivated by the observation that large pretrained models possess strong in-context learning abilities. The objective is to exploit this property to generate high-quality, demonstration-conditioned teaching signals without external expert models or complex reward functions.
6. **Maintain Internal Reasoning Styles with Answer-Only Data:** A practical motivation is to enable effective fine-tuning for reasoning models using datasets that provide only final answers, rather than requiring expensive, explicitly labeled chain-of-thought traces. The goal is to prevent the "collapse" of reasoning depth often seen when SFT is applied to such abbreviated supervision.
7. **Scalability and Practicality:** To develop a method that is not only theoretically sound but also computationally feasible and scalable, especially with the growing size and complexity of foundation models.

In essence, the paper seeks to establish "on-policy distillation as a practical path to continual learning from demonstrations" for the next generation of adaptive AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SDFT introduces a novel framework for on-policy learning directly from demonstrations, building on the principle of student-teacher distillation. Its core innovation lies in using the foundation model itself in two distinct roles: a student and a teacher, leveraging its inherent in-context learning (ICL) capabilities.

#### 3.1. Core Self-Distillation Mechanism

The method uses a single foundation model, parameterized by `θ`, as both the student and the teacher.
- **Student Role:** The student is the base model `π_θ(·|x)`, conditioned only on the task input `x`.
- **Teacher Role:** The teacher is the *same model* `π(·|x, c)`, but additionally conditioned on an expert demonstration `c` relevant to the task input `x`. This demonstration `c` guides the teacher's behavior.

#### 3.2. Prompting for Teacher Context

A specific prompt template is designed to elicit the desired behavior from the teacher model, enabling it to act as a demonstration-aware expert without merely copying the demonstration verbatim. The template used is:
```
<Question>
This is an example for a response to the question:
<Demonstration>
Now answer with a response of your own, including the thinking process:
```
This structure encourages the teacher to internalize the intent behind the demonstration and produce a response that reflects the model’s understanding, rather than just rote imitation.

#### 3.3. On-Policy Distillation Objective

SDFT trains the student using an on-policy distillation objective. For each prompt `x`, the algorithm samples responses `y` from the *student policy* `y ~ π_θ(·|x)`. The training then minimizes the reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the student and teacher distributions:
`L(θ) = D_KL(π_θ(·|x) || π(·|x, c)) = E_y~π_θ(y|x) [log(π_θ(y|x) / π(y|x, c))]`

This **reverse KL divergence** is crucial for on-policy learning because it means the student is updated based on trajectories it *itself* generates. The gradient is taken with respect to the student parameters `θ`, treating the teacher distribution `π(·|x, c)` as fixed during the gradient computation. The paper decomposes this sequence-level objective into a token-level loss for autoregressive models, using an analytic per-token estimator for efficient and stable gradient computation.

#### 3.4. Teacher Parameterization

For stable and effective training, the teacher model's weights (`ϕ`) are maintained as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's parameters (`θ`). This allows the teacher to track the student's learning progress while smoothing out high-variance updates, preventing instabilities that would arise if the student acted as its own teacher directly or if the teacher remained entirely static (frozen base model).

#### 3.5. Interpretation as Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)

The paper provides a formal derivation showing that the SDFT objective (minimizing reverse KL divergence) is mathematically equivalent to an on-policy RL algorithm that maximizes an *implicit reward function*. This reward function is defined by comparing the log-probabilities of the demonstration-conditioned teacher (`π(y|x, c)`) to the student's current policy (`π_k(y|x)`), effectively `r(y, x, c) = log π(y|x, c) - log π_k(y|x)`. This interpretation highlights how SDFT extracts an on-policy learning signal from demonstrations without explicit reward engineering.

#### 3.6. Empirical Validation of the In-Context Learning (ICL) Assumption

The core hypothesis, that `π(y|x, c)` approximates the optimal policy and remains close to the current policy, is empirically validated:
- **Optimality:** On the Tool Use task, the demonstration-conditioned teacher achieves 100% success, with reconstructed reasoning traces demonstrating genuine understanding.
- **Minimal Deviation:** The KL divergence between the teacher and the base policy is significantly lower than that between an SFT-trained model and the base policy, confirming that the teacher produces high-quality outputs without drastically deviating from the base model's capabilities.

#### 3.7. Experimental Settings

SDFT is evaluated across two key continual learning settings:
- **Skill Learning:** Acquiring new, narrowly defined skills (e.g., Science Q&A, Tool Use, Medical QA) without degrading existing broad capabilities.
- **Knowledge Acquisition:** Integrating genuinely new factual content not present in the model's pretraining data (e.g., facts about 2025 natural disasters).

Baselines include Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Distillation Fine-Tuning (DFT), SFT with "Re-invocation," Continual Pre-Training (CPT), and RAG with an oracle retriever. Experiments primarily use the Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SDFT consistently outperforms existing methods in enabling continual learning from demonstrations:

1. **Superior Continual Learning and Reduced Catastrophic Forgetting:**
 - In challenging multi-task sequential learning experiments (Figure 3), SDFT allows a single model to acquire new skills (Tool Use, Science Q&A, Medical) over time while *retaining* performance on previously learned skills and general capabilities. In stark contrast, SFT exhibits severe catastrophic forgetting, with performance on earlier tasks rapidly degrading as new tasks are introduced.
 - For single-task learning (Figure 4, Table 5), SDFT is the only method that improves new-task accuracy without significant degradation in prior capabilities across a suite of benchmarks (HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, MMLU, IFEval, Winogrande, HumanEval). SFT shows substantial forgetting, while DFT and SFT+Re-invoke offer only partial mitigation.

2. **Enhanced Generalization:**
 - **In-Distribution Generalization:** SDFT achieves higher new-task accuracy compared to SFT across all skill learning tasks, indicating better generalization to unseen examples from the target distribution (Figure 4).
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization for Knowledge Acquisition:** In the knowledge acquisition setting (Table 1), SDFT significantly outperforms SFT on both strict and lenient accuracy for questions directly related to injected knowledge, and remarkably, achieves near-perfect accuracy on "indirect" OOD questions. This suggests that SDFT leads to a deeper integration of new information into the model's knowledge base, rather than mere memorization of specific answers.

3. **Positive Correlation with Model Scale:**
 - SDFT's effectiveness strongly correlates with the base model's in-context learning (ICL) capabilities (Figure 5, left). The performance gap between SDFT and SFT widens with increasing model size (from 3B to 14B Qwen 2.5 variants). This implies that as larger models develop stronger ICL abilities, SDFT will become even more advantageous.

4. **Enabling Reasoning Models with Answer-Only Data:**
 - SDFT successfully fine-tunes reasoning models (Olmo-3-7B-Think) using datasets that contain only final answers, significantly improving accuracy and preserving the model's internal reasoning depth (average number of tokens generated) (Table 2). SFT, when applied to such data, causes a "reasoning collapse," degrading both performance and reasoning behavior. This highlights SDFT's ability to maintain the model's inherent reasoning style by distilling from its own conditioned self.

5. **Crucial Role of On-Policy Learning:**
 - Ablation studies confirm that the on-policy nature of SDFT is critical for its performance gains (Figure 6). Even with the same high-quality, demonstration-conditioned teacher, offline distillation methods (SFT from teacher, offline distillation) consistently underperform SDFT, underscoring the necessity of on-policy updates.

6. **Genuine Skill Acquisition, Not Entropy Collapse:**
 - Pass@k analysis (Figure 5, right) shows uniform performance gains across a wide range of `k`, indicating that SDFT facilitates genuine skill acquisition and not merely a superficial reduction in entropy or sharpening of predictions.

7. **Optimal Teacher Configuration:**
 - Using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student parameters for the teacher model provides the most stable and effective training, outperforming both a frozen base model teacher and directly using the current student as the teacher (Figure 8).
 - Conditioning the teacher on both the article text and the worked answer is essential for effective knowledge transfer, significantly outperforming text-only or answer-only conditioning (Figure 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT) represents a significant advancement in the field of continual learning and has several profound potential impacts:

1. **Practical Continual Learning for Foundation Models:** SDFT offers a robust and effective framework for enabling foundation models to truly learn and improve over time. This capability is critical for deploying AI systems in dynamic, real-world environments where new information and tasks constantly emerge, moving beyond the current paradigm of static models requiring expensive, episodic retraining.
2. **Overcoming a Core Limitation of SFT:** By providing a method for on-policy learning directly from demonstrations, SDFT addresses the fundamental shortcomings of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) – catastrophic forgetting and poor generalization – which have long hampered the development of continually adaptive AI from observational data.
3. **Simplifying On-Policy Learning from Demonstrations:** SDFT bypasses the complexities and limitations of Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) by eliminating the need to explicitly infer a reward function. This makes on-policy learning from demonstrations much more accessible and scalable for a broader range of applications and researchers.
4. **Leveraging In-Context Learning (ICL) Power:** The method ingeniously harnesses the emergent in-context learning abilities of large language models, showcasing a novel and powerful application of this capability to create effective self-supervision. As models continue to scale and ICL abilities improve, SDFT's effectiveness is poised to grow.
5. **Reducing Data Collection Burdens:** The ability to effectively train reasoning models using only final answers, without requiring expensive and labor-intensive chain-of-thought annotations, is a major practical benefit. This could significantly accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent agents in domains where explicit reasoning traces are scarce.
6. **Foundation for Hybrid Learning Systems:** SDFT can serve as a powerful initialization method for subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning, or be combined with reward-based signals, potentially leading to hybrid learning systems that benefit from both demonstration-based and reward-based adaptation. This could create more versatile and robust AI.
7. **Broader Applicability Across AI Domains:** The principles demonstrated by SDFT could be extended beyond large language models to other foundation models in vision, robotics, and multimodal AI. The ability to continually acquire new skills and knowledge while preserving old ones is universally desirable for intelligent agents operating in complex environments.
8. **Driving Future Research:** The paper opens several promising avenues for future work, including the integration with traditional on-policy RL, further minimization of residual forgetting, and adaptation to learning from non-expert, noisy, or unstructured data sources.

In conclusion, "Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning" introduces a simple yet powerful mechanism that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in continual learning. By effectively bridging the gap between the theoretical advantages of on-policy learning and the practical realities of learning from demonstrations, SDFT paves the way for a new generation of AI systems that are truly adaptive, robust, and capable of accumulating knowledge and skills over their lifetime, much like humans.
