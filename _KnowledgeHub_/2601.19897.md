# Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT and ETH Zurich developed Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT), a method that enables large language models to continually acquire new skills and knowledge from demonstrations without experiencing catastrophic forgetting. SDFT achieves this by using the model itself as an in-context learning teacher, consistently outperforming traditional fine-tuning methods and enhancing generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Foundation models suffer from catastrophic forgetting when sequentially learning new skills and knowledge from demonstrations, leading to performance degradation on previously mastered tasks.
- Traditional methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) are inherently off-policy, resulting in poor generalization and an inability to adapt to the model's evolving behavior, while on-policy learning from demonstrations typically requires complex Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) or explicit reward functions.
- Training reasoning models often requires expensive, explicitly labeled chain-of-thought annotations; using SFT with only final answers can degrade a model's internal reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- SDFT introduces a novel framework where the foundation model acts as both a student and an ==in-context learning (ICL) teacher==.
- The teacher model, conditioned on expert demonstrations, generates an ==on-policy signal== that guides the student through minimizing a ==reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence== objective, with updates based on trajectories the student itself generates.
- Teacher parameters are maintained as an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA)== of the student's parameters to ensure stable and effective training, tracking student progress while smoothing updates.

## Results

- SDFT consistently achieved superior continual learning, allowing models to acquire new skills (e.g., Tool Use, Science Q&A) while retaining performance on previously learned and general capabilities, in contrast to SFT which exhibited severe catastrophic forgetting.
- The method demonstrated enhanced generalization, achieving higher new-task accuracy for in-distribution examples and near-perfect accuracy on 'indirect' out-of-distribution knowledge acquisition questions.
- SDFT successfully fine-tuned reasoning models using only final answers, preserving internal reasoning depth and significantly improving accuracy where SFT caused a 'reasoning collapse,' with its effectiveness scaling positively with model size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The SDFT objective of minimizing reverse KL divergence is mathematically equivalent to an on-policy Reinforcement Learning algorithm that maximizes an implicit reward function derived from the demonstration-conditioned teacher, circumventing the need for explicit reward engineering.
> - Large pretrained models' inherent in-context learning (ICL) abilities can be effectively leveraged to create high-quality, demonstration-conditioned teaching signals for self-distillation, eliminating reliance on external expert models.
> - Ablation studies confirm that the on-policy nature of SDFT is critical for mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enhancing generalization, as offline distillation methods consistently underperform.
