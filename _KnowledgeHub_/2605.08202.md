# Beyond Penalization: Diffusion-based Out-of-Distribution Detection and Selective Regularization in Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University developed DOSER, an offline reinforcement learning framework that leverages diffusion models for precise out-of-distribution (OOD) action detection. It classifies OOD actions as beneficial or detrimental to apply a dual regularization strategy, achieving strong performance on D4RL benchmarks, especially in suboptimal and heterogeneous data environments.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning policies often suffer from value overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, leading to performance degradation.
- Existing OOD detection methods struggle with accurately identifying OOD samples due to reliance on restrictive assumptions (e.g., Gaussian) or limitations with multi-modal data.
- Uniform penalization of all OOD actions suppresses potentially beneficial explorations, hindering the policy's ability to extrapolate beyond the training data.

## Method

- Utilizes ==two diffusion models== (for the conditional behavior policy and the state distribution) to detect OOD actions and states based on their single-step denoising reconstruction error.
- Classifies OOD actions into "beneficial" or "detrimental" categories by predicting future states with a learned dynamics model and evaluating state distribution shift and value potential.
- Applies a dual regularization strategy: detrimental OOD actions are penalized towards a minimum Q-value, while beneficial OOD actions receive an adaptive bonus.

## Results

- DOSER's diffusion-based OOD detection accurately distinguishes in-distribution from OOD samples in both 1D and high-dimensional tasks, outperforming CVAE-based and uncertainty-based methods.
- Achieved competitive or superior performance on D4RL benchmarks, particularly in "medium" and "medium-replay" datasets, demonstrating effectiveness in suboptimal and heterogeneous data settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the OOD action classification and value compensation components are critical for DOSER's superior performance, validating the dual regularization strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models provide a robust, likelihood-free, and assumption-free mechanism for accurately detecting OOD samples, effectively handling multi-modal distributions via reconstruction error.
> - Differentiating between beneficial and detrimental OOD actions allows for targeted regularization, balancing necessary conservatism with encouraging valuable exploration beyond the dataset support.
> - Integrating a learned dynamics model enables a forward-looking assessment of OOD actions, providing a more informed basis for their classification and adaptive regularization.
