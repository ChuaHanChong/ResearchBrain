# Beyond Penalization: Diffusion-based Out-of-Distribution Detection and Selective Regularization in Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University developed DOSER, an offline reinforcement learning framework that leverages diffusion models for precise out-of-distribution (OOD) action detection. It classifies OOD actions as beneficial or detrimental to apply a dual regularization strategy, achieving strong performance on D4RL benchmarks, especially in suboptimal and heterogeneous data environments.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning policies often suffer from value overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, leading to performance degradation.
- Existing OOD detection methods struggle with accurately identifying OOD samples due to reliance on restrictive assumptions (e.g., Gaussian) or limitations with multi-modal data.
- Uniform penalization of all OOD actions suppresses potentially beneficial explorations, hindering the policy's ability to extrapolate beyond the training data.

## Method

- Utilizes ==two diffusion models== (for the conditional behavior policy and the state distribution) to detect OOD actions and states based on their single-step denoising reconstruction error.
- Classifies OOD actions into "beneficial" or "detrimental" categories by predicting future states with a learned dynamics model and evaluating state distribution shift and value potential.
- Applies a dual regularization strategy: detrimental OOD actions are penalized towards a minimum Q-value, while beneficial OOD actions receive an adaptive bonus.

## Results

- DOSER's diffusion-based OOD detection accurately distinguishes in-distribution from OOD samples in both 1D and high-dimensional tasks, outperforming CVAE-based and uncertainty-based methods.
- Achieved competitive or superior performance on D4RL benchmarks, particularly in "medium" and "medium-replay" datasets, demonstrating effectiveness in suboptimal and heterogeneous data settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the OOD action classification and value compensation components are critical for DOSER's superior performance, validating the dual regularization strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models provide a robust, likelihood-free, and assumption-free mechanism for accurately detecting OOD samples, effectively handling multi-modal distributions via reconstruction error.
> - Differentiating between beneficial and detrimental OOD actions allows for targeted regularization, balancing necessary conservatism with encouraging valuable exploration beyond the dataset support.
> - Integrating a learned dynamics model enables a forward-looking assessment of OOD actions, providing a more informed basis for their classification and adaptive regularization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) is a paradigm that aims to learn optimal policies exclusively from static, pre-collected datasets, eliminating the need for costly or risky online interactions. This approach is particularly relevant for real-world applications where active exploration is prohibitive, such as robotics, healthcare, and autonomous systems. A central challenge in offline RL is the phenomenon of "distribution shift," where a learned policy may generate actions that significantly deviate from the distribution of actions present in the training data. When such "out-of-distribution" (OOD) actions are encountered, standard off-policy RL algorithms often suffer from erroneous value extrapolation, leading to overestimation of action values and subsequent performance degradation.

Existing methods to address this problem generally fall into two categories:
1. **Policy Constraint Methods:** These approaches aim to restrict the learned policy to remain close to the behavior policy observed in the training data. Techniques like variational auto-encoders (VAEs) are frequently used to model the behavior policy's support. A limitation of these methods is their potential inability to accurately capture multi-modal real-world behaviors, sometimes collapsing diverse action distributions into suboptimal, averaged outputs within low-density regions of the data.
2. **Value Regularization Methods:** These methods involve learning conservative Q-functions that explicitly penalize OOD actions. Their effectiveness is contingent on the accuracy of the underlying OOD identification mechanism, which itself is a complex task. Furthermore, many such methods apply uniform penalties across the entire out-of-support region, potentially suppressing valuable explorations that could otherwise lead to improved policy performance.

Recent advancements have attempted to refine OOD handling by introducing more nuanced control over conservatism. Examples include conditioning Q-functions on confidence levels (CCVL), adaptively modulating conservatism at the state-action level using learnable weighting functions (ACL-QL), or employing VAE-based detectors to differentiate OOD actions with distinct properties (DoRL-VC). However, these approaches often incur additional training overhead (e.g., Q-ensemble learning) or rely on restrictive assumptions about the behavior policy (e.g., Gaussian distributions), which can limit their ability to reliably identify OOD samples and selectively regularize them.

This work, DOSER, positions itself by addressing these limitations through two primary innovations: leveraging the generative capabilities of diffusion models for more accurate and assumption-free OOD detection, and introducing a mechanism to distinguish between beneficial and detrimental OOD actions, moving "beyond uniform penalization" to enable "selective regularization." This aims to strike a better balance between maintaining conservatism and encouraging meaningful exploration in offline RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework for offline reinforcement learning that effectively mitigates the issue of value overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, which is a critical challenge in the field. The core motivations stem from observed shortcomings in existing methodologies:

1. **Inaccurate OOD Detection:** Current methods for identifying OOD actions often struggle with reliably distinguishing samples that deviate from the training data distribution. This is partly due to their reliance on restrictive assumptions about data distribution, such as unimodal Gaussian distributions, which are insufficient for capturing the complex, multi-modal nature of real-world behaviors present in offline datasets. Consequently, these methods may incorrectly classify in-distribution (ID) samples as OOD or fail to detect true OOD samples.

2. **Suppression of Beneficial Exploration:** Many existing value regularization techniques apply a uniform penalty to all OOD actions, regardless of their potential utility. This indiscriminate penalization can excessively suppress actions that, while technically OOD, might lead to novel, high-value states or trajectories within the state distribution. Such beneficial OOD actions are crucial for improving policy performance beyond the immediate support of the behavioral data, and their suppression hinders the learned policy's ability to extrapolate effectively.

3. **Computational and Representational Limitations of Prior Approaches:** Some attempts to refine OOD handling either introduce significant computational overhead (e.g., large Q-ensembles) or inherit the representational limitations of models like VAEs, which can produce over-smoothed reconstructions that diminish sensitivity to anomalous inputs and struggle with multi-modal distributions.

To address these motivations, DOSER sets out two key objectives:

1. **Develop a Diffusion-based OOD Detection Mechanism:** The first objective is to leverage the expressive power of diffusion models to achieve a more precise and theoretically grounded method for OOD detection. This involves training two diffusion models: one to approximate the conditional behavior policy `πβ(a|s)` and another to model the state distribution `d0(s)`. By using the single-step denoising reconstruction error from these models as a direct measure of distributional conformity, the framework aims to avoid restrictive assumptions (like Gaussianity) and provide a reliable OOD indicator that can capture multi-modal distributions.

2. **Implement an Adaptive OOD Action Classification and Selective Regularization Strategy:** The second objective is to move beyond binary OOD classification and uniform penalization. This involves introducing a mechanism to further classify OOD actions into "beneficial" and "detrimental" categories. This classification is achieved by integrating a learned dynamics model to predict future states and then evaluating these predicted transitions. Detrimental OOD actions (those likely to induce state distribution shifts or lead to lower value outcomes compared to optimal in-distribution actions) are selectively suppressed. Conversely, beneficial OOD actions (those with the potential to improve performance while remaining within the broader state distribution or leading to higher value outcomes) are encouraged through an adaptive bonus. This dual regularization strategy aims to maintain necessary conservatism while facilitating promising explorations, thereby yielding a robust framework that balances safety and policy improvement.

The motivation is to create a more sophisticated OOD handling framework that can accurately discern OOD samples, differentiate their potential impact, and apply targeted regularization, thereby enabling more effective and safer policy learning in offline RL environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, DOSER (Diffusion-based OOD Detection and SElective Regularization), integrates diffusion models for robust OOD detection with an adaptive regularization strategy. The methodology consists of three main components: Diffusion-based Behavior and State Modeling, OOD Detection via Reconstruction Error, and Adaptive OOD Action Classification, all integrated into a unified algorithmic framework.

#### 3.1. Diffusion-based Behavior and State Modeling

DOSER establishes two diffusion models based on the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022) to capture the underlying data distributions of the offline dataset:

1. **Conditional Diffusion Model for Behavior Policy:** A denoising network `ϵθa(at, σt, s)` is trained to approximate the empirical behavior policy distribution `ˆπβ(a|s)`. This model learns to reconstruct the original action `a0` from a noisy version `at` (where `at = a0 + σtϵ` with `ϵ ~ N(0, I)`) conditioned on the state `s`. The training objective minimizes a reweighted L2 loss (Equation 4) over sampled noise scales `σt`, original state-action pairs `(s, a0)` from the dataset `D`, and Gaussian noise `ϵ`.

2. **Unconditional Diffusion Model for State Distribution:** A separate denoising network `ϵθs(st, σt)` is trained to capture the state distribution `d0(s)` of the dataset. This model reconstructs original states `s0` from their noisy counterparts `st` (where `st = s0 + σtϵ`). Its training objective similarly minimizes a reweighted L2 loss (Equation 5) over sampled noise scales `σt` and states `s` from `D`.

The EDM framework is chosen for its efficiency, enabling optimized noise schedules and faster sampling compared to discrete Markov chain models.

#### 3.2. OOD Detection via Reconstruction Error

The core of DOSER's OOD detection mechanism relies on the reconstruction capabilities of the pretrained diffusion models. For a given state-action pair `(s, a0)` or state `s0`:

1. **Action OOD Score:** A noise scale `σt` is sampled from the training schedule, and the action `a0` is perturbed to `at = a0 + σtϵ`. The OOD score `Ea(s, a0)` is defined as the L2 reconstruction error between the original action `a0` and its denoised counterpart `ϵθa(at, σt, s)` (Equation 6).

2. **State OOD Score:** Similarly, for a state `s0`, its OOD score `Es(s0)` is the L2 reconstruction error between `s0` and its denoised version `ϵθs(st, σt)` (Equation 7).

3. **OOD Indicator Functions:** Binary OOD indicator functions `Iood(a0)` and `Iood(s0)` (Equation 8) are then defined by comparing these reconstruction errors against thresholds `τa` and `τs`, respectively. These thresholds are set as p-th percentiles of the reconstruction errors observed on the training dataset `D`, with `p` determining the level of conservatism.

This method offers advantages such as providing a likelihood-free measure of distributional alignment, naturally handling multi-modal distributions without restrictive assumptions, and being computationally efficient due to single-pass evaluation. Robustness is further improved by evaluating errors across multiple randomly sampled diffusion timesteps.

#### 3.3. Adaptive OOD Action Classification

DOSER introduces a mechanism to classify OOD actions as either "beneficial" or "detrimental" during policy optimization, rather than applying uniform penalties. This involves a two-stage assessment for each policy-generated OOD action `aood` in state `s`:

1. **Next-State Prediction:** A separate dynamics model `pψ(s'|s, a)` is pretrained via supervised learning on the offline dataset `D` (minimizing L2 loss in Equation 13). This model predicts the subsequent state `s'π` resulting from `(s, aood)`.

2. **Outcome Evaluation:** The predicted next state `s'π` is evaluated along two dimensions:
 - **State Distribution Shift:** Whether `s'π` lies outside the training state distribution, determined by comparing `Es(s'π)` against the state OOD threshold `τs`.
 - **Value Potential:** If `s'π` is in-distribution, whether its value `V(s'π)` exceeds the value of the next state `V(s'id)` obtained by executing the optimal in-distribution action `a*id` at state `s`. The optimal in-distribution action `a*id` is approximated by selecting the action with the maximum Q-value among `N` sampled actions from the empirical behavior policy `ˆπβ(·|s)` (Equation 11).

Based on this evaluation, actions are classified (Definition 1):
- **Beneficial OOD actions (`A+ood`):** Actions where `s'π` is in-distribution (`Es(s'π) ≤ τs`) AND `V(s'π)` is greater than or equal to `V(s'id)`.
- **Detrimental OOD actions (`A-ood`):**  Actions where `s'π` is OOD (`Es(s'π) > τs`) OR `V(s'π)` is less than `V(s'id)`.

#### 3.4. Dual Regularization Strategy

This classification informs a dual regularization strategy for the Q-function learning (Equation 10):
- **Detrimental OOD Actions:** These are penalized by driving their Q-values towards `Qmin` (the theoretical minimum Q-value).
- **Beneficial OOD Actions:** These receive an adaptive bonus `δV = V(s'π) - V(s'id)`, which is added to `Q(s, a*id)` and weighted by `η` to form their target Q-value. This bonus encourages exploration towards high-potential regions.

#### 3.5. Practical Implementation

- **Value Learning:** The value network `Vθ` is trained using expectile regression (Equation 12), similar to IQL, to provide robust value estimates.
- **Policy Learning:** The actor network `πϕ` is optimized with maximum entropy regularization (Equation 14), guided by the learned Q-function.
- **Target Networks:** Standard target networks `Qθ'` and `Vθ'` are used for stability.

The overall algorithm alternates between updating critic networks (Q and V), and actor network, incorporating the OOD detection and selective regularization mechanisms.

#### 3.6. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical guarantees for DOSER, including:
- **Contraction Mapping Property (Theorem 1):** Proves that the DOSER operator `TDOSER` is a `γ`-contraction mapping, guaranteeing a unique fixed point and convergence.
- **Bounded Value Estimation (Theorem 2):** Shows that the fixed point `QπDOSER` has bounded value estimates, preventing uncontrolled overestimation while allowing controlled exploration.
- **Bounded Critic Deviation (Theorem 3):** Provides an upper bound on the deviation of the learned critic from an ideal reference policy under dynamics model approximation and OOD detection errors.
- **Performance Gap Guarantee (Theorem 4):** Shows that the performance gap between the optimal policy and the DOSER-learned policy is bounded by function approximation, OOD detection, and dynamics model approximation errors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence supporting the effectiveness of DOSER across several experiments, focusing on OOD detection capability, overall policy performance on standard benchmarks, and the contribution of individual components.

#### 4.1. OOD Detection Effectiveness

- **1D Navigation Task (Figure 3):** DOSER's diffusion-based reconstruction error effectively differentiates between in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) samples across the entire state-action space in a simple 1D navigation task. In contrast, baseline methods such as model ensemble, MC dropout, and CVAE-based reconstruction error struggled to achieve reliable OOD identification. The model ensemble and MC dropout methods reportedly misclassify OOD samples due to an inability to disentangle epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. CVAE-based reconstruction, while better than uncertainty-based methods, exhibited limitations in modeling multi-modal distributions.
- **D4RL MuJoCo Domain (Figure 4, Appendix C.1):** On high-dimensional continuous control tasks, the diffusion-based OOD detection consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to CVAE-based reconstruction error. CVAE-based methods were observed to produce over-smoothed reconstructions, which diminished their sensitivity to anomalous action inputs. Visualizations in Appendix C.1 show clear separation in reconstruction error distributions between ID and OOD datasets, indicating a robust OOD signal.
- **Synthetic OOD and Anomaly Detection Benchmarks (Appendix C.1, C.2):** Quantitative analysis on synthetic OOD datasets (generated by perturbing D4RL actions with noise) revealed high precision, recall, F1-score, and AUROC, especially with increased noise scales (e.g., AUROC reached 1.0 for larger perturbations). Validation on general anomaly detection benchmarks (KDDCUP, Arrhythmia) also showed that the diffusion-based approach achieved competitive or superior F1-scores compared to various state-of-the-art anomaly detection methods, suggesting broader applicability and reliability of reconstruction error as an OOD indicator.
- **Correlation with Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) (Appendix C.3):** A strong positive correlation (Pearson ρ=0.9718) was observed between diffusion-based reconstruction error and true NLL in a synthetic Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) setting, confirming its consistency with likelihood. A substantial correlation (ρ=0.7848) was also found with CVAE-based NLL on a D4RL dataset, further supporting reconstruction error as a meaningful proxy for likelihood estimation in complex environments.

#### 4.2. Comparisons on D4RL Benchmarks

- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** DOSER consistently achieved strong performance on standard D4RL benchmarks, often outperforming or matching a wide range of prior methods, including conventional algorithms and recent diffusion-based approaches, across both Gym-MuJoCo and Adroit tasks.
- **Performance on Suboptimal Datasets:** DOSER's advantage was particularly notable in "medium" and "medium-replay" settings, which contain suboptimal and heterogeneous behaviors. This suggests the effectiveness of its diffusion-based OOD detection and selective regularization in scenarios where the policy needs to extrapolate beyond dense data regions.
- **Comparison to Other Diffusion/CVAE Methods:** While existing diffusion-based baselines showed improvements over traditional methods due to their expressive modeling, DOSER further enhanced performance by explicitly classifying OOD actions. Compared to CVAE-based methods (e.g., SVR, A2PR), DOSER's use of diffusion models for more accurate OOD detection and its targeted regularization strategy contributed to superior empirical performance, enabling the learned policy to explore high-value regions beyond the dataset support. Additional experiments on expert and random datasets (Appendix C.8) also demonstrated DOSER's competitive performance on expert data and stronger performance on low-quality random data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Components in DOSER

- **DOSER w/o AC and VC (Action Classification and Value Compensation):** This variant, which relies solely on diffusion-based OOD detection with uniform penalization, performed competitively but showed performance degradation compared to the full DOSER. This indicates that the core diffusion-based OOD detection is effective, but uniform penalization limits its potential.
- **DOSER w/o VC (Value Compensation):** This variant, which incorporates OOD action classification (distinguishing beneficial from detrimental without the explicit value bonus `δV`), showed smaller performance drops than "DOSER w/o AC and VC." This finding validates the effectiveness of the fine-grained classification mechanism in selectively regularizing OOD actions.
- **Conclusion:** The full DOSER framework, incorporating both OOD action classification and value compensation, consistently yielded the best performance, confirming the contribution of each component to balancing conservatism and exploration.

#### 4.4. Sensitivity Analysis

- **OOD Detection Thresholds (τa, τs) (Figure 5a):** Performance was sensitive to these thresholds. Smaller thresholds were beneficial for narrow behavioral distributions (e.g., "medium-expert") to capture more OOD samples, while larger thresholds were preferred for diverse datasets (e.g., "medium", "medium-replay") to prevent misclassification of ID samples.
- **Penalty Coefficient (β) (Figure 5b):** Narrow distributions required a larger `β` to prevent overestimation, while diverse datasets benefited from a smaller `β` to avoid over-suppressing beneficial OOD actions.
- **Compensation Coefficient (λ) (Figure 5c):** DOSER performed well across a range of `λ` values on diverse datasets. Excessively large `λ` could amplify compensation and lead to overestimation.
- **Dynamics Model Error (Appendix C.6.1):** Poorly trained dynamics models, used for OOD action classification, consistently degraded performance, sometimes below the "DOSER w/o AC and VC" baseline. This highlights the importance of dynamics model accuracy for the selective regularization mechanism.
- **Number of Critic Networks (Appendix C.6.2):** Using two critics yielded comparable performance to four critics, suggesting DOSER's core contributions are not dependent on a larger critic ensemble size.
- **Compensation Target Weight (η) (Appendix C.6.3):** DOSER maintained stable performance across `η` values of 0.8 to 1.0, with a slight degradation at `η=1.0` due to mild overestimation from full adoption of target Q-values.
- **Number of Sampled In-Distribution Actions (N) (Appendix C.6.4):** DOSER was robust to varying `N` (number of actions sampled to estimate `a*id`), indicating that precise estimation of `a*id` is not strictly necessary for guiding beneficial OOD actions.
- **Q-value Lower Bound (Qmin) (Appendix C.6.5):** Setting `Qmin` to 0 (less conservative) degraded performance compared to the environment-derived `Qmin = Rmin/(1-γ)`, indicating that proper penalization of detrimental OOD actions is important.

#### 4.5. Action Type Proportions and Q-Value Distribution

- **Action Type Proportions (Figure 11):** During training, the proportion of ID actions increased while OOD actions decreased, indicating policy alignment with behavioral support. The relative proportions of ID, beneficial OOD, and detrimental OOD actions varied with dataset quality. For diverse datasets ("medium", "medium-replay"), beneficial OOD actions substantially outnumbered detrimental ones. For narrower datasets ("medium-expert"), these proportions were more balanced.
- **Q-Value Distribution (Figure 12):** Beneficial OOD actions were consistently associated with higher predicted Q-values, while detrimental OOD actions were concentrated in low-value regions. This empirically confirmed that the classification mechanism guides the policy towards high-value explorations and suppresses risky actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DOSER contributes to the field of offline reinforcement learning by addressing the critical challenge of value overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions through a multi-faceted approach. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced OOD Detection Fidelity:** The work establishes diffusion models as a robust and theoretically grounded tool for OOD detection in RL. By leveraging reconstruction error, DOSER overcomes the limitations of prior methods that rely on restrictive distributional assumptions (e.g., Gaussian) or struggle with multi-modal data (e.g., VAEs and some uncertainty estimation techniques). This improved fidelity in identifying OOD samples is fundamental for any effective offline RL strategy, as it provides a more accurate basis for subsequent policy adjustments.

2. **Adaptive and Selective Regularization:** Moving beyond uniform penalization, DOSER introduces a novel mechanism to categorize OOD actions as either "beneficial" or "detrimental." This adaptive classification, informed by a learned dynamics model and value function, allows for a nuanced regularization strategy: selectively penalizing risky, detrimental actions while encouraging high-potential, beneficial explorations. This approach facilitates policy improvement beyond the strict confines of the behavioral dataset, which is crucial for achieving high performance in many real-world scenarios.

3. **Theoretical Foundations:** The theoretical analysis demonstrating the contraction mapping property of the DOSER operator and providing bounds on value estimates and performance gaps offers a rigorous basis for the algorithm's stability and effectiveness. These guarantees are important for building confidence in the method's ability to prevent uncontrolled value overestimation and converge to a desirable policy.

4. **Superior Empirical Performance:** DOSER's consistent outperformance or competitive results on challenging D4RL benchmarks, particularly on suboptimal datasets (e.g., "medium" and "medium-replay"), demonstrate its practical utility. This indicates that the framework can derive more effective policies from diverse and potentially low-quality offline data, a common characteristic of real-world datasets.

5. **Broader Applicability and Future Directions:** The successful application of diffusion models for OOD detection in this context suggests their potential for other areas of robust machine learning and decision-making where distribution shift is a concern. The framework's ability to balance conservatism and exploration can lead to safer and more effective policies in domains where online interaction is expensive or dangerous, such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and medical applications.

While DOSER exhibits strong performance, the paper acknowledges limitations related to the reliance on the accuracy of the dynamics model and the computational overhead of iterative diffusion sampling. Future research can focus on enhancing the robustness of dynamics models and improving computational efficiency through techniques like model distillation or accelerated sampling. Nevertheless, DOSER represents a significant step towards more reliable and adaptable offline reinforcement learning systems capable of navigating distribution shifts with greater precision and strategic insight.
