# InterDiff: Generating 3D Human-Object Interactions with Physics-Informed Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> InterDiff predicts long-term 3D whole-body human-object interactions with dynamic objects. A transformer DDPM conditioned on past motion and PointNet shape features generates future mesh HOIs; a physics-informed corrector then re-predicts object motion in a contact-point-relative reference frame and blends it back into each late denoising step, cutting penetration and contact-floating artifacts while beating VAE and RNN baselines.

## Problem

- Prior HOI synthesis is limited to hand-object or small static objects, egocentric views, or simplified skeletons, ignoring whole-body motion with dynamic objects of varied shape.
- Motion diffusion models encode no physical laws, so predictions show floating contact and penetration, and errors compound during autoregressive rollout to long horizons.
- Existing fixes need post-hoc optimization or physics simulators requiring per-object friction, stiffness, and mass registration plus costly control-policy training, none of which motion-capture datasets supply.

## Method

- **Interaction Diffusion**: a ==DDPM== transformer encoder-decoder denoising the joint human-object sequence, conditioned on encoded past HOI frames and a ==PointNet== embedding of object shape.
- **Interaction Correction**: a correction scheduler fires only in late steps when signed-distance penetration or contact-distance thresholds fail, then selects the contacting body marker as reference frame.
- **Interaction Predictor**: an ==STGNN== over a spatial-temporal graph of object motion in ground plus 67 marker frames, using ==DCT bases==, blended back with weight t/T.

## Results

- On BEHAVE, the full model reaches **140** mm MPJPE-H and **123** mm translation error, beating InterVAE (**145/125**) and InterRNN (**165/139**); correction cuts penetration **228** to **164**.
- On the skeletal Human-Object Interaction dataset with unseen objects, object MPJPE drops **117** to **84** and translation error **92** to **60**, versus HO-GCN's **153** and **123**.
- Long-term 100-frame autoregressive rollout with ten samples cuts penetration **187** to **59**; 23 judges preferred the full model in **67.8%** of pairwise comparisons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing a contact-anchored reference frame turns complex global interaction dynamics into near-deterministic relative motion that small networks predict reliably, a cheap substitute for simulation.
> - Physics priors can be injected inside the denoising loop rather than post-hoc; generator and corrector train separately yet compose at inference without fine-tuning.
> - Correcting object motion also improves predicted human motion, and gains grow with horizon and sample count, though contact inconsistency persists and post-optimization still helps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of 3D human-object interaction (HOI) synthesis, human motion prediction, and generative modeling, particularly leveraging the recent advancements in diffusion models. It addresses several critical limitations of existing research, pushing the boundaries of what automated systems can achieve in understanding and forecasting complex physical interactions.

Historically, HOI synthesis has been constrained by various factors. Many approaches focused solely on hand-object interactions, often within an ego-centric view, or limited themselves to manipulating small or static objects. Research attempting whole-body interactions frequently relied on simplified skeletal representations, which fail to capture the nuances of mesh-based contact and deformation, or overlooked the dynamic nature of objects. Ensuring physical validity (e.g., preventing limb penetration into objects, maintaining realistic contact) has traditionally been tackled through computationally expensive post-hoc optimization techniques or by developing intricate physics simulation environments, which require precise registration of objects with detailed physical properties (friction, stiffness, mass) that are rarely available in standard motion capture datasets. These methods are also time-consuming, both in setup and during inference, as they often involve training control policies for specific interactions.

The broader landscape of human motion prediction has seen significant progress with deep generative models, including VAEs, GANs, and more recently, diffusion models. While these models excel at generating diverse and realistic human movements, they typically lack an inherent understanding of physical laws. When applied to HOI, this deficiency leads to common artifacts such as "floating contact" (human body parts appearing to hover over objects without proper engagement) or object penetration.

InterDiff distinguishes itself by introducing a *novel task*: anticipating 3D mesh-based whole-body HOIs with dynamic objects. Crucially, it proposes a purely learning-based approach that directly injects "intuitive physics" into a diffusion model, thereby sidestepping the complexities and costs of post-optimization or full-scale physics simulations. The key innovation lies in recognizing that while global human-object dynamics are complex, the *relative motion* of an object with respect to its contact point on the human body often follows much simpler, more predictable patterns. This insight allows the model to enforce physical plausibility more effectively and efficiently. By integrating a physics-informed correction step directly into the iterative denoising process of a diffusion model, InterDiff provides a significant advancement over prior work, which either sacrificed physical realism for generative diversity or achieved realism at a high computational and setup cost. It extends the power of diffusion models to a more challenging domain, where physical constraints are paramount for perceptual realism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of InterDiff is to develop an automated system capable of **accurately forecasting long-term, diverse, and physically valid 3D human-object interactions (HOIs)**. This involves predicting whole-body mesh-based human movements and dynamic object states simultaneously, conditioned on past interaction history and object shape.

The motivation behind this ambitious goal stems from several key observations and needs:

- **Mimicking Human Cognitive Abilities:** Humans possess a remarkable ability to anticipate future events, including how their actions will interact with dynamic environments. Creating an artificial system that can "look into the future" in the context of HOIs is a significant step towards more intelligent and autonomous AI agents.
- **Addressing Real-World Complexity:** Existing HOI synthesis methods are often inadequate for the complexities of real-world scenarios. They frequently fall short in modeling:
 - **Whole-body interactions:** Focusing only on hands or simplified skeletons fails to capture the full physical engagement of the human body with objects.
 - **Dynamic objects:** Many systems assume static environments or objects, which is unrealistic for many practical applications.
 - **Variety in object shapes:** Handling diverse object geometries presents a considerable challenge.
 - **Physical validity:** Generating interactions that are perceptually realistic and adhere to physical laws (e.g., no penetration, proper contact) is crucial for believability and usability in applications.
- **Impact across Multiple Fields:** An accurate HOI forecasting system holds significant implications for:
 - **Robotics:** Enabling robots to anticipate human actions and intentions in shared workspaces, leading to safer and more efficient human-robot collaboration. This includes applications in assistive robotics, manufacturing, and even surgical assistance.
 - **Animation and Virtual Reality:** Generating highly realistic and believable character interactions with virtual objects, enhancing immersion and reducing manual animation effort in games, film, and virtual training environments.
 - **Computer Vision:** Improving scene understanding, activity recognition, and event forecasting in video analysis, with potential applications in surveillance, sports analytics, and autonomous driving.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Prior Approaches:** The paper explicitly motivates moving beyond methods that rely on:
 - **Post-hoc optimization:** These methods often add physical constraints *after* motion generation, which can be computationally intensive, difficult to converge, and may distort the original motion.
 - **Physics simulation environments:** While physically accurate, these require extensive effort to set up (object properties, friction models) and train control policies, which is impractical for diverse, real-world datasets lacking such detailed information.
- **Leveraging a Key Insight:** The core motivation for InterDiff's specific approach is the authors' crucial observation: despite the overall complexity of human and object motion, the *short-term relative motion of an object with respect to its contact point on the human body exhibits simpler, more deterministic patterns*. This insight forms the bedrock for injecting physics knowledge in an intuitive and computationally efficient manner, differentiating InterDiff from prior purely data-driven generative models that struggle with physical plausibility.

In summary, InterDiff aims to fill a critical gap in realistic 3D HOI generation by developing an intelligent, learning-based system that can predict complex, dynamic, whole-body interactions while inherently ensuring physical realism, thus unlocking significant potential across various application domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InterDiff proposes a novel, purely learning-based framework to anticipate 3D human-object interactions, comprising two main components: **Interaction Diffusion** and **Interaction Correction**. The framework takes a sequence of historical HOI frames and object shape information to predict future frames that are both accurate and physically valid.

**Problem Formulation:** A 3D HOI sequence is defined by `x = [x^1, ..., x^(H+F)]`, where `x^i` includes human pose `h^i` (J joints, D_h-dimensional) and object pose `o^i` (D_o features like position, rotation). Given object shape `c`, the goal is to predict `x_0` for `F` future frames that is close to ground truth and physically valid.

#### 3.1. Interaction Diffusion (DDPM-based Generator)

This component is responsible for modeling the complex distribution of future human-object interactions.
- **Basic Diffusion Model:** InterDiff employs a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM). The **forward diffusion process** gradually adds Gaussian noise to the true data `x_0` over `T` steps, transforming it into `x_T` (an isotropic Gaussian distribution). The **reverse diffusion process** aims to iteratively denoise `x_T` back to `x_0`.
- **Interaction Diffusion Model Structure:** The reverse process is handled by a neural network `G`, which directly estimates the clean signal `~x` at each step `t`, given the noisy input `x_t` and a condition `c`.
 - `G` utilizes a **transformer architecture** (encoder-decoder design). The encoder processes historical HOI frames (`x_1:H`) along with an embedded representation of the object shape `c` (generated by a PointNet). This conditional encoding informs the generation of future HOIs.
 - The noisy HOI `x_t` and a time-step embedding are fed into the transformer decoder.
- **Learning Objectives:** The model `G` is trained with an L2 objective `L_r` to minimize the difference between the predicted clean signal `~x` and the ground truth `x`. This objective is further disentangled into separate rotation and translation losses for both human (`h`) and object (`o`) states, and these losses are re-weighted. Additionally, velocity regularizations (`L_vh`, `L_vo`) are introduced to promote smooth and realistic motion over time.

#### 3.2. Interaction Correction (Physics-informed Predictor)

This is the core innovation for ensuring physical validity and mitigating common artifacts like floating contact and penetration. It directly integrates physics-informed priors into the diffusion process.
- **Key Insight:** The authors leverage the observation that the short-term *relative motion* of an object with respect to its contact point on the human body is simpler and easier to predict than its global motion. For example, a ball being juggled might have a complex global trajectory, but its motion relative to the hand is simply up and down.
- **Correction Schedule:** Rather than correcting at every diffusion step, a scheduler `S` determines *when* and *if* a correction is needed.
 - **Trigger Conditions:** Correction is primarily applied in later diffusion iterations (when `~x` has more signal than noise) and only when the current denoised HOI `~x` exhibits physical implausibilities:
 - Significant **penetration** (`||P|| > ε_1`) between human and object meshes.
 - Lack of **contact** (`min ||C[j]|| > ε_2`) when contact should be present.
 - **Reference System Selection:** If correction is triggered, `S` identifies a reference system `s`. This is either the default ground reference system (if no contact is detected) or the human marker/joint that is closest to the object, serving as the contact point. This is crucial for localizing the interaction and exploiting the "simple relative motion" insight.
- **Interaction Prediction:** An interaction predictor `P` then refines the object motion.
 - **Coordinate Transformation:** Past object motion (initially in the ground system) is transformed into relative motions with respect to all potential human markers/joints (including the ground as a reference).
 - **Spatial-Temporal Graph Neural Network (STGNN):** An STGNN processes these transformed past motions. This network is designed to predict future object motions within each of these reference systems. To further simplify motion patterns and aid prediction, **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT/IDCT)** is applied as a preprocessing step within the STGNN.
 - **Reference-Specific Prediction:** The `STGNN` outputs future object motions for all reference systems. The predictor then selects the specific object motion corresponding to the reference system `s` identified by the correction scheduler.
 - **Inverse Transformation & Blending:** This selected relative object motion is transformed back into the ground reference system, yielding a physically plausible `^x`. This corrected `^x` is then blended with the original denoised `~x` (from the diffusion model) using a weighted average based on the diffusion time step, allowing for gradual integration of physics-informed corrections.
- **Decoupled Training:** A key advantage is that the interaction diffusion model and the interaction predictor can be trained *separately*. They are then composed during inference without requiring further fine-tuning, simplifying the training pipeline.
- **Additional Losses for Predictor:** The interaction predictor's training includes specific loss functions to promote contact (`L_c`) and penalize penetration (`L_p`), especially for mesh-based representations (e.g., using Signed Distance Fields).

**Data Representation:** The methodology is flexible, tested on datasets using different representations: SMPL-H for mesh-based human representation and 6D rotation/translation for objects (BEHAVE dataset), and 21-joint skeletons for humans with 12 key points for objects (Human-Object Interaction dataset).

This dual-component approach allows InterDiff to harness the generative power of diffusion models for diverse motion synthesis while intelligently injecting physics-informed corrections to guarantee realistic and plausible interactions, all within a computationally efficient, learning-based framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InterDiff demonstrates significant advancements in 3D human-object interaction (HOI) prediction, validated through extensive quantitative evaluations, qualitative visualizations, user studies, and ablation experiments across multiple datasets.

- **Quantitative Superiority over Baselines:**
 - InterDiff consistently outperforms established baselines such as InterRNN (LSTM-based), InterVAE (Transformer-based VAE), CAHMP, and HO-GCN across all evaluated metrics.
 - On the BEHAVE dataset, InterDiff (full model) shows improvements in Mean Per Joint Position Error for Human (MPJPE-H), Object Translation Error (Trans. Err.), Object Rotation Error (Rot. Err.), and notably, a substantial reduction in Penetration (Pene.). For example, Pene. drops from 228 (InterVAE) and 228 (InterDiff w/o correction) to 164 with the full InterDiff model.
 - Similar improvements are observed on the Human-Object Interaction dataset, particularly with unseen objects, where InterDiff (full) achieves significantly lower MPJPE-O (Object Keypoint Error), Trans. Err., and Rot. Err. compared to other methods.

- **Effectiveness of Interaction Correction:**
 - The "interaction correction" step is unequivocally shown to be crucial. Quantitatively, it leads to a marked decrease in penetration artifacts and more accurate object motions.
 - Interestingly, incorporating more precise object motion via correction also positively impacts the accuracy of predicted human motion (MPJPE-H), even with the same underlying diffusion model, highlighting the interconnectedness of human and object dynamics.

- **Long-Term Forecasting and Diversity:**
 - InterDiff is capable of producing remarkably long-term HOI predictions, extending to 100 future frames autoregressively, a challenging task where error accumulation is typically high.
 - Through "Best-of-Many" evaluations, the paper demonstrates that InterDiff can generate diverse and legitimate HOI sequences. As more samples are generated, the best predictions (lowest error) increasingly converge closer to the ground truth, particularly for longer horizons and with the full correction mechanism.

- **Generalizability:**
 - The model exhibits strong generalizability, effectively predicting future HOIs involving **unseen objects** during training on the Human-Object Interaction dataset.
 - Crucially, InterDiff trained on the BEHAVE dataset successfully generalizes to the **GRAB dataset** (focused on grasping small objects) without any fine-tuning. This cross-dataset generalizability is attributed to the key design strategies, such as promoting simple motion patterns and leveraging local reference systems for interaction prediction.

- **Qualitative Results and User Study:**
 - Qualitative visualizations (Figures 4, 5, 6, 7) confirm that InterDiff, especially with correction, produces visually plausible and vivid interactions, mitigating contact floating and penetration artifacts.
 - A double-blind user study further substantiates the model's performance. Users rated InterDiff (full model) as producing more realistic interactions with a 67.8% success rate against ablated baselines, confirming its superior physical fidelity.

- **Ablation Studies Insights:** The ablation studies provide critical validation for each component of the interaction correction:
 - **`w/o correction`:** Confirmed significant contact floating and penetration artifacts, underlining the necessity of the correction step.
 - **`w/o relative` (no coordinate transformation for object motion):** This variant performed poorly, leading to severe contact penetration. This strongly emphasizes the pivotal role of transforming object motion into a local, contact-point-relative reference system for accurate and physically consistent predictions.
 - **`w/o contact` (omitting contact/penetration losses in predictor):** Results in unrealistic outcomes, demonstrating the importance of explicitly enforcing these physical constraints during prediction.
 - **`w/o schedule` (blindly applying correction):** Shows that unjudicious application of correction can destabilize otherwise good motion quality, underscoring the value of the intelligent correction schedule.
 - **Effect of DCT bases:** An additional ablation showed that DCT/IDCT plays a role in promoting simpler motion patterns, with an optimal number of bases balancing translation and rotation errors.

In summary, InterDiff not only establishes a new state-of-the-art for the challenging task of 3D HOI prediction but also robustly demonstrates that integrating physics-informed priors, particularly through local reference systems and a judicious correction schedule, is a highly effective and efficient strategy for achieving physically plausible and diverse long-term generative outcomes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The InterDiff framework represents a significant step forward in the field of 3D human-object interaction (HOI) prediction, offering a novel and practical approach to a highly complex task. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

- **Pioneering a Novel and Realistic Task:** InterDiff addresses a crucial gap by tackling mesh-based 3D HOI prediction with *dynamic objects* and *whole-body interactions*. This goes beyond previous limitations of static objects, hand-centric interactions, or simplified skeletal representations, bringing HOI synthesis closer to real-world complexity and applications. This novel task itself provides a new benchmark and direction for future research.

- **Bridging the Gap between Generative Models and Physical Realism:** One of InterDiff's most profound impacts is its ability to infuse complex deep generative models (diffusion models) with intuitive physical priors. By intelligently correcting implausible interactions within the diffusion process, it overcomes a longstanding challenge: producing diverse and novel motions that also adhere to physical laws without resorting to computationally expensive post-optimization or full physics simulations. This "physics-informed diffusion" paradigm could inspire similar integrations in other generative tasks requiring physical plausibility.

- **Efficiency and Practicality:** The decoupled training of the diffusion model and the interaction predictor, combined with their seamless integration during inference without fine-tuning, makes InterDiff a highly efficient and practical solution. This stands in stark contrast to previous methods that required extensive setup for physics engines or iterative optimization, making it more accessible for deployment in real-world systems.

- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The demonstrated ability of InterDiff to generalize to unseen objects and even new datasets (like GRAB, from BEHAVE training) without fine-tuning is highly significant. This suggests that the model learns fundamental principles of interaction and relative motion, rather than merely memorizing training data. This generalizability is crucial for real-world applications where object variations are immense.

- **Applications Across Multiple Industries:**
 - **Robotics:** InterDiff's capacity to anticipate realistic HOIs is transformative for human-robot interaction. Robots can leverage this predictive ability to proactively adjust their actions, ensure safety, and collaborate more naturally with humans in tasks ranging from manufacturing and logistics to assistive care.
 - **Computer Graphics and Animation:** Animators can generate incredibly realistic and dynamic interactions between characters and objects with minimal manual effort, drastically speeding up production pipelines for films, video games, and virtual reality content. The diversity of generated motions adds artistic freedom.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** For immersive experiences, realistic virtual human-object interactions are paramount. InterDiff can drive avatars with believable physical engagement, enhancing user presence and realism in virtual environments.
 - **Computer Vision and Human Behavior Analysis:** The model could be used to generate synthetic data for training HOI detection and recognition models, or directly applied to predict future actions in surveillance, sports analytics, or elder care monitoring.

- **Foundational for Future Research:** The paper opens doors for several exciting future research directions, as noted by the authors. Generalizing to more complex environments involving multiple dynamic objects, articulated or deformable objects, and even interactions with other humans, represents the next frontier. InterDiff provides a strong foundation for tackling these increasingly intricate scenarios.

In conclusion, InterDiff is a landmark contribution that not only solves a challenging problem in 3D HOI prediction but also offers a powerful, efficient, and generalizable framework for integrating physical realism into deep generative models. Its impact will be felt across various scientific and industrial domains, pushing the boundaries of autonomous systems and digital content creation.
