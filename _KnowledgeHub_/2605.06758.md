# R$^3$L: Reasoning 3D Layouts from Relative Spatial Relations

> [!summary] Summary
> R³L is a framework from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University that improves 3D layout generation from natural language instructions by mitigating semantic and metric drift in MLLM-based multi-hop spatial reasoning. It consistently achieves strong semantic performance and perfect physical feasibility, with average collision and out-of-bound ratios of 0.0% across diverse scene types.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with reliably inferring relative spatial relations, especially in multi-hop reasoning scenarios.
- Semantic drift occurs when repeated reference-frame transformations during multi-hop reasoning distort directional semantics, leading to semantically inconsistent layouts.
- Metric drift arises from compounding geometric misalignments during per-hop vector composition, resulting in physically infeasible layouts with object collisions or inconsistent spacing.

## Method

- Invariant Spatial Decomposition partitions scene assets into frame-invariant units, factoring relations into intra-unit and inter-unit levels to reduce global reference-frame transformations and mitigate semantic drift.
- Consistent Spatial Imagination employs global-local cognitive maps and an iterative imagine-and-revise loop where the MLLM explicitly outputs geometric primitives and corrects geometric violations to suppress metric drift.
- Supportive Spatial Optimization utilizes global-to-local pose re-parameterization and a two-level objective function to decouple intra-unit gradients from global poses, improving convergence speed and stability during differentiable optimization.

## Results

- R³L consistently outperformed baselines (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, LayoutVLM) across diverse scene types, achieving the strongest semantic performance and perfect physical feasibility with **0.0%** collision and out-of-bound ratios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Invariant Spatial Decomposition and Consistent Spatial Imagination are complementary and crucial, with decomposition stabilizing semantics and imagination suppressing metric drift.
- The supportive spatial optimization converged approximately 1.8 times faster than standard global optimization, demonstrating improved efficiency and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Addressing semantic and metric drift through modular components (decomposition and imagination) during the MLLM's reasoning phase is more effective than post-hoc corrections.
> - Structuring multi-hop spatial reasoning into invariant units significantly reduces the accumulation of errors from repeated reference-frame transformations.
> - Decoupling gradients in spatial optimization via global-to-local pose re-parameterization substantially enhances convergence speed and stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of 3D layout generation, particularly focusing on methods that leverage natural language instructions for scene composition. This area is crucial for applications in vision and robotics, where grounding high-level relational intent into concrete spatial arrangements is required.

Traditionally, 3D layout generation has relied on hand-crafted rules or dataset-specific priors (e.g., Merrell et al., 2011; Paschalidou et al., 2021; Tang ets al., 2024), which often exhibit limited generalization capabilities. More recently, the advent of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has enabled more open-vocabulary approaches. These MLLMs are used either to directly output numerical object positions and orientations (e.g., LayoutGPT by Feng et al., 2023; Bucher & Armeni, 2025; Yang et al., 2024a) or to infer relative spatial relations which are then instantiated into object poses (e.g., HOLODECK by Yang et al., 2024b; LayoutVLM by Sun et al., 2025).

The direct output paradigm, while straightforward, frequently produces physically invalid layouts. Subsequent improvements include fine-tuning MLLMs with supervised data or preference alignment, and agentic or tool-augmented frameworks that iteratively refine layouts using semantic and physical feedback. However, these often involve explicit trial-and-error, starting from infeasible drafts and requiring repeated external feedback.

The constraint-driven paradigm, where relations are first inferred and then optimized, includes methods that use DFS-based solvers (e.g., HOLODECK), which can restrict the solution space and sacrifice semantic fidelity. Differentiable optimization approaches (e.g., LayoutVLM) aim to balance semantic coherence and physical plausibility but remain sensitive to initialization and errors in upstream relation inference.

A common limitation across many prior works is the reliance on post-hoc heuristics (e.g., grid-map discretization, pruning conflicting relations) to address inconsistencies or infeasibilities in inferred relations. Such methods often resolve issues by modifying the relations *after* they have been reasoned, potentially distorting the original semantic intent.

This work, R³L, aims to advance the constraint-driven paradigm by addressing the reliability and consistency of the relative spatial reasoning process *itself*, particularly for multi-hop relations. It proposes to resolve inconsistencies during the MLLM's reasoning phase rather than through post-hoc adjustments. It introduces mechanisms to mitigate "semantic drift" and "metric drift" that arise from repeated reference-frame transformations during complex multi-hop reasoning, thereby producing more robust and accurate relations for 3D layout generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to improve the reliability and consistency of relative spatial reasoning for 3D layout generation. Specifically, the authors aim to enable MLLMs to infer semantically consistent and physically feasible relative spatial relations directly, rather than relying on post-hoc heuristics to correct unreliable relations.

The motivation stems from a recognized challenge in current MLLM-based 3D layout generation: the inherent unreliability of inferred relative spatial relations, particularly in scenarios involving multi-hop reasoning. When MLLMs attempt to infer relations across multiple objects that are not directly linked (i.e., composing per-hop relations through intermediate objects), this process inevitably involves repeated transformations across object-centric reference frames. This repeated re-expression of intermediate conclusions under new frames is analogous to mental rotation in cognitive science and leads to an accumulation of errors along the relation chain.

These accumulated errors manifest in two critical failure modes:
1. **Semantic Drift:** This occurs when directional relations are re-interpreted under different reference frames. Even minor frame-induced mismatches (e.g., a local-axis swap) can distort directional semantics, leading to inferred relations that are semantically inconsistent with the original instruction. For example, an object intended to be "in front of" another might be placed "behind" it due to a misaligned reference frame.
2. **Metric Drift:** This arises when metric displacements are composed as per-hop vectors across changing reference frames. Small geometric misalignments can compound, resulting in inconsistent spacing, object collisions, and ultimately physically infeasible layouts.

Existing methods that employ post-hoc heuristics (such as discretizing layout spaces or pruning conflicting relations) mitigate these issues at the cost of semantic fidelity or controllability. The core motivation behind R³L is to address these fundamental problems *during* the relation reasoning process, ensuring the inferred relations are "correct" from the outset, both semantically and physically. This approach seeks to fully leverage MLLMs' potential for complex spatial reasoning by introducing mechanisms that promote internal consistency and reliability throughout the reasoning chain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

R³L is structured into two main stages: reasoning and solving. The reasoning stage employs MLLMs to infer metricized relative spatial relations, incorporating two key modules: invariant spatial decomposition and consistent spatial imagination. The solving stage translates these relations into differentiable constraints for pose optimization, utilizing supportive spatial optimization.

#### 3.1. Invariant Spatial Decomposition

This module addresses the MLLM's struggle with maintaining consistent intermediate states and accumulating reasoning errors as scene complexity grows. Traditional semantic grouping, while reducing scale, does not inherently reduce the number of reference-frame transformations in multi-hop inference.

- **Mechanism:** The scene's N assets are partitioned into K "frame-invariant units" (U = {U_k}), where each unit consists of an anchor asset and its member assets. This partitioning is guided by the MLLM, which identifies strongly coupled spatial components. Each unit's anchor defines a unit-local frame. Independent assets are treated as "atomic units" with poses defined in the global frame.
- **Relation Factorization:** Relations are factorized into two levels:
 - **Intra-unit relations (R_intra_k):** Specify relations among assets *within* each unit, expressed in the unit-local frame.
 - **Inter-unit relations (R_inter):** Govern the placement of unit anchors (and independent assets) within the global scene.
- **Benefits:** This decomposition breaks long relation chains by allowing the MLLM to reason locally in anchor-defined frames for unit members. This reduces the cumulative number of global reference-frame transformations for unit members, thereby mitigating semantic drift. From a graph perspective, this introduces a vertex cut formed by unit anchors, simplifying the relation graph and reducing the path length for member-related inferences.

#### 3.2. Consistent Spatial Imagination

This module targets the difficulty MLLMs face in inferring reliable metric displacements and preventing metric drift, which often arises from composing per-hop errors across changing reference frames.

- **Mechanism:** R³L constructs global-local cognitive maps. Local maps stabilize intra-unit configurations, while the global map allocates space among units and independent assets. The MLLM is prompted to externalize its spatial mental model by explicitly outputting geometric primitives:
 - **Poses:** (x, y, rz) for object/unit centers and rotation.
 - **Footprints:** Rotated axis-aligned bounding box dimensions (lx, ly).
 - **Bounds:** Axis-aligned minimum and maximum extents (x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max) for collision detection.
- **Imagine-and-Revise Loop:** The MLLM performs an iterative process:
 1. **Imagination:** It instantiates the cognitive maps based on the current relation set, estimating object/unit placements.
 2. **Detection:** It detects geometric violations (e.g., collisions) using a lightweight axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) overlap check.
 3. **Revision:** It revises the relations responsible for conflicts, editing intra-unit relations for intra-unit collisions and inter-unit relations for inter-unit collisions.
- **Benefits:** By grounding metricized relations in explicit scene geometry and iteratively correcting violations, this module suppresses metric drift and yields more feasible and self-consistent relations. This localized revision process prevents errors from propagating globally.

#### 3.3. Supportive Spatial Optimization

This module addresses the instability and slow convergence often observed in joint optimization of all object poses, which can be caused by uneven constraint distribution and strong gradient coupling.

- **Mechanism: Global-to-Local Pose Re-parameterization:** Instead of optimizing all object poses in a single global frame, R³L uses a mixed representation (˜p):
 - Independent assets are optimized in the global frame.
 - For each unit, a unit-level pose (P_k) is optimized in the global frame, while its members' local poses (p_ℓ_i) are optimized *within* the unit-local frame. A member's global pose is recovered by composing the unit's global pose with its local pose.
- **Objective Function:** A two-level objective function L(˜p) is minimized:
 - **Local term (L_k_local):** For each unit U_k, this includes member-level collision losses and intra-unit relational losses.
 - **Global term (L_global):** Evaluates inter-unit constraints, including boundary losses for units/independent assets within the room, collision losses among units/independent assets, and inter-entity relational losses.
 - Various differentiable penalties are defined for specific spatial constraints (e.g., distance, gap, against-wall, corner, facing, directional relations, angle, placement, around arrangement).
- **Benefits:** This re-parameterization decouples intra-unit gradients from the unit's global pose (as demonstrated in Appendix B). This reduces gradient coupling, particularly for unit anchors connected to many members, which can otherwise lead to oscillatory updates and slow convergence. The module also incorporates parameter sharing for symmetric relations and a two-stage optimization strategy (fixed parameters then learnable parameters) to further stabilize the process and prevent trivial solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate R³L focused on its performance in 3D layout generation, the contribution of its individual modules, and its optimization characteristics.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on 3D Layout Generation

- **Overall Performance:** R³L consistently outperformed baselines (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, LayoutVLM) across nine diverse scene types and varying instruction difficulties. It achieved the strongest semantic performance (Realism, Functionality, Instruction Following) while maintaining physical feasibility (0.0% Collision Ratio, 0.0% Out-of-Bound Ratio on average).
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **LayoutGPT:** While having strong semantic scores, LayoutGPT struggled with physical feasibility, averaging 4.3% collision ratio and 8.1% out-of-bound ratio. R³L improved layout coherence and instruction following while eliminating physical violations.
 - **Holodeck:** Holodeck achieved strong physical feasibility but yielded substantially lower semantic scores, particularly in instruction following (+6.1 gain for R³L). This was attributed to its coarse relation modeling and grid-based solver.
 - **LayoutVLM:** LayoutVLM provided a better balance but remained sensitive to relation-inference errors. R³L consistently achieved higher semantic scores with perfect physical feasibility.
- **Pairwise Comparisons and Qualitative Results:** Pairwise human evaluation (Table 2) showed R³L was preferred in over 85% of comparisons against other baselines, achieving the highest Elo rating. Qualitative examples (Figure 3) demonstrated R³L's ability to follow complex instructions and produce physically plausible, visually coherent layouts, such as correctly arranging "multiple study stations" in a bookstore.

#### 4.2. Additional Analyses

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing both Invariant Spatial Decomposition (Decomp.) and Consistent Spatial Imagination (Imag.) resulted in a substantial drop in both semantic quality and physical plausibility.
 - Semantic grouping alone performed worst, suggesting its inadequacy for stable relative spatial reasoning.
 - Decomp. alone improved semantic consistency but led to physically implausible layouts due to uncorrected metric composition errors.
 - Imag. alone improved physical plausibility but degraded semantic consistency due to frequent reference-frame transformations.
 - The results indicated that Decomp. and Imag. are complementary: Decomp. reduces reference-frame transformations and stabilizes multi-hop semantics, while Imag. promotes self-consistency and suppresses metric drift. The combination yielded the strongest overall performance (Table 3).
- **Placement Error Rate by Hop Count:** Analysis of misplaced objects relative to hop count in the relation graph (Figure 5, Table 15) demonstrated that without Decomp. and Imag., error rates increased significantly with hop count. Decomp. flattened this growth, while Imag. lowered the overall error level. The full R³L model maintained consistently low error rates across all hop counts, indicating reduced error accumulation.
- **User Study and Human-LLM Alignment:** A user study corroborated the automatic evaluation, showing R³L achieved the best performance across realism, functionality, and instruction following, and was preferred in over 90% of pairwise comparisons (Tables 4, 5). Kendall's τ-b correlation showed good alignment between LLM-based evaluations and human judgments (Table 6).
- **Convergence Speed:** The supportive spatial optimization (global-to-local re-parameterization) was observed to converge approximately 1.8 times faster than standard global optimization, maintaining lower loss throughout most of the optimization process (Figure 6). This indicated improved optimization efficiency due to reduced gradient coupling.
- **Generality:** R³L's performance gains were consistent when using a different MLLM backbone (Gemini 3.1 Pro), suggesting its generality beyond GPT-5 (Table 11).
- **Comparison with Visual-Intermediate Methods:** R³L outperformed visual-intermediate baselines (GPT-Image-1.5, Nano Banana with 3D lifting) in both physical feasibility and semantic coherence, suggesting its suitability for tasks requiring precise spatial control and instruction following (Table 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of R³L represents a notable advancement in the field of language-driven 3D layout generation and more broadly, in the domain of spatial reasoning with MLLMs.

### 6. Significance

- **Addresses Fundamental MLLM Limitations:** R³L directly tackles the core challenges of semantic and metric drift in multi-hop relative spatial reasoning, which are inherent limitations of MLLMs when performing complex spatial tasks. By resolving these issues *during* the reasoning process rather than via post-hoc adjustments, it represents a more principled approach to generating reliable spatial relations.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Consistency:** The framework's modular design, incorporating invariant spatial decomposition and consistent spatial imagination, provides mechanisms to reduce reference-frame transformations and promote self-consistency in relation inference. This leads to layouts that are not only physically feasible (e.g., collision-free) but also semantically faithful to the input instructions.
- **Improved Optimization:** The supportive spatial optimization with global-to-local pose re-parameterization offers a practical solution to the instability and slow convergence often encountered in differentiable layout optimization, demonstrating a method to achieve faster and more stable results.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advanced 3D Content Creation:** R³L can facilitate the more efficient and accurate generation of 3D indoor scenes for various applications, including architectural visualization, interior design, virtual reality (VR), and gaming, where precise control over object placement and adherence to spatial intent are critical.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** The ability to translate high-level language instructions into physically plausible and semantically consistent 3D arrangements is directly applicable to embodied AI and robotics. Robots could more reliably interpret spatial commands (e.g., "place the cup to the left of the plate on the table") and execute them within complex environments.
- **Further MLLM Development:** The work provides insights into how to better guide and constrain MLLMs for complex reasoning tasks. The explicit externalization of spatial mental models and iterative self-correction mechanisms could inform future research into more robust and reliable MLLM architectures for diverse cognitive tasks.
- **Foundation for Broader Spatial Reasoning:** While currently focused on 3D layout generation, the principles of invariant decomposition, consistent imagination, and stabilized optimization could be extended to other spatial reasoning problems in areas like path planning, object manipulation, or understanding dynamic scenes.

The authors acknowledge limitations such as the restriction to planar poses, reliance on OBB geometry (which struggles with non-convex objects), and dependence on a predefined asset library. These aspects represent avenues for future work, indicating that while R³L makes substantial progress, there are further opportunities for enhancing its capabilities and generality.
