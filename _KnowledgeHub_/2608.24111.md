# Trajectory-Level Continuous Action Representation for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CAT is a trajectory-level continuous action representation for robotic manipulation. A frequency-aware transformer encoder compresses each one-second action window into a fixed set of continuous latent tokens, using frequency-scaled rotary positional encoding and contrastive latent regularization, decoded by an asymmetric causal decoder. Plugged into flow-matching policies, it lifts LIBERO average success from 94.2% to 96.5% and real-world long-horizon scores from 45.0 to 60.5.

## Problem

- Timestep-level action tokenization ties representation size to the control grid, so higher sampling rates inflate token counts without adding task-relevant trajectory variation.
- Fixed temporal reparameterizations such as DCT coefficients, B-spline control points, or spectral bases constrain representational structure to a predetermined basis chosen in advance.
- Modeling capacity spread evenly over time lets smooth redundant segments dominate gradients, diluting the signal from brief contact transitions and corrective adjustments that decide success.

## Method

- **CAT**'s ==structural decoupling==: learnable register tokens attend over a one-second action window and compress it into K fixed continuous latents, independent of timestep density.
- ==F-RoPE==, a frequency-aware ==rotary positional embedding== scaling timestep index by control frequency, places trajectories from different control rates in a shared temporal coordinate system.
- Training combines an L2 reconstruction loss with a batch-level ==contrastive regularizer== dispersing trajectory embeddings, decoded by an asymmetric ==causal-mask transformer decoder==; latents feed a ==flow-matching== policy.

## Results

- On LIBERO, CAT raises SmolVLA from **87.3%** to **90.8%** and pi-zero from **94.2%** to **96.5%**, beating FAST and VQ-VLA variants under matched training.
- On RoboTwin 2.0 across **10, 16.7, 25, and 50 Hz**, gains of **+10.3, +4.0, +10.2, +5.3 points**, winning **24 of 32** task-frequency pairs; real-world scores **45.0** to **60.5**.
- Ablations: **K=4** registers best (**90.8%** vs **87.3%** at K=8), regularization weight **0.1** optimal, causal decoding beats full attention by **2.25 points**, F-RoPE beats learned PE **46.1%** vs **19.5%** on RoboTwin.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling action-representation size from the sampling grid is the key lever; a fixed token budget over a real-time window generalizes across control frequencies.
> - Compression capacity is non-monotonic: more latent tokens hurt, suggesting a sweet spot balancing expressiveness against optimization stability rather than maximal capacity.
> - Gains concentrate on tasks with brief critical interaction transitions and long horizons, and are marginal on smooth motions, so expect benefit to be task-dependent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation, specifically concerning action representation. Current paradigms in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as autoregressive and diffusion-based approaches, commonly face issues with action representation.

Existing methods for representing continuous action trajectories often exhibit two primary limitations. Firstly, many systems couple action representation to the control frequency or timestep-level discretization. This means that as the sampling rate increases, the representational structure expands proportionally, potentially introducing redundancy if task-relevant variations do not scale at the same rate. This can lead to modeling capacity being spread thinly across redundant temporal regions, diluting learning signals for critical, localized motion segments like contact transitions or corrective adjustments.

Secondly, some approaches attempt to mitigate this by using fixed temporal parameterizations, such as Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) coefficients or B-spline control points. While these methods prevent representation size from scaling with sampling frequency, they constrain the representational structure to a predefined temporal basis. This can limit their ability to capture diverse and dynamic action patterns, especially those with non-uniform temporal importance.

This paper positions itself by proposing a novel trajectory-level continuous action representation, termed CAT (Continuous Action Representation), designed to decouple the action representation from control frequency and predefined temporal parameterizations. It aims to offer a more scalable and robust solution for robotic manipulation policies, particularly in environments with varying control rates and complex, long-horizon tasks, by focusing on representing entire action trajectories as fixed-size continuous latent tokens. This approach seeks to improve upon both timestep-level discretization and fixed-basis reparameterizations, integrating with existing diffusion-based VLA frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an action representation framework for robotic manipulation that addresses the limitations of existing methods, specifically the entanglement of action representation with control frequency and reliance on fixed temporal parameterizations. The authors aim to achieve a representation that is both compact and temporally consistent across varying control rates.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges identified in current visuomotor systems:
1. **Uneven Temporal Importance in Trajectories:** Robotic manipulation tasks often involve action trajectories where the influence on task outcomes is not uniformly distributed over time. Critical motion segments, such as contact transitions, changes between free motion and interaction, or brief corrective adjustments, are often short but highly consequential. Conversely, other parts of the trajectory may consist of smooth, repetitive motion. Existing action representations that spread modeling capacity evenly across the entire trajectory can inadvertently dilute the learning signal for these crucial but shorter segments due to the dominance of temporally redundant regions. This can lead to information loss regarding localized, important action variations.
2. **Representational Redundancy with High Control Frequencies:** When action representations are tied to timestep-level discretization, increasing the control frequency (sampling rate) directly increases the length and dimensionality of the action representation. This leads to representational growth that may not correspond to an increase in task-relevant trajectory variation, thus introducing temporal redundancy. This expansion can make policy learning less efficient and harder to scale.
3. **Limitations of Predefined Temporal Parameterizations:** To avoid the frequency-driven expansion, some methods use predefined temporal reparameterizations (e.g., DCT coefficients, B-spline control points) to represent trajectories with a fixed set of parameters. While this prevents size scaling, the representational structure is constrained by the chosen temporal basis. This can limit the flexibility and expressiveness of the representation, potentially hindering its ability to model diverse or complex motion patterns accurately.
4. **Need for Stable and Discriminative Latent Spaces:** Compressing long action trajectories into a fixed, compact latent space, as proposed by the authors, necessitates careful design to ensure the latent representations remain stable and discriminative. Without explicit mechanisms, distinct trajectories could collapse into overlapping regions in the latent space, undermining the effectiveness of downstream policy learning.

In response to these motivations, the authors propose CAT to create a trajectory-level continuous action representation that decouples representational complexity from control frequency, maintains temporal consistency across different sampling rates, and learns a stable, discriminative latent space without relying on predefined temporal bases.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CAT (Continuous Action Representation) is a trajectory-level continuous action representation framework designed around three core principles: Structural Decoupling, Frequency Alignment, and Latent Stability.

#### 3.1. Structural Decoupling

Unlike timestep-level or fixed-parameter approaches, CAT encodes action trajectories within a fixed real-time interval (e.g., T = 1 second) into a fixed number of continuous latent tokens, K. This number K is constant and independent of the timestep density (N = f_s * T, where f_s is control frequency). A frequency-aware Transformer encoder maps an input action sequence {a_t} (for t=1 to N) and its frequency-scaled timestep indices {t/f_s} to a set of latent tokens {z_i} (for i=1 to K), where K << N. These learnable register tokens aggregate trajectory-level information, ensuring the representation's dimensionality does not scale with the control frequency.

To reconstruct actions, a Transformer decoder takes the register tokens concatenated with a set of learnable action embeddings {e_t}. Each action embedding e_t corresponds to one timestep and is decoded into a reconstructed action ˆa_t. An asymmetric causal attention mask is employed in the decoder: action embeddings can attend to all register tokens, but only to preceding action embeddings in the temporal dimension, enforcing causality during decoding.

#### 3.2. Frequency Alignment

To ensure temporal consistency across different control frequencies, CAT incorporates a Frequency-aware Rotary Positional Embedding (F-RoPE). Instead of using raw timestep indices, each index t is scaled by the control frequency (t/f_s), creating normalized time coordinates. The F-RoPE is then applied to these normalized time coordinates, embedding control-frequency information directly into the positional encoding. This allows trajectories sampled at various control rates to be placed in a shared temporal space, enabling the representation to distinguish frequency-dependent variations.

#### 3.3. Latent Stability and Training Objective

To ensure the compact latent tokens remain expressive and discriminative, CAT uses a combined training objective:
- **Reconstruction Loss (L_rec):** This is an L2 loss that penalizes discrepancies between the original action sequence {a_t} and the decoder's reconstruction {ˆa_t}. This term ensures that the latent tokens can faithfully represent the original trajectory.
 `L_rec = (1/N) * Σ ||a_t - ˆa_t||^2`
- **Trajectory-Level Contrastive Regularization (L_reg):** To prevent distinct trajectories from collapsing into overlapping regions in the latent space, a contrastive regularization term is introduced. For each trajectory, a concatenated embedding ¯z (from all K register tokens) is formed. The loss encourages separation between embeddings of different trajectories within a mini-batch.
 `L_reg = (1/B) * Σ log(Σ exp(-D(¯z_i, ¯z_j) / η))`
 where B is batch size, D is a distance metric, and η is a temperature hyperparameter. This term promotes discriminability between trajectory-level embeddings.

The overall training objective is a weighted sum of these two losses:
`L_CAT = L_rec + λ_reg * L_reg`
where λ_reg balances the contribution of the regularization term.

#### 3.4. Integration into Diffusion-Based VLA

CAT is designed to be compatible with continuous generative policies. The learned compact continuous latent tokens replace timestep-level action parameterizations within a diffusion-based VLA framework. The diffusion model then operates on these latent trajectories (Z = {z_i}) and learns a continuous-time flow between noisy (Z1) and clean (Z0) latents, predicting a time-dependent velocity field. During inference, the diffusion model generates latent trajectories, which are then decoded by the pretrained CAT into continuous actions, using the frequency-aware decoding mechanism to ensure consistency across varying control rates.

#### 3.5. Architectural Details

CAT employs an 8-layer Transformer encoder and an 8-layer Transformer decoder. Both have a hidden size of 512, 8 attention heads, and feed-forward networks with a 4x expansion ratio. The encoder uses full attention. The decoder utilizes an asymmetric causal mask, allowing queries to attend to all encoder outputs but only to past decoder positions. RMSNorm is used in all Transformer blocks, and the latent dimensionality is set to match the original action dimension.

#### 3.6. Training Pipeline

Training proceeds in two stages:
1. **CAT Pre-training:** CAT is trained to learn the continuous latent representations from action trajectories.
2. **Policy Training:** The pretrained CAT is integrated into a policy model (e.g., SmolVLA or π0), and the policy is trained for downstream control tasks, with the CAT component kept fixed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates CAT across simulated and real-world environments, testing three hypotheses: (H1) Trajectory-Level Advantage, (H2) Frequency Robustness, and (H3) Long-Horizon Stability.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **LIBERO Benchmark (H1: Trajectory-Level Advantage):**
 - CAT-based policies demonstrated improved performance compared to competitive baselines under matched training settings.
 - Using the SmolVLA backbone, SmolVLA-CAT achieved an average success rate of 90.8%, outperforming the original SmolVLA (87.3%), SmolVLA-FAST, and SmolVLA-VQ-VLA. This indicates that trajectory-level continuous latent representation provides benefits over timestep-level or discretization-dependent representations.
 - Integrating CAT into the larger diffusion-based model π0 further improved its performance from 94.2% to 96.5%, suggesting the generalizability of the representational gain across different model scales.

- **MimicGen Benchmark (H1: Trajectory-Level Advantage):**
 - On 8 multi-task Robosuite tasks, DP-CAT (Diffusion Policy with CAT) achieved an average success rate of 86.9%, surpassing the baseline Diffusion Policy (85.8%) with matched parameter counts.
 - Significant improvement was observed on the "Stack 3" task (+9 points), which involves sequential multi-stage stacking and longer effective control horizons, suggesting the effectiveness of trajectory-level representation in extended sequential settings.

- **Control-Frequency Evaluation on RoboTwin 2.0 (H2: Frequency Robustness):**
 - SmolVLA-CAT maintained higher average success rates than the baseline SmolVLA across all tested control frequencies (10Hz, 16.7Hz, 25Hz, 50Hz).
 - Average gains ranged from +4.0 to +10.3 points across different frequencies.
 - CAT outperformed the baseline in 24 out of 32 task-frequency pairs, indicating broad consistency.
 - Task-level improvements were more pronounced in tasks involving short but critical interaction transitions (e.g., Grab Roller, Press Stapler, Place Container Plate), supporting the hypothesis that trajectory-level representations are beneficial when success depends on localized but important action variations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Long-Horizon Manipulation (H3: Long-Horizon Stability)

- On four long-horizon real-world manipulation tasks (Stack-5, Drawer, Rope, Flower), CAT improved the average normalized task score from 45.0% (baseline SmolVLA) to 60.5%, a gain of 15.5 points.
- CAT outperformed the baseline on all four tasks, with gains of +14 on Stack-5, +12 on Drawer, +8 on Rope, and +28 on Flower.
- These results suggest that trajectory-level continuous latent modeling enhances temporal coherence and stability in real-world sequential manipulation tasks, where intermediate progress directly affects the final outcome.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Positional Encoding:** F-RoPE (Frequency-aware Rotary Positional Embedding) yielded slightly better performance on LIBERO and larger gains on RoboTwin 2.0 compared to learned positional embeddings, particularly when dealing with varying sampling rates.
- **Number of Registers (K):** An optimal number of registers was found at K=4, improving success rates compared to K=2 or K=6/8. This indicated a balance between expressiveness and optimization stability.
- **Regularization Loss Weight (λ_reg):** A moderate regularization weight of λ_reg = 0.1 resulted in the highest average success rate. Removing regularization or using excessively large weights degraded performance, suggesting that moderate regularization helps maintain discriminative latent structure without over-constraining the space.
- **Decoder Attention Type:** Causal attention in the decoder consistently outperformed full attention, improving the average success rate by 2.25 points. This indicated that enforcing temporal directionality during decoding is beneficial even with trajectory-level action representation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of CAT, a trajectory-level continuous action representation framework, addresses several limitations in current visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation. By decoupling action representation from control frequency and predefined temporal parameterizations, CAT offers a more robust and scalable approach.

The significance of this work can be understood through its demonstrated capabilities:
1. **Enhanced Policy Performance:** Consistent improvements in success rates across diverse simulated benchmarks (LIBERO, MimicGen) and real-world long-horizon tasks indicate that modeling actions as fixed-size continuous latent tokens at the trajectory level provides a more effective inductive bias for policy learning. This suggests that the approach can better capture the essential dynamics of motion while mitigating redundancy.
2. **Robustness to Varying Control Frequencies:** The framework's ability to maintain stable performance under different control rates, as demonstrated on RoboTwin 2.0, is significant for practical robotic deployment. Robots often operate with varied sensing and actuation frequencies, and a representation that is insensitive to these variations can simplify policy design and deployment across different hardware or operational contexts. The frequency-aware positional encoding is central to this robustness.
3. **Improved Long-Horizon Task Execution:** The observed gains in real-world, multi-stage manipulation tasks suggest that CAT contributes to enhanced temporal coherence and stability over extended horizons. This is crucial for complex tasks where errors in early stages can propagate and lead to overall task failure.
4. **Architectural Flexibility:** The ability to integrate CAT into existing diffusion-based VLA frameworks, and improve the performance of both smaller (SmolVLA) and larger (π0) models, highlights its generalizability as a foundational component for action representation.
5. **Addressing Representational Redundancy:** By encoding entire trajectories into a fixed set of latent tokens, CAT effectively prevents the linear scaling of representation size with increasing control frequency. This can lead to more efficient learning and inference, especially in high-fidelity control scenarios.
6. **Principled Latent Space Management:** The combination of reconstruction loss and trajectory-level contrastive regularization provides a mechanism for learning a compact yet discriminative latent space. This prevents distinct trajectories from occupying overlapping regions, which can be critical for generating diverse and precise motions.

The potential impact of this work is in advancing the state-of-the-art in robotic control, particularly for developing generalist robot policies that are more scalable, adaptable, and robust to real-world complexities. By providing a more effective way to represent actions, CAT could enable the training of more capable visuomotor policies from large, diverse datasets, potentially facilitating broader adoption of learned robot manipulation skills in industrial and service robotics.
