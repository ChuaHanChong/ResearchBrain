# Designing Time Series Experiments in A/B Testing with Transformer Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel Transformer Reinforcement Learning (TRL) framework optimizes experimental design for A/B testing in time series settings by directly minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) of the Average Treatment Effect (ATE) estimator. The approach leverages full historical data for treatment allocation and consistently reduces MSE compared to traditional designs across various simulators, especially in complex, real-world environments.

## Problem

- Carryover effects and complex temporal dependencies in time series A/B tests often lead to biased and inefficient Average Treatment Effect (ATE) estimates.
- Existing experimental designs for time series A/B testing restrict treatment allocation to depend on only limited historical information, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Optimizing current experimental designs often relies on strong, simplifying assumptions (e.g., Markovian models) about the data generating process that may not hold in real-world scenarios.

## Method

- A Transformer Reinforcement Learning (TRL) framework is developed to learn an optimal ==treatment== allocation policy that directly minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) of the ATE estimator.
- The framework defines the state as the full historical context of observations, actions, and outcomes, training an RL agent in a simulation environment.
- A Transformer-based Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) is employed to parameterize the Q-function, enabling the model to handle varying-length historical inputs and capture long-term temporal dependencies.

## Results

- TRL consistently achieved the lowest Mean Squared Error (MSE) for ATE estimation across synthetic, real-data-based ridesharing, and public dispatch simulators, outperforming traditional MDP-based and switchback designs.
- The framework showed a more pronounced advantage in real-world simulator settings, particularly with small experimental durations, due to its ability to handle complex temporal correlations and non-Markovian dynamics.
- Ablation studies validated the benefits of the Transformer architecture and full-history context, while TRL demonstrated robustness to moderate simulator misspecification and hyperparameter variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An 'impossibility theorem' demonstrates that the optimal treatment allocation strategy for ATE estimation, particularly for doubly robust estimators, generally requires conditioning on the entire past history.
> - Reinforcement learning provides a data-driven approach to directly optimize the experimental design objective (MSE) without requiring restrictive parametric assumptions about the underlying data generating process.
> - Transformer architectures are uniquely suited for experimental design in time series, capable of effectively encoding and utilizing long-range temporal dependencies from the complete historical context.
