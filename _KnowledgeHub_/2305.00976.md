# TMR: Text-to-Motion Retrieval Using Contrastive 3D Human Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> TMR proposes a high-performance system for text-to-3D human motion retrieval, addressing limitations of prior retrieval methods and offering a robust alternative to motion synthesis. It achieves state-of-the-art performance by combining a probabilistic motion synthesis framework with contrastive learning and a novel negative filtering mechanism, while also demonstrating emergent zero-shot moment retrieval capabilities.

## Problem

- Prior text-to-motion retrieval models exhibited low performance, particularly for fine-grained descriptions or large galleries.
- Text-to-motion synthesis models often struggle to produce physically plausible or artifact-free motions (e.g., foot sliding).
- Standard contrastive learning methods face challenges with motion-language data due to semantic similarities between different text descriptions, leading to "wrong negatives" that hinder training.

## Method

- Extends the **TEMOS** text-to-motion synthesis model by incorporating an ==InfoNCE contrastive loss== function to optimize for retrieval.
- Trains the **TMR** model jointly for both motion synthesis (retaining the decoder) and ==cross-modal retrieval==, sharing a common ==probabilistic latent space==.
- Introduces a ==negative filtering== mechanism that leverages an external large language model to identify and discard semantically similar "wrong negatives" from the InfoNCE loss calculation during training.

## Results

- **TMR** consistently outperforms prior text-to-motion retrieval models across all evaluation protocols, reducing the median rank for text-to-motion retrieval on HumanML3D (All with Threshold protocol) from **54** to **19**, and increasing Recall@1 from **5.30%** to **11.60%**.
- Ablation studies confirm the positive impact of joint training, InfoNCE loss, and the negative filtering mechanism, with negative filtering improving Recall@3 on KIT-ML from **36.02%** to **41.93%**.
- The model exhibits an emergent zero-shot temporal localization (moment retrieval) capability, accurately identifying specific action spans in long, untrimmed motion sequences despite not being explicitly trained for this task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a model for both synthesis and retrieval jointly significantly improves retrieval performance compared to training for retrieval alone, as the generative capability ensures a more holistic latent representation.
> - The InfoNCE contrastive loss proves more effective than margin-based losses for structuring the joint latent space for text-to-motion retrieval.
> - Filtering out semantically similar "wrong negatives" is crucial for robust contrastive learning in motion-language datasets, preventing the model from learning superficial distinctions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the burgeoning field of multimodal AI, specifically at the intersection of natural language processing and 3D human motion modeling. Its placement in the landscape is defined by several key aspects:

#### 1.1. Vision and Language Beyond Images

The success of models like CLIP, BLIP, and CoCa in linking natural language with images and videos has revolutionized cross-modal understanding and generation. TMR extends this paradigm to a less explored, but equally complex, domain: 3D human motion. While 3D motion is a form of "vision data," its temporal and kinematic complexities, along with the nuanced nature of human action descriptions, pose unique challenges compared to static images or even 2D videos.

#### 1.2. Shifting Focus from Synthesis to Retrieval

A major trend in human motion modeling has been text-to-motion *synthesis* – generating novel 3D motion sequences from textual descriptions. While impressive, these generative models often struggle with producing physically plausible, realistic, and artifact-free motions (e.g., "foot sliding"). TMR critically re-evaluates this landscape by elevating *retrieval* from a mere evaluation metric to a standalone, primary task. The authors argue that for many applications, searching an existing, high-quality motion capture dataset for a matching motion can be a more robust and practical solution, guaranteeing realism and plausibility that generative models often lack. This offers a complementary approach to synthesis, providing a "search engine" for existing motion assets.

#### 1.3. Advancing Cross-Modal Learning for 3D Motion

Previous attempts at text-to-motion retrieval, such as that by Guo et al., primarily treated it as a proxy for evaluating synthesis models, leading to limited performance, especially for fine-grained descriptions. TMR aims to overcome this by deeply integrating state-of-the-art contrastive learning techniques, similar to those successful in image-text domains (e.g., InfoNCE), into a motion synthesis framework. This makes it one of the first dedicated, high-performance models for text-to-motion retrieval.

#### 1.4. Addressing Unique Challenges of Motion-Language Data

The paper acknowledges a critical distinction between image-text and motion-text datasets: motion descriptions tend to be more semantically similar (e.g., "person walks" vs. "human walks") even for distinct motions, or conversely, identical texts might describe subtly different motions. This poses a problem for standard contrastive learning, where all non-matching pairs in a batch are treated as "hard negatives." TMR's "negative filtering" mechanism directly addresses this inherent data characteristic, marking a significant advancement in adapting general-purpose contrastive learning to the peculiarities of motion data.

In essence, TMR establishes a new benchmark and a robust methodology for text-to-3D human motion retrieval, recognizing its independent value and addressing the specific challenges of this domain. It pushes the boundaries of multimodal understanding into the complex realm of human kinematics, building on established techniques while innovating to suit the unique nature of motion data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of TMR are multi-faceted, ranging from fundamental research questions to practical application needs:

#### 2.1. Primary Objective: Establishing Text-to-Motion Retrieval as a Standalone Task

The central aim of TMR is to develop a highly effective system for text-to-3D human motion retrieval, treating it as an independent and valuable problem rather than merely a proxy evaluation metric for motion synthesis. This represents a significant conceptual shift in the field.

#### 2.2. Motivation for Focusing on Retrieval

- **Guaranteed Realism and Physical Plausibility:** Unlike generative models that often struggle with artifacts (e.g., foot sliding) or physical inconsistencies, retrieved motions from existing datasets are inherently realistic and physically plausible. For many applications (e.g., animation, gaming, robotics), this guarantee is paramount.
- **Viable Alternative to Synthesis:** For scenarios where existing motion capture libraries are sufficiently diverse, retrieval can serve as a simpler, more reliable, and often more efficient alternative to generating new, potentially flawed, motions.
- **Enhanced Data Management and Accessibility:** A robust retrieval system can enable automatic indexing and searching of vast motion capture collections using natural language queries, transforming how animators, game developers, and researchers access and utilize these valuable assets.
- **Facilitating Annotation Processes:** By identifying the nearest text description for a given motion (motion-to-text retrieval), the system can aid in initializing or streamlining the often cumbersome and time-consuming process of manually labeling motion capture data.
- **Symmetrical Cross-Modal Understanding:** Developing a joint embedding space allows for both text-to-motion and motion-to-text queries, offering a comprehensive cross-modal understanding between language and human movement.

#### 2.3. Addressing Limitations of Prior Work

- **Improving Performance:** Previous text-to-motion retrieval models (e.g., Guo et al.) exhibited low performance, especially when dealing with fine-grained descriptions or large galleries. TMR aims for substantial performance improvements.
- **Handling Fine-Grained Similarities:** The authors explicitly note the challenge posed by the inherent similarities within motion descriptions (e.g., "walks" vs. "walking"). This requires a sophisticated approach to distinguish true negatives from "wrong negatives" (semantically similar but distinct pairs) during contrastive training.

#### 2.4. Exploring Emergent Capabilities: Zero-Shot Moment Retrieval

Beyond its primary retrieval objective, TMR also aims to showcase the emergent grounding capabilities of its model by applying it to zero-shot temporal localization (moment retrieval) in long motion sequences. This demonstrates the model's potential to understand and pinpoint specific actions within a longer kinematic context, opening new avenues for research in motion understanding.

In summary, TMR is driven by the vision of making 3D human motion data more accessible and usable through natural language, by building a high-performance retrieval system that overcomes the realism limitations of synthesis and intelligently handles the unique characteristics of motion-language datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TMR's methodology revolves around extending a state-of-the-art text-to-motion synthesis model, TEMOS, by incorporating advanced contrastive learning techniques and a novel negative filtering mechanism to optimize for retrieval performance.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture: Extending TEMOS

TMR builds directly upon the architecture of **TEMOS (Transformer-based text-to-motion synthesis)**. TEMOS itself is based on a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework with Transformer encoders and a non-autoregressive decoder.
- **Dual Encoders:** TMR retains TEMOS's core dual-encoder structure. A **motion encoder** processes 3D human motion sequences (represented as SMPL joint positions, velocities, and foot contacts) and maps them to a latent space. A **text encoder** processes natural language descriptions, leveraging pre-trained and frozen DistilBERT features as input, mapping them into the same joint latent space. Both encoders are Transformer-based and probabilistic, outputting parameters (mean and covariance) of a Gaussian distribution, from which a latent vector `z` is sampled. For retrieval, the mean of the latent distribution is used as the embedding.
- **Motion Decoder:** Crucially, TMR *retains* TEMOS's **motion decoder branch**. This decoder takes a latent vector and positional encoding to generate a 3D motion sequence in a single forward pass. This allows TMR to be trained for *both* synthesis and retrieval simultaneously.

#### 3.2. Loss Functions: Joint Synthesis and Retrieval Training

TMR's training objective is a weighted sum of TEMOS's original losses and a new contrastive loss:
- **L_TEMOS (Synthesis-Oriented Losses):**
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L_R):** Measures the fidelity of the reconstructed motion given either text or motion input using a smooth L1 loss.
 - **Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence Loss (L_KL):** Regularizes the latent distributions from both motion and text encoders to approximate a standard normal distribution, and also enforces similarity between the motion and text latent distributions.
 - **Cross-Modal Embedding Similarity Loss (L_E):** Encourages positive text-motion latent code pairs to be similar using a smooth L1 loss.
- **L_NCE (Contrastive Loss for Retrieval):** This is the primary addition for retrieval. TMR adopts the **InfoNCE loss**, which is widely effective in cross-modal retrieval (e.g., CLIP). Given a batch of `N` positive (text, motion) pairs, InfoNCE aims to maximize the similarity between corresponding positive pairs while minimizing similarity with all other (negative) pairs within the batch. This effectively structures the joint latent space for effective nearest neighbor search. The similarity is computed using cosine similarity between text and motion embeddings.
- **Total Loss:** The overall training loss is `L_TEMOS + λ_NCE * L_NCE`, where `λ_NCE` is a weighting parameter controlling the importance of the contrastive objective.

#### 3.3. Negative Filtering: Addressing "Wrong Negatives"

A key innovation for the motion domain is the implementation of **negative filtering**. The challenge is that in motion datasets, different text descriptions might refer to highly similar motions (e.g., "walking" vs. "a person walks"), or vice versa, leading to seemingly "negative" pairs in a batch that are semantically very close. Treating these as hard negatives can force the model to learn superficial distinctions, hindering generalization and robustness.
- **Mechanism:** TMR leverages an external, pre-trained large language model (MPNet) to compute sentence similarity scores between text descriptions.
- **Filtering Rule:** During training, if the similarity between the text labels of two *different* (i.e., non-positive) text-motion pairs in a batch exceeds a certain threshold (e.g., 0.8), that particular pair is *discarded* from the InfoNCE loss calculation. These pairs are not treated as negatives. This prevents the model from being penalized for finding similarities between semantically alike but technically "negative" text-motion pairs.

#### 3.4. Datasets and Preprocessing

- **HumanML3D (H3D):** A large dataset of 3D human motions with natural language labels, derived from AMASS and HumanAct12.
- **KIT Motion-Language (KIT):** Another dataset of motion capture data with text descriptions, focusing on locomotion.
- **Preprocessing:** Motions are preprocessed to a consistent SMPL representation, extracting joint positions, velocities, and foot contacts, similar to prior work. Data augmentation includes mirroring motions and their corresponding text descriptions (e.g., "left" becomes "right").

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

The authors establish a rigorous evaluation protocol to thoroughly assess retrieval performance:
- **Metrics:** Standard Recall@k (R@1, R@5, R@10) and Median Rank (MedR) for both text-to-motion and motion-to-text retrieval.
- **Gallery Variations:** Four distinct evaluation protocols are introduced to test different levels of difficulty and dataset characteristics:
 - **(a) All:** Uses the entire test set as the gallery.
 - **(b) All with Threshold:** Uses the entire test set, but a retrieved motion is considered correct if its text label is highly similar (above 0.95 threshold) to the query text, accounting for natural language variations.
 - **(c) Dissimilar Subset:** A smaller gallery of 100 motion-text pairs whose texts are maximally dissimilar, representing an "easier" and cleaner retrieval scenario.
 - **(d) Small Batches:** Mimics the protocol of Guo et al. using randomly picked batches of 32 motion-text pairs, representing a common but less rigorous evaluation setup.

This comprehensive methodology, combining existing strong foundations with targeted innovations for the motion domain, allows TMR to achieve superior performance in text-to-motion retrieval.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TMR demonstrates significant advancements in text-to-motion retrieval, backed by extensive quantitative and qualitative results, and thorough ablation studies.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Retrieval Performance

- **Outperformance:** TMR consistently and substantially outperforms prior work, including the direct baseline TEMOS and Guo et al.'s retrieval model, across all four challenging evaluation protocols (All, All with Threshold, Dissimilar Subset, Small Batches) on both HumanML3D (H3D) and KIT-ML datasets.
- **Quantifiable Improvement:** For instance, on the challenging H3D dataset using the "All with Threshold" protocol, TMR reduces the median rank (MedR) from 54 (Guo et al.) to an impressive 19 for text-to-motion retrieval. This indicates that on average, the correct motion is found much higher in the ranked list. Recall@1 also sees significant boosts (e.g., 5.30% to 11.60% on H3D for the same protocol).

#### 4.2. Importance of Joint Synthesis and Retrieval Training

- **Empirical Evidence:** Ablation studies reveal that retaining the motion *synthesis* branch (decoder) during training alongside the contrastive loss significantly boosts retrieval performance. For example, on KIT-ML, R@3 improves from 36.87% (contrastive only) to 41.93% (TMR with synthesis and contrastive).
- **Underlying Reason:** The authors hypothesize that the generative capability of the synthesis branch forces the latent vector to capture a more complete and holistic understanding of the input text, preventing the model from relying on superficial "bag-of-words" understanding, which can be a shortcut when only contrastive loss is present.

#### 4.3. Superiority of InfoNCE Contrastive Loss

- **Loss Function Comparison:** When a contrastive loss is used, the InfoNCE formulation employed by TMR proves to be significantly more effective than the margin-based contrastive loss used in prior work like Guo et al.. On KIT-ML, R@1 improves from 19.88% (margin loss with synthesis) to 24.58% (InfoNCE with synthesis). This highlights the effectiveness of InfoNCE in structuring the cross-modal latent space.

#### 4.4. Efficacy of Negative Filtering

- **Addressing Data Nuances:** The proposed negative filtering mechanism, which discards semantically similar "wrong negative" pairs from the contrastive loss, consistently improves performance. Without filtering, R@3 on KIT-ML drops from 41.93% to 36.02%.
- **Optimal Threshold:** An empirical analysis found that a text similarity threshold of 0.8 for filtering negatives yielded the best overall results, balancing the removal of truly wrong negatives with maintaining sufficient negative samples for effective contrastive learning.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

- The model's performance is sensitive to the InfoNCE temperature (τ), a common observation in contrastive learning.
- The weight of the contrastive loss (`λ_NCE`) also influences performance, indicating the importance of balancing retrieval and synthesis objectives.
- Batch size, which determines the number of in-batch negatives, had a relatively smaller impact but still influenced results, with batch size 32 showing good performance.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Retrieval Results

- **Semantic Coherence:** Qualitative examples demonstrate that TMR can retrieve motions that are semantically coherent with complex natural language queries, often finding the ground truth motion at rank 1.
- **Generalization:** The model shows a capacity to generalize to free-form prompts (e.g., "Someone is swimming") even when the exact phrase is not in the gallery, successfully retrieving relevant motions.
- **Handling Near Misses:** While highly ranked incorrect motions may occur, their similarity scores are generally lower, and they often depict visually related actions (e.g., "cartwheel" when searching for "handstand" due to similar body poses).

#### 4.7. Emergent Moment Retrieval Capability

- **Zero-Shot Localization:** TMR exhibits an unexpected ability for zero-shot temporal localization (moment retrieval). When applied to long, untrimmed BABEL sequences, the model can identify the correct temporal span corresponding to a given text query, even though it was not explicitly trained for this task.
- **Domain Robustness:** This capability emerges despite a domain gap between the BABEL dataset (for moment retrieval) and the H3D/KIT datasets (for training). This suggests a strong grounding of language within motion representations.

These findings collectively affirm TMR's position as a leading model for text-to-motion retrieval, showcasing the critical role of joint training, robust contrastive learning, and intelligent handling of dataset peculiarities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TMR research carries significant implications for both fundamental AI research and practical applications involving human motion.

#### 5.1. Elevating Text-to-Motion Retrieval

By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance and treating text-to-motion retrieval as a primary task, TMR validates its importance as a viable and often superior alternative to motion synthesis. This shifts the research paradigm, opening new avenues for dedicated work in this area rather than it being a secondary concern.

#### 5.2. Enhancing Realism and Plausibility in Motion Production

One of the biggest challenges in generated 3D human motion is ensuring physical plausibility and avoiding artifacts. TMR directly addresses this by facilitating the retrieval of *real*, high-quality motion capture data. This has immediate and profound impact on industries like gaming, animation, and virtual reality, where realism is paramount but creating diverse, high-quality motions manually is time-consuming and expensive. It provides a robust "search engine" for existing motion assets.

#### 5.3. Improving Accessibility and Management of Motion Data

Large motion capture datasets are valuable but often difficult to navigate. TMR enables natural language-based search, effectively turning raw motion data into a searchable database. This can drastically improve workflows for animators, researchers, and developers, making it easier to find specific actions or styles of movement within vast archives. It can also assist in automatically indexing and semi-automating the labeling of new motion data.

#### 5.4. Advancing Cross-Modal Learning in Niche Domains

TMR's success, particularly its effective handling of "wrong negatives" through filtering, provides a blueprint for adapting general contrastive learning frameworks to domain-specific challenges where textual descriptions may be highly similar or ambiguous. This methodological contribution could be generalized to other fine-grained multimodal datasets beyond human motion.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research and Applications

- **Motion Grounding:** The emergent zero-shot moment retrieval capability is highly significant. It suggests that TMR's learned joint embedding space possesses a deep understanding of how language maps to specific temporal segments of motion. This could pave the way for future research into robust motion grounding, action localization, and even motion understanding for robotics or assistive technologies.
- **Symmetrical Frameworks:** The paper explicitly suggests future work incorporating a language synthesis branch to create a fully symmetrical framework (text-to-motion synthesis/retrieval and motion-to-text synthesis/retrieval). This would push towards truly unified motion-language models.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The authors acknowledge the limitation of relatively small motion-text datasets compared to image-text counterparts. TMR's robust performance despite this limitation, combined with the call for more data augmentation or larger datasets, highlights a critical area for community effort.
- **Industrial Adoption:** The public availability of code and models greatly reduces the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, accelerating further innovation and potential industrial adoption of text-based motion search.

In conclusion, TMR is a pivotal step in bridging the gap between natural language and 3D human motion, offering a powerful and practical solution for motion retrieval while simultaneously laying the groundwork for more sophisticated motion understanding and generation systems.
