# MOKA: Open-World Robotic Manipulation through Mark-Based Visual Prompting

> [!summary] Summary
> MOKA enables robotic systems to perform diverse manipulation tasks in novel, unstructured environments by directly leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) through a mark-based visual prompting approach. The system achieved comparable or superior success rates to baselines in zero-shot settings and demonstrated the capacity to generate high-quality demonstration data for distilling knowledge into learned policies.

## Problem

- Robotic systems struggle with open-world generalization, performing diverse tasks specified by humans in unstructured and previously unseen environments.
- Bridging the gap between the abstract understanding of powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the concrete physical requirements of robotic control remains a challenge.
- Prior VLM/LLM-based approaches often convert visual inputs to language, losing crucial visual details, or require extensive data/prompt engineering for new tasks.

## Method

- **MOKA** introduces a compact, point-based affordance representation on 2D images that unifies various manipulation skills, acting as an interface between VLM predictions and robot actions.
- It employs a two-stage hierarchical VLM reasoning framework: high-level task decomposition followed by low-level, detailed affordance prediction for subtasks.
- A novel mark-based visual prompting strategy converts the continuous problem of 2D point generation into a multiple-choice visual question-answering task for the VLM, using visually annotated candidate points and grid regions.

## Results

- MOKA Zero-Shot achieved comparable or superior success rates to baselines (Code-as-Policies, VoxPoser) on multi-stage manipulation tasks, particularly excelling in tool-use scenarios.
- Providing just 2-3 successful examples via MOKA In-Context learning significantly boosted success rates across tasks, frequently reaching **0.9** or **1.0**.
- Policy distillation, using 50 MOKA-generated trajectories to fine-tune a pre-trained robot foundation model (Octo), produced an end-to-end policy that matched or exceeded MOKA's in-context performance.
- The system demonstrated robustness to variations in language instructions, initial object arrangements, and object types (including deformable or transparent items).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly leveraging visual prompting on pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can enable zero-shot, open-world robotic manipulation without task-specific training.
> - A unified, point-based affordance representation can effectively bridge VLM predictions and diverse robot actions for object-centric manipulation.
> - A training-free VLM approach like MOKA can generate high-quality demonstration data to bootstrap and distill knowledge into learned visuomotor policies for improved robustness and efficiency.
> - Hierarchical reasoning and structured visual prompts are crucial for guiding VLMs to make precise, actionable predictions for complex manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is striving towards "open-world generalization," where robots can perform diverse tasks specified by humans in unstructured and previously unseen environments. This pursuit necessitates that robotic systems possess a profound understanding of the physical world and the nuances of human commands. Traditionally, achieving such generalization has been challenging due to the need for extensive task-specific data and finely tuned control policies.

Recent advancements in large pre-trained models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs), have presented unprecedented opportunities for addressing open-world problems. These models, trained on vast internet-scale datasets, exhibit remarkable emergent capabilities in conceptual understanding, commonsense reasoning, and language comprehension. They have revolutionized areas of natural language processing and computer vision by demonstrating strong zero-shot and few-shot learning abilities.

However, a significant gap persists in directly applying these powerful models to robotic control. Existing pre-trained models, despite their intelligence, inherently lack the understanding of 3D spatial relationships, contact physics, and the intricacies of real-world robotic embodiment and environment dynamics. Prior approaches to bridge this gap often fall into several categories:
- **Language-only reasoning:** Many methods first convert raw sensory inputs into high-level language descriptions and then query LLMs for planning (e.g., Code-as-Policies, Inner Monologue, VoxPoser). While effective, this translation process can discard crucial visual details vital for precise manipulation. These methods also often require non-trivial effort in designing in-context examples to guide LLM behavior.
- **Direct action prediction:** Some approaches fine-tune or train large transformer models to directly predict robot actions from visual inputs (e.g., RT-2, BC-Z). These typically demand extensive, often simulation-generated, training data for open-world scenarios.
- **Foundation models for specific modules:** Other works use foundation models to generate rewards, code, or value functions, which then guide lower-level robotic controllers.

MOKA distinguishes itself by directly leveraging the visual reasoning capabilities of VLMs (specifically GPT-4V) on raw image observations, rather than relying solely on text-based representations of the environment. Its key innovation lies in proposing an *intermediate point-based affordance representation* that bridges the VLM's image predictions with the robot's physical actions. This representation is designed to be both interpretable by the VLM and directly executable by a robot's motion planner.

Crucially, MOKA introduces a novel *mark-based visual prompting* technique. This allows the VLM to interpret complex manipulation problems as a series of visual question-answering (VQA) tasks, transforming the challenge of generating continuous coordinates into a more tractable multiple-choice selection problem. This approach stands in contrast to prior works that either use pre-defined low-level skills (Hu et al.) or require substantial fine-tuning. By avoiding explicit model training for task execution, MOKA offers a training-free, zero-shot solution, while also demonstrating the capacity to *generate high-quality data* for distilling knowledge into learned policies, further pushing the boundaries of generalist robot learning. This positions MOKA as a significant step towards truly open-world, adaptable robotic systems that can learn from and interact with diverse real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the MOKA research is to enable robotic systems to perform a wide variety of **novel manipulation tasks** in **open-world environments**, specified by **free-form language instructions**. The authors aim to achieve this by effectively harnessing the advanced capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

The core motivations driving this work stem from critical challenges in contemporary robotics:

- **Bridging the Perception-Action Gap in Open Worlds:** Traditional robotic systems often struggle with generalization to unseen objects, environments, and tasks. Humans intuitively understand how to manipulate objects based on their appearance and function (affordances) and can easily adapt to new scenarios. Replicating this "commonsense understanding" in robots is difficult. The paper seeks to bridge the gap between the rich conceptual understanding and commonsense knowledge encoded within VLMs (learned from vast internet data) and the physical interaction requirements of robotic control.
- **Leveraging VLMs for Direct Robotic Control:** While VLMs have shown remarkable progress in understanding and generating text and images, their direct application to robot control is not straightforward. They lack an inherent understanding of 3D spatial geometry, real-world physics, and robot kinematics. The motivation is to devise an effective interface that allows VLMs to generate actionable commands from visual observations and language instructions, bypassing the limitations of purely language-based planning which can lose critical visual details.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - Many prior approaches rely on converting visual observations into high-level textual descriptions for LLMs, which can lead to a loss of nuanced visual information crucial for precise manipulation.
 - Existing methods often necessitate considerable manual effort in designing detailed in-context examples (prompt engineering) or collecting vast datasets for fine-tuning large models for each new task. MOKA aims to minimize this burden by enabling zero-shot and few-shot learning directly from the VLM's pre-trained knowledge.
- **Developing a Compact and Executable Affordance Representation:** A fundamental motivation is to find an intermediate representation that is both easily predictable by a VLM from visual observations and language, and simultaneously capable of compactly capturing the necessary information for a robot to execute a desired motion. This representation should be versatile enough to cover a wide range of manipulation skills.

In essence, MOKA is motivated by the vision of intelligent robots that can interpret human commands naturally, perceive their surroundings effectively, and perform complex manipulations in novel, unstructured settings by directly tapping into the extensive knowledge embedded within powerful pre-trained VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MOKA (Marking Open-world Keypoint Affordances) proposes a novel methodology that integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robotic manipulation through a unique combination of point-based affordance representations and mark-based visual prompting within a hierarchical reasoning framework.

**A. Point-Based Affordance Representations:**
At the core of MOKA is a compact, point-based affordance representation defined on 2D images, which serves as the crucial interface between the VLM's abstract predictions and the robot's physical actions. This representation is unified across diverse manipulation tasks and avoids the need for task-specific motion primitives. It specifies robot motion in an object-centric manner, considering two types of objects: `o_in-hand` (grasped object or tool) and `o_unattached` (target object to be interacted with).
The representation consists of:
- **Keypoints:**
 - `x_grasp`: The 2D point on `o_in-hand` where the robot gripper should grasp.
 - `x_function`: The 2D point on `o_in-hand` (or robot gripper if no tool) that will make contact with `o_unattached`.
 - `x_target`: The 2D point on `o_unattached` that will be contacted by `x_function` (or the target location for placement).
- **Waypoints:**
 - `x_pre-contact`: A 2D point in free space before making contact.
 - `x_post-contact`: A 2D point in free space after making contact.
- **Additional Attributes:** Textual descriptions for orientation (`R_grasp`, `R_manipulate`, specified as discrete options like "forward," "above") and height for free-space waypoints ("same" or "above" target point).
The VLM's final output for a subtask is a structured dictionary containing these predicted attributes.

**B. Hierarchical Affordance Reasoning with VLMs:**
MOKA employs a two-stage hierarchical prompting framework to leverage the VLM (specifically GPT-4V, given its strong VQA capabilities) for affordance reasoning:
1. **High-Level Reasoning:** Given the initial RGB observation (`s_0`) and the free-form language task description (`l`), the VLM is first prompted to decompose the complex task into a sequence of simpler subtasks. The output (`y_high`) is a structured list of dictionaries, where each dictionary describes a subtask with fields like `instruction`, `object_grasped` (e.g., "broom"), `object_unattached` (e.g., "trash"), and `motion_direction` (e.g., "to the right"). This provides a high-level plan and identifies relevant objects for the next stage.
2. **Low-Level Reasoning:** For each subtask identified by the high-level reasoning, the VLM is queried again. It takes the specific subtask description (`y_k_high`) and a *processed visual observation* (`f(s_t(k))`, which is the mark-annotated image) as input. The VLM then produces the detailed point-based affordance representation (`y_k_low`) as a dictionary. Crucially, the VLM predicts 2D coordinates on the image along with textual attributes like height and orientation, which are then deprojected into 3D space for robot execution.

**C. Mark-Based Visual Prompting:**
To enable the VLM to predict 2D points effectively, MOKA introduces a novel mark-based visual prompting strategy, converting the continuous point generation problem into tractable multiple-choice visual question-answering.
- **Keypoint Marking:** For `o_in-hand` and `o_unattached` identified in the high-level reasoning, MOKA uses GroundedSAM (combining GroundingDINO for object detection and SAM for segmentation) to obtain segmentation masks. On the contours and at the geometric center of these masks, a set of `K+1` candidate keypoints are sampled using farthest point sampling. These candidate points are then visually annotated on the RGB image with distinct labels (e.g., "P1," "Q2") and colors, allowing the VLM to select from these discrete options.
- **Waypoint Marking:** To specify free-space waypoints, the observed RGB image is overlaid with an `m x n` grid (e.g., 5x5), where each tile is labeled (e.g., "a1," "b2"). The VLM is prompted to choose the appropriate grid tiles for `x_pre-contact` and `x_post-contact`, and the exact 3D waypoint is then sampled uniformly within the selected tile, with its height determined by the VLM's textual output.

**D. Motion Generation with Predicted Affordances:**
Once the VLM predicts the 2D keypoints, waypoints, and attributes, they are converted into executable robot motions:
- 2D keypoints on objects are lifted to 3D using the corresponding depth image and camera parameters.
- Free-space waypoints are deprojected using the VLM-predicted height relative to the target.
- Robot grasping involves selecting a robust grasp from a set of analytical proposals (similar to Dex-Net 2.0) that is closest to the VLM-predicted `x_grasp`.
- The robot's end-effector follows a smooth trajectory, moving `x_function` sequentially through `x_pre-contact`, `x_target`, and `x_post-contact`, while maintaining the VLM-specified orientations.

**E. Bootstrapping Through Physical Interactions:**
MOKA further explores methods to improve performance by leveraging real-world experience:
- **In-context learning:** Successful robot trajectories collected by MOKA are converted into examples (annotated images and VLM responses). A handful (e.g., 2-3) of these examples are appended to the VLM prompts, guiding its reasoning for new tasks.
- **Policy Distillation:** MOKA's successful trajectories are used as demonstration data to fine-tune a pre-trained generalist robot policy (e.g., Octo). This allows for distilling the VLM's knowledge into a learned visuomotor policy that can operate end-to-end without direct VLM queries, potentially offering more robust and lower-latency execution.

This comprehensive approach allows MOKA to perform open-world manipulation tasks in a flexible, zero-shot, and few-shot manner, directly leveraging the visual and conceptual understanding of large pre-trained models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on various tabletop manipulation tasks demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of MOKA across different settings.

**A. Quantitative Evaluation:**
MOKA was quantitatively evaluated against two prominent baselines, Code-as-Policies and VoxPoser, on four multi-stage manipulation tasks (Table Wiping, Watch Cleaning, Gift Preparation, Laptop Packing), totaling eight distinct subtasks.
- **Zero-Shot Performance:** MOKA Zero-Shot consistently achieved comparable or superior success rates compared to both baselines. Notably, for tool-use tasks (like table wiping, subtask 2), MOKA maintained a strong performance (0.6 success rate) where VoxPoser failed (0.0 success rate), indicating its better handling of complex interactions. This highlights MOKA's ability to effectively reason about affordances for unseen tasks without specific prior training.
- **In-Context Learning Improvement:** MOKA In-Context, which involved providing two successful examples to GPT-4V, demonstrated significant and consistent improvements across almost all subtasks. Its success rates frequently reached 0.9 or 1.0, indicating that even a small number of relevant in-context examples can substantially boost the VLM's reasoning and prediction accuracy.
- **Policy Distillation Success:** MOKA Distilled, achieved by fine-tuning the Octo foundation model with 50 successful trajectories collected by MOKA, showed excellent performance, often matching or exceeding MOKA In-Context. This is a crucial finding, suggesting that MOKA can reliably generate high-quality demonstration data that is valuable for training end-to-end visuomotor policies. It implies that the knowledge from a training-free VLM approach can be effectively "distilled" into a learned policy, offering a path to robust, potentially lower-latency robot control.

**B. Failure Analysis:**
The paper categorized failures into two types:
- **Reasoning failures:** Incorrect predictions from the VLM (e.g., grasping the tool upside down, wrong target point).
- **Execution failures:** Robot fails to complete the task despite correct VLM predictions (e.g., gripper narrowly missing the target, object slipping).
The analysis revealed that both in-context learning and policy distillation significantly reduced the total number of failures. Policy distillation, by entirely replacing the VLM in the execution loop with a learned policy, inherently eliminated reasoning failures. This further validates the quality of MOKA's generated data for imitation learning.

**C. Qualitative Evaluation and Robustness:**
Qualitative results illustrate MOKA's ability to generate appropriate motions for diverse skills such as pressing buttons, closing lids, object rearrangement, and various forms of tool use.
- **Robustness to Instruction Variation:** MOKA demonstrated consistent keypoint and waypoint predictions even when tasks were specified with different free-form language instructions.
- **Robustness to Initial Arrangements:** The system showed resilience to variations in the initial poses and configurations of objects on the tabletop.
- **Robustness to Object Variation:** MOKA successfully adapted to different objects with varying geometries, colors, and materials, including deformable or transparent items, underscoring its generalizability in open-world scenarios.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies confirmed the importance of key design choices:
- **Hierarchical Reasoning:** Removing the high-level task decomposition (MOKA w/o hierarchy) drastically reduced performance, indicating its critical role in structuring complex tasks and narrowing down the VLM's focus.
- **Prompt Design:** Removing the explicit descriptions of keypoint definitions or the "chain-of-thought" prompting (MOKA w/o description of points, MOKA w/o CoT) also led to performance drops, affirming the value of detailed and structured prompts in eliciting better VLM reasoning.

Overall, the findings strongly support MOKA's premise: that pre-trained VLMs, when appropriately prompted with mark-based visual cues and a structured affordance representation, can effectively guide robots to solve a wide range of novel manipulation tasks in a highly generalizable manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MOKA represents a significant step forward in the quest for open-world robotic manipulation, primarily due to its novel integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robot control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Visual Prompting for Robotics:** MOKA is touted as the first work to explicitly leverage "visual prompting" on pre-trained VLMs for open-world robot manipulation. This establishes a new paradigm for interfacing powerful general-purpose vision models with robotic systems, moving beyond purely language-based instructions or text-based scene descriptions. This direct visual grounding allows for capturing more nuanced visual details crucial for manipulation.
- **Enabling Training-Free Zero-Shot Generalization:** By intelligently querying large pre-trained VLMs, MOKA enables robots to tackle entirely novel tasks and interact with unseen objects in a zero-shot manner, without requiring any task-specific data collection or model fine-tuning. This dramatically reduces the engineering effort and data requirements typically associated with deploying robots in diverse environments.
- **Unified and Flexible Affordance Representation:** The proposed point-based affordance representation is a key technical contribution. It offers a compact and unified way to specify a wide range of manipulation skills (grasping, pushing, placing, tool use) directly from VLM predictions. This versatility eliminates the need for a library of pre-defined motion primitives, leading to a more generalizable system.
- **A "Data Generator" for Robot Learning:** One of MOKA's most impactful findings is its ability to serve as a high-quality "data generator" for learning-based robot policies. By distilling the knowledge from MOKA's successful zero-shot trials into a pre-trained robot foundation model (like Octo), the authors demonstrate that an effective end-to-end policy can be trained. This opens up an exciting pathway for continuous improvement systems, where the VLM initially bootstraps performance by generating diverse demonstrations, which then train a more robust and efficient learned policy. This could accelerate data collection for complex open-world tasks.
- **Reduced Reliance on Complex Perception Pipelines:** By leveraging a single, powerful VLM (GPT-4V) for both high-level reasoning and low-level affordance prediction from raw images, MOKA simplifies the overall system architecture. This contrasts with traditional approaches that often require multiple, distinct perception modules (e.g., object detection, pose estimation, scene graphing) feeding into a separate language model for planning.
- **Inspiring Future Research:** MOKA's limitations also point towards promising avenues for future work. It highlights the need for VLMs with a deeper understanding of 3D space, contact physics, and precise robotic control. Overcoming the high latency of VLM queries for dynamic manipulation, and extending the affordance representation to more complex scenarios like bi-manual or whole-body control, are clear next steps. The success of MOKA suggests that continued advancements in generalist VLMs and robotic foundation models will directly translate to more capable robotic systems.

In summary, MOKA provides a practical and innovative framework that unlocks the immense potential of pre-trained VLMs for real-world robotic manipulation. Its training-free nature, robust performance, and proven capability as a data generation tool position it as a foundational work that could significantly influence the development of more autonomous, adaptable, and human-friendly robotic agents in open-world settings.
